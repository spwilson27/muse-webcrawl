The Menil Collection is a museum and neighbourhood campus in Houston, Texas, housing the art collection of philanthropists John and Dominique de Menil. Opened in 1987 in a building designed by Renzo Piano, the museum holds roughly 17,000 paintings, sculptures, prints, drawings, photographs, and rare books, spanning Paleolithic and Byzantine antiquities, the arts of Africa, Oceania, and the Indigenous Americas, and major holdings of Surrealism and modern and contemporary art. Admission is free, in keeping with the de Menils' philosophy of art as a spiritual and public good.

The collectors

John de Menil, an executive of the Schlumberger oilfield services company, and his wife Dominique, born a Schlumberger heiress, settled in Houston in the 1940s and became leading patrons of art, architecture, and civil rights. Inspired by the Dominican priest Marie-Alain Couturier, they collected with an eye to the spiritual dimension of art, commissioned works from Mark Rothko and others, and founded institutions including the Institute for the Arts at Rice University.

Campus

The 30-acre campus includes the main museum building; the Cy Twombly Gallery (1995, also by Piano); the Byzantine Fresco Chapel (1997–2012), built to house two 13th-century Cypriot frescoes that were returned to the Church of Cyprus in 2012; the Menil Drawing Institute (2018); and the nearby Rothko Chapel (1971), a non-denominational chapel with fourteen Rothko paintings, in front of which stands Barnett Newman's Broken Obelisk, dedicated to Martin Luther King Jr. The surrounding blocks of grey-painted bungalows are owned by the foundation, preserving the residential character Dominique de Menil intended.

Holdings

Particular strengths include one of the world's finest Surrealist collections, with major works by Max Ernst, René Magritte, and Yves Tanguy; antiquities and Byzantine art; African art; and contemporary works by artists the de Menils championed early, such as Cy Twombly and Robert Rauschenberg.
