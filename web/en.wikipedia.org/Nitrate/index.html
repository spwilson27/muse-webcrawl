Nitrate

Nitrate is a polyatomic ion with the chemical formula NO3−. Salts containing this ion are called nitrates. Nitrates are common components of fertilizers in agriculture and are also used as oxidizing agents and in explosives.

Structure

The ion consists of a central nitrogen atom surrounded by three identically bonded oxygen atoms in a trigonal planar arrangement, with the negative charge delocalised over the oxygens. Nearly all inorganic nitrate salts are soluble in water at standard temperature and pressure.

Occurrence and production

Natural deposits of sodium nitrate ("Chile saltpetre") were historically mined in the Atacama Desert. Today most nitrate is produced by the oxidation of ammonia via the Ostwald process to nitric acid, which is then neutralised to form salts such as ammonium nitrate and potassium nitrate.

Uses and health

Nitrates are essential plant nutrients and key fertilizer ingredients; dietary nitrate from vegetables is converted in the body to nitric oxide with cardiovascular effects. Excess nitrate runoff can cause eutrophication of waterways, and high levels in drinking water are regulated because of the risk of methemoglobinemia in infants.
