Interquartile range

In descriptive statistics, the interquartile range (IQR) is a measure of statistical dispersion, the spread of the data. It is defined as the difference between the third quartile (Q3, the 75th percentile) and the first quartile (Q1, the 25th percentile):

IQR = Q3 − Q1

The IQR describes the range of the middle 50% of the data. Unlike the overall range, it is not strongly affected by outliers, which makes it a useful robust measure of spread, particularly for skewed distributions.

Use

The IQR is displayed directly in a box plot (box-and-whisker plot), where the box spans from Q1 to Q3 with a line at the median. John Tukey's convention identifies outliers as data points lying more than 1.5 × IQR below Q1 or above Q3 (a "1.5 IQR rule"), and these are typically plotted individually beyond the whiskers.

Quartile definitions

There are several methods in use for computing quartiles from a finite data set (among them Tukey's hinges and various percentile interpolation methods adopted by statistical software), which can give slightly different values on small data sets. For continuous distributions the quartiles are defined exactly: Q1 and Q3 are the values at cumulative probabilities 0.25 and 0.75. For a normal distribution, the IQR is approximately 1.349 standard deviations.
