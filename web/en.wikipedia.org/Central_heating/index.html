Central heating

Central heating is a system that provides warmth to multiple spaces within a building (and optionally domestic hot water) from one main source of heat, as opposed to heating each room with separate stoves or fires. Heat is distributed through water, steam, or air circulated by pipes and ducts.

History

Ancient precedents include the Roman hypocaust, which circulated hot air under floors, and the Korean ondol. Modern central heating developed in the 19th century with steam and hot-water systems in factories, hospitals, and large houses; the invention of the radiator and improved boilers made it common in homes during the 20th century.

Systems

In a hydronic system, a boiler heats water that is pumped through radiators or underfloor pipes; heat sources include natural gas, oil, electricity, biomass, and increasingly heat pumps. Forced-air systems, common in North America, push heated air through ducts from a furnace and can be combined with air conditioning. District heating distributes heat from a central plant to many buildings. Controls such as thermostats and thermostatic radiator valves regulate temperature room by room.
