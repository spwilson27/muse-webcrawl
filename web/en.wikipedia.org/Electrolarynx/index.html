An electrolarynx, sometimes called a "throat vibrator", is a handheld medical device that produces vibrations used as a substitute voice source for people who have lost their larynx, usually following a total laryngectomy for laryngeal cancer.

Use

The device is held against the neck or cheek, or coupled to the mouth by a small tube. Its vibrating head transmits sound into the tissues of the throat and mouth, and the user shapes the buzzing sound into speech using the tongue, lips and palate, much as with normal articulation. Pitch and volume controls allow limited variation in tone.

Characteristics

Speech produced with an electrolarynx is intelligible but typically sounds mechanical and monotone. Alternatives include oesophageal speech and tracheoesophageal voice prostheses. Electrolarynges are valued for being usable soon after surgery and requiring relatively little training.

History

Electrolarynx devices date to the 1930s and 1940s, with early models developed from doorbell-like vibrators; they became widely available after the Second World War.
