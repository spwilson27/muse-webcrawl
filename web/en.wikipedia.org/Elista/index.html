Elista

Elista is the capital city of the Republic of Kalmykia, Russia. With a population of roughly 100,000, it sits on the Kalmyk steppe in the southwestern part of the country, near the Caspian lowlands.

History

Founded in 1865, Elista grew as an administrative centre for the Kalmyks, a Mongolic people of Oirat origin. In 1943 the entire Kalmyk population was deported to Siberia by Stalin; the city was renamed Stepnoy and only regained its name and people after their return in 1957.

Culture

Elista is notable as a centre of Buddhism — Kalmykia is one of the few predominantly Buddhist regions of Europe — and is home to the Geden Sheddup Choikorling monastery and the Golden Abode of the Buddha Shakyamuni temple. Under long-serving leader Kirsan Ilyumzhinov, the city also became known for chess, hosting the 1998 Chess Olympiad and a purpose-built Chess City district.
