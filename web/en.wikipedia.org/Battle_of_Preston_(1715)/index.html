The Battle of Preston took place on 9–14 November 1715 during the Jacobite rising of 1715 in England. Government forces under Generals Charles Wills and George Carpenter besieged a Jacobite force in the town of Preston, Lancashire, and compelled its surrender, ending the rising in England.

Background

A combined force of English Jacobites and Scottish Borderers under Thomas Forster and the Highland chief William Mackintosh of Borlum marched south into Lancashire, hoping to rally support, and occupied Preston. Their numbers grew to around 3,000–4,000 men, but the expected mass rising of English Jacobites failed to materialise.

The battle

Government troops attacked the barricaded streets on 12 November and were repulsed with loss in fierce house-to-house fighting, in which the Jacobite commander Colonel Henry Oxburgh's men held the churchyard and barricades. Reinforced by Carpenter, Wills surrounded the town; after negotiations and internal disagreement, the Jacobites surrendered on 14 November.

Aftermath

Some 1,500 prisoners were taken. Several leaders, including Oxburgh and later Lord Derwentwater and Lord Kenmure (captured in Scotland), were executed, and many rank and file were transported. Together with the indecisive Battle of Sheriffmuir fought the same day, Preston ended the Fifteen.
