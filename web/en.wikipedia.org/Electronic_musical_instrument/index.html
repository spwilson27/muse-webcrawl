Electronic musical instrument

An electronic musical instrument is a musical instrument that produces sound using electronic circuits. Such instruments generate an electrical audio signal that is amplified and heard through a loudspeaker, distinguishing them from traditional acoustic instruments and from electro-acoustic instruments that merely amplify a mechanical vibration.

History

Early examples include the Telharmonium (1897), the theremin (1920), the ondes Martenot and the Hammond organ. The development of the transistor and later digital technology led to the modern synthesizer, popularised by instruments from Moog and others in the 1960s and 1970s.

Types

They include synthesizers, drum machines, samplers, electronic pianos and organs, and computer-based instruments controlled via MIDI. Electronic instruments are central to genres such as electronic dance music, synth-pop and hip hop.
