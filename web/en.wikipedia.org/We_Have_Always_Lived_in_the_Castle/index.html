We Have Always Lived in the Castle

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

We Have Always Lived in the Castle is a 1962 mystery novel by American author Shirley Jackson. It was Jackson's final work, published with a dedication to Pascal Covici, her editor at Viking Press, three years before the author's death in 1965. The novel is widely regarded as Jackson's masterpiece and a classic of Gothic and psychological horror literature.

Plot

Eighteen-year-old Mary Katherine "Merricat" Blackwood lives on the family estate in Vermont with her older sister Constance and their uncle Julian. Six years earlier, the rest of the Blackwood family died after arsenic was put in the family sugar bowl; Constance was tried and acquitted of the murders, but the villagers remain hostile to the surviving sisters. Merricat, the narrator, practices protective magic, burying objects around the grounds and nailing a book to a tree. The arrival of their cousin Charles Blackwood, who courts Constance and seeks the family fortune, disrupts the household and precipitates a fire and a final confrontation with the village. The novel gradually reveals that it was Merricat herself who poisoned the family.

Themes and reception

The novel explores isolation, ritual, agoraphobia, and the persecution of women who live outside social norms. Critics have praised its unreliable narration and fairy-tale atmosphere. It was named by Time magazine as one of the "Ten Best Novels" of 1962 and has grown steadily in reputation, frequently appearing on lists of the finest horror novels.

Adaptations

The novel was adapted into a stage play by Hugh Wheeler in 1966 and into a film directed by Stacie Passon, which premiered in 2018 and stars Taissa Farmiga as Merricat, Alexandra Daddario as Constance, and Sebastian Stan as Charles.
