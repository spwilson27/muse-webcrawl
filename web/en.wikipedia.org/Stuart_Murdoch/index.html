Stuart Murdoch

Stuart Lee Murdoch (born 25 August 1968) is a Scottish musician, writer and filmmaker. He is the lead singer, songwriter and principal creative force of the indie pop band Belle and Sebastian, which he founded in Glasgow in 1996. Murdoch's literate, narrative songwriting and the band's gentle, orchestral pop style have made Belle and Sebastian one of the most acclaimed acts in British independent music.

Early life and illness

Murdoch grew up in Ayr and studied at the University of Glasgow. In his early twenties he developed chronic fatigue syndrome (ME), which forced him to give up work and study for seven years; he has described being housebound, living on benefits and writing songs during his recovery. Many of Belle and Sebastian's early songs were written during this period of convalescence, drawing on his experiences of isolation, faith and observation of everyday life.

Belle and Sebastian

In 1996 Murdoch and bassist Stuart David formed Belle and Sebastian as part of a college music business course, recording the debut album Tigermilk in three days; the follow-up, If You're Feeling Sinister (1996), is widely regarded as a classic of the genre. The band won the BRIT Award for Best Newcomer in 1999 — famously defeating Steps in a public vote — and went on to release albums including The Boy with the Arab Strap (1998), Dear Catastrophe Waitress (2003), The Life Pursuit (2006) and A Bit of Previous (2022).

Other work

Murdoch directed the musical film God Help the Girl (2014), which grew out of his side project of the same name, and published a memoir, The Celestial Café (2010), drawn from his online diaries. His fiction novel Nobody's Empire (2025) draws on his experience of ME. He is also known for his long-running interest in long-distance running and for organising the Boaty Weekender festival cruise.
