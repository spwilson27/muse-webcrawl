Schwarzian derivative


Image placeholder: Schwarzian derivative

In mathematics, the Schwarzian derivative is a differential operator that is invariant under Möbius transformations, that is, under linear fractional transformations of the complex plane. It is named after the German mathematician Hermann Schwarz. For a holomorphic function f with non-vanishing derivative, the Schwarzian derivative is defined by S(f) = (f′′/f′)′ − (1/2)(f′′/f′)2.

Properties

The Schwarzian derivative of a function vanishes identically if and only if the function is a Möbius transformation. It satisfies a chain rule (cocycle property) that involves the Schwarzian of the composition and the inner function, which makes it central to the theory of univalent functions, Teichmüller theory and the study of projective structures on Riemann surfaces. A classical bound, Nehari's theorem, gives conditions on the size of the Schwarzian that guarantee univalence of a function on the unit disk.

Applications

The Schwarzian appears in conformal field theory and string theory, where it governs the transformation of the stress-energy tensor under conformal maps and the Virasoro algebra's central charge; it also arises in the theory of iterated function systems and one-dimensional dynamics, where a negative Schwarzian derivative controls the number of attracting cycles of interval maps (Singer's theorem).
