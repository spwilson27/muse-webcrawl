Respiratory acidosis

Respiratory acidosis
SpecialtyPulmonology, intensive care medicine
SymptomsHeadache, confusion, sleepiness, tremor
CausesHypoventilation

Respiratory acidosis is a state in which decreased ventilation (hypoventilation) increases the concentration of carbon dioxide in the blood and decreases the blood's pH (a condition generally called acidosis). Carbon dioxide is produced continuously as the body's cells respire, and it normally accumulates if the lungs do not adequately expel it through alveolar ventilation.

Causes

Respiratory acidosis can result from anything that interferes with ventilation: depression of the central respiratory centre by drugs such as opioids or by brain injury; neuromuscular disorders such as myasthenia gravis and Guillain–Barré syndrome; airway obstruction; and severe lung disease, including advanced chronic obstructive pulmonary disease and pneumonia. Acute respiratory acidosis develops quickly, whereas in chronic respiratory acidosis the kidneys compensate by retaining bicarbonate.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is by arterial blood gas analysis, which shows an elevated partial pressure of carbon dioxide (PaCO2) and a reduced pH. Treatment is directed at the underlying cause and at improving ventilation, which may require oxygen therapy, non-invasive ventilation, or mechanical ventilation in severe cases.
