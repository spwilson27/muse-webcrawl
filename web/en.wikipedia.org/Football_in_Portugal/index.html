Football in Portugal

Football is the most popular sport in Portugal, where it has been played since the late nineteenth century. The sport is governed by the Portuguese Football Federation (FPF), and its top division is the Primeira Liga. Portuguese football is dominated by the "Big Three" clubs — SL Benfica, FC Porto and Sporting CP — who have won nearly every league title between them.

History

Football was introduced to Portugal by Portuguese students returning from England and by British expatriates; the first recorded match took place in 1875 in Madeira, and the game spread through Lisbon and Porto in the 1880s and 1890s. Benfica and Sporting were both founded in the first decade of the twentieth century and FC Porto in 1893. The national championship began in the 1930s.

Club success

Portuguese clubs have achieved notable European success. Benfica won the European Cup in 1961 and 1962 under Béla Guttmann, led by Eusébio. FC Porto won the European Cup in 1987 and the UEFA Champions League in 2004 under José Mourinho, as well as the UEFA Cup/Europa League in 2003 and 2011.

National team

The Portugal national team finished third at the 1966 World Cup and reached the final of Euro 2004 on home soil. Portugal won its first major trophy at UEFA Euro 2016, beating France in the final, and won the inaugural UEFA Nations League in 2019. Cristiano Ronaldo, born in Madeira, became the most-capped male international footballer and the top international goalscorer in history.
