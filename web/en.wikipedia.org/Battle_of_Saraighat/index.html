Battle of Saraighat

Date1671
LocationSaraighat, on the Brahmaputra River near Guwahati, Assam, India
ResultDecisive Ahom victory
BelligerentsAhom Kingdom vs. Mughal Empire
CommandersLachit Borphukan vs. Ram Singh I of Amber

The Battle of Saraighat was a naval battle fought in 1671 between the Ahom Kingdom and the Mughal Empire on the Brahmaputra River at Saraighat, near modern Guwahati in Assam, India. It was the decisive engagement of the Ahom–Mughal conflicts: the Ahom fleet under the commander Lachit Borphukan destroyed the invading Mughal force, recovered Guwahati, and effectively ended Mughal ambitions in Assam.

Background

The Mughals had occupied Guwahati in 1662 under Mir Jumla's invasion, and the humiliating Treaty of Ghilajharighat imposed heavy tribute on the Ahom king. King Chakradhwaj Singha, determined to expel the Mughals, appointed Lachit Borphukan as commander and rebuilt the Ahom army and river fleet. The Ahoms retook Guwahati in 1667. The Mughal emperor Aurangzeb responded by sending a large expedition under Ram Singh I of Amber, which reached Assam in 1669 and spent two years in inconclusive campaigning. Lachit, famous for his preparations — including the rampart built in a single night and the execution of his own uncle for shirking the work ("my uncle is not greater than my country") — chose the narrow, fast-flowing stretch of the Brahmaputra at Saraighat, where the Mughal superiority in cavalry and heavy ships would be least effective.

Battle

In the final naval action of 1671, Lachit, though seriously ill, had himself carried aboard his boat to rally his men when the Ahom line wavered before the Mughal attack. The sight of their commander, and his call to fight to the death, turned the battle: the Ahom war boats, smaller and more manoeuvrable in the currents, overwhelmed the Mughal flotilla. Ram Singh withdrew, pursued by the Ahoms westward to the Manas River, which became the enduring frontier between Assam and the Mughal Empire.

Legacy

Lachit Borphukan died of his illness the following year, in 1672, and is remembered as Assam's greatest national hero; his birthday, Lachit Divas, is celebrated on 24 November, and the best cadet at India's National Defence Academy receives the Lachit Borphukan medal. The Saraighat bridges at Guwahati commemorate the battle. The Ahom kingdom remained independent until the Burmese invasions and British annexation of the 1820s.
