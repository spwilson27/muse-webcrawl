BNY Mellon

The Bank of New York Mellon Corporation, doing business as BNY (formerly BNY Mellon), is an American investment banking services holding company headquartered in New York City. It was formed in 2007 from the merger of the Bank of New York and Mellon Financial Corporation.

History

The Bank of New York was founded in 1784 by Alexander Hamilton, making it one of the oldest banks in the United States. Mellon Financial traced its roots to T. Mellon and Sons, founded in Pittsburgh in 1869 by Thomas Mellon. The 2007 merger created the world's largest custodian bank.

Business

BNY is primarily a custodian and asset-servicing bank, holding tens of trillions of dollars in assets under custody and administration, alongside an asset management business. Its services include securities servicing, treasury services, clearing and collateral management, and wealth management.

Role in the financial system

As one of the global systemically important banks, BNY plays a central role in tri-party repo, payment clearing and the safekeeping of assets for pension funds, sovereign wealth funds and other institutions. See also custodian bank and Alexander Hamilton.
