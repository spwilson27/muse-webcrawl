Tonyukuk inscriptions

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: The Tonyukuk stelae at Bain Tsokto, Mongolia]

The Tonyukuk inscriptions are two stelae erected in the early 8th century in memory — in fact, by the order and in the lifetime — of Tonyukuk (c. 646 – c. 726), chief counsellor to the first khagans of the Second Turkic Khaganate. They stand at Bain Tsokto, near the Tuul River southeast of modern Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia, and are among the oldest known texts written in a Turkic language, in the Old Turkic ("runic") script.

Content

Uniquely among the Orkhon inscriptions, Tonyukuk's text is autobiographical: the aged statesman narrates his own career, from the restoration of the Turkic khaganate by Ilterish Qaghan in 682 through the reigns of Kapagan and Bilge Khagan, describing campaigns against the Chinese Tang dynasty, the Kyrgyz, the Türgesh and the Oghuz, and explaining his strategic decisions — why he advised against building walled cities and adopting Chinese ways, and how small Turkic forces survived against overwhelming numbers. The inscription functions as both memoir and political testament.

Significance

Together with the slightly later Khushoo Tsaidam monuments to Kül Tigin (732) and Bilge Khagan (735), the Tonyukuk stelae form the Orkhon inscriptions corpus, deciphered in 1893 by the Danish linguist Vilhelm Thomsen. The site lies within the UNESCO World Heritage listing "Orkhon Valley Cultural Landscape".
