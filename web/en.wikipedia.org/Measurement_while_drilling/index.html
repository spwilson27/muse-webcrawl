Measurement while drilling

A measurement-while-drilling (MWD) tool is a type of well logging that is incorporated into the drill string of a drilling rig and provides real-time information to help with steering the drill. Data transmitted from the tool may include directional survey information (inclination and azimuth), drilling mechanics data, and formation evaluation measurements such as gamma ray logs.

An MWD tool in the bottom-hole assembly transmits downhole data to the surface in real time.

Data transmission

Most MWD tools transmit data to the surface using mud pulse telemetry: a valve in the tool modulates the flow of drilling fluid ("mud") inside the drill string, encoding data as pressure pulses that travel to the surface and are detected by pressure transducers on the standpipe. Mud pulse data rates are slow — typically a few bits per second — because of attenuation through the mud column. Electromagnetic telemetry, which sends signals through the surrounding rock, is an alternative used especially in onshore and underbalanced drilling.

Measurements

  • Directional: inclination, azimuth, toolface, magnetic and gravity field values, borehole position.
  • Drilling mechanics: weight on bit and torque measured downhole, downhole pressure (ECD), temperature, vibration and shock.
  • Formation evaluation: natural gamma ray, resistivity, density and neutron porosity — these overlap with logging while drilling (LWD) tools.

History

MWD technology was developed in the 1970s, driven by the need for directional survey data in offshore drilling where wireline surveys were costly. Commercial mud-pulse systems appeared around 1978, and the technology became standard practice in directional and horizontal drilling, enabling precise well placement and geosteering.
