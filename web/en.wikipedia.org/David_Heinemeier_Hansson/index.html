David Heinemeier Hansson (born 15 October 1979), also known as DHH, is a Danish programmer, entrepreneur, and racing driver. He is the creator of the Ruby on Rails web application framework and a partner at the software company 37signals (now Basecamp), creators of the project management tool Basecamp and the email service HEY.

Ruby on Rails

Hansson created Ruby on Rails in 2004, extracting the framework from his work on the Basecamp project management application. Rails popularised conventions such as "convention over configuration" and "don't repeat yourself" (DRY), and it became one of the most influential web frameworks. In 2005 Google and O'Reilly named him Hacker of the Year, and he received the Jolt Award for the framework.

Business and writing

With Jason Fried, Hansson co-authored several bestselling business books, including Getting Real (2006), Rework (2010), Remote: Office Not Required (2013), and It Doesn't Have to Be Crazy at Work (2018). He is a vocal critic of venture-capital-funded growth culture and of large cloud providers, and in the 2020s led Basecamp's high-profile move away from public cloud hosting.

Racing

Hansson competes in endurance motor racing. He has raced in the FIA World Endurance Championship and won his class (LMP2) at the 24 Hours of Le Mans in 2014 with the Aston Martin Racing team.
