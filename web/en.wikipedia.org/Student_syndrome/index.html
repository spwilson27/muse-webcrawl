Student syndrome

Student syndrome
Field: Project management, behavioural science
Coined in: Critical chain (Eliyahu Goldratt, 1997)

Student syndrome is the phenomenon in which people start working on a task only at the last possible moment before its deadline — as students cramming the night before an exam — wasting any safety time built into estimates and leaving no buffer for problems.

In critical chain project management

Eliyahu M. Goldratt described the effect in his 1997 novel Critical Chain. Because workers delay starting until urgency forces action, task safety margins are squandered, and delays then propagate through dependent tasks. Critical chain methods counter it by aggregating safety into project buffers and encouraging relay-race task handoffs.

Related concepts

Student syndrome is related to procrastination and Parkinson's law — work expands to fill the time available.
