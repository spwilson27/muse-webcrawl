Kumbhaka

Breath retention is a core technique of prāṇāyāma.

Kumbhaka is the retention of breath in the process of prāṇāyāma, the yogic practice of breath control. The term comes from the Sanskrit kumbha, meaning a pot or vessel — the torso is likened to a vessel that holds the breath.

Types

Classical texts, including the Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā, distinguish several forms:

  • Antara kumbhaka — retention after inhalation, with the lungs full.
  • Bahya kumbhaka — retention after exhalation, with the lungs empty.
  • Kevala kumbhaka — spontaneous, effortless suspension of breath said to arise in advanced meditation.
  • Sahita kumbhaka — retention deliberately combined with inhalation and exhalation, often in fixed ratios and accompanied by bandhas (body locks) and mental focus.

Practice and physiology

Kumbhaka is traditionally practised with the three bandhas — mūla bandha, uḍḍīyāna bandha and jālandhara bandha — and is said to awaken kuṇḍalinī and still the mind. Modern research associates prolonged breath retention with changes in blood carbon dioxide tolerance, autonomic tone and, in freediving-style training, increased erythropoietin production. Teachers caution that long retentions should be learned under supervision because of the risk of fainting.
