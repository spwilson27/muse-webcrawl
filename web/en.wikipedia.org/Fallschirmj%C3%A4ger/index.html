The Fallschirmjäger were the paratrooper branch of the German Luftwaffe before and during World War II. They were the first paratroopers to be committed in large-scale airborne operations, assaulting Denmark, Norway, the Low Countries, and Crete in 1940–41.

Early operations

Fallschirmjäger seized bridges and airfields in the invasions of Denmark and Norway and captured the Belgian fortress of Eben-Emael in May 1940. Their airborne invasion of Crete in May 1941 succeeded but at such heavy cost that large-scale airborne assaults were largely abandoned thereafter.

Later war role

For the rest of the war the Fallschirmjäger fought mainly as elite ground infantry, notably in the defense of Monte Cassino in Italy in 1944, where Allied troops dubbed them the "Green Devils" for their tenacity and feldgrau-green jump smocks.
