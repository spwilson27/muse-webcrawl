Robert E. Howard (1906–1936)

Robert Ervin Howard (January 22, 1906 – June 11, 1936) was an American writer who wrote pulp fiction in a diverse range of genres. He created the character Conan the Barbarian and is regarded as the founding father of the sword and sorcery subgenre.

Life

Howard lived most of his life in Cross Plains, Texas, supporting his family while writing prolifically for magazines such as Weird Tales. He corresponded with H. P. Lovecraft and contributed Cthulhu Mythos stories. On 11 June 1936, on learning that his chronically ill mother would never regain consciousness, Howard took his own life; he was thirty years old.

Works

Howard's creations include Conan the Barbarian, set in the invented Hyborian Age; the Puritan swordsman Solomon Kane; the Pictish king Bran Mak Morn; and the boxer Sailor Steve Costigan. He also wrote historical adventures, westerns and horror.

Legacy

Rediscovered in the 1960s through Lancer paperback editions of the Conan stories, Howard's work became hugely influential on fantasy fiction, heavy metal culture and role-playing games. The Robert E. Howard Foundation and an annual Howard Days festival in Cross Plains preserve his legacy.
