Clark Kerr

Born: May 17, 1911, Stony Creek, Pennsylvania
Died: December 1, 2003 (aged 92)
Offices: Chancellor of UC Berkeley (1952–58); President of the University of California (1958–67)

Clark Kerr (May 17, 1911 – December 1, 2003) was an American economist and academic administrator who served as the first chancellor of the University of California, Berkeley (1952–1958) and as president of the University of California system (1958–1967).

Career

Educated at Swarthmore College and UC Berkeley, Kerr began as a labor economist and wartime labor arbitrator. As Berkeley's first chancellor during the loyalty-oath controversy, he defended academic freedom. As UC president he oversaw explosive growth, the founding of new campuses at San Diego, Irvine, and Santa Cruz, and the 1960 California Master Plan for Higher Education, which he helped architect and which structured the state's three-tier public system.

The multiversity

Kerr's 1963 Godkin Lectures at Harvard, published as The Uses of the University, coined the term "multiversity" for the modern research university's many communities and functions, and included his famous quip that a president's duties amounted to providing "sex for the students, athletics for the alumni, and parking for the faculty." He was dismissed by the UC Regents in 1967 at the urging of incoming governor Ronald Reagan, amid conflicts over the Free Speech Movement and campus unrest.

Later work

Kerr went on to chair the Carnegie Commission on Higher Education and remained a leading voice on university governance until his death in 2003. His memoir, The Gold and the Blue, was published in 2001–2003.
