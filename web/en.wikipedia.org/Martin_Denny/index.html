Martin Denny (April 10, 1911 – March 2, 2005) was an American pianist, composer and arranger. He was a pioneer of the exotica genre of lounge music, blending jazz and Latin rhythms with simulated jungle sounds, bird calls, and instruments drawn from Pacific and Asian traditions. His recording of Les Baxter's "Quiet Village" became a top-five hit in 1959.

Career

Denny studied classical piano as a child and toured South America in the 1930s with the Don Dean Orchestra, absorbing Latin American music. After serving in the U.S. Army Air Forces during World War II, he settled in Hawaii in 1954, where bandleader Henry Kaiser hired him to play at the Shell Bar of the Hawaiian Village Hotel in Waikiki. There his small group — including vibraphonist Arthur Lyman and bassist John Kramer — developed the signature exotica sound, with percussionist Augie Colón imitating frogs and birds from the surrounding gardens.

His debut album Exotica (1957), whose cover featuring model Sandy Warner became iconic, reached number one on the Billboard charts. Denny recorded dozens of albums for Liberty Records, including Forbidden Island, Primitiva, and Hypnotique. In the 1990s the lounge revival brought renewed attention, and his albums were widely reissued as foundational documents of tiki culture.

Death

Denny died in Honolulu on 2 March 2005, at the age of 93.
