Grinding wheel

A grinding wheel is a wheel composed of an abrasive compound and used for various grinding (abrasive cutting) and abrasive machining operations. Such wheels are used in grinding machines.

The wheels are generally made of a composite material consisting of coarse-particle aggregate pressed and bonded together by a cementing matrix (called the bond in grinding wheel terminology) to form a solid, circular shape. Various profiles and cross sections are available depending on the intended usage for the wheel. The disc diameter of grinding wheels typically ranges from a few centimetres to about a metre, and the wheels are consumable: they wear down as they are used and must be replaced regularly.

Characteristics

Five characteristics are used to describe a grinding wheel: the abrasive material, the grain size, the wheel grade, the grain spacing (structure), and the type of bond. These are commonly encoded in a standard marking system printed on the wheel.

Abrasive materials

Common abrasives include aluminium oxide (corundum), silicon carbide, cubic boron nitride (CBN), and diamond. Aluminium oxide wheels are the most widely used for grinding steels and other ferrous metals, while silicon carbide wheels are suited to harder and more brittle materials such as cast iron, stone, and non-ferrous metals. Diamond and CBN "superabrasive" wheels are used for precision work on very hard materials and for tool and cutter grinding.

Bond types

The bond holds the abrasive grains together and determines the wheel's strength and maximum operating speed. Vitrified bonds (ceramic) are rigid and resistant to heat, oil, and water, and are used for most precision grinding. Resinoid (organic) bonds are tougher and more shock-resistant, suiting high-speed cutting-off wheels. Rubber, shellac, silicate, and metallic bonds are used for specialised applications such as control wheels in centreless grinding and superabrasive wheels.

Applications and safety

Grinding wheels are used on bench grinders, angle grinders, surface grinders, cylindrical grinders, and cutting-off machines. Because a wheel rotating at high speed can fragment violently if damaged or oversped, wheels are rated with a maximum safe operating speed, are ring-tested for cracks before mounting, and are used with guards and blotters. Personal protective equipment, especially eye protection, is essential when grinding.
