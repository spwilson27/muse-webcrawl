Paraguay River

The Paraguay River (Spanish: Río Paraguay; Portuguese: Rio Paraguai; Guarani: Ysyry Paraguái) is a major river in south-central South America, running through Brazil, Bolivia, Paraguay and Argentina. It is about 2,695 kilometres (1,675 mi) long, making it the fifth-longest river in South America. From its headwaters in the Brazilian state of Mato Grosso it flows south, through the Pantanal wetlands, and joins the Paraná River near the Argentine city of Corrientes. It is the principal tributary of the Paraná and a key artery of the Río de la Plata basin.

Course

The river rises on the Parecis Plateau in Mato Grosso and flows generally southward, forming part of the Brazil–Bolivia and Brazil–Paraguay borders. In its upper reach it meanders through the Pantanal, one of the world's largest tropical wetlands, whose seasonal flooding dramatically regulates the river's flow. Below the Pantanal it passes the Paraguayan capital, Asunción, receives tributaries including the Pilcomayo and Bermejo from the west, and finally merges with the Paraná.

Significance

The Paraguay River is the economic lifeline of landlocked Paraguay and Bolivia: the Paraguay–Paraná waterway (Hidrovía) carries soybeans, grains, minerals and fuel over 3,400 km from Puerto Cáceres in Brazil to the Atlantic via the Río de la Plata. The river gives Paraguay its name and divides the country into the eastern Región Oriental and the western Gran Chaco. Its floodplains support rich fisheries and wildlife, including capybaras, caimans and hundreds of bird species.

History

European exploration began in the 1520s–1530s, when Sebastian Cabot and others ascended the river seeking a route to the silver of Peru; Asunción was founded on its banks in 1537. The river was a theatre of the Paraguayan War (1864–1870), and disputes over navigation and the Chaco boundary contributed to the Chaco War (1932–1935) between Paraguay and Bolivia. In the 20th and 21st centuries, proposals for large-scale dredging and channelization of the Hidrovía have been debated against concerns for the Pantanal's ecology.
