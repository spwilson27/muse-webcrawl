National Parks of Canada

The National Parks of Canada are a system of protected areas managed by Parks Canada, an agency of the Government of Canada. The system comprises more than 40 national parks and national park reserves spread across all ten provinces and three territories, covering roughly 450,000 square kilometres of land and water — about 3 per cent of the country's landmass.

The national parks system began on 25 November 1885, when the federal government set aside a reserve around the Cave and Basin hot springs at Banff, Alberta. The Rocky Mountains Park Act of 1887 formally created Canada's first national park, later renamed Banff National Park. In 1911 the Dominion Forest Reserves and Parks Act created the Dominion Parks Branch, the world's first national parks service, under Commissioner James B. Harkin.

Administration and mandate

Under the Canada National Parks Act (2000), the parks are dedicated to the people of Canada "for their benefit, education and enjoyment" and are to be maintained so as to leave them unimpaired for future generations. Ecological integrity is the first priority in park management. Parks Canada also administers national historic sites and national marine conservation areas.

Notable parks

  • Banff National Park (1885) — Alberta; Canada's first and most visited park.
  • Wood Buffalo National Park (1922) — Alberta/Northwest Territories; the largest national park in Canada, protecting wood bison and whooping crane nesting habitat.
  • Nahanni National Park Reserve (1976) — Northwest Territories; famous for the South Nahanni River and Virginia Falls.
  • Quttinirpaaq National Park (1988) — Nunavut; the northernmost park, on Ellesmere Island.
  • Gros Morne National Park (1973) — Newfoundland and Labrador; noted for its geology and the Tablelands.
  • Pacific Rim National Park Reserve (1970) — British Columbia; Long Beach, the Broken Group Islands and the West Coast Trail.

Creation of new parks

Parks Canada works toward representing each of Canada's 39 terrestrial natural regions within the system. National park reserves are areas set aside as parks pending settlement of Indigenous land claims, and are managed under the same principles in the interim.
