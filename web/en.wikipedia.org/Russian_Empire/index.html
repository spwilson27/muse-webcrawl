The Russian Empire was a vast empire that spanned Eastern Europe, North Asia, and — through Alaska — reached into North America. Officially proclaimed in 1721 by Peter I following victory in the Great Northern War, it lasted until the February Revolution of 1917, when Nicholas II abdicated. At its greatest extent it was the third-largest contiguous empire in world history, after the British and Mongol empires, encompassing one-sixth of the Earth's land surface.

History

The empire grew out of the Tsardom of Russia. Peter the Great modernised the state, built Saint Petersburg as a new capital, and reformed the army, navy, and administration on Western lines. Catherine the Great expanded its territory at the expense of the Ottoman Empire and partitioned Poland with Prussia and Austria. During the 19th century Russia annexed the Caucasus, Central Asia, and the Far East, while competing with Britain in the "Great Game" and with France in the Napoleonic Wars, which ended in the Russian victory of 1812 and a leading role at the Congress of Vienna.

Society and reform

The empire was an autocracy: the emperor ruled by divine right over more than a hundred ethnic groups and many faiths. Serfdom bound most peasants until Alexander II's Emancipation reform of 1861. Industrialisation accelerated under Sergei Witte in the 1890s, but political repression, military defeats in the Crimean War (1853–56) and Russo-Japanese War (1904–05), and the Revolution of 1905 exposed the regime's fragility.

Fall

Catastrophic losses and shortages in the First World War triggered the February Revolution of 1917. Nicholas II abdicated, the Provisional Government failed to hold power, and the Bolsheviks seized control in the October Revolution, ending the empire and leading to the Russian Civil War and the formation of the Soviet Union.
