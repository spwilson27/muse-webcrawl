Phase-detection autofocus

Phase-detection autofocus (PDAF) is a type of autofocus system used in cameras that determines focus by comparing light from opposite sides of the lens. By measuring how far apart two partial images are, the system can calculate not only whether the subject is out of focus but also in which direction and by how much, allowing fast, decisive focusing.

How it works

In DSLR cameras, a dedicated sensor receives light split by the lens and directed through pairs of separator lenses onto matched sensor pairs; the displacement between the two images (the phase difference) indicates the focus error. In mirrorless cameras and smartphones, phase-detection pixels are embedded directly in the image sensor, often combined with contrast-detection autofocus for accuracy.

Advantages

Compared with contrast-detection autofocus, PDAF is typically much faster and better able to track moving subjects, which is why it is favoured for sports, wildlife, and video autofocus.
