Lockheed bribery scandals

The Lockheed bribery scandals were a series of corrupt practices by officials of the American aerospace company Lockheed, revealed in 1975–1976, involving bribes and contributions paid to foreign officials and politicians to secure sales of Lockheed aircraft.

Revelations

United States Senate hearings under Senator Frank Church disclosed that Lockheed had paid millions of dollars in the process of negotiating the sale of aircraft including the F-104 Starfighter and L-1011 TriStar. Lockheed's chairman Daniel Haughton and president Carl Kotchian resigned in 1976 after the payments came to light.

Effects in other countries

In Japan, the scandal led to the arrest of former prime minister Kakuei Tanaka in 1976 and a long criminal trial. In the Netherlands, Prince Bernhard, husband of Queen Juliana, was implicated in receiving payments and resigned his military and business posts. In West Germany and Italy, payments connected to Starfighter purchases caused major political controversies and official investigations.

Legacy

The scandals were a principal impetus for the United States Foreign Corrupt Practices Act of 1977, which prohibited American companies from bribing foreign officials, and for later international anti-bribery conventions.
