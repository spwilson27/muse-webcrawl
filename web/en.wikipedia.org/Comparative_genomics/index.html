Comparative genomics

Comparative genomics
Field: genomics, evolutionary biology
Methods: sequence alignment, synteny, phylogenetics
Applications: gene discovery, function prediction, evolution

Comparative genomics is the field of biology in which the genome sequences of different species are compared. By aligning genomes, researchers identify genes, regulatory elements and evolutionary changes, using conservation across species as a signal of functional importance.

Principles

Sequences that remain similar over long evolutionary distances are usually conserved by natural selection, flagging protein-coding genes and functional non-coding elements. Conversely, lineage-specific changes illuminate adaptation, such as the evolution of lactase persistence or high-altitude physiology.

Methods

Analyses include whole-genome alignment, detection of orthologs and paralogs, synteny (conserved gene order) mapping, and estimation of selection pressure from substitution rates (dN/dS). Landmark comparisons include human–chimpanzee and human–mouse genome analyses.

Applications

Comparative genomics annotates newly sequenced genomes, traces pathogen evolution and antimicrobial resistance, guides crop and livestock improvement, and reconstructs the history of life, including ancient gene duplications and horizontal gene transfer.
