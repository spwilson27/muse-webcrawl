ISRO

Full name: Indian Space Research Organisation
Founded: 15 August 1969
Headquarters: Bengaluru, India
Founder: Vikram Sarabhai
Launch site: Satish Dhawan Space Centre, Sriharikota

The Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) is India's national space agency, operating under the Department of Space. Founded by physicist Vikram Sarabhai in 1969, succeeding the INCOSPAR committee of 1962, it is one of only a handful of agencies with full launch capability, interplanetary missions, and a human spaceflight programme in development.

Achievements

India's first satellite, Aryabhata, flew in 1975 on a Soviet rocket; the SLV-3 made India self-reliant in launch in 1980. The workhorse PSLV and the heavier LVM3 launchers followed. Chandrayaan-1 (2008) helped confirm water molecules on the Moon; the Mars Orbiter Mission (Mangalyaan, 2014) made India the first country to reach Mars orbit on its first attempt; Chandrayaan-3 achieved the first soft landing near the lunar south pole in August 2023. Aditya-L1 observes the Sun, and the Gaganyaan programme aims to fly Indian astronauts.

Organisation and industry

ISRO centres include the Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre (rockets), UR Rao Satellite Centre, and the Satish Dhawan launch range. Its commercial arm, NewSpace India Limited, markets launches; in 2017 a single PSLV deployed 104 satellites, then a world record. The agency is noted for comparatively low mission costs.
