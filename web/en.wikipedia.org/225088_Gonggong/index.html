225088 Gonggong

225088 Gonggong (provisional designation 2007 OR10) is a dwarf planet in the scattered disc of the Solar System. It is one of the largest known trans-Neptunian objects, with a diameter of about 1,230 km, comparable to Pluto's moon Charon, and is the largest known Solar System body without a name until it received its official name in 2020.

Discovery

Gonggong was discovered on 17 July 2007 by astronomers Megan Schwamb, Michael Brown and David Rabinowitz at Palomar Observatory as part of Schwamb's doctoral research. It was nicknamed "Snow White" informally because of its initially assumed size and colour.

Physical characteristics

Observations indicate a reddish colour attributed to tholins on its surface. It rotates slowly, with a period of about 22.4 or 44.8 hours. Thermal measurements from the Herschel and other telescopes established its size and low albedo.

Moon

Gonggong has one known moon, Xiangliu, discovered in images from the Hubble Space Telescope. The moon's orbit allowed astronomers to estimate the system's mass.

Name

The name Gonggong, approved by the International Astronomical Union in 2020 following a public vote, refers to Gonggong, a Chinese water god. Its moon is named after Xiangliu, the nine-headed serpent attendant of Gonggong in Chinese mythology.
