Kaifeng (Chinese: 开封; pinyin: Kāifēng) is a prefecture-level city in eastern Henan province, China, on the southern bank of the Yellow River. One of the Eight Great Ancient Capitals of China, it served as the capital of several dynasties, most famously the Northern Song dynasty (960–1127), when — under the name Dongjing or Bianliang — it was plausibly the largest city in the world, with a population of over a million.

History

Kaifeng began as the capital of the state of Wei in the Warring States period and gained importance when the Grand Canal connected it to the Yellow River and the Yangtze under the Sui dynasty. As the Northern Song capital it became a centre of commerce, printing, and art; the famous handscroll Along the River During the Qingming Festival by Zhang Zeduan depicts its bustling streets and waterways. The Jurchen Jin dynasty captured the city in the Jingkang incident of 1127, and Kaifeng later suffered repeated catastrophic Yellow River floods — including those of 1194, 1642, and 1841 — which buried whole earlier cities beneath metres of silt, creating Kaifeng's unusual "cities stacked upon cities" archaeology. The city is also noted for its historic Jewish community, the Kaifeng Jews, whose synagogue stood from 1163 until the nineteenth century.

Landmarks

Surviving sights include the Iron Pagoda (1049), a glazed-brick tower of the Youguo Temple; the Fan Tower; Dragon Pavilion; and the reconstructed Qingming Riverside Landscape Garden, a theme park built around the celebrated scroll painting. Kaifeng is known for its night markets and for an annual chrysanthemum festival.
