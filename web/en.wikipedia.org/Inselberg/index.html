Inselberg

An inselberg or monadnock is an isolated rock hill, knob, ridge or small mountain that rises abruptly from a gently sloping or virtually level surrounding plain. The word inselberg is a loanword from German, meaning "island mountain". Prominent examples include Uluru in Australia and the Sugarloaf Mountain in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil.

Formation

Inselbergs arise through differential erosion: resistant rock survives while surrounding, less resistant rock is worn away, or through the retreat of an escarpment. Volcanic and other processes may also be involved. In arid and semi-arid landscapes, inselbergs of granite and gneiss are characteristic features, often formed by the exposure of subsurface weathering fronts during periods of landscape lowering.

Ecology

Because they are isolated, inselbergs often host distinctive plant and animal communities that differ from the surrounding lowlands, acting as refuges and centres of endemism. Rock pools, weathering pits and seepage zones on their slopes support species adapted to harsh, fluctuating conditions.
