Volume

Volume is a measure of three-dimensional space — the amount of space that a substance or object occupies, or that is enclosed within a container. The SI unit of volume is the cubic metre (m³). Volume is often measured in litres, where one litre equals one cubic decimetre (0.001 m³). In mathematics, the volume of a region is given by a triple integral of the constant function 1 over that region, or by elementary formulas for simple solids.

Formulas

Standard formulas include: cube, s³; rectangular box, lwh; cylinder, πr²h; sphere, (4/3)πr³; cone, (1/3)πr²h; and pyramid, one third of base area times height. Cavalieri's principle states that solids whose cross-sections have equal area at every height have equal volumes. More general volumes are computed with integral calculus, while the volumes of irregular objects can be found by liquid displacement — the method legend attributes to Archimedes' "Eureka" moment.

Measurement

Liquids are measured with graduated cylinders, volumetric flasks, pipettes and burettes; gas volumes depend strongly on temperature and pressure and are often quoted at standard conditions. For regular solids, dimensions are measured directly; for porous or irregular solids, displacement or gas pycnometry is used.

Volume in physics

Volume enters thermodynamics as a state variable conjugate to pressure; the product of pressure and volume change is work. Density is mass per unit volume. Specific volume is volume per unit mass. Because most materials expand when heated, a volume quoted without conditions may be ambiguous.
