William Edward Boeing (1 October 1881 – 28 September 1956) was an American aviation pioneer who founded the Boeing Company in Seattle, Washington, in 1916. Born in Detroit to a wealthy timber family, he made his own fortune in the Pacific Northwest timber business before turning to aviation.

Founding Boeing

Boeing first flew as a passenger at the Alaska–Yukon–Pacific Exposition in 1909-era demonstrations and became convinced he could build a better airplane. After taking flying lessons and buying a seaplane, he co-founded the Pacific Aero Products Company in 1916 with George Conrad Westervelt; the firm's first design, the B&W seaplane, flew that year. Renamed the Boeing Airplane Company in 1917, it won Navy orders during the First World War and afterwards expanded into airmail — its Boeing Air Transport became a core part of United Aircraft and Transport Corporation, and later of United Airlines.

Under the Air Mail Act of 1934, which forced the separation of airlines from aircraft manufacturers, Boeing was required to break up his aviation empire and resigned in protest, leaving the industry. He spent his later years breeding thoroughbred horses and developing real estate. The company he founded grew into one of the world's largest aerospace manufacturers. Boeing died aboard his yacht near Seattle in 1956.
