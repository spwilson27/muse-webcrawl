Vogel's model is a mathematical model for the distribution of florets in the head of a sunflower, proposed by Helmut Vogel in 1979. It is a prominent example of phyllotaxis, the arrangement of leaves and florets in plants, and generates the spiral patterns visible in sunflowers, daisies, pine cones, and pineapples.

The model

In polar coordinates, the n-th floret is placed at angle θ = n × 137.5077640° (the golden angle, in radians 2π/φ²) and radius r = c√n, where c is a scaling constant. Because the golden angle is an irrational multiple of a full turn, successive florets never line up in radial rows; instead the eye picks out families of spirals (parastichies) whose counts are consecutive Fibonacci numbers, such as 34 and 55 or 55 and 89 in large sunflower heads.

Significance

The model shows how a simple local growth rule — each new primordium emerging at the golden angle from the previous one, as occurs at a plant's shoot apical meristem — produces globally optimal packing, maximizing the exposure of seeds and the efficiency of packing in the flower head. Vogel's formula is widely used in computer graphics and generative art to synthesize realistic flower heads.
