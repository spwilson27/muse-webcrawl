Truss bridge

Truss bridge
Structural elementTruss of connected triangular units
MaterialsTimber, iron, steel, concrete
Common designsPratt, Howe, Warren, Parker, Baltimore

A truss bridge is a bridge whose load-bearing superstructure is composed of a truss — connected elements, usually forming triangular units, that carry loads primarily in tension and compression. Truss bridges are among the oldest modern bridge types and are economical because they use material efficiently.

Designs

Classic forms include the Pratt truss (vertical members in compression, diagonals in tension), the Howe truss (opposite arrangement), the Warren truss (equilateral triangles) and the Parker or camelback variant with a polygonal top chord. Trusses may be through trusses (traffic passes between them), pony trusses, or deck trusses.

History

Timber and later iron trusses dominated 19th-century road and railway construction in North America and Europe. Notable examples include the Forth Bridge in Scotland, a cantilever truss, and thousands of covered wooden truss bridges.
