Kilwa Sultanate

The Kilwa Sultanate was a Swahili sultanate centred at Kilwa, an island off the coast of present-day southern Tanzania, which at its height in the 13th to 15th centuries controlled much of the Swahili Coast trade, from Mogadishu in the north to Sofala in Mozambique in the south.

History

According to tradition, Kilwa was founded around the 10th century by Ali ibn al-Hassan Shirazi, a prince said to have come from Shiraz in Persia. The sultanate grew wealthy from the Indian Ocean trade in gold from the interior kingdom centred on Great Zimbabwe, ivory, and enslaved people, exchanging them for cloth, beads, and porcelain from Arabia, Persia, India, and China. The Great Mosque of Kilwa and the palace of Husuni Kubwa, built of coral stone, testify to its prosperity; the traveller Ibn Battuta visited in 1331 and praised its ruler's generosity.

The arrival of the Portuguese after 1498, and their seizure of Kilwa in 1505, began its decline, though the city later revived under Omani influence. The ruins of Kilwa Kisiwani are now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
