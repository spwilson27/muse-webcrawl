Silver Age of Comic Books

The Silver Age of Comic Books was a period of artistic advancement and widespread commercial success in mainstream American comic books, predominantly those featuring the superhero archetype. Following the Golden Age of Comic Books and an interregnum in the early to mid-1950s, the Silver Age is considered to cover the period from 1956 to approximately 1970, and was succeeded by the Bronze and Modern Ages.

Origins

The Silver Age is generally held to begin with DC Comics' Showcase #4 (October 1956), in which editor Julius Schwartz, writer Robert Kanigher, and artist Carmine Infantino revived the Flash as a new character, Barry Allen. DC followed with a modernised Green Lantern and the Justice League of America, reviving the superhero genre that had faded after the Second World War.

Marvel Comics

In 1961, Marvel Comics, under writer-editor Stan Lee and artists Jack Kirby and Steve Ditko, transformed the field with Fantastic Four #1, followed by Spider-Man, the Hulk, Thor, Iron Man, and the X-Men. Marvel's heroes had human flaws, personal problems, and ongoing soap-opera subplots, attracting older readers and college students.

End of the era

The period operated under the restrictive Comics Code Authority, formed in 1954. The Silver Age is conventionally said to end around 1970–1973, with events such as Jack Kirby's departure from Marvel, a price rise from 12 to 15 cents, and landmark stories of greater social realism — including the death of Gwen Stacy in The Amazing Spider-Man — ushering in the Bronze Age.
