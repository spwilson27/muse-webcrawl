Citigroup Inc. (doing business as Citi) is an American multinational investment bank and financial services corporation headquartered in New York City. It was formed on October 8, 1998, by the merger of Citicorp and the financial conglomerate Travelers Group, in one of the largest corporate mergers in history.

History

Citicorp traced its origins to the City Bank of New York, founded in 1812 (later National City Bank). The 1998 merger with Travelers, led by Citicorp's John Reed and Travelers' Sandy Weill, created a financial supermarket spanning banking, insurance, and securities, and prompted the repeal of the Glass–Steagall Act's separation provisions through the Gramm–Leach–Bliley Act of 1999.

During the 2007–2008 financial crisis, Citigroup suffered huge losses on mortgage-related assets and received exceptional government support, including Troubled Asset Relief Program funds and guarantees on a pool of troubled assets; the U.S. Treasury later exited its stake at a profit. After the crisis, the bank split into Citicorp (core businesses) and Citi Holdings (non-core assets) and progressively shrank its global consumer footprint, announcing exits from consumer banking in numerous markets in the 2020s.

Operations

Citi operates institutional clients, wealth, and U.S. personal banking segments, with a network spanning roughly 90-plus countries and a leading global payments and transaction services business.
