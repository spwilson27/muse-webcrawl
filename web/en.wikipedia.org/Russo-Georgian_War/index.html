Russo-Georgian War

Date7–12 August 2008
LocationGeorgia (South Ossetia, Abkhazia)
BelligerentsRussia, South Ossetia, Abkhazia vs. Georgia
ResultRussian victory; Russian recognition of South Ossetia and Abkhazia

The Russo-Georgian War was a war between Georgia on one side and Russia together with the Russian-backed self-proclaimed republics of South Ossetia and Abkhazia on the other, fought in August 2008. It was the first war in Europe in the 21st century and followed years of tension over Georgia's pro-Western course under President Mikheil Saakashvili and the frozen conflicts in its breakaway regions.

Armoured vehicles in Georgia during the August 2008 war.

Course of the war

After escalating clashes between Georgian forces and South Ossetian separatists, Georgia launched a large-scale assault on Tskhinvali, the South Ossetian capital, on the night of 7–8 August. Russia responded with a full-scale invasion: its forces routed the Georgian army in South Ossetia, bombed targets across Georgia, opened a second front from Abkhazia into western Georgia, and occupied the ports and towns of Poti, Gori, Senaki, and Zugdidi. Russian naval forces blockaded the Georgian coast. A ceasefire was brokered on 12 August by French President Nicolas Sarkozy on behalf of the European Union.

Aftermath

On 26 August 2008 Russia recognised South Ossetia and Abkhazia as independent states, a step condemned by most of the international community, and established permanent military bases in both territories. An EU-commissioned independent fact-finding mission (the Tagliavini Report, 2009) concluded that Georgia had initiated the open war with its attack on Tskhinvali, but that Russia's response went far beyond lawful defence, and it criticised both sides for violations of international humanitarian law. The war displaced tens of thousands of ethnic Georgians from South Ossetia and remains a central obstacle to Georgia's aspirations to join NATO.
