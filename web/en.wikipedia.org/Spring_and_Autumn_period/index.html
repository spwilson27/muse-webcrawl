Spring and Autumn period

The Spring and Autumn period (c. 770 – c. 481 BCE) was a period in Chinese history corresponding roughly to the first half of the Eastern Zhou dynasty. The period takes its name from the Spring and Autumn Annals, a chronicle of the state of Lu traditionally attributed to Confucius. During this era, the authority of the Zhou kings waned and power passed to regional states ruled by feudal lords.

Background

In 770 BCE, after the sack of the Zhou capital Haojing, King Ping moved the capital east to Luoyi, beginning the Eastern Zhou. The Zhou king retained ritual prestige but little real power; hundreds of small states competed, allied and were absorbed, so that by the end of the period only a handful of major powers remained.

Hegemons

Powerful state rulers known as hegemons (ba) convened alliances and claimed to protect the Zhou order against "barbarian" peoples. The first hegemon was Duke Huan of Qi, aided by his minister Guan Zhong; he was followed by Duke Wen of Jin and others. The Five Hegemons are traditionally listed as the leading rulers of the age.

Legacy

The period saw the development of ironworking, coinage, infantry warfare and private landholding. It was also the age of Confucius, whose teachings about ritual, hierarchy and good government were a response to the era's disorder. The Spring and Autumn period gave way to the more violent Warring States period.
