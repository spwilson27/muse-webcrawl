Three successive designs served as the official national flags of the Confederate States of America (the "Confederate States" or the "Confederacy") during its existence from 1861 to 1865. In addition, the Army of Northern Virginia battle flag — a blue St Andrew's cross (saltire) with white stars on a red field — became the most widely recognised symbol of the Confederacy and the one most often meant by the phrase "Confederate flag" in modern usage.

First national flag: "Stars and Bars"

Adopted on 4 March 1861, the first national flag consisted of three horizontal bars of red, white, and red, with a blue canton bearing a circle of white stars — seven at first, eventually thirteen. Its resemblance to the United States flag caused confusion in the smoke of battle, notably at the First Battle of Bull Run, which spurred the adoption of a distinct battle flag for field use. The design was modelled on a proposal by Nicola Marschall, adapting elements of the Austrian and American flags.

Battle flag

The battle flag of the Army of Northern Virginia, designed by William Porcher Miles and adopted in late 1861, was square, with a blue saltire bordered in white on a red field and thirteen white stars in the cross. Rectangular versions were used by other armies and as the Confederate naval jack from 1863. Designer Miles's original proposal for the national flag had been rejected, but General P. G. T. Beauregard championed the saltire design for battle use. In the twentieth century the rectangular version was incorporated into some U.S. state flags and became a potent, contested symbol — associated by many with heritage and by many others with slavery and white supremacy; it was widely removed from official display after 2015 and 2020.

Second national flag: "Stainless Banner"

Adopted on 1 May 1863, the second national flag placed the battle flag as a canton on a field of pure white, symbolising, in its proponents' words, the Confederacy's cause. In practice the large white field was criticised for resembling a flag of truce when hanging limp. The designer was William T. Thompson, a Savannah newspaper editor whose published racial justifications for the design are frequently cited in modern debates over the flag.

Third national flag

On 4 March 1865, weeks before the Confederacy's collapse, a third flag was adopted adding a vertical red bar at the fly end of the Stainless Banner to remove the truce-flag appearance. It saw almost no service before the war ended in April 1865.

Other flags

Other Confederate flags include the Bonnie Blue Flag (a single white star on a blue field, an unofficial early symbol), various state and regimental colours, department flags such as the Hardee and Van Dorn patterns, and the Confederate ensigns used at sea by commerce raiders such as CSS Alabama and CSS Shenandoah.
