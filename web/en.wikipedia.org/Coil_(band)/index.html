Coil (band)

Coil were an English experimental music group formed in London in 1982 by John Balance (Geoffrey Rushton) and Peter "Sleazy" Christopherson. Emerging from the industrial music scene — Christopherson had been a member of Throbbing Gristle and co-founded Psychic TV — Coil pursued an esoteric, ever-shifting sound encompassing industrial, dark ambient, drone, acid house and neoclassical influences.

OriginLondon, England
Years active1982–2004
GenresIndustrial, experimental, dark ambient, post-industrial
MembersJohn Balance, Peter Christopherson

History

Balance began Coil as a solo project, releasing the How to Destroy Angels EP in 1984 after Christopherson joined. Their debut album Scatology (1984) and Horse Rotorvator (1986) established their reputation for transgressive, occult-themed work. Later releases included Love's Secret Domain (1991), influenced by acid house; the ambient Time Machines (1998), credited to their drone project of the same name; and the Musick to Play in the Dark volumes (1999–2000). Coil also recorded under aliases including ELpH, Black Light District and The Eskaton.

End and legacy

John Balance died in an accident at home in November 2004; Christopherson announced that Coil was finished as a project. Christopherson died in 2010. Coil's work has been continually reissued and is regarded as a major influence on experimental and electronic music.
