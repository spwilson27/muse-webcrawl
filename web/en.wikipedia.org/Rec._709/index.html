ITU-R Recommendation BT.709, usually abbreviated Rec. 709 or BT.709, is a standard developed by the Radiocommunication Sector of the International Telecommunication Union that sets the image format, frame rates, colour encoding and signal parameters for high-definition television. First approved in 1990, it standardized a square-pixel, progressively-scanned-or-interlaced HDTV format with 16:9 aspect ratio and 1080 active lines in its most common implementation.

Colour characteristics

Rec. 709 defines primary colours with CIE 1931 chromaticities — red at (0.64, 0.33), green at (0.30, 0.60) and blue at (0.15, 0.06) — and the D65 standard illuminant as the white point. These primaries are identical to those of the earlier sRGB standard, so the Rec. 709 gamut is the familiar triangle used by HD broadcasting, Blu-ray Disc and most streaming video. The reference electro-optical behaviour is commonly described as an approximately 2.2–2.4 display gamma, with the encoding opto-electronic transfer function defined piecewise with a linear segment near black and a 0.45 power law elsewhere.

Digital representation

Digital Rec. 709 signals are normally coded with limited-range luma and chroma, using the BT.709 matrix coefficients (Kr = 0.2126, Kb = 0.0722) to derive Y′, Cb and Cr components from R′G′B′. Frame rates specified include 24, 25, 30, 50 and 60 Hz in progressive and interlaced variants.

Successors

Rec. 709 remains the default colour space for HD production and delivery. For ultra-high-definition and high dynamic range television, the ITU defined Rec. 2020 with a much wider gamut and Rec. 2100 with HDR transfer functions, but Rec. 709 content continues to dominate broadcast and online video.
