Old East Slavic

Old East Slavic (also called Old Russian) is the language of the East Slavs between roughly the tenth and fourteenth centuries AD, ancestral to modern Russian, Ukrainian, and Belarusian. It was the vernacular and administrative language of Kievan Rus', written in the Cyrillic script, while Church Slavonic served as the high literary and liturgical register alongside it.

Sources

Old East Slavic is known from chronicles such as the Primary Chronicle, legal texts like the Russkaya Pravda, epics including the Tale of Igor's Campaign, and — uniquely — the Novgorod birch-bark letters, hundreds of everyday notes on birch bark that reveal the living vernacular rather than bookish style. These letters also show that the northern dialect around Novgorod differed noticeably from the Kievan south.

Development

From the fourteenth century, as political centres diverged under Mongol, Lithuanian, and Muscovite rule, Old East Slavic differentiated into Ruthenian in the west and the Muscovite chancery language in the northeast — the bases of modern Belarusian and Ukrainian on the one hand, and Russian on the other.
