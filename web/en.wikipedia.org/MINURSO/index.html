The United Nations Mission for the Referendum in Western Sahara (French: Mission des Nations Unies pour l'organisation d'un référendum au Sahara occidental; Spanish: Misión de Naciones Unidas para la Organización de un Referéndum en el Sáhara Occidental; MINURSO) is the United Nations peacekeeping mission in Western Sahara, established in 1991 under United Nations Security Council Resolution 690 as part of the Settlement Plan, which had been accepted by Morocco and the Polisario Front.

The mission's original mandate was to monitor the ceasefire between Morocco and the Polisario Front that ended the Western Sahara War, and to organise and conduct a referendum in which the people of Western Sahara would choose between independence and integration with Morocco. Although the ceasefire held for nearly three decades, the referendum has never been held, largely because of persistent disagreement over who is eligible to vote. MINURSO remains deployed primarily as a ceasefire-monitoring mission, and its mandate is renewed annually by the Security Council.

Background

Western Sahara, a former Spanish colony, was claimed by both Morocco and Mauritania when Spain withdrew in 1975 under the Madrid Accords. The Polisario Front, a Sahrawi nationalist movement, proclaimed the Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic and fought a guerrilla war against Moroccan and Mauritanian forces. Mauritania withdrew in 1979, after which Morocco extended its control to most of the territory, including the sand wall (the Berm) constructed during the 1980s. A UN- and OAU-backed Settlement Plan in 1988 led to a ceasefire in September 1991 and the creation of MINURSO.

Mandate and activities

MINURSO's mandated tasks include monitoring the ceasefire, verifying the reduction of Moroccan troops in the territory, overseeing the exchange of prisoners of war, implementing a repatriation programme for refugees, identifying and registering qualified voters, and organising a free and fair referendum. In practice, the mission's work has concentrated on ceasefire observation from team sites on both sides of the Berm, support to the UNHCR confidence-building measures between Sahrawi refugees in the Tindouf camps in Algeria and their families in the territory, and mine-action activities.

The identification of eligible voters was substantially completed by 1999, producing a provisional list of about 86,000 voters, but the process generated more than 130,000 appeals and the parties could not agree on how to resolve them. Successive UN envoys, including former US Secretary of State James Baker, whose 2003 "Baker Plan II" was accepted by the Polisario but rejected by Morocco, failed to break the deadlock. Since 2007 Morocco has promoted an autonomy plan for the territory under Moroccan sovereignty, while the Polisario insists on a referendum including the option of independence.

Ceasefire breakdown and recent developments

In November 2020, following a confrontation at the Guerguerat buffer strip in the south of the territory, the Polisario Front announced that it no longer considered itself bound by the 1991 ceasefire, and low-intensity hostilities resumed. MINURSO continues to operate and report to the Security Council, with a strength of roughly 200–250 uniformed personnel, mostly unarmed military observers, plus civilian staff. The mission is unusual among modern UN peacekeeping operations in having no explicit human-rights monitoring mandate, a point of recurring debate in the Security Council.
