Prana

In yoga, Ayurveda, and Indian martial arts, prana (Sanskrit: प्राण, prāṇa, "vital force" or "life energy") is a central concept referring to the universal life force that permeates reality and sustains all living beings. The concept appears in the early Upanishads, where prana is associated with the breath and sometimes identified with the self (ātman).

The five vayus

Tradition distinguishes five principal currents or "winds" (vāyus) of prana in the body: prāṇa proper, governing intake and centred in the chest; apāna, governing elimination and seated in the lower abdomen; samāna, governing digestion at the navel; udāna, governing speech and upward movement in the throat; and vyāna, pervading the whole body and governing circulation. Health is understood as the balanced flow of these currents through the channels (nāḍī) of the subtle body, including the central suṣumṇā and the side channels iḍā and piṅgalā.

Pranayama

The regulation of prana through breath control is prāṇāyāma, the fourth limb of Patañjali's eight-limbed yoga. Techniques involve controlled inhalation (pūraka), retention (kumbhaka) and exhalation (recaka), and are intended to purify the channels, steady the mind and awaken the dormant kuṇḍalinī energy.

Related concepts

Prana is frequently compared with analogous concepts in other traditions: qi in Chinese medicine and martial arts, ki in Japanese traditions, pneuma in Greek thought, and lung in Tibetan Buddhism. In Ayurveda, prana is the vital principle associated with vāta doṣa and with the functioning of the mind and senses.
