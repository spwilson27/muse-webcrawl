Philip V (238–179 BC) was king of the ancient Greek kingdom of Macedon from 221 BC until his death. He belonged to the Antigonid dynasty and was the son of Demetrius II of Macedon. His reign was dominated by the struggle with the expanding Roman Republic in the Macedonian Wars.

Reign

Philip came to the throne at seventeen and proved an energetic ruler, fighting the Social War in Greece and allying with Hannibal of Carthage during the Second Punic War, which provoked Rome's first intervention in Greece. In the Second Macedonian War he was decisively defeated by Titus Quinctius Flamininus at the Battle of Cynoscephalae in 197 BC, after which Macedon lost its holdings in Greece.

Later years

Philip spent his later years rebuilding Macedon's economy, mines and army, but was compelled by Rome to execute his younger son Demetrius on suspicion of treason. He died in 179 BC and was succeeded by his eldest son, Perseus, the last king of Macedon.
