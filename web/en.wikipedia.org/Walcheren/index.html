Walcheren is a former island in the province of Zeeland in the Netherlands, at the mouth of the Scheldt estuary. It lies between the Eastern Scheldt in the north and the Western Scheldt in the south and is roughly the shape of a rhombus. The two sides facing the North Sea consist of dunes; the rest of its coastline is made up of dykes. Walcheren is not an island anymore since it was joined to the mainland by land reclamation and the construction of dams, including the Sloedam (1871) and connections to South Beveland.

Towns

Middelburg, the capital of Zeeland, lies in the centre of Walcheren; Vlissingen (Flushing) is a major port on the southern coast. Other settlements include Domburg, Westkapelle, Zoutelande, Koudekerke, Veere and Arnemuiden. Tourism, centred on the beaches and seaside resorts of the north-west coast, is a major industry.

History

In Roman times Walcheren was an area of settlement and trade at the Scheldt mouth. In 1809, during the Napoleonic Wars, a British expedition landed on the island in the disastrous Walcheren Campaign, in which thousands of troops died of "Walcheren fever" (malaria and other diseases).

During the Second World War, German forces fortified Walcheren as part of the Atlantic Wall, commanding the approaches to Antwerp. In October–November 1944, in the Battle of the Scheldt, Canadian and British forces captured the island after RAF Bomber Command breached the dykes at Westkapelle, deliberately flooding much of Walcheren to dislodge the defenders. The land was reclaimed and the dykes repaired after the war; the disastrous North Sea flood of 1953 also inundated parts of the island, prompting the Delta Works.
