Detroit Institute of Arts

The Detroit Institute of Arts (DIA), located in Midtown Detroit, Michigan, is one of the largest and most significant art museums in the United States. Founded in 1885, it holds more than 65,000 works spanning global art from ancient Egyptian pieces to contemporary art.

Building and collection

The museum's Beaux-Arts building on Woodward Avenue, designed by Paul Philippe Cret, opened in 1927 and has been expanded several times, now offering some 658,000 square feet (61,100 m²). Highlights include the first Van Gogh painting to enter an American museum collection, works by Rembrandt, Matisse, and Picasso, a notable collection of American art, and murals painted by Diego Rivera.

Detroit Industry Murals

Rivera's Detroit Industry Murals (1932–33), twenty-seven fresco panels celebrating the city's workers and factories, cover the museum's central courtyard and are considered among the finest works of the Mexican muralist movement in the United States.

Bankruptcy and the "grand bargain"

During Detroit's 2013 municipal bankruptcy, the city-owned collection was threatened with sale to pay creditors. The "grand bargain" — a fund assembled from foundations, the state, and donors — raised over $800 million to protect pensions while transferring the museum to an independent charitable trust, keeping the collection intact.
