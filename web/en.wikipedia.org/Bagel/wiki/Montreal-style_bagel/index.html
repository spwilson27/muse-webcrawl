Montreal-style bagel

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Montreal-style bagel or Montreal bagel (sometimes beigl; in French, bagel de Montréal) is a distinctive variety of handmade and wood-fired bagel. In contrast to the New York-style bagel, the Montreal bagel is smaller, thinner, sweeter and denser, with a larger hole, and is always baked in a wood-fired oven. It contains malt, egg, and no salt, and is boiled in honey-sweetened water before being baked.

History

Bagel-making came to Montreal with Jewish immigrants from Eastern Europe in the early 20th century. The two shops most associated with the style — St-Viateur Bagel, founded in 1957 by Myer Lewkowicz, and Fairmount Bagel, founded in 1919 — still operate in the Mile End neighbourhood and compete for the title of the city's original bagel bakery. Montreal bagels are a celebrated symbol of the city's Jewish culinary heritage and of Montreal food culture generally.

Method

Montreal bagels are rolled by hand (a skilled roller can shape dozens per minute), proofed briefly, poached in water sweetened with honey, coated with sesame or poppy seeds, and baked on long wooden boards in a wood-fired oven. The result is a golden, slightly crisp crust with a chewy, dense crumb and a subtle sweetness. Traditionally they are sold hot, in paper bags or threaded onto wooden dowels, and eaten fresh rather than toasted or split for sandwiches, though both are common today.

Variations and reach

Sesame is the classic topping by a wide margin, followed by poppy ("black seed"). Bakeries also produce all-dressed, cinnamon-raisin, and whole-wheat versions. Montreal-style bagel shops have opened in other Canadian cities and abroad, and in 2008 astronaut Gregory Chamitoff, whose wife is from Montreal, carried Fairmount sesame bagels aboard the International Space Station — often cited as the first bagels in space.
