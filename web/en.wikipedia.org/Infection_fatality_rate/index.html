Infection fatality rate

The infection fatality rate or infection fatality ratio (IFR) is the proportion of deaths among all infected individuals, including all asymptomatic and undiagnosed subjects. It is related to the case fatality rate (CFR), which denotes only the proportion of deaths among confirmed cases, but differs in that the IFR attempts to account for every infection, diagnosed or not.

IFR = (number of deaths from the disease) / (total number of infected individuals)

Estimation

Because most infections are never laboratory-confirmed, the IFR cannot be observed directly. It is usually estimated using seroprevalence surveys, which measure the proportion of a population carrying antibodies to the pathogen, combined with recorded death counts. Such estimates are sensitive to the sensitivity and specificity of antibody tests, the completeness of death reporting, and waning immunity, so published IFRs often carry wide confidence intervals.

Comparison with case fatality rate

The case fatality rate is computed only over confirmed cases and is therefore usually higher than the IFR, sometimes by an order of magnitude for diseases with many mild or asymptomatic infections. Early in an outbreak the CFR may be inflated by testing that is restricted to severe cases, and deflated when deaths lag behind case detection. For this reason the IFR is generally considered the more meaningful measure of the intrinsic deadliness of a pathogen, while the CFR describes outcomes among detected patients in a given healthcare setting.

Variation

For many diseases the IFR varies sharply with age and with comorbidities. During the COVID-19 pandemic, meta-analyses estimated an overall IFR well under 1 percent in many populations, but the risk rose steeply with age, roughly doubling every seven to eight years of age, so that the IFR for the oldest age groups was several percent while that for children was a tiny fraction of a percent. Similar age gradients are seen with influenza, whose seasonal IFR is typically estimated at around 0.1 percent or less.
