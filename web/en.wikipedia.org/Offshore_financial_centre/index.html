Offshore financial centre

An offshore financial centre (OFC) is defined as a country or jurisdiction that provides financial services to nonresidents on a scale that is incommensurate with the size and financing of its domestic economy. "Offshore" does not refer to the location of the centre (many are onshore) but to the fact that the bulk of its financial activity is for non-residents.

Characteristics

OFCs typically feature low or zero taxation for foreign investors, light financial regulation, secrecy or confidentiality laws, and ease of company incorporation. Major centres include the Cayman Islands, British Virgin Islands, Bermuda, Jersey, Guernsey, Luxembourg, Switzerland, Singapore, and Hong Kong. Offshore banking, captive insurance, and the registration of special-purpose vehicles and investment funds are common activities.

Criticism and regulation

International bodies including the IMF, OECD, and Financial Stability Forum have scrutinised OFCs over tax avoidance and evasion, money laundering, and regulatory arbitrage. Initiatives such as the OECD's harmful tax practices project, the EU list of non-cooperative jurisdictions, and automatic exchange of information under the Common Reporting Standard have increased transparency since the 2000s.

Distinction from tax havens

The terms are often used interchangeably, but "tax haven" emphasises low taxation while "offshore financial centre" emphasises the scale of non-resident financial services; some jurisdictions prefer the latter term as less pejorative.
