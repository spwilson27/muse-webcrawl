James Quinn Wilson (27 May 1931 – 2 March 2012) was an American political scientist and a prominent authority on public administration, bureaucracy, and criminology. He is best known for co-authoring the "broken windows" theory of policing.

Work

In a 1982 Atlantic Monthly article with George L. Kelling, Wilson proposed the broken windows theory: visible signs of disorder encourage further crime, so maintaining order in neighbourhoods helps prevent serious offences. The idea influenced policing strategies in New York and other cities, and remains debated by criminologists.

Books and career

Wilson taught at Harvard University and UCLA. His books include Varieties of Police Behavior (1968), Thinking About Crime (1975), Bureaucracy (1989), and The Moral Sense (1993), as well as the widely used textbook American Government with John J. DiIulio Jr. He received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2003.
