Real estate investment trust

A real estate investment trust (REIT) is a company that owns, operates or finances income-producing real estate. Modelled on mutual funds, REITs pool the capital of many investors, allowing individuals to earn dividends from property investments — offices, apartments, warehouses, hospitals, hotels, data centres and more — without buying or managing properties themselves. Most REITs are publicly traded on stock exchanges.

Characteristics and requirements

REITs were created in the United States by the Real Estate Investment Trust Act of 1960. To qualify for favourable tax treatment, a US REIT must invest at least 75% of its assets in real estate, derive at least 75% of gross income from rents or mortgage interest, and distribute at least 90% of its taxable income to shareholders as dividends; in return, it generally pays no corporate income tax on distributed earnings. Similar regimes now exist in dozens of countries — including J-REITs in Japan, SIICs in France, A-REITs in Australia and REITs in the UK since 2007.

Types

Equity REITs own and manage properties and earn rental income. Mortgage REITs (mREITs) lend money or buy mortgage-backed securities. Hybrid REITs combine both. REITs are often classified by sector: residential, retail, office, industrial/logistics, healthcare, lodging, self-storage, timberland and infrastructure.

Investing

REIT shares offer liquidity, diversification and historically competitive total returns, and they are included in major indices such as the FTSE Nareit All REITs Index. Their performance is sensitive to interest rates, occupancy and property cycles, and they are analysed using funds from operations (FFO) rather than net income because depreciation distorts property earnings.
