Dysmenorrhea

Dysmenorrhea is painful menstruation, typically involving cramping pain in the lower abdomen that occurs just before or during a menstrual period. It is one of the most common gynaecological complaints, affecting a large proportion of women of reproductive age.

Types and causes

Primary dysmenorrhea occurs without underlying pelvic disease and is attributed to prostaglandins that cause uterine contractions. Secondary dysmenorrhea results from conditions such as endometriosis, adenomyosis or fibroids, and tends to begin later in life and worsen over time.

Symptoms and treatment

Pain may be accompanied by nausea, headache, backache and fatigue, and can be severe enough to interfere with daily activities. First-line treatments include nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), hormonal contraception and heat therapy; secondary dysmenorrhea is managed by treating the underlying cause.
