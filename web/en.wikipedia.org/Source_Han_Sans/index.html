Source Han Sans


Source Han Sans in its four regional variants

Source Han Sans is a sans-serif typeface family created by Adobe in collaboration with Google. It is also released by Google under the name Noto Sans CJK. First released in 2014, it is a Pan-CJK font: a single typeface design intended to support the Chinese, Japanese, and Korean languages, covering Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese, Japanese, and Korean scripts with regional glyph variants.

Design

The design was led by Adobe with contributions from partner foundries Sandoll Communications (Korean), Changzhou SinoType and Iwata Corporation (Chinese and Japanese). The family was released in seven weights, from ExtraLight to Heavy, and contains roughly 65,000 glyphs per language variant — among the largest fonts ever produced — covering the unified Han ideographs as well as kana, hangul, Latin, Greek, and Cyrillic characters.

Licensing and distribution

Source Han Sans is available under the SIL Open Font License. Adobe distributes it through its open-source channels, while Google distributes the essentially identical design as Noto Sans CJK as part of the Noto font project, whose aim is to support all the world's languages. Its companion serif design, Source Han Serif (Noto Serif CJK), was released in 2017.

Usage

The font is widely used in operating systems, applications, and interfaces that need consistent CJK text rendering, and it serves as a default fallback font in many Linux distributions and Android devices.
