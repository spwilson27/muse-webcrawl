Fiat Tagliero Building

The Fiat Tagliero Building is a Futurist-style service station in Asmara, Eritrea, completed in 1938. Designed by the Italian architect Giuseppe Pettazzi, the building is one of the most famous examples of Italian colonial architecture in the Horn of Africa and a landmark of the modernist heritage of Asmara, which was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2017.

Design

The building was commissioned as a petrol station for the Fiat company, whose local agent was Tagliero. Pettazzi designed it to resemble an aeroplane: a central tower evokes the cockpit and fuselage, while two enormous cantilevered concrete wings, each about 15 metres long, sweep out on either side like the wings of an aircraft. Remarkably, the wings are unsupported by pillars. According to a frequently repeated account, when the scaffolding was due to be removed, local authorities doubted the wings would hold; Pettazzi is said to have stood beneath them — reportedly with a revolver pointed at the builder, or standing on the wing himself in other versions — as the supports were taken away, and the wings held.

History

Asmara was developed as the capital of Italian Eritrea, and in the 1930s it acquired an exceptional concentration of modernist and Art Deco buildings, earning it the nickname "Little Rome" (Piccola Roma). The Fiat Tagliero Building survived the upheavals of the twentieth century largely intact. Today it remains in use as a service station and offices, and it is protected as part of the UNESCO-listed historic perimeter of Asmara.
