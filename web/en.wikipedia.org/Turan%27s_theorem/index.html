Turán's theorem

Turán's theorem is a fundamental result in extremal graph theory, proved by Hungarian mathematician Pál Turán in 1941. It determines the maximum number of edges in a graph on n vertices that does not contain a complete graph Kr+1 as a subgraph.

Statement

The extremal graph is the Turán graph T(n, r), the complete r-partite graph on n vertices whose parts are as equal in size as possible. Turán's theorem states that any Kr+1-free graph has at most as many edges as T(n, r), and this bound is attained uniquely by the Turán graph.

Special cases

For r = 2, the theorem reduces to Mantel's theorem: a triangle-free graph on n vertices has at most ⌊n²/4⌋ edges.

Significance

The theorem founded extremal graph theory and has spawned stability versions, counting generalizations, and extensions such as the Erdős–Stone theorem.
