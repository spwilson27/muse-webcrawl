Andrey Markov

Andrey Andreyevich Markov (14 June 1856 – 20 July 1922) was a Russian mathematician best known for his work on stochastic processes. His primary subject of research became known as Markov chains and Markov processes — sequences of random variables in which the future state depends only on the present state, not on the sequence of events that preceded it (the Markov property). His work laid foundations now applied in physics, biology, linguistics, economics and computer science, including the algorithms behind search engines and speech recognition.

Biography

Markov was born in Ryazan, the son of a forestry official. He studied at St. Petersburg University, where he was a student of Pafnuty Chebyshev, the founder of the St. Petersburg school of probability. After graduating in 1878 he remained at the university, defending his master's thesis in 1880 and his doctoral thesis in 1884, and became a professor in 1893. He was elected to the St. Petersburg Academy of Sciences and remained active in academic life until his death, despite chronic ill health after 1919.

Work

Beginning around 1906, Markov extended the law of large numbers and the central limit theorem to sequences of dependent random variables — chains of linked trials. In a celebrated 1913 application, he counted vowel–consonant transitions in 20,000 letters of Pushkin's Eugene Onegin, demonstrating his chains on real data in what is regarded as an early feat of statistical text analysis and stylometry. He also contributed to analysis, continued fractions, and number theory, and his textbook on probability (1900) long served as a standard reference.

Activism

Markov was outspoken on public affairs: he protested against political restrictions on academics, resigned academic honours in disputes with the authorities, and in 1912 asked to be excommunicated from the Russian Orthodox Church in protest at the church's treatment of Leo Tolstoy, remarking that he did not wish to belong to a church that excommunicated the writer.

Legacy

The concepts he created — Markov chains, Markov random fields, Markov decision processes, and the Markov property — underpin Monte Carlo methods (Markov chain Monte Carlo), hidden Markov models used in speech and bioinformatics, reinforcement learning, and Google's PageRank. His son, Andrey Markov Jr., became a noted mathematician in constructive logic.
