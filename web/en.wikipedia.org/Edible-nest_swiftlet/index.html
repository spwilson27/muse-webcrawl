The edible-nest swiftlet (Aerodramus fuciphagus), also known as the white-nest swiftlet, is a small bird of the swift family Apodidae. It is found in Southeast Asia — including Indonesia, Malaysia, Vietnam, Thailand, and the Philippines — where it nests colonially in limestone caves and, increasingly, in purpose-built "swiftlet houses". Its nest is made entirely of solidified saliva and is the main ingredient of bird's nest soup, a delicacy in Chinese cuisine.

Edible-nest swiftlet with its saliva nest

Description

The edible-nest swiftlet is about 11–12 cm (4.3–4.7 in) long, with dark grey-brown upperparts and paler underparts. Like other Aerodramus swiftlets it navigates in dark caves by echolocation, emitting audible clicking calls. It feeds on the wing, catching small flying insects over forests, coasts, and farmland.

Nests and harvest

During the breeding season, the male's salivary glands enlarge and produce strands of viscous saliva, which harden in the air into a translucent white bracket-shaped nest attached to a cave wall. Because the nest consists almost purely of saliva, it commands a very high price — edible bird's nests are among the most expensive animal products in the world, retailing for thousands of dollars per kilogram. Harvesting from caves such as those at Gomantong and Niah in Borneo is dangerous and is regulated by licences and closed seasons. Commercial "swiftlet farming" in concrete houses, where recorded calls attract nesting birds, now produces a large share of the supply in Indonesia and Malaysia, raising issues of quality control (including nitrite contamination) and consumer protection.
