Trophy truck

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A trophy truck, also known as a trick truck in some sanctioning bodies, is the fastest class of off-road racing vehicle, designed for high-speed desert racing. Trophy trucks compete in series such as the SCORE International desert championships, including the Baja 1000 and Baja 500, and in the Best in the Desert series in the United States.

Design

Trophy trucks are purpose-built tube-chassis machines clothed in a fibreglass body resembling a production pickup. Most are powered by large-displacement V8 engines producing 800–900 horsepower or more, driving the rear wheels (or, in newer designs, all four wheels) through an automatic transmission. Suspension travel is extreme — up to about 24 inches at the front and 36 inches at the rear — allowing the trucks to cross desert whoops and ruts at speeds exceeding 120 mph on rough terrain. They typically run 39- or 40-inch tyres and carry large fuel cells for long desert stages.

History

The class was formally launched by SCORE in 1994 to consolidate the premier unlimited truck categories. It has since become the headline class of desert racing, with notable drivers including Ivan Stewart, Robby Gordon, Rob MacCachren, and B. J. Baldwin. A separate spec class, Trophy Truck Spec, limits engines to control costs.
