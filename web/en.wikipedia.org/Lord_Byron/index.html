George Gordon Byron, 6th Baron Byron (22 January 1788 – 19 April 1824), known as Lord Byron, was an English poet and peer, one of the leading figures of the Romantic movement. He is regarded as one of the greatest English poets and remains widely read and influential. Among his best-known works are the lengthy narrative poems Don Juan and Childe Harold's Pilgrimage, and the short lyric "She Walks in Beauty".

Life

Byron was born in London, the son of Captain John "Mad Jack" Byron and Catherine Gordon of Gight. He inherited the barony at the age of ten. Educated at Harrow and Trinity College, Cambridge, he published his first collection, Hours of Idleness, in 1807. After a grand tour of the Mediterranean he published the first cantos of Childe Harold's Pilgrimage in 1812 and, in his famous phrase, "awoke one morning and found myself famous."

His marriage to Annabella Milbanke collapsed amid scandal, and in 1816 Byron left England permanently. He lived in Switzerland — famously spending the "Year Without a Summer" with Percy Bysshe Shelley and Mary Shelley at Lake Geneva — and then in Italy, where he wrote Manfred, Cain and much of Don Juan.

Death in Greece

In 1823 Byron joined the Greek war of independence against the Ottoman Empire, equipping a fleet and troops at his own expense. He died of fever at Missolonghi in April 1824 and is revered in Greece as a national hero.

Legacy

The figure of the "Byronic hero" — brooding, defiant and self-destructive — derives from Byron's works and his own flamboyant life. His daughter Ada Lovelace is remembered as a pioneering figure in computing.
