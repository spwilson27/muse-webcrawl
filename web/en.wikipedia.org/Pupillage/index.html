Pupillage

A pupillage, in England and Wales, is the final vocational stage in the training of a barrister, lasting twelve months, undertaken in barristers' chambers after successful completion of a Bar training course (formerly the Bar Professional Training Course). Pupils are supervised by an experienced barrister, their pupil supervisor.

Structure

Pupillage is divided into two six-month stages ("sixes"). In the first, non-practising six, the pupil shadows the supervisor and undertakes research and drafting. In the practising second six, the pupil may accept their own instructions and appear in court. Pupils receive a guaranteed minimum award funded by chambers, set by the Bar Standards Board. Applications are made largely through the Pupillage Gateway, a centralized online system.

Other jurisdictions

Similar periods of supervised practice exist in other common-law jurisdictions; in Hong Kong pupillage is likewise a requirement for barristers, while in Scotland the equivalent for advocates is called "devilling".
