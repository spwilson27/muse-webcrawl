Megalithic tomb

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A megalithic tomb is a burial chamber built of large stones (megaliths), typically during the Neolithic and early Bronze Age. Such tombs are found across Europe, the Mediterranean, the Near East, and parts of Asia and Africa, and include forms such as dolmens, passage graves, gallery graves and portal tombs.

Types

A dolmen consists of upright stones supporting a large capstone over a chamber. A passage grave, such as Newgrange in Ireland or Maeshowe in Orkney, has a chamber reached by a stone-lined passage, often covered by a mound or cairn. Gallery graves are elongated chambers, sometimes divided into segments.

Distribution and dating

In Europe, megalithic tombs were built roughly between 4800 and 2300 BCE, with notable concentrations in Iberia, France, Britain, Ireland, Scandinavia and northern Germany. Many were used for collective burials over long periods.

Interpretation

Archaeologists debate the social organisation behind their construction, which required coordinated labour, and their roles as territorial markers, ancestral monuments and ceremonial sites.
