Hyperbaric medicine

A multiplace hyperbaric chamber.

Hyperbaric medicine is medical treatment in which an ambient pressure greater than sea level atmospheric pressure is a necessary component. The treatment comprises hyperbaric oxygen therapy (HBOT), the medical use of oxygen at an ambient pressure higher than atmospheric pressure, and therapeutic recompression for decompression illness, intended to reduce injurious effects of systemic gas bubbles by physically reducing their size and providing improved conditions for elimination of bubbles and excess dissolved gas.

The equipment required for hyperbaric oxygen treatment consists of a pressure chamber, which may be of rigid or flexible construction, and a means of delivering 100% oxygen. Monoplace chambers treat a single patient, while multiplace chambers accommodate several patients and attendants. Treatment typically takes place at pressures of about 2 to 3 atmospheres absolute for sessions of 90 minutes to two hours.

Indications

Recognised indications include decompression sickness, gas embolism, carbon monoxide poisoning, gas gangrene (clostridial myonecrosis), crush injuries and compartment syndrome, problem wounds such as diabetic foot ulcers, delayed radiation injury, necrotising soft-tissue infections, refractory osteomyelitis, compromised skin grafts and flaps, thermal burns, and sudden sensorineural hearing loss. Many further uses are promoted "off-label", though evidence for some of them is weak.

Mechanism

Breathing pure oxygen under pressure greatly increases the amount of oxygen dissolved in blood plasma, according to Henry's law, allowing oxygenation of tissues with poor blood supply. Elevated pressure also shrinks gas bubbles in decompression sickness, and high oxygen tensions enhance white-cell killing of bacteria, stimulate angiogenesis, and reduce oedema through vasoconstriction.

Risks

Complications include middle-ear and sinus barotrauma, temporary myopia, and, rarely, oxygen-toxicity seizures. Fire risk inside chambers is a serious hazard and is controlled by strict protocols.
