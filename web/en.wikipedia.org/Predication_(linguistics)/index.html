Predication (linguistics)

In linguistics, predication is the relation between a subject and a predicate: the act of attributing a property, state or action to an entity. In the traditional analysis, a clause is divided into a subject, denoting what is spoken about, and a predicate, which says something about the subject — as in "The cat sleeps".

Approaches

In formal semantics, predication is modelled by function application: a predicate such as an intransitive verb denotes a function from individuals to truth values, and combining it with a subject term yields a proposition. Predicate logic takes its name from this analysis. Generative grammar distinguishes external predication, where the subject originates outside the verb phrase, from secondary predication, in which an additional predicate is applied to an argument, as in "She wiped the table clean" or depictive constructions such as "He ate the meat raw".

Types

Predicates may be verbal, adjectival, nominal or prepositional, and languages differ in whether a copula is required, as in Russian or Arabic present-tense nominal sentences where no copula appears. Predication is also central to theories of information structure, where the predicate typically carries the comment made about a topic.
