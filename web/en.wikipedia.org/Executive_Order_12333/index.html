Executive Order 12333, titled "United States Intelligence Activities", is an executive order issued on 4 December 1981 by President Ronald Reagan. It is the principal legal framework governing the organisation and conduct of the United States Intelligence Community.

Provisions

The order defines the goals and responsibilities of intelligence agencies, authorises the collection of foreign intelligence, and sets rules intended to protect the rights of United States persons, including restrictions on intelligence gathering within the United States and a prohibition on assassination. It empowers agencies such as the National Security Agency to collect, retain and disseminate foreign intelligence information.

Amendments and significance

The order was significantly amended by Executive Order 13470 in 2008, which strengthened the role of the Director of National Intelligence. Because much signals intelligence collection occurs abroad, Order 12333 governs a large share of NSA surveillance activity, a role that attracted public attention following the disclosures by Edward Snowden in 2013.
