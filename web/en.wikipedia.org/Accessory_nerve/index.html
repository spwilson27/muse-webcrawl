Accessory nerve

Accessory nerve
Cranial nerve: XI
Innervates: Sternocleidomastoid, trapezius
Exits via: Jugular foramen

The accessory nerve, also known as the spinal accessory nerve, is the eleventh cranial nerve (CN XI). It supplies the sternocleidomastoid and trapezius muscles. The nerve arises from the upper spinal cord and medulla, enters the skull through the foramen magnum, and exits through the jugular foramen.

Structure

The nerve has a spinal root, arising from the accessory nucleus in the upper five or six cervical segments, and in some descriptions a cranial root from the nucleus ambiguus, which largely joins the vagus nerve.

Function

The accessory nerve controls head rotation and tilting via the sternocleidomastoid and shoulder elevation and scapular movement via the trapezius.

Clinical significance

Injury, often from neck surgery or trauma, causes weakness in turning the head and shrugging the shoulder, with atrophy of the trapezius and a characteristic drooping shoulder.
