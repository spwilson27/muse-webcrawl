Franz Josef Strauss (German: Franz Josef Strauß; 6 September 1915 – 3 October 1988) was a German politician of the Christian Social Union (CSU), which he led from 1961 until his death. A dominant and polarising figure in West German politics, he served as federal minister for atomic affairs, defence and finance, and from 1978 as Minister-President of Bavaria.

Career

Born in Munich, Strauss studied at the University of Munich and served in the Wehrmacht on the Eastern Front during the Second World War. After the war he co-founded the CSU in Bavaria, entered the Bundestag in 1949, and rose quickly under Konrad Adenauer: as Minister of Defence from 1956 he rebuilt the Bundeswehr and pushed West German rearmament within NATO. His career was shaken by the Spiegel affair of 1962, when his role in the arrest of journalists of Der Spiegel forced his resignation, an episode seen as a landmark for press freedom in the Federal Republic.

Later years

Strauss returned as Minister of Finance in the Grand Coalition of 1966–69, working with the SPD's Karl Schiller. In 1980 he ran as the CDU/CSU candidate for Chancellor but lost to Helmut Schmidt. As Bavarian Minister-President he modernised the state's economy, championed the aerospace industry (Munich's airport now bears his name), and remained a combative national figure until his sudden death from a heart attack in 1988.
