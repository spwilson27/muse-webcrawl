Endogamy

Endogamy is the cultural practice of mating or marrying within a specific social group, religious denomination, caste, or ethnic group, rejecting or discouraging unions with those from outside the group. It is the opposite of exogamy, which is the practice of marrying outside one's group.

Social functions

Endogamy helps preserve group identity, cultural traditions, property, and social boundaries across generations. It has historically been practiced by royal families, religious communities such as the Parsis and the Amish, and within the caste system of India.

Genetic effects

Long-term endogamy within a small population can reduce genetic diversity and increase the prevalence of certain inherited disorders, a phenomenon studied in population genetics through the founder effect.
