The Straits of Tiran are the narrow sea passages between the Sinai and Arabian peninsulas that connect the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aqaba. They are the sole maritime access to the ports of Eilat (Israel) and Aqaba (Jordan), which gives them exceptional strategic importance; blockades of the straits were a central cause of both the Suez Crisis of 1956 and the Six-Day War of 1967.

Geography

The straits are named for Tiran Island, at their mouth, and are divided by islands and coral reefs — Tiran, Sanafir and the Gordon, Thomas, Woodhouse and Jackson reefs — into channels only a few hundred metres wide in places. Two passages are navigable by large ships: the Enterprise Passage near the Sinai shore and the Grafton Passage nearer Tiran Island. The reefs are famed diving sites within and near Egypt's Ras Muhammad National Park area.

History

Egypt blockaded the straits to Israeli shipping from 1949, and again closed them in May 1967 — a step Israel had declared would be an act of war, precipitating the Six-Day War, after which Israel occupied the Sinai shore. The 1979 Egypt–Israel peace treaty guaranteed freedom of navigation through the straits, policed since 1982 by the Multinational Force and Observers. Sovereignty over Tiran and Sanafir islands was transferred from Egypt to Saudi Arabia by an agreement ratified in 2017. A proposed Saudi causeway, the King Salman Bridge, would cross the straits to Egypt.
