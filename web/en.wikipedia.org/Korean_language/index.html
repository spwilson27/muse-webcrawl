Korean language

Korean
한국어 / 조선말
Native to: Korea
Speakers: ~82 million
Family: Koreanic
Writing system: Hangul

The Korean language is native to the Korean Peninsula and is the official language of both South Korea (the Republic of Korea) and North Korea (the Democratic People's Republic of Korea). It is spoken by about 82 million people worldwide, including large diaspora communities in China, the United States, Japan, and Central Asia.

Classification

Korean is a member of the Koreanic language family, which also includes the Jeju language of Jeju Island. Historically Korean was grouped with the disputed Altaic hypothesis, but most linguists today treat Koreanic as a family of its own. The language is agglutinative, uses subject–object–verb word order, and has an elaborate system of honorifics that encode the social relationship between speaker and listener.

Writing system

Korean is written in Hangul, a featural alphabet created in 1443 under King Sejong the Great and promulgated in the document Hunminjeongeum. Hangul consists of 24 basic letters (14 consonants and 10 vowels) combined into syllabic blocks. Before Hangul, Korean was written using Chinese characters (Hanja), which are still seen in South Korea in academic and legal contexts.

Dialects

Dialects of Korean are generally mutually intelligible across the peninsula, with the notable exception of the divergent Jejuan varieties. Major dialect groups include the Seoul–Gyeonggi dialect (the basis of the South Korean standard, pyojun-eo), the Gyeongsang and Jeolla dialects in the southeast and southwest, and the Hamgyong and P'yŏngan dialects in the north. Since division in 1948, the standards of North and South Korea have drifted apart in vocabulary, spelling, and pronunciation.

Grammar

Korean marks grammatical relations with particles attached to nouns, and verbs are inflected for tense, aspect, mood, honorification, and speech level. Speech levels range from formal deferential styles to intimate plain speech. Pronouns are often omitted when inferable from context.
