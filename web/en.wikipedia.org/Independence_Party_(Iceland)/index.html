The Independence Party (Icelandic: Sjálfstæðisflokkurinn) is a liberal-conservative political party in Iceland. Formed in 1929 by the merger of the Conservative Party and the Liberal Party, it has historically been the largest and most successful political party in the country, participating in government for most of Iceland's history as an independent republic.

The party supports Iceland's membership of NATO, low taxation, free-market economics, and limited government intervention, while historically opposing membership of the European Union. Its voter base traditionally spans urban professionals, business interests, and much of the fishing industry.

History

The Independence Party was founded in 1929, during Iceland's period as a kingdom in personal union with Denmark, and took its name from its advocacy of full Icelandic independence, achieved in 1944. From the 1930s onward it routinely won between a quarter and two-fifths of the vote. Long-serving leader Davíð Oddsson was Prime Minister from 1991 to 2004, the longest-serving in Icelandic history, overseeing privatisation and economic liberalisation. The party was in government during the 2008 Icelandic financial crisis, after which its support temporarily dropped, though it later recovered and returned to coalition governments, including under Bjarni Benediktsson.

Ideology

The party combines economic liberalism — low taxes, deregulation, private enterprise — with elements of conservatism on social questions, though it has liberalised over time. It favours strong ties with the United States and NATO membership, and it supports the Icelandic króna rather than adoption of the euro.

Election results

The party has won pluralities in most Althing elections since 1929, and its leaders have frequently served as Prime Minister of Iceland, including Jóhann Hafstein, Ólafur Thors, Bjarne Benediktsson (the elder), Geir Hallgrímsson, Þorsteinn Pálsson, Davíð Oddsson, and Bjarni Benediktsson.
