Madeira, officially the Autonomous Region of Madeira, is an archipelago situated in the North Atlantic Ocean, in a region known as Macaronesia, just under 400 kilometres north of the Canary Islands and about 1,000 kilometres southwest of mainland Portugal, to which it belongs as an autonomous region. The archipelago comprises the islands of Madeira, Porto Santo, and the uninhabited Desertas and Savage Islands. Its capital and largest city is Funchal, on the main island's south coast.

History

The islands were officially discovered by Portuguese sailors in 1419–1420, in the service of Prince Henry the Navigator, and were settled shortly afterwards; the name Madeira means "wood" in Portuguese, referring to the dense forests that once covered the island. Madeira became an important producer first of sugar and then of its famous fortified Madeira wine, which was shipped around the world and prized in colonial America. After Portugal's 1974 revolution, Madeira gained political autonomy in 1976.

Geography and economy

The islands are volcanic and mountainous, with a mild subtropical climate often described as an "eternal spring." A network of irrigation channels called levadas carries water from the wet north to the drier south and doubles as walking paths. The native laurisilva forest is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Tourism is the largest economic sector, alongside wine, embroidery, and financial services supported by the region's free-trade zone. Cristiano Ronaldo International Airport serves the island.
