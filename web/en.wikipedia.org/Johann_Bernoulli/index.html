Johann Bernoulli

[Image placeholder: portrait of Johann Bernoulli]

Johann Bernoulli (also known as Jean in French or John in English; 6 August 1667 [O.S. 27 July] – 1 January 1748) was a Swiss mathematician and one of the many prominent mathematicians in the Bernoulli family. He is known for his contributions to infinitesimal calculus and for educating Leonhard Euler in the subject during his youth.

Born in Basel, Johann initially studied medicine at the University of Basel but, with his elder brother Jacob, secretly studied the new calculus of Leibniz. The two brothers became the foremost exponents of Leibniz's methods. Johann held chairs at Groningen (1695–1705) and then at Basel, succeeding Jacob after his death.

Contributions

Johann posed the famous brachistochrone problem in 1696 — finding the curve of fastest descent — whose solutions by himself, Jacob, Leibniz, Newton, and de l'Hôpital effectively launched the calculus of variations. He worked on differential equations, the catenary, optics, and mechanics, and mentored the young Euler. His textbook on integral calculus and his correspondence with Leibniz and others made him a central figure in European mathematics.

Disputes

Johann was famously irascible. His rivalry with his brother Jacob escalated into public feuding, and later he quarrelled bitterly with his own son Daniel over a prize from the Paris Academy and over the publication date of Daniel's Hydrodynamica. He was also an ardent defender of Leibniz in the calculus priority dispute with Newton.
