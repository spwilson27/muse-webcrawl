Morgestraich at the Basler Fasnacht

Fasnacht (also Fastnacht, Fasnet, or Fasching in some regions) is the traditional pre-Lenten carnival celebrated in Switzerland, southern and western Germany, Austria, and parts of France. The name derives from the Middle High German vasnaht, the night before the Lenten fast (Fastenzeit). Celebrations feature masked processions, drummers and pipers, satirical verses lampooning current events, and elaborate hand-carved wooden masks.

Basler Fasnacht

The best-known Fasnacht is the Basler Fasnacht in Basel, Switzerland, which begins on the Monday after Ash Wednesday at 4:00 a.m. with the Morgestraich: all lights in the old town are extinguished and thousands of costumed drummers and pipers, illuminated only by painted lanterns, march through the streets playing a single traditional march. The festival lasts exactly 72 hours and features cliques (Cliquen) performing satirical subjects (Sujets) on decorated lanterns, and the recitation of satirical verses called Schnitzelbänk. In 2017 the Basler Fasnacht was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Other regional forms

The Swabian-Alemannic Fastnet in southwestern Germany uses grotesque hand-carved masks and traditional guild figures such as the Narren (fools), with rituals meant to drive out winter. Lucerne's Fasnacht begins with the Fötzeliräge ("paper rain") on Dirty Thursday, and the carnival of Binche in Belgium and the Rhineland Karneval are related traditions, though they begin on Rosenmontag (Rose Monday) rather than after Ash Wednesday.
