7.62×39mm

The 7.62×39mm is a rimless bottlenecked intermediate cartridge of Soviet origin. It was designed during World War II and is best known as the chambering of the AK-47 and its derivatives, as well as the SKS carbine. It remains one of the most widely used military rifle cartridges in the world.

History

Soviet designers, influenced by the German 7.92×33mm Kurz and the requirements of modern mechanised warfare, developed the cartridge in 1943. It was first fielded in the SKS and the RPD light machine gun before becoming standard with the adoption of the AK-47 in 1949.

Characteristics

The cartridge fires a 7.62 mm bullet from a 39 mm case. Typical loadings use a bullet of about 7.9 g (122 grains) at a muzzle velocity around 730 m/s, giving effective performance at ranges of roughly 300–400 metres. Its tapered case aids reliable feeding in automatic weapons.

Weapons chambered

  • AK-47 and AKM
  • SKS carbine
  • RPD and RPK light machine guns
  • Vz. 58
