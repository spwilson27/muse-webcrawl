Narodniks

The Narodniks (Russian: народники, from narod, "the people") were members of a movement of Russian revolutionary populism in the 1860s and 1870s. Influenced by Alexander Herzen, Nikolai Chernyshevsky, Mikhail Bakunin and Pyotr Lavrov, they believed that Russia could reach socialism directly through its peasant commune (the obshchina), bypassing capitalism.

Going to the People

In the "mad summer" of 1874, thousands of young idealists from the cities went to the countryside to live among and propagandize the peasantry. The peasants were largely unreceptive, often handing the propagandists over to the police; hundreds were arrested and subjected to mass trials, including the Trial of the 193.

From propaganda to terror

In 1876 the populists regrouped as Zemlya i Volya (Land and Liberty), which in 1879 split into the agrarian Black Repartition and the terrorist Narodnaya Volya (People's Will), which assassinated Tsar Alexander II in 1881. The populist tradition later fed into the Socialist Revolutionary Party, while Marxists such as Plekhanov emerged from former Narodniks who had abandoned populism. Lenin's elder brother Alexander Ulyanov was executed for a Narodnaya Volya plot against Alexander III.
