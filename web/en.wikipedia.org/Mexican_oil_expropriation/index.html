Mexican oil expropriation

The Mexican oil expropriation (Expropiación petrolera) was the nationalisation of all petroleum reserves, facilities, and foreign oil companies in Mexico on 18 March 1938 by President Lázaro Cárdenas. Acting under Article 27 of the Mexican Constitution and the Expropriation Law, Cárdenas seized the assets of 17 foreign companies, including Mexican Eagle (a Royal Dutch Shell subsidiary) and American firms such as Standard Oil interests, and created the state-owned company Pemex (Petróleos Mexicanos) on 7 June 1938.

Background

The expropriation followed a bitter labour dispute: Mexican oil workers struck for higher wages and better conditions, and after the companies refused to comply with a Supreme Court ruling upholding an arbitration award, Cárdenas acted. The decision was greeted by massive public celebrations in Mexico.

International reaction

The United States, under the Good Neighbor policy, responded moderately, while Britain broke diplomatic relations with Mexico. A boycott of Mexican oil was organised by the companies, but Mexico found new markets, including Germany and Italy. Compensation settlements were eventually reached in the 1940s, paying the companies a fraction of their claimed valuations.

Legacy

18 March is commemorated in Mexico as a national civic holiday anniversary. Pemex remained the state monopoly on oil for 75 years, until energy reforms under President Enrique Peña Nieto in 2013 opened the sector to private investment.
