Spider web

A spider web is a structure created by a spider out of proteinaceous spider silk extruded from its spinnerets, generally meant to catch its prey. Webs also serve as shelters and, in some species, as courtship and nursery structures.

Types

Well-known forms include spiral orb webs, funnel webs, sheet webs, cobwebs (tangle webs), and tube webs. Many spiders build a new web daily, often eating the old one to recycle its protein.

Silk

Spider silk is exceptionally strong and elastic for its weight. Different glands produce different silks: dragline silk for frames, viscid silk for sticky capture spirals, and softer silks for wrapping prey and egg sacs. The stickiness of orb webs comes from droplets of glue coating the capture spiral.
