Maldhari

The Maldhari are a community of traditional pastoralists and cattle herders in the Indian state of Gujarat, particularly associated with the Gir Forest region of Saurashtra. The name derives from the Gujarati words mal (livestock) and dhari (keeper or owner). Maldharis comprise several Hindu and Muslim sub-groups, including Rabari, Bharwad, Charan and Ahir herders.

Way of life

Maldharis traditionally live in villages called nesses inside and around the Gir forest, grazing buffalo and cattle and selling milk and ghee. Their herds are famous for producing the milk used in many Gujarati dairy products. The community is renowned for its coexistence with the wild Asiatic lions of Gir: despite occasional predation on their livestock, Maldharis have historically tolerated the lions, and this relationship is considered a key factor in the lions' survival. Studies show lions take a significant share of their diet from Maldhari livestock, for which herders may receive compensation.

Modern pressures

From the 1970s, many Maldhari families were relocated out of the Gir sanctuary area as part of conservation policy, a process that remains socially controversial; a few thousand people still reside in nesses within the protected area. Grazing rights, compensation and resettlement continue to be debated between the community and forest authorities.
