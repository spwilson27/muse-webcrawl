Adolphe Thiers

Louis Adolphe Thiers (1797–1877) was a French statesman and historian who served as the first president of the French Third Republic, from 1871 to 1873.

Career

A journalist and liberal deputy under the July Monarchy, Thiers served as prime minister in 1836 and 1840. He opposed Louis-Napoleon's coup, returned to prominence in the 1860s, and after France's defeat in the Franco-Prussian War negotiated the peace with Germany and led the government that suppressed the Paris Commune in 1871.

Presidency and later life

As chief of the executive and then president, he secured the early payment of the war indemnity and the withdrawal of German troops. He resigned in 1873 after losing parliamentary support. He also wrote major histories of the French Revolution and of the Consulate and Empire.
