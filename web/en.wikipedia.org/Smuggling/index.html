Smuggling

DefinitionIllegal transportation of goods or people across a border, evading duties or prohibitions
Common goodsDrugs, weapons, tobacco, alcohol, wildlife, counterfeit goods
Countered byCustoms and border agencies

Smuggling is the illegal transportation of objects, substances, information or people, such as across an international border, in violation of applicable laws or other regulations. Motives include evading taxes and customs duties, moving prohibited goods such as narcotics and weapons, and transporting people who lack permission to cross — the latter, when done for profit with the consent of those moved, being distinguished from human trafficking.

History

Smuggling is as old as the first customs duties. In 18th-century Britain, high taxes on tea, spirits and tobacco sustained vast smuggling networks along the Cornish and Kent coasts; gangs landed goods by night and moved them inland along "smugglers' paths", sometimes clashing violently with revenue officers. During American Prohibition (1920–1933), rum-running from ships offshore and across the Canadian border made fortunes for organised crime.

Methods

Smugglers conceal contraband in vehicles, luggage, cargo and the body; use false documents and corrupt officials; and employ tunnels, drones, ultralight aircraft, semi-submersible vessels and "mules". Goods are also misdeclared — undervalued, misclassified, or hidden among legitimate freight in shipping containers, only a small fraction of which can be physically inspected.

Enforcement

Customs and border agencies counter smuggling with intelligence-led targeting, scanners and detection dogs, patrols and international cooperation; offences typically carry fines and imprisonment, with asset forfeiture common. Economists note that high tariffs and prohibitions create the price differences that make smuggling profitable.
