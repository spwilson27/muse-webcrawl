Gazprom

Gazprom (Russian: Газпром) is a Russian majority state-owned multinational energy corporation headquartered in Saint Petersburg. Founded in 1989 from the Soviet Ministry of Gas Industry, it is the world's largest extractor of natural gas and was for decades the principal supplier of pipeline gas to Europe.

History

Gazprom was created in 1989 when the Soviet Ministry of Gas Industry was converted into a state corporation, retaining control of the USSR's vast gas reserves, centred on the giant fields of Western Siberia such as Urengoy and Yamburg. It survived the post-Soviet privatisations largely intact and was re-registered as a joint-stock company in 1993, with the Russian state gradually reasserting majority control in the 2000s.

Operations

The company controls roughly a sixth of global gas reserves and operates the world's largest gas transmission system, over 175,000 km of pipelines. Major export projects have included the Nord Stream pipelines under the Baltic Sea to Germany, TurkStream under the Black Sea, and Power of Siberia to China. Its subsidiaries include the oil producer Gazprom Neft and the power generator Gazprom Energoholding.

Europe and the 2022 crisis

Before 2022 Gazprom supplied about 40 per cent of the European Union's imported gas. Following Russia's invasion of Ukraine, deliveries through Ukraine and the Yamal pipeline dwindled, Nord Stream was halted and then sabotaged in September 2022, and European imports of Russian pipeline gas collapsed, driving Gazprom to its first annual loss in decades in 2023 and a strategic pivot toward Asian markets.
