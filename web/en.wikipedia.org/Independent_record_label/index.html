Independent record label

An independent record label (or indie label) is a record label that operates without the funding or distribution of the major record labels — today principally Universal Music Group, Sony Music, and Warner Music Group. Independent labels range from bedroom operations to large companies with international distribution deals, and they have historically championed genres neglected by majors, from rhythm and blues and rock and roll to punk, indie rock, and hip hop.

Independent labels have launched genres from punk to hip hop.

History

In the 1940s and 1950s, American independents such as Sun, Chess, and Atlantic recorded artists the majors ignored. In Britain, labels like Island and Virgin grew from independent roots. The punk movement of the late 1970s inspired a wave of DIY labels — Rough Trade, Factory, SST, and Dischord among them — that built independent distribution networks and fostered the indie music culture of the 1980s and 1990s.

Business models

Indies typically offer smaller advances but more creative freedom and sometimes profit-sharing deals. Many license distribution through major-owned distributors while retaining ownership of masters. The digital era and streaming have reduced manufacturing and distribution barriers, though playlist economics and consolidation continue to shape the sector. Trade bodies such as AIM and A2IM represent independent labels' interests.
