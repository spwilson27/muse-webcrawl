Karmann

Wilhelm Karmann GmbH, commonly known as Karmann, was a German automobile manufacturer and contract manufacturer based in Osnabrück. Founded in 1901 as a coachbuilder, it became Germany's largest independent motor vehicle manufacturing company, producing complete vehicles and convertible roof systems for major carmakers.

History

The company was founded by Wilhelm Karmann in 1901, when he took over the Christian Klages carriage works in Osnabrück. It first built horse-drawn carriages and then car bodies. After the Second World War, Karmann began its long association with Volkswagen, producing the Beetle Cabriolet from 1949.

Karmann's best-known product was the Volkswagen Karmann Ghia, designed by Italy's Ghia studio and built by Karmann from 1955 to 1974. Other notable vehicles included the Volkswagen Scirocco, the Ford Sierra, the Audi Cabriolet, the Mercedes-Benz CLK convertible, the Chrysler Crossfire and numerous convertible roof systems, including for the Volkswagen New Beetle Cabriolet and the Bentley Continental GTC.

Insolvency

Hit by the 2008 financial crisis and the loss of contracts, Karmann filed for insolvency in April 2009. Parts of the business were acquired by Volkswagen and by the Finnish company Valmet Automotive, and the convertible roof division was sold to Magna International. The Osnabrück plant was taken over by Volkswagen, which continued vehicle production there.
