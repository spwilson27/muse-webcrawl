Nilotic peoples

The Nilotic peoples are a group of related Indigenous peoples of the Nile Valley who speak Nilotic languages, a large sub-group of the Nilo-Saharan languages. They inhabit South Sudan, Sudan, Egypt, Ethiopia, Uganda, Kenya, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Rwanda, Burundi, and Tanzania. Among the better-known Nilotic groups are the Dinka, Nuer, Shilluk, Luo, Maasai, Kalenjin, Turkana, Samburu, Acholi, Lango, and Alur.

History

Nilotic peoples are believed to have originated in the region of the upper Nile and the Sudd wetlands, expanding southward and eastward over the past several thousand years. Southward migrations brought Luo-speaking peoples to the Lake Victoria basin and western Kenya, while other groups moved into the Rift Valley and the plains of what is now Kenya and Tanzania, where the Maasai became prominent pastoralists.

Culture

Cattle pastoralism is central to many Nilotic cultures: cattle are a measure of wealth, a medium of bridewealth, and a focus of ritual life. Traditionally, many Nilotic groups are noted for tall stature, age-set social organisation, and semi-nomadic herding adapted to seasonal flooding and dry-season grazing. Today Nilotic peoples practise a wide range of livelihoods and religions, including Christianity, Islam, and traditional beliefs.
