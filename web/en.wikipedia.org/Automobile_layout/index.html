An automobile layout describes the location of a car's engine and its driven wheels. Layout strongly influences interior space, handling, traction, manufacturing cost and safety, and is a basic classification used in automotive engineering.

Front-engine layouts

In the front-engine, rear-wheel-drive (FR) layout, long standard on cars worldwide, the engine drives the rear wheels through a propeller shaft. The front-engine, front-wheel-drive (FF) layout, popularised in the 1960s and 1970s, combines engine, gearbox and driven wheels at the front, freeing cabin space and improving traction in slippery conditions; it is now the most common layout for passenger cars. Four-wheel-drive variants (F4) build on both arrangements.

Rear and mid-engine layouts

Rear-engine, rear-wheel-drive (RR) cars such as the original Volkswagen Beetle and Porsche 911 place the engine over or behind the rear axle. Mid-engine layouts place the engine ahead of the rear axle, favouring weight distribution, and are typical of sports cars and supercars. Electric vehicles add further flexibility, with motors at one or both axles and flat battery floors.
