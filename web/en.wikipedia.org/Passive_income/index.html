Passive income

Passive income is income that requires little to no effort to earn or maintain. It stands in contrast to active income, such as wages, and portfolio income in some tax frameworks. In United States tax law, passive activities are defined principally as rental activities and businesses in which the taxpayer does not materially participate.

Examples

Common sources described as passive include rental property income, dividends and interest, royalties from books or patents, pensions, and earnings from businesses in which the owner is not actively involved. Digital products and online content are also frequently promoted as passive income sources, though they often require substantial initial or ongoing work.

Taxation

Tax treatment varies by country. In the United States, passive activity losses are generally deductible only against passive income, under rules introduced in 1986, with special provisions for rental real estate.

Criticism

The popular notion of entirely effort-free income is often criticized as misleading, since most such streams demand upfront capital, work, risk, or continuing management.
