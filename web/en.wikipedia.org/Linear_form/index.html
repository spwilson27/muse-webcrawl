Linear form

In mathematics, a linear form (also known as a linear functional, a one-form, or a covector) is a linear map from a vector space to its field of scalars (often the real numbers or complex numbers). If V is a vector space over a field k, a linear form is a function f : V → k satisfying f(x + y) = f(x) + f(y) and f(ax) = af(x) for all vectors x, y and scalars a.

Examples

The set of all linear forms on V forms the dual vector space V*, which has the same dimension as V when V is finite-dimensional. In Euclidean space, every linear form can be written as a dot product with a fixed vector: f(x) = a₁x₁ + … + aₙxₙ. Integration against a function, evaluation of a function at a point, and differentiation are examples of linear forms on spaces of functions. In physics, the components of a covector such as the gradient transform oppositely to those of a vector under change of coordinates.

In functional analysis

For topological vector spaces, continuous linear forms are of special importance: by the Riesz representation theorem, every continuous linear functional on a Hilbert space is given by an inner product with a unique fixed vector. The Hahn–Banach theorem guarantees that continuous linear forms can be extended from subspaces under suitable conditions.
