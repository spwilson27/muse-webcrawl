Afrikaners are a South African ethnic group descended from predominantly Dutch settlers first arriving at the Cape of Good Hope in 1652. Until 1994, they dominated South Africa's politics as well as its commercial agricultural sector. Afrikaans, a language derived mainly from Dutch, is the mother tongue of Afrikaners and of most of the mixed-race Cape Coloureds.

History

The Dutch East India Company established a refreshment station at the Cape in 1652 under Jan van Riebeeck. Free burghers, joined by French Huguenot refugees and German settlers, formed the nucleus of the population. Frontier farmers known as Trekboers expanded inland, and in the 1830s thousands of Voortrekkers migrated into the interior during the Great Trek, founding republics such as the South African Republic (Transvaal) and the Orange Free State.

The discovery of diamonds and gold brought conflict with Britain, culminating in the Second Boer War (1899–1902), after which the republics were absorbed into the Union of South Africa. Afrikaner nationalism rose in the 20th century, and the National Party's victory in 1948 introduced the apartheid system of racial segregation, which lasted until the first democratic elections in 1994.

Culture

Afrikaner culture is closely associated with the Afrikaans language, the Dutch Reformed churches, and rural traditions such as the braai. Afrikaans literature and music remain central expressions of the community's identity in modern South Africa.
