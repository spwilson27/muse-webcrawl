Nasi goreng

Nasi goreng (Indonesian and Malay for "fried rice") is a Southeast Asian rice dish, widely considered the national dish of Indonesia. It is distinguished from other Asian fried rices by its dark colour and distinctive flavour of kecap manis (sweet soy sauce), along with shallots, garlic, tamarind, chilli and shrimp paste.

Preparation

Pre-cooked, cooled rice is stir-fried in a wok with kecap manis and a ground spice paste (bumbu) of shallot, garlic, chilli and terasi (shrimp paste). Common additions include chicken, prawns, egg, cabbage and kecambah (bean sprouts). It is typically served with a fried egg on top, krupuk (crackers), acar (pickled vegetables) and sambal.

Varieties

Regional and household variations are countless: nasi goreng kampung (village style, with anchovies), nasi goreng Jawa (Javanese, sweeter), nasi goreng seafood, nasi goreng merah (red, from Makassar, coloured with tomato sauce), and nasi goreng pattaya in Malaysia, where the rice is wrapped in an omelette. It is eaten at any hour — for breakfast from the previous evening's rice, sold by street vendors (kaki lima) and served in fine restaurants alike.

Origins

Fried rice reached Southeast Asia with Chinese migration, but the use of kecap manis and local spices — and the practice of frying leftover morning rice to avoid waste and spoilage in a tropical climate — made it distinctly Indonesian.
