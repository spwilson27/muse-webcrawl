Higher education

Higher education is the stage of formal learning that follows secondary education, typically provided by universities, colleges and other tertiary institutions. It includes undergraduate and postgraduate teaching, research and scholarship, and leads to qualifications such as bachelor's, master's and doctoral degrees, as well as diplomas and certificates.

History

Institutions of advanced learning existed in many civilisations, but the modern university developed in medieval Europe, with foundations such as Bologna (1088), Oxford and Paris combining teaching corporations with degree-granting powers. Universities expanded greatly in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, and mass participation became common in developed countries after the Second World War; women, long excluded from many institutions, gained broad access over the same period.

Structure and purposes

Higher education systems vary by country but commonly combine liberal arts or specialised undergraduate study with professional training and graduate research. Governments fund or regulate much of the sector, regarding it as a driver of economic growth, social mobility and scientific progress, while debates continue over tuition costs, access, academic freedom and the balance between teaching and research.
