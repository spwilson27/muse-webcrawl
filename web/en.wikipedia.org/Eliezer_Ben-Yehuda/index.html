Eliezer Ben-Yehuda

Eliezer Ben-Yehuda (7 January 1858 – 16 December 1922) was a Russian Jewish lexicographer and newspaper editor who is regarded as the reviver of the Hebrew language in the modern era. He was the driving spirit behind the rebirth of Hebrew as a spoken language, after centuries in which it had been used mainly for liturgy and scholarship.

Early life

Ben-Yehuda was born Eliezer Yitzhak Perlman in Luzhki, in the Russian Empire (present-day Belarus). Exposed to the Haskalah (Jewish Enlightenment) and to early Zionist ideas, he concluded while studying in Paris that the revival of the Hebrew language in the Land of Israel could unite Jews worldwide. In 1879 he published the article "A Burning Question", arguing for a national home and a living national language.

Life in Jerusalem

In 1881 Ben-Yehuda emigrated to Ottoman Palestine and settled in Jerusalem. He resolved that his household would speak only Hebrew; his son, Ben-Zion (later Itamar Ben-Avi), is often described as the first native speaker of modern Hebrew. Ben-Yehuda founded Hebrew-language newspapers, including HaZvi, promoted Hebrew in schools, and co-founded the Committee of the Hebrew Language, the forerunner of the Academy of the Hebrew Language.

The dictionary

His life's work was the Complete Dictionary of Ancient and Modern Hebrew, for which he coined thousands of new words from old Hebrew roots to name modern objects and ideas. Many of his coinages remain in everyday use. The dictionary's later volumes were completed by his wife Hemda and his son after his death.

Legacy

Ben-Yehuda died of tuberculosis in Jerusalem in 1922, shortly before Hebrew was recognised as an official language of Mandatory Palestine. His work is central to the story of modern Hebrew and of Zionism.
