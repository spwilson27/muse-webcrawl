Polity

A polity is a term in political science for an organized political unit or form of government. The word derives from the Ancient Greek politeia (πολιτεία), meaning the organization of a city-state, its constitution, and the body of its citizens. A polity can be any group of people organized under a leadership or governing structure, ranging from a small community to a sovereign state or an international body such as the United Nations.

Overview

Not every political unit is a polity: a polity is generally understood to possess an organized political identity, a capacity for mobilizing resources, and a degree of institutional separation between rulers and ruled. States are the most familiar modern polities, but the term also covers empires, kingdoms, city-states, municipalities, tribal confederacies, churches with governing structures, and supranational organizations.

Typologies

Aristotle's Politics classified polities by who rules — the one, the few, or the many — and whether they rule in the common interest or their own. His preferred practical arrangement, which he himself called polity (politeia), was a mixture of oligarchy and democracy in which the middle class predominated. Modern typologies distinguish polities by their structure (unitary or federal), their head of state (monarchy or republic), and the distribution of power (democratic, authoritarian, or totalitarian).

In political science

Contemporary scholars use "polity" as a neutral umbrella term when studying political organization across cultures and historical periods, avoiding the assumptions carried by the modern concept of the state. The term also appears in ecclesiastical governance ("church polity") and in analyses of the European Union, which is often described as a polity without being a state.
