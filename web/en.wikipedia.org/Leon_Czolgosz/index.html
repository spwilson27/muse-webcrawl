Leon Frank Czolgosz (May 5, 1873 – October 29, 1901) was an American wire mill worker and anarchist who assassinated United States President William McKinley on September 6, 1901, in Buffalo, New York. McKinley died on September 14 after his wounds became infected.

Background

Born in Michigan to Polish immigrant parents, Czolgosz worked in wire mills in Ohio and grew disillusioned after witnessing labour strikes and economic inequality during the depression of the 1890s. He became interested in anarchism, attended lectures by Emma Goldman, and was influenced by the assassination of King Umberto I of Italy by the anarchist Gaetano Bresci in 1900. Mainstream anarchist circles largely regarded him — an awkward loner — with suspicion, and some suspected him of being a police provocateur.

Assassination and execution

At the Pan-American Exposition's Temple of Music, Czolgosz concealed a .32-calibre Iver Johnson revolver under a handkerchief and shot McKinley twice at close range during a public reception. He was immediately seized and beaten by bystanders and guards — McKinley reportedly urged that he not be harmed. Tried within days of the President's death, Czolgosz offered no defence, was convicted of first-degree murder, and was executed in the electric chair at Auburn Prison on October 29, 1901. His last words were reported as regret that he would not see his father again and defiance about his act. His body was destroyed with acid to prevent relic-taking.
