Mass-energy equivalence

EquationE = mc²
Proposed byAlbert Einstein
Year1905

Mass-energy equivalence is the principle, formulated by Albert Einstein in 1905, that mass and energy are the same physical property and can be converted into one another. It is expressed by the famous equation E = mc², where E is energy, m is mass, and c is the speed of light in vacuum.

Origins

The principle emerged from Einstein's special theory of relativity. Because the square of the speed of light is an enormous number, a small amount of mass corresponds to a vast amount of energy.

Applications

Mass-energy equivalence explains the energy released in nuclear fission and fusion, underpinning nuclear power and nuclear weapons, as well as the processes powering the Sun and stars.
