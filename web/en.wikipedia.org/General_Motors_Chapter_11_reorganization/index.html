The General Motors Chapter 11 reorganization of 2009 was the bankruptcy filing of the American automobile manufacturer General Motors, then one of the largest industrial corporations in the world. GM filed for protection under Chapter 11 of the United States Bankruptcy Code on June 1, 2009, in New York City, in what was at the time the fourth-largest bankruptcy in U.S. history and the largest ever for an industrial company.

Background

GM had been losing market share for decades and was burdened by high legacy costs, including pension and healthcare obligations to retirees. The financial crisis of 2008 caused a collapse in vehicle sales, and by late 2008 the company warned it would run out of cash. The U.S. federal government provided emergency bridge loans in December 2008, conditional on GM producing a viable restructuring plan.

The section 363 sale

The centerpiece of the case was a sale under section 363 of the Bankruptcy Code of substantially all of GM's productive assets to a newly formed company, NGMCO Inc., later renamed General Motors Company. The United States Treasury was the majority owner of the new company, alongside the governments of Canada and Ontario, the United Auto Workers' retiree healthcare trust, and the old GM's bondholders. The sale closed on July 10, 2009, only 40 days after the filing — an unusually rapid timetable for a case of this size.

Brands and restructuring

The reorganized company retained four core North American brands: Chevrolet, Cadillac, Buick, and GMC. The Pontiac brand was discontinued, while Saturn, Hummer, and Saab were sold or wound down. The old corporation was renamed Motors Liquidation Company and remained in bankruptcy to wind down its remaining assets and settle claims.

Aftermath

General Motors Company returned to public markets with an initial public offering in November 2010. The U.S. Treasury sold its final shares in December 2013, reporting a loss of roughly $10 billion on its investment, though government studies argued the intervention saved over a million jobs.
