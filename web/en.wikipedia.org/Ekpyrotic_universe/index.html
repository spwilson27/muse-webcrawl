Ekpyrotic universe

The ekpyrotic universe is a cosmological model proposed as an alternative to cosmic inflation, in which the hot early universe originated from the collision of two three-dimensional worlds (branes) moving in a higher-dimensional space. The name derives from the Greek ekpyrosis, meaning "conflagration", reflecting the ancient Stoic idea of a universe periodically consumed by fire and reborn.

The model

The scenario was introduced in 2001 by Justin Khoury, Burt Ovrut, Paul Steinhardt, and Neil Turok, drawing on heterotic M-theory and earlier ideas by Gibbons, Hawking, and others. In the model, our universe resides on a brane separated from a "hidden" brane by a small extra dimension. The big bang corresponds to the moment the branes collide; the kinetic energy of the collision converts into the matter and radiation of the hot early universe.

Cyclic extension

In the related cyclic model, collisions recur periodically over trillions of years: after each collision, the universe expands, dark energy accelerates the expansion and smooths it out, and the branes slowly approach again. This aims to explain the flatness, homogeneity, and the observed pattern of density fluctuations — generated here by ripples in the brane separation before collision — without an inflationary phase or an initial singularity in the usual sense.

Criticism and tests

The model has been debated: critics question the physics of brane collisions, the "bounce", and predictions for gravitational waves and non-Gaussianity. Observations of the cosmic microwave background, particularly by the Planck satellite, are used to compare ekpyrotic/cyclic predictions with inflation's, since the two scenarios differ notably in the expected primordial gravitational-wave background.
