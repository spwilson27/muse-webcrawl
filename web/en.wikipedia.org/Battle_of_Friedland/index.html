The Battle of Friedland (14 June 1807) was a major engagement of the Napoleonic Wars in which Napoleon's French army decisively defeated a Russian army under General Levin August von Bennigsen near the town of Friedland in East Prussia. Coming just over a week after the Battle of Heilsberg and four months after the bloody stalemate at Eylau, Friedland ended the War of the Fourth Coalition in Napoleon's favour.

Battle

Bennigsen crossed the River Alle (Łyna) at Friedland and occupied the town, placing his army in a cramped bend of the river with its back to the water — a dangerously exposed position. In the morning the French had only Lannes's corps on the field, which skirmished to pin the Russians while Napoleon concentrated some 80,000 men. Arriving around midday, Napoleon judged the Russian position and, in late afternoon, launched the main attack: Marshal Ney's corps, supported by massed artillery under Sénarmont, which advanced to very close range (about 150 paces) and shattered the Russian left with canister fire.

Ney's infantry broke into Friedland itself while French cavalry under Grouchy and Latour-Maubourg harried the flanks. The Russian left collapsed against the river; thousands drowned in the Alle or were cut down trying to cross the few bridges and fords. Russian losses were on the order of 20,000–30,000 killed, wounded, and captured out of about 60,000 engaged, plus most of their artillery; French losses were around 8,000–10,000.

Consequences

Friedland destroyed Russia's capacity to continue the war. Tsar Alexander I sought peace, meeting Napoleon on a raft in the Niemen River at Tilsit. The resulting Treaties of Tilsit (July 1807) left France dominant in western and central Europe, reduced Prussia to a second-rate power, and made Russia Napoleon's nominal ally under the Continental System.
