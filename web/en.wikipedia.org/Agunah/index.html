Agunah

An agunah (Hebrew: עגונה, plural: agunot; literally "anchored" or "chained") is a Jewish woman who is chained to her marriage because her husband is either missing or unwilling to grant her a divorce (get). Under traditional Jewish law (halakha), a married woman may not remarry until her marriage is formally dissolved by a get delivered by the husband or by proof of his death. A woman in this situation is sometimes called a "chained wife" in English-language coverage.

Categories

Classical rabbinic literature recognizes several situations producing an agunah: the husband has disappeared and it is unknown whether he is alive (for example, a soldier missing in war); the husband is mentally incapacitated and cannot grant a divorce; or, in the modern era, the husband is present but refuses to give a get, sometimes to extort money or concessions in a civil divorce settlement. The children of an agunah who remarries without a valid divorce may be classified as mamzerim under Orthodox interpretation, a status with serious consequences in Jewish law, which intensifies rabbinic caution.

Efforts at resolution

Rabbinic courts have historically searched for leniencies, such as accepting testimony about a husband's death that would not be admissible in other cases. Modern proposed solutions include prenuptial agreements obligating a recalcitrant husband to pay significant daily support until he grants the divorce, conditional marriages, and — controversially — annulment. In Israel, where marriage and divorce of Jews fall under the Chief Rabbinate's courts, rabbinical tribunals can impose sanctions on get-refusers, including imprisonment. Advocacy organizations support agunot and campaign for systemic reform.
