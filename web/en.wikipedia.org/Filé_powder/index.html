Filé powder

Filé powder, also called gumbo filé, is a spicy herb seasoning made from the dried and ground leaves of the North American sassafras tree (Sassafras albidum). It is used in Louisiana Creole cuisine, most famously as a thickener and flavouring for gumbo.

History

The use of ground sassafras leaves was learned by French-speaking settlers in Louisiana from the Choctaw people, who called the ingredient kombo — a word some scholars link to the name "gumbo" itself. Filé became a staple of Creole cooking, offering a way to thicken gumbo when okra was out of season.

Use

Filé powder is traditionally stirred into gumbo after it is removed from the heat, or passed at the table for diners to add to their own bowls. Adding it during vigorous boiling can make the dish stringy or ropey, so cooks typically add it at the end of cooking. It imparts an earthy flavour sometimes compared to root beer or thyme.
