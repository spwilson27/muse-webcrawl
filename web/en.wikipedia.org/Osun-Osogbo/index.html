Osun-Osogbo

Osun-Osogbo Sacred Grove

Location: Osogbo, Osun State, Nigeria

Area: 75 hectares

Designation: UNESCO World Heritage Site (2005)

Osun-Osogbo refers to the city of Osogbo in southwestern Nigeria and to the Osun-Osogbo Sacred Grove, a dense forest on the outskirts of the city along the banks of the Osun River. The grove is one of the last remnants of primary high forest in southern Nigeria and is sacred to the Yoruba goddess of fertility, Osun. It was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2005.

According to Yoruba tradition, the city of Osogbo was founded when a group of settlers from Ipole, led by the hunter Olutimehin, made a pact with the river goddess Osun, who promised to protect them and bring them prosperity in return for an annual sacrifice and the protection of her sacred grove. The Osun-Osogbo Festival, held every August, commemorates this pact and attracts thousands of worshippers and tourists.

The sacred grove

The grove covers some 75 hectares of undisturbed forest, threaded by the meandering Osun River and trails lined with sanctuaries, shrines, sculptures and artworks erected in honour of Osun and other Yoruba deities. Forty shrines and nine worship points are scattered through the forest. Fishing, farming and hunting are forbidden in the grove, which serves as a reservoir of Yoruba cultural and religious knowledge, including herbal medicine.

Susanne Wenger and the New Sacred Art movement

In the mid-20th century the grove had fallen into neglect, with shrines falling down and trees being felled. The Austrian artist Susanne Wenger (1915–2009), who had settled in Nigeria and become a Yoruba priestess, led a movement of local artists — the New Sacred Art movement — that from the late 1950s rebuilt and recreated the shrines in monumental contemporary forms, working in cement, iron and mud. Their great sculptures, some more than ten metres high, combined traditional Yoruba motifs with modern artistic expression, and helped revive the grove's sanctity and the annual festival.

The festival

The Osun-Osogbo Festival culminates in a procession to the grove led by the reigning king (Ataoja of Osogbo) and a young maiden, the Arugba, who carries a calabash of offerings to the river goddess. Devotees seek blessings for fertility, prosperity and health, and the festival is regarded as a symbol of the identity of the Yoruba people.
