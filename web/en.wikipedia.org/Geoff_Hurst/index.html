Geoff Hurst

Sir Geoff Hurst
Born: 8 December 1941, Ashton-under-Lyne, England
Position: Striker
Clubs: West Ham United, Stoke City, West Bromwich Albion
England caps: 49 (24 goals)

Sir Geoffrey Charles Hurst (born 8 December 1941) is an English former professional footballer. A striker, he remains the only man to score a hat-trick in a World Cup final, when England recorded a 4–2 victory over West Germany at Wembley Stadium in 1966. He spent most of his club career with West Ham United, scoring 242 goals in 500 first-team appearances, and also played for Stoke City and West Bromwich Albion.

Club career

Born in Ashton-under-Lyne, Lancashire, Hurst joined West Ham as a teenager and made his debut in 1960. Initially a wing-half, he was converted to a centre-forward by manager Ron Greenwood and formed a celebrated partnership with Martin Peters and Bobby Moore. He won the FA Cup with West Ham in 1964 and the European Cup Winners' Cup in 1965. In 1972 he joined Stoke City for £80,000 and later had a loan spell with West Bromwich Albion before retiring in 1976.

International career

Hurst made his England debut in February 1966. He was not Alf Ramsey's first-choice centre-forward for that year's World Cup, but an injury to Jimmy Greaves gave him his chance in the quarter-final against Argentina, which he settled with the only goal. In the final against West Germany on 30 July 1966, he scored three times: a header to equalise, the disputed extra-time goal that struck the crossbar and was judged to have crossed the line — the famous "Wembley goal" debated ever since — and a final goal on the counter-attack as spectators spilled onto the pitch, prompting Kenneth Wolstenholme's celebrated commentary, "They think it's all over... it is now!" Hurst also played at the 1970 World Cup in Mexico and won 49 caps in total, scoring 24 goals.

Later life and honours

After retiring, Hurst managed Telford United and was an assistant coach with England and Chelsea. He was knighted in 1998 for services to football. He was also a talented cricketer, playing one first-class match for Essex in 1962. As surviving members of the 1966 team have died, Hurst has remained one of its most prominent public representatives.
