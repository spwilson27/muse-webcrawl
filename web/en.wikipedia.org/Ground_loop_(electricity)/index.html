Ground loop (electricity)

Ground loop
FieldElectrical engineering, audio engineering
CauseMultiple ground paths at differing potentials
SymptomsMains hum (50/60 Hz), buzz, interference
RemediesStar grounding, isolation transformers, balanced lines, DI boxes

In an electrical system, a ground loop occurs when two or more points of a circuit intended to be at the same ground potential instead sit at different potentials, so that current flows through the grounding conductors connecting them. The loop formed by the conductors acts both as a path for stray currents and as an antenna picking up magnetic interference.

Effects

The classic symptom in audio systems is a low hum at the mains frequency (50 or 60 Hz) and its harmonics, heard when equipment interconnected by unbalanced cables is powered from different outlets. In video systems, ground loops produce moving hum bars; in instrumentation they add noise and measurement error; in severe cases, circulating currents can overheat conductors.

Causes

Ground potential differences arise from voltage drops in building wiring carrying load currents, leakage currents, and induced voltages from nearby magnetic fields. Cable shields connected at both ends between separately grounded devices complete the loop.

Prevention and cures

Standard remedies include powering interconnected equipment from a single outlet strip (star grounding), using balanced audio connections that reject common-mode noise, inserting audio isolation transformers or ground-loop isolators, optical links, and direct boxes with ground-lift switches in live sound. Removing safety earth connections is dangerous and is not an acceptable cure.
