Bicycle lanes

Bicycle lanes (US) or cycle lanes (UK) are types of bikeways (cycleways) with lanes on the roadway for cyclists only. In the United Kingdom, a cycle lane is a lane on the carriageway marked with a solid white line for mandatory use or a broken white line for advisory use. In the United States, a designated bicycle lane is a portion of the roadway designated by striping, signage, and pavement markings for the exclusive or preferential use of bicyclists.

Design

Bike lanes are usually placed on both sides of the road, following the direction of traffic flow. Designs vary from simple painted lanes to lanes buffered by additional space or physically protected by parked cars, kerbs, or bollards. At intersections, lanes may be routed behind turning traffic or given separate signal phases to reduce conflicts. Guidelines from bodies such as NACTO and the Dutch CROW Design Manual recommend greater separation as motor traffic speeds and volumes increase.

Debate

Supporters argue that lanes increase safety, comfort, and cycling rates. Some vehicular-cycling advocates historically argued that lanes can encourage unsafe positioning at junctions, though research increasingly shows that dedicated infrastructure, particularly protected lanes, improves safety outcomes and attracts new riders.
