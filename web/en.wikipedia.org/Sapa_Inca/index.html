The Sapa Inca (Quechua: Sapa Inka, "the only Inca") was the title of the emperor of the Inca Empire (Tawantinsuyu), and later of the Neo-Inca State at Vilcabamba. The Sapa Inca was the absolute ruler of the empire and, uniquely among its people, regarded as a descendant and living representative of the sun god Inti, which lent him a divine quality. In theory, the Sapa Inca held total power over all lives and property in the empire, though in practice his rule was mediated by a hereditary nobility and a council of advisers.

A Sapa Inca wearing the mascaypacha, the fringed imperial headdress.

Functions and attributes

The most important symbol of the Sapa Inca's authority was the mascaypacha, a distinctive headdress of multicoloured fringe worn across the forehead. The emperor was considered the son of Inti and placed his children throughout the empire's administration. He presided over major religious festivals such as Inti Raymi, led armies in the field, and was the ultimate judge in legal matters. His person was sacred: subjects approaching him had to bear a burden on their backs as a sign of humility and were forbidden to look directly at his face.

Succession

The Inca did not practise strict primogeniture. The Sapa Inca designated as his heir the son — usually by his principal wife, the Qoya, who was often his full sister — considered most capable of ruling. This flexible system produced stability under strong rulers such as Pachacuti and Huayna Capac, but also the catastrophic succession war between Huáscar and Atahualpa that had just ended when Francisco Pizarro arrived in 1532.

List of Sapa Incas

The traditional dynasty begins with the legendary Manco Cápac. Historical rulers of the empire at its height include Pachacuti (1438–1471), who transformed the Kingdom of Cusco into a vast empire, his son Túpac Inca Yupanqui, and Huayna Capac (1493–1527), at whose death the empire was divided between his sons. Atahualpa emerged victorious from the ensuing civil war but was captured at Cajamarca and executed by the Spanish in 1533. Puppet rulers followed under Spanish control, while Manco Inca Yupanqui's descendants maintained the Neo-Inca State at Vilcabamba until the execution of Túpac Amaru I in 1572.
