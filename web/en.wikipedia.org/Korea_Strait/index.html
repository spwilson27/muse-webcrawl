Korea Strait

Connects: East China Sea – Sea of Japan
Between: South Korea and Japan
Key island: Tsushima

The Korea Strait is a sea passage between South Korea and Japan, connecting the East China Sea to the Sea of Japan. It is divided by Tsushima Island into a western channel (sometimes called the Busan Strait) and the Tsushima Strait to the east.

Geography

The strait is roughly 200 km wide in total and generally shallow, around 90 metres deep. The warm Tsushima Current, a branch of the Kuroshio, flows northeastward through it into the Sea of Japan.

History

The strait was the scene of the Battle of Tsushima in May 1905, in which Admiral Tōgō Heihachirō's Japanese fleet destroyed the Russian Baltic Fleet, decisively ending the Russo-Japanese War. Ferry routes link Busan with Fukuoka and other Japanese ports, and a Korea–Japan undersea tunnel has been proposed periodically.
