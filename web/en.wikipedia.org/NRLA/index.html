National Rugby League of Australia


Labelled image: National Rugby League match action

The National Rugby League of Australia (NRLA) is the governing body historically associated with professional rugby league in Australia, responsible for the national team, the Kangaroos, and — for most of the twentieth century — the elite club competition. In 2012 most of its functions were folded into the Australian Rugby League Commission (ARLC), which now administers the sport and the National Rugby League (NRL) premiership under licence.

History

Rugby league split from rugby union in New South Wales and Queensland in 1907–08 over payments to working-class players. The New South Wales Rugby League and Queensland Rugby League ran their own competitions, while the Australian Rugby League — formed in 1986 out of the earlier Australian Rugby League Board of Control — coordinated interstate and international matters, the State of Origin series, and the Kangaroos. During the Super League war of 1995–97, the ARL's premiership competed with News Limited's breakaway Super League before the two merged to form the NRL in 1998, a partnership between the ARL and News Limited.

Governance

The formation of the independent Australian Rugby League Commission in 2012 ended the News Limited partnership era and unified administration of the game: the Commission took over the ARL's assets and role, runs the NRL competition, the State of Origin series, and the national teams, and licenses the state leagues. The Kangaroos remain Australia's representative side, the most successful in Rugby League World Cup history.
