Analytical psychology

FounderCarl Gustav Jung
Foundedc. 1913
Key conceptsCollective unconscious, archetypes, individuation, complexes

Analytical psychology is the school of psychology founded by Swiss psychiatrist Carl Gustav Jung after his break with Sigmund Freud in 1913. It emphasizes the role of the unconscious, symbolic experience, and the lifelong process of psychological development Jung called individuation.

Key concepts

Jung distinguished the personal unconscious, containing repressed or forgotten material organized into complexes, from the collective unconscious, a deeper layer shared by all humanity and populated by archetypes — universal patterns and images such as the Self, the Shadow, the Anima and Animus, and the Persona. Jung also developed the concepts of psychological types, distinguishing attitudes of extraversion and introversion and functions of thinking, feeling, sensation, and intuition.

Therapy

Jungian analysis works with dreams, active imagination, and symbolic material to help the patient integrate unconscious contents into consciousness. The goal is not merely symptom relief but the achievement of a more complete personality through individuation.

Legacy

Analytical psychology has influenced psychotherapy, religious studies, literature, and popular culture. Its concepts underlie instruments such as the Myers–Briggs Type Indicator, though Jung's ideas have also been criticized for limited empirical testability.
