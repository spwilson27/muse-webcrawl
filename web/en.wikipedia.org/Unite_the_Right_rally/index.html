Unite the Right rally

The Unite the Right rally was a white supremacist rally that took place in Charlottesville, Virginia, from August 11 to 12, 2017. Marchers included members of the alt-right, neo-Confederates, neo-fascists, white nationalists, neo-Nazis, Klansmen, and far-right militias. The marchers were protesting the planned removal of a statue of Confederate general Robert E. Lee in Charlottesville's Lee Park (later renamed Emancipation Park and then Market Street Park).

Events

On the evening of August 11, marchers carrying tiki torches crossed the University of Virginia campus chanting slogans and clashed with counter-protesters around a statue of Thomas Jefferson. The next day, before the rally's scheduled noon start, violent clashes between rally attendees and counter-protesters led Virginia's governor to declare a state of emergency and police to declare the assembly unlawful.

That afternoon, a rally participant drove a car into a crowd of counter-protesters on a downtown street, killing 32-year-old Heather Heyer and injuring at least 19 others. The driver was later convicted of first-degree murder in state court and pleaded guilty to federal hate crimes, receiving life sentences. Two Virginia State Police troopers also died when their helicopter, which had been monitoring the events, crashed.

Response and aftermath

The rally and the responses of public officials drew intense national and international debate about Confederate monuments, the First Amendment, and domestic extremism. President Donald Trump's remarks — including that there were "very fine people on both sides" — were widely criticized. Lawsuits followed against organizers; in 2021 a federal civil jury found several rally organizers liable for conspiracy and awarded millions in damages. The events led numerous organizers and platforms to be deplatformed, and the word "Charlottesville" became shorthand for the resurgence of organized white supremacism in the United States.
