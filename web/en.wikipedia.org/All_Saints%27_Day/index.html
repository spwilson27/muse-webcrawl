All Saints' Day

Date1 November (Western Christianity); first Sunday after Pentecost (Eastern Christianity)
Also calledAll Hallows' Day, Solemnity of All Saints
Related toAll Souls' Day, Halloween, Day of the Dead

All Saints' Day, also known as All Hallows' Day, is a Christian solemnity celebrated in honour of all the saints of the church, whether they are known or unknown. In Western Christianity it is observed on 1 November; the Eastern Orthodox Church celebrates it on the first Sunday after Pentecost.

History

The feast has roots in early Christian commemorations of martyrs. Pope Gregory III dedicated a chapel in St Peter's Basilica to all saints on 1 November in the 8th century, and Pope Gregory IV extended the feast to the whole church in 837. Its eve is Halloween (All Hallows' Eve), and it is followed on 2 November by All Souls' Day.

Observance

In many Catholic countries it is a public holiday marked by attending Mass and visiting cemeteries to place flowers and candles on the graves of relatives. In the Philippines (Undas) and Mexico (where it overlaps with the Day of the Dead) the customs are especially elaborate.
