Studio Ghibli

Studio Ghibli, Inc. (Japanese: 株式会社スタジオジブリ) is a Japanese animation studio based in Koganei, Tokyo. It has a strong presence in the animation industry and has expanded its operations to include various media such as short subjects, television commercials, and two television films. Their work has been well received by both critics and audiences, and has won numerous awards. Their mascot and most recognisable symbol is the character Totoro from the 1988 film My Neighbor Totoro. Among the studio's highest-grossing films are Spirited Away (2001) and The Boy and the Heron (2023).

History

The studio was founded on June 15, 1985, by directors Hayao Miyazaki and Isao Takahata and producer Toshio Suzuki, following the success of Miyazaki's 1984 film Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind. Its first official film was Laputa: Castle in the Sky (1986). The name "Ghibli" — an Italian word for a hot desert wind, also used for an Italian reconnaissance aircraft — was chosen by Miyazaki with the idea that the studio would "blow a new wind through the anime industry".

Films

Ghibli's films frequently top Japan's box office and are acclaimed worldwide. Spirited Away won the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature in 2003, and The Boy and the Heron won the same award in 2024. Other celebrated works include Grave of the Fireflies (1988), Princess Mononoke (1997), Howl's Moving Castle (2004), and Ponyo (2008). Six of the studio's films are among the highest-grossing anime films of all time.

Ghibli Museum and Park

The Ghibli Museum in Mitaka, Tokyo, opened in 2001 and displays the studio's work and exclusive short films. Ghibli Park, a theme park representing the world of Ghibli's films, opened in Aichi Prefecture in November 2022.
