Kurt Alder

Kurt Alder (10 July 1902 – 20 June 1958) was a German chemist and Nobel laureate. He shared the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1950 with his teacher Otto Diels for their work on the Diels–Alder reaction, the diene synthesis.

Life and career

Alder was born in Königshütte, Silesia (now Chorzów, Poland), and studied chemistry at the University of Berlin and the University of Kiel, where he completed his doctorate under Diels in 1926. He worked at IG Farben in Leverkusen from 1934, and in 1940 became professor of chemistry and director of the chemical institute at the University of Cologne.

Work

With Diels, Alder published the diene synthesis in 1928: a cycloaddition between a conjugated diene and a substituted alkene (the dienophile) that forms a cyclohexene ring. The Diels–Alder reaction became one of the most widely used methods for forming carbon–carbon bonds in organic synthesis. Alder also developed the related "ene reaction" (Alder-ene reaction).
