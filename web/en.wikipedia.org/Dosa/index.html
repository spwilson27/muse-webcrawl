Dosa


A dosa served with chutney and sambar.

A dosa is a thin, savoury crêpe-like dish originating in South India. It is made from a fermented batter of rice and black lentils (urad dal), spread thin on a hot griddle, and typically served with sambar and coconut chutney. Dosas are a staple across India and popular worldwide.

Preparation

Rice and dal are soaked, ground, and fermented overnight, producing a tangy batter. The batter is poured onto a hot tawa and spread in a spiral, cooking until crisp and golden. The plain variety is called sada dosa; the widely popular masala dosa is filled with a spiced potato mixture. Other variants include the thinner, crispier rava dosa (made with semolina), onion dosa, and cheese dosa.

History and spread

Dosa is associated especially with the cuisines of Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, and Kerala, and its origins are traced to the region around Udupi. It became a restaurant staple across India in the twentieth century and later spread internationally through South Indian diaspora restaurants.
