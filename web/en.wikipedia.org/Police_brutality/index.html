Police brutality

TypePolice misconduct; excessive force
Related conceptsUse of force, accountability, qualified immunity

Police brutality is the excessive and unwarranted use of force by law enforcement against an individual or group. It is an extreme form of police misconduct and a civil rights violation, encompassing physical assault, torture, and killings, and in some usages also verbal abuse and psychological intimidation.

Definition and scope

International law permits police to use force only when strictly necessary and proportionate. Brutality occurs when force exceeds that standard. Researchers study its prevalence through hospital records, media databases, and official statistics, though under-recording is widely acknowledged. In the United States, databases such as Mapping Police Violence have counted roughly a thousand police killings per year.

Causes

Explanations include occupational culture and the "blue wall of silence", inadequate training, weak accountability mechanisms, and individual officer characteristics. Structural accounts point to the legacy of policing's origins in slave patrols and strikebreaking in some countries, and to legal doctrines such as qualified immunity that shield officers from civil liability.

Responses and reform

Proposed remedies include body-worn cameras, civilian review boards, independent prosecution of officers, duty-to-intervene policies, restrictions on chokeholds and no-knock warrants, and alternative response models for mental-health calls. High-profile cases — including the 2020 murder of George Floyd in Minneapolis — have repeatedly sparked protest movements such as Black Lives Matter and global debates over police reform and abolition.
