Information system

An information system (IS) is a formal, sociotechnical, organisational system designed to collect, process, store and distribute information. From a sociotechnical perspective, information systems consist of four components: task, people, structure (or roles) and technology. In a business context, an information system is the combination of hardware, software, data, procedures and the people who interact with them, supporting operations, management and decision-making.

Components

The classic components of a computer-based information system are hardware (computers, servers, networks), software (system software and applications), data (organised facts stored in databases), procedures (documented processes for using and maintaining the system) and people (operators, analysts, managers and end users). Communication networks connect these components and allow information to flow within and between organisations.

Types

Information systems are often classified by the organisational level and function they serve. Transaction processing systems record routine daily transactions such as sales and payroll. Management information systems produce summarised reports for middle managers. Decision support systems help with semi-structured and unstructured decisions through models and analytical tools, while executive information systems present strategic indicators to senior leadership. Functional systems include enterprise resource planning (ERP), customer relationship management (CRM), supply chain management and knowledge management systems. Geographic information systems (GIS) handle spatially referenced data, and office automation systems support document handling and communication.

Development

Systems are created through a systems development life cycle of planning, analysis, design, implementation and maintenance, carried out by systems analysts, developers and project managers. Alternative paradigms such as prototyping, agile methods and end-user development modify this cycle. The academic discipline of information systems, studied in business schools and computing departments, examines the design, use and organisational impact of these systems.

History

The field emerged in the 1960s as computers moved from scientific calculation into business data processing, with early work on management information systems and journals and associations such as the Association for Information Systems professionalising the discipline in later decades.
