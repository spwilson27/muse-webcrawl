Stephanie Kwolek

Stephanie Kwolek (1923–2014), whose 1965 discovery led to Kevlar.

Stephanie Louise Kwolek (July 31, 1923 – June 18, 2014) was an American chemist of Polish heritage who worked at DuPont for more than 40 years. She is best known for inventing Kevlar, the first of a family of synthetic fibres of exceptional strength and heat resistance used in bulletproof vests, tyres, cables and hundreds of other products.

Early life and education

Kwolek was born in New Kensington, Pennsylvania, to Polish immigrant parents. She earned a Bachelor of Science in chemistry from Margaret Morrison Carnegie College (now Carnegie Mellon University) in 1946 and joined DuPont's Pioneering Research Laboratory in Buffalo, later moving to Wilmington, Delaware.

Discovery of Kevlar

In 1965, anticipating a gasoline shortage, Kwolek's group sought lightweight fibres for tyres. Working with aromatic polyamides (aramids), she obtained an unusual cloudy, low-viscosity solution that other researchers might have discarded; she persuaded a technician to spin it, and the resulting fibre proved extraordinarily strong and stiff. The polymer, poly-paraphenylene terephthalamide, was commercialised as Kevlar in 1971. Pound for pound, Kevlar is about five times stronger than steel.

Honours

Kwolek received numerous awards, including the National Medal of Technology (1996), the Perkin Medal, and induction into the National Inventors Hall of Fame (1995) and the National Women's Hall of Fame. She held or co-held dozens of patents and mentored other women in science throughout her retirement.
