Almendres Cromlech

The Almendres Cromlech (Portuguese: Cromeleque dos Almendres) is a megalithic complex near Évora in the Alentejo region of Portugal. It is the largest surviving group of structured menhirs in the Iberian Peninsula and one of the largest in Europe, consisting of about 95 granite monoliths arranged in two concentric oval rings on a hillside sloping toward the east.

History and discovery

The monument was built in several phases during the Neolithic and Chalcolithic periods, roughly between the 6th and 3rd millennia BCE, making parts of it older than Stonehenge. Some stones bear carved motifs, including lines and circles thought to have religious or astronomical significance; the alignment of the enclosure with the slope and the sunrise at the equinoxes has led researchers to propose calendrical functions. The site was identified by the geologist Henrique Leonor Pina in 1964 while he was mapping the region.

Setting

The cromlech stands in a cork oak landscape near the related Almendres Menhir, a single tall standing stone a few kilometres away. It is classified as a national monument of Portugal and is one of the country's best-known prehistoric sites.
