Meniscus (anatomy)

A meniscus (plural: menisci) is a crescent-shaped fibrocartilaginous anatomical structure that partly divides a joint cavity. In humans they are present in the knee, wrist, acromioclavicular, sternoclavicular, and temporomandibular joints.

Meniscus
SystemMusculoskeletal
LocationKnee and other joints
FunctionShock absorption, load distribution

Knee menisci

The knee has a medial meniscus and a lateral meniscus, sitting between the femoral condyles and tibial plateau. They deepen the joint, distribute weight, reduce friction and absorb shock.

Injury

Tears are common sports injuries, often from twisting under load, and also occur degeneratively with age. Symptoms include pain, swelling, locking and instability. Diagnosis uses examination and MRI; treatment ranges from physiotherapy to arthroscopic repair or partial meniscectomy.

Structure

Menisci are composed mainly of type I collagen and water, with a relatively avascular inner zone that heals poorly compared with the vascular outer third.
