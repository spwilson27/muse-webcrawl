Black September

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Black September (Arabic: أيلول الأسود) was a conflict fought in the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan between the Jordanian Armed Forces under King Hussein and the Palestine Liberation Organisation (PLO), headed by Yasser Arafat, primarily from 16 to 27 September 1970, with continued fighting into July 1971.

Background

After the 1967 Six-Day War, Palestinian fedayeen organisations based themselves in Jordan and increasingly operated as a state within a state, clashing with Jordanian authorities. In September 1970 the Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine hijacked four airliners, landing three at Dawson's Field in Jordan and blowing them up before the world's press.

The conflict

King Hussein declared martial law and moved against the fedayeen. Syria intervened with armoured forces in support of the Palestinians but withdrew under Jordanian air attack and international pressure. Thousands were killed — estimates vary, with Palestinian casualties commonly cited in the thousands — and the PLO leadership and fighters were expelled, relocating to Lebanon.

Aftermath

The expulsion shifted the centre of the Palestinian armed movement to Lebanon, contributing to that country's slide into civil war. A clandestine Palestinian group took the name Black September Organization and carried out the 1972 Munich Olympics massacre of Israeli athletes.
