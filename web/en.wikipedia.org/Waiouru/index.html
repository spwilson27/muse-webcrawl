Waiouru


Country: New Zealand
Region: Manawatū-Whanganui
District: Ruapehu
Elevation: about 815 m (one of NZ's highest settlements)
Known for: Waiouru Military Camp; National Army Museum

Waiouru is a small town in the Ruapehu District of the Manawatū-Whanganui region of New Zealand's North Island. Situated on the Volcanic Plateau at the southern end of the Rangipo Desert and overlooked by Mount Ruapehu, it lies on State Highway 1 at its junction with State Highway 49 to Ohakune. Its high elevation makes it one of the coldest inhabited places in the North Island, with occasional snow in winter.

Military camp

The town exists principally to serve the Waiouru Military Camp, a major New Zealand Army training base established in 1939–40. The surrounding training area, much of it tussock grassland extending toward the Kaimanawa Range, is used for live firing and exercises with armoured vehicles and artillery. The camp's schools have included the School of Artillery and other corps training establishments.

On State Highway 1 at the edge of the town stands the National Army Museum (officially the National Army Museum Te Mata Toa), opened in 1978, which holds collections on New Zealand's military history from the New Zealand Wars to modern peacekeeping, including Victoria Crosses awarded to New Zealanders.

History and transport

Waiouru grew as a coach and later rail stop on the North Island Main Trunk line, which passes west of the town; its railway station once served the camp and the Central Plateau's farming and timber districts. The Tangiwai disaster of 1953, New Zealand's worst rail accident, occurred at the Whangaehu River bridge north-east of Waiouru when a lahar from Ruapehu's crater lake destroyed the bridge shortly before a Wellington–Auckland express train arrived, killing 151 people.
