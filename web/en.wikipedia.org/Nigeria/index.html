Nigeria

Capital: Abuja
Largest city: Lagos
Population: ~220 million
Official language: English
Independence: 1 October 1960 (from Britain)

Nigeria, officially the Federal Republic of Nigeria, is a country in West Africa bordering Niger, Chad, Cameroon, and the Gulf of Guinea. It is Africa's most populous country and largest economy, a federation of 36 states and the Federal Capital Territory.

History

The region was home to early civilizations such as the Nok culture and later states including the Hausa kingdoms, the Yoruba empires of Ife and Oyo, the Igbo Nri kingdom, and the Benin Empire, famed for its bronze art. Britain amalgamated its northern and southern protectorates in 1914. Nigeria became independent in 1960, endured the Biafran civil war (1967–1970) and long periods of military rule, and returned to civilian government in 1999.

Economy and culture

Petroleum, first exported in 1958, dominates government revenue. Nigeria has over 250 ethnic groups — the largest being the Hausa, Yoruba, and Igbo — and roughly balanced Muslim (north) and Christian (south) populations. Its film industry, Nollywood, is among the world's largest by output, and Nigerian literature includes Nobel laureate Wole Soyinka and Chinua Achebe, author of Things Fall Apart.
