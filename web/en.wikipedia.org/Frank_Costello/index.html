Frank Costello (born Francesco Castiglia; January 26, 1891 – February 18, 1973) was an Italian-American crime boss of the Luciano crime family. Born in Italy and raised in New York City, he rose to the top of American organized crime, becoming one of the most powerful and influential mob bosses of his era. He was nicknamed the "Prime Minister of the Underworld" for his extensive political connections.

Early life and rise

Castiglia immigrated to the United States with his family as a child and grew up in East Harlem. He became associated with Lucky Luciano in the 1920s, engaging in bootlegging during Prohibition and in gambling operations including slot machines and bookmaking. After the Castellammarese War reordered the New York Mafia, Costello became a senior figure in Luciano's organization, and when Luciano was imprisoned in 1936 and later deported, Costello assumed effective leadership of the family.

Kefauver hearings and later years

Costello achieved national notoriety in 1951 when he testified before the Senate's Kefauver Committee investigating organized crime; television cameras famously showed only his hands while his voice was heard. He was convicted of contempt and later of tax evasion, spending time in federal prison. In 1957 he survived an assassination attempt ordered by rival Vito Genovese — a bullet grazed his head — after which he largely retired from active control of the family. He died of a heart attack in Manhattan in 1973.
