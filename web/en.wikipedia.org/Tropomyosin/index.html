Tropomyosin

Tropomyosin
TypeActin-binding protein, coiled coil
FunctionRegulates actin–myosin interaction in muscle
Genes (human)TPM1, TPM2, TPM3, TPM4

Tropomyosin is a two-stranded alpha-helical coiled-coil protein found in the cytoskeleton of cells and in muscle fibres. In skeletal and cardiac muscle it lies along the grooves of the actin filament and, together with the troponin complex, regulates muscle contraction by blocking or exposing myosin-binding sites on actin in response to calcium ions.

Function in muscle

At rest, tropomyosin covers the active sites on actin so myosin heads cannot bind. When calcium binds troponin C, the troponin complex shifts tropomyosin deeper into the actin groove, permitting cross-bridge formation and contraction.

Isoforms and disease

Four genes encode numerous tropomyosin isoforms expressed in muscle and non-muscle cells, where they stabilise actin filaments and influence cell shape and motility. Mutations cause diseases including familial hypertrophic cardiomyopathy and nemaline myopathy; tropomyosin is also a major allergen in shellfish and house dust mites.
