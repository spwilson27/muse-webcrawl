Lubrication

FieldTribology
PurposeReduce friction and wear

Lubrication is the process or technique of using a lubricant to reduce friction and wear and tear in a contact between two surfaces. The study of lubrication is a discipline in the field of tribology.

Mechanisms

Lubrication mechanisms can be divided into regimes such as hydrodynamic lubrication, in which the surfaces are fully separated by a fluid film; elastohydrodynamic lubrication, found in gears and rolling bearings; boundary lubrication, where surfaces are separated only by adsorbed molecular films; and mixed lubrication, a combination of these. Solid lubricants such as graphite and molybdenum disulfide are used where liquids cannot function.

Lubricants

Common lubricants include mineral and synthetic oils, greases, and dry films. Additives are blended into oils to improve viscosity behavior, resist oxidation, and prevent corrosion and wear.

Applications

Lubrication is essential in engines, gearboxes, bearings, chains and countless other machines, as well as in biological systems such as human joints, where synovial fluid lubricates cartilage.
