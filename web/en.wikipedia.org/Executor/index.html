Executor

An executor is a person or institution appointed by a testator in a will to carry out the terms of that will after the testator's death. The executor's role, recognized throughout common-law jurisdictions, is to administer the deceased's estate: gathering assets, paying debts and taxes, and distributing what remains to the beneficiaries named in the will.

Duties

On the testator's death, the executor typically petitions the probate court for a grant of probate confirming the appointment, inventories the estate, notifies creditors, settles outstanding obligations and taxes, manages assets during administration, and finally distributes the residue. Executors owe fiduciary duties to the beneficiaries and can be personally liable for mismanagement.

Appointment and related roles

A testator may name one or more executors, plus substitutes. Where a will names no executor, or the named executor cannot serve, the court appoints an administrator; where there is no will at all, an administrator is likewise appointed under intestacy rules. A woman was historically styled an executrix, a term now largely obsolete. Banks and trust companies commonly serve as professional executors for large estates.

Compensation

Executors are generally entitled to reasonable compensation, either fixed by the will, by statute, or by the court. The role is distinct from that of a trustee, though the same person often fills both.
