Vestmannaeyjar

Vestmannaeyjar (the Westman Islands) is an archipelago and municipality off the south coast of Iceland. The largest island, Heimaey, is the only inhabited one, with a population of about 4,400. The islands are named after Irish slaves (the "Westmen") of early Norse settlers. Vestmannaeyjar is famous for the dramatic 1973 eruption of the volcano Eldfell, which forced the evacuation of Heimaey and nearly closed its harbour.

Geography and islands

The archipelago comprises some 15 islands and numerous skerries, all formed by submarine volcanism on the Mid-Atlantic Ridge system. Heimaey is the largest, dominated by the cones of Eldfell and Helgafell. Surtsey, the second-largest, emerged from the sea during an eruption lasting from 1963 to 1967 and has been a protected scientific reserve ever since; it was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2008. The smaller islands support summer cottages and large colonies of puffins and other seabirds.

The Eldfell eruption, 1973

In the early hours of 23 January 1973 a fissure opened on Heimaey and the volcano Eldfell began erupting. The island's entire population was evacuated by the fishing fleet within hours, with no loss of life. Lava threatened to block the harbour — the lifeline of the community — and in a celebrated operation seawater was pumped onto the lava front to cool and divert it. When the eruption ended in July, the island had grown by about 2.2 km², hundreds of houses had been destroyed or buried, but the harbour was saved and improved. Many residents returned, and the event is commemorated at the Eldheimar museum.

Economy and culture

Fishing and fish processing dominate the economy; Vestmannaeyjar is among Iceland's most important fishing ports. The islands host the annual Þjóðhátíð festival, one of Iceland's largest outdoor gatherings, and are renowned for their puffin colonies — island children traditionally rescue stray pufflings ("pysjur") each autumn and release them to the sea.
