San Lorenzo de El Escorial is a town and municipality in the Community of Madrid, Spain, situated about 45 kilometres northwest of the capital in the Sierra de Guadarrama. It is best known as the home of the Royal Site of San Lorenzo de El Escorial, the great monastery-palace built for King Philip II.

The monastery

The Monastery of El Escorial, built between 1563 and 1584 under architects Juan Bautista de Toledo and Juan de Herrera, combines a royal palace, basilica, pantheon of the Spanish kings, library and monastery in a vast granite complex. It was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1984.

The town

The town grew around the monastery and the neighbouring settlement of El Escorial. Its elevation gives it cooler summers than Madrid, making it a traditional summer retreat; it also hosts summer courses of the Complutense University.
