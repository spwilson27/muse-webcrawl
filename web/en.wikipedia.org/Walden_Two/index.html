Walden Two


Author: B. F. Skinner
Published: 1948
Genre: Utopian science fiction novel
Sequel: "News from Nowhere, 1984" (preface to 1976 reprint)

Walden Two is a utopian novel written by behavioural psychologist B. F. Skinner and first published in 1948. Its title echoes Henry David Thoreau's Walden (1854), but where Thoreau described one man's retreat from society, Skinner imagines a small experimental community whose social problems have been solved through behavioral engineering.

Plot and ideas

Two Second World War veterans, accompanied by a professor of psychology, Burris — the narrator — and others, visit Walden Two, a community of about a thousand people founded by T. E. Frazier, a character partly modelled on Skinner himself. Members work short days under a labour-credit system, children are raised communally in a scientifically designed nursery, government is by a board of Planners and expert Managers rather than elections, and behaviour is shaped exclusively through positive reinforcement — punishment and coercion have supposedly been designed out. The residents report high levels of happiness, productivity and free time.

Through long dialogues between Frazier and Burris, Skinner argues that human behaviour is shaped by its consequences, that culture itself can be deliberately designed, and that freedom and dignity as traditionally conceived are obstacles to solving social problems — themes he later expanded in Beyond Freedom and Dignity (1971).

Reception and legacy

Initially little noticed, the book became widely read in the 1960s and 1970s, selling hundreds of thousands of copies. It directly inspired real intentional communities, most notably Twin Oaks Community in Virginia (founded 1967, which retains labour credits and a planner-manager system) and Los Horcones in Mexico. Critics have debated whether the fictional community is a hopeful model of well-being or a chilling portrait of manipulation by its founder; Skinner returned to the community in a 1976 preface, "News from Nowhere, 1984", written as a fictional follow-up.
