Castle Bravo

Castle Bravo was the code name given to the first United States test of a dry-fuel hydrogen bomb, detonated on 1 March 1954 at Bikini Atoll in the Marshall Islands, as part of Operation Castle. The device, nicknamed "Shrimp", was expected to yield about 5 megatons but instead produced 15 megatons of TNT — the largest nuclear explosion ever conducted by the United States — because the lithium-7 in its fuel unexpectedly contributed to the fusion reactions.

Fallout

The unexpectedly large yield and unfavourable winds spread radioactive fallout over a far wider area than planned. Inhabitants of Rongelap and Utirik atolls were exposed and evacuated days later, suffering radiation sickness. The 23 crew members of the Japanese fishing vessel Daigo Fukuryū Maru (Lucky Dragon No. 5) were also contaminated; one died, and the incident became a major international controversy that intensified worldwide opposition to atmospheric nuclear testing.

Legacy

Castle Bravo contributed to the political pressure that led to the Partial Nuclear Test Ban Treaty of 1963. The displaced Bikinians and Rongelapese and their descendants have pursued resettlement and compensation for decades.
