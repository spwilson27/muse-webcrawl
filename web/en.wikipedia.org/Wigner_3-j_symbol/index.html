Wigner 3-j symbol

The Wigner 3-j symbol is a mathematical quantity used in the quantum theory of angular momentum. Introduced in the work of Eugene Wigner, 3-j symbols are closely related to Clebsch–Gordan coefficients but have higher symmetry, making them convenient for coupling three angular momenta.

Properties

A 3-j symbol is written as two rows of three angular-momentum quantum numbers and their projections. It is nonzero only when selection rules are satisfied: the projections sum to zero, the three angular momenta satisfy triangle inequalities, and their sum is an integer. Permuting columns leaves the symbol unchanged up to a sign determined by the total angular momentum.

Applications

Wigner 3-j symbols appear in atomic and nuclear spectroscopy, molecular physics, spherical harmonics, and the evaluation of matrix elements through the Wigner–Eckart theorem.
