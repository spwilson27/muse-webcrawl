Blake Snyder

Blake Snyder (October 3, 1957 – August 4, 2009) was an American screenwriter, author, and lecturer best known for his 2005 book Save the Cat! The Last Book on Screenwriting You'll Ever Need, one of the most influential modern manuals of screenplay structure.

Career

Snyder grew up in the entertainment industry; his father, Ken Snyder, was an Emmy-winning television producer. After graduating from Georgetown University, he worked at Walt Disney Television before becoming a screenwriter. He sold numerous scripts, including the Sylvester Stallone comedy Stop! Or My Mom Will Shoot (1992) and the family film Blank Check (1994), and was known in Hollywood as a specialist in family and comedy scripts.

Save the Cat!

In Save the Cat!, Snyder codified his "BS2" (Blake Snyder Beat Sheet), a fifteen-beat structural template including the Opening Image, Theme Stated, Catalyst, Debate, Break into Two, Fun and Games, Midpoint, Bad Guys Close In, All Is Lost, Dark Night of the Soul, Break into Three, Finale, and Final Image. He also classified stories into ten genre types, such as "Monster in the House", "Golden Fleece", "Superhero", and "Rites of Passage". The title refers to his advice that a hero should do something sympathetic — like saving a cat — early so the audience roots for them.

Death and legacy

Snyder died suddenly of a pulmonary embolism on August 4, 2009. Two sequels, Save the Cat! Goes to the Movies (2007) and the posthumous Save the Cat! Strikes Back (2009), extended his method, which remains widely used — and debated — in screenwriting education.
