Jennifer Jones (born Phylly Isley; March 2, 1919 – December 17, 2009) was an American actress and mental health advocate. Over five decades of stardom she was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Actress five times, winning for her portrayal of Bernadette Soubirous in The Song of Bernadette (1943).

Career

Born in Tulsa, Oklahoma, Isley came to Hollywood after studying at the American Academy of Dramatic Arts; her early films under her own name made little impression. Producer David O. Selznick signed her, renamed her Jennifer Jones, and launched her with The Song of Bernadette, for which she won the Oscar on her 25th birthday. She went on to leading roles in Since You Went Away (1944), Love Letters (1945), Duel in the Sun (1946), Portrait of Jennie (1948), Madame Bovary (1949), Love Is a Many-Splendored Thing (1955), and The Man in the Gray Flannel Suit (1956). Her final film was the disaster epic The Towering Inferno (1974).

Personal life

Jones was married three times: to actor Robert Walker, to Selznick (until his death in 1965), and to industrialist and art collector Norton Simon. After the death of her daughter by suicide, she became a prominent advocate for mental-health education and helped establish the Jennifer Jones Simon Foundation.
