Black tea

Black tea
Type: Tea
Origin: China
Caffeine: Higher than green tea

Black tea is a type of tea that is more oxidized than oolong, yellow, white and green teas. In Chinese and other languages where it originated, it is known as "red tea" (紅茶) because of the colour of its liquor. Black tea is generally stronger in flavour and contains more caffeine than less oxidized teas.

Production

After plucking, the leaves are withered, rolled or crushed to promote oxidation, then fired to halt the enzymatic process. Traditional varieties include Assam, Darjeeling, Nilgiri and Ceylon teas, while Chinese black teas include Keemun and Dian hong.

Varieties and blends

Famous blends include Earl Grey, scented with bergamot oil, and English breakfast tea. Black tea forms the basis of masala chai in India and of sweet tea in the southern United States.

Health

Black tea contains polyphenols such as theaflavins and thearubigins formed during oxidation. It retains its flavour for several years, far longer than green tea.
