Batrachochytrium dendrobatidis

PhylumChytridiomycota
OrderRhizophydiales
GenusBatrachochytrium
SpeciesB. dendrobatidis

Batrachochytrium dendrobatidis (Bd), also known as the amphibian chytrid fungus, is a fungus that causes the disease chytridiomycosis in amphibians. It was described scientifically in 1999, following mass die-offs of frogs in Australia and Central America. The species name refers to the poison dart frog genus Dendrobates, from a member of which it was first isolated in captivity.

Bd infects the keratinised skin of amphibians. Its motile zoospores swim in water and encyst in the skin, forming sporangia that thicken and damage the skin, disrupting the frog's ability to regulate water, electrolytes and respiration through its skin. Heavily infected animals often die of cardiac arrest caused by electrolyte imbalance. In tadpoles, which have keratin only in the mouthparts, infection is usually less lethal but can deform mouthparts and affect feeding.

Global impact

Chytridiomycosis is considered the most devastating infectious disease affecting vertebrate biodiversity: a major 2019 study attributed declines of over 500 amphibian species and around 90 presumed extinctions in part to Bd. The fungus has spread worldwide, probably through the international trade in amphibians such as the African clawed frog and the American bullfrog, which can carry it without symptoms. A related species, B. salamandrivorans, similarly threatens salamanders.
