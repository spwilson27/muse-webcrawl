Probate

Probate is the legal process whereby a will is "proved" in a court of law and accepted as a valid public document that is the true last testament of the deceased, or whereby the estate is settled according to the laws of intestacy in the state of residence of the deceased at time of death in the absence of a legal will.

Process

After a death, the executor named in the will (or an administrator, if there is no will or no executor) applies to the probate court. The court verifies the will, and grants legal authority — in England and Wales, a "grant of probate"; in many US states, "letters testamentary" — to collect the deceased's assets, pay debts and taxes, and distribute the remainder to beneficiaries. The process may take months or longer and is generally a public record.

Intestacy

If a person dies without a valid will (intestate), the estate is distributed according to statutory rules of intestate succession, which typically favour the surviving spouse and children, then wider relatives.

Avoiding probate

Because probate can be slow and costly, estate planning commonly uses mechanisms that pass assets outside probate: joint tenancy with right of survivorship, beneficiary designations on accounts, payable-on-death accounts, and revocable living trusts. Some jurisdictions offer simplified procedures for small estates.
