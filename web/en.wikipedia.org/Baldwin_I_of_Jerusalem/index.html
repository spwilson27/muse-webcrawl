Baldwin I (c. 1058 – 2 April 1118) was one of the leaders of the First Crusade and became the first Count of Edessa (1098–1100) and then the first King of Jerusalem after the crusader state was founded. He was the younger brother of Godfrey of Bouillon, whom he succeeded as ruler of Jerusalem in 1100 — unlike Godfrey, who had styled himself Advocate of the Holy Sepulchre, Baldwin accepted a royal crown, being crowned in Bethlehem on Christmas Day 1100.

First Crusade and Edessa

Baldwin accompanied his brothers Godfrey and Eustace on the First Crusade in 1096. After separating from the main army at Heraclea, he was invited to Edessa by its Armenian ruler Thoros, who adopted him as heir. After Thoros was killed in a revolt in March 1098 — in circumstances that have aroused historians' suspicions — Baldwin became the first Count of Edessa, founding the first of the crusader states.

King of Jerusalem

On Godfrey's death in July 1100, Baldwin was summoned to Jerusalem, leaving Edessa to his cousin Baldwin of Le Bourcq. As king he proved an energetic and expansionist ruler. With naval help from Genoa, Venice, and Pisa he captured the coastal cities essential to the kingdom's survival: Arsuf and Caesarea (1101), Acre (1104), Beirut and Sidon (1110), and built castles such as Montreal (1115) across the Jordan to control the routes to the Red Sea. He repelled repeated Fatimid invasions from Egypt at the battles of Ramla.

Death and succession

Baldwin died in April 1118 while campaigning near Al-Arish in the Sinai, reportedly after falling ill from an old wound reopened while fishing. Having no surviving children, he was succeeded by his cousin Baldwin of Le Bourcq, count of Edessa, as Baldwin II. His reign established the Kingdom of Jerusalem as a durable state, and he is generally judged one of the most capable of its kings.
