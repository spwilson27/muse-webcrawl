Honningsvåg

Honningsvåg is the northernmost town in Norway. It is located in Nordkapp Municipality in Finnmark county. The town is situated on a bay on the southeastern side of the large island of Magerøya, while the famous North Cape and its visitor centre is on the northern side of the island. Honningsvåg was granted town (Norwegian: by) status in 1996 by its municipal council, despite having far fewer inhabitants than the usual threshold; legislation passed in 1997 removed the minimum population criterion.

Geography and climate

Honningsvåg lies at latitude 70°59′N, among the northernmost settlements in the world. Despite its extreme latitude, its port is ice-free year-round owing to the North Atlantic Drift, and the climate is subarctic, moderated by the sea. The town experiences the midnight sun in summer and polar night in winter. Vegetation is sparse tundra; the only naturally occurring tree in the area is said to be a willow in a sheltered garden.

Economy and transport

Fishing has historically been the mainstay of the local economy, together with fish processing and, increasingly, tourism associated with the North Cape, which attracts hundreds of thousands of visitors each year. The town is a port of call for the Hurtigruten coastal express and for cruise ships. Honningsvåg Airport, Valan, lies just north of the town, and the European route E69 highway connects Honningsvåg to the North Cape and, via the North Cape Tunnel, to the mainland.

History

The area has been a fishing settlement for centuries. During the Second World War, after the German occupation of Norway, the town was evacuated in 1944 and nearly all buildings were burned in the German scorched-earth retreat from Finnmark; the church survived and served as a home for returning residents after liberation. The town was rebuilt after the war.
