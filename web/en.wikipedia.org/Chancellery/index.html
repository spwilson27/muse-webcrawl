Chancellery
TypeGovernment office / administrative staff
HeadChancellor or chief of chancellery
FunctionsRecords, correspondence, coordination of government

A chancellery is the office of a chancellor, or the staff and building associated with it. Historically the term denoted the office where a ruler's charters and official documents were drafted, sealed, and archived; in modern government it typically refers to the administrative apparatus supporting a head of government.

History

Medieval chancelleries grew from royal chapels whose clerks kept the king's seal and wrote his letters; the English Chancery became a major court of equity, while the papal chancery developed elaborate rules (cursus) for documents. The office gave its name to the formal "chancery hand" style of handwriting.

Modern usage

In Germany and Austria, the Federal Chancellery (Bundeskanzleramt) is the office of the chancellor and the coordinating centre of the federal government; its Berlin building is among the largest government headquarters in the world. In diplomacy, a chancery is the principal office building of an embassy. In the Catholic Church, a diocesan chancery administers records and official acts of the bishop.
