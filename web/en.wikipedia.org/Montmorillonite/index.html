Montmorillonite

CategoryPhyllosilicate (clay mineral)
GroupSmectite
Crystal systemMonoclinic
Named forMontmorillon, France

Montmorillonite is a very soft phyllosilicate clay mineral of the smectite group, named after the commune of Montmorillon in France, where it was described in 1847. Its idealised formula is (Na,Ca)0.33(Al,Mg)2(Si4O10)(OH)2·nH2O. It is the principal component of bentonite, which forms by the weathering of volcanic ash.

Montmorillonite crystals consist of stacked silicate sheets.

Structure and swelling

Montmorillonite particles are plate-shaped, about a nanometre thick, and consist of an octahedral alumina sheet sandwiched between two tetrahedral silica sheets. Isomorphous substitution gives the layers a net negative charge balanced by exchangeable cations and water between them. The interlayer water allows the mineral to swell dramatically — sodium montmorillonite can expand many times its dry volume — and gives it a high cation-exchange capacity.

Uses

Because of its absorbency and swelling, montmorillonite is used in drilling muds, foundry sands, cat litter, civil-engineering barriers and landfill liners, as a desiccant, in pharmaceuticals and cosmetics, and as a catalyst support. Experiments showing that montmorillonite clays can catalyse the formation of RNA oligomers from activated nucleotides have made the mineral relevant to origin-of-life research.
