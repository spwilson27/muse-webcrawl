Rookery

A rookery is a colony of breeding animals, generally gregarious birds such as rooks, penguins, or herons, or marine mammals such as seals and sea lions. The term derives from the rook, a Eurasian corvid that nests communally in treetops.

Bird rookeries

Rooks build large stick nests high in trees, returning to the same rookery year after year. Wading birds — herons, egrets, and ibises — form mixed-species rookeries in wetlands and on coastal islands, where concentrated nesting offers protection from predators. Penguin colonies on Antarctic and sub-Antarctic shores, sometimes numbering hundreds of thousands of pairs, are also called rookeries.

Mammal rookeries

For pinnipeds, a rookery is the breeding beach where males establish territories and females give birth and nurse pups, distinct from a non-breeding "haul-out" site.

Other uses

In the nineteenth century, "rookery" was applied to crowded slum housing districts, notably in Victorian London, and to cramped tenement quarters in American cities.
