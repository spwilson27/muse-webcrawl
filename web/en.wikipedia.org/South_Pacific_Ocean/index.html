South Pacific Ocean

South Pacific Ocean
Part of: Pacific Ocean, south of the Equator
Bordered by: Australia and Asia (west), the Americas (east)
Notable features: South Pacific Gyre, Great Barrier Reef (Coral Sea), Polynesia

The South Pacific Ocean is the portion of the Pacific Ocean south of the Equator, lying between Australia and Southeast Asia to the west and South America to the east, and extending south to the Southern Ocean. It contains the island groups of Melanesia, Micronesia's southern atolls, and Polynesia, including New Zealand, Fiji, Samoa, Tonga, French Polynesia and Easter Island.

Oceanography

The region is dominated by the South Pacific Gyre, a vast clockwise-to-anticlockwise circulation whose centre — the oceanic pole of inaccessibility, known as Point Nemo — is the most remote place in the world ocean, more than 2,600 km from the nearest land. The South Equatorial Current flows westward, feeding the East Australian Current, while the cold Peru (Humboldt) Current flows north along the South American coast, supporting one of the world's richest fisheries. The South Pacific is also the engine of the El Niño–Southern Oscillation, whose warm and cold phases alter weather worldwide.

Human history

The ocean was settled in one of humanity's greatest migrations: Austronesian voyagers, using double-hulled canoes and navigation by stars and swells, colonised even its most isolated islands by about 1200 AD. Europeans entered with Magellan's crossing of 1520–21; Abel Tasman, James Cook and others charted the region in the 17th and 18th centuries. The South Pacific was a major theatre of the Second World War, the scene of battles such as the Coral Sea and Guadalcanal, and later of French nuclear testing at Mururoa.

Environment

Low-lying atoll nations such as Tuvalu and Kiribati are acutely threatened by sea-level rise, and the gyre's remoteness has made it an accumulation zone for plastic debris, the so-called South Pacific garbage patch.
