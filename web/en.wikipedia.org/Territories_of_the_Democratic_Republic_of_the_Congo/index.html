Territories of the Democratic Republic of the Congo

The territories of the Democratic Republic of the Congo are the third-level administrative subdivisions of the country, below the provinces and the districts. The DRC is divided into 26 provinces, which are in turn divided into districts and territories; a territory is administered by a territorial administrator appointed by the central government.

Overview

Under the administrative framework inherited from the colonial era and revised by the 2006 constitution, the country contains roughly 145 territories. Each territory groups together chiefdoms (chefferies) and sectors, which are themselves subdivided into groupings and villages. Major cities (villes) and communes stand outside the territory system.

Role and administration

The territorial administrator represents the state at the local level, overseeing security, civil registration, and coordination of public services. In practice, customary chiefs retain considerable authority in rural areas, and the reach of the territorial administration varies widely across the country's vast interior.

Examples

Well-known territories include Idiofa in Kwilu Province, Bunia's surrounding territories in Ituri, and Lubero and Beni in North Kivu, regions frequently mentioned in reporting on eastern Congo.
