The California State University (Cal State or CSU) is a public university system in California, and the largest public university system in the United States. With 23 campuses and eight off-campus centres enrolling roughly 480,000 students, it is one of the largest systems of higher education in the world, employing more than 50,000 faculty and staff.

History

The system's roots lie in the Minns Evening Normal School of 1857, which became San José State University, the oldest public university on the West Coast. The modern system was created by the Donahoe Higher Education Act of 1960, part of the California Master Plan for Higher Education, which assigned the CSU the mission of undergraduate and graduate instruction through the master's degree, with an emphasis on teaching, professional education and applied research.

Campuses and role

Campuses include San Diego State, San Francisco State, San José State, Cal State Long Beach, Cal State Fullerton, Cal Poly San Luis Obispo and Cal Poly Pomona. Under the Master Plan, the CSU draws students from the top third of California high school graduates, while the University of California handles most doctoral research — although the CSU now grants a limited number of professional doctorates. The system awards nearly half of the bachelor's degrees in California and is a major producer of teachers, nurses, engineers and business graduates for the state workforce.

Governance and impact

The CSU is governed by a Board of Trustees and a Chancellor based in Long Beach. Its alumni number in the millions, and system studies credit it with substantial contributions to California's economy and social mobility, with relatively affordable tuition among American public universities.
