Continued fraction

A continued fraction is an iterated nested fraction.

A continued fraction is a mathematical expression that can be written as a fraction with a denominator that is a sum that itself contains a fraction, and so on, in an infinite (or finite) nesting. In general form it is written a0 + 1/(a1 + 1/(a2 + 1/(a3 + …))), where the ai are integers (or, in generalised continued fractions, other quantities).

Properties

Every rational number has a finite simple continued fraction, obtainable from the Euclidean algorithm, while every irrational number has a unique infinite one. Truncating the expansion at successive points yields the convergents, which are the best possible rational approximations to the number. Periodic continued fractions correspond exactly to quadratic irrationals (Lagrange's theorem); famously, the square root of 2 is [1; 2, 2, 2, …] and the golden ratio is [1; 1, 1, 1, …], the slowest-converging of all.

History and applications

Continued fractions appear in the work of Indian mathematicians, in Rafael Bombelli's method for square roots (1572), and in John Wallis's writings, which coined the term. They are used in number theory (Diophantine approximation, Pell's equation), in solving calendrical problems (gear ratios for astronomical clocks), and in algorithms such as Shor's and the continued fraction factorisation method.
