DVB-T2

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

DVB-T2 is the second-generation standard for digital terrestrial television broadcasting, developed by the DVB Project and published by ETSI as EN 302 755 in 2009. It offers a substantial increase in capacity — typically 30 to 50 percent more payload than its predecessor DVB-T in the same channel — and has become the standard platform for HD and UHD terrestrial services in many countries.

Technical features

DVB-T2 retains COFDM modulation but adds larger FFT sizes (up to 32K), 256-QAM constellations, rotated constellations for robustness, and powerful LDPC/BCH forward error correction inherited from DVB-S2. It supports multiple Physical Layer Pipes (PLPs), allowing services with different robustness levels to share a multiplex, and includes provisions for single-frequency networks and, in its "Lite" profile, mobile reception.

Deployment

The United Kingdom was the first country to launch DVB-T2 services, with Freeview HD beginning broadcasts in December 2009. The standard has since been adopted across most of Europe, Russia, much of Africa and Asia, and parts of Latin America, both for new digital launches and for second-generation transitions from DVB-T. Combined with HEVC coding, T2 multiplexes can carry UHD (4K) services, as trialled and launched in Germany, South Korea (using ATSC 3.0 domestically instead), and elsewhere.

Comparison with DVB-T

Unlike DVB-T, DVB-T2 is not backwards compatible, so viewers require new receivers; however, in a typical 8 MHz channel it can deliver around 40 Mbit/s — enough for several HD channels or a mix of HD and SD services where DVB-T carried roughly 24 Mbit/s.
