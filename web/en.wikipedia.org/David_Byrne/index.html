David Byrne

David Byrne (born 14 May 1952) is an American musician, writer, visual artist, and filmmaker. He was the founding member, principal songwriter, lead singer, and guitarist of the American new wave band Talking Heads.

Born in Dumbarton, Scotland, Byrne moved with his family first to Canada and then to Maryland. After studying at the Rhode Island School of Design, he formed Talking Heads in New York in 1975 with Chris Frantz and Tina Weymouth, later joined by Jerry Harrison. The band produced landmark albums including Remain in Light (1980) and Speaking in Tongues (1983), and the celebrated concert film Stop Making Sense (1984), directed by Jonathan Demme.

Solo and collaborative work

Byrne's solo career has ranged across genres: the Brian Eno collaboration My Life in the Bush of Ghosts (1981), the Latin-influenced Rei Momo (1989), the orchestral The Forest (1991), and American Utopia (2018), which became a Broadway production and a 2020 film directed by Spike Lee. He won an Academy Award for the score of The Last Emperor (1987), composed with Ryuichi Sakamoto and Cong Su.

Byrne founded the record label Luaka Bop, the online radio station Radio David Byrne, and has written books including How Music Works (2012) and Bicycle Diaries (2009). He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame with Talking Heads in 2002.
