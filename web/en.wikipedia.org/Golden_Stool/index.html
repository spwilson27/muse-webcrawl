Golden Stool

The Golden Stool (Akan: Sika Dwa Kofi, "the Golden Stool born on a Friday") is the sacred throne of the rulers of the Ashanti Empire and the Ashanti people of present-day Ghana. It is believed to embody the sunsum — the soul and spirit — of the Ashanti nation itself, containing the collective destiny of its people.

Legend and history

According to tradition, around 1700 the priest Okomfo Anokye called the stool down from the heavens, whereupon it descended gently onto the lap of Osei Tutu I, the first Asantehene, uniting the Ashanti states. The wooden stool, plated with gold, must never touch the ground and no one — not even the Asantehene — may sit on it; it rests on its own chair and is carried in procession on ceremonial occasions.

War of the Golden Stool

In 1900, the British governor Sir Frederick Hodgson's demand to sit on the stool provoked the War of the Golden Stool, led by Queen Mother Yaa Asantewaa. The Ashanti hid the stool; when it was accidentally uncovered by road workers in 1920 and desecrated by goldsmiths, those responsible were tried by the Ashanti themselves to avert war. The stool remains in Kumasi, periodically displayed at royal ceremonies.
