Lin Biao

Lin Biao (5 December 1907 – 13 September 1971) was a Chinese marshal and politician who played a leading role in the Communist victory in the Chinese Civil War and became Mao Zedong's designated successor during the Cultural Revolution, before dying in a plane crash in Mongolia amid allegations that he had plotted a coup.

Military career

A graduate of the Whampoa Military Academy, Lin rose rapidly in the Red Army, commanding forces on the Long March and defeating Nationalist armies in the decisive Liaoshen and Pingjin campaigns of 1948–49. Made one of the People's Republic's ten marshals in 1955, he became Minister of National Defense in 1959 and reorganised the People's Liberation Army along Maoist political lines, promoting the Quotations from Chairman Mao (the "Little Red Book").

Rise and fall

Lin was named vice chairman of the Chinese Communist Party and Mao's successor in the 1969 party constitution. Relations with Mao then soured. On 13 September 1971, after what the Chinese government describes as a failed coup plot ("Project 571") by Lin's son Lin Liguo, Lin Biao, his wife Ye Qun, and others fled by air and died when their Trident jet crashed near Öndörkhaan in Mongolia, officially from fuel exhaustion. The event shocked China and discredited the Cultural Revolution's leadership cult; historians continue to debate the full circumstances.
