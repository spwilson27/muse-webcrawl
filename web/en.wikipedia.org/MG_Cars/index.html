MG Cars

MG is a British automotive marque founded in Oxford in the 1920s by Cecil Kimber, manager of Morris Garages — from which the initials MG derive. The company became famous for small, affordable open two-seater sports cars such as the MG T-type, MGA, MGB, and Midget, which popularised the sports car in Britain and the United States.

History

MG production began in earnest in 1924 with the 14/28 Super Sports. Under Kimber the marque built a reputation in motorsport and for stylish coachwork. After the Second World War the T-series midgets were exported in large numbers to America, where returning servicemen created a lasting market for British roadsters. The MGB (1962–1980) became one of the best-selling sports cars of all time, and the marque passed through a succession of owners: the Nuffield Organisation, BMC, British Leyland, the Rover Group, and BMW.

Revival

After the collapse of MG Rover in 2005, the MG brand and the Longbridge plant were acquired by China's Nanjing Automobile, which in turn merged into SAIC Motor. Under SAIC, MG was relaunched as a maker of mainstream hatchbacks, SUVs, and electric vehicles, with design and engineering input in the UK. Models such as the MG ZS, MG HS, and the electric MG4 EV made MG one of the fastest-growing car brands in Britain and Europe in the early 2020s, while the Cyberster roadster revived the marque's sporting tradition.
