The World Health Organization (WHO) is a specialised agency of the United Nations responsible for international public health. Its constitution was adopted in 1946 and came into force on 7 April 1948, a date now celebrated as World Health Day. Headquartered in Geneva, Switzerland, it has six regional offices and 150 country offices worldwide.

Role and work

The WHO's mandate is "the attainment by all peoples of the highest possible level of health". It sets global health norms and standards, coordinates responses to health emergencies, monitors disease trends and supports research. Its notable achievements include the eradication of smallpox, declared in 1980, and the near-eradication of poliomyelitis through the Global Polio Eradication Initiative. The agency maintains the International Classification of Diseases (ICD) and led the international response coordination during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Governance and funding

The WHO is governed by the World Health Assembly, composed of its 194 member states, which elects the Director-General. Its budget combines assessed contributions from member states with voluntary contributions, the latter now making up the majority and raising recurring debate about donor influence on the agency's agenda.
