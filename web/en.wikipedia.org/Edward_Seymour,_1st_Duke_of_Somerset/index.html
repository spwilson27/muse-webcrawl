Edward Seymour, 1st Duke of Somerset

Edward Seymour, 1st Duke of Somerset (c. 1500 – 22 January 1552) was an English nobleman and the eldest surviving brother of Queen Jane Seymour, third wife of Henry VIII. After Henry's death he became Lord Protector of England and effective ruler of the kingdom during the minority of his nephew, Edward VI, from 1547 to 1549. His rule advanced the Protestant Reformation in England before his fall from power and execution under charges of treason.

Rise

Seymour rose rapidly after his sister married Henry VIII in 1536, becoming Earl of Hertford and a leading figure at court. He won military distinction against Scotland, including the burning of Edinburgh in 1544, and against France. On Henry's death in 1547 he secured the regency, took the title Lord Protector, and was created Duke of Somerset.

Protectorate

Somerset's government repealed the heresy and treason laws, encouraged religious reform culminating in the first Book of Common Prayer (1549), and pursued war with Scotland — winning the Battle of Pinkie (1547) but at ruinous cost. His sympathy for the poor and proclamations against enclosure earned him the popular title "the Good Duke", but alienated landowners. Rebellions in 1549 — the Prayer Book Rebellion in the West and Kett's Rebellion in Norfolk — exposed the regime's weakness.

Fall and execution

In October 1549 the Privy Council, led by John Dudley, Earl of Warwick, removed Somerset from the Protectorate. Briefly restored to the council after imprisonment, he was arrested again in 1551 and executed on Tower Hill on 22 January 1552 on a charge of felony, amid public sympathy for the fallen duke.
