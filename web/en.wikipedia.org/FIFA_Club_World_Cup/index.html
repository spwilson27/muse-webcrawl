FIFA Club World Cup

The FIFA Club World Cup is an international men's association football competition organised by FIFA, the sport's global governing body. The competition was first contested in 2000 as the FIFA Club World Championship and was held annually from 2005 to 2023 between the winners of each of the six continental confederations' top club competitions, along with the host nation's league champions. From 2025, the tournament was expanded to 32 teams and is held every four years.

History

The tournament grew out of the Intercontinental Cup, contested from 1960 to 2004 by the European and South American champions. After an initial edition in Brazil in 2000, won by Corinthians, the competition settled into an annual December slot, hosted frequently by Japan, the UAE, Morocco, Qatar, and Saudi Arabia. European clubs dominated: Real Madrid won five editions, Barcelona three, and Bayern Munich and Corinthians two each. Chelsea won the first expanded 32-team edition, held in the United States in the summer of 2025, defeating Paris Saint-Germain in the final. The older annual competition was rebranded as the FIFA Intercontinental Cup from 2024.

Format

In the former annual format, seven teams played a short knockout bracket in which the European and South American champions entered at the semi-final stage — a structure criticised for its brevity. The expanded format mirrors the FIFA World Cup: a group stage of eight groups of four, followed by knockout rounds, with qualification through continental champions league results over a four-year cycle.

Reception

The expanded tournament drew both commercial interest and criticism over player workload and fixture congestion, but attracted large audiences and a prize fund reported at around one billion dollars for the 2025 edition.
