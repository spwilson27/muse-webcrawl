Filter (signal processing)

In signal processing, a filter is a device or process that removes some unwanted components or features from a signal. Filters are widely used in electronics and telecommunications, in radio, television, audio recording, radar, control systems, and digital signal processing.

Classification

Filters are commonly classified by their frequency response:

  • Low-pass filter – passes signals with a frequency lower than a selected cutoff frequency and attenuates higher frequencies.
  • High-pass filter – passes signals above a cutoff and attenuates lower ones.
  • Band-pass filter – passes frequencies within a certain range and rejects those outside it.
  • Band-stop filter (notch filter) – rejects frequencies within a certain range and passes those outside it.

Implementation

Analog filters are built from passive components (resistors, capacitors, inductors) or active circuits using operational amplifiers. Classic analog designs include the Butterworth, Chebyshev, Bessel, and elliptic filters, each trading off passband flatness, rolloff steepness, and phase response. Digital filters operate on sampled signals and are divided into finite impulse response (FIR) and infinite impulse response (IIR) types; they offer precise, repeatable, and reprogrammable behavior.

Characteristics

A filter is described by its transfer function, its cutoff frequency, its order (which determines the steepness of the transition between passband and stopband), and its phase and group-delay behavior. Linear filters preserve superposition, while nonlinear filters such as the median filter are used especially in image processing.

Applications

Filters separate channels in radio receivers, remove hum and noise from audio, anti-alias signals before digitization, smooth control signals, equalize recordings, and extract features in biomedical and seismic data.
