Tava

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A tava or tawa is a flat or slightly concave disc-shaped griddle used across South Asian, Central Asian, and Middle Eastern cooking for preparing flatbreads and other foods. Tavas are typically made of cast iron, aluminium, or steel, and range from about 20 to 45 centimetres in diameter. In Indian, Pakistani, Bangladeshi, and Afghan kitchens the tava is one of the most essential everyday cooking implements.

Uses

The tava is used to cook a wide variety of unleavened and leavened flatbreads, including chapati, paratha, dosa, naan (in home kitchens), puri, and thepla. It is also used for shallow-frying items such as aloo tikki, for roasting spices and nuts, for toasting sandwiches, and for preparing tawa-style dishes — vegetables, paneer, or meats cooked with spiced masala directly on the griddle, a preparation popularised by street vendors in Mumbai and other cities.

In Turkey and the Balkans, a related shallow pan called a tava is used for dishes such as tavë kosi in Albania. In Central Asia, the larger flat saj serves a similar griddling function. The word itself derives from Persian and Turkic roots meaning a frying pan or griddle, and cognates appear across the region.

Design and care

Traditional cast-iron tavas are heavy, develop a seasoned non-stick surface with use, and are prized for even heat distribution when puffing chapatis directly over a flame. Modern versions may have non-stick coatings, wooden or heat-resistant handles, and slightly raised rims. A concave tava is preferred for dosas, spreading the thin batter in a spiral.
