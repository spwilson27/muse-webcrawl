International Polar Year

An International Polar Year (IPY) is a collaborative, international effort with intensive scientific research in the polar regions. To date, four IPYs have been held: the first in 1882–1883, the second in 1932–1933, the third (also known as the International Geophysical Year) in 1957–1958, and the fourth in 2007–2008, which in practice extended from 1 March 2007 to 1 March 2009 to cover two full annual cycles in both polar regions.

The first and second Polar Years

The concept was proposed by the Austro-Hungarian naval officer Karl Weyprecht, who argued that polar science required simultaneous, coordinated observations at circumpolar stations rather than isolated expeditions. The first IPY, organised by the International Polar Commission, involved eleven nations operating fourteen stations in the Arctic and two in the sub-Antarctic. The second IPY, fifty years later, focused on meteorology, magnetism, auroral phenomena and the effects of radio wave propagation in the ionosphere, though it was hindered by the Great Depression.

International Geophysical Year

The third polar year became the International Geophysical Year (IGY) of 1957–1958, a vast programme involving 67 nations that produced landmark results, including the launch of the first artificial satellites, the discovery of the Van Allen radiation belts, and research that helped confirm plate tectonics. Antarctica was a major focus, and the IGY's scientific cooperation there laid the groundwork for the Antarctic Treaty of 1959.

The 2007–2008 Polar Year

The fourth IPY, sponsored by the International Council for Science and the World Meteorological Organization, involved over 200 projects and scientists from more than 60 nations, with an estimated 50,000 participants. Its research addressed the rapid changes under way in the Arctic and Antarctic, including melting ice sheets and sea ice, thawing permafrost, ocean circulation, polar ecosystems, and the health and cultures of northern communities. A fifth International Polar Year is planned for 2032–2033.
