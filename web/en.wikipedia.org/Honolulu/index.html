Honolulu is the capital and most populous city of the U.S. state of Hawaii, located on the southeastern coast of the island of Oahu. The consolidated City and County of Honolulu governs the city together with the rest of Oahu and the outlying Northwestern Hawaiian Islands. The name means "sheltered harbour" or "calm port" in Hawaiian.

History

Honolulu Harbour became a hub for trans-Pacific trade after Kamehameha I moved his court to Oahu in the early nineteenth century; Honolulu was made capital of the Hawaiian Kingdom in 1845 (formally 1850). Following annexation by the United States in 1898, it grew with military installations at Pearl Harbor — attacked by Japan on 7 December 1941, bringing the United States into the Second World War — and with post-war tourism centred on Waikiki.

Geography and landmarks

The city stretches along Oahu's south shore between Pearl Harbor and Makapuʻu Point, backed by the Koʻolau Range. Landmarks include Diamond Head crater, Waikiki Beach, Iolani Palace (the only royal palace in the United States), the USS Arizona Memorial, and Ala Moana Center. Honolulu is a major Pacific hub for business, defence and tourism, and is consistently ranked among the most liveable — and most expensive — cities in the United States.
