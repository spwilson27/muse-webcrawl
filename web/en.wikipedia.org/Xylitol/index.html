Xylitol

Xylitol is a sugar alcohol used as a sweetener, with the formula C5H12O5. It occurs naturally in small amounts in fruits, vegetables and birch, and is produced industrially by hydrogenating xylose from plant material.

Properties and uses

Xylitol is about as sweet as sucrose but with roughly 40% fewer calories, a low glycaemic impact, and it does not feed the oral bacteria that cause dental caries; it is widely used in sugar-free gum, mints and dental products, and research supports a role in reducing tooth decay.

Safety

Xylitol is safe for humans in normal amounts, though excess can cause digestive upset. It is highly toxic to dogs, in whom it can trigger insulin release, hypoglycaemia and liver failure.
