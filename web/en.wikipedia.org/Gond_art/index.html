Gond art

Gond art is a form of painting and folk art practised by the Gond people, one of the largest tribal (Adivasi) communities of central India, centred in Madhya Pradesh with populations in Chhattisgarh, Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh, and Odisha. Traditionally painted on the mud walls and floors of houses for festivals and rituals, it has since the 1980s also flourished on paper and canvas for an international art market.

Style and themes

Gond painting is characterised by meticulous detail, fine dots and dashes built up into textures, and vivid colour. Subjects are drawn from the natural world — especially trees (the mahua and peepal are sacred), deer, peacocks, birds, and fish — and from Gond mythology, folklore, songs, and deities such as Bada Deo. The imagery reflects the Gond belief that everything in nature is inhabited by a spirit and is worthy of reverence and good fortune.

Modern revival

The contemporary movement began with Jangarh Singh Shyam (1962–2001) of Patangarh village, whose talent was recognised by the artist Jagdish Swaminathan at Bharat Bhavan in Bhopal; Jangarh's work was exhibited internationally, including in Paris and Tokyo, and founded the style sometimes called "Jangarh Kalam." A large community of Gond artists — including Bhajju Shyam and Durga Bai — now works in the tradition, which has gained wider institutional recognition, including a Geographical Indication tag.
