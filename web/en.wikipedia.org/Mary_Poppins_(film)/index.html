Mary Poppins is a 1964 American musical fantasy film produced by Walt Disney, directed by Robert Stevenson and based on P. L. Travers's series of children's books. Julie Andrews, in her feature film debut, stars as the magical nanny who descends by umbrella into the lives of the Banks family in Edwardian London, with Dick Van Dyke as the chimney sweep Bert.

Plot

In 1910 London, the stern banker George Banks advertises for a strict nanny for his children Jane and Michael; instead Mary Poppins arrives, carried by the east wind. Through outings inside a chalk pavement picture, a tea party on a ceiling and a lesson for their father about what truly matters, she transforms the household, then departs on the west wind once her work is done.

Music and reception

The Sherman brothers' score includes "A Spoonful of Sugar", "Supercalifragilisticexpialidocious", "Feed the Birds" and the Oscar-winning "Chim Chim Cher-ee". The film was nominated for 13 Academy Awards and won five, including Best Actress for Andrews. It was Disney's biggest live-action success of its era, and a sequel, Mary Poppins Returns, was released in 2018.
