Soviet invasion of Manchuria


Soviet forces entering Manchuria, 1945

The Soviet invasion of Manchuria, formally the Manchurian strategic offensive operation and also known as Operation August Storm, was a major military campaign of the Second World War that began on 9 August 1945. More than 1.5 million Soviet and Mongolian troops attacked the Japanese Kwantung Army in Manchukuo (Japanese-occupied Manchuria), rapidly destroying it and contributing decisively to Japan's surrender.

Background

At the Yalta Conference in February 1945, Stalin agreed that the USSR would enter the war against Japan within three months of Germany's defeat. The Soviet–Japanese Neutrality Pact was denounced in April 1945, and Soviet forces, battle-hardened formations transferred from Europe, concentrated in the Far East under Marshal Aleksandr Vasilevsky. Facing them, the Kwantung Army — once Japan's most prestigious field army — had been stripped of its best units and numbered about 700,000 to 1 million men with outdated equipment.

The offensive

On 9 August 1945, Soviet forces attacked on three fronts across a 4,000-kilometre frontier: the Transbaikal Front from Mongolia across the desert and Greater Khingan mountains, and the 1st and 2nd Far Eastern Fronts from the Maritime Territory and along the Amur. The speed and depth of the advance overwhelmed Japanese defences; within days Soviet spearheads had penetrated hundreds of kilometres. Soviet forces also landed in northern Korea, seized southern Sakhalin, and occupied the Kuril Islands. Japanese losses were around 84,000 killed and nearly 600,000 captured, against some 12,000 Soviet dead.

Aftermath

The destruction of the Kwantung Army, together with the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki, confronted Japan's leadership with hopeless defeat; Emperor Hirohito announced Japan's acceptance of the Potsdam Declaration on 15 August. Manchuria was returned to China, Port Arthur and Dalian passed under Soviet control for a time, Korea was divided at the 38th parallel between Soviet and American occupation zones, and hundreds of thousands of Japanese prisoners were sent to Siberian labour camps.
