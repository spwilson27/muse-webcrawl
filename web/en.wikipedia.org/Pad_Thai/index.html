Pad Thai

CourseMain dish
Place of originThailand
Main ingredientsRice noodles, eggs, tofu, tamarind, fish sauce, peanuts, bean sprouts
Serving temperatureHot

Pad Thai (Thai: ผัดไทย) is a stir-fried rice noodle dish commonly served as street food in Thailand as part of the country's cuisine. It is typically made with rice noodles, shrimp, peanuts, scrambled eggs, and bean sprouts, among other vegetables. The ingredients are fried in a wok. The dish's signature sauce is based on tamarind pulp, fish sauce, and palm sugar, giving it a characteristic balance of sour, salty, and sweet flavours.

History

Although stir-fried noodles have existed in Thailand for centuries, pad Thai in its modern form is generally believed to have been popularised during the mid-20th century under Prime Minister Plaek Phibunsongkhram, whose government promoted the dish as part of a nation-building campaign and encouraged rice noodle consumption to reduce rice exports strain. It has since become one of Thailand's national dishes and one of the best-known Thai foods worldwide.

Variations

Common variations include versions made with shrimp (pad Thai goong), chicken, or pork, as well as vegetarian versions using soy sauce in place of fish sauce. The dish is typically garnished with crushed roasted peanuts, chives, fresh bean sprouts, and a wedge of lime.
