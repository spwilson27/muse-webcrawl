Vietnam, officially the Socialist Republic of Vietnam, is a country at the eastern edge of mainland Southeast Asia. It borders China to the north, Laos and Cambodia to the west, and the South China Sea to the east. With a population of around 100 million, it is one of the most populous countries in the world. Its capital is Hanoi and its largest city is Ho Chi Minh City (formerly Saigon).

History

Vietnamese civilisation emerged in the Red River Delta, and independent Vietnamese states existed from the 10th century AD, repeatedly resisting invasions from China and later expanding southward at the expense of Champa and the Khmer Empire. France colonised Vietnam in the later 19th century, forming French Indochina. After the First Indochina War, the 1954 Geneva Accords divided the country into a communist North and an anti-communist South. The resulting Vietnam War drew in the United States on a massive scale and ended in 1975 with the fall of Saigon and reunification under the communist government in 1976.

Politics and economy

Vietnam is a one-party state governed by the Communist Party of Vietnam. Since the Đổi Mới ("renovation") reforms of 1986, the country has followed a socialist-oriented market economy and become one of Asia's fastest-growing economies, a major exporter of electronics, textiles, footwear, coffee, and rice, and a popular tourist destination.

Geography and culture

The country is long and narrow, with the fertile deltas of the Red River in the north and the Mekong in the south, a mountainous interior, and famous coastal landscapes such as Ha Long Bay and Phong Nha-Kẻ Bàng. Vietnamese culture combines indigenous traditions with deep Chinese influence and Confucian, Buddhist, and Taoist heritage; the language is written in a Latin-based script, chữ Quốc ngữ.
