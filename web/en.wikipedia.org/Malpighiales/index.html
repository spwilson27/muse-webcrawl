Malpighiales

Malpighiales is one of the largest orders of flowering plants, comprising about 16,000 species in some 36 families — roughly 7.8 percent of all eudicots. Molecular phylogenetic studies revealed the order as a strongly supported clade whose members had not previously been grouped together because of their extraordinary morphological diversity.

Diversity

Members include economically and ecologically important plants: the spurge family Euphorbiaceae (cassava, rubber tree, poinsettia, castor oil plant), Salicaceae (willows and poplars), Violaceae (violets and pansies), Passifloraceae (passionfruit), Linaceae (flax), Rhizophoraceae (red mangroves), and Hypericaceae (St John's wort). The order ranges from tiny aquatic herbs to canopy trees of tropical rainforests.

Classification

The order was first recognised in its modern sense by the Angiosperm Phylogeny Group; the APG IV system retains it within the fabid rosids, in the COM clade together with Celastrales and Oxalidales. Relationships among its families were long difficult to resolve, earning the order a reputation as one of the most intractable problems in angiosperm phylogeny.
