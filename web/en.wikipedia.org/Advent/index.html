Advent

Advent is a season observed in most Christian denominations as a time of expectant waiting and preparation for both the celebration of the Nativity of Christ at Christmas and the return of Christ at the Second Coming. The term is a version of the Latin word adventus, meaning "coming" or "arrival". Advent begins on the fourth Sunday before Christmas (the Sunday nearest St Andrew's Day, 30 November) and marks the start of the liturgical year in Western Christianity.

Practices

The liturgical colour of Advent is violet or purple, symbolising penitence and preparation. Traditions include lighting the candles of an Advent wreath — one on each Sunday of the season — Advent calendars counting the days from 1 December, fasting observances in Eastern churches (where the Nativity Fast lasts 40 days), and special music and readings including the "O Antiphons" of the final week.

History

A preparatory season before Christmas developed in the 4th and 5th centuries in Gaul and Spain, originally centred on baptism or fasting; by the Middle Ages its length and themes — hope, peace, joy and love, associated with the four candles — had settled into recognisable form.
