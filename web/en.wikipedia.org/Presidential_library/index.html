Presidential library

In the United States, a presidential library is a repository of papers, records and other historical materials relating to a president. The presidential library system is administered by the National Archives and Records Administration (NARA) through the Office of Presidential Libraries.

History

The system began with Franklin D. Roosevelt, who in 1939 donated his papers to the federal government and raised funds to build a library on his Hyde Park estate — breaking with the tradition of papers being treated as personal property. The Presidential Libraries Act of 1955 formalised the system.

Libraries

There are 13 NARA-operated presidential libraries, from Herbert Hoover to George W. Bush, with later centres operating under new arrangements. Each combines an archives with a museum open to the public.
