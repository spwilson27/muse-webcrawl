Recrystallization (metallurgy)

During recrystallization, new strain-free grains nucleate and consume the deformed microstructure.

In metallurgy and materials science, recrystallization is a process by which deformed grains of a crystalline material are replaced by a new set of defect-free grains, usually when the material is heated after cold working. It is one stage of the annealing sequence, preceded by recovery and followed, at higher temperatures or longer times, by grain growth. Recrystallization restores ductility to work-hardened metal, enabling further forming operations.

Mechanism

Cold work — rolling, drawing or forging below the recrystallization temperature — stores energy in the metal in the form of dislocations and other crystal defects. On heating, atoms gain enough mobility for new, strain-free grains to nucleate, typically at grain boundaries and deformation bands, and to grow by boundary migration until the deformed structure is consumed. The driving force is the reduction in the total stored energy of the system. During the earlier recovery stage, dislocations rearrange and annihilate, lowering internal stress without new grain formation; recrystallization proper is recognised by the appearance of new grains with a distinct orientation.

Recrystallization temperature

The recrystallization temperature — loosely, the temperature at which recrystallization completes in about an hour — is roughly one-third to one-half of the absolute melting temperature for pure metals, and is lower the greater the prior cold work. It is not a fixed property: alloying elements and fine second-phase particles retard boundary motion and raise the temperature, an effect exploited in alloys designed for high-temperature service. Working above the recrystallization temperature is called hot working, since the metal recrystallises as fast as it is deformed, and so does not harden; below it, cold working progressively strengthens the material but reduces ductility until annealing is required.

Dynamic recrystallization

When deformation and heating occur together, as in hot rolling and extrusion, recrystallization may proceed during the deformation itself — dynamic recrystallization — refining the grain size and lowering flow stress. The resulting fine, uniform grain structure improves both strength and toughness, so controlled rolling schedules in steel mills are designed around recrystallization behaviour.
