Baji Rao I

Baji Rao I (18 August 1700 – 28 April 1740), born Visaji Bhat, was the seventh Peshwa (prime minister) of the Maratha Empire, serving the Chhatrapati Shahu I from 1720 until his death. He is widely regarded as one of the greatest cavalry generals of Indian history, famous for never having lost a battle.

Campaigns

Succeeding his father Balaji Vishwanath at the age of twenty, Baji Rao shifted Maratha power northward, defeating Mughal and regional forces across Malwa, Bundelkhand and Gujarat. His notable victories include the Battle of Palkhed (1728) against the Nizam of Hyderabad, won through brilliant manoeuvre, and the campaign of 1737 in which his horsemen swept up to the outskirts of Delhi itself. He also intervened in Bundelkhand to aid Raja Chhatrasal, whose gratitude included the gift of territory and the hand of his daughter Mastani, Baji Rao's celebrated consort.

Administration and legacy

Baji Rao consolidated the Maratha confederacy by assigning spheres of influence to rising houses such as the Scindias, Holkars, Gaekwads and Bhonsles, and founded the Peshwa's seat firmly at Pune. His strategy of striking north — summarised in his attributed exhortation to "strike at the trunk of the withering tree" — transformed the Marathas from a regional power into the dominant force in India. He died of fever on campaign near the Narmada in 1740, aged 39, and was succeeded by his son Balaji Baji Rao.
