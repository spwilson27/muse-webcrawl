Giorgio Vasari

Born: 30 July 1511, Arezzo, Tuscany
Died: 27 June 1574, Florence
Occupations: Painter, architect, writer, historian
Known for: Lives of the Most Excellent Painters, Sculptors, and Architects; Uffizi

Giorgio Vasari (30 July 1511 – 27 June 1574) was an Italian Renaissance painter, architect, and writer, best known for his Lives of the Most Excellent Painters, Sculptors, and Architects (1550, expanded 1568), considered the foundational work of art history and the source of many anecdotes about Renaissance artists.

Career

Born in Arezzo, Vasari trained in Florence and came under the patronage of the Medici. As court artist to Grand Duke Cosimo I, he painted fresco cycles in the Palazzo Vecchio and designed the Uffizi in Florence (begun 1560), including the Vasari Corridor over the Ponte Vecchio. His own painting is in the Mannerist style.

The Lives

The Lives gives biographies of Italian artists from Cimabue and Giotto — whom Vasari credited with reviving art after its medieval decline — to Michelangelo, his contemporary and hero. Vasari coined or popularised much of the vocabulary of art history, including the term "Renaissance" (rinascita) and "Gothic" as a pejorative. Though biased toward Florentine artists and sometimes unreliable, the work remains indispensable.
