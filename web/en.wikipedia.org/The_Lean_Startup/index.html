The Lean Startup

The Lean Startup: How Today's Entrepreneurs Use Continuous Innovation to Create Radically Successful Businesses is a 2011 book by Eric Ries. It proposes a methodology for developing businesses and products under conditions of extreme uncertainty.

Methodology

Core concepts include the Build–Measure–Learn feedback loop, the minimum viable product (MVP) — the fastest way to test a hypothesis — validated learning, innovation accounting, and the decision to pivot or persevere. Ries drew on lean manufacturing, Steve Blank's customer development, and his experience at IMVU.

Reception

The book became a bestseller and its vocabulary entered mainstream business and even government (e.g., lean approaches to public services). Critics argue the method can encourage short-termism and ill-fitting application to science-based ventures. See also The Power Broker.
