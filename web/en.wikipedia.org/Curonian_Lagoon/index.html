The Curonian Lagoon (Lithuanian: Kuršių marios; Russian: Куршский залив, Kurshskiy zaliv; German: Kurisches Haff) is a lagoon separated from the Baltic Sea by the Curonian Spit. Its surface area is 1,619 square kilometres (625 sq mi), making it the largest lagoon in Europe. The Neman River supplies about 90% of its inflows; its watershed consists of about 100,450 square kilometres in Lithuania and Russia's Kaliningrad Oblast.

Geography

The lagoon is shallow, with an average depth of only 3.8 metres. Its northern part belongs to Lithuania and its southern part to Russia. The lagoon connects to the Baltic Sea through the narrow Klaipėda Strait at its northern end, near the port city of Klaipėda. Because most of its inflow is fresh river water while the outflow is a narrow strait, the lagoon's water is nearly fresh in the south and only slightly brackish near the outlet.

Ecology

The lagoon is an important habitat for fish and migratory birds. Eutrophication caused by nutrient runoff, including summer algal blooms, has been a persistent environmental concern. The surrounding region includes the Curonian Spit National Park in Lithuania and its Russian counterpart, both protecting the unique dune landscape.
