Southern theater of the American Revolutionary War

The southern theater of the American Revolutionary War covers operations in the Southern colonies — Virginia, North and South Carolina, and Georgia — together with related actions in Florida, the Gulf Coast, and the West Indies. After stalemate in the North, British strategy from 1778, the "Southern Strategy," sought to exploit supposed Loyalist strength in the South and use the region as a base to reconquer the colonies one by one.

Campaigns

British forces captured Savannah (1778) and Charleston (1780), the worst American defeat of the war, and won a crushing victory at Camden under Lord Cornwallis. Yet partisan leaders such as Francis Marion and Thomas Sumter kept resistance alive, and Patriot victories at King's Mountain (1780) and Cowpens (1781) blunted British momentum. Major General Nathanael Greene, commanding the Continental Southern Army, wore Cornwallis down through battles including Guilford Court House and Eutaw Springs — tactical defeats that cost Britain irreplaceable men.

Cornwallis marched into Virginia in 1781 and fortified Yorktown, where, trapped by Washington's army and the French fleet, he surrendered on 19 October 1781 — the decisive event that ended major fighting and led to peace recognizing American independence.
