Jaccard index

The Jaccard index, also known as the Jaccard similarity coefficient, is a statistic used for gauging the similarity and diversity of sample sets. It was developed by Grove Karl Gilbert in 1884 as the ratio of verification and popularised by Paul Jaccard. It measures the size of the intersection of two sets divided by the size of their union.

J(A, B) = |A ∩ B| / |A ∪ B|

Definition

For two finite sets A and B, the Jaccard index is the cardinality of their intersection divided by the cardinality of their union. It ranges from 0 (no elements in common) to 1 (the sets are identical). If both sets are empty, the index is conventionally defined as 1.

Jaccard distance

The Jaccard distance is defined as one minus the Jaccard index, and measures dissimilarity between sets. It is a metric on the collection of all finite sets.

Applications

The Jaccard index is widely used in ecology for comparing species diversity between sites, in information retrieval and text mining for measuring document similarity, in plagiarism detection, and in recommender systems. The related Tanimoto coefficient extends the idea to binary vectors and is common in cheminformatics.

Weighted and probabilistic variants

Generalisations include the weighted Jaccard similarity for multiset or non-negative vector data, and probabilistic variants used in locality-sensitive hashing schemes such as MinHash, which estimates Jaccard similarity from small sketches.
