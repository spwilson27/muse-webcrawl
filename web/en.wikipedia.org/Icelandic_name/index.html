Icelandic name

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Icelandic names follow a patronymic (and sometimes matronymic) system quite different from the family-name systems used in most Western countries. An Icelander's last name is not, in general, a surname shared by the family, but is formed from the given name of a parent: a son of Jón takes the name Jónsson ("Jón's son"), and a daughter takes Jónsdóttir ("Jón's daughter").

Formation

The patronymic is formed by adding -son (for a son) or -dóttir (for a daughter) to the genitive form of the parent's first name. Since 2019, following amendments to the Personal Names Act, the gender-neutral suffix -bur ("child") has also been available, and names are no longer restricted by the bearer's registered gender. Matronymics, formed from the mother's name, are equally legal and have become more common.

Given names and the Naming Committee

Given names not previously used in Iceland traditionally required approval by the Icelandic Naming Committee (Mannanafnanefnd), which maintains a register of approved names; reforms in 2019 abolished the committee's role for most purposes while retaining an official name register. A small minority of Icelanders bear inherited family names, usually of Danish or other foreign origin, which were permitted to be kept when the adoption of new family names was banned in 1925.

Usage

Icelanders are almost always addressed by their given name, even in formal settings; the telephone directory lists people by given name first. Brothers and sisters normally have different last names, and a husband and wife typically retain their own patronymics on marriage.
