Public bathing

Public bathing is the practice of bathing in communal facilities open to the public, a tradition found in many cultures from ancient Roman thermae and Byzantine baths to Ottoman hammams, Japanese sentō and onsen, Finnish saunas, Russian banyas and Korean jjimjilbang.

History

Communal baths served hygiene, health, religious and social functions. Roman baths were major civic institutions; Islamic hammams developed from them alongside ritual washing requirements. In nineteenth-century industrial cities, municipal bathhouses provided washing facilities for residents of tenements lacking plumbing, and served as important public-health measures.

Modern practice

With the spread of private bathrooms, public baths declined in many countries, though Japan's sentō, Finnish sauna culture and Middle Eastern hammams persist, often with a social or wellness emphasis. Customs regarding nudity, gender segregation and bathing etiquette vary widely between cultures.
