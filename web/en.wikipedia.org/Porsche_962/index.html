The Porsche 962 is a sports-prototype racing car designed and built by Porsche. Created to comply with IMSA's GTP regulations, the 962 was based on the successful Porsche 956 of Group C, modified with a longer wheelbase so that the driver's feet sat behind the front axle line as required by IMSA rules. Introduced in 1984, it became one of the most successful and long-lived racing cars of all time.

Racing history

The 962 debuted at the 1984 24 Hours of Daytona. In its IMSA GTP form (the 962) and its Group C form (the 962C), the car dominated international sports car racing through the mid-1980s. The 962 won the 24 Hours of Le Mans in 1986 and 1987 with the factory Rothmans Porsche team, and later gave Dauer's road-car-based version another Le Mans win in 1994. In North America it won multiple IMSA championships and the 24 Hours of Daytona several times, while in Japan it was a mainstay of the All Japan Sports Prototype Championship.

Design

The car used an aluminium monocoque chassis and a twin-turbocharged flat-six engine derived from the 956, producing around 630–700 horsepower depending on specification. Its ground-effect aerodynamics and proven reliability made it the default choice for privateer teams worldwide; many chassis were built and sold to customers, and private teams developed their own evolutions, extending the car's competitive life into the early 1990s.

Legacy

Alongside the 956, the 962 won dozens of championships and hundreds of races, and it remains a cornerstone of Porsche's motorsport heritage, frequently appearing in historic racing events.
