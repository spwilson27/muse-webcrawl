Dodge Caravan

The Dodge Caravan is a series of minivans produced by Chrysler (later FCA US) from the 1984 to 2020 model years. Together with its Plymouth Voyager and Chrysler Town & Country siblings, it effectively created the modern minivan segment.

History

Introduced in November 1983 under Lee Iacocca's leadership, the original Caravan was based on the front-wheel-drive K-car platform and was small enough to fit in a standard garage while seating seven. It became an enormous sales success and a defining family vehicle of the 1980s and 1990s.

Successive generations added innovations such as sliding doors on both sides, all-wheel drive, and the Stow 'n Go seating system (2005), whose seats folded flat into the floor. The extended-wheelbase version was sold as the Grand Caravan, the name under which the model continued alone from 2008 until production ended in 2020.
