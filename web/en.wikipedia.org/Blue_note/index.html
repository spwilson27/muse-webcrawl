Blue note

In jazz and blues, blue notes (also known as "worried notes") are notes that are sung or played at a slightly lower pitch than standard for expressive effect. Typically the alteration applies to the third, fifth, and seventh degrees of the major scale, producing the characteristic sound of the blues.

Characteristics

Blue notes are not fixed pitches: singers and players of flexible-pitch instruments bend and inflect them between the minor and major third, or between the perfect fifth and the flattened fifth. On fixed-pitch instruments such as the piano, the effect is approximated by playing the flattened and natural forms together or in quick succession. The blue third, fifth, and seventh underlie the blues scale and the twelve-bar blues.

Origins

The practice derives from African-American musical traditions, in which African scales and vocal inflections met European harmony and instruments in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. Blue notes became foundational to blues, jazz, rhythm and blues, and rock and roll, and the term gave its name to the Blue Note record label and jazz club.
