Division ring

In algebra, a division ring, also called a skew field, is a nontrivial ring in which division by nonzero elements is always possible. Specifically, it is a ring such that every nonzero element has a multiplicative inverse. Equivalently, a division ring is a ring whose nonzero elements form a group under multiplication. A division ring that is also commutative is simply a field. Much of the theory of linear algebra over fields can be carried over to division rings, although care is needed because multiplication need not commute.

Definition

A division ring is a set D equipped with two binary operations, addition and multiplication, forming a ring with identity 1 ≠ 0, in which every nonzero element a has an element a−1 such that a·a−1 = a−1·a = 1. The term "skew field" emphasises the possible failure of commutativity of multiplication.

Examples

The best-known example of a noncommutative division ring is the ring of quaternions, discovered by William Rowan Hamilton in 1843. Rational functions and formal Laurent series over a division ring provide other examples: the field of rational functions with coefficients in a division ring, and twisted polynomial and Laurent series rings, generate further division rings. Finite-dimensional division algebras over the real numbers are classified by the Frobenius theorem: the only ones are the real numbers, the complex numbers and the quaternions.

Wedderburn's little theorem

A celebrated result, Wedderburn's little theorem (proved in 1905), states that every finite division ring is commutative, hence a finite field. Thus noncommutative division rings must be infinite. The theorem was an early landmark of modern algebra, and dozens of proofs have since been published.

Properties

Division rings have no nonzero zero divisors, and every finitely generated module over a division ring is free, with a well-defined rank; consequently, linear algebra—including determinants (via the Dieudonné determinant), eigenvalues and systems of linear equations—has analogues over division rings, though the theory is subtler than over fields. The centre of a division ring is a field, and division rings figure prominently in the study of central simple algebras and the Brauer group.
