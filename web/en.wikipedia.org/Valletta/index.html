Valletta is the capital city of Malta, located on the main island between the Grand Harbour to the east and Marsamxett Harbour to the west. It is one of the smallest national capitals in the European Union by area and population, yet contains one of the densest concentrations of historic monuments in the world: UNESCO, which inscribed the whole city as a World Heritage Site in 1980, counts some 320 monuments within its 55 hectares. Valletta was European Capital of Culture in 2018.

History

After the Great Siege of Malta in 1565, in which the Knights of St John and the Maltese repelled an Ottoman invasion, Grand Master Jean Parisot de Valette resolved to build a new fortified city on the Sceberras peninsula. The foundation stone was laid on 28 March 1566. Designed by the Italian military engineer Francesco Laparelli on a strict gridiron plan — remarkable for incorporating building regulations and an early municipal drainage system — the city was substantially complete by the 1570s and named for Valette, who died in 1568 and is buried in St John's Co-Cathedral.

Under the Knights, Valletta flourished as a naval and commercial port, acquiring the palaces (auberges) of the Order's langues, churches and the Sacra Infermeria hospital. Napoleon seized Malta in 1798; after a Maltese uprising and blockade, the British took over in 1800 and Malta became a key imperial naval base. During the Second World War, Valletta and its harbours endured one of the heaviest bombing campaigns of the war during the second Siege of Malta (1940–42), for which King George VI awarded the island the George Cross.

Geography and cityscape

The city sits on a narrow, sloping ridge; its streets are stepped in places and its skyline is dominated by church domes and spires. Fort Saint Elmo guards the seaward tip, and the ring of bastions built by the Knights still encloses the city, which is entered through the modern City Gate (designed by Renzo Piano, completed 2014, alongside his Parliament House and open-air theatre).

Main sights

  • St John's Co-Cathedral (1572–77) — austere outside, sumptuously Baroque within, housing Caravaggio's The Beheading of Saint John the Baptist (1608), his largest painting;
  • Grandmaster's Palace — seat of the President of Malta and home to the Palace Armoury and Gobelins tapestries;
  • Upper Barrakka Gardens — terraced gardens overlooking the Grand Harbour, with the Saluting Battery;
  • National Museum of Archaeology — with artefacts from Malta's prehistoric temples;
  • Fort Saint Elmo and the National War Museum.

Government and culture

Valletta hosts Malta's parliament, courts, ministries and the offices of the President and Prime Minister (the latter at the Auberge de Castille). It is the island's commercial and administrative hub, and most of its daytime population commutes in from the surrounding conurbation. The city stages the Malta International Arts Festival, the Valletta Baroque Festival and the carnival celebrations, among many events.
