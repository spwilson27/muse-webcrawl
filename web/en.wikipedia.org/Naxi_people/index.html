Naxi people

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Naxi (Naxi: Naqxi; Chinese: 纳西族) are an ethnic group native to the foothills of the Himalayas in northwestern Yunnan province, China, principally around the city of Lijiang. Numbering roughly 300,000–350,000, they are recognized as one of the 56 official ethnic groups of China. The closely related Mosuo are regarded by some as a subgroup of the Naxi.

Culture and religion

Naxi traditional religion is Dongba, an animist and shamanistic system whose priest-scholars (dongba) recite and write ritual texts in the Dongba script—one of the few pictographic writing systems still in use, supplemented by the syllabic Geba script. The Naxi language belongs to the Tibeto-Burman family. Dongba manuscripts and pictographs were inscribed on UNESCO's Memory of the World register in 2003.

Society

Historically the Naxi of Lijiang were ruled by the Mu family chieftains under the Chinese tusi system. Naxi society around Lijiang is broadly patrilineal, while the Mosuo communities near Lugu Lake are famous for matrilineal households and "walking marriages".

Lijiang

The Old Town of Lijiang, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1997, preserves Naxi architecture of timber-framed houses, canals and bridges, and is a major centre of Naxi music, including the Dongjing and Baisha xiyue ensembles performed by elderly Naxi musicians.
