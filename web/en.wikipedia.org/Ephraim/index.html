Ephraim (Hebrew: אֶפְרַיִם, ʾEp̄rayim) is a figure in the Hebrew Bible, the second son of Joseph and Asenath, and the younger brother of Manasseh. He was born in Egypt before the years of famine. The name is traditionally connected with the Hebrew root for fruitfulness: in Genesis, Joseph says "God has made me fruitful in the land of my affliction".

The blessing of Jacob

In Genesis 48, the dying Jacob adopts Ephraim and Manasseh as his own sons, giving them status as founders of tribes. When Joseph presents the boys — Manasseh the firstborn at Jacob's right hand and Ephraim at his left — Jacob crosses his hands, placing his right hand on Ephraim's head. Despite Joseph's protest, Jacob insists that the younger brother will be the greater: "his offspring shall become a multitude of nations." This reversal of primogeniture is a recurring biblical theme.

Tribe of Ephraim

The Tribe of Ephraim, descended from Ephraim, received territory in the central hill country of Canaan, including Shiloh, which housed the Tabernacle, and later Shechem. Ephraim became the leading tribe of the northern Kingdom of Israel after the division of the monarchy — Jeroboam I, the northern kingdom's first king, was an Ephraimite — and the prophets Hosea, Isaiah and Jeremiah often use "Ephraim" as a synonym for the northern kingdom itself.

Later usage

Ephraim persisted as a Hebrew personal name. In some later religious traditions, notably the Latter Day Saint movement, Ephraim has been given symbolic significance in connection with the gathering of Israel.
