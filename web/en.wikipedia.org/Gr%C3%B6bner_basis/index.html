Gröbner basis

In mathematics, and more specifically in computer algebra, computational algebraic geometry, and computational commutative algebra, a Gröbner basis is a particular kind of generating set of an ideal in a polynomial ring over a field. Gröbner bases were introduced by Bruno Buchberger in his 1965 Ph.D. thesis, and are named after his advisor, Wolfgang Gröbner.

Definition

Given a monomial ordering on the multivariate polynomial ring, the leading term of each polynomial in an ideal generates the leading term ideal. A finite subset of the ideal is a Gröbner basis if the leading terms of its elements generate the whole leading term ideal. Equivalently, division by a Gröbner basis yields a unique remainder for every polynomial, which turns ideal membership and many other questions into mechanical computations.

Buchberger's algorithm

Buchberger's algorithm computes a Gröbner basis from any generating set by repeatedly forming S-polynomials of pairs of polynomials and reducing them; nonzero remainders are added to the basis until all S-polynomials reduce to zero. Later algorithms, such as Faugère's F4 and F5, greatly improved efficiency. A Gröbner basis can be made reduced and unique for a given ideal and monomial order.

Applications

Gröbner bases are used to solve systems of polynomial equations, to compute elimination ideals (implicitization), to test ideal membership and equality, in robotics (inverse kinematics), coding theory, integer programming, and automated geometric theorem proving. They are implemented in computer algebra systems such as Macaulay2, Singular, Magma, and Mathematica.
