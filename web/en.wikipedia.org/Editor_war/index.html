Editor war

The editor war is the rivalry between users of the Emacs and vi (now usually Vim) text editors. The rivalry has become a lasting part of hacker culture and the free software community, spawning countless debates, jokes, and satirical works since the 1980s.

Background

Both editors date to 1976: vi was written by Bill Joy, and Emacs by Richard Stallman, who became the most prominent champion of the Emacs side. Arguments concern their opposing design philosophies: vi's modal, keyboard-efficient editing versus Emacs's extensibility — Emacs is famously described as "a great operating system, only lacking a decent editor", while vi users mock Emacs's size ("Eight Megabytes And Constantly Swapping"). Stallman jokingly founded the parody "Church of Emacs" and portrayed himself as St IGNUcius.

Usage

The term "editor war" has been generalised to similar holy wars in computing, such as tabs versus spaces or browser wars, and borrowed in other contexts — including disputes over Wikipedia articles described as "edit wars".
