The SCMaglev (superconducting magnetic levitation) is a magnetic levitation railway system developed in Japan by the Central Japan Railway Company (JR Central) and the Railway Technical Research Institute. Trains levitate about 10 centimetres above the guideway using superconducting magnets, eliminating wheel friction.

Technology

Superconducting magnets mounted on the train's bogies interact with propulsion and levitation coils in the guideway walls: the same coil system lifts, propels and guides the vehicle. Onboard superconducting magnets are cooled by refrigerators to maintain their state, an approach pioneered in Japan from the 1970s ML series test vehicles onward.

Records and the Chūō Shinkansen

On the Yamanashi test track, a seven-car L0 series train reached 603 km/h in April 2015, a world speed record for a crewed railway vehicle. JR Central is building the Chūō Shinkansen using SCMaglev technology, initially between Tokyo (Shinagawa) and Nagoya, with a planned operating speed of 500 km/h; most of the route runs through deep tunnels under the Japanese Alps.
