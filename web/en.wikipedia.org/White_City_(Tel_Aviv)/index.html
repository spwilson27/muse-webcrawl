White City (Tel Aviv)

White City
Location: Tel Aviv, Israel
UNESCO World Heritage Site: inscribed 2003
Notable style: Bauhaus / International Style
Buildings: over 4,000

The White City (Hebrew: העיר הלבנה) is a collection of over 4,000 buildings in Tel Aviv, Israel, built in the 1930s and 1940s in the Bauhaus and International styles. In 2003, UNESCO proclaimed the White City a World Cultural Heritage site, describing it as "an outstanding example of new town planning and architecture in the early 20th century".

Architecture

Many of the architects were German Jews who had studied at the Bauhaus before fleeing Nazi Germany. The buildings are characterized by white plaster facades, pilotis (stilts), flat roofs, ribbon windows, curved balconies and asymmetrical compositions, adapted to the local climate with small windows to reduce heat and glare. Architect Patrick Geddes laid out an early town plan for Tel Aviv in the 1920s, and architects such as Arieh Sharon, Dov Karmi and Ze'ev Rechter shaped the city's modernist fabric.

Preservation

After decades of neglect, municipal preservation plans adopted in the early 2000s protected some 1,000 buildings. The Bauhaus Center Tel Aviv and the Bauhaus Museum promote the heritage of the area, which remains one of the largest concentrations of International Style architecture in the world.
