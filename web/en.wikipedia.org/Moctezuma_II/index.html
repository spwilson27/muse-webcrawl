Moctezuma II

Moctezuma II (Classical Nahuatl: Motēuczōma Xōcoyōtzin; c. 1466 – 29 June 1520), also spelled Motecuhzoma, Montezuma, and Moteuczoma, was the ninth Aztec emperor (tlatoani) of Tenochtitlan, reigning from 1502 or 1503 to 1520. Through his decades of warfare, Moctezuma expanded the territory controlled by the Aztec Triple Alliance to its maximum extent.

During his reign, the Aztec Empire attained its greatest size and expansion. Through warfare, Moctezuma expanded the empire southwards to Xoconosco in Chiapas and the Isthmus of Tehuantepec, and incorporated the Zapotec and Yopi people into the empire. He changed the previous meritocratic system of social hierarchy and widened the divide between the nobles (pipiltin) and commoners (macehualtin) by prohibiting commoners from working in the royal palaces.

Contact with the Spanish

In 1519, Hernán Cortés landed on the Gulf Coast and marched inland. Moctezuma received the Spanish in Tenochtitlan in November 1519, but relations deteriorated; Cortés took Moctezuma prisoner in his own palace. After the massacre at the Great Temple by Pedro de Alvarado's men in May 1520, the city rose in revolt.

Death

Moctezuma died in June 1520 during the Spanish retreat from Tenochtitlan. Spanish accounts claim he was stoned by his own people while trying to address them; Aztec accounts claim the Spanish killed him. The circumstances remain disputed. He was succeeded briefly by his brother Cuitláhuac and then by his nephew Cuauhtémoc, the last independent tlatoani.

Legacy

Moctezuma's name and image are widespread in Mexico and beyond: the Palace of Montezuma, the former imperial residence, and the phrase "Montezuma's revenge" for traveller's diarrhoea. Portrayals in popular culture range from the operas of Vivaldi and Graun to modern novels and games, often reflecting longstanding debates about his conduct during the conquest.
