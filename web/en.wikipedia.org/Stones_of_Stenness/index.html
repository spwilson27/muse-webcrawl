Stones of Stenness

LocationMainland, Orkney, Scotland
TypeStone circle / henge
DateNeolithic, c. 3100 BC
StatusPart of the Heart of Neolithic Orkney World Heritage Site

The Stones of Stenness are a Neolithic henge monument in Orkney, Scotland, standing on a promontory between the Loch of Stenness and the Loch of Harray. They are among the oldest stone circles in Britain, dating to around 3100 BC, and form part of the Heart of Neolithic Orkney UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Description

The circle originally comprised up to twelve tall standing stones arranged in an ellipse within a rock-cut ditch. Only four stones survive upright, the tallest nearly six metres high, together with a central hearth-like setting. Nearby stand the Barnhouse settlement and the Watch Stone.

History

In 1814 the landowner attempted to remove the stones, destroying the so-called Odin Stone, a holed stone prominent in local folklore; public outcry halted the destruction and the site passed into state care. Excavations in the twentieth century confirmed the ditch and ceremonial character of the monument.
