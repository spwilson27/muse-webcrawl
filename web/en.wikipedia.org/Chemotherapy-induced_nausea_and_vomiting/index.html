Chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting

Chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting (CINV) is a common and distressing side effect of cancer chemotherapy. It is classified as acute (within 24 hours), delayed (24–120 hours), anticipatory, breakthrough, or refractory.

Risk depends on the emetogenicity of the regimen — highly emetogenic agents include cisplatin and anthracycline-cyclophosphamide combinations — and patient factors such as prior CINV, motion sickness, and anxiety.

Modern prophylaxis combines drug classes targeting different pathways: 5-HT3 receptor antagonists (ondansetron), NK1 receptor antagonists (aprepitant), dexamethasone, and olanzapine. Guidelines from ASCO, NCCN and MASCC recommend multi-drug prophylaxis for high-risk regimens, which has greatly improved control.
