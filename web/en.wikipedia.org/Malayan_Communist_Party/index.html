Malayan Communist Party

The Malayan Communist Party (MCP), also known as the Communist Party of Malaya, was a political party in British Malaya and later Malaysia and Singapore. Founded in 1930, it led the anti-Japanese resistance during the Second World War and later fought a long insurgency against the British and then the Malaysian government.

History

The party emerged from earlier communist movements among Chinese communities in Malaya. During the Japanese occupation its armed wing, the Malayan Peoples' Anti-Japanese Army, cooperated with the British. After 1945 relations with the colonial authorities collapsed, and in 1948 the party turned to armed struggle, beginning the Malayan Emergency (1948–1960).

Insurgency and end

Under leader Chin Peng, the party's guerrilla army operated from jungle bases, later retreating to the Thai border. A second insurgency continued after 1968. The MCP finally laid down its arms under the peace agreement signed at Hat Yai, Thailand, in 1989, and the party was dissolved.
