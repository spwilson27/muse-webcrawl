Parícutin is a cinder cone volcano in the Mexican state of Michoacán, famous as the first volcano whose entire life cycle — birth, growth and extinction — was witnessed and documented by scientists. It grew out of a cornfield in 1943 and erupted continuously for nine years before falling silent in 1952.

The eruption

On 20 February 1943 the farmer Dionisio Pulido saw a fissure open in his cornfield, accompanied by rumbling, sulphurous smoke and explosions of ash and incandescent bombs. Within a day the cone was 50 metres high; within a week, more than 100 metres. Over its first year it reached about 336 metres, and it eventually stood 424 metres above the plain. Lava flows buried the villages of Parícutin and San Juan Parangaricutiro, leaving only the church tower and altar of San Juan rising above the hardened lava — an iconic image of the eruption. Remarkably, there were no direct deaths, and the inhabitants were evacuated and resettled.

Legacy

Parícutin is one of more than 1,400 volcanic vents in the Michoacán–Guanajuato volcanic field. It has been named one of the natural wonders of the world in some lists, and it transformed volcanology by showing how quickly a monogenetic volcano can be born.
