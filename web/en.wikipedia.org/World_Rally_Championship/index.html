The World Rally Championship (abbreviated as WRC) is an international rallying series organised by the FIA, culminating with a champion driver, co-driver and manufacturer. The series began in 1973 and consists of rallies held on surfaces ranging from gravel and asphalt to snow and ice, in countries across several continents.

Format

Each rally is run over several days on closed-road "special stages", where crews drive against the clock, linked by public-road sections. Drivers rely on pace notes read by their co-driver. Famous events include the Monte Carlo Rally, Rally Finland and the Safari Rally in Kenya. World champions are decided by points accumulated across the season's events, typically 13–14 rallies.

History

The championship was initially for manufacturers only; a drivers' title followed in 1979. The Group B era of the mid-1980s produced spectacular but dangerous supercars and was cancelled after fatal accidents. Frenchman Sébastien Loeb won a record nine consecutive drivers' titles (2004–2012) with Citroën; Sébastien Ogier later won eight. Since 2022 the premier class has used Rally1 cars with plug-in hybrid powertrains.
