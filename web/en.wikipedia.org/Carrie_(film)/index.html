Carrie is a 1976 American supernatural horror film directed by Brian De Palma from a screenplay by Lawrence D. Cohen, adapted from Stephen King's 1974 epistolary novel of the same name. It was the first screen adaptation of a King work. The film stars Sissy Spacek as Carrie White, a shy teenage girl abused by her fanatically religious mother and bullied by classmates, who discovers she has telekinetic powers.

Plot

Carrie White is a timid 16-year-old raised by her domineering, religiously obsessed mother, Margaret. After classmates humiliate her in the school showers, one of them, Sue Snell, repents and persuades her boyfriend Tommy Ross to invite Carrie to the prom. Another student, Chris Hargensen, seeks revenge by arranging for Carrie to be crowned prom queen and then drenched in a bucket of pig's blood. The prank triggers Carrie's telekinetic powers; she seals the gym doors and kills many of the students and teachers inside before returning home, where she kills her mother and dies as the house collapses and burns.

Reception and legacy

The film was a critical and commercial success, grossing over $33 million against a budget of about $1.8 million. Spacek and Laurie both received Academy Award nominations. Carrie is widely considered one of the greatest horror films ever made; its prom sequence is among the most famous in cinema. It spawned a sequel, The Rage: Carrie 2 (1999), a 2002 television adaptation, and a 2013 remake.
