Polynesian languages

The Polynesian languages form a genealogical group of about 38 languages within the Oceanic branch of the Austronesian language family. They are spoken across the Polynesian Triangle in the Pacific Ocean — from Hawaiʻi in the north to Rapa Nui (Easter Island) in the east and New Zealand in the southwest — as well as in Polynesian outlier communities in Melanesia and Micronesia.

Classification

The family divides principally into a Tongic branch (Tongan and Niuean) and Nuclear Polynesian, from which Samoan, the Ellicean languages, and Eastern Polynesian descend. Eastern Polynesian includes Tahitian, Māori, Hawaiian, Rapa Nui, and the Marquesan languages. The languages are closely related, reflecting settlement of the region within roughly the last two to three thousand years by voyaging peoples ancestral to modern Polynesians.

Characteristics

Polynesian languages are noted for small phoneme inventories — Hawaiian has only eight consonants — open syllables, and largely analytic grammar. Many are endangered, though revitalisation movements for Hawaiian and te reo Māori have had notable success. See also Austronesian languages and Polynesia.
