Reed–Solomon error correction

Reed–Solomon codes are a group of error-correcting codes introduced by Irving S. Reed and Gustave Solomon in 1960. They are block codes over finite fields that add redundant symbols to data, enabling a decoder to detect and correct multiple symbol errors, and they are especially effective against burst errors.

Applications

Reed–Solomon correction is used in compact discs and DVDs, QR codes, digital television and DSL transmission, satellite communications such as the Voyager missions' deep-space links, and in storage systems such as RAID 6. The codes used on CDs can conceal scratches thousands of bits long through cross-interleaving.

Principle

Data is treated as coefficients of a polynomial over a finite field and evaluated at more points than are strictly needed; the redundancy allows the original polynomial — and thus the data — to be recovered even when some values are corrupted. Practical decoders commonly use the Berlekamp–Massey or Euclidean algorithms.
