Lockdown

A deserted street during a pandemic lockdown

A lockdown is a restriction policy for people or a community to stay where they are, usually due to specific risks, such as a public health emergency or a security threat, that would be worsened by free movement. The term is used for a variety of protocols, from prison security measures to emergency measures imposed by governments during epidemics and disasters.

Prison lockdowns

In correctional facilities, a lockdown is a period in which prisoners are confined to their cells, typically in response to disturbances, violence, or the need to conduct a search or restore order. Movement, visits, work and programmes are suspended for the duration.

Emergency and school lockdowns

Schools and workplaces use lockdown procedures in response to an active threat, such as an armed intruder. Occupants secure doors, turn off lights, and remain silent and out of sight until an all-clear is given. Lockdown drills are a routine part of safety training in many countries.

Pandemic lockdowns

During the COVID-19 pandemic, governments around the world imposed lockdowns on an unprecedented scale beginning in early 2020. Measures included stay-at-home orders, closure of non-essential businesses and schools, restrictions on gatherings and travel, and quarantine requirements. These measures were intended to reduce transmission of SARS-CoV-2 and prevent health systems from being overwhelmed. Their economic, social and mental-health consequences were extensive and remain widely debated.
