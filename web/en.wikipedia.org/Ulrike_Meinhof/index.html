Ulrike Meinhof

Ulrike Marie Meinhof (7 October 1934 – 9 May 1976) was a German left-wing journalist and founding member of the Red Army Faction (RAF), the West German far-left militant organization often called the Baader–Meinhof Group.

Meinhof worked as a journalist for the left-wing magazine konkret, becoming its editor-in-chief, and was a prominent voice of the 1960s student protest movement. After the shooting of student Benno Ohnesorg in 1967 and the attempted assassination of Rudi Dutschke in 1968, she moved toward militancy. In May 1970 she helped free the imprisoned Andreas Baader, an act that launched the RAF and drove her underground.

She authored many of the group's communiqués, including its 1971 manifesto "The Concept of the Urban Guerrilla," while the RAF carried out bombings, robberies, and kidnappings. Meinhof was arrested in 1972 and put on trial at Stammheim Prison in Stuttgart. She was found hanged in her cell on 9 May 1976, before the trial concluded; her death was ruled a suicide, though supporters alleged murder. Her brain was removed without her family's consent for research and was not returned and buried until 2002.
