Cisco Systems, Inc., doing business as Cisco, is an American multinational digital communications technology conglomerate headquartered in San Jose, California. Cisco develops, manufactures, and sells networking hardware, software, telecommunications equipment, and other high-technology services and products. It is one of the largest technology companies in the world and has long been a dominant supplier of the routers and switches that form the backbone of the Internet and corporate networks.

History

Cisco was founded in 1984 by Leonard Bosack and Sandy Lerner, two Stanford University computer scientists who had pioneered the local area network (LAN) concept at Stanford. The company's first product was the Advanced Gateway Server (AGS), a router based on software developed at Stanford. The name "Cisco" derives from San Francisco, and its early logo depicts the Golden Gate Bridge. Cisco went public on February 16, 1990, with a market capitalization of $224 million. Under chief executive John Chambers (1995–2015), Cisco grew aggressively through acquisitions, purchasing dozens of companies during the dot-com boom. At the peak of the boom in March 2000, Cisco briefly became the most valuable company in the world by market capitalization.

Products and services

Cisco's portfolio includes enterprise and service-provider routing and switching (including the Catalyst and Nexus switch lines), network security products, data center products, collaboration tools such as Webex, and cloud-managed networking through its Meraki division. In 2023 Cisco announced its acquisition of the observability company Splunk for approximately $28 billion, its largest acquisition ever, completed in March 2024.

Certifications

Cisco is also known for its professional certification program, including the CCNA (Cisco Certified Network Associate) and CCNP credentials, which are widely recognized standards for networking careers.
