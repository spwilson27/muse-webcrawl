Video game developer

A video game developer is a person or company that creates video games, handling some or all of the design, programming, art, sound, and testing involved. Developers range from single individuals ("solo devs") and small independent teams to studio divisions of major publishers employing thousands of people on one title.

Categories

First-party developers are owned by a platform holder (such as Nintendo, Sony, or Microsoft) and make games, often exclusively, for that owner's hardware. Second-party describes independent studios that work mainly or exclusively with one platform under contract. Third-party developers are independent of any platform holder and may publish their own games or work with a separate publisher. Independent ("indie") developers typically self-fund or crowdfund smaller titles and self-publish, often through digital storefronts; the indie sector grew enormously after digital distribution lowered barriers in the late 2000s.

Roles and work

Game development is multidisciplinary. Designers define rules, mechanics, and levels; programmers build the game engine code and gameplay systems; artists produce 2D and 3D assets and animation; composers and sound designers create music and audio; producers coordinate schedule and budget; and quality-assurance testers find defects. The industry is known for "crunch" — periods of extended overtime near deadlines — which has prompted ongoing debate and unionisation efforts.

Business

Developers are usually funded by publishers through milestone-based contracts, by platform holders, or increasingly by crowdfunding, early access sales, and subscription and live-service revenue. Major development hubs include the United States, Japan, Canada, the United Kingdom, and increasingly China and Poland. Notable developers historically include Nintendo, Atari, id Software, Valve, Naughty Dog, and CD Projekt Red.
