Benzopyrene

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Benzopyrene is a polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon with the formula C20H12. It consists of a pyrene molecule fused to an additional benzene ring. Two isomers are commonly distinguished according to the position of the fused ring:

  • Benzo[a]pyrene — the better known and far more carcinogenic isomer, found in coal tar and combustion products.
  • Benzo[e]pyrene — a less common isomer, much less studied and considerably less potent as a carcinogen.

Properties

The benzopyrenes are yellowish, crystalline solids with low solubility in water but good solubility in organic solvents and fats. Like other PAHs they are formed during the incomplete combustion of organic material and are widely distributed environmental contaminants, occurring in soot, exhaust gases, cigarette smoke and grilled foods.

Benzo[a]pyrene is metabolized in the liver to a reactive diol epoxide that binds to DNA and is used as a benchmark compound in toxicology; the toxicity of other PAHs is often expressed relative to it using toxic equivalency factors.
