Sacramento is the capital city of the U.S. state of California and the seat of Sacramento County. Located at the confluence of the Sacramento River and the American River in Northern California's Sacramento Valley, it is the sixth-most populous city in California and the ninth-largest state capital in the United States, with a population of 524,669 at the 2020 census.

History

The Nisenan and Plains Miwok peoples inhabited the region for thousands of years before Spanish and Mexican rule. In 1839 Swiss immigrant John Sutter established the agricultural colony of New Helvetia, centered on Sutter's Fort. After gold was discovered at Sutter's Mill in nearby Coloma in 1848, Sutter's son laid out the city of Sacramento, which grew explosively as a supply center and river port during the California Gold Rush. Sacramento was incorporated in 1850 and became the state capital in 1854.

The city was the western terminus of the First Transcontinental Railroad: in 1863 the Central Pacific Railroad began building eastward from Sacramento, and Theodore Judah, Leland Stanford, Collis Huntington, Mark Hopkins, and Charles Crocker — the "Big Four" — drove its construction. Sacramento was also the western end of the Pony Express.

Geography and climate

Sacramento has a Mediterranean climate with hot, dry summers and mild, wet winters. The city is prone to river flooding and is protected by an extensive levee system. Its mature urban tree canopy has earned it the nickname "City of Trees."

Government and economy

As the state capital, Sacramento hosts the California State Capitol, the Governor's office, and the state legislature. Government, healthcare, education, and food processing are major employers. Old Sacramento, a historic district of Gold Rush-era buildings along the riverfront, is a major tourist attraction. The city's NBA team is the Sacramento Kings.
