Garonne

The Garonne is a major river of southwestern France and northern Spain, about 529 kilometres (329 miles) long. It rises in the Spanish Pyrenees, in the Val d'Aran of Catalonia, flows north into France past Toulouse, Agen, and Bordeaux, and joins the Dordogne to form the Gironde estuary, which empties into the Atlantic Ocean in the Bay of Biscay.

Course

The Garonne's source is traditionally placed on the Pla de Beret in the Aran valley, though its farthest headwater is the Ruda-Garona stream on the slopes of the Aneto massif area. In France it passes Saint-Gaudens, then Toulouse, where it is met by canals linking it to the Mediterranean (the Canal du Midi joins the canalised river system at Toulouse). Its principal tributaries include the Ariège, Tarn, Lot, Save, and Gers. Below Bordeaux the river is tidal and broad, and its confluence with the Dordogne forms the Gironde, the largest estuary in western Europe.

Significance

The Garonne valley is an important agricultural region, known for vineyards around Bordeaux and for fruit and cereal farming in its fertile plain. The river has a history of severe flooding, notably in 1875, 1930, and 1952. It is also home to the last French population of the European sturgeon and supports migratory fish such as shad and salmon, with conservation measures to restore their passage past dams and weirs.
