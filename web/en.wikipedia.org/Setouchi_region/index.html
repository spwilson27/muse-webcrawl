The Setouchi region (瀬戸内地方, Setouchi-chihō) is a geographical region of western Japan comprising the coastal areas and islands surrounding the Seto Inland Sea. It spans parts of the Chūgoku, Shikoku, Kansai and Kyushu regions, facing one of Japan's most important historic waterways, dotted with some 700 islands.

Geography and climate

The Inland Sea is sheltered by the mountains of Shikoku and Chūgoku, giving Setouchi a mild, relatively dry "Setouchi climate" with low rainfall and many sunny days — conditions suited to citrus farming (notably Ehime's mikan), olives on Shōdoshima, and vineyards. Major ports and industrial cities include Hiroshima, Kure (famous for shipbuilding and the battleship Yamato), Okayama, Takamatsu and Matsuyama. The region is linked to Shikoku by three bridge systems, including the Great Seto Bridge and the Shimanami Kaidō cycle route.

Culture and economy

Historically a maritime corridor between Kyushu, Kansai and the capital, the Inland Sea carried trade, pilgrims to Shikoku's 88 temples, and the fleets of medieval naval clans (suigun). The area suffered severe industrial pollution in the postwar decades, prompting environmental legislation that made the Seto Inland Sea a model of clean-up efforts. Since 2010 the Setouchi Triennale art festival, centred on Naoshima and other "art islands", has become a major driver of tourism and rural revitalisation, drawing visitors to museums designed by architects such as Tadao Ando and art by Yayoi Kusama.
