Wałbrzych (German: Waldenburg) is a city in southwestern Poland, in the Lower Silesian Voivodeship, situated in the Central Sudetes near the Czech border. It was historically a center of coal mining and textile manufacturing.

History

The settlement developed in the Middle Ages under Piast rule and later passed to Bohemia and Prussia. As Waldenburg, it became an industrial city in the 19th century thanks to hard-coal mining and weaving. After the Second World War the city became part of Poland and its German population was expelled. Coal mining declined and the last mine closed in the 1990s, prompting economic restructuring.

Landmarks

The city's best-known landmark is Książ Castle, one of the largest castles in Poland, perched above the Pełcznica gorge a few kilometres north of the center. The Palm House in the Lubiechów district and the Old Mine Science and Culture Centre, built in a former colliery, are also popular attractions. In 2015 the city gained international attention over reports of a buried Nazi "gold train", which searches failed to locate.
