Lacrimal apparatus

The lacrimal apparatus is the physiological system containing the orbital structures for tear production and drainage. It consists of the lacrimal gland, which secretes tears; the lacrimal canaliculi, lacrimal sac, and nasolacrimal duct, which convey the fluid to the cavity of the nose; and accessory glands that contribute to the tear film.

Structure

The lacrimal gland is a compound tubuloacinar gland located in the upper lateral part of each orbit. Tears spread across the surface of the eye and drain through small openings called the lacrimal puncta at the inner corner of the eyelids, passing through the canaliculi into the lacrimal sac and then down the nasolacrimal duct into the inferior meatus of the nose — which is why crying makes the nose run.

Function

Tears lubricate the eye, wash away foreign material, and contain lysozyme and other antimicrobial components that protect against infection. Blinking spreads the tear film evenly over the cornea.

Clinical significance

Blockage of the nasolacrimal duct causes tearing (epiphora) and can lead to infection of the lacrimal sac (dacryocystitis). Insufficient tear production contributes to dry eye syndrome (keratoconjunctivitis sicca), notably in Sjögren syndrome.
