Federative units of Brazil

Brazil has 26 states and one federal district.

The federative units of Brazil (Portuguese: unidades federativas do Brasil) are subnational entities with a certain degree of autonomy — self-government, self-legislation, and self-collection of taxes — and endowed with their own government and constitution. Together they form the Federative Republic of Brazil. There are 27 federative units in total: the 26 states (estados) and the Federal District (Distrito Federal), which contains the capital city, Brasília.

Government

Each state has its own executive headed by a governor, a unicameral legislative assembly, and a judiciary. The Federal District combines characteristics of both a state and a municipality and is governed by a governor and a legislative chamber. Federative units are grouped into five geographic regions: North, Northeast, Central-West, Southeast, and South, used principally for statistical purposes.

History

The states derive largely from the captaincies created during Portuguese colonisation and the provinces of the Empire of Brazil; they became states with the proclamation of the republic in 1889. Newer states include Mato Grosso do Sul (1977), Rondônia (1981), and Tocantins (1988). The largest state by area is Amazonas, while São Paulo is the most populous and the country's economic powerhouse.
