Wolter telescope

A Wolter telescope is a telescope for X-rays that only uses grazing incidence optics — that is, light so energetic that it reflects only at very shallow angles. The design was proposed in 1952 by the German physicist Hans Wolter, who described three configurations, Types I, II, and III, each combining a paraboloid primary mirror with a hyperboloid secondary mirror.

Design

In the most commonly used Type I design, X-rays graze a concave paraboloid and then a convex hyperboloid, coming to a focus behind the mirrors. Because the mirrors work only at grazing angles of about one degree or less, the effective collecting area is small, so many nested mirror shells are combined to increase sensitivity.

Use in astronomy

Wolter optics made true imaging X-ray astronomy possible. Observatories using them include the Einstein Observatory, ROSAT, BeppoSAX, the Chandra X-ray Observatory, XMM-Newton, and the eROSITA instrument.
