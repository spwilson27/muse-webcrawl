Mercer's theorem

In mathematics, Mercer's theorem is a representation of a symmetric positive-definite function on a square as a sum of a convergent sequence of product functions. Introduced by James Mercer in 1909, the theorem serves as an infinite-dimensional analogue of the fact that a symmetric positive-definite matrix can be diagonalized by an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors.

Statement

Let K be a continuous symmetric positive-definite kernel on a compact domain. Then there exists an orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions of the associated integral operator such that K can be expanded as a uniformly and absolutely convergent series of products of these eigenfunctions weighted by their (non-negative) eigenvalues.

Applications

Mercer's theorem underlies the theory of reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces and justifies the "kernel trick" used in support vector machines and other kernel methods in machine learning, where it guarantees that a valid kernel corresponds to an inner product in some feature space.
