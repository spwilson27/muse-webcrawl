Boomslang

Scientific name: Dispholidus typus
Family: Colubridae
Range: Sub-Saharan Africa
Length: 100–170 cm

The boomslang (Dispholidus typus) is a highly venomous snake in the family Colubridae. The species is native to and widespread in sub-Saharan Africa. Its name means "tree snake" in Afrikaans (from boom, "tree", and slang, "snake").

Description

The average adult boomslang is 100–160 cm in total length, occasionally exceeding 183 cm. The head is distinct from the neck, with very large eyes and round pupils. Colouration is highly variable: males are usually light green with black or blue scale edges, while females may be brown.

Behaviour

Boomslangs are diurnal and largely arboreal, feeding on chameleons, frogs, lizards, small mammals, birds and eggs. They are shy and flee from anything too large to eat, but will bite if threatened.

Venom

The venom is a potent haemotoxin that disrupts blood coagulation, and bites can be fatal to humans if untreated. The herpetologist Karl Schmidt died in 1957 after a boomslang bite, famously documenting his symptoms as he died. A monovalent antivenom is produced in South Africa.
