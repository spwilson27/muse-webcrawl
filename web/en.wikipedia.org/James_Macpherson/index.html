James Macpherson

Born27 October 1736, Ruthven, Inverness-shire, Scotland
Died17 February 1796, London, England
Known forThe Ossian poems

James Macpherson (27 October 1736 – 17 February 1796) was a Scottish writer, poet, and politician, best known as the "translator" of the Ossian cycle of epic poems — works he claimed to have translated from ancient Scottish Gaelic sources but which he largely composed himself.

Ossian

In 1760 Macpherson published Fragments of Ancient Poetry, followed by the epics Fingal (1762) and Temora (1763), attributed to the legendary bard Ossian. The poems became a European sensation, influencing Goethe, Herder, and Napoleon, and inspiring the Romantic movement's fascination with Celtic and northern antiquity.

Controversy

Samuel Johnson and others demanded Macpherson produce his original manuscripts, which he never did. Later scholarship concluded the poems were Macpherson's own compositions based loosely on genuine Gaelic ballad fragments. The affair remains a famous case of literary forgery.

Later life

Macpherson prospered as a government pamphleteer and Member of Parliament for Camelford. He is buried in Westminster Abbey.
