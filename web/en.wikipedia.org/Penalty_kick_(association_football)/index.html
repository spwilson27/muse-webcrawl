Penalty kick (association football)


A penalty is taken from the penalty mark, 11 m from goal.

A penalty kick is a method of restarting play in association football, awarded when a player commits an offence punishable by a direct free kick inside their own penalty area. The kick is taken from the penalty mark, 11 metres (12 yards) from the goal line, with only the goalkeeper between the taker and the goal.

Procedure

The ball is placed on the penalty mark; the goalkeeper must remain on (or behind, in recent law interpretations, touching) the goal line until the kick is taken. Other players must be outside the penalty area and at least 9.15 m from the mark until the ball is kicked. The taker may feint during the run-up but not once it is completed. A goal scored directly counts; if the ball rebounds from the post or goalkeeper, play continues (except in a shootout).

History and shootouts

The penalty kick was introduced in 1891, championed by Irish goalkeeper and businessman William McCrum, replacing the earlier assumption that a gentleman would not deliberately foul. Penalty shootouts, adopted from around 1970 to decide drawn knockout matches, use the same mark but with no rebounds and a set series of kicks. Roughly three quarters of in-game penalties are scored.
