Emmett Till

Emmett Louis Till (July 25, 1941 – August 28, 1955) was a 14-year-old African American youth who was abducted, tortured, and lynched in Mississippi in 1955 after being accused of offending a white woman, Carolyn Bryant, in her family's grocery store. His murder galvanized the emerging civil rights movement.

Born: July 25, 1941, Chicago, Illinois, U.S.
Died: August 28, 1955 (aged 14), Drew, Mississippi, U.S.
Cause of death: Lynching
Mother: Mamie Till

Early life

Till was born and raised in Chicago, Illinois, the son of Mamie Carthan and Louis Till. In August 1955 he traveled to visit relatives near Money, Mississippi.

Murder and trial

On August 24, 1955, Till visited Bryant's Grocery and Meat Market. Four days later Roy Bryant and J. W. Milam abducted him, beat and mutilated him, shot him, and sank his body in the Tallahatchie River. An all-white jury acquitted both men; they later admitted the killing in a magazine interview, protected by double jeopardy.

Legacy

Mamie Till held an open-casket funeral in Chicago; photographs of her son's body published in Jet magazine drew national attention. The case is widely cited as a catalyst for the civil rights movement. In 2022 the United States enacted the Emmett Till Antilynching Act, and in 2023 a national monument was designated in his honor.
