Adams–Onís Treaty

Signed: 22 February 1819, Washington, D.C.
Effective: 22 February 1821
Parties: United States and Spain
Negotiators: John Quincy Adams and Luis de Onís

The Adams–Onís Treaty of 1819, also called the Transcontinental Treaty, the Florida Purchase Treaty, or the Florida Treaty, was an agreement between the United States and Spain that ceded Florida to the United States and defined the boundary between the United States and New Spain.

Terms

Spain ceded East Florida and renounced its claims to West Florida. The United States assumed up to $5 million in claims of its citizens against Spain. A boundary was drawn from the Sabine River along the Red and Arkansas rivers to the 42nd parallel and west to the Pacific Ocean, with Spain surrendering its claims to the Oregon Country while the United States recognized Spanish sovereignty over Texas.

Significance

Negotiated by Secretary of State John Quincy Adams and Spanish minister Luis de Onís, the treaty was one of the great diplomatic achievements of the early republic, giving the United States a transcontinental boundary claim to the Pacific.
