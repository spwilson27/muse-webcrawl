Arthur Surridge Hunt (1 March 1871 – 18 June 1934) was an English papyrologist and fellow of The Queen's College, Oxford. With his lifelong collaborator Bernard Pyne Grenfell, he excavated and published the vast cache of ancient manuscripts known as the Oxyrhynchus Papyri, transforming the study of the Greco-Roman world.

Career

Hunt studied at The Queen's College, Oxford, and under the Egypt Exploration Society began excavating with Grenfell at Oxyrhynchus (el-Bahnasa) in Egypt in 1896–97. From the ancient rubbish mounds they recovered hundreds of thousands of papyrus fragments — literary texts (including lost works of Sappho, Pindar, Sophocles and Menander), early Christian writings, and everyday documents such as letters, tax receipts and census returns. The pair edited the ongoing series The Oxyrhynchus Papyri (1898– ), one of the great enterprises of classical scholarship.

Later life

After Grenfell's declining health and death in 1926, Hunt continued the editorial work nearly alone. He became Oxford's first Professor of Papyrology in 1913 and was elected a Fellow of the British Academy. His eyesight failed in later years; a cataract operation in 1933 temporarily restored it. He died in Oxford in 1934. The duo's methods set the standard for the discipline of papyrology.
