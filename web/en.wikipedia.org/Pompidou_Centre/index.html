Pompidou Centre

Centre Pompidou
LocationBeaubourg, Paris, France
Opened31 January 1977
ArchitectsRenzo Piano, Richard Rogers, Gianfranco Franchini

The Centre Pompidou, more fully the Centre national d'art et de culture Georges-Pompidou, is a complex building in the Beaubourg area of the 4th arrondissement of Paris. It houses the Musée national d'art moderne, the largest museum for modern art in Europe, along with a vast public library and a centre for music and acoustic research (IRCAM).

Architecture

Designed by Renzo Piano, Richard Rogers, and Gianfranco Franchini, the building exemplifies high-tech architecture: its structural system, mechanical services, and circulation are exposed on the exterior, with brightly colour-coded pipes—blue for air, green for water, yellow for electricity, and red for movement. The escalator tube snakes up the west façade.

History

The centre was commissioned by President Georges Pompidou and opened in 1977 after his death. Initially controversial, it became one of Paris's most popular cultural destinations, drawing millions of visitors annually.
