The Central Board of Film Certification (CBFC), popularly known as the Censor Board, is a statutory film-certification body under the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting of the Government of India. It is tasked with regulating the public exhibition of films under the Cinematograph Act, 1952. No film may be publicly exhibited in India without CBFC certification; the Board examines films and may certify them (with or without cuts), restrict them to adult audiences, or refuse certification.

Certificates

CertificateMeaning
UUnrestricted public exhibition (suitable for all ages)
U/AUnrestricted, but parental guidance for children (subdivided in later reforms into U/A 7+, 13+, 16+)
ARestricted to adults (18+)
SRestricted to specialized audiences (e.g., doctors, scientists)

History and structure

Film censorship in India began under colonial rule with the Cinematograph Act 1918 and regional censor boards in cities such as Bombay, Calcutta, Madras, and Lahore. Centralized certification began in 1951. The CBFC is headquartered in Mumbai with regional offices in several cities, including Chennai, Kolkata, Delhi, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, and Thiruvananthapuram. Members are appointed by the central government; examining committees include board members and outside advisers drawn from various walks of life.

Controversies

The Board has frequently been criticized for moral policing — ordering cuts to kissing scenes, profanity, and political content — and for inconsistent decisions, prompting debate between freedom of expression (Article 19 of the Indian Constitution) and "reasonable restrictions." Notable flashpoints include demands for extensive cuts to films such as Udta Punjab (2016, overturned by the Bombay High Court) and delays or refusals for politically sensitive documentaries and features. The Cinematograph (Amendment) Act 2023 introduced age-based subdivisions of the U/A category and perpetual validity of certificates.
