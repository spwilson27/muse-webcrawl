The Battle of Yarmouk was a major battle between the army of the Byzantine Empire and the Arab Muslim forces of the Rashidun Caliphate, fought over six days in August 636 near the Yarmouk River. The result was a complete Muslim victory that ended Byzantine rule in Syria and opened the Levant to the Muslim conquests.

Background

After a series of Muslim victories in Syria, the Emperor Heraclius assembled a large army to drive the invaders out. The Muslim army, commanded in the field by Khalid ibn al-Walid, withdrew from occupied towns to concentrate on a strong position near the Yarmouk, where ravines protected its flanks.

The battle

Over several days of fighting, the Byzantine army repeatedly attacked the Muslim line. Khalid used his mobile cavalry reserve to reinforce threatened sectors and then counterattack. On the final day, Muslim cavalry turned the Byzantine flank and drove the infantry toward the steep ravines of the Yarmouk valley, where much of the Byzantine army was destroyed or scattered.

Aftermath

Yarmouk is regarded as one of the most decisive battles in history. Byzantine Syria was lost permanently, Heraclius abandoned the province, and Muslim armies went on to conquer Egypt, Mesopotamia and Persia within a generation.
