Pelagos Sanctuary


The sanctuary protects Mediterranean cetaceans.

The Pelagos Sanctuary for Mediterranean Marine Mammals is a marine protected area of about 87,500 km² in the north-western Mediterranean Sea, between Italy, France, and Monaco, encompassing waters around Corsica and the Ligurian Sea. It was established by a treaty signed by the three countries in Rome in 1999, which entered into force in 2002; it is listed as a Specially Protected Area of Mediterranean Importance (SPAMI) under the Barcelona Convention.

Wildlife

The area's upwelling and productivity support an unusually rich cetacean fauna for the Mediterranean, including fin whales (the largest population in the basin), sperm whales, Cuvier's beaked whales, Risso's dolphins, striped dolphins, and common dolphins. Threats addressed by the sanctuary include ship strikes, bycatch in driftnets, noise, and pollution.

Management

The sanctuary was the first transboundary marine protected area of its kind in the Mediterranean and serves as a model for international cooperation in high-seas conservation, though enforcement powers remain with the individual states.
