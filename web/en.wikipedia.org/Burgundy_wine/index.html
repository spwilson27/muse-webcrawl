Burgundy wine

Burgundy wine (French: Bourgogne) is wine made in the Burgundy region of eastern France, in the valleys of the Saône River. The region is famed above all for dry red wines made from Pinot noir and white wines made from Chardonnay, though Aligoté and Gamay also feature.

Geography and classification

Burgundy runs from Auxerre in the north to Mâcon in the south. Its vineyards are divided into the Côte de Nuits, Côte de Beaune, Côte Chalonnaise, Mâconnais, Beaujolais and Chablis districts. Wines are classified in an appellation hierarchy from regional Bourgogne AOC up through village and premier cru to the exalted grand cru vineyards such as Romanée-Conti, Montrachet and Chambertin.

History

Viticulture in Burgundy dates to the Roman era and was nurtured by Cistercian monks in the Middle Ages, who carefully mapped the region's climats — precisely delimited vineyard plots whose names and boundaries were inscribed by UNESCO in 2015.
