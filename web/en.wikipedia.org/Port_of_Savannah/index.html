Port of Savannah

The Port of Savannah is a major U.S. seaport located in Savannah, Georgia, on the Savannah River about 18 miles (29 km) from the Atlantic Ocean. Operated by the Georgia Ports Authority, it is one of the largest container ports in the United States and the busiest single-terminal container facility in North America, centred on the Garden City Terminal, a single complex of more than 1,300 acres.

Operations

The port handles around five million TEU (twenty-foot equivalent units) of containerized cargo annually, along with breakbulk, bulk, and roll-on/roll-off cargo through its terminals, which include Ocean Terminal on the Savannah River. The Savannah Harbor Expansion Project, completed in 2022, deepened the shipping channel to 47 feet, allowing the port to receive the largest neo-Panamax container ships calling on the U.S. East Coast. Its location provides direct interstate and rail access to the population centres of the Southeast and Midwest, supporting a large logistics and warehousing sector around Savannah.

History

Savannah's port dates to the founding of the Georgia colony in 1733; in the 19th century it was a major exporter of cotton. The modern container port grew rapidly from the 1990s onward as shippers diversified from West Coast ports, and Savannah repeatedly ranked as the fastest-growing major container gateway in the United States.
