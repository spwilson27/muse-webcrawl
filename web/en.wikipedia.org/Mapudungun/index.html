Mapudungun

Native toChile, Argentina
Speakers~260,000
FamilyAraucanian (often treated as isolate)

Mapudungun (also spelled Mapuzugun, from mapu "land" and zugun "speech") is the language of the Mapuche people of south-central Chile and west-central Argentina. It is the largest indigenous language of the Southern Cone, though the number of fluent speakers has declined under pressure from Spanish.

Features

Mapudungun is a polysynthetic, agglutinative language with complex verbal morphology that can encode subjects, objects, direction and evidential nuances in a single word. It distinguishes dual as well as singular and plural number, and an inclusive/exclusive distinction in first-person plural pronouns.

Status

The language has no official status nationally in Chile or Argentina, though it is recognised in some regions and promoted by Mapuche revitalisation movements, intercultural bilingual education and broadcast media.
