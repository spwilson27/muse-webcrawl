Daniel Balavoine

Daniel Balavoine, French singer-songwriter

Daniel Xavier-Marie Balavoine (5 February 1952 – 14 January 1986) was a French singer-songwriter and musician. Hugely popular in the French-speaking world, he is remembered for his powerful high voice, his socially engaged songs, and his role as Johnny Rockfort in the rock opera Starmania. His best-known songs include "Le Chanteur", "L'Aziza", "Tous les cris les S.O.S." and "Sauver l'amour".

Career

Born in Alençon, Normandy, Balavoine sang in progressive rock bands before going solo. His breakthrough came in 1978 with the album Le Chanteur and his casting in Michel Berger and Luc Plamondon's rock opera Starmania. Through the early 1980s, albums such as Un autre monde (1980), Vendeurs de larmes (1982) and Loin des yeux de l'Occident (1983) made him one of France's biggest stars, noted for songs addressing politics, racism and humanitarian crises in Africa.

Balavoine became famous for his outspokenness, including a celebrated televised confrontation with Socialist leader François Mitterrand in 1980 over the concerns of French youth. He was also a keen motorsport enthusiast and competed in the Paris–Dakar Rally.

Death and legacy

On 14 January 1986, Balavoine was killed in a helicopter crash in Mali, near Rharous, while travelling with the Paris–Dakar Rally organisation on a humanitarian mission to bring water pumps to villages along the route. He was 33. His final album, Sauver l'amour (1985), became his best-selling record, and his songs remain staples of French popular music.
