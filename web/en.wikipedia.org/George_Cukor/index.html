George Cukor

George Dewey Cukor
Born: 7 July 1899, New York City, U.S.
Died: 24 January 1983, Los Angeles, U.S.
Occupation: Film director
Awards: Academy Award for Best Director (My Fair Lady)

George Dewey Cukor (July 7, 1899 – January 24, 1983) was an American film director and producer. He mainly concentrated on comedies and literary adaptations. His career flourished at RKO and MGM, where he directed Little Women (1933), David Copperfield (1935), Camille (1936), The Philadelphia Story (1940), Gaslight (1944), Adam's Rib (1949), Born Yesterday (1950), A Star Is Born (1954), and My Fair Lady (1964), for which he won the Academy Award for Best Director. Known as a "women's director" for the performances he drew from actresses such as Katharine Hepburn, he worked with Hepburn on ten films.

Early career

The son of Hungarian Jewish immigrants, Cukor began in the theatre, working as a stage manager and director on Broadway in the 1920s. He went to Hollywood in 1929 as a dialogue director for early sound films, including All Quiet on the Western Front, before becoming a director in his own right. At RKO he made A Bill of Divorcement (1932), Katharine Hepburn's film debut, beginning their lifelong collaboration.

MGM years

At MGM Cukor became the studio's prestige director of literary adaptations. He was famously fired from Gone with the Wind in 1939 shortly after filming began, though he went on to direct The Women (1939) and The Philadelphia Story (1940) to great acclaim. His postwar work included the psychological thriller Gaslight, for which Ingrid Bergman won an Oscar, a series of sophisticated comedies pairing Spencer Tracy and Hepburn, and Judy Holliday's Oscar-winning turn in Born Yesterday. In 1954 he directed Judy Garland in A Star Is Born, widely regarded as one of the great Hollywood musicals.

Later work and death

Cukor's triumph came with My Fair Lady (1964), which won eight Academy Awards including Best Picture and Best Director. He continued working into his eighties, directing the television film Love Among the Ruins (1975) with Hepburn and Laurence Olivier, and his final feature, Rich and Famous (1981). He died in Los Angeles on 24 January 1983, aged 83, leaving a reputation as one of the supreme directors of actors in classical Hollywood cinema.
