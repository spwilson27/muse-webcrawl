Incus

The incus (Latin for "anvil") is one of the three ossicles of the middle ear. Together with the malleus (hammer) and the stapes (stirrup), it transmits vibrations from the eardrum to the inner ear.

Anatomy

The incus lies between the other two ossicles: its body articulates with the head of the malleus, and its long process ends in a lenticular process that connects with the stapes. It resembles an anvil — or a molar tooth with two roots — and is about 7 mm long.

Function

The ossicular chain acts as a lever system that matches the low impedance of air to the higher impedance of the fluid-filled cochlea, amplifying pressure at the oval window so that hearing is efficient.

Clinical notes

The incus is the ossicle most vulnerable to damage from chronic ear infection, because of its relatively tenuous blood supply; it can be replaced or bypassed with prostheses in reconstructive ear surgery.
