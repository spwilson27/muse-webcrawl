Technical drawing

Technical drawing, also known as drafting or draughting, is the act and discipline of composing drawings that visually communicate how something functions or is to be constructed. Technical drawings are made to standardised conventions for layout, line types, dimensioning, and notation (governed by standards such as ISO 128 and ASME Y14.5) so that engineers, architects, manufacturers, and builders can interpret them unambiguously. Unlike artistic drawing, a technical drawing is a precise, legally meaningful document — an engineering drawing may form part of a contract.

Projections and views

Objects are usually represented by orthographic projections: front, top, and side views aligned under either first-angle (ISO) or third-angle (ASME) projection, supplemented by section views, auxiliary views, and detail views. Pictorial representations — isometric, oblique, and perspective drawings — aid visualisation but are generally not dimensioned for manufacture. Drawings specify scale, dimensions, and tolerances; geometric dimensioning and tolerancing (GD&T) precisely controls form, orientation, and location of features.

Methods

Until the late 20th century, drawings were produced by hand: first sketched, then inked on paper, vellum, or polyester film using drafting machines and lettering templates, and reproduced as blueprints or diazo prints. Computer-aided design (CAD) transformed the discipline from the 1980s onward, enabling exact geometry, easy revision, and direct links to simulation and computer-aided manufacturing. Three-dimensional parametric modelling has partly displaced 2D drawings, but the dimensioned 2D drawing remains the authoritative definition of a part in most industries.

Applications

Major branches include architectural drawing (plans, elevations, sections of buildings), engineering drawing (machined parts and assemblies), electrical and piping schematics, and patent drawings. A skilled drafter must understand projection geometry, manufacturing processes, and the standards of their industry.
