Oliver Hazard Perry (August 23, 1785 – August 23, 1819) was an American naval commander best known for his victory over a British squadron at the Battle of Lake Erie during the War of 1812, one of the most celebrated American naval triumphs of the conflict.

Early life and career

Perry was born in Rhode Island into a naval family; his father was a captain in the United States Navy and his younger brother, Matthew C. Perry, later became famous for opening Japan to American trade. Perry entered the navy as a midshipman at age 13 and served in the Quasi-War with France, the First Barbary War, and aboard several vessels before the War of 1812.

Battle of Lake Erie

In 1813 Perry was sent to Erie, Pennsylvania, where he supervised the construction of a small fleet on the lake's shore. On September 10, 1813, his squadron engaged the British fleet under Robert Heriot Barclay near Put-in-Bay, Ohio. Perry's flagship, the brig Lawrence, was battered into a wreck, but Perry rowed through heavy fire to the brig Niagara and continued the fight, compelling the entire British squadron to surrender — the first time in history a full British naval squadron surrendered to an enemy. His laconic dispatch to General William Henry Harrison became famous:

"We have met the enemy and they are ours: two ships, two brigs, one schooner and one sloop."

His battle flag bore the words "DONT GIVE UP THE SHIP", the dying command of his friend Captain James Lawrence.

Later life and legacy

Perry was promoted to captain and served in the Mediterranean during the Second Barbary War. On a diplomatic mission to Venezuela in 1819 he contracted yellow fever on the Orinoco River and died on his 34th birthday aboard ship off Trinidad. Numerous towns, counties, ships, and monuments — most prominently Perry's Victory and International Peace Memorial on South Bass Island — commemorate him.
