Eagle Scout is the highest rank attainable in the Scouts BSA programme of Scouting America (formerly the Boy Scouts of America). Introduced in 1911, the rank is earned by only a small percentage of Scouts — around 4–6 percent of those who join — and has been awarded to more than 2.7 million young people.

Requirements

To earn the rank, a Scout must advance through the preceding ranks, earn at least 21 merit badges (including a set of required subjects such as first aid, citizenship, camping, cooking and environmental science), serve in a troop leadership position, and plan, develop and lead a substantial service project — the Eagle Scout Service Project — that benefits a community organisation. All requirements must be completed before the Scout's 18th birthday, followed by a board of review.

Recognition

Eagle Scouts receive a distinctive medal, badge and certificate, and the achievement is widely recognised by colleges, employers and the United States military, which grants advanced enlistment rank. Notable Eagle Scouts include astronaut Neil Armstrong, President Gerald Ford and film director Steven Spielberg. Since girls were admitted to the programme in 2019, the inaugural class of female Eagle Scouts was recognised in 2021.
