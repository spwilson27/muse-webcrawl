William Richard Morris, 1st Viscount Nuffield (10 October 1877 – 22 August 1963) was an English motor manufacturer and philanthropist. He was the founder of Morris Motors and is remembered as the "Henry Ford of Britain" for bringing mass-produced, affordable cars to the British market.

Morris Motors

Morris began in Oxford repairing and selling bicycles and motorcycles before launching his first car, the Morris Oxford "Bullnose", in 1913. Adopting American mass-production methods and bought-in components, he cut prices sharply; by the mid-1920s Morris Motors was Britain's largest car maker. Popular models included the Morris Cowley and the Morris Minor. In 1952 his company merged with Austin to form the British Motor Corporation.

Philanthropy

Morris gave away much of his fortune — an estimated £30 million in his lifetime — funding hospitals, medical research and education. He founded the Nuffield Foundation in 1943 with £10 million, endowed Nuffield College, Oxford (the first Oxford college for graduate students and the first co-educational one), and supported the Nuffield Orthopaedic Centre and numerous medical chairs. He was raised to the peerage as Baron Nuffield in 1934 and Viscount Nuffield in 1938, taking his title from the Oxfordshire village where he lived.
