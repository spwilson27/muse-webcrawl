Label

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A label (as distinct from signage) is a piece of paper, plastic film, cloth, metal, or other material affixed to a container or product, on which is written or printed information or symbols about the product or item. Information printed directly on a container or article can also be considered labelling.

Functions

Labels serve many functions: identification of the product or brand, description of contents and ingredients, instructions for use, warnings, promotion, and compliance with legal requirements. Food labels in many jurisdictions must list ingredients, nutritional information, allergens, and expiry or best-before dates. Labels on hazardous materials communicate risks through standardized pictograms.

Types and production

Labels may be permanently or temporarily affixed, removable, or designed as shrink sleeves or moulded-in labels. Modern label production uses flexography, offset, gravure, and digital printing, often on pressure-sensitive (self-adhesive) stock. Technologies such as barcodes, QR codes, and RFID tags extend the label's role into inventory control, traceability, and anti-counterfeiting.

History

Label use dates to antiquity — wine amphorae in ancient Egypt and Rome bore markings of origin and vintage. Paper labels became widespread with lithography in the 19th century, when colourful chromolithographed labels adorned crates, tins, and bottles, and gummed labels entered postal and retail use.
