John Boyd Dunlop (1840–1921) was a Scottish veterinary surgeon working in Belfast who re-invented the pneumatic tyre in 1887, transforming cycling and, later, motoring.

The pneumatic tyre

To ease his young son's tricycle rides — the boy suffered headaches from jolting on solid rubber tyres — Dunlop fitted an air-filled rubber tube to the wheels. He patented the design in 1888. Cyclist Willie Hume's race victories on pneumatic tyres in 1889 proved their superiority, and the Dunlop Pneumatic Tyre Company was founded to manufacture them; the company grew into the Dunlop tyre brand.

Note on priority

Robert William Thomson had patented a pneumatic tyre in 1845, but it had been forgotten; Dunlop's patent was later invalidated on that ground, though his practical development drove the industry.
