Advanced Micro Devices, Inc. (AMD) is an American multinational semiconductor company headquartered in Santa Clara, California, that develops computer processors and related technologies for business and consumer markets. Founded in 1969 by Jerry Sanders and seven colleagues from Fairchild Semiconductor, AMD is a major supplier of x86 microprocessors, graphics processing units (GPUs), and adaptive computing products.

History

AMD began as a manufacturer of logic chips and second-source producer of Intel and other companies' designs. A 1982 technology exchange agreement made AMD a second source for Intel's x86 processors, a relationship that ended in litigation when Intel withdrew from the agreement; AMD nevertheless continued with its own compatible designs, including the Am386 and Am486. In 1993 AMD introduced the K5, its first fully in-house x86 microarchitecture, and the Athlon line launched in 1999 won the race to 1 GHz.

In 2003 AMD released the Opteron and Athlon 64 processors, which introduced the AMD64 64-bit extension of the x86 architecture, later adopted by Intel. AMD acquired graphics company ATI Technologies in 2006 for $5.4 billion, gaining the Radeon GPU line. In 2009 AMD spun off its manufacturing operations into GlobalFoundries, becoming a fabless company.

Under CEO Lisa Su, who took over in 2014, AMD launched the Zen microarchitecture in 2017 with the Ryzen, Threadripper and EPYC processor families, restoring its competitiveness against Intel in desktops, laptops and servers. In 2022 AMD completed its acquisition of Xilinx, a maker of field-programmable gate arrays, in an all-stock transaction valued at about $49 billion. AMD's Instinct accelerators compete in the data-centre artificial intelligence market.

Products

AMD's product lines include Ryzen CPUs and Ryzen AI processors for consumers, EPYC server processors, Radeon graphics cards, Instinct data-centre accelerators, and adaptive system-on-chip products from the Xilinx acquisition. AMD also supplies semi-custom processors for game consoles, including the PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X/S.
