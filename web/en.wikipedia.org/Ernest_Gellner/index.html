Ernest Gellner

Ernest André Gellner
Born: December 9, 1925, Paris, France
Died: November 5, 1995, Prague, Czech Republic
Fields: Philosophy, anthropology, sociology
Known for: Theory of nationalism

Ernest André Gellner was a British-Czech philosopher and social anthropologist. He was a professor at the London School of Economics and later at Cambridge, and founded the Centre for the Study of Nationalism at the Central European University. His 1983 book Nations and Nationalism is one of the most influential theories of nationalism.

Theory of nationalism

Gellner argued that nationalism is a product of industrial society: industrial economies require a mobile, literate workforce sharing a standardised "high culture" transmitted through universal state education, and nationalism is the political doctrine that the cultural and political unit should be congruent. This modernist account contrasted with views of nations as ancient or primordial.

Other work

His first book, Words and Things (1959), attacked the linguistic philosophy of ordinary-language analysis then dominant at Oxford. He conducted fieldwork in the Atlas Mountains of Morocco, published as Saints of the Atlas (1969), and wrote widely on Islam, Soviet Marxism and psychoanalysis, notably in The Psychoanalytic Movement (1985).
