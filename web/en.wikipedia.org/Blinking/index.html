Blinking is a semi-autonomic rapid closing of the eyelids. A single blink lasts roughly 100–400 milliseconds and is controlled by the orbicularis oculi muscle, innervated by the facial nerve. Blinking spreads tears across the surface of the eye, clearing dust and irritants and keeping the cornea moist and optically smooth.

Types and control

Spontaneous blinking occurs regularly without external stimuli, driven by brainstem circuits whose rate is modulated by dopamine; it increases with fatigue and decreases during focused visual tasks such as reading or screen use. Reflex blinking occurs in response to touch, bright light, or looming objects, and voluntary blinking is under conscious control.

Blink rate and conditions

Healthy resting adults blink about 15–20 times per minute, infants far less often. Abnormally reduced blinking occurs in Parkinson's disease, while excessive blinking can accompany dry eye, blepharospasm, or Tourette syndrome. Blink patterns are studied in psychology and human–computer interaction as indicators of attention and cognitive load.
