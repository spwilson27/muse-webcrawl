Methionine

CodonsAUG (start codon)
SymbolMet, M
FormulaC5H11NO2S
Essential in humansYes

Methionine (symbol Met or M) is an essential proteinogenic amino acid — one of the building blocks of proteins that humans must obtain from food. It contains a sulfur atom in a thioether side chain, making it one of only two sulfur-containing amino acids commonly found in proteins (the other is cysteine). Methionine was first isolated in 1921 by John Howard Mueller from casein.

Roles in biology

Methionine is special because its codon, AUG, is the start codon that initiates protein synthesis, so nearly every protein begins with methionine (often removed afterwards). Beyond proteins, methionine is the precursor of S-adenosylmethionine (SAM), the cell's principal methyl-group donor for DNA, RNA, protein and lipid methylation, and of the antioxidant glutathione via cysteine. Methionine metabolism intersects with folate and vitamin B12 pathways; its intermediate homocysteine, when elevated, is associated with cardiovascular disease.

Dietary aspects

Rich sources include eggs, meat, fish, sesame seeds and Brazil nuts; grains are lower, so plant-based diets must combine sources carefully. Methionine is produced industrially on a large scale — mainly by chemical synthesis — as a supplement in poultry and livestock feed, where it is often the first limiting amino acid. Methionine restriction has been studied in animal models for effects on lifespan and metabolism, and its role in cancer cell metabolism is an active research area.

Structure

Methionine's side chain, –CH2–CH2–S–CH3, is relatively non-polar and flexible; the sulfur can be oxidised to methionine sulfoxide, a form of oxidative protein damage that cells can repair with dedicated enzymes, suggesting a role for methionine residues as built-in antioxidants.
