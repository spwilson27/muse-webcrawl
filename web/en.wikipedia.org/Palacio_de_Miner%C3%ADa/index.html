Palacio de Minería

LocationMexico City, Mexico
ArchitectManuel Tolsá
Built1797–1813
StyleNeoclassical

The Palacio de Minería (Palace of Mining) is a Neoclassical building in the historic centre of Mexico City, considered one of the masterpieces of Spanish American architecture. It was designed by the Valencian sculptor and architect Manuel Tolsá and built between 1797 and 1813 to house the Royal School of Mines (Real Seminario de Minería), one of the first technical schools in the Americas.

History

The palace was commissioned by the Tribunal de Minería to train specialists for New Spain's mining industry. After independence it housed various institutions, including the engineering school of the National University. The building survived earthquake damage and subsidence, and underwent major restoration in the 1970s. Today it belongs to the Faculty of Engineering of the National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM).

Current use

The Palacio de Minería is used for conferences, exhibitions, and cultural events. Since 1979 it has hosted the Feria Internacional del Libro del Palacio de Minería, one of Mexico's most important book fairs. Its former library and meteorite collection (including fragments of the Toluca meteorite) are notable features.
