Tributary

A tributary, or affluent, is a stream or river that flows into a larger stream or main stem (or parent) river or a lake. A tributary does not flow directly into a sea or ocean. Tributaries and the main stem river drain the surrounding drainage basin (watershed), and their confluence — the point where they meet — shapes the size and discharge of the river system.

Terminology

A tributary is described as a "left-bank" or "right-bank" tributary according to the side on which it joins the main river when facing downstream. The opposite of a tributary is a distributary, a channel that branches off and flows away from the main stem, common in river deltas. Streams are ordered using systems such as the Strahler stream order: headwater streams with no tributaries are first-order, and orders increase where streams of equal order join.

Examples

Major tributaries include the Ohio and Missouri rivers, which join the Mississippi; the Madeira, Rio Negro and Tapajós, which join the Amazon; and the Blue Nile and White Nile, whose confluence at Khartoum forms the main Nile. Sometimes a tributary is longer or carries more water than the nominal main stem at their confluence — the Missouri River, for instance, is longer than the Mississippi above their meeting at St. Louis — in which case the naming reflects historical rather than hydrological priority.
