Sexual Offences Act 1967

The Act was passed by the UK Parliament at Westminster.

The Sexual Offences Act 1967 is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that partially decriminalised homosexual acts in private between two men aged 21 or over in England and Wales. It was a landmark in the legal history of LGBT rights in Britain, though it left many restrictions in place.

Background

Male homosexual acts had been criminal offences in England since the Buggery Act of 1533, and "gross indecency" between men was criminalised by the Labouchère Amendment of 1885, under which Oscar Wilde and, much later, Alan Turing were prosecuted. The Wolfenden report of 1957 recommended that homosexual behaviour between consenting adults in private should no longer be a crime, arguing that the law should not intrude into private morality. After a decade of campaigning by the Homosexual Law Reform Society, Labour MP Leo Abse introduced the bill with government support under Home Secretary Roy Jenkins, and it received royal assent on 27 July 1967.

Provisions and limits

The Act exempted acts done "in private" between exactly two consenting men over 21, but set the age of consent higher than for heterosexual acts (21 rather than 16), defined privacy narrowly — excluding anywhere a third person was present or a public lavatory — and did not apply to the armed forces, merchant navy, Scotland or Northern Ireland. Scotland decriminalised in 1980 and Northern Ireland in 1982, following a European Court of Human Rights ruling in the Dudgeon case. The unequal age of consent was reduced to 18 in 1994 and equalised at 16 in 2000.

Legacy

The Act is generally regarded as a first, cautious step rather than full equality: prosecutions for offences such as gross indecency in public continued, and some historians note enforcement in some categories initially increased. Nevertheless it ended the blanket criminalisation of private adult homosexuality in England and Wales and began the sequence of reforms culminating in the Sexual Offences Act 2003 and equal marriage legislation.
