Infante Jaime of Spain, Duke of Madrid (27 June 1870 – 2 October 1931) was the Carlist claimant to the throne of Spain under the name Jaime III and the Legitimist claimant to the throne of France as Jacques I. He succeeded his father, Carlos, Duke of Madrid, as head of the Carlist movement in 1909 and led the movement until his death.

Early life

Jaime was born at Vevey in Switzerland, the second child and only surviving son of Carlos, Duke of Madrid, the Carlist pretender to the Spanish throne, and his wife Princess Margherita of Bourbon-Parma. He was educated at the Jesuit college of Beaumont in England and later studied at the Theresian Military Academy at Wiener Neustadt in Austria. He served as an officer in the Imperial Russian Army, holding a commission in a dragoon regiment of the Russian Imperial Guard.

Military service and travels

During his years in Russian service Jaime saw active duty in the Boxer Rebellion in China in 1900 and in the Russo-Japanese War of 1904–1905, experiences that gave him a reputation as a professional soldier unusual among royal pretenders. He was decorated for his service and travelled widely in Europe and Asia.

Carlist claimant

On the death of his father on 18 July 1909, Jaime succeeded as Carlist claimant to the Spanish throne and as Legitimist claimant to the French throne. As head of the movement he issued manifestos from his residence at Frohsdorf Castle in Austria. His leadership was marked by efforts to reorganise the Traditionalist Communion, tensions with the Integrist faction led by Juan Vázquez de Mella, and the eventual split of the Mellistas in 1919. During the First World War he was placed under house arrest in Austria because of his pro-French sympathies.

Jaime never married. On his death in Paris in 1931, the Carlist claim passed to his elderly uncle, Infante Alfonso Carlos, Duke of San Jaime.

Titles

As claimant he was styled Duke of Madrid in Spain and Duke of Anjou in France. He was a knight of the Order of the Golden Fleece and of the Order of the Holy Spirit in the Legitimist reckoning.
