Brown University

University Hall (1770), the oldest building on campus

Brown University is a private Ivy League research university in Providence, Rhode Island. Founded in 1764 as the College in the English Colony of Rhode Island and Providence Plantations, it is the seventh-oldest institution of higher education in the United States. It is known for its undergraduate Open Curriculum, adopted in 1969, which lets students design their own programme without core distribution requirements. Brown enrols roughly 11,000 students and has an endowment of about $7 billion.

History

The college was chartered in 1764 with Baptist support but admitted students of all denominations — a founding commitment reflected in its charter's guarantee of religious liberty. It moved from Warren to Providence in 1770, and was renamed Brown University in 1804 after benefactor Nicholas Brown Jr. Women were admitted through Pembroke College from 1891 until a full merger in 1971. The 1969 "New Curriculum," following a student-led report by Ira Magaziner and Elliot Maxwell, introduced the Open Curriculum and satisfactory/no-credit grading options. Brown admitted its first students of colour in the 1870s and has become one of the most selective US universities, with an acceptance rate around 5 per cent.

Academics

Brown comprises the College, the Graduate School, the Warren Alpert Medical School, the School of Public Health, and the School of Engineering, among others. The university is a member of the Association of American Universities and is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities — very high research activity." Faculty and alumni include multiple Nobel laureates, Fields medallists, and Pulitzer Prize winners.

Campus and student life

The main campus on College Hill, overlooking downtown Providence, contains many Georgian and colonial-revival buildings, including University Hall, a National Historic Landmark. The mascot is Bruno the bear and the teams are the Brown Bears. Traditions include the Van Wickle Gates, opened only for processions at Convocation and Commencement.
