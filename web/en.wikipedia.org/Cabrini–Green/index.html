Cabrini–Green

Cabrini–Green was a public housing project administered by the Chicago Housing Authority on the Near North Side of Chicago, Illinois. Built between 1942 and 1962, it combined the low-rise Frances Cabrini row houses with the high-rise William Green Homes. At its peak it housed some 15,000 people, overwhelmingly African American families, in mid- and high-rise buildings on land close to Chicago's wealthy Gold Coast.

Decline and notoriety

From the 1970s, disinvestment, poor maintenance, gang violence and notorious crimes—including the 1992 sniper killing of seven-year-old Dantrell Davis on his way to school—made Cabrini–Green a national symbol of failed public housing, an image amplified by films such as Candyman (1992), which was set and partly filmed there. At the same time, residents and later scholarship stressed the strong community networks that the media accounts overlooked.

Demolition

Under the Chicago Housing Authority's Plan for Transformation, the high-rises were demolished between 1995 and 2011, the last tower coming down in March 2011. The site has been redeveloped as mixed-income housing and retail, and the episode remains central to debates about displacement, HOPE VI policy and the design of public housing.
