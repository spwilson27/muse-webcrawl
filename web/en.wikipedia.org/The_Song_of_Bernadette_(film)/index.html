The Song of Bernadette (film)

The Song of Bernadette is a 1943 American religious drama film directed by Henry King and produced by 20th Century Fox. Based on Franz Werfel's 1941 novel of the same name, it tells the story of Bernadette Soubirous, the French peasant girl whose visions of the Virgin Mary at Lourdes in 1858 made the town a major Catholic pilgrimage site. Jennifer Jones stars in the title role.

Plot

Fourteen-year-old Bernadette, gathering firewood near the grotto of Massabielle, sees a beautiful lady in white who eventually reveals herself with the words "I am the Immaculate Conception." Facing scepticism and hostility from civil and church authorities, including the imperial prosecutor played by Vincent Price, Bernadette holds to her account; a spring she uncovers at the grotto is credited with miraculous healings. She dies young in a convent at Nevers and was canonised in 1933.

Reception

The film was a major critical and commercial success. It won four Academy Awards, including Best Actress for Jennifer Jones, who became a star with the performance, and was nominated for Best Picture and Best Director.
