Cheyenne Mountain Complex

The Cheyenne Mountain Complex is a United States Space Force installation and defensive bunker located in unincorporated El Paso County, Colorado, next to Colorado Springs. Built deep inside Cheyenne Mountain during the Cold War, it was designed to survive a nuclear attack and served for decades as the operations center of the North American Aerospace Defense Command (NORAD).

Construction

Excavation began in 1961. Workers removed about 693,000 tons of granite, carving out a grid of chambers and tunnels some 2,000 feet (600 m) beneath the surface. Within the caverns, fifteen freestanding buildings were mounted on massive steel springs to absorb the shock of a blast or earthquake, and protected by 25-ton blast doors. The complex became operational in 1967.

Operations

The complex housed NORAD's command center, which monitored North American airspace and missile launches. In 2006, NORAD's day-to-day operations moved to Peterson Air Force Base, with Cheyenne Mountain placed in "warm standby" as an alternate command center. In 2015 the military announced plans to move communications equipment back into the mountain, in part because its shielded interior protects electronics from electromagnetic pulse attack.

In popular culture

The facility famously appears in the 1983 film WarGames and as the home of Stargate Command in the Stargate television franchise.
