Wood carving
MediumWood
ToolsKnives, chisels, gouges, mallets
FormsRelief, in-the-round, chip carving, whittling

Wood carving is a form of woodworking by means of a cutting tool in one hand or a chisel in two hands, or with one hand on a chisel and one hand on a mallet, resulting in a wooden figure or figurine, or in the sculptural ornamentation of a wooden object.

Techniques

The main techniques are whittling, chip carving, relief carving (in low, high, and pierced forms), and carving in the round. Carvers select woods for grain, colour, and workability: basswood, butternut, and pine are favoured for ease of cutting, while boxwood and fruitwoods allow fine detail.

History

Carved wood survives from ancient Egypt, medieval church screens and misericords, Gothic altarpieces by masters such as Tilman Riemenschneider, Māori meeting houses, Japanese netsuke, and the figureheads of sailing ships.

Tools

The basic kit comprises carving knives, gouges of varying sweep, V-tools, chisels, rasps, and sharpening stones; power carving with rotary tools and chainsaws is now common for large work.
