Manuel Tolsá

Born: 4 May 1757, Enguera, Spain
Died: 24 December 1816, Mexico City
Occupation: Architect and sculptor
Known for: El Caballito; Palacio de Minería

Manuel Tolsá (1757–1816) was a Spanish architect and sculptor who worked in New Spain, now Mexico, where he became the leading exponent of neoclassical art in the late colonial period.

Life and career

Tolsá trained at the academies of San Carlos in Valencia and San Fernando in Madrid. In 1791 he arrived in New Spain as director of sculpture at the newly founded Academy of San Carlos in Mexico City, bringing casts of classical statues for teaching. He soon became the colony's most sought-after architect.

Works

His most famous creation is the equestrian statue of Charles IV, known as El Caballito ("The Little Horse"), cast in 1803 and considered one of the finest equestrian monuments in the Americas. His architectural masterpiece is the Palacio de Minería (Mining Palace) in Mexico City (1797–1813), a landmark of neoclassicism. He also completed the towers and façade of Mexico City Cathedral, designed the Palacio del Marqués del Apartado and worked on the Hospicio Cabañas in Guadalajara, among many other projects.

Legacy

Tolsá's buildings and his teaching shaped the transition from baroque to neoclassical taste in Mexico, and his works remain central monuments of Mexico City.
