Navigation

Navigation is a field of study that focuses on the process of monitoring and controlling the movement of a craft or vehicle from one place to another. It includes land, marine, aeronautic, and space navigation, combining position fixing, course plotting, and the management of charts, instruments, and waypoints.

Methods

Dead reckoning advances a known position by course and speed. Pilotage uses landmarks and charts. Celestial navigation fixes position by measuring angles to the sun, moon, planets, and stars with a sextant, solving longitude through accurate timekeeping. Radio navigation (LORAN, VOR, DME) and satellite navigation (GPS and other GNSS systems) now dominate, giving continuous position worldwide, while inertial systems navigate without any external reference.

History

Polynesian navigators crossed the Pacific using stars, swells, and bird flight; Mediterranean sailors relied on the magnetic compass and portolan charts. The 18th-century solution of the longitude problem — Harrison's marine chronometer and the lunar-distance method — made oceanic navigation precise, and the 20th century added radar, radio beacons, and satellites.
