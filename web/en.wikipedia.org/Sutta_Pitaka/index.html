Sutta Pitaka

The Sutta Pitaka preserves the discourses attributed to the Buddha.

The Sutta Pitaka (Pali: Sutta Piṭaka, "Basket of Discourses") is the second of the three divisions of the Pali Canon, the scripture of Theravada Buddhism. It contains more than 10,000 suttas — discourses attributed to the Buddha and his close disciples — dealing mainly with doctrine and practice.

Divisions

The Sutta Pitaka is divided into five collections called Nikayas:

  • Digha Nikaya — the "long collection" of 34 extended discourses.
  • Majjhima Nikaya — the "middle-length collection" of 152 suttas.
  • Samyutta Nikaya — the "connected collection," grouped by subject.
  • Anguttara Nikaya — the "numerical collection," arranged by the number of items discussed.
  • Khuddaka Nikaya — the "minor collection," including the Dhammapada, Udana, and Jataka tales.

Content

The suttas record dialogues, sermons, and verses covering the Four Noble Truths, the Noble Eightfold Path, dependent origination, meditation practice, and monastic life. Parallel collections (Agamas) are preserved in Chinese and Tibetan translation in other Buddhist schools.
