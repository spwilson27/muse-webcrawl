Electronic signature


Capturing a handwritten electronic signature on a signature pad

An electronic signature, or e-signature, is data in electronic form which is associated with other data in electronic form and which is used by the signatory to sign. In many jurisdictions, electronic signatures have the same legal effect as handwritten signatures for most purposes, provided certain requirements are met.

Definitions and types

Definitions vary. Broadly, an electronic signature can be anything from a typed name at the end of an email, to a scanned image of a handwritten signature, to a click-to-sign action, to a cryptographically secure digital signature. A digital signature is a specific technical implementation using public-key cryptography and certificates to guarantee authenticity and integrity; every digital signature is an electronic signature, but not vice versa.

The European Union's eIDAS Regulation (2014) distinguishes simple electronic signatures, advanced electronic signatures (uniquely linked to the signatory, capable of identifying them, and tamper-evident), and qualified electronic signatures, which are created with a qualified device and certificate and are legally equivalent to handwritten signatures throughout the EU.

Legal frameworks

  • United States – the federal ESIGN Act (2000) and the state-level Uniform Electronic Transactions Act (UETA) give electronic signatures legal effect, with exceptions such as wills.
  • European Union – the eIDAS Regulation provides a common framework and cross-border recognition of qualified signatures.
  • United Kingdom – the Electronic Communications Act 2000 and retained eIDAS principles.

Technology and use

E-signature platforms route documents to signatories, capture consent and signatures, and produce an audit trail recording identity, timestamps and document hashes. They are widely used for contracts, HR onboarding, financial and real-estate transactions. Security concerns include identity verification, long-term validity of signatures as algorithms age, and the evidentiary weight of audit records.
