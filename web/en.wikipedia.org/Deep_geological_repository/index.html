Deep geological repository


Illustration: Deep geological repository

A deep geological repository is a radioactive waste repository excavated deep within a stable geologic environment, typically several hundred metres below the surface. It is designed to isolate high-level radioactive waste from the biosphere for tens of thousands to millions of years, until radioactivity has decayed to safe levels.

Design

The multi-barrier concept combines the waste form itself (such as vitrified glass or spent fuel), corrosion-resistant canisters (often copper or steel), buffer materials such as bentonite clay, and the host rock — commonly granite, clay or bedded salt.

Examples

Finland's Onkalo spent nuclear fuel repository, built in the granite at Olkiluoto, was the first deep repository for spent fuel to enter operation. The Waste Isolation Pilot Plant (WIPP) in New Mexico, excavated in a salt formation, disposes of transuranic waste. Surface interim storage methods such as dry cask storage complement geological disposal.
