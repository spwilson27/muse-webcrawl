Rudolf IV (1 November 1339 – 27 July 1365), also called Rudolf the Founder (German: der Stifter), was Duke of Austria, Styria and Carinthia from 1358, Count of Tyrol from 1363 and first Duke of Carniola, ruling over the Habsburg hereditary lands from Vienna.

Reign

Rudolf is remembered for the Privilegium Maius, a set of forged documents produced under his direction that purported to grant Austria special privileges dating from Roman times, including the title of Archduke for its rulers. Although Emperor Charles IV refused to confirm most of its claims, the archducal title was eventually adopted by the Habsburgs.

Foundations

In 1365 Rudolf founded the University of Vienna, the oldest university in the German-speaking world, and initiated the Gothic rebuilding of St. Stephen's Cathedral in Vienna, where his cenotaph stands. He also promoted the economic development of Vienna, founding institutions such as a mint and encouraging trade.

Death

Rudolf died suddenly in Milan in 1365, aged only 25, while on a diplomatic mission. He was succeeded by his younger brothers Albert III and Leopold III.
