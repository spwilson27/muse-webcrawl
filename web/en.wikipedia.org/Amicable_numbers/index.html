Amicable numbers

Amicable numbers are two different numbers so related that the sum of the proper divisors of each is equal to the other number. A pair of amicable numbers constitutes an aliquot sequence of period 2. The smallest pair is (220, 284): the proper divisors of 220 sum to 284, and the proper divisors of 284 sum to 220.

History

Amicable numbers were known to the Pythagoreans, who attributed mystical significance to them. A formula for generating pairs was given by the 9th-century Arab mathematician Thabit ibn Qurra: if p = 3·2n−1 − 1, q = 3·2n − 1, and r = 9·22n−1 − 1 are all prime, then 2npq and 2nr form an amicable pair. Fermat and Descartes rediscovered pairs in the 17th century, and Euler greatly extended the list.

Examples

The first few amicable pairs (in increasing order of the smaller number) are (220, 284), (1184, 1210), (2620, 2924), (5020, 5564), (6232, 6368), (10744, 10856), and (12285, 14595).

Open questions

It is unknown whether there are infinitely many amicable pairs, or whether any pair exists with one even and one odd number. A related concept is that of sociable numbers, whose aliquot sequences form longer cycles.
