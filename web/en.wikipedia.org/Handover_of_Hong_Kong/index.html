Handover of Hong Kong

The handover of Hong Kong from the United Kingdom to the People's Republic of China took place at midnight on 1 July 1997. The ceremony, attended by Prince Charles (now Charles III) and Chinese leaders, ended 156 years of British rule and established Hong Kong as a Special Administrative Region of China.

Background

Hong Kong Island was ceded in 1842 after the First Opium War, Kowloon in 1860, and the New Territories leased for 99 years in 1898. With the lease expiring in 1997, Britain and China negotiated the territory's future, signing the Sino-British Joint Declaration in 1984, which promised Hong Kong a high degree of autonomy under the "one country, two systems" principle for 50 years.

Ceremony and aftermath

The flags were lowered and raised at the Hong Kong Convention and Exhibition Centre as the last governor, Chris Patten, departed. Tung Chee-hwa became the first chief executive. The Basic Law serves as Hong Kong's constitutional document. Political developments in later decades, including the 2019 protests and the 2020 national security law, have kept the terms of the handover internationally debated.
