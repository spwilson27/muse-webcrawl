Israel Moiseevich Gelfand (Russian: Израиль Моисеевич Гельфанд; 2 September 1913 – 5 October 2009) was a prominent Soviet and American mathematician whose work ranged across group theory, representation theory, functional analysis, algebraic geometry and mathematical biology. Largely self-educated as a young man, he became one of the most influential mathematicians of the twentieth century.

Work

Gelfand's early work in functional analysis produced the Gelfand representation of commutative Banach algebras and, with Mark Naimark, the Gelfand–Naimark theorem characterising C*-algebras. With students and collaborators he built the modern theory of representations of Lie groups, introducing integral geometry on homogeneous spaces, and with Zetlin developed the Gelfand–Tsetlin basis for representations of the classical groups. In physics he worked on inverse scattering (the Gelfand–Levitan–Marchenko equation). Later in life he turned to mathematical biology and medicine, cell biology, and education.

Seminar and legacy

His famous seminar at Moscow State University, run for nearly five decades, was a central institution of Soviet mathematics, notable for its wide scope and demanding style. He founded an influential correspondence school for school students across the USSR. Barred for decades from travelling, he emigrated to the United States in 1989, at age 76, and taught at Rutgers University. He published over 800 papers and some 30 books, and is commemorated by the many mathematical objects bearing his name.
