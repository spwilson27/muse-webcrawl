Spin quantum number

In atomic physics and chemistry, the spin quantum number is a quantum number that describes the intrinsic angular momentum (or spin angular momentum, or simply spin) of an electron or other particle. It is usually denoted s for the total spin of a particle, and ms for the component of spin along a chosen axis.

Electrons in atoms

For an electron, the spin quantum number s = 1/2, and the magnetic spin quantum number ms can take only two values, +1/2 ("spin up") and −1/2 ("spin down"). Together with the principal, azimuthal, and magnetic quantum numbers, the spin quantum number specifies the complete quantum state of an electron in an atom. The Pauli exclusion principle states that no two electrons in an atom can share the same set of four quantum numbers.

History

The concept was introduced by George Uhlenbeck and Samuel Goudsmit in 1925 to explain the fine structure of atomic spectra and the anomalous Zeeman effect. The Stern–Gerlach experiment of 1922 had already demonstrated the quantisation of angular momentum in space.

General particles

Particles with integer spin (0, 1, 2, …) are bosons, while those with half-integer spin (1/2, 3/2, …) are fermions, a distinction that governs their statistical behaviour.
