Sixth man

The sixth man in basketball is a player who is not a starter but comes off the bench much more often than other reserves, often being the first player called in. The sixth man plays a major role and often scores and plays minutes comparable to starters.

A famous sixth man, such as Manu Ginóbili, Jamal Crawford, or Lou Williams, often specializes in scoring and provides a spark when substituted in. Since 1983, the NBA has awarded the NBA Sixth Man of the Year Award to the league's best-performing substitute.

The position requires versatility and acceptance of a non-starting role; notable historical sixth men include John Havlicek of the Boston Celtics, who made the role famous in the 1960s.
