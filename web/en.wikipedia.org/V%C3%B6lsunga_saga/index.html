Völsunga saga

The Völsunga saga (Old Norse: Vǫlsunga saga, "the saga of the Völsungs") is a legendary saga from late 13th-century Iceland, telling the origin and decline of the Völsung clan. It is the fullest prose account of material also found in heroic poems of the Poetic Edda and is closely related to the Middle High German Nibelungenlied.

Synopsis

The saga traces the lineage from Sigi, said to be a son of Odin, through Völsung and his children Sigmund and Signý, to Sigmund's son Sigurd (Siegfried), who slays the dragon Fáfnir and wins the cursed gold of Andvari. Sigurd's love for the valkyrie Brynhildr, his marriage to Gudrun of the Gjúkungs, his murder, Brynhildr's suicide, and Gudrun's later marriage to Atli (Attila) — ending in the destruction of the Burgundian royal house — form the saga's tragic core.

Sources and influence

The anonymous author compiled and adapted older eddic poems, some now lost, into continuous prose. The saga influenced Wagner's Der Ring des Nibelungen and, through William Morris's translation and retelling, modern fantasy literature including J. R. R. Tolkien's The Legend of Sigurd and Gudrún.
