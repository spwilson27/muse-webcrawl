Contusion

A contusion, commonly known as a bruise, is a type of hematoma of tissue, the most common cause being capillaries damaged by trauma, causing localised bleeding that extravasates into the surrounding interstitial tissues. Most contusions are not very serious and occur after a fall or impact with a hard object.

Other namesBruise
SpecialtyEmergency medicine
CauseBlunt trauma damaging capillaries

Appearance

Bruises typically change colour over time. Fresh bruises appear red or purple; over several days haemoglobin in the escaped blood breaks down into biliverdin (green) and then bilirubin (yellow-brown) before fading. The colour sequence can help estimate the age of an injury, though it is not precise.

Severity and treatment

Most contusions heal without treatment within about two weeks. Recommended care for minor bruises includes rest, ice, compression, and elevation (RICE). Unusually large, painful, or spontaneously appearing bruises can indicate an underlying bleeding disorder or a more serious internal injury, such as a muscle contusion with compartment syndrome.

Other types

Contusions can also affect internal organs: a pulmonary contusion is bruising of the lung, and a cerebral contusion is bruising of brain tissue, often occurring together with concussion in head injuries. See also hematoma.
