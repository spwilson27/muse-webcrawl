El Bulli (Catalan pronunciation roughly "el BOO-yee") was a restaurant near the town of Roses in Catalonia, Spain, run by chef Ferran Adrià. Widely regarded as the most influential restaurant of its era, it pioneered the avant-garde approach sometimes called molecular gastronomy and was named the world's best restaurant a record five times by Restaurant magazine.

History

The site began in 1961 as a beach shack and miniature golf course founded by the German Schilling family; the name came from their French bulldogs, known as bullis. Ferran Adrià joined the kitchen in 1984 and became head chef in 1987. The restaurant earned its third Michelin star in 1997.

Cuisine

El Bulli was open only about six months a year, serving a single nightly seating of around 50 guests a tasting menu of some 30–40 small courses; the kitchen spent the closed months developing new dishes in its Barcelona workshop, elBullitaller. Signature creations included foams made with a siphon, "spherified" liquids, hot gelatines and the famous deconstructed Spanish omelette. Demand vastly exceeded supply, with millions of requests for a few thousand seats each season.

Closure and legacy

Adrià closed El Bulli as a restaurant on 30 July 2011 and converted the project into the elBullifoundation, devoted to documenting and researching culinary creativity. Its techniques and alumni — including chefs such as René Redzepi, Grant Achatz and the Roca brothers — spread its influence across world fine dining. The site reopened in 2023 as elBulli1846, a museum dedicated to the restaurant's history.
