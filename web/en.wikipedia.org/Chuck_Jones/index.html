Chuck Jones

Charles Martin Jones (September 21, 1912 – February 22, 2002) was an American animator, filmmaker, painter and voice actor. He is best known for his work with Warner Bros. Cartoons on the Looney Tunes and Merrie Melodies series of shorts, and for creating or codifying characters including Wile E. Coyote and the Road Runner, Marvin the Martian, Pepé Le Pew, Michigan J. Frog and Ralph Wolf and Sam Sheepdog.

Career

Jones joined Leon Schlesinger Productions, the studio that made cartoons for Warner Bros., in 1933 and became a director (then called a "supervisor") in 1938. Working with writers such as Michael Maltese and animators including Ken Harris, he directed classics such as The Dover Boys (1942), Duck Amuck (1953), One Froggy Evening (1955), What's Opera, Doc? (1957) and the "Hunting Trilogy" of Rabbit Fire, Rabbit Seasoning and Duck! Rabbit, Duck! (1951–1953). After Warner Bros. closed its cartoon studio in 1963, Jones worked at MGM Animation/Visual Arts, where he produced Tom and Jerry shorts and directed the television special Dr. Seuss' How the Grinch Stole Christmas! (1966). He also directed the animated feature The Phantom Tollbooth (1970).

Awards and legacy

Jones's cartoons won Academy Awards for For Scent-imental Reasons (1949), So Much for So Little (1949) and The Dot and the Line (1965), and he received an Honorary Academy Award in 1996 for his lifetime contribution to animation. What's Opera, Doc? was the first cartoon inducted into the National Film Registry. He spent his later years painting, lecturing and mentoring animators, and is widely regarded as one of the most influential animation directors of all time.
