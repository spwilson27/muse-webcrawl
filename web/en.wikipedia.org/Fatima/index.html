Fatima

Fatima is a given name of Arabic origin, borne most famously by Fatima bint Muhammad, daughter of the Prophet Muhammad.

Fatima (Arabic: فَاطِمَة, Fāṭima) is a feminine given name of Arabic origin, meaning "the weaning one" or "the one who abstains." It is borne most famously by Fatima bint Muhammad (c. 605–632), the daughter of the Islamic prophet Muhammad and his wife Khadija, and the wife of Ali ibn Abi Talib, the fourth caliph and first Shia Imam. Fatima is revered in Islam, particularly by Shia Muslims, as one of the four perfect women.

Other uses

The name also refers to Fátima, a city in Portugal famous as a Catholic pilgrimage site following reported Marian apparitions in 1917 to three shepherd children, Lúcia dos Santos and her cousins Francisco and Jacinta Marto. The Sanctuary of Fátima is one of the most important Catholic shrines in the world.

Fatima is also a common personal name across the Muslim world and in countries such as Portugal, Spain, and Brazil. In Catholic tradition, "Our Lady of Fátima" is a title of the Virgin Mary associated with the Portuguese apparitions.

People

Notable bearers include Fatima Whitbread, British javelin thrower; Fatima Bhutto, Pakistani writer; and Fátima Bernardes, Brazilian journalist. The Hand of Fatima (hamsa) is a protective amulet symbol popular in the Middle East and North Africa.
