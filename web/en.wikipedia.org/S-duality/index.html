S-duality

FieldTheoretical physics, string theory
RelatesStrong and weak coupling regimes

S-duality is a relationship between two physical theories in which the strong-coupling regime of one theory corresponds to the weak-coupling regime of the other, typically through an inversion of the coupling constant, g → 1/g. It is a central example of the dualities that connect the various string theories and supersymmetric gauge theories.

Examples

The earliest example is Montonen–Olive duality in N=4 supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory, which exchanges electric and magnetic charges and inverts the gauge coupling. In string theory, S-duality relates Type IIB string theory to itself (a self-duality under the modular group SL(2,Z)), and the Type I string to the SO(32) heterotic string, whose coupling constants are reciprocals of one another.

Role in M-theory

Together with T-duality, which relates theories compactified on circles of radius R and 1/R, S-duality forms part of the web of dualities suggesting that the five consistent superstring theories are limits of a single underlying theory, M-theory. Because calculations that are intractable at strong coupling can be performed at weak coupling in the dual description, S-duality is a powerful tool for studying non-perturbative physics.
