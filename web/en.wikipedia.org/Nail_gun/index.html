Nail gun

A nail gun, nailgun or nailer is a type of tool used to drive nails into wood or other materials. It is usually driven by compressed air (pneumatic), electromagnetism, highly flammable gases such as butane or propane, or, for powder-actuated tools, a small explosive charge. Nail guns have in many ways replaced hammers as tools of choice among builders.

History

The nail gun was designed by Morris Pynoos, an American architect, engineer and inventor, during the 1950s to speed the construction of airplane hangars and other large structures; air-powered nailers were commercialised in the following decade and became widespread in house framing and roofing.

Types

  • Framing nailers drive large nails (typically 60–100 mm) for timber framing; nails are held in sticks or coils.
  • Finish and brad nailers drive thin brads and pins for trim and cabinetry.
  • Roofing nailers use coil magazines of short, wide-headed nails.
  • Powder-actuated tools use a blank cartridge to drive hardened pins into steel or concrete.
  • Cordless nailers are powered by batteries, sometimes combined with a fuel cell.

Mechanisms and safety

Pneumatic nailers use a piston driven by compressed air; combustion nailers ignite a fuel–air mixture; solenoid and flywheel designs are also used. Firing mechanisms include contact-trip ("bump fire") and sequential-trip triggers. Nail guns cause tens of thousands of emergency-room-treated injuries each year, most often puncture wounds to the hands and fingers, and occasionally severe injuries when nails penetrate the skull or heart; safety training and sequential-trip triggers reduce the risk.
