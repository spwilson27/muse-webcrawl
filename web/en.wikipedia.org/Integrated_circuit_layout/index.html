Integrated circuit layout

Integrated circuit layout, also known as IC layout, mask layout, or mask design, is the representation of an integrated circuit in terms of planar geometric shapes corresponding to the patterns of metal, oxide, or semiconductor layers that make up its components.

Process

Layout is performed by layout engineers and designers using electronic design automation (EDA) tools. The process begins from a schematic or netlist and must satisfy design rules imposed by the fabrication process, verified by design rule checking (DRC) and layout versus schematic (LVS) verification. Common file formats for mask data include GDSII and OASIS.

Considerations

Layout designers must account for parasitic capacitance and resistance, electromigration, latch-up, antenna effects, and matching requirements for analog circuits. Techniques such as common-centroid layout improve the matching of transistors. At advanced nodes, layout must also accommodate lithographic effects through resolution enhancement and optical proximity correction.
