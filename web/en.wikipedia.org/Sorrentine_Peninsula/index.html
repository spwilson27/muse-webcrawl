The Sorrentine Peninsula (Italian: Penisola sorrentina) is a peninsula in Campania, southern Italy, separating the Bay of Naples to the north from the Gulf of Salerno to the south. It is named after its main town, Sorrento, on the northern shore.

Geography

The mountainous spine of the peninsula is formed by the Monti Lattari (Lattari Mountains), limestone peaks rising above terraced lemon and olive groves. The northern coast — Sorrento, Meta, Piano di Sorrento, Vico Equense, Castellammare di Stabia — faces Vesuvius across the bay. The southern coast forms the western end of the Amalfi Coast, with Positano and Amalfi beyond Massa Lubrense, the commune at the peninsula's tip, opposite the island of Capri.

Economy and culture

The peninsula is a classic tourist destination, famous since the Grand Tour and the 19th-century era of Tasso and Ibsen. Local products include Sorrento lemons (used for limoncello), olive oil, walnuts, mozzarella and Gragnano pasta. Sorrento is linked to Naples by the Circumvesuviana railway and by hydrofoils; the song "Torna a Surriento" (Come Back to Sorrento) immortalised the town worldwide.
