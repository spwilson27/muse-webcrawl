Tokaj

Tokaj is a town in Borsod-Abaúj-Zemplén county, northern Hungary, at the confluence of the Tisza and Bodrog rivers. It is the centre of the Tokaj wine region, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, famous for Tokaji, the world's oldest classified wine, including the botrytised sweet wine Tokaji Aszú.

Wine

Viticulture around Tokaj dates to Roman times. The region's vineyards were classified in the early 18th century, among the first such classifications in the world. The volcanic soils, the rivers' autumn mists, and the noble rot (Botrytis cinerea) that shrivels grapes on the vine produce wines of distinctive sweetness and acidity. Louis XIV of France is said to have called Tokaji "the wine of kings, the king of wines". Principal grape varieties include Furmint, Hárslevelű, and Sárga Muskotály (Yellow Muscat).

The town

Sights include the Tokaj Museum, the former synagogue now used as a cultural centre, and Rákóczi Castle. The town marks the meeting of the Great Hungarian Plain with the Zemplén Mountains and is a gateway for river tourism and cycling.
