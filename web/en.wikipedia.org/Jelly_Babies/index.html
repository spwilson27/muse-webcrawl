Jelly Babies


Jelly Babies are a type of soft sugar jelly sweet in the shape of plump babies, sold in the United Kingdom and elsewhere.

Jelly Babies are a type of sugar jelly sweet. Like gummy bears, they are small, fruit-flavoured, and made in the shape of plump babies. They are a long-established product of British confectionery, most famously manufactured by Bassett's in Sheffield, and are also made by many other companies.

History

The sweets are generally credited to an Austrian immigrant working for Fryers of Lancashire, who is said to have invented them in 1864; they were originally sold as "Unclaimed Babies." By 1885 they were being made by various firms. Bassett's began mass production in Sheffield in 1918, marketing them as "Peace Babies" to mark the end of the First World War. Production was interrupted during the Second World War by shortages and rationing, and the sweets were relaunched under the name Jelly Babies in 1953.

Characteristics

Jelly Babies have a soft, jelly-like centre and a slightly firmer, delicately crisp coating dusted with starch. In Bassett's assortment, each colour and shape traditionally has its own flavour and name: Brilliant (red, strawberry), Bubbles (yellow, lemon), Boofuls (green, lime), Bigheart (purple, blackcurrant), Bumper (orange), and Baby Bonny (pink, raspberry). The recipe and flavourings have changed over the years; modern versions are made with natural colours and flavourings and reduced sugar.

In popular culture

Jelly Babies became internationally known through the British science-fiction series Doctor Who, in which the Fourth Doctor, played by Tom Baker, frequently offered them from a paper bag. George Harrison of the Beatles was famously fond of them, and fans pelted the band with jelly babies on stage after his preference became known.
