Siachen conflict

The Siachen conflict, sometimes called the Siachen War, is a military conflict between India and Pakistan over the disputed Siachen Glacier region in Kashmir. It began in 1984 with India's successful Operation Meghdoot, which pre-empted Pakistani plans and gave India control of the entire glacier and its tributary passes.

Background

The 1949 Karachi Agreement and the 1972 Simla Agreement delineated the Line of Control in Kashmir only as far as grid point NJ9842, describing the boundary beyond as running "thence north to the glaciers". Both countries interpreted this ambiguity in their favour.

The conflict

On 13 April 1984 Indian troops airlifted onto the Bilafond La and Sia La passes, seizing the commanding heights before Pakistani forces could occupy them. The front that formed along the Saltoro Ridge has been described as the highest battlefield on Earth, with posts above 6,000 metres. Most casualties have resulted from weather, altitude and terrain rather than combat.

Ceasefire

A ceasefire along the Actual Ground Position Line took effect in November 2003 and has largely held, although both armies continue to man the glacier at great cost. Periodic proposals for demilitarisation have foundered on the question of authenticating troop positions.
