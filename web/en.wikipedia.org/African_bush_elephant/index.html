African bush elephant
Scientific name: Loxodonta africana
Conservation status: Endangered (IUCN)
Mass: up to ~6 tonnes; shoulder height up to 3.3 m

The African bush elephant (Loxodonta africana), also known as the African savanna elephant, is one of two extant African elephant species and one of three extant elephant species. It is the largest living terrestrial animal, with bulls standing up to 3.3 metres (10.8 ft) at the shoulder and weighing up to about 6 tonnes.

Description

The bush elephant has a large rounded head, two finger-like projections at the tip of its trunk, large ears resembling the shape of Africa, and long curved tusks of ivory in both sexes. Its skin is grey and heavily wrinkled. It is distinguished from the smaller African forest elephant by its larger size, more triangular ears and differing skull and tusk shape; the IUCN recognised them as separate species in 2021.

Behaviour and conservation

Bush elephants live in matriarchal herds in savannas, grasslands and woodlands across sub-Saharan Africa, feeding on grasses, leaves, fruit and bark and consuming up to 150 kg of vegetation a day. Populations declined sharply due to poaching for ivory and habitat loss; the species was listed as Endangered on the IUCN Red List in 2021.
