The University of Vienna (German: Universität Wien) is a public research university in Vienna, Austria. Founded by Duke Rudolf IV in 1365, it is the oldest university in the German-speaking world and one of the largest universities in Europe, with around 85,000 students enrolled. Its main building, designed by Heinrich Ferstel in Italian Renaissance style, stands on the Ringstraße and was opened in 1884.

History

Modelled on the University of Paris, the university initially lacked a theology faculty until papal approval in 1384. It flourished as a centre of humanism under Maximilian I, declined during the Reformation and Ottoman sieges, and was reformed under Maria Theresa and Joseph II. In the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries it was home to figures including physicist Ludwig Boltzmann, founder of psychoanalysis Sigmund Freud, economist Ludwig von Mises and the Vienna Circle of philosophers. Women were admitted to degree programmes from 1897.

Academics

The university has fifteen faculties and five centres, covering theology, law, business, computer science, humanities, social sciences, natural sciences, life sciences, psychology and sport science. It is associated with seventeen Nobel laureates, including Hans Fischer, Erwin Schrödinger and Konrad Lorenz. Research strengths include quantum physics, molecular biology and Austrian history.

Campus

Besides the main building, the university occupies more than sixty locations across Vienna, including the modern Campus Altes AKH, built in the grounds of the old general hospital, and the Biology Centre in St. Marx.
