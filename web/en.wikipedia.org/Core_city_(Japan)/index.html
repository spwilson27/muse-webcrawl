Core city (Japan)

A core city (中核市, chūkakushi) is a class of Japanese city created by the Local Autonomy Law. Core cities are delegated many functions normally performed by prefectural governments, but not as many as designated cities. To become a core city, a city must have a population of at least 200,000 (the threshold was 300,000 until a 2014 amendment).

The core city system was introduced in 1994 and the first designations took effect in 1996. Core cities take over prefectural responsibilities in areas such as public health (including operating public health centres), welfare, environmental protection, and urban planning. As of the 2020s there are more than 60 core cities, including Toyama, Kanazawa, Gifu, Matsuyama, Kagoshima, and Naha.

History

The category sits between ordinary cities and the larger designated cities (seirei shitei toshi) and the former special cities (tokureishi), the latter abolished in 2015 when their remaining members were promoted to core city status.
