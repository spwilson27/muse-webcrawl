Ulfilas (also Wulfila, "little wolf"; c. 311 – 383) was a bishop, missionary, and translator who spent time in the Roman Empire. He translated the Bible into the Gothic language and participated in the Arian controversy. Ulfilas was ordained a bishop by Eusebius of Nicomedia and returned to his missionary work in the land north of the Danube, where he spent the rest of his life.

The Gothic Bible

To render the Greek Bible into Gothic, Ulfilas devised the Gothic alphabet, adapting Greek letters with some Latin and runic elements. His translation is the earliest substantial text in any Germanic language and the principal source for the Gothic language. Legend held that he omitted the Books of Kings so as not to inflame the Goths' love of war. Surviving portions are preserved in the 6th-century Codex Argenteus in Uppsala, written in silver and gold ink on purple parchment.

Mission and exile

Ulfilas was of Cappadocian Greek descent, his ancestors having been carried off by Gothic raiders. Consecrated bishop of the Goths around 341, he preached among the Visigoths for seven years until persecution by a Gothic chieftain forced him and his converts across the Danube into Roman territory, where Emperor Constantius II settled them near Nicopolis in Moesia (in modern Bulgaria).

Theology

Ulfilas adopted the Arian (more precisely, Homoian) position dominant at the imperial court of his day, confessing the Son to be "like the Father" rather than of one substance with him. Because the Goths received Christianity through Ulfilas, Gothic, Vandal, and Lombard kingdoms remained Arian for centuries, a major factor in early medieval church politics.
