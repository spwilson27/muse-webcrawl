Strathspey, Scotland

Strathspey (Scottish Gaelic: Srath Spè) is the region around the strath of the River Spey, in the northeast of Scotland, in the present Council Areas of Moray and the Highland council area (Badenoch and Strathspey committee area). It extends from the source of the Spey in the Monadhliath Mountains down to the river's mouth at Spey Bay on the Moray Firth, passing through towns and villages including Kingussie, Aviemore, Grantown-on-Spey, Aberlour, Craigellachie and Rothes.

Whisky

Strathspey is the heartland of Scotch whisky production: the Speyside region, centred on the Spey valley and its tributaries, contains more than half of Scotland's malt whisky distilleries, including Glenfiddich, Glenlivet and Macallan. The area is the most densely concentrated whisky region in the world, and "Speyside" is recognised as a distinct geographical indication for Scotch whisky.

Geography and tourism

The upper part of the strath, Badenoch and Strathspey, lies partly within the Cairngorms National Park and is a centre for winter sports (at the Cairngorm Mountain ski area near Aviemore), hillwalking, and fishing for salmon and sea trout on the Spey, one of Scotland's most famous angling rivers. The Strathspey Railway operates preserved steam trains between Aviemore and Broomhill.

Culture

The strath gives its name to the strathspey, a type of Scottish dance music in 4/4 time, slower and more mannered than a reel, characterised by dotted rhythms, and to the dance performed to it. The area was historically the territory of Clan Grant and Clan Macpherson.
