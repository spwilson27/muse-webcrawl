Deaflympics

The Deaflympics are a periodic series of multi-sport events sanctioned by the International Olympic Committee at which deaf athletes compete at an elite level. First held in Paris in 1924 as the International Silent Games, they are the second-oldest international multi-sport event after the Olympic Games themselves, and are organised by the International Committee of Sports for the Deaf (CISS/ICSD).

Format and eligibility

Summer and Winter Deaflympics are held every four years, offset from the Olympic cycle. Athletes must have a hearing loss of at least 55 decibels in their better ear, and hearing aids and cochlear implants are forbidden during competition so that all competitors are on an equal footing. Because athletes cannot hear starting guns, whistles or instructions, events use visual signals such as flashing lights, flags and referees' gestures. Unlike the Paralympics, the Deaflympics are organised by deaf sportspeople themselves, and communication at the games is conducted in sign language.

History

The games were founded by French deaf sports organiser Eugène Rubens-Alcais and grew from nine nations in 1924 to more than one hundred in recent editions. Winter Games were added in 1949. The event was long known as the World Games for the Deaf before adopting the name Deaflympics in 2001. Notable recent editions include the 2017 Summer Games in Samsun, Turkey, and the 2022 Games held in Caxias do Sul, Brazil.
