Do not resuscitate

Do not resuscitate (DNR), also known as do not attempt resuscitation (DNAR), do not attempt cardiopulmonary resuscitation (DNACPR), no code or allow natural death, is a medical order, written or oral depending on jurisdiction, stating that a person should not receive cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) if their heart stops or they stop breathing. A DNR does not affect other treatments, such as pain medicine, other medicines or nutrition, though in practice there is concern that it is sometimes misinterpreted as limiting broader care.

Legal basis

DNR orders rest on the rights of patients (or their surrogates) to refuse treatment, recognised in case law such as Cruzan (1990) in the United States. Many jurisdictions provide for advance directives, portable orders such as the POLST/MOLST paradigm, and out-of-hospital DNR forms and bracelets that emergency medical services must honour.

Ethics and practice

CPR survival rates are low for patients with advanced terminal illness, and guidelines encourage discussing resuscitation preferences as part of end-of-life care planning. Debates concern unilateral DNR orders written without consent in cases judged "futile", disparities in how orders are discussed across patient groups, and documentation quality. In the United Kingdom, DNACPR decisions came under public scrutiny during the COVID-19 pandemic, and the Care Quality Commission reviewed practice in 2021.

Terminology variants

"Allow natural death" (AND) is preferred by some clinicians as less negative framing. Related orders include "do not intubate" (DNI) and comfort measures only (CMO).
