Infant mortality

DefinitionDeath of a child before the first birthday
MeasureInfant mortality rate (IMR) per 1,000 live births
Global IMR (2022)Approximately 28 per 1,000 live births

Infant mortality is the death of an infant before his or her first birthday. The infant mortality rate (IMR) is the number of deaths of infants under one year of age per 1,000 live births in the same period, and it is widely used as an indicator of the overall health, nutrition, and socioeconomic conditions of a population.

Causes

Worldwide, the leading causes of infant death include preterm birth complications, intrapartum-related events such as birth asphyxia, pneumonia, diarrhoea, malaria, and congenital anomalies. A large share of deaths occur in the neonatal period (the first 28 days of life), and many are preventable with access to skilled birth attendance, immunisation, clean water, adequate nutrition, and timely medical care.

Trends

Global infant mortality has fallen dramatically over the past century. In 1990 the world IMR was about 65 deaths per 1,000 live births; by the early 2020s it had dropped below 30. The declines reflect improvements in sanitation, vaccination, maternal education, and access to healthcare, though rates remain far higher in sub-Saharan Africa and parts of South Asia than in Europe or East Asia.

Determinants

Infant mortality is strongly associated with maternal health and education, household income, birth spacing, breastfeeding practices, and the quality of local health systems. Public health interventions such as oral rehydration therapy, insecticide-treated bed nets, and expanded immunisation programmes have been credited with major reductions in many countries.
