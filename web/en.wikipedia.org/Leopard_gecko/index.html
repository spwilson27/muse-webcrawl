Leopard gecko

The leopard gecko (Eublepharis macularius) is a ground-dwelling lizard native to rocky, arid regions of Afghanistan, Pakistan, Iran and northern India. Named for the spotted pattern of wild adults, it is one of the most commonly kept pet reptiles in the world.

Biology

Leopard geckos are nocturnal, sheltering by day and hunting insects at night. Unlike most geckos they have movable eyelids and lack adhesive toe pads, reflecting their terrestrial life. They store fat in their tails and can shed the tail when threatened, regrowing a shorter replacement.

In captivity

Decades of captive breeding have produced many colour and pattern morphs, from albinos to patternless forms. They are hardy, long-lived — often 15 to 20 years — and breed readily, which made them a standard pet-trade and laboratory lizard.
