Crystal momentum

In solid-state physics, crystal momentum or quasimomentum is a momentum-like vector associated with electrons and other quasiparticles in a crystal lattice. It is defined from the wavevector of a Bloch state and, unlike true momentum, is conserved only modulo additions of a reciprocal lattice vector.

Definition

For a Bloch wave with wavevector k, the crystal momentum is p = ħk. Because states differing by a reciprocal lattice vector are equivalent, crystal momentum is usually restricted to the first Brillouin zone.

Role in physics

Crystal momentum governs electronic band structure, selection rules for optical transitions, and transport phenomena. In scattering processes such as phonon interactions, the sum of crystal momenta is conserved up to a reciprocal lattice vector — the difference between normal processes and Umklapp processes, the latter enabling thermal resistance in insulators.
