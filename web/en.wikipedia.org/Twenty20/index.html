Twenty20 (often abbreviated to T20) is a shortened format of cricket in which each team bats for a single innings of at most twenty overs. A typical match lasts about three hours and was designed to produce a fast-paced, television-friendly game with frequent boundaries and a decisive result within a single evening.

History

The format was devised by the England and Wales Cricket Board (ECB) as a response to declining attendances at county matches, and the first professional competition, the Twenty20 Cup, was played in England in 2003. The first Twenty20 International took place in 2005 between Australia and New Zealand. The inaugural ICC World Twenty20 was held in South Africa in 2007 and won by India, whose victory helped ignite the format's popularity in the subcontinent.

Leagues and impact

Franchise leagues — most prominently the Indian Premier League (founded 2008), Australia's Big Bash League and the Caribbean Premier League — have made Twenty20 the commercially dominant form of the game, transforming player economics through auctions and broadcasting deals. The format's aggressive batting, athletic fielding and innovations such as the scoop and switch hit have also fed back into one-day and even Test cricket.

Rules

Each side fields eleven players; bowlers may deliver at most four overs each. Fielding restrictions in the first six overs (the "powerplay") allow only two fielders outside the circle. Tied matches may be decided by a Super Over. A free hit follows a front-foot no-ball.
