Chief petty officer

Chief petty officer (CPO) is the seventh enlisted rank (with the pay grade E-7) in the United States Navy and U.S. Coast Guard, just above petty officer first class and below senior chief petty officer. The term "rating" is used to identify enlisted job specialties. In this way, enlisted personnel are segregated into three segments: (1) the division of responsibility and training—the rating; (2) the division of military authority and precedence—the pay grade; and (3) the division of status and technical authority—the rate.

History

The United States Navy formally established the rank of chief petty officer on 1 April 1893. Chiefs are regarded as the backbone of naval leadership: they combine technical expertise in their rating with deckplate leadership of junior sailors. Advancement to chief is by selection board and is marked by a formal initiation and transition training. In the Royal Navy and other Commonwealth navies, chief petty officer is likewise a senior non-commissioned rate (in Canada, chief petty officer is divided into 2nd and 1st classes).

Role

A chief petty officer serves as both a technical expert and a leader, typically supervising a division or work centre and advising officers. The Chiefs Mess (the collective body of chiefs aboard ship or at a command) is a distinctive institution, and the fouled anchor is the emblem of the rank. Chiefs wear a uniform distinct from junior enlisted sailors, similar in style to officers' uniforms but with different insignia.
