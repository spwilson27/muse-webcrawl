Funafuti

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Funafuti is an atoll of the Pacific island nation of Tuvalu and serves as its capital. It consists of a narrow sweep of land between about 20 and 400 metres wide, encircling a large lagoon (Te Namo) some 18 kilometres long. With a population of roughly 6,000–6,300, Funafuti is home to about 60 percent of Tuvalu's population and contains the country's government offices, its only hospital, its main port, and Funafuti International Airport.

Geography

The atoll comprises at least 29 islets surrounding a lagoon entered by several reef passages. The largest islet, Fongafale, hosts the main village, Vaiaku, where the government buildings stand, as well as the airstrip — built by United States naval forces in 1943 during the Pacific War, when Funafuti served as an Allied base. The airstrip doubles as the island's principal public open space, used for football and gatherings when no plane is due. The Funafuti Conservation Area, established in 1996, protects marine habitat on the western side of the lagoon.

History

Tuvaluan oral tradition credits the founding ancestor of Funafuti as Telematua. The atoll was sighted by Europeans in 1819 (by Arent de Peyster) and later visited by the United States Exploring Expedition. In the late nineteenth century, scientists of the Royal Society drilled boreholes on Funafuti (1896–1898) to test Charles Darwin's theory of atoll formation by subsidence; the results supported Darwin. Tuvalu became independent in 1978 with Funafuti as its capital.

Climate change

Funafuti's land averages less than 3 metres above sea level, making it acutely vulnerable to sea-level rise and king tides, which periodically flood low-lying areas and salinise the freshwater lens. Tuvalu has become a prominent voice in international climate negotiations, and in 2023 signed the Falepili Union treaty with Australia, which includes a climate-migration pathway.
