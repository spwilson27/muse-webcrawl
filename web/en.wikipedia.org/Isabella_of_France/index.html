Isabella of France

Queen consort of England: 1308–1327

Born: c. 1295, Paris, France

Died: 22 August 1358, Hertford Castle, England

Spouse: Edward II of England

Issue: Edward III, John of Eltham, Eleanor of Woodstock, Joan of the Tower

Isabella of France (c. 1295 – 22 August 1358), sometimes described as the She-Wolf of France (French: Louve de France), was Queen of England as the wife of King Edward II, and regent of England from 1327 until 1330. She was the daughter of Philip IV of France and Joan I of Navarre, and the only surviving daughter of Philip to produce a surviving son.

Marriage and conflict

Isabella married Edward II at Boulogne in January 1308, aged about twelve. The marriage was strained by Edward's reliance on favourites — first Piers Gaveston, then Hugh Despenser the Younger, whose land-grabbing and influence drove the queen from court and her lands were seized in 1324. In 1325 she travelled to France to negotiate peace, refused to return, and aligned with the exiled Roger Mortimer.

Invasion and regency

In September 1326 Isabella and Mortimer invaded England with a small force; support for Edward collapsed, he was captured and forced to abdicate in January 1327, and died that year at Berkeley Castle, probably murdered. Isabella and Mortimer ruled as regents for the young Edward III, but their greed and an unpopular peace with Scotland made them hated. In 1330 Edward III seized power in a coup at Nottingham Castle; Mortimer was executed and Isabella retired honourably, living another 28 years and remaining close to her son. She was buried in the Franciscan church at Newgate, London, in her wedding dress.
