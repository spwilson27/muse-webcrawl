Pozole

Pozole (Spanish pronunciation: [poˈsole]; from Nahuatl pozolli, meaning "hominy") is a traditional soup or stew from Mexican cuisine. It is made from hominy with meat (typically pork, but also chicken), and can be seasoned and garnished with shredded lettuce or cabbage, chilli peppers, onion, garlic, radishes, avocado, salsa or limes. Known in Mesoamerica since the pre-Columbian era, today the stew is common across Mexico and neighbouring countries, and is often served as a celebratory dish.

Varieties

The best-known styles are named for the colour of their broth: pozole rojo (red), made with dried red chillies such as guajillo, ancho, or de árbol, characteristic of Jalisco; pozole verde (green), often associated with Guerrero, prepared with tomatillos, epazote, jalapeños, and sometimes ground pumpkin seeds (pipián); and pozole blanco (white), an uncoloured hominy broth. Other regional variations include seafood pozole on the Pacific coast, pozole de trigo (made with wheat instead of corn), and the Day of the Dead and Independence Day celebrations in which pozole commonly figures. The dish is traditionally prepared for Mexican Independence Day (16 September), Christmas Eve, birthdays, and other festivities.

History

Pozole originated with the Indigenous peoples of Mesoamerica, where maize was sacred and hominy (cacahuazintle corn treated with lime, in a process called nixtamalization) was prepared for special occasions. Accounts in colonial sources, including the Florentine Codex, describe pozole as a ritual dish in Aztec ceremonies; the meat used before the Spanish conquest reflected ritual practice, and after colonization pork became standard because pigs were plentiful and inexpensive.

Preparation

To make pozole, dried hominy is soaked and simmered until it blooms, or canned hominy is used; meat is cooked in the same pot with onion and garlic, then the broth is seasoned. Diners garnish their own bowls at the table with shredded lettuce or cabbage, sliced radishes, diced onion, dried oregano, ground chilli, and lime, accompanied by tostadas, often spread with cream.
