Date23 October 1702
LocationVigo Bay, Galicia, Spain
ResultAnglo-Dutch victory
BelligerentsEngland and the Dutch Republic; France and Spain

The Battle of Vigo Bay was a naval battle fought on 23 October 1702 during the War of the Spanish Succession. An Anglo-Dutch fleet under Admiral Sir George Rooke attacked a Franco-Spanish treasure fleet that had taken refuge in Vigo Bay in northwestern Spain, escorted by French warships under the Count of Château-Renault.

Fresh from the failure to take Cádiz, Rooke learned that the Spanish silver fleet from America had put into Vigo. The harbour entrance was defended by a boom and shore batteries, but the boom was broken by the torpedo-like rush of HMS Torbay, and troops landed under the Duke of Ormonde stormed the forts. Every French and Spanish ship in the bay was captured or destroyed, though much of the silver had already been unloaded and carried inland; the allies still seized a substantial haul of treasure and merchandise.

Legacy

Vigo was the first major Allied success of the war and restored Rooke's reputation. In England the captured silver was minted into coins stamped "VIGO", and the battle is fictionalised in the opening of Jules Verne's Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas, where Captain Nemo salvages its sunken treasure.
