Defecation

Defecation is the final act of digestion, by which organisms eliminate solid or semisolid waste material (feces) from the digestive tract via the anus. In humans it normally occurs from a few times a day to a few times a week, varying with diet, health, and habit.

Physiology

Waste accumulates in the rectum, whose stretching triggers the defecation reflex: the internal anal sphincter relaxes involuntarily while the external sphincter remains under voluntary control. When defecation proceeds, peristaltic contractions of the rectum and relaxation of the sphincters, often assisted by abdominal muscle contraction (the Valsalva manoeuvre), expel the feces.

Health aspects

Disorders of defecation include constipation, diarrhoea, and fecal incontinence. Posture affects ease of defecation; squatting aligns the rectum differently from sitting, a factor invoked in discussions of toilet design. Open defecation — still practised where sanitation is lacking — is a significant public-health issue addressed by global sanitation programmes.

Cultural aspects

Defecation is surrounded by strong cultural taboos and norms of privacy in most societies, shaping architecture (toilets and latrines), hygiene practices such as hand washing and anal cleansing with water or paper, and a large body of euphemism.
