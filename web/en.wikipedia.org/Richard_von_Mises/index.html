Richard Edler von Mises (19 April 1883 – 14 July 1953) was an Austrian scientist and mathematician who worked on solid mechanics, fluid mechanics, aerodynamics, aeronautics, statistics and probability theory. He held the position of Gordon McKay Professor of Aerodynamics and Applied Mathematics at Harvard University. He was the younger brother of the economist Ludwig von Mises.

Von Mises is perhaps best known for the frequency interpretation of probability, developed in his Wahrscheinlichkeit, Statistik und Wahrheit (1928; English: Probability, Statistics and Truth), which grounded probability in the limiting relative frequencies of "collectives" (infinite sequences of observations); and in engineering for the von Mises yield criterion used in the theory of plasticity, and the von Mises distribution of circular statistics.

Life

Von Mises was born in Lemberg (now Lviv) into a Jewish family ennobled in the Austrian Empire; his father Arthur Edler von Mises was a railway engineer. He studied mechanical engineering at the Vienna University of Technology, receiving his doctorate in 1907, and lectured at Brno before becoming professor of applied mathematics at Strasbourg in 1909. During the First World War he served as a pilot and aeronautical engineer for Austria-Hungary, designing and supervising construction of a large military aircraft (the "Mises-Flugzeug") and writing an early treatise on flight theory. In 1919 he became director of the new Institute of Applied Mathematics at the University of Berlin, where he edited the Zeitschrift für Angewandte Mathematik und Mechanik (founded 1921) and made Berlin a centre of applied mathematics.

Dismissed under Nazi racial laws in 1933, he emigrated to Turkey, becoming professor at Istanbul University, and in 1939 moved to the United States and Harvard, where he remained until his death in Boston in 1953. He was married to the mathematician Hilda Geiringer. Beyond mathematics, von Mises was a notable scholar of the poet Rainer Maria Rilke and a member of the intellectual world remembered in his writings on positivism.

Work

In probability, von Mises defined a collective as an infinite sequence in which relative frequencies converge and are invariant under admissible place selections; probability is the limiting frequency. The axiomatic criticisms raised against place selection led later workers (notably Abraham Wald and Alonzo Church) to refine the idea, which survives as an ancestor of algorithmic randomness. His 1919 paper founded the field of directional (circular) statistics through the distribution that bears his name, the analogue of the normal distribution on a circle.

In mechanics he proposed (1913) the yield criterion that plastic yielding begins when the second invariant of the deviatoric stress reaches a critical value — widely used as the von Mises stress in finite element analysis — and made contributions to boundary-layer theory, plasticity, and numerical analysis, including the power method (von Mises iteration) for eigenvalues. With Hilda Geiringer he wrote on statistical mechanics and the theory of flight; his Mathematical Theory of Compressible Fluid Flow was completed posthumously by Geiringer and G. S. S. Ludford (1958).
