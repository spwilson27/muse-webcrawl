Citroën DS

Citroën DS
Manufacturer: Citroën
Production: 1955–1975
Class: Mid-size luxury / executive car
Designer: Flaminio Bertoni

The Citroën DS is a front mid-engined, front-wheel drive executive car manufactured and marketed by the French company Citroën from 1955 to 1975, in fastback/sedan, wagon/estate, and convertible body configurations, across three series of one generation.

Marketed with a less expensive variant, the Citroën ID, the DS was known for its aerodynamic, futuristic body design; unorthodox, quirky, and innovative technology, and an interior set the standard for ride quality, handling, and braking. The DS was the first mass-produced car equipped with hydropneumatic suspension, as well as disc brakes.

Unveiling and reception

After 18 years of secret development as the successor to the Traction Avant, the DS 19 was introduced on 6 October 1955 at the Paris Motor Show. In the first 15 minutes of the show, 743 orders were taken, and orders for the first day totalled 12,000. The car's avant-garde design by Italian sculptor and industrial designer Flaminio Bertoni made it an icon of French design and engineering.

Technology

The DS featured a hydropneumatic self-levelling suspension, power steering, a semi-automatic gearbox, and inboard front disc brakes. Later models introduced swivelling headlamps that turned with the steering wheel. The spare wheel and jack were stored under the bonnet.

Motorsport and legacy

The DS achieved success in rallying, including wins at the Monte Carlo Rally, and remains celebrated as one of the most influential car designs of the 20th century; it was named the most beautiful car of all time by Classic & Sports Car magazine. Nearly 1.5 million were built.
