Hash browns

Hash browns, also spelled hashed browns, are a popular American breakfast food consisting of finely julienned potatoes that have been pan-fried until golden brown and crisp, often in a rectangular patty. They are a staple item on breakfast menus in diners in North America and the United Kingdom, where they form part of a traditional full breakfast.

History

Hash browns began appearing on menus in New York City in the 1890s, the name deriving from the French hacher, "to chop", by way of "hashed brown potatoes". Originally a way to use leftover boiled potatoes, the dish evolved into a preparation of raw or par-cooked shredded potato fried on a griddle. Frozen prepared hash brown patties became widespread in the mid-20th century with the growth of fast food; McDonald's introduced its hash brown patty in the 1970s with its breakfast menu and now sells them in many countries. American diner chains such as Waffle House offer hash browns with a celebrated range of customization options — "scattered, smothered, covered, chunked, diced, peppered, capped, topped, and country".

Preparation

Potatoes are grated or shredded, rinsed and squeezed dry to remove starch and water, seasoned, and fried in oil, butter, or fat on a hot griddle until a crisp crust forms. Variations include loose shreds, formed patties, and diced "home fries" (technically a distinct dish). Hash browns are typically served with eggs, bacon, and toast at breakfast. Similar dishes elsewhere include Swiss rösti, the Jewish latke, and the Spanish tortilla's potato base, though rösti is usually made without flour or egg binders and cooked as a single cake.
