Tirzepatide, sold under the brand names Mounjaro and Zepbound, is an antidiabetic and weight-loss medication developed by Eli Lilly and Company. It is a dual agonist of the glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP) and glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptors, given as a once-weekly subcutaneous injection. It is used for the treatment of type 2 diabetes and, as Zepbound, for chronic weight management in adults with obesity or overweight.

Medical uses

Tirzepatide was approved by the US Food and Drug Administration in May 2022 for improving glycaemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes. In the SURPASS clinical trial programme it reduced glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c) more than comparators including insulin and semaglutide. In November 2023 it was approved for chronic weight management; in the SURMOUNT-1 trial, participants lost on average up to about 21% of body weight at the highest dose over 72 weeks. In December 2024 it was also approved for moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea in adults with obesity.

Mechanism of action

Tirzepatide is a synthetic peptide based on the structure of GIP, engineered to activate both GIP and GLP-1 receptors. These incretin pathways enhance glucose-dependent insulin secretion, suppress glucagon, slow gastric emptying and reduce appetite. A fatty-acid side chain binds albumin, extending its half-life to about five days and permitting weekly dosing.

Adverse effects

The most common adverse effects are gastrointestinal: nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation and decreased appetite, which are usually dose-dependent and most pronounced during dose escalation. The labelling carries a boxed warning for the risk of thyroid C-cell tumours observed in rodents; it is contraindicated in people with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2. Other concerns include pancreatitis, gastroparesis and gallbladder disease.
