Title

A title is, in its various senses, a name or designation: the name of a creative work, an honorific or office held by a person, or a legal term for ownership rights in property. The word derives from Latin titulus, an inscription or label.

Works

The title of a book, film, song or other work identifies it and often hints at its theme or content. Works may have subtitles, working titles used during production, and different titles in different markets. Titles themselves are generally not protected by copyright, though they may be protected as trademarks.

Personal titles

Personal titles include forms of address and honorifics (Mr, Mrs, Dr, Professor), titles of nobility (Duke, Count, Baron), royal styles (His Majesty), ecclesiastical titles (Pope, Bishop) and professional or military ranks. Their correct use is governed by etiquette and, historically, by law.

Legal title

In property law, title is the bundle of rights evidencing ownership of real or personal property. A title deed documents ownership of land; vehicle titles register ownership of cars and boats. Title insurance protects buyers and lenders against defects in a property's title, and land registration systems (such as the Torrens system) record title officially.
