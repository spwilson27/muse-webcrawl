Universal Media Disc

Media type: Optical disc
Encoding: Red Book audio / video
Capacity: ~900 MB – 2 GB
Developed by: Sony

The Universal Media Disc (UMD) is a discontinued optical disc medium developed by Sony for use on its PlayStation Portable (PSP) handheld game console. UMDs could store up to 1.8 gigabytes of data on a dual-layer disc, including full-length feature films, television shows and video games.

Design

The disc itself is 60 mm in diameter and is permanently housed in a protective caddy with a spring-loaded shutter, protecting the surface from scratches and fingerprints. The casing uses a proprietary format, and the discs include region coding similar to that used on DVDs.

History

UMD was introduced in 2003 alongside the PSP. Sony marketed the format aggressively for movies, and several major Hollywood studios released films on UMD. However, sales of movies were disappointing: the discs could only be played on the PSP, and prices were often higher than the equivalent DVD. By 2006 most studios had withdrawn support, and the format survived principally as a game medium until the PSP line was discontinued.

Technical specifications

Single-layer UMDs hold about 900 MB and dual-layer versions about 1.8 GB. Game discs use a 660 nm red laser, and the format includes strong copy protection with a unique disc ID system.
