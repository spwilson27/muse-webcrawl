Exponential map (Lie theory)

The exponential map sends Lie algebra elements to group elements.

In the theory of Lie groups, the exponential map is a map from the Lie algebra 𝔤 of a Lie group G to G itself, generalizing the ordinary exponential function. For a matrix Lie group it is given by the matrix exponential: exp(X) = Σ Xk/k!. In general, exp(X) is defined as γ(1), where γ is the unique one-parameter subgroup of G whose tangent vector at the identity is X.

Properties

The exponential map is smooth, and its derivative at the origin is the identity, so by the inverse function theorem it is a diffeomorphism from a neighborhood of 0 in 𝔤 onto a neighborhood of the identity in G. It intertwines scaling in the algebra with the one-parameter subgroup: exp(tX) = γ(t). For compact connected Lie groups, the exponential map is surjective: every group element lies on some one-parameter subgroup. It is not surjective in general—for example, some elements of SL(2, ℝ) are not exponentials of real matrices.

Examples

For the circle group, the exponential map wraps the real line onto the circle. For SO(3), exp maps a vector representing an axis and angle to the corresponding rotation. In quantum mechanics and control theory, exponentials of Lie algebra elements generate rotations, Lorentz transformations, and other continuous symmetries.

Related concepts

The logarithm map is a local inverse of the exponential map near the identity. The Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff formula expresses the product exp(X)exp(Y) as a single exponential whose exponent is a series in X, Y and their commutators.
