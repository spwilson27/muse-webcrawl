Sloan Digital Sky Survey

The Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) is a major multi-spectral imaging and spectroscopic redshift survey using a dedicated 2.5-metre wide-angle optical telescope at Apache Point Observatory in New Mexico. Begun in 2000, it has created some of the most detailed three-dimensional maps of the Universe ever made, and its data releases are freely available to the public.

Observations

The survey's camera imaged the sky in five filters (u, g, r, i, z), covering more than a quarter of the sky and cataloguing hundreds of millions of stars and galaxies. Fibre-optic spectrographs measured spectra — and hence redshifts and distances — for millions of galaxies, quasars, and stars. Successive phases (SDSS-I through SDSS-V) added projects such as SEGUE (Galactic structure), the BOSS and eBOSS baryon oscillation surveys, APOGEE (infrared stellar spectroscopy), and MaNGA (integral-field spectroscopy of galaxies). SDSS-V added an all-sky spectroscopic programme with telescopes in both hemispheres.

Impact

SDSS data have been used in tens of thousands of papers across topics from asteroid families and stellar populations to the large-scale structure of the cosmos and dark energy. Its open-data model influenced later surveys, and its SkyServer and citizen-science offspring such as Galaxy Zoo made the catalogue a public resource.
