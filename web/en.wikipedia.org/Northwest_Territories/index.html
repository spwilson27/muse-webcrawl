Northwest Territories

The Northwest Territories (French: Territoires du Nord-Ouest; commonly abbreviated as NWT) is a federal territory of Canada. At a land area of approximately 1,144,000 km² (442,000 sq mi) and a population of around 41,000–45,000, it is the second-largest and the most populous of the three territories in Northern Canada. Its capital, largest community, and only city is Yellowknife.

Boreal forest and tundra cover much of the territory.

History

The present-day territory originated from the vast North-Western Territory and Rupert's Land transferred from the Hudson's Bay Company to Canada in 1870. Over time, provinces and the territory of Yukon were carved from it, and on 1 April 1999 its eastern portion became the new territory of Nunavut following the Nunavut Act and the settlement of the Inuit land claim. The Indigenous peoples of the region — including the Dene, Inuvialuit and Métis — have inhabited the land for thousands of years, and several comprehensive land-claim agreements govern much of the territory.

Geography

The territory stretches from the 60th parallel north to the Arctic Ocean and includes Great Slave Lake and Great Bear Lake, among the largest lakes entirely in Canada, as well as part of the Mackenzie River, the second-longest river system in North America. It borders Yukon to the west, British Columbia, Alberta and Saskatchewan to the south, and Nunavut to the east.

Economy

The economy is heavily based on natural resources: diamond mining (the Ekati and Diavik mines), gold, oil and natural gas, and hydroelectricity. Tourism, fishing and aurora viewing also contribute.

Demographics and languages

Roughly half the population is Indigenous. The Northwest Territories is the only jurisdiction in Canada that recognises eleven official languages, including Chipewyan (Dënesųłıné), Cree, Gwich'in, Inuinnaqtun, Inuktitut, Inuvialuktun, North and South Slavey, and Tłı̨chǫ, alongside English and French.
