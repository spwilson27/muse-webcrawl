Zygote

A zygote (from Ancient Greek ζυγωτός zygōtós, "joined, yoked") is a eukaryotic cell formed by a fertilisation event between two gametes. The zygote's genome is a combination of the DNA in each gamete, and contains all of the genetic information of a new individual organism.

In multicellular organisms, the zygote is the earliest developmental stage. In humans and most other anisogamous organisms, a zygote is formed when an egg cell and a sperm cell come together to create a new unique organism. The zygote subsequently undergoes rapid mitotic divisions — a process termed cleavage — to form a blastocyst, which implants in the uterus.

Development

During fertilisation, the sperm's plasma membrane fuses with the egg's membrane, triggering changes (the cortical reaction) that prevent additional sperm from entering. The male and female pronuclei then unite, restoring the diploid number of chromosomes. In mammals, the zygote stage ends when the first cleavage division occurs; thereafter it is considered a pre-embryo, then an embryo.

In other organisms

In plants, the zygote typically develops within the archegonium after fertilisation and later gives rise to the sporophyte generation. In fungi, a zygote formed by karyogamy may enter a resting state before undergoing meiosis. Some single-celled organisms can reproduce by zygote formation followed by meiosis and mitotic growth.
