Indian martial arts

Indian martial arts refers to the fighting systems of the Indian subcontinent. A variety of terms are used for the English phrases "Indian martial arts", usually deriving from the Dravidian, Sanskrit or combined forms. A common term is shastravidya, from the words shastra (weapon) and vidya (knowledge). The earliest recorded martial traditions of India appear in the Vedic period, where the Dhanurveda, an ancient treatise on warfare and archery appended to the Vedas, codified the principles of military science.

History

In the 3rd century BCE, the Sushruta Samhita identified 107 vital points (marma) on the human body, of which 64 were classified as lethal if struck properly. Combat techniques were refined over centuries in the military camps of the subcontinent, and regional styles developed distinctive weapons and methods: the sword-centred traditions of Rajasthan and the Maratha states, the wrestling culture of the akharas, the stick-fighting lathi systems of Bengal and Punjab, and the sophisticated bare-hand and weapons system of Kerala known as kalaripayattu, often regarded as one of the oldest surviving martial arts in the world.

Major styles

  • Kalaripayattu (Kerala) — weapons, strikes, grappling and marma point knowledge, practised in the kalari pit.
  • Silambam (Tamil Nadu) — fighting primarily with the bamboo staff (silambam), also sword, spear and bare-hand.
  • Thang ta (Manipur) — "the art of the sword and spear", combining combat with ritual dance.
  • Gatka (Punjab) — Sikh martial art using wooden sticks simulating swords.
  • Pehlwani — South Asian wrestling practised in the akhara, blending indigenous malla-yuddha with Persian influences.
  • Mardani khel (Maharashtra) — Maratha weapons art using the pata gauntlet sword and vita corded lance.
  • Thoda (Himachal Pradesh) — a martial dance form based on archery.
  • Sqay (Kashmir) — sword and shield art.

Influence

Through Buddhist missionaries and trade, Indian fighting methods influenced the martial arts of Southeast Asia (such as bokator and pencak silat) and, by tradition, the Shaolin arts of China, via the legendary figure of Bodhidharma. Today the Indian martial arts are promoted by state cultural bodies, and kalaripayattu and silambam have gained international recognition.
