Black Warrior River

The Black Warrior River is a waterway in west-central Alabama, about 178 miles (286 km) long, formed by the confluence of the Mulberry Fork and Locust Fork in Jefferson County. It flows southwest to join the Tombigbee River near Demopolis, forming part of the Mobile River system draining to the Gulf of Mexico.

Course and navigation

The river passes the city of Tuscaloosa, which grew at the fall line where navigation from below was historically blocked by shoals. A series of locks and dams, built and maintained by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, makes the river navigable for barges carrying coal and other bulk goods from the Birmingham area to the Port of Mobile.

Name and history

The name derives from the Choctaw chief Tuskaloosa ("Black Warrior"), killed in the Battle of Mabila in 1540 during Hernando de Soto's expedition. Coal mining in the river's basin was central to Birmingham's growth as an industrial centre.
