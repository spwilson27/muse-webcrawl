Semper Fidelis

Semper Fidelis (Latin for "Always Faithful" or "Always Loyal") is the motto of the United States Marine Corps. It was adopted in 1883 as the third motto of the Corps; the previous mottos were Fortitudine (With Fortitude) and Per Mare, Per Terram (By Sea, By Land). The phrase is often shortened to "Semper Fi" in informal use.

Usage

The motto expresses the perpetual loyalty of Marines to the Corps and to their fellow Marines, even after leaving the service. It is a central element of Marine Corps esprit de corps, and departing Marines are often bid "Semper Fi" as a farewell. The phrase is also the title of the official march of the Marine Corps, composed by John Philip Sousa in 1888.

Other users

Semper Fidelis has been used as a motto by many other organizations and families throughout history, including the city of Exeter in England (since 1660), the Swiss Grenadier Regiment, the French 47th Infantry Regiment, and numerous schools and military units around the world. It appears in heraldry as a declaration of loyalty to a lord, city, or sovereign.

The march

"Semper Fidelis" is also the name of the official march of the United States Marine Corps, composed by John Philip Sousa in 1888 at the request of Commandant Charles Heywood. Sousa regarded it as his "most musical" march.
