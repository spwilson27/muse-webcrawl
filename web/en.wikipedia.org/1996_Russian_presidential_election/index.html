1996 Russian presidential election

The 1996 Russian presidential election was held in Russia on 16 June 1996, with a second-round runoff on 3 July. Incumbent President Boris Yeltsin defeated Communist Party candidate Gennady Zyuganov in the runoff, winning re-election despite approval ratings that had sunk to single digits early in the year. The campaign is remembered for the overwhelming support Yeltsin received from Russia's new business oligarchs and media, for Western backing, and for enduring allegations of fraud and of concealed illness.

Background

Yeltsin's first term brought economic collapse for many Russians during shock-therapy reforms and the unpopular First Chechen War. Zyuganov's Communists had won the 1995 parliamentary elections, and in early 1996 Yeltsin's approval rating was around 6 percent. Fearing a Communist return to power, Russia's leading bankers and media owners — the group around Boris Berezovsky that had issued the "Davos pact" appeal — bankrolled Yeltsin's campaign, and oligarch-owned television gave it saturation, largely favourable coverage.

The campaign

The Yeltsin campaign, advised by his daughter Tatyana Dyachenko and campaign manager Anatoly Chubais, presented the election as a choice between reform and a return to the Soviet past, using the slogan "Vote with your heart". Yeltsin campaigned energetically in public despite suffering a heart attack between the two rounds, which was concealed from voters. In the first round on 16 June, Yeltsin won 35.3 percent to Zyuganov's 32.0 percent; retired general Alexander Lebed finished third with 14.5 percent and was appointed secretary of the Security Council, bringing his supporters behind Yeltsin.

Results and controversies

In the runoff on 3 July, Yeltsin won 53.8 percent against Zyuganov's 40.3 percent. International observers assessed the vote as generally reflecting the electorate's will despite media bias, but later analyses and statements by participants have alleged manipulation of counts in some regions. Yeltsin was inaugurated on 9 August 1996 in a shortened ceremony; he underwent quintuple heart bypass surgery that November and never fully regained his health or authority, resigning on 31 December 1999 in favour of Vladimir Putin.

Significance

The election fixed the alliance between the Kremlin and the oligarchs forged in the 1995 loans-for-shares privatisations, and it is often cited as a turning point at which Russian electoral politics became dominated by money, media control, and administrative resources.
