Stereolithography

Type: Additive manufacturing (vat photopolymerisation)
Invented: 1980s, Chuck Hull
File format: STL
Materials: Photopolymer resins

Stereolithography (SLA or SL) is a form of 3D printing technology used for creating models, prototypes, patterns, and production parts, in which a laser (or other light source) cures liquid photopolymer resin layer by layer into hardened plastic. It was the first rapid prototyping technology to be commercialised.

History

The process was pioneered by Hideo Kodama in Japan and patented by Chuck Hull in 1986; Hull co-founded 3D Systems, whose SLA-1 machine of 1987 launched the commercial 3D printing industry. Hull also devised the STL file format, still standard for 3D printing.

Process

A build platform is submerged in a vat of liquid resin. An ultraviolet laser traces each cross-section of the model on the resin surface, curing and solidifying it; the platform then moves by one layer thickness (often 25–100 micrometres) and the next layer is traced. Variants include masked stereolithography (MSLA) and digital light processing (DLP), which project an entire layer at once. Finished parts typically require washing in solvent and post-curing under ultraviolet light. SLA produces parts with high accuracy and smooth surfaces, and is widely used in dentistry, jewellery, engineering prototyping and hearing aids.
