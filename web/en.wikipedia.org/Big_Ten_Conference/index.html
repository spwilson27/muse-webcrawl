Big Ten Conference

Big Ten Conference
Founded: 1896
Association: NCAA Division I FBS
Members: 18 (from 2024)
Headquarters: Rosemont, Illinois

The Big Ten Conference (stylised as B1G) is the oldest Division I collegiate athletic conference in the United States. Founded in 1896 as the Intercollegiate Conference of Faculty Representatives, it is based in Rosemont, Illinois. Its members are predominantly large public research universities in the Midwest, and following expansion the conference spans from the East Coast to the West Coast.

History

The conference was founded at a meeting in Chicago in 1896 by seven universities, including Chicago, Illinois, Michigan, Minnesota, Northwestern, Purdue, and Wisconsin. Despite its name, membership grew to eleven with Penn State in 1990, twelve with Nebraska in 2011, fourteen with Maryland and Rutgers in 2014, and eighteen in 2024 with the addition of Oregon, UCLA, USC, and Washington from the Pac-12.

Sports and media

The Big Ten sponsors competition in 28 sports. Its football champion traditionally played in the Rose Bowl. The conference operates the Big Ten Network (launched 2007) and has negotiated some of the richest media-rights deals in college sports, distributing large revenues to member schools.

Academics

All members belong to the Association of American Universities or have belonged historically, and members (except Nebraska) participate in the Big Ten Academic Alliance, a consortium for research and library cooperation.
