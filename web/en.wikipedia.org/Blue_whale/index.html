Blue whale

The blue whale (Balaenoptera musculus) is a marine mammal and a baleen whale. Reaching a maximum confirmed length of 29.9 metres (98 ft) and weighing up to 199 tonnes, it is the largest animal known ever to have existed. The blue whale's long and slender body can be of various shades of greyish-blue on its upper surface and somewhat lighter underneath.

Taxonomy and populations

Four subspecies are recognized: B. m. musculus in the North Atlantic and North Pacific, B. m. intermedia in the Southern Ocean, the pygmy blue whale B. m. brevicauda in the Indian Ocean and South Pacific, and B. m. indica. Blue whales were abundant in nearly all oceans until the early 20th century.

Feeding and biology

Blue whales feed almost exclusively on krill, filtering huge volumes of water through their baleen plates and consuming up to several tonnes of krill per day. Their vocalizations are among the loudest and lowest-frequency sounds produced by any animal, audible over hundreds of kilometres. Females give birth to a single calf about 7 metres long after a gestation of roughly 10–12 months.

Whaling and conservation

Commercial whaling reduced the global population drastically; Antarctic numbers fell from roughly 240,000 to a few hundred by the 1970s. Protected internationally since 1966 and by the International Whaling Commission's moratorium, populations are slowly recovering but remain listed as endangered. Threats today include ship strikes, entanglement, and ocean noise.
