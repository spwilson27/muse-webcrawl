Tall tale

A tall tale is a story with unbelievable or exaggerated elements, told as if it were true and factual. Some tall tales are exaggerations of actual events, while others are completely fictional tales set in a familiar setting, such as the European countryside or the American frontier. Tall tales are often told so as to make the narrator seem to have been a part of the story; the tone is generally good-natured.

American tall tales

The tall tale is a fundamental element of American folk literature. Characters such as Paul Bunyan, the giant lumberjack; Pecos Bill, the cowboy who rode a tornado; John Henry, the steel-driving man; and Davy Crockett became central figures of the genre. The stories celebrate the settling of the frontier and the exploits of workers in dangerous occupations.

Origins

The genre grew out of the bragging contests of the American frontier in the early 19th century and draws on older European traditions of humorous exaggeration, including the Münchhausen stories.
