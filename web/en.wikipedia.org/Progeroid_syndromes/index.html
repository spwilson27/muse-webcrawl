Progeroid syndromes

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Abnormal cell nuclei in Hutchinson–Gilford progeria syndrome

Progeroid syndromes (PS) are a group of rare genetic disorders that mimic physiological aging, making affected individuals appear to be older than they are. The term progeroid is used because the affected persons display characteristics of accelerated aging, although usually only a subset of the features of normal ageing (hence "segmental" progerias). These syndromes have attracted intensive research because they illuminate the molecular mechanisms of human ageing.

Examples

The best known are Hutchinson–Gilford progeria syndrome (HGPS), caused by a mutation in the LMNA gene that produces the abnormal protein progerin and leads to rapid ageing in childhood, and Werner syndrome, an adult-onset progeria caused by mutations in the WRN helicase gene. Others include Cockayne syndrome, caused by defects in DNA repair; Bloom syndrome; Rothmund–Thomson syndrome; xeroderma pigmentosum; dyskeratosis congenita, a telomere biology disorder; and mandibuloacral dysplasia.

Causes

Most progeroid syndromes arise from defects in the maintenance of the genome: mutations in genes encoding DNA repair enzymes, helicases, telomere maintenance proteins, or the nuclear lamina. The resulting accumulation of DNA damage and genomic instability is thought to drive cellular senescence and stem cell exhaustion, producing phenotypes such as hair loss, skin atrophy, atherosclerosis, osteoporosis and increased cancer risk. In 2020 the first drug for HGPS, the farnesyltransferase inhibitor lonafarnib, was approved.
