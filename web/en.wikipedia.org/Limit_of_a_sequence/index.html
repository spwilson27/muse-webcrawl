Limit of a sequence


The limit of a sequence is the value that the terms of a sequence "tend to" as the index approaches infinity. If such a limit exists, the sequence is convergent.

In mathematics, the limit of a sequence is the value that the terms of a sequence "tend to", and is often denoted using the lim symbol (e.g., limn→∞ an). If such a limit exists, the sequence is called convergent; a sequence that does not converge is said to be divergent.

Definition

A real sequence (an) converges to L if for every ε > 0 there exists a natural number N such that for all n ≥ N, |an − L| < ε. This ε–N definition was made rigorous in the 19th century by Cauchy and Weierstrass.

Properties

Limits of sequences are unique when they exist, and they respect arithmetic operations: the limit of a sum is the sum of the limits, and similarly for products and quotients. Every bounded monotone sequence of real numbers converges.
