Duchy of Florence

The Duchy of Florence (Italian: Ducato di Firenze) was an Italian principality centred on the city of Florence in Tuscany. It existed from 1532, when Pope Clement VII and Emperor Charles V installed Alessandro de' Medici as hereditary Duke of the Florentine Republic, until 1569, when Cosimo I de' Medici was raised to Grand Duke of Tuscany.

History

The duchy replaced the Republic of Florence after imperial and Spanish troops besieged the city in 1529–30 and restored Medici rule. Alessandro, the first duke, was assassinated in 1537 by his cousin Lorenzino de' Medici. He was succeeded, unexpectedly, by the 17-year-old Cosimo I from a cadet branch of the family, who defeated republican exiles at the Battle of Montemurlo and set about consolidating Medici power.

Cosimo expanded Florentine territory, conquering the Republic of Siena in 1555–59 after a gruelling siege, so that by his death the duchy covered most of modern Tuscany. He reorganised the administration and army, founded the Order of Saint Stephen to combat Ottoman and Barbary piracy from Livorno, and patronised the Uffizi offices and the Boboli Gardens. In 1569 Pope Pius V granted him the title Grand Duke of Tuscany, converting the duchy into the Grand Duchy of Tuscany, which the Medici ruled until 1737.
