Music of Brazil

The music of Brazil encompasses various regional musical styles influenced by Indigenous, African and European (especially Portuguese) forms. It is world-famous for genres such as samba, bossa nova, choro, forró, maracatu, MPB (música popular brasileira), tropicália, sertanejo, funk carioca and Brazilian rock.

History and genres

Colonial-era sacred and salon music gave way in the 19th century to the modinha and lundu, and to choro, an virtuoso instrumental style. Samba emerged in Rio de Janeiro in the early 20th century from Afro-Brazilian communities and became the emblematic sound of Carnival. In the late 1950s bossa nova — the refined, harmonically rich style of João Gilberto, Antônio Carlos Jobim and Vinicius de Moraes — conquered the world with songs like "Garota de Ipanema" ("The Girl from Ipanema"). The tropicália movement of the late 1960s, led by Caetano Veloso and Gilberto Gil, fused Brazilian traditions with rock and avant-garde art.

Regional and modern forms

The northeast sustains forró, frevo and maracatu; the south, gaúcho traditions; and nationwide, sertanejo duos dominate popular charts. Axé, pagode and Rio's funk carioca emerged from the late 20th century, while Brazilian musicians remain influential in jazz and electronic music worldwide.
