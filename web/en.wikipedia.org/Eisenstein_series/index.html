Eisenstein series

Eisenstein series, named after the German mathematician Gotthold Eisenstein, are particular modular forms with infinite series expansions that may be written down directly. They are among the simplest and most important examples of modular forms, and their Fourier coefficients encode arithmetic information such as divisor sums and values of the Riemann zeta function.

Definition

For an even integer k ≥ 4 and a point τ in the upper half-plane, the Eisenstein series of weight k is defined by summing over the lattice generated by 1 and τ:

G2k(τ) = Σ′ (m + nτ)−2k,

where the sum runs over all pairs of integers (m, n) ≠ (0, 0). The series converges absolutely for k > 2 and defines a modular form of weight 2k for the modular group SL(2, Z). The case of weight 2 does not converge absolutely and leads to a quasimodular form.

Fourier expansion

After normalisation, the Eisenstein series E4 and E6 have q-expansions whose coefficients involve the divisor sum functions σ3(n) and σ5(n). For example, E4(τ) = 1 + 240 Σ σ3(n) qn and E6(τ) = 1 − 504 Σ σ5(n) qn, where q = e2πiτ. The general formula uses values of the Riemann zeta function and Bernoulli numbers.

Relations and applications

Every modular form for SL(2, Z) can be expressed as a polynomial in E4 and E6; the combination (E4³ − E6²)/1728 gives the modular discriminant Δ, whose coefficients are given by Ramanujan's tau function. Eisenstein series also appear in the theory of elliptic functions (as the Weierstrass invariants g2 and g3), in string theory, and, through the work of Robert Langlands, in the general theory of automorphic forms, where Eisenstein series constructed from parabolic induction play a central role.
