Lola Cars Limited is a British automobile designer and manufacturer founded in 1958 by Eric Broadley. Based in Huntingdon, England, Lola became one of the most prolific and successful racing car constructors in the world, building cars for Formula One, Indianapolis 500, Can-Am, sports car racing, hill climbing and many junior formulae.

The Lola T70, one of the company's best-known sports cars.

History

Eric Broadley built his first car, the Broadley Special, in the 1950s before producing the Lola Mk1 sports car in 1958. The company expanded rapidly through the 1960s; the Lola T70 became a mainstay of sports car racing, and Lola chassis won the Indianapolis 500 in 1966 (Graham Hill), 1978 (Al Unser) and 1990 (Arie Luyendyk). Lola cars also claimed multiple Can-Am championships and Formula 5000 titles.

The company designed Formula One cars raced by teams including Honda (the 1967 "Hondola"), Embassy Hill, and Larrousse, and supplied chassis to the Newman/Haas and other CART teams for decades. An ambitious independent Formula One entry in 1997, the Mastercard Lola team, failed to qualify at its only event and folded with heavy debts. Lola Cars itself entered administration in 2012, though the marque was later revived under new ownership with ambitions in electric and sustainable racing technology, including a partnership in Formula E development.

Notable cars

  • Lola Mk1 (1958) – first production Lola
  • Lola T70 (1965–) – sports prototype
  • Lola T332 – Formula 5000
  • Lola B02/00 and B2K/00 – Champ Car
  • Lola B08/60 and B10/60 – Le Mans prototypes
