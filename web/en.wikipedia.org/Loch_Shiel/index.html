Loch Shiel (Scottish Gaelic: Loch Seile) is a freshwater loch in the west of Scotland, in the Highland council area, lying roughly 20 kilometres west of Fort William. It is a long, narrow loch, about 28 kilometres in length, running from Glenfinnan in the northeast to Acharacle in the southwest, where the short River Shiel carries its waters into Loch Moidart and the sea.

Geography and history

The loch is surrounded by mountainous country. At its head stands the Glenfinnan Monument, erected in 1815 to commemorate the raising of the Jacobite standard there by Prince Charles Edward Stuart in 1745. The Glenfinnan Viaduct, famous for its association with the Harry Potter films, crosses the valley near the head of the loch.

Nature

Loch Shiel is noted for its scenery and wildlife, including golden eagles and red-throated divers, and parts of its shores are protected for their ancient Atlantic oak woodland. Boat trips operate on the loch from Glenfinnan.
