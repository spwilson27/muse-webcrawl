Autonomy

Autonomy denotes the capacity for self-governance.

Autonomy is the capacity to make an informed, uncoerced decision. In moral and political philosophy, autonomy (from Ancient Greek: αὐτονομία, autonomia, from αὐτόνομος, autonomos, from αὐτο-, auto- "self" and νόμος, nomos "law," hence "one who gives oneself one's own law") is the capacity of a rational individual to make an informed, un-coerced decision.

In philosophy

Immanuel Kant placed autonomy at the centre of his moral philosophy: a person acts autonomously when they follow a law they give to themselves through reason, as opposed to heteronomy, acting on laws imposed by inclination or external authority. Later philosophers, including John Stuart Mill and contemporary theorists of personal autonomy, developed accounts of self-rule, authenticity, and independence of mind. Autonomy is a foundational principle in medical ethics, where respect for patient autonomy underlies informed consent.

In politics

In political science and law, autonomy refers to self-government of a polity or region, as in an autonomous administrative division that exercises a degree of independence from a central state while remaining part of it. The term is also applied to institutions, such as universities, and to autonomous systems in engineering and robotics.
