Triumph of the Will (German: Triumph des Willens) is a 1935 German propaganda film directed by Leni Riefenstahl. It chronicles the 1934 Nazi Party Congress in Nuremberg, attended by more than 700,000 Nazi supporters, and features footage of massed formations of the SA and SS, speeches by Adolf Hitler and other party leaders, and aerial views of the city.

Production

Hitler commissioned the film and was closely involved in its production; Albert Speer's rally grounds designs were planned partly with the filming in mind. Riefenstahl employed large camera crews, moving cameras, telephoto lenses and aerial photography in ways that were innovative for the time.

Reception and legacy

The film won awards in Germany, at the Venice Film Festival and at the Paris World's Fair. It is regarded as one of the most effective propaganda films ever made and a landmark of film technique, while remaining banned or restricted in Germany, where it may only be shown for educational purposes with expert introduction.
