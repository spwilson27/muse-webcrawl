GTPase

GTPases
EC number: 3.6.5
Substrate: Guanosine triphosphate (GTP)
Products: GDP + inorganic phosphate
Examples: Ras, Rho, Rab, heterotrimeric G proteins, EF-Tu, dynamin

GTPases are a large family of hydrolase enzymes that bind to the nucleotide guanosine triphosphate (GTP) and hydrolyze it to guanosine diphosphate (GDP). The GTP binding and hydrolysis takes place in the highly conserved P-loop "G domain", a protein domain common to many GTPases. Because the GTP-bound form is typically "on" and the GDP-bound form "off", GTPases function as molecular switches in the cell.

Classification

The GTPase superfamily includes the heterotrimeric G proteins that transduce signals from G protein-coupled receptors; the Ras superfamily of small monomeric GTPases (Ras, Rho, Rac, Rab, Ran, and Arf families), which regulate cell growth, cytoskeletal dynamics, vesicle trafficking, and nucleocytoplasmic transport; translation factors such as EF-Tu and EF-G; tubulin; and the dynamin family of large GTPases involved in membrane fission.

Mechanism and regulation

GTPases in their GDP-bound state are inactive and are switched on by guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs), which promote release of GDP so that GTP — present at higher cellular concentrations — can bind. They are switched off by their own hydrolysis of GTP to GDP, which is usually slow and is accelerated by GTPase-activating proteins (GAPs). In some GTPases, effector proteins bind preferentially to the GTP-bound form, propagating the signal.

Clinical relevance

Mutations that impair the GTPase activity of Ras proteins lock them in the active state and are found in roughly a fifth of human cancers, making Ras a major oncogene; inhibitors of the KRAS G12C mutant have reached the clinic. Defects in other GTPases underlie diseases including retinitis pigmentosa (small GTPases of the cilium) and various neurodevelopmental disorders.
