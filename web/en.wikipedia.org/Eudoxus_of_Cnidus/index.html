Eudoxus of Cnidus

Bornc. 408 BC, Cnidus, Asia Minor (modern Turkey)
Diedc. 355 BC, Cnidus
EraAncient Greek philosophy and science
Known forMethod of exhaustion; theory of proportion; homocentric spheres
InfluencesPlato, Archytas, Philistion

Eudoxus of Cnidus (c. 408 – c. 355 BC) was a Greek astronomer, mathematician, scholar, and student of Plato. All of his original works are lost, but his contributions are known from secondary sources such as Aratus's poem on astronomy. He is regarded as one of the greatest mathematicians of antiquity, second only to Archimedes in the judgment of many historians.

Mathematics

Eudoxus developed the method of exhaustion, a precursor of integral calculus used to find areas and volumes by successive approximation, which Archimedes later used masterfully. His theory of proportion, preserved in Book V of Euclid's Elements, handled incommensurable magnitudes rigorously through the famous definition of sameness of ratio, anticipating in some respects the 19th-century construction of the real numbers by Dedekind. He also proved theorems such as the volume of a cone being one-third of the cylinder on the same base and height.

Astronomy

In astronomy, Eudoxus proposed the first mathematical model of the heavens: a system of 27 concentric (homocentric) spheres centred on the Earth, whose combined rotations accounted for the motions of the Sun, Moon, and planets, including retrograde motion via a curve called the hippopede. Aristotle adopted and expanded the scheme in his cosmology. Eudoxus also produced a star catalogue and a description of the constellations that underlies Aratus's Phaenomena, and he is credited with a celebrated value of the year at 365¼ days.

Life

According to Diogenes Laërtius, Eudoxus studied mathematics with Archytas of Tarentum and medicine with Philistion, travelled to Egypt to study astronomy with priests at Heliopolis, and later taught at Cyzicus and at Plato's Academy in Athens before returning to his native Cnidus, where he built an observatory and was honoured by his fellow citizens.
