Exocet

The Exocet (French for "flying fish") is a French anti-ship missile family whose variants can be launched from surface vessels, submarines, helicopters and fixed-wing aircraft. Built by MBDA, it is a sea-skimming missile that flies only a metre or two above the waves, activating its radar seeker only in the final phase of flight to delay detection.

Combat use

Exocet achieved worldwide fame during the 1982 Falklands War, when Argentine Navy Super Étendard aircraft launched AM39 missiles that sank the British destroyer HMS Sheffield and the container ship Atlantic Conveyor. During the Iran–Iraq War, an Iraqi-launched pair of Exocets struck the US frigate USS Stark in 1987, killing 37 sailors.

Variants

The family includes the ship- and ground-launched MM38 and MM40 (later Block 2 and Block 3 with extended range and GPS guidance), the air-launched AM39, and the submarine-launched SM39, fired from a torpedo tube in a watertight capsule. Exocets have been exported to dozens of navies worldwide.
