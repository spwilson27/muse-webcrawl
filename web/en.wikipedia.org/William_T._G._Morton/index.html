William Thomas Green Morton (August 9, 1819 – July 15, 1868) was an American dentist and physician who first publicly demonstrated the use of inhaled ether as a surgical anaesthetic in 1846, an event that transformed surgery and dentistry.

The ether demonstration

Morton trained at the Baltimore College of Dental Surgery and practised dentistry in Boston, seeking a way to extract teeth painlessly. Advised by the chemist Charles T. Jackson, he experimented with sulphuric ether. On September 30, 1846, he painlessly extracted a tooth from Eben Frost after administering ether. On October 16, 1846, in the operating theatre of Massachusetts General Hospital — now known as the Ether Dome — surgeon John Collins Warren removed a tumour from the neck of Gilbert Abbott while Morton administered ether, and the patient felt no pain. Warren reportedly declared, "Gentlemen, this is no humbug."

Priority disputes

Morton sought to patent his "Letheon" and profit from it, and he spent much of the rest of his life in bitter disputes over credit with Jackson, Horace Wells (who had pioneered nitrous oxide) and Crawford Long (who had used ether in Georgia in 1842 but published later). Congress debated awarding him a prize without settling the question. Impoverished and embittered, Morton died of a stroke in 1868. His grave monument in Mount Auburn Cemetery credits him as "Inventor and Revealer of Inhalation Anaesthesia".
