Tiber

The Tiber (Italian: Tevere; Latin: Tiberis) is the third-longest river in Italy and the longest in central Italy, rising in the Apennine Mountains of Emilia-Romagna and flowing 406 kilometres (252 miles) through Tuscany, Umbria and Lazio to the Tyrrhenian Sea between Ostia and Fiumicino. It is most famous as the river of Rome, the city founded on its eastern banks, and its drainage basin covers about 17,375 square kilometres.

Course

The Tiber rises at Mount Fumaiolo near Balze in the Tuscan-Romagnol Apennines, where a monument marks its twin springs. It flows generally south, passing Città di Castello, Umbertide, Perugia (to the east), Todi and Orvieto, and is joined by the Chiascio, Nera (its largest tributary) and Aniene rivers. In Rome the river divides around Tiber Island, a boat-shaped island associated since antiquity with healing and the Temple of Aesculapius, before continuing past the ancient port districts to the sea.

History

According to legend, the twins Romulus and Remus were set adrift on the Tiber and washed ashore at the foot of the Palatine Hill. The river was personified as the god Tiberinus. Rome's position at a convenient ford and at the lowest bridging point made the Tiber a highway of trade: seagoing ships reached the port at Ostia at the river's mouth, and barges carried goods upstream. The river also flooded Rome regularly and destructively for millennia; after the great flood of 1870, high embankment walls (the muraglioni) were built, which finally ended the inundations but transformed the river's relationship with the city. Famous bridges include the ancient Pons Fabricius (62 BC), still in use, and the Ponte Sant'Angelo, adorned with Bernini's angels.

The river today

The Tiber's waters are heavily used for agriculture and hydroelectric power upstream and receive treated and untreated effluent from the Roman metropolitan area; water quality has improved with modern treatment plants but remains a matter of concern. The riverbanks in Rome host a summer festival and cycling paths, and the mouth at Fiumicino borders a protected coastal wetland.
