The Lockheed SR-71, nicknamed the Blackbird, is a retired long-range, high-altitude, Mach 3+ strategic reconnaissance aircraft developed and manufactured by the American aerospace company Lockheed Corporation. It was operated by the United States Air Force and NASA. The SR-71 was built largely of titanium to withstand the frictional heating of sustained flight above Mach 3, and was designed under Clarence "Kelly" Johnson's Skunk Works as a successor to the A-12 Oxcart.

The SR-71 remains the fastest air-breathing manned aircraft ever flown.

Operational history

Introduced in 1966, the Blackbird flew reconnaissance missions over some of the most heavily defended airspace in the world for more than two decades. Its defence was speed and altitude: no SR-71 was ever shot down, the standard evasion tactic being simply to accelerate and outrun a fired missile. Crews wore pressure suits similar to those of astronauts, and the aircraft's Pratt & Whitney J58 engines operated as turboramjets at cruise. The type set absolute speed and altitude records in 1976, including a sustained speed of 2,193 mph, that still stand for air-breathing manned aircraft.

Retirement

The SR-71 was retired by the USAF in 1989, briefly reactivated in the mid-1990s, and finally retired in 1998 amid high operating costs and the rise of satellite reconnaissance; NASA flew its last Blackbird in 1999. Most surviving aircraft are now displayed in museums in the United States and one at Duxford in the United Kingdom.
