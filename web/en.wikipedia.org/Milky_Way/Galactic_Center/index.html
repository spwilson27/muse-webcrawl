Galactic Center

ConstellationSagittarius
Right ascension17h 45m 40s
Declination−29° 00′ 28″
Distance~26,000 light-years (8 kpc)
Central objectSagittarius A*

The Galactic Center is the rotational center and barycenter of the Milky Way galaxy. It lies in the direction of the constellation Sagittarius, where the Milky Way appears brightest because of the dense concentration of stars, gas, and dust along the galactic plane. Its central massive object is the supermassive black hole Sagittarius A*, with a mass of about 4.3 million solar masses.

Because the Galactic Center is obscured by roughly 30 magnitudes of visual extinction from interstellar dust, it is studied primarily at radio, infrared, and X-ray wavelengths. The region around the center contains the galactic bulge, a dense spheroidal swarm of mostly old stars, as well as young massive star clusters such as the Arches and Quintuplet clusters near the center.

Sagittarius A*

Sagittarius A* (pronounced "Sagittarius A-star") is a bright and very compact radio source at the exact center. Observations of stars orbiting it — including the star S2, which completes an orbit in about 16 years — demonstrate that it is a supermassive black hole. The 2020 Nobel Prize in Physics was awarded in part for the discovery of this compact massive object, and in May 2022 the Event Horizon Telescope released the first image of Sagittarius A*.

Surrounding structure

Within the central parsec, dense clouds of molecular gas orbit in a circumnuclear disk. The central stellar cluster includes the S-star cluster, a group of young B-type stars on tight, eccentric orbits around the black hole, which have been used to test general relativity, including gravitational redshift and Schwarzschild precession.
