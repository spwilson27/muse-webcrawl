NGC 604

TypeH II region
Host galaxyTriangulum Galaxy (M33)
Distance~2.73 million light-years

NGC 604 is a giant H II region — an enormous cloud of ionized hydrogen gas where stars are being born — located in the Triangulum Galaxy, a member of our Local Group. It is one of the largest known star-forming regions in the Local Group, spanning about 1,520 light-years across, roughly 40 times the size of the Orion Nebula and thousands of times more luminous.

The nebula was discovered by William Herschel on 11 September 1784. It is powered by a central cluster of more than 200 hot, massive O and Wolf–Rayet stars, only a few million years old, whose stellar winds have carved cavities and loops into the surrounding gas and dust. Hubble Space Telescope images reveal the intricate, filamentary structure of the nebula's glowing walls.

Significance

NGC 604 is a scaled-up analogue of the Orion Nebula in the Milky Way: a nearby example of the "starburst" regions common in distant galaxies. Because it is so large and bright, it has been used as a benchmark for studying massive-star feedback, the initial mass function, and how the energy of young stars shapes the interstellar medium of their host galaxy.
