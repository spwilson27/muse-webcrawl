Supermassive black hole

A supermassive black hole (SMBH) is the largest type of black hole, with a mass on the order of millions to billions of times the mass of the Sun. Astronomical evidence indicates that nearly every large galaxy, including the Milky Way, contains a supermassive black hole at its centre.

Properties

Supermassive black holes have masses from about 100,000 up to tens of billions of solar masses. The one at the centre of the Milky Way, Sagittarius A*, has about 4.3 million solar masses, while the black hole in Messier 87 measures roughly 6.5 billion solar masses. Because their average density can be comparatively low, the tidal forces near the event horizon of a very large SMBH can be weaker than those of a stellar-mass black hole.

Formation

The formation of supermassive black holes remains an open question. Proposed mechanisms include the accretion of gas onto "seed" black holes formed from the first stars, the direct collapse of enormous gas clouds, and growth through mergers of smaller black holes as galaxies collide.

Observational evidence

SMBHs are detected through the motion of stars and gas orbiting them, through bright accretion disks and quasars, and since 2019 through direct imaging by the Event Horizon Telescope, which imaged the black holes in M87 and the Milky Way.
