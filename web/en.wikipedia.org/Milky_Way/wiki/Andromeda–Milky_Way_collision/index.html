Andromeda–Milky Way collision

The Andromeda–Milky Way collision is a predicted galactic collision that will take place in about 4.5 billion years between the Milky Way galaxy and the Andromeda Galaxy (M31), the two largest members of the Local Group. The stars within the galaxies are so far apart that direct collisions between individual stars are considered very unlikely.

Predictions

Andromeda is approaching the Milky Way at about 110 kilometres per second. Measurements from the Hubble Space Telescope, announced in 2012, indicated that a head-on collision is likely; later analysis using Gaia data refined the picture, suggesting the first close pass may occur in about 4.5 billion years, possibly involving the Triangulum Galaxy earlier.

Outcome

Simulations suggest the two galaxies will merge into a single large elliptical or disc galaxy, sometimes nicknamed "Milkomeda" or "Milkdromeda". The Solar System is expected to be flung to a new position in the outer regions of the merged galaxy rather than destroyed; by that time the Sun will be nearing the end of its main-sequence life.
