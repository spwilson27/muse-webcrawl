Messier object

The Messier objects are a set of 110 astronomical objects catalogued by the French astronomer Charles Messier in his Catalogue des Nébuleuses et des Amas d'Étoiles (Catalogue of Nebulae and Star Clusters), published in stages between 1774 and 1781. Because Messier was hunting comets, he compiled the list of diffuse "nuisances" that were not comets, so that he and other observers would not mistake them for new comets.

The catalogue

The catalogue contains 39 galaxies (including the Andromeda Galaxy M31 and the Whirlpool Galaxy M51), 5 planetary nebulae, 7 other nebulae including the Orion Nebula (M42) and the Eagle Nebula (M16), and 55 star clusters, among them the globular cluster in Hercules (M13) and the Pleiades open cluster (M45). The first version (1774) listed 45 objects; with contributions from Pierre Méchain the printed catalogue reached 103, and researchers later added M104–M110 from Messier's and Méchain's notes and letters.

Legacy

All Messier objects are visible with binoculars or a small telescope from the Northern Hemisphere, making the catalogue the classic observing list for amateur astronomers. The "Messier marathon" challenges observers to view all 110 objects in a single night, possible around the spring equinox. Messier's catalogue was superseded scientifically by the New General Catalogue (NGC) of 1888, but its designations remain in universal use.
