The Local Group is the galaxy group that includes the Milky Way. It contains more than 80 known galaxies, most of them small dwarf galaxies gravitationally bound to the two dominant spirals — the Andromeda Galaxy (M31) and the Milky Way — and to the third-largest member, the Triangulum Galaxy (M33). The group spans about 10 million light-years and forms part of the Virgo Supercluster, itself a component of the Laniakea Supercluster.

The Local Group is dominated by the Andromeda and Milky Way subgroups.

Membership

Two giant barred spiral galaxies anchor the group. Andromeda, the largest, is accompanied by satellites including M32, M110, NGC 185 and NGC 147, and possibly by the Triangulum Galaxy, which may be on its first infall toward Andromeda. The Milky Way's system includes the Large and Small Magellanic Clouds and dozens of dwarf spheroidal and ultra-faint dwarf galaxies, many discovered only in the 21st century by digital sky surveys. Other members, such as the isolated irregular galaxy IC 10 and the dwarf elliptical NGC 6822 (Barnard's Galaxy), belong to neither subgroup. New faint members continue to be found, and the total count is expected to rise with surveys such as the Vera Rubin Observatory's.

Properties and dynamics

The group's two main subclusters are approaching each other at about 123 km/s and will merge over the coming billions of years, with the Andromeda–Milky Way collision expected to begin in roughly 4–5 billion years. The "timing argument", first applied by Franz Kahn and Lodewijk Woltjer in 1959, uses the galaxies' approach speed and the age of the universe to estimate the group's total mass — several trillion solar masses, most of it dark matter. The Local Group is unusual among galaxy groups in containing two large spirals of comparable size, making it a key laboratory for studying galaxy formation in a relatively quiet environment.

Location in the universe

The Local Group sits on the outskirts of the Virgo Cluster, within the Local Sheet of galaxies. It is moving at roughly 630 km/s relative to the cosmic microwave background, toward the Great Attractor region and the Shapley Supercluster beyond.
