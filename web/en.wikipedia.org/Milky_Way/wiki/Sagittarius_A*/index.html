Sagittarius A*

Designation: Sagittarius A* (Sgr A*)

Type: Supermassive black hole

Mass: ≈ 4.3 million solar masses

Distance: ≈ 26,700 light-years

Constellation: Sagittarius

Sagittarius A* (pronounced "Sagittarius A-star", abbreviated Sgr A*) is a supermassive black hole located at the Galactic Center of the Milky Way. It is the nearest known supermassive black hole to Earth and has a mass of about 4.3 million times that of the Sun.

Discovery

The compact radio source was discovered in 1974 by Bruce Balick and Robert Brown. In May 2022, the Event Horizon Telescope collaboration released the first direct image of the accretion disk surrounding Sagittarius A*, confirming its nature as a black hole.

Observations

Because intervening dust obscures the Galactic Center at visible wavelengths, Sgr A* is studied in radio, infrared, and X-ray light. Stars in the S-star cluster orbit it at extreme speeds; the star S2 completes an orbit in about 16 years and has been used to test general relativity. Andrea Ghez and Reinhard Genzel shared part of the 2020 Nobel Prize in Physics for their work establishing the compact massive object at the galactic centre.

Activity

Sgr A* is currently relatively quiescent, accreting matter at a very low rate, but it produces daily X-ray and near-infrared flares. Evidence from X-ray light echoes suggests it was far more active a few centuries ago.
