Laniakea Supercluster

Laniakea Supercluster
Diameter: ≈ 160 Mpc (520 million ly)
Mass: ≈ 1017 solar masses
Galaxies: ≈ 100,000
Constituent parts: Virgo, Hydra–Centaurus, Pavo–Indus, Southern superclusters

The Laniakea Supercluster (from Hawaiian laniākea, "immense heaven") is the galaxy supercluster that is home to the Milky Way and approximately 100,000 other nearby galaxies. It was defined in September 2014 by a team led by R. Brent Tully, who mapped the peculiar velocities of more than 8,000 galaxies and identified the region within which galaxy motions converge toward a common gravitational basin of attraction.

Definition and structure

Superclusters are not gravitationally bound objects; Laniakea is defined kinematically, by the watershed of galaxy flows. Galaxies inside Laniakea stream toward the Great Attractor region in the Hydra–Centaurus area, while neighbouring structures — the Perseus–Pisces, Coma and Shapley superclusters — drain toward their own attractors. Laniakea encompasses the previously recognised Virgo Supercluster (now a lobe of Laniakea containing the Local Group), Hydra–Centaurus, Pavo–Indus and the Southern Supercluster. It spans about 160 megaparsecs (520 million light-years) and contains roughly 1017 solar masses.

Discovery

The definition used the Cosmicflows-2 catalogue of galaxy distances and peculiar velocities and a Wiener-filter reconstruction of the local velocity field, published in Nature in 2014. The name was proposed by Hawaiian language professor Nawaʻa Napoleon to honour Polynesian navigators who crossed immense ocean distances by the stars.
