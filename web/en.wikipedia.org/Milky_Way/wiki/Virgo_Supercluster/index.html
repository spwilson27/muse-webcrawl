Virgo Supercluster

Parent structure: Laniakea Supercluster

Diameter: ≈ 110 million light-years

Number of galaxies: ≈ 100,000

Brightest member: Virgo Cluster

The Virgo Supercluster (Virgo SC) or the Local Supercluster (LSC) is a mass concentration of galaxies containing the Virgo Cluster and the Local Group, which in turn contains the Milky Way and Andromeda galaxies. At least 100 galaxy groups and clusters are located within its diameter of 33 megaparsecs (110 million light-years).

Structure

The supercluster is flattened, roughly disk-shaped, and is dominated gravitationally by the massive Virgo Cluster near its centre. The Local Group lies near the edge, in a small spur associated with the Virgo Cluster. Studies of galactic motion show the whole structure is part of the much larger Laniakea Supercluster, defined in 2014 from galaxy velocity flows.

History

Beginning with Charles Messier's catalogue, astronomers noted a concentration of nebulae in Virgo. Gérard de Vaucouleurs established in the 1950s that this concentration formed a vast flattened supergalaxy — the first recognised supercluster.
