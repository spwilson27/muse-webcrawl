The Galactic Center is the rotational centre, and barycentre, of the Milky Way galaxy. It lies about 26,000 light-years from the Solar System in the direction of the constellation Sagittarius, where the Milky Way's band appears brightest. Dense interstellar dust blocks the centre in visible light, so it is studied mainly at radio, infrared and X-ray wavelengths, which penetrate the obscuring material.

Infrared imaging reveals the dense star cluster surrounding the Galactic Center.

Sagittarius A*

At the dynamical centre sits the compact radio source Sagittarius A* (Sgr A*), a supermassive black hole of about 4.3 million solar masses confined within a radius smaller than the orbit of Mercury. Its existence was established by tracking the orbits of stars (the "S-stars") whipping around an unseen mass at speeds of thousands of kilometres per second — work by the teams of Reinhard Genzel and Andrea Ghez that earned a share of the 2020 Nobel Prize in Physics. In May 2022 the Event Horizon Telescope collaboration released the first image of Sgr A*, showing a glowing ring of hot gas around the black hole's shadow.

Environment

The central parsec contains several million stars packed into a nuclear star cluster, including the young, massive S-star cluster and bizarre dusty objects called G objects. Enormous molecular clouds — among the most massive in the galaxy — circle the centre, including Sagittarius B2. Thread-like radio filaments, such as the Radio Arc, trace magnetic fields, and the Fermi Bubbles — giant gamma-ray lobes extending 25,000 light-years above and below the plane — record past energetic activity, perhaps from a more active phase of Sgr A* or a burst of star formation.

History of study

Harlow Shapley located the galactic centre in the direction of Sagittarius in 1918 from the distribution of globular clusters. Karl Jansky detected radio emission from the region in the 1930s, founding radio astronomy. Infrared and speckle imaging in the 1990s enabled the star-orbit measurements that confirmed the central black hole.
