Galaxy group

A galaxy group (or group of galaxies) is a gravitationally bound aggregation of galaxies containing roughly 3 to 50 members within a region about 1–2 megaparsecs across. Groups are the most common structures of galaxies in the universe, containing at least half of all galaxies in the local universe. Collections larger than groups are galaxy clusters; looser, unbound associations of groups form superclusters.

Properties

Typical groups have total masses of 1012–1013 solar masses and velocity dispersions of a few hundred kilometres per second. Unlike rich clusters, they usually lack a massive, extended atmosphere of hot X-ray-emitting intracluster gas, though some groups do show diffuse X-ray halos. Because members orbit slowly and closely, galaxy interactions and mergers are frequent and important in shaping morphology — the Milky Way and Andromeda galaxies are expected to merge in about 4–5 billion years.

Examples

The Local Group contains the Milky Way, Andromeda (M31), the Triangulum Galaxy (M33) and some 80 known dwarf galaxies. Other well-known groups include the M81 Group, the Centaurus A/M83 Group, the Leo Triplet, and the compact group Stephan's Quintet, where tidal tails and shock fronts trace violent interactions. Catalogues such as Hickson's compact groups and the Lyon Groups of Galaxies systematically enumerate nearby systems.
