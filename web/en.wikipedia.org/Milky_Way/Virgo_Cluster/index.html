The Virgo Cluster is a large cluster of galaxies whose center lies about 53.8 million light-years away in the constellation Virgo. Containing roughly 1,300 and possibly up to 2,000 member galaxies, it is the nearest large galaxy cluster to the Local Group, and forms the heart of the Local (Virgo) Supercluster, of which the Milky Way is an outlying member.

The cluster is dominated by three giant elliptical galaxies — Messier 49, Messier 60, and especially Messier 87 (Virgo A), whose supermassive black hole was the first ever directly imaged, by the Event Horizon Telescope in 2019. Charles Messier catalogued many of its brightest members in the 1780s as fuzzy "nebulae" that avoided confusion with comets.

Composition

The Virgo Cluster contains a rich mixture of spirals, ellipticals, and lenticulars, plus a diffuse intracluster medium of hot plasma emitting X-rays. It has several distinct subclusters centered on M87, M86, and M49, which are still in the process of merging. Because of its proximity, the cluster is a key laboratory for studying galaxy evolution in dense environments, including ram-pressure stripping of spiral galaxies falling into it.
