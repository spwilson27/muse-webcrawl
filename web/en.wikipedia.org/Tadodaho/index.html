Tadodaho

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Tadodaho was a legendary Onondaga chief and the most well-known of the individuals to hold the title Tadodaho, the presiding leader of the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) Confederacy. According to oral tradition, Tadodaho was a powerful and feared sorcerer-chief of the Onondaga whose hair was made of snakes, who resisted the message of peace brought by the Great Peacemaker (Deganawida) and Hiawatha. His eventual conversion and acceptance of the Great Law of Peace was a pivotal moment in the founding of the confederacy.

Legend

In Haudenosaunee oral tradition, Tadodaho was the last holdout against the Peacemaker's mission to unite the five warring nations — the Mohawk, Oneida, Onondaga, Cayuga, and Seneca. He was said to live alone by Onondaga Lake, his body twisted and crooked, his head covered with writhing snakes, feeding on human flesh. Every embassy sent to him was turned away or destroyed by his magic.

Hiawatha, whose family had been killed through the machinations of Tadodaho (in some tellings by a rival chief), journeyed with the Peacemaker to confront him. In the climactic episode, the Peacemaker combed the snakes from Tadodaho's hair — an act remembered in Hiawatha's name and in the symbolism of the wampum belt — straightened his crooked body, and cleared his mind. Tadodaho then accepted the Great Law, and the Onondaga became the keepers of the council fire of the confederacy, with their capital at Onondaga.

The title today

"Tadodaho" remains the title of the chief who presides over the Grand Council of the Haudenosaunee at Onondaga. The Tadodaho is chosen from among the Onondaga chiefs and serves as a spiritual and diplomatic leader; he traditionally opens and closes council sessions and keeps the wampum records of the confederacy. Sidney Hill, an Onondaga faithkeeper, has served as Tadodaho in recent decades and has represented the Haudenosaunee in international forums on Indigenous rights and environmental issues.
