The United States Marine Corps (USMC), also referred to as the United States Marines, is the maritime land force service branch of the United States Armed Forces responsible for conducting expeditionary and amphibious operations through combined arms, implementing its own infantry, artillery, aerial, and special operations forces. The Corps is one of the eight uniformed services of the United States and one of two — with the Navy — administered by the Department of the Navy.

History

The Continental Marines were established by the Second Continental Congress on 10 November 1775, a date still celebrated as the Marine Corps birthday. Marines fought in the American Revolutionary War, the Quasi-War, and the Barbary Wars — the "shores of Tripoli" of the Marines' Hymn refer to the Battle of Derna in 1805. The modern Corps dates from an act of Congress of 11 July 1798.

In the 19th century Marines served in the Mexican–American War and the American Civil War. The Corps expanded enormously in the World Wars: at Belleau Wood in 1918 Marines earned the German nickname "Devil Dogs" (Teufel Hunden), and in the Pacific theatre of World War II the Corps conducted its famous island-hopping campaign — Guadalcanal, Tarawa, Peleliu, Iwo Jima, and Okinawa. The flag raising on Mount Suribachi, Iwo Jima, in February 1945 became one of the most reproduced images of the war.

Modern role

Marines fought at Inchon and the Chosin Reservoir in Korea, at Hue and Khe Sanh in Vietnam, and in Iraq and Afghanistan after 2001. The Corps is organised around Marine Air-Ground Task Forces (MAGTFs) combining ground, aviation, and logistics elements under a single commander, and Marine Expeditionary Units embarked on Navy amphibious ships provide a forward-deployed rapid-reaction force. Marine recruits train at Parris Island, South Carolina, or San Diego, California, in an famously demanding thirteen-week boot camp.

Culture

Marine culture emphasises the maxim "Every Marine a rifleman," the title "Marine" as earned rather than given, and the motto Semper Fidelis. The Eagle, Globe, and Anchor is the Corps' emblem, and its members are sometimes colloquially called "jarheads" or "leathernecks," the latter from the leather stock once worn around the neck.
