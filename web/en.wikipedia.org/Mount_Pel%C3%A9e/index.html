Mount Pelée (French: Montagne Pelée, "bald mountain") is an active volcano at the northern end of Martinique, an island and French overseas department in the Lesser Antilles Volcanic Arc of the Caribbean. It is best known for its eruption on 8 May 1902, one of the deadliest volcanic events in recorded history.

1902 eruption

On the morning of 8 May 1902, a pyroclastic flow — a superheated cloud of gas, ash and rock later named a nuée ardente — swept down the mountain and destroyed the city of Saint-Pierre within minutes. Of a population of roughly 28,000, nearly everyone was killed; the most famous survivor was Ludger Sylbaris, a prisoner held in a thick-walled cell. The eruption gave its name to the "Peléan" type of volcanic eruption.

Geology

Pelée is a stratovolcano formed by subduction of the Atlantic oceanic plate beneath the Caribbean Plate. Its eruptions are characterized by the growth and collapse of viscous lava domes, generating pyroclastic flows.

Later activity

A further eruptive period from 1929 to 1932 built a new dome in the crater. The volcano is now continuously monitored by the Observatoire Volcanologique et Sismologique de Martinique, and Saint-Pierre has been partially rebuilt.
