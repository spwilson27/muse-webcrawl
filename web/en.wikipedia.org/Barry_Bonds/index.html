Barry Bonds

Born: July 24, 1964, Riverside, California
Position: Left fielder
Teams: Pittsburgh Pirates (1986–1992), San Francisco Giants (1993–2007)

Barry Lamar Bonds (born July 24, 1964) is an American former professional baseball left fielder who played 22 seasons in Major League Baseball (MLB) with the Pittsburgh Pirates and San Francisco Giants. Widely regarded as one of the greatest players of all time, he holds the MLB records for career home runs (762), single-season home runs (73, set in 2001), career walks (2,558), and career intentional walks, and won a record seven National League Most Valuable Player Awards.

Career

The son of former MLB star Bobby Bonds and godson of Willie Mays, Bonds debuted with Pittsburgh in 1986, winning two MVP awards and leading the Pirates to three consecutive division titles. He signed with San Francisco as a free agent in 1993 and won five more MVP awards, including four consecutively from 2001 to 2004. On August 7, 2007, he hit his 756th career home run, passing Hank Aaron for the all-time record.

Controversy and legacy

Bonds's achievements were overshadowed by the BALCO scandal and allegations that he used performance-enhancing drugs; he was indicted on perjury and obstruction charges in 2007, convicted of one count of obstruction of justice in 2011, and the conviction was overturned on appeal in 2015. Despite his records, he was not elected to the National Baseball Hall of Fame in his ten years on the writers' ballot (2013–2022), and a contemporary era committee also declined to elect him in 2022.
