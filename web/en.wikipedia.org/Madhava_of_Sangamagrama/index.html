Madhava of Sangamagrama

Madhava of Sangamagrama (c. 1340 – c. 1425) was an Indian mathematician and astronomer from the town of Sangamagrama (modern Irinjalakuda) in present-day Kerala. He is considered the founder of the Kerala school of astronomy and mathematics, and one of the greatest mathematicians of medieval India.

Contributions

Madhava is credited with pioneering the use of infinite series in mathematics, roughly two and a half centuries before Newton and Leibniz. He derived infinite series expansions for the trigonometric functions sine, cosine, and arctangent, and for π — including the series now known in the West as the Madhava–Leibniz series for π — together with rational approximations and error-correction terms that yield remarkably accurate values. He also computed precise tables of sines, gave a value of π correct to many decimal places, and worked on continued fractions and the analysis of polynomial methods for solving equations.

Works and legacy

Most of Madhava's original writings are lost; his results survive through the works of his successors in the Kerala school, such as Nilakantha Somayaji and Jyesthadeva, whose Yuktibhasa sets out proofs of the series results. The historian Charles Whish publicized the Kerala discoveries to European audiences in the 1830s. Madhava's work is now recognized as a major early development of mathematical analysis.
