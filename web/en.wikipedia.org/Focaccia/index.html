Focaccia

Focaccia with rosemary and olive oil.

Focaccia is a flat leavened oven-baked Italian bread. In some places, such as Rome, it is a style of pizza called pizza bianca ("white pizza"). Focaccia may be served as a side dish or as sandwich bread and it may be round, rectangular, or square in shape. Its name derives from the Latin panis focacius, "bread baked in the hearth", referring to the Roman practice of baking bread in the ashes of the fireplace.

Description

Focaccia dough is made from flour, water, yeast, salt and olive oil, and is characterised by a generous amount of oil worked into and over the dough. Before baking, the baker presses fingertips into the dough to create characteristic dimples, which hold pools of olive oil and brine or salt water. The result is a bread with a crisp, golden crust, a soft, airy interior, and a rich flavour of olive oil. Common toppings include coarse salt, rosemary, olives, onions, cherry tomatoes, and herbs.

Regional varieties

  • Focaccia genovese — the Ligurian classic from Genoa, about 2 cm thick, topped with olive oil, salt and a salt-water brine, eaten at any time of day, sometimes dipped in cappuccino for breakfast.
  • Focaccia barese — from Bari in Apulia, topped with tomatoes and olives.
  • Focaccia di Recco — a thin, unleavened Ligurian speciality filled with crescenza cheese, holding protected geographical status.
  • Schiacciata — the Tuscan relative, often made with grapes (schiacciata con l'uva) at harvest time.
  • Fougasse — the related Provençal bread of southern France, often slashed into a leaf pattern.

History

Flatbreads baked on a hearth are ancient throughout the Mediterranean; the Etruscans and Romans made versions of hearth bread, and the basic recipe is thought to descend from them. Focaccia became particularly associated with Liguria and its port city of Genoa, whose bakers supplied sailors with durable, oil-rich bread. From Italy it spread worldwide with Italian emigration and is now a staple of bakeries and sandwich shops internationally.
