Chern class

In mathematics, Chern classes are characteristic classes associated to complex vector bundles, introduced by Shiing-Shen Chern in the 1940s. They take values in the cohomology ring of the base space and measure the twisting of the bundle.

Definition

The total Chern class of a complex vector bundle E is written c(E) = 1 + c1(E) + c2(E) + …, where the first Chern class of a line bundle equals (up to convention) its Euler class. Chern classes can be characterised axiomatically by functoriality, the Whitney sum formula, and a normalisation condition, or constructed geometrically via Chern–Weil theory from the curvature of a connection.

Properties and applications

The Chern character relates Chern classes to Todd classes in the Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem, and Chern numbers of complex manifolds are important cobordism invariants. In physics, Chern classes appear in the theory of the quantum Hall effect and topological insulators, where the first Chern number is a topological invariant.
