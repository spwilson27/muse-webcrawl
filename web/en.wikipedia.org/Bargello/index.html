Bargello

The Bargello, also known as the Palazzo del Bargello or Palazzo del Podestà, is a former barracks and prison in Florence, Italy. Since 1865 it has housed the Museo Nazionale del Bargello, Italy's national museum of sculpture, containing masterpieces by Michelangelo, Donatello, and other Renaissance artists.

History

Built beginning in 1255, the palace was the seat of the podestà, the chief magistrate of Florence, and later of the bargello, the chief of police from whom it takes its name. It served as a prison for centuries; executions were carried out in its courtyard until capital punishment was abolished in Tuscany in 1786. The building's crenellated tower, the Torre Volognana, is a landmark of the Florentine skyline. In the 19th century the palace was restored and converted into a museum, and the search for a lost portrait of Dante by Giotto in its chapel led to the rediscovery of the fresco in 1841.

Collection

The museum holds the most important collection of Italian Renaissance sculpture, including Donatello's bronze David and his St. George, Michelangelo's Bacchus, Brutus, and David-Apollo, works by Verrocchio, Cellini, and Giambologna, as well as ivories, enamels, maiolica, and the competition panels for the Florence Baptistery doors by Brunelleschi and Ghiberti (1401).
