Percy Bysshe Shelley

Born: 4 August 1792, Field Place, Sussex, England
Died: 8 July 1822 (aged 29), Gulf of La Spezia, Kingdom of Sardinia
Occupation: Poet, dramatist, essayist, novelist
Spouse: Harriet Westbrook (m. 1811; d. 1816); Mary Godwin (m. 1816)
Movement: Romanticism

Percy Bysshe Shelley (4 August 1792 – 8 July 1822) was an English Romantic poet who is regarded as one of the finest lyric and philosophical poets in the English language. A radical in his poetry as well as in his political and social views, Shelley did not achieve fame during his lifetime, but recognition of his achievements in poetry grew steadily following his death, and he exerted a major influence on subsequent generations of poets.

Shelley was a key member of a close circle of visionary poets and writers that included Lord Byron, John Keats, Leigh Hunt and his second wife, Mary Shelley, the author of Frankenstein (1818).

Life

Shelley was born at Field Place, Sussex, the eldest son of Timothy Shelley, later Sir Timothy Shelley, MP. He was educated at Eton College and University College, Oxford, from which he was expelled in 1811 for publishing the pamphlet The Necessity of Atheism. He eloped to Scotland with the 16-year-old Harriet Westbrook; after her death in 1816 he married Mary Godwin, daughter of the philosopher William Godwin and the feminist writer Mary Wollstonecraft.

From 1818 Shelley lived mainly in Italy, where he wrote many of his most celebrated works, including Prometheus Unbound, Adonais, and the lyrics "Ode to the West Wind" and "To a Skylark". On 8 July 1822, shortly before his thirtieth birthday, he drowned in a sudden storm while sailing his schooner, the Don Juan, in the Gulf of La Spezia.

Works

Major works include the long visionary poem Queen Mab (1813), the verse drama The Cenci (1819), the lyrical drama Prometheus Unbound (1820), the elegy Adonais (1821), written for John Keats, and the prose essay A Defence of Poetry (written 1821, published 1840), which famously concludes that "poets are the unacknowledged legislators of the world". His short poems "Ozymandias", "Ode to the West Wind", "To a Skylark" and "The Masque of Anarchy" are among the best known in English literature.
