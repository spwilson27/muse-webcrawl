Jinshin War
Date672
LocationJapan
ResultVictory of Prince Ōama (Emperor Tenmu)

The Jinshin War (672) was a succession dispute in Asuka-period Japan fought after the death of Emperor Tenji. Tenji's brother Prince Ōama, who had retired to Yoshino, rose against Tenji's son Prince Ōtomo, who was backed by the court at Ōmi Ōtsu.

Course

Ōama raised forces in the eastern provinces, secured the passes into Yamato, and won a series of battles advancing on the capital. After defeat, Ōtomo took his own life. Ōama ascended the throne as Emperor Tenmu in 673.

Significance

Tenmu went on to centralise imperial power, reform government, compile histories, and promote Buddhism and court ritual. The war is a classic example of succession conflict in early Japanese history, recorded in the Nihon Shoki.
