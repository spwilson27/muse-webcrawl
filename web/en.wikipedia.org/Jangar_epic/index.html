Jangar epic

The Jangar epic (Kalmyk: Җаңһр) is the central heroic epic of the Kalmyk people, the western Mongols (Oirats) who migrated to the lower Volga region in the 17th century. Together with the Mongolian Geser and the Kyrgyz Manas, it is one of the great oral epics of Central Asia.

Content

The epic consists of cycles of songs, each a self-contained adventure, recounting the deeds of Khan Jangar and his twelve lion-like heroes (bogatyrs), among them the mighty Hongor, in the utopian land of Bumba, where everyone is eternally twenty-five years old and abundance never fails. The heroes defend Bumba against invading khans and monsters, undertake quests and single combats, and feast in Jangar's palace.

Transmission

Jangar was sung by storytellers called jangarchi, accompanied by the two-stringed tovshuur lute. Versions vary greatly; individual singers held thousands of lines in memory. Cycles were written down from the 19th century onwards, and the epic was celebrated in the Kalmyk Republic, where a 600th anniversary of the epic was marked in 1990. Suppressed in the Stalin era along with Kalmyk culture during the people's deportation (1943–57), the epic has since been revived as a symbol of Kalmyk national identity.
