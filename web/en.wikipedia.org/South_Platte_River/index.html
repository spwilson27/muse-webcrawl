South Platte River

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The South Platte River in Colorado

The South Platte River is one of the two principal tributaries, along with the North Platte River, that join to form the Platte River in western Nebraska. The South Platte rises in the Colorado mountains near Fairplay in Park County, flows through the South Platte canyon and the Denver metropolitan area, then northeast across the Colorado plains through Greeley before joining the North Platte near the town of North Platte, Nebraska. It is about 439 miles (707 km) long.

Course and use

The river's watershed drains much of Colorado's Front Range and eastern plains. Dams and reservoirs (including Eleven Mile, Cheesman, Chatfield, and Barr Lake) store water for irrigation and municipal supply; the river is heavily managed, with flows augmented by transbasin diversions from the Colorado River basin. In Denver, the confluence with Cherry Creek — where gold was discovered in 1858–59 — is marked by Confluence Park.
