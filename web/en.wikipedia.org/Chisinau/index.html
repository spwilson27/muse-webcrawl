Chisinau

Chisinau (Romanian: Chișinău) is the capital and largest city of the Republic of Moldova. Situated on the Bâc River in the centre of the country, it is Moldova's principal industrial, commercial, and cultural centre, with a population of roughly 700,000 in the city proper and more in the metropolitan area.

History

First mentioned in documents in 1436 as a small monastic village in the Principality of Moldavia, Chisinau grew after the Russian Empire annexed Bessarabia in 1812 and made it the regional capital. In 1903 and 1905 the city was the site of notorious anti-Jewish pogroms that drew international attention. Between the world wars it belonged to Romania; it was annexed by the Soviet Union in 1940 and became the capital of the Moldavian SSR. Chisinau became the capital of independent Moldova in 1991.

Geography and sights

The city is laid out around Ștefan cel Mare Boulevard, lined with government buildings, the Triumphal Arch (1840), the Nativity Cathedral, and Stephen the Great Park. Much of the centre was rebuilt in Soviet styles after heavy damage in the Second World War and the earthquake of 1940. Chisinau is surrounded by some of Moldova's wine country, including the vast underground cellars at Cricova.

Transport and institutions

Chișinău International Airport and the central railway station connect the city regionally, while trolleybuses form the backbone of public transport. The city hosts Moldova State University, the Academy of Sciences, and the country's principal theatres and museums.
