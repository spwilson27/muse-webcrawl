The Yorktown class was a class of three aircraft carriers built for the United States Navy and completed shortly before World War II: Yorktown (CV-5), Enterprise (CV-6), and Hornet (CV-8). Designed within the 20,000-ton limit of the Washington Naval Treaty, they formed the backbone of the US carrier force in the first year of the Pacific War.

Design

The ships carried about 90 aircraft from a full-length flight deck, with a hangar below served by three lifts. Light armour, a speed of 32.5 knots, and anti-aircraft armament of 5-inch and machine guns reflected pre-war expectations; wartime experience showed their vulnerability to torpedoes but also their capacity to absorb punishment.

Service

Hornet launched the Doolittle Raid on Tokyo in April 1942. All three ships fought at the Battle of Midway in June 1942, where Yorktown was sunk. Hornet was lost at the Battle of the Santa Cruz Islands in October 1942. Enterprise survived the war as the most decorated US Navy ship of World War II, earning 20 battle stars, before being decommissioned in 1947 and scrapped in 1958–60.
