Amateur radio

Amateur radio, also known as ham radio, is the use of radio frequency spectrum for purposes of non-commercial exchange of messages, wireless experimentation, self-training, private recreation, radiosport, contesting and emergency communications. Licensed amateur radio operators ("hams") operate on frequency bands allocated by the International Telecommunication Union and national regulators, using a personal call sign.

Licensing and activities

Operators must pass examinations on radio theory, regulations and operating practice; licence classes grant differing frequency and power privileges. Activities range from voice, Morse code (CW) and digital-mode contacts around the world via ionospheric propagation, to moonbounce, satellite operation (including amateur satellites carrying the OSCAR designation), contesting, DXing (seeking distant stations), and building home-made equipment and antennas.

Public service

Amateur radio has a long record of emergency communication when commercial infrastructure fails, through organisations such as ARES and RACES in the United States. Hams also assist at public events and provide weather spotter reports. National societies, such as the ARRL in the US and the RSGB in the UK, represent operators' interests; the service numbers roughly three million licensed operators worldwide.
