History of Facebook

The history of Facebook traces the growth of the social networking service from a Harvard University dormitory project in 2004 into one of the world's largest technology companies, Meta Platforms.

Founding

Facebook was founded by Mark Zuckerberg, along with Eduardo Saverin, Andrew McCollum, Dustin Moskovitz, and Chris Hughes. Launched on 4 February 2004 as "TheFacebook", it was initially restricted to Harvard students before expanding to other universities, then high schools, and in September 2006 to everyone aged 13 and over. The company moved to Palo Alto, California, in 2004, and dropped "The" from its name after purchasing the domain facebook.com in 2005.

Growth and IPO

Facebook introduced the News Feed in 2006 and the "Like" button in 2009. It surpassed one billion users in 2012, the year of its initial public offering, then one of the largest in technology history. The company acquired Instagram (2012), WhatsApp (2014), and Oculus VR (2014).

Controversies and Meta

Facebook has faced sustained criticism over privacy, misinformation, and its role in elections, notably the Cambridge Analytica scandal of 2018. In October 2021 the parent company rebranded as Meta Platforms, signalling a focus on the "metaverse", while Facebook remained the name of the social network itself.
