Xanthine oxidase

EC number1.17.3.2
GeneXDH (chromosome 2 in humans)
SubstratesHypoxanthine, xanthine
ProductUric acid
CofactorsMolybdenum, FAD, iron–sulfur clusters

Xanthine oxidase (XO) is an enzyme that catalyzes the oxidation of hypoxanthine to xanthine and of xanthine to uric acid, the final steps of purine catabolism in humans. It is a form of xanthine oxidoreductase, a large molybdenum-containing protein found especially in the liver and intestine, and also present in milk.

Reaction and structure

Each subunit of the homodimeric enzyme contains a molybdopterin centre, an FAD cofactor, and two iron–sulfur clusters. In the oxidase form, electrons flow from the molybdenum centre to molecular oxygen, generating superoxide and hydrogen peroxide as by-products; in the dehydrogenase form, NAD+ is the electron acceptor instead.

Clinical significance

Because xanthine oxidase produces uric acid, its inhibitors — allopurinol and febuxostat — are mainstays in the treatment of gout and hyperuricemia. Inherited deficiency of the enzyme causes xanthinuria. The reactive oxygen species the enzyme generates have also been implicated in ischemia–reperfusion injury.
