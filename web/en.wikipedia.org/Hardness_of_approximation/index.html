Hardness of approximation

FieldComputational complexity theory
Key toolsPCP theorem, gap-preserving reductions
RelatedApproximation algorithm, APX

Hardness of approximation is a field of computational complexity theory that studies the limits of efficiently approximating the optimal solutions of NP-hard optimisation problems. While an approximation algorithm guarantees a solution within some factor of optimal, hardness results show that no polynomial-time algorithm can achieve a better factor unless P = NP.

Background

Many combinatorial problems, such as vertex cover and the travelling salesman problem, are NP-hard to solve exactly but admit good approximations. Others, like the maximum clique problem, resist approximation within almost any factor. The field seeks matching upper and lower bounds on the best achievable approximation ratio.

PCP theorem

The breakthrough PCP theorem (Arora, Lund, Motwani, Sudan, Szegedy, 1990s) showed that proofs can be verified by reading only a constant number of randomly chosen bits. Applied through gap amplification, it implies, for example, that MAX-3SAT cannot be approximated beyond a specific constant factor (7/8, shown optimal by Håstad) unless P = NP.

Unique games

Subhash Khot's unique games conjecture (2002), if true, would settle the optimal hardness of many problems, including vertex cover and max cut. Major results include the Goemans–Williamson algorithm for max cut and its matching hardness under the conjecture.
