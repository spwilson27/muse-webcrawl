Durango and Silverton Narrow Gauge Railroad

A train traversing the Animas River gorge on the High Line

The Durango and Silverton Narrow Gauge Railroad (D&SNG) is a 3 ft (914 mm) narrow-gauge heritage railroad that operates on 45.2 miles (72.7 km) of track between Durango and Silverton, in the U.S. state of Colorado. The route provides a surviving link to the region's historic railroad and mining past and was originally built by the Denver and Rio Grande Railroad.

History

The line was constructed in 1881–82 to haul silver and gold ore from the San Juan Mountains to the smelter at Durango. Freight service continued into the 20th century, but the scenic passenger trade grew increasingly important after the Second World War. The Denver and Rio Grande Western sold the branch in 1981 to Charles Bradshaw Jr., and it has since operated as a tourist railroad; it is now owned by American Heritage Railways.

Route

Trains follow the Animas River north from Durango, climbing through the Animas Gorge and along the famous High Line shelf high above the river, before reaching the former mining town of Silverton at 9,318 feet (2,840 m). Steam locomotives, including former Rio Grande K-class Mikados, haul most trains.

Recognition

The railroad was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1961 (as part of the Denver & Rio Grande Western Railroad–San Juan Extension) and a National Historic Civil Engineering Landmark. It has appeared in numerous films, including Butch Cassidy and the Sundance Kid (1969).
