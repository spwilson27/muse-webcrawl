High Seas Treaty

Full nameAgreement under the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea on the Conservation and Sustainable Use of Marine Biological Diversity of Areas beyond National Jurisdiction
Short nameBBNJ Agreement / High Seas Treaty
Adopted19 June 2023
LocationNew York, United States
Condition60 ratifications required for entry into force

The High Seas Treaty, formally the Agreement under the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea on the Conservation and Sustainable Use of Marine Biological Diversity of Areas beyond National Jurisdiction (the BBNJ Agreement), is an international treaty adopted on 19 June 2023 under the framework of the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS). It is the first legally binding international instrument dedicated to protecting marine life in the high seas — the areas of ocean beyond any state's exclusive economic zone, which cover nearly two-thirds of the world's ocean surface and almost half the planet's surface area.

Background

Under UNCLOS, states exercise jurisdiction over waters within 200 nautical miles of their coasts, but the high seas beyond were governed by a patchwork of regional fisheries bodies and sectoral rules, with no comprehensive mechanism for creating protected areas or assessing the environmental impact of activities. Negotiations toward a new implementing agreement began formally in 2018, following more than a decade of preparatory discussions in a UN working group. After five negotiating sessions — and a final marathon session in March 2023 that ran more than 36 hours past its scheduled close — delegates agreed the text on 4 March 2023, and it was formally adopted that June.

Main provisions

The agreement rests on four pillars:

  • Marine genetic resources — rules on access to, and fair and equitable sharing of benefits arising from, genetic material collected from areas beyond national jurisdiction, including digital sequence information.
  • Area-based management tools — a process for establishing marine protected areas on the high seas, a key instrument toward the "30 by 30" goal of protecting 30 percent of the ocean by 2030 adopted under the Kunming–Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework.
  • Environmental impact assessments — requirements to assess and publicly report the potential impacts of planned activities in areas beyond national jurisdiction.
  • Capacity building and transfer of marine technology — support for developing states so they can participate in and benefit from high-seas science and governance.

Entry into force

The treaty opens for signature for two years from 20 September 2023 and enters into force 120 days after the sixtieth state deposits its instrument of ratification. By the time the signature period closed, more than 140 states had signed. Environmental organisations, including the High Seas Alliance coalition, hailed the agreement as the most significant ocean treaty in decades, while noting that its effectiveness would depend on rapid ratification and on the strength of the protected areas eventually designated under it.
