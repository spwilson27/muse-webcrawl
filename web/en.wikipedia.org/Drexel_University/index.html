Drexel University is a private research university with its main campus in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, United States. Founded in 1891 by financier and philanthropist Anthony J. Drexel, the university is known for its cooperative education ("co-op") program, in which students alternate periods of classroom study with full-time professional employment.

History

The Drexel Institute of Art, Science and Industry opened in 1891, offering education to men and women regardless of race, religion, or background—unusual for the era. It became the Drexel Institute of Technology in 1936 and gained university status as Drexel University in 1970. Drexel expanded significantly in the 2000s, adding a medical school (through the acquisition of MCP Hahnemann University's health colleges), a law school (now the Thomas R. Kline School of Law), and other units.

Cooperative education

Drexel's co-op program, begun in 1919, is one of the oldest and largest in the United States. Most undergraduates complete one to three six-month co-op employment periods with employers in their field before graduating, and the university partners with thousands of employers worldwide.

Campus and academics

The University City Campus sits adjacent to the University of Pennsylvania in West Philadelphia, with additional campuses including the Center City Campus and the Queen Lane Campus in East Falls. Drexel is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity" and enrolls over 20,000 students across more than a dozen colleges and schools.
