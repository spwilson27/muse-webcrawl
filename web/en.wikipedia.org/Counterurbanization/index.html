Counterurbanization

Counterurbanization
FieldHuman geography, demography
DefinitionNet migration from urban to rural areas
Term popularised byBrian Berry (1976)

Counterurbanization is a demographic and social process in which people move from urban areas to rural areas. It is the reverse of urbanization and was identified as a significant trend in the United States and other developed countries in the 1970s.

Causes

Drivers include the search for a perceived better quality of life, lower housing costs, dislike of congestion and pollution, retirement migration, and — with improvements in transport and communications — the ability to live rurally while working in or commuting to cities. More recently, remote working has been linked to renewed counterurban moves.

Patterns and effects

Counterurbanization often shows up first in accessible rural areas near cities, producing exurbs and "rural sprawl." Incoming wealthier residents can raise house prices, change the social composition of villages, and strain or revitalise local services — effects studied under the related heading of rural gentrification.

Debate

Geographers debate how far counterurbanization represents a genuine reversal of urbanisation rather than a stage of metropolitan deconcentration, since many movers remain within the functional orbit of cities. Trends vary by country and period, with re-urbanisation of large cities occurring in some places since the 1990s.
