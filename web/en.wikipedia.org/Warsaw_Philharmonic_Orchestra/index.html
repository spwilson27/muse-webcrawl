The Warsaw Philharmonic Orchestra (Polish: Orkiestra Filharmonii Narodowej) is Poland's national orchestra, based at the Warsaw National Philharmonic. Founded in 1901 under the patronage of Warsaw's musical and financial elite, it gave its inaugural concert on 5 November 1901 under its first artistic director, Emil Młynarski, with Ignacy Jan Paderewski as soloist.

History

Before the First World War the orchestra hosted guest artists such as Richard Strauss, Sergei Rachmaninoff, and Pablo de Sarasate, and in the interwar years, under Grzegorz Fitelberg, it became a leading European ensemble closely associated with Polish composers including Karol Szymanowski. The Philharmonic Hall was destroyed in the Siege of Warsaw in 1939; the present building, in socialist-realist style modelled on the old hall, opened in 1955 to host the V International Chopin Piano Competition.

The orchestra today

The orchestra is the resident ensemble of the International Chopin Piano Competition, accompanying finalists and recording extensively — through the competition it has been heard worldwide. Under long-serving directors Witold Rowicki, Kazimierz Kord, and Antoni Wit, it made hundreds of recordings, many for Naxos, including acclaimed cycles of Polish symphonic music. The orchestra tours internationally and performs with the Warsaw Philharmonic Choir in the National Philharmonic's concert season.
