Iron(III) oxide

Chemical formula: Fe2O3

Molar mass: 159.69 g/mol

Appearance: Red-brown solid

Density: 5.25 g/cm3

Melting point: 1,565 °C (decomposes)

Iron(III) oxide or ferric oxide is the inorganic compound with the formula Fe2O3. It is one of the three main oxides of iron, the others being iron(II) oxide (FeO) and iron(II,III) oxide (Fe3O4), which occurs naturally as the mineral magnetite. Iron(III) oxide occurs naturally as the mineral hematite, which is mined as the main ore of iron. It is also a major component of rust.

Structure and properties

The most important polymorph, α-Fe2O3 (hematite), adopts the corundum structure and is antiferromagnetic below about 260 K (the Morin transition) and weakly ferromagnetic at room temperature. The γ polymorph, maghemite, is ferrimagnetic and used in magnetic recording. Iron(III) oxide is insoluble in water but dissolves in strong acids such as hydrochloric and sulfuric acid.

Uses

Iron(III) oxide is the principal source of iron for the steel industry, reduced in blast furnaces. Finely ground hematite is used as a pigment (red ochre, "rouge") in paints, cosmetics and ceramics, and as a polishing agent for jewellery and lenses. It is the "red" component of the thermite reaction with aluminium powder, and hydrated forms (rust) are the familiar corrosion product of iron exposed to air and water.
