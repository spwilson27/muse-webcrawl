Sebring International Raceway is a road course auto racing facility in the southeastern United States, located near Sebring, Florida. The track occupies a portion of Sebring Regional Airport, originally built as Hendricks Army Airfield, a World War II training base for B-17 Flying Fortress crews.

History

The first race at Sebring was held on New Year's Eve 1950, when a six-hour endurance event organised by Alec Ulmann was won by a tiny Crosley. Ulmann developed the race into the 12 Hours of Sebring, first run in 1952, which became part of the World Sportscar Championship and one of the premier endurance races in the world, forming with Le Mans and Daytona an unofficial triple crown of endurance racing. The track also hosted the United States Grand Prix in 1959, the first Formula One race held under that name in the US.

The circuit

The full circuit measures 3.741 miles (6.019 km) with 17 turns, combining long runways of the old airfield with a winding infield section. Its notoriously rough, bumpy concrete surface — much of it original 1940s airfield pavement — is a punishing test of machinery, giving rise to the saying "Respect the bumps".

Events

The 12 Hours of Sebring, held each March, is now the second round of the IMSA SportsCar Championship. The World Endurance Championship's 1000 Miles of Sebring shared the weekend from 2019. The facility is also a popular winter testing venue for IndyCar and sports car teams.
