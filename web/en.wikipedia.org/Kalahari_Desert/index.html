Kalahari Desert

Kalahari Desert
Area: about 900,000 km²
Countries: Botswana, Namibia, South Africa
Features: Red sand; Okavango Delta (north); Central Kalahari Game Reserve

The Kalahari Desert is a large semi-arid sandy savanna in Southern Africa covering some 900,000 square kilometres across much of Botswana and parts of Namibia and South Africa. Despite its name, much of the Kalahari receives more rain than a true desert and supports grassland, camelthorn and shepherd's trees, and abundant wildlife; its name derives from the Tswana word Kgala, "the great thirst."

Geography and climate

The Kalahari is a vast, flat sand-covered basin — the Kalahari Basin — filled with red dune sand contrasting with the white sands of the Makgadikgadi Pans. Rainfall ranges from around 110 mm in the arid southwest to over 500 mm in the north. The Okavango River flows into the basin's northwest and dissipates in the Okavango Delta, one of the world's largest inland wetlands. The Central Kalahari Game Reserve in Botswana is among the largest protected areas in Africa.

People and wildlife

The San peoples have inhabited the Kalahari for tens of thousands of years as hunter-gatherers, and Tswana, Herero, and other groups pastoralists graze its margins. Wildlife includes lions famed for their black manes, meerkats, gemsbok, springbok, giraffes, and large populations of elephants in the north. Beneath the surface lie major mineral deposits, including the diamond mines of Orapa and Jwaneng in Botswana, among the richest in the world.
