Algol, designated Beta Persei (β Persei), is a bright multiple star system in the constellation of Perseus and one of the best-known eclipsing binaries. Its name derives from the Arabic raʾs al-ghūl, "the head of the ogre" (or demon), and it represents the head of the Gorgon Medusa in Greek mythology.

Variability

Every 2.867 days Algol dims dramatically for about ten hours as its larger but fainter companion passes in front of the bright primary star. The variability was formally described by John Goodricke in 1783, who proposed the eclipse explanation. Algol is the prototype of the class of Algol variables.

The Algol paradox

The system posed the "Algol paradox": the less massive component is more evolved (a subgiant) than the more massive primary, contradicting the rule that more massive stars evolve faster. The paradox was resolved by recognising mass transfer — the subgiant was originally the more massive star and has transferred much of its mass to its companion.

System

Algol is a triple system: the eclipsing pair is orbited by a third star with a period of about 680 days. It lies roughly 90 light-years from the Sun.
