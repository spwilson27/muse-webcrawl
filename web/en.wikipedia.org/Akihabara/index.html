Akihabara

Akihabara is a district in Chiyoda, Tokyo, famous as a centre for electronics retail and otaku culture. Nicknamed "Electric Town" (Akiba Denki-gai), it became a post-war black market for radio parts before developing into a major shopping area for electronics and computers.

Otaku culture

From the 1990s, Akihabara increasingly catered to fans of anime, manga and video games, with multi-storey shops, arcades and maid cafés. Its pedestrianised main street on Sundays (suspended after a 2008 mass stabbing, later resumed) is an iconic scene.

Landmarks

Notable sites include the Yodobashi-Akiba department store, the Radio Kaikan building and Akihabara Station, served by JR lines and the Tokyo Metro. The district's name derives from a shrine to a fire-quelling deity, Akiba.
