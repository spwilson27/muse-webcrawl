Ryotwari

TypeLand revenue settlement system
RegionBritish India (chiefly Madras and Bombay presidencies, Assam, Coorg)
Introducedexperimented 1792; widely applied from 1820s
Principal architectsAlexander Read, Thomas Munro
Contrasts withZamindari (Permanent Settlement), Mahalwari

The ryotwari system was one of the three principal methods of collecting land revenue in British India. Under ryotwari, the land revenue settlement was made directly between the government and the individual cultivator (the ryot, from an Arabic/Urdu word meaning peasant), who was recognised as the proprietor of the land he tilled, subject to paying the assessed revenue. It covered about 51 per cent of the area of British India, predominating in the Madras and Bombay presidencies and parts of Assam and Coorg.

Origins and operation

The system was first tried by Captain Alexander Read in the Baramahal district of the Madras Presidency in 1792 and was systematised by Thomas Munro, who applied it widely when he became Governor of Madras in 1820. Each field was surveyed and classified by soil quality, and revenue was assessed — initially at very high rates, often a third to a half of the gross produce — under settlements that were nominally temporary, usually for thirty years. The ryot received a patta (title document), could sell, mortgage or lease the land, and could relinquish fields he did not wish to cultivate, but was liable to lose the land for default in payment.

Effects and criticism

Advocates argued that ryotwari eliminated intermediaries and gave the cultivator a direct interest in improvement. In practice, heavy and inflexible assessments, collected in cash regardless of harvest conditions, drove many ryots into the hands of moneylenders and contributed to rural indebtedness and distress, factors in the Deccan Riots of 1875. Over-assessment was progressively moderated by later survey settlements, but historians continue to debate the system's effects on Indian agriculture and village society compared with zamindari and mahalwari regions.
