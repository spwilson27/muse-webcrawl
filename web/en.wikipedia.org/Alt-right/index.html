Alt-right

The alt-right grew largely through online forums, blogs and social media.

The alt-right (abbreviated from alternative right) is a loosely connected far-right, white nationalist movement that originated in the United States in the early twenty-first century. Its adherents reject mainstream conservatism and support white identity politics, opposing immigration, multiculturalism and political correctness. The term was popularised by Richard B. Spencer, who founded the AlternativeRight.com webzine in 2010.

Beliefs

Though ideologically diverse, the movement is characterised by white nationalism, anti-egalitarianism, and in many strands antisemitism and Islamophobia. Many adherents advocate an explicit white ethnostate. The movement made heavy use of internet memes, trolling and platforms such as 4chan and Reddit to spread its message, and it intersected with online subcultures around Gamergate and men's rights activism.

Rise and decline

The alt-right gained national attention during the 2016 United States presidential election. After the violent Unite the Right rally in Charlottesville, Virginia, in August 2017 — at which a counter-protester was killed — the movement faced lawsuits, deplatforming from major technology services and internal fragmentation, and its public prominence declined markedly.

Criticism and analysis

Civil-rights organisations such as the Southern Poverty Law Center and the Anti-Defamation League classify leading alt-right figures and outlets as extremist. Scholars debate the term's usefulness, since it was embraced by the movement itself partly as a rebranding of older white-supremacist politics.
