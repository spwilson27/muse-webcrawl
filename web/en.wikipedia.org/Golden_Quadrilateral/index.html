Golden Quadrilateral

The Golden Quadrilateral (Hindi: Svarnim Chaturbhuj) is a national highway network in India connecting the four major metropolitan cities of Delhi (north), Kolkata (east), Mumbai (west), and Chennai (south), forming a quadrilateral with a total length of about 5,846 kilometres (3,633 miles). It is the largest highway project in India and one of the longest in the world.

History and construction

The project was launched in 2001 as the flagship first phase of the National Highways Development Project (NHDP), under Prime Minister Atal Bihari Vajpayee, and was executed by the National Highways Authority of India. Most sections were substantially complete by 2012. The four legs — Delhi–Kolkata (1,453 km), Kolkata–Chennai (1,684 km), Chennai–Mumbai (1,290 km), and Mumbai–Delhi (1,419 km) — are four- or six-lane divided highways passing through 13 states.

Impact

The network carries a large share of India's road freight and passenger traffic despite comprising a small fraction of the national highway length, and is credited with reducing travel times, integrating regional markets, and spurring industrial corridors along its routes.
