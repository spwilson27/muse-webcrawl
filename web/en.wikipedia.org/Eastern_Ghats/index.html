Eastern Ghats

The Eastern Ghats are a discontinuous range of mountains along India's eastern coast, running from West Bengal through Odisha and Andhra Pradesh to Tamil Nadu. Unlike the continuous Western Ghats, they are broken by the great peninsular rivers — the Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri — on their way to the Bay of Bengal.

Geography

The range is lower and older than the Western Ghats, heavily eroded into separate hill groups such as the Nilgiri Hills (where the two ranges meet), the Nallamala Hills, the Papi Hills and the Javadi and Shevaroy hills of Tamil Nadu. The Godavari cuts a dramatic gorge through the Papi range.

Ecology

The Ghats support tropical dry and moist deciduous forests, home to tigers, elephants, gaur and endemic birds. Important protected areas include Simlipal, Satkosia, Nagarjunsagar-Srisailam and Sri Venkateswara national parks. The region is a centre of diversity for rice landraces and medicinal plants.

People

Numerous Adivasi (indigenous) communities, including the Kondh, Saora and Chenchu, inhabit the hill tracts, many practising shifting cultivation on the slopes.
