Tractatus Theologico-Politicus

AuthorBaruch Spinoza
LanguageLatin
Published1670 (anonymously, Hamburg imprint)
SubjectPhilosophy of religion, political philosophy, biblical criticism

The Tractatus Theologico-Politicus (Theological-Political Treatise) is a philosophical work by the Dutch philosopher Baruch Spinoza, published anonymously in 1670. Written in Latin with a false Hamburg imprint, it is an early example of biblical criticism and a landmark defence of freedom of thought and speech.

Content

In the first part of the treatise, Spinoza argues that prophecy was a product of vivid imagination rather than supernatural knowledge, that miracles are contrary to the fixed order of nature and properly understood as natural events, and that Scripture must be interpreted historically and philologically, from the text itself. He questions Mosaic authorship of the Pentateuch and maintains that the Bible's core moral teaching — obedience expressed as justice and charity — is distinct from philosophical truth.

The second part turns to politics: Spinoza separates theology from philosophy, argues that freedom to philosophize is compatible with piety and public peace, and defends a democratic state in which sovereign authority over religious practice rests with the civil power while inward belief remains free.

Reception

The treatise was denounced by religious authorities and banned by the States of Holland in 1674, along with Hobbes's Leviathan. It was nevertheless widely read and is now regarded as a foundational text of the Radical Enlightenment, liberal political thought, and modern historical-critical study of the Bible.
