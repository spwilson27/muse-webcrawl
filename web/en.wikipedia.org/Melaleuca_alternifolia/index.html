Melaleuca alternifolia, commonly known as tea tree, is a species of tree or tall shrub in the myrtle family, Myrtaceae. It is endemic to Australia, occurring in southeast Queensland and the north coast and adjacent ranges of New South Wales, where it grows along streams and on swampy flats.

Description

The plant grows to about 7 metres with a bushy crown and whitish, papery bark. Its leaves are arranged alternately, sometimes in whorls, and are narrow and aromatic. Small white or cream flowers appear in spikes in spring and summer, followed by woody, cup-shaped fruits.

Uses

The species is the principal source of tea tree oil, an essential oil steam-distilled from the leaves, traditionally used by Aboriginal Australians and now widely sold in topical products. Commercial plantations were established in Australia in the later twentieth century; tea tree oil is toxic if swallowed and is intended for external use only.
