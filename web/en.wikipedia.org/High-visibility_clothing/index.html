High-visibility clothing

High-visibility clothing, sometimes shortened to hi-vis, is any clothing worn that is highly luminescent in its natural matt property or a colour that is easily discernible from any background. It is most commonly worn in areas with low light and poor visibility, especially by people who work on or near roads, railways, construction sites, ports and airports. The garments use fluorescent background material for daytime conspicuity together with retroreflective bands that shine brightly when illuminated by headlights at night.

Standards and classes

Performance is governed by standards such as EN ISO 20471 in Europe and ANSI/ISEA 107 in the United States, which define classes (1, 2 and 3 in the European scheme; Types O, R and P in the American) according to the minimum areas of fluorescent and reflective material and the garment's intended use. Approved background colours are fluorescent yellow-green, fluorescent orange-red, and fluorescent red. Railway workers in the United Kingdom traditionally wear orange hi-vis (to avoid confusion with signal aspects), specified in the RIS-3279-TOM standard.

Users

Hi-vis clothing is worn by road and rail workers, construction workers, emergency services, airport ground crew, refuse collectors, cyclists and motorcyclists. Studies of cyclist conspicuity have found that fluorescent and reflective garments can increase the distance at which drivers detect riders, particularly at night when retroreflective material on moving joints (the "biomotion" pattern) is used.
