The Colosseum (Italian: Colosseo) is an elliptical amphitheatre in the centre of the city of Rome, Italy, just east of the Roman Forum. It is the largest ancient amphitheatre ever built, and is still the largest standing amphitheatre in the world, despite its age. Construction began under the Emperor Vespasian in AD 72 and was completed in AD 80 under his successor and heir, Titus.

The Colosseum is built of travertine limestone, tuff (volcanic rock), and brick-faced concrete. It could hold an estimated 50,000 to 80,000 spectators at various points in its history, and was used for gladiatorial contests and public spectacles including animal hunts, executions, re-enactments of famous battles, dramas based on Roman mythology, and briefly mock sea battles. The arena floor could be removed to reveal the hypogeum, a two-storey network of tunnels and cages where animals and gladiators were held.

History

Vespasian sited the amphitheatre on the drained lake of Nero's Domus Aurea, returning to the Roman people land the tyrant had taken — a political gesture celebrated at its opening with 100 days of games under Titus. The building was in use for some four centuries; the last gladiatorial fights are recorded in the 5th century and animal hunts continued into the 6th. Earthquakes in 847 and 1231 damaged the structure, and much of its stone was quarried for later buildings, including St Peter's Basilica.

Description

The outer wall stands 48 metres high, its three storeys of arcades framed by engaged columns in the Tuscan, Ionic, and Corinthian orders, with an attic above. A retractable awning, the velarium, shaded the crowd, rigged by sailors of the imperial fleet. Seating was strictly ordered by social rank, from the emperor's box and senatorial marble seats down to wooden benches for the poor at the top.

Modern significance

The Colosseum is an iconic symbol of Imperial Rome and one of Italy's most popular tourist attractions, receiving several million visitors a year. It was named one of the New7Wonders of the World in 2007 and is part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site covering the historic centre of Rome.
