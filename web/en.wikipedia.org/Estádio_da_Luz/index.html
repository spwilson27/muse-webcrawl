Estádio da Luz

The Estádio da Luz, home of S.L. Benfica

The Estádio da Luz (Portuguese pronunciation: [ɨˈʃtaðju ðɐ ˈluʃ]), officially named the Estádio do Sport Lisboa e Benfica, is a multi-purpose stadium in Lisbon, Portugal. It is the home ground of S.L. Benfica and, with a capacity of about 65,000, is the largest stadium in Portugal. Its name, "Stadium of Light," refers to the parish of Luz and to Our Lady of Light (Nossa Senhora da Luz); supporters nickname it A Catedral ("The Cathedral").

History

The current stadium, designed by Damon Lavelle of Populous, opened on 25 October 2003 on a site adjacent to the original Estádio da Luz (1954–2003), which had once been Europe's largest stadium by capacity. The new stadium hosted matches of UEFA Euro 2004, including the final, in which Greece defeated hosts Portugal 1–0. It also staged the 2014 UEFA Champions League Final, in which Real Madrid beat Atlético Madrid 4–1 after extra time, and the 2020 final, moved there during the COVID-19 pandemic, in which Bayern Munich defeated Paris Saint-Germain 1–0.

Features

The stadium's signature feature is its roof of translucent polycarbonate panels supported by great steel arches about 43 metres high, designed to flood the pitch with natural light. The complex includes the Benfica Museum (Museu Benfica – Cosme Damião), opened in 2013, and a statue of Eusébio, the club's greatest player, stands outside.
