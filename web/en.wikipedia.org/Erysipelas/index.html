Erysipelas

Erysipelas
Specialty: Infectious disease, dermatology
Cause: Streptococcus pyogenes
Symptoms: Red, swollen, warm skin; fever
Treatment: Antibiotics (penicillin)

Erysipelas is a bacterial infection of the upper layers of the skin (superficial epidermis and dermis), extending into the superficial lymphatics. It presents as a raised, well-demarcated, fiery-red rash, usually on the face or legs, accompanied by fever and malaise. It is caused most often by Streptococcus pyogenes (group A streptococcus).

Signs and symptoms

The affected skin is bright red, swollen, warm and painful, with a sharply defined border raised above normal skin; blistering may occur in severe cases. Onset is typically abrupt, with high fever, chills, headache and sometimes vomiting. Historically, facial erysipelas was called "St Anthony's fire" (a name also applied to ergotism).

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is usually clinical. Erysipelas differs from cellulitis in involving more superficial tissues and having a clearly demarcated edge. Treatment is with antibiotics — penicillin is first-line — together with rest and elevation of the affected limb. Recurrence is common, especially where lymphoedema or breaks in the skin persist, and preventive measures address predisposing conditions such as athlete's foot and venous ulcers.
