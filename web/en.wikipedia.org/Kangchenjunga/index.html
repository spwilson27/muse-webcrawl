Kangchenjunga, also spelled Kanchenjunga and Kangchendzönga, is the third-highest mountain in the world, with an elevation of 8,586 metres (28,169 ft). It lies on the border between Nepal and the Indian state of Sikkim. Its name derives from Tibetan words meaning "the five treasures of the great snow", after its five peaks, four of which exceed 8,450 metres.

Geography

The Kangchenjunga massif is drained by great glaciers including the Zemu in Sikkim and the Yalung and Kangchenjunga glaciers in Nepal. The mountain and its surroundings are protected by Khangchendzonga National Park in India — a UNESCO World Heritage Site and biosphere reserve — and by the Kanchenjunga Conservation Area in Nepal. The region is home to red pandas, snow leopards and, in local belief, the "Kangchenjunga Demon", a yeti-like creature.

Climbing history

Until 1852 Kangchenjunga was assumed to be the highest mountain in the world, before calculations by the Great Trigonometrical Survey of India assigned that status to Everest. The first ascent was made on 25 May 1955 by Joe Brown and George Band of a British expedition, who honoured the wishes of the people of Sikkim by stopping a few feet short of the summit itself — a tradition respected by most subsequent climbers. The mountain has a high fatality rate and comparatively few ascents, in part because India long restricted access and climbing from the Sikkim side.

In culture

Kangchenjunga is sacred to the Lepcha and other peoples of Sikkim, who worship it as a guardian deity, and it dominates views from Darjeeling and Gangtok.
