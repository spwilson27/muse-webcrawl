The Giant Dipper is a wooden roller coaster located at the Santa Cruz Beach Boardwalk in Santa Cruz, California, United States. Built by Arthur Looff to a design by Frank Prior and Frederick Church, it opened on 17 May 1924 and is one of the oldest roller coasters in the world still operating in its original location.

History

The Giant Dipper was constructed in 1924 at a cost of about $50,000, taking just 47 days to build. It quickly became the Boardwalk's signature attraction and has carried tens of millions of riders. The coaster survived the 1989 Loma Prieta earthquake and has been maintained in near-original condition, with its red-and-white painted structure becoming a landmark on the Santa Cruz waterfront. It was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1987 as part of the Boardwalk's recognition.

Ride experience

The ride begins with a passage through a dark tunnel before the chain lift carries trains up the 70-foot lift hill. The layout features a series of steep drops and banked turns along the Boardwalk, reaching speeds of about 55 miles per hour. The Giant Dipper consistently ranks highly in enthusiast polls of wooden coasters and appears in several films and television programmes.

Namesake coasters

Several other coasters have borne the Giant Dipper name, most notably the Giant Dipper at Belmont Park in San Diego, opened in 1925 and also designated a National Historic Landmark.
