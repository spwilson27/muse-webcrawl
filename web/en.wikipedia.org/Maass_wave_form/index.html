Maass wave form

In mathematics, Maass forms, also called Maass wave forms, are a class of periodic functions on the upper half-plane that are studied within the theory of automorphic forms. They were introduced by Hans Maass in 1949 and are non-holomorphic analogues of modular forms.

Definition

A Maass wave form is a smooth function on the upper half-plane that is invariant under the action of a discrete subgroup such as the modular group, is an eigenfunction of the hyperbolic Laplacian with a given eigenvalue, and satisfies moderate growth conditions at the cusps. Forms vanishing at the cusps are called Maass cusp forms. Their Fourier expansions involve Bessel functions (K-Bessel functions) rather than the exponentials that appear for holomorphic modular forms.

Significance

Maass forms connect number theory, spectral theory, and mathematical physics. Atle Selberg's work on the spectral decomposition of the Laplacian on modular surfaces placed Maass cusp forms at the heart of analytic number theory; their eigenvalues are connected with the Selberg eigenvalue conjecture. Maass forms also appear in quantum chaos as eigenfunctions on arithmetic hyperbolic surfaces, and their associated L-functions play a role parallel to those of holomorphic forms in the Langlands programme.
