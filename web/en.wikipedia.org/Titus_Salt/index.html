Sir Titus Salt, 1st Baronet (20 September 1803 – 29 December 1876) was a manufacturer, politician and philanthropist in Bradford, West Riding of Yorkshire, England, who built Salt's Mill and the village of Saltaire. He was a leading figure in the worsted and alpaca trades, making his fortune by introducing alpaca wool and Russian Donskoi wool into English textile manufacture.

Life and business

Salt worked in his father Daniel Salt's wool business in Bradford and by the 1830s had become one of the city's leading worsted spinners. His breakthrough came when he found a use for imported alpaca fleece with its long, silky fibres, developing machinery to spin it into lustrous cloth that became very fashionable. By mid-century his five mills employed thousands of workers in Bradford's notoriously polluted and unhealthy conditions.

Saltaire

To escape Bradford's congestion and provide his workers with better conditions, Salt built a vast new mill on the River Aire at Shipley, opened on his 50th birthday in 1853, and around it the model village of Saltaire. The village provided stone houses with running water and sanitation, a church, school, hospital, library, reading room, concert hall (now Victoria Hall), almshouses, and allotments — though, notably, no public house, reflecting Salt's temperance views and Congregationalist faith.

Politics and honors

Salt served as a Liberal Member of Parliament for Bradford from 1859 to 1861, was mayor of Bradford's predecessor bodies, and was active in civic affairs. He was created a baronet in 1869. Saltaire is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site (inscribed 2001), recognised as an exceptionally complete and well-preserved example of a mid-19th-century industrial town.
