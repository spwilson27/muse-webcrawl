Calendar of Saints

The calendar of saints is the traditional Christian method of organising a liturgical year by associating each day with one or more saints and referring to the day as the feast day or feast of said saint.

Origins

The system grew from the early Church's practice of commemorating martyrs on the anniversaries of their deaths — their 'birthdays' into heaven. Local lists of martyrs merged over centuries into general calendars.

Different traditions

The Roman Catholic General Roman Calendar, revised after the Second Vatican Council, assigns feasts of universal significance; national and diocesan calendars add local saints. Eastern Orthodox, Anglican, and Lutheran churches maintain their own calendars, which overlap substantially for early saints and diverge for later ones.

Ranking of feasts

In the current Roman rite feasts are ranked as solemnities, feasts, and memorials (obligatory or optional). Older calendars used doubles and other grades.

Cultural legacy

Saints' days long structured the agricultural year and naming customs: Michaelmas, Candlemas, St Swithin's Day, and name days still celebrated in many European countries descend from the calendar.
