Concussion

A concussion, also known as a mild traumatic brain injury (mTBI), is a head injury that temporarily affects brain functioning. Symptoms may include headache, dizziness, confusion, nausea, problems with balance, and temporary loss of consciousness, although most concussions do not involve losing consciousness. Causes include falls, motor vehicle collisions, sports injuries, and assaults.

Mechanism and diagnosis

Concussion results from mechanical forces — direct blows or rapid acceleration and deceleration — transmitted to the brain, causing a temporary disturbance of function without gross structural damage visible on standard CT or MRI scans. Diagnosis is clinical, based on the mechanism of injury and symptoms; tools such as the Glasgow Coma Scale and standardised symptom checklists assist assessment.

Treatment and recovery

Treatment generally involves a brief period of physical and cognitive rest followed by a gradual return to activity. Most people recover fully within days to a few weeks, but a minority experience persistent post-concussion symptoms. Repeated concussions, particularly in contact sports, are associated with long-term risks including chronic traumatic encephalopathy (CTE). Prevention includes helmets, rule changes, and prompt removal from play after a suspected injury.
