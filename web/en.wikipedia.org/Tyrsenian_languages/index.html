The Tyrsenian languages (also Tyrrhenian) are a proposed language family consisting of the Etruscan language of ancient Italy, the Raetic language of the Alps, and the Lemnian language of the island of Lemnos in the Aegean Sea. The grouping, suggested on the basis of shared phonological, morphological, and syntactic features — and notably related sets of past-tense verb endings — was developed in detail by the linguist Helmut Rix and is accepted by many specialists in ancient Italic languages, though the small surviving corpora make it difficult to prove conclusively.

The name derives from the Tyrrhenians (Tyrsenoi), the Greek name for the Etruscans. Herodotus reported a tradition that the Etruscans migrated from Lydia in Anatolia; the presence of the closely related Lemnian language on an island near Anatolia has been cited both in support of and against migration theories.

The languages

  • Etruscan — attested in some 13,000 inscriptions from Etruria, from the 7th century BC into the Roman era; largely decipherable in script but only partly understood.
  • Raetic — attested in about 280 inscriptions in the eastern Alps, written in an Etruscan-derived alphabet.
  • Lemnian — known mainly from a 6th-century BC funerary stele found at Kaminia on Lemnos in 1885.
