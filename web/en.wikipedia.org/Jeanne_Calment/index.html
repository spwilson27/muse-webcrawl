Jeanne Louise Calment (1875–1997) was a French supercentenarian whose lifespan of 122 years and 164 days is the longest verified in history. Born in Arles in 1875, she lived through the entire span from the era of horse-drawn carriages to the internet age, and as a girl met Vincent van Gogh when he bought art supplies from her family's shop in 1888.

Life

Calment married into a wealthy family, lived comfortably, and outlived her daughter and grandson. In 1965, aged 90, she made a famous life-annuity agreement with lawyer André-François Raffray, who paid her a monthly sum for her apartment — only to die before her, having paid more than the flat's value. She took up fencing at 85, cycled until 100, and remained mentally sharp and quotable in interviews past 120.

Verification and debate

Her age was extensively documented by researchers Jean-Marie Robine and Michel Allard. In 2018 a Russian theory proposed that Jeanne had assumed her mother's identity in 1934, but French researchers reaffirmed the original records after detailed investigation in 2019.
