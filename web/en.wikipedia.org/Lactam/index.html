Lactam

Type: Cyclic amide
Examples: β-lactam (penicillin core), ε-caprolactam

A lactam is a cyclic amide: a ring compound in which an amide group forms part of the ring. The name is a portmanteau of lactone and amide. Lactams are named by ring size with Greek prefixes — β-lactams have four-membered rings, γ-lactams five, δ-lactams six, and ε-lactams seven.

Synthesis

Lactams are commonly prepared by the Beckmann rearrangement of cyclic ketone oximes, by cyclisation of amino acids, or, for β-lactams, by the Staudinger (ketene–imine) synthesis.

Applications

The β-lactam ring is the core of penicillin and cephalosporin antibiotics; bacterial β-lactamase enzymes that cleave it are a major source of antibiotic resistance. ε-Caprolactam is polymerised on an industrial scale to make nylon-6. 2-Pyrrolidone and related γ-lactams are common solvents and pharmaceutical building blocks.
