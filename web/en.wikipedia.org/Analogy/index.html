Analogy

Analogy is a cognitive process of transferring information or meaning from a particular subject (the analogue or source) to another (the target), or a linguistic expression corresponding to such a process. In a narrower sense, analogy is an inference or an argument from one particular to another particular, as opposed to deduction, induction, and abduction, in which at least one of the premises is general.

In reasoning

Analogical reasoning plays a central role in problem solving, decision making, perception, memory, creativity, and communication. Philosophers have examined argument by analogy — for example in ethics, law (reasoning from precedent), and science, where analogical models such as the "solar system" model of the atom are used heuristically.

In linguistics and mathematics

In linguistics, analogy is a process by which new forms are created on the pattern of existing ones (such as children producing "goed" by analogy with regular verbs). In mathematics and logic, analogy often connects with the concept of proportionality and structural similarity, and in cognitive science, structure-mapping theory (Dedre Gentner) models analogy as the alignment of relational structures between source and target domains.
