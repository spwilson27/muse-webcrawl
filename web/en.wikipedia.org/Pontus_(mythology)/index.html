In Greek mythology, Pontus (Greek: Πόντος, Póntos, "Sea") was an ancient primordial sea-god, one of the first-born deities. According to Hesiod's Theogony, Pontus was the son of Gaia (Earth) alone, born "without sweet union of love"; Hyginus describes him as the offspring of Gaia and Aether. He personified the sea itself, in contrast to the later Olympian sea-god Poseidon who ruled it.

Offspring

By Gaia, Pontus fathered the elder sea deities: Nereus, the truthful "Old Man of the Sea"; Thaumas; Phorcys and his sister-consort Ceto, progenitors of many sea monsters; and Eurybia, noted for her heart of flint. With Thalassa, the sea personified as female, he was said to be the father of the Telchines and of the fish of the sea. His grandchildren include the Nereids, the Gorgons and the Graeae.

Depiction and legacy

Pontus had no temples or developed cult and only a small role in myth, appearing mainly in genealogies. In Roman mosaic art he is occasionally shown as a large, aged bearded head rising from the water, with crab-claw horns and seaweed hair. The name Pontus also became the Greek name for the Black Sea (the Pontos Euxeinos, "Hospitable Sea") and for the region of Pontus on its southern shore in Anatolia — a separate usage from the god.
