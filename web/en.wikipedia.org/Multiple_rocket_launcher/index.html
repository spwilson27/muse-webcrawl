A multiple rocket launcher (MRL) or multiple launch rocket system (MLRS) is a type of rocket artillery system that contains multiple launchers, typically mounted on a self-propelled vehicle chassis, from which rockets can be fired in rapid succession. MRLs are capable of delivering a large volume of fire onto a target area in a very short time, saturating it with high-explosive warheads.

History

Early precursors include the Korean hwacha of the 15th century and Congreve rockets used in the 19th century. The modern multiple rocket launcher emerged during the Second World War, most famously with the Soviet Katyusha systems, which fired salvos of rockets from truck-mounted rails and had a devastating psychological and material effect. Germany fielded the Nebelwerfer series, and the United States deployed the T34 Calliope.

Characteristics

Unlike conventional tube artillery, MRLs trade sustained accuracy and rate of fire for the ability to deliver a massive opening salvo. Unguided rockets follow simple ballistic trajectories, while modern systems such as the American M142 HIMARS fire guided rockets with GPS precision. Typical calibres range from about 107 mm to over 300 mm, with ranges from a few kilometres to several hundred kilometres for the largest tactical systems.

Employment

MRLs are used for counter-battery fire, suppression of air defences, area denial with submunitions or mines, and deep strikes against logistics and command targets. Their "shoot-and-scoot" mobility helps them survive counter-battery fire after revealing their position by firing.
