Sarilumab, sold under the brand name Kevzara, is a human monoclonal antibody medication used for the treatment of rheumatoid arthritis. It works by blocking the interleukin-6 receptor (IL-6R), thereby inhibiting signalling by interleukin-6, a cytokine involved in inflammation. Sarilumab was developed by Regeneron Pharmaceuticals and Sanofi and was approved by the US Food and Drug Administration in May 2017 and by the European Commission shortly afterwards.

Sarilumab is administered by subcutaneous injection, typically every two weeks.

Medical uses

Sarilumab is indicated for adults with moderately to severely active rheumatoid arthritis who have had an inadequate response to, or are intolerant of, conventional disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs). It is given by subcutaneous injection, usually 200 mg every two weeks, alone or in combination with methotrexate or other DMARDs. It has also been approved for polymyalgia rheumatica in patients with an inadequate response to corticosteroids. During the COVID-19 pandemic, IL-6 receptor blockers were investigated and used in some settings for severe disease, although results for sarilumab specifically were mixed.

Adverse effects

Common adverse effects include injection-site reactions, neutropenia, elevated liver enzymes, and increased cholesterol. As an immunosuppressant, sarilumab increases susceptibility to serious infections, and patients are screened for latent tuberculosis before treatment. Unlike some related antibodies, it is not used in patients with diverticulitis risk factors without caution, because of a risk of gastrointestinal perforation.
