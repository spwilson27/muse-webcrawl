Coworking

Coworking is an arrangement in which workers for different companies share an office space. It allows cost savings and convenience through the use of common infrastructure, such as equipment, utilities, and receptionist and custodial services, and in some cases refreshments and parcel acceptance services.

History

The term is generally credited to game designer and writer Bernard DeKoven, who used it in 1999 for computer-supported collaborative work; the first physical space marketed as a "coworking space", Brad Neuberg's Citizen Space in San Francisco, opened in 2005. The model expanded rapidly in the 2010s, and large operators such as WeWork grew the concept into a global industry offering hot desks, dedicated desks, private offices, and meeting rooms on flexible memberships.

Users and appeal

Coworking spaces attract freelancers, remote employees, startups, and small companies seeking flexibility, professional amenities, networking, and a community they would not have working from home. Surveys report that many members value the social atmosphere and expanded professional networks. After the COVID-19 pandemic, growth in hybrid work pushed many larger employers to take flexible space as well.

Criticism

Critics note distractions and noise, limited privacy and security for sensitive work, costs that can exceed conventional leases at scale, and business-model risks exposed by WeWork's failed 2019 IPO and later bankruptcy filing in 2023.
