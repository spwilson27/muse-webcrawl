Machine learning

Machine learning (ML) is a field of artificial intelligence concerned with building systems that learn from data — improving their performance on a task through experience rather than explicit programming of rules. The term was coined by Arthur Samuel in 1959. Modern machine learning underlies applications from speech recognition and machine translation to recommendation systems, medical diagnosis, and large language models.

Approaches

In supervised learning, models are trained on labelled examples to perform classification or regression; common algorithms include linear and logistic regression, decision trees, random forests, support vector machines, and neural networks. Unsupervised learning finds structure in unlabelled data, as in clustering and dimensionality reduction. Reinforcement learning trains agents to act by rewarding desired behaviour. Deep learning, using multilayer neural networks, drove breakthroughs in the 2010s in image recognition, natural language processing (via the transformer architecture), and game playing (AlphaGo).

Training and evaluation

Models are fitted by minimising a loss function, typically with gradient-based optimisation, and evaluated on held-out data to detect overfitting. Key concerns include bias–variance trade-off, data quality and representativeness, interpretability, and robustness to distribution shift.

Applications and concerns

Machine learning powers search engines, spam filters, fraud detection, drug discovery, and generative AI. Its deployment raises issues of algorithmic bias, privacy, transparency, safety, and environmental cost, which are active areas of research and regulation.
