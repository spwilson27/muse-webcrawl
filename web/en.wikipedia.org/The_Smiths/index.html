The Smiths were an English rock band formed in Manchester in 1982, composed of singer Morrissey, guitarist Johnny Marr, bassist Andy Rourke and drummer Mike Joyce. They are widely regarded as one of the most important alternative rock bands to emerge from the British independent music scene of the 1980s.

History

The songwriting partnership of Morrissey and Marr produced a distinctive sound — Marr's jangling guitar work paired with Morrissey's literate, wry lyrics — on Rough Trade Records. Their debut album The Smiths (1984) was followed by Meat Is Murder (1985), which topped the UK chart, and The Queen Is Dead (1986), frequently voted among the greatest albums of all time. Internal tensions, principally between Morrissey and Marr, led Marr to leave in 1987; the band split shortly after the release of Strangeways, Here We Come (1987). A 1996 court case over royalties, in which Joyce successfully sued Morrissey and Marr, deepened the estrangement, and the band have consistently refused lucrative reunion offers. Rourke died in 2023.
