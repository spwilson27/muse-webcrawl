Speech

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Speech is the use of the human voice as a medium of communication and the faculty of doing so. Spoken language combines voice sounds into sequences of words with conventional meanings. Speech is one of the main ways in which humans convey meaning, alongside writing, sign language, and gesture. The study of speech sounds is called phonetics, and the study of how speech is produced and perceived spans linguistics, cognitive science, and medicine.

Production

Speech production begins with conceptualization and linguistic formulation in the brain, followed by articulation. Air from the lungs passes through the larynx, where the vocal folds vibrate to produce voiced sounds, and the resulting sound is shaped by the tongue, lips, palate, and other articulators in the vocal tract. Resonances of the vocal tract, called formants, give vowels their characteristic qualities, and are visible in a spectrogram. Areas of the brain including Broca's area and Wernicke's area are central to producing and comprehending speech.

Perception and development

Listeners perceive speech by mapping continuous acoustic signals onto discrete phonemes — a phenomenon involving categorical perception. Infants begin babbling in the first year of life and typically produce first words around twelve months, rapidly acquiring the sound system and grammar of their native language during early childhood.

Disorders

Speech disorders include stuttering, dysarthria, apraxia of speech, articulation disorders, and aphasia following brain injury. Their assessment and treatment is the domain of speech-language pathology. Technologies such as automatic speech recognition and speech synthesis reproduce aspects of human speech with computers.
