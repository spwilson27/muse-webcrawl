Bamako is the capital and largest city of Mali, located on the Niger River in the southwestern part of the country. It is Mali's administrative, commercial and industrial centre, one of the fastest-growing cities in Africa, and the country's principal river port. The name Bamako derives from Bambara words often interpreted as "crocodile river".

History

The Bamako area has been inhabited since Palaeolithic times. Bamako itself was a modest Bambara settlement until the French established a fort there in 1883; it became the capital of French Sudan in 1908 and was connected by rail to Dakar, Senegal (the Dakar–Niger Railway). When Mali gained independence in 1960 under President Modibo Keïta, Bamako became the capital of the new republic.

Geography and climate

The city straddles both banks of the Niger River, linked by several bridges, including the Martyrs Bridge and the King Fahd Bridge, and is ringed by hills. Bamako has a tropical savanna climate, with a rainy season from about June to October and a hot dry season dominated by the harmattan wind; April and May are the hottest months.

Economy and culture

Bamako's economy centres on government, trade, textiles, processed meat, metalworking and the river trade. Landmarks include the Grand Mosque, the National Museum of Mali, the Bamako Zoo and botanical gardens, and the BCEAO Tower. The city is famed for its music scene — home to artists such as Salif Keita and Amadou & Mariam — and hosted the Rencontres de Bamako photography festival. The University of Bamako serves tens of thousands of students.
