Larmor formula

The Larmor formula, derived by J. J. Larmor in 1897, gives the total power radiated by a non-relativistic accelerating charged particle: P = q²a² / (6πε₀c³), where q is the charge and a the acceleration. It shows that accelerated charges radiate electromagnetic energy.

Implications

The formula underlies synchrotron radiation losses, bremsstrahlung estimates, and the classical problem of radiation reaction. It posed a difficulty for classical atomic models: an orbiting electron should radiate and spiral into the nucleus, one of the puzzles resolved by quantum mechanics.

Generalisation

The relativistic generalisation is the Liénard formula. The angular distribution of the radiation is proportional to sin²θ, where θ is measured from the direction of acceleration.
