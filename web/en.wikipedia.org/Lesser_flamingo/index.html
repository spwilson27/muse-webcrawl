Lesser flamingo

The lesser flamingo (Phoeniconaias minor) is the smallest species of flamingo and, with an estimated population of two to three million, probably the most numerous. It occurs mainly in sub-Saharan Africa, with a separate population in north-western India and Pakistan.

Description and feeding

Standing about 80–90 cm tall, the lesser flamingo has deep pink plumage, dark red legs and eyes, and a dark red-and-black bill with a uniquely deep keel. It is a specialist filter feeder, sweeping its bill through shallow alkaline and saline lakes to strain out blue-green algae (Spirulina) and diatoms, which it consumes in enormous quantities — a single flock may take tonnes of algae per day.

Breeding and conservation

The species breeds at very few sites, notably Lake Natron in Tanzania — the source of the great majority of the world population — and lakes in the Rift Valley of Kenya and Ethiopia, building mud-mound nests on soda flats when water levels are suitable. Breeding failures, water extraction, soda-ash mining proposals at Lake Natron and pollution have led the IUCN to list the lesser flamingo as Near Threatened.
