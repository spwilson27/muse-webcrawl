Word (computer architecture)

In computing, a word is the natural unit of data used by a particular processor design: the fixed-sized group of bits that the processor handles as a unit in its instruction set and registers. The word size is a defining characteristic of a computer architecture.

Word sizes

Early computers used word sizes of 12, 18, 24, or 36 bits. The microprocessor era standardised on 8-bit bytes and word sizes of 16 bits (8086 era), 32 bits (the dominant size through the 1990s), and 64 bits (modern desktops, servers, and mobile processors).

Significance

Word size determines the width of registers and data paths, the amount of memory directly addressable (a 32-bit word conventionally addresses 4 GiB), and the size of integers processed in a single operation. Related units include the halfword, double word (dword), and quad word (qword).

Terminology

Conventions differ: in Intel terminology a "word" is permanently 16 bits regardless of architecture, whereas in many other contexts the word size follows the machine's native register width.
