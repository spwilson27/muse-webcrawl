National Endowment for the Arts

The National Endowment for the Arts (NEA) is an independent agency of the United States federal government that supports participation in the arts. It was established by Congress in 1965 as part of the National Foundation on the Arts and the Humanities Act, signed by President Lyndon B. Johnson.

Role and funding

The NEA provides grants to nonprofit arts organisations, state and regional arts agencies, and individual artists and writers (historically through literature fellowships). Its funding is designed to leverage matching support from state, local and private sources. The agency's budget is appropriated annually by Congress and has frequently been the subject of political debate.

History

During the 1980s and 1990s, controversies over grants to provocative artists — the so-called "culture wars" — led to significant budget cuts and the elimination of most individual artist fellowships. The agency survived attempts to abolish it and continues to fund arts education, folk and traditional arts, and community arts projects in every congressional district.

Leadership

The NEA is headed by a chairperson appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate. Notable chairs have included Nancy Hanks, who greatly expanded the agency in the 1970s, poet Dana Gioia, and Broadway producer Rocco Landesman.
