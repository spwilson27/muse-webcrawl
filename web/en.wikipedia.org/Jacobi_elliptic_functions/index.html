Jacobi elliptic functions

In mathematics, the Jacobi elliptic functions are a set of basic elliptic functions, together with auxiliary theta functions, that are important in the theory of elliptic functions and have historical and practical applications. They were introduced by Carl Gustav Jacob Jacobi around 1829 and generalise the trigonometric functions to doubly periodic meromorphic functions of a complex variable.

Definition

The three fundamental functions are sn, cn and dn, defined as inverses of elliptic integrals or through the amplitude function am. If the amplitude φ satisfies an elliptic integral involving the parameter m (or modulus k), then sn u = sin φ, cn u = cos φ, and dn u = √(1 − m sin²φ). They satisfy the identities sn²u + cn²u = 1 and dn²u + m·sn²u = 1.

Other functions

From the three fundamental functions, nine further functions are formed as ratios and reciprocals (such as ns, dc, sc), giving twelve Jacobi elliptic functions in all, conventionally named in "Glaisher notation" using pairs of letters.

Properties and applications

The functions sn and cn have two independent periods (real and imaginary), while dn has one real and one imaginary period. They reduce to sine and cosine (or to hyperbolic functions) in limiting cases of the parameter. Jacobi elliptic functions solve nonlinear ordinary differential equations such as the equation of motion of a pendulum and the Euler equations for a rotating rigid body, and appear in conformal mapping and in the analysis of the simple pendulum beyond the small-angle approximation.
