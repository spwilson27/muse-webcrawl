Pediment

A pediment is a triangular gable forming the end of the roof slope above the portico of a classical building, framed by a horizontal entablature below and raking cornices along the sloping sides. The recessed triangular field within is called the tympanum.

History

Pediments were a defining feature of ancient Greek temple architecture; the pediments of the Parthenon in Athens were filled with monumental sculpture depicting the birth of Athena (east) and her contest with Poseidon (west). Roman architects adopted the form widely, and it was revived in Renaissance, Baroque, and Neoclassical architecture — appearing on churches, palaces, banks, courthouses, and porticoed houses.

Variants

In Baroque and Rococo architecture the strict triangle was varied: broken pediments have a gap at the apex, often filled with an urn or cartouche; segmental pediments follow a shallow arc; swan's neck pediments use S-curved sloping sides. In domestic architecture and furniture design, smaller pediments crown doors, windows, and bookcases.
