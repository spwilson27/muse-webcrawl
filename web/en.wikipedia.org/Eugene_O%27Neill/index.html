Eugene O'Neill

Eugene Gladstone O'Neill (October 16, 1888 – November 27, 1953) was an American playwright and Nobel laureate in literature. His poetically titled plays were among the first in the United States to include characters on the fringes of society and to use vernacular language.

BornOctober 16, 1888, New York City, U.S.
DiedNovember 27, 1953 (aged 65), Boston, Massachusetts

Notable awardsNobel Prize in Literature (1936); Pulitzer Prize for Drama (1920, 1922, 1928, 1957)

Life

O'Neill was the son of the actor James O'Neill, famous for playing the Count of Monte Cristo. After a restless youth spent at sea, in gold prospecting, and in journalism, he turned to playwriting, studying with George Pierce Baker at Harvard. He became associated with the Provincetown Players, whose productions launched his career.

Major works

His breakthrough play, Beyond the Horizon (1920), won the Pulitzer Prize, as did Anna Christie (1921) and Strange Interlude (1928). Other major works include Desire Under the Elms (1924), Mourning Becomes Electra (1931), Ah, Wilderness! (1933), and The Iceman Cometh (written 1939, produced 1946). His autobiographical masterpiece Long Day's Journey into Night was produced posthumously in 1956 and won the Pulitzer Prize in 1957.

Legacy

O'Neill is widely regarded as the father of modern American drama, bringing psychological depth, tragedy, and experimental forms such as expressionism and the soliloquy-aside to the American stage.
