Uniform convergence

Uniform convergence
Field: Mathematical analysis
Stronger than: Pointwise convergence
Key test: Weierstrass M-test

In mathematical analysis, uniform convergence is a mode of convergence of sequences of functions that is stronger than pointwise convergence. A sequence of functions fn converges uniformly to a limit f if, for every ε > 0, there is an index N such that for all n ≥ N the values fn(x) and f(x) differ by less than ε for every x in the domain simultaneously.

Properties

Uniform convergence preserves continuity: a uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous. It also justifies interchanging limits with integration over bounded intervals, and, under additional hypotheses, differentiation. The Weierstrass M-test gives a convenient sufficient condition, and uniform convergence on compact sets is central to the theory of power series and Fourier series.
