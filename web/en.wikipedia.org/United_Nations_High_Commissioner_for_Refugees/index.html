The Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR), the UN Refugee Agency, is a United Nations programme mandated to protect and support refugees, forcibly displaced communities, and stateless people, and to assist in their voluntary repatriation, local integration, or resettlement to a third country. It was established by the UN General Assembly on 14 December 1950, initially to help Europeans displaced by the Second World War, and is headquartered in Geneva.

Mandate and work

UNHCR's work is grounded in the 1951 Refugee Convention and its 1967 Protocol, which define a refugee and enshrine the principle of non-refoulement — that no one should be returned to a country where they face serious threats to life or freedom. The agency provides shelter, food, water, health care, and education in emergencies, registers and documents displaced people, advocates for asylum rights, and seeks durable solutions. Its mandate has expanded over the decades to cover internally displaced persons and stateless populations.

By the mid-2020s, the number of forcibly displaced people worldwide exceeded 110 million, driven by conflicts in Syria, Ukraine, Sudan, Myanmar, Afghanistan, and elsewhere, straining UNHCR's operations across roughly 130 countries with a workforce of around 20,000.

Recognition

UNHCR has twice received the Nobel Peace Prize, in 1954 and 1981. The High Commissioner is elected by the UN General Assembly on the nomination of the Secretary-General; Filippo Grandi of Italy has held the post since 2016. The agency is funded almost entirely by voluntary contributions from governments and private donors, and it presents the annual Nansen Refugee Award for outstanding service to displaced people.
