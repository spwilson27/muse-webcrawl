Six Nations Championship

SportRugby union
Founded1883 (as Home Championship)
TeamsEngland, France, Ireland, Italy, Scotland, Wales
FormatSingle round-robin, February–March

The Six Nations Championship is an annual international rugby union competition between the teams of England, France, Ireland, Italy, Scotland and Wales. It is the oldest international rugby championship in the world, and the premier annual tournament of the Northern Hemisphere game, played over five weekends each February and March.

History

The competition began as the Home Championship in 1883, contested by England, Ireland, Scotland and Wales. France joined in 1910, creating the Five Nations, though France was expelled in 1931 over professionalism and violence and did not return until 1947. Italy was admitted in 2000, completing the present six-team format. Matches for heritage trophies are contested within the tournament, including the Calcutta Cup (England v Scotland, dating to 1879), the Triple Crown (awarded if one of the home nations beats the other three), and the Giuseppe Garibaldi Trophy (France v Italy).

Format and honours

Each team plays the other five once, alternating home and away fixtures year by year, so every pair meets at home once every two years. League points are awarded (since 2017 with bonus points for scoring four tries or losing by seven points or fewer). Winning all five matches is a Grand Slam; finishing last brings the tongue-in-cheek "wooden spoon." England and Wales have historically won the most titles, with Ireland and France also enjoying dominant eras; Italy has never won the championship. A parallel Women's Six Nations, and an Under-20 championship, are played each year.

Significance

The championship draws some of the largest crowds in world sport — matches at Twickenham, the Principality Stadium, Murrayfield, the Aviva Stadium, the Stade de France and the Stadio Olimpico regularly attract 50,000–82,000 spectators — and its fixtures are among the most-watched annual events in British, Irish, French and Italian sport. Its roots in 19th-century fixtures between the home nations make it a direct descendant of rugby's first internationals.
