AmeriCorps

AmeriCorps
Agency overview
Formed: 1993
Headquarters: Washington, D.C.
Parent agency: Corporation for National and Community Service

AmeriCorps is an independent agency of the United States government that engages more than five million Americans in service through a variety of stipended volunteer work programs in many sectors. The agency was established in 1993 as the Corporation for National and Community Service and adopted the AmeriCorps name as its primary brand in 2020.

Programs

AmeriCorps programs include AmeriCorps State and National, which funds local and national organizations; AmeriCorps VISTA, focused on alleviating poverty; the National Civilian Community Corps (NCCC), a residential program for young adults; and AmeriCorps Seniors, which includes Foster Grandparents, Senior Companions, and RSVP.

History

The National and Community Service Trust Act of 1993, signed by President Bill Clinton, created the modern AmeriCorps. Members who complete a term of service receive the Segal AmeriCorps Education Award, which can be used for education expenses or student loan repayment.
