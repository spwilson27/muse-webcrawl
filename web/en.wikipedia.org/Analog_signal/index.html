Analog signal

An analog signal is a continuous signal representing some physical quantity, in contrast to a digital signal, which represents information as a sequence of discrete values. An analog signal varies over a continuous range and can in principle take an infinite number of values within that range.

Description

In electrical engineering, an analog signal is typically a varying voltage or current that mirrors changes in sound, light, temperature, position, or pressure, transduced by devices such as microphones and thermocouples. Signals are described in terms of amplitude, frequency, and phase.

Advantages and disadvantages

Analog signals can offer fine resolution and simple, inexpensive processing, and they describe natural phenomena directly. Their chief drawback is noise: every copy or amplification stage degrades the signal, whereas digital signals can be regenerated perfectly. This is the principal reason modern telecommunications, recording, and broadcasting have migrated from analog to digital representations.

Modulation

Analog signals are transmitted over radio by modulating a carrier wave — classically by amplitude modulation (AM) or frequency modulation (FM).
