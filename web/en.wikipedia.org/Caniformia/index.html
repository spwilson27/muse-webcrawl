Caniformia is a suborder within the order Carnivora, consisting of the "dog-like" carnivorans. It includes the dogs and their relatives (Canidae), bears (Ursidae), raccoons (Procyonidae), weasels, otters and badgers (Mustelidae), skunks (Mephitidae), the red panda (Ailuridae), and the pinnipeds — seals, sea lions, and the walrus.

Characteristics

Caniforms generally have long snouts and non-retractile claws, in contrast to the cat-like feliforms, and most are plantigrade or digitigrade quadrupeds. The earliest caniforms appeared in the Eocene of North America, about 40 million years ago.

Classification

Modern Caniformia comprises nine extant families. Molecular studies group the pinnipeds (Pinnipedia) with the musteloids and bears within the clade Arctoidea, while the dog family Canidae is the sister group to all other living caniforms (Canoidea/Arctoidea split). Caniforms are distinguished from the other carnivoran suborder, Feliformia, by features including the structure of the auditory bulla, which in caniforms is single-chambered or partially divided and formed mainly of the entotympanic bone.
