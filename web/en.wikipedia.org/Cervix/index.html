Cervix

Part ofUterus; female reproductive system

The cervix (from Latin cervix, "neck") is the lower part of the uterus in the human female reproductive system. The cervix is usually 2 to 3 cm long and roughly cylindrical in shape. The narrow central passage through the cervix, the endocervical canal, runs along its entire length, connecting the uterine cavity with the lumen of the vagina. The opening into the uterus is called the internal os, and the opening into the vagina, the external os.

Function

The cervix produces cervical mucus whose consistency changes through the menstrual cycle under hormonal influence, either impeding or facilitating the passage of sperm. During pregnancy the cervix remains firm and closed, sealed by a mucus plug; during labour it softens, effaces, and dilates to about 10 cm to allow the baby to pass through the birth canal.

Clinical significance

The transformation zone of the cervix, where columnar epithelium is replaced by squamous epithelium, is the site of most cervical cancers, and is sampled during Pap smear screening. Persistent infection with high-risk human papillomavirus types is the principal cause of cervical cancer. Inflammation of the cervix is called cervicitis, commonly caused by sexually transmitted infections such as chlamydia and gonorrhoea.
