Liturgy

Liturgy is the customary public ritual of worship in a religious tradition, especially Christianity. The word derives from the Greek leitourgia, a public work or service. In Christian usage it most often denotes the Eucharistic service — in Eastern Christianity the Divine Liturgy — and more broadly the whole ordered pattern of prayer, readings, hymns and sacraments.

Christian liturgies

Historic rites include the Roman Rite of the Catholic Church, codified after the Council of Trent and revised after the Second Vatican Council; the Byzantine Rite of the Eastern Orthodox and Eastern Catholic churches, using the Liturgies of St John Chrysostom and St Basil; and Alexandrine, Antiochene, East Syriac and Armenian traditions. Protestant traditions vary from the set forms of the Anglican Book of Common Prayer and Lutheran orders to largely free worship.

Elements

A eucharistic liturgy typically comprises an entrance, readings from Scripture, a sermon, intercessions, the presentation of bread and wine, a great thanksgiving prayer including the institution narrative, communion and dismissal. The Liturgy of the Hours orders prayer through the day.
