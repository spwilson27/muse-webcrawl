Crown Heights riot

The Crown Heights riot was a three-day outbreak of violence in August 1991 in the Crown Heights neighbourhood of Brooklyn, New York City, between African American and Caribbean American residents on one side and the neighbourhood's large Lubavitch Hasidic Jewish community on the other, with clashes also involving the New York City Police Department.

Trigger and events

On 19 August 1991, a station wagon in the motorcade of the Lubavitcher Rebbe, Menachem Mendel Schneerson, ran a red light, struck another vehicle and mounted the pavement, killing seven-year-old Gavin Cato, a Guyanese immigrant boy, and seriously injuring his cousin. Hours later, a group of young Black men fatally stabbed Yankel Rosenbaum, a 29-year-old Australian Jewish doctoral student, in an apparent act of retaliation. Three days of rioting followed, with crowds throwing stones and bottles, overturning cars and attacking police; hundreds were injured and property was looted and burned.

Aftermath

The handling of the disorder by Mayor David Dinkins and the police became a major political issue and contributed to Dinkins's defeat by Rudolph Giuliani in 1993. A state report criticised the city's response. Lemrick Nelson Jr. was acquitted of Rosenbaum's murder in state court but later convicted in federal court of violating Rosenbaum's civil rights. The events became a lasting reference point in debates over race, policing and intercommunal relations in New York.
