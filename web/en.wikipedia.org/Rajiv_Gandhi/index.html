Rajiv Gandhi (20 August 1944 – 21 May 1991) was an Indian politician who served as the sixth prime minister of India from 1984 to 1989. He took office after the assassination of his mother, then prime minister Indira Gandhi, to become, at age 40, the youngest Indian prime minister. He was a scion of the politically powerful Nehru–Gandhi family, which had been associated with the Indian National Congress party.

Early life and career

Rajiv Gandhi was born in Bombay to Indira Gandhi, daughter of India's first prime minister Jawaharlal Nehru, and Feroze Gandhi. He was educated at the Doon School and Imperial College London and Trinity College, Cambridge, though he did not complete a degree. Before entering politics he worked as a professional pilot for Indian Airlines.

Entry into politics

After the death of his younger brother Sanjay Gandhi in a plane crash in 1980, Rajiv reluctantly entered politics, winning election to the Lok Sabha from Amethi, Uttar Pradesh, in 1981. He became a general secretary of the Congress party and organized the 1982 Asian Games in New Delhi.

Prime Ministership

Within hours of Indira Gandhi's assassination on 31 October 1984, Rajiv was sworn in as prime minister. He led Congress to its largest-ever Lok Sabha victory in the December 1984 general election, winning 411 of 542 seats. His tenure saw the beginnings of economic liberalization, support for science and technology, the expansion of telecommunications and computers in India, and peace accords in Punjab and Assam. His government was also damaged by the Bofors scandal, allegations of corruption in a howitzer procurement deal, which contributed to his defeat in the 1989 election.

Assassination

While campaigning in Sriperumbudur, Tamil Nadu, on 21 May 1991, Rajiv Gandhi was assassinated by a suicide bomber of the Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam (LTTE), in retaliation for India's intervention in the Sri Lankan civil war through the Indian Peace Keeping Force.

Legacy

Rajiv Gandhi was posthumously awarded the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian honour. Numerous institutions, schemes and infrastructure projects in India bear his name, and his birth anniversary is observed as Sadbhavana Diwas (Harmony Day).
