Halifax, Nova Scotia

Halifax is the capital and most populous municipality of the Canadian province of Nova Scotia, and the most populous municipality in Atlantic Canada. As of the 2021 census, the municipal population was 439,819, with 348,634 in the urban area.

History

Halifax was established by the British in 1749 as a fortified naval base, named after George Montagu-Dunk, 2nd Earl of Halifax. Built on the world's second-largest natural harbour, it became a principal Royal Navy station and played a central role in British operations in North America during the Seven Years' War, the American Revolutionary War, the Napoleonic Wars, and both World Wars. The city was devastated by the Halifax Explosion of December 6, 1917, the largest artificial non-nuclear explosion at the time, which killed about 2,000 people.

The current municipality, the Halifax Regional Municipality (HRM), was formed in 1996 by amalgamating the former cities of Halifax and Dartmouth, the town of Bedford, and Halifax County.

Economy and culture

Halifax is a major port, home to the Atlantic fleet of the Royal Canadian Navy at CFB Halifax, and a centre for shipbuilding, defence, finance, and post-secondary education, with universities including Dalhousie University and Saint Mary's University. Landmarks include the Halifax Citadel National Historic Site, the Maritime Museum of the Atlantic, and Pier 21, Canada's national museum of immigration.
