Wenceslaus Square

Wenceslaus Square (Czech: Václavské náměstí) is one of the main city squares and the centre of the business and cultural communities in the New Town of Prague, Czech Republic. More a boulevard than a square, it is 750 metres long and has been the scene of many of the most important events in modern Czech history.

History

Laid out in 1348 as the Horse Market (Koňský trh) of Charles IV's New Town, the square was renamed in the 19th century for Saint Wenceslas, Duke of Bohemia. The National Museum (opened 1890) dominates its upper end, in front of which Josef Václav Myslbek's equestrian statue of Saint Wenceslas was completed in 1912.

Historic events

The square witnessed the proclamation of Czechoslovak independence in 1918, Nazi demonstrations in 1939, the student Jan Palach's self-immolation nearby in January 1969 in protest at the Warsaw Pact invasion, and the mass demonstrations of the Velvet Revolution in November 1989 that brought down communist rule. It remains Prague's principal site for celebrations and protests, as well as a commercial strip of shops, hotels and nightclubs.
