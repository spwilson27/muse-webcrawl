Direct marketing

Direct marketing is a form of advertising in which organisations communicate directly to customers through a variety of media, including mail, email, text messaging, telemarketing, and targeted online advertising, and elicit a measurable response, typically an order, an enquiry, or a visit to a store or website.

Characteristics

The most distinctive feature of direct marketing is that it seeks a direct response from individual customers rather than relying on intermediate retailers, and that its results can be tracked and measured. Marketers build and maintain databases of customer information to segment audiences and personalise messages.

Channels

Common channels include direct mail catalogues, email marketing, mobile messaging, telemarketing, direct-response television, and digital advertising. Direct mail dates to the nineteenth-century mail-order catalogues of firms such as Montgomery Ward and Sears.

Regulation and criticism

Direct marketing is subject to privacy and consumer-protection laws in many jurisdictions, including data-protection regimes and "do not call" registries. It is frequently criticised for contributing to junk mail and spam.
