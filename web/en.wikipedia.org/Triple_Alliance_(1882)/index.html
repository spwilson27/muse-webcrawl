Triple Alliance (1882)

Signed20 May 1882, Vienna
MembersGerman Empire, Austria-Hungary, Kingdom of Italy
Ended1915 (Italian defection)

The Triple Alliance was a secret defensive military alliance between the German Empire, Austria-Hungary and the Kingdom of Italy, signed on 20 May 1882 in Vienna. It grew out of the Dual Alliance of 1879 between Germany and Austria-Hungary, part of Chancellor Otto von Bismarck's system for isolating France.

Terms

The treaty promised that if Italy or Germany were attacked by France, the other would assist; if Austria-Hungary were attacked by Russia, Italy would remain neutral (a clause Italy valued, as its chief rivalries were with France over Tunisia and with Austria over Italian-speaking territories). The alliance was renewed periodically, in 1887, 1891, 1902, 1907 and 1912.

Weaknesses and collapse

The alliance was always strained by the rivalry between Italy and Austria-Hungary over Italia irredenta — Trentino and Trieste. Italy secretly concluded a neutrality agreement with France in 1902 and refused to join its allies in 1914, arguing the alliance was defensive and Austria-Hungary the aggressor against Serbia. In May 1915 Italy entered the First World War on the side of the Allies under the Treaty of London, leaving Germany and Austria-Hungary as the core of the Central Powers.
