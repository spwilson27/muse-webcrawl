Colony-forming unit

A colony-forming unit (CFU) is a unit of measurement used in microbiology to estimate the number of viable bacteria or fungal cells in a sample that are capable of multiplying and forming colonies under controlled conditions. Results are typically expressed as CFU per millilitre (CFU/mL) for liquids or CFU per gram for solids.

Method

A sample is serially diluted and a known volume is spread or poured onto an agar plate. After incubation, each visible colony is assumed to have grown from a single viable cell or clump of cells — one colony-forming unit. Counting plates with a statistically suitable number of colonies (often 30–300) and multiplying by the dilution factor gives the concentration in the original sample. Because a colony may originate from a chain or cluster of cells rather than a single cell, CFU counts can underestimate the true number of individual cells, which is why the unit is used instead of "cells".

Applications

CFU counting is a standard method in food safety, water quality testing, pharmaceutical quality control and clinical microbiology, including regulatory limits for bacteria in drinking water and food products. The term is also used in stem cell biology for progenitor cell assays (e.g. CFU-GM), where colonies grown in culture indicate the presence of a single multipotent precursor cell.
