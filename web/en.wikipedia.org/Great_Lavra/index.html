The Monastery of Great Lavra (Greek: Μονή Μεγίστης Λαύρας, "Monastery of the Greatest Lavra") is the oldest and largest monastery on Mount Athos and ranks first among the peninsula's twenty ruling monasteries. It was founded in 963 by Saint Athanasius the Athonite with the support of the Byzantine emperor Nikephoros II Phokas, and its foundation marks the beginning of organised cenobitic monasticism on the Holy Mountain.

History

Athanasius, initially a hermit on Athos, built the Lavra near the mountain's south-eastern tip, organising a community that combined communal life with the older eremitic traditions of lavra cells. Imperial chrysobulls endowed it with lands and revenues across the empire, and by the 11th century it housed hundreds of monks. The monastery survived the Latin occupation after 1204, Catalan raids, fires and Ottoman rule, and in the 19th and early 20th centuries hosted large numbers of Russian monks. Fires and earthquakes over the centuries repeatedly required rebuilding of parts of the complex.

Architecture and treasures

The fortified complex centres on the katholikon (main church), dedicated to the Annunciation, with frescoes in the refectory and chapels attributed to the school of Theophanes the Cretan (16th century). The monastery's treasury preserves Byzantine imperial documents, illuminated manuscripts — its library holds some 2,000 manuscripts and 20,000 printed books — liturgical vessels and the staff and cross associated with its founder. Relics of Saint Athanasius are kept in the church of the Protaton tradition and in the Lavra itself. Nearby stand its dependent sketes, including the Romanian Skete of Prodromos and the hermitages of the Athonite desert.

The Lavra today

The Great Lavra remains the primus of Athonite monasteries, seat of the first of the four abbots who historically governed the Holy Mountain, and a working monastery of the Ecumenical Patriarchate. Pilgrims (men only, by the ancient avaton rule) arrive by boat at its harbour below the monastery walls.
