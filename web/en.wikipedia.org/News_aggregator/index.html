News aggregator

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In computing, a news aggregator, also termed a feed aggregator, feed reader, news reader, or simply an aggregator, is client software or a web application that aggregates digital content such as online newspapers, blogs, podcasts, and video blogs (vlogs) in one location for easy viewing. Aggregators usually retrieve syndicated web content provided through web feeds, most commonly in formats such as RSS and Atom, and display new content automatically as it appears.

Types

Planet-style aggregators collect posts from a community of blogs onto a single page. Hosted aggregator websites — such as Google News, Apple News, and Flipboard — assemble headlines from thousands of publishers, organising them by topic and, increasingly, personalising them algorithmically for each user. Social news sites such as Reddit and Hacker News rely on user submissions and voting rather than automated crawling. Standalone feed reader applications let users subscribe to feeds directly and became especially popular in the 2000s; the shutdown of Google Reader in 2013 is widely regarded as a turning point in the decline of RSS as a mainstream technology.

Issues

Aggregators have been the subject of disputes with publishers, who argue that aggregation diverts traffic and advertising revenue from original journalism and repackages it without payment. These conflicts have led to legal cases, licensing regimes for news content in some jurisdictions, and "link tax" laws in the European Union, Australia, and Canada. Aggregators are also criticised for reinforcing filter bubbles through personalised ranking, and for spreading misinformation when low-quality sources are collected alongside established outlets.
