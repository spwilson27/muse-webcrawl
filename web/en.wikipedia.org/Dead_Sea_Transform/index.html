Dead Sea Transform

The Dead Sea Transform (DST), also known as the Dead Sea Rift, is a series of faults that run for about 1,000 kilometres from the Red Sea Rift at the Gulf of Aqaba northward to the Taurus Mountains collision zone in Turkey. It forms the boundary between the Arabian Plate to the east and the Sinai–Palestinian subplate of the African Plate to the west.

Geology

The transform is a left-lateral strike-slip system that began forming in the Miocene as Arabia separated from Africa. Total left-lateral displacement is about 105 km. Pull-apart basins along the fault system created the deep depressions occupied by the Dead Sea, the Sea of Galilee, and the Hula Valley.

Segments

Major segments include the Arava (Wadi Araba) fault, the Jordan Valley fault, the Yammoûneh fault in Lebanon, and the Ghab fault in Syria. The system steps left at Lebanon, where transpression raises the Lebanon and Anti-Lebanon mountain ranges.

Seismicity

The transform has produced destructive historical earthquakes, including events in 1033, 1202, 1546, 1759, and the 1927 Jericho earthquake. Geodesy indicates present-day slip of roughly 4–5 mm per year, and paleoseismic studies suggest significant seismic hazard for the densely populated Levant.
