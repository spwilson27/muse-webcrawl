Goldstone boson

In particle and condensed matter physics, Goldstone bosons (also called Nambu–Goldstone bosons) are bosons that appear necessarily in models exhibiting spontaneous breakdown of continuous symmetries. They are predicted by Goldstone's theorem, formulated by Jeffrey Goldstone and developed with Abdus Salam and Steven Weinberg, building on work by Yoichiro Nambu.

Goldstone's theorem

The theorem states that for every spontaneously broken continuous symmetry of a theory, there exists a massless spin-0 particle. Intuitively, when a system chooses a ground state that does not share the full symmetry of its laws — pictured by a ball settling anywhere in the circular trough of a "Mexican hat" potential — oscillations along the flat direction of the trough cost no energy, corresponding to the massless Goldstone mode.

Examples

In quantum chromodynamics, the approximate chiral symmetry of the light quarks is spontaneously broken, and the pions act as pseudo-Goldstone bosons: they are light, but not massless, because the symmetry is also broken explicitly by the quark masses. In condensed matter, phonons are Goldstone bosons of broken translational symmetry, and magnons arise from broken spin-rotation symmetry.

Gauge theories and the Higgs mechanism

When the spontaneously broken symmetry is a local gauge symmetry, the would-be Goldstone bosons are instead "eaten" by the gauge fields, which acquire mass — the Higgs mechanism. This is how the W and Z bosons gain mass in the Standard Model while the photon remains massless.
