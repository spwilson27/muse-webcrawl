The Krishna River (known in antiquity as the Kṛṣṇaveṇā) is one of the major rivers of peninsular India and its fourth-longest river overall. It rises in the Western Ghats near Mahabaleshwar in Maharashtra, flows eastward across the Deccan Plateau through Karnataka, Telangana and Andhra Pradesh, and empties into the Bay of Bengal. (The river shares its name with the Hindu deity Krishna, but the two articles concern different subjects.)

Course

From its source at about 1,300 m elevation, the Krishna flows through the cities of Sangli and Vijayapura (Bijapur) region, receives the Bhima from the north and the Tungabhadra from the southwest, passes Hyderabad's twin reservoirs region to the north, and reaches the plains at Vijayawada, where the Prakasam Barrage bridges it. Below Vijayawada it spreads into a fertile delta before reaching the sea at Hamsaladeevi.

Tributaries

Major tributaries include the Koyna, Bhima, Tungabhadra, Malaprabha, Ghataprabha, Musi, Munneru and Paleru. The Tungabhadra, itself formed by the Tunga and Bhadra rivers, is the largest by volume.

Dams and irrigation

The basin is heavily developed for irrigation and hydroelectricity. Major projects include the Almatti Dam, the Nagarjuna Sagar Dam — one of the world's largest masonry dams, completed in 1967 — the Srisailam Dam, the Jurala project and the Prakasam Barrage. The sharing of Krishna waters among Maharashtra, Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh (later Telangana) has been adjudicated by two Krishna Water Disputes Tribunals (the Bachawat and Brijesh Kumar tribunals), and allocations remain a live political issue.

Significance

The river is sacred in Hinduism: the Pushkaram festival, held once every twelve years, draws millions of pilgrims to its banks. Its delta is one of India's most productive rice-growing regions, and its banks host historic temple towns such as Srisailam, Amaravati and Alampur.
