The Castra Praetoria was the fortified camp of the Praetorian Guard in ancient Rome. It was established in AD 23 by Lucius Aelius Sejanus, the powerful prefect of the Guard under the emperor Tiberius, who persuaded the emperor to concentrate the previously scattered Praetorian cohorts into a single permanent camp on the Viminal Hill, just outside the Servian Wall in the north-east of the city.

Description

The camp formed a rectangle roughly 440 by 380 metres, enclosed by walls of concrete faced with brick, with four gates. It housed the emperor's bodyguard close to the centre of power and greatly increased the Guard's political influence. Later emperors strengthened the fortifications, and parts of the camp's northern and eastern walls were incorporated into the Aurelian Walls built in the third century AD.

History

From the Castra Praetoria the Guard repeatedly intervened in imperial politics: it was here that the Praetorians proclaimed Claudius emperor in AD 41, and in AD 193 the Guard notoriously auctioned the empire to Didius Julianus. After the Battle of the Milvian Bridge in AD 312, Constantine the Great disbanded the Praetorian Guard and largely demolished the camp, although substantial sections of its walls remain standing in Rome today. The modern Castro Pretorio district and metro station take their names from the camp.
