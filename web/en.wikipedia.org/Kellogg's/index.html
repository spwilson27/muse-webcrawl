Kellogg's

Kellogg's is the brand name of the breakfast cereals and convenience foods long produced by the Kellogg Company, founded by Will Keith Kellogg in Battle Creek, Michigan, in 1906. Its products — including Corn Flakes, Rice Krispies, Frosted Flakes (Frosties), Special K and Pop-Tarts — made it one of the world's best-known food brands. In 2023 the Kellogg Company split into two businesses: the cereal company WK Kellogg Co and the snacks-led company Kellanova, with the Kellogg's brand used by both.

Origins

The brand grew out of the Battle Creek Sanitarium, a health resort run by Dr John Harvey Kellogg, where he and his brother Will Keith Kellogg developed flaked grain foods in the 1890s — according to company legend, after accidentally leaving cooked wheat to stand and rolling it into flakes. W. K. Kellogg founded his own company in 1906 to market toasted corn flakes, signing each box with his signature to distinguish it from dozens of imitators in "Cereal City".

Growth

Kellogg's expanded internationally early, opening plants in Canada and Britain, and built its brands with memorable advertising characters: Tony the Tiger for Frosted Flakes (1952), Snap, Crackle and Pop for Rice Krispies, and Toucan Sam for Froot Loops. Through the 20th century it diversified into toaster pastries (Pop-Tarts, 1964), crackers and snacks, acquiring brands such as Keebler (2001) and Pringles (2012).

Corporate split

In 2023 Kellogg Company separated its North American cereal business as WK Kellogg Co, while the remaining global snacking and international cereal business became Kellanova, both trading on the heritage of the Kellogg's name. In 2024 Mars, Incorporated agreed to acquire Kellanova in one of the largest food-industry deals of the decade.
