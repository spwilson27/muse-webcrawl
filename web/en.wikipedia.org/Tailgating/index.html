Tailgating

Tailgating is the practice of driving too closely behind another vehicle, leaving insufficient distance to stop safely if the vehicle ahead slows or halts. It is a common cause of rear-end collisions and is penalised in many jurisdictions.

Safe following distances

Guidelines such as the "two-second rule" (or three seconds in poor weather) advise drivers to keep a time gap to the vehicle ahead. Tailgating reduces reaction time and is associated with aggressive driving and road rage.

Other meanings

The term also refers to social gatherings held around the open tailgate of a vehicle at sporting events ("tailgate parties"), and in security, to an unauthorised person following an authorised one through a secure door.

Enforcement

Many traffic codes define offences such as "following too closely". Some regions use automated following-distance enforcement and public campaigns to discourage the behaviour.
