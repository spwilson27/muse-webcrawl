Suriname River

The Suriname River (Dutch: Surinamerivier) is a river of about 480 kilometres (300 miles) in length in the South American country of Suriname, after which the country is named. It rises in the Guiana Highlands, at the confluence of the Gran Rio and Pikin Rio in the Sipaliwini District, and flows northward to empty into the Atlantic Ocean near the capital, Paramaribo.

Course

From its headwaters the river flows north through dense rainforest, passing the Brokopondo Reservoir — one of the largest man-made lakes in the world, created in 1964 by the Afobaka Dam, which supplies hydroelectric power chiefly for the aluminium industry. Below the dam the river passes the towns of Brokopondo and Berg en Dal, then the former plantation settlements along its lower, tidal reaches. At Leonsberg, near the mouth, its principal tributary the Commewijne River joins from the east. The capital Paramaribo lies on the left bank of the lower river, and the port of Nieuw Amsterdam lies opposite on the right bank; the two are linked by the Jules Wijdenbosch Bridge, opened in 2000.

History and use

The lower river was the axis of the Dutch plantation colony of Surinam, with sugar and coffee estates lining its banks, worked first by enslaved Africans and later by indentured labourers from India and Java. Today the river is used for shipping bauxite and alumina, for fishing, and for ferry and recreational traffic. Its upper course, including the rapids near Atjoni, serves Maroon communities of the interior.
