Capture the flag (cybersecurity)

Capture the flag (CTF) is a type of information security competition in which participants solve challenges to find hidden strings of text, called "flags", which are then submitted to a scoring server to earn points. CTF competitions are held both online and in person, at conferences such as DEF CON, and are widely used for training, recruitment, and skill development in computer security.

Formats

The two most common formats are Jeopardy-style and attack-defence. In Jeopardy-style events, teams solve independent challenges in categories such as web exploitation, cryptography, reverse engineering, binary exploitation (pwn), forensics, and open-source intelligence. Each challenge contains a flag, typically in a format such as flag{...}. In attack-defence events, each team runs identical vulnerable services on a network and must patch its own services while exploiting opponents' services to capture their flags.

Challenge categories

Common categories include:

  • Web — exploiting vulnerabilities in web applications, such as SQL injection and cross-site scripting.
  • Cryptography — breaking or misusing cryptographic constructions.
  • Reverse engineering — analysing binaries or firmware to recover logic or keys.
  • Pwn — developing exploits for memory-corruption vulnerabilities.
  • Forensics — recovering evidence from disk images, memory dumps, and network captures.
  • OSINT — finding information from publicly available sources.

History

The first widely recognised CTF was organised at DEF CON in 1996. The DEF CON CTF finals remain among the most prestigious events, with qualifying rounds run by teams such as Legitimate Business Syndicate and later organisers. University competitions such as CSAW, and platforms like picoCTF (aimed at school students), Hack The Box, and TryHackMe, have broadened participation considerably.
