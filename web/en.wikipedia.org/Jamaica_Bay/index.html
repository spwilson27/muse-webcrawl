Jamaica Bay is a shallow estuary of about 13,000 acres on the southwestern shore of Long Island, at the southeastern edge of New York City, between the boroughs of Brooklyn and Queens and Nassau County. The bay is connected to the Atlantic Ocean through Rockaway Inlet and is protected from the open sea by the Rockaway Peninsula. Despite its urban setting — John F. Kennedy International Airport was built on its northern shore — Jamaica Bay is one of the most significant bird sanctuaries and estuarine habitats in the northeastern United States.

Ecology

The bay contains salt marshes, tidal flats, islands, and open water supporting more than 325 species of birds, including migratory waterfowl on the Atlantic Flyway, nesting osprey, herons and egrets, and one of the largest horseshoe crab spawning areas on the coast; diamondback terrapins nest on its shores. The Jamaica Bay Wildlife Refuge, administered by the National Park Service as part of Gateway National Recreation Area since 1972, covers some 9,000 acres and includes two freshwater ponds created in the 1950s.

History and threats

The bay's name derives from the Lenape word Yameco ("beaver"), via the Dutch and English town of Jamaica in Queens. In the 20th century the bay suffered extensive dredging, landfilling, pollution from sewage and industry, and the construction of JFK Airport, which filled much of the northwestern marsh. Marsh islands have been disappearing at an accelerating rate — attributed to sea-level rise, sediment starvation, and past dredging — prompting large-scale marsh restoration projects in which dredged sand is sprayed onto subsiding islands and replanted with cordgrass. Hurricane Sandy in 2012 inundated surrounding neighbourhoods such as Broad Channel and the Rockaways, intensifying debate about the bay's role in coastal resilience.
