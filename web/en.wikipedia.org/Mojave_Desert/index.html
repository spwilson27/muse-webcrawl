The Mojave Desert (also Mohave Desert) is a rain-shadow desert and the driest desert in North America, occupying some 48,000 square miles (124,000 km²) across southeastern California, southern Nevada, and smaller parts of southwestern Utah and northwestern Arizona. It is bounded by the Tehachapi Mountains and the San Gabriel and San Bernardino ranges to the west, the Great Basin Desert to the north, and the Sonoran Desert and Colorado Plateau to the south and east.

Climate and landscape

Elevations mostly lie between 2,000 and 5,000 feet (600–1,500 m), giving the Mojave cooler winters than the lower Sonoran Desert. The desert contains both the lowest point in North America — Badwater Basin in Death Valley, 282 feet (86 m) below sea level — and vast sand dunes (the Kelso Dunes), cinder cones, and dry lake beds. Temperatures in Death Valley have reached 134 °F (56.7 °C), the highest reliably recorded air temperature on Earth.

Flora, fauna, and land use

The Joshua tree is the desert's iconic indicator species, alongside creosote bush, Mojave yucca, desert tortoise (Gopherus agassizii), and bighorn sheep. Protected areas include Death Valley National Park, Joshua Tree National Park, and the Mojave National Preserve. Las Vegas is the largest city within the desert; the region also hosts major solar installations such as Ivanpah, Edwards Air Force Base, and the bright lights of Interstate 15 between Barstow and Las Vegas.
