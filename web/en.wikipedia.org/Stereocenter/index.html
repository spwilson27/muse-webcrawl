Stereocenter

Field: Stereochemistry
Types: Tetrahedral atoms, double bonds, axes
Related: Chirality, enantiomers, E–Z isomerism

In stereochemistry, a stereocenter of a molecule is an atom or grouping bearing groups such that the interchange of any two groups at that centre leads to a stereoisomer. The term was introduced in 1984 by Kurt Mislow and Jay Siegel. Stereocenters are the structural features responsible for stereoisomerism.

Types

The most common stereocenters are tetrahedral carbon atoms bearing four different substituents; such a carbon is a chirality centre, and the two arrangements are non-superimposable mirror images (enantiomers), described by the Cahn–Ingold–Prelog R/S notation. Carbon–carbon double bonds are stereogenic units of a different kind: the two arrangements of substituents give E and Z isomers. Stereocenters can also occur at atoms such as nitrogen, phosphorus and sulfur (for example in sulfoxides), and in axially chiral systems such as allenes and atropisomeric biaryls.

Counting stereoisomers

A molecule with n stereogenic centres can have at most 2n stereoisomers, though symmetry may reduce this number, as in meso compounds, which contain stereocenters yet are achiral overall.
