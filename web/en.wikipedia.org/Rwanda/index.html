Rwanda, officially the Republic of Rwanda, is a landlocked country in the Great Rift Valley of East Africa, bordered by Uganda, Tanzania, Burundi and the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Known as the "land of a thousand hills", it is dominated by mountains in the west and savanna in the east, with Lake Kivu along its western border.

History

The Kingdom of Rwanda was ruled for centuries by Tutsi kings (mwami) over a population of Hutu, Tutsi and Twa peoples sharing the Kinyarwanda language. Germany and then Belgium administered Rwanda as a colony, ruling through the monarchy and hardening ethnic categories. Independence came in 1962 under Hutu-led governments. In 1994, during the Rwandan Civil War, extremist Hutu militias and officials carried out the Rwandan genocide, murdering an estimated 500,000–800,000 Tutsi and moderate Hutu in about one hundred days, until the Tutsi-led Rwandan Patriotic Front under Paul Kagame took power.

Modern Rwanda

Since 2000, President Kagame's government has pursued rapid economic development, infrastructure building and national unity policies, achieving strong growth and notable gains in health and education, while drawing criticism for restrictions on political opposition and press freedom. Rwanda is a member of the African Union, the East African Community and, since 2009, the Commonwealth of Nations. Its Volcanoes National Park is famed for mountain gorilla tourism.
