Tree of life

The tree of life is a widespread motif and archetype in the mythologies, religions and philosophies of the world. It appears as a sacred tree connecting heaven, earth and the underworld, as the source of life and immortality, and — in biology — as the metaphor for the common descent of all living things. This article surveys the motif in mythology and religion; for the scientific concept see Tree of life (biology).

Religious and mythological traditions

In the Hebrew Bible, the tree of life stands in the Garden of Eden beside the tree of the knowledge of good and evil; its fruit confers immortality, and after the Fall, cherubim guard the way to it. The image recurs in Proverbs and in the Book of Revelation, where it grows beside the river of the water of life. In Norse mythology, Yggdrasil is the immense world tree linking the nine worlds, beneath whose roots lie wells of wisdom and fate. Ancient Egyptian religion associated the sycamore fig tree with goddesses who nourished the dead, while in Mesopotamia the "sacred tree" flanked by genii appears throughout Assyrian relief sculpture. In the Qur'an, a tree of immortality (shajarat al-khuld) appears in Paradise, and in Buddhism the Bodhi tree at Bodh Gaya, under which Siddhartha Gautama attained enlightenment, is venerated as a tree of life. Kabbalah's Etz Chaim is a diagram of ten sefirot arranged as a tree mapping the emanation of the divine.

Symbolism and interpretation

Comparative mythologists and psychologists such as Mircea Eliade and Carl Jung treated the world tree as an axis mundi, a cosmic centre mediating between realms, and as a symbol of growth, death and regeneration tied to the cycles of the seasons. The motif is often paired with a spring or river of life and with guardian serpents or birds. It persists in modern culture in forms ranging from the Tree of Life in Disney's Animal Kingdom to the "Tree of Life" mosaic and jewellery motifs, and to the evolutionary tree used as a popular science emblem.

Specific named trees

Several real trees bear the name: the Tree of Life in Bahrain is a lone Prosopis cineraria in the desert, famed for surviving without an obvious water source; various monumental oaks, banyans and baobabs are similarly venerated locally. In Baha'i writings the tree of life symbolises the Manifestation of God, and in Mormon scripture it is the centrepiece of Lehi's dream, symbolising the love of God.
