Volksdeutsche

Volksdeutsche (singular Volksdeutscher) is a German term meaning "ethnic Germans", used historically for people of German ancestry living outside the borders of the German Reich, particularly in Central and Eastern Europe. It was contrasted with Reichsdeutsche, German citizens within the Reich.

Historical usage

The term gained prominence in the early 20th century as German nationalist movements focused on ethnic German communities abroad, including Baltic Germans, Transylvanian Saxons, Danube Swabians and Germans in Poland and Czechoslovakia. The Nazi regime made the concept central to its expansionist ideology, using Volksdeutsche organisations and the Deutsche Volksliste classification in occupied territories.

Aftermath

Following the Second World War, millions of ethnic Germans were expelled or fled from Eastern Europe under the Potsdam Agreement arrangements, resettling in West and East Germany and Austria. In modern usage, Volksdeutsche is largely a historical term; ethnic Germans abroad are more often described as Deutschstämmige or members of German minorities.
