Thunderbolt

A thunderbolt is a symbolic representation of lightning when accompanied by a loud thunder clap. In Indo-European mythology, the thunderbolt was identified with a weapon of the sky god: in Greek mythology, the thunderbolt was a weapon given to Zeus by the Cyclopes; in Hinduism, the vajra is the thunderbolt weapon of Indra; and in Norse mythology, Thor's hammer Mjölnir produces thunder and lightning.

In mythology and religion

Thunderbolts appear as divine weapons across many traditions. Zeus, king of the Greek gods, wielded thunderbolts forged by the Cyclopes and used them against the Titans and the monster Typhon. In Roman mythology the equivalent weapon belonged to Jupiter. The Etruscans and Romans studied lightning strikes as omens, and sites struck by lightning could be consecrated as sacred enclosures.

In heraldry and symbolism

In heraldry, a thunderbolt is depicted as a zigzag bolt, often with wings or flames, and appears in the arms of families and military units. The thunderbolt is widely used as a symbol of speed, power, and electricity, and features in the insignia of signal corps, electrical trades, and numerous commercial logos.

Scientific usage

Historically the word was also applied to fulgurites and to prehistoric stone tools, which were once believed to be objects cast down from the sky during storms, sometimes called “thunderstones”. In modern usage, a thunderbolt is distinct from a lightning bolt in emphasizing the combined flash and crash of a near strike.
