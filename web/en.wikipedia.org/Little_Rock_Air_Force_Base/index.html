Little Rock Air Force Base (IATA: LRF, ICAO: KLRF) is a United States Air Force base located near Jacksonville, Arkansas, northeast of Little Rock. It is the principal training center for the Lockheed C-130 Hercules, the Department of Defense's largest fleet of the type, and styles itself the "Home of the Hercules."

History

Construction began in 1953 on land donated by local communities, and the base opened in 1955 under Strategic Air Command, hosting B-47 Stratojet bombers and later Titan II intercontinental ballistic missile sites operated by the 308th Strategic Missile Wing. One of those sites, near Damascus, Arkansas, was the scene of a major accident in September 1980 when a missile exploded in its silo.

Present mission

After the Cold War the base became the center of C-130 operations. The host 19th Airlift Wing (Air Mobility Command) flies the C-130J Super Hercules on tactical airlift missions. The 314th Airlift Wing (Air Education and Training Command) conducts initial and advanced C-130 training for aircrews from the Air Force, other U.S. services, and more than thirty allied nations; the 189th Airlift Wing of the Arkansas Air National Guard is also based there. Together these units make Little Rock AFB the largest C-130 operation in the world.
