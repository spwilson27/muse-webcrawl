Hatfield House, a leading example of Jacobean prodigy house architecture.

Hatfield House is a Grade I listed country house in Hatfield, Hertfordshire, England. Built between 1607 and 1611 by Robert Cecil, 1st Earl of Salisbury and chief minister to King James I, it is a prime example of Jacobean architecture and has remained the home of the Cecil family, the Marquesses of Salisbury, for over 400 years.

History

The estate was originally the site of the Royal Palace of Hatfield, where the future Queen Elizabeth I spent much of her childhood; it was beneath an oak tree in the park that she is said to have learned of her accession in 1558. Only part of the old palace survives, including the Great Hall. James I exchanged the estate with his minister Robert Cecil for Theobalds House, and Cecil demolished much of the palace to build the present house, designed under the direction of Robert Lyminge with contributions attributed to Simon Basil and possibly Inigo Jones.

Architecture and interiors

The house is built of red brick with stone dressings in an E-plan. Highlights include the Marble Hall, the Grand Staircase — one of the finest Jacobean carved wooden staircases — the Long Gallery with its gilded ceiling, and the King James Drawing Room. The house holds important paintings, including the famous "Rainbow Portrait" and "Ermine Portrait" of Elizabeth I, as well as historic furniture, tapestries, and armor.

Gardens and park

The gardens, originally laid out in the early 17th century by John Tradescant the Elder, include a knot garden, herb garden, and a famous maze; the park extends over some 1,500 acres (600 ha) with a vineyard, woodland, and the site of Elizabeth's Oak. Hatfield House and its grounds are open to the public seasonally and are a popular filming location.
