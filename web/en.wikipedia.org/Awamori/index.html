Awamori

Awamori is an alcoholic beverage native to Okinawa, Japan. It is a distilled rice spirit, typically about 30% alcohol (with some varieties up to 60%), made exclusively from long-grain indica rice and black koji mould (Aspergillus luchuensis), and produced by a single fermentation and single distillation.

History

Awamori descends from Thai rice spirits (lao khao) traded to the Ryukyu Kingdom in the 15th century, blended with techniques that may have arrived from China; it is Japan's oldest distilled spirit tradition. It is protected as a regional product of Okinawa. Aged awamori, called kusu, is matured in clay pots and develops a mellow, sometimes vanilla-like character; before the Second World War some stocks were centuries old, but most were lost in the Battle of Okinawa.

Serving

Awamori is commonly served on the rocks, diluted with water, or in cocktails, traditionally poured from a small vessel called a karakara. A fiery variant, habushu, is bottled with a pit viper (habu) steeped in it.
