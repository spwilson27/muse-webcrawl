Buddhist symbolism is the use of symbols (Sanskrit: pratīka) to represent certain aspects of the Buddha's Dharma (teaching). Early Buddhist symbols, which remain important today, include the Dharma wheel, the Indian lotus, the three jewels and the Bodhi tree. Ancient Buddhist art rarely showed the Buddha in human form; instead, symbols such as footprints, an empty throne, a riderless horse and the Bodhi tree stood in for his presence.

Ancient Buddhist texts such as the Divyāvadāna, the Mahāvastu and the Milindapañha refer to these early symbols. In Mahayana Buddhism, the distinctive "eight auspicious symbols" (aṣṭamaṅgala) were elaborated, and later Vajrayana Buddhism incorporated additional tantric symbolism.

Early symbols

The earliest Buddhist art, found at sites such as Bharhut and Sanchi, depicts scenes of the Buddha's life and his previous lives (Jataka tales) without portraying the Buddha himself. Instead, his presence is indicated by symbols: a wheel for his first sermon at Sarnath, footprints (buddhapada) for his steps upon the earth, an empty throne beneath a parasol, and the Bodhi tree for his enlightenment at Bodh Gaya.

The eight auspicious symbols

The aṣṭamaṅgala are a sacred suite of eight symbols: the conch shell (right), the endless knot, a pair of golden fish, the lotus flower, the parasol, the treasure vase, the dharma wheel and the victory banner. In Tibetan Buddhism these appear on thangkas, ritual objects and monastery walls, and are often drawn on the ground with coloured flour to welcome religious dignitaries.

The lotus

The lotus flower (padma, Nelumbo nucifera) symbolises purity and spiritual awakening: though rooted in muddy water, it rises unstained above the surface. Different colours carry different meanings — white for purity of mind, pink for the Buddha himself, blue for wisdom, purple for mysticism and gold for enlightenment.

Mudras

Symbolic hand gestures (mudras) convey meaning in Buddhist statuary: the earth-witness gesture (bhūmisparśa), meditation (dhyāna), teaching (dharmacakra), fearlessness (abhaya) and gift-giving (varada).
