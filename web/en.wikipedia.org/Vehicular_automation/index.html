Vehicular automation

Vehicular automation is the use of technology to assist or replace human control of a vehicle. It ranges from driver-assistance systems such as cruise control and lane-keeping to fully self-driving cars that require no human driver. SAE International defines six levels of driving automation, from Level 0 (no automation) to Level 5 (full automation in all conditions).

Technology

Automated vehicles perceive their surroundings with cameras, radar, lidar, ultrasonic sensors, and GPS, building a model of the road, and control steering, acceleration, and braking by computer. Commercial deployments include advanced driver-assistance systems (ADAS) sold by most carmakers, and limited driverless taxi services operating in geofenced areas.

Issues

Debate continues over safety validation, liability for crashes, effects on employment (trucking and taxi driving), cybersecurity, and regulation. Fatal crashes involving driver-assistance systems have prompted investigations and recalls, while proponents argue automation could reduce the vast majority of crashes caused by human error.
