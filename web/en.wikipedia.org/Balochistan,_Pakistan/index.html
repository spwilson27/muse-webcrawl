Balochistan, Pakistan

Balochistan
Capital: Quetta
Area: 347,190 km² (largest province)
Population: ~15 million
Languages: Balochi, Pashto, Brahui

Balochistan is a province of Pakistan, located in the southwestern part of the country. It is Pakistan's largest province by area — covering about 44% of the national territory — but its least populous and most sparsely settled. The capital is Quetta.

Geography

The province borders Iran to the west, Afghanistan to the north, and the Arabian Sea to the south, where the deep-water port of Gwadar is a key node of the China–Pakistan Economic Corridor. The terrain is largely arid mountains and desert plateaus, including the Sulaiman Range and the Chagai Hills.

People and history

The largest ethnic group is the Baloch, alongside Pashtuns in the north and Brahui speakers in the central region. Balochistan joined Pakistan in 1948 through the accession of the Khanate of Kalat and neighbouring states, a history that continues to fuel grievances and a long-running nationalist insurgency over autonomy and control of natural resources.

Economy

The province is rich in natural gas (the Sui gas field), coal, copper and gold — the Reko Diq deposit is among the world's largest undeveloped copper-gold resources. Agriculture, livestock and fisheries are major employers, and Quetta is known for its fruit orchards.
