Yaqui Wars

Period1533–1929 (intermittent)
LocationSonora, Mexico; Arizona, United States
PartiesYaqui people vs. Spain, Mexico, and the United States

The Yaqui Wars were a series of armed conflicts between the Yaqui people of the Yaqui River valley in Sonora and, successively, Spanish colonial forces, the Mexican state, and the United States. Notable for their duration, they were among the longest-lasting Indigenous resistances in the Americas.

Background

The Yaqui fiercely defended their fertile river valley and political autonomy. After Jesuit missions were established in the 1600s, relations alternated between alliance and revolt, including a major uprising in 1740.

Nineteenth and twentieth centuries

Under leaders such as Cajemé in the 1880s, the Yaqui resisted Mexican campaigns to seize their land. The Porfirian government responded with brutal deportations, sending thousands of Yaqui to work on henequen plantations in Yucatán. During the Mexican Revolution, Yaqui soldiers served in federal and revolutionary armies while others continued guerrilla resistance; the last major campaigns occurred in the 1920s, and Yaqui troops fought a final engagement with the US Army in Arizona in 1918.

Legacy

President Lázaro Cárdenas formally recognised Yaqui land rights in 1937–1940. The wars remain central to Yaqui identity in both Mexico and the Pascua Yaqui community in Arizona.
