National Outdoor Leadership School


Labelled image: a NOLS expedition in the Wind River Range

The National Outdoor Leadership School (NOLS) is a non-profit outdoor education school based in Lander, Wyoming, United States. Founded in 1965 by mountaineer Paul Petzoldt, NOLS teaches wilderness skills, leadership, and environmental ethics on extended expeditions, and is credited with helping to codify the Leave No Trace ethic of minimum-impact outdoor recreation.

Programs

NOLS runs courses ranging from about a week to full semesters in locations around the world, including the Rocky Mountains, Alaska, Patagonia, the Himalaya, East Africa, Scandinavia, Australia, New Zealand, and Baja California. Curriculum areas include backpacking, mountaineering, rock climbing, sea kayaking, sailing, whitewater paddling, backcountry skiing, and wilderness medicine; its Wilderness Medicine Institute offers widely recognized certifications such as Wilderness First Responder. NOLS has also trained NASA astronauts and military personnel in expedition behavior and leadership.

Philosophy

Petzoldt, a veteran of the US Army's 10th Mountain Division, founded NOLS on the principle that leadership in the outdoors could be taught experientially. Students take progressive responsibility for navigation, campcraft, group decisions, and risk management under instructor mentorship. With Outward Bound, NOLS is one of the principal institutions of American outdoor education, and its alumni number in the hundreds of thousands.
