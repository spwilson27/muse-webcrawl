Food science

Food science (or bromatology) is the study of the physical, biological, and chemical makeup of food, and the concepts underlying food processing and preservation. Food scientists and technologists study the harvesting, processing, distribution, storage, and evaluation of food, applying principles from chemistry, biology, microbiology, engineering, and nutrition. The discipline emerged in the early 20th century as food manufacturing industrialised; Nicolas Appert's canning process (1810) and Louis Pasteur's work on fermentation and spoilage are foundational milestones.

Food scientists analyse the safety and composition of food products.

Subdisciplines

Food chemistry examines the composition of foods and the chemical changes they undergo, such as the Maillard reaction. Food microbiology studies the microorganisms that inhabit, create, or contaminate food, including pathogens and the microbes used in fermentation. Food engineering applies engineering principles to industrial processing and packaging. Sensory analysis measures how food is perceived by consumers, and food safety concerns the prevention of foodborne illness through systems such as HACCP (Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points).

Applications

Food science underpins shelf-life extension, fortification, the development of meat and dairy analogues, frozen food systems, and quality control in manufacturing. Professional bodies include the Institute of Food Technologists (IFT), founded in 1939.
