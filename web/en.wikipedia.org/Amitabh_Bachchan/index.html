Amitabh Bachchan (born 11 October 1942) is an Indian actor who works in Hindi cinema. He has starred in over 200 films, widely considered one of the greatest and most influential actors in the history of Indian cinema. He first gained popularity in the early 1970s for films such as Anand, Zanjeer, Roti Kapada Aur Makaan, and achieved greater stardom in later years, dubbed India's "angry young man" for his portrayal of angry, defiant antiheroes in action films like Deewaar and Sholay (both 1975).

Career

Bachchan made his debut as a voice narrator in Bhuvan Shome (1969) and his acting debut in Saat Hindustani the same year. After a string of commercial failures, Zanjeer (1973) established him as a star. Sholay (1975) became one of the highest-grossing Indian films of all time. In 1982 he was seriously injured in an on-set accident during Coolie, an event that drew nationwide concern.

He briefly entered politics in the 1980s, serving as a member of parliament, and his career faltered in the 1990s amid the failure of his production company ABCL. A remarkable comeback began in 2000 with Mohabbatein and his role as host of the television game show Kaun Banega Crorepati, the Indian version of Who Wants to Be a Millionaire?, which he has hosted for most of its seasons.

Honors

Bachchan has won numerous accolades, including four National Film Awards for Best Actor, and the Government of India honored him with the Padma Shri (1984), Padma Bhushan (2001), Padma Vibhushan (2015), and the Dadasaheb Phalke Award (2018). France awarded him the Legion of Honour in 2007.
