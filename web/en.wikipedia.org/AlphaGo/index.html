AlphaGo

AlphaGo is a computer program that plays the board game Go. It was developed by DeepMind Technologies, a subsidiary of Alphabet Inc. AlphaGo was the first computer Go program to defeat a human professional player without handicaps on a full-sized 19×19 board, and in March 2016 it beat Lee Sedol, one of the world's strongest players, in a five-game match by four games to one, a landmark achievement in artificial intelligence widely compared to Deep Blue's 1997 chess victory over Garry Kasparov.

Approach

Unlike earlier game-playing programs built mainly on search and hand-crafted rules, AlphaGo combined Monte Carlo tree search with two deep neural networks: a policy network, which suggests promising moves, and a value network, which evaluates board positions. The networks were trained first on a database of expert human games and then improved by reinforcement learning from games the program played against itself. Later versions were stronger still: AlphaGo Master won 60 straight games online against top professionals in early 2017 and defeated world number one Ke Jie 3–0 in May 2017, after which DeepMind retired AlphaGo from competition.

Legacy

AlphaGo's unexpected and creative moves — most famously move 37 in game two against Lee Sedol — changed how professional Go is played and studied. Its successor, AlphaGo Zero (2017), learned the game entirely by self-play without human game data and surpassed all previous versions, demonstrating a more general approach to reinforcement learning that led to AlphaZero.
