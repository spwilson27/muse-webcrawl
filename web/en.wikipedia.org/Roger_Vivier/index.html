Roger Vivier

Born: 13 November 1907, Paris, France
Died: 2 October 1998, Toulouse, France
Occupation: Fashion designer, shoemaker
Known for: Stiletto heel, buckle pump

Roger Vivier (13 November 1907 – 2 October 1998) was a French fashion designer who specialised in women's footwear. He is credited with creating the modern stiletto heel in 1954 and designed the iconic buckle-adorned pumps worn by Catherine Deneuve in the film Belle de Jour (1967). The house that bears his name remains a prominent luxury brand.

Career

Vivier studied sculpture at the École des Beaux-Arts in Paris before turning to shoemaking in the late 1920s. He designed for manufacturers in France and the United States and, from 1953, created shoes for Christian Dior, whose "New Look" collections he complemented with ever more refined heels. His 1954 stiletto, made possible by a thin steel core, revolutionised footwear. He also pioneered the comma heel and platform styles, and designed the beaded shoes worn by Queen Elizabeth II at her coronation in 1953.

The buckle shoe

Vivier's most famous creation, the low-heeled pump with a large chrome buckle, was designed in 1965 for Yves Saint Laurent's Mondrian collection and immortalised by Deneuve in Luis Buñuel's Belle de Jour. It remains the emblem of the brand.

Later years and legacy

After losing the rights to his name for a period, Vivier continued designing and was honoured with a major retrospective at the Musée de la Mode et du Textile in Paris in 1987. The revived Roger Vivier brand, under creative directors including Bruno Frisoni and later Gherardo Felloni, has expanded into handbags and accessories while retaining his sculptural approach to the shoe as an object of art.
