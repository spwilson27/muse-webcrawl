Chanson de geste

The chanson de geste (Old French for "song of heroic deeds", from Latin gesta "deeds, actions accomplished") is a medieval narrative, a type of epic poem sung by jongleurs, that appears in the early period of French literature. The earliest surviving chansons date from the late 11th and early 12th centuries, though they recount events — embellished with legend — from centuries earlier, chiefly the age of Charlemagne and his successors.

Form

Chansons are composed in laisses, stanzas of irregular length, each line typically ten syllables (decasyllabic) linked by assonance and later by rhyme. Around 100 epics survive, often huge: the most famous, The Song of Roland, runs to about 4,000 lines, while some chansons exceed 20,000.

Cycles

Medieval classifiers grouped the poems into cycles: the Geste du roi (Charlemagne and his knights, including Roland), the Geste de Garin de Monglane (Guillaume d'Orange) and the Geste de Doon de Mayence (rebellious feudal barons). Themes include loyalty and betrayal, feudal duty, and conflict with Saracen enemies.
