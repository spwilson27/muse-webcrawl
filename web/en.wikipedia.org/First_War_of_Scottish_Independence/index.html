First War of Scottish Independence

The First War of Scottish Independence was the first of a series of wars fought between the Kingdom of Scotland and the Kingdom of England from 1296 until the Treaty of Edinburgh–Northampton in 1328, which recognised Robert the Bruce as King of Scots and Scotland as an independent kingdom.

Origins

The succession crisis following the death of Alexander III (1286) and the Maid of Norway (1290) invited Edward I of England to arbitrate and then to assert overlordship over Scotland. When the Scots resisted and allied with France, Edward invaded in 1296, deposed John Balliol and carried off the Stone of Scone. Scottish resistance under William Wallace and Andrew Moray won a celebrated victory at Stirling Bridge in 1297, but Wallace was defeated at Falkirk in 1298 and executed in 1305.

Robert the Bruce

Robert the Bruce seized the throne in 1306 and, after early setbacks, waged a guerrilla war that gradually recovered the kingdom, culminating in his victory over Edward II at the Battle of Bannockburn in 1314. Cross-border raiding into northern England, the Declaration of Arbroath (1320) asserting Scottish independence to the Pope, and internal English turmoil eventually forced a settlement: the Treaty of Edinburgh–Northampton (1328) recognised Bruce's kingship, though hostilities resumed in the Second War of Scottish Independence a few years later.
