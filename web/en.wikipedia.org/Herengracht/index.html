The Herengracht (Dutch for "Lords' Canal" or "Patricians' Canal") is one of the three main canals of the Grachtengordel, the concentric canal belt of Amsterdam, Netherlands. It was the first of the three principal canals to be dug during the city's great seventeenth-century expansion and became its most prestigious residential address.

History

Work on the canal ring began in 1612–1613 under city carpenter Hendrick Staets and surveyor Lucas Jansz Sinck. The Herengracht was named after the heren regeerders, the patrician regents who governed Amsterdam. Its most famous stretch, the "Golden Bend" (Gouden Bocht) between the Leidsestraat and Vijzelstraat, was laid out from 1663 on double-width plots, where the wealthiest merchants, bankers, and mayors built grand double-fronted mansions.

Notable buildings

Landmarks along the canal include the Bartolotti House, the Cromhouthuizen, and the Museum Geelvinck-Hinlopen Huis. The official residence of the Mayor of Amsterdam stands at Herengracht 502. Many canal houses now serve as banks, law offices, and museums, and the canal belt as a whole has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2010.

The canal belt

From the inside out, the three main canals are the Herengracht, the Keizersgracht ("Emperor's Canal"), and the Prinsengracht ("Prince's Canal"), all radiating from the Singel and crossed by the Brouwersgracht to the north and the Amstel river to the east.
