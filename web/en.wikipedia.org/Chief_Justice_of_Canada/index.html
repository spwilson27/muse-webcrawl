Chief Justice of Canada

The Chief Justice of Canada is the presiding judge of the nine-member Supreme Court of Canada, the highest judicial body in the country. The chief justice is appointed by the Governor in Council and, by convention, ranks third in the Canadian order of precedence after the monarch and the governor general.

Role

The chief justice chairs the Supreme Court's hearings and the Canadian Judicial Council, and acts as the deputy of the governor general — as administrator of the government of Canada whenever the governor general dies, is incapacitated, or is absent for an extended period. The chief justice also traditionally holds leadership of the Order of Canada's advisory council.

Selection

Chief justices are appointed by the prime minister's recommendation to the governor in council from among the judges of the Supreme Court or, rarely, from outside the court. The position has no fixed term; chief justices serve until retirement at age 75.

Notable holders

The first chief justice was Sir William Buell Richards (1875–1879). Beverley McLachlin, who served from 2000 to 2017, was the first woman to hold the office and the longest-serving chief justice in Canadian history. She was succeeded by Richard Wagner in December 2017.
