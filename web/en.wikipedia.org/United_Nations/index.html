United Nations

United Nations
Founded: 24 October 1945
Headquarters: New York City
Member states: 193
Secretary-General: António Guterres (from 2017)

The United Nations (UN) is an intergovernmental organisation founded after World War II to maintain international peace and security, develop friendly relations among nations, and promote human rights and development.

Structure

Principal organs are the General Assembly, Security Council, Economic and Social Council, International Court of Justice, Secretariat and (suspended) Trusteeship Council. Specialised agencies include WHO, UNESCO, UNICEF and the World Bank group affiliates.

History

The UN Charter was signed in San Francisco in 1945 by 50 states, succeeding the League of Nations. Membership grew with decolonisation; regional bodies such as the African Union cooperate with it. Members include Canada and nearly every recognised state.

Work

Peacekeeping, humanitarian aid, the Sustainable Development Goals, and international law are core activities, alongside criticism over Security Council vetoes and funding.
