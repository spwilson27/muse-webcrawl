Quadratic function

In algebra, a quadratic function, a quadratic polynomial, a polynomial of degree 2, or simply a quadratic, is a polynomial function with one or more variables in which the highest-degree term is of the second degree.

For example, a univariate (single-variable) quadratic function has the form

f(x) = ax2 + bx + c,   a ≠ 0

where x is its variable. The graph of a univariate quadratic function is a parabola whose axis of symmetry is parallel to the y-axis.

Roots

The solutions of the quadratic equation ax2 + bx + c = 0 are given by the quadratic formula:

x = (−b ± √(b2 − 4ac)) / 2a

The expression inside the square root, b2 − 4ac, is called the discriminant. If the discriminant is positive, the quadratic has two distinct real roots; if zero, one repeated real root; if negative, two complex conjugate roots.

Vertex form

By completing the square, any quadratic can be written in vertex form f(x) = a(x − h)2 + k, where (h, k) is the vertex of the parabola — its maximum or minimum point depending on the sign of a. The vertex has coordinates h = −b/2a and k = f(h).

Applications

Quadratic functions arise in many areas of physics and engineering, describing projectile motion under gravity, the area of geometric figures, optimisation problems in economics, and the behaviour of uniformly accelerated systems.
