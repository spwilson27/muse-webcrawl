Sacramento

The Tower Bridge spans the Sacramento River, linking Sacramento with West Sacramento.

Sacramento (Spanish for "sacrament") is a name associated principally with the city of Sacramento, California, the capital of the U.S. state of California and the seat of Sacramento County. The name also refers to the Sacramento River, the principal river of Northern California, and to the surrounding Sacramento Valley.

Etymology

The name derives from the Spanish Santísimo Sacramento ("Most Holy Sacrament"), a reference to the Eucharist. Spanish explorer Gabriel Moraga named the river in the early 19th century, reportedly exclaiming that its waters and setting were like the holy sacrament. Swiss immigrant John Sutter founded the settlement that became the city near his colony of New Helvetia, and his son laid out the city in 1848 during the California Gold Rush.

Geography

The Sacramento metropolitan area lies at the confluence of the Sacramento and American rivers in the northern Sacramento Valley. The region is an agricultural and governmental center, sometimes nicknamed the "Farm-to-Fork Capital" for its surrounding farmland. The broader Sacramento–Roseville–Folsom metropolitan statistical area has a population of roughly 2.4 million.
