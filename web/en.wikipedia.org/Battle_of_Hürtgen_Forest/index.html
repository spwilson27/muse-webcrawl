Battle of Hürtgen Forest

The Battle of Hürtgen Forest (German: Schlacht im Hürtgenwald) was a series of battles fought from 19 September to 16 December 1944 between American and German forces on the Western Front during the Second World War. The battle, fought in a heavily forested area southeast of Aachen, was the longest battle on German soil during the war and one of the costliest American engagements of the conflict in Europe.

Background

After the Allied breakout from Normandy and the liberation of France and Belgium, American forces pressed toward the German border and the Siegfried Line. The Hürtgen Forest, some 140 square kilometres of dense coniferous woodland, ridges and muddy trails, covered the approaches to the Roer River dams, whose floodwaters could threaten any Allied advance toward the Rhine. American planners initially underestimated the dams' importance and ordered repeated attacks to clear the forest.

The fighting

In the forest, American advantages in armour, artillery and air power were largely nullified. Tree bursts from artillery detonating in the canopy rained splinters on troops below; mines, bunkers and interlocking machine-gun fire from prepared Westwall defences exacted a heavy toll. Successive American divisions—including the 9th, 28th ("Bloody Bucket"), 4th, 1st and 83rd Infantry Divisions—were ground down in attacks on villages such as Schmidt, Vossenack and Kommerscheidt. The battle ended in mid-December 1944 when the German Ardennes offensive (the Battle of the Bulge) shifted American attention southward.

Aftermath and legacy

American casualties in the Hürtgen Forest and associated operations have been estimated at 33,000 or more, including about 9,000 from non-combat causes such as disease and trench foot; German casualties were heavy as well but lower. The Roer dams were eventually captured in February 1945 during Operation Grenade, but only after the Germans opened them and flooded the valley, delaying the Allied advance. Historians frequently cite Hürtgen as an example of flawed operational planning and the high price of attacking prepared defences in terrain unsuited to the attacker's strengths.
