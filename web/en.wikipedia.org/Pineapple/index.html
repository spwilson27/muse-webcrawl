Pineapple

The pineapple (Ananas comosus) is a tropical plant with an edible fruit; it is the most economically significant plant in the family Bromeliaceae. Native to South America, it spread throughout the tropics after the Columbian exchange and is now grown worldwide for fresh consumption, juice, and canning.

Botany and cultivation

The pineapple is a herbaceous perennial forming a rosette of long, sword-like leaves. Its fruit is a multiple fruit: an entire inflorescence of individual berries fuses into a single mass around a central core, crowned by a tuft of leaves that can be replanted vegetatively. The plant is a CAM photosynthesizer adapted to hot, dry conditions. Fruit contains the enzyme bromelain, used as a meat tenderizer and supplement.

History

Indigenous peoples of South America and the Caribbean (who called it ananas, "excellent fruit") had spread the pineapple widely before Columbus encountered it in 1493. Carried to Europe, Asia, and Africa by Portuguese and Spanish traders, it became a status symbol of wealth in 18th-century Europe. Commercial canning in Hawaii under James Dole and in Southeast Asia industrialized production in the 20th century; the sweet, low-acid MD-2 cultivar dominates world trade today.
