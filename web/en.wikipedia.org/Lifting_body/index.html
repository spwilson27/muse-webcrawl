Lifting body

A lifting body is a fixed-wing aircraft or spacecraft configuration in which the body itself produces lift. In contrast to a flying wing, which is a wing with minimal or no conventional fuselage, a lifting body is a fuselage with little or no conventional wing. The related term body lift describes lift produced by the fuselage of an otherwise conventional aircraft.

History

NASA tested a series of lifting body research aircraft at the Flight Research Center in California in the 1960s and 1970s, including the lightweight M2-F1 and the rocket-powered M2-F2, M2-F3, HL-10, X-24A, and X-24B. Dropped from a B-52 carrier aircraft, they demonstrated that a wingless vehicle could be flown and landed precisely — data that informed the design of the Space Shuttle.

Applications

Lifting body designs offer volume and structural efficiency for reentry vehicles. Later examples and descendants include the X-38 crew return vehicle prototype and the Sierra Space Dream Chaser spaceplane, which uses a lifting body shape for runway landings after orbital flight.
