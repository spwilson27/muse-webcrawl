Retail apocalypse

The retail apocalypse is a phrase used to describe the large-scale closure of brick-and-mortar retail stores, especially chain stores, in the United States beginning in the 2010s. Thousands of stores closed as major retailers such as Sears, Toys "R" Us, Payless ShoeSource, RadioShack, and Borders went bankrupt, shrank, or liquidated.

Causes

Commentators attribute the phenomenon to several factors: the growth of e-commerce, led by Amazon, which shifted consumer spending online; the prior overbuilding of retail space and shopping malls in the United States; changing consumer habits, with spending moving toward experiences, restaurants, and services; and heavy debt burdens, in many cases from leveraged buyouts by private equity firms, which left chains unable to invest in their stores.

Course

Store closures peaked in waves in 2017 and 2019, when analysts tracked thousands of announced closures in the US, and accelerated again during the COVID-19 pandemic, which pushed more shopping online and bankrupted weakened chains. The decline of anchor department stores has contributed to the failure of many shopping malls, some of which have been demolished or converted to housing, logistics, or mixed uses.

Debate

Some economists argue "apocalypse" overstates the case: retail sales overall continued to grow, new store formats and chains continued to open, and the closures represent a restructuring toward online and experience-based retail rather than a collapse of retail itself.
