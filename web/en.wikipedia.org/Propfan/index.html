Propfan

A propfan combines a turbine engine with an unducted propeller.

A propfan, also called an open rotor engine or unducted fan, is a type of aircraft engine related to both the turboprop and turbofan, combining a gas turbine core with a large-diameter, highly swept multi-blade propeller not enclosed in a duct. The design aims to deliver turbofan speeds and altitude performance with turboprop-like fuel efficiency.

History

NASA and engine makers studied propfans intensively during the 1970s–80s oil crises. The General Electric GE36 unducted fan flew on a Boeing 727 and a McDonnell Douglas MD-80 testbed in 1986–87, demonstrating substantial fuel-burn reductions, but cabin and community noise, blade containment, and falling fuel prices kept it from production. The Soviet Progress D-27 propfan powered the Antonov An-70 transport into limited service.

Modern revival

Rising fuel costs and emissions targets revived interest; the CFM International RISE programme, announced in 2021, is developing an open-fan demonstrator intended for entry into service in the mid-2030s, targeting about 20 percent lower fuel consumption than current turbofans.
