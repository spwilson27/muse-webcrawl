Red supergiant

A red supergiant (RSG) is a star with a supergiant luminosity class (Yerkes class I) of spectral type K or M. They are among the largest stars by volume — radii of hundreds to over a thousand times the Sun's — though cool and relatively low in surface temperature, about 3,500–4,500 K.

Red supergiants are evolved massive stars (initially about 10–40 solar masses) that have exhausted hydrogen in their cores and expanded as they burn heavier elements in shells. Well-known examples include Betelgeuse and Antares. They end their lives in core-collapse Type II supernovae, leaving neutron stars or black holes.
