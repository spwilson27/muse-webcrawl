Multimedia Messaging Service

Multimedia Messaging Service (MMS) is a standard way to send messages that include multimedia content over a cellular network, extending the text-only SMS to allow pictures, audio, video clips and longer formatted text. It was developed by the 3GPP and the Open Mobile Alliance and first deployed commercially in the early 2000s as camera phones became common.

Technical operation

When an MMS message is sent, the content is uploaded to the operator's Multimedia Messaging Service Centre (MMSC), and the recipient receives an SMS notification containing a URL from which the phone downloads the message over the mobile data connection, typically using WAP or HTTP. Messages are encoded in a MIME-like format with a SMIL presentation description. Size limits — commonly a few hundred kilobytes to a few megabytes — have historically constrained image and video quality.

Use and decline

MMS became the default method of sending photos between feature phones and early smartphones, but interoperability problems and per-message charges limited its growth in some markets. From the 2010s it was increasingly displaced by internet-based services such as WhatsApp, iMessage and other over-the-top messaging apps offering free, richer media exchange. RCS has been positioned as the carrier-backed successor to both SMS and MMS.
