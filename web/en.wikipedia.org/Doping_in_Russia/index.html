Doping in Russia

Doping in Russia refers to the systematic, state-sponsored use of banned performance-enhancing drugs by Russian athletes, uncovered in the 2010s and described by investigators as an institutional conspiracy involving sports officials, the national anti-doping agency RUSADA, security services, and accredited laboratories. The scandal led to Russia being barred from competing under its own flag at successive Olympic Games and world championships.

Background

Doping had been a feature of Soviet sport, and Russian athletes accounted for a disproportionate share of positive tests after the USSR's collapse; many Olympic medals won by Russians were later stripped on retesting. In December 2014 a documentary by the German broadcaster ARD, featuring whistleblower athlete Yuliya Stepanova and her husband Vitaly Stepanov, a former RUSADA official, alleged systematic doping and cover-ups in Russian athletics.

Investigations and the McLaren reports

The World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA) established an Independent Commission chaired by Richard Pound, whose November 2015 report confirmed widespread cheating and recommended suspending the Russian athletics federation, which the IAAF (now World Athletics) did. Grigory Rodchenkov, former director of the Moscow anti-doping laboratory, fled to the United States and described a state-run programme in which steroids were administered to athletes and positive urine samples were concealed. Canadian lawyer Richard McLaren's reports in 2016 confirmed the account, finding that more than 1,000 Russian athletes across some 30 sports benefited, and that at the 2014 Sochi Winter Olympics, tamper-proof sample bottles were passed through a hole in the laboratory wall and swapped for clean urine with the involvement of the FSB security service.

Consequences

Russia's track and field team was excluded from the Rio 2016 Olympics; the entire Russian team was barred from the 2016 Paralympics. The IOC formally banned Russia from the PyeongChang 2018 Winter Games, though vetted athletes competed as "Olympic Athletes from Russia". In December 2019 WADA imposed a four-year ban on Russia's flag, anthem, and team name at major events, reduced to two years by the Court of Arbitration for Sport in 2020; Russian athletes competed at Tokyo 2020 and Beijing 2022 as the "Russian Olympic Committee" (ROC). Separately, World Athletics maintained its own suspension of the Russian federation, and data manipulation at the Moscow laboratory — the withholding and alteration of the lab's database handed to WADA in 2019 — prolonged the affair.
