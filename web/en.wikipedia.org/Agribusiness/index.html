Agribusiness

Agribusiness is the industry engaged in the production, processing, and distribution of agricultural products, encompassing the entire value chain of agriculture and food production. The term — a portmanteau of "agriculture" and "business" — was coined in 1957 by Harvard academics John Davis and Ray Goldberg.

Scope

Agribusiness includes input suppliers (seed, fertiliser, pesticides, and farm machinery), farm production, storage, processing, wholesale and retail distribution, and marketing. Major agribusiness firms include Cargill, Archer Daniels Midland, Bunge, Bayer, Corteva, and John Deere.

Trends

The sector has seen increasing vertical integration and consolidation, contract farming, and the application of biotechnology, precision agriculture, and data analytics. Agribusiness plays a central role in global food security, trade, and rural employment.

Criticism

Critics raise concerns about environmental impact, market concentration, the treatment of farm workers, and the influence of large firms on food policy and smallholder farmers.
