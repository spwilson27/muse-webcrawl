Barn raising

A barn raising, also called a raising bee, is a collective action of a community to construct a barn for one of its members. It was especially common in rural North America in the 18th and 19th centuries, and remains a tradition among Amish and Old Order Mennonite communities.

Practice

Because a barn was essential to a farming family's livelihood but too large for one family to build alone, neighbours gathered to assemble the pre-cut timber frame in a single day. Men raised the heavy bents with pike poles while women prepared food for the workers. The event combined necessary labour with a social occasion, reinforcing community bonds.
