Asparaginase

Drug classAntineoplastic agent (enzyme)
Main useAcute lymphoblastic leukaemia (ALL)
MechanismDepletes circulating L-asparagine
SourcesEscherichia coli, Erwinia chrysanthemi

Asparaginase is an enzyme that catalyses the hydrolysis of the amino acid asparagine into aspartic acid and ammonia. In medicine it is used as a chemotherapy drug, chiefly in the treatment of acute lymphoblastic leukaemia (ALL), particularly in children.

Mechanism of action

Certain leukaemic lymphoblasts cannot synthesise their own asparagine and depend on the amino acid circulating in the blood. Administering asparaginase depletes plasma asparagine, starving these malignant cells of an essential nutrient and halting their protein synthesis, while most normal cells can produce asparagine themselves and are relatively spared.

Preparations and adverse effects

Preparations are derived from bacteria, principally E. coli and Erwinia chrysanthemi; a pegylated form (pegaspargase) has a longer half-life. Because the drug is a foreign protein it can provoke allergic reactions and antibody formation. Other adverse effects include pancreatitis, liver dysfunction, coagulation abnormalities, and hyperglycaemia.
