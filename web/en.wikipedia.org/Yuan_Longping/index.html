Yuan Longping (Chinese: 袁隆平; 7 September 1930 – 22 May 2021) was a Chinese agronomist celebrated as the "Father of Hybrid Rice". His development of high-yield hybrid rice varieties from the 1970s onward is credited with helping feed tens of millions of people and transforming food security in China and across Asia and Africa.

Hybrid rice research

Trained at Southwest Agricultural College in Chongqing, Yuan began researching hybrid rice after witnessing famine during the Great Leap Forward. Although rice is self-pollinating and was widely thought incapable of practical hybridisation, Yuan found a naturally male-sterile wild rice plant in Hainan in 1970 — the key to the "three-line" hybrid system. By 1973 his team had produced the first high-yield hybrid rice combination, and by 1976 hybrids were planted commercially, yielding roughly 20 percent more than conventional varieties.

Later work and legacy

Yuan later led work on "super hybrid rice" with progressively higher yield targets, and on salt-tolerant "seawater rice" grown in saline soils. He received the World Food Prize in 2004, China's Medal of the Republic in 2019, and an asteroid (8117 Yuanlongping) was named after him. His death in Changsha in 2021 prompted national mourning in China.
