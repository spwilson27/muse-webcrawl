Market equilibrium

Market equilibrium is a situation in a market where the quantity demanded of a good equals the quantity supplied, at a price called the equilibrium price (or market-clearing price). At equilibrium there is no inherent tendency for price or quantity to change.

Mechanism

If price is above equilibrium, supply exceeds demand, inventories accumulate and sellers cut prices. If price is below equilibrium, demand exceeds supply, shortages appear and buyers bid prices up. Graphically, equilibrium lies at the intersection of the supply and demand curves.

Shifts

Changes in income, tastes, input costs or technology shift the supply or demand curves, moving the market to a new equilibrium. Comparative statics analyses how equilibrium prices and quantities respond to such shifts.

General equilibrium

General equilibrium theory, developed by Léon Walras and formalised by Arrow and Debreu, studies the simultaneous equilibrium of all markets in an economy and underlies results about the efficiency of competitive markets.

Criticisms

Critics note that real markets may exhibit multiple equilibria, disequilibrium dynamics, sticky prices and market power, so equilibrium is an approximation rather than a literal description.
