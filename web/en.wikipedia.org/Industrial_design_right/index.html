Industrial design right

An industrial design right is an intellectual property right that protects the visual design of objects that are not purely utilitarian — the shape, configuration, pattern, or ornamentation of a product. It is one of the categories of industrial property under the Paris Convention and the TRIPS Agreement, which requires member states to protect independently created, new or original industrial designs for at least ten years. Registration systems exist in most jurisdictions, administered internationally through the Hague Agreement administered by WIPO.

Design registrations protect a product's appearance, not its function.

Scope and requirements

To be protected, a design generally must be novel and have individual character; purely functional features are excluded. The right holder can prevent others from making, selling, or importing products bearing the design. In the European Union, a registered Community design lasts up to 25 years, while an unregistered design is protected for three years from disclosure. In the United States, the equivalent protection is the design patent, lasting 15 years from grant for applications filed after May 2015.

Relation to other rights

Industrial design rights complement patents (which protect function), trademarks (which protect source-identifying signs), and copyright (which may overlap for artistic works). Notable disputes have arisen over designs for furniture, consumer electronics, and vehicle spare parts, where a "repair clause" debate concerns whether component-part designs should be enforceable against independent repairers.
