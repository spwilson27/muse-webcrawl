New York Journal-American

The New York Journal-American was an American daily newspaper published in New York City from 1937 to 1966. It was formed by the merger of William Randolph Hearst's New York American and New York Evening Journal, and was a flagship of the Hearst chain and of "yellow journalism" in its earlier incarnations.

Predecessors

Hearst bought the New York Morning Journal in 1895 and fought a famous circulation war with Joseph Pulitzer's New York World, a rivalry associated with sensational "yellow journalism" and agitation before the Spanish-American War of 1898. The evening edition, the Evening Journal, and the morning New York American (formerly the Morning Journal) were combined in 1937.

Journal-American years

The merged paper was one of the largest-circulation afternoon dailies in the United States, known for conservative politics, extensive comics, photography and sports coverage, and columnists such as Westbrook Pegler. Its photo morgue became an important archive.

Closure

Falling afternoon-paper circulation and a long newspaper strike led to its end: in April 1966 the Journal-American ceased independent publication and briefly merged into the short-lived World Journal Tribune, which folded in 1967.
