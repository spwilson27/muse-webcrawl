Packard V-1650 Merlin

Packard V-1650 Merlin
Type: Liquid-cooled V-12 piston aero engine
National origin: United States
Manufacturer: Packard
First run: 1941
Major applications: P-51 Mustang, Supermarine Spitfire, Avro Lancaster, Hawker Hurricane, de Havilland Mosquito
Number built: 58,032
Developed from: Rolls-Royce Merlin

The Packard V-1650 Merlin is a version of the Rolls-Royce Merlin aircraft engine, produced under license in the United States by the Packard Motor Car Company. The engine also filled the needs of the British Commonwealth, whose Merlin production capacity was insufficient. The V-1650 was used in many famous Second World War aircraft, most notably the North American P-51 Mustang, whose switch from the Allison V-1710 to the Merlin transformed it into one of the outstanding long-range fighters of the war.

Changes from the Rolls-Royce design included a different supercharger drive and the replacement of some British components with American equivalents, such as a Bendix-Stromberg pressure-injection carburettor. Packard built 58,032 Merlins in its Detroit plant between 1941 and 1945 — more than half of all Merlins produced.

Specifications (V-1650-7)

The V-1650-7 was a 27-litre (1,649 cu in) liquid-cooled supercharged 60° V-12, producing about 1,490 hp (1,111 kW) at 3,000 rpm for takeoff, and up to roughly 1,720 hp with war emergency power. It powered the P-51D Mustang.
