Die-cast toy

A die-cast toy is a toy or a collectible model produced by using the die casting method of putting molten lead, zinc alloy or plastic into a mold to produce a particular shape. Such toys are made of metal, with plastic, rubber, glass, or other machined metal parts. Wholly plastic toys are differentiated by having been made by the injection-moulding process.

History

Die-cast toys were first produced in the early 20th century by manufacturers such as Meccano (Dinky Toys) in the United Kingdom and Dowst Brothers (Tootsietoy) in the United States. The first models on the market were basic car and truck models with no interiors. In the early days, it was common for the impurities in the zinc alloy to cause the casting to crack, distort or crumble, a condition popularly known as zinc pest or metal fatigue; models made before 1947 are therefore vulnerable to this damage.

In 1947, the British Lesney company introduced its Matchbox series of small-scale die-cast vehicles sold in boxes resembling matchboxes, making the toys affordable and highly collectible. In 1968, Mattel introduced Hot Wheels in the United States, featuring low-friction axles that made the cars far faster than competitors, revolutionising the market.

Scales and collecting

Common scales for die-cast vehicle models include 1:18, 1:24, 1:43, 1:64 and 1:87 (HO scale). Die-cast toys range from inexpensive children's playthings to highly detailed adult collectibles produced by brands such as Corgi, Minichamps and Autoart. Besides cars, die-cast models are made of aircraft, trains, buses, construction equipment, military vehicles and farm machinery. Collecting die-cast models is a widespread hobby, supported by price guides, conventions and online marketplaces.

Manufacturing

Most die-cast toys are made from Zamak (also called Mazak), an alloy of zinc with small quantities of aluminium, magnesium and copper. The molten alloy is injected under high pressure into steel dies, cooled, ejected, trimmed, painted—often by spray or tampo printing—and assembled with plastic windows, interiors, rubber tyres and other fittings.
