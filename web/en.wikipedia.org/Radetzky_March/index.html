Radetzky March

Field Marshal Joseph Radetzky, in whose honour the march was composed.

The Radetzky March, Op. 228, is a march composed by Johann Strauss I in 1848. It was dedicated to Field Marshal Joseph Radetzky von Radetz, the Austrian commander who had won the Battle of Custoza against Piedmontese forces during the First Italian War of Independence. Alongside the Blue Danube waltz, it is among the most famous tunes of the Strauss family.

Composition and reception

The march was first performed on 31 August 1848 in Vienna to celebrate the Austrian victory. Its trio section reuses a theme from Strauss's own Jubel-Quadrille. The rousing main theme is said to have been modelled on popular Viennese folk tunes.

Traditions

The Radetzky March traditionally closes the Vienna Philharmonic's New Year's Concert; by convention, the audience claps along in time with the music, with the conductor turning to direct the applause. It is also the regimental quick march of several military units.
