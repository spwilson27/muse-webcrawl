Pinyin

Hanyu Pinyin, usually shortened to pinyin, is the official romanisation system for Standard Chinese in mainland China, Singapore and Taiwan (since 2009). It transcribes the sounds of Mandarin using the Latin alphabet and diacritic tone marks.

History

Pinyin was developed in the 1950s by a committee of Chinese linguists including Zhou Youguang, building on earlier systems such as Wade–Giles and Gwoyeu Romatzyh. It was approved by the National People's Congress in 1958 and adopted internationally: the United Nations in 1977 and the International Organization for Standardization (ISO 7098) in 1982. Its adoption changed English spellings of Chinese names from forms like "Peking" to "Beijing".

System

Each Chinese syllable is written with an initial consonant (such as b, p, m, f, zh, q, x) and a final, plus one of four tone marks (mā, má, mǎ, mà) or a neutral tone. Some letters have values differing from English: c is [tsʰ], q is [tɕʰ] and x is [ɕ]. The system represents pronunciation, not characters, and is used to teach reading in schools, order dictionaries, type Chinese on computers and phones, and transcribe Chinese in libraries worldwide.
