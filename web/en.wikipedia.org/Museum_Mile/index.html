Museum Mile

Location: Fifth Avenue, Upper East Side, Manhattan, New York City
Extent: 82nd to 105th Streets
Known for: One of the world's densest concentrations of museums

Museum Mile is the name given to a stretch of Fifth Avenue on the Upper East Side of Manhattan in New York City, running roughly from 82nd to 105th Streets along Central Park, which contains one of the finest concentrations of museums in the world. The name plays on the "Miracle Mile" of commercial districts; the avenue here is lined with grand former mansions converted into cultural institutions.

Institutions

The museums of Museum Mile include:

  • The Metropolitan Museum of Art (82nd Street), among the largest art museums in the world
  • The Frick Collection (70th Street, just south of the mile)
  • The Solomon R. Guggenheim Museum, in Frank Lloyd Wright's spiral building
  • The Cooper Hewitt, Smithsonian Design Museum, in the Andrew Carnegie Mansion
  • The Jewish Museum
  • The Museum of the City of New York
  • El Museo del Barrio, devoted to Latino and Latin American art
  • The Neue Galerie and the Africa Center (formerly the Museum for African Art)

Museum Mile Festival

Since 1978 the institutions have jointly hosted the Museum Mile Festival, usually on a Tuesday evening in June, when the avenue is closed to traffic and the museums open free to the public, with street performances and activities drawing tens of thousands of visitors.
