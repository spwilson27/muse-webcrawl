Edwyn Stephen Collins (born 23 August 1959) is a Scottish musician, producer and record label owner. As frontman of the post-punk band Orange Juice, he pioneered the jangling, soul-influenced indie pop sound associated with Glasgow's Postcard Records in the early 1980s; Orange Juice's 1983 single "Rip It Up" reached the UK top ten. His best-known solo work is the worldwide 1994 hit "A Girl Like You", from the album Gorgeous George.

Career

Collins formed the Nu-Sonics in 1976, which became Orange Juice in 1979. After the band split in 1984, he pursued a solo career and co-founded the Analogue Enhanced Digital (AED) record label. He also produced albums for other artists at his West Heath Studios in London. His solo albums include Hellbent on Compromise (1990), Doctor Syntax (2002), Home Again (2007) and Losing Sleep (2010), which won wide acclaim for songs written with collaborators including Johnny Marr and Franz Ferdinand's Alex Kapranos.

Illness and recovery

In February 2005 Collins suffered two cerebral haemorrhages and underwent a long rehabilitation that left him initially able to say only a few words. His recovery, supported by his wife and manager Grace Maxwell, was documented in the 2014 film The Possibilities Are Endless. He returned to recording and live performance, and received an Ivor Novello Award in 2009. Maxwell's memoir Falling and Laughing recounts the experience.
