Lewisham

Lewisham is a district of south-east London, England, and the principal settlement of the London Borough of Lewisham. It lies about 9.5 km (5.9 miles) south-east of Charing Cross, on the River Ravensbourne. It is identified in the London Plan as one of Greater London's 35 major centres.

History

Lewisham's name is thought to derive from Old English, meaning "Leofsa's village". Long a rural parish in Kent, it developed rapidly after the arrival of the railway in 1849, becoming a busy commercial and residential suburb. The town centre is dominated by a large shopping centre opened in 1977 and a landmark clock tower erected in 1900 to mark Queen Victoria's Diamond Jubilee.

Transport

Lewisham station is a major interchange between National Rail services and the Docklands Light Railway, whose southern terminus is here. The district is also served by numerous bus routes.
