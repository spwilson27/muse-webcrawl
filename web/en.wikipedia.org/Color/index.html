Color

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Color (or colour in Commonwealth English) is the visual perception based on the electromagnetic spectrum. Though color is not an inherent property of matter, color perception is related to an object's light absorption, emission, reflection and transmission. For most humans, colors are perceived in the visible light spectrum with three types of cone cells (trichromacy). Additionally, some animals have significantly different color vision compared to humans.

Physics and perception

Visible light spans wavelengths from roughly 380 to 750 nanometres. When light enters the eye and stimulates the three classes of cone photoreceptors in the retina (sensitive mainly to short, medium, and long wavelengths), the brain constructs the sensation of color. Different spectral distributions can produce the same perceived color, a phenomenon called metamerism. Isaac Newton showed that white light can be split by a prism into the colors of the spectrum and recombined. The opponent-process theory, developed by Ewald Hering, complements trichromatic theory by describing how colors are encoded in red–green, blue–yellow, and black–white channels.

Color systems

Colors are described and reproduced by many systems: the additive RGB model used in displays, the subtractive CMYK model used in printing, hue–saturation–value (HSV) and hue–saturation–lightness (HSL) models, standardized systems such as the Munsell color system and CIE 1931 color space, and commercial systems such as Pantone. The spectral colors of the rainbow are conventionally grouped as red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, and violet.

Color in culture

Colors carry symbolic and psychological associations that vary by culture: white is associated with purity in many Western traditions and with mourning in parts of Asia; red can signify luck and celebration in China and danger or debt elsewhere. Color terminology itself varies across languages, and researchers such as Berlin and Kay have studied the order in which basic color terms emerge in languages. Color plays central roles in art, design, heraldry, politics, and safety signaling.
