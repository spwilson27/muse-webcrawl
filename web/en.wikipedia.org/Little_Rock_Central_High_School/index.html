Little Rock Central High School is a public school in Little Rock, Arkansas, United States. Built in 1927 and once described as the most beautiful high school in America, it became internationally famous in 1957 as the centre of the Little Rock Crisis, a defining confrontation of the American civil rights movement.

The Little Rock Nine

Following the Supreme Court's 1954 decision in Brown v. Board of Education, nine African American students — the Little Rock Nine — enrolled at Central High for the 1957–58 school year. Arkansas governor Orval Faubus deployed the National Guard to block their entry, prompting President Dwight D. Eisenhower to federalise the Guard and send troops of the 101st Airborne Division to escort the students into the school. The students endured a year of harassment; Ernest Green became the school's first Black graduate in May 1958.

Aftermath

In 1958–59, Faubus closed all of Little Rock's public high schools — the "Lost Year" — rather than accept integration. The schools reopened in 1959. Central High remains a functioning school and is administered in part as a National Historic Site, with a visitor centre across the street interpreting the events of 1957.

Architecture

The building combines Gothic Revival and Art Deco elements, with statues of the spirits of Ambition, Personality, Opportunity and Preparation above the entrance.
