Twelfth

Twelfth is the ordinal form of the number twelve, denoting the position of an item in a sequence that comes after the eleventh and before the thirteenth. The word derives from Old English twelfta. A twelfth part is one of twelve equal parts of a whole; in music, a twelfth is an interval spanning twelve letter names — an octave plus a fifth — and in historical land measurement and taxation a "twelfth" could refer to a fractional levy or share.

Uses of the term

"The Twelfth" commonly refers to the Twelfth of July, a Ulster Protestant celebration of the victory of William of Orange at the Battle of the Boyne. Twelfth Night, the eve of Epiphany and the twelfth day of Christmas, marks the end of the Christmas season in many traditions. "Twelfth" also appears in the names of regiments, grades in schooling (twelfth grade is the final year of secondary education in the United States) and legislative bodies such as the Twelfth Amendment to the United States Constitution.
