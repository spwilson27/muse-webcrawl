Mass of Paul VI

The Mass of Paul VI, also known as the Ordinary Form of the Roman Rite (and formerly sometimes as the Novus Ordo Missae), is the most commonly used liturgy of the Roman Rite of the Catholic Church. It was promulgated by Pope Paul VI in 1969, following the Second Vatican Council, and published in the Roman Missal issued in 1970, with later editions in 1975 and 2002.

History

The liturgical constitution Sacrosanctum Concilium (1963) called for revision of the rites of the Mass. The Consilium for the implementation of the constitution prepared the new Order of Mass, promulgated by the apostolic constitution Missale Romanum on 3 April 1969. The revised Missal permitted celebration in vernacular languages and facing the people, and expanded the lectionary and the choices of Eucharistic Prayers.

Structure

The Mass consists of the Introductory Rites, the Liturgy of the Word, the Liturgy of the Eucharist, and the Concluding Rites. Compared with the older Tridentine Mass (the Extraordinary Form), it features a three-year cycle of readings, prayers of the faithful, and greater participation by the congregation. Pope Benedict XVI's 2007 motu proprio Summorum Pontificum and Pope Francis's 2021 Traditionis Custodes addressed the use of the earlier form.
