The Houmuwu ding (Chinese: 后母戊鼎) is the largest known bronze vessel from the ancient world. It is a rectangular ding (cooking vessel) cast in bronze during the late Shang dynasty of China. Weighing 832.84 kilograms (about 1,836 pounds), it is the heaviest surviving Chinese ritual bronze.

History

The ding was unearthed in 1939 at Wuguan Village near Anyang in Henan province, on the site of the Shang capital Yin. Villagers buried it again to hide it from Japanese forces during the Second Sino-Japanese War; it was re-excavated in 1946. The vessel is believed to have been cast by a Shang king (often identified with Zu Geng or Zu Jia) in honour of his mother.

Description

The rectangular body is supported on four legs and decorated with taotie masks and other motifs typical of Shang bronzes. Inside the vessel is an inscription of three characters — read as "Hou Mu Wu" — identifying it as dedicated to a royal mother, "Wu". The name was earlier read as "Simuwu", and the vessel is still sometimes called the Simuwu ding.
