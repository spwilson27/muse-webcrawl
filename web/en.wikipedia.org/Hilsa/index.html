Hilsa (Tenualosa ilisha), also known as ilish, is a species of fish in the herring family (Clupeidae/Dorosomatidae) and a popular food fish in the Indian subcontinent. It is the national fish of Bangladesh and the state fish of West Bengal, India. Hilsa is famous for its distinctive flavour, soft oily texture, and rich content of omega-3 fatty acids.

Description

The hilsa is a silvery, laterally compressed fish with a deep body, growing to about 60 cm in length. It has a dark blotch behind the gill opening, followed by a series of smaller spots along the flank. Like other shads, it is anadromous: adults live in the sea and migrate up rivers to spawn in fresh water.

Distribution and habitat

Hilsa is found in the coastal waters and estuaries of the northern Indian Ocean, from the Persian Gulf eastward through Pakistan, India, Bangladesh and Myanmar. The most important fisheries are in the Padma–Meghna river system of Bangladesh and the Hooghly River in West Bengal. Bangladesh produces the majority of the world's hilsa catch.

Fisheries and cuisine

Hilsa is economically vital to Bangladesh, where it accounts for a significant share of total fish production and employs millions directly or indirectly. In Bengali cuisine, the fish is prepared in dozens of ways — steamed with mustard paste (shorshe ilish), smoked, fried, or cooked in curries. Its many fine bones are a famous challenge for diners. Bangladesh has registered hilsa as a geographical indication (GI) product.

Conservation

Although currently listed as Least Concern by the IUCN, hilsa populations face pressure from overfishing, river siltation, pollution and the capture of juveniles (called jatka in Bangladesh, where their catch is seasonally banned to protect the fishery).
