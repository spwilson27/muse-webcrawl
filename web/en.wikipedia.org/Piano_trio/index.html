Piano trio

A piano trio is a group of piano and two other instruments, usually a violin and a cello, or a piece of music written for such a group. It is one of the most common forms found in classical chamber music. (The term is distinct from the unrelated "piano trio" of jazz, which typically means piano, bass, and drums.)

History

The piano trio emerged in the mid-18th century from the Baroque trio sonata and the accompanied keyboard sonata, in which the strings at first doubled or lightly supported the keyboard part. Haydn wrote dozens of trios that gradually emancipated the cello and violin; Mozart and Beethoven brought the three instruments to full equality, Beethoven's "Archduke" Trio, Op. 97 (1811), being a landmark. The form flourished in the Romantic era with major works by Schubert, Mendelssohn, Schumann, Brahms, Dvořák, and Tchaikovsky (whose Trio in A minor was written "in memory of a great artist," Nikolai Rubinstein).

Repertoire and ensembles

Notable 20th-century trios include those of Ravel, Shostakovich, and Charles Ives. Celebrated performing ensembles have included the Beaux Arts Trio, the Kalichstein–Laredo–Robinson Trio, and the Trio Wanderer.
