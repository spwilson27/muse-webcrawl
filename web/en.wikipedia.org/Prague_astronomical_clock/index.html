Prague astronomical clock

The Prague Astronomical Clock, or Prague Orloj (Czech: Pražský orloj), is a medieval astronomical clock attached to the Old Town Hall in Prague, the capital of the Czech Republic. The clock was first installed in 1410, making it the third-oldest astronomical clock in the world and the oldest clock still in operation.

Components

The clock mechanism has three main components: the astronomical dial, representing the position of the Sun and Moon in the sky and displaying various astronomical details; statues of various Catholic saints on both sides of the dial; "The Walk of the Apostles", an hourly show of moving Apostle figures and other sculptures, notably a figure of a skeleton that represents Death striking the time; and a calendar dial with medallions representing the months. The astronomical dial shows Old Bohemian time, Babylonian time, the position of the Sun in the zodiac, sunrise and sunset, and the phase of the Moon. Legend associates the clockmaker Master Hanuš with a tale—historically unfounded—that the councillors had him blinded so he could never build a rival clock.

History

The oldest part of the Orloj, the mechanical clock and astronomical dial, dates to 1410 and was built by clockmaker Mikuláš of Kadaň and astronomer Jan Šindel. The calendar dial was added around 1490, along with Gothic sculptures. The clock was repeatedly repaired over the centuries, stopped working and was neglected for long periods, and was seriously damaged by German fire during the Prague uprising in May 1945; it was repaired and restored to operation by 1948. It underwent major renovation in 2018, which included restoring the original 1860s appearance of the calendar dial and replacing a controversial LED display with the restored mechanism behind glass.

The hourly show

Every hour on the hour, crowds gather in Old Town Square to watch the Walk of the Apostles: windows above the dial open and figures of the Twelve Apostles pass by, while side figures—a skeleton ringing a bell, a Turk, a miser with his bag of gold, and Vanity admiring himself in a mirror—shake their heads or move, ending with a rooster crowing and the bell ringing.
