Soay sheep

The Soay sheep is a breed of domestic sheep descended from a population of feral sheep on the 100-hectare (250-acre) island of Soay in the St Kilda archipelago, about 65 kilometres (40 mi) west of the Outer Hebrides in Scotland. It is one of the Northern European short-tailed sheep breeds and is considered close to the wild type of early domestic sheep brought to Britain in the Neolithic.

Characteristics

Soay sheep are small and hardy — rams weigh about 40 kg and ewes 25 kg — with short tails, and a fleece that is naturally shed (rooed) in summer rather than shorn. Both sexes may be horned. Their coats are typically dark brown with pale belly and rump patches resembling those of the mouflon, though lighter colour morphs occur.

The St Kilda population study

In 1932, after the human evacuation of St Kilda, 107 Soay sheep were moved from Soay to the larger island of Hirta, where the unmanaged population has since been the subject of one of the longest-running ecological and evolutionary studies of a wild mammal. Researchers have tracked individual life histories since 1985, documenting dramatic population crashes in harsh winters and rapid microevolution — notably a decrease in average body size attributed to climate change.
