Glass fiber

Glass fiber (also spelled glass fibre) is a material consisting of numerous extremely fine fibers of glass. It is used as a reinforcing agent in fiber-reinforced polymer (FRP) composites — commonly called fiberglass — and in insulation wool, optical fibers, and various technical textiles.

Properties

Glass fibers are valued for their high strength-to-weight ratio, low cost, chemical resistance, and electrical insulation properties. Common compositions include E-glass (alumino-borosilicate, the general-purpose grade), S-glass (higher-strength magnesio-aluminosilicate), and C-glass (chemical-resistant). Individual filaments range from a few micrometres to about 20 micrometres in diameter.

Manufacture

Fibers are drawn from molten glass through fine platinum-alloy bushings, coated with a sizing to protect them and promote bonding with resin, and gathered into strands, rovings, or chopped mats. Glass wool for insulation is instead produced by spinning or blowing molten glass into fluffy, air-trapping masses.

Applications

Composites of glass fiber reinforce boat hulls, aircraft parts, wind-turbine blades, pipes, and printed circuit boards. Optical fibers of high-purity silica glass carry most of the world's telecommunications traffic. Fine airborne fibers can irritate skin and lungs, so handling precautions are standard in industry.
