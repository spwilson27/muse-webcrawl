Alkire–Foster method

The Alkire–Foster (AF) method is a way of measuring multidimensional poverty developed by Sabina Alkire and James Foster at the Oxford Poverty & Human Development Initiative (OPHI). It underlies the global Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) published by OPHI and the United Nations Development Programme.

How it works

The method uses a dual-cutoff approach. First, a deprivation cutoff is set for each indicator (for example, years of schooling or access to clean water) and each person is identified as deprived or not in each dimension. Second, the weighted deprivations are summed, and a poverty cutoff k identifies as poor those whose deprivation score meets or exceeds k. The headline measure M0 is the product of the incidence of poverty (the headcount ratio H) and its intensity (the average deprivation share A).

Properties

The method is decomposable by population subgroup and by dimension, allowing policy-makers to see which deprivations drive poverty in each region. It can be applied to any set of indicators and weights, and many countries have adopted national MPIs based on it.
