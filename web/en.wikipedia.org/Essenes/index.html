The Essenes were a Jewish sect that flourished from the second century BCE to the first century CE, during the Second Temple period. They are described by the ancient writers Josephus, Philo of Alexandria and Pliny the Elder as living communally, holding property in common, practising strict ritual purity and celibacy (in at least one branch), and devoting themselves to prayer, study and manual labour.

Since the discovery of the Dead Sea Scrolls in 1947, most scholars have associated the Essenes with the community that lived at Qumran and produced or preserved those manuscripts, although the identification remains debated.

Ancient accounts

Josephus gives the fullest description in The Jewish War and Antiquities of the Jews. He numbers the Essenes at about four thousand, says they rejected slavery and animal sacrifice (or sacrificed separately), admitted members only after a probationary period of up to three years, and practised a rigorous daily routine of prayer before sunrise, work, ritual immersion and communal meals eaten in silence. Philo praises their freedom and communal ethics; Pliny locates them on the western shore of the Dead Sea, "above" Ein Gedi.

Beliefs and practices

The Essenes are reported to have believed in the immortality of the soul, divine providence or fate, and a coming judgement. Scrolls attributed to the Qumran community add a strongly dualistic and apocalyptic worldview: a war between the "Sons of Light" and the "Sons of Darkness", expectation of two messiahs (of Aaron and of Israel), and a solar calendar of 364 days that set the community apart from the Temple authorities in Jerusalem.

Qumran and the Dead Sea Scrolls

The settlement at Khirbet Qumran contained ritual baths, a scriptorium-like room with inkwells, and a large cemetery; nearby caves yielded the scrolls. The community's rule books — the Community Rule, the Damascus Document and the War Scroll — match the Essene profile of the classical sources in many particulars. The site was destroyed by the Romans during the First Jewish–Roman War, around 68 CE, after which the Essenes disappear from history.
