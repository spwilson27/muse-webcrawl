Cologne

Cologne (German: Köln [kœln]; Kölsch: Kölle) is the largest city of the German state of North Rhine-Westphalia and the fourth-most populous city of Germany, with around 1.1 million inhabitants in the city proper and more than 3 million in the wider urban region. It stands on the banks of the River Rhine in western Germany and is a major cultural, economic and media centre.

The city's landmark is Cologne Cathedral (the Kölner Dom), a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the largest Gothic cathedrals in Europe, whose twin spires dominate the skyline. Cologne is also famous for its carnival, its Romanesque churches, its Eau de Cologne, and its local beer, Kölsch.

History

The Romans founded the settlement as Colonia Claudia Ara Agrippinensium in AD 50, from which the modern name derives. Cologne grew into one of the largest and richest cities of the medieval Holy Roman Empire, a free imperial city and a member of the Hanseatic League. Its archbishops were electors of the Empire. Much of the city was destroyed by bombing in the Second World War and rebuilt in the post-war decades.

Culture

Cologne's carnival season, culminating in the Rose Monday parade, is among the largest in Germany. The city hosts numerous museums, including the Romano-Germanic Museum, Museum Ludwig with its collection of modern art, and the Chocolate Museum. It is a major broadcasting centre, home to WDR and other media companies, and its university, founded in 1388, is one of Germany's largest.

Economy and transport

Media, insurance, chemicals, and trade fairs anchor the economy; the Koelnmesse hosts international exhibitions such as Gamescom and Art Cologne. Cologne Bonn Airport and a dense rail network, centred on Köln Hauptbahnhof beneath the cathedral, connect the city across Europe.
