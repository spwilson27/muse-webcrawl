John Pennekamp Coral Reef State Park is a Florida State Park located on Key Largo in Florida. It was the first undersea park in the United States, established in 1963 to protect a portion of the only living coral barrier reef in the continental United States.

History

The park was created largely through the efforts of John Pennekamp, an editor of the Miami Herald who had earlier championed Everglades National Park. It originally included much of what later became part of the adjacent Florida Keys National Marine Sanctuary.

Recreation

The park is famous for snorkelling and scuba diving over coral reefs and seagrass beds, glass-bottom boat tours, kayaking through mangroves, and a visitor center aquarium. Notable dive sites include the sunken Christ of the Abyss statue, a bronze cast placed on the reef in 1965.

Ecology

Its waters shelter hundreds of species of fish, sea turtles, manatees, staghorn and elkhorn corals, and the mangrove-lined shore protects juvenile reef animals.
