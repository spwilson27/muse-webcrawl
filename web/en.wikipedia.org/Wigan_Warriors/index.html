The Wigan Warriors are a professional rugby league club from Wigan, Greater Manchester, England, which competes in the Super League. The club is one of the most successful in the history of the sport, having won numerous league championships and Challenge Cups, including a dominant era between the mid-1980s and mid-1990s in which it won the Challenge Cup a record eight times in succession.

History

Wigan Football Club was formed on 21 November 1872 by members of Wigan Cricket Club. The club was a founding member of the Northern Rugby Football Union in 1895, the breakaway that created rugby league. Wigan played at Springfield Park before moving to Central Park in 1902, its home for most of the twentieth century. In 1999 the club moved to the new stadium it now shares with Wigan Athletic football club, and adopted the Warriors name in 1997.

Golden era

Under coach Graham Lowe and then John Monie, Wigan assembled a celebrated full-time professional side featuring players such as Ellery Hanley, Shaun Edwards, Andy Gregory, Joe Lydon and Steve Hampson. Between 1988 and 1995 the club won the Challenge Cup at Wembley eight years running, alongside multiple league titles and a famous victory over Brisbane Broncos in the 1994 World Club Challenge in Australia.

Honours

Wigan have won the league championship more than twenty times, the Challenge Cup a record number of times (passing twenty titles in 2024), and the World Club Challenge five times. The club's fiercest rivalry is with St Helens, contested in the Good Friday derby, one of the oldest and most famous fixtures in rugby league.

Stadium

Since 1999, home games have been played at the 25,138-capacity stadium in the Robin Park area of Wigan, known since 2024 as The Brick Community Stadium, shared with Wigan Athletic.
