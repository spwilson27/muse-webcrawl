Deneb

Deneb is a first-magnitude star in the constellation of Cygnus. It is one of the vertices of the asterism known as the Summer Triangle and forms the "tail" of the Northern Cross. Deneb's name derives from the Arabic dhanab, meaning "tail", from the phrase meaning "the tail of the hen". Its Bayer designation is α Cygni (Alpha Cygni).

Deneb marks the tail of Cygnus, the Swan

Properties

Deneb is a blue-white supergiant of spectral type A2 Ia. It is one of the most luminous stars known: estimates of its luminosity range around 200,000 times that of the Sun, with a radius roughly 200 times solar. Its distance is uncertain but on the order of 2,600 light-years. Deneb loses mass through a powerful stellar wind and is expected to evolve into a red supergiant and eventually explode as a supernova.

Observation

With an apparent magnitude of about 1.25, Deneb is the 19th-brightest star in the night sky and the brightest star in Cygnus. Together with Vega and Altair it forms the Summer Triangle, prominent in northern summer skies. Because of precession, Deneb was the pole star around 9800 BC and will be again around AD 23600.

In culture

In Chinese astronomy, Deneb forms part of the "Magpie Bridge" across the Milky Way in the legend of the Cowherd and the Weaver Girl. The star also appears frequently in science fiction, and its name is reflected in the class of Denebian entities in various fictional universes.
