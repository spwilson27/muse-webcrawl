The University of Twente (Dutch: Universiteit Twente; UT) is a public technical university located in Enschede, Netherlands. Founded in 1961 as the Technische Hogeschool Twente, it was the third technical university established in the Netherlands, after Delft and Eindhoven. It styles itself "the entrepreneurial university" and is known for combining engineering and technology with social sciences and behavioural research.

History

The Dutch government decided in the late 1950s to establish a third technical university in the east of the country to support the Twente region, then in decline as a textile-manufacturing centre. The university opened in 1964 on the Drienerlo estate, becoming the first Dutch university with an American-style residential campus, where students and staff live, study and work on one site.

Campus

The 146-hectare campus between Enschede and Hengelo consists of parkland with faculty buildings, student housing, sports and cultural facilities, and the Kennispark Twente business park. Landmark buildings include the Cubicus, the Horst tower and the Langezijds building.

Education and research

Teaching is organised into five faculties: Engineering Technology; Electrical Engineering, Mathematics and Computer Science; Science and Technology; Behavioural, Management and Social Sciences; and Geo-Information Science and Earth Observation (ITC). Research institutes include the MESA+ Institute for Nanotechnology and the TechMed Centre. The university has generated more than a thousand spin-off companies, and Booking.com was founded by alumni of the university.

Student life

Most students live on campus or in Enschede. The Student Union manages student facilities, and numerous study and student associations organise activities, including the annual Batavierenrace relay run from Nijmegen to Enschede, one of the largest student relay races in the world.
