Dannebrog

NameDannebrog ("Danish cloth")
DesignRed field with a white Scandinavian cross extending to the edges, shifted toward the hoist
Proportion28:37 (commonly cited)
StatusNational flag of Denmark; oldest continuously used national flag in the world

The Dannebrog is the national flag of Denmark: a red field charged with a white Nordic cross whose vertical arm is shifted toward the hoist. It is the oldest national flag in continuous use, and its design was adopted by the other Nordic countries (Norway, Sweden, Finland, and Iceland) for their own flags.

Legend and history

According to tradition, the flag fell miraculously from the sky during the Battle of Lyndanisse (Tallinn) on 15 June 1219, turning the tide for King Valdemar II's crusading army in Estonia. Historically, a banner with the white cross on red appears in sources from the 14th century and was in use as a Danish war banner in the late medieval period; its use as a merchant and eventually national flag spread over the following centuries, with private use formally permitted in the 19th century.

Use

The Dannebrog is flown from public buildings on official flag days and is ubiquitous at birthdays, celebrations, and sporting events. A swallow-tailed variant serves as the state and naval flag.
