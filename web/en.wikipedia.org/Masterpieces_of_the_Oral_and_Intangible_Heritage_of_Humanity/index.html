The Masterpieces of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity was a programme of UNESCO that recognised outstanding examples of intangible cultural heritage, such as oral traditions, performing arts, rituals, and craftsmanship, through proclamations in 2001, 2003, and 2005.

History

Ninety elements from seventy countries were proclaimed Masterpieces. In 2008 the programme was superseded by the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity under the 2003 Convention for the Safeguarding of the Intangible Cultural Heritage.

Examples

Recognised masterpieces included the Kunqu opera of China, the Samba de Roda of Brazil, and the polyphonic singing of the Aka people of Central Africa.
