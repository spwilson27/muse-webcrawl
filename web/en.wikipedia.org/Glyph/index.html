Glyph

A glyph is any kind of purposeful mark — a single written character, a carved symbol, or an individual graphic unit in a writing system or notation. In typography, a glyph is the specific graphical representation, or shape, of a character: one abstract character may be drawn with several different glyphs (for example, the letter "a" in its single-storey and double-storey forms), and conversely one glyph, such as a ligature, may represent several characters.

Uses of the term

The word comes from the Greek glyphe, "carving", and was historically applied to carved symbols such as Egyptian hieroglyphs and the Maya glyphs of Mesoamerican writing. In archaeology and epigraphy, a glyph is a single carved or inscribed sign. In computing and font technology, the distinction between character (an abstract unit of meaning, such as a Unicode code point) and glyph (the concrete shape stored in a font) is fundamental: fonts are collections of glyphs, and text layout software selects and positions glyphs to render characters.

Glyphs and related concepts

A grapheme is the smallest functional unit of a writing system, while a glyph is its physical realisation; several glyphs representing the same grapheme are called allographs. In music notation, cartography and technical drawing, glyphs similarly denote the individual symbols placed on a page or screen.
