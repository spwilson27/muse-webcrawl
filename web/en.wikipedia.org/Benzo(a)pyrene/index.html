Benzo[a]pyrene

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Benzo[a]pyrene, or BaP, is a polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) with the formula C20H12. It is a pentacyclic hydrocarbon formed by the fusion of a benzene ring to pyrene. It is found in coal tar, tobacco smoke and many foods, especially grilled meats, and is the product of incomplete combustion of organic matter at temperatures between 300 °C and 600 °C.

Carcinogenicity

Benzo[a]pyrene is one of the most potent carcinogens among the PAHs and is classified by the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) as a Group 1 carcinogen, carcinogenic to humans. In the body it is metabolized by cytochrome P450 enzymes to the diol epoxide BPDE, which binds covalently to DNA, forming adducts that can cause mutations. The compound has long been used experimentally to induce tumors in laboratory animals, and historical studies of chimney sweeps' cancers were among the first to link PAHs to cancer.

Occurrence

BaP is formed in vehicle exhaust, wood smoke, cigarette smoke, and charred and smoked foods. Regulatory limits exist for its concentration in drinking water and foodstuffs in many jurisdictions; it is commonly used as a marker for the total PAH content of a sample.
