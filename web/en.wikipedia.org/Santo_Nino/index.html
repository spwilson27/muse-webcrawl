Santo Niño

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Santo Niño (Spanish for "Holy Child") is a Roman Catholic title for the child Jesus and the name of famous devotional images of him, above all the Santo Niño de Cebú in the Philippines. The image — a wooden statue of the Christ Child dressed in royal robes, holding a globus cruciger and sceptre — is considered the oldest Christian relic in the Philippines and is the centre of one of the country's largest devotions.

History

According to tradition, the statue was given by Ferdinand Magellan in 1521 to Rajah Humabon and his wife of Cebu on their baptism. After Magellan's death it disappeared, and was found in 1565 by a soldier of Miguel López de Legazpi's expedition in a partially burned nipa house; its rediscovery was taken as a founding sign of Spanish Christianity in the islands. The image is enshrined in the Basílica Minore del Santo Niño in Cebu City, founded on the spot where it was found.

Devotion and festivals

The feast of the Santo Niño is celebrated on the third Sunday of January. In Cebu it culminates in the Sinulog festival, a massive procession and street celebration; related Ati-Atihan festivals are held in Kalibo, Aklan, and elsewhere. Other famous Santo Niño images include the Santo Niño de Tondo, the Santo Niño de Pandacan, and, outside the Philippines, the Santo Niño de Atocha in Spain and Mexico and the Infant Jesus of Prague in the Czech Republic.
