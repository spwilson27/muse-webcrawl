South Australia (commonly abbreviated as SA) is a state in the southern central part of Australia. It covers some of the most arid parts of the country, while its south-east corner, where most of the population lives, enjoys a Mediterranean climate along the coast of the Southern Ocean. The state is known for its wine regions — the Barossa Valley, McLaren Vale, Clare Valley and Coonawarra — its opal-mining towns, and the natural wonders of Kangaroo Island and the Flinders Ranges.

History

Aboriginal Australians have lived in the region for tens of thousands of years. The first European sighting of the coast was by the Dutch ship Gulden Zeepaert in 1627, and Matthew Flinders and Nicolas Baudin charted the coastline in 1802. Uniquely among Australian colonies, South Australia was founded in 1836 as a planned settlement of free colonists under Edward Gibbon Wakefield's scheme of systematic colonisation, rather than as a convict colony. German Lutheran settlers established towns such as Hahndorf and shaped the Barossa. The state federated with the other colonies as part of the Commonwealth of Australia in 1901.

Geography

South Australia borders all other mainland states. Its north is dominated by arid deserts — the Great Victoria Desert in the west and the Simpson Desert towards Queensland — while the far north-east corner around Innamincka is known for the Channel Country. The Mount Lofty Ranges and Flinders Ranges run north from Adelaide. The River Murray flows through the state to its mouth near Goolwa. The Nullarbor Plain stretches along the Great Australian Bight. Notable islands include Kangaroo Island, Australia's third-largest island.

Climate

The southern parts of the state have a Mediterranean climate with hot, dry summers and mild, wetter winters. The interior is arid to semi-arid; the far north experiences extreme summer heat, with Oodnadatta recording one of Australia's highest temperatures. Most rainfall falls in the winter months in the south.

Economy

Key industries include agriculture (wheat, barley, wine grapes), mining (copper and uranium at Olympic Dam, opals at Coober Pedy), manufacturing, defence and shipbuilding at Osborne, health care, and education. The state has positioned itself as a leader in renewable energy, generating a large share of its electricity from wind and solar, supported by large-scale battery storage.

Government

South Australia is a constitutional monarchy with a parliamentary system: a bicameral Parliament (House of Assembly and Legislative Council) sitting in Adelaide, headed by a Premier, with a Governor representing the Crown. Local government areas cover the settled parts of the state; large outback areas are administered directly.
