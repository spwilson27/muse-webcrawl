The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn (Latin: Ordo Hermeticus Aurorae Aureae) was a secret society devoted to the study and practice of ceremonial magic, theurgy and spiritual development, founded in London in 1888. It is widely considered the most influential organisation in the modern Western occult revival, systematising teachings drawn from Hermeticism, Kabbalah, alchemy, astrology, tarot, Egyptian religion and Rosicrucianism into a graded initiatory curriculum.

Founding

The order was founded by three Freemasons — William Wynn Westcott, Samuel Liddell MacGregor Mathers and William Robert Woodman — who claimed authority from the "Cipher Manuscripts", a set of encoded documents outlining the grade rituals, and from correspondence with a supposed German adept, Anna Sprengel. Historians regard the Sprengel letters as fabrications by Westcott, but the manuscripts produced a genuine and elaborate system. The first temple, Isis-Urania in London, attracted a remarkable membership of artists, intellectuals and clergy.

Structure and teaching

Members progressed through grades mapped to the Kabbalistic Tree of Life and the classical elements: Neophyte, Zelator, Theoricus, Practicus and Philosophus formed the Outer Order; adepts of the Second Order (the Rosae Rubeae et Aureae Crucis) studied practical magic, including skrying, consecration of talismans and Enochian magic derived from John Dee. Distinctive practices included the Lesser Banishing Ritual of the Pentagram, still widely used in modern occultism, and a synthesis of tarot attributions that shaped all later Western tarot tradition, including the Rider–Waite deck designed by former member A. E. Waite.

Schism and decline

Around 1900 the order fractured amid conflicts over Mathers's autocratic leadership, a revolt of the London adepts, scandal, and the brief, notorious membership of Aleister Crowley. Successor groups — including the Stella Matutina, Waite's Fellowship of the Rosy Cross, and later revivals — carried its system forward. W. B. Yeats, the order's most famous literary member, remained involved for decades.

Legacy

Israel Regardie's publication of the order's complete rituals in the 1930s made its system publicly available, seeding modern Wicca-adjacent ritual practice, Thelema, chaos magic and much of contemporary Western esotericism.
