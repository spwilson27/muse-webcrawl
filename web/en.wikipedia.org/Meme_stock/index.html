Meme stock

A meme stock is a listed company share that gains popularity among retail investors through the internet and social media platforms. Interest in such stocks is typically driven by online communities rather than by fundamentals, producing sharp increases in price and trading volume, followed by high volatility. The phenomenon became prominent in early 2021 with the GameStop short squeeze.

GameStop short squeeze

In January 2021, users of the Reddit forum r/wallstreetbets coordinated mass purchases of shares and call options in GameStop (GME), a video-game retailer that had been heavily shorted by hedge funds. The buying drove the price from under US$20 to an intraday peak of over US$480, inflicting heavy losses on short sellers such as Melvin Capital and prompting some brokers, including Robinhood, to controversially restrict buying of the stock. AMC Entertainment, BlackBerry, Nokia, Bed Bath & Beyond and others experienced related surges and were widely labelled meme stocks.

Characteristics and criticism

Meme stocks are characterised by prices detached from earnings or assets, extremely high retail order flow, and communities sustained by internet memes and a David-versus-Goliath narrative about institutional investors. Regulators including the US Securities and Exchange Commission examined the 2021 events, and the episode led to congressional hearings and films and books about the squeeze. Later waves of speculation affected companies such as AMC and, in subsequent years, other heavily shorted names.
