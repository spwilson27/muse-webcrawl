Avalokiteśvara

SanskritAvalokiteśvara
ChineseGuānyīn (觀音)
JapaneseKannon
TibetanChenrezig
AttributeCompassion (karuṇā)

Avalokiteśvara is a bodhisattva in Mahayana Buddhism who embodies the compassion of all Buddhas and is regarded as one of the most widely revered bodhisattvas in mainstream Mahayana Buddhism. The name is commonly interpreted as "the lord who looks down (in compassion)", or "one who hears the cries of the world".

Forms and regional manifestations

In China, Avalokiteśvara became the goddess of mercy Guanyin, frequently depicted in female form; as Kannon in Japan and Chenrezig in Tibet, the bodhisattva may appear in many forms, including the thousand-armed and eleven-headed forms symbolizing the ability to perceive and relieve suffering everywhere. The Dalai Lamas are considered manifestations of Avalokiteśvara in Tibetan Buddhism.

Sutras

The bodhisattva is prominent in the Lotus Sutra, whose chapter on the "Universal Gate" describes calling on Avalokiteśvara's name in times of peril, and in the Heart Sutra, which is framed as Avalokiteśvara's teaching on emptiness. The famous mantra Oṃ maṇi padme hūṃ is associated with Avalokiteśvara.
