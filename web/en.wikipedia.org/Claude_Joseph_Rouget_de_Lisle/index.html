Claude Joseph Rouget de Lisle

Claude Joseph Rouget de Lisle (10 May 1760 – 26 June 1836) was a French army officer of the Revolutionary Wars who is best known as the author of the words and music of "La Marseillaise", which became the national anthem of France. He wrote the song — originally titled "Chant de guerre pour l'Armée du Rhin" ("War Song for the Army of the Rhine") — in a single night in Strasbourg in April 1792.

Life and the Marseillaise

Born in Lons-le-Saunier in the Jura, Rouget de Lisle entered the army as a military engineer and was posted to Strasbourg, where he frequented the house of the mayor, Philippe-Frédéric de Dietrich. Learning that France had declared war on Austria, Dietrich urged him to write a marching song for the Army of the Rhine; Rouget composed the words and music overnight on 25–26 April 1792. The song spread to Paris with volunteer battalions from Marseille — hence its name — was sung at the storming of the Tuileries, and was adopted as the national anthem by the Convention in 1795.

A moderate revolutionary, Rouget de Lisle refused to take the oath against the monarchy and was imprisoned during the Terror, escaping the guillotine only by Robespierre's fall. He served briefly in the Vendée, resigned his commission in 1796, and lived thereafter in straitened circumstances, writing songs, essays and an opera libretto (Macbeth, with music by Chelard) that failed to repeat his early success. Under the July Monarchy he received a small pension; he died at Choisy-le-Roi in 1836. In 1915 his remains were transferred to Les Invalides in Paris.

Legacy

"La Marseillaise" remains France's national anthem (with interruptions under Napoleon, the Restoration and Vichy) and one of the world's most recognisable patriotic songs. Rouget de Lisle's authorship of both words and music, occasionally disputed in the 19th century, has been confirmed by modern scholarship, though the melody may incorporate earlier musical material then in common circulation.
