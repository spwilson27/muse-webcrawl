Southern Dobruja

Southern Dobruja
Bulgarian: Yuzhna Dobrudzha; Romanian: Dobrogea de Sud / Cadrilater
Country: Bulgaria (Dobrich and Silistra provinces)
Largest towns: Silistra, Dobrich (partly)
Area: about 7,500 km²

Southern Dobruja, historically known in Romania as the Cadrilater ("Quadrilateral"), is the southern part of the Dobruja region, lying between the Danube River and the Black Sea in north-eastern Bulgaria. It comprises most of Bulgaria's Dobrich and Silistra provinces and is one of the country's principal grain-producing areas, a fertile steppe plain sometimes called Bulgaria's granary.

History

The region passed from the medieval Bulgarian and Byzantine empires to Ottoman rule, and in 1878 its northern boundary was set so that Southern Dobruja became Bulgarian. Romania annexed it after victory in the Second Balkan War (Treaty of Bucharest, 1913) and administered it until 1940, when the Treaty of Craiova — reached under Axis pressure — returned it to Bulgaria. The settlement, which involved a population exchange between Bulgarians and Romanians, was confirmed by the Paris Peace Treaties of 1947 and is unusual among the territorial changes of the era in remaining undisputed by both countries.

Population

Today the population is predominantly ethnic Bulgarian, with significant Turkish and Roma minorities. Silistra, on the Danube, is the main river town; other centres include Tutrakan, Dulovo and General Toshevo.
