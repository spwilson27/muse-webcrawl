Tomoyuki Yamashita (8 November 1885 – 23 February 1946) was a Japanese general of the Imperial Japanese Army during the Second World War, best known for conquering Malaya and Singapore in 70 days in 1941–42, an achievement that earned him the nickname "the Tiger of Malaya".

The Malaya campaign

Commanding the Twenty-Fifth Army, Yamashita landed in northern Malaya in December 1941 with some 70,000 troops, using bicycle infantry and light tanks to race down the peninsula against a larger British-led force. At Singapore in February 1942 he bluffed Lieutenant-General Arthur Percival — whose garrison of about 85,000 outnumbered Yamashita's exhausted, nearly out-of-ammunition force — into surrendering, in what Churchill called the worst disaster in British military history. Yamashita's demand at the Ford Factory negotiations was a famous brusque "yes or no".

Philippines, trial and execution

Sidelined in Manchuria by Prime Minister Tojo, Yamashita was recalled in October 1944 to command the Fourteenth Area Army defending the Philippines. His troops committed extensive atrocities, including the Manila massacre of February 1945 by naval forces nominally under his command, though Yamashita maintained he had ordered Manila evacuated and knew nothing of the killings. After Japan's surrender he was tried by a U.S. military commission in Manila on the theory that he had unlawfully failed to control his troops — the "Yamashita standard" of command responsibility — convicted, and hanged on 23 February 1946. The case remains a foundational and controversial precedent in the law of war. Persistent legends of "Yamashita's gold", war loot supposedly buried in the Philippines, have never been substantiated.
