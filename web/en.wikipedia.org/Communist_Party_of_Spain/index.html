Communist Party of Spain

Founded: 1921
Headquarters: Madrid, Spain
Ideology: Communism, Marxism
International affiliation: International Meeting of Communist and Workers' Parties

The Communist Party of Spain (Spanish: Partido Comunista de España, PCE) is a communist party in Spain. Founded in 1921 through the merger of two smaller communist groups, it became a significant force during the Second Spanish Republic and the Spanish Civil War (1936–1939), when it grew rapidly and played a leading role in the Republican war effort.

History

Under Francisco Franco's dictatorship the PCE was banned and driven into exile and clandestinity, becoming the principal organised opposition to the regime. Its leader Santiago Carrillo steered the party toward Eurocommunism in the 1970s, distancing it from the Soviet Union. The party was legalised in April 1977, weeks before Spain's first democratic elections in four decades, in which it won just over 9 per cent of the vote.

Electoral decline in the 1980s led the PCE to co-found the United Left (Izquierda Unida, IU) coalition in 1986, within which it remains the largest component. The party's youth organisation is the Union of Communist Youth of Spain (UJCE), and it publishes the newspaper Mundo Obrero.

Ideology

The PCE advocates a democratic and federal republic, public ownership of strategic sectors, and workers' rights, describing its goal as a socialist and ultimately communist society achieved through democratic means.
