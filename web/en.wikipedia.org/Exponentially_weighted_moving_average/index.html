Exponentially weighted moving average

Weights assigned to past observations decay exponentially with age.

The exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA), also called the exponential moving average (EMA), is a statistic and filtering technique that averages a time series with weights that decrease exponentially for older observations, so recent data influence the average most. It is computed recursively: the new average equals a fraction α of the latest observation plus (1 − α) times the previous average, where the smoothing factor α lies between 0 and 1.

Applications

EWMA is used in statistical process control, where EWMA control charts detect small sustained shifts in a process mean more quickly than Shewhart charts; in finance, where the EMA is a standard technical-analysis overlay and forms part of indicators such as the MACD; and in engineering and computing, for example in estimating network round-trip times and in exponentially decaying load averages. It is closely related to exponential smoothing in forecasting and to the first-order low-pass filter in signal processing.

Choice of smoothing factor

Larger α values track recent changes quickly but retain more noise; smaller α values give a smoother but slower series. A common convention matches α to a span N of observations via α = 2/(N + 1).
