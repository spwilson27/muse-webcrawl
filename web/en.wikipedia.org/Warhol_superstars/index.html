Warhol superstars

Warhol superstars were a clique of New York City personalities promoted by the pop artist Andy Warhol during the 1960s and early 1970s. These personalities appeared in Warhol's artworks and accompanied him in his social life, epitomizing his famous dictum that "in the future everyone will be famous for fifteen minutes." They were the subject of his underground films at his studio, the Factory, and created a prototype of modern celebrity manufactured around image rather than accomplishment.

Notable superstars

The best-known superstars included Edie Sedgwick, the wealthy socialite who became Warhol's muse in 1965; Nico, the German singer of the Velvet Underground; Ultra Violet (Isabelle Collin Dufresne); Viva (Janet Susan Mary Hoffmann); Joe Dallesandro, star of Flesh, Trash and Heat; the transgender actresses Candy Darling, Holly Woodlawn and Jackie Curtis; and figures such as Ondine, Gerard Malanga, Mary Woronov, and Taylor Mead. Singer-songwriter Lou Reed chronicled the scene in songs such as "Walk on the Wild Side."

Decline

The superstar era waned after Valerie Solanas shot Warhol in 1968, after which the Factory became more guarded and businesslike. Many superstars struggled with drug addiction and poverty once Warhol's attention moved on; several died young, though others, such as Dallesandro and Woodlawn, went on to lasting careers and memoirs.
