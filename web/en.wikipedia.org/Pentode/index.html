Pentode

A pentode is an electronic amplifying vacuum tube with five active electrodes: a cathode, a control grid, a screen grid, a suppressor grid, and an anode (plate).

Operation

The pentode was developed in the 1920s (notably by Gilles Holst and Bernard Tellegen at Philips) to improve on the tetrode, whose secondary emission from the plate caused a kink in its characteristic curve. The suppressor grid, held at cathode potential, returns secondary electrons to the plate, eliminating this distortion and yielding high gain with a high plate resistance.

Applications

Pentodes such as the EL34, EL84, 6L6 family variants, and EF86 were widely used in radio receivers and audio amplifiers, including many classic guitar amplifiers, and remain in production for audio use.
