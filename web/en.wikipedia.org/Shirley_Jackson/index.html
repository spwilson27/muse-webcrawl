Shirley Jackson

Shirley Hardie Jackson (December 14, 1916 – August 8, 1965) was an American writer known primarily for her works of horror and mystery. Over a career spanning two decades she wrote six novels, two memoirs and more than 200 short stories. She achieved fame with the short story "The Lottery" (1948), a chilling depiction of a ritual stoning in a small American village, which provoked the largest volume of reader mail in The New Yorker's history to that date. Her novels The Haunting of Hill House (1959) and We Have Always Lived in the Castle (1962) are now regarded as classics of literary horror, and she is widely credited as a foundational influence on modern horror writers including Stephen King and Neil Gaiman.

Life

Jackson was born in San Francisco and raised in Burlingame, California. She attended the University of Rochester and Syracuse University, where she met her husband, the critic Stanley Edgar Hyman, and helped found the literary magazine Spectre. The couple settled in North Bennington, Vermont, where Hyman taught at Bennington College; Jackson wrote while raising four children, mining domestic life for her comic memoirs Life Among the Savages (1953) and Raising Demons (1957) as well as for her fiction's darker studies of village conformity and female isolation. Plagued by ill health and agoraphobia in later years, she died of heart failure in her sleep at 48.

Works

Her novels — The Road Through the Wall (1948), Hangsaman (1951), The Bird's Nest (1954), The Sundial (1958), The Haunting of Hill House and We Have Always Lived in the Castle — explore psychologically fragile protagonists, hostile communities and haunted houses, real or imagined. "The Lottery" remains one of the most anthologised American short stories, and is routinely taught in schools. Her story collections include The Lottery and Other Stories (1949) and the posthumous Come Along with Me (1968) and Let Me Tell You (2015).

Legacy

The annual Shirley Jackson Awards, established in 2007, honour outstanding achievement in horror, dark fantasy and psychological suspense. The Haunting of Hill House has been adapted for film (1963, 1999) and as a Netflix series (2018); We Have Always Lived in the Castle was filmed in 2018. Jackson's reputation, modest beside her husband's in her lifetime, has grown steadily, and she is now recognised as one of the most important American writers of the 20th century.
