Winnipeg is the capital and largest city of the Canadian province of Manitoba. It stands at the confluence of the Red and Assiniboine rivers, near the longitudinal centre of North America. The name derives from the Cree words for "muddy waters", referring to nearby Lake Winnipeg.

History

The Forks area was a trading and meeting place for Indigenous peoples for thousands of years. French traders arrived in the eighteenth century, and Fort Rouge was built in 1738. The Selkirk settlers founded the Red River Colony in 1812; after the Métis resistance led by Louis Riel in 1869–70, Manitoba entered Confederation with Winnipeg as its capital. The arrival of the Canadian Pacific Railway in the 1880s made the city a grain and transport hub, and the 1919 Winnipeg general strike became a landmark in Canadian labour history.

Geography and climate

Winnipeg lies on the flat Red River flood plain and has an extreme continental climate with very cold winters — earning it the nickname "Winterpeg" — and warm summers. The Red River Floodway, built after the devastating 1950 flood, protects the city from inundation.

Culture and economy

The city is home to the Royal Winnipeg Ballet, the Winnipeg Symphony Orchestra, the Canadian Museum for Human Rights, and the NHL's Winnipeg Jets. Major employers include government, finance, manufacturing, and health care, and the city hosts one of the largest Francophone communities in western Canada.
