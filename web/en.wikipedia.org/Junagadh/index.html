Junagadh

Junagadh is a city in the western Indian state of Gujarat, located at the foot of the Girnar hills. One of the oldest cities in the region, it was the capital of the former princely state of Junagadh and is today the headquarters of Junagadh district.

History

Junagadh's antiquity is attested by the rock edicts of the Mauryan emperor Ashoka (3rd century BCE) inscribed near the city, beside which the Western Kshatrapa ruler Rudradaman I added a celebrated Sanskrit inscription (c. 150 CE) recording the repair of the Sudarshana lake dam — one of the earliest long Sanskrit inscriptions. The city passed through Gupta, Maitraka, Chudasama, Mughal, and Babi Nawab rule. At the partition of India in 1947, the Muslim Nawab acceded to Pakistan while his largely Hindu subjects favored India; after a plebiscite in 1948, Junagadh joined India.

Places of interest

The city's landmarks include Uparkot Fort, the Mahabat Maqbara palace complex with its Indo-Islamic and Gothic ornamentation, the Buddhist caves of Khapra Kodiya, and the sacred Girnar mountain, an important pilgrimage site for Hindus and Jains, climbed by thousands of steps to its temples. The nearby Gir National Park is the last refuge of the Asiatic lion.
