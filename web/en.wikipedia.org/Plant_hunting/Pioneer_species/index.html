Pioneer species

Pioneer species are the first organisms to colonise a previously disrupted or newly formed habitat, initiating ecological succession. They are typically r-selected species with rapid growth, abundant wind-dispersed propagules and tolerance of low nutrients, drought and temperature extremes.

Examples

Lichens and cyanobacteria colonise bare rock; grasses, fireweed and birch follow fires and floods; and nitrogen-fixing plants such as alders prepare ground for forests. In marine settings, pioneer species include certain algae and barnacles on new shore surfaces.

Ecological role

By breaking down rock, accumulating organic matter and providing shade and shelter, pioneers facilitate the establishment of more demanding species, and are themselves eventually outcompeted as conditions change.
