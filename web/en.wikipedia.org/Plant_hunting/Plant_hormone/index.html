Plant hormone

Plant hormones (phytohormones) are naturally occurring organic substances that influence plant growth and development at low concentrations. Unlike animal hormones they are not produced in specialised glands; each cell is capable of producing them, and they may act locally or be transported through the plant.

Main groups

Auxins control cell elongation, apical dominance and root formation; gibberellins promote stem elongation, seed germination and flowering; cytokinins stimulate cell division and delay leaf senescence; abscisic acid closes stomata and maintains seed dormancy; and ethylene regulates fruit ripening, leaf abscission and responses to stress.

Discovery

The first plant hormone studied was auxin, following experiments by Charles and Francis Darwin on phototropism and the isolation work of Frits Went in the 1920s.
