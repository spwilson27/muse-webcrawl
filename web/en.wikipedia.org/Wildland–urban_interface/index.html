Wildland–urban interface

The wildland–urban interface (WUI) is the zone where human development — houses, infrastructure and communities — meets or intermingles with undeveloped wildland vegetation. It is the area where wildfire risk to people and property is greatest, and the term is widely used in fire management, particularly in the United States, Canada and Australia.

Types

Researchers distinguish the interface, where development borders wildland in a fairly clear line; the intermix, where houses are scattered within continuous vegetation; and occluded areas, where small islands of wildland sit within a city. In the United States the WUI covers roughly 9% of land area but contains about a third of all housing, and it is growing quickly as people move to amenity-rich rural areas.

Fire risk and mitigation

WUI fires have caused some of the most destructive wildfire disasters, including the 2018 Camp Fire in California, which destroyed the town of Paradise. Mitigation strategies include creating defensible space around buildings, using fire-resistant roofing and vents, fuel-reduction treatments, evacuation planning, and building codes and zoning that account for fire danger. Climate change, which lengthens fire seasons and intensifies drought, compounds the risk.
