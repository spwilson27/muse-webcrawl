Sweeney Todd

Sweeney Todd is a fictional character who first appeared as the villain of the Victorian penny dreadful serial The String of Pearls (1846–47), subtitled with his enduring epithet, "the Demon Barber of Fleet Street". Todd is a barber in Fleet Street, London, who murders his customers with a straight razor and disposes of the bodies with the help of his accomplice, Mrs. Lovett, who bakes the victims into meat pies sold in her pie shop. Although often retold as if historical, no evidence connects the character to a real person.

Development

The String of Pearls was attributed over the years to various hack writers, modern scholarship favouring James Malcolm Rymer and/or Thomas Peckett Prest. The story was adapted for the stage almost immediately, beginning with George Dibdin Pitt's melodrama of 1847, and Todd became a fixture of Victorian popular theatre and later of Tod Slaughter's barnstorming performances and 1936 film.

Modern adaptations

Christopher Bond's 1973 play gave Todd a motive — revenge on the judge who wronged him — and formed the basis of Stephen Sondheim and Hugh Wheeler's musical Sweeney Todd: The Demon Barber of Fleet Street (1979), which won the Tony Award for Best Musical and is considered one of Sondheim's masterpieces. Tim Burton directed a film adaptation of the musical in 2007 starring Johnny Depp and Helena Bonham Carter. The tale continues to be retold in theatre, television and popular culture as a dark fable of revenge corrupted into madness.
