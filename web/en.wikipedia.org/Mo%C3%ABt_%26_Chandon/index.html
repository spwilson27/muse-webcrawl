Moët & Chandon is a French champagne house founded in 1743 by wine trader Claude Moët in Épernay. It is one of the world's largest champagne producers and the flagship house of the Moët Hennessy division of the luxury group LVMH. Its best-known cuvées include the non-vintage Moët Impérial and the prestige vintage Dom Pérignon, named after the Benedictine monk traditionally credited with advances in champagne making.

History

Claude Moët began shipping wines from Champagne to Paris and supplied the court of Louis XV; his grandson Jean-Rémy Moët expanded the house, entertaining Napoleon Bonaparte, whose visits are commemorated by the "Impérial" name. In the nineteenth century the firm became Moët & Chandon after Pierre-Gabriel Chandon de Briailles joined the family. In 1971 the house merged with Hennessy cognac, and in 1987 with Louis Vuitton to form LVMH.

Vineyards and production

The house owns extensive vineyards across the Champagne region and supplements them with purchased grapes. Its cellars in Épernay extend for some 28 kilometres beneath the Avenue de Champagne. Moët & Chandon produces tens of millions of bottles annually, more than any other champagne house.

Brands

Besides Moët Impérial and Dom Pérignon (marketed as a separate brand), the house produces the Grand Vintage series and the Ice Impérial style designed to be served over ice. The house also has sparkling wine operations outside France, including Domaine Chandon in California, Argentina, Brazil, Australia, India, and China.
