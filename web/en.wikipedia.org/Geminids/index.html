Geminids

Parent body3200 Phaethon (asteroid)
RadiantGemini, near Castor
Occurs4–17 December; peak night of 13–14 December
Peak rate (ZHR)~120–150 per hour
Velocity~35 km/s

The Geminids are a prolific meteor shower caused by the object 3200 Phaethon, an asteroid with a comet-like orbit — sometimes called a "rock comet". The shower peaks on the night of 13–14 December and is considered, together with the Perseids, one of the best and most reliable annual displays, with rates that have increased over recent decades.

Characteristics

Geminid meteors are relatively slow (about 35 km/s) and often bright and colourful, and because the radiant, in the constellation Gemini, rises in the early evening, the shower can be observed for much of the night. The meteors are denser than typical cometary debris, so they penetrate deeper into the atmosphere.

Origin

Unusually for a major shower, the Geminids' parent is an asteroid rather than a comet. Phaethon follows an eccentric 1.4-year orbit that brings it closer to the Sun than any other named asteroid; heating and thermal fracturing near perihelion are thought to shed the dust that forms the stream. The shower was first noticed in the 1860s and has strengthened steadily since.
