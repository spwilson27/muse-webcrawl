Cava (Spanish wine)

Cava is a sparkling wine from Spain produced by the traditional method, with a second fermentation in the bottle. It holds Denominación de Origen (DO) status and is made predominantly in the Penedès region of Catalonia, especially around the town of Sant Sadurní d'Anoia, though the DO also covers areas in several other Spanish regions. The word cava means "cellar" in Catalan.

Production and grapes

Cava is principally made from the indigenous white grapes Macabeo, Xarel·lo and Parellada, with Chardonnay and Pinot Noir (the latter also for rosé, cava rosado) permitted. Wines must age on their lees for a minimum of nine months; categories include Cava de Guarda (minimum 9 months), Reserva (18 months), Gran Reserva (30 months, brut styles only) and Cava de Paraje Calificado, from a single qualified site with 36 months' ageing. Sweetness levels run from Brut Nature to Dulce.

History

Sparkling wine production in Penedès began in the nineteenth century; Josep Raventós of Codorníu produced the first bottles in 1872 after visiting Champagne, and the industry expanded as phylloxera forced replanting. The name "cava" was adopted in the 1970s to replace "Spanish Champagne," and the DO was formalised in the 1980s. In recent years many leading producers have left the DO to market under the Corpinnat collective brand, while the DO has tightened its rules to emphasise origin and ageing.
