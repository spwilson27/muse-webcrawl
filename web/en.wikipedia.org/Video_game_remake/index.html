Video game remake

A video game remake is a video game that is based on an earlier title, usually for the purpose of modernizing a game for newer hardware and audiences. A remake typically rebuilds the game's assets from scratch in a new engine, and may change gameplay, story elements and presentation to varying degrees. It differs from a remaster, which upgrades the original game's visuals and performance while retaining its underlying code and assets, and from a port, which adapts a game to another platform.

Notable examples

Well-known remakes include Resident Evil (2002) for the GameCube, Pokémon FireRed and LeafGreen, Final Fantasy VII Remake (2020), which substantially reworked the original's story and combat, and The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening (2019). Remakes became increasingly prominent in the late 2010s and 2020s as publishers revisited classic catalogues.
