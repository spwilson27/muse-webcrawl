Teletraffic engineering

[Placeholder image: Teletraffic_engineering.png]

Teletraffic engineering, or telecommunications traffic engineering, is the application of traffic engineering theory to telecommunications networks. Teletraffic engineers use their knowledge of statistics including queuing theory, the nature of traffic, their practical models, their measurements and simulations, to make predictions and to plan telecommunication networks such as a telephone network or the Internet.

History

The field was founded by the Danish mathematician Agner Krarup Erlang, who published foundational work on telephone traffic in 1909 and formulated the Erlang unit and Erlang distributions, which remain central to dimensioning circuit-switched networks.

Methods

Key tools include the Erlang B and Erlang C formulas, Poisson traffic models, and discrete event simulation, used to determine the number of trunks or amount of bandwidth needed to meet a target grade of service.
