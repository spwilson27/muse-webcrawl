William Kennedy Dickson

William Kennedy Laurie Dickson (3 August 1860 – 28 September 1935) was a Scottish inventor who devised an early motion picture camera under the employment of Thomas Edison, and is often credited with the practical invention of the motion picture camera and viewer.

Work at Edison's laboratory

Dickson joined Edison's laboratory at Menlo Park in 1883. Tasked in 1888 with developing a device that would "do for the eye what the phonograph does for the ear," he and his assistant William Heise developed the Kinetograph camera and Kinetoscope peephole viewer, using Eastman celluloid film cut into 35 mm strips with four pairs of sprocket holes per frame — a format that became the industry standard for a century. At the purpose-built "Black Maria" studio in West Orange, New Jersey, Dickson directed hundreds of early films, including Fred Ott's Sneeze (1894), the first motion picture to receive a U.S. copyright.

Later career

After leaving Edison in 1895 amid credit disputes, Dickson co-founded the American Mutoscope Company (later Biograph), whose Mutoscope and large-format films competed with Edison's. He later returned to Britain, spent years in South Africa filming the Boer War, and died in Twickenham in 1935.
