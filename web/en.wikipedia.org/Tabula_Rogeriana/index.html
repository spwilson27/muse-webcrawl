Full titleNuzhat al-Mushtāq fī Ikhtirāq al-Āfāq ("The Excursion of One Who Is Eager to Traverse the Regions of the World")

The Tabula Rogeriana, formally the Kitāb nuzhat al-mushtāq fī ikhtirāq al-āfāq ("The Book of the Pleasant Journey into Faraway Lands"), is a description of the world and a world map created by the Arab geographer Muhammad al-Idrisi in 1154. Al-Idrisi worked on the accompanying commentaries and illustrations of the map for fifteen years at the court of King Roger II of Sicily in Palermo.

Description

The work is written in Arabic, with the map drawn on a silver planisphere (now lost) and reproduced in manuscripts as a set of seventy rectangular sectional maps accompanied by a small circular world map. Following the Ptolemaic tradition, the inhabited world is divided into seven climates, each subdivided into ten sections. The map is oriented with south at the top, as was customary in Islamic cartography.

Sources and significance

Al-Idrisi combined classical Greek knowledge—above all Ptolemy's Geography—with information gathered from merchants, travellers and envoys questioned at Roger's cosmopolitan court. The result was the most accurate world map of the pre-modern era, and it remained influential for centuries; its sectional maps were still being copied three hundred years later. Surviving manuscript copies are held in libraries including the Bodleian Library and the Bibliothèque nationale de France.
