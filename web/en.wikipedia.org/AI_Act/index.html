Artificial Intelligence Act

The Artificial Intelligence Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) is a European Union regulation on artificial intelligence, and the first comprehensive legal framework on AI enacted by a major regulator. Proposed by the European Commission in April 2021, it was adopted in 2024 and entered into force on 1 August 2024, with most provisions applying in phases over the following years.

Risk-based approach

The Act classifies AI applications into risk categories. Practices deemed to pose an unacceptable risk, such as social scoring by governments and certain forms of manipulative or biometric surveillance, are prohibited. High-risk systems, including those used in critical infrastructure, education, employment, law enforcement, and as safety components of regulated products, are subject to requirements on data quality, documentation, human oversight, and conformity assessment. Limited-risk systems such as chatbots and deepfakes carry transparency obligations, while minimal-risk applications are largely unregulated.

General-purpose AI

The Act also imposes obligations on providers of general-purpose AI models, with additional requirements for models considered to pose systemic risk, commonly assessed by a training compute threshold of 1025 floating-point operations.

Enforcement

Supervision is shared between national market-surveillance authorities and the European AI Office. Fines for the most serious infringements can reach 35 million euros or 7% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher.
