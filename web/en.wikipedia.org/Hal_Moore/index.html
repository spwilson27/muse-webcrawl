Harold Gregory Moore Jr. (February 13, 1922 – February 10, 2017) was a United States Army lieutenant general and author. He was best known as the lieutenant colonel in command of the 1st Battalion, 7th Cavalry Regiment, at the Battle of Ia Drang in 1965, the first large-unit battle of the Vietnam War between American forces and the People's Army of Vietnam.

Early life and career

Born in Bardstown, Kentucky, Moore secured an appointment to West Point after moving to Washington, D.C., and graduated in 1945, too late for combat in the Second World War. He served in occupied Japan, jumped with the 82nd Airborne, and fought in the Korean War as an infantry company commander. He later helped develop the Army's airmobile concept and air-assault tactics at Fort Benning.

Ia Drang

On November 14, 1965, Moore led his battalion into Landing Zone X-Ray in South Vietnam's Ia Drang Valley. Surrounded and heavily outnumbered, his men held the position through three days of close combat, relying on artillery and air support. Moore is remembered for his pledge that he would be the first man on the battlefield and the last to step off, and that he would leave no man behind. He was awarded the Distinguished Service Cross for his actions; the battle was later immortalised in his 1992 book with journalist Joseph L. Galloway, We Were Soldiers Once… and Young, and the 2002 film We Were Soldiers, in which he was portrayed by Mel Gibson.

Later career and writing

Moore went on to command a brigade of the 1st Cavalry Division, the 7th Infantry Division, and served as the Army's deputy chief of staff for personnel, where he worked on rebuilding the post-Vietnam volunteer Army. He retired in 1977. With Galloway he later wrote We Are Soldiers Still (2008), returning to Vietnam and meeting former adversaries. Moore died at his home in Auburn, Alabama, in 2017 and was buried at Fort Benning.
