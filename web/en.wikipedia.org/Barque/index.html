Barque


A barque under sail.

A barque, barc, or bark is a type of sailing vessel with three or more masts having the foremasts rigged square and only the aftermast (mizzen in three-masted barques) rigged fore and aft. Sometimes, the mizzen is only partially fore-and-aft rigged, bearing a square-rigged sail above a gaff sail.

Characteristics

The barque rig combined much of the driving power of the full-rigged ship with reduced crew requirements, because fore-and-aft sails on the mizzen were easier to handle than a square-rigged mizzen. Barques were economical workhorses of 19th-century commerce and became especially common for long ocean passages in the windjammer era, carrying bulk cargoes such as grain, nitrate, and coal. Four- and five-masted steel barques, some exceeding 4,000 tons, were built into the early 20th century, notably by German and French owners.

Terminology

The word "bark" entered English from French and ultimately from Latin barca. In the United States Navy and in some European usage, "bark" is the common spelling. A vessel rigged as a barque but lacking a mizzen topgallant or other refinements has occasionally been termed differently, and the related barquentine has only the foremast square-rigged with all other masts fore-and-aft.

Surviving examples

Preserved barques include the James Craig in Sydney, the Star of Bengal, and training ships such as the Italian Palinuro. The four-masted barque Pommern is preserved as a museum ship in Mariehamn, Åland.
