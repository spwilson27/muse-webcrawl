Grand Army Plaza, originally known as Prospect Park Plaza, is a large oval plaza at the main entrance of Prospect Park in Brooklyn, New York City. It was built in 1866–1867 as part of Frederick Law Olmsted and Calvert Vaux's design for Prospect Park, and forms the landmark junction of Flatbush Avenue, Eastern Parkway, Prospect Park West, and Union Street. The plaza is dominated by the Soldiers' and Sailors' Arch, a grand triumphal arch honouring Union forces in the American Civil War.

Features

The centrepiece is the Soldiers' and Sailors' Arch, designed by John H. Duncan (architect of Grant's Tomb) and dedicated in 1892; its quadriga and statuary groups by sculptors including Frederick MacMonnies were added over the following years. The plaza also contains the Bailey Fountain (1932), with figures of Neptune and a Triton, a monument to John F. Kennedy, and bronze busts of notable Brooklynites. The central Brooklyn Public Library stands just southeast of the plaza, and the Brooklyn Museum and Brooklyn Botanic Garden lie nearby along Eastern Parkway. The plaza hosts a popular Saturday farmers' market (greenmarket) year-round.

Another Grand Army Plaza

Manhattan also has a smaller Grand Army Plaza, at Fifth Avenue and 59th Street by Central Park, containing the Pulitzer Fountain and Augustus Saint-Gaudens's gilded statue of General William Tecumseh Sherman — both plazas named for the Grand Army of the Republic, the Union veterans' organization.
