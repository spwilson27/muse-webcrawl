Concentrated animal feeding operation

A concentrated animal feeding operation (CAFO) is an intensive animal feeding operation in which over 1,000 animal units are confined for over 45 days a year. The term is defined by the United States Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) under the Clean Water Act, which regulates CAFOs as point sources of pollution because of the large quantities of manure they generate.

Definition and regulation

An animal feeding operation (AFO) confines animals and feeds them in a concentrated area rather than grazing. A facility qualifies as a large CAFO based on animal numbers — for example, 1,000 or more beef cattle, 700 or more mature dairy cows, or 125,000 or more chickens — while smaller facilities may be designated CAFOs if they discharge pollutants into waterways. Large CAFOs are required to obtain discharge permits and maintain nutrient management plans.

Environmental and health concerns

Critics point to water pollution from manure runoff, air emissions including ammonia and hydrogen sulfide, odour impacts on neighbouring communities, the spread of antibiotic-resistant bacteria associated with routine antibiotic use, and animal welfare concerns. Supporters note that intensive operations produce meat, milk, and eggs more cheaply and with less land per unit of output than extensive methods.
