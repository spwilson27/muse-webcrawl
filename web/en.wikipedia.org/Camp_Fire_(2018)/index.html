Camp Fire (2018)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Camp Fire was the deadliest and most destructive wildfire in California history. It started on November 8, 2018, in Butte County, Northern California, near the community of Pulga, and burned for 17 days. The fire killed 85 people, destroyed the town of Paradise and much of Concow and Magalia, burned 153,336 acres, and destroyed more than 18,000 structures.

Course of the fire

Ignited under a Pacific Gas and Electric Company (PG&E) transmission line on the morning of November 8, the fire spread explosively through the Feather River Canyon driven by strong downslope winds and extremely dry vegetation. Within hours it reached Paradise (population about 27,000), overwhelming evacuation routes; many victims died in their vehicles or homes. By the time the fire was contained on November 25, roughly 95 percent of Paradise's structures were destroyed.

Cause and investigation

Investigators determined that the fire began when a nearly century-old steel hook on a PG&E transmission tower failed, dropping a live conductor that ignited dry brush below. In 2020 PG&E pleaded guilty to 84 counts of involuntary manslaughter and one count of unlawfully causing a fire, and agreed to pay billions in compensation. The disaster drove PG&E into bankruptcy in 2019 and prompted sweeping changes to California utility regulation, including grid shutoffs during extreme fire weather.

Impact

The Camp Fire displaced tens of thousands of residents, many of whom relocated to nearby Chico and beyond, and remains the costliest disaster in the world in 2018 in insured losses. Paradise has slowly rebuilt, with fire-resistant construction standards, buried power lines, and improved evacuation planning.
