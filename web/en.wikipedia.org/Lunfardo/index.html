Lunfardo

Lunfardo is an argot originated and developed in the late 19th and early 20th centuries in the lower classes in the Rio de la Plata region, especially Buenos Aires, Argentina, and Montevideo, Uruguay. Many Lunfardo expressions have entered Spanish as spoken in Argentina and Uruguay.

History

Lunfardo emerged among prisoners and immigrants, drawing heavily on Italian dialects brought by large-scale Italian immigration, as well as Spanish, French, Portuguese, English and African languages. It is closely associated with tango lyrics, which spread its vocabulary widely.

Features

A characteristic device is vesre, reversing syllables (for example, tango becoming gotán). Words such as laburo (work), morfar (to eat), mina (woman) and pibe (kid) remain common in informal Rioplatense speech.
