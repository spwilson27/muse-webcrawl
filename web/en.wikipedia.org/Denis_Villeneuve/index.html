Denis Villeneuve (born 3 October 1967) is a Canadian film director and screenwriter. Regarded as one of the leading filmmakers of his generation, he first gained acclaim in Quebec cinema before achieving international success with English-language films.

Career

Villeneuve's early features August 32nd on Earth (1998) and Maelström (2000) screened at international festivals. After a hiatus he returned with Polytechnique (2009) and Incendies (2010), the latter nominated for the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film. His Hollywood films include Prisoners (2013), Sicario (2015), Arrival (2016) — which earned him a Best Director Oscar nomination — and Blade Runner 2049 (2017).

Dune

Villeneuve directed Dune (2021), an adaptation of Frank Herbert's novel, which won six Academy Awards, and its sequel Dune: Part Two (2024), both commercial and critical successes that cemented his reputation for large-scale, atmospheric filmmaking.

Style

His films are noted for meticulous cinematography, often with Roger Deakins; slow-burning tension; monumental imagery; and themes of memory, identity and moral ambiguity.
