Trauma care

Trauma care is the organised system of pre-hospital, hospital, and rehabilitative services for people with serious injuries. It encompasses emergency medical services (EMS), designated trauma centres, trauma surgery, critical care, and rehabilitation, coordinated regionally so that patients reach the right facility in the shortest possible time.

Trauma systems and centres

Trauma centres are verified or designated by levels (Level I providing the highest, comprehensive care with in-house trauma surgeons, research, and education, through Level IV/V facilities that stabilise and transfer). Field triage guidelines direct ambulances and helicopters to the appropriate centre, sometimes bypassing closer hospitals.

Phases of care

Pre-hospital care focuses on airway, haemorrhage control (including tourniquets and tranexamic acid), and rapid transport. In the emergency department, the primary survey and resuscitation proceed alongside imaging such as the FAST examination and whole-body CT. Definitive care involves surgery, interventional radiology, and intensive care, followed by rehabilitation and psychological support.

Quality and prevention

Trauma registries and performance improvement programmes track outcomes, and injury prevention — road safety, fall prevention, violence intervention — is recognised as an integral part of trauma systems.
