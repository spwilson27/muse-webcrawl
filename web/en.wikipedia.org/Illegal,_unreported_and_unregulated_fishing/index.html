Illegal, unreported and unregulated fishing

Fisheries patrol inspecting a vessel suspected of IUU fishing.

Illegal, unreported and unregulated fishing (IUU fishing) is a broad term capturing a wide variety of fishing activity in violation of, or outside, applicable fisheries laws and management measures. The concept was formalised in the 2001 International Plan of Action adopted by the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) of the United Nations. Illegal fishing contravenes national laws or regional management rules; unreported fishing is not reported, or is misreported, to authorities; and unregulated fishing occurs in areas or for stocks where no applicable conservation measures exist, or by vessels without nationality or under flags of non-cooperating states.

Scale and impacts

Studies have estimated that IUU catches amount to between 11 and 26 million tonnes of fish per year worldwide, worth on the order of 10 to 23.5 billion US dollars. IUU fishing undermines fisheries management, depletes stocks such as Patagonian toothfish and bluefin tuna, harms marine ecosystems through destructive gear and bycatch, and disadvantages honest fishers and coastal communities that depend on fisheries for food and income.

Countermeasures

Responses include the FAO's Port State Measures Agreement (in force since 2016), the first binding international treaty aimed specifically at IUU fishing, which lets port states deny entry or landing to suspect vessels; catch documentation schemes; vessel monitoring systems and satellite tracking; blacklists maintained by regional fisheries management organisations; and market measures by major importers such as the European Union's carding system for non-cooperating countries.
