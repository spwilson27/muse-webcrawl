Fernando Álvarez de Toledo y Pimentel, 3rd Duke of Alba (29 October 1507 – 11 December 1582), known as the Grand Duke of Alba and nicknamed "the Iron Duke", was a Spanish general and statesman who served under Emperor Charles V and King Philip II. He is remembered both as one of the foremost commanders of the 16th century and, especially in Dutch memory, for the severity of his rule in the Netherlands.

Military career

Raised by his grandfather the 2nd Duke, Alba campaigned from youth: he fought at the Battle of Pavia (1525), defended Vienna against the Ottomans (1532), and joined Charles V's expeditions against Tunis and Algiers. He commanded Imperial forces at the Battle of Mühlberg (1547), where the Schmalkaldic League was crushed, and later served as governor of Milan and viceroy of Naples, directing Spanish operations in Italy during the Italian War of 1551–1559.

Governor of the Netherlands

In 1567 Philip II sent Alba to the Habsburg Netherlands to suppress Protestant unrest and the revolt led by William of Orange. Alba established the Council of Troubles — remembered by the Dutch as the "Council of Blood" — which condemned thousands and executed the Counts of Egmont and Horn in 1568. His punitive taxes, notably the proposed "Tenth Penny", alienated even loyal provinces, and his campaigns could not subdue the rebellious north. Recalled in 1573, he left a reputation that made him a central villain of the Dutch "Black Legend" of Spanish cruelty.

Portuguese succession and death

In 1580, at the age of 72, Alba led the Spanish army that conquered Portugal for Philip II in the War of the Portuguese Succession, winning the Battle of Alcântara and entering Lisbon. He served as the first Spanish viceroy of Portugal until his death in Lisbon in 1582.
