Virtual Private LAN Service

Virtual Private LAN Service (VPLS) is a way to provide Ethernet-based multipoint-to-multipoint communication over IP or MPLS networks. It allows geographically dispersed sites to share an Ethernet broadcast domain by connecting sites through pseudo-wires. VPLS is one of the family of layer-2 VPN services, contrasted with point-to-point VPWS and layer-3 VPNs.

Architecture

In a VPLS network, provider edge (PE) routers emulate a virtual switch or bridge for each customer: frames received from a customer edge device are forwarded across provider tunnels to the appropriate PE, based on learned MAC addresses, and broadcast and multicast traffic is replicated as needed. Two main control-plane approaches are defined: BGP-based autodiscovery and signalling (RFC 4761) and LDP-based signalling (RFC 4762).

Applications

Carriers offer VPLS as a metro and wide-area Ethernet service so enterprises can extend a single LAN across branches transparently, using their own routing. Challenges include MAC-address table scaling, loop prevention, and replication overhead; Ethernet VPN (EVPN) has increasingly been adopted as a more scalable successor for similar multipoint services.
