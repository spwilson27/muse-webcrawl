Paul Otlet
BornAugust 23, 1868, Brussels, Belgium
DiedDecember 10, 1944, Brussels
Known forMundaneum; Universal Decimal Classification

Paul Marie Ghislain Otlet (1868–1944) was a Belgian author, entrepreneur, lawyer, and peace activist, regarded as one of the founders of information science. With Henri La Fontaine he created the Universal Decimal Classification and founded the Mundaneum, an ambitious project to gather all of the world's knowledge in one place.

The Mundaneum

In 1895 Otlet and La Fontaine established the International Institute of Bibliography in Brussels and began compiling the Répertoire Bibliographique Universel, a card catalog that eventually held more than 18 million entries cross-referenced by subject. Their Universal Decimal Classification expanded Melvil Dewey's system with symbols allowing concepts to be combined and linked — an early analogue of hypertext.

The collection was housed from 1920 in a wing of the Palais du Cinquantenaire as the Palais Mondial–Mundaneum. In writings such as Traité de documentation (1934), Otlet envisioned a network of "electric telescopes" by which users at home could consult documents remotely — a vision often compared to the World Wide Web, decades before its invention.

Legacy

Financial difficulties and the Nazi occupation shut the Mundaneum down, and Otlet died in 1944. The surviving archives were rediscovered decades later and are now preserved in Mons, Belgium. Otlet is now widely recognized as a visionary precursor of modern information retrieval and the internet.
