Advanced glycation end-product

Advanced glycation end-products (AGEs) are proteins or lipids that become glycated through non-enzymatic reaction with sugars, via the Maillard reaction. Well-studied examples include carboxymethyllysine and pentosidine.

Formation

AGEs form slowly in the body, faster under hyperglycaemia, and are also ingested from foods cooked at high dry heat such as grilled and fried foods.

Health effects

AGEs accumulate with age and in diabetes, cross-linking collagen and activating the RAGE receptor, and are implicated in vascular stiffness, kidney disease and other diabetic complications.
