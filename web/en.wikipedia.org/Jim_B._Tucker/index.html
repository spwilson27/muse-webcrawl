Jim B. Tucker


Jim B. Tucker (born 1960) is an American psychiatrist who studies children's reports of past-life memories.

Jim B. Tucker (born 1960) is an American child psychiatrist and professor at the University of Virginia School of Medicine. He is known for continuing and developing the research of Ian Stevenson into children who report memories of previous lives, and he directed the university's Division of Perceptual Studies (DOPS) after 2014.

Career

Tucker was born in Goldsboro, North Carolina, and graduated from the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, where he also earned his medical degree. He practised child psychiatry in Charlotte before joining the University of Virginia in 2000 to work with Stevenson and psychiatrist Bruce Greyson. His research focuses on American children reporting past-life memories, complementing Stevenson's case collections drawn mainly from Asia.

Work and publications

Tucker developed the Strength of Case Scale, a system for rating the evidential strength of reported past-life cases, and maintains the division's database of more than 2,000 cases. He is the author of Life Before Life: A Scientific Investigation of Children's Memories of Previous Lives (2005) and Return to Life: Extraordinary Cases of Children Who Remember Past Lives (2013), the latter featuring prominent American cases including that of James Leininger. Tucker has presented the research as an open-ended scientific inquiry, while critics argue the cases can be explained by fantasy, suggestion, and selective reporting.
