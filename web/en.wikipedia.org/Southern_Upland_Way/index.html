Southern Upland Way

The Southern Upland Way is a 344-kilometre (214 mi) coast-to-coast long-distance footpath in southern Scotland. It runs from Portpatrick on the west coast, on the Rhins of Galloway overlooking the Irish Sea, to Cockburnspath on the east coast near the North Sea. It is recognised as one of Scotland's Great Trails and is the longest of the official long-distance routes in Scotland.

Route

The route passes through Dumfries and Galloway and the Scottish Borders, crossing the Galloway Hills, the Lowther Hills and the Lammermuir Hills. Settlements on or near the route include Stranraer, Castle Kennedy, New Luce, Bargrennan, Newton Stewart, St John's Town of Dalry, Sanquhar, Wanlockhead, Beattock, Moffat, Ettrick, Traquair, Innerleithen, Melrose, Lauder and Longformacus. Wanlockhead, at around 467 metres (1,532 ft), is the highest village in Scotland and marks roughly the halfway point of the trail.

History

The Southern Upland Way was the first officially designated coast-to-coast long-distance footpath in the United Kingdom. It was proposed in the 1970s and opened in stages, with the full route officially opened in 1984. The way is waymarked with a thistle symbol, in common with other Scotland's Great Trails.

Features

Much of the route crosses remote upland and moorland, and walkers must be self-sufficient over long sections. Thirteen "kists" — small metal boxes hidden near the path containing collectable commemorative coins — were installed along the route as part of a public art project, encouraging walkers to search for them. The trail is usually walked from west to east so that the prevailing wind is at the walker's back, and most people take between 12 and 16 days to complete it.
