European Cenozoic Rift System

The European Cenozoic Rift System (ECRIS) is a 1,100 km (680 mi) long system of rifts formed in the foreland of the Alps as the lithosphere responded to the effects of the Alpine and Pyrenean orogenies. The system began to form during the Late Eocene and parts of it, particularly the Lower and Upper Rhine Grabens, remain seismically active today and are responsible for major earthquakes in northern Europe.

Components

The rift system includes the Limagne Graben and Bresse Graben in France, the Rhône and Saône grabens, the Upper Rhine Graben between Basel and Frankfurt, the Lower Rhine Graben (including the Roer Valley Graben) in Germany and the Netherlands, the Eger Graben in the Czech Republic, and the Hessian grabens. Volcanic fields such as the Eifel, the Vogelsberg, and the Chaîne des Puys in the Massif Central are associated with the rifting.

Formation

Rifting began in the Late Eocene under a changed regional stress field linked to collision between the African and Eurasian plates. Extension and subsidence continued through the Oligocene and Miocene, accumulating thick sedimentary fills in the grabens, locally including potash and hydrocarbon-bearing deposits. Several segments are still active: the Lower Rhine area experiences ongoing subsidence and earthquakes, most notably the 1992 Roermond earthquake.
