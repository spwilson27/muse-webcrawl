Shechem

Shechem was an ancient city in the southern Levant, located in the pass between Mount Gerizim and Mount Ebal, at the site of modern Tell Balata near the Palestinian city of Nablus in the West Bank. It was an important Canaanite and later Israelite city and appears frequently in the Hebrew Bible.

History

Shechem was inhabited from the Chalcolithic period and was a fortified Middle Bronze Age city mentioned in Egyptian texts, including the execration texts and the Amarna letters, whose ruler Labayu features in correspondence with Pharaoh. According to the Book of Genesis, Abraham built an altar there, Jacob purchased land and his sons Simeon and Levi attacked the city after the violation of their sister Dinah.

Biblical significance

Shechem was the site of Joshua's covenant renewal ceremony and was allotted to the tribe of Manasseh. After Solomon's death, the northern tribes assembled at Shechem and crowned Rehoboam, then rejected him and made Jeroboam king of Israel. The city was largely destroyed by the Assyrians with the fall of the northern kingdom in 722 BCE; the site later became sacred to the Samaritans, whose temple stood on nearby Mount Gerizim.
