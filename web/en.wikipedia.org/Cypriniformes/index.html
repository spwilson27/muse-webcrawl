Cypriniformes

Scientific classification
Kingdom:Animalia
Phylum:Chordata
Class:Actinopterygii
Order:Cypriniformes

The Cypriniformes are an order of ray-finned fishes that includes the carps, minnows, loaches and related forms. With more than 4,000 described species, it is one of the most diverse orders of freshwater fish in the world, found on every continent except Australia and Antarctica.

Characteristics

Cypriniforms are distinguished by the Weberian apparatus, a chain of small bones connecting the swim bladder to the inner ear, which greatly enhances their hearing. Most species lack teeth in the jaws, instead bearing pharyngeal teeth in the throat that process food against a chewing pad. They also typically have a protrusible mouth and lack an adipose fin.

Diversity and distribution

The order is dominated by the family Cyprinidae, which includes the common carp, goldfish, zebrafish and thousands of minnow species. Other families include the suckers (Catostomidae) of North America, the hillstream loaches, and the algae eaters. Cypriniforms dominate the freshwater fish faunas of Eurasia and North America, and are also widespread in Africa.

Importance to humans

Many cypriniforms are important food fish, and carp aquaculture is among the oldest forms of fish farming. Others, such as koi and goldfish, are popular in the ornamental trade, while the zebrafish is a major model organism in biological research.
