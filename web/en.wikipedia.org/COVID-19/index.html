Coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) is a contagious disease caused by the coronavirus SARS-CoV-2. In January 2020, the disease spread worldwide, resulting in the COVID-19 pandemic. The World Health Organization announced the name "COVID-19" on 11 February 2020.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms vary from none to life-threatening illness and typically appear 2–14 days after exposure. Common symptoms include fever, cough, tiredness, and loss of taste or smell; severe cases develop pneumonia and acute respiratory distress. Older people and those with conditions such as diabetes, cardiovascular disease and obesity are at higher risk of severe illness. Some people develop long COVID, with symptoms persisting for months.

Transmission and diagnosis

The virus spreads mainly through respiratory droplets and aerosols released when an infected person breathes, coughs or speaks, especially indoors. Diagnosis is by molecular tests such as RT-PCR and by rapid antigen tests on nasal or throat swabs.

Prevention and treatment

COVID-19 vaccines, first deployed in December 2020, greatly reduce severe disease and death; variants such as Delta and Omicron partially evaded immunity, prompting updated boosters. Treatment is mostly supportive, with oxygen and mechanical ventilation in severe cases; the corticosteroid dexamethasone and antivirals such as nirmatrelvir/ritonavir and remdesivir reduce mortality or progression in at-risk patients.
