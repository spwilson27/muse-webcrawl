Opet Festival

The Opet Festival (Ancient Egyptian: heb nefer en Ipet, "beautiful festival of Opet") was an annual festival of ancient Egypt held at Thebes (modern Luxor) during the second month of the season of Akhet, the inundation. During the festival, the statues of the Theban Triad — Amun, his consort Mut and their son Khonsu — were carried in procession from the Karnak Temple to the Luxor Temple, a journey of about two kilometres along the Avenue of Sphinxes or by sacred barque on the Nile.

Meaning and ritual

The festival celebrated the divine marriage of Amun and Mut and the renewal of the king's power: at Luxor, the pharaoh was ritually rejuvenated and reaffirmed as the son of Amun, legitimising his rule. Cult images travelled on gilded barques, borne on priests' shoulders amid music, dancing and offerings, while crowds lined the route. Reliefs commissioned by Hatshepsut, Tutankhamun and others in the Luxor Temple colonnade provide the fullest visual record of the celebration.

Duration and history

In the early New Kingdom the festival lasted about 11 days; by the reign of Ramesses III in the 20th Dynasty it had grown to 27 days. It remained one of the great events of the Theban calendar into the Late Period. In 2021 Egypt revived a spectacle inspired by the festival for the reopening of the restored Avenue of Sphinxes between Karnak and Luxor, an event televised internationally.
