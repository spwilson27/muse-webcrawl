Xi Kang

Xi Kang (also written Ji Kang; 223–262 CE) was a Chinese philosopher, poet, musician and writer of the late Han and Three Kingdoms period, and one of the Seven Sages of the Bamboo Grove.

Life and thought

Xi Kang withdrew from official life under the Wei court dominated by the Sima clan, living as a blacksmith and recluse near Luoyang with friends such as Ruan Ji and Shan Tao. His essays, including 'On the Sound of Music Having Neither Sorrow nor Joy' and writings on Yang Sheng (nourishing life), blend Daoist free-spiritedness with Confucian learning. He was famed as a player of the qin; the melody Guangling San is traditionally linked to him.

Death

Entangled in a court slander case and refusing to recant his independence, he was executed in 262 on the advice of Zhong Hui. His calm at the execution ground, where he played the qin before a crowd of petitioning students, became a famous emblem of the scholar's integrity.
