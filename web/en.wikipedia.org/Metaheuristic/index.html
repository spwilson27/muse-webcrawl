Metaheuristic

In computer science and mathematical optimisation, a metaheuristic is a high-level, problem-independent algorithmic framework that provides a set of guidelines or strategies for developing heuristic optimisation algorithms. Metaheuristics are used for problems where finding an exact optimum is impractical, and they aim to find a sufficiently good solution with limited computation.

Common metaheuristics

Well-known examples include simulated annealing, genetic algorithms and other evolutionary algorithms, tabu search, ant colony optimisation, particle swarm optimisation, iterated local search and variable neighbourhood search. Many are inspired by natural processes such as evolution, thermodynamics or swarm behaviour.

Properties

Metaheuristics typically balance exploration of the search space with exploitation of promising regions, use stochastic components, and offer no guarantee of optimality. Their effectiveness depends on problem-specific adaptation of the generic framework, including the solution representation and neighbourhood operators. The "no free lunch" theorems imply that no single metaheuristic performs best across all problems.

Applications

They are applied to scheduling, routing (such as the travelling salesman problem), engineering design, machine learning hyperparameter tuning and many other combinatorial and continuous optimisation tasks.
