Carmen Saeculare

The Secular Games celebrated by Augustus in 17 BC

The Carmen Saeculare (Latin for "Secular Hymn" or "Song of the Ages") is a hymn in Sapphic meter written by the Roman poet Horace. It was commissioned by the Roman emperor Augustus in 17 BC. The hymn was sung by a chorus of twenty-seven young men and twenty-seven young women at the Secular Games (Ludi Saeculares).

Background

The Secular Games were a religious celebration in ancient Rome, involving sacrifices and theatrical performances, held in 17 BC to mark the dawn of a new saeculum (a period of roughly 110 years, the longest span of a human lifetime). Augustus used the games to celebrate the stability and renewed moral order of his regime after decades of civil war.

Content and performance

The hymn, seventy-six lines long, is addressed principally to the gods Apollo and Diana, with invocations also to Jupiter, Juno, and other deities. It asks for blessings on Rome, fertility for the land and its people, success for the emperor, and the continuation of Roman power and virtue. It was performed first on the Palatine Hill before the temple of Apollo and then on the Capitoline Hill.

The Carmen Saeculare is unusual among Horace's surviving works as a public, commissioned poem for choral performance, written for a specific state occasion rather than for private recitation or publication alone. An inscription recording the games, discovered in 1890, records the hymn's performance and names Horace as its composer.
