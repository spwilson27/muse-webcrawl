Air India Flight 182 was a scheduled passenger flight from Montreal to London and Delhi that was destroyed by a bomb on June 23, 1985, while flying over the Atlantic Ocean off the coast of Ireland. All 329 people aboard were killed, most of them Canadians of Indian origin, making it the deadliest act of aviation terrorism until the September 11 attacks and the worst mass murder in Canadian history.

The bombing

The flight, operated by a Boeing 747 named Emperor Kanishka, disintegrated at 31,000 feet after a suitcase bomb checked in at Vancouver exploded in the forward cargo hold. A second bomb the same day exploded at Tokyo's Narita Airport, killing two baggage handlers; it was intended for another Air India flight. The bombings were attributed to Sikh extremists based in Canada, including members of Babbar Khalsa, seeking revenge for the Indian Army's 1984 storming of the Golden Temple.

Investigation and trial

The investigation and prosecution became the longest and most expensive in Canadian history. Inderjit Singh Reyat pleaded guilty to manslaughter in 2003; the two principal accused, Ripudaman Singh Malik and Ajaib Singh Bagri, were acquitted in 2005. A subsequent public inquiry, led by former Supreme Court justice John Major, reported in 2010 that a "cascading series of errors" by Canadian agencies had failed to prevent the attack.
