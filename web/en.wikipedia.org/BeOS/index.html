BeOS

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

BeOS is a discontinued operating system for personal computers, developed by Be Inc. and first released in 1995. Initially written for the company's own BeBox hardware, it was later ported to PowerPC-based Macintosh clones and to Intel x86 systems.

Design

Written largely from scratch in C++, BeOS was designed for digital media work: it featured pervasive multithreading, a microkernel-inspired architecture, a 64-bit journaling file system (BFS) with extended attributes, and low-latency audio and video handling. Its interface used tabs in window title bars and a Deskbar for launching applications.

History

Be Inc. was founded in 1990 by former Apple executive Jean-Louis Gassée. Apple considered acquiring Be in 1996 but instead bought NeXT. BeOS was sold to Palm, Inc. in 2001. The open-source Haiku operating system continues BeOS's design as a compatible reimplementation. A free version, BeOS Personal Edition (BeOS 5), could be launched from within Windows or Linux.
