Gross domestic product

Countries by nominal GDP.

Gross domestic product (GDP) is a monetary measure of the market value of all the final goods and services produced and rendered in a specific time period by a country or countries. It is the most widely used measure of the size of an economy, and is published by national statistical agencies and international bodies such as the IMF, World Bank and OECD.

Definition and calculation

GDP can be measured by three equivalent approaches:

  • Production (value added): the sum of value added at each stage of production, plus taxes less subsidies on products.
  • Expenditure: GDP = consumption (C) + investment (I) + government spending (G) + exports (X) − imports (M).
  • Income: the sum of wages, profits, rents and taxes less subsidies generated by production.

Nominal GDP is valued at current prices, while real GDP is adjusted for inflation to measure changes in output volume. GDP per capita divides by population and is a rough proxy for average material living standards; comparisons between countries may use exchange rates or purchasing power parity (PPP).

History

The modern concept was developed by Simon Kuznets in a 1934 report to the U.S. Congress, and national income accounting was formalised during the Second World War by Keynes and others. The United Nations System of National Accounts (first issued 1953) standardised the measure internationally.

Criticisms and alternatives

Kuznets himself warned that GDP is not a measure of welfare. Critics note that GDP counts defensive and remedial spending (cleaning up pollution adds to GDP), ignores most unpaid household work and volunteering, says nothing about distribution of income or depletion of natural capital, and can grow while well-being stagnates. Alternative indicators include gross national income (GNI), the Human Development Index, the Genuine Progress Indicator, and "beyond GDP" dashboards of well-being. Nevertheless GDP remains the standard headline statistic for economic performance and the denominator of key ratios such as debt-to-GDP.
