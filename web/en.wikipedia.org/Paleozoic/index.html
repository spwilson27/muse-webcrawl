Paleozoic

Paleozoic Era
Time span: 538.8 ± 0.2 to 251.9 million years ago
Eon: Phanerozoic
Periods: Cambrian, Ordovician, Silurian, Devonian, Carboniferous, Permian
Etymology: Greek palaiós (old) + zōḗ (life)

The Paleozoic (/ˌpæli.əˈzoʊ.ɪk, -i.oʊ-, ˌpeɪ-/ PAL-ee-ə-ZOH-ik) Era (from Greek palaiós "old" and zōḗ "life", meaning "ancient life") is the earliest of three geologic eras of the Phanerozoic Eon. It is the longest of the Phanerozoic eras, lasting from 538.8 to 251.902 million years ago, and is subdivided into six geologic periods: the Cambrian, Ordovician, Silurian, Devonian, Carboniferous and Permian.

The Paleozoic saw the greatest diversification of life in Earth's history: the Cambrian explosion produced most major animal groups; life colonized land with plants, fungi, arthropods and eventually vertebrates; vast coal forests formed during the Carboniferous; and the first reptiles and seed plants appeared. Geologically, the era began with the breakup of the supercontinent Pannotia and ended with the assembly of Pangaea.

End

The era ended with the Permian–Triassic extinction event, the largest mass extinction in Earth's history, in which an estimated 81–96% of marine species and about 70% of terrestrial vertebrate species died out, likely driven by massive volcanism of the Siberian Traps and associated climate change.
