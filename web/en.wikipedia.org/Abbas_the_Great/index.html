Born27 January 1571, Herat

Abbas I (Persian: شاه عباس یکم; 1571–1629), commonly known as Abbas the Great, was the fifth Safavid shah of Iran and is generally considered the greatest ruler of the Safavid dynasty. He reigned from 1588 until his death, transforming a state on the brink of collapse into a major power.

Reign

Abbas came to power at sixteen after the deposition of his father, Mohammad Khodabanda. The empire was beset by Uzbek and Ottoman invasions and internal Qizilbash factionalism. Abbas first made a humiliating peace with the Ottomans to free his hands in the east, reformed the army with the help of the English adventurers Robert and Anthony Sherley, and created new infantry and artillery corps of ghulams loyal directly to the shah.

With his reformed forces he recovered Herat and Mashhad from the Uzbeks, then defeated the Ottomans in a long war (1603–1618), regaining Tabriz, Yerevan, Baghdad and much of the Caucasus and Mesopotamia. In 1622, with English naval help, he expelled the Portuguese from Hormuz.

Achievements

In 1598 Abbas transferred the capital to Isfahan, which he rebuilt on a grand scale around the Naqsh-e Jahan Square with the Shah Mosque, the Sheikh Lotfollah Mosque and the Ali Qapu palace. He encouraged trade, welcomed European embassies and merchants, and patronised the arts, under which Persian carpet-weaving, ceramics and miniature painting flourished.
