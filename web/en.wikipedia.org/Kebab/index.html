Kebab

Kebab (also kabob) is a variety of roasted meat dishes that originated in the Middle East. Kebabs consist of cut-up or ground meat — most often lamb or beef, sometimes chicken, pork or fish — cooked on a skewer or spit, frequently with vegetables. The word derives ultimately from Persian kabāb, and dishes of this kind are found across the Middle East, the Caucasus, Central and South Asia, the Balkans and, through migration, worldwide.

Varieties

  • Shish kebab — cubes of marinated meat grilled on skewers, often with peppers and onions.
  • Doner kebab — seasoned meat stacked and roasted on a vertical rotisserie, sliced as it cooks; popularised as street food in Berlin in the 1970s and the ancestor of the Greek gyro and Arab shawarma.
  • Adana and Urfa kebabs — hand-minced, spiced lamb moulded onto wide flat skewers, from south-eastern Turkey.
  • Kofta kebab — meatballs or logs of ground meat mixed with onions and spices, common from the Balkans to India.
  • Seekh kebab — spiced minced meat grilled on skewers in a tandoor in South Asian cuisine.
  • Chelow kebab — skewered meat served with buttered saffron rice, the national dish of Iran.

History

Grilling meat on skewers is ancient: evidence of stone supports for skewers exists from Minoan Crete around 1700 BCE, and medieval Arabic and Persian cookbooks describe kebab dishes in detail. The vertical doner developed in the 19th-century Ottoman Empire, in Bursa, where İskender Efendi is credited with turning the spit upright. Turkish and other migrants carried kebab shops across Europe in the late 20th century, making the doner kebab one of the most popular fast foods in Germany, Britain and beyond.
