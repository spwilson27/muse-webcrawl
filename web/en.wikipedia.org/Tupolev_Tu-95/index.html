The Tupolev Tu-95 (Russian: Туполев Ту-95; NATO reporting name: "Bear") is a large, four-engine turboprop-powered strategic bomber and missile platform first flown in 1952 and introduced into service with the Soviet Long Range Aviation in 1956. It remains in service with the Russian Aerospace Forces and is expected to fly into the 2040s — a service life of nearly a century for the basic design.

Design

The Tu-95 is powered by four Kuznetsov NK-12 turboprops, the most powerful turboprop engines ever flown, driving huge contra-rotating propellers whose tips exceed the speed of sound. This makes the Bear one of the fastest propeller-driven aircraft ever built — and one of the noisiest, reportedly detectable even by submarine hydrophones. Its wings are swept at 35 degrees, unusual for a propeller aircraft.

Variants and service

Derivatives include the Tu-142 maritime patrol and anti-submarine aircraft, the Tu-95MS cruise-missile carrier armed with Kh-55/Kh-101 missiles, and the Tu-114 airliner. A Tu-95 dropped the Tsar Bomba, the most powerful nuclear weapon ever tested, in 1961. During the Cold War, Bears conducted long-range patrols that were routinely intercepted and escorted by NATO fighters; such flights resumed in 2007 and Tu-95MS aircraft have launched cruise missiles in combat over Syria and Ukraine.
