In the United States, a third party is any political party other than the two dominant parties — the Democratic Party and the Republican Party. Although the country has a long history of third parties, structural features such as winner-take-all elections, ballot-access laws, and the Electoral College have limited their success.

History

Influential historical third parties include the Progressive ("Bull Moose") Party of Theodore Roosevelt, which won 27 percent of the vote in 1912; the Socialist Party under Eugene V. Debs; and George Wallace's American Independent Party, which carried five states in 1968. Ross Perot's independent candidacy in 1992 won 19 percent of the popular vote, the strongest modern showing, though no electoral votes.

Modern third parties

The largest contemporary third parties are the Libertarian Party and the Green Party, which regularly appear on most state ballots, along with the Constitution Party and various state-level parties. Third parties are often credited with introducing issues — such as women's suffrage, labour protections, and deficit reduction — later adopted by major parties.
