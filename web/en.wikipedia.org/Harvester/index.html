Harvester

A harvester is a machine — or, historically, a person — that harvests crops or other natural resources. In modern agriculture the term most often refers to the combine harvester, which combines reaping, threshing, and winnowing of grain crops in a single operation; in forestry, a harvester is a vehicle that fells, delimbs, and bucks trees.

Harvester
Uses: Agriculture, forestry
Types: Combine harvester, forage harvester, forestry harvester
Replaced: Manual reaping and stationary threshers

Agricultural harvesters

The combine harvester, developed in the nineteenth century and powered first by horses and steam and later by internal combustion engines, transformed grain farming by drastically reducing labour. A header cuts the crop, a threshing drum separates grain from straw, and sieves and fans clean the grain before it is stored in an onboard tank. Forage harvesters chop grasses and maize for silage, while specialised machines harvest cotton, sugar cane, grapes, potatoes, and other crops.

Forestry harvesters

A forestry harvester is a wheeled or tracked machine with an articulated boom and a harvester head that grabs a tree, cuts it at the base, removes branches, and saws the trunk into logs of specified lengths, measuring diameters and lengths automatically as part of the cut-to-length logging method.
