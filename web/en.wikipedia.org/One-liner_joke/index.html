One-liner joke

A one-liner is a joke delivered in a single short sentence, typically relying on brevity, wordplay, and an unexpected twist. One-liners are a staple of stand-up comedy, allowing comedians to deliver many jokes in rapid succession without lengthy setups or narratives.

Structure

Although expressed in one sentence, a successful one-liner usually contains both a setup and a punchline compressed together: the opening words establish an expectation that the closing words subvert. The format favors puns, double meanings, self-deprecation, and incongruity. Because they are short and self-contained, one-liners are easily quoted and shared, and many circulate as aphorisms detached from their originators.

Notable practitioners

Comedians famous for one-liners include Henny Youngman ("Take my wife — please!"), Rodney Dangerfield, Steven Wright, Mitch Hedberg, Tim Vine, and Milton Berle. In Britain, the one-liner is a hallmark of music-hall and Edinburgh Fringe comedy, with an annual award for the funniest joke of the Fringe frequently going to a single-sentence gag.

In other media

One-liners also appear as memorable single-line quips in film and television, though in that context the term can mean any short, witty line of dialogue rather than a complete joke.
