Campanile

A campanile is an Italian bell tower, or a freestanding tower elsewhere, especially one next to a church or a town hall. The word comes from the Italian campana, meaning bell. Italy has many famous campaniles, most of them attached to churches in medieval and Renaissance cities.

Famous examples

The most famous campanile is probably the Leaning Tower of Pisa, the bell tower of Pisa Cathedral, whose unintended tilt has made it a world landmark. St Mark's Campanile in Venice, nearly 99 metres tall, is one of the city's principal symbols. Giotto's Campanile in Florence, clad in white, green and pink marble, stands beside Florence Cathedral.

Design

Campaniles are typically tall, square or round towers with openings (belfry windows) near the top housing the bells. In the United States, freestanding bell towers on university campuses — such as Sather Tower at Berkeley, nicknamed "The Campanile" — took the form into collegiate architecture.
