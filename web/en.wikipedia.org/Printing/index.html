Printing

Printing is a process for mass reproducing text and images using a master form or template. The earliest non-paper products involving printing include cylinder seals and objects such as the Cyrus Cylinder (6th century BC). The earliest known form of printing evolved from ink rubbings made on paper or cloth from texts on stone tablets, used during the sixth century. Printing by pressing an inked surface onto paper spread from China and was transformed in Europe by the movable-type printing press.

History

Woodblock printing originated in China during the Tang dynasty; the Diamond Sutra (868 AD) is the earliest dated printed book. Bi Sheng invented movable type with baked clay pieces around 1040, and metal movable type was used in Korea by the 13th century (the Jikji, 1377, is the oldest extant book printed with metal movable type). In Europe, Johannes Gutenberg's printing press (c. 1440) combined movable metal type, oil-based ink, and a screw press, dramatically lowering the cost of books and helping to spread the Renaissance, the Reformation, and the Scientific Revolution.

Processes

Conventional printing processes include relief printing (letterpress, flexography), planographic printing (offset lithography — now dominant for books and newspapers), intaglio (gravure), and stencil methods (screen printing). Modern digital printing — inkjet and laser (electrophotography) — prints directly from digital files without plates, enabling short runs and print-on-demand. 3D printing extends the concept to layer-by-layer fabrication of objects.

Impact

Printing standardised texts, preserved knowledge, and enabled mass literacy and the newspaper. It remains a major global industry encompassing publishing, packaging, and commercial printing, though print media circulation has declined with digital media.
