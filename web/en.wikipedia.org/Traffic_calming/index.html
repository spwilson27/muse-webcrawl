Traffic calming

Traffic calming uses physical design and other measures to improve safety for motorists, pedestrians and cyclists. It aims to reduce vehicle speeds and cut-through traffic, particularly on residential streets, using features of the built environment rather than enforcement alone.

Measures

Common measures include speed humps and speed tables, chicanes and curb extensions, mini-roundabouts, raised crossings, narrowed lanes, and street trees. Area-wide schemes in the Netherlands — the home of the woonerf, or living street — pioneered many of these techniques in the 1970s.

Effects

Studies generally associate well-designed traffic calming with substantial reductions in collisions and injuries, especially for children. Criticisms include slower emergency-vehicle response, diversion of traffic to untreated streets, and discomfort for cyclists and bus passengers at some features.
