Mikhail Vorotynsky

Prince Mikhail Ivanovich Vorotynsky
Born: c. 1510
Died: 1573, Beloozero
Allegiance: Tsardom of Russia
Known for: Victory at the Battle of Molodi (1572); author of the first Russian military statute

Prince Mikhail Ivanovich Vorotynsky (c. 1510 – 1573) was one of the foremost Russian military commanders of the reign of Ivan the Terrible. A scion of the Upper Oka principalities, he defended Russia's southern frontier against the Crimean Tatars and played a leading role in the conquest of Kazan.

Career

Vorotynsky organised the frontier defence service along the steppe border and in 1571 compiled the Boyar Sentence on Frontier Service, regarded as the first Russian military statute. In 1552 he distinguished himself at the siege and storming of Kazan, where he was wounded. He fell in and out of favour with Ivan IV several times, suffering exile during the oprichnina.

Battle of Molodi

His greatest triumph came at the Battle of Molodi in July–August 1572, south of Moscow. Commanding the Russian army with Prince Dmitry Khvorostinin, Vorotynsky destroyed the invading force of Khan Devlet I Giray of Crimea, which had burned Moscow the previous year. The victory, won with the aid of a mobile fortification (the gulyay-gorod), ended serious Crimean threats to the capital for a generation.

Fall and death

Within a year of Molodi, Vorotynsky was denounced, tortured and exiled to Beloozero, dying on the way in 1573. His estates were confiscated, though his family was later partially rehabilitated.
