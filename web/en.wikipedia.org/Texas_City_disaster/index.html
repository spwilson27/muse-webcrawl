The Texas City disaster was an industrial accident that occurred on 16 April 1947 in the port of Texas City, Texas, United States. It is the deadliest industrial accident in US history and one of the largest non-nuclear explosions ever recorded. A fire aboard the French-registered Liberty ship SS Grandcamp detonated her cargo of approximately 2,300 tons of ammonium nitrate, killing at least 581 people. The blast and ensuing fires destroyed much of the port and surrounding industrial area, including the Monsanto chemical plant, and set a second ship, High Flyer, ablaze; she exploded the following day.

The explosion

A fire discovered in the hold of the Grandcamp on the morning of 16 April drew crowds of spectators to the waterfront. At 9:12 a.m. the cargo detonated. The explosion flattened the dock area, knocked two small aircraft out of the sky, and generated a 15-foot (4.6 m) tidal wave. The ship's 1.5-short-ton anchor was hurled more than 1.6 miles (2.6 km) and is preserved as a memorial. All but one member of the Texas City fire department — 27 firefighters — died in the initial blast. The second explosion, of the High Flyer in the early hours of 17 April, spread further destruction.

Aftermath

More than 5,000 people were injured, over 500 homes destroyed, and hundreds of businesses damaged; thousands were left homeless. The disaster prompted the first class-action lawsuit against the United States government, brought under the Federal Tort Claims Act on behalf of 8,485 claimants; it ultimately failed at the Supreme Court, though Congress later provided compensation by special act. The accident led to major changes in the regulation of ammonium nitrate handling and shipping, and in industrial safety and disaster planning.
