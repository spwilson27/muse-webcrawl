I and Thou

I and Thou (German: Ich und Du, 1923) is a book by the philosopher Martin Buber, his most famous work and a classic of modern religious thought and existential philosophy.

Content

Buber distinguishes two fundamental attitudes, expressed by the word pairs I–Thou (Ich–Du) and I–It (Ich–Es). The I–It relation treats the world as objects to be experienced and used, while the I–Thou relation is one of direct, mutual encounter in which the other is met as a whole being rather than a sum of qualities. For Buber, genuine dialogue — including humanity's relation to God, the eternal Thou — occurs in the sphere of "the between."

Influence

The book influenced theology, philosophy of dialogue, psychotherapy, and education, and remains a central text of existentialism and Jewish philosophy.
