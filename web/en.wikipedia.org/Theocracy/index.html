Theocracy

Theocracy is a form of government in which one or more deities are recognized as the supreme ruling authority, with earthly governance conducted by religious officials who claim to act on divine guidance, or in which religious law forms the basis of the legal system. The word derives from the Greek theokratia, "rule of God", a term coined by the historian Josephus.

Characteristics

In a theocracy, political and religious leadership are fused: rulers are typically clergy or are considered divinely appointed, civil law is grounded in scripture or religious doctrine, and dissent may be treated as heresy. Pure theocracies are rare; more common are states with an established religion exercising substantial political power.

Examples

Modern examples most often cited include Iran, where a Supreme Leader drawn from the Shia clergy oversees elected institutions, and Vatican City, ruled by the Pope. Historical examples include Calvin's Geneva, Puritan Massachusetts Bay Colony, and Tibet under the Dalai Lamas. Saudi Arabia, whose constitution is declared to be the Quran and the Sunnah, is sometimes described as theocratic, though ruled by a monarchy. See also Separation of church and state.
