Facial recognition system

A facial recognition system is a technology capable of matching a human face from a digital image or a video frame against a database of faces. Such systems are typically employed to authenticate users through ID verification services, and work by pinpointing and measuring facial features from a given image. Development began in the 1960s with the pioneering work of Woody Bledsoe and others, and accelerated with eigenface methods in the 1990s and deep-learning approaches after 2012.

How it works

Modern systems detect faces in an image, align them, extract a numerical representation (an "embedding") using a deep convolutional neural network, and compare the embedding against stored references. Verification (1:1 matching) confirms a claimed identity — as in unlocking a phone with Apple's Face ID (introduced 2017) — while identification (1:N matching) searches a database for the closest match. Performance is benchmarked by NIST's Face Recognition Vendor Tests.

Applications

Facial recognition is used in smartphone authentication, airport e-gates and border systems, law-enforcement identification, retail and event access, attendance systems, and consumer photo organisation. China has deployed extensive public systems, while many other jurisdictions restrict police use.

Concerns and regulation

Research, including the 2018 "Gender Shades" study, found significantly higher error rates for darker-skinned and female faces in commercial systems, and false matches have led to wrongful arrests. Privacy advocates object to mass surveillance and to databases built without consent, such as that of Clearview AI. In response, cities including San Francisco banned government use, the Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act imposed consent requirements, and the European Union's AI Act (2024) restricts real-time remote biometric identification in public spaces.
