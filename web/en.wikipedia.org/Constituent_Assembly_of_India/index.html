Constituent Assembly of India

The Constituent Assembly of India was elected to frame the Constitution of India, serving also as the country's first parliament after independence in 1947 until the elections of 1952. It was formed under the scheme formulated by the Cabinet Mission Plan in 1946. The Assembly was not elected on the basis of universal adult suffrage; its members were chosen by indirect election by the members of the provincial assemblies, with representatives of the princely states nominated.

Composition and leadership

The Assembly had 389 members before Partition; after the creation of Pakistan, 299 members remained for India, of whom 284 were present to sign the final document. Sachchidananda Sinha was the first (interim) president; Rajendra Prasad was elected president, and B. R. Ambedkar chaired the Drafting Committee. Jawaharlal Nehru moved the landmark Objectives Resolution on 13 December 1946.

Drafting the constitution

The Assembly sat for 166 days spread over two years, eleven months and eighteen days. A constitutional adviser, B. N. Rau, prepared an initial draft drawing on several world constitutions. The Drafting Committee's draft was published in February 1948 and debated clause by clause. The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 (celebrated as Constitution Day) and came into force on 26 January 1950, making India a republic.

Legacy

The debates of the Assembly, published as the Constituent Assembly Debates, remain a principal source for interpreting the Indian Constitution, which established universal adult franchise, fundamental rights, and a parliamentary federal system.
