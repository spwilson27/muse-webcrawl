Chemical synapse

A chemical synapse is a junction between two neurons (or a neuron and another cell) at which signals are transmitted by neurotransmitter molecules released into a small gap called the synaptic cleft.

When an action potential reaches the presynaptic terminal, voltage-gated calcium channels open; calcium influx triggers synaptic vesicles to fuse and release neurotransmitter. The molecules diffuse across the ~20–40 nm cleft and bind receptors on the postsynaptic membrane, producing excitatory or inhibitory postsynaptic potentials.

Unlike electrical synapses, chemical synapses are unidirectional and can amplify and modulate signals. They are the predominant synapse type in the mammalian nervous system and the targets of most psychoactive drugs. Common neurotransmitters include glutamate, GABA, dopamine, serotonin and acetylcholine.
