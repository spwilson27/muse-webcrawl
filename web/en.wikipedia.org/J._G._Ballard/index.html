J. G. Ballard

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

James Graham Ballard (15 November 1930 – 19 April 2009) was an English novelist and short-story writer, satirist, and essayist. He first became associated with the New Wave of science fiction for novels such as The Drowned World (1962), but gained wider recognition with the semi-autobiographical Empire of the Sun (1984), about his childhood internment in Japanese-occupied Shanghai.

Works

Ballard's fiction explores the effects of technology, media, and modern landscapes on the human psyche. His controversial novel Crash (1973) explores the eroticism of car crashes and was filmed by David Cronenberg; Empire of the Sun was filmed by Steven Spielberg. Later works include Super-Cannes and Kingdom Come.

Influence

The adjective "Ballardian" — entered in dictionaries for its depiction of dystopian modernity, bleak man-made landscapes, and the psychological effects of technological and social change — testifies to his wide influence on literature, music, and the visual arts.
