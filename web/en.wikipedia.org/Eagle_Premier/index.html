Eagle Premier

Manufacturer: American Motors / Chrysler (Eagle)
Production: 1987–1992
Class: Full-size car
Designer: Giorgetto Giugiaro (Italdesign)
Assembly: Brampton, Ontario, Canada

The Eagle Premier is a full-size executive car that was developed by the American Motors Corporation (AMC) during the 1980s through its partnership with Renault. The model was manufactured by Chrysler, which acquired AMC in 1987, and marketed under the new Eagle brand from the 1988 to 1992 model years.

Development

The Premier was based on the Renault 25's underpinnings but featured a new body styled by Giorgetto Giugiaro at Italdesign and engineered in North America. It was built at the purpose-built Brampton Assembly plant in Ontario. Engines included a 2.5-litre AMC four-cylinder and a 3.0-litre PRV V6, with a longitudinally mounted layout unusual for a front-wheel-drive car of the era.

Legacy

Sales never met expectations, and production ended in December 1991. However, the Premier's architecture became the basis for Chrysler's successful LH platform cars (Chrysler Concorde, Dodge Intrepid, and Eagle Vision) of the 1990s, and the Brampton plant went on to build those and later rear-drive LX cars.
