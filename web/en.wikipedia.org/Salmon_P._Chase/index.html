Salmon P. Chase

Salmon Portland Chase (January 13, 1808 – May 7, 1873) was an American politician and jurist who served as the sixth Chief Justice of the United States from 1864 to 1873. Before that he was Secretary of the Treasury under Abraham Lincoln during the Civil War, a United States Senator and Governor of Ohio.

Anti-slavery lawyer and politician

Born in Cornish, New Hampshire, and raised in Ohio, Chase became known as the "attorney general for fugitive slaves" for defending escaped slaves and abolitionists in Cincinnati. He helped build the Free Soil Party and then the Republican Party, writing influential anti-slavery platforms built on the slogan "Free Soil, Free Labor, Free Men." He served in the Senate (1849–1855) and as Governor of Ohio (1856–1860), and sought the Republican presidential nomination in 1860, losing to Lincoln.

Secretary of the Treasury

As Lincoln's Treasury Secretary (1861–1864), Chase financed the Union war effort: he created the national banking system (National Banking Acts of 1863–64), issued demand notes and the first federal paper currency — the "greenbacks" — under the Legal Tender Acts, and sold bonds to the public through Jay Cooke's network. His portrait appeared on the $1 bill of 1862 and, later, on the $10,000 bill. He resigned in 1864 amid friction with Lincoln and his own presidential ambitions.

Chief Justice

Lincoln appointed Chase Chief Justice in December 1864, expecting antislavery rulings from the bench. Chase presided over the impeachment trial of President Andrew Johnson in 1868, insisting on judicial fairness in a political proceeding — the Senate acquitted by one vote. His court decided Texas v. White (1869), holding the Union indestructible, and the Legal Tender Cases; Chase himself, reversing his wartime position, authored the opinion in Hepburn v. Griswold (1870) striking down legal tender for pre-existing debts, a decision soon overturned. He died in New York City in 1873. The Chase National Bank, ancestor of JPMorgan Chase, was named in his honour.
