Dog Star Man

A still from Stan Brakhage's Dog Star Man

Dog Star Man is a series of short experimental films by the American filmmaker Stan Brakhage, created between 1961 and 1964. The work comprises a prelude and four parts, running about 75 minutes in total, and is regarded as one of the landmarks of avant-garde cinema.

Content

The films follow a man (played by Brakhage) and his dog climbing a snow-covered mountain, intercut with images of the man's wife, his children, cosmic imagery, and close-ups of the human body. Brakhage employed multiple superimpositions, hand-painting, scratching, and rapid editing to evoke cycles of life, death, and rebirth, with the mountain ascent symbolizing human struggle and aspiration.

Reception and legacy

Dog Star Man was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry in 1992 as being “culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant”. Critics have praised its dense, layered imagery as a central achievement of the New American Cinema movement.
