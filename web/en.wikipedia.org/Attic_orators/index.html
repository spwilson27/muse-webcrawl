The Attic orators were considered the greatest orators and rhetoricians of classical antiquity. They are included in the "Canon of Ten", a list compiled by Hellenistic scholars, probably at Alexandria, of the ten greatest Attic speech-writers and logographers. The canon comprises Antiphon, Andocides, Lysias, Isocrates, Isaeus, Demosthenes, Aeschines, Lycurgus, Hyperides, and Dinarchus.

Their speeches, delivered in the law courts and the assembly of democratic Athens, became models of classical prose style and were studied throughout antiquity, in the Byzantine Empire, and again after their recovery in the Renaissance.

The Canon of Ten

  • Antiphon (c. 480–411 BC) — the earliest of the ten, a logographer and sophist executed after the fall of the Four Hundred.
  • Andocides (c. 440–c. 390 BC) — known for On the Mysteries, his defence against charges arising from the mutilation of the Herms.
  • Lysias (c. 445–c. 380 BC) — a metic logographer celebrated for plain, elegant style.
  • Isocrates (436–338 BC) — rhetorical teacher and pamphleteer, famed for long periodic sentences.
  • Isaeus (fl. early 4th century BC) — specialist in inheritance cases.
  • Demosthenes (384–322 BC) — the greatest of the canon, author of the Philippics against Philip II of Macedon.
  • Aeschines (389–314 BC) — Demosthenes' chief rival, known for Against Ctesiphon.
  • Lycurgus (c. 390–324 BC) — administrator of Athenian finances and prosecutor in Against Leocrates.
  • Hyperides (c. 390–322 BC) — opponent of Macedon, executed by Antipater.
  • Dinarchus (c. 361–c. 290 BC) — the last of the ten, a Corinthian metic and logographer.

Characteristics

The Attic orators wrote for the institutions of the Athenian democracy: the popular courts (dikasteria), the council, and the assembly. Professional speechwriters (logographers) composed speeches for clients who were required to plead their own cases. Styles ranged from the austere and plain (Lysias) to the grand and vehement (Demosthenes), and later critics classified Attic prose as the purest standard of the Greek language.
