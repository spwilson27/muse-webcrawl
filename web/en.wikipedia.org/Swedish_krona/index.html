Swedish krona

Image placeholder: modern Swedish krona notes and coins.

The krona (Swedish: [ˈkrûːna]; plural: kronor; sign: kr; code: SEK) is the official currency of Sweden. Krona literally means "crown" in Swedish. It is issued by Sveriges Riksbank, the world's oldest central bank.

History

The krona was introduced in 1873, replacing the riksdaler at par, as a result of the formation of the Scandinavian Monetary Union with Denmark and Norway, which lasted until the First World War. Sweden remained on the gold standard until 1931 and, after periods of pegging to the pound sterling and the US dollar and membership of the European Monetary System, adopted a floating exchange rate in 1992. In a 2003 referendum, Swedish voters rejected adopting the euro, and the krona remains in use.

Coins and banknotes

The krona is subdivided into 100 öre, though öre coins are no longer in circulation. Coins are issued in denominations of 1, 2, 5 and 10 kronor. A new series of banknotes introduced in 2015–2016 features prominent Swedish cultural figures, including Astrid Lindgren (20 kr), Evert Taube (50 kr), Greta Garbo (100 kr), Ingmar Bergman (200 kr), Birger Sjöberg (500 kr) and Dag Hammarskjöld (1000 kr).

Economy

Sweden is among the most cashless societies in the world, with most transactions made by card or mobile payment. See also Sweden.
