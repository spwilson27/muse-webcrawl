Ethnic violence

Ethnic violence
FormsPogroms, riots, ethnic cleansing, genocide
Related conceptsEthnic conflict, communal violence, hate crime

Ethnic violence is violence that is directed against individuals or groups because of their ethnic identity, and violence in conflicts where ethnic divisions are a central organising line. It ranges from hate crimes and communal riots to pogroms, ethnic cleansing, and genocide.

Causes

Scholars debate the roots of ethnic violence. "Primordialist" accounts stress deep-seated group antagonisms, while instrumentalist accounts emphasise elites who mobilise ethnic identity for political gain, and constructivist accounts stress how identities are hardened by institutions, segregation, competition over land and jobs, and state weakness. Security dilemmas — where groups arm out of mutual fear when central authority collapses — are a recurring mechanism in civil wars.

Forms and examples

Historical instances include the pogroms against Jews in the Russian Empire, the communal violence during the Partition of India (1947), the Rwandan genocide (1994), ethnic cleansing during the Bosnian War (1992–95), and violence against the Rohingya in Myanmar. Ethnic riots have also occurred in multi-ethnic cities worldwide, often triggered by rumours, elections, or economic shocks.

Prevention and response

Responses include power-sharing constitutions, federalism, integrated policing, prosecution of incitement, truth and reconciliation commissions, and international intervention. International law criminalises genocide and ethnic cleansing through instruments such as the Genocide Convention and the Rome Statute of the International Criminal Court.
