Anton Ivanovich Denikin (1872–1947) was a Russian lieutenant general and a leading commander of the anti-Bolshevik White movement during the Russian Civil War. He led the Armed Forces of South Russia, whose 1919 offensive came closer than any other White campaign to capturing Moscow before collapsing.

Early career

Born into a modest family—his father was a former serf who rose to major—Denikin graduated from the General Staff Academy and served in the Russo-Japanese War and the First World War, commanding the "Iron Division" and later armies on the Romanian front. After the February Revolution he supported General Kornilov and was arrested following the Kornilov affair.

Civil War

Escaping to the Don region, Denikin helped found the Volunteer Army and succeeded Kornilov in command after his death in April 1918. In 1919 his forces swept north through the Donbas and Ukraine, taking Kharkov, Tsaritsyn, and Kiev, and advanced to Orel, within about 360 km of Moscow. Overextended, disunited, and plagued by pogroms committed by troops under his command (for which his response is criticised), his armies were driven back by the Red Army; in March 1920 the remnants evacuated from Novorossiysk to Crimea. Denikin resigned in favour of Pyotr Wrangel in April 1920.

Exile

Denikin lived in exile in England, Belgium, Hungary, and France, writing his memoirs, The Russian Turmoil. He opposed cooperation with Nazi Germany against the USSR during the Second World War and moved to the United States in 1945, dying in Ann Arbor, Michigan. In 2005 his remains were reburied at Donskoy Monastery in Moscow.
