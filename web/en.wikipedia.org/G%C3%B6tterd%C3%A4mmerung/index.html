Götterdämmerung ("Twilight of the Gods"), WWV 86D, is the last of the four music dramas constituting Richard Wagner's cycle Der Ring des Nibelungen. It premiered at the Bayreuth Festspielhaus on 17 August 1876 as part of the first complete performance of the Ring. The title is Wagner's German translation of Ragnarök, the Norse apocalypse in which the gods perish.

Synopsis

Years after the events of Siegfried, the hero leaves Brünnhilde and journeys to the hall of the Gibichungs, where, drugged into forgetfulness, he is manipulated into betraying her and seizing the ring for Gunther. In the great final act, Siegfried is murdered by Hagen; Brünnhilde, recognising the truth, takes the ring and immolates herself on Siegfried's funeral pyre. The flames consume Valhalla itself, the Rhine overflows, and the ring returns to the Rhinemaidens.

Music

The opera contains the celebrated orchestral interlude "Siegfried's Rhine Journey" and the closing "Immolation Scene". Its long span — roughly four and a half hours of music — and dense web of leitmotifs make it the culminating summary of the cycle's musical language. Wagner's original draft libretto, written in reverse order beginning in 1848 as Siegfrieds Tod, makes Götterdämmerung in part the oldest-conceived portion of the Ring.

Reception

The 1876 premiere, conducted by Hans Richter, completed one of the most ambitious projects in Western music. The work is regularly performed in complete Ring cycles by the world's major opera houses.
