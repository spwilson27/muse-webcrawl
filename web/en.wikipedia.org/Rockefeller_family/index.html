Rockefeller family

The Rockefeller family is an American industrial, political, and banking family that owns one of the world's largest fortunes. The fortune was made in the oil business by John D. Rockefeller Sr. and his brother William A. Rockefeller Jr., founders of Standard Oil.

History

John D. Rockefeller Sr. (1839–1937) built Standard Oil into a near-monopoly before the US Supreme Court ordered its breakup in 1911; he became the first billionaire in the United States and devoted his later life to philanthropy, founding the University of Chicago and the Rockefeller Foundation. His son John D. Rockefeller Jr. built Rockefeller Center and continued the family's charitable work.

Later generations

The third generation included Nelson Rockefeller, Governor of New York and Vice President under Gerald Ford; David Rockefeller, chairman of Chase Manhattan Bank; and John D. Rockefeller III, founder of the Asia Society. The family's philanthropy spans the Rockefeller Foundation, Rockefeller University, the Museum of Modern Art, and Colonial Williamsburg.
