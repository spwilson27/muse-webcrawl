Hominini

Scientific classification
Kingdom: Animalia
Phylum: Chordata
Class: Mammalia
Order: Primates
Family: Hominidae
Subfamily: Homininae
Tribe: Hominini

The Hominini (hominins) form a taxonomic tribe of the subfamily Homininae. Hominini includes the extant genera Homo (humans) and Pan (chimpanzees and bonobos) in some classifications, together with their extinct ancestors and relatives, such as Australopithecus and Paranthropus. In other schemes the tribe is divided into subtribes, with Pan placed in Panina and the bipedal human lineage in Hominina.

Characteristics

Members of the human subtribe Hominina — often called hominins in the narrower sense — are distinguished by habitual bipedal locomotion, reduced canine teeth, and, in later forms, enlarged brains and tool use. The earliest proposed members of the lineage after the split from chimpanzees include Sahelanthropus tchadensis (about 7 million years old), Orrorin tugenensis, and Ardipithecus.

Taxonomy debates

Because humans and chimpanzees share roughly 98–99% of their DNA, some taxonomists have proposed folding Pan into Homo or placing both in the same tribe with separate subtribes. The prevailing convention retains Pan within Hominini and refers to the human branch after the split as hominins.
