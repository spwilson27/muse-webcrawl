Sundance Institute

The Sundance Institute is an American non-profit organization founded by actor and director Robert Redford in 1981 with the mission of fostering independent storytelling in film, theatre, and media. Based in Park City, Utah, with offices in Los Angeles and New York City, the Institute is best known as the presenter of the annual Sundance Film Festival, the largest independent film festival in the United States.

History

Redford founded the Institute in the mountains of Utah with the idea of creating a supportive environment where emerging filmmakers could develop their work away from the commercial pressures of Hollywood. The Institute took its name from Redford's character, the Sundance Kid, in the 1969 film Butch Cassidy and the Sundance Kid. In 1985 the Institute took over management of the struggling U.S. Film Festival, which became the Sundance Film Festival.

Programs

The Institute runs a series of residential labs, fellowships, and grants across feature film, documentary, episodic, and theatre programs. Its Screenwriters and Directors Labs, held each summer at the Sundance Resort, have supported filmmakers such as Quentin Tarantino, Ryan Coogler, and Chloé Zhao. The Documentary Film Program supports nonfiction filmmakers with creative and financial resources, and the Institute also administers funds such as the Sundance Institute Documentary Fund.

Film Festival

Held each January in Park City, Salt Lake City, and at the Sundance Resort, the Sundance Film Festival premieres independent films from around the world and has launched the careers of numerous directors. It is considered one of the most important festivals for independent cinema alongside Cannes, Venice, Berlin, and Toronto.
