Disability insurance is a form of insurance that insures the beneficiary's earned income against the risk that a disability — illness or injury — makes working uncomfortable or impossible. It encompasses paid sick leave, short-term disability benefits, and long-term disability benefits. Policies typically replace a portion (often 45 to 65 percent) of the insured's gross income on a tax-free or taxable basis depending on how premiums are paid.

Definitions of disability

Policies differ in how "disability" is defined. Own-occupation policies pay when the insured cannot perform the duties of his or her specific occupation, while any-occupation policies pay only when the insured cannot work in any occupation for which he or she is reasonably suited. Key contract terms include the elimination (waiting) period before benefits begin and the benefit period over which payments continue.

Public programs

In the United States, the federal government operates Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI) for long-term disabilities, while workers' compensation covers work-related injuries and is administered by individual states. Several states (including California, New York, and New Jersey) run temporary disability insurance programs. Many employers provide group short- and long-term disability coverage as an employee benefit, supplemented by individually purchased policies.
