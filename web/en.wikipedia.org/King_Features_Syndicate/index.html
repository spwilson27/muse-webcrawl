King Features Syndicate

King Features Syndicate, Inc. is an American content syndication company founded in 1914 by newspaper magnate William Randolph Hearst. One of the largest print syndicates in the world, it distributes comic strips, editorial cartoons, columns, puzzles and features to newspapers and digital outlets internationally, and manages licensing for its classic cartoon properties.

History

Hearst consolidated his newspaper features under editor Moses Koenigsberg, who reportedly suggested the name "King" — echoing both Hearst's power and the German word König. King Features drove the golden age of newspaper comics, championing strips such as Krazy Kat, Bringing Up Father, Flash Gordon, Mandrake the Magician and Prince Valiant.

Properties

The syndicate's best-known characters include Popeye (introduced in E. C. Segar's Thimble Theatre in 1929), Blondie, Beetle Bailey, Hagar the Horrible, The Family Circus, Dennis the Menace (US), Mutts and Zits. Beyond print, King Features has licensed its properties for animated series, films and merchandise, including modern reboots of Popeye and The Cuphead Show! (via its characters' wider licensing programmes).

Ownership

King Features remains a unit of Hearst Communications, headquartered in New York City. Its Comics Kingdom platform delivers strips digitally to subscribers and newspaper partners.
