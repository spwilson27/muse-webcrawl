Perylene

Perylene is a polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon with the chemical formula C20H12, composed of two naphthalene units connected by carbon–carbon bonds. It occurs naturally in tar and as the fossil pigment hypericin-like compounds, and appears in sediments as a product of fungal degradation of wood.

PeryleneFormula: C20H12
Molar mass: 252.31 g/mol
Appearance: Brown solid
Melting point: 276–279 °C

Properties and uses

Perylene is a brown solid that displays blue fluorescence. It is used as an organic semiconductor and as the parent compound of the perylene diimide dyes, a class of high-performance pigments and n-type semiconductors widely used in paints, plastics, and organic electronics research.

Occurrence

Perylene's presence in sediments and crude oil is of interest to geochemists because, unlike many other polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, it appears to derive largely from natural biological precursors rather than combustion.
