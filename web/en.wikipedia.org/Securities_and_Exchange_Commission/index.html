U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission

The SEC headquarters in Washington, D.C.

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) is an independent agency of the United States federal government responsible for enforcing federal securities laws, proposing securities rules, and regulating the securities industry, the nation's stock and options exchanges, and other activities and organizations in the electronic securities markets. It was created by the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, and its first chairman was Joseph P. Kennedy Sr.

Mission and structure

The SEC states its mission as protecting investors, maintaining fair, orderly and efficient markets, and facilitating capital formation. The agency is headed by five commissioners appointed by the President with the advice and consent of the Senate; no more than three may belong to the same political party. The chair is designated by the President. Major divisions cover corporation finance, trading and markets, investment management, examinations and enforcement, supported by offices such as the chief accountant and the EDGAR filing system, through which companies submit their disclosures electronically.

History

Before federal regulation, securities were governed largely by state "blue sky laws". The crash of 1929 and Congressional hearings — the Pecora hearings — exposed widespread abuses, leading to the Securities Act of 1933 and the 1934 Act. Over the decades the SEC investigated abuses ranging from the mutual-fund scandals of the 1960s to the Enron and WorldCom accounting frauds and the Bernie Madoff Ponzi scheme, which prompted criticism of the agency's oversight. The Dodd–Frank Act of 2010 expanded its authority over derivatives, hedge funds and credit rating agencies.

Enforcement

The Division of Enforcement investigates violations such as insider trading, accounting fraud and unregistered offerings, and can bring civil actions in federal court or administrative proceedings. The SEC cannot bring criminal charges but refers cases to the Department of Justice. It also runs a whistleblower programme paying awards to those who provide original information leading to sanctions.
