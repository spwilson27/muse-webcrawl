Tsushima Island

Tsushima Island (Japanese: 対馬, Tsushima) is an island of the Japanese archipelago situated in the Korea Strait, roughly halfway between the Japanese mainland and the Korean Peninsula. It is part of Nagasaki Prefecture and is administered as the single city of Tsushima. The island group consists of two main islands and numerous smaller ones, with a population of around 28,000.

History

Tsushima historically served as a stepping-stone for contact between Japan and Korea, and its Sō clan lords handled diplomacy and trade with Joseon Korea throughout the Edo period. The island was the landing point of the Mongol invasions of Japan in 1274 and 1281, where its defenders were overwhelmed, and it gave its name to the decisive Battle of Tsushima in 1905 during the Russo-Japanese War, fought in the nearby straits.

Geography and wildlife

Tsushima has a rugged, deeply indented ria coastline and is largely forested. It is home to the Tsushima leopard cat, a critically endangered subspecies found nowhere else in Japan, and much of the island lies within the Iki-Tsushima Quasi-National Park.
