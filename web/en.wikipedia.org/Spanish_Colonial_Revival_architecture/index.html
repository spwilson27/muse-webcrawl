Spanish Colonial Revival architecture

Years activec. 1915–1931 (and later revivals)
CountryUnited States, especially California and Florida
InfluencesSpanish Baroque, Moorish, Mexican colonial

Spanish Colonial Revival architecture is an architectural stylistic movement in the United States that arose in the early twentieth century, drawing on the architecture of the Spanish colonies in the Americas. It is characterised by stucco wall finishes, low-pitched red tile roofs, arched windows and doorways, decorative ironwork, and asymmetrical façades.

Origins

The style gained national attention at the 1915 Panama–California Exposition in San Diego, where architect Bertram Goodhue's buildings reimagined Spanish and Latin American colonial precedents. The movement spread rapidly through California, Florida, Texas, and the Southwest during the 1920s building boom.

Notable examples

Santa Barbara, California rebuilt its downtown in the style after the 1925 earthquake. Other examples include the Beverly Wilshire Hotel, the Santa Barbara County Courthouse, and numerous houses by architects such as George Washington Smith and Wallace Neff.
