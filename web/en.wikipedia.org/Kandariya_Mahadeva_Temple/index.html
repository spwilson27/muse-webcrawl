Kandariya Mahadeva Temple

Location: Khajuraho, Madhya Pradesh, India

Deity: Shiva

Built: c. 1025–1050 CE

Dynasty: Chandela

The Kandariya Mahadeva Temple ("Great God of the Cave") is the largest and most ornate temple of the Khajuraho Group of Monuments in Madhya Pradesh, India. Built during the reign of the Chandela king Vidyadhara (r. c. 1003–1035) or his immediate successors, around 1025–1050 CE, it is considered the finest example of the Nagara style of temple architecture and is dedicated to the god Shiva.

Architecture

The temple rises from a high platform to a shikhara (spire) about 31 metres (102 ft) tall, its profile composed of ascending smaller spires meant to evoke Mount Kailasa, Shiva's Himalayan abode. The plan follows the classic sequence of entrance porch, hall, great hall, vestibule and sanctum, which houses a marble lingam. Built of sandstone without mortar, the structure is oriented east–west and measures about 31 metres long and 20 metres wide.

Sculpture

The temple is famed for its sculpture: some 872 statues — 226 inside and 646 outside — including celestial maidens (surasundaris), deities, musicians, and the erotic couples (mithuna) for which Khajuraho is famous. The temple forms part of the Western Group of the Khajuraho monuments, designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1986.
