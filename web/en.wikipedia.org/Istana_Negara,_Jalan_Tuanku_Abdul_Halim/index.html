The Istana Negara (National Palace) along Jalan Tuanku Abdul Halim (formerly Jalan Duta) in Kuala Lumpur is the official residence of the Yang di-Pertuan Agong, the King of Malaysia. It replaced the old Istana Negara at Jalan Istana, which had served as the royal palace since 1957.

History

The palace complex occupies a 97.65-hectare site. Construction began in 1976 and was completed in 1980, with the palace formally opened in conjunction with the installation of Sultan Ahmad Shah of Pahang as the seventh Yang di-Pertuan Agong. The first royal occupant, however, only moved in later, and the palace became fully operational as the king's official residence in 2011, when it superseded the old palace in that role.

Architecture

The complex blends traditional Malay architecture with Islamic design elements. Its most recognisable features are its 22 domes, including two large onion-shaped domes, and its main ceremonial hall, the Balairung Seri, used for official functions and royal installations. The grounds include guard posts staffed by members of the Royal Malay Regiment in ceremonial dress.
