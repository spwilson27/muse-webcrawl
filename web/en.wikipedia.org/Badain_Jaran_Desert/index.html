Badain Jaran Desert

The Badain Jaran Desert is a desert in northern China, lying within Inner Mongolia and extending into Gansu and Ningxia. It covers an area of roughly 49,000 square kilometres, making it one of the largest deserts in China, and forms part of the wider Gobi region.

Megadunes

The desert is renowned for its megadunes, among the tallest stationary dunes on Earth. Some reach heights of around 500 metres. Unlike most dunes, they remain remarkably stable in position despite the wind, a stability attributed to moisture within the dune sand supplied by groundwater and meltwater from surrounding mountains.

Lakes

Scattered among the dunes are more than a hundred permanent lakes fed by springs, an unusual feature for such an arid landscape. These lakes give the desert its Mongolian name, often rendered as "mysterious lakes". The largest, and the freshwater and saltwater bodies among the dunes, support small oases used by herders.

Singing sands

Some of the dunes produce a low booming or "singing" sound when sand cascades down their slip faces, a phenomenon also reported in a few other deserts worldwide. In 2024, the Badain Jaran Desert's dunes and lakes were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.
