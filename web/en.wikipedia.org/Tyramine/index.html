Tyramine

Tyramine
Chemical class: trace amine; phenethylamine derivative
Derived from: tyrosine (decarboxylation)
Found in: aged cheese, fermented foods, cured meats

Tyramine is a naturally occurring trace amine derived from the amino acid tyrosine. It acts as a catecholamine releasing agent, prompting the release of norepinephrine from sympathetic nerve terminals, and occurs in many fermented, aged or spoiled foods.

Occurrence and metabolism

Tyramine forms by bacterial decarboxylation of tyrosine during fermentation and ageing, so aged cheeses, cured meats, soy products and some alcoholic drinks can contain significant amounts. In healthy people it is rapidly broken down by monoamine oxidase in the gut and liver.

Clinical significance

In patients taking monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs), dietary tyramine can accumulate and trigger a hypertensive crisis — the "cheese effect" — leading to dietary restrictions. Tyramine has also been investigated, with mixed evidence, as a dietary trigger of migraine.
