Caucasus Mountains

The Caucasus Mountains are a mountain range at the intersection of Asia and Europe, stretching between the Black Sea and the Caspian Sea across the Caucasus region of Russia, Georgia, Azerbaijan and Armenia. The range includes Mount Elbrus, at 5,642 metres the highest peak in Europe and one of the Seven Summits.

Geography

The system divides into the Greater Caucasus in the north — a high, glaciated barrier running about 1,100 km from near Sochi on the Black Sea to near Baku on the Caspian — and the Lesser Caucasus in the south, connected by the Likhi Range and separated by the lowlands of Georgia and the Kura river basin. Other major peaks include Dykh-Tau, Shkhara, Kazbek and Bazardüzü. The range forms the watershed between the Kuban and Terek rivers flowing north and the Rioni and Kura flowing south.

Geology and nature

The Caucasus was uplifted by the collision of the Arabian and Eurasian plates and remains seismically active. Slopes are heavily forested at lower elevations, with alpine meadows above, and the region is one of the world's most significant biodiversity hotspots, home to endemic species and large mammals such as the Caucasian tur, leopard and brown bear. The 2014 Winter Olympics were held at Sochi on the range's western foothills.
