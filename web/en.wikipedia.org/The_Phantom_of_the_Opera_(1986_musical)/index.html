The Phantom of the Opera (1986 musical)

MusicAndrew Lloyd Webber
LyricsCharles Hart; additional lyrics by Richard Stilgoe
BookAndrew Lloyd Webber, Richard Stilgoe
BasisGaston Leroux's novel Le Fantôme de l'Opéra (1910)
Premiere9 October 1986, Her Majesty's Theatre, London

The Phantom of the Opera is a musical with music by Andrew Lloyd Webber, lyrics by Charles Hart and a book by Lloyd Webber and Richard Stilgoe. Based on Gaston Leroux's 1910 French novel, it tells of a beautiful soprano, Christine Daaé, who becomes the obsession of a mysterious, disfigured musical genius living beneath the Paris Opéra House.

Synopsis

Beneath the opera house lives the Phantom, a deformed composer who tutors the young chorus singer Christine, whom he calls his "angel of music," while terrorising the management into casting her in leading roles and keeping Box Five empty for him. Christine falls in love with her childhood friend Raoul, the theatre's new patron, provoking the Phantom's jealousy: he drops the chandelier, kidnaps Christine to his lair beneath the opera, and murders to keep her. In the climax Christine shows the Phantom compassion — kissing him — and he, moved, releases the couple and vanishes, leaving only his mask.

Production history

Directed by Hal Prince and choreographed by Gillian Lynne, the production opened in London's West End in 1986 with Michael Crawford as the Phantom and Sarah Brightman as Christine, and on Broadway in 1988. The Broadway production ran for 35 years, closing in April 2023 after 13,981 performances — the longest run in Broadway history. The London production closed in 2020 and reopened in 2021 in a reduced staging.

Reception and legacy

The musical has been staged in dozens of countries and languages, seen by well over 100 million people, and grossed billions of dollars worldwide; it won the Olivier and Tony Awards for Best Musical. Signature songs include "The Music of the Night," "Think of Me," "All I Ask of You" and the title song. A film adaptation directed by Joel Schumacher was released in 2004, and a sequel, Love Never Dies, opened in London in 2010.
