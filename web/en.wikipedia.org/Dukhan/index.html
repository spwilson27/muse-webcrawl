Dukhan

CountryQatar
MunicipalityAl-Shahaniya
SignificanceSite of Qatar's first oil discovery

Dukhan (Arabic: دخان) is a city in the western municipality of Al-Shahaniya in Qatar, on the western coast of the peninsula about 80 kilometres west of Doha. It is owned and administered by QatarEnergy and is the centre of Qatar's onshore oil industry.

History

Oil was struck at Dukhan in 1939–1940 by the Petroleum Development (Qatar) company, but development was halted during the Second World War; exports began in 1949. The Dukhan field, a large anticline running along the west coast, became the foundation of Qatar's petroleum economy before the offshore and North Dome gas fields were developed.

The town

Dukhan is a closed company town serving field workers and their families, with housing, schools, a hospital, recreational facilities and the Dukhan English School. Access is restricted. Nearby attractions include the Zekreet Peninsula, the film city set, and Richard Serra's East–West/West–East sculpture in the surrounding desert.

Dukhan field

The field produces crude oil and associated gas from Jurassic Arab reservoirs. QatarEnergy operates production, water injection and gas processing facilities there, and the field remains one of Qatar's principal onshore producing assets.
