Midnight Mass

TypeChristian liturgy
OccasionChristmas Eve / Christmas Day
First recorded5th century, Rome

Midnight Mass is a tradition in the Roman Catholic Church and in many other Christian denominations of celebrating Mass (or a Eucharistic service) on Christmas Eve, beginning at or shortly before midnight. It is the first liturgy of Christmas Day, marking the beginning of the celebration of the Nativity of Jesus.

History

The custom traces to Rome, where by the 5th century the Pope celebrated a Mass at cockcrow in the Basilica of St Mary Major, which housed a relic venerated as the crib of the Christ child. The Christmas liturgy eventually expanded into three Masses — at midnight, at dawn, and during the day — each with its own readings and prayers, a sequence retained in the traditional Roman Rite. Midnight Mass became one of the most widely attended services of the year, including by people who rarely attend church otherwise.

Practice

Services typically feature carols by candlelight, incense, and the blessing of the crib. The Pope's Midnight Mass in St Peter's Basilica is broadcast worldwide; in recent years it has often been celebrated earlier in the evening for practical reasons. Anglican, Lutheran and Methodist churches hold similar late-night Christmas Eve services, and the term is also used for the 2021 Netflix horror miniseries Midnight Mass created by Mike Flanagan.
