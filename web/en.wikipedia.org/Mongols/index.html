The Mongols are an East Asian ethnic group native to Mongolia, Inner Mongolia in China, and parts of Russia. They speak Mongolic languages, principally Mongolian, and share a history centred on the nomadic peoples of the Mongolian Plateau.

History

In 1206 Temüjin, titled Genghis Khan, united the Mongol and Turkic tribes of the steppe and founded the Mongol Empire, which became the largest contiguous land empire in history, stretching from the Pacific to Eastern Europe. After the death of Möngke Khan the empire fragmented into khanates including the Yuan dynasty in China, the Ilkhanate in Persia, the Golden Horde, and the Chagatai Khanate.

Culture

Traditional Mongol life revolved around pastoral nomadism, the portable felt dwelling known as the ger, and herds of sheep, goats, horses, cattle, and camels. The Naadam festival celebrates wrestling, horse racing, and archery. The Secret History of the Mongols is the foundational literary work of the culture.

Modern Mongols

Today most ethnic Mongols live in the independent state of Mongolia and in China's Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region, with smaller communities in Buryatia and Kalmykia in Russia.
