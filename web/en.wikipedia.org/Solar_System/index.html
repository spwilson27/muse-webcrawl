The Solar System is the gravitationally bound system of the Sun and the objects that orbit it. It formed 4.6 billion years ago from the gravitational collapse of a giant interstellar molecular cloud. The vast majority of the system's mass (99.86%) is in the Sun; most of the rest is in Jupiter.

Structure

Eight planets orbit the Sun: the four inner terrestrial planets (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars), composed mainly of rock and metal; and the four outer giant planets — gas giants Jupiter and Saturn and ice giants Uranus and Neptune. Between Mars and Jupiter lies the asteroid belt; beyond Neptune lie the Kuiper belt (home to Pluto and other dwarf planets), the scattered disc, and the spherical Oort cloud of comets, which may extend a quarter of the way to the nearest star.

The system also contains hundreds of moons, millions of asteroids and comets, and interplanetary dust, all embedded in the solar wind streaming from the Sun out to the heliopause.

Formation and evolution

In the nebular hypothesis, a rotating cloud of gas and dust collapsed under gravity, flattening into a protoplanetary disk; the Sun ignited at the centre while planets accreted from the disk — rocky worlds inside the frost line, giants beyond it. Collisions in the early system, including the impact believed to have formed the Moon, shaped the planets we see. In about 5 billion years the Sun will become a red giant, engulfing the inner planets.

Exploration

Every planet has been visited by spacecraft; humans have landed only on the Moon. Probes have landed on Mars, Venus, Titan and asteroids and comets, and Voyager 1 and 2 have entered interstellar space.
