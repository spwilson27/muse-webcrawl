Sahrawi people

The Sahrawi people, or Sahraouis (Arabic: صحراويون, Spanish: Saharauis), are an ethnic group indigenous to the western part of the Sahara desert, encompassing Western Sahara, southern Morocco, much of Mauritania, and the extreme southwest of Algeria. They are of mixed Berber, Arab, and sub-Saharan African descent, speak the Hassaniya dialect of Arabic, and are overwhelmingly Sunni Muslim. Their name derives from the Arabic word for "desert".

History

Sahrawi society developed from the intermingling of indigenous Berber (Sanhaja) tribes with Arab Maqil tribes who migrated into the region from the 13th century onwards, bringing the Hassaniya language. Traditionally nomadic camel herders, Sahrawis organised into tribal confederations with a stratified social order. Spain colonised the territory as Spanish Sahara from 1884 until 1975–76, when it withdrew under the Madrid Accords, handing administration to Morocco and Mauritania despite the International Court of Justice's finding that the Sahrawi people had a right to self-determination.

Western Sahara conflict

The Polisario Front, founded in 1973, proclaimed the Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic (SADR) in 1976 and waged war against Morocco and Mauritania. Mauritania withdrew in 1979; Morocco and Polisario accepted a UN-brokered ceasefire in 1991 that promised a referendum on independence, which has never been held. Morocco controls most of the territory west of a fortified sand wall (the berm), while Polisario administers the eastern strip and refugee camps near Tindouf, Algeria, where well over 100,000 Sahrawis have lived for decades. The SADR is recognised by dozens of states and is a member of the African Union.

Culture

Sahrawi culture is noted for its oral poetry in Hassaniya, elaborate tea ceremony, distinctive dress — the men's blue or white daraa robe and black turban, and the women's colourful malhafa veil — and musical traditions. Many Sahrawis also speak Spanish, a legacy of the colonial period, and Spanish remains a language of education and media in the refugee camps.
