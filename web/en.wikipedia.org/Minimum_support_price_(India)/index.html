Minimum support price (MSP) is a form of market intervention by the Government of India to insure agricultural producers against sharp falls in farm prices. The prices are announced at the beginning of the sowing season on the recommendations of the Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices.

Coverage and procurement

MSP is fixed for 22 mandated crops, including paddy, wheat, pulses, oilseeds and cotton, while sugarcane is covered by a Fair and Remunerative Price. In practice, large-scale public procurement at MSP—carried out by the Food Corporation of India and state agencies—is concentrated in rice and wheat, mainly in states such as Punjab, Haryana and Madhya Pradesh, and underpins the public distribution system.

Debate

Farmer organisations have long demanded that MSP be made a legal guarantee and calculated using the Swaminathan committee formula (comprehensive cost plus 50 percent). The demand became central to the 2020–2021 Indian farmers' protest. Economists debate its fiscal cost, effects on cropping patterns, groundwater use and market distortion.
