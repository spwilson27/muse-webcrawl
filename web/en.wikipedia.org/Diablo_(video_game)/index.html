Diablo (video game)

DeveloperBlizzard North
PublisherBlizzard Entertainment
DesignerDavid Brevik
ReleaseJanuary 3, 1997
GenreAction role-playing, hack and slash

Diablo is an action role-playing video game developed by Blizzard North and released by Blizzard Entertainment in January 1997. Set in the fictional Kingdom of Khanduras, beneath the town of Tristram, the player controls a lone hero battling to rid the world of Diablo, the Lord of Terror.

Gameplay

The player chooses from three character classes — the Warrior, Rogue, and Sorcerer — and descends through sixteen procedurally generated dungeon levels beneath Tristram Cathedral, culminating in a confrontation with Diablo. Combat is conducted in real time with mouse clicks, and players collect gold, weapons, armor, and magical items dropped by monsters.

Development

Diablo was conceived by David Brevik and developed at Condor Games, which Blizzard acquired and renamed Blizzard North. The game was originally planned as a turn-based title before shifting to real-time combat. Its dark gothic art style and atmospheric score by Matt Uelmen became hallmarks of the series.

Reception and legacy

The game was a commercial and critical success, selling over 2.5 million copies. It spawned the expansion Hellfire, the sequel Diablo II (2000), and a long-running franchise that helped define the action role-playing genre. Its online multiplayer through Blizzard's Battle.net service was pioneering for the genre.
