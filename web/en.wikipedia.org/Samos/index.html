Samos (Greek: Σάμος) is a Greek island in the eastern Aegean Sea, separated from the western coast of Turkey by the narrow Mycale Strait, in places less than two kilometres wide. It is the capital island of the regional unit of Samos within the North Aegean region, and its principal town is Vathy, also known as Samos town.

The island is famed in antiquity as the birthplace of the mathematician and philosopher Pythagoras, the philosopher Epicurus, and the astronomer Aristarchus of Samos, who first proposed a heliocentric model of the solar system. Its sanctuary of Hera, the Heraion, together with the ancient fortified port of Pythagoreion, was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1992.

History

Samos was colonised by Ionians around the 11th century BC and rose to prominence as a maritime and trading power in the 6th century BC, when under the tyrant Polycrates it built a great fleet and works such as the Tunnel of Eupalinos, an aqueduct tunnel over a kilometre long driven through Mount Kastro. The island later passed through Athenian, Hellenistic, Roman, Byzantine, Genoese and Ottoman hands before joining the modern Greek state in 1912–1913.

Geography

The island covers about 477 square kilometres and is dominated by two mountains: Mount Kerkis in the west, rising to 1,434 metres, and Mount Ampelos in the centre. Samos is one of the greenest and most fertile Aegean islands, known for its vineyards producing the celebrated sweet Muscat wine, as well as olive oil and tobacco.
