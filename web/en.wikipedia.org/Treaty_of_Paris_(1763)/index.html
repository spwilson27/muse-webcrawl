Treaty of Paris (1763)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Treaty of Paris, also known as the Treaty of 1763, was signed on 10 February 1763 by the kingdoms of Great Britain, France and Spain, with Portugal in agreement, after Great Britain and Prussia's victory over France and Austria in the Seven Years' War. Together with the Treaty of Hubertusburg, it ended the war and marked the beginning of an era of British dominance outside Europe.

Terms

France ceded to Britain its North American possessions east of the Mississippi River, including Canada and Acadia, retaining only the fishing islands of Saint Pierre and Miquelon. France also ceded Louisiana to Spain (by the separate Treaty of Fontainebleau, 1762) as compensation for Spain's loss of Florida to Britain. In India, France recovered its trading posts but recognised British ascendancy; in the Caribbean, Britain returned Martinique and Guadeloupe to France while keeping Grenada and other islands.

Consequences

The treaty reshaped the global colonial map and removed the French threat to Britain's American colonies — a change that, by removing the colonists' need for British protection and saddling Britain with war debt, contributed to the tensions that led to the American Revolution.
