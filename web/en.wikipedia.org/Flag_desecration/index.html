Flag desecration

Flag desecration is the set of various acts that violate flag protocol. Definitions vary by country and by the flag in question, but commonly include burning, trampling, defacing, or otherwise destroying or dishonouring a national flag, usually as a form of political protest. Laws on the subject differ widely: some countries criminalise desecration of their own flag or of foreign flags, while in others, notably the United States, flag desecration is constitutionally protected symbolic speech.

United States

The first U.S. state flag-desecration statutes appeared in the late nineteenth century, and a federal Flag Desecration Act followed in 1968, during the Vietnam War era, when flag burning became a prominent protest gesture. In Street v. New York (1969) the Supreme Court set limits on punishing spoken contempt for the flag, and in Texas v. Johnson (1989) the Court held, 5–4, that Gregory Lee Johnson's burning of an American flag during a protest was protected under the First Amendment, invalidating flag-desecration laws in 48 states. The decision was reaffirmed in United States v. Eichman (1990), which struck down the federal Flag Protection Act of 1989. Repeated efforts to amend the Constitution to permit a ban — the Flag Desecration Amendment — have passed the House of Representatives several times but failed in the Senate, most narrowly by a single vote in 2006. Paradoxically, the U.S. Flag Code prescribes burning as the dignified method for disposing of worn-out flags.

International practice

Several countries prohibit insulting their national symbols: in Germany, publicly disparaging or damaging the federal flag (and, since 2020, flags of the European Union and foreign states in some circumstances) can be punished under the criminal code; France penalises desecration of the flag at events organised by public authorities; China and several other states impose prison terms for flag desecration. Other countries, such as Denmark, permit burning their own flag while protecting foreign flags for diplomatic reasons. Flag desecration frequently occurs in international protests, making it a recurring subject in debates over free expression, national honour, and the limits of symbolic speech.
