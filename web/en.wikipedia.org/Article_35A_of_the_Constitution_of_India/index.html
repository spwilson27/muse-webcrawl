Article 35A of the Constitution of India

Article 35A of the Constitution of India was an article that empowered the Jammu and Kashmir state legislature to define "permanent residents" of the state and to provide special rights and privileges to those permanent residents, including in employment under the state government, acquisition of immovable property, settlement, and scholarships and other aid.

History

The article was inserted into the Constitution by the Constitution (Application to Jammu and Kashmir) Order of 14 May 1954, issued by President Rajendra Prasad under Article 370 with the concurrence of the government of Jammu and Kashmir. It was never presented before Parliament as an amendment under Article 368, which led to legal challenges over its validity.

Provisions

Article 35A shielded laws defining permanent residents from being challenged on the ground that they were inconsistent with the fundamental rights of other Indian citizens, such as the rights to equality and residence guaranteed by Articles 14, 19 and 21.

Abrogation

In August 2019, the Government of India issued constitutional orders that rendered Article 35A inoperative as part of the revocation of the special status of Jammu and Kashmir under Article 370. The Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019, subsequently divided the state into the union territories of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh, and the permanent resident regime was replaced by a new domicile framework in 2020.
