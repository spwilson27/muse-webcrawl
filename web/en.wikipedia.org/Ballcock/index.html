Ballcock

A ballcock (also balltap or float valve) is a mechanism for filling water tanks, such as those found in flush toilets, while avoiding overflow. It uses a float — traditionally a hollow ball on a lever arm — that rises with the water level and closes the inlet valve when the tank is full.

Operation

As water drains during a flush, the float falls and opens the valve; as the tank refills, the float rises until the lever shuts off the flow. The invention is commonly attributed to Thomas Crapper's era of Victorian plumbing improvements, though float-valve designs are older.

Modern designs

Contemporary fill valves often replace the ball float with a compact cup float sliding on the valve body, reducing noise and space while serving the same function.
