Black panther

A melanistic leopard; its spots are visible under close inspection.

A black panther is the melanistic colour variant of the leopard (Panthera pardus) and the jaguar (Panthera onca). Black panthers of both species have excess black pigments (melanin), but their typical spotted markings — rosettes — are still present, especially visible in bright light or infrared photography. "Black panther" is not a distinct species.

Distribution

Melanism is caused by a recessive allele in leopards and a dominant allele in jaguars. Melanistic leopards occur most frequently in the dense tropical rainforests of Southeast Asia, including the Malay Peninsula and Java, where the dark coat may offer camouflage advantages in low-light forest habitat; they are rarer in Africa and India. Melanistic jaguars occur in Central and South America. In the United States, "black panther" is colloquially applied to the cougar (puma), although no confirmed melanistic cougar specimen has ever been recorded; the Florida panther is a normally coloured cougar subspecies.

Behaviour and breeding

Black panthers share the behaviour of their parent species: solitary, nocturnal ambush predators feeding on deer, monkeys, birds, and other animals depending on habitat. Melanistic and normally coloured cubs can occur in the same litter. Black leopards have occasionally been reported to be more aggressive, but such claims are not well supported scientifically.

In culture

The black panther features in literature — most famously as Bagheera in Rudyard Kipling's The Jungle Book — and gave its name to the Black Panther Party and the Marvel Comics superhero Black Panther.
