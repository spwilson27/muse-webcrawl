Italian Grand Prix

Racing at the Autodromo Nazionale Monza
Venue: Autodromo Nazionale Monza
First held: 1921
Most wins (driver): Michael Schumacher / Lewis Hamilton (5)

The Italian Grand Prix (Italian: Gran Premio d'Italia) is one of the longest-running events in motor racing and a fixture of the Formula One World Championship since its inaugural season in 1950. It has been held at the Autodromo Nazionale Monza in nearly every year, the notable exception being 1980, when it moved to Imola.

History

The first Italian Grand Prix was held in 1921 at Brescia. In 1922 racing moved to the newly built Monza circuit, purpose-constructed in the Royal Park north of Milan. The race was marred by tragedy in its early decades — including the 1928 crash that killed Emilio Materassi and 27 spectators, and the 1961 accident in which Wolfgang von Trips and 15 spectators died — leading to the abandonment of the combined banked oval after 1961.

Monza

Monza is famous as the fastest circuit in Formula One, earning the nickname "Temple of Speed." Its long straights and the passionate Ferrari-supporting crowd, the tifosi, make the Italian Grand Prix one of the sport's signature events. Michael Schumacher and Lewis Hamilton share the record of five victories each; Ferrari is the most successful constructor at its home race.

Notable races

Memorable editions include Peter Gethin's win in 1971 (the fastest race in F1 history for over three decades), Sebastian Vettel's first victory in 2008 for Toro Rosso, Pierre Gasly's surprise 2020 win for AlphaTauri, and Daniel Ricciardo's 2021 victory for McLaren.
