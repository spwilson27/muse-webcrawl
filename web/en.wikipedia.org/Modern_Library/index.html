Modern Library

FoundersAlbert Boni, Horace Liveright

The Modern Library is an American book publishing imprint, founded in 1917 by Albert Boni and Horace Liveright, dedicated to inexpensive reprints of classic works of English-language and translated European literature. It was sold to Random House in 1925 and became one of the best-known classics series in the United States.

History

The series began with twelve titles and grew to hundreds, distinguished by its torchbearer colophon, designed by Rockwell Kent. Under Random House the Modern Library expanded to include hardcover "Giants" editions and, later, paperback lines. Its list included works by Joyce, Faulkner, Woolf, Proust, and Mann.

Modern Library's 100 Best Novels

In 1998 the Modern Library published a widely publicised list of the 100 best English-language novels of the 20th century, chosen by a board of writers and scholars. James Joyce's Ulysses ranked first, followed by The Great Gatsby and A Portrait of the Artist as a Young Man. A separate readers' poll produced a markedly different list. A companion list covered the 100 best nonfiction books of the century.

Legacy

The imprint continues under Penguin Random House, publishing classics in hardcover and paperback editions.
