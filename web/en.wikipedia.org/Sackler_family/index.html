Sackler family

The Sackler name appeared on museum and university buildings worldwide before institutions began removing it.

The Sackler family is an American family known for founding and owning the pharmaceutical company Purdue Pharma, and for their philanthropy toward universities, museums, and medical institutions. The family fortune derives largely from Purdue Pharma, maker of the prescription painkiller OxyContin (oxycodone), whose aggressive marketing became a central focus of the opioid epidemic in the United States.

History

The family's business began with three brothers — Arthur, Mortimer, and Raymond Sackler — sons of Jewish immigrants from Eastern Europe who settled in Brooklyn, New York. All three became psychiatrists. In 1952 they purchased the Purdue Frederick Company, a small pharmaceutical firm that later became Purdue Pharma. Arthur Sackler, a pioneer of medical advertising, built much of the family's early fortune through pharmaceutical marketing and the promotion of the tranquilizers Librium and Valium.

Purdue Pharma introduced OxyContin in 1996 and marketed it as a less addictive pain treatment. The drug generated tens of billions of dollars in revenue. As evidence mounted that OxyContin contributed to widespread addiction and overdose deaths, Purdue and members of the Sackler family faced thousands of lawsuits from states, cities, tribes, and individuals.

Bankruptcy and settlement

Purdue Pharma filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in 2019. Negotiated settlements proposed that Sackler family members contribute billions of dollars toward opioid abatement in exchange for releases from civil liability. In June 2024, the United States Supreme Court ruled in Harrington v. Purdue Pharma that the bankruptcy code did not permit releases shielding the Sacklers from future claims without claimants' consent, prompting renegotiation. A revised settlement framework announced in 2025 contemplated Sackler payments of roughly $6.5–7 billion over time.

Philanthropy and name removal

For decades the family donated to institutions including the Metropolitan Museum of Art, the Louvre, the British Museum, Yale University, and Oxford University. Beginning in 2019, amid protests led by artist Nan Goldin and the group P.A.I.N., many institutions announced they would no longer accept Sackler donations, and several — including the Met and the Louvre — removed the Sackler name from galleries and wings.
