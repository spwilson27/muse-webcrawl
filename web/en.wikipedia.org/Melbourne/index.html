Melbourne

Melbourne (Boonwurrung/Woiwurrung: Naarm) is the capital and most populous city of the Australian state of Victoria, and the second-most populous city in Australia, with a metropolitan population of over five million. It lies on the northern shore of Port Phillip Bay, centred on the Yarra River, and is known for its Victorian-era architecture, laneways, cultural institutions and sporting events.

History

The area had been inhabited by Aboriginal peoples, including the Wurundjeri and Boonwurrung of the Kulin nation, for tens of thousands of years. A permanent European settlement was founded in 1835 by settlers from Tasmania led by John Batman and John Pascoe Fawkner, and the city was named in 1837 for William Lamb, 2nd Viscount Melbourne, then British prime minister. The Victorian gold rush of the 1850s transformed Melbourne into one of the richest and largest cities in the British Empire — "Marvellous Melbourne". It served as Australia's federal capital from Federation in 1901 until Canberra took over in 1927.

Culture and sport

Melbourne is regarded as Australia's cultural and sporting capital: it hosts the Australian Open tennis, the Formula One Australian Grand Prix, the Melbourne Cup horse race and the AFL Grand Final at the Melbourne Cricket Ground, and it hosted the 1956 Olympic Games. Its tram network is the largest in the world. The city was repeatedly ranked among the world's most liveable cities in the 2010s.
