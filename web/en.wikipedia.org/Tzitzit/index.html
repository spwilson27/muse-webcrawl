Tzitzit

Tzitzit (Hebrew: ציצית) are the specially knotted ritual fringes, or tassels, worn by observant Jews on the corners of garments, in fulfilment of commandments in the Torah (Numbers 15:38 and Deuteronomy 22:12).

The commandment

The Torah requires fringes on the four corners of a garment, including a thread of blue (tekhelet), so that the wearer will look at them and remember the commandments. The fringes are typically attached to the tallit (prayer shawl) and to the tallit katan, a four-cornered undershirt worn daily by many Orthodox Jewish men and boys.

Construction

Each corner has four threads passed through a hole, doubled to make eight strands, tied with a series of double knots and windings; traditional counts of the windings correspond to the numerical value of the word tzitzit or to the 613 commandments. The blue tekhelet dye, historically derived from a marine snail, was lost for centuries and its identity remains debated.
