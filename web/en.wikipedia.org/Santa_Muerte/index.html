Santa Muerte

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Santa Muerte (Spanish for "Saint Death"), formally Nuestra Señora de la Santa Muerte, is a Mexican folk saint and female deity venerated as a personification of death. She is typically depicted as a female skeletal figure draped in a robe, holding a scythe and a globe. Devotees petition her for healing, protection, prosperity, and a safe passage to the afterlife. Estimates of her following have run into the millions in Mexico, parts of Central America, and the United States, making the cult one of the fastest-growing religious movements in the Americas.

Origins

The cult combines elements of pre-Hispanic Aztec and Maya death deities such as Mictecacihuatl with Spanish Catholic traditions of venerating death and the Virgin Mary. Public devotion emerged into view in the late 20th century, notably in Mexico City's Tepito neighbourhood, where a prominent public shrine was erected in 2001. Unlike recognised saints, Santa Muerte has never been canonised, and the Roman Catholic Church condemns her veneration as incompatible with Catholic teaching.

Devotion

Shrines and altars are dressed with candles, flowers, fruit, cigarettes, and alcohol; the colours of her robes and candles carry symbolic meanings — white for purity, red for love, gold for wealth, black for protection against enemies. Devotees include many who feel marginalised by mainstream institutions — the poor, sex workers, LGBT people — as well as, controversially, members of criminal organisations, an association that has led authorities in Mexico to demolish roadside shrines. Scholars caution that the great majority of devotees are law-abiding, and that the saint is valued precisely because she is believed not to judge her petitioners.
