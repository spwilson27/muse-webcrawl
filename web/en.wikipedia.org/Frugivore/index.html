Frugivore

A frugivore is an animal that thrives mostly on raw fruits or succulent fruit-like produce of plants such as roots, shoots, nuts and seeds. Frugivores are a type of herbivore, and many of them also serve as important seed dispersers for the plants whose fruit they eat.

Diet and ecology

Fruits are rich in sugars and water but often poor in protein, so many frugivores supplement their diet with insects, leaves, or other foods. Obligate frugivores include many species of fruit bats, numerous birds such as toucans, hornbills, and birds of paradise, and some primates. Facultative frugivores eat fruit opportunistically alongside other food sources.

Seed dispersal

Because seeds often pass through a frugivore's digestive tract unharmed, frugivores move seeds away from the parent plant, reducing competition and helping colonize new areas. This mutualism has shaped the evolution of fleshy fruits, which advertise ripeness through colour and scent.

Conservation

The decline of large frugivores through hunting and habitat loss can reduce seed dispersal for large-seeded trees, with knock-on effects for forest regeneration and carbon storage.
