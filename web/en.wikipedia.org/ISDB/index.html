Integrated Services Digital Broadcasting (ISDB; Japanese: 統合デジタル放送サービス) is a Japanese broadcasting standard for digital television (DTV) and digital radio. It replaced NTSC-J and the MUSE Hi-vision analogue HDTV system in Japan. Since 2003, its terrestrial form (ISDB-T) has spread outside Japan, notably to Brazil — whose modified version, ISDB-Tb (SBTVD), was adopted across most of Latin America — and to the Philippines and several other countries.

Overview

ISDB was standardized by Japan's Association of Radio Industries and Businesses (ARIB), building on NHK research. The standard uses band-segmented transmission OFDM (BST-OFDM), in which a channel is divided into 13 segments that can be allocated flexibly between services. One segment ("1seg") can carry a low-resolution service for mobile phones and portable receivers, a feature showcased when mobile TV became popular in Japan. Video coding is MPEG-2 in the original Japanese services, while Brazil's ISDB-Tb adopted MPEG-4 AVC (H.264) and the Ginga middleware for interactivity.

Variants

  • ISDB-T — terrestrial television, launched in the Tokyo region in December 2003; analogue broadcasts in Japan ended in 2011–2012.
  • ISDB-S — satellite broadcasting, used for BS digital services (and ISDB-S3 for 4K/8K).
  • ISDB-C — cable television.
  • ISDB-Tb — the Brazilian international variant, adopted from 2007 by most South American countries and others.
