A surface-to-air missile (SAM), also known as a ground-to-air missile or surface-to-air guided weapon, is a missile designed to be launched from the ground or a ship to destroy aircraft, helicopters, cruise missiles, unmanned aerial vehicles, and, in some systems, ballistic missiles. SAMs range from small shoulder-launched man-portable air-defense systems (MANPADS) to large long-range strategic systems.

History

Development began during the Second World War, notably with Germany's Wasserfall project, as anti-aircraft artillery proved inadequate against high, fast aircraft. The first operational systems appeared in the 1950s, including the Soviet S-75 Dvina (SA-2 Guideline), which famously shot down a U-2 spyplane over the Soviet Union in 1960, and the American Nike Ajax and Nike Hercules. SAMs played major roles in the Vietnam War, the Arab–Israeli wars of 1967 and 1973, the Falklands War, and later conflicts, forcing aircraft to fly lower, adopt electronic countermeasures, and employ specialized suppression-of-enemy-air-defenses missions.

Types

  • Long-range systems, such as the Russian S-400 and American Patriot, defend wide areas and may engage ballistic missiles.
  • Medium-range systems, such as Buk and NASAMS, protect field formations and infrastructure.
  • Short-range systems and MANPADS, such as the FIM-92 Stinger and 9K38 Igla, use infrared homing and protect troops at low altitude.
  • Naval SAMs, such as the Standard Missile family, are launched from warships, frequently from vertical launching systems.

Guidance and operation

A SAM battery typically combines surveillance and fire-control radars, a command post, and launchers. Guidance methods include command guidance, semi-active radar homing, active radar homing, infrared homing, and combinations thereof. Proximity fuzes detonate the warhead near the target, projecting a fragmentation pattern. Modern integrated air defense systems network multiple sensors and weapons, complicating attacks against them.

Countermeasures

Aircraft defend against SAMs with radar warning receivers, chaff, flares, electronic jamming, evasive maneuvering, stealth shaping, and anti-radiation missiles designed to home on air-defense radars.
