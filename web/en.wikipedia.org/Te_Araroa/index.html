Te Araroa

Te Araroa (Māori for "The Long Pathway") is New Zealand's national long-distance walking trail, stretching roughly 3,000 kilometres from Cape Reinga at the northern tip of the North Island to Bluff at the southern end of the South Island. Officially opened on 3 December 2011, it links tracks, roads, river corridors, and beaches into a continuous route through the country.

Route

The trail is divided into about 160 sections. In the North Island it follows Ninety Mile Beach, passes through Auckland and Wellington, crosses the Tararua Range, and includes a canoe section down the Whanganui River. In the South Island it traverses the Richmond Range, crosses the Southern Alps via passes such as Waiau Pass, follows the Rangitata and other braided rivers, passes through Queenstown and the Mavora Lakes, and finishes at Stirling Point in Bluff.

Development

The concept of a national trail was proposed by journalist Graeme Dingle in the 1970s and advanced by the Te Araroa Trust, founded in 1994 under the leadership of Geoff Chapple, who walked the route and campaigned for its completion. The trail is maintained through partnerships with the Department of Conservation, local councils, and thousands of volunteers.

Walking the trail

Through-walkers typically take four to five months, walking southbound from October or November to avoid alpine snow. Several hundred people complete the full trail each season, and day and section walkers number in the tens of thousands. The trail is one of New Zealand's Great Walks-adjacent attractions, though unlike the Great Walks it is a through-route rather than a single booked circuit.
