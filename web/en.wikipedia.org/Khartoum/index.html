Khartoum

Khartoum (Arabic: الخرطوم, romanized: al-Khurṭūm) is the capital and largest city of Sudan. With a population of about 6.3 million in its metropolitan area — the "triple city" of Khartoum, Omdurman and Khartoum North (Bahri) — it lies at the confluence of the White Nile, flowing north from Lake Victoria, and the Blue Nile, flowing west from Lake Tana in Ethiopia. The two rivers join to form the River Nile, which continues northward through Egypt to the Mediterranean Sea.

History

Khartoum was founded in 1821–23 as an outpost of the Egyptian army after Muhammad Ali's conquest of Sudan, and grew into a centre of the trade in slaves and ivory. Under Egyptian rule it became the seat of the governor-general. During the Mahdist War, Sudanese forces under Muhammad Ahmad, the Mahdi, besieged the city for ten months; Khartoum fell on 26 January 1885 and the British general Charles Gordon was killed. The Mahdist state ruled from Omdurman until 1898, when Lord Kitchener's Anglo-Egyptian army won the Battle of Omdurman and re-established Khartoum as the capital of Anglo-Egyptian Sudan, laying out a new city whose street plan legendarily mimics the Union Jack.

Khartoum became capital of the independent Republic of Sudan in 1956. It hosted the 1967 Arab League summit that issued the Khartoum Resolution. Oil wealth in the 2000s produced a building boom. In April 2023 Khartoum became the main battlefield of the war between the Sudanese Armed Forces and the Rapid Support Forces, causing catastrophic destruction, mass displacement of its population, and the evacuation of foreign embassies.

Geography and climate

The city occupies a flat, arid plain where the Blue Nile (swift and silt-laden) meets the White Nile (broader and clearer); their contrasting waters run side by side for some distance. Tuti Island, at the confluence, is an old farming community now linked by a suspension bridge. Khartoum has a hot desert climate, among the hottest major cities in the world, with temperatures often exceeding 40 °C and a brief rainy season in July–September. The name is popularly derived from the Arabic for "elephant's trunk", describing the curve of the Nile.

Landmarks

  • Sudan National Museum, with rescued temples from Nubia
  • Presidential Palace (former Governor-General's Palace) on the Blue Nile
  • The confluence (al-Muqran) and Tuti Bridge
  • University of Khartoum (founded 1902 as Gordon Memorial College)
  • Khartoum International Airport
  • Farouk Cemetery and the Mahdi's Tomb in Omdurman
