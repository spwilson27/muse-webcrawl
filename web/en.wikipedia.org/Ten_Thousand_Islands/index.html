Ten Thousand Islands

The Ten Thousand Islands are a chain of islands and mangrove islets off the coast of southwest Florida, between Cape Romano (near Marco Island) and the mouth of Lostmans River. Despite the name, the number of islets is in the hundreds rather than thousands. Many of the islands are protected within the Ten Thousand Islands National Wildlife Refuge, established in 1996.

Geography and ecology

The islands formed on ancient oyster bars and are fringed with red mangroves. The estuaries are important nursery habitat for fish, shrimp, and crabs, and the area supports manatees, dolphins, sea turtles, and wading birds.

History

The Calusa people inhabited the region for centuries and built shell mounds on some islands. The area is now popular for boating, fishing, and paddling, and is part of the Everglades ecosystem.
