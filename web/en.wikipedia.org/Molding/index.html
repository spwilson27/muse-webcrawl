Molding

Molding (American English) or moulding (British and Commonwealth English) is the process of manufacturing by shaping liquid or pliable raw material using a rigid frame called a mold or matrix. A mold is a hollow container that gives the material its final form as it cools, cures or sets. Molding is one of the principal families of manufacturing processes and is used to make an enormous range of products, from plastic bottles and automotive components to cast-metal parts and confectionery.

Principal processes

Injection molding forces molten plastic or metal into a closed steel mold under high pressure and is the dominant process for mass-produced plastic articles. Blow molding inflates a hot plastic tube inside a mold to make hollow objects such as bottles. Compression molding presses a measured charge of material in a heated mold and is widely used for thermosetting plastics and composites. Rotational molding rotates a heated mold so that powdered plastic coats its inner walls, producing large hollow parts. Related forming techniques include casting, in which liquid metal or resin is poured into a mold, and thermoforming, in which a heated sheet is drawn over or into a mold.

Materials and design

Molded materials include thermoplastics (which can be remelted), thermosets (which cure irreversibly), elastomers, glass, ceramics and metals. Mold design must account for shrinkage as the part cools, draft angles so parts release cleanly, and the placement of gates, runners and parting lines. Although the tooling for high-volume molding is expensive, the per-part cost is very low, which makes molding economical for large production runs.
