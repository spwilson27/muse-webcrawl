The Ed Sullivan Show was an American television variety show that ran on CBS from June 20, 1948, to June 6, 1971, hosted by New York newspaper columnist Ed Sullivan. Originally titled Toast of the Town, it was renamed in 1955.

Format and guests

Broadway from the Ed Sullivan Theater in New York, the Sunday-night show presented an eclectic mix of opera singers, comedians, dancers, novelty acts, and popular musicians, introduced by the stiff, deadpan Sullivan with his famous phrase "a really big shoe" (show). Performers who made landmark appearances included Elvis Presley in 1956 — controversially filmed from the waist up on his final visit — and the Beatles, whose February 9, 1964 debut drew an audience of some 73 million.

Legacy

For more than two decades the show was a cornerstone of American popular culture and a rite of passage for entertainers. It was cancelled in 1971 as CBS shifted toward younger demographics, but its archive remains a major record of mid-century performance.
