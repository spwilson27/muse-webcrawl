WTA Tour

The WTA Tour is the principal organising body and worldwide professional tennis circuit for women, administered by the Women's Tennis Association (WTA), founded in 1973 by Billie Jean King. It comprises tournaments held across the globe culminating in the year-end WTA Finals.

Structure

Tournament categories include WTA 1000, WTA 500 and WTA 250 events, alongside the WTA 125 series and the ITF Women's World Tennis Tour. Player rankings are computed on a rolling 52-week basis and determine entry and seeding. The four Grand Slam tournaments are organised by the ITF and national federations but award WTA ranking points.

History

The tour grew from the Virginia Slims Circuit of the 1970s. In 2021 the WTA suspended tournaments in China over concerns about the safety of player Peng Shuai, returning to the country in 2023. Leading champions of the tour era include Martina Navratilova, Steffi Graf, Serena Williams and Iga Świątek.
