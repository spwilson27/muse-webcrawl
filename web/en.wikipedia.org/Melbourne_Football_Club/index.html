The Melbourne Football Club, nicknamed the Demons, is a professional Australian rules football club that plays in the Australian Football League (AFL), the sport's pre-eminent league. Founded in 1858, it is one of the world's oldest football clubs of any code and is credited with helping to write the original rules of Australian rules football in 1859.

History

The club was formed by members of the Melbourne Cricket Club, including captain Tom Wills, whose letter of July 1858 called for a game of football to keep cricketers fit during winter. On 17 May 1859 the first rules of the game were drawn up at the Parade Hotel in Melbourne. Melbourne has played at the Melbourne Cricket Ground (MCG) for most of its existence.

The Demons dominated the Victorian Football League in the 1950s and early 1960s under coach Norm Smith and captain Ron Barassi, winning six premierships between 1955 and 1964. A long premiership drought followed, lasting 57 years, until Melbourne broke through in 2021, defeating the Western Bulldogs in the Grand Final at Perth's Optus Stadium.

Club symbols

Melbourne's guernsey is predominantly navy blue with a red yoke. The club song, "It's a Grand Old Flag", is sung to the tune of "You're a Grand Old Flag". Its mascot and nickname, the Demons, date to the 1930s.

Honours

Melbourne has won 13 VFL/AFL premierships, most recently in 2021, and has produced many champion players including Dick Reynolds (as an opponent-era figure), Ron Barassi, Robert Flower, Garry Lyon, and Clayton Oliver.
