WorldCat

WorldCat is a union catalogue that itemises the collections of tens of thousands of libraries in more than a hundred countries. Maintained by the cooperative OCLC, it is the world's largest bibliographic database, containing billions of holdings records for books, journals, maps, music, films, archival materials and digital resources.

History

OCLC was founded in 1967 as the Ohio College Library Center, and its shared online cataloguing system began operating in 1971 under the leadership of Fred Kilgour. Libraries catalogued items once and shared the records, dramatically reducing duplicated effort. The database opened to the public through the WorldCat.org website in 2006, allowing anyone to search library holdings and locate nearby copies.

Use

Each bibliographic record lists the libraries holding an item, supporting interlibrary loan, collection analysis and scholarly citation. Records carry the OCLC number as a unique identifier. Librarians contribute and enhance records cooperatively, while publishers and aggregators supply additional data. WorldCat underpins services such as WorldCat Discovery and is a key piece of global library infrastructure. See also wordmark.
