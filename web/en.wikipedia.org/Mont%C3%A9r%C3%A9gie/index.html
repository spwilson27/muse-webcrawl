Montérégie is an administrative region of Quebec, Canada, lying on the south shore of the Saint Lawrence River opposite Montreal and extending to the United States border. It takes its name from the Monteregian Hills that rise from its agricultural plain.

Geography

The region includes the suburban communities of the South Shore, such as Longueuil, Brossard, and Saint-Jean-sur-Richelieu, as well as fertile farmland along the Richelieu River that makes it one of Quebec's principal agricultural areas, sometimes called the province's garden.

History

The Richelieu valley was a strategic corridor in the colonial period, guarded by forts such as Fort Chambly, and saw fighting during the Rebellions of 1837–1838, including the battles of Saint-Denis and Saint-Charles.
