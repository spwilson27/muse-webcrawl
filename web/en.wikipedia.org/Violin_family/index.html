Violin family

From smallest to largest: violin, viola, cello, and double bass.

The violin family of musical instruments was developed in Italy in the 16th century. At the time the name of this family of instruments was viole da braccio which was used to distinguish them from the viol family (viole da gamba). The standard modern violin family consists of the violin, viola, cello, and double bass.

Instrument names in the violin family are all derived from the root viola, itself a diminutive of vitula or fiedel terms for stringed instruments. The violin ("little viola") emerged in northern Italy around the 1520s–1550s in the workshops of Brescia and Cremona; Andrea Amati is credited with the earliest surviving instruments of the recognisable form, and his descendants and rivals — the Guarneri and Stradivari families — perfected the design in the following century.

Members

  • Violin — the smallest and highest-pitched, tuned in fifths G–D–A–E, played under the chin.
  • Viola — slightly larger, tuned C–G–D–A, a fifth below the violin, with a darker timbre.
  • Cello (violoncello) — tuned C–G–D–A, an octave below the viola, played upright between the knees.
  • Double bass — the largest, tuned (usually) in fourths E–A–D–G; it retains some viol features, such as sloped shoulders, a legacy of its mixed ancestry.

Construction and acoustics

All the instruments share a hollow wooden body with sound holes shaped as f-holes, a sound post and bass bar inside, four strings stretched over a bridge, and a neck with a scroll. They are played with a bow of horsehair or by plucking (pizzicato). Their arched plates are carved from spruce (top) and maple (back, ribs, neck), and the varnish of the great Cremonese makers remains a subject of study for its reputed role in their sound.

Role in music

The violin family forms the backbone of the modern symphony orchestra: the string section of violins, violas, cellos, and basses is its largest body of players. The family also dominates chamber music through the string quartet (two violins, viola, cello) and appears widely in folk, jazz, and popular music traditions.
