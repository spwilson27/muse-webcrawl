Santander Cycles is a public bicycle hire scheme in London, England. Launched on 30 July 2010 as Barclays Cycle Hire, it lets users rent bicycles from automated docking stations distributed across central London and return them to any other station. The scheme is one of the largest cycle hire systems in the world and a fixture of London street life, its bicycles popularly nicknamed "Boris bikes" after Boris Johnson, the mayor who introduced them.

History

Planning began under Mayor Ken Livingstone, inspired by Paris's Vélib' system, and the scheme opened under Johnson with 5,000 bicycles at 315 docking stations sponsored by Barclays. It expanded eastwards in 2012 and southwest in 2013. In 2015 sponsorship passed to Santander, whose red livery replaced the original blue. Journeys number in the millions annually; the COVID-19 pandemic period brought record use, including a free-access offer for NHS staff. Electric bikes joined the fleet in 2022, and the scheme has been progressively integrated with the TfL app for hiring.

Operation

Users unlock a bicycle using the official app, a contactless bank card at the docking terminal, or a member key. Pricing is based on hire duration, with journeys traditionally charged in 30-minute increments; the pricing structure was simplified in 2023 to a flat unlock-and-ride tariff with e-bikes at a premium. The rugged, single-gear-to-three-speed bikes feature dynamo lights, a small front rack and shaft or chain drive with a full guard; fleets are rebalanced across the network by van, increasingly electric, to match commuter flows.

Impact and criticism

Studies credit the scheme with bringing new cyclists onto London's roads and displacing short car and taxi trips, with a safety record broadly comparable to private cycling. Criticisms have included cost to the public purse, patchy coverage in outer boroughs, theft and vandalism of the e-bikes, and the effect of dockless competitors arriving in the late 2010s.
