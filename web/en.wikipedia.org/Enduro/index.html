Enduro

Enduro is a form of motorcycle sport run on extended cross-country, off-road courses. The discipline tests the endurance of both rider and machine over rough natural terrain, combining timed special tests with road-legal liaison sections ridden to a schedule. The name also applies to the dual-purpose motorcycles used, and, by extension, to related formats in mountain biking.

Format

In a traditional timecard enduro, riders start in small groups at intervals and must arrive at checkpoints at predetermined times, with penalties for arriving early or late; interspersed special tests — cross tests on grass or motocross-style tracks and extreme tests over obstacles — are timed to the second and decide results. The World Enduro Championship (EnduroGP) uses this format over two days of long laps. Medals at the International Six Days Enduro (ISDE), the sport's oldest event, dating to 1913, are awarded by finishing within percentages of class winners' times.

Machines

Enduro bikes are road-legal off-road motorcycles with headlights, silencers meeting noise limits, wide-ratio gearboxes, protective handguards and skid plates, and softer, longer-travel suspension than motocross machines. Classes are divided by engine capacity and stroke (Enduro 1, 2, 3).

Related disciplines

Hare scrambles and cross-country rallies are close cousins; hard enduro events such as Erzbergrodeo and Romaniacs emphasise extreme terrain. Mountain-bike enduro adapts the format: riders climb untimed liaison stages and race timed downhill stages.
