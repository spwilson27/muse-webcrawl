The Kingdom of Navarre was a Basque kingdom centred on the city of Pamplona in the western Pyrenees. It emerged in the ninth century, traditionally dated to 824, when Íñigo Arista was chosen as ruler of Pamplona in defiance of Frankish overlordship, and it endured for a thousand years on both sides of the mountains.

History

Under Sancho III the Great (1004–1035), Navarre reached its greatest extent, ruling much of Christian Iberia. After his death the realm was divided among his sons, and Navarre shrank to a modest Pyrenean kingdom, passing through dynasties from Champagne, France and Foix.

In 1512 Ferdinand II of Aragon conquered the Iberian part of Navarre, which was incorporated into Spain while retaining its fueros until 1841. The smaller northern part, Lower Navarre, remained independent and its kings went on to inherit the French crown in 1589; French kings styled themselves "King of France and Navarre" until the Revolution.

Institutions

Navarre was famous for its fueros, the body of customary law and privileges confirmed by each monarch, and for the Cortes of Navarre. Its position on the pilgrim road to Santiago de Compostela shaped a distinctive frontier culture.
