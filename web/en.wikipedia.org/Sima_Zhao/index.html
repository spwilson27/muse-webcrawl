Sima Zhao

Sima Zhao (211–265), courtesy name Zishang, was a Chinese military general and politician of Cao Wei during the Three Kingdoms period. The second son of Sima Yi, he succeeded his brother Sima Shi as regent and de facto ruler of Wei, conquered Shu Han in 263, and paved the way for his son Sima Yan to found the Jin dynasty — hence the Chinese saying "Sima Zhao's heart is known to everyone on the street," meaning an ambition too obvious to hide.

Rise to power

Sima Zhao helped suppress the Three Rebellions in Shouchun and, after Sima Shi's death in 255, became regent. In 260 his retainers killed the emperor Cao Mao, who had tried to overthrow him; Sima Zhao installed Cao Huan as a puppet ruler and denied direct responsibility.

Conquest of Shu

In 263 Sima Zhao sent Zhong Hui and Deng Ai to invade Shu Han, which surrendered the same year. He was made King of Jin in 264 but died in 265 before taking the throne; his son Sima Yan forced Cao Huan's abdication months later, ending Wei and founding Jin.
