Energy transition

GoalShift from fossil fuels to low-carbon energy

The energy transition is the ongoing global shift of the energy sector from systems based on fossil fuels (oil, natural gas and coal) to low-carbon sources, principally renewable energy such as wind and solar, together with electrification of transport and heating, energy efficiency improvements, and supporting infrastructure such as grids and battery storage. Its principal motivation is mitigating climate change by reducing greenhouse gas emissions, in line with the goals of the Paris Agreement to limit warming to well below 2 °C.

History

Earlier transitions — from wood to coal in the 19th century and from coal to oil in the 20th — were driven mainly by cost and convenience over many decades. The current transition, sometimes called the Energiewende after Germany's pioneering policy programme of the 2000s, is policy-driven and intended to proceed much faster. Since about 2010, dramatic cost reductions in solar photovoltaics, wind turbines, lithium-ion batteries and electric vehicles have made clean technologies cost-competitive in many markets.

Components

  • Rapid deployment of wind and solar generation
  • Electrification of vehicles, heating (heat pumps) and industry
  • Grid expansion, interconnection and energy storage
  • Energy efficiency and demand management
  • Green hydrogen for hard-to-abate sectors
  • Phase-out of coal and, later, unabated oil and gas

Challenges

Challenges include the intermittency of renewables, the scale of grid investment, supply chains for critical minerals such as lithium and rare earths, permitting delays, and the social and economic impacts on fossil-fuel-dependent regions — addressed in policy debates as the "just transition".
