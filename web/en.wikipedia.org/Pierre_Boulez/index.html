Pierre Boulez (26 March 1925 – 5 January 2016) was a French composer, conductor, and writer who was one of the dominant figures of post-war classical music. A leader of the European avant-garde in his youth and later a central institution-builder, he founded the research institute IRCAM in Paris and conducted the world's leading orchestras with celebrated precision. He is unrelated to the 19th-century mathematician Pierre Alphonse Laurent.

Early life and serialism

Boulez studied mathematics before entering the Paris Conservatoire, where Olivier Messiaen's analysis class shaped his technique and René Leibowitz introduced him to the twelve-tone method of Schoenberg. His early works — the Second Piano Sonata (1948), Structures I for two pianos (1952) — pushed toward "total serialism", organising pitch, rhythm, dynamics, and articulation by series. His polemical essay "Schoenberg Is Dead" (1951) announced the new generation's ambitions.

Major works

Le Marteau sans maître ("The Hammer Without a Master", 1955), a setting of poems by René Char for contralto and ensemble, became his most admired score, hailed by Stravinsky. Other key works include Pli selon pli (1962), a portrait of Mallarmé for soprano and orchestra; the explosive Éclat/Multiples; and Répons (1981–84), which used live electronics developed at IRCAM to transform instrumental sound in real time. Many pieces remained in perpetual revision, reflecting his view of the work as an open process ("work in progress").

Conductor and institution-builder

From the 1960s Boulez was a leading conductor of 20th-century repertoire and of Wagner (the centenary Ring at Bayreuth, 1976), serving as chief conductor of the BBC Symphony Orchestra (1971–75) and music director of the New York Philharmonic (1971–77). In 1970 President Pompidou invited him to found IRCAM (Institut de Recherche et Coordination Acoustique/Musique), which opened in 1977 beneath the Centre Pompidou in Paris; its concert ensemble, the Ensemble Intercontemporain, defined standards in new-music performance. In later years he founded the Lucerne Festival Academy and continued to conduct and compose until shortly before his death in Baden-Baden in 2016.

Legacy

Boulez's influence spans composition, conducting, musical scholarship, and cultural policy. His writings, collected as Stocktakings from an Apprenticeship and other volumes, remain central texts of musical modernism, and his recordings — notably of Debussy, Ravel, Mahler, and the Second Viennese School — are widely regarded as benchmarks.
