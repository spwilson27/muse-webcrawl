Sacrosanctum Concilium

Sacrosanctum Concilium, the Constitution on the Sacred Liturgy, is one of the constitutions of the Second Vatican Council. It was approved by a vote of 2,147 to 4 of the bishops assembled and promulgated by Pope Paul VI on 4 December 1963. The title is taken from the opening words of the Latin text, which mean "This sacred council".

Contents

The constitution set out principles for the reform of the Roman Catholic liturgy, calling for the "full, conscious, and active participation" of the faithful in liturgical celebrations. It affirmed that Latin was to be preserved in the Latin rites but permitted extended use of vernacular languages, especially in readings and directives and in some prayers and chants. It also encouraged revision of the rites of the sacraments, greater use of Scripture in the Mass (leading to the expanded three-year lectionary), and adaptation of the liturgy to local cultures, a process later termed inculturation.

Significance

As the first document promulgated by the Council, Sacrosanctum Concilium laid the groundwork for the post-conciliar liturgical reform, culminating in the revised Roman Missal of Paul VI in 1969. Its implementation by the Consilium and national bishops' conferences transformed Catholic worship worldwide, replacing the Tridentine Mass of 1570 as the ordinary form of celebration.
