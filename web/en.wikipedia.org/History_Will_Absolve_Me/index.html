History Will Absolve Me

"History Will Absolve Me" (Spanish: La historia me absolverá) is the title of the speech — and later the manifesto — given by Fidel Castro in his own defence at his trial on 16 October 1953, following the failed attack on the Moncada Barracks in Santiago de Cuba on 26 July 1953.

Background and content

Castro, a young lawyer, led some 160 rebels in attacks on the Moncada and Carlos Manuel de Céspedes barracks, hoping to spark an uprising against the Batista regime. The assaults failed and many rebels were killed or captured. Acting as his own defence counsel, Castro spoke for several hours, denouncing the regime's unconstitutionality and detailing Cuba's social ills — landlessness, illiteracy, poor housing and healthcare — and setting out a programme of revolutionary laws, including land reform and the restoration of the 1940 constitution. He closed with the words: "Condemn me. It does not matter. History will absolve me."

Legacy

Sentenced to fifteen years' imprisonment, Castro reconstructed the speech from memory in prison and published it clandestinely; it became the founding programme of the 26th of July Movement, which took power in the Cuban Revolution of 1959.
