Flemish tapestries

Flemish tapestries are wall hangings woven in the workshops of Flanders and Brabant — principally Brussels, but also Bruges, Ghent, Oudenaarde, Tournai, Arras, and Enghien — which dominated the European luxury tapestry trade from the fifteenth to the seventeenth century. Woven by hand on looms from wool, silk, and gilt-metal thread, Flemish tapestries clothed the walls of palaces and cathedrals across Europe, serving simultaneously as decoration, insulation, portable propaganda, and stores of wealth; a fine set could cost more than a warship.

History

The industry grew under the patronage of the Dukes of Burgundy in the fifteenth century, when Arras (whose name became a generic word for tapestry in English and Italian) and Tournai produced celebrated sets such as those of the Burgundian court. Around 1500, Brussels emerged as the leading centre, aided by papal and imperial commissions; in 1515–1519 the Brussels workshop of Pieter van Aelst wove the monumental Acts of the Apostles for Pope Leo X from cartoons by Raphael, now in the Vatican Museums with cartoons preserved in London. A Brussels ordinance of 1528 required weavers to mark tapestries with the city's mark (a red shield between two B's) and their own monogram, which aids modern attribution. In the seventeenth century, Brussels workshops led by weavers such as the Raes, Geubels, and van den Hecke families produced designs by Peter Paul Rubens, including the Triumph of the Eucharist series for the Spanish court. French royal manufactories (the Gobelins) and war and economic decline eroded Flemish dominance after about 1670, though production continued into the eighteenth century.

Subjects and technique

Subjects ranged across biblical and classical history, allegory, hunting and verdure landscapes (the millefleurs type, covered with small flowers), armorial hangings, and contemporary history such as the Battle of Pavia series. Tapestries were woven sideways on high-warp or low-warp looms by teams of weavers working from painted cartoons; a large hanging could take a team a year or more. Oudenaarde and Enghien specialised in the cheaper, greener verdure tapestries that furnished less exalted interiors.
