Chronicle of Current Events

The Chronicle of Current Events (Russian: Khronika tekushchikh sobytiy) was one of the longest-running samizdat periodicals of the Soviet Union. Published in Moscow from 1968 to 1983, it documented human rights violations in the USSR — arrests, trials, psychiatric abuse, censorship and the persecution of religious believers, national movements and would-be emigrants — in a deliberately dry, factual style.

History

The first issue appeared on 30 April 1968, the year proclaimed by the United Nations as International Year for Human Rights; its masthead quoted Article 19 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights. The journal was produced anonymously by typewriter and passed hand to hand, with copies smuggled to the West where they were reprinted and translated. Natalya Gorbanevskaya was the founding editor; after her arrest, subsequent editors and contributors — including figures associated with the Soviet human rights movement such as Sergei Kovalev and Tatyana Velikanova — continued the work despite arrests, searches and KGB pressure, including the dedicated campaign known as Case No. 24. Between 1968 and 1983 a total of 65 issues appeared.

Legacy

The Chronicle inspired similar samizdat bulletins across the Soviet republics and is regarded as a cornerstone of the Soviet dissident movement and of modern human rights reporting. A nearly complete run has been preserved and published online by the Memorial society.
