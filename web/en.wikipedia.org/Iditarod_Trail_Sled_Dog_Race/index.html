Iditarod Trail Sled Dog Race

Iditarod Trail Sled Dog Race
Location: Alaska, United States
Route: Anchorage to Nome
Distance: ~1,000 miles (1,600 km)
First held: 1973
Nickname: "The Last Great Race on Earth"

The Iditarod Trail Sled Dog Race is an annual long-distance sled dog race held in Alaska, United States, in early March. Mushers and a team of dogs travel the Iditarod Trail from the Anchorage area to Nome on the Bering Sea coast, covering roughly 1,000 miles (1,600 km) in 8 to 15 days or more.

History

The race was founded in 1973 by Joe Redington Sr. and others to preserve the historic Iditarod Trail and the sled dog culture of Alaska. The trail itself is famous for the 1925 serum run, when dog teams relayed diphtheria antitoxin to Nome during an epidemic.

The race

Teams typically start with 12 to 14 dogs and must finish with at least five on the towline. The route alternates between a northern and a southern path in different years, passing through checkpoints in villages and remote cabins where food drops and veterinary care are provided. Mushers endure temperatures far below zero, whiteouts, and wind on sea ice.

Notable mushers

Rick Swenson won the race five times. Susan Butcher became a celebrated champion in the 1980s, and Lance Mackey won four consecutive races from 2007 to 2010. The fastest winning times are just over eight days. See also Sled dog and Mushing.
