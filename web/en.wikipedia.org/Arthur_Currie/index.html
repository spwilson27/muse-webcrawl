Arthur Currie

Born: 5 December 1875, Napperton, Ontario

Died: 30 November 1933, Montreal, Quebec

Rank: General

Commands: Canadian Corps (1917–1919)

General Sir Arthur William Currie, GCMG, KCB (5 December 1875 – 30 November 1933) was a senior officer of the Canadian Army who fought during the First World War. He is widely regarded as one of the most capable commanders of the war and became the first Canadian commander of the Canadian Corps in June 1917.

Early life

Born in Napperton, Ontario, Currie worked as a teacher and later in insurance and real estate in Victoria, British Columbia, while serving as a part-time militia officer. Before the war he was implicated in an embezzlement scandal after diverting regimental funds to cover personal debts, an episode that shadowed his later career, though he eventually repaid the money.

First World War

Currie commanded the 2nd Canadian Brigade at the Second Battle of Ypres in 1915, then the 1st Canadian Division. After planning the Canadian assault at Vimy Ridge while commanding the division, he succeeded Julian Byng as corps commander. Under Currie the Canadian Corps won a series of victories, including Hill 70, Passchendaele, and throughout the Hundred Days Offensive of 1918, earning a reputation for meticulous preparation, massive artillery support and a determination to minimise casualties where possible.

Later life

After the war Currie became principal and vice-chancellor of McGill University in Montreal, serving from 1920 until his death in 1933. A libel action he brought in 1928 over an accusation that he had needlessly wasted lives at Mons on the last day of the war ended in a partial legal victory but took a heavy toll on his health.
