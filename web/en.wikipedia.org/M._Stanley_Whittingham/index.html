Sir Michael Stanley Whittingham (born 22 December 1941) is a British-American chemist. He is a professor of chemistry and director of both the Institute for Materials Research and the Materials Science and Engineering program at Binghamton University, State University of New York.

Career

Whittingham was educated at New College, Oxford, receiving his DPhil in 1968. After postdoctoral work at Stanford University, he joined Exxon Research and Engineering Company in 1972. There, during the 1970s energy crisis, he discovered the concept of intercalation electrodes and created the first functional rechargeable lithium battery, using a titanium disulfide cathode and a lithium metal anode. Exxon patented his invention.

Nobel Prize

In 2019 Whittingham shared the Nobel Prize in Chemistry with John B. Goodenough and Akira Yoshino "for the development of lithium-ion batteries." He was knighted in the 2024 King's Birthday Honours.
