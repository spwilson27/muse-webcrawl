Opificio delle Pietre Dure

The Opificio delle Pietre Dure (Workshop of Semi-precious Stones) is a Florentine institution devoted to the art of pietre dure — inlay work in polished hardstones — and to the conservation and restoration of works of art. It is one of the world's leading centres for art restoration, with its own museum in Florence, Italy.

History

Grand Duke Ferdinando I de' Medici formally established the workshop in 1588, gathering craftsmen to produce the lavish hardstone inlays — commesso fiorentino — that decorated Medici chapels, cabinets and tabletops. The workshops were first housed near the Casino di San Marco and later in the former convent of San Niccolò.

Restoration work

In the 20th century the Opificio became a state restoration institute under the Italian Ministry of Culture. Its laboratories played a central role in rescuing Florentine art after the catastrophic flood of 1966, developing influential methods in the cleaning of paintings, stone conservation and the restoration of frescoes, panels and textiles.

Museum

The Museo dell'Opificio delle Pietre Dure displays examples of Florentine mosaic in stone: tabletops, cabinets, vases and pictures cut in lapis lazuli, jasper, agate and marble, together with the tools and semi-finished works of the craft.
