Kiev Pechersk Lavra

The Kyiv Pechersk Lavra (Ukrainian: Києво-Печерська лавра), also known as the Kyiv Monastery of the Caves, is a historic Eastern Orthodox Christian monastery complex in Kyiv, Ukraine. Founded in 1051 by the monks Anthony and Theodosius, it grew into one of the most important centres of Orthodox Christianity in Eastern Europe and gave its name to the surrounding Pecherskyi District.

History

The monastery began as a cave settlement on the Berestove hill above the Dnieper River. As the community grew, an above-ground complex of churches, bell towers and monastic buildings developed, supported by the princes of Kievan Rus'. The complex suffered fires, Mongol and Tatar raids, and repeated rebuilding; its Great Church (Dormition Cathedral) was destroyed by an explosion in 1941 and reconstructed in 2000.

The caves

The Near and Far Caves contain the mummified remains of more than a hundred monks and saints, preserved in glass reliquaries and venerated by pilgrims. The labyrinthine passages also hold chapels and burial niches dating from the monastery's earliest centuries.

Modern status

Designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1990 together with Saint Sophia Cathedral, the Lavra functions both as a national historic preserve and a working monastery. Its 96-metre Great Lavra Bell Tower, completed in 1745, is a landmark of the Kyiv skyline. Since 2023, the site's administration and worship arrangements have been reshaped amid Ukraine's separation of church structures from Moscow following Russia's full-scale invasion.
