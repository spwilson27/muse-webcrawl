Milkfish

The milkfish (Chanos chanos) is a widespread species of ray-finned fish found throughout the Indo-Pacific. It is the sole living species in the family Chanidae. Milkfish are silvery, streamlined fish that can grow to about 1.8 m, though market size is usually much smaller. They are of major importance in Southeast Asian aquaculture, having been farmed in brackish-water ponds in Indonesia, the Philippines and Taiwan for centuries.

Biology

Adults live in offshore marine waters and spawn near coral reefs; the tiny larvae drift inshore and settle in estuaries, mangroves and even freshwater lakes, tolerating a wide range of salinities. They feed on algae and small invertebrates. Milkfish are notoriously bony, with numerous fine intermuscular bones.

Aquaculture and cuisine

Fry collected from the wild — and increasingly produced in hatcheries — are grown in ponds and pens. In the Philippines the fish, called bangus, is the national fish and is served as daing (split and dried), sinigang and grilled stuffed bangus; in Indonesia, bandeng presto is pressure-cooked to soften the bones. Annual production is on the order of hundreds of thousands of tonnes.
