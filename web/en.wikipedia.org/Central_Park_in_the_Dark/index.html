Central Park in the Dark is a programmatic orchestral work by the American composer Charles Ives. Composed in 1906 and revised in 1909, it forms the second of Two Contemplations, paired with The Unanswered Question. The piece depicts a summer night in New York City's Central Park, contrasting the stillness of nature with the distant sounds of human activity drifting in from the city.

Music

Ives layers independent musical strata: a quiet, sustained string background representing the night sounds of nature; a piano suggesting the leaves and breezes; and, layered over these, fragments of popular tunes, ragtime rhythms, a brass band, and street noises that swell into a chaotic climax before subsiding back into silence. The work is an early and striking example of musical collage and polytonality, techniques Ives developed well before they became common in twentieth-century music.

The full title Ives gave the piece was Central Park in the Dark in "The Good Old Summer Time", referencing the popular song quoted within it. The premiere took place in 1946, decades after its composition, under conductor Theodore Bloomfield in New York.
