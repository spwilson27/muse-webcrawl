Guan Yu

Guan Yu (關羽)
Died: 220 AD
Courtesy name: Yunchang (雲長)
Allegiance: Liu Bei / Shu Han
Weapon: Green Dragon Crescent Blade

Guan Yu (died 220), courtesy name Yunchang, was a Chinese military general serving under the warlord Liu Bei during the late Eastern Han dynasty of China. Together with Zhang Fei, he shared a brotherly relationship with Liu Bei, sealed — in later tradition — by the Oath of the Peach Garden.

Career

Guan Yu served Liu Bei from his earliest campaigns. Briefly captured by Cao Cao in 200, he repaid Cao Cao's generosity by slaying the general Yan Liang at the Battle of Boma before returning to Liu Bei. He later guarded Jing Province for Liu Bei and won fame at the Battle of Fancheng in 219, where he flooded the city and captured the general Yu Jin. Soon afterwards he was defeated by the combined forces of Sun Quan and Cao Cao, captured, and executed.

Deification

Over the following centuries Guan Yu was progressively deified. By the Sui dynasty he had become a god, and by the Ming and Qing dynasties he was worshipped as Guan Di ("Emperor Guan"), a god of war, loyalty, righteousness and wealth, venerated in Chinese folk religion, Taoism, Confucianism and Chinese Buddhism. His figure was greatly popularised by the fourteenth-century historical novel Romance of the Three Kingdoms, which depicts him wielding the legendary Green Dragon Crescent Blade and riding the horse Red Hare.
