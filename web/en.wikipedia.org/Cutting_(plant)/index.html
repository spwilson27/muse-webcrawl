Cutting (plant)

A plant cutting is a piece of a plant that is used in horticulture for vegetative (asexual) propagation. A piece of the stem or root of the source plant is placed in a suitable medium such as moist soil or water. If conditions are right, the plant piece begins to grow as a new plant independent of the parent, a process known as striking. The new plant is a clone, genetically identical to the parent.

Types of cuttings

Stem cuttings are classified as hardwood, semi-hardwood, softwood, or herbaceous, according to the maturity of the wood taken. Leaf cuttings are used for plants such as African violets and snake plants, and root cuttings for plants like oriental poppies and raspberries. Rooting is encouraged by wounding the base of the cutting and applying rooting hormone, usually containing auxins such as indole-3-butyric acid.

Technique

Cuttings are generally taken just below a node, kept humid and warm, and shaded from direct sun until roots form. Mist benches and plastic covers reduce water loss from leaves before the cutting can absorb water through roots. Bottom heat speeds rooting in many species.

Uses

Propagation by cuttings preserves the exact characteristics of cultivars that do not come true from seed, and is standard practice for grapes, figs, olives, roses, and many houseplants and fruit trees. It also allows rapid multiplication of a desirable plant without waiting for seed production.
