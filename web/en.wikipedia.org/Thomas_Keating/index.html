Thomas Keating (March 7, 1923 – October 25, 2018) was an American Trappist monk and priest, known as one of the principal developers of Centering Prayer, a method of contemplative prayer that emerged from the Cistercian tradition of St. Joseph's Abbey in Spencer, Massachusetts. He served as abbot of St. Joseph's from 1961 to 1981 and in 1984 co-founded Contemplative Outreach, an organisation dedicated to teaching Centering Prayer and Lectio Divina.

Life and work

Keating was born in New York City and studied at Deerfield Academy, Yale, and Fordham before entering the Order of Cistercians of the Strict Observance in 1944 at Valley Falls, Rhode Island. After the community's monastery burned, he helped to build St. Joseph's Abbey in Spencer. As abbot, he participated in the interreligious dialogues of the post–Vatican II era and hosted exchanges with Buddhist and other contemplatives. Together with fellow monks William Meninger and Basil Pennington, he drew on the medieval classic The Cloud of Unknowing, Thomas Merton's writings, and the Desert Fathers to formulate Centering Prayer as an accessible practice for laypeople.

Teachings and books

Keating taught that contemplative prayer rests on consenting to God's presence and action within, and he presented the psychological process of purification — the unloading of the "false self" — in works such as Open Mind, Open Heart (1986), Invitation to Love (1992), and Intimacy with God. He spent his later years at St. Benedict's Monastery in Snowmass, Colorado, and became a prominent voice in Christian contemplative and interreligious circles.
