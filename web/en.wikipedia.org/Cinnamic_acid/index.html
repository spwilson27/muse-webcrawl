Cinnamic acid

Cinnamic acid
Formula: C9H8O2
Molar mass: 148.16 g/mol
Appearance: white crystalline solid

Cinnamic acid is an organic compound with the formula C6H5–CH=CH–COOH. It is a white crystalline compound that is slightly soluble in water and freely soluble in many organic solvents. It occurs naturally in cinnamon, balsams, and shea butter, and gives cinnamon much of its characteristic flavour and odour.

Synthesis and uses

Cinnamic acid was first prepared by the Perkin reaction, the condensation of benzaldehyde with acetic anhydride. Industrially it is used as a precursor to esters such as methyl cinnamate and cinnamyl alcohol for perfumes and flavourings, and as an intermediate in the manufacture of pharmaceuticals and the sweetener aspartame via the phenylalanine pathway. In plants it is a central intermediate in the biosynthesis of lignin and numerous phenylpropanoids.

Properties

The naturally occurring form is predominantly the trans (E) isomer. Cinnamic acid has a honey-like odour and a melting point of about 133 °C.
