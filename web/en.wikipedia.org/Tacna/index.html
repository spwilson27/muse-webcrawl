Tacna is a city in southern Peru and the regional capital of the Department of Tacna. It lies in the valley of the Caplina River, about 35 kilometres north of the border with Chile, in the Atacama Desert region. Tacna is known in Peru as the "Heroic City" for its role in the War of the Pacific and its subsequent return to Peruvian sovereignty.

The Arco Parabólico in Tacna's civic plaza, honoring heroes of the War of the Pacific

History

The area was inhabited in pre-Columbian times and later incorporated into the Inca Empire before Spanish colonization in the 16th century. During the War of the Pacific (1879–1884), Chilean forces occupied Tacna after the Battle of Alto de la Alianza in 1880. Under the Treaty of Ancón (1883), the provinces of Tacna and Arica were to remain under Chilean administration pending a plebiscite that was never held, leaving the "Question of Tacna and Arica" unresolved for decades.

In 1929, the Treaty of Lima settled the dispute: Tacna was returned to Peru while Arica remained with Chile. Tacna was formally reincorporated into Peru on 28 August 1929, a date commemorated each year with the Procesión de la Bandera (Flag Procession), one of Peru's most important patriotic ceremonies.

Geography and economy

Tacna has an arid desert climate moderated by its elevation of about 562 metres. Its economy is based on commerce—especially trade with nearby Chile—agriculture in the surrounding valleys (noted for olives and wine grapes), and mining in the wider region. The historic Tacna–Arica railway, opened in 1856, still links the city with the Chilean port of Arica.
