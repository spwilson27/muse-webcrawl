Timeline of fundamental physics discoveries

This timeline of fundamental physics discoveries records landmark experiments, observations and theories that transformed the understanding of nature, from ancient natural philosophy to the modern Standard Model of particle physics.

Early discoveries

  • c. 300 BCE — Euclid writes the Elements of geometry and treats optics systematically.
  • 1543 — Nicolaus Copernicus publishes the heliocentric model of the solar system.
  • 1609–1619 — Johannes Kepler states his three laws of planetary motion.
  • 1687 — Isaac Newton publishes the Principia Mathematica, formulating the laws of motion and universal gravitation.

Classical physics

  • 1785 — Charles-Augustin de Coulomb formulates the inverse-square law of electrostatics.
  • 1865 — James Clerk Maxwell publishes the equations uniting electricity, magnetism and light.
  • 1895 — Wilhelm Röntgen discovers X-rays.
  • 1897 — J. J. Thomson discovers the electron.

Modern physics

  • 1905 — Albert Einstein publishes papers on the photoelectric effect, Brownian motion, special relativity and mass–energy equivalence.
  • 1915 — Einstein completes general relativity.
  • 1925–1927 — Heisenberg, Schrödinger and others formulate quantum mechanics.
  • 1964 — Arno Penzias and Robert Wilson discover the cosmic microwave background.
  • 2012 — The Higgs boson is discovered at CERN's Large Hadron Collider.
  • 2015 — Gravitational waves are directly detected by LIGO.
