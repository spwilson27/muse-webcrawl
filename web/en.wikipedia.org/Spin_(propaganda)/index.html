Spin (propaganda)

In public relations and politics, spin is a form of propaganda, achieved through knowingly providing a biased interpretation of an event or campaigning to influence public opinion about some organization or public figure. It often, though not always, implies disingenuous, deceptive and manipulative tactics. The person responsible for crafting the spin is called a "spin doctor".

Techniques

Spin techniques include selectively presenting facts, burying bad news by releasing it when attention is elsewhere (for example late on a Friday), cherry-picking statistics, and using euphemisms or loaded language. "No comment" responses, strategic leaks, and flooding the news cycle are also associated with spin.

History of the term

The metaphor draws on the spin a bowler or pitcher puts on a ball to alter its trajectory. Edward Bernays, a founder of public relations, is often regarded as an early master of the craft. The phrase "spin doctor" gained currency in American politics in the 1980s and in Britain during the era of advisers such as Alastair Campbell.

Criticism

Spin is criticised for undermining democratic debate by substituting presentation for substance, though practitioners argue that framing one's case is a legitimate part of communication.
