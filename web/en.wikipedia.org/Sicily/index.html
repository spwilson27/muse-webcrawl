Sicily (Italian: Sicilia) is the largest island in the Mediterranean Sea and one of the twenty regions of Italy, with autonomous status. It lies off the "toe" of the Italian peninsula, separated from Calabria by the Strait of Messina. Its capital and largest city is Palermo. The island's triangular shape gave it the ancient Greek name Trinacria, reflected in its symbol, the three-legged trinacria.

History

Sicily's strategic position made it a crossroads of civilisations. Indigenous Sicani, Sicels and Elymians were joined by Phoenician and Greek colonists from the 8th century BC; Greek Syracuse became one of the great cities of the ancient world, home of Archimedes. Rome annexed the island in the Punic Wars, making it its first province and a granary of the empire. After the fall of Rome it passed through Vandal, Ostrogothic and Byzantine hands before the Arab conquest (831–902) made it a flourishing emirate. The Normans under Roger I conquered it in the 11th century, and Roger II founded the Kingdom of Sicily in 1130, ruling a famously tolerant, multilingual court.

Later dynasties — Hohenstaufen, Angevin, Aragonese, Spanish Habsburg and Bourbon — followed; the Sicilian Vespers revolt of 1282 split the island kingdom from Naples. Sicily joined unified Italy in 1860–61. The Allied invasion of 1943 (Operation Husky) opened the Italian campaign of the Second World War. The island's organised crime syndicate, Cosa Nostra, and the anti-Mafia struggle of judges Falcone and Borsellino, assassinated in 1992, remain central to its modern history.

Geography

Sicily is hilly and intensively cultivated, with Mount Etna — among the world's most active volcanoes — dominating the east, and the Aeolian, Egadi and Pelagie island groups offshore. Its climate is Mediterranean, with hot dry summers; the sirocco wind can push temperatures above 40 °C.

Culture

Sicilian culture blends Greek, Arab, Norman and Spanish influences in its architecture (the Arab-Norman cathedrals of Palermo and Monreale are UNESCO sites), its cuisine (arancini, cannoli, granita, pasta alla Norma), its literature (Pirandello, Verga, Lampedusa's The Leopard) and the Sicilian language, still widely spoken.
