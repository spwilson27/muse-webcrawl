Res judicata

Res judicata (Latin for "a matter judged") is a legal doctrine providing that a final judgment on the merits by a competent court is conclusive as to the rights of the parties and bars any further litigation on the same claim. It encompasses two related concepts: claim preclusion, which prevents relitigating a claim that was or could have been raised, and issue preclusion (collateral estoppel), which prevents relitigating an issue actually decided.

Rationale

The doctrine serves judicial economy, protects parties from vexatious multiple lawsuits, and promotes the finality and consistency of judgments. Without it, a losing party could repeatedly sue until obtaining a favourable result.

Requirements

Generally, res judicata requires: (1) a final judgment, (2) rendered on the merits, (3) by a court of competent jurisdiction, (4) involving the same parties or their privies, and (5) the same claim or cause of action. Exceptions exist for fraud, lack of jurisdiction, and certain equitable circumstances.

Civil and common law

The doctrine originated in Roman law and is recognised in both common law and civil law systems, though its scope and terminology vary by jurisdiction.
