Human evolution

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Human evolution is the evolutionary process within the history of primates that led to the emergence of Homo sapiens as a distinct species of the hominid family, which includes all the great apes. This process involved the gradual development of traits such as human bipedalism, dexterity, and complex language, as well as interbreeding with other hominins (a tribe of the African hominid subfamily), indicating that human evolution was not linear but weblike.

The study of human evolution involves several scientific disciplines, including physical and evolutionary anthropology, palaeontology, and genetics. Primates diverged from other mammals about 85 million years ago, in the Late Cretaceous period, with their fossil record first appearing in the Paleocene.

Hominin divergence

Humans and chimpanzees are the closest living relatives, sharing a last common ancestor that lived roughly 6–8 million years ago in Africa. Molecular evidence indicates that the human lineage separated from that of chimpanzees and bonobos around this time. Early candidate hominins include Sahelanthropus tchadensis (about 7 million years ago), Orrorin tugenensis, and Ardipithecus.

Australopithecines

The genus Australopithecus appeared about 4 million years ago. Australopithecines such as Australopithecus afarensis — represented by the famous partial skeleton "Lucy", found at Hadar, Ethiopia, in 1974 — were habitual bipeds with relatively small brains (around 400–500 cm³) and ape-like faces. Bipedalism preceded large brains by millions of years. Footprints preserved at Laetoli, Tanzania, about 3.66 million years old, confirm upright walking.

Genus Homo

The genus Homo emerged roughly 2.8–2.3 million years ago. Homo habilis is associated with Oldowan stone tools. Homo erectus (from about 1.9 million years ago) had a larger brain, a more human body plan, and was the first hominin to spread widely out of Africa into Asia and Europe; it is associated with Acheulean handaxes and likely controlled use of fire. Archaic humans such as Homo heidelbergensis gave rise, in Africa, to anatomically modern humans, and in Eurasia to Neanderthals and Denisovans.

Homo sapiens

Anatomically modern humans originated in Africa, with the earliest known fossils from Jebel Irhoud, Morocco, dated to about 300,000 years ago, and from Omo Kibish, Ethiopia, around 195,000–233,000 years ago. A major expansion out of Africa occurred about 60,000–70,000 years ago. Modern humans interbred with Neanderthals and Denisovans; non-African genomes today carry roughly 1–4% Neanderthal ancestry, and some Oceanian populations carry up to about 5% Denisovan ancestry. Brain volume in Homo sapiens averages about 1,350 cm³.

Behavioural modernity

By around 50,000 years ago, humans displayed a package of behaviours including sophisticated blade tools, fishing, long-distance trade, personal ornamentation, and figurative art, as seen in the cave paintings of Sulawesi and Chauvet. Agriculture arose independently in several regions beginning around 12,000 years ago, transforming human societies.
