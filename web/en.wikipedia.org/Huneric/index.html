Huneric (died 23 December 484) was king of the Vandal Kingdom of North Africa from 477 to 484, the eldest son and successor of the kingdom's founder, Genseric. As a hostage in Ravenna he had been betrothed to Eudocia, daughter of the Western Roman emperor Valentinian III, whom he married after the Vandal sack of Rome in 455; she later fled to Jerusalem.

Reign

Huneric initially pursued conciliatory policies toward his Roman subjects and allowed the election of a Catholic bishop of Carthage after a long vacancy. From about 483, however, influenced by Arian clergy, he launched the most systematic persecution of Nicene (Catholic) Christians in the Vandal kingdom: churches were confiscated, bishops exiled — Victor of Vita chronicled the sufferings — and in 484 an edict ordered all Catholics to convert to Arianism on pain of severe penalties.

Death and succession

Huneric died in December 484, reportedly of plague, according to hostile Catholic sources suffering horribly. In violation of Genseric's law of succession by seniority, he had attempted to secure the throne for his own son Hilderic, but was succeeded by his nephew Gunthamund.
