Coherence length

In physics, the coherence length is the propagation distance over which a coherent wave (such as an electromagnetic wave) maintains a specified degree of temporal coherence; that is, the distance after which the phase relationship between two points on the wave can no longer be reliably predicted. Wave interference is strong when the paths taken by all of the interfering waves differ by less than the coherence length.

Formulas

For a light source with central wavelength λ and spectral width Δλ, the coherence length is approximately Lc ≈ λ2/Δλ. It is related to the coherence time τc by Lc = c·τc, where c is the speed of light in the medium. For a laser with frequency bandwidth Δν, Lc ≈ c/Δν.

Applications

Coherence lengths range from micrometres for broad-spectrum sources such as incandescent lamps and LEDs to hundreds of metres or more for narrow-linewidth lasers. The concept is central to interferometry, holography, and optical coherence tomography, where the path difference in an interferometer must be kept within the coherence length for fringes to appear. The term is also used in superconductivity, where the superconducting coherence length describes the characteristic size of a Cooper pair and the distance over which the superconducting order parameter can vary, and in particle physics for related quantities.
