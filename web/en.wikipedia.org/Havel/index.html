Havel

The Havel (German pronunciation: [ˈhaːfl̩]) is a river in northeastern Germany, flowing through the states of Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, Brandenburg, Berlin and Saxony-Anhalt. The longest right tributary of the Elbe, it is 325 kilometres (202 mi) long. However, the direct distance from its source to its mouth is only 94 kilometres (58 mi), as the river meanders widely through a landscape of interconnected lakes.

Course

The Havel rises in the Mecklenburg Lake District near Ankershagen and flows south through a series of lakes — including the Müritz region waterways and the lakes around Fürstenberg — before reaching Berlin. Within the capital it widens into large lake-like expanses, notably the Großer Wannsee and the Tegeler See, and passes the historic island city of Spandau, where it is joined by its largest tributary, the Spree. Continuing southwest through Potsdam, with its chain of Havel lakes bordered by palaces and parks, the river turns northwest past Brandenburg an der Havel and finally joins the Elbe near Havelberg.

History

The river's name derives from the Hevelli (Stodorani), a Slavic tribe that settled its banks in the early Middle Ages. Brandenburg an der Havel, founded on a cathedral island in the river, gave its name to the Margraviate of Brandenburg and the modern German state. The Havel lakes around Potsdam were landscaped under the Prussian kings into one of Europe's great cultural landscapes, now a UNESCO World Heritage site.

Navigation and canals

The Havel is navigable for most of its length and forms part of the waterway network linking Berlin with the Elbe and the Oder. Important artificial waterways connected to it include the Elbe–Havel Canal, the Havel–Oder Waterway, the Sacrow–Paretz Canal and the Teltow Canal (via the Spree and Dahme system).

Recreation

The lake-dotted Havel is one of Germany's most popular regions for sailing, rowing, kayaking and swimming, with the Wannsee lido in Berlin among the country's best-known bathing beaches.
