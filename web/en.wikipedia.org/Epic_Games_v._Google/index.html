Epic Games v. Google

Epic Games v. Google was an antitrust lawsuit brought by Epic Games against Google in the United States District Court for the Northern District of California, challenging Google's practices in operating the Google Play app store on Android devices.

The case concerned competition in Android app distribution and in-app payments

Background

In August 2020, Epic introduced a direct payment option in its game Fortnite that bypassed the app stores' payment systems and their commissions of up to 30 percent. Google removed Fortnite from Google Play, and Epic sued the same day, alleging that Google monopolized Android app distribution and in-app payment processing in violation of the Sherman Act and California law. Epic brought a parallel suit against Apple, which largely ended in Apple's favor.

Trial and verdict

The case went to a jury trial in late 2023. Unlike the Apple case, which was decided by a judge, a jury in December 2023 found for Epic on all counts, concluding that Google had an illegal monopoly in Android app distribution and in-app billing and had engaged in anticompetitive conduct, including revenue-sharing deals with manufacturers and developers (internally called "Project Hug") that suppressed rival app stores.

Remedies

In October 2024, Judge James Donato issued an injunction ordering Google to open Google Play to rival app stores and to stop requiring use of Google Play Billing, among other measures, for a period of three years. Google appealed, and portions of the injunction were stayed pending appeal.
