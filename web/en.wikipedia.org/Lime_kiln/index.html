Lime kiln

A lime kiln is a kiln used for the calcination of limestone (calcium carbonate) to produce quicklime (calcium oxide). Heating limestone to around 900–1000 °C drives off carbon dioxide, leaving quicklime, which can then be slaked with water to make hydrated lime.

History

Lime burning is an ancient technology; lime mortars and plasters were used in early buildings across the ancient world. Traditional kilns were often stone structures built into hillsides, charged with alternating layers of limestone and fuel and burned in batches or continuously as draw kilns.

Modern kilns

Industrial production uses shaft kilns, rotary kilns, and fluidised-bed designs, aiming to control temperature and residence time for consistent reactivity. Lime is used in steelmaking, construction, chemical manufacture, water treatment, and flue-gas desulfurisation. The calcination process is a significant source of industrial carbon dioxide emissions, both from fuel and from the chemistry itself.
