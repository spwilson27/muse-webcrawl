Abdallahi ibn Muhammad

Abdallahi ibn Muhammad (Arabic: عبد الله ابن محمد; 1846 – 25 November 1899), known as Khalifa Abdallahi, was the successor of Muhammad Ahmad, the Sudanese Mahdi. He ruled the Mahdist State in Sudan from the Mahdi's death in 1885 until the Anglo-Egyptian reconquest, and was killed at the Battle of Umm Diwaykarat in 1899.

Rise

Born into the Ta'aisha tribe of Baggara Arabs in Darfur, Abdallahi became one of the Mahdi's earliest and most trusted followers. The Mahdi appointed him Khalifat al-Siddiq, the senior of his three deputies (khalifas), modelled on the first caliphs of Islam. On the Mahdi's death in June 1885, shortly after the fall of Khartoum, Abdallahi overcame rivals and consolidated his rule at Omdurman, the new capital.

Rule

His reign was marked by famine, smallpox, and warfare: campaigns against Ethiopia (culminating in the Ethiopian victory at Gallabat in 1889, where Emperor Yohannes IV was killed), resistance to the Egyptian frontier forces, and expeditions to reconquer Egypt that were defeated at Toski in 1889. He centralised power, promoted his Ta'aisha kinsmen, and maintained a large standing army. Internal dissent, including from the Mahdi's relatives (the Ashraf), was suppressed.

Defeat and death

After the Anglo-Egyptian army under Herbert Kitchener destroyed his forces at the Battle of Omdurman on 2 September 1898, Abdallahi fled south. He was pursued and killed at Umm Diwaykarat in Kordofan on 25 November 1899, fighting alongside his remaining emirs.
