Upper air

Upper air refers to the atmosphere above the surface layer, typically studied with weather balloons, radiosondes, aircraft and satellites. Upper-air observations of wind, temperature, pressure and humidity are essential to weather forecasting.

Observation

Radiosondes launched worldwide, commonly at 00 and 12 UTC, measure profiles up to the stratosphere. Data feed charts at standard pressure levels such as 850, 700, 500 and 250 hPa.

Features

Upper-air patterns include troughs, ridges, jet streams and cut-off lows that steer surface weather systems.

Uses

Forecasters use upper-air analysis to predict storms, aviation turbulence and temperature changes.
