Commentarii de Bello Civili

AuthorJulius Caesar (with continuations by others)
LanguageLatin
SubjectThe Roman Civil War of 49–45 BC
BooksThree (by Caesar)

The Commentarii de Bello Civili ("Commentaries on the Civil War") is an account written by Julius Caesar of his war against Gnaeus Pompeius and the Roman Senate, covering events from the crossing of the Rubicon in 49 BC through the campaign in Egypt in 47 BC. Like its predecessor, the Commentarii de Bello Gallico, it is written in the third person in a clear, economical Latin style that has made it a standard school text ever since.

Content

The three books describe Caesar's advance through Italy, the campaign in Spain, the siege of Massilia, the defeat at Dyrrhachium, Caesar's decisive victory over Pompey at Pharsalus in 48 BC, and the Alexandrine War in Egypt. The narrative is openly partisan: Caesar presents his cause as the defence of his honour and of the tribunes' rights against a corrupt oligarchy.

Continuations

Caesar's account ends abruptly, and the war was continued by anonymous authors, probably officers in his army, in the Bellum Alexandrinum, Bellum Africum, and Bellum Hispaniense, which follow the conflict to its conclusion at Munda in 45 BC. Together with the Gallic War commentaries, these works form the corpus of Caesar's writings and a principal primary source for the fall of the Roman Republic.
