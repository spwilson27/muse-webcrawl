Kalmyk Khanate

The Kalmyk Khanate was an Oirat khanate on the Eurasian steppe, established in the 17th century in the Lower Volga region of present-day Kalmykia, Russia. It was the only Buddhist polity in Europe, founded by Kalmyk (Torghut and Dörbet Oirat) nomads who migrated westward from Dzungaria.

Migration and founding

In the early 1600s, groups of Oirats under Kho Orluk moved west across the Kazakh steppe to the Volga, displacing the Nogai Horde. Their descendants built a khanate nominally within the Russian sphere; Ayuka Khan (r. 1672–1724) received the title of Khan from the 6th Dalai Lama's representatives and allied with Peter the Great, supplying cavalry for Russian campaigns.

Relations with Russia

Kalmyk khans swore successive oaths of allegiance to the Tsar while retaining internal autonomy. Kalmyk cavalry served in the Great Northern War, the Russo-Persian wars, and against the Ottoman Empire. Over the 18th century, Russian settlement and administrative pressure steadily eroded the khanate's independence.

The return to Dzungaria and abolition

In January 1771, Ubashi Khan led most of the Volga Kalmyks on a disastrous migration back to Dzungaria in Qing China; large numbers died en route from attacks, starvation, and disease. Catherine the Great then abolished the khanate later that year, incorporating its remaining people directly into the Russian Empire. Those who stayed are the ancestors of today's Kalmyks of Kalmykia.
