Government-owned and controlled corporation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A government-owned and controlled corporation (GOCC) is a term used particularly in the Philippines for a corporation created by special law or registered under corporation law in which the government owns a majority — at least 51 per cent — of the outstanding capital stock. GOCCs may be stock or non-stock corporations and perform governmental, proprietary, or mixed functions.

Legal framework in the Philippines

The GOCC Governance Act of 2011 (Republic Act No. 10149) created the Governance Commission for GOCCs (GCG), which classifies, monitors, and evaluates state corporations and sets compensation and performance standards for their boards. Well-known Philippine GOCCs include the Government Service Insurance System (GSIS), the Social Security System (SSS), PhilHealth, the Philippine Amusement and Gaming Corporation (PAGCOR), and the Land Bank of the Philippines.

Oversight and issues

GOCCs are subject to audit by the Commission on Audit and are expected to remit a portion of their income as dividends to the national treasury. They have periodically attracted criticism over executive pay, "midnight" appointments to boards, and losses in some corporations.
