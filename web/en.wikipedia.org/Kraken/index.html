The kraken is a legendary sea monster of enormous size said to dwell off the coasts of Norway and Greenland. In Scandinavian folklore it was described as a gigantic octopus- or squid-like creature, sometimes said to be so large that sailors mistook its body for an island, and capable of dragging entire ships down with its arms.

History of the legend

Early Norse sources mention a vast sea creature called the hafgufa, sometimes identified with the kraken. Bishop Erik Pontoppidan gave the most influential account in his Natural History of Norway (1753), describing the kraken as "mile-wide" and claiming it could pull down the largest warship. Carl Linnaeus included the kraken (as Microcosmus marinus) in the first edition of Systema Naturae (1735), though he removed it from later editions.

Scientific basis

Scholars generally link kraken tales to sightings of the giant squid, which can reach 12–13 metres and was long known only from carcasses washed ashore and sailors' reports. The first photographs of a living giant squid were taken in 2004, lending retrospective plausibility to the legends.

In popular culture

The kraken entered literature through works such as Alfred Tennyson's sonnet "The Kraken" (1830) and Jules Verne's Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas (1870), and remains a staple of film, games and fantasy fiction, often summoned with the phrase "Release the Kraken!"
