Cooperstown is a village in and the county seat of Otsego County, New York, United States. Most of the village lies within the town of Otsego, but some of the eastern part is in the town of Middlefield. Located at the foot of Otsego Lake in the Central New York Region, Cooperstown is known as the home of the National Baseball Hall of Fame and Museum.

History

The village was established in 1786 by Judge William Cooper, father of the novelist James Fenimore Cooper, whose Leatherstocking Tales were set in the region. Cooperstown was incorporated as a village in 1812.

Baseball

According to the now-discredited Mills Commission account, Abner Doubleday was said to have invented baseball in Cooperstown in 1839. Stephen Carlton Clark founded the National Baseball Hall of Fame and Museum there, and it opened in 1939 in connection with the supposed centennial of the game. The annual induction ceremony draws large crowds each summer, and Doubleday Field hosts amateur games.

Other attractions

The Fenimore Art Museum, the Farmers' Museum, and the Glimmerglass Festival (an opera festival founded in 1975) are located in or near the village, as is Brewery Ommegang.

Geography

Cooperstown is situated at the southern end of Otsego Lake, the source of the Susquehanna River.
