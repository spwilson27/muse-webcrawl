Blue baby syndrome

SpecialtyPediatrics
SymptomsCyanosis (bluish skin), breathing difficulty
CausesMethemoglobinemia (often nitrate-contaminated water); congenital heart defects

Blue baby syndrome can refer to a number of conditions that affect oxygen transportation in the blood, resulting in blueness of the skin in babies, a condition called cyanosis. Historically, the term referred to cyanosis in infants caused by congenital heart defects; in environmental health contexts it most often refers to methemoglobinemia acquired from nitrate-contaminated drinking water.

Nitrate-induced methemoglobinemia

Infants who drink formula prepared with water high in nitrates convert nitrate to nitrite in their digestive tracts. Nitrite oxidizes the iron in hemoglobin to form methemoglobin, which cannot carry oxygen effectively. Infants under six months are especially vulnerable because of their stomach chemistry and the presence of fetal hemoglobin. Cases were documented in rural areas of the United States in the 1940s and 1950s where well water was contaminated by fertilizer runoff, leading to drinking-water standards limiting nitrate levels.

Cardiac causes

Congenital heart defects that cause right-to-left shunting of blood, such as tetralogy of Fallot, transposition of the great arteries, and tricuspid atresia, can produce cyanosis in newborns. The first successful surgical treatment, the Blalock–Thomas–Taussig shunt (1944), was developed for "blue baby" cases of tetralogy of Fallot.

Treatment

Acquired methemoglobinemia is treated by removing the source of nitrates and, in significant cases, administering methylene blue. Cardiac causes may require surgical correction.
