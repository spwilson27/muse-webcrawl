The World Pipe Band Championships is the premier competition for pipe bands, held annually in August on Glasgow Green in Glasgow, Scotland. Organized by the Royal Scottish Pipe Band Association (RSPBA), the event attracts more than 200 bands and tens of thousands of spectators from around the world.

History

Pipe band competitions date to the nineteenth century, when Highland games and military gatherings featured contests between regimental and civilian bands. The first championship recognized as the "Worlds" was held in Edinburgh in 1947. The event later settled permanently in Glasgow, and in recent years has formed the finale of the week-long Piping Live! festival.

Competition format

Bands compete in grades from Grade 4 (novice and juvenile) up to Grade 1, the elite level. Grade 1 bands must perform both a March, Strathspey and Reel (MSR) set and a medley selection, judged by panels assessing piping, drumming, ensemble, and — in the drum corps contest — the separate title of World Drum Corps Champions. Qualification heats in the morning are followed by finals in the afternoon, with the day closing in the march-past and prize-giving.

Notable champions

Successful Grade 1 bands have included Field Marshal Montgomery Pipe Band of Northern Ireland, the Simon Fraser University Pipe Band of Canada, Shotts and Dykehead Caledonia, Inveraray & District, and the St. Laurence O'Toole Pipe Band of Dublin. The dominance of Scottish bands has increasingly been challenged by strong contingents from Canada, Ireland, Australia, and New Zealand.
