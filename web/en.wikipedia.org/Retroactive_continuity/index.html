Retroactive continuity

Retroactive continuity, usually shortened to retcon, is a literary device in which facts previously established in a fictional work are adjusted, reinterpreted or contradicted by a later work, typically to accommodate sequels, resolve inconsistencies, or update a story for new audiences.

Types

Retcons are often categorised as additions (new information filling in gaps, such as a previously unknown sibling), alterations (established facts changed outright, e.g. a character's death reversed or backstory rewritten), and subtractions (elements quietly ignored or declared non-canonical). Long-running franchises — superhero comics, Star Wars, Star Trek, Doctor Who — retcon frequently, sometimes through large-scale "reboots".

Origin of the term

The phrase "retroactive continuity" appeared in print in the 1970s (in comics fandom, associated with Roy Thomas's work at DC Comics), and the shortened "retcon" spread via Usenet in the 1980s.
