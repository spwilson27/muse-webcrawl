Michael Ralph Stonebraker (born October 11, 1943) is an American computer scientist specialising in database systems. At the University of California, Berkeley he led the development of the Ingres relational database system and then the Postgres project, which introduced object-relational concepts that underlie PostgreSQL.

Career

Stonebraker co-founded numerous database companies, including Ingres Corporation, Illustra, StreamBase, Vertica (column-store analytics), VoltDB (in-memory OLTP) and Tamr. He later joined MIT as an adjunct professor. His research argued that "one size fits all" database architectures should give way to specialised engines for different workloads.

Awards

He received the 2014 ACM A.M. Turing Award for fundamental contributions to the concepts and practices underlying modern database systems.
