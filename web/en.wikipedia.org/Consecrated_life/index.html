Consecrated life

The consecrated life is a state of life in the Catholic Church lived by those called to follow Jesus Christ in a more exacting way. It includes those in institutes of consecrated life (religious and secular), societies of apostolic life, and individual forms such as consecrated virgins, hermits, and widows. Members profess the evangelical counsels of chastity, poverty, and obedience.

Forms

Religious institutes — orders and congregations of monks, friars, nuns, brothers, and sisters — are the best-known form, whose members take public vows and live in community. Secular institutes are composed of lay people or clerics living in the world who consecrate their lives through binding commitments. Other recognized forms include eremitical life, the order of virgins, and societies of apostolic life, whose members pursue an apostolic purpose without religious vows strictly speaking.

Canon law

The 1983 Code of Canon Law (canons 573–746) governs institutes of consecrated life. Canon 573 describes the life consecrated through profession of the evangelical counsels as a stable form of living in which the faithful, called by God, dedicate themselves totally to God and to the mission of the Church.

Other traditions

Analogous forms of dedicated religious life exist in other churches and religions: Anglican religious orders, Lutheran deaconess communities, Orthodox monasticism, and in non-Christian traditions such as Buddhist monasticism (the sangha) and Jain ascetic orders.
