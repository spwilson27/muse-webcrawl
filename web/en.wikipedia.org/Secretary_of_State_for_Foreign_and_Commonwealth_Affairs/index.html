Secretary of State for Foreign and Commonwealth Affairs

The Secretary of State for Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Affairs, commonly referred to as the Foreign Secretary, is a senior minister of the Crown in the Government of the United Kingdom and head of the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO).

Role

The Foreign Secretary is responsible for the United Kingdom's relations with foreign countries and governments, matters relating to the Commonwealth and British Overseas Territories, and promotion of British interests abroad. The office is one of the four Great Offices of State, alongside the Prime Minister, Chancellor of the Exchequer, and Home Secretary.

History

The post was created in 1782 when the Northern and Southern Departments were reorganised into the Foreign Office and Home Office. The Foreign Office merged with the Commonwealth Office in 1968 to form the Foreign and Commonwealth Office, which in 2020 absorbed the Department for International Development.
