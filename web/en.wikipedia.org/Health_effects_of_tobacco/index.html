Health effects of tobacco

Tobacco smoking is the leading preventable cause of death worldwide. The World Health Organization estimates that tobacco kills more than 8 million people each year, including about 1.3 million non-smokers exposed to second-hand smoke. Cigarette smoke contains thousands of chemicals, dozens of which are known carcinogens, along with the addictive drug nicotine.

Diseases caused

Smoking is a major cause of cancers of the lung, mouth, throat, oesophagus, bladder, pancreas, kidney and cervix, among others. It is responsible for most cases of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and greatly increases the risk of heart attacks, stroke and peripheral vascular disease. Smoking in pregnancy raises the risk of miscarriage, premature birth and low birth weight.

Second-hand smoke

Involuntary exposure to tobacco smoke increases the risk of lung cancer and heart disease in non-smoking adults and of respiratory infections and asthma attacks in children. Smoke-free legislation in workplaces and public places has been adopted in many countries to reduce this exposure.

Cessation

Quitting at any age improves health: within a few years the excess risk of heart disease falls substantially, and lung-cancer risk declines over a decade or more. Nicotine replacement therapy, medications such as varenicline and bupropion, and behavioural support all increase quit success.
