LGM-35 Sentinel

The LGM-35 Sentinel ICBM, under development to replace the Minuteman III.

The LGM-35 Sentinel, formerly known as the Ground Based Strategic Deterrent (GBSD), is an American land-based intercontinental ballistic missile system under development by Northrop Grumman for the United States Air Force. It is intended to replace the aging LGM-30 Minuteman III missiles, which entered service in 1970, as the land leg of the United States nuclear triad beginning in the late 2020s and remaining in service through about 2075.

Programme

The programme encompasses not just the missile but an entirely new infrastructure: some 400 missiles deployed in existing and modernized silos across Montana, North Dakota, Wyoming, Colorado, and Nebraska, plus new launch control centres and thousands of miles of cabling. Northrop Grumman won the engineering and manufacturing development contract in September 2020, worth about $13.3 billion for that phase. The missile is expected to carry the W87-1 thermonuclear warhead.

Cost growth and restructuring

In January 2024 the Air Force notified Congress that the programme had breached the Nunn–McCurdy Act thresholds, with unit cost growth exceeding 25 percent — later estimated at roughly 37 percent growth, pushing total programme cost estimates to about $141 billion, driven mainly by the vast construction and command-and-control work rather than the missile itself. In July 2024 the Department of Defense nonetheless certified the programme as essential to national security, directing a restructuring, and the Air Force began re-planning deployment timelines, with initial capability slipping into the 2030s.
