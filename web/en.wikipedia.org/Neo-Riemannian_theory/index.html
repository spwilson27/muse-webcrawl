Neo-Riemannian theory

Neo-Riemannian theory is a loose collection of ideas in music theory that arose in the work of theorists such as David Lewin, Brian Hyer, Richard Cohn, and Henry Klumpenhouwer, drawing on the dualist harmonic theories of Hugo Riemann. It studies transformations between triads that are maximally smooth in voice leading, typically outside functional diatonic analysis.

The PLR transformations

The theory centres on three elementary operations on major and minor triads: P (parallel, exchanging a triad for its parallel major or minor), L (leading-tone exchange), and R (relative). Combinations of these generate a group of 24 transformations acting on the 24 major and minor triads, often visualised on the Tonnetz lattice.

Applications

Neo-Riemannian analysis is especially suited to chromatic nineteenth-century music (Wagner, Liszt, Mahler) and to film scores, where triads move by common tones rather than by conventional harmonic function.
