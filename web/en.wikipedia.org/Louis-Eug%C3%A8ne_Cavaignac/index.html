Louis-Eugène Cavaignac

Louis-Eugène Cavaignac (15 October 1802 – 28 October 1857) was a French general and politician. As chief of the executive power of the French Second Republic in 1848, he directed the suppression of the June Days uprising in Paris, and he stood unsuccessfully for the presidency later that year against Louis-Napoléon Bonaparte.

Early career

The son of a revolutionary-era politician and brother of the republican journalist Godefroi Cavaignac, Louis-Eugène graduated from the École Polytechnique and served as a military engineer. He fought in Algeria, where he gained experience in colonial warfare and became a general and, in 1848, governor-general of Algeria.

The June Days

After the February Revolution of 1848, Cavaignac became Minister of War. When the closure of the National Workshops provoked the workers' insurrection of the June Days (23–26 June 1848), the National Assembly granted him dictatorial powers. He organised a methodical military reconquest of eastern Paris, using regular troops brought in from the provinces; the uprising was crushed with heavy loss of life and mass arrests and deportations. On 28 June he laid down his emergency powers and was confirmed as chief of the executive power.

Defeat and later life

Standing as the candidate of the moderate republicans in the presidential election of December 1848, Cavaignac gained about 1.45 million votes but was decisively beaten by Louis-Napoléon Bonaparte. He was arrested briefly at the coup d'état of 1851 and later refused to swear the oath to the Second Empire, withdrawing from public office. He died in 1857.
