The Glenn Research Center (GRC) is a NASA research facility located in Cleveland, Ohio, with its main campus spanning the communities of Brook Park and Fairview Park adjacent to Cleveland Hopkins International Airport. Established in 1941, it is one of NASA's ten major field centers and is the agency's lead center for aeropropulsion, in-space propulsion, power systems, and communications technologies.

History

The center was founded by the National Advisory Committee for Aeronautics (NACA) as the Aircraft Engine Research Laboratory, ground being broken in January 1941 as the United States prepared for the possibility of war. In 1948 it was renamed the Lewis Flight Propulsion Laboratory in honour of George W. Lewis, NACA's long-serving director of aeronautical research, and in 1958 it became the Lewis Research Center when NACA was absorbed into the newly created NASA.

On 1 March 1999 the center was renamed the John H. Glenn Research Center at Lewis Field, honouring astronaut and senator John Glenn, the first American to orbit the Earth and a native of Ohio.

Research areas

Glenn's core competencies include air-breathing and in-space propulsion, spacecraft power generation and storage, communications technologies, and materials and structures for extreme environments. Its facilities include large wind tunnels, the Icing Research Tunnel, vacuum chambers for electric propulsion testing, and the Space Environments Complex.

The center also manages the Neil A. Armstrong Test Facility (formerly Plum Brook Station) near Sandusky, Ohio, which hosts some of the world's largest space-environment simulation chambers and a nuclear reactor research facility (decommissioned).
