Travancore

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Kingdom of Travancore was an Indian kingdom from c. 1729 until 1949. It was ruled by the Travancore royal family from Padmanabhapuram, and later Thiruvananthapuram. The kingdom covered most of modern-day southern Kerala and the southernmost part of Tamil Nadu (Kanyakumari district). Its king took the title of Maharaja, and in 1949 Travancore merged with neighbouring Cochin to form Travancore-Cochin, which in 1956 became part of the state of Kerala.

History

The kingdom was consolidated under Marthanda Varma (reigned 1729–1758), who expanded Venad by conquest, defeated the Dutch East India Company at the Battle of Colachel (1741), and dedicated the kingdom to the deity Padmanabha in 1750. Travancore became a princely state in subsidiary alliance with the British in 1795. Under 19th-century rulers such as Swathi Thirunal, it gained a reputation for progressive administration, early investment in education and public health, and abolition of slavery in 1812.

Administration and society

Later reforms included the Temple Entry Proclamation of 1936, issued by Chithira Thirunal, which opened Hindu temples in the state to all castes. Travancore maintained high literacy relative to other princely states. At Indian independence in 1947, Dewan C. P. Ramaswami Iyer briefly pursued independence before Travancore acceded to India in July 1947.
