Gurugram (Hindi: गुरुग्राम, pronounced [ɡʊɾʊɡraːm]), formerly known as Gurgaon, is a city located in the northern Indian state of Haryana, situated in the National Capital Region near the Delhi–Haryana border, about 30 kilometres (19 mi) southwest of New Delhi. It is one of India's major financial and banking cities and forms part of the National Capital Region (NCR).

History

According to Hindu tradition, the region is associated with Guru Dronacharya, the teacher of the Pandavas and Kauravas in the Mahabharata; the name Gurugram means "village of the guru," and the area is said to have been gifted to Dronacharya by his disciples. For most of its history the settlement remained a small agricultural town. Its modern transformation began in the 1970s–80s with the arrival of Maruti Suzuki's car plant, followed in the 1990s by large-scale private real-estate development led by DLF and the decision of companies such as GE's Genpact to locate back-office operations there. In 2016 the Haryana government officially renamed the city from Gurgaon to Gurugram.

Economy

Gurugram hosts offices of hundreds of Fortune 500 companies and is a hub for information technology, finance, banking, and business-process outsourcing, centred on districts such as Cyber City and Golf Course Road. It has one of the highest per-capita incomes of any Indian city. Rapid, largely private-led growth has also produced chronic challenges: traffic congestion, waterlogging during monsoons, air pollution, and strained water and power infrastructure.

Transport

The city is served by the Delhi–Jaipur highway (NH-48), the Delhi Metro's Yellow Line and the Rapid Metro Gurgaon, and lies close to Indira Gandhi International Airport in Delhi. The Kundli–Manesar–Palwal and Dwarka Expressways have further connected it to the region.
