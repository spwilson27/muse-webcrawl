Pornography

Pornography is material — written, drawn, filmed, photographed, or digital — that depicts sexual subjects primarily for the purpose of sexual arousal. The word derives from the Greek pornographos, "writing about prostitutes". Pornography is distinguished from erotica, though the boundary is contested and culturally variable.

History

Erotic depictions are ancient, appearing in Paleolithic art, in Roman frescoes at Pompeii, and in texts such as the Kama Sutra. The invention of printing, photography, cinema, home video, and the internet each transformed production and distribution, generally making material cheaper and more accessible. Landmark legal battles, including British and American obscenity trials over novels such as Lady Chatterley's Lover and Fanny Hill, reshaped censorship law in the 20th century.

Regulation and debate

Most jurisdictions restrict pornography involving minors absolutely and regulate adult material to varying degrees under obscenity and indecency laws. Public debate involves feminist criticism (both anti-pornography and sex-positive), religious and moral objections, free-speech arguments, and research on social and psychological effects, findings on which remain contested.

Industry

The commercial pornography industry encompasses magazines, films, and, since the 2000s, predominantly internet streaming and subscription platforms. Piracy and free tube sites sharply reduced revenues of traditional studios in the 21st century.
