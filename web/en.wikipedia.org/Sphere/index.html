A sphere (from Greek sphaīra, "globe, ball") is a geometrical object that is a three-dimensional analogue to a two-dimensional circle. A sphere is the set of points that are all at the same distance r from a given point in three-dimensional space; that given point is the centre of the sphere, and r is the sphere's radius. The longest straight line segment through the centre, connecting two points on the sphere, is called a diameter; its length is twice the radius.

Basic properties

The sphere has the smallest surface area of all surfaces that enclose a given volume, and it encloses the largest volume among all closed surfaces with a given surface area, a consequence of the isoperimetric inequality. Its surface area and volume are:

A = 4πr2,    V = (4/3)πr3

Archimedes first derived these formulae, showing that the volume of a sphere is two-thirds the volume of its circumscribed cylinder — a result he prized so much that a sphere and cylinder were placed on his tomb.

Equation

In Cartesian coordinates, a sphere centred at (x0, y0, z0) with radius r satisfies (x − x0)2 + (y − y0)2 + (z − z0)2 = r2. Points on the sphere can also be given parametrically using spherical coordinates (radius, polar angle, azimuth). Great circles — circles on the sphere with the same centre — are the "straight lines" (geodesics) of spherical geometry.

Generalisations

In mathematics, an n-sphere is the set of points at a fixed distance from a centre in (n+1)-dimensional Euclidean space; the ordinary sphere is a 2-sphere. In topology, spheres play a central role, and the Poincaré conjecture concerned characterising the 3-sphere. In common usage, "sphere" may also refer to the solid ball enclosed by the surface.
