The Golden Globe Awards are accolades bestowed for excellence in both American and international film and television. They are an annual ceremony held since 1944 to honour artists and professionals, and form a major part of the film industry's awards season, culminating each year in the Academy Awards. The ceremony is traditionally held in January at the Beverly Hilton hotel in Beverly Hills, California.

History

The awards were founded by the Hollywood Foreign Correspondents Association, which later became the Hollywood Foreign Press Association (HFPA), a group of Los Angeles-based journalists covering Hollywood for foreign outlets. The first ceremony took place in 1944 at the studios of 20th Century Fox. Television categories were added in the 1950s, making the Globes unusual in honouring both media at one event.

Categories

Film awards are split between drama and musical or comedy in the major categories, including Best Motion Picture, Best Director, and acting prizes. Television awards cover drama and comedy series, limited series, and performances. The Cecil B. DeMille Award is an honorary lifetime-achievement prize for film, and the Carol Burnett Award its television counterpart.

Restructuring

Following criticism of the HFPA's membership and ethics in 2021, the organisation was dissolved, and from 2024 the awards have been presented by the Golden Globe Foundation with the ceremony produced commercially. The Globes remain known for their comparatively informal, dinner-party atmosphere.
