Loitering munition

A loitering munition, also known as a suicide drone, kamikaze drone, or exploding drone, is a type of aerial weapon with a built-in warhead that is typically designed to loiter around a target area until a target is located, then attack it by crashing into it.

Characteristics

Loitering munitions combine features of cruise missiles and unmanned aerial vehicles: they are launched, search or wait under operator or autonomous control, and are themselves expended in the attack. Their ability to wait for fleeting targets, and to abort an attack, distinguishes them from conventional missiles.

History and examples

Early systems appeared in the 1980s for suppressing enemy air defences, notably Israel's Harpy anti-radiation weapon. Modern examples include the Israeli Harop, the American AeroVironment Switchblade, the Polish Warmate, the Russian Lancet and the Iranian-designed Shahed 136. Such weapons were used extensively in the Second Nagorno-Karabakh War (2020) and the Russian invasion of Ukraine.

Debate

Systems with autonomous target selection have prompted international debate about meaningful human control over lethal force and the accountability of autonomous weapons.
