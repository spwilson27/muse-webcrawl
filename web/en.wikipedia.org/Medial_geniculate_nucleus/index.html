Medial geniculate nucleus

The medial geniculate nucleus (MGN) or medial geniculate body (MGB) is part of the auditory thalamus and represents the thalamic relay between the inferior colliculus (IC) and the auditory cortex (AC). It is composed of a number of sub-nuclei that are distinguished by their neuronal morphology and density, by their afferent and efferent connections, and by the coding properties of their neurons. It is thought that the MGN influences the direction and maintenance of attention.

The medial geniculate nucleus is the auditory relay station of the thalamus.

Divisions

The MGN is divided into three major parts:

  • Ventral division (MGNv) — the lemniscal, tonotopically organized relay. Its neurons are sharply tuned to frequency and relay precise auditory information via the acoustic radiation to the primary auditory cortex.
  • Dorsal division (MGNd) — broadly tuned neurons responding to multiple frequencies; projects to secondary (belt) auditory areas and is involved in more integrative processing.
  • Medial division (MGNm) — contains multisensory neurons that also respond to somatosensory input; projects widely, including to the amygdala, where it plays a role in auditory fear conditioning.

Function

The MGN receives its main ascending input from the inferior colliculus of the midbrain via the brachium of the inferior colliculus, and massive descending feedback from the auditory cortex. Beyond a simple relay, it gates and modulates auditory information according to attention and arousal, partly through its interactions with the thalamic reticular nucleus.
