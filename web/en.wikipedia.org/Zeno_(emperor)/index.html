Zeno the Isaurian (c. 425 – 9 April 491), born Tarasis, was Eastern Roman emperor from 474 to 475 and again from 476 to 491. His reign was marked by domestic rebellion and religious dissension, but under pressure from the Ostrogoths he managed to preserve the Eastern Empire, while the Western Empire fell during his reign in 476.

An Isaurian chieftain from the Taurus mountains, Tarasis rose in the service of Emperor Leo I, who valued the Isaurians as a counterweight to Germanic influence at court. He took the Greek name Zeno and married the emperor's daughter Ariadne. On the death of Leo I in 474, Zeno became co-emperor with his young son Leo II and sole emperor on the boy's death later that year. His unpopularity — he was widely resented as a provincial outsider — led to a coup in 475 by Basiliscus, brother-in-law of Leo I, which drove Zeno to flee to Isauria. Basiliscus's misrule allowed Zeno to recover Constantinople in August 476 without a battle.

Later reign

Weeks after his restoration, Zeno received the imperial insignia of the West from Odoacer, who had deposed Romulus Augustulus, and formally accepted Odoacer as king of Italy. In 488 Zeno rid himself of the Ostrogothic king Theodoric by commissioning him to conquer Italy, which Theodoric accomplished by 493. Religiously, Zeno's Henotikon (482), an edict of union intended to reconcile Chalcedonian and Miaphysite Christians, instead provoked the Acacian schism with Rome (484–519). Zeno died of dysentery or epilepsy in April 491 and was succeeded by Anastasius I, chosen by the widowed Ariadne, whom Anastasius then married.
