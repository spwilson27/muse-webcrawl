Alexy II (born Aleksey Mikhailovich Ridiger, 1929–2008) was the 15th Patriarch of Moscow and all Rus', head of the Russian Orthodox Church from 1990 until his death in 2008. Born in Tallinn, Estonia, to a family of Baltic German and Russian descent, he entered church service as a young man and was ordained a priest in 1950, serving in Estonia before becoming Bishop of Tallinn in 1961.

Patriarchate

Alexy rose through the hierarchy — Metropolitan of Leningrad and Novgorod from 1986 — and was elected Patriarch in June 1990, the first post-Soviet-era election held shortly before the USSR's collapse. His patriarchate oversaw the massive revival of Russian Orthodoxy after decades of state atheism: thousands of parishes and monasteries reopened, and the Cathedral of Christ the Saviour in Moscow was rebuilt. He worked to restore the Church's public role while guarding its unity and its relations with the state, and he presided over the 2007 reunification with the Russian Orthodox Church Outside Russia, healing a schism dating to the 1920s. He was succeeded by Patriarch Kirill.
