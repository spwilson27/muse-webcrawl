Tomb of the Unknown Soldier

Tombs of unknown soldiers honour the unidentified dead of a nation's wars.

A Tomb of the Unknown Soldier or Tomb of Unknown Soldiers is a monument dedicated to the services of an unknown soldier and to the common memories of all soldiers killed in war. Such tombs are located in many nations and are usually high-profile national monuments. Throughout history, many soldiers have died in war with their remains being unidentified. Following World War I, a movement arose to commemorate these soldiers with a single tomb, containing the body of one such unidentified soldier.

History

The modern tradition began in 1920, when the United Kingdom interred the Unknown Warrior in Westminster Abbey and France placed an unknown soldier beneath the Arc de Triomphe in Paris, both on 11 November 1920. The United States followed in 1921, interring a World War I unknown at Arlington National Cemetery; unknowns from World War II, the Korean War and the Vietnam War were later added (the Vietnam unknown was identified by DNA testing in 1998 as Michael J. Blassie and reburied elsewhere, leaving that crypt empty). Canada interred an unknown soldier at the National War Memorial in Ottawa in 2000.

Notable examples

Many countries maintain such monuments, including Australia's Tomb of the Unknown Australian Soldier at the Australian War Memorial in Canberra, India's Amar Jawan Jyoti, Russia's Tomb of the Unknown Soldier in the Alexander Garden in Moscow, and Greece's monument in Syntagma Square, Athens. At Arlington, the tomb is guarded around the clock by sentinels of the U.S. 3rd Infantry Regiment ("The Old Guard"), whose changing-of-the-guard ceremony is a well-known ritual.

Symbolism

The unknown soldier represents every service member who died without a known grave, allowing the bereaved to mourn at a single national shrine and embodying the idea that no sacrifice is forgotten merely because a name is lost.
