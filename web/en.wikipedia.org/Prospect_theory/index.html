Prospect theory

Prospect theory is a behavioural-economic theory of decision under risk developed by Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky in 1979. It describes how people actually choose between probabilistic alternatives, departing systematically from expected utility theory.

Key features

Outcomes are evaluated as gains and losses relative to a reference point, not as final wealth. The value function is concave for gains (risk aversion), convex for losses (risk seeking), and steeper for losses — loss aversion. Probabilities are transformed by a weighting function overweighting small probabilities. Framing effects follow naturally. Cumulative prospect theory (1992) extended the model to rank-dependent weighting. Kahneman received the 2002 Nobel Memorial Prize in part for this work.
