The House of Griffin (German: Greifen; Polish: Gryfici) was a Slavic dynasty of dukes who ruled the Duchy of Pomerania from the 12th century until 1637. The dynasty takes its name from the griffin in its coat of arms, which remains the heraldic symbol of Pomerania today.

History

The first known duke was Wartislaw I, who in the 1120s accepted Polish suzerainty and welcomed the Christian missions of Otto of Bamberg. His descendants ruled a duchy that was repeatedly divided among branches (notably the lines of Stettin/Szczecin and Demmin) and reunited. Pomerania became an imperial fief of the Holy Roman Empire in 1181. Under dukes such as Barnim I and Bogislaw X, the duchy was unified and its towns joined the Hanseatic League; Bogislaw X united all Pomeranian lands in 1478.

Extinction

The duchy adopted the Lutheran Reformation in 1534. The last Griffin duke, Bogislaw XIV, died in 1637 during the Thirty Years' War; under the Peace of Westphalia (1648) Pomerania was divided between Sweden and Brandenburg-Prussia, ending more than five centuries of Griffin rule.
