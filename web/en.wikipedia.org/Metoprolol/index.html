Metoprolol

Metoprolol, sold under brand names including Lopressor and Toprol-XL, is a beta blocker medication — a selective β1 receptor blocker — used to treat high blood pressure, angina (chest pain due to poor blood flow to the heart), heart failure, and to reduce the risk of further heart problems after a heart attack. It is also used for rate control in some abnormal heart rhythms and off-label for migraine prevention. It is taken by mouth or given intravenously.

Medical uses and forms

Metoprolol tartrate is immediate-release and usually taken twice daily; metoprolol succinate is an extended-release form taken once daily and is the form with evidence in chronic heart failure with reduced ejection fraction. By blocking beta-1 adrenergic receptors it slows heart rate, reduces contractility and lowers blood pressure.

Side effects

Common side effects include tiredness, dizziness, slow heart rate, low blood pressure, diarrhoea and cold hands and feet. It can mask the warning signs of low blood sugar in people with diabetes, worsen asthma despite its relative beta-1 selectivity, and should not be stopped abruptly because of rebound risk. It is metabolised mainly by the liver enzyme CYP2D6, so levels vary with genotype and interacting drugs.

History

Metoprolol was developed by Astra (Hässle) in Sweden and patented in the 1960s-70s; it is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines and is available as a generic worldwide.
