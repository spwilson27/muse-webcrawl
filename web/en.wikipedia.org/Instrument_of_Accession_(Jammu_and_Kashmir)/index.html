Instrument of Accession (Jammu and Kashmir)

Signed26 October 1947
SignatoryMaharaja Hari Singh
Accepted byLord Mountbatten, Governor-General of India, 27 October 1947

The Instrument of Accession is a legal document executed by Maharaja Hari Singh, ruler of the princely state of Jammu and Kashmir, on 26 October 1947, acceding the state to the Dominion of India. It was accepted by Lord Mountbatten, the Governor-General of India, on 27 October 1947.

Background

At the partition of British India in 1947, princely states were free to accede to India or Pakistan or remain independent. Hari Singh initially sought independence, signing a standstill agreement with Pakistan. Following an invasion by tribal forces from Pakistan in October 1947, the Maharaja appealed to India for military assistance and signed the Instrument of Accession.

Terms

Like other instruments of accession, the document ceded to the Dominion control over defence, external affairs and communications, while preserving the ruler's internal autonomy. The accession became the legal basis of India's claim to Jammu and Kashmir and a central element of the Kashmir conflict.
