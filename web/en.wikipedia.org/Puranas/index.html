Puranas

The Puranas (Sanskrit: पुराण, "ancient, old") are a vast genre of Indian literature—Hindu, Jain, and Buddhist—comprising myths, legends, royal genealogies, cosmology, pilgrimage guides, theology, and philosophy. Traditionally attributed to the sage Vyasa, the eighteen major Hindu Puranas (Mahapuranas) were composed and redacted over many centuries, roughly from the early centuries CE to the early medieval period, and remain primary vehicles of Hindu devotional teaching.

Contents and structure

A canonical Purana is said to treat five topics (pancha-lakshana): primary creation (sarga), secondary creation (pratisarga), genealogies of gods and sages (vamsha), cosmic cycles or ages of Manu (manvantara), and dynastic histories (vamshanucharita). In practice the texts range far beyond this scheme, covering temple lore, rituals, vows, festivals, ethics, and sectarian theology.

The eighteen Mahapuranas

The major list comprises the Brahma, Padma, Vishnu, Shiva, Bhagavata, Narada, Markandeya, Agni, Bhavishya, Brahmavaivarta, Linga, Varaha, Skanda, Vamana, Kurma, Matsya, Garuda, and Brahmanda Puranas. Tradition classifies them by the presiding deity and by the three gunas: sattvic Puranas glorify Vishnu, rajasic ones Brahma, and tamasic ones Shiva. The Bhagavata Purana, centered on Krishna, is perhaps the most influential and beloved, while the Markandeya Purana contains the Devi Mahatmyam, the foundational text of goddess worship. Eighteen Upapuranas (minor Puranas) supplement the main list.

Role and study

Puranic storytelling through recitation (katha) shaped popular Hinduism, transmitting temple myths and festival origins to broad audiences. Scholars date the surviving texts through internal evidence, noting layers of composition; the Puranas are also mined, cautiously, as sources for ancient Indian political history through their dynastic lists. Jain and Buddhist Puranas adapt the genre to their own universal histories and lineages of teachers.
