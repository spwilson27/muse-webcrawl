Shark fin soup

Shark fin soup is a traditional Chinese soup made with shark fin as a primary ingredient. The shark fins provide texture — gelatinous, noodle-like strands of cartilage — while the taste comes primarily from the broth, often enriched with chicken, ham, and mushrooms. The dish has been served at banquets and celebrations as a symbol of prestige and prosperity since the Ming dynasty.

Preparation

Fins are dried, skinned, and processed to remove their cartilage needles, which are then simmered in a rich stock. The soup is commonly served at weddings, Lunar New Year banquets, and important business dinners in China and overseas Chinese communities.

Controversy

Demand for fins drives the practice of shark finning — cutting off a shark's fins and discarding the body at sea — and has contributed to steep declines in many shark populations. Conservation campaigns, celebrity advocacy, and changing tastes have reduced consumption, and many jurisdictions, airlines, hotels, and shipping companies have banned the trade or transport of fins. Imitation shark fin soup, made with substitutes such as mung bean vermicelli or konjac, is increasingly offered.
