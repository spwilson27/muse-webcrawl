Adverse effect

SynonymAdverse event (broader term)
FieldPharmacology, medicine
Related termsSide effect, adverse drug reaction, iatrogenesis

An adverse effect is an undesired harmful effect resulting from a medical treatment such as medication, surgery or another intervention. Adverse effects are sometimes informally called "side effects", although strictly a side effect may be harmful, beneficial or neutral, whereas an adverse effect is by definition unwanted and noxious. An adverse effect caused by a drug is termed an adverse drug reaction.

Classification and reporting

Adverse effects vary in severity from mild (transient nausea, headache) to serious (hospitalisation, disability, birth defects or death). Regulatory systems classify events as expected or unexpected and grade severity; spontaneous reporting systems — such as the FDA's MedWatch, the UK's Yellow Card, and the WHO's VigiBase — collect reports from clinicians and patients so that safety signals can be detected after a drug reaches the market. Clinical trials systematically record adverse events and compare their rates between treatment and control groups.

Mechanisms

Many adverse effects are predictable extensions of a drug's pharmacology (dose-related, "type A"), while others are unpredictable, immune-mediated or idiosyncratic ("type B"). Effects may also arise from drug interactions, medical errors, or withdrawal. The prospect of adverse effects, weighed against therapeutic benefit, is central to prescribing decisions and informed consent.
