Micronesia

The region of Micronesia in the western Pacific Ocean.

Micronesia is a subregion of Oceania, consisting of approximately 2,000 small islands in the northwestern Pacific Ocean. It has a close shared cultural history with three other island regions in the Pacific: Polynesia to the east and Melanesia to the south, as well as with the wider Austronesian peoples.

The name Micronesia derives from the Greek words μικρός (mikrós, "small") and νῆσος (nêsos, "island"), and was proposed in the 19th century (notably by Jules Dumont d'Urville) to distinguish the region's small islands from Polynesia and Melanesia.

Geography and states

The region includes the sovereign states of the Federated States of Micronesia, the Marshall Islands, Palau, Nauru, and Kiribati (whose Gilbert Islands lie in Micronesia), as well as the US territories of Guam, the Northern Mariana Islands, and Wake Island. Most islands are low coral atolls, though the region also includes raised limestone islands and some volcanic high islands such as Pohnpei and Kosrae.

History

Micronesia was settled by Austronesian seafarers in waves beginning more than 3,000 years ago, from Island Southeast Asia and through the Bismarck Archipelago. Sophisticated navigational traditions allowed long voyages by outrigger canoe. The monumental stone city of Nan Madol on Pohnpei, built between roughly 1180 and 1500, testifies to powerful chiefdoms.

European contact began with Magellan's landfall on Guam in 1521. Spain claimed the Marianas and Carolines; Germany purchased the Caroline and Marshall Islands in 1899. Japan administered most of the region under a League of Nations mandate after World War I. The Pacific War brought major battles, including those of Tarawa, Kwajalein, and Peleliu, and post-war the islands formed the US-administered Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands. Between 1978 and 1994 the region's states became independent, several in free association with the United States, and the US Bikini and Enewetak atolls remained marked by Cold War nuclear testing.

Culture

Languages of the region mostly belong to the Micronesian branch of the Oceanic Austronesian languages (including Marshallese, Chuukese, Pohnpeian, and Gilbertese/Kiribati), with Palauan and Chamorro in separate Western Malayo-Polynesian branches. Traditional societies are noted for matrilineal clans, master navigation, and distinctive arts such as the stone money of Yap.
