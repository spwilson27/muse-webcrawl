The 1928 United States presidential election was the 36th quadrennial presidential election, held on Tuesday, November 6, 1928. The Republican candidate, former Secretary of Commerce Herbert Hoover, defeated the Democratic nominee, Governor Al Smith of New York, in a landslide.

Campaign

Incumbent Republican President Calvin Coolidge chose not to run for a second full term. Hoover, celebrated for his humanitarian relief work and administrative competence, campaigned on continued prosperity during the Roaring Twenties. Smith, the first Catholic major-party nominee, faced significant anti-Catholic prejudice, and his opposition to Prohibition divided his party in the rural South and West.

Results

Hoover won 444 electoral votes to Smith's 87, carrying 40 states including Smith's home state of New York and several traditionally Democratic Southern states. Smith carried only eight states but greatly increased Democratic turnout in major cities, foreshadowing the New Deal coalition. Within a year of Hoover's inauguration, the Wall Street Crash of 1929 ushered in the Great Depression.
