South Kensington is a district of West London in the Royal Borough of Kensington and Chelsea. It is best known for the cluster of great national museums and academic institutions along Exhibition Road — sometimes called "Albertopolis" after Prince Albert — including the Natural History Museum, the Victoria and Albert Museum, the Science Museum, Imperial College London, the Royal College of Music and the Royal Albert Hall.

History

The area was rural until the 19th century. Profits from the Great Exhibition of 1851 were used to buy land in the district for cultural and scientific institutions, fulfilling Prince Albert's vision. Grand terraces of white stucco town houses followed, and the district became a fashionable residential area, later notable for a large French expatriate community — the Lycée Français Charles de Gaulle, the French Institute and the French consulate are all located there.

Transport

South Kensington station, served by the District, Circle and Piccadilly lines, connects the museums via a pedestrian subway from the station to Exhibition Road.
