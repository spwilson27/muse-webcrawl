z/OS is a 64-bit operating system for IBM Z mainframe computers, produced by IBM since 2001 as the successor to OS/390. It descends in an unbroken line from OS/360 (1966) through MVS and OS/390, and preserves compatibility with applications and data from those systems while adding modern networking, UNIX services and workload management.

z/OS is designed for very large-scale batch processing and transaction serving: features such as the Workload Manager (WLM), Parallel Sysplex clustering, CICS and Db2 integration, and pervasive-encryption support make it a mainstay of banking, insurance and government computing. It includes UNIX System Services, a POSIX-compliant environment, alongside classic MVS facilities such as JCL, TSO/ISPF and JES2. Major new releases appear roughly every two years, with continuous delivery of functions between releases.
