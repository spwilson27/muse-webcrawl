Fin whale

Scientific nameBalaenoptera physalus
LengthUp to ≈ 27 m
StatusVulnerable (IUCN)

The fin whale (Balaenoptera physalus), also known as the finback whale or common rorqual, is a species of baleen whale and the second-largest animal on Earth after the blue whale, reaching lengths of about 27 metres and weights of around 70 tonnes.

Description

The fin whale has a long, slender, streamlined body with a distinctive asymmetrical colouration of the lower jaw — white on the right side and black on the left. It is one of the fastest cetaceans, capable of bursts of 37 km/h, earning it the nickname "greyhound of the sea".

Distribution and diet

Fin whales live in all major oceans from polar to tropical waters. They feed mainly on krill, small schooling fish, and squid, lunging through swarms with mouths open and filtering food through baleen plates.

Conservation

Commercial whaling in the twentieth century killed an estimated 750,000 fin whales in the Southern Hemisphere alone. The species is listed as Vulnerable by the IUCN, and populations have slowly recovered under the whaling moratorium.
