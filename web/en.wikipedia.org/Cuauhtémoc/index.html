Cuauhtémoc

Cuauhtémoc (c. 1497 – 1525), also known as Cuauhtemotzín, was the last Aztec emperor (tlatoani) of Tenochtitlan, the Mexica capital. His name in Nahuatl means "one who has descended like an eagle". He succeeded his cousin Cuitláhuac, who died of smallpox during the Spanish siege, and led the defence of the city against Hernán Cortés in 1521.

Reign and fall

Cuauhtémoc organized the resistance during the final siege of Tenochtitlan. After the city's fall on 13 August 1521, he was captured while attempting to escape across Lake Texcoco. He was later tortured by the Spanish, who burned his feet to force him to reveal the location of Aztec treasure. In 1525, during Cortés's expedition to Honduras, Cuauhtémoc was accused of plotting a rebellion and hanged.

Legacy

Cuauhtémoc is revered in Mexico as a national hero symbolizing resistance to conquest. His name is borne by a borough of Mexico City, numerous towns, and a Mexican Navy training ship. The authenticity of remains said to be his, discovered at Ixcateopan in 1949, remains disputed.
