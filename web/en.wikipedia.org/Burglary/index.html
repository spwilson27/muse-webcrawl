Burglary, also called breaking and entering and sometimes housebreaking, is the act of illegally entering a building or other structure with the intent to commit a crime inside, usually theft. At common law, burglary was defined as the breaking and entering of the dwelling house of another in the nighttime with intent to commit a felony therein. Modern statutes have generally broadened the offence to cover entry of any building or vehicle, at any time, with intent to commit theft or another crime inside.

Elements and modern law

Under modern law, burglary typically requires (1) an unlawful or unprivileged entry into (2) a building or occupied structure (3) with intent to commit a crime inside; neither actual breaking nor completion of the intended crime is required. In England and Wales, burglary is defined by section 9 of the Theft Act 1968, with aggravated burglary — committed with a weapon or explosive — under section 10. In the United States, definitions vary by state, but the FBI's Uniform Crime Reporting program classifies burglary as the unlawful entry of a structure to commit a felony or theft, divided into forcible entry, unlawful entry without force, and attempted forcible entry.

Statistics and prevention

Burglary rates in many Western countries rose through the mid-20th century and have generally declined since the 1990s, a trend attributed in part to improved locks, alarms, and target hardening. Burglary is distinguished from robbery, which involves force or threat against a person, and from simple trespass, which lacks criminal intent.
