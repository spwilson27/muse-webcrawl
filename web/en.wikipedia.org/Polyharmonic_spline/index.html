Polyharmonic spline

In applied mathematics, polyharmonic splines are a family of functions used for interpolation and approximation of scattered data. They are formed as linear combinations of radially symmetric basis functions together with a polynomial term, and they minimise a bending-energy functional involving iterated Laplacians—hence the name, from the polyharmonic equation.

Definition

A polyharmonic spline of order k combines radial basis functions of the form r2k−n (or r2k−n ln r when 2k−n is an even integer), where r is the Euclidean distance from a data centre and n is the spatial dimension, plus a polynomial of degree k−1. The coefficients are determined by requiring interpolation of the given data values and side conditions that the coefficients of the basis functions be orthogonal to the polynomial terms. The most widely used member of the family is the thin-plate spline, corresponding to k = 2 in two dimensions, whose basis function is r² ln r.

Properties and applications

Polyharmonic splines work in any dimension, require no mesh or tuning parameter such as a shape parameter, and are conditionally positive definite, guaranteeing that the interpolation system has a unique solution. Drawbacks include dense linear systems and global support, which can be costly for very large data sets. Applications include image warping and morphing, three-dimensional surface reconstruction, topography and geophysics, and machine-learning methods for scattered-data approximation.
