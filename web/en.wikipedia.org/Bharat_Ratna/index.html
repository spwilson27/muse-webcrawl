The Bharat Ratna (Hindi: भारत रत्न, "Jewel of India") is the highest civilian award of the Republic of India. Instituted on 2 January 1954, it is conferred "in recognition of exceptional service or performance of the highest order" in any field of human endeavour, without distinction of race, occupation, position, or sex.

History

The award was established by President Rajendra Prasad alongside the Padma Vibhushan hierarchy. The first recipients, in 1954, were statesman C. Rajagopalachari, philosopher and Vice-President Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan, and physicist C. V. Raman. Recommendations are made by the Prime Minister to the President, and by convention no more than three awards are given in a year — though five were announced in 2024, an exception that drew wide comment.

Design

The original 1954 design was a circular gold medal; the current medallion is shaped like a leaf of the peepal tree (Ficus religiosa), bearing a sun-burst design and the words "Bharat Ratna" in Devanagari, worn on a white ribbon around the neck. Recipients also receive a certificate signed by the President; the award carries no monetary grant.

Notable recipients

Recipients include Jawaharlal Nehru, Indira Gandhi (awarded while Prime Minister), Mother Teresa, Nelson Mandela (one of few non-citizens honoured), A. P. J. Abdul Kalam, Lata Mangeshkar, and cricketer Sachin Tendulkar, in 2014 the youngest recipient and first sportsperson. Posthumous awards have been made to figures including B. R. Ambedkar (1990) and Madan Mohan Malaviya (2015).
