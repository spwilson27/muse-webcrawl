Coriander

Scientific nameCoriandrum sativum
FamilyApiaceae
Parts usedLeaves (cilantro) and dried seeds
OriginMediterranean and Near East

Coriander (Coriandrum sativum) is an annual herb in the family Apiaceae. All parts of the plant are edible, but the fresh leaves and the dried seeds are most commonly used in cooking. In North America the leaves are usually called cilantro, while the seeds are called coriander.

Flavour and perception

The leaves taste citrusy and bright to most people, but a significant minority perceive a soapy or metallic taste, an aversion linked to variation in the olfactory receptor gene OR6A2, which heightens sensitivity to aldehydes present in the leaf. The dried seeds have a warm, nutty, lemony flavour and are a staple of spice blends such as garam masala and curry powders.

History and uses

Coriander is one of the oldest known spices, with seeds found in Neolithic sites and in the tomb of Tutankhamun. It is essential in the cuisines of South Asia, the Middle East, Latin America, China and Southeast Asia, and its root is used in Thai cooking.
