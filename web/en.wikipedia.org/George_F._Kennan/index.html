George Frost Kennan (February 16, 1904 – March 17, 2005) was an American diplomat and historian. He was a leading figure in the group of foreign-policy elders known as "The Wise Men", and is best known as the author of the "Long Telegram" of 1946 and the subsequent "X Article", which together supplied the intellectual foundation for the United States policy of containment toward the Soviet Union during the Cold War.

George F. Kennan, architect of containment.

The Long Telegram and the X Article

As chargé d'affaires in Moscow, Kennan sent his 8,000-word Long Telegram to Washington in February 1946, analysing the sources of Soviet conduct and recommending patient resistance to Soviet expansion. In July 1947 he published the argument anonymously as "The Sources of Soviet Conduct" in Foreign Affairs, signed "X". As the first head of the State Department's Policy Planning Staff, he helped design the Marshall Plan. Kennan later insisted that he had intended containment to be primarily political and economic, and he became a prominent critic of the militarised form it later took, including the Vietnam War.

Later life

After a brief and turbulent ambassadorship to the Soviet Union in 1952 — Moscow declared him persona non grata — and service as ambassador to Yugoslavia (1961–1963), Kennan spent the rest of his long life as a scholar at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton. His historical works, including Russia Leaves the War (1956), won the Pulitzer Prize. He died in 2005 at the age of 101.
