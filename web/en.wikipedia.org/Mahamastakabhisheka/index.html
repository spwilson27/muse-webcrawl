Mahamastakabhisheka

The Mahamastakabhisheka ("great head anointing") is a major Jain festival held once every twelve years at Shravanabelagola in Karnataka, India, in honour of the Jain deity Bahubali (Gommateshwara). During the ceremony, the 17.4-metre (57-foot) monolithic statue of Bahubali — carved around 983 CE under the Western Ganga dynasty — is anointed from scaffolding with water, milk, sandalwood paste, turmeric, vermilion and other sacred substances poured from hundreds of pots.

The festival

The event draws hundreds of thousands of devotees and tourists. Rituals extend over many days and include processions, prayers and cultural programmes. The most recent Mahamastakabhisheka at Shravanabelagola was held in February 2018, inaugurated by the President of India. Similar anointing ceremonies are held for Bahubali statues at other Jain sites, including Karkala, Dharmasthala and Venur.

Significance

Bahubali, son of the first Jain Tirthankara Rishabhanatha, is revered for renouncing his kingdom and attaining liberation after standing in meditation for a year, so still that vines grew around his legs — an episode depicted in the statue.
