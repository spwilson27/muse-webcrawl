Sandia National Laboratories (SNL) is one of three National Nuclear Security Administration research and development laboratories in the United States. Its primary responsibilities are the engineering of nuclear weapons systems — developing, testing and maintaining the non-nuclear components of the US nuclear arsenal — together with broader work in energy, homeland security and defence science. The main campus is in Albuquerque, New Mexico, on Kirtland Air Force Base, with a second major site in Livermore, California.

History

Sandia began in 1945 as the ordnance engineering division ("Z Division") of Los Alamos, set up on the former Oxnard Field near Albuquerque to handle weapons assembly and testing near the Salton Sea test site in California. In 1948 it became Sandia Laboratory, and in 1949 President Harry S. Truman asked Western Electric (part of AT&T) to manage it as a public service. It was renamed Sandia National Laboratories in 1979. Management passed to Lockheed Martin in 1993 and to NTESS, a Honeywell subsidiary, in 2017.

Work and facilities

Sandia is the engineering laboratory for the nuclear stockpile, responsible for systems such as arming, fuzing and firing sets, neutron generators, and the safety and security of weapons across their life cycle. Major facilities have included the Z Pulsed Power Facility (the Z machine), one of the world's most powerful X-ray generators used in fusion and materials research; large centrifuge and rocket-sled test tracks; and extensive microelectronics and computing programmes. During and after the Cold War the laboratory expanded into arms-control verification, energy research (including solar, wind and battery storage), cybersecurity and border security technology.
