Electromagnetic field

Components: Electric field, magnetic field

Governed by: Maxwell's equations

Sources: Electric charges and currents

An electromagnetic field (also EM field) is a physical field, mathematical functions of position and time, representing the influences on and due to electric charges. The field affects the behavior of charged objects in its vicinity and is described by two components: the electric field and the magnetic field. Underlying the field is the electromagnetic force, one of the four fundamental interactions of nature.

Description

The electromagnetic field is described classically by Maxwell's equations, formulated by James Clerk Maxwell in the 1860s, which unify electricity and magnetism and predict electromagnetic waves propagating at the speed of light — identifying light itself as an electromagnetic phenomenon. The force on a charged particle is given by the Lorentz force law, combining the effects of both field components.

Structure

The field may be viewed as the combination of an electric field, produced by stationary charges and by changing magnetic fields, and a magnetic field, produced by moving charges (currents) and by changing electric fields. In the near field, close to sources, the two components behave quasi-statically; in the far field they propagate together as a self-sustaining electromagnetic wave.

Quantization and health

In quantum electrodynamics the field is quantized and its excitations are photons. Everyday exposures at low levels are regulated by safety guidelines; ionizing electromagnetic radiation (ultraviolet and above) can damage tissue, while non-ionizing fields are studied for possible thermal and biological effects.
