Federal Chancellery (Berlin)

The Federal Chancellery in Berlin is the official seat of the Chancellor of Germany.

The Federal Chancellery (German: Bundeskanzleramt) in Berlin is the official seat of the Chancellor of Germany as well as the executive office (agency) of the Chancellor. Located in the government district (Regierungsviertel) near the Reichstag building, the current building was opened in 2001 and is one of the largest government headquarters buildings in the world.

The building was designed by architects Axel Schultes and Charlotte Frank as part of the "Band des Bundes" (Federal Ribbon) complex of government buildings spanning the Spree River in the former East and West Berlin. It is sometimes nicknamed "Kohllosseum" or "Waschmaschine" ("washing machine") in Berlin slang, references to Chancellor Helmut Kohl, under whom it was planned, and to its blocky modernist design.

History

After German reunification and the decision to move the capital from Bonn to Berlin, a new Chancellery was built in the capital. Chancellor Gerhard Schröder moved into the building in 2001. The open courtyard (Ehrenhof) is used for official receptions with military honours, and the building's park hosts large-scale sculptures, including a monumental work by Eduardo Chillida.
