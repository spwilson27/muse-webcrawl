Montane ecosystems

Montane ecosystems are ecosystems found on mountains. These ecosystems are strongly affected by climate, which gets colder as elevation increases, and they often display a sequence of distinct habitat bands stacked vertically along a slope. Montane ecosystems are important reservoirs of biodiversity and endemism, because isolation between high-elevation habitats promotes speciation.

Habitat typeMountain ecosystem
Key driversElevation, temperature, precipitation
ZonesFoothill, montane forest, subalpine, alpine, nival

Life zones

As elevation rises, temperature drops at an average lapse rate of about 6 °C per 1,000 metres. This produces a zonation comparable to travelling from the equator toward the poles: foothill habitats give way to montane forest, then subalpine forest, then open alpine tundra above the tree line, and finally the nival zone of rock, snow and ice.

Climate

Mountain climates are characterised by strong daily and seasonal temperature swings, high ultraviolet radiation at altitude, thin soils, and precipitation that often increases with elevation on windward slopes while leeward slopes lie in a rain shadow.

Biodiversity

Montane regions such as the Andes, the Himalayas and the East African mountains harbour exceptional numbers of endemic species. Because warming climates push species upslope, montane ecosystems are considered especially vulnerable to climate change.
