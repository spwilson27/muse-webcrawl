Conservation agriculture

Conservation agriculture (CA) is a farming system built on three linked principles: minimal mechanical soil disturbance (no-till or reduced tillage), permanent organic soil cover (crop residues or cover crops), and diversification of crop species through rotations and associations. Promoted by the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations, it aims to sustain yields while improving soil health, water retention and resilience to climate change.

Benefits and spread

By leaving residues on the surface, conservation agriculture reduces erosion and evaporation, builds soil organic matter and cuts fuel use, since fewer tractor passes are needed. It is widely practised in the Americas — in parts of Brazil, Argentina, the United States and Canada, no-till methods cover a majority of cropland — and has spread to Australia, Europe and parts of Africa and Asia.

Challenges

Adoption can be hindered by the cost of specialised seeders, weed pressure during the transition (sometimes increasing herbicide reliance), and competition for crop residues used as livestock fodder or fuel in smallholder systems. Long-term trials generally show stable or improved yields after soil conditions recover, though results vary with climate and soil type.
