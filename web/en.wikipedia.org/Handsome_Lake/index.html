Handsome Lake

Handsome Lake (1735–1815) was a Seneca religious leader of the Iroquois people.

Handsome Lake (Seneca: Sganyadái:yo, "He Who Is Handsome"; 1735 – 10 August 1815) was a Seneca religious leader of the Iroquois (Haudenosaunee) people. He was a half-brother of the Seneca chief Cornplanter and an uncle of Governor Blacksnake. In 1799, having sunk into despair, alcoholism, and ill health after the devastation of the Seneca by the American Revolutionary War and subsequent land losses, Handsome Lake experienced a series of visions in which messengers of the Creator instructed him in a moral code. He revived and preached this teaching — the Gaiwiio, the "Good Message" — for the rest of his life.

The Good Message (Gaiwiio)

The Code of Handsome Lake combined elements of traditional Iroquois religion with ideas influenced by Quaker teaching, whom the Seneca knew through missions in the 1790s. It condemned alcohol, witchcraft, abortion, and domestic abuse; encouraged temperance, family stability, land retention, and adoption of Euro-American farming by men (a significant change, since agriculture had traditionally been women's work in Seneca society); and preserved the traditional ceremonial cycle of thanksgiving festivals and the longhouse as its centre. The code also foretold apocalyptic punishments for those who abandoned the teachings.

Legacy

Handsome Lake's preaching is credited with revitalising Seneca and wider Haudenosaunee society at a moment of crisis, helping communities resist removal and cultural dissolution. After his death at Onondaga in 1815, his followers preserved the Gaiwiio by memorised recitation; it was written down in various versions in the 19th and early 20th centuries, including a version published in 1913 by the New York State Museum. The religion he founded — the Longhouse Religion — is still practiced today in longhouses across Iroquois communities in New York, Ontario, and Quebec, where the Code is recited in full over several days at intervals of a few years.
