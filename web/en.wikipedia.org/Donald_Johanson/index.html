Donald Johanson

Donald Carl Johanson (born 1943) is an American paleoanthropologist known for discovering the fossil skeleton of Australopithecus afarensis nicknamed "Lucy" at Hadar, Ethiopia, in 1974, one of the most famous discoveries in human evolutionary science.

Discovery of Lucy

On November 24, 1974, Johanson, working with graduate student Tom Gray, found a partial skeleton — about 40 percent complete — of an early hominin that was later identified as a new species, Australopithecus afarensis, dated to about 3.2 million years ago. The skeleton, catalogued as AL 288-1, was nicknamed "Lucy" after the Beatles song "Lucy in the Sky with Diamonds," which was playing at the camp.

Later career

Johanson founded the Institute of Human Origins at the University of California, Berkeley in 1981, later moved to Arizona State University, and has written popular books including Lucy: The Beginnings of Humankind (1981).
