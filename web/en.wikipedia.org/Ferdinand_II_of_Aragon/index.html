Ferdinand II of Aragon

Ferdinand II (10 March 1452 – 23 January 1516), called Ferdinand the Catholic (Spanish: Fernando el Católico), was King of Aragon from 1479 until his death in 1516. As the husband of Queen Isabella I of Castile, he was also King of Castile from 1475 to 1504 (as Ferdinand V). Together, the Catholic Monarchs completed the Reconquista, sponsored Columbus's voyage of 1492, and laid the foundations of Spain as a unified dynastic state and a world power.

Reign

Ferdinand and Isabella's reign saw the conquest of Granada (1492), the expulsion of the Jews under the Alhambra Decree (1492), the establishment of the Spanish Inquisition, and the beginning of the Spanish Empire in the Americas. Ferdinand later conquered Navarre (1512) and acted as regent of Castile after Isabella's death. His political skill led Niccolò Machiavelli to cite him in The Prince as an exemplar of the successful "new prince".

Death and succession

He died in 1516 and was succeeded in both crowns by his grandson, Charles I (the future Holy Roman Emperor Charles V).
