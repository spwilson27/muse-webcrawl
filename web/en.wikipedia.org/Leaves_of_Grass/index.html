Leaves of Grass is a poetry collection by the American poet Walt Whitman (1819–1892). First published in 1855 with twelve untitled poems, it was continuously revised, expanded and rearranged by Whitman throughout his life, reaching more than 400 poems in the final "deathbed edition" of 1891–92.

Content and style

Written largely in free verse with long, cadenced lines, the collection celebrates democracy, nature, the human body and the unity of all life. Its best-known poems include "Song of Myself", "I Sing the Body Electric", "Out of the Cradle Endlessly Rocking", and the Abraham Lincoln elegies "When Lilacs Last in the Dooryard Bloom'd" and "O Captain! My Captain!".

Reception

Initially controversial for its frank treatment of sexuality and its departure from conventional metre, the book was praised by Ralph Waldo Emerson in a famous letter. It is now regarded as a landmark of American literature.
