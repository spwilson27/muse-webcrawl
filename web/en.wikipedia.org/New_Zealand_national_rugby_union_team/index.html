The New Zealand national rugby union team, commonly known as the All Blacks, represents New Zealand in men's international rugby union and is widely regarded as one of the most successful national teams in any sport. Administered by New Zealand Rugby, the team has won the Rugby World Cup three times — the inaugural tournament in 1987 and consecutively in 2011 and 2015 — and has held the number-one world ranking longer than any other nation.

History

New Zealand played its first test match against Australia in Sydney in 1903. The 1905–06 "Originals" tour of Britain, France and North America cemented the All Blacks legend, losing only once, controversially, to Wales. The name "All Blacks" derives from the team's wholly black playing strip. Tours and rivalries with South Africa's Springboks became defining sporting sagas of the twentieth century, while the amateur era gave way to professionalism in 1995; Jonah Lomu's performances at that year's World Cup made him the sport's first global superstar.

Playing record and style

The All Blacks have won more than three-quarters of all their test matches, a record unmatched in international rugby, and have dominated the Rugby Championship (formerly Tri Nations) against Australia, South Africa and Argentina. Before matches the team performs the haka, a Māori posture dance — most famously Ka Mate, composed by Te Rauparaha — which has become one of world sport's most recognizable rituals.

Rivalries and honours

Principal rivals include Australia (contesting the Bledisloe Cup), South Africa, England and France. New Zealand players such as Richie McCaw, Dan Carter, Jonah Lomu and Kieran Read are among the most decorated in the game's history.
