Antisemitism
DefinitionHostility to, prejudice towards, or discrimination against Jews

Antisemitism is hostility to, prejudice towards, or discrimination against Jews. It has been called "the longest hatred": manifestations range from individual prejudice and social exclusion to discriminatory laws, expulsions, pogroms and, in its most extreme form, the Nazi genocide of European Jewry — the Holocaust — in which six million Jews were murdered. The term itself was popularised in 1879 by the German agitator Wilhelm Marr, who gave a pseudo-scientific, racial veneer to older religious anti-Judaism.

History

Antiquity and the Middle Ages

Anti-Jewish hostility existed in the pagan ancient world, but distinctively Christian anti-Judaism developed around the charge of deicide and supersessionism. In medieval Europe Jews were confined to certain occupations (including moneylending, which Christians were forbidden), subjected to special taxes and badges, and targeted by false accusations such as ritual murder (the "blood libel", first recorded at Norwich in 1144) and host desecration. Crusades, the Black Death massacres of 1348–51, and expulsions from England (1290), France (1306, 1394) and Spain (1492) devastated Jewish communities.

Modern racial antisemitism

The emancipation of Jews in 19th-century Europe was accompanied by a new racial antisemitism, which treated Jewishness as an immutable biological taint rather than a religion. It fed conspiracy theories — exemplified by the fabricated Protocols of the Elders of Zion (1903) — blaming Jews simultaneously for capitalism and communism, and produced episodes such as the Dreyfus affair in France and pogroms in the Russian Empire. In the Soviet Union, antisemitism later appeared in state campaigns against "rootless cosmopolitans".

The Holocaust

Under Adolf Hitler, Nazi Germany made antisemitism the core of state policy: the Nuremberg Laws (1935) stripped Jews of citizenship, Kristallnacht (1938) unleashed organised violence, and from 1941 the regime implemented the "Final Solution" — the systematic murder of six million Jews in ghettos, mass shootings and extermination camps — together with the murder of millions of other victims.

Contemporary antisemitism

After 1945 open antisemitism was discredited in much of the West, but it persisted and periodically resurged: in Holocaust denial, in conspiracy theories amplified by the internet, in attacks on synagogues and Jewish institutions, and in debates over the boundary between criticism of Israel and antisemitism. The International Holocaust Remembrance Alliance's working definition (2016) has been widely adopted by governments and institutions as a practical guide. Jewish communities worldwide continue to record significant numbers of antisemitic incidents annually.
