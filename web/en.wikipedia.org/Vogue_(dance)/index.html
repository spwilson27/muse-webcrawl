Vogue (dance)

Vogue, or voguing, is a highly stylized form of dance that evolved out of the Harlem ballroom scene in New York City, created primarily by African-American and Latino LGBTQ communities. Dancers imitate and exaggerate the poses of fashion models — as seen in Vogue magazine — combining them with angular, linear body movements, spins, dips and "duckwalks".

History

The style developed within ball culture, in which competitors organized into "houses" competed in balls for trophies and prestige. Voguing moved through forms known as old way, new way and vogue fem. It reached mainstream attention through the 1990 documentary Paris Is Burning and Madonna's 1990 hit song "Vogue", and has continued to influence pop culture, fashion and television.

Elements

Vogue fem performance is often judged on five elements: hand performance, catwalk, duckwalk, floor performance, and spins and dips. Battles pair dancers against one another to a DJ's beats, judged on precision, style and attitude.
