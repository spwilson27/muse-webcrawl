Half-Breeds (politics)

The "Half-Breeds" were a political faction of the United States Republican Party in the late nineteenth century, during the Gilded Age. Led by Senator James G. Blaine of Maine, they favoured civil service reform and merit-based government appointments, and were opposed by the rival "Stalwarts", led by Senator Roscoe Conkling of New York, who defended the spoils system of political patronage.

Rivalry

The feud split the Republican Party through the presidencies of Rutherford B. Hayes, James A. Garfield, and Chester A. Arthur. The Stalwarts sneered that their opponents were only "half" Republican—hence "Half-Breeds"—but the reformers embraced the label. Garfield, a Half-Breed sympathiser, won the 1880 nomination; his assassination by a disgruntled office-seeker in 1881 spurred passage of the Pendleton Civil Service Reform Act of 1883.
