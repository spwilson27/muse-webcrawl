Marvel Studios

Marvel Studios, LLC (originally known as Marvel Films from 1993 to 1996) is an American film and television production company that is a subsidiary of Walt Disney Studios, a division of Disney Entertainment, which is part of the Walt Disney Company. Marvel Studios produces the Marvel Cinematic Universe (MCU) films and series, based on characters that appear in Marvel Comics publications.

History

In the 1990s Marvel licensed its characters to other studios — resulting in hits such as Fox's X-Men and Sony's Spider-Man — while Marvel itself earned little. Under producer David Maisel and creative head Kevin Feige, Marvel Studios arranged a $525 million credit facility from Merrill Lynch in 2005, collateralised by film rights to its remaining characters, to self-produce films. The gamble began with Iron Man (2008), directed by Jon Favreau and starring Robert Downey Jr., followed by The Incredible Hulk (2008), building a shared universe that culminated in The Avengers (2012). Disney had acquired Marvel Entertainment in 2009 for about $4 billion; in 2015 Marvel Studios was moved out of Marvel Entertainment and placed directly under Walt Disney Studios.

The MCU and expansion

The MCU became the highest-grossing film franchise of all time, exceeding $30 billion at the box office, organised into multi-film "Phases" and "Sagas". Highlights include Black Panther (2018), the first superhero film nominated for the Academy Award for Best Picture, and Avengers: Endgame (2019), briefly the highest-grossing film ever. From 2021 the studio expanded into streaming series for Disney+ (beginning with WandaVision) and animation (Marvel Animation). Feige has served as president since 2007 and chief creative officer of Marvel since 2019.
