Sphenic number

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In number theory, a sphenic number (from Greek: σφήν, "wedge") is a positive integer that is the product of three distinct prime numbers. Because each divisor is squarefree, a sphenic number has exactly eight divisors. The smallest sphenic number is 30 = 2 × 3 × 5, whose divisors are 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 15, and 30. The first few sphenic numbers are 30, 42, 66, 70, 78, 102, 105, 110, 114, 130, 138, 154, 165, and 170.

Properties

All sphenic numbers have exactly eight divisors and a Möbius function value of −1, since they have an odd number (three) of distinct prime factors and are squarefree. A sphenic number is always composite and never a perfect power. The largest known sphenic number at any time is typically the product of the three largest known primes, though proving that requires no special construction.

Distribution

Sphenic numbers have a positive asymptotic density among the integers: the count of sphenic numbers up to x grows as a constant multiple of x / (ln x), related to results on integers with a fixed number of prime factors (the Landau–Ramanujan-type generalizations of the prime number theorem). Consecutive sphenic numbers occur (e.g., 230 = 2 × 5 × 23 and 231 = 3 × 7 × 11), and there are infinitely many such pairs by suitable constructions.
