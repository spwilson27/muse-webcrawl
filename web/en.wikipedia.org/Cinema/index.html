Cinema

Cinema is a visual art form and medium consisting of moving images, typically presented in a theatrical setting for entertainment, education, or artistic purposes. The word "cinema", short for cinematography, is often used to refer to filmmaking and the film industry, and to the art form that is the result of it. A cinema is also a building — a movie theater — containing an auditorium for viewing films.

History

The moving images of a film are created by photographing actual scenes with a motion-picture camera, by photographing drawings or miniature models using traditional animation techniques, by means of CGI and computer animation, or by a combination of any or all of these techniques, and other visual effects. The first public screening of films at which admission was charged occurred on 28 December 1895 in Paris, when the Lumière brothers showed a programme of short films including Workers Leaving the Lumière Factory using their Cinématographe.

Within two decades the initially modest, novelty medium grew into an established mass entertainment industry, with purpose-built picture palaces, star systems, and studio production lines in Hollywood and elsewhere. Sound arrived commercially with The Jazz Singer (1927), colour processes such as Technicolor became standard by the 1930s and 1940s, and widescreen formats proliferated in the 1950s in response to television.

Technology

Traditionally, films were recorded onto celluloid film stock through a photochemical process and then shown through a movie projector onto a large projection screen. Contemporary films are often fully digital through the entire process of production, distribution, and exhibition. A cinema auditorium combines the projection system, a large screen, and a multi-channel sound system; modern venues use digital projectors, stadium seating, and immersive audio formats.

Film theory and criticism

The discipline of film studies examines cinema as an art form: its language of shots, editing, mise-en-scène, montage, and genre; its industries and audiences; and its place in culture and ideology. Major movements shaping cinema's history include German Expressionism, Soviet montage, Italian neorealism, the French New Wave, New Hollywood, and the national cinemas of India, Japan, China, Iran, and many other countries.

Industry

The global film industry is centred on a small number of production hubs, of which Hollywood (Los Angeles) and India's Hindi-language industry in Mumbai ("Bollywood") are the best known by output and revenue. Films are financed, produced, distributed, and exhibited through an international network of studios, independents, festivals, and streaming platforms, the last of which has reshaped theatrical exhibition in the twenty-first century.
