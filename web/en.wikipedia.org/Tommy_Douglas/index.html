Thomas Clement "Tommy" Douglas (20 October 1904 – 24 February 1986) was a Canadian politician who served as premier of Saskatchewan from 1944 to 1961 and as the first leader of the federal New Democratic Party from 1961 to 1971. A Baptist minister turned democratic socialist, he is best known as the father of Canadian universal health care.

Saskatchewan premier

Born in Falkirk, Scotland, Douglas emigrated to Winnipeg as a child. His own brush with osteomyelitis — cured only because a doctor treated him free as a teaching case — shaped his conviction that no one should face ruin for medical care. Elected to Parliament in 1935 with the Co-operative Commonwealth Federation (CCF), he led the Saskatchewan CCF to victory in 1944, forming the first democratic socialist government in North America. His government introduced public auto insurance, a bill of rights, union rights, and in 1947 North America's first universal hospital insurance plan; in 1962, under his successor Woodrow Lloyd, Saskatchewan introduced full medicare — surviving a bitter doctors' strike — which became the model for Canada's national system.

Federal leadership and legacy

In 1961 Douglas became the founding leader of the New Democratic Party, formed from the CCF and the Canadian Labour Congress, and returned to Parliament in 1962. He led the NDP for a decade and remained an MP until 1979. His story "Mouseland", a parable about mice electing cats to govern them, is a staple of Canadian political folklore. In 2004, viewers of the CBC series The Greatest Canadian voted him the greatest Canadian of all time.
