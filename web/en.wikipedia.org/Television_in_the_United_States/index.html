Television in the United States

Television is one of the major mass media outlets in the United States. As of 2011, household ownership of television sets in the country is 96.7%, and approximately 114,200,000 American households own at least one television set. The first official television broadcasts in the United States began in 1928, though regular commercial broadcasting is generally dated from 1941.

History

Network television grew rapidly after World War II, dominated by the "Big Three" networks: NBC, CBS, and ABC. Color television became standard in the late 1960s. Cable television expanded in the 1980s, bringing channels such as CNN, ESPN, MTV, and HBO to most households, and satellite providers followed in the 1990s. The transition to digital broadcasting was completed for full-power stations in 2009.

Programming

American television programming includes news, sports, reality shows, sitcoms, dramas, and children's programming. The country has produced many of the world's most widely exported television series.

Streaming

Since the 2010s, streaming services such as Netflix, Hulu, and Amazon Prime Video have transformed how Americans watch television, leading to widespread "cord-cutting" of cable subscriptions.
