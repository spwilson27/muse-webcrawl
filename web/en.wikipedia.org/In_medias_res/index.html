In medias res

In medias res (Latin: "into the middle of things") is a literary technique in which a narrative opens in the midst of the plot, rather than at its chronological beginning. Often, exposition is bypassed at the opening and filled in gradually through flashbacks or characters relating past events to each other.

The term was coined by the Roman poet Horace in his Ars Poetica, describing the ideal epic poet who "hastens on the outcome, and bears his hearer into the midst of the story" rather than beginning the Trojan War "from the egg" (ab ovo).

History

The technique is characteristic of classical epic poetry. Homer's Iliad opens in the ninth year of the Trojan War, with the quarrel between Achilles and Agamemnon, and the Odyssey begins with Odysseus stranded on Calypso's island, his earlier wanderings recounted later in flashback. Virgil's Aeneid similarly opens with Aeneas shipwrecked near Carthage. Dante's Divine Comedy famously begins "midway upon the journey of our life."

Usage

Opening in medias res is standard practice in modern film, television, and fiction, where beginning with action or conflict is used to engage the audience immediately. The technique is often combined with non-linear narrative structures and is related to, but distinct from, the cold open and the flashforward.
