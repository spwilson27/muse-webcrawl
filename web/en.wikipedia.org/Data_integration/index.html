Data integration

Data integration involves combining data residing in different sources and providing users with a unified view of them. This process becomes significant in a variety of situations, both commercial (when two companies need to merge their databases) and scientific (combining research results from different bioinformatics repositories, for example).

Approaches

Two broad architectures exist. In materialised integration, data is copied into a central repository such as a data warehouse, typically through extract, transform, load (ETL) processes. In virtual (federated) integration, data remains in its sources and a mediator system rewrites user queries posed against a global schema into queries over the local sources, using schema mappings — a major research challenge involving schema matching and ontology alignment.

Challenges

Data integration must resolve structural and semantic heterogeneity: different schemas, formats, naming conventions and meanings, as well as data-quality problems such as duplicates and inconsistencies, addressed by entity resolution and data cleaning techniques.
