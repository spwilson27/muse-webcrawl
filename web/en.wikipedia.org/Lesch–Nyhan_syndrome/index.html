Lesch–Nyhan syndrome

Lesch–Nyhan syndrome (LNS) is a rare X-linked recessive disorder caused by deficiency of the enzyme hypoxanthine-guanine phosphoribosyltransferase (HGPRT), encoded by HPRT1 on the X chromosome. Described by Michael Lesch and William Nyhan in 1964.

Lesch–Nyhan syndrome
Gene: HPRT1
Inheritance: X-linked recessive
Features: Self-mutilation, hyperuricemia, dystonia

Features

Overproduction of uric acid (gout, kidney stones, orange crystals in nappies), neurological disability with dystonia and intellectual impairment, and compulsive self-injury — biting lips and fingers — beginning in early childhood.

Treatment

Allopurinol controls uric acid but not neurological symptoms; care is supportive with protective measures and behavioural management.
