Naval Act of 1794

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Naval Act of 1794, formally "An Act to provide a Naval Armament", was passed by the United States Congress on 27 March 1794. It authorized the construction of six frigates, thereby creating the United States Navy. The act appropriated $688,888.82 for the ships, designed principally by Joshua Humphreys.

Background

After the Revolutionary War the Continental Navy was disbanded and its last ship sold in 1785. Attacks by Barbary corsairs on American merchant shipping in the Mediterranean, and the inability of the young republic to protect its commerce, prompted debate between advocates of a standing navy and those who feared its cost. The capture of American ships and crews by Algiers in 1793 tipped Congress toward action.

The six frigates

The act authorized four 44-gun and two 36-gun frigates. Humphreys designed unusually large, heavily built ships intended to outgun European frigates and outrun ships of the line. Three were completed initially—United States, Constellation, and Constitution—after a clause suspending construction on peace with Algiers briefly halted the programme; the remaining three (President, Congress, Chesapeake) followed under later authorization.

Significance

The act marks the birth of the permanent United States Navy. USS Constitution, launched in 1797, remains in commission today as the world's oldest commissioned naval vessel afloat.
