Birch bark letter

A birch bark letter is a document written on a piece of birch bark. In medieval Rus', particularly in Novgorod, birch bark was a cheap and widely available writing material, and letters scratched into it with a stylus have survived in waterlogged soil in large numbers.

Novgorod letters

The first Novgorod birch bark letter was found on 26 July 1951 during excavations led by Artemiy Artsikhovsky; more than a thousand have since been recovered in Novgorod and hundreds more in other towns. They date mainly from the 11th to the 15th centuries and are written in Old Novgorod dialect and other East Slavic varieties. Their contents — private correspondence, business notes, debts, orders, and a schoolboy's exercises and drawings by the boy Onfim — show that literacy extended well beyond clerical and princely circles.

Other traditions

Birch bark was also used for writing in other regions; birch bark manuscripts from Gandhara preserve some of the oldest surviving Buddhist texts.
