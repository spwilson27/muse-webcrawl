Norway Debate

[Image placeholder: Norway Debate]

The Norway Debate was a famous debate in the British House of Commons during the Second World War, held on 7 and 8 May 1940. Although nominally about the failed Allied campaign in Norway, it became a devastating verdict on Neville Chamberlain's government and led directly to his resignation as Prime Minister and the appointment of Winston Churchill.

The debate

The debate followed the evacuation of Allied troops from central Norway after the German invasion of April 1940. Leo Amery, a Conservative critic of the government, concluded his speech by quoting Oliver Cromwell to the Long Parliament: "You have sat too long here for any good you have been doing. Depart, I say, and let us have done with you. In the name of God, go." David Lloyd George also spoke forcefully against Chamberlain.

Outcome

The government won the vote, but its majority fell from the usual level to 81, and dozens of Conservatives voted against or abstained. Chamberlain concluded he could not form the national government the crisis required. On 10 May 1940, the day Germany attacked the Low Countries, he resigned and Churchill became Prime Minister.
