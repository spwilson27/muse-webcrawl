The Polavaram Project is a multi-purpose irrigation and hydroelectric project on the Godavari River in the Eluru district (formerly West Godavari district) of Andhra Pradesh, India. The project, centred on a large dam near the village of Polavaram where the Godavari emerges from the Eastern Ghats, is intended to provide irrigation water, drinking water, hydropower and inter-basin transfer of Godavari water to the Krishna River basin. It was declared a national project under the Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act, 2014, with the Government of India bearing its cost.

Features

The main dam is an earth-cum-rock-fill structure with a concrete spillway designed to pass one of the largest flood discharges of any dam in the world. The reservoir and canal system is designed to irrigate roughly 295,000 hectares, supply drinking water to Visakhapatnam and other towns, generate 960 MW of hydropower, and transfer about 80 TMC of water annually to the Krishna delta via the Polavaram right main canal, partly following the alignment envisioned by the engineer Sir Arthur Cotton and planned by statesman K. L. Rao, who first proposed the project in 1941.

History and controversies

Construction began in 2004, but the project has faced long delays, cost escalations, contractor changes and disputes with the neighbouring states of Odisha and Chhattisgarh over submergence of their territory. Flood damage to the diaphragm wall in 2020 forced major redesign and repair works. The reservoir's submergence zone displaces a large population, many of them Adivasi (Scheduled Tribe) communities, and rehabilitation and resettlement has been a persistent controversy alongside environmental concerns about the Papikonda National Park ecosystem.
