High-speed rail in China

The high-speed rail (HSR) network in China is the world's longest and most extensively used high-speed railway network, with about 45,000 kilometres (28,000 mi) of lines in service by the end of 2023 — roughly two-thirds of the world total. Conventional high-speed trains on the network can reach speeds of up to 350 km/h (217 mph). The network is operated largely by China Railway under the China Railway High-speed (CRH) brand, using trains branded Hexie ("Harmony") and Fuxing ("Rejuvenation").

History

China began planning high-speed rail in the 1990s, initially raising speeds on conventional lines. The first dedicated passenger line, between Qinhuangdao and Shenyang, opened in 2003, and the Beijing–Tianjin intercity railway, designed for 350 km/h, opened in time for the 2008 Summer Olympics. Early trainsets were developed with foreign partners — Siemens, Alstom, Bombardier, and Kawasaki — under technology-transfer agreements; China subsequently developed the indigenous Fuxing series, which entered service in 2017.

Network

The network is organized around national corridors, summarized in successive plans as "four vertical and four horizontal" lines and later the "eight vertical and eight horizontal" grid. Major routes include Beijing–Shanghai (1,318 km, opened 2011), the Beijing–Guangzhou corridor, and the Shanghai–Kunming line. The Shanghai maglev, a separate 30 km Transrapid line reaching 431 km/h, connects Pudong airport to the city. Annual ridership runs into the billions of passenger trips, and cumulative ridership exceeded ten billion trips within the network's first decade and a half.

Economics and criticism

The build-out was financed largely by debt held by China Railway and provincial partnerships, and is criticized for high indebtedness and low profitability on some western lines, while supporters cite network effects, reduced air travel on short routes, and regional development. Safety debate intensified after the 2011 Wenzhou collision, which killed 40 people and led to temporary speed reductions; top speeds were restored to 350 km/h on flagship lines in 2017.

Exports

China has exported its high-speed technology, most prominently completing the Jakarta–Bandung high-speed railway in Indonesia, which opened in 2023 as Southeast Asia's first high-speed line.
