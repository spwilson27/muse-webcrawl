Nüwa

Nüwa (Chinese: 女媧), also read Nügua, is a mother goddess in Chinese mythology credited with creating humanity and repairing the pillars of heaven. She is often depicted with a human head and the body of a serpent, and is the sister and sometimes wife of Fuxi, with whom she is shown entwined, holding the compass and square — symbols of order and measure.

According to the myth, Nüwa fashioned the first human beings from yellow clay, moulding the finest figures by hand — said to be the ancestors of the noble — and producing the rest by flicking mud from a rope. When the water god Gonggong smashed Mount Buzhou, one of the pillars supporting the sky, causing floods and chaos, Nüwa melted five coloured stones to repair the broken heaven and cut the legs from a great tortoise to serve as new supports.

Worship and legacy

Nüwa appears in early texts such as the Chu Ci and the Huainanzi, and her creation and sky-repairing myths remain among the most famous in Chinese tradition. She also plays a role in the Ming-dynasty novel Fengshen Yanyi (Investiture of the Gods), where her anger at King Zhou of Shang sets the dynasty's downfall in motion.
