Gallia Lugdunensis

Gallia Lugdunensis was a province of the Roman Empire in what is now the modern country of France, part of the Roman province of Gaul formerly called Gallia Celtica. It is named after its capital Lugdunum (modern Lyon), possibly Roman Europe's most important city after Rome and the birthplace of the emperors Claudius and Caracalla.

History

The province was created by Augustus around 22 BC when he divided Gaul into several provinces. Lugdunensis stretched from Brittany and Normandy in the northwest to Lyon in the southeast, taking in the Loire valley and much of central Gaul. Its capital Lugdunum, founded in 43 BC, stood at the confluence of the Rhône and Saône rivers and was the seat of the Sanctuary of the Three Gauls, where representatives of the Gallic tribes gathered annually at the altar of Rome and Augustus.

Later divisions

Under Diocletian's reforms at the end of the 3rd century AD, Lugdunensis was divided into smaller provinces (Lugdunensis Prima, Secunda, Tertia and Senonia), grouped in the Diocese of Gaul. The region remained a core part of Roman and then Frankish Gaul.
