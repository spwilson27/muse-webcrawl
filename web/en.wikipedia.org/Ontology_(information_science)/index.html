Ontology (information science)

In information science, an ontology encompasses a representation, formal naming, and definitions of the categories, properties, and relations between concepts, data, or entities that pertain to one, many, or all domains of discourse. More simply, it is a way of showing the properties of a subject area and how they are related, by defining a set of terms and relational expressions.

The term was borrowed from philosophy, where ontology is the study of being. Tom Gruber's widely cited definition describes an ontology as an explicit specification of a conceptualisation. Ontologies are expressed in languages such as OWL and RDF, and underpin the Semantic Web, knowledge graphs, biomedical informatics (e.g. the Gene Ontology and SNOMED CT) and data integration.

Components

Typical components are individuals (instances), classes, attributes, relations, functions, restrictions, rules and axioms. Upper ontologies such as BFO and DOLCE attempt to describe very general concepts applicable across domains.
