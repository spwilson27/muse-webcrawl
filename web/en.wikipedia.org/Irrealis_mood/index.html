Irrealis mood

In linguistics, irrealis moods (abbreviated IRR) are the set of grammatical moods that indicate a situation or action is not known to have happened at the moment of speaking. They contrast with realis moods, such as the indicative, which present events as actual. Irrealis moods include the subjunctive, conditional, optative, jussive, potential and imperative in various languages.

Usage

Irrealis marking is used for hypothetical and counterfactual statements, wishes, commands, possibilities and future events that have not yet occurred. Many languages, including numerous Australian Aboriginal and Native American languages, have a single general irrealis marker where European languages use several distinct moods.

Relation to other categories

The boundary between mood and modality is debated; some linguists treat irrealis as a broad semantic category rather than a single morphological form. Related topics include irregular verbs, whose forms often preserve older mood distinctions.
