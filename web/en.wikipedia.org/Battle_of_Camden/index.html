The Battle of Camden (August 16, 1780) was a major victory for British forces in the Southern campaign of the American Revolutionary War. An American army under Major General Horatio Gates was decisively defeated by a smaller British force led by Lord Cornwallis near Camden, South Carolina.

Background

After the fall of Charleston in May 1780, Congress sent Gates — the "hero of Saratoga" — south to command the American Southern Department. Gates advanced on the British supply post at Camden with an army swelled by militia, though his men were short of food and many were sick. Both armies marched by night on August 15 and collided unexpectedly in the darkness, then deployed for battle at dawn.

The battle

Gates placed his inexperienced Virginia and North Carolina militia opposite the British regulars. When Lieutenant Colonel James Webster's troops advanced with the bayonet, the militia broke and fled almost without firing, exposing the Continental regiments under Baron Johann de Kalb on the American right. The Continentals fought on stubbornly until overwhelmed; de Kalb was mortally wounded. Gates fled the field on horseback, riding some 60 miles to Charlotte, a retreat that destroyed his reputation.

Aftermath

American losses exceeded 2,000 killed, wounded and captured, with all artillery taken. The defeat — compounded the same week by the rout of Sumter's partisans at Fishing Creek — left South Carolina firmly in British hands for the moment and led Congress to replace Gates with Nathanael Greene.
