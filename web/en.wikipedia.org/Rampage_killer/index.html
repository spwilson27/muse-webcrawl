Rampage killer

ClassificationType of mass murderer
Also known asSpree killer (in some definitions)
Distinguishing featureMultiple victims attacked in a single continuous episode, usually in public

A rampage killer is a person who commits a rampage — a sudden, usually solitary episode of violence in which multiple people are killed or injured, typically in a public place, in a short period of time. In criminological usage the term overlaps with, but is often distinguished from, mass murder and spree killing: unlike a spree killer, who moves between separate locations with cooling-off intervals, the rampage killer's attack is usually a single continuous event, and unlike many mass murderers the victims are often strangers chosen at random or as symbols of a grievance.

Characteristics

Research on rampage killers describes common patterns including social isolation, accumulated grievances and perceived humiliation, prior threats or "leakage" of intent, and fascination with weapons or earlier attackers. Most perpetrators are male, and a large proportion die during the attack, by suicide or by police gunfire. Scholars debate the role of mental illness, media contagion, and access to firearms in shaping incidence across countries.

Terminology

News media often label such offenders "lone wolf" attackers, though investigators have frequently found online influences and manifestos modelled on previous killings. Some researchers advocate minimising publicity of perpetrators' names and images to reduce copycat effects.
