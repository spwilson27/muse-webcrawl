Bipartite graph

In graph theory, a bipartite graph is a graph whose vertices can be divided into two disjoint and independent sets, U and V, such that every edge connects a vertex in U to one in V. Equivalently, a graph is bipartite if and only if it contains no odd-length cycles, and if and only if it is 2-colourable.

Examples

Every tree is bipartite, as are grid graphs with even dimensions and the utility graph K3,3. A complete bipartite graph Km,n joins every vertex of one part to every vertex of the other. König's theorem states that in any bipartite graph the size of a maximum matching equals the size of a minimum vertex cover.

Applications

Bipartite graphs model relationships between two different kinds of objects: assigning jobs to workers, matching students to schools or hospitals to residents, recommendation systems linking users to items, and the incidence structure of codes and configurations. The Hopcroft–Karp algorithm finds maximum matchings in bipartite graphs efficiently.

Generalizations

Related concepts include multipartite graphs (with more than two parts), bipartite permutation graphs, and the bipartite double cover of a general graph.
