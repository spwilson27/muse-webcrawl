Treaty of Lima (1929)

The Treaty of Lima (Spanish: Tratado de Lima) was signed on 3 June 1929 in Lima, Peru, by Peru and Chile, resolving the long-standing Tacna-Arica dispute arising from the War of the Pacific. Under the 1883 Treaty of Ancón, Chile had occupied the provinces of Tacna and Arica pending a plebiscite that was never held, and decades of "Chilenization" policies and failed American-mediated negotiations followed.

Terms

Negotiated under the good offices of U.S. President Herbert Hoover, the treaty awarded Tacna to Peru and Arica to Chile, drawing the Concordia Line as the boundary between them. Chile paid Peru US$6 million in compensation and undertook to build port facilities for Peru at Arica, with free-port guarantees. Tacna was formally returned to Peru on 28 August 1929, a date still celebrated there as the region's reincorporation. The settlement ended one of South America's most intractable territorial disputes, although related maritime-boundary questions were litigated by the two states at The Hague as late as 2014.
