The Battle of Saipan was fought from 15 June to 9 July 1944 on the island of Saipan in the Mariana Islands during the Pacific War. United States Marine and Army forces under the overall command of Lieutenant General Holland Smith defeated the Imperial Japanese Army garrison commanded by Lieutenant General Yoshitsugu Saitō. The loss of Saipan brought mainland Japan within range of American B-29 bombers and precipitated the fall of Prime Minister Hideki Tōjō's government.

Background

Saipan was a key objective of Operation Forager, the American drive into the Marianas. Its airfields would allow the new B-29 Superfortress to strike the Japanese home islands. The Japanese garrison numbered about 30,000 troops, supported by tanks and artillery in prepared positions.

The battle

After heavy naval bombardment, the 2nd and 4th Marine Divisions landed on 15 June, followed by the Army's 27th Infantry Division. Fighting was bitter, through sugar-cane fields, caves and the heights of Mount Tapochau. The simultaneous American victory in the Battle of the Philippine Sea destroyed Japanese naval air support. On 7 July the defenders launched one of the largest banzai charges of the war, which briefly overran American lines before being annihilated; Saitō then took his own life.

Civilian casualties

Thousands of Japanese civilians on Saipan died during the battle, many by suicide at the island's northern cliffs — remembered as Suicide Cliff and Banzai Cliff — amid fears stoked by propaganda about American treatment of captives.
