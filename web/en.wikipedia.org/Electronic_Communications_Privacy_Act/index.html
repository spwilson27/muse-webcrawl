Electronic Communications Privacy Act

The Electronic Communications Privacy Act of 1986 (ECPA) is a United States federal statute that extended government restrictions on wiretaps to electronic communications and set rules for government access to stored electronic records.

Structure

The Act has three titles. Title I amended the Wiretap Act to cover electronic communications. Title II, the Stored Communications Act, governs access to stored communications and transaction records held by third-party providers. Title III addresses pen registers and trap-and-trace devices.

Provisions

The ECPA generally prohibits the unauthorised interception of electronic communications and their disclosure. It establishes procedures under which the government may compel providers to disclose content and metadata, with differing standards depending on how long content has been stored.

Criticism and reform

Critics argue the Act is outdated in the era of cloud computing, particularly its distinction for emails stored longer than 180 days. Proposed reforms, such as the Email Privacy Act, have sought to require a warrant for stored content.
