Heart valve

A heart valve is a one-way valve that allows blood to flow in one direction through the chambers of the heart. The mammalian heart has four valves: the mitral and tricuspid atrioventricular valves, which control flow from the atria to the ventricles, and the aortic and pulmonary semilunar valves, which control flow out of the ventricles into the great arteries. Their opening and closing in response to pressure changes produces the familiar "lub-dub" heart sounds.

Structure and function

Valve leaflets (cusps) are thin flaps of endocardium reinforced with connective tissue. The atrioventricular valves are anchored by chordae tendineae to papillary muscles, preventing them from prolapsing into the atria when the ventricles contract. The semilunar valves have pocket-like cusps that fill with blood and snap shut when flow reverses. Proper function depends on the whole valve apparatus — annulus, leaflets, chords and surrounding muscle — working together through each phase of the cardiac cycle.

Disease

Valves can become narrowed (stenosis), restricting flow, or leaky (regurgitation/insufficiency), allowing backflow; both burden the heart and can cause heart failure. Causes include age-related calcification of the aortic valve, rheumatic fever (chiefly mitral stenosis), infective endocarditis, congenital defects such as a bicuspid aortic valve, and dilation of supporting structures. Abnormal valve function often produces a heart murmur detectable by stethoscope, and echocardiography is the main diagnostic tool.

Treatment

Management ranges from monitoring and treating the underlying cause to surgical repair or replacement. Replacement valves are mechanical (durable but requiring lifelong anticoagulation) or bioprosthetic (from pig or cow tissue, less durable but usually needing no long-term anticoagulants). Catheter-based options have expanded rapidly: transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR/TAVI), first performed in 2002, is now standard for many patients with aortic stenosis, and edge-to-edge clips can treat mitral regurgitation in selected cases.
