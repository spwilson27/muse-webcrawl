NCAA Division III

NCAA Division III
Founded: 1973
Members: ~440 schools (largest NCAA division)
Conferences: 42
Championships: 28 sports
Athletic scholarships: Prohibited

NCAA Division III (D-III) is the largest division of the National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA) by number of member institutions and participants. Its defining rule is that member schools may not award scholarships based on athletic ability.

Philosophy

Division III was created in the NCAA's 1973 reorganization. Its philosophy statement places the highest priority on the overall quality of the educational experience, treating athletics as an integral part of a student-athlete's education rather than a commercial enterprise. Seasons are shorter, practice hours limited, and competition is primarily regional and conference-based.

Membership

The division includes many small private liberal-arts colleges as well as public institutions. Well-known members include Johns Hopkins, the University of Chicago, Emory, Washington University in St. Louis, and the public Wisconsin Intercollegiate Athletic Conference schools, which dominate several sports. Roughly 180,000 student-athletes compete in Division III, more than in any other NCAA division, and participation rates are high because many schools field large rosters without scholarship costs.

Championships

Division III stages championships in 28 sports. Its football championship game is the Stagg Bowl, named for pioneering coach Amos Alonzo Stagg; historically it was dominated for decades by Mount Union and Wisconsin–Whitewater. Financial aid for D-III athletes is limited to need-based and academic awards available to students generally, and the NCAA audits schools to ensure athletes receive aid at rates comparable to the general student body.
