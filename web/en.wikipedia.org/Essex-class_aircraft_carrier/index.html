Essex-class aircraft carrier

The Essex class was a class of aircraft carriers of the United States Navy. The 24 vessels built formed the backbone of the US Navy's combat strength during World War II and, with later modernisations, served into the 1990s.

Design

The ships displaced about 27,100 tons standard, were 872 feet long in the short-hull group, and carried around 90–100 aircraft. The later Ticonderoga or "long hull" group had slightly longer hulls. They were powered by steam turbines giving 33 knots.

Service

Lead ship Essex was commissioned on 31 December 1942. Class members fought in every major Pacific carrier action; none was lost, though several were heavily damaged by kamikazes. Modernised ships served in Korea and Vietnam, and four survive as museum ships, including Intrepid in New York and Yorktown at Patriots Point.
