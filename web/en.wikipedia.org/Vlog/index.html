Vlog

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A vlog (short for video blog or video log) is a form of blog for which the medium is video. Entries often combine embedded video with supporting text, images, and metadata. Vlogs are a popular form of web television and are frequently uploaded to video-sharing platforms such as YouTube, where creators known as vloggers build audiences around their personalities and daily lives.

History

Early video blogging emerged around 2000, when Adam Kontras posted a video alongside a blog entry documenting his move to Los Angeles; broadband access and cheap cameras soon made the format practical. The launch of YouTube in 2005 and dedicated communities such as Vloggercon (2005) accelerated growth. By the 2010s, daily vlogging — filming and posting one's everyday activities — had become a major genre, with prominent creators such as Casey Neistat popularising a cinematic, fast-cut style.

Styles

Common formats include talking-head commentary, "day in the life" diaries, travel vlogs, product reviews and unboxings, challenges, and educational explainer videos. Vlogs are typically monetised through advertising revenue, sponsorships and merchandise, and many creators treat vlogging as a full-time profession managed through talent networks and platform partner programs.
