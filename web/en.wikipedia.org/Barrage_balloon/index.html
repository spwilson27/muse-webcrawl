Barrage balloon

A barrage balloon is a type of airborne barrage: a large uncrewed tethered balloon flown above a location to deter enemy aircraft. Colliding with the balloon's heavy steel tether cables could badly damage or destroy an attacking aircraft, so the balloons forced enemy planes to fly higher and less accurately, or to avoid defended areas entirely.

History

Barrage balloons were used extensively in both world wars. In the First World War they formed "balloon aprons" around London and defended French and Italian cities. In the Second World War, Britain's Balloon Command operated over 2,000 balloons around cities, ports, and industrial sites. They were credited with helping defend against low-level air attack and dive bombing, and during the V-1 flying bomb campaign of 1944 a barrage of nearly 2,000 balloons around London was credited with bringing down well over a hundred V-1s whose wings were severed by the cables. Balloons were also flown from ships in convoys. The United States and the Soviet Union also deployed them in large numbers.

Design

Typical barrage balloons were hydrogen-filled, roughly 19 metres long, with stabilising fins, flown at altitudes up to about 1,500 metres from winches mounted on trucks or ships. A serious drawback was vulnerability to lightning and storms, which occasionally tore balloons loose.
