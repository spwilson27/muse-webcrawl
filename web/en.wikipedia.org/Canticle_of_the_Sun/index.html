Canticle of the Sun

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Canticle of the Sun, also known as the Canticle of the Creatures and the Laudes Creaturarum (Praise of the Creatures), is a religious song composed by Saint Francis of Assisi in an Umbrian dialect of Italian. It was written in stages during the final year of Francis's life, in 1224 and 1225, while he was ill and nearly blind at San Damiano. It is believed to be among the first works of literature written in the Italian language.

Content

The canticle praises God through the elements of creation, personifying them as family: "Brother Sun," "Sister Moon and the stars," "Brother Wind," "Sister Water," "Brother Fire," and "Sister Mother Earth, who sustains and governs us." A stanza added later praises those who forgive and endure sickness in peace, and a final stanza, composed as Francis approached death, welcomes "Sister Bodily Death."

"Praised be You, my Lord, through our Sister, Mother Earth, who sustains and governs us, and who produces various fruit with coloured flowers and herbs."

Legacy

The Canticle is revered in Catholic and broader Christian tradition and is a foundational text of Franciscan spirituality. It inspired Pope Francis's 2015 encyclical on the environment, Laudato si', whose title quotes the canticle's recurring refrain ("Praised be you"). The hymn "All Creatures of Our God and King" is an English paraphrase of the Canticle.
