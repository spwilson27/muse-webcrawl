Greater Caucasus

The main ridge of the Greater Caucasus.

The Greater Caucasus (Russian: Большой Кавказ, Bolshoy Kavkaz; Georgian: დიდი კავკასიონი) is the major mountain range of the Caucasus Mountains, stretching about 1,200 kilometres (750 mi) from the Taman Peninsula on the Black Sea to the Absheron Peninsula on the Caspian Sea. It forms a natural barrier between Russia to the north and Georgia and Azerbaijan to the south, and its watershed is commonly taken as part of the boundary between Europe and Asia.

Geography

The range is divided into the Western Caucasus, the high Central Caucasus, and the Eastern Caucasus. The Central Caucasus contains the range's highest summits, most of them volcanic or crystalline peaks over 5,000 metres. The highest point is Mount Elbrus (5,642 m), a dormant stratovolcano that is also the highest mountain in Europe by the usual convention. Other major peaks include Dykh-Tau, Shkhara, Kazbek, and Ushba. Glaciers cover extensive areas of the central range, and major rivers such as the Terek, Kuban, Inguri, and Rioni rise on its slopes.

Crossings

Historically few passes cross the Greater Caucasus. The most important routes are the Georgian Military Road through the Darial Gorge, the Ossetian Military Road over the Mamison Pass, the Transkam highway through the Roki Tunnel, and the road over the Klukhor Pass. These crossings have repeatedly figured in the military and political history of the region.
