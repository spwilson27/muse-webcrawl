Oven

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An oven is a tool which is used to expose materials to a hot environment. Ovens contain a hollow chamber and provide a means of heating the chamber in a controlled way. In common use since antiquity, they have served a wide variety of tasks, from cooking and baking to pottery, metalworking, and industrial processing.

History

The earliest ovens were fire pits and masonry structures built by ancient civilisations. Excavations at Çatalhöyük and other Neolithic sites show enclosed clay hearths, and by the time of ancient Egypt, Greece, and Rome, purpose-built bread ovens of brick and stone were common. Greek ovens with front loading influenced designs used for centuries. The enclosed wood- and coal-fired range developed in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, followed by gas ovens in the 1820s and electric ovens in the late nineteenth century. Convection ovens, which circulate hot air with a fan, and microwave ovens, patented in the 1940s after the invention of the cavity magnetron, are twentieth-century developments.

Types

Cooking ovens

Domestic cooking ovens may be powered by natural gas, electricity, or (historically) wood or coal. Common variants include conventional ovens, convection (fan) ovens, microwave ovens, steam ovens, and toaster ovens. Baking, roasting, and broiling are the characteristic cooking methods. Typical domestic temperatures range from about 110 °C (225 °F) to 260 °C (500 °F), with self-cleaning cycles reaching much higher temperatures.

Industrial and craft ovens

Kilns are high-temperature ovens used to fire pottery, bricks, and cement, often exceeding 1,000 °C. Furnaces heat metals for smelting, forging, annealing, and heat treatment. Industrial ovens also cure coatings, dry materials, and process chemicals under controlled atmospheres.

Construction and operation

Ovens are typically insulated chambers with a heating element or burner, a thermostat or temperature controller, and racks or hearths to support the load. Heat reaches the food or material by radiation, conduction, and — in convection designs — forced circulation of hot air. Modern ovens add timers, probes, and programmable controls.
