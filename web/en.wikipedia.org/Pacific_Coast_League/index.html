The Pacific Coast League (PCL) is a Minor League Baseball league that operates in the Western United States. Along with the International League, it is one of two leagues playing at the Triple-A level, one step below Major League Baseball. The league was founded in 1903 and, in its early decades, was so strong that it was granted its own "Open" classification in 1952 in an attempt to become a third major league.

History

The original PCL grew out of the California League and fielded teams up and down the West Coast, including the San Francisco Seals, Los Angeles Angels, Oakland Oaks, Portland Beavers, San Diego Padres and Seattle Rainiers. With long seasons — at times exceeding 200 games — and mild weather, it attracted established stars and set minor-league attendance records. The 1958 arrival of the Dodgers and Giants in California ended its major-league ambitions, displacing its Los Angeles and San Francisco franchises; the league nonetheless endured as a top farm circuit, shifting eastward into interior cities.

Modern league

Following the 2021 reorganisation of Minor League Baseball, the PCL was reduced to ten teams, all in the Mountain and Pacific time zones; the "Triple-A West" name used in 2021 was dropped and the historic Pacific Coast League name restored in 2022. Recent member clubs have included the Albuquerque Isotopes, El Paso Chihuahuas, Las Vegas Aviators, Oklahoma City Comets, Reno Aces, Round Rock Express, Sacramento River Cats, Salt Lake Bees, Sugar Land Space Cowboys and Tacoma Rainiers. The league champion plays the International League champion in the Triple-A National Championship Game.
