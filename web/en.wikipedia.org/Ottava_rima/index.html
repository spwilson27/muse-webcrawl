Ottava rima

Ottava rima is a rhyming stanza form consisting of eight iambic lines, usually iambic pentameter in English, with the rhyme scheme ABABABCC. It originated in Italy in the 14th century, probably developing from the Sicilian octave.

History

Boccaccio used ottava rima in works such as the Teseida and Il Filostrato. It became the standard form for Italian epic poetry in Boiardo's Orlando Innamorato, Ariosto's Orlando Furioso, and Tasso's Gerusalemme Liberata.

In English

English poets adopted the form for works including Byron's Don Juan — whose witty concluding couplet in each stanza made the form famous for satire — as well as Keats's Isabella, Shelley's The Witch of Atlas, and Yeats's Sailing to Byzantium and Among School Children.

Form

The six alternating rhymes build narrative momentum and the closing couplet provides summary, turn, or punchline — a balance of propulsion and closure suited to long poems.
