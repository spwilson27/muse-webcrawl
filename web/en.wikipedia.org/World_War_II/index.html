World War II or the Second World War (1 September 1939 – 2 September 1945) was a global conflict between two coalitions: the Allies and the Axis powers. Nearly all of the world's countries participated, with many nations mobilising all resources in pursuit of total war. Tanks and aircraft played major roles, with the latter enabling the strategic bombing of population centres and the delivery of the only two nuclear weapons ever used in war. World War II was the deadliest conflict in history, causing the deaths of 70 to 85 million people, most of whom were civilians. Millions died in genocides, including the Holocaust, and by massacres, starvation, and disease.

The causes of the war included unresolved issues from the First World War, the rise of fascism in Europe, and the expansionist policies of Nazi Germany, Fascist Italy, and Imperial Japan. The war began in Europe on 1 September 1939 with Germany's invasion of Poland, after which the United Kingdom and France declared war on Germany. By 1941, Germany and its allies controlled much of continental Europe, and the Soviet Union joined the war after the German invasion of June 1941 (Operation Barbarossa). The United States entered the war in December 1941 following the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor.

Course of the war

The turning points of the war included the Battle of Stalingrad (1942–43), the Allied landings in Normandy on D-Day (6 June 1944), and the island-hopping campaign in the Pacific. Germany surrendered in May 1945 after the fall of Berlin to the Red Army and the suicide of Adolf Hitler. Japan surrendered on 2 September 1945, following the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki and the Soviet declaration of war.

Aftermath

The war reshaped the global order: the United Nations was founded, the United States and the Soviet Union emerged as superpowers, and the Cold War followed. European colonial empires began to dissolve, and Germany was divided. The war's legacy includes the Geneva Conventions of 1949, the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, and enduring remembrance of the Holocaust and other atrocities.
