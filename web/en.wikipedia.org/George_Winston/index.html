George Winston (December 26, 1949 – June 4, 2023) was an American pianist best known for his solo piano albums of melodic, seasonally themed music. Often categorised as new age, Winston preferred to describe his style as "rural folk piano", drawing on stride piano, New Orleans R&B, and the music of Fats Waller and Vince Guaraldi.

George Winston, the best-selling solo pianist of the Windham Hill era.

Career

Winston was discovered by William Ackerman and became the signature artist of the Windham Hill label. His trilogy of seasonal albums — Autumn (1980), Winter into Spring (1982), and December (1982) — sold millions of copies; December became a perennial holiday bestseller and was certified triple platinum in the United States. His 1994 album Forest won the Grammy Award for Best New Age Album. Through his own Dancing Cat label he also documented Hawaiian slack-key guitar music, and he released Linus and Lucy: The Music of Vince Guaraldi (1996) in tribute to one of his principal influences.

Style and philanthropy

Winston performed barefoot, in stocking feet at the piano, and regularly turned concerts into benefits for local food banks, asking audiences to bring donations. He died in 2023 after a decade-long struggle with several forms of cancer, having survived earlier bouts with thyroid cancer and a bone-marrow transplant.
