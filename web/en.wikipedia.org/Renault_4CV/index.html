Manufacturer: Renault
Production: 1947–1961
Class: Supermini
Engine: 747 cc Ventoux I4

The Renault 4CV is an economy car produced by the French manufacturer Renault from August 1947 to July 1961. It was the first French car to sell more than a million units, and was superseded by the Dauphine.

Development

The 4CV was conceived secretly during the German occupation of France in the Second World War by engineers Fernand Picard and Charles-Edmond Serre. It was a rear-engined, four-door sedan with a monocoque body, strongly influenced by the Volkswagen Beetle concept.

Production

More than 1.1 million cars were built. The 4CV was also manufactured under licence in Japan by Hino Motors and in Spain by FASA-Renault. It achieved notable motorsport success, including class wins at the Mille Miglia and Le Mans in the early 1950s.
