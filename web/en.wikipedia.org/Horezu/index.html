Horezu is a town in Vâlcea County, Oltenia, Romania, situated at the foot of the Carpathian Mountains. It is best known as a centre of traditional Romanian pottery and as the gateway to the Horezu Monastery, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Horezu Monastery

The Monastery of Horezu (also Horezu Monastery of Saints Constantine and Elena) was founded in 1690–1693 by Prince Constantin Brâncoveanu and is regarded as the masterpiece of the Brâncovenesc style, with richly carved stone ornamentation and frescoes by the painters Constantinos and Ioan. It forms part of the UNESCO-listed Churches of Moldavia's counterpart grouping, the Monasteries of Oltenia inscribed in 1993.

Pottery

Horezu ceramics, produced in the Olari district by families such as the Iorga, Mischiu and Paloși potters, are decorated with traditional motifs — notably the Horezu rooster — in earthy tones using techniques such as slip trailing with a bull's horn and feather quill. In 2012 the craftsmanship of Horezu ceramics was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. The town hosts the annual "Cocoșul de Hurez" pottery fair each June.
