Number theory

Number theory is a branch of pure mathematics devoted primarily to the study of the integers and arithmetic functions. German mathematician Carl Friedrich Gauss famously called mathematics the "queen of the sciences" and said number theory was the "queen of mathematics". Number theorists study prime numbers as well as the properties of mathematical objects constructed from integers, such as rational numbers, or defined as generalisations of the integers, such as algebraic integers.

The distribution of prime numbers has fascinated number theorists since antiquity.

History

The study of numbers is ancient: Babylonian tablets record Pythagorean triples, and Euclid's Elements (c. 300 BC) proved the infinitude of the primes and the fundamental theorem of arithmetic. Diophantus of Alexandria studied equations seeking integer or rational solutions. In the 17th century Pierre de Fermat laid early foundations of the modern field; his "Last Theorem" was proved only in 1994 by Andrew Wiles. Gauss's Disquisitiones Arithmeticae (1801) systematised the subject.

Branches

Elementary number theory uses no techniques from other fields, studying divisibility, congruences and multiplicative functions. Analytic number theory applies calculus and complex analysis — above all the Riemann zeta function — to questions about the distribution of primes, yielding the prime number theorem. Algebraic number theory studies algebraic number fields and their rings of integers. Diophantine geometry uses methods of algebraic geometry on number-theoretic problems, while computational number theory develops algorithms relevant to cryptography.

Applications

Long regarded as the purest of pure mathematics, number theory found dramatic application in the 20th century: public-key cryptosystems such as RSA rest on the difficulty of factoring large integers, and error-correcting codes and computer science draw on finite fields and modular arithmetic.

Famous problems

Open problems include the Riemann hypothesis, the twin prime conjecture and Goldbach's conjecture. Solved landmarks include Fermat's Last Theorem and the classification of the distribution of primes in arithmetic progressions.
