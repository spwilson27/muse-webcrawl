Shiplap

Shiplap is a type of wooden board used commonly as exterior siding in the construction of residences, barns, sheds, and outbuildings. It is either rough-sawn 1-inch (25 mm) or milled wood board with a rabbet on the top and bottom of each board so that they overlap to form a continuous flat surface.

The advantage of shiplap siding, besides its basic appearance, is that when used for exterior siding it forms a fairly tight seal, protecting against wind and water. It was historically popular in colder climates such as Scandinavia.

In modern interior design, the term 'shiplap' has come to be used more loosely for any horizontal plank panelling with visible gaps between boards, popularized by home renovation television shows.
