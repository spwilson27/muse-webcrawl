Ethnic stereotype

An ethnic stereotype is a generalised belief about a characteristic attributed to members of an ethnic group, nationality or race. Stereotypes may be positive, negative or seemingly neutral, but they oversimplify diverse populations and can underpin prejudice and discrimination.

Formation and function

Social psychologists study stereotypes as cognitive shortcuts that categorise people, influenced by media portrayals, folklore, humour, historical conflicts and colonial ideologies. Stereotype content research distinguishes judgments of warmth and competence. Self-fulfilling prophecies and stereotype threat — anxiety about confirming a negative stereotype — can affect performance and behaviour.

Examples and representation

National character stereotypes appear in jokes, advertising and film stock characters. Scholars of media and postcolonial studies examine how such images naturalise hierarchies. Legal and educational responses include anti-discrimination law, counter-stereotypical representation and intercultural education.

Criticism and change

Stereotypes change with migration, intergroup contact and political events, and members of stereotyped groups actively contest or reclaim them. See also Stereotype, Prejudice and Racial stereotype.
