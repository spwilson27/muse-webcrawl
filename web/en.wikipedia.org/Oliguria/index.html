Oliguria

Oliguria is a decreased production of urine — in adults, commonly defined as urine output of less than 400–500 millilitres per 24 hours (or less than 0.5 mL/kg/hour over a sustained period). The complete absence of urine production is called anuria. Oliguria is a clinical sign rather than a disease in itself and is an important early marker of acute kidney injury.

Causes

Causes are conventionally divided into three categories:

  • Prerenal — reduced blood flow to the kidneys, as in dehydration, haemorrhage, heart failure, or sepsis.
  • Renal (intrinsic) — direct kidney damage, such as acute tubular necrosis, glomerulonephritis, or interstitial nephritis.
  • Postrenal — obstruction of urine outflow, for example by an enlarged prostate, kidney stones, or tumours.

Diagnosis and management

Evaluation includes history, fluid-balance and urine-output monitoring (often with a urinary catheter), blood tests such as creatinine and urea, urinalysis, and imaging to exclude obstruction. Oliguria forms part of the KDIGO staging criteria for acute kidney injury, based on urine output thresholds over 6–24 hours. Treatment addresses the underlying cause: restoring circulating volume and perfusion, stopping nephrotoxic drugs, relieving obstruction, and, in severe cases, renal replacement therapy (dialysis).
