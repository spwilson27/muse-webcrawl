The Muhammad Ali dynasty was the ruling dynasty of Egypt and Sudan from the 19th to the mid-20th century. It is named after its progenitor, Muhammad Ali of Egypt, an Ottoman Albanian commander who seized power in 1805 and is regarded as the founder of modern Egypt.

History

Muhammad Ali's successors ruled first as Ottoman governors (wali), and from 1867 as Khedives. After a brief period as Sultans, they became Kings of Egypt following formal independence from Britain in 1922. Notable rulers include Ibrahim Pasha, Abbas I, Sa'id, Isma'il the Magnificent, Tewfik, Abbas II, Fuad I and Farouk I.

End of the monarchy

King Farouk was overthrown in the Egyptian revolution of 1952. His infant son Fuad II reigned nominally until the monarchy was formally abolished and a republic proclaimed on 18 June 1953.
