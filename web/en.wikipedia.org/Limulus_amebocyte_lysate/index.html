Limulus amebocyte lysate

Limulus amebocyte lysate (LAL) is an aqueous extract of blood cells (amebocytes) from the Atlantic horseshoe crab (Limulus polyphemus). LAL reacts with bacterial endotoxin (lipopolysaccharide) to form a gel clot, a property exploited in the pharmaceutical industry's standard test for endotoxin contamination of injectable drugs, vaccines and medical devices.

History

The test was developed from the work of Frederik Bang and Jack Levin in the 1950s–60s, who showed that horseshoe crab blood clots in the presence of Gram-negative bacteria. The cascade involves serine protease zymogens — Factor C, activated directly by endotoxin, then Factor B and the proclotting enzyme — culminating in cleavage of coagulogen to the clot protein coagulin. The LAL test largely replaced the older rabbit pyrogen test from the late 1970s.

Methods and alternatives

Modern LAL assays include gel-clot, turbidimetric and chromogenic formats and can detect endotoxin at picogram levels. Harvesting requires bleeding wild horseshoe crabs and returning them to the sea, raising conservation concerns as horseshoe crab populations decline. A synthetic alternative, recombinant Factor C (rFC), reproduces the first step of the cascade without animal material and has gained regulatory acceptance in several pharmacopoeias. The related TAL reagent is prepared from Asian horseshoe crabs.
