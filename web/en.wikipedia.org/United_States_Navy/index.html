United States Navy

The United States Navy (USN) is the naval warfare service branch of the United States Armed Forces and one of the eight uniformed services. It is the largest and most powerful navy in the world, with a battle fleet tonnage greater than that of the next several navies combined. It operates more than 290 deployable ships — including 11 nuclear-powered aircraft carriers — over 2,600 aircraft, the largest ballistic-missile submarine fleet in the world, and roughly 340,000 active-duty personnel. The Navy was formally founded on 27 March 1794, though it traces its roots to the Continental Navy of 13 October 1775.

History

Created to fight Barbary pirates, the young Navy won fame in the War of 1812 (including USS Constitution, "Old Ironsides") and blockaded the Confederacy in the Civil War, where the battle of the ironclads Monitor and Virginia (1862) changed naval warfare. The "New Navy" of the 1880s and Alfred Thayer Mahan's The Influence of Sea Power upon History (1890) drove a battleship build-up capped by the Great White Fleet's world cruise (1907–09). In World War II the Navy became the world's largest, fighting the carrier battles of Midway, the Philippine Sea and Leyte Gulf, and waging an unrestricted submarine campaign that strangled Japanese shipping. The nuclear Navy built under Admiral Hyman Rickover produced the first nuclear submarine, USS Nautilus (1954), and the "41 for Freedom" ballistic missile boats that still form the sea leg of the nuclear triad.

Structure

The Navy is organised into numbered fleets (Second through Seventh, and Tenth Fleet, the cyber component) under Fleet Forces Command and Pacific Fleet. Its principal surface forces are carrier strike groups centred on Nimitz- and Gerald R. Ford-class carriers; its submarine force operates Los Angeles-, Seawolf- and Virginia-class attack boats and Ohio-class ballistic and guided-missile submarines. Naval Special Warfare Command fields the SEAL Teams. Officers are commissioned principally through the Naval Academy at Annapolis, NROTC and Officer Candidate School.
