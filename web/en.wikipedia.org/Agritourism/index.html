Agritourism

Visitors at a pick-your-own farm, a common form of agritourism.

Agritourism or agrotourism is any agriculturally based operation or activity that brings visitors to a farm or ranch. It includes a wide variety of activities such as farm stays, tours, pick-your-own produce, corn mazes, hayrides, farm stands and farmers' markets, vineyard and winery visits, farm-to-table dinners, festivals and harvest celebrations, and educational programmes for schoolchildren.

Development

Agritourism developed as a means for farmers — particularly small and family farms — to diversify income beyond commodity production, connect directly with consumers, and preserve rural landscapes and traditions. In Italy, the agriturismo model was formalized in law in 1985 and became a major part of rural tourism, offering accommodation and meals in converted farm buildings. Similar farm-stay traditions exist across Europe, and the sector has grown in the United States, Australia, India and many other countries, often supported by government rural-development programmes.

Benefits and challenges

For farmers, agritourism can provide higher margins through direct sales, employ family members year-round, and build customer loyalty for farm products. For visitors, it offers recreation and education about food production. For rural communities, it supports local businesses and can help keep farmland in production. Challenges include liability and food-safety regulation, zoning rules, seasonality, labour demands, insurance costs and tensions between tourism activity and farm operations. The COVID-19 pandemic accelerated interest in outdoor, local farm experiences in many regions.
