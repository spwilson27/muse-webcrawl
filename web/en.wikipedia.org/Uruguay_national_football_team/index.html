Uruguay national football team

The Uruguay national football team (Spanish: Selección de fútbol de Uruguay), nicknamed La Celeste ("The Sky Blue"), represents Uruguay in international football and is controlled by the Uruguayan Football Association (AUF), founded in 1900.

Uruguay is famous for winning the first FIFA World Cup in 1930, which it hosted at the Estadio Centenario in Montevideo, defeating Argentina 4–2 in the final. The team won its second World Cup title in 1950, famously beating host nation Brazil 2–1 in the deciding match at the Maracanã — an event remembered as the Maracanazo. Uruguay also won the Olympic football gold medals in 1924 and 1928, tournaments then regarded as equivalent to world championships, and has won the Copa América a record-tying 15 times, most recently in 2011.

History

Despite a population of only about 3.5 million, Uruguay has been one of the most successful national teams in world football. After decades of relative decline following 1970, the team experienced a resurgence under coach Óscar Tabárez, finishing fourth at the 2010 World Cup and winning the 2011 Copa América. Notable players include Luis Suárez, the team's all-time top scorer, Edinson Cavani, Diego Forlán, and José Nasazzi, captain of the 1930 side.

Stadium and colours

The team's home stadium is the Estadio Centenario in Montevideo. The traditional kit consists of a sky-blue shirt, black shorts, and black socks.
