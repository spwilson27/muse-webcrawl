Court

Type: Judicial institution
Jurisdiction: Defined by law
Presided by: Judge or panel of judges

A court is any person or institution, often a government institution, with the authority to adjudicate legal disputes between parties and to carry out the administration of justice in civil, criminal, and administrative law in accordance with the rule of law. Courts are a central means for resolving disputes, and the term also denotes the physical place — the courtroom — where proceedings are held.

Types of courts

Most legal systems distinguish trial courts, which hear evidence and determine facts, from appellate courts, which review lower courts' decisions for errors of law. Above these sit supreme or constitutional courts, whose rulings are final. Specialized tribunals handle matters such as tax, family, military discipline, and international crimes.

Personnel and procedure

Proceedings are presided over by one or more judges; in many common-law systems a jury of lay citizens may decide questions of fact. Parties are typically represented by lawyers, and courts operate under rules of procedure and evidence designed to ensure fairness, notice, and an opportunity to be heard — the core of due process.

Other meanings

"Court" also refers to the retinue of a monarch or noble (the royal court), an enclosed playing area in sports such as tennis and basketball, and, as a verb, the act of seeking someone's favour or romantic attention.
