Eleonora di Toledo (1522–1562) was a Spanish noblewoman and, by marriage to Cosimo I de' Medici, the first Duchess of Florence. She was a major patron of the arts and an effective regent who governed Florence during her husband's absences and illnesses.

Life

Eleonora was the daughter of Pedro Álvarez de Toledo, the Spanish viceroy of Naples. Her marriage to Cosimo in 1539 brought the young Medici duke a prestigious imperial alliance and a large dowry, which helped finance his government. The couple had eleven children, including Francesco I and Ferdinando I, both later Grand Dukes of Tuscany.

Patronage and court

Eleonora bought the Pitti Palace in 1549 and began the creation of the Boboli Gardens. She patronised artists such as Bronzino, whose state portraits of her — notably the portrait with her son Giovanni, showing her in a famous pomegranate-patterned brocade dress — became iconic images of Medici rule. She also supported the Jesuits and founded convents, while her management of estates made her one of the wealthiest women in Italy.

Death

Eleonora died of malaria in Pisa in December 1562, shortly after the deaths of two of her sons from the same disease during the same journey.
