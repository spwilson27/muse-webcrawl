Mastitis

Mastitis is inflammation of the breast or udder, usually associated with breastfeeding. Symptoms typically include local pain and redness. There is often an associated fever and general soreness. Onset is typically fairly rapid and usually occurs within the first few months of delivery. Complications can include abscess formation.

Causes

Risk factors include poor attachment leading to ineffective milk removal, infrequent feedings, illness in the mother or baby, rapid weaning, and cracked nipples that allow bacteria — commonly Staphylococcus aureus — to enter the breast. Non-breastfeeding women can also develop mastitis (periductal mastitis), as can, rarely, men.

Treatment

Prevention is by properly positioning and attaching the baby and feeding frequently. Treatment is generally antibiotics such as cephalexin. Continuing to breastfeed or pump breastmilk is recommended: emptying the breast helps recovery and the milk itself remains safe for the infant. Probiotics may be useful. In about 3% of breastfeeding cases, an abscess develops, which may require drainage.

In animals

Mastitis is a major disease of dairy cattle, reducing milk yield and quality; it is managed through milking hygiene, teat disinfection and antibiotic therapy, and is one of the most economically important diseases in the dairy industry.
