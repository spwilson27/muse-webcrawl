Hinduism is the third-largest religion in Indonesia, practised by about 1.7% of the population. Indonesia has the fourth-largest Hindu population in the world, overwhelmingly concentrated on the island of Bali, where about 87% of the population is Hindu. Indonesian Hinduism, especially in its Balinese form (Agama Hindu Dharma), is a distinctive synthesis of Indian Hindu traditions with indigenous animist beliefs and ancestor worship.

History

Hindu influences arrived in the archipelago from India in the early centuries CE through trade. Hindu kingdoms such as Kutai, Tarumanagara, Mataram, Kediri, Singhasari and Majapahit flourished in Java and Sumatra between the 4th and 15th centuries, leaving monumental temples such as the 9th-century Prambanan complex. With the spread of Islam from the 13th century onward, Hinduism receded to Bali and pockets elsewhere, including the Tenggerese of East Java and communities in Kalimantan, Sulawesi and Sumatra.

Balinese Hinduism

Balinese Hinduism worships a supreme god, Sang Hyang Widhi Wasa (identified with Acintya), in accordance with the state philosophy of Pancasila, alongside the Trimurti of Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva and a host of local deities and deified ancestors. It is famous for its dense calendar of temple festivals, offerings (canang sari), cremation ceremonies (ngaben) and the day of silence, Nyepi. The Parisada Hindu Dharma Indonesia (PHDI), founded in 1959, is the religion's principal national organisation.
