Robert Stephenson Smyth Baden-Powell, 1st Baron Baden-Powell (22 February 1857 – 8 January 1941) was a British Army officer and writer, best known as the founder of the Scout Movement. His experiences in the Boer War — particularly the Siege of Mafeking — made him a national hero, and his 1908 book Scouting for Boys became one of the best-selling books of the twentieth century.

Scouting

In 1907 Baden-Powell held an experimental camp on Brownsea Island in Dorset to test his ideas on youth training. The following year, Scouting for Boys was published in instalments, and Boy Scout troops sprang up across Britain and around the world. With his wife Olave and his sister Agnes, he also helped establish the Girl Guide movement.

Legacy

Baden-Powell served as Chief Scout of the World until his death. Scouting and Guiding together count tens of millions of members worldwide, and his vision of learning by doing in the outdoors remains central to the movement.
