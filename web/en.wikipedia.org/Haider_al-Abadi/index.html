Haider Jawad Kadhim al-Abadi (born 25 April 1952) is an Iraqi politician who served as Prime Minister of Iraq from September 2014 to October 2018. A member of the Islamic Dawa Party, he spent much of the Saddam Hussein era in exile in the United Kingdom, where he worked as an engineer and telecommunications consultant after earning a PhD in electrical engineering from the University of Manchester.

Premiership

Al-Abadi became prime minister amid the crisis of the Islamic State's 2014 offensive, which had captured Mosul and threatened Baghdad. His government, backed by the Popular Mobilization Forces and an international coalition, prosecuted the war against ISIL, culminating in the liberation of Mosul in July 2017 and his declaration of victory over the group in December 2017. His tenure also saw the 2017 Kurdish independence referendum and the federal reassertion of control over Kirkuk. After the 2018 elections he was succeeded by Adil Abdul-Mahdi.
