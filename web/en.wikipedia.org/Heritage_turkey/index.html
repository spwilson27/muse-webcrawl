Heritage turkey

A heritage turkey is one of a variety of strains of domestic turkey that retains historic characteristics no longer present in the majority of turkeys raised for consumption since the mid-20th century.

Definition and breeds

According to criteria developed by the American Livestock Breeds Conservancy (now The Livestock Conservancy), a heritage turkey must mate naturally, have a long productive outdoor lifespan, and grow at a slow to moderate rate. Recognised heritage varieties include the Narragansett, Bourbon Red, Standard Bronze, Royal Palm, Blue Slate, Black Spanish, Jersey Buff and White Holland.

Heritage turkeys contrast with the Broad Breasted White, the commercially dominant variety, which grows faster but cannot reproduce naturally and must be artificially inseminated. Interest in heritage birds has grown among small farmers and chefs who value their flavour and genetic diversity.
