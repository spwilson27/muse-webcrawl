Fibreboard

Fibreboard (American English: fiberboard) is a type of engineered wood product that is made out of wood fibers. Types of fibreboard (in order of increasing density) include particle board, medium-density fibreboard (MDF), and hardboard.

Manufacture

Fibreboard is made by breaking down hardwood or softwood residuals into wood fibres, combining them with wax and a resin binder such as urea-formaldehyde, and forming panels by applying high temperature and pressure. The process was pioneered in the 19th century; Masonite hardboard, produced by the steam-explosion method invented by William H. Mason in 1924, was one of the earliest successful products.

Types

Low-density fibreboard (softboard) is used for insulation and sound deadening. Medium-density fibreboard (MDF), with a density of roughly 600–800 kg/m³, is widely used in furniture and laminate flooring because of its smooth, uniform surface. High-density fibreboard (hardboard) is used in construction, automotive panels, and pegboard.

Advantages and disadvantages

Fibreboard is inexpensive, uniform, and free of grain defects, and it machines cleanly. However, it is weaker than solid wood in some applications, swells when exposed to water, and older boards can release formaldehyde from their resin binders, which has led to emissions standards and low-formaldehyde formulations.
