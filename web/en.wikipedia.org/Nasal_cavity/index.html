Nasal cavity

SystemRespiratory system
Part ofNose
Opens intoNasopharynx via the choanae

The nasal cavity is the large, air-filled space inside the nose, extending from the nostrils (nares) at the front to the choanae at the back, where it opens into the nasopharynx. It is divided into right and left halves by the nasal septum and forms the uppermost part of the respiratory tract.

Structure

Each lateral wall of the cavity bears three curved bony shelves, the superior, middle and inferior nasal conchae (turbinates), beneath which lie passages called meatuses. The paranasal sinuses and the nasolacrimal duct drain into these meatuses. The roof of the cavity contains the olfactory epithelium, whose receptor cells mediate the sense of smell.

Function

The nasal cavity conditions inspired air: its richly vascularised, mucus-covered respiratory epithelium warms, humidifies and filters air, trapping dust and microbes in mucus that ciliated cells sweep toward the pharynx. It also contributes to the resonance of the voice. The mucosa undergoes a normal alternating cycle of congestion and decongestion between the two sides (the nasal cycle).

Clinical significance

Common disorders include rhinitis (inflammation, often allergic), a deviated septum, nasal polyps, and epistaxis (nosebleeds), which most often arise from Kiesselbach's plexus on the anterior septum.
