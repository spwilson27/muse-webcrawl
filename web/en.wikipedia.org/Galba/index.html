Servius Galba Caesar Augustus (born Servius Sulpicius Galba; 24 December 3 BC – 15 January AD 69) was Roman emperor from AD 68 to 69. He was the first emperor in the Year of the Four Emperors and the first from outside the Julio-Claudian dynasty, ending Nero's reign and the dynasty founded by Augustus. A wealthy aristocrat and respected general, he governed Hispania Tarraconensis for eight years before being proclaimed emperor by his legions.

Early life and career

Galba was born into the patrician gens Sulpicia. He followed a distinguished public career under Tiberius, Caligula and Claudius, serving as consul in AD 33, governing Aquitania and Upper Germany — where he was known as a strict disciplinarian — and receiving triumphal honours for campaigns in Britain's era of conquest and as proconsul of Africa. Under Nero he was appointed governor of Hispania Tarraconensis in AD 60/61.

Rise to power

In AD 68, as revolts broke out against Nero — notably that of Vindex in Gaul — Galba declared himself legate of the Senate and people of Rome, minted coins, and marched on the capital after being hailed emperor by his troops at Clunia. Nero's suicide in June 68 left Galba master of the empire; the Senate recognised him, and he entered Rome in October.

Reign and fall

Galba's seven-month reign was undermined by his parsimony, severity and reliance on unpopular favourites (Titus Vinius, Cornelius Laco and Icelus). He refused the donative promised to the Praetorian Guard in his name — declaring that he levied his soldiers, he did not buy them — alienating the troops. His adoption of the young Piso Licinianus as heir disappointed the ambitious Otho, who bribed the Praetorians and had Galba murdered in the Roman Forum on 15 January AD 69. Tacitus famously wrote that Galba was judged capable of ruling — had he never ruled (omnium consensu capax imperii nisi imperasset).
