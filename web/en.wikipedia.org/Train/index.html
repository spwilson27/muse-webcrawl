Train


A modern passenger train

A train is a series of connected vehicles that run along a railway track and transport people or freight. Trains are typically pulled or pushed by locomotives (often known as "engines"), though some are self-propelled multiple units. Passengers and cargo are carried in railway carriages or wagons.

History

Railways began with horse-drawn wagonways in mines, and steam traction transformed them in the early 19th century: Richard Trevithick's locomotive ran in 1804, and George Stephenson's Stockton and Darlington Railway opened in 1825, followed by the Liverpool and Manchester Railway in 1830. Steam locomotives dominated for over a century before being supplanted by diesel and electric traction in the mid-20th century. High-speed rail began with Japan's Shinkansen in 1964; experimental maglev trains have reached speeds above 600 km/h.

Types

Passenger trains range from commuter and metro services to long-distance and high-speed trains. Freight trains haul bulk goods, containers and vehicles, and can be several kilometres long. Trains run on tracks of various gauges, are controlled by signalling systems for safety, and are powered by diesel engines, overhead or third-rail electricity, or (historically) steam. Rail transport is notably energy-efficient per tonne-kilometre compared with road transport.
