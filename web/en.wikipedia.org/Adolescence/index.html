Adolescence (from Latin adolescere, "to grow up") is a transitional stage of physical and psychological development that generally occurs during the period from puberty to adulthood. The World Health Organization defines adolescents as people between the ages of 10 and 19, though because brain maturation and social transitions now extend well into the twenties, some researchers propose defining adolescence as spanning roughly ages 10 to 24.

Biologically, adolescence begins with the hormonal changes of puberty: growth spurts, the development of secondary sex characteristics, and reproductive maturation. Neurologically, the brain undergoes prolonged development, with the prefrontal cortex — involved in planning and impulse control — among the last regions to mature, while sensitivity to reward and to peers increases, a combination often invoked to explain adolescent risk-taking.

Psychological and social development

Psychologically, adolescence involves the formation of personal identity (theorised by Erik Erikson as the crisis of "identity versus role confusion"), increasing independence from parents, the growing importance of peer relationships, and the development of abstract reasoning and moral judgement. Socially, it is marked in most societies by secondary schooling, exploration of work and sexuality, and culturally variable rites of passage; the modern concept of the "teenager" as a distinct life stage and consumer group emerged in the mid-20th century.

Health

Key health issues of adolescence include mental-health conditions such as depression and anxiety, injuries, substance use, nutrition, and the establishment of lifelong health behaviours, making the period a major focus of public-health policy.
