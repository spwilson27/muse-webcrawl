Price–earnings ratio

FormulaP/E = Share price ÷ Earnings per share
TypeValuation ratio
VariantsTrailing P/E; forward P/E; Shiller P/E (CAPE)

The price–earnings ratio (P/E ratio) is a ratio for valuing a company that measures its current share price relative to its earnings per share (EPS). It indicates how much investors are willing to pay for one unit of a company's earnings and is widely used to judge whether a stock is over- or under-valued relative to its own history, its sector, or the broader market.

Variants

The trailing P/E uses earnings from the most recent twelve months, while the forward P/E uses analysts' forecasts of future earnings. The Shiller P/E, or cyclically adjusted price-to-earnings ratio (CAPE), divides price by the average of ten years of inflation-adjusted earnings in order to smooth business-cycle fluctuations; it was popularised by economist Robert Shiller.

Interpretation

A high P/E may signal that investors expect high future growth, or that the share is overvalued; a low P/E may reflect undervaluation or weak prospects. The ratio is meaningless or misleading when earnings are negative, and comparisons are most reliable between companies in the same industry. The reciprocal of the P/E, the earnings yield, can be compared with bond yields. As a rough historical benchmark, broad United States indices have often traded at trailing ratios in the mid-teens to mid-twenties.
