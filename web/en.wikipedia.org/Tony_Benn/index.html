Anthony Neil Wedgwood Benn (3 April 1925 – 14 March 2014), commonly known as Tony Benn, was a British Labour politician, writer and diarist who served as a Member of Parliament for 47 years. Born Anthony Wedgwood Benn, he inherited the title Viscount Stansgate in 1960, which initially barred him from the Commons; his campaign led to the Peerage Act 1963, allowing him to renounce the title.

Career

Benn held cabinet office under Harold Wilson and James Callaghan as Minister of Technology, Secretary of State for Industry and Secretary of State for Energy. After Labour's 1979 defeat he became the standard-bearer of the party's left, narrowly losing the 1981 deputy leadership contest to Denis Healey. His "Bennite" programme emphasised public ownership, unilateral nuclear disarmament and withdrawal from the European Community.

Later life

On leaving Parliament in 2001, Benn joked he was retiring "to spend more time on politics", becoming a prominent anti-war campaigner, president of the Stop the War Coalition, and a celebrated public speaker and diarist. His published diaries are considered a major record of post-war British politics.
