Cooktop

A cooktop, also known as a hob, is a kitchen appliance for cooking food using direct heat, typically built into a kitchen countertop and separate from the oven. Cooktops may use gas burners, electric resistance elements, or electromagnetic induction.

Types

Gas cooktops burn natural gas or propane through open burners, offering instant visual control of the flame. Electric cooktops use coiled elements or radiant elements beneath a smooth glass-ceramic surface. Induction cooktops generate heat directly in ferrous cookware via a magnetic field, providing fast response, high efficiency, and a surface that stays comparatively cool, though they require magnetic pots and pans.

History

The cooktop evolved from cast-iron stoves and ranges of the 19th century. In the 20th century, separate built-in cooktops and wall ovens became common in modern kitchen design, and induction technology, developed in the 1970s, gained wide adoption in the 2000s.
