Tynemouth

Tynemouth is a coastal town and conservation area in the metropolitan borough of North Tyneside, Tyne and Wear, England, at the mouth of the River Tyne, about 8 miles (13 km) east of Newcastle upon Tyne. Historically in Northumberland, it is a popular seaside resort and commuter town.

History and landmarks

The town is dominated by the ruins of Tynemouth Priory and Castle, perched on a headland guarding the river mouth; three kings of Northumbria were buried in the priory church. In the Victorian era Tynemouth became a fashionable resort, and its long sandy beaches — Longsands, and King Edward's Bay — remain popular, with Longsands a noted surfing beach. Other landmarks include the Tynemouth Volunteer Life Brigade watch house, the Grand Hotel, and the mouth of the Tyne piers, and the town is served by a station on the Tyne and Wear Metro, housed in an ornate Victorian building that hosts a weekend market.
