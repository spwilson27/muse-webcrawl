Form 990

Form 990 is the principal public-disclosure document for US tax-exempt organizations.

Form 990, officially the Return of Organization Exempt from Income Tax, is a United States Internal Revenue Service form that tax-exempt organizations must file annually. It provides the IRS and the public with financial information about the organization, including its revenue, expenses, assets, programme accomplishments, governance practices and the compensation of its officers and highest-paid employees.

Variants

  • Form 990 — required for organizations with gross receipts of $200,000 or more, or total assets of $500,000 or more.
  • Form 990-EZ — a shorter version for mid-sized organizations below those thresholds.
  • Form 990-N (the "e-Postcard") — for small organizations with gross receipts normally $50,000 or less.
  • Form 990-PF — filed by private foundations.

Churches and certain religious organizations are generally exempt from filing. Failure to file for three consecutive years results in automatic revocation of tax-exempt status.

Public disclosure

Unlike individual tax returns, Forms 990 are public documents. Organizations must make their three most recent returns available on request, and the IRS publishes them in bulk; databases such as ProPublica's Nonprofit Explorer and Candid's GuideStar make them searchable. Journalists and researchers use the data to scrutinize charity finances, executive pay and political spending by 501(c)(4) organizations.

Redesign

The form was substantially redesigned for tax year 2008, expanding from a short financial statement into an eleven-page core form with sixteen schedules, adding extensive questions on governance, conflicts of interest and foreign activities.
