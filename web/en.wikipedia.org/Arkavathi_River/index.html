Arkavathi River

Location: Karnataka, India

Source: Nandi Hills, Chikkaballapura district

Mouth: Kaveri River at Kanakapura area

Length: Approximately 193 km

Basin: Kaveri basin

The Arkavathi is a mountain river in Karnataka state of India, originating in the Nandi Hills of Chikkaballapura district. It is a tributary of the Kaveri, joining the south of Kanakapura, Ramanagara District.

Course

The river originates in the Nandi Hills and flows southward, passing near Bangalore. It creates the Chunchi Falls near Kanakapura and joins the Kaveri near the Mekedatu area. Two reservoirs have been built across the river within the Bangalore Urban district: the Hesaraghatta Lake and the Thippagondanahalli Reservoir (also called Chamarajendra Reservoir), both historically important sources of water supply to Bangalore.

Water supply

The Arkavathi was once a major source of drinking water for Bangalore. Tippegondanahalli reservoir, built in 1933 under the direction of Sir M. Visvesvaraya, supplied the city for decades. Urbanisation, catchment degradation, encroachments, and reduced inflows have diminished the river's contribution, and the city has come to rely primarily on the Kaveri.

Environmental concerns

The Arkavathi basin faces problems of pollution from urban wastewater and industries, sand mining, and loss of connected lake systems that once fed the river. Various citizen and government initiatives have sought to rejuvenate the river and its feeder lakes.
