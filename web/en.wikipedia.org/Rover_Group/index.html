The Rover Group was a British state-owned, and later privately owned, motor vehicle manufacturer based at Longbridge in Birmingham. It was the successor to the car-making businesses of British Leyland and owned the Rover, Mini, MG and Land Rover marques.

History

In 1986 the volume car business of British Leyland, then trading as Austin Rover, was renamed the Rover Group under chairman Graham Day, as part of a government plan to prepare it for privatisation. Land Rover had already been separated as a distinct business within the group. The group's collaboration with Honda, begun in 1979, supplied joint models such as the Rover 200/400 and 600/800 series and underpinned a marked improvement in quality.

In 1988 the government sold Rover Group to British Aerospace. In 1994 British Aerospace sold the company to BMW for £800 million. Under BMW the group launched the Rover 75 and the Land Rover Freelander, but heavy losses — around £600 million a year by the late 1990s — and the strength of sterling strained the relationship.

Break-up

In 2000 BMW broke up the group. Land Rover was sold to Ford, the new Mini was retained by BMW, and the remaining Rover and MG car operations at Longbridge were sold for a nominal £10 to the Phoenix Consortium, which renamed them MG Rover Group. MG Rover entered administration in April 2005, ending volume car production at Longbridge.

The Rover brand itself passed to Ford in 2006 and then, with Land Rover and Jaguar, to Tata Motors in 2008, where it remains dormant.
