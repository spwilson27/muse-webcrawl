Zone plate

A zone plate is a device used to focus light or other things exhibiting wave character. Unlike lenses or curved mirrors, zone plates use diffraction instead of refraction or reflection. Based on analysis by French physicist Augustin-Jean Fresnel, they are sometimes called Fresnel zone plates in his honour.

Design

A zone plate consists of a set of concentric rings, called Fresnel zones, alternating between transparent and opaque. The radii of the rings increase in proportion to the square root of successive integers, so that waves passing through the transparent zones arrive at the desired focal point in phase and interfere constructively. The focal length depends on wavelength, so a zone plate acts as a strongly chromatic lens; it also produces subsidiary foci at fractions of the main focal length. A variant in which the opacity varies sinusoidally focuses light into a single point without the higher-order foci.

Applications

Because they can be made flat and lightweight and work at wavelengths where ordinary lenses absorb, zone plates are used to focus X-rays in synchrotrons and X-ray telescopes, and in extreme ultraviolet lithography. Simpler versions serve in zone plate photography, an alternative to pinhole photography producing soft, glowing images.
