Quid pro quo

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Quid pro quo ("something for something" in Latin) is a Latin phrase used in English to mean an exchange of goods or services in which one transfer is contingent upon the other; it is also rendered "a favour for a favour" or "tit for tat". The phrase originally arose in the context of apothecaries substituting one medicine for another, often with a pejorative sense, before taking on its modern meaning of reciprocal exchange.

Legal uses

In law, quid pro quo describes the mutual consideration that makes contracts binding: each party gives something of value in return. The term appears in several specific doctrines — notably in United States sexual-harassment law, where quid pro quo harassment means that submission to unwelcome sexual conduct is made a condition of employment benefits; and in political-corruption law, where campaign contributions or favours exchanged for specific official acts may constitute bribery. The phrase featured prominently in the 2019 impeachment inquiry into President Donald Trump, which centred on an alleged quid pro quo involving military aid to Ukraine.

Related concepts

Reciprocity of this kind is a fundamental social mechanism studied in game theory and anthropology, and in economics quid pro quo exchanges underlie barter and market transactions generally.
