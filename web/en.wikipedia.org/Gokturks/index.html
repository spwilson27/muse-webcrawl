Göktürks

The Göktürks (Old Turkic: "Celestial Turks" or "Blue Turks") were a nomadic confederation of Turkic peoples in medieval Inner Asia. Under the leadership of Bumin Qaghan and the Ashina clan, they founded the First Turkic Khaganate in 552, which rapidly grew into the largest land empire of its time, stretching from Manchuria to the Black Sea, before splitting into eastern and western khaganates. The Göktürks were the first Turkic people to write the name "Turk" in their own inscriptions.

History

The Ashina Turks, originally metalworkers subject to the Rouran Khaganate, revolted in 552 and destroyed their overlords. The resulting empire controlled the Silk Road trade between China, Persia, and Byzantium; Göktürk envoys reached Constantinople in 568. Civil war and Chinese pressure split the khaganate in 603. The Eastern Khaganate was subjugated by Tang China in 630, but was restored in 682 by Ilterish Qaghan as the Second Turkic Khaganate, whose monuments — the Orkhon inscriptions erected for Bilge Khagan and Kul Tigin — are the oldest known texts in a Turkic language. The Second Khaganate fell to the Uyghurs in 744.

Culture

The Göktürks wrote in a runiform script (Orkhon script) and worshipped the sky god Tengri. Their political legacy — the khaganate model and the name "Turk" — shaped all later Turkic states across Eurasia.
