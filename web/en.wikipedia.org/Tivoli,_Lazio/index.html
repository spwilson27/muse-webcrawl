Tivoli, Lazio

Tivoli
Comune in Lazio, Italy
Population: approx. 55,000
Elevation: 235 m

Tivoli is a town and comune in Lazio, central Italy, about 30 kilometres east-northeast of Rome, on the falls of the Aniene river and the slopes of the Sabine hills. Known in antiquity as Tibur, it was a resort for wealthy Romans and later a favoured summer retreat of Renaissance cardinals and popes.

Sights

The town is famous for two UNESCO World Heritage Sites: Hadrian's Villa (Villa Adriana), the vast 2nd-century imperial palace complex of the emperor Hadrian, and the Villa d'Este, a 16th-century Renaissance villa celebrated for its terraced gardens and hundreds of fountains. The older Villa Gregoriana, a park of gorges and waterfalls created in the 1830s, lies in the ravine of the Aniene. Ancient remains include the Temple of Vesta and the Temple of the Sibyl overlooking the falls.

History

Tibur was an early member of the Latin League and a Roman municipium; its cool climate made it a retreat for Augustus, Maecenas, and Hadrian. The scenic beauty of the town and its waterfalls made it a central motif of landscape painting and a stop on the Grand Tour.
