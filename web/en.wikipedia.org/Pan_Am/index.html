Pan American World Airways, originally founded as Pan American Airways and more commonly known as Pan Am, was an American airline that was the principal and largest international carrier and unofficial overseas flag carrier of the United States for much of the 20th century. Founded in 1927 by two former U.S. Army Air Corps majors, it began scheduled airmail and passenger service between Key West, Florida, and Havana, Cuba.

History

Under the leadership of Juan Trippe, Pan Am expanded aggressively through Latin America and across the Pacific and Atlantic, often acting as the United States' "chosen instrument" for international aviation. The airline held a virtual monopoly on American international routes for decades, regulated by the Civil Aeronautics Board.

Clipper era and jet age

Pan Am's flying boats — the Sikorsky S-42, Martin M-130 ("China Clipper") and Boeing 314 — pioneered transoceanic air travel in the 1930s, with all aircraft named as "Clippers". After the Second World War, Pan Am led the transition to jetliners: it was the launch customer for both the Boeing 707 (1958) and the Boeing 747 (1970), whose introduction it championed. At its peak in the late 1960s, Pan Am carried 6.7 million passengers to 86 countries on every continent except Antarctica.

Decline and collapse

The 1973 oil crisis, losses on the 747 fleet, and competition unleashed by the Airline Deregulation Act of 1978 began Pan Am's decline; lacking a domestic route network, it bought National Airlines in 1980 at a high price. The 1988 bombing of Pan Am Flight 103 over Lockerbie, Scotland, deepened public and financial damage. Pan Am sold its Pacific routes (1985), its London Heathrow slots and other assets, and finally ceased operations on 4 December 1991 after the collapse of a rescue deal with Delta Air Lines.

Legacy

Pan Am's blue globe logo and the word "Clipper" remain icons of the golden age of air travel. The brand has been revived several times by unrelated operators, and the name also survives on Pan Am Railways. The MetLife Building in New York was originally the Pan Am Building (1963), and the airline's Miami headquarters and JFK Worldport terminal remain landmarks of aviation history.
