Drowning

Drowning is a type of suffocation induced by the submersion or immersion of the mouth and nose in a liquid. The World Health Organization defines drowning as the process of experiencing respiratory impairment from submersion or immersion in liquid. Most instances occur in fresh water or seawater, and outcomes range from no injury, to injury, to death; cases that are not fatal are sometimes called non-fatal drowning.

Epidemiology

Drowning is among the leading causes of unintentional injury death worldwide, with an estimated 236,000 deaths in 2019 according to the WHO. Children aged one to four are at the highest risk, and males are about twice as likely to drown as females. Most drownings occur in low- and middle-income countries, often during daily activities such as collecting water, fishing, or travelling by boat. Alcohol use, lack of swimming ability, and absence of barriers around water are major risk factors.

Physiology

A drowning person typically undergoes an instinctive response and may be unable to call for help. Breath-holding eventually gives way to involuntary inhalation; water entering the lungs interferes with gas exchange, causing hypoxia. Loss of consciousness follows within minutes, and cardiac arrest can occur if rescue and resuscitation are delayed.

Rescue and treatment

Rescue should be attempted without endangering the rescuer, using reaching aids, flotation, or a boat where possible. Once removed from the water, a person who is not breathing normally should receive cardiopulmonary resuscitation, with emphasis on rescue breaths, and emergency services should be called. People who have been submerged should be evaluated in hospital even if they appear to recover, because respiratory complications can develop later.

Prevention

Effective measures include fencing around pools, swimming and water-safety lessons for children, life jacket use, lifeguard supervision, safe boating regulation, and public education. The WHO and United Nations promote national drowning-prevention strategies.
