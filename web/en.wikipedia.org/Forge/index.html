Forge

A forge is a type of hearth used for heating metals, or the workplace (smithy) where such a hearth is located. The forge is used by the smith to heat a piece of metal to a temperature at which it becomes easier to shape by forging, or to the point where it will melt or work hardening no longer occurs.

Types

Traditional forges burn coal, coke, or charcoal in a hearth with forced air supplied by bellows or an electric blower; the fire is typically contained in a firepot and surrounded by a hearth table. Modern blacksmiths also use gas forges fuelled by propane, which are cleaner and easier to regulate, and induction forges that heat metal electrically.

Operation

The smith heats the workpiece in the fire until it reaches forging temperature—indicated by colour, from dull red through orange to yellow-white heat—then shapes it with hammer and anvil, punches, chisels, and tongs. Quenching, tempering, and annealing may follow to set the metal's properties.

History

Forges date to the Bronze and Iron Ages and were central to metalworking for millennia, producing tools, weapons, horseshoes, and hardware. Although industrial forging largely replaced the village smithy, forges remain in use by farriers, bladesmiths, and hobbyist blacksmiths.
