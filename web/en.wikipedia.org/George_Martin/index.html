George Martin

Sir George Henry Martin CBE
Born: 3 January 1926, London, England
Died: 8 March 2016, Wiltshire, England
Occupation: Record producer, arranger, composer
Known for: Producer of the Beatles

Sir George Henry Martin (3 January 1926 – 8 March 2016) was an English record producer, arranger, composer, conductor, and musician. He was referred to as the "Fifth Beatle" because of his extensive involvement in each of the Beatles' original albums. Martin's formal musical expertise and interest in novel recording practices facilitated the group's rudimentary musical education and desire for new musical sounds to record. Most of their orchestral and string arrangements were written by Martin, and he played piano on many of their records.

Early life and Parlophone

Martin was born in Highbury, London. After wartime service in the Fleet Air Arm, he studied piano and oboe at the Guildhall School of Music and Drama on a veteran's grant. In 1950 he joined EMI's Parlophone label, becoming its head in 1955. At Parlophone he produced classical and baroque recordings, jazz, and a string of successful comedy records with Peter Sellers, Spike Milligan and the Goons, and Flanders and Swann — experience in studio experimentation that later served him with the Beatles.

The Beatles

Martin signed the Beatles to Parlophone in 1962 after every other major British label had turned them down, and — deciding drummer Pete Best was not suitable for studio work — presided over his replacement by Ringo Starr. From "Please Please Me" onward he produced nearly all of their recordings at Abbey Road Studios, contributing arrangements such as the string quartet on "Yesterday," the orchestral crescendo of "A Day in the Life," the harpsichord-like piano solo of "In My Life" (played by Martin at half speed and sped up), and the score of Yellow Submarine. All told, Martin produced 13 albums and 22 number-one singles with the group in the UK.

Later career

In 1965 Martin co-founded Associated Independent Recording (AIR), one of Britain's first independent production companies, and built AIR Studios in London and later on Montserrat. He produced artists including Cilla Black, Gerry and the Pacemakers, America, Jeff Beck, and Elton John, whose 1997 "Candle in the Wind" — produced by Martin — became the best-selling single of all time. He was knighted in 1996 and in his last decades supervised reissues of the Beatles' catalogue. Martin died in his sleep at his home in Wiltshire on 8 March 2016, aged 90.
