The Three Gorges Dam (simplified Chinese: 三峡大坝) is a hydroelectric gravity dam that spans the Yangtze River near Sandouping in Yiling District, Yichang, Hubei province, central China, downstream of the Three Gorges. The world's largest power station in terms of installed capacity (22,500 MW) since 2012, the Three Gorges Dam generates an average of 95±20 TWh of electricity per year, depending on the precipitation in the river basin.

Aside from fulfilling China's hydroelectricity needs, the stated goals of the Three Gorges Dam include improving the Yangtze's navigation capacity and reducing the potential for floods downstream. These goals have prompted debate in China, as the dam has led to the relocation of at least 1.3 million people, caused significant ecological changes and damage to nearby wildlife and their habitats, and resulted in the loss of many cultural and archaeological sites.

History

The idea of a large dam on the Yangtze River was originally envisioned by Sun Yat-sen in The International Development of China in 1919. After several decades of planning, surveying, and political debate, the National People's Congress approved construction in 1992. Construction began on 14 December 1994, and the dam body was completed in 2006. The power plant portion of the project was completed in 2012, when the last of the main water turbines in the underground plant began production. The ship lift, the final major component, was completed in December 2015.

Composition and dimensions

Made of concrete and steel, the dam is 2,335 metres (7,661 ft) long and 185 metres (607 ft) in elevation at its top. The project used 27.2 million cubic metres of concrete, 463,000 tonnes of steel, and moved about 102.6 million cubic metres of earth. The dam's reservoir stretches 660 kilometres (410 mi) upstream to Chongqing and has an average width of 1.1 kilometres (0.68 mi).

Power generation

Power is distributed by multiple 500 kV transmission lines. The main generators weigh about 6,000 tonnes each and were designed to produce more than 700 MW of power each. The dam is equipped with 32 main generators, plus two smaller generators to power the plant itself, for a total generating capacity of 22,500 MW.

Environmental impact

The reservoir flooded large areas, submerged 13 cities, 140 towns, and 1,350 villages, and displaced approximately 1.3 million people. The dam has been implicated in the decline of species such as the baiji (Yangtze river dolphin), which was declared functionally extinct in 2006, and in increased landslide risk along the reservoir's banks due to fluctuating water levels.

Navigation

The dam includes a five-stage ship lock that allows vessels of up to 10,000 tonnes to pass, and a ship lift capable of raising vessels of up to 3,000 tonnes in as little as 40 minutes. River traffic capacity was expected to increase from 10 million to 100 million tonnes annually.
