Kansas State Capitol

Kansas State Capitol
Location: Topeka, Kansas
Construction: 1866–1903
Style: French Renaissance Revival
Dome height: 304 ft (93 m)
Listed: National Register of Historic Places

The Kansas State Capitol, known also as the Kansas Statehouse, is the building housing the executive and legislative branches of the government of the U.S. state of Kansas. It is located in Topeka and is built of native Kansas limestone.

Construction

Construction began in 1866 and proceeded in stages over 37 years; the building was declared complete in 1903. Its copper dome rises 304 feet, taller than that of the United States Capitol. A bronze statue of a Kansa warrior, titled Ad Astra, was placed atop the dome in 2002.

Interior

The capitol is known for murals by John Steuart Curry, including the famous depiction of abolitionist John Brown, and for the work of Lumen Martin Winter. A major restoration and expansion project was completed in 2014. See also Topeka.
