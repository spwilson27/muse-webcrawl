Incus Records

Incus Records is a British independent record label founded in 1970, notable as the first record company in the United Kingdom to be owned and run by improvising musicians themselves. It was established by guitarist Derek Bailey, saxophonist Evan Parker and percussionist Tony Oxley, initially together with critic Michael Walters, to release free improvised music.

History

The label's first release was The Topography of the Lungs (1970) by Bailey, Parker and Han Bennink. Over the following decades Incus issued a catalogue central to the development of European free improvisation, including solo recordings by Bailey and Parker and group projects such as Iskra 1903. Bailey remained the guiding figure of the label until his death in 2005; the label was subsequently run by Karen Brookman-Bailey.

Significance

Incus pioneered the musician-run label model in improvised music, giving artists control over recording and production, and its catalogue is regarded as a primary document of the British and European improvisation scenes.
