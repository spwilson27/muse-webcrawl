Tokamak

A tokamak is a device that confines a hot plasma in the shape of a torus (doughnut) using powerful magnetic fields, in order to produce controlled thermonuclear fusion power. The name is a Russian acronym for "toroidal chamber with magnetic coils". Invented in the Soviet Union in the 1950s by Igor Tamm and Andrei Sakharov and developed at the Kurchatov Institute, the tokamak became, and remains, the leading design in magnetic confinement fusion research.

How it works

External toroidal field coils produce a strong magnetic field around the torus. A central solenoid acts as a transformer inducing a large electric current in the plasma itself; this current generates a second, poloidal magnetic field, and the combination of the two fields produces helical field lines that twist around the torus, confining the plasma away from the vessel walls. The plasma current also heats the plasma (ohmic heating), supplemented by neutral beam injection and radio-frequency heating to reach temperatures above 100 million kelvin — hotter than the core of the Sun — at which deuterium and tritium nuclei fuse, releasing helium, neutrons and energy.

History and record devices

Results from the Soviet T-3 tokamak, reported in 1968 with electron temperatures around 1 keV, convinced the world community and redirected fusion research for decades. The largest completed tokamak, JET in the United Kingdom, holds the world record for fusion energy produced in a single pulse (69 megajoules, in 2023) and first achieved significant fusion power in 1997. ITER, under construction in southern France by a 35-nation collaboration, aims for 500 MW of fusion power from 50 MW of heating (Q = 10). China's EAST and South Korea's KSTAR have set records for sustained high-temperature operation, and compact high-field designs such as SPARC exploit modern superconducting magnets.

Challenges

Tokamaks face plasma instabilities and disruptive events, erosion and neutron damage of wall materials, the need to breed tritium fuel, and the pulsed nature of transformer-driven current. Stellarators, which generate the twisting field entirely with external coils, are the main alternative configuration.
