Jules Rimet

Jules Rimet
Born: October 14, 1873, Theuley, France
Died: October 16, 1956, Suresnes, France
Known for: Founder of the FIFA World Cup; FIFA president 1921–1954

Jules Rimet (October 14, 1873 – October 16, 1956) was a French football administrator who served as the third president of FIFA from 1921 to 1954 — the longest tenure in the organization's history — and is credited as the creator of the FIFA World Cup.

Early involvement in football

A lawyer by training, Rimet was a co-founder of the Paris club Red Star in 1897 and helped organize French football's governing structures. He became the first president of the French Football Federation in 1919. Having seen the football tournament at the Olympic Games, Rimet became convinced that the sport needed its own global championship open to professionals.

The World Cup

Elected FIFA president in 1921, Rimet spent nearly a decade persuading national associations to support a world championship. His efforts culminated in the first FIFA World Cup, held in Uruguay in 1930; Rimet famously carried the trophy to Montevideo in his luggage aboard the SS Conte Verde. The tournament survived the Great Depression and went on to become the world's most watched sporting event.

Legacy

The original World Cup trophy was named the Jules Rimet Trophy in his honor in 1946; Brazil won it outright in 1970 after their third title, and it was stolen in 1983 and never recovered. Rimet was nominated for the Nobel Peace Prize in 1956 for his work promoting international understanding through sport. He died that same year, two days after his 83rd birthday.
