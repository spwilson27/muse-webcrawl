The 1956 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the XVI Olympiad, were held from 22 November to 8 December 1956 in Melbourne, Victoria, Australia. They were the first Olympic Games held in the Southern Hemisphere and the first held outside Europe and North America. Because of Australian quarantine laws, the equestrian events were held separately in Stockholm, Sweden, in June of the same year.

Background

Melbourne was awarded the Games in 1949, defeating Buenos Aires by one vote in the final round. Preparation was marked by disputes between the organising committee, the Victorian government and the IOC, and IOC president Avery Brundage at one point considered moving the Games to Rome. Construction delays at the Melbourne Cricket Ground were resolved only shortly before the opening.

Political turmoil

The Games took place amid two international crises. Egypt, Iraq and Lebanon boycotted in protest at the invasion of Egypt during the Suez Crisis, while the Netherlands, Spain and Switzerland withdrew over the Soviet suppression of the Hungarian uprising. Hungary nevertheless sent a team; its water polo semi-final against the Soviet Union — the famous "Blood in the Water" match — was played in an atmosphere of intense hostility, Hungary winning 4–0 before the match was stopped amid crowd unrest. Hungary went on to win gold. China withdrew over the participation of Taiwan.

Highlights

Australian swimmer Dawn Fraser and Murray Rose starred in the pool, while Australia's Betty Cuthbert won three sprint gold medals. Soviet gymnast Larisa Latynina began her Olympic medal accumulation, and the Soviet Union topped the medal table ahead of the United States and Australia. For the first time, athletes of all nations mingled together rather than marching separately in the closing ceremony — a suggestion from a young Australian schoolboy, John Ian Wing, that became an enduring Olympic tradition. Ron Clarke lit the Olympic flame at the opening ceremony.
