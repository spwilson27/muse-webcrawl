Postnuptial agreement

Postnuptial agreement

Type: Marital contract

Timing: Entered after marriage

Purpose: Division of assets and responsibilities on divorce or death

Contrast: Prenuptial agreement (before marriage)

A postnuptial agreement is a written contract entered into by a married couple after their wedding, setting out how their assets, debts and responsibilities would be divided in the event of divorce, separation or death. It is the counterpart of the prenuptial agreement, which is signed before the marriage takes place.

Couples may choose a postnuptial agreement for many reasons: a change in financial circumstances such as an inheritance or a new business, a wish to protect children from a previous relationship, or a reconciliation after a period of marital difficulty in which financial terms help rebuild trust.

Legal requirements

Enforceability varies by jurisdiction. Courts commonly require that the agreement be in writing, entered into voluntarily by both spouses, based on full financial disclosure, and not unconscionable. Independent legal advice for each party is strongly recommended and in some jurisdictions effectively required. Some courts scrutinise postnuptial agreements more closely than prenuptial ones, because spouses already owe each other fiduciary duties.

Contents

Agreements typically address the classification of property as separate or marital, the division of real estate and investments, responsibility for debts, spousal support, and the ownership of businesses. Provisions attempting to determine child custody or child support are generally not binding, since courts decide those matters in the child's best interests.
