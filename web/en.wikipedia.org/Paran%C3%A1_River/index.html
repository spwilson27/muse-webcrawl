The Paraná River (Spanish: Río Paraná) is a river in south-central South America, running through Brazil, Paraguay, and Argentina for some 4,880 kilometres (3,030 mi). It is second in length only to the Amazon River among South American rivers. The name Paraná is an abbreviation of the phrase "para rehe onáva", which comes from the Tupi language and means "like the sea".

Course

The Paraná is formed at the confluence of the Paranaíba and Grande rivers in southern Brazil. It flows generally southwest, forming the border between Brazil and Paraguay, and after receiving the Iguazu River (just downstream of the Iguazu Falls) it becomes the border between Paraguay and Argentina. Flowing south through Argentina, it passes Rosario and joins the Uruguay River to form the Río de la Plata estuary at Buenos Aires.

Uses

The river is a major shipping route and water source for agriculture and cities. Two of the world's largest hydroelectric dams stand on it: the Itaipu Dam (Brazil–Paraguay, 14 GW) and the Yacyretá Dam (Paraguay–Argentina). The Paraná Delta, near its mouth, is a vast wetland of some 14,000 km². Severe drought in 2020–2022 dropped the river to its lowest levels in decades, disrupting navigation and hydropower.
