Diprotodon is an extinct genus of giant marsupial from Australia. The only species, Diprotodon optatum, was the largest known marsupial to have ever lived, reaching about 3 metres in length, 1.8 metres at the shoulder, and weighing up to roughly 2,800 kg — about the size of a rhinoceros or hippopotamus. It was a relative of the modern wombat and koala, and a member of the Australian megafauna.

Description

Diprotodon was a heavily built quadruped with a massive skull, large forward-facing ("diprotodont") incisor teeth adapted for cropping vegetation, and powerful limbs with in-turned, pigeon-toed feet. Its skeleton suggests it was a browser and grazer of leaves, shrubs, and grasses. Fossils show sexual dimorphism, with males considerably larger than females, and evidence from bonebeds suggests it may have lived in herds and made seasonal migrations across the Australian interior.

Extinction

Diprotodon lived through much of the Pleistocene and survived until roughly 44,000 years ago, overlapping in time with the first Aboriginal Australians. The causes of its extinction remain debated: proposed factors include climate-driven aridification of the Australian interior, human hunting, and landscape burning by humans. Well-preserved fossil sites such as Lake Callabonna in South Australia have yielded many complete skeletons, suggesting herds became mired in drying lake beds.

Discovery

Fossils of Diprotodon were among the first Australian megafauna described to science; remains found at Wellington Caves in New South Wales in the 1830s were described by Sir Richard Owen, who named the genus in 1838. Owen initially misjudged its relationships, and early reconstructions varied widely before complete skeletons clarified its wombat-like form.
