Multilingualism

Multilingualism is the use of more than one language by an individual speaker or a community of speakers. Individuals who speak more than one language are called multilingual or polyglots; the use of two languages is bilingualism. Multilingualism is the norm worldwide: most of the world's population speaks more than one language, while monolingualism is concentrated in a few large countries such as the United States, Brazil and parts of Europe.

Individual multilingualism

Languages may be acquired simultaneously in childhood, sequentially through schooling, or later in life. Research once suggested a "bilingual advantage" in executive function, though the size and robustness of that effect remain debated. Multilingual speakers commonly code-switch between languages within a conversation, and languages in a multilingual mind interact rather than being stored in isolation.

Societal multilingualism

Multilingual societies manage diversity in different ways: through official multilingualism (as in India, South Africa, Switzerland, Belgium and Canada), through a lingua franca, or through diglossia, where different languages serve formal and informal functions. The European Union operates in 24 official languages. Language policy choices affect education, courts, media and the survival of minority and indigenous languages, thousands of which are endangered.
