Rum

TypeDistilled alcoholic beverage
OriginBarbados / Caribbean
Introduced17th century
Alcohol by volume37.5–80%
IngredientsSugarcane juice or molasses

Rum is a liquor made by fermenting and then distilling sugarcane molasses or sugarcane juice. The distillate, a clear liquid, is often aged in barrels of oak. Rum originated in the Caribbean in the 17th century, most notably on Barbados, where sugarcane planters discovered that molasses — a byproduct of sugar refining — could be fermented and distilled into a spirit.

Dark rum, aged in oak barrels, takes on colour and flavour from the wood.

History

Precursors of rum were produced in the ancient world, but modern rum emerged on the sugarcane plantations of the Caribbean. By the mid-1600s Barbadian planters were distilling a spirit they called "kill-devil" or "rumbullion", the latter probably the source of the word rum. Rum became a major trade good: it was bound up with the transatlantic slave trade, the sugar economy, and naval tradition. The British Royal Navy issued a daily rum ration — the "tot" — from the 18th century until its abolition on 31 July 1970, known as Black Tot Day. During American Prohibition, "rum-running" smuggled Caribbean rum into the United States.

Production and grades

Most rum is made from molasses; in the French-speaking Caribbean, rhum agricole is made directly from fresh sugarcane juice. After fermentation, the wash is distilled in pot stills or column stills, then aged in oak barrels, often charred bourbon casks. Grades include light (white) rum, gold rum, dark rum, spiced rum, flavoured rum, and overproof rum exceeding 57.5% alcohol by volume. The warm Caribbean climate accelerates maturation, so a rum aged ten years in the tropics may show more wood character than a whisky aged far longer in Scotland.

Cocktails

Rum is the base of classic cocktails including the daiquiri, mojito, piña colada, Cuba libre, Mai Tai, and dark 'n' stormy. It is also mixed with hot buttered water in the winter drink hot buttered rum.
