Gjøa
Launched1872, Rosendal, Norway
TypeSloop, 21 m (70 ft), 47 tons
FateMuseum ship, Oslo

Gjøa was the first vessel to complete a transit of the Northwest Passage, in 1906. A 21-metre sloop built in 1872 at Rosendal, Norway, she had served as a herring fishing boat before Roald Amundsen purchased her in 1900.

The Amundsen expedition

With a crew of six, Amundsen sailed from Kristiania (Oslo) in June 1903. The shallow-drafted Gjøa threaded James Ross Strait and wintered at a harbour on King William Island the crew named Gjøahavn (now Gjoa Haven, Nunavut), where they conducted magnetic observations near the North Magnetic Pole for two years. Continuing west through Queen Maud Gulf and the Beaufort Sea, the ship reached Nome, Alaska, in August 1906.

Aftermath

Gjøa sailed on to San Francisco, where she was displayed in Golden Gate Park for decades. Repatriated in 1972, she is now preserved beside the Fram at the Fram Museum in Oslo.
