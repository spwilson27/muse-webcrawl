The 1934 FIFA World Cup was the second edition of the FIFA World Cup, the quadrennial international football championship for men's national teams. It was held in Italy from 27 May to 10 June 1934. Italy was chosen as the host nation by FIFA in October 1932, after a lengthy bidding process in which it beat competition from Sweden.

The tournament was the first in which teams had to qualify to take part; 32 nations entered, and after qualification 16 teams participated in the finals. Reigning champions Uruguay boycotted the tournament in protest at the low number of European teams that had travelled to their country for the 1930 World Cup. As a result, the 1934 World Cup is the only one in which the reigning champions did not participate.

Tournament summary

The finals tournament used a straight knockout format. All eight first-round matches were played simultaneously, with Italy defeating the United States 7–1 in Rome. Defending semi-finalists Austria, Czechoslovakia, Germany, Hungary, Italy, Spain, Sweden and Switzerland won through to the quarter-finals. Spain and Italy drew 1–1 after extra time in Florence, forcing a replay the following day, which Italy won 1–0.

In the semi-finals, Italy beat Austria's famed Wunderteam 1–0 in Milan, while Czechoslovakia defeated Germany 3–1 in Rome, with Oldřich Nejedlý scoring a hat-trick. Germany beat Austria 3–2 in the third-place match in Naples.

Final

The final was held at the Stadio Nazionale PNF in Rome on 10 June 1934. Czechoslovakia took the lead through Antonín Puč with 19 minutes remaining, but Raimundo Orsi equalised for Italy nine minutes from time. Angelo Schiavio scored the winning goal in extra time, giving Italy a 2–1 victory and its first World Cup title. Italy's manager was Vittorio Pozzo, and the team was heavily influenced by oriundi — South American-born players of Italian descent such as Orsi and Luis Monti, who had played for Argentina in the 1930 final.

Political context

The tournament took place under the fascist regime of Benito Mussolini, who used the event as a propaganda showcase for Italy. It has frequently been alleged that Mussolini pressured officials and that refereeing decisions favoured the hosts, though the extent of direct interference remains debated by historians.

Goalscorers

With five goals, Oldřich Nejedlý of Czechoslovakia was the tournament's top scorer. Edmund Conen of Germany and Angelo Schiavio of Italy each scored four.
