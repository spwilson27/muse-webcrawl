The Israeli-Palestinian conflict is an ongoing political and armed conflict between Israel and Palestinians over territory, self-determination, and security in the land between the Jordan River and the Mediterranean Sea. It is one of the world's longest-running conflicts, with roots in the competing national movements of Zionism and Palestinian Arab nationalism that emerged in the late Ottoman and British Mandate periods.

Background

Jewish immigration to Ottoman and then British-ruled Palestine, driven by Zionism and later by persecution in Europe, grew during the late 19th and early 20th centuries, accompanied by land purchases and periodic communal violence. Britain's Balfour Declaration (1917) promised a "national home for the Jewish people" in Palestine, while the territory's Arab majority sought independence. The United Nations' 1947 partition plan proposed separate Jewish and Arab states; Jewish leaders accepted it and Arab leaders rejected it.

1948 and its aftermath

Israel declared independence in May 1948 and defeated the invading Arab armies in the 1948 Arab–Israeli War. Some 700,000 Palestinians fled or were expelled from their homes in what Palestinians call the Nakba ("catastrophe"), and Israel refused their return; Jordan held the West Bank and Egypt the Gaza Strip. The Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO) was founded in 1964. In the Six-Day War of 1967 Israel captured the West Bank, East Jerusalem, the Gaza Strip, the Sinai, and the Golan Heights, beginning a military occupation of Palestinian territories and, later, the construction of Israeli settlements widely regarded as illegal under international law.

Peace process and uprisings

The First Intifada (1987–1993), a mass Palestinian uprising, led to the Oslo Accords (1993–95), in which Israel and the PLO recognized each other and created the Palestinian Authority with limited self-rule — but final-status issues (borders, Jerusalem, refugees, settlements, security) were left unresolved. The process collapsed amid the assassination of Israeli prime minister Yitzhak Rabin (1995) and the violence of the Second Intifada (2000–2005). Israel withdrew its settlers and soldiers from Gaza in 2005; Hamas won Palestinian legislative elections in 2006 and seized control of Gaza in 2007, leading to an Israeli and Egyptian blockade and repeated Gaza wars (2008–09, 2012, 2014, 2021).

Gaza war (2023– )

On 7 October 2023 Hamas-led militants attacked southern Israel, killing about 1,200 people and taking some 250 hostages. Israel responded with a large-scale air and ground offensive in Gaza that killed tens of thousands of Palestinians, displaced nearly the entire population, and produced a severe humanitarian crisis, drawing international legal proceedings and widespread protest. The war also sparked fighting with Hezbollah in Lebanon and direct exchanges with Iran, while the fundamental political disputes of the conflict remained unresolved.
