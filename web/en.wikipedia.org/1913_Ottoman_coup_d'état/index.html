The 1913 Ottoman coup d'état (23 January 1913), also known as the Raid on the Sublime Porte (Turkish: Bâb-ı Âlî Baskını), was a coup carried out in the Ottoman Empire by members of the Committee of Union and Progress (CUP) under the leadership of Enver Bey (later Enver Pasha) and Talat Bey (later Talat Pasha).

Background

The coup came during the First Balkan War, in which the Balkan League had inflicted heavy defeats on the empire, overrunning most of its European territory. The government of Grand Vizier Kâmil Pasha, dominated by the rival Freedom and Accord Party, was preparing to accept peace terms that would cede the city of Edirne (Adrianople) to Bulgaria. The Unionists seized on the prospect of losing the empire's former capital as a pretext to act.

The raid

On 23 January 1913, a group of Unionist officers led by Enver stormed the Sublime Porte during a cabinet meeting. In the confrontation, the Minister of War, Nazım Pasha, was shot and killed — by whom remains disputed. Kâmil Pasha was forced at gunpoint to write out his resignation to Sultan Mehmed V.

Consequences

Mahmud Shevket Pasha became Grand Vizier and Minister of War, but after his assassination in June 1913 the CUP consolidated undisputed control. The coup marked the beginning of the so-called "triumvirate" of Enver, Talat, and Djemal Pasha, which governed the empire through the First World War.
