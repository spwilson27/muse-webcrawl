Eggplant

Eggplant (US, Canada, Australia), aubergine (UK, Ireland) or brinjal (Indian subcontinent) is a plant species in the nightshade family Solanaceae, Solanum melongena. Its spiny fruit, of the same name, is used in cuisines worldwide. The plant is most commonly purple, but white, green and striped varieties exist.

History and etymology

The eggplant was domesticated from the wild nightshade Solanum incanum, probably in South or Southeast Asia, and has been cultivated in southern and eastern Asia since prehistory. It spread westward through the Islamic world, reaching Spain by the medieval period; the word "aubergine" comes from Arabic al-bāḏinjān, itself from Persian and ultimately Sanskrit. "Eggplant" refers to the white, egg-shaped fruits of some early European varieties.

Description

Solanum melongena is grown as a tender perennial though usually cultivated as an annual. It grows 40 to 150 cm tall, with large, lobed leaves and white-to-purple flowers. The fruit is a glossy berry containing many small seeds; its colour comes from anthocyanin pigments such as nasunin. Raw eggplant has a bitter taste, but cooked it becomes tender and develops a rich, complex flavour.

Culinary use

Eggplant is a staple of many cuisines: it is central to dishes such as Middle Eastern baba ghanoush and moussaka, Greek moussaka, Italian melanzane alla parmigiana and caponata, French ratatouille, Turkish imam bayıldı, and numerous Indian curries such as baingan bharta. In 2022, world production of eggplants was about 59 million tonnes, with China accounting for roughly two-thirds of the total, followed by India.

Nutrition

Raw eggplant consists of about 92% water, 6% carbohydrates, 1% protein and negligible fat, providing modest amounts of manganese and dietary fibre. Like other nightshades it contains small amounts of alkaloids, including solanine in the leaves and unripe fruit.
