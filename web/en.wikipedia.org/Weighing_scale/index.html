Weighing scale

A weighing scale (or weighing balance) is a device to measure weight or mass. Traditional balances compare the unknown mass against known reference masses, while modern spring and electronic scales measure the force exerted by the load.

History

The balance scale is among the oldest measuring instruments, depicted in ancient Egyptian art from around 2400 BC. Balances were essential for trade in grain, metals and coinage. The steelyard, a lever balance with a sliding counterweight, was used in Roman times. Spring scales appeared in the 18th century, and strain-gauge load cells made accurate electronic scales widespread in the 20th century.

Types

Balance scales include the equal-arm beam balance and the Roberval balance. Spring scales measure force via Hooke's law. Electronic scales use load cells and digital readouts and range from milligram-precision laboratory analytical balances to industrial weighbridges for trucks. Bathroom scales and kitchen scales are common household examples.

Accuracy and calibration

Scales used in commerce are typically regulated and must be calibrated with standard weights. Precision is expressed in terms of readability and repeatability; analytical balances can resolve 0.1 mg or less.
