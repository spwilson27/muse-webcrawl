Mgahinga Gorilla National Park is a national park in southwestern Uganda, in the Kisoro District near the town of Kisoro. At about 33.9 km² it is Uganda's smallest national park. It forms part of the Virunga Conservation Area, contiguous with Rwanda's Volcanoes National Park and the Democratic Republic of Congo's Virunga National Park, and protects the Ugandan slopes of three extinct Virunga volcanoes: Mount Muhabura (4,127 m), Mount Gahinga (3,474 m — from which the park takes its name, gahinga meaning a small pile of stones), and Mount Sabyinyo (3,669 m).

Wildlife

The park was created in 1991, having been a game sanctuary since 1930. Its afro-montane forests of bamboo, hagenia, and giant lobelia and senecio protect one habituated group of endangered mountain gorillas (the Nyakagezi group), which historically ranged across the three countries' parks, as well as the endangered golden monkey, which is endemic to the Virunga massif. Other wildlife includes elephants, buffalo, bushbuck, black-fronted duiker, and some 180 bird species including Albertine Rift endemics such as the Rwenzori turaco.

People and tourism

The forest was traditionally home to the Batwa (Twa) pygmy people, hunter-gatherers who were evicted when the park was gazetted and remain landless; the Batwa Trail experience in the park presents their culture and is partly intended to benefit the community. Tourism centres on gorilla trekking (permits issued by the Uganda Wildlife Authority for the park's single habituated group), golden monkey tracking, volcano climbing, and birdwatching. The park is reached from Kisoro, roughly 14 km from the park gate at Ntebeko.
