US Open (tennis)

The US Open Tennis Championships, commonly called the US Open, is a hardcourt tennis tournament organised by the United States Tennis Association. It is one of the four Grand Slam tournaments, alongside the Australian Open, the French Open and Wimbledon. Held annually over two weeks in late August and early September at the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center in Flushing Meadows, Queens, New York City, it is the final Grand Slam of the calendar year.

History

The tournament traces its origins to the U.S. National Championship first held in Newport, Rhode Island, in 1881. It moved to the West Side Tennis Club at Forest Hills in 1915 and to Flushing Meadows in 1978, when the surface changed from clay to hardcourt (DecoTurf, later Laykold). The US Open was the first Grand Slam to introduce the tiebreak (1970) and equal prize money for men and women (1973), and it pioneered night sessions.

Venue and events

The main court, Arthur Ashe Stadium, opened in 1997 and is the largest tennis stadium in the world, with a retractable roof added in 2016; Louis Armstrong Stadium is the second show court. Events comprise men's and women's singles and doubles, mixed doubles, junior and wheelchair competitions. Record singles champions include Roger Federer, Pete Sampras and Jimmy Connors with five men's titles in the Open Era, and Chris Evert and Serena Williams with six women's titles each.

Characteristics

The tournament is known for its loud, festive atmosphere, evening matches under lights, and its role as the climax of the North American summer hardcourt season.
