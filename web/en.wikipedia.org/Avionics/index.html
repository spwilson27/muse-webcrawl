Avionics

Avionics (a portmanteau of aviation and electronics) are the electronic systems used on aircraft. Avionic systems include communications, navigation, the display and management of multiple systems, and the hundreds of systems that are fitted to aircraft to perform individual functions. These can be as simple as a searchlight for a police helicopter or as complicated as the tactical system for an airborne early warning platform.

History

The term "avionics" was coined in the 1930s and came into common use after the Second World War, when radio communication and radar systems became standard equipment on military and then civil aircraft. The development of integrated circuits in the 1970s and 1980s allowed flight instruments to be replaced by electronic displays, giving rise to the "glass cockpit". Modern airliners devote a substantial fraction of their cost — often around 30 per cent — to avionics.

Systems

Communications

Communications connect the flight deck to the ground and to passengers. Very high frequency (VHF) aviation communication is the primary means of communication with air traffic control, supplemented by satellite communications on long-haul routes.

Navigation

Navigation is the determination of position and direction on or above the surface of the Earth. Avionic navigation systems include inertial navigation systems, satellite navigation receivers such as GPS, radio beacons such as VOR and instrument landing systems.

Monitoring and flight management

Flight management systems integrate navigation, performance data and fuel planning, guiding the aircraft along a programmed route via the autopilot. Monitoring systems range from flight data recorders to engine-indicating and crew-alerting systems.

Standards and regulation

Avionics are developed under rigorous certification standards because of their safety-critical nature; widely used standards include DO-178C for software and DO-254 for complex electronic hardware. Data communication between systems commonly follows standards such as ARINC 429 and the avionics full-duplex switched Ethernet (AFDX).
