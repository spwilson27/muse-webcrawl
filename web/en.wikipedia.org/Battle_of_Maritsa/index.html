Battle of Maritsa

The Battle of Maritsa or Battle of Chernomen (Serbian: Маричка битка; Turkish: Çirmen Muharebesi) took place at the Maritsa River on 26 September 1371 between Ottoman forces under Lala Shahin Pasha and Evrenos and a Serbian army led by the brothers King Vukašin Mrnjavčević and Despot Jovan Uglješa. The Serbian army was annihilated and both its commanders killed, clearing the way for Ottoman expansion into Macedonia and the southern Balkans.

Background

By 1371 the Ottoman Turks had established themselves in Thrace, with Edirne as their European capital. The Serbian lords of Serres and Prilep, Jovan Uglješa and his brother King Vukašin (co-ruler with Tsar Stefan Uroš V), resolved to strike east before the Ottomans grew stronger. They marched toward Edirne with an army that later chronicles inflated to 60,000 men; modern estimates are lower but the force was substantial.

The battle

The Serbian army camped near the village of Chernomen by the Maritsa River, not far from Edirne. On the night of 26 September, Ottoman forces under Lala Shahin Pasha launched a surprise attack on the camp. Caught unprepared—later tradition blames drunkenness and negligence—most of the Serbian army was killed, drowned in the river, or scattered without offering organised resistance. Both Vukašin and Uglješa perished.

Aftermath

The disaster broke organised Serbian resistance in Macedonia. In its wake the Ottomans occupied Serres, Drama and much of Macedonia, and surviving Serbian and Bulgarian princes, including Vukašin's son Marko (the later epic hero Prince Marko), became Ottoman vassals. Maritsa is often paired with the Battle of Kosovo (1389) as one of the pivotal steps in the Ottoman conquest of the Balkans.
