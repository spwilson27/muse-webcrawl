Participatory culture

Participatory culture, a concept developed by media scholar Henry Jenkins, describes a culture in which members believe their contributions matter and feel some degree of social connection with one another, and in which the barriers to artistic expression and civic engagement are relatively low. It is contrasted with older models of media consumption in which audiences were largely passive: in a participatory culture, fans and users are active creators and circulators of content — writing fan fiction, making videos and remixes, contributing to wikis, and organising communities around shared interests.

Characteristics

In the 2009 white paper Confronting the Challenges of Participatory Culture, Jenkins and co-authors characterised a participatory culture as one with low barriers to expression and engagement, strong support for creating and sharing, informal mentorship between experienced and novice members, and a belief among members that contributions matter. Characteristic activities include affiliations in online communities, creative expressions and remixes, collaborative problem-solving (as in gaming guilds or Wikipedia), and circulations that shape the flow of media, such as podcasting and fan sharing.

Participation gap and literacy

Jenkins argued that the educational challenge of the digital age is not only the "digital divide" in access to technology but a participation gap: unequal access to the experiences, skills, and mentorships that prepare young people to participate fully. He proposed a set of "new media literacies" — including play, appropriation, collective intelligence, transmedia navigation, and networking — that schools should cultivate. Critics have since noted that corporate platforms channel and monetise participation, that much online "participation" is shallow or unpaid labour, and that harassment and misinformation are also products of lowered barriers to publication.
