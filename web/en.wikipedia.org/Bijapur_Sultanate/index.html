Bijapur Sultanate
Capital: Bijapur
Period: 1490–1686
Dynasty: Adil Shahi
Founder: Yusuf Adil Shah

The Bijapur Sultanate was a late medieval Indian kingdom in the western Deccan, ruled by the Adil Shahi dynasty from 1490 until its annexation by the Mughal Empire in 1686. At its height under Ibrahim Adil Shah II and Mohammed Adil Shah it encompassed much of present-day northern Karnataka and parts of Maharashtra and Goa.

History

Yusuf Adil Shah, a former Bahmani governor of possibly Georgian or Turkish origin, declared independence as the Bahmani Sultanate fragmented into the five Deccan sultanates. Bijapur frequently fought and allied with its neighbours — Ahmadnagar, Golconda, Bidar, and Berar — and joined the Deccan alliance that destroyed the Vijayanagara Empire at the Battle of Talikota in 1565. Ibrahim Adil Shah II (r. 1580–1627) was famed for religious tolerance and patronage of music and painting. In the 17th century the sultanate faced Maratha resistance under Shivaji and growing Mughal pressure; after a long siege Aurangzeb captured Bijapur in 1686 and imprisoned the last sultan, Sikandar Adil Shah.

Culture

Bijapur was a centre of Deccani Urdu literature, miniature painting, and Indo-Islamic architecture. Its monuments, including the Ibrahim Rauza and the Gol Gumbaz, are among the finest of the Deccan. The court blended Persian, Turkish, Marathi, and local traditions.
