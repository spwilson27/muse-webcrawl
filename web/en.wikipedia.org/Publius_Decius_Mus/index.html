Publius Decius Mus

Died: 340 BC, near Mount Vesuvius
Office: Consul of the Roman Republic (340 BC)
Known for: Devotio at the Battle of Veseris

Publius Decius Mus (died 340 BC) was a Roman politician and general of the plebeian gens Decia. He is famed for his devotio — the ritual self-sacrifice of his own life to the gods of the underworld in exchange for victory — at the Battle of Veseris during the Latin War.

Career

Decius first distinguished himself as a military tribune in 343 BC during the First Samnite War, when, according to Livy, he led a detachment to seize heights and save the trapped army of the consul Aulus Cornelius Cossus Arvina. He served as quaestor and then consul in 340 BC with Titus Manlius Imperiosus Torquatus as his colleague, when Rome faced the revolt of the Latin League.

Devotio at Veseris

Before the Battle of Veseris (also called the Battle of Mount Vesuvius), both consuls were said to have dreamed that the side whose general died would win. When the Roman left wing, commanded by Decius, began to give way, he called the pontifex maximus to recite the formula of devotion, dedicating himself and the enemy army to the gods of the dead, then rode alone into the Latin ranks and was killed. The Romans rallied and won the battle. His son, also Publius Decius Mus, and his grandson performed similar acts of devotio in later generations, making the Decii the exemplary family of patriotic self-sacrifice in Roman tradition.
