Bollywood is the informal name for the Hindi-language film industry based in Mumbai (formerly Bombay), India. The term, a portmanteau of "Bombay" and "Hollywood," is sometimes applied loosely to Indian cinema as a whole, but properly refers to the Mumbai-based Hindi industry, as distinct from regional industries such as Tollywood (Telugu and Bengali) and Kollywood (Tamil). Bollywood films are known for their music and elaborate song-and-dance numbers, melodrama, and the "masala" format mixing romance, comedy, action, and family drama.

History

Indian cinema began with Dadasaheb Phalke's Raja Harishchandra (1913), and the first Hindi talkie, Alam Ara, appeared in 1931. The 1950s are often called a golden age, with directors such as Raj Kapoor, Guru Dutt, Bimal Roy, and Mehboob Khan — whose Mother India (1957) was nominated for an Academy Award. The 1970s brought the "angry young man" persona of Amitabh Bachchan and the rise of the masala film, exemplified by Sholay (1975). In the 1990s, stars such as Shah Rukh Khan, Aamir Khan, and Salman Khan drove a wave of family romances, many aimed at the growing overseas diaspora market, as with Dilwale Dulhania Le Jayenge (1995).

Music

Music is integral to Bollywood: most films feature four to six songs, sung by playback singers — artists such as Lata Mangeshkar, Kishore Kumar, Mohammed Rafi, and Asha Bhosle recorded tens of thousands of songs — while actors lip-sync on screen. Film soundtracks historically accounted for a large share of India's total music sales, and a hit soundtrack can make or break a film's commercial prospects.

Industry

India produces more feature films annually than any other country (across all its language industries), and Bollywood titles reach audiences throughout South Asia, the Middle East, Africa, and the diaspora. Major studios and producers include Yash Raj Films, Dharma Productions, and Excel Entertainment. In the 21st century, streaming platforms have expanded Bollywood's global reach, while southern Indian industries — notably Telugu cinema with hits such as RRR (2022) — have grown in pan-Indian and international prominence.
