Amazon.com, Inc. is an American multinational technology company focusing on e-commerce, cloud computing, online advertising, digital streaming and artificial intelligence. Founded by Jeff Bezos in 1994 as an online bookstore, it grew into the world's largest online retailer and one of the most valuable publicly traded companies, counted among the "Big Five" American technology firms.

Amazon's headquarters campus in Seattle, including the Amazon Spheres.

History

Jeff Bezos incorporated the company as Cadabra in July 1994, renaming it Amazon after the world's largest river, and launched Amazon.com in 1995 selling books online. The company went public in 1997 and expanded from books into music, video and general merchandise, surviving the dot-com crash to post its first annual profit in 2003. Amazon launched its third-party Marketplace, the Prime membership programme (2005), Amazon Web Services (2006), the Kindle e-reader (2007) and the Echo/Alexa voice platform (2014), and acquired Whole Foods Market for $13.7 billion in 2017. Bezos stepped down as CEO in 2021, succeeded by Andy Jassy.

Products and services

Amazon's retail arm sells its own inventory and hosts millions of third-party sellers, supported by a vast logistics network of fulfilment centres and delivery operations. Amazon Prime bundles fast shipping with Prime Video streaming and other perks for hundreds of millions of members. Amazon Web Services (AWS) is the world's largest cloud-computing platform and the company's most profitable segment. Amazon also sells Kindle e-readers, Fire tablets and TV devices, Ring and Blink home-security products, operates advertising and payment businesses, and produces films and series through Amazon MGM Studios.

Finances and scale

Amazon's annual revenue exceeds half a trillion dollars, and it is one of the largest private employers in the world, with roughly 1.5 million employees. Its market capitalisation has surpassed two trillion dollars. AWS alone generates tens of billions in annual operating income, subsidising the thinner margins of retail.

Criticism

Amazon has faced sustained criticism over warehouse working conditions, anti-union efforts, tax practices, marketplace counterfeits, market power over suppliers and sellers, and the privacy implications of its devices and data collection. It has also drawn antitrust scrutiny, including a major U.S. Federal Trade Commission lawsuit filed in 2023.
