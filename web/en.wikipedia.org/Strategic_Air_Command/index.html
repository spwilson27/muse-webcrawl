Strategic Air Command

The Strategic Air Command (SAC) was a United States Department of Defense Specified Command and a United States Air Force Major Command responsible for command and control of the strategic bomber and intercontinental ballistic missile components of the United States military's strategic nuclear forces from 1946 to 1992. SAC was also responsible for strategic reconnaissance aircraft. Headquartered at Offutt Air Force Base, Nebraska, SAC was the operational heart of American nuclear deterrence throughout the Cold War.

SAC's motto was "Peace is Our Profession". At its peak in the late 1950s and 1960s, SAC controlled a large share of the nation's nuclear delivery systems and maintained bombers on continuous ground alert — and, for several years under Operation Chrome Dome, airborne alert — ready to launch retaliatory strikes against the Soviet Union.

History

SAC was established on 21 March 1946 under General George Kenney. Its most influential commander, General Curtis LeMay, took over in October 1948 and transformed it into a rigorously trained, highly disciplined force built around long-range bombers — the B-36 Peacemaker, B-47 Stratojet, B-52 Stratofortress and B-58 Hustler — supported by fleets of aerial refuelling tankers and bases around the world. From the late 1950s SAC also operated land-based intercontinental ballistic missiles: Atlas, Titan, Minuteman and Peacekeeper.

During the Cuban Missile Crisis of October 1962 SAC went to DEFCON 2, the only time the command reached that readiness level. SAC forces flew combat missions in the Korean and Vietnam wars (including the B-52 Arc Light strikes) and in the 1991 Gulf War. With the end of the Cold War, SAC stood down its alert forces in September 1991 and was inactivated on 1 June 1992; its bomber and reconnaissance assets passed largely to the new Air Combat Command, its missile and bomber mission to U.S. Strategic Command, and ultimately (2009) its nuclear strike mission to Air Force Global Strike Command.

Aircraft and missiles

Principal SAC aircraft included the B-29 and B-50 Superfortress, B-36, B-47, B-52, B-58, FB-111 and B-1B bombers; KC-97 and KC-135 tankers; and U-2 and SR-71 reconnaissance aircraft. Its missile force grew from early Atlas squadrons to a peak of more than 1,000 Minuteman ICBMs.
