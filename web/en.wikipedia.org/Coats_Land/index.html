Coats Land

Coats Land is a region of Antarctica lying along the eastern shore of the Weddell Sea, between roughly 20°W and 36°W. It forms part of East Antarctica and extends from the Stancomb-Wills Glacier area south-westward to the Riiser-Larsen Ice Shelf and the Luitpold Coast. The territory is claimed by the United Kingdom as part of the British Antarctic Territory, a claim held in abeyance under the Antarctic Treaty.

Discovery and naming

The coast was discovered in March 1904 by William Speirs Bruce, leader of the Scottish National Antarctic Expedition (1902–1904), aboard the ship Scotia. Bruce named the land in honour of the expedition's principal benefactors, the Paisley thread-manufacturing brothers James Coats Jr. and Sir Thomas Glen Coats, whose donations had financed the voyage. The expedition made the first landings and oceanographic surveys in the region and established the Orcadas station in the South Orkney Islands.

Exploration

In 1914–1917 Ernest Shackleton's Imperial Trans-Antarctic Expedition aimed to make its Weddell Sea base on this coast, but its ship Endurance was beset and crushed by ice off Coats Land before a landing could be made, leading to the famous survival story and rescue of 1916. The coastline was further charted by the Ronne Antarctic Research Expedition (1947–48) and by later British, Norwegian, and German expeditions operating from bases in and around the region.

Research stations

The British Halley Research Station, on the nearby Brunt Ice Shelf of the Caird Coast to the east, has operated since 1956 and is reached across the Coats Land frontier region; Germany's Neumayer stations sit on the Ekström Ice Shelf further along the coast toward Queen Maud Land. The inland plateau behind the coast rises to the Theron Mountains and the Shackleton Range, which mark the region's southern hinterland.

Geography

The coast is fringed by ice shelves and steep ice cliffs. Drainage from the East Antarctic Ice Sheet reaches the sea here through outlet glaciers, and the offshore waters of the Weddell Gyre are a major site of Antarctic Bottom Water formation, giving the region significance for oceanography and climate science.
