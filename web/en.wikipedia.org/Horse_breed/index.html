Horse breed

Horse breeds vary widely in size, build and temperament

A horse breed is a selectively bred population of domesticated horses, often with pedigrees recorded in a breed registry. A breed typically has a studbook maintained by a breed association, and individual horses are registered only if they meet defined standards of ancestry, conformation and type.

Origins

Systematic horse breeding developed over centuries as humans selected animals for riding, racing, draft work, warfare and show. Distinct populations emerged in particular regions — the Arabian of the Arabian Peninsula, the Thoroughbred of England, the Belgian and Clydesdale draft breeds of northern Europe — each shaped by local needs and deliberate selection.

Types

Beyond formal breeds, horses are grouped into types: hot-blooded light riding horses, heavier warmbloods used in sport, and cold-blooded draft horses bred for pulling power. Ponies are defined largely by height. Many modern sport breeds remain "open," admitting approved outside blood, while others, such as the Thoroughbred, keep closed studbooks.

Numbers

There are more than 300 recognised horse breeds worldwide. Breed conservation bodies track rare breeds whose numbers have fallen with the mechanization of farming and transport.
