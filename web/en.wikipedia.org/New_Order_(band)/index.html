New Order (band)

New Order are an English rock band formed in Salford in 1980 by Bernard Sumner, Peter Hook, and Stephen Morris, the remaining members of Joy Division following the death of lead singer Ian Curtis. Gillian Gilbert joined later in 1980. Blending post-punk with electronic dance music, they became one of the most influential bands of the 1980s.

Career

Their 1983 single "Blue Monday" became the best-selling 12-inch single of all time, its sequencer-driven sound defining the emerging synth-pop and dance-rock genres. Key albums include Power, Corruption & Lies (1983), Low-Life (1985), Brotherhood (1986), and Technique (1989). The band was central to Manchester's Factory Records and the Haçienda nightclub. Their 1990 single "World in Motion," recorded for England's World Cup campaign, reached number one in the UK.

Later years

After hiatuses in the 1990s, the band re-formed; bassist Peter Hook departed in 2007 amid acrimony. New Order released Music Complete in 2015 and were inducted into the UK Music Hall of Fame in 2005.
