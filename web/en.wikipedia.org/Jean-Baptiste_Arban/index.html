Jean-Baptiste Arban

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Joseph Jean-Baptiste Laurent Arban (28 February 1825 – 8 April 1889) was a French cornetist, conductor, composer, and pedagogue. He is best known as the author of the Grande méthode complète pour cornet à pistons et de saxhorn, published in 1864 and known in English as Arban's Complete Conservatory Method for Trumpet, which remains the most widely used method book for trumpet and cornet.

Life and career

Arban was born in Lyon, France. He studied trumpet with François Dauverné at the Paris Conservatoire and won first prize in 1845. He became a celebrated soloist on the cornet à pistons, performing throughout Europe, and was appointed professor of saxhorn at the École Militaire before returning to the Paris Conservatoire in 1869 as professor of cornet — a chair created for him.

The Arban method

The Grande méthode covers all aspects of brass playing: tone production, articulation, fingering exercises, scales, arpeggios, slurring, ornamentation, and a large set of studies, characteristic pieces, and airs with variations. Its most famous piece is Arban's set of variations on "The Carnival of Venice", a virtuoso showpiece that remains a staple of the brass repertoire. The method has been edited and expanded by figures including Edwin Franko Goldman and Claude Gordon, and trumpet players commonly refer to it simply as "the Arban book".

Compositions and conducting

Arban composed numerous fantasies and variations for cornet on operatic themes, popular in the 19th century. He also worked as a conductor, leading popular ballroom and promenade concerts in Paris, and conducted a notable performance of Berlioz's Symphonie funèbre et triomphale using the composer-instrument maker Adolphe Sax's brass instruments. He died in Paris in 1889.
