PEGI (Pan-European Game Information) is a European video game content rating system established to help European consumers make informed decisions about buying video games with the help of age recommendations and content descriptors. It was developed by the Interactive Software Federation of Europe (ISFE, now Video Games Europe) and came into use in April 2003, replacing many national rating systems with a single European system.

Age ratings

PEGI uses five age categories: 3, 7, 12, 16 and 18. The ratings indicate the minimum age for which the content is considered suitable; they do not indicate difficulty. Games rated 3 and 7 use green labels, 12 and 16 use orange labels, and 18 uses a red label. Portugal historically used slightly different lower categories (4 and 6), and Finland formerly used 11 and 15, before harmonising with the standard system.

Content descriptors

Alongside the age rating, games carry content descriptor icons indicating the presence of: violence; bad language; fear; gambling; sex; drugs; discrimination; and in-game purchases. The descriptors explain the main reasons for the age rating and appear on the back of game packaging and in digital storefronts.

Use and legal status

PEGI is used in more than 35 countries. In some, such as the United Kingdom, it has legal force: since 2012 PEGI has been the statutory rating system for video games in the UK, administered by the Video Standards Council, and selling an age-restricted game to an underage person is an offence. In other countries the system is advisory or co-regulatory. Online and mobile games may be rated through the International Age Rating Coalition (IARC), in which PEGI participates.
