Timur (9 April 1336 – 17/19 February 1405), historically known as Tamerlane, was a Turco-Mongol conqueror who founded the Timurid Empire in and around modern-day Afghanistan, Iran and Central Asia, becoming the first ruler of the Timurid dynasty. An undefeated commander, he is widely regarded as one of the greatest military leaders and tacticians in history, as well as one of the most brutal; his campaigns caused the deaths of millions of people.

Rise to power

Timur was born near Kesh in Transoxiana into the Barlas clan, a Mongol tribe that had become Turkified. A leg wound — the source of his name Timur-i-Leng, "Timur the Lame" — left him lame in his right leg and stiff in his right arm. By 1370 he had seized control of the western Chagatai Khanate and made Samarkand his capital, styling himself the restorer of the Mongol Empire of Genghis Khan and ruling nominally through a puppet khan of the Chagatai line.

Conquests

Over the following decades Timur conquered Persia, defeated the Golden Horde at the battles of Kunduzcha (1391) and the Terek River (1395), and sacked Delhi in 1398 after defeating the Delhi Sultanate at the Battle of Delhi, an event followed by a massacre of the city's inhabitants. In the west he crushed the Mamluks of Syria, taking Aleppo and Damascus, and in 1402 destroyed the Ottoman army at the Battle of Ankara, capturing Sultan Bayezid I, who died in captivity. He died at Otrar in February 1405 while marching to invade Ming China.

Legacy

Timur's campaigns were accompanied by deliberate massacres — pyramids of severed heads were raised at Isfahan, Baghdad and elsewhere — yet he also patronised scholars and artisans, deporting craftsmen to Samarkand, which he filled with monumental architecture such as the Bibi-Khanym Mosque and the Gur-e-Amir mausoleum where he is buried. His descendants included Ulugh Beg, the astronomer-king, and Babur, who founded the Mughal Empire in India. In 1941 Soviet anthropologists exhumed his body, confirming his lameness; legend holds that the exhumation unleashed a curse shortly before the German invasion of the USSR.
