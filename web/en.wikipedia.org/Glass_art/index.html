Glass art

Glass art refers to individual works of art made substantially or wholly of glass. These range from small jewellery and vessels to monumental sculpture and architectural installations. Artistic glassworking has ancient roots — in Egyptian and Roman glass, medieval stained glass, and Venetian (Murano) glassmaking — but "glass art" as a distinct fine-art category is largely a product of the 20th-century studio glass movement.

History

The studio glass movement began in 1962, when Harvey Littleton, a ceramics professor at the University of Wisconsin–Madison, held workshops demonstrating that glass could be melted in a small studio furnace using technology developed with Dominick Labino. Artists could now work with hot glass independently of factories. Dale Chihuly, who studied with Littleton, became the movement's most famous figure, known for large, brightly coloured installations and for founding the Pilchuck Glass School in Washington State in 1971.

Techniques

  • Glassblowing — inflating molten glass with a blowpipe;
  • Kiln-forming — fusing and slumping glass in a kiln;
  • Casting — pouring or packing glass into moulds (see glass casting);
  • Lampworking (flameworking) — shaping glass with a torch;
  • Stained glass and cold working — cutting, grinding, engraving, and assembling glass.

Major centres of glass art include Murano (Italy), the Czech Republic (Bohemian glass), Sweden's "Glass Kingdom", and the Pacific Northwest of the United States. Museums such as the Corning Museum of Glass and the Museum of Glass in Tacoma are dedicated to the medium.
