Inari, Finland

Inari (Finnish: Inari; Northern Sámi: Anár; Inari Sámi: Aanaar; Skolt Sámi: Aanar) is a municipality in Finnish Lapland. With an area of over 17,000 square kilometres, it is the largest municipality in Finland by land area, though its population is only about 7,000. The administrative centre is the village of Ivalo; the village of Inari itself, on the shore of Lake Inari, is the cultural centre of the Finnish Sámi.

Geography

Lake Inari (Inarijärvi), Finland's third-largest lake, covers over 1,000 km² and contains thousands of islands, including Hautuumaasaari, an ancient Sámi burial island. The municipality stretches to the Norwegian and Russian borders and includes parts of Lemmenjoki and Urho Kekkonen National Parks.

Sámi culture

Inari is the only Finnish municipality with four official languages: Finnish and the Northern Sámi, Inari Sámi, and Skolt Sámi languages. It hosts Sajos, the Sámi Cultural Centre and seat of the Sámi Parliament of Finland, and the Siida museum of Sámi culture and northern nature. Inari Sámi, spoken almost exclusively in this municipality, is a notable example of successful language revitalization.
