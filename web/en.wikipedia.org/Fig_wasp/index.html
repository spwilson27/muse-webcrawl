Fig wasp

FamilyAgaonidae
OrderHymenoptera
HostFicus species

Fig wasps are wasps of the family Agaonidae which pollinate fig trees (Ficus) in an obligate mutualism. Each species of fig is pollinated by one or a few specific wasp species, and the wasps can reproduce only inside the fig syconium.

Life cycle

A fertilised female wasp enters a receptive fig through the ostiole, losing her wings and antennae in the process. Inside, she pollinates the female flowers and lays eggs in some of them, then dies. Wingless males hatch first, mate with females, chew exit tunnels, and die; the females emerge carrying pollen and fly to another fig.

Mutualism

The relationship is a classic example of coevolution, estimated to be at least 60–90 million years old. Non-pollinating fig wasps also exploit the system as parasites or gall-makers.
