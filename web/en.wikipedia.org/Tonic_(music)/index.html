Tonic (music)

Tonic
DefinitionFirst scale degree of a diatonic scale; tonal centre of a key

In music theory, the tonic is the first scale degree of the diatonic scale and the tonal centre or final resolution tone of a key. A triad formed on the tonic is called the tonic chord; in harmonic analysis it is symbolised by the Roman numeral I in major keys and i in minor keys. The tonic is the most stable and restful pitch in a key, and melodies and chord progressions in tonal music characteristically depart from and return to it.

Role in tonal music

The tonic anchors the hierarchy of scale degrees: the dominant (fifth degree) creates tension that resolves to the tonic, while the subdominant (fourth degree) typically moves towards the dominant. A piece in a major or minor key usually begins and ends on the tonic chord, and confirmation of a key — for example in a perfect authentic cadence (V–I) — is defined by arrival at the tonic. Modulation is described in terms of new tonics: moving to the dominant key, the relative major or minor, and so on.

Tonicisation and related terms

A chord other than the tonic may be temporarily treated as a tonic — a process called tonicisation — often through a secondary dominant. The tonic should not be confused with the keynote of a scale (the same pitch considered as a note name), the final of a mode, or the root of an individual chord. In scale-degree nomenclature the degrees following the tonic are the supertonic, mediant, subdominant, dominant, submediant and leading tone (or subtonic).
