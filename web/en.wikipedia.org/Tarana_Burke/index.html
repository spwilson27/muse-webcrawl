Tarana Burke

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Tarana J. Burke (born September 12, 1973) is an American activist from New York City, who started the MeToo movement. In 2006, Burke began using the phrase "Me Too" on social media site Myspace to promote "empowerment through empathy" among women of colour who have experienced sexual abuse.

Early life and activism

Burke was born in the Bronx, New York City, and raised in a low-income, working-class family in a housing project there. She developed an interest in social justice as a teenager and worked with organisations addressing racial discrimination and housing inequality in Alabama, where she attended Alabama State University and later Auburn University at Montgomery. In 2003 she co-founded Just Be Inc., a non-profit supporting young girls of colour, under which the Me Too campaign was launched in 2006.

The Me Too movement

The phrase went viral in October 2017 when it was adopted as a hashtag following the Harvey Weinstein sexual abuse allegations, bringing Burke's decade of grassroots work to global attention. Time named Burke and other prominent activists "The Silence Breakers" as its Person of the Year for 2017. Burke has emphasised that the movement addresses systemic power imbalances and supports marginalised survivors, not only celebrity cases.

Burke published a memoir, Unbound: My Story of Liberation and the Birth of the Me Too Movement, in 2021. She serves as a senior director at Girls for Gender Equity in Brooklyn.
