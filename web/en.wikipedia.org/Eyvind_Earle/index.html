Eyvind Earle

Eyvind Earle (April 26, 1916 – July 20, 2000) was an American artist, author and illustrator, known principally for his background illustrations and stylistic direction of the Walt Disney animated film Sleeping Beauty (1959). His distinctive style, marked by stylized, almost Gothic landscapes with striking colour and simplified forms, influenced generations of animation artists.

Earle's stylized landscapes combined medieval and modernist influences

Early life and career

Earle was born in New York City and began painting seriously at a young age, holding his first one-man show in France at fourteen. In the 1930s he cycled across the United States, supporting himself by selling watercolours. He joined Walt Disney Studios in 1951, working on background painting for films including Peter Pan and Lady and the Tramp. Walt Disney gave him substantial artistic freedom on Sleeping Beauty, for which Earle painted the majority of the backgrounds and set the film's visual style, drawing on medieval tapestries, Persian miniatures, and early Renaissance art.

Later work

After leaving Disney in 1958, Earle produced fine art, serigraphs, and Christmas card designs — he designed more than 800 cards for the American Artists Group. His paintings are held in major museum collections. He was named a Disney Legend in 2015, posthumously.
