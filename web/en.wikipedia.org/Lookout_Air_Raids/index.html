The Lookout Air Raids were two bombing attacks carried out by Japan against the continental United States in September 1942, during the Second World War. A single floatplane launched from the submarine I-25 dropped incendiary bombs on forests near Brookings, Oregon, in the hope of starting devastating wildfires. They were the only aerial bombings of the mainland United States by a foreign power during the war.

The raids

On September 9, 1942, the Japanese submarine I-25 surfaced off the Oregon coast and catapulted a Yokosuka E14Y "Glen" floatplane piloted by Warrant Officer Nobuo Fujita, with observer Shoji Okuda. Fujita dropped two 76 kg incendiary bombs on Wheeler Ridge, near the Mount Emily lookout tower, intending to ignite the forests of the Pacific Northwest. Fire lookouts spotted the small fires, which were quickly extinguished thanks to damp conditions and prompt response.

Fujita flew a second mission on September 29, dropping bombs east of Port Orford; the fires from this raid were never conclusively located and caused no significant damage. The attacks were kept largely secret by American authorities to avoid panic.

Aftermath

Fujita survived the war (I-25 was sunk in 1943, but he was not aboard). In 1962, at the invitation of the town of Brookings, he visited the community he had bombed, presented his family's 400-year-old samurai sword as a gesture of reconciliation, and was made an honorary citizen. He returned several times, planted trees at the bomb site, and died in 1997; some of his ashes were placed at the site. The bomb sites are marked on the National Register of Historic Places.
