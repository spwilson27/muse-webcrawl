Dictionary

A dictionary is a listing of lexemes from the lexicon of one or more specific languages, often arranged alphabetically (or by consonantal root for Semitic languages or radical and stroke for logographic languages), which may include information on definitions, usage, etymologies, pronunciations, translation, etc. It is a lexicographical reference that shows inter-relationships among the data.

History

The earliest dictionaries were Akkadian word lists from the second millennium BC. In Europe, early glossaries gave way to works such as Samuel Johnson's A Dictionary of the English Language (1755) and Noah Webster's An American Dictionary of the English Language (1828). The Oxford English Dictionary, begun in the 19th century, established the historical principles of modern lexicography by recording word usage with dated quotations.

Types

Dictionaries may be monolingual or bilingual, general or specialised (for example medical or legal dictionaries), and descriptive — recording how words are actually used — or prescriptive. Learner's dictionaries add grammatical and example-sentence information. Many dictionaries are now published primarily in electronic form, as websites and apps.

Entries

A typical entry gives the headword, its pronunciation in the International Phonetic Alphabet, part of speech, numbered senses, usage examples and etymology. Arrangement and labelling conventions vary between publishers.
