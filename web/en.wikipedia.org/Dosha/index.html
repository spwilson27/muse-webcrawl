Dosha

The three doshas: vata, pitta, and kapha.

In Ayurveda, the traditional medicine of India, a dosha is one of three fundamental regulatory principles said to govern physiological and psychological processes in the body. The three doshas — vata, pitta, and kapha — are each composed of combinations of the five elements (earth, water, fire, air, and ether). Health is understood as a state in which the doshas are in their proper balance for an individual; illness follows when they become aggravated or depleted.

The three doshas

Vata

Composed of air and ether, vata governs movement, including breathing, circulation, nerve impulses, and elimination. It is associated with qualities such as dryness, lightness, and cold.

Pitta

Composed of fire and water, pitta governs digestion, metabolism, and transformation. It is associated with heat, sharpness, and intensity.

Kapha

Composed of earth and water, kapha provides structure, stability, lubrication, and endurance.

Prakriti and vikriti

Each person is said to be born with a unique constitutional proportion of the doshas (prakriti), while their current, possibly imbalanced state is called vikriti. Ayurvedic practitioners assess constitution through history-taking and pulse diagnosis and prescribe diet, herbs, daily routines, and treatments such as panchakarma to restore balance.

Criticism

Mainstream medicine regards dosha theory as a pre-scientific framework without empirical support; clinical evidence for Ayurvedic interventions is mixed, and some Ayurvedic preparations have been found to contain toxic heavy metals.
