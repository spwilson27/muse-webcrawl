Mammography

Mammography (also called mastography) is the process of using low-energy X-rays (usually about 30 kVp) to examine the human breast for diagnosis and screening. The goal of mammography is the early detection of breast cancer, typically through detection of characteristic masses, calcifications or architectural distortions.

Procedure and types

During the procedure the breast is compressed between two plates to spread the tissue, reduce motion and scatter, and lower the radiation dose needed. Screening mammography is performed on asymptomatic women, while diagnostic mammography investigates symptoms or abnormal screening findings. Digital mammography has largely replaced film-screen systems, and digital breast tomosynthesis ("3D mammography") acquires images at multiple angles to reduce the masking effect of overlapping tissue.

Benefits and harms

Organised screening programmes reduce breast-cancer mortality in the age groups studied, but screening also carries harms: false positives leading to recall and biopsy, radiation exposure, and overdiagnosis of cancers that would never have caused symptoms. Guidelines differ between countries and organisations over the ages and intervals at which screening should be offered. Results are commonly reported using standardised systems such as BI-RADS.
