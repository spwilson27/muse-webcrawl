Tacos al pastor

Tacos al pastor ("shepherd-style tacos") are a Mexican dish of marinated pork cooked on a vertical spit called a trompo, shaved off in thin slices onto corn tortillas and served with onion, coriander, salsa and a small piece of pineapple. They are the signature taco of Mexico City and among the most popular street foods in Mexico.

History

Lebanese immigrants arrived in Mexico in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, bringing shawarma-style spit-roasted lamb. Mexican cooks substituted pork, replaced pita with corn tortillas, and flavoured the meat with a dried-chile and achiote adobo, creating first the "tacos árabes" of Puebla and then, by the 1960s in Mexico City, tacos al pastor — so named because the technique recalled shepherds' spit roasting. The name trompo ("spinning top") refers to the shape of the stacked meat.

Preparation

Thin pork slices are marinated in a blend of guajillo and ancho chiles, achiote, vinegar and spices, stacked on the vertical spit and topped with an onion and a pineapple. As the outer layer crisps, the taquero shaves it with a long knife, flicking a sliver of roasted pineapple onto each taco. A splash of salsa, diced onion and coriander finish the dish.

Variations and recognition

In northern Mexico a cheese-filled variant is the gringa; chicken and even fish versions exist. In 2019 Taste Atlas ranked tacos al pastor as the best-rated food in the world, reflecting their global popularity. Compare tahini-based shawarma traditions from the dish's Levantine roots.
