The Fox Film Corporation (also known as Fox Studios) was an American independent film production studio formed by William Fox in 1915 by combining his earlier Greater New York Film Rental Company and Box Office Attractions Film Company. It became one of the major studios of the silent and early sound eras, famous for stars such as Theda Bara and Tom Mix, the director John Ford, and the Fox Movietone newsreels, which pioneered sound-on-film news coverage.

History

William Fox built the company from film exhibition and distribution into production, opening studios in Fort Lee, New Jersey, and later in Hollywood, where Fox Hills (later Century City) became its home lot. Fox was an early leader in the transition to sound with its Movietone system. The 1929 stock market crash and Fox's leveraged expansion led to his loss of control in 1930; a 1937 vault fire in New Jersey destroyed most of the studio's silent-era library.

Merger

In 1935, under president Sidney Kent, Fox Film merged with the independent Twentieth Century Pictures, headed by Joseph Schenck and Darryl F. Zanuck, to form the 20th Century Fox Film Corporation, one of the "Big Five" major studios of Hollywood's Golden Age.
