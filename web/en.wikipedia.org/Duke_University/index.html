Duke University is a private research university in Durham, North Carolina, United States. Founded by Methodists and Quakers in the present-day town of Trinity in 1838, the school moved to Durham in 1892. In 1924, tobacco and electric power industrialist James Buchanan Duke established The Duke Endowment and the institution changed its name to honour his deceased father, Washington Duke.

Campus and academics

Duke's campus spans over 8,600 acres, including the Gothic-style West Campus centred on Duke Chapel, the Georgian East Campus, and the adjacent Duke Forest. The university has ten schools and colleges, including Trinity College of Arts and Sciences, the Pratt School of Engineering, the Fuqua School of Business, the School of Law, the School of Medicine, and the Sanford School of Public Policy. Duke is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity" and is a member of the Association of American Universities. Its library system, medical center and research institutes, including the Nicholas School of the Environment and the Duke Lemur Center, are internationally known.

Admissions and rankings

Duke is among the most selective universities in the United States, with an undergraduate acceptance rate in the range of five to six percent in recent years, and is regularly ranked among the top ten national universities by U.S. News & World Report. Notable alumni include President Richard Nixon, Apple CEO Tim Cook, and numerous Rhodes Scholars and Nobel laureates.

Athletics

Duke's athletic teams, the Blue Devils, compete in the Atlantic Coast Conference. The men's basketball program, long led by coach Mike Krzyzewski ("Coach K"), has won five NCAA national championships and is famed for its rivalry with the University of North Carolina. Cameron Indoor Stadium is one of college basketball's most storied venues.
