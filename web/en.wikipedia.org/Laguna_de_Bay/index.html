Laguna de Bay (Filipino: Lawa ng Laguna) is the largest lake in the Philippines, located east of Metro Manila between the provinces of Laguna to the south and Rizal to the north. Its surface area of about 911 square kilometres makes it also one of the largest lakes in Southeast Asia.

Geography

The lake is roughly W-shaped, with two peninsulas jutting from the northern shore, and the large island of Talim in its middle. It drains westward through the Pasig River into Manila Bay. The lake was formed by volcanic and tectonic activity; its name derives from the town of Bay, Laguna, and the Spanish word laguna ("lagoon").

Ecology and use

Laguna de Bay is a major source of fish for the surrounding region, supporting extensive fish pen and fish cage aquaculture, and it provides irrigation, transport, and a potential source of drinking water for Metro Manila. It suffers from pollution, siltation, and eutrophication from industrial, agricultural, and domestic waste carried by its many tributary rivers.

Management

The Laguna Lake Development Authority (LLDA), created in 1966, oversees the lake's environmental management and development.
