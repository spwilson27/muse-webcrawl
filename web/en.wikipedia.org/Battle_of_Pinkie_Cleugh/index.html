Battle of Pinkie Cleugh

The River Esk near Musselburgh, where the Scots army was destroyed on 10 September 1547.

Date10 September 1547
LocationMusselburgh, East Lothian, Scotland
ResultDecisive English victory
CommandersEdward Seymour, Duke of Somerset vs. James Hamilton, Earl of Arran

The Battle of Pinkie Cleugh was fought on 10 September 1547 on the banks of the River Esk near Musselburgh, Scotland, and was the last great pitched battle between the kingdoms of England and Scotland. It formed the climax of the war known as the Rough Wooing, England's attempt to force the marriage of the infant Mary, Queen of Scots, to the young Edward VI.

Background

After the Scots repudiated the Treaty of Greenwich, Henry VIII and then the government of the boy king Edward VI pursued the marriage by force. In September 1547 the English Protector, Edward Seymour, Duke of Somerset, invaded Scotland with a large and modern army of some 17,000 men, supported by a fleet in the Firth of Forth.

The battle

The Scots, under the Regent Arran, held a strong position across the Esk but fatally abandoned it to advance against the English. Scottish pikemen, unsupported and exposed to combined fire from English artillery, ships' guns and archers, and to cavalry charges, were cut down in huge numbers as they crossed open ground and were driven back into the river. English pursuit turned defeat into massacre: Scottish losses are estimated at 6,000 to 15,000 killed — the day was long remembered in Scotland as "Black Saturday" — while English losses were only a few hundred.

Aftermath

Somerset occupied much of southern Scotland and planted garrisons, but he could not deliver the marriage. The Scots turned to France: in 1548 the young Mary was sent to France to marry the Dauphin, French troops landed in Scotland, and the English eventually withdrew. Pinkie thus won England a battle but lost it the strategic contest, binding Scotland more tightly to the Auld Alliance.
