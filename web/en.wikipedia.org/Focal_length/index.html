Focal length

The focal length of an optical system is a measure of how strongly the system converges or diverges light; it is the distance over which initially collimated rays are brought to a focus. For a thin lens in air, the focal length is the distance from the centre of the lens to its principal focal point. A short focal length bends light more strongly than a long one; the optical power of a lens, measured in dioptres, is the reciprocal of its focal length in metres.

Thin-lens equation

For a thin lens, the focal length f, object distance do, and image distance di satisfy 1/f = 1/do + 1/di. The focal length can be computed from the lensmaker's equation using the refractive index of the material and the radii of curvature of the two surfaces.

In photography

In photography, focal length determines the angle of view and magnification. Lenses are informally classed as wide-angle (short focal length), "normal" (roughly equal to the sensor diagonal — about 50 mm for full-frame 35 mm format), and telephoto (long focal length). The f-number of a lens is the ratio of its focal length to the diameter of its entrance pupil, and governs exposure and depth of field.

Mirrors and systems

A concave mirror's focal length is half its radius of curvature. For compound systems such as camera zooms and telescopes, an effective focal length is defined from the system's overall ray-bending behaviour, and zoom lenses vary it mechanically.
