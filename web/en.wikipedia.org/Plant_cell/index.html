Plant cells are the cells present in green plants, photosynthetic eukaryotes of the kingdom Plantae. Their distinctive features include primary cell walls containing cellulose, hemicelluloses and pectin; the presence of plastids with the capability to perform photosynthesis and store starch; a large central vacuole that regulates turgor pressure against the cell wall; and the absence of flagella and centrioles in mature flowering plant cells, except in the gametes of some groups.

Cell wall

The cell wall is a rigid layer surrounding the plasma membrane. It is composed mainly of cellulose microfibrils embedded in a matrix of hemicellulose and pectin. The wall provides structural support, determines cell shape, and allows the cell to develop turgor pressure. Adjacent plant cells are joined by plasmodesmata, microscopic channels that traverse the cell walls and enable transport and communication.

Chloroplasts and photosynthesis

Chloroplasts are a type of plastid that conduct photosynthesis, capturing light energy and converting it into chemical energy stored as sugars. They contain the green pigment chlorophyll and are believed to have originated through endosymbiosis of a cyanobacterium. A typical photosynthetic plant cell may contain 40 to 50 chloroplasts.

Central vacuole

The central vacuole can occupy up to 90 percent of the volume of a mature plant cell. It stores water, ions, pigments and waste products, and its pressure against the cell wall — turgor pressure — is the main determinant of the rigidity of plant tissues. When turgor is lost, the plant wilts.

Other organelles

Like other eukaryotic cells, plant cells contain a nucleus, mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, cytoskeleton and ribosomes. Unlike animal cells, they generally lack lysosomes and centrosomes with centrioles; the functions of lysosomes are largely performed by the vacuole.

Cell types

Plant cells differentiate into several types, including parenchyma (photosynthetic and storage cells), collenchyma (flexible support), sclerenchyma (rigid support, often dead at maturity), xylem vessels and tracheids (water conduction), and phloem sieve elements and companion cells (sugar transport).
