FieldTheoretical physics
Key figuresAbhay Ashtekar, Carlo Rovelli, Lee Smolin
Key conceptsSpin networks, spin foam, quantised area and volume

Loop quantum gravity (LQG) is a theory of quantum gravity that attempts to merge quantum mechanics and general relativity by quantising spacetime itself, without assuming any fixed background geometry. It arose in the 1980s from Abhay Ashtekar's reformulation of general relativity in terms of connection variables and was developed by Carlo Rovelli, Lee Smolin and others.

Core ideas

In LQG, the fundamental excitations of the gravitational field are one-dimensional "loops" organised into spin networks — graphs whose edges carry quantum numbers (spins). Geometric quantities such as the area of a surface and the volume of a region are predicted to have discrete spectra at the Planck scale: there is a smallest possible nonzero area of roughly the Planck length squared. The evolution of spin networks in time is described by spin foams.

Applications and status

Loop quantum cosmology applies the theory to the early universe, replacing the Big Bang singularity with a "Big Bounce". LQG is one of the two most studied approaches to quantum gravity, alongside string theory, but like its rival it has not yet produced confirmed experimental predictions.
