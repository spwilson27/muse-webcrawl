Wojciech H. Zurek

Born1951, Bielsko-Biała, Poland
FieldsQuantum physics, decoherence, cosmology

Wojciech Hubert Zurek (born 1951) is a Polish-American theoretical physicist and a leading authority on quantum theory, especially the emergence of classical behaviour from quantum systems. He is a Laboratory Fellow at Los Alamos National Laboratory and an external faculty member of the Santa Fe Institute.

Research

Zurek is a principal architect of the theory of decoherence, in which interaction with the environment selects preferred "pointer states" of a quantum system — an effect he named einselection (environment-induced superselection) in work from the early 1980s. He later developed quantum Darwinism, explaining objectivity by the proliferation of redundant information about pointer states in the environment. With Tom Kibble, he is associated with the Kibble–Zurek mechanism, which predicts the density of defects formed when a system crosses a phase transition at finite speed and has been tested in systems from liquid crystals to ultracold gases.

Career and honours

Zurek studied at the AGH University of Science and Technology in Kraków, receiving his PhD in 1979, and later worked at Caltech before joining Los Alamos. He has received honours including the Marian Smoluchowski Medal and has served as a visiting professor at institutions such as the University of Oxford.
