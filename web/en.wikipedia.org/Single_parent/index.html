Single parent

A single parent is a person who lives with a child or children and who does not have a spouse or live-in partner to assist in the upbringing or support of his or her children. Reasons for becoming a single parent include divorce, break-up, abandonment, the death of a partner, single-person adoption, or an unplanned pregnancy.

Historically, death of a partner was the major cause of single parenting. Today, divorce, separation and births outside marriage account for most single-parent households in developed countries. The large majority of single parents are mothers, although the proportion of single-father households has been rising.

Demographics

Single-parent families are a substantial minority of households with children in most Western countries. In the United States, roughly a quarter of children live with a single parent. Rates vary widely internationally, and single parenthood is strongly associated with economic disadvantage: single-parent households have higher poverty rates than two-parent households in most countries, which has made them a focus of social policy, child support enforcement and welfare programmes.

Effects and research

Research on outcomes for children in single-parent families is extensive and often contested. Studies generally find that apparent effects are strongly mediated by income, parental conflict, stability of care and social support rather than by family structure alone.
