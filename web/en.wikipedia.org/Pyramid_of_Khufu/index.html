Pyramid of Khufu

The Pyramid of Khufu, also known as the Great Pyramid of Giza, is the largest of the Egyptian pyramids and the tomb of the Fourth Dynasty pharaoh Khufu. Built over a period of roughly 20 to 30 years in the 26th century BC, it is the oldest of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World and the only one to remain largely intact.

Pyramid of Khufu
Location: Giza, Egypt
Built: c. 2580–2560 BC
Original height: 146.6 m (481 ft)
Base: 230.3 m (756 ft)
Material: Limestone, granite

Construction

The pyramid originally stood 146.6 metres tall and was covered by smooth white Tura limestone casing stones, most of which have since been removed. Its base covers about 5.3 hectares and its mass is estimated at roughly 5.75 million tonnes, comprising some 2.3 million stone blocks. Scholars believe a large paid workforce of skilled Egyptian labourers, rather than slaves, quarried, transported, and set the stones, working in rotating crews.

Interior

Inside are three main chambers: the unfinished subterranean chamber cut into the bedrock, the Queen's Chamber, and the King's Chamber high within the structure, reached by the ascending Grand Gallery. The King's Chamber contains a granite sarcophagus thought to be Khufu's. Narrow shafts extend from the chambers; their purpose remains debated.
