Waikato River

The Waikato River is the longest river in New Zealand, running 425 kilometres (264 mi) through the Waikato region of the North Island. It rises on the eastern slopes of Mount Ruapehu, flows through Lake Taupō, and continues northwest past Hamilton to enter the Tasman Sea at Port Waikato, south of Auckland.

Course and features

Below Taupō the river passes the Huka Falls and a chain of hydroelectric lakes — including Aratiatia, Ohakuri, Atiamuri, Maraetai, and Karapiro — whose eight power stations supply a significant share of New Zealand's electricity. It also provides cooling water for the coal/gas-fired Huntly Power Station and drinking water for Auckland. Near Taupiri the Waipa River, its largest tributary, joins from the south.

Significance

The river holds deep spiritual and cultural importance for Māori, particularly the Waikato-Tainui iwi, for whom it is a revered ancestor (awa tupuna). Its name means "flowing water". The Waikato was a principal theatre of the New Zealand Wars in 1863–64. Co-governance arrangements under the Waikato-Tainui Raupatu Claims (Waikato River) Settlement Act 2010 aim to restore the river's health.
