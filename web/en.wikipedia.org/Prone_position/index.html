Prone position

The prone position is a body position in which a person lies flat with their chest down and their back up. In anatomical terms, the opposite of prone is supine. Prone positioning is used in medicine, surgery, infant care guidance, marksmanship, and various sports and exercises.

OrientationFace down, chest down, back up
OppositeSupine position
Medical usesARDS ventilation, spinal surgery, oxygenation

Medical use

Placing patients with acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) in the prone position during mechanical ventilation ("proning") improves oxygenation by recruiting collapsed lung regions, improving ventilation–perfusion matching, and making lung inflation more uniform. The PROSEVA trial (2013) showed that early, prolonged prone positioning significantly reduced mortality in severe ARDS. Prone positioning gained widespread public attention during the COVID-19 pandemic, when awake proning was also used in non-intubated patients. The position is also standard for many spinal and posterior surgical procedures.

Infants

Infants sleeping in the prone position have a significantly increased risk of sudden infant death syndrome (SIDS), which is why health authorities recommend that babies sleep on their backs. Supervised awake "tummy time" is nonetheless recommended for motor development.

Other uses

Prone is one of the standard shooting positions in marksmanship and is generally the most stable. In swimming, the front crawl and breaststroke are swum in a prone posture, and prone exercises are used in physical therapy and fitness training.
