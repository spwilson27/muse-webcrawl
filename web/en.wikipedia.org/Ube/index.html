Ube

Ube (Dioscorea alata)
KingdomPlantae
FamilyDioscoreaceae
GenusDioscorea
SpeciesD. alata
OriginSoutheast Asia / Maritime Southeast Asia

Ube (Tagalog: ubi) is the Philippine name for Dioscorea alata, a species of yam commonly called the purple yam, greater yam or water yam. The tuber is notable for its vivid violet-purple flesh, and it is a staple ingredient in Filipino cuisine, especially in desserts.

Dioscorea alata is one of the oldest cultivated yam species, originating in Southeast Asia and spread widely through Austronesian voyaging across the Pacific and to Madagascar and East Africa. Cultivars vary: some have deep purple flesh, others are lavender or almost white.

Culinary use

In the Philippines, ube is most often boiled and mashed with milk, sugar and butter to make ube halaya (purple yam jam), which is eaten on its own or used as a filling and topping. Ube features in many well-known Filipino desserts and snacks, including:

  • Halo-halo, the shaved-ice dessert, where ube halaya and ube ice cream provide its signature purple component;
  • Ube ice cream, a popular flavour in the Philippines and abroad;
  • Ube pandesal, cakes, cheesecakes, crinkles and other pastries, which surged in popularity in the 2010s;
  • Ginataan and other coconut-milk-based sweets.

The intense natural colour of ube made it a fashionable flavour and natural colouring in international food trends in the 2010s and 2020s, appearing in doughnuts, lattes and soft-serve ice cream outside the Philippines.

Confusion with other purple tubers

Ube is sometimes confused with the purple sweet potato (a cultivar of Ipomoea batatas, such as the Okinawan sweet potato) and with taro, which is pale lavender-grey when cooked. The three are botanically unrelated and differ in taste and texture: ube has a moist, slightly nutty and vanilla-like flavour compared with the drier sweet potato.

Cultivation

Dioscorea alata is a vigorous climbing vine with winged stems ("alata" means "winged"). It produces underground tubers that can grow very large, and in some regions also aerial bulbils. It is propagated vegetatively from tuber pieces, and is an important food crop across the tropics, though in some places it has become an invasive species, notably in Florida.
