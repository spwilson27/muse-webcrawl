The Sultanate of Yogyakarta (Javanese: Kasultanan Ngayogyakarta Hadiningrat) is a Javanese monarchy in the Special Region of Yogyakarta, Indonesia. Founded in 1755, it is one of the few Indonesian royal houses whose ruler still holds formal governmental office, serving ex officio as governor of the special region.

History

The sultanate arose from the Treaty of Giyanti (13 February 1755), which divided the Mataram Sultanate between Pakubuwono III of Surakarta and Prince Mangkubumi, who took the title Hamengkubuwono I and founded Yogyakarta. Under Dutch colonial rule the sultanate retained internal autonomy as a self-governing territory. During the Indonesian National Revolution, Sultan Hamengkubuwono IX supported the republic, and Yogyakarta served as the revolutionary capital from 1946 to 1949; in gratitude, the region received special autonomous status after independence.

The Kraton and court culture

The sultan's palace, the Kraton, lies at the heart of the city on a cosmological axis running from Mount Merapi to the Indian Ocean. The court remains a centre of classical Javanese arts, including gamelan music, wayang kulit shadow puppetry, court dance, and batik. Ceremonies such as the Garebeg festivals and the Sekaten celebration continue to be held.

Modern role

Under a 2012 law, the reigning sultan automatically serves as governor of the Special Region of Yogyakarta and the head of the Pakualaman principality as vice-governor, a unique arrangement in republican Indonesia. Hamengkubuwono X has reigned since 1989.
