Kevin Andrew Lynch (January 7, 1918 – April 25, 1984) was an American urban planner and author. He is known for his contributions to the field of urban planning, most importantly his 1960 book The Image of the City, a landmark in the study of how people perceive and organise their mental images of urban environments.

The Image of the City

Based on studies of Boston, Jersey City and Los Angeles, Lynch argued that people form mental maps of cities using five elements: paths (routes of movement), edges (boundaries), districts (recognisable areas), nodes (strategic points or junctions), and landmarks (external reference points). Cities that are rich in these elements possess "imageability" and are easier to navigate and remember. The work has been influential in urban design, architecture and wayfinding.

Later work

Lynch taught at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology for over thirty years. His later books include What Time is This Place? (1972), on change and historical preservation in cities, and Wasting Away (1990, posthumous), on waste and society.
