Sir Steven Geoffrey Redgrave (born 1962) is a retired British rower who won gold medals at five consecutive Olympic Games, from 1984 to 2000 — a record for an endurance athlete in British sport.

Career

Born in Marlow, Redgrave won his first Olympic title in the coxed four at Los Angeles in 1984, then successive golds in the coxless pair with Andy Holmes and later Matthew Pinsent (1988, 1992, 1996), before triumphing in the coxless four at Sydney 2000. He also won nine World Championship golds. Diagnosed with diabetes in 1997 and having battled colitis, he famously said after Atlanta that anyone who saw him in a boat again could shoot him — then returned to win once more.

Honours

Redgrave was knighted in 2001 and carried the Olympic flame into the stadium at the London 2012 opening ceremony. He is widely regarded as one of Britain's greatest Olympians.
