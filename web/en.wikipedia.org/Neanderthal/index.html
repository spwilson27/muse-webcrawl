Neanderthals ( Homo neanderthalensis) are an extinct group of archaic humans who inhabited Europe and Asian regions up to the Altai Mountains from roughly 430,000 to 40,000 years ago. They were adapted to the cold climates of Ice Age Eurasia, with stocky, robust bodies, large noses, prominent brow ridges, and brains as large as or larger than those of modern humans. Neanderthals made and used sophisticated Mousterian stone tools, controlled fire, built shelters, buried their dead, cared for injured group members, and produced some of the earliest known cave art and personal ornaments.

The type specimen, Neanderthal 1, was found in a limestone quarry in the Neander Valley near Düsseldorf in 1856 — three years before On the Origin of Species — and was recognised as evidence of an extinct human population, a landmark in the founding of palaeoanthropology.

Extinction and interbreeding

Neanderthals disappear from the fossil record around 40,000 years ago, some millennia after anatomically modern humans entered Europe. Causes proposed include competitive exclusion, assimilation, climate instability, and small, inbred populations. Sequencing of the Neanderthal genome (draft published 2010, led by Svante Pääbo's group) demonstrated that Neanderthals interbred with the ancestors of present-day non-Africans: about 1–4% of non-African genomes derive from Neanderthals, affecting traits including immunity, skin, and hair. Neanderthals also interbred with the Denisovans, their Siberian sister group — one fossil individual had a Neanderthal mother and a Denisovan father.

Culture

Evidence increasingly portrays Neanderthals as cognitively sophisticated: they hafted tools with birch-tar glue, made string and simple clothing, used pigments and eagle-talon ornaments, hunted large game cooperatively, and at Bruniquel Cave in France built ring-like structures of broken stalagmites deep underground some 176,000 years ago. Uranium-thorium dating of Spanish cave paintings to more than 64,000 years ago, before modern humans reached Iberia, has been attributed to Neanderthal artists, though this remains debated.
