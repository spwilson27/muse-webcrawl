Australian House of Representatives

House of Representatives
Type: Lower house of the Parliament of Australia
Seats: 151
Meeting place: Parliament House, Canberra

The Australian House of Representatives is the lower house of the bicameral Parliament of Australia, the upper house being the Senate. Its composition and powers are established under Chapter I of the Constitution of Australia.

Composition

The House is composed of 151 members, each elected by a system of preferential (instant-runoff) voting to represent a single-member electoral division for a term of up to three years. The number of members is constitutionally linked to the size of the Senate: the House must be "as nearly as practicable" twice the size of the Senate.

Role

The government of Australia is formed in the House of Representatives: the leader of the party or coalition able to command a majority becomes Prime Minister. Money bills must originate in the House. The leader of the House's largest non-government party is the Leader of the Opposition. The presiding officer is the Speaker.
