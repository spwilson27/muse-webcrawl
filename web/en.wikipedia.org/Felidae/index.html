Felidae

Felidae is the family of mammals in the order Carnivora colloquially referred to as cats. A member of this family is also called a felid. It includes the subfamilies Pantherinae (the "big cats"—lions, tigers, leopards, jaguars and snow leopards) and Felinae (including the cheetah, cougar, lynxes, ocelot and the domestic cat). The Felidae appeared during the Oligocene, about 25–30 million years ago.

Characteristics

Felids are obligate carnivores with digitigrade posture, retractile claws (except in the cheetah), forward-facing eyes providing binocular vision, rounded heads and short muzzles, and sensitive vibrissae (whiskers). Most are solitary ambush hunters with excellent night vision and hearing. Body size ranges from the rusty-spotted cat (~1 kg) to the Siberian tiger (over 300 kg).

Evolution

The family descends from the Oligocene Proailurus, the first true felid. The sabre-toothed cats (Machairodontinae), including Smilodon, were an extinct subfamily that died out at the end of the Pleistocene.
