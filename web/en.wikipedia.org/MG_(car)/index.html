MG (car)

Founded1920s, Oxford, England
FounderCecil Kimber (general manager)
Headquarters (historic)Abingdon, Oxfordshire
ProductsSports cars, saloons

MG is a British automotive marque founded in Oxford in the 1920s, famous for its open two-seater sports cars and recognised by its octagonal badge. The letters stand for Morris Garages, the Oxford dealer and workshop from which the marque grew under its general manager Cecil Kimber, who began building sporting versions of Morris cars.

History

Early MGs such as the 14/28 (1924) established the formula of affordable sporting motoring. In the 1930s the Midget, Magnette, and record-breaking EX-series cars brought competition success at Brooklands and in trials. After the Second World War the TC Midget introduced American drivers to the British sports car, launching a huge export trade. Through BMC, British Leyland, and later the Rover Group, MG produced the MGA (1955), the hugely successful MGB (1962–1980), the mid-engined MGF (1995), and badge-engineered sporting versions of family saloons. The Abingdon factory closed in 1980.

Revival

After the collapse of MG Rover in 2005, the marque was acquired by China's Nanjing Automobile and then SAIC Motor, which relaunched MG as a volume brand — today's MG Motor — selling cars such as the MG3, ZS crossover, and the electric MG4 in Britain and many export markets, while the octagon badge retains its sporting heritage.
