Wh&

Wh& is a short-form title fragment that may occur in automatically generated link lists, abbreviated references, or truncated citations of longer titles beginning with the letters "Wh" followed by an ampersand. In cataloguing and web indexing, such fragments typically arise when a title is cut short or when a query string is split at the "&" character, which in URLs and HTML introduces a parameter or an entity reference.

In URLs and markup

The ampersand is a reserved character in URLs, where it separates query parameters, and in HTML, where it begins a character reference such as &amp;. Titles containing "&" therefore frequently appear truncated in indexes, feeds, and link aggregations at exactly this point. Writers and editors generally expand such fragments to the full title of the referenced work when citing them.

Usage

Because "Wh&" conveys no complete subject on its own, it is treated as a navigational or clerical artifact rather than a topic in its own right. Related subjects include the ampersand itself, a logogram descended from the Latin ligature of e and t ("et", meaning "and"), and the family of English interrogative and relative words beginning in wh- (who, what, when, where, why, which), known in linguistics as wh-words.
