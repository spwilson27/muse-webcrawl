Sacca-vibhanga Sutta

The Sacca-vibhanga Sutta (Pali: Saccavibhaṅga Sutta, "The Analysis of the Truths") is a discourse of the Buddha recorded in the Pali Canon, numbered as Majjhima Nikaya 141 (MN 141). In this sutta, the Buddha presents the Four Noble Truths and then, rather than expounding them himself in full, invites his chief disciple Sariputta to give the detailed analysis (vibhanga). The discourse is closely related to the Buddha's first sermon, the Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta, and is regarded as an authoritative elaboration of the Four Noble Truths and the Noble Eightfold Path.

Setting and summary

According to the text, the sutta was delivered at the deer park of Isipatana near Varanasi, where the Buddha had given his first sermon. The Buddha begins by announcing that he had set in motion the "Dhamma Wheel" and that he would now analyse the truths in summary, while Sariputta would explain them in detail. He then recites the four truths in their standard formula:

  • The noble truth of suffering (dukkha)
  • The noble truth of the origin of suffering (samudaya)
  • The noble truth of the cessation of suffering (nirodha)
  • The noble truth of the path leading to the cessation of suffering (magga)

At the Buddha's indication, Mahamoggallana points the assembly to Sariputta, who then delivers the extended analysis. Sariputta defines suffering as birth, ageing, sickness, death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, grief and despair, association with the disliked, separation from the liked, and not getting what one wants — in short, the five aggregates subject to clinging. The origin of suffering is identified as craving (tanha): craving for sensual pleasures, craving for existence, and craving for non-existence. Cessation is the complete fading away of that craving, and the path is the Noble Eightfold Path: right view, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, and right concentration.

Analysis of the path factors

Sariputta's exposition defines each factor of the Noble Eightfold Path in terms closely paralleling the Magga-vibhanga Sutta (SN 45.8). Right view, for instance, is knowledge of suffering, its origin, its cessation, and the path. Right concentration is identified with the four jhanas. This makes MN 141 an important compendium text in Theravada Buddhism, frequently cited in commentarial literature such as the Visuddhimagga of Buddhaghosa.

Parallels and significance

A parallel version of the discourse survives in the Chinese Agamas (Madhyama Agama 202), indicating the text's early date and importance across Buddhist traditions. Within Theravada practice, the sutta is studied as a bridge between the Buddha's first sermon and the systematic analysis of the path found in later Abhidhamma literature.
