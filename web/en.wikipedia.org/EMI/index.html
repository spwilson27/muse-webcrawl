EMI

EMI Group Limited (originally Electric and Musical Industries, also known as EMI Music or simply EMI) was a British transnational conglomerate founded in London in March 1931 from the merger of the Gramophone Company and the Columbia Graphophone Company. For much of the twentieth century it was one of the world's leading record companies — one of the "Big Four" major labels — and also a major manufacturer of electronics, including pioneering work in radar and the first commercially successful CT scanner.

History

EMI's music business encompassed labels including His Master's Voice (HMV), Parlophone, Capitol Records and Virgin Records, and its Abbey Road Studios in London became famous as the recording home of the Beatles, who were signed to Parlophone. The company's roster over the decades included Pink Floyd, Queen, Radiohead, Coldplay and many other major acts. EMI's electronics division, led by figures such as Alan Blumlein (inventor of stereo recording) and Godfrey Hounsfield (developer of the CT scanner, for which he shared the 1979 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine), was gradually divested from the 1970s onward.

Break-up and sale

After being taken over by private equity firm Terra Firma in 2007, EMI struggled with heavy debt. In 2011–2012 the group was broken up: its recorded music division was sold to Universal Music Group, and its music publishing division to a consortium led by Sony/ATV. The Parlophone Label Group was subsequently sold to Warner Music Group in 2013 as part of regulatory remedies. The EMI name survives as a brand within Universal, relaunched as a label imprint in 2020.
