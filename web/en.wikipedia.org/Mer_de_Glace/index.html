Mer de Glace

The Mer de Glace ("Sea of Ice") is a valley glacier located on the northern slopes of the Mont Blanc massif, in the French Alps. It is 7.5 km (4.7 mi) long and 200 m (660 ft) deep, and when all of its tributary glaciers are taken into account it forms the largest glacier in France, and the second largest in the Alps after the Aletsch Glacier.

Geography

The glacier lies above the Chamonix valley. It originates at an elevation of 2,400 m, fed by the confluence of the Glacier du Géant, Glacier de Lechaud and Cascade du Talèfre, and flows north-northwest between the Aiguille du Midi area and the Aiguilles de Chamonix. The Montenvers rack railway climbs from Chamonix to a viewing station above the glacier.

History and retreat

In the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries the glacier descended much lower into the valley, and it was described by early tourists such as William Windham and Richard Pococke, who visited in 1741 and publicized the "glaciers of Savoy". Like most Alpine glaciers it has retreated dramatically: the ice surface has lowered by more than 200 m at Montenvers since 1800. A grotto is carved into the ice each year, and a gondola and hundreds of steps descend to it — the growing number of steps is a famous illustration of the retreat.
