Fifth Avenue is a major thoroughfare in the borough of Manhattan in New York City. It stretches north from Washington Square Park in Greenwich Village to West 143rd Street in Harlem, and the section between 34th and 59th Streets is one of the most expensive and prestigious shopping streets in the world.

Character

Fifth Avenue divides Manhattan's streets into east and west designations and serves as the origin for the borough's address numbering. Its Midtown stretch hosts flagship stores — Saks Fifth Avenue, Tiffany & Co., Bergdorf Goodman — and landmarks including the Empire State Building, the New York Public Library Main Branch, St. Patrick's Cathedral, and Rockefeller Center. North of 59th Street, the avenue borders Central Park and the "Museum Mile" of the Upper East Side, lined by the Metropolitan Museum of Art, the Frick Collection, and the Guggenheim Museum.

History

Laid out in the Commissioners' Plan of 1811, Fifth Avenue became in the late 19th century the address of choice for Gilded Age mansions of families such as the Vanderbilts and Astors, most of which were later replaced by apartment houses and clubs. Through the 20th century its lower section commercialised while its upper reaches remained residential and institutional.

Parades

The avenue is the route of major New York parades, including the St. Patrick's Day Parade and the Puerto Rican Day Parade, and is closed to traffic for the Easter Parade tradition on Easter Sunday.
