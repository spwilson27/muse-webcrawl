Cassini–Huygens, commonly called Cassini, was a space-research mission by NASA, the European Space Agency (ESA), and the Italian Space Agency (ASI) to send a space probe to study the planet Saturn and its system, including its rings and natural satellites. The mission consisted of the Cassini orbiter and the Huygens lander, which touched down on Saturn's moon Titan.

Mission

Launched aboard a Titan IVB/Centaur rocket in 1997, Cassini traveled for nearly seven years, using gravity assists from Venus, Earth, and Jupiter before entering orbit around Saturn on 1 July 2004. The Huygens probe separated on 25 December 2004 and landed on Titan on 14 January 2005, the most distant landing ever made.

Discoveries

Cassini discovered water-ice plumes erupting from the south pole of Enceladus, evidence of a subsurface ocean; lakes and seas of liquid methane on Titan; new moons and ring structures; and a giant hexagonal storm at Saturn's north pole.

Grand Finale

After nearly 13 years in orbit, Cassini ended its mission on 15 September 2017 with a deliberate plunge into Saturn's atmosphere, ensuring its moons remained uncontaminated.
