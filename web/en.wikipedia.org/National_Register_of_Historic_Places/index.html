The National Register of Historic Places (NRHP) is the United States federal government's official list of districts, sites, buildings, structures and objects deemed worthy of preservation for their historical significance. It was established by the National Historic Preservation Act of 1966 and is administered by the National Park Service.

Criteria and process

Properties are typically at least 50 years old and are evaluated against criteria of association with significant events or persons, distinctive design or construction, or potential to yield important information. Nominations are usually prepared by State Historic Preservation Offices and reviewed by the Keeper of the National Register. Listing is largely honorary for private owners — it restricts only federal undertakings — but qualifies properties for tax incentives and grants, and carries prestige. More than 98,000 individual listings contribute some 1.8 million resources, including contributing buildings within historic districts.

History

The register grew out of the preservation movement's reaction to urban renewal demolitions of the 1950s and 1960s. The 1966 Act also created the Advisory Council on Historic Preservation and the Section 106 review process for federal projects affecting historic properties.
