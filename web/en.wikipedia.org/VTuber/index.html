VTuber

A VTuber (Japanese: ブイチューバー, buichūbā), or virtual YouTuber, is an online entertainer who uses a virtual avatar generated using computer graphics and motion capture technology. VTubers are often livestreamers who play video games, sing, chat with audiences, or produce other entertainment content, typically presenting an anime-influenced character persona.

History

The trend began in Japan in the mid-2010s. Kizuna AI, who debuted on YouTube in late 2016 and coined the term "virtual YouTuber", is widely credited with popularising the format, reaching millions of subscribers. A boom followed in 2017–2018 as the necessary face-tracking software became accessible to independent creators, and talent agencies such as Hololive Production and Nijisanji began recruiting and managing groups of VTubers.

Technology

Most VTubers use real-time motion capture to animate a 2D model (commonly made with Live2D) or a 3D model. A webcam or dedicated tracking software maps the performer's facial expressions and movements onto the avatar, while the performer's real face and identity generally remain hidden. Voice changing is sometimes used to match the character.

Industry and culture

Major agencies operate branches in Japan and internationally, and their performers attract large audiences on YouTube, Twitch, and other platforms. VTubers participate in concerts, merchandise sales, sponsorships, and collaborations with game developers and anime franchises. The phenomenon has been credited with renewing interest in avatar-based performance and in the Japanese idol industry, and it spread globally during the COVID-19 pandemic, when streaming viewership grew rapidly.
