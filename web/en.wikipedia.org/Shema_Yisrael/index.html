Shema Yisrael

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: a mezuzah parchment with Hebrew text]

Shema Yisrael (or Sh'ma Yisrael; Hebrew: שְׁמַע יִשְׂרָאֵל, "Hear, O Israel") is a Jewish prayer. It serves as a centerpiece of the morning and evening Jewish prayer services. Its first verse encapsulates the monotheistic essence of Judaism: "Hear, O Israel: the LORD is our God, the LORD is one" (Deuteronomy 6:4).

The first, pivotal words of the Shema are traditionally considered the most important part of the prayer, and it is customary for Jews to recite them as their last words. Covering the eyes while reciting the first verse is a common practice, intended to increase concentration.

Content

The Shema consists of three scriptural passages: Deuteronomy 6:4–9, Deuteronomy 11:13–21, and Numbers 15:37–41. Among other things, the passages command the teaching of Torah to one's children, the recitation of the words upon lying down and rising up, and the binding of tefillin and affixing of mezuzot.

The opening verse appears in the Torah and is followed by the line "Blessed be the name of His glorious kingdom for ever and ever," traditionally whispered aloud. The prayer is also inscribed on the parchment of the mezuzah attached to doorposts.

History

Recitation of the Shema was an element of the Temple service in Jerusalem and was instituted in communal prayer from ancient times, predating the destruction of the Second Temple in 70 CE. It remains, alongside the Amidah, one of the two central components of the Jewish liturgy.
