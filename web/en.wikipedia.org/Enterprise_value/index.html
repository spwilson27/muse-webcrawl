Enterprise value

Enterprise value (EV) is an economic measure of the total value of a company, used as a more comprehensive alternative to market capitalization. It represents the theoretical takeover price of a business: what an acquirer would effectively pay, taking into account the company's debt and cash as well as its equity.

Calculation

A common formulation is:

EV = market capitalization + total debt + minority interest + preferred shares − cash and cash equivalents

Debt is added because an acquirer assumes the company's obligations, while cash is subtracted because the acquirer gains the cash and could use it to pay down the purchase price. Variants also include pension deficits, lease liabilities, and other debt-like items, and exclude non-operating assets.

Use in valuation

Enterprise value is the numerator in widely used valuation multiples such as EV/EBITDA and EV/sales, which allow comparisons between companies with different capital structures and tax regimes, since EV is capital-structure neutral whereas equity value alone is not. A negative enterprise value can occur when a company's cash exceeds its market capitalization plus debt, sometimes interpreted as the market valuing the operating business at less than nothing.
