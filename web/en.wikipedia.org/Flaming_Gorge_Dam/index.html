Flaming Gorge Dam

Flaming Gorge Dam is a concrete thin-arch dam on the Green River in northeastern Utah, United States. Completed in 1964, the dam impounds Flaming Gorge Reservoir, which extends into Wyoming, and forms the centrepiece of the Flaming Gorge National Recreation Area. It is operated by the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation as part of the Colorado River Storage Project.

Construction

Authorised under the Colorado River Storage Project Act of 1956, construction began in 1958. The dam rises 502 feet (153 m) above bedrock and has a crest length of 1,285 feet (392 m). Its powerplant contains three generators with a combined capacity of about 152 megawatts. The reservoir, named for the brilliant red-orange canyon walls described by explorer John Wesley Powell, covers roughly 42,000 acres at full pool.

Purposes

The dam provides long-term storage of Colorado River water for use in the Upper Basin states, hydroelectric power generation, flood control and recreation. Releases from the dam are also managed to benefit endangered fish species in the Green River downstream.
