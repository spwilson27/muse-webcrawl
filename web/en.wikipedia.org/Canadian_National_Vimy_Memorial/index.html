Canadian National Vimy Memorial

The Canadian National Vimy Memorial is a war memorial near Vimy in the Pas-de-Calais region of northern France, dedicated to the memory of Canadian Expeditionary Force members killed during the First World War. It also serves as the place of commemoration for Canadian soldiers of the First World War who were killed or presumed dead in France and have no known grave.

The battle

The memorial overlooks the battlefield of the Battle of Vimy Ridge (9–12 April 1917), in which all four divisions of the Canadian Corps attacked together for the first time and captured the ridge from the German army, at a cost of more than 10,000 Canadian casualties, including 3,598 killed.

Design and unveiling

Designed by Toronto sculptor and architect Walter Seymour Allward, the memorial was built between 1925 and 1936 on land granted by France to Canada in perpetuity. Its twin white limestone pylons rise 30 metres above the base, and the structure carries 20 allegorical figures and the names of 11,285 Canadians with no known grave. King Edward VIII unveiled the memorial on 26 July 1936 before a crowd of over 100,000 people, including some 6,000 Canadian veterans who had made the "Vimy Pilgrimage".

Restoration

The memorial underwent a major restoration and was rededicated by Queen Elizabeth II on 9 April 2007, the 90th anniversary of the battle. The surrounding battlefield park, including preserved trench lines and tunnels such as the Grange Subway, is operated by Veterans Affairs Canada and staffed in part by Canadian student guides.
