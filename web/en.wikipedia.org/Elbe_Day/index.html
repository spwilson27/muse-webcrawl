Elbe Day
Date25 April 1945
LocationTorgau and Strehla, on the Elbe river, Germany
ParticipantsU.S. First Army; Soviet 1st Ukrainian Front

Elbe Day, April 25, 1945, is the day Soviet and American troops met at the Elbe River, near Torgau in Germany, marking an important step toward the end of World War II in Europe. This contact between the Soviets, advancing from the east, and the Americans, advancing from the west, meant that the two powers had effectively cut Germany in two.

The meeting

The first contact was made by a patrol under U.S. First Lieutenant Albert Kotzebue near Strehla, where he crossed the Elbe in a boat and met elements of a Soviet Guards rifle regiment. Later the same day, another patrol under Second Lieutenant William Robertson photographed a famous staged handshake with Soviet Lieutenant Alexander Silvashko on the destroyed Elbe bridge at Torgau. On April 26 the encounter was officially commemorated by the commanders of the two divisions.

Significance

The link-up was celebrated in simultaneous statements in London, Moscow, and Washington. The following day, the joint statement by the Allied governments affirmed their determination to complete the destruction of the remaining German resistance. Germany surrendered unconditionally less than two weeks later.

Commemoration

Elbe Day has been remembered in both Russia and the United States as a symbol of wartime cooperation; in 2020 the two countries issued a joint statement marking its 75th anniversary, recalling the "Spirit of the Elbe."
