COVAX

COVID-19 Vaccines Global Access, abbreviated as COVAX, was a worldwide initiative launched in April 2020 aimed at equitable access to COVID-19 vaccines. It was led by Gavi, the Vaccine Alliance, the Coalition for Epidemic Preparedness Innovations (CEPI), and the World Health Organization (WHO), together with UNICEF as a key delivery partner. COVAX was one of three pillars of the Access to COVID-19 Tools Accelerator.

Mechanism

COVAX pooled purchasing power from participating economies to invest in a portfolio of vaccine candidates, intending to distribute doses so that every participating country could vaccinate priority groups — initially at least 20% of each population — regardless of income. Low- and middle-income economies were funded through the Gavi COVAX Advance Market Commitment (AMC), supported by official development assistance. In total 193 economies participated or were eligible.

Deliveries and criticism

The first COVAX delivery arrived in Ghana on February 24, 2021. Shipments were badly disrupted in 2021 when India halted exports from the Serum Institute of India during its Delta-variant wave, and wealthy countries' bilateral deals and booster campaigns constrained supply. COVAX nonetheless shipped its one-billionth dose in January 2022 and ultimately delivered roughly two billion doses to 146 economies before the programme closed at the end of 2023. Critics argued it fell short of its equity ambitions; supporters note it supplied the great majority of doses received by many low-income countries.
