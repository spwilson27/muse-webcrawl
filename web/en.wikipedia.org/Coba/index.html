Coba

Coba (Spanish: Cobá) is an ancient Maya city in the state of Quintana Roo, Mexico, near the Caribbean coast of the Yucatán Peninsula. It was one of the largest Maya cities of the Classic period and is noted for its extensive network of raised stone causeways.

History

Coba was occupied from the Preclassic period and reached its height between roughly AD 600 and 900, with a population estimated in the tens of thousands. It maintained ties with cities such as Tikal and competed with Chichén Itzá before declining after about AD 1000.

Features

The site contains several groups of structures connected by sacbeob (white roads), including the longest known Maya causeway, about 100 km to Yaxuná. Its tallest pyramid, Nohoch Mul, rises about 42 metres (138 ft) and is among the tallest in the Maya world. Lakes Macanxoc and Cobá lie within the site, an unusual water source in the region.
