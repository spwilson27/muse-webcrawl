Philippines Campaign (1941–1942)

The Philippines campaign (December 8, 1941 – May 8, 1942) was the conquest of the Philippine Islands by Imperial Japan during World War II. Japanese forces under General Masaharu Homma invaded the main island of Luzon while American and Filipino forces, commanded by General Douglas MacArthur, attempted to hold the archipelago with limited air, naval, and ground strength.

Background

On December 8, 1941 (Philippine time), hours after the attack on Pearl Harbor, Japanese aircraft bombed Clark Field and other American air bases, destroying much of the Far East Air Force on the ground. Japanese landing forces came ashore at several points on Luzon beginning December 10, with the main landing at Lingayen Gulf on December 22.

The fighting

MacArthur activated War Plan Orange, withdrawing his forces into the Bataan Peninsula and the island fortress of Corregidor. The defenders held out on Bataan until April 9, 1942, when Major General Edward P. King surrendered some 76,000 troops — the largest surrender in American military history. The prisoners were then subjected to the Bataan Death March, in which thousands died on the forced march to prison camps.

Corregidor held until May 6, 1942, when Lieutenant General Jonathan Wainwright surrendered the remaining forces in the Philippines. MacArthur had been evacuated to Australia in March by order of President Franklin D. Roosevelt.

Aftermath

The Philippines remained under Japanese occupation until 1944–1945, when MacArthur's forces returned. The campaign's defenders were celebrated for delaying the Japanese timetable despite inadequate supplies, and the fall of Bataan and Corregidor became enduring symbols of sacrifice in the early Pacific war.
