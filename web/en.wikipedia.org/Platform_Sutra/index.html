The Platform Sutra of the Sixth Patriarch (Chinese: 六祖壇經; pinyin: Liùzǔ Tánjīng) is a Chan Buddhist scripture composed in China during the 8th to 13th centuries. It is attributed to Huineng (638–713), the Sixth Patriarch of Chan, and purports to record his sermons and encounters. Uniquely among works attributed to a Chinese author, it is designated a "sutra" — a title normally reserved for the words of the Buddha — reflecting the exalted status of Huineng in the Chan tradition.

Contents

The text opens with Huineng's autobiography, including the famous verse contest at the monastery of the Fifth Patriarch Hongren: the head monk Shenxiu compared the mind to a mirror that must be kept polished, while the illiterate labourer Huineng replied that there is no mirror and nowhere for dust to alight — a response that won him the patriarch's robe and bowl. The sutra then expounds Huineng's teachings on sudden enlightenment, the identity of meditation and wisdom, "no-thought" (wunian), and the doctrine that one's own nature is originally pure, presenting formless precepts and repentance.

Versions and influence

The oldest surviving version, a manuscript found at Dunhuang (now held by the British Library), dates to around the ninth century and is considerably shorter than the standard Yuan-dynasty edition of 1291 through which the work is usually read. The Platform Sutra became the foundational text of the Southern school of Chan and of later Zen in Japan and Korea, and remains one of the most widely read works of East Asian Buddhism. Modern scholars regard it as a composite work assembled by Huineng's disciples and later editors rather than a verbatim record.
