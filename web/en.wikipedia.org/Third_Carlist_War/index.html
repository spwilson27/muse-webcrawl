Third Carlist War

Date21 April 1872 – 28 February 1876
LocationSpain (mainly Basque Country, Navarre, Catalonia)
ResultGovernment victory; abolition of Basque fueros
BelligerentsKingdom/Republic of Spain vs. Carlists
CommandersAmadeo I; Alfonso XII; Arsenio Martínez Campos — Carlos VII; Ramón Cabrera (briefly)

The Third Carlist War (Spanish: Tercera Guerra Carlista) was the last of the Carlist Wars in Spain. It began in 1872, when the Carlist pretender Carlos VII, grandson of the original claimant Don Carlos, called his supporters to arms against the government of King Amadeo I. The war was fought mainly in the Basque provinces, Navarre, Catalonia, and parts of Aragón and Valencia.

Course of the war

The rising coincided with political chaos in Madrid: Amadeo abdicated in February 1873 and the First Spanish Republic was proclaimed, which was soon beset by cantonal rebellions and a war in Cuba. The Carlists, freed from facing a stable government, built a functioning state in the north with a capital at Estella, its own currency, postal service and university, and besieged Bilbao in 1874, though they failed to take it. The restoration of the monarchy under Alfonso XII in December 1874 (the pronunciamiento of General Martínez Campos at Sagunto) unified the anti-Carlist forces. With greatly enlarged government armies under generals including Martínez Campos and Jovellar, the Carlists were progressively driven back; Estella fell in February 1876, and on 28 February 1876 Carlos VII crossed into France, reportedly declaring he would return. The government then abolished the remaining Basque fueros (chartered privileges) in 1876, though they were partly replaced by the economic agreements (conciertos económicos) of 1878.

Legacy

The defeat ended Carlism as a serious military threat, though it survived as a political movement into the 20th century, and Carlist requetés later fought on the Nationalist side in the Spanish Civil War. The war also deepened Basque grievances over the loss of the fueros, contributing to the rise of Basque nationalism.
