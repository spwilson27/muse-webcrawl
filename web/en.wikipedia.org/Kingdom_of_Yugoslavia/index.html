The Kingdom of Yugoslavia was a country in Central and Southeast Europe that existed from 1918 until 1945. It was created after the First World War as the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes, uniting the Kingdom of Serbia with the former Habsburg South Slav lands and Montenegro, and was renamed Yugoslavia ("land of the South Slavs") in 1929 by King Alexander I.

Interwar history

The kingdom’s politics were dominated by the struggle between Serbian centralism, embodied in the Vidovdan Constitution of 1921, and Croat demands for autonomy. Political violence culminated in the shooting of the Croatian leader Stjepan Radić in parliament in 1928 and King Alexander’s royal dictatorship of 1929, which banned national political parties and redrew internal boundaries into banovinas that ignored historical borders.

Alexander was assassinated in Marseille in 1934. His successor as regent, Prince Paul, moved the kingdom closer to the Axis powers; a coup in March 1941 that repudiated the pact with Germany was followed within days by the Axis invasion and dismemberment of Yugoslavia in April 1941.

Second World War and end

The exiled King Peter II’s government was recognised by the Allies, but power on the ground passed to Tito’s communist Partisans, who liberated most of the country with Soviet assistance. In 1945 the monarchy was abolished and the Federal People’s Republic of Yugoslavia proclaimed.
