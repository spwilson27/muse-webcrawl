Red Sea crisis

The Red Sea crisis is an ongoing military and shipping crisis that began in late 2023, when Yemen's Houthi movement began attacking commercial vessels in the Red Sea and the Bab-el-Mandeb strait, in response to the Israel–Hamas war. The attacks forced most major shipping lines to divert container traffic around the Cape of Good Hope, sharply lengthening Asia–Europe voyages.

Background and attacks

The Houthis, who control much of northern Yemen including the capital Sanaa, declared they would target ships linked to Israel until aid flowed freely into Gaza; in practice, many vessels with no clear Israeli connection were attacked with missiles, drones, and unmanned surface craft. Notable incidents included the seizure of the Galaxy Leader in November 2023 and the sinking of the bulk carriers Rubymar (2024) and later vessels, along with crew fatalities aboard True Confidence.

Response and impact

The United States formed the multinational Operation Prosperity Guardian in December 2023 and, with the United Kingdom, began airstrikes on Houthi targets in Yemen in January 2024; the EU launched the defensive Operation Aspides in February 2024. Suez Canal transits fell by more than half, freight rates spiked, and Egypt lost substantial canal revenue. A US–Houthi ceasefire arrangement brokered via Oman was announced in May 2025, though attacks and regional tensions later fluctuated. See also Houthi movement, Suez Canal and Yemen.
