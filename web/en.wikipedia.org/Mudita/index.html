Mudita

Muditā (Pāli and Sanskrit: मुदिता) in Buddhism is a concept of sympathetic or vicarious joy: the pleasure that comes from delighting in other people's well-being. It is traditionally regarded as the most difficult of the four brahmavihāras ("divine abidings" or immeasurables) to cultivate, because it requires rejoicing in others' good fortune without envy.

Muditā, sympathetic joy

In the four immeasurables

Along with loving-kindness (mettā), compassion (karuṇā) and equanimity (upekkhā), muditā is one of the four sublime states of mind cultivated in meditation. Its "far enemy" is jealousy and envy, and its "near enemy" is exuberant joy tainted by self-interest. Traditional practice involves rejoicing in the virtues and successes of a benefactor, a neutral person, and ultimately even a hostile person.

Modern usage

The concept is sometimes compared to the German notion of Schadenfreude as its exact opposite — joy in others' good fortune rather than their misfortune — and is increasingly studied in positive psychology.
