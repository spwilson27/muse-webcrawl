The water buffalo (Bubalus bubalis), also called the domestic water buffalo or Asian water buffalo, is a large bovid domesticated in Asia more than 5,000 years ago. It is distinct from the wild water buffalo (Bubalus arnee) and unrelated to the African buffalo or the American bison. More people depend on the water buffalo for their livelihood than on any other domestic animal.

Types and distribution

Two main types are recognised: the river buffalo of the Indian subcontinent and westward, bred chiefly for milk, and the swamp buffalo of China and Southeast Asia, used mainly for draught in rice paddies. Populations also exist in Egypt, southern Europe, Australia (where feral herds roam the north), and South America.

Characteristics

Water buffalo are well adapted to hot, wet climates: they have few sweat glands and wallow in water or mud to cool themselves. Adults may weigh 400–1,000 kg and bear large crescent-shaped horns. Buffalo milk is richer in fat and protein than cow's milk and is used to make mozzarella di bufala in Italy and dairy products across South Asia.

Importance

The species provides draught power for ploughing rice fields, transport, milk, meat and leather for hundreds of millions of smallholder farmers, especially in India, Pakistan, China, Nepal, the Philippines and Vietnam.
