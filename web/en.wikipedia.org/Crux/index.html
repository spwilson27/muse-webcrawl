Crux

Crux
Genitive: Crucis
Abbreviation: Cru
Brightest star: Acrux (Alpha Crucis)

Crux is a constellation of the southern sky centred on four bright stars forming a distinctive cross shape, known as the Southern Cross. It is the smallest of the 88 modern constellations by area, but one of the most recognisable.

Stars

The cross is formed by Acrux and Mimosa (Alpha and Beta Crucis), Gacrux (Gamma) and Delta Crucis, with the fainter Epsilon Crucis nearby. The line through Acrux and Gacrux points close to the south celestial pole. Near Beta Crucis lies the Jewel Box, a colourful open cluster; the dark Coalsack Nebula sits beside the cross.

Cultural significance

The Southern Cross appears on the flags of Australia, New Zealand, Brazil, Papua New Guinea and Samoa, and has long served as a navigational aid and symbol in southern-hemisphere cultures, including many Indigenous Australian traditions.
