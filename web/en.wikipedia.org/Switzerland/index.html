Switzerland

The Alps cover about 60 percent of Switzerland's territory.

Switzerland, officially the Swiss Confederation, is a landlocked country located in west-central Europe. It is bordered by Italy to the south, France to the west, Germany to the north and Austria and Liechtenstein to the east. Switzerland is geographically divided among the Swiss Plateau, the Alps and the Jura; the Alps occupy the greater part of the territory, whereas most of the country's nearly 9 million population are concentrated on the plateau, which contains many of its largest cities, including Zurich, Geneva and Bern.

History

The Old Swiss Confederacy began with the Federal Charter of 1291, an alliance among the valley communities of Uri, Schwyz, and Unterwalden. The confederation expanded over centuries, won de facto independence from the Holy Roman Empire in 1499, and had its neutrality recognized in 1815. The federal constitution of 1848 established modern Switzerland as a federal state.

Government

Switzerland is a federal republic of 26 cantons, noted for its system of direct democracy: citizens can challenge laws through referendums and propose constitutional changes through popular initiatives. The seven-member Federal Council serves as the collective head of state and government. Switzerland maintains a long-standing policy of armed neutrality and hosts many international organizations, notably in Geneva.

Economy and languages

Switzerland has one of the world's most prosperous economies, known for banking and finance, pharmaceuticals, precision instruments, and watches. It has four national languages: German (spoken by the majority), French, Italian, and Romansh.
