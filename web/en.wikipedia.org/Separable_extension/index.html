Separable extension

In abstract algebra, an algebraic field extension E/F is separable if every element of E has a separable minimal polynomial over F — that is, a polynomial with no repeated roots, equivalently one coprime to its formal derivative. Elements satisfying this are called separable over F.

Properties

All algebraic extensions of fields of characteristic 0 are separable, as are all extensions of finite fields. Inseparability can arise only in positive characteristic p, typically through the Frobenius map, as in the extension Fp(t)/Fp(tp). Finite separable extensions are exactly those with the maximum possible number of F-embeddings into an algebraic closure, and they are precisely the extensions generated by a single element (the primitive element theorem).

Galois theory

Finite extensions that are both normal and separable are Galois extensions, the central objects of Galois theory.
