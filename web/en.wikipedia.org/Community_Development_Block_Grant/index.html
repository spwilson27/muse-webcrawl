Community Development Block Grant

The Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) program is a flexible program of the United States Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) that provides annual grants on a formula basis to states, cities, and counties to develop viable urban communities by providing decent housing and a suitable living environment, and by expanding economic opportunities, principally for low- and moderate-income persons.

History

The program was created by the Housing and Community Development Act of 1974, consolidating several earlier categorical grant programs — including urban renewal and Model Cities — into a single block grant, giving local governments greater discretion over spending. It is one of the longest continuously run HUD programs.

Allocation and eligible activities

Seventy per cent of funds go to "entitlement communities" (metropolitan cities of at least 50,000 people and urban counties of at least 200,000) and 30 per cent to states for distribution to smaller communities. Eligible activities include acquisition and rehabilitation of property, public facilities and infrastructure, housing services, economic development assistance, and public services. At least 70 per cent of a grantee's funds must benefit low- and moderate-income persons over a one- to three-year period.

Related programs

Related programs include the Disaster Recovery (CDBG-DR) and Mitigation (CDBG-MIT) appropriations made after natural disasters, and the Section 108 loan guarantee program.
