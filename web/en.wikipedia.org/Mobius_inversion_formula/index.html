Möbius inversion formula

The Möbius inversion formula is a result in number theory, introduced by August Ferdinand Möbius in 1832, which expresses an arithmetic function in terms of its summatory function over divisors, and vice versa, using the Möbius function μ(n). If g(n) = Σd|n f(d) for all positive integers n, then f(n) = Σd|n μ(d) g(n/d).

Statement and proof idea

The formula rests on the identity that the sum of μ(d) over the divisors d of n equals 1 if n = 1 and 0 otherwise. Substituting the definition of g and interchanging the order of summation collapses the double sum to f(n). Equivalently, in the ring of arithmetic functions under Dirichlet convolution, the Möbius function is the inverse of the constant function 1.

Applications

The formula is used to derive Euler's totient function summation identities, to invert formulas involving the floor-sum counting of fractions (Farey sequences), and in counting aperiodic necklaces and irreducible polynomials over finite fields. Generalisations include inversion on partially ordered sets via incidence algebras, developed by Gian-Carlo Rota, of which the classical formula and inclusion–exclusion are special cases.
