Tourism in the Republic of Ireland

The Cliffs of Moher, among Ireland's most visited attractions

Tourism in the Republic of Ireland is one of the country's largest economic sectors, employing roughly one in nine Irish workers and welcoming several million overseas visitors each year. The industry is promoted internationally by Tourism Ireland and developed domestically by Fáilte Ireland, with signature touring routes including the Wild Atlantic Way, Ireland's Ancient East and the Hidden Heartlands.

Attractions

Dublin is the main gateway and home to attractions such as the Guinness Storehouse, Trinity College and the Book of Kells, Temple Bar and St Patrick's Cathedral. Outside the capital, major draws include the Cliffs of Moher, the Ring of Kerry, the Giant's Causeway (in Northern Ireland), Killarney National Park, the Rock of Cashel, Newgrange in the Boyne Valley, Galway city and the Aran Islands. Ireland's pub culture, traditional music, literary heritage and festivals — including St Patrick's Day — are central to its appeal.

Visitors

The largest source markets are Great Britain, the United States, Germany and France; visitors of Irish descent form a significant diaspora tourism segment. Access is principally through Dublin, Shannon and Cork airports and by ferry from Britain and France.
