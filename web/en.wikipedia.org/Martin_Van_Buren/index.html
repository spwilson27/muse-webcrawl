Martin Van Buren

Martin Van Buren (December 5, 1782 – July 24, 1862) was the eighth president of the United States, serving from 1837 to 1841. A primary founder of the Democratic Party, he served as New York's attorney general, governor, a US senator, secretary of state under Andrew Jackson, and Jackson's vice president before winning the presidency in his own right.

Early life and rise

Born in Kinderhook, New York, to a family of Dutch descent — he was the first president born a US citizen and the only one for whom English was a second language — Van Buren became a lawyer and a skilled political organiser, leading New York's "Albany Regency" machine. Nicknamed the "Little Magician" for his political skill, he helped build the modern Democratic Party as a national organisation.

Presidency

Van Buren's presidency was dominated by the Panic of 1837, a severe economic depression triggered by bank failures and speculation. He refused government relief and instead established an Independent Treasury system, separating federal funds from private banks. He also continued Jackson's Indian removal policies, including the Cherokee removal on the Trail of Tears, and avoided annexing Texas to prevent a fight over slavery. Blamed for the depression, he lost re-election in 1840 to William Henry Harrison.

Later life

Van Buren sought the Democratic nomination in 1844 but lost after opposing Texas annexation. In 1848 he ran for president as the candidate of the antislavery Free Soil Party. He retired to his estate, Lindenwald, in Kinderhook, where he died in 1862.
