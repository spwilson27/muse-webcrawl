Antarctic Bottom Water

Antarctic Bottom Water (AABW) is a type of water mass in the Southern Ocean surrounding Antarctica with temperatures ranging from −0.8 to 2 °C (35 °F) and salinities from 34.6 to 34.7 psu. It is the densest water mass of the world ocean and is found at depths below 4000 m in all ocean basins that have a connection to the Southern Ocean at that level.

Formation

AABW forms principally in the Weddell Sea and Ross Sea, where surface cooling and brine rejection during sea-ice formation on the Antarctic continental shelf produce very dense shelf water. This water spills down the continental slope, mixing with surrounding Circumpolar Deep Water, and spreads northward along the ocean floor into the Atlantic, Indian and Pacific basins.

Role in circulation

As the lower limb of the global meridional overturning circulation, AABW transports cold, oxygen-rich water into the deep ocean, stores heat and carbon, and influences sea level and climate. Observations indicate that AABW has warmed, freshened and contracted in volume in recent decades, changes linked to melting Antarctic ice and shifting winds.
