Shinto

Shinto (Japanese: 神道, "the way of the kami") is an indigenous religion of Japan centred on ritual practices and reverence for kami — spirits or deities inhabiting natural phenomena, places, ancestors, and remarkable persons.

Beliefs and practice

Shinto has no dogma, scripture in the doctrinal sense, or concept of absolute good and evil; its central concerns are purity, harmony with nature, and community festivals (matsuri). Worship takes place at tens of thousands of shrines (jinja), marked by torii gates, and at home altars (kamidana). Rituals include purification with water and salt, offerings, prayers (norito), and sacred dance (kagura). Major kami include the sun goddess Amaterasu, enshrined at the Grand Shrine of Ise, the most sacred Shinto site, rebuilt ritually every twenty years.

History

Shinto developed from ancient Japanese folk religion and was systematised under the imperial state, intertwining for centuries with Buddhism in a syncretic arrangement. In the Meiji era the state separated the two and promoted State Shinto; after 1945 Shinto was disestablished, and shrines are now coordinated largely by the Association of Shinto Shrines. Most Japanese participate in Shinto practices such as New Year shrine visits and life-cycle rites alongside Buddhist funerals.
