Lemnian language

The Lemnian language was spoken on the island of Lemnos in the northern Aegean in the 6th century BC. It is known mainly from a funerary stele found in 1885 at Kaminia, together with a handful of short inscriptions on pottery.

Classification

Lemnian is generally grouped with Etruscan and Raetic in the proposed Tyrsenian language family, based on shared features of grammar and vocabulary. It was written in a Greek-derived alphabet, in red letters on the stele beside a carved portrait of the deceased.

History

Ancient authors said Lemnos was inhabited by Tyrrhenians or Pelasgians before Athens conquered the island around 500 BC, after which Greek replaced the language and it vanished from the record.
