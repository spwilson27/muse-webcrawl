Hadron

In particle physics, a hadron is a subatomic composite particle made of two or more quarks held together by the strong interaction. They are categorized into two broad families: baryons, made of an odd number of quarks (usually three), and mesons, made of an even number of quarks (usually one quark and one antiquark).

Protons and neutrons, the constituents of atomic nuclei, are the best-known baryons; pions and kaons are examples of mesons. Almost all "free" hadrons — those not bound within nuclei — are unstable and eventually decay; the proton is the notable exception, with a lifetime so long that no decay has ever been observed.

Properties

Hadrons are excitations of the strong force field and are described by quantum chromodynamics (QCD). Their properties are governed by quantum numbers including baryon number, strangeness, charm, and isospin, and by the flavours of their constituent quarks. Because of color confinement, quarks are never observed in isolation: they are always combined in colour-neutral hadrons.

Exotic hadrons beyond the simple quark-model classification have been found, including tetraquarks and pentaquarks, confirmed at experiments such as LHCb at CERN.
