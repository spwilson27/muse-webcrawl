Medicare Advantage

Medicare Advantage (Medicare Part C) is a programme of the United States Medicare system in which beneficiaries receive their Part A and Part B benefits through private health insurance plans approved by Medicare, rather than directly from the federal government.

How it works

Insurers — commonly offering HMO and PPO plans — are paid a capitated amount per enrollee. Plans must cover everything Original Medicare covers and often add prescription drugs (Part D), dental, vision and hearing benefits, sometimes with provider networks and prior authorisation requirements.

Debate

Created in its modern form by the Medicare Modernization Act of 2003, Advantage enrolment has grown to cover more than half of Medicare beneficiaries. Critics argue risk-score coding makes the programme cost more per person than traditional Medicare; supporters cite care coordination and extra benefits.
