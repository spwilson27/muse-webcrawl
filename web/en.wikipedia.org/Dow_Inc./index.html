Dow Inc.

Dow Inc. is an American commodity chemical company, the materials science business spun off from the former DowDuPont in 2019 and the direct corporate successor in that segment to the Dow Chemical Company founded by Herbert Henry Dow in 1897. Headquartered in Midland, Michigan, Dow is one of the world's largest producers of plastics and chemicals, including polyethylene and other packaging, infrastructure, and consumer materials.

History

The Dow Chemical Company was founded in 1897 by Canadian-born chemist Herbert H. Dow to extract bromine from the brine deposits beneath Midland, Michigan. It grew into a diversified chemical giant known for products from Saran wrap and Styrofoam to major commodity plastics. In 2017 Dow merged with DuPont to form DowDuPont, with the plan of splitting into three specialised companies. On 1 April 2019 the materials science division was spun off as the new Dow Inc., which retained the Midland headquarters, the famous diamond-shaped red Dow logo, and the company's seat in the Dow Jones Industrial Average; the other two businesses became Corteva (agriculture) and the new DuPont de Nemours (specialty products).

Operations

Dow operates in three segments: Packaging & Specialty Plastics (polyethylene and elastomers for packaging), Industrial Intermediates & Infrastructure (polyurethanes, construction chemicals, and industrial solutions), and Performance Materials & Coatings (silicones — including the former Dow Corning business — acrylics, and coatings). It manufactures in dozens of countries, with major integrated sites on the U.S. Gulf Coast, in Europe, and in Argentina and elsewhere. The company has announced plans for large-scale "net-zero" ethylene capacity in Alberta, Canada, and has faced long-running environmental scrutiny, including legacy contamination and litigation associated with the historical Dow Chemical, such as over Agent Orange and dioxins.
