Wars of Alexander the Great

The wars of Alexander the Great were a series of conquests carried out by Alexander III of Macedon between 336 and 323 BC. Beginning with the reconquest of Greece and the Balkan campaigns, Alexander destroyed the Achaemenid Persian Empire, conquering territory from Asia Minor and Egypt to Mesopotamia, Persia, and the Indus Valley, creating one of the largest empires of the ancient world before his death at age 32.

The Persian campaign

Crossing the Hellespont in 334 BC with some 40,000 troops, Alexander defeated Persian satraps at the Granicus River, then routed Darius III in person at Issus in 333 BC, capturing the Persian royal family. After the seven-month siege of Tyre and the capture of Gaza, Egypt welcomed him as liberator; there he founded Alexandria and was proclaimed son of Zeus-Ammon at Siwah. At Gaugamela in 331 BC he destroyed the main Persian army, then occupied Babylon, Susa, and Persepolis, which was burned in 330 BC. Darius was murdered by his own satrap Bessus, whom Alexander hunted down and executed.

Central Asia and India

Hard guerrilla campaigns subdued Bactria and Sogdiana (336–327 BC), where he married Roxana. In India he defeated King Porus at the Hydaspes in 326 BC, but his exhausted army mutinied at the Hyphasis and forced a return. The disastrous march through the Gedrosian desert and the parallel voyage of Nearchus's fleet brought the army back to Babylon, where Alexander died in June 323 BC, leaving the empire "to the strongest" — his generals, the Diadochi, fought over it for decades.
