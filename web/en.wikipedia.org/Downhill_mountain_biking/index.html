Downhill mountain biking

A rider on a downhill course.

Downhill mountain biking (DH) is a genre of mountain biking practised on steep, rough terrain featuring jumps, drops, rock gardens and other obstacles. Riders are timed on descents, typically reaching the course by chairlift, gondola or shuttle vehicle rather than pedalling uphill.

Equipment

Downhill bikes are heavy-duty machines with long-travel suspension (often around 200 mm), slack geometry, powerful disc brakes and strong wheels, weighing considerably more than cross-country bikes. Riders wear full-face helmets, goggles, gloves and body armour.

Competition

The UCI Mountain Bike World Cup includes a downhill series held at venues such as Fort William in Scotland and Mont-Sainte-Anne in Canada, and an annual World Championship decides the rainbow jersey. Courses usually run two to five minutes for elite riders. The discipline originated in California in the 1970s with races down Mount Tamalpais and developed through events such as the Kamikaze race at Mammoth Mountain.
