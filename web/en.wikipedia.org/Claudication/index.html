Claudication

Claudication
SpecialtyVascular medicine, cardiology
SymptomsCramping leg pain on exertion, relieved by rest
Usual causePeripheral artery disease (atherosclerosis)

Claudication is pain, cramping or fatigue in the muscles of the legs (occasionally the arms) that is brought on by exercise and relieved by rest. It is classically a symptom of peripheral artery disease, in which atherosclerotic narrowing of the arteries limits blood flow to working muscle. The word derives from the Latin claudicatio, "limping".

Intermittent claudication

In intermittent claudication the pain occurs predictably after walking a certain distance and subsides within minutes of stopping. The calf is most commonly affected, but pain in the thigh or buttock points to disease higher in the aorta or iliac arteries. The distance walked before pain begins is a rough measure of severity. Because its cause is systemic atherosclerosis, people with claudication are at substantially increased risk of heart attack and stroke.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis rests on the characteristic history and physical examination, in which peripheral pulses may be diminished. The ankle–brachial index — the ratio of blood pressure at the ankle to that in the arm — is the standard confirmatory test; a value below 0.9 indicates peripheral artery disease. Duplex ultrasound, CT or MR angiography can map the disease when intervention is planned.

Other forms

Neurogenic or "pseudo" claudication results from lumbar spinal stenosis: pain and weakness develop on walking or standing and are relieved by sitting or bending forward rather than by simply stopping. Venous claudication, a bursting pain from venous outflow obstruction, is a rarer cause.

Treatment

Management aims first at cardiovascular risk reduction: smoking cessation, statins, antiplatelet therapy, and control of blood pressure and diabetes. Supervised exercise programmes significantly improve walking distance. The drug cilostazol may help selected patients. Revascularization — angioplasty, stenting, or bypass surgery — is reserved for disabling symptoms or limb-threatening ischaemia.
