Lend-Lease

Lend-Lease, formally the Lend-Lease Act (Pub. L. 77-11), was a programme under which the United States supplied the United Kingdom, the Soviet Union, China, France, and other Allied nations with food, oil, and materiel during World War II, largely free of charge. Enacted on March 11, 1941, months before the United States entered the war, it marked the end of American neutrality in practice and made the U.S. the "arsenal of democracy", in President Franklin D. Roosevelt's phrase.

Origins

By late 1940 Britain had nearly exhausted its dollar reserves paying for American supplies under "cash and carry". Roosevelt proposed lending supplies instead, comparing it to lending a neighbour a garden hose to put out a fire. After fierce debate with isolationists, Congress passed the Act, authorising the President to "sell, transfer title to, exchange, lease, lend, or otherwise dispose of" defence articles to any country whose defence he deemed vital to that of the United States.

Scale and recipients

In total the United States shipped some $50 billion (about $700 billion in modern terms) in supplies, of which roughly $31 billion went to Britain and the Commonwealth and $11 billion to the Soviet Union. Deliveries included 400,000 trucks, thousands of aircraft and tanks, locomotives, food, and raw materials. Soviet supplies reached the USSR via the Arctic convoys to Murmansk, the Persian Corridor through Iran, and the Pacific route to Vladivostok; American trucks and food were widely credited by Soviet leaders with sustaining the Red Army's mobility and logistics. A smaller "Reverse Lend-Lease" flowed back, with Britain providing bases, technology (such as radar and the jet engine), and supplies to American forces abroad.

End and settlement

The programme was terminated abruptly in August 1945 at the war's end. Surplus goods already delivered to Britain were sold at a steep discount as part of the 1946 Anglo-American loan; the Soviet Union's debt was disputed for decades and settled only in 1972, with final payments completed by Russia in 2006. Lend-Lease set a precedent for post-war American foreign aid, including the Marshall Plan.
