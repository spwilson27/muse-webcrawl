Hillsborough Stadium is a football stadium in Owlerton, a north-western suburb of Sheffield, South Yorkshire, England. It has been the home ground of Sheffield Wednesday since opening in 1899 as Owlerton Stadium, taking its present name from the parliamentary constituency in which it lies in 1914. With a capacity of 34,854, it is one of the larger club grounds in England and has hosted FA Cup semi-finals, a 1966 World Cup quarter-final, and matches at Euro 1996.

The stadium is also known internationally as the site of the Hillsborough disaster of 15 April 1989, in which 97 Liverpool supporters died as a result of a crush at an FA Cup semi-final. The disaster led to the Taylor Report and the conversion of top-flight English grounds to all-seater stadiums.

History

Sheffield Wednesday moved to Owlerton in 1899 after the lease on their previous ground at Olive Grove expired, buying land in the rural Owlerton district. The first match, against Chesterfield on 2 September 1899, ended in a 5–1 defeat. The ground developed piecemeal: a record crowd of 72,841 watched an FA Cup tie against Manchester City in 1934, and the ground staged World Cup matches in 1966 and became one of England's principal semi-final venues.

Hillsborough disaster

On 15 April 1989, during an FA Cup semi-final between Liverpool and Nottingham Forest, a crush developed in the Leppings Lane standing pens after police ordered an exit gate opened to relieve congestion outside while the tunnel to the already-full central pens was left open. 94 people died that day and in the following days; the toll eventually reached 97. Inquests concluded in 2016 that the victims had been unlawfully killed and that supporters bore no blame. The disaster transformed stadium safety in Britain, leading to all-seater stadiums in the top divisions.

Stands

The ground comprises four main structures: the North Stand (a cantilever stand opened in 1961), the South Stand on Penistone Road — the oldest surviving part of the ground, originating in 1914 — the Kop at the east end, built on a large earth bank, and the West Stand (Leppings Lane End), which houses visiting supporters. The stadium sits alongside the River Don, which flooded the ground severely in 2007.

Other uses

Hillsborough hosted the 1995 World Student Games closing events, rock concerts, and rugby league matches, and was selected as a venue in England's unsuccessful 2018 World Cup bid. Sheffield Wednesday considered relocation in the 2000s but remained at Hillsborough, whose freehold was sold to chairman Dejphon Chansiri's company in 2019 for financial fair play purposes.
