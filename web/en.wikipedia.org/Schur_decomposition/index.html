Schur decomposition

Every square complex matrix is unitarily similar to an upper triangular matrix.

In linear algebra, the Schur decomposition (named after Issai Schur) states that any square matrix with complex entries can be expressed as the product of a unitary matrix, an upper triangular matrix, and the inverse of the unitary matrix. Specifically, for a square matrix A there exists a unitary matrix Q such that Q−1AQ is upper triangular; the diagonal entries of the triangular matrix are the eigenvalues of A.

Real Schur form

For a real matrix, a real orthogonal matrix can be chosen so that the result is in quasi-triangular form: 1×1 blocks on the diagonal correspond to real eigenvalues and 2×2 blocks correspond to pairs of complex conjugate eigenvalues. This is called the real Schur form.

Computation

The Schur decomposition is computed numerically by the QR algorithm, which iteratively applies orthogonal similarity transformations. It is a key intermediate step in solving eigenvalue problems, because the decomposition is backward stable and reveals the eigenvalues directly on the diagonal of the triangular factor. Libraries such as LAPACK, and tools built on it such as SciPy, provide routines for both the complex and real Schur forms.

Generalised Schur decomposition

The generalised Schur decomposition (or QZ decomposition) applies to a pair of matrices, factoring them simultaneously into triangular form; it is used to solve generalised eigenvalue problems.
