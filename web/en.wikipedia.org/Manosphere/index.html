Manosphere

The manosphere is a diverse collection of websites, blogs, and online forums promoting masculinity, misogyny, and opposition to feminism. Communities within the manosphere include men's rights activists (MRAs), incels (involuntary celibates), Men Going Their Own Way (MGTOW), pickup artists (PUAs), and fathers' rights groups. The term is a portmanteau, coined by analogy with "blogosphere"; it entered wider usage in the 2010s as these communities grew on platforms such as Reddit, YouTube and Twitter.

History

The movements forming the manosphere originated in the men's rights and men's liberation movements of the 1970s and expanded with the internet in the 1990s and 2000s, through sites such as A Voice for Men and the pickup-artist community popularised by Neil Strauss's 2005 book The Game. In the 2010s the manosphere gained notoriety through the harassment campaign of Gamergate (2014), and through violent attacks committed by men linked to incel communities, including the 2014 Isla Vista killings by Elliot Rodger and the 2018 Toronto van attack by Alek Minassian. Influencers such as Andrew Tate later carried manosphere themes to mass audiences on social media.

Ideology

Manosphere communities typically share a vocabulary and worldview: feminism is portrayed as having corrupted society, men are cast as victims of a "gynocentric" order, and masculine status is framed hierarchically (so-called "alpha" and "beta" males). The "red pill" metaphor, from the film The Matrix, denotes awakening to these supposed truths. Specific beliefs vary: MRAs focus on legal and institutional grievances, MGTOW advocate withdrawal from relationships with women, incels centre their identity on perceived inability to form romantic relationships, and PUAs sell seduction techniques.

Scholarship and response

Researchers describe the manosphere as part of a wider anti-feminist backlash and study its role in radicalisation and harassment; the Southern Poverty Law Center added male-supremacist ideology to its tracking of hate movements in 2018. Platforms have banned several manosphere communities, including Reddit's r/incels (2017) and r/TheRedPill's quarantine (2018), while creators associated with the milieu have been removed from major social networks at times.
