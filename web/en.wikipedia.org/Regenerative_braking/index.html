Regenerative braking

Regenerative braking is an energy recovery mechanism that slows a vehicle by converting its kinetic energy into a form that can be stored or immediately reused, rather than wasting it as heat in friction brakes. In electric and hybrid vehicles the traction motor runs as a generator during deceleration, charging the battery.

The system extends driving range — especially in stop-and-go traffic — and reduces brake wear, enabling 'one-pedal driving' in many EVs. It is also long used on electric trains, where recovered power feeds the supply network, and in Formula 1's KERS/ERS systems. Regenerative braking cannot hold a vehicle at a complete stop, so friction brakes supplement it.
