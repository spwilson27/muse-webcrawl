Avranches is a commune in the Manche department in the Normandy region in northwestern France. It is a subprefecture of the department. The inhabitants are called Avranchinais. Perched on a hill, the town overlooks the bay of Mont Saint-Michel, which can be seen from its gardens.

History

Avranches was the ancient Legedia of the Abrincatui tribe and later a Roman city. Its cathedral, where Henry II of England did penance in 1172 for the murder of Thomas Becket, largely collapsed in the nineteenth century. In the Second World War, the town was a key point in the Normandy breakout: during Operation Cobra in July 1944, General George S. Patton's Third Army passed through Avranches into Brittany, commemorated today by Patton Square and a Sherman tank monument.

Sights

The town holds the Scriptorial museum, displaying medieval manuscripts from the abbey of Mont Saint-Michel, and the Jardin des Plantes with its celebrated view of the abbey across the bay.
