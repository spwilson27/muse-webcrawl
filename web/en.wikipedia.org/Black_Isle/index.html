Black Isle

The Black Isle (Scottish Gaelic: an t-Eilean Dubh) is a peninsula in the Scottish Highlands, in the historic county of Ross and Cromarty. Despite its name it is not an island: it is bounded by the Moray Firth to the east, the Cromarty Firth to the north, and the Beauly Firth and the River Conon to the south and west.

Name and landscape

The name is traditionally explained by the peninsula's dark, fertile soil and by the fact that its land, sheltered and low, rarely stays white with snow in winter. The Black Isle is noted for rich farmland, barley growing for the whisky industry, and villages including Fortrose, Rosemarkie, Avoch, and Cromarty.

History and attractions

Fortrose Cathedral dates to the thirteenth century. Chanonry Point, on the Moray Firth, is one of the best places in Britain to watch bottlenose dolphins from land. The peninsula is crossed by the A9 road via the Kessock Bridge to Inverness and the Cromarty Bridge to the north.
