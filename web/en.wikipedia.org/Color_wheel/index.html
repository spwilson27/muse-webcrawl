Color wheel

A color wheel or color circle is an abstract illustrative organisation of color hues around a circle, which shows the relationships between primary colors, secondary colors, tertiary colors, and so on. Color wheels make it easy to see complementary, analogous, split-complementary and triadic relationships at a glance, and they are widely used in art, design and color theory teaching.

History

Isaac Newton arranged the spectral colors in a circle in his Opticks (1704), joining the ends of the spectrum with purple to close the loop. Later wheels by Moses Harris, Johann Wolfgang von Goethe and others systematised the arrangement for painters. The traditional artists' wheel is based on the RYB model (red, yellow, blue), while modern colour science more often uses the RGB model of light or the CMYK model of printing.

Color schemes

Relationships read off the wheel define common harmonies: complementary colors lie opposite each other and maximise contrast; analogous colors sit side by side and produce a serene, unified effect; triadic schemes use three colors evenly spaced around the wheel; split-complementary and tetradic schemes combine a base color with neighbours of its complement or with two complementary pairs.

Warm and cool colors

The wheel divides roughly into warm colors (reds, oranges, yellows) associated with warmth and advance, and cool colors (greens, blues, violets) associated with coolness and recession, a distinction exploited in painting to model light and depth.
