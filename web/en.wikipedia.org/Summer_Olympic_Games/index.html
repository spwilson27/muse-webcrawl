Summer Olympic Games

The Olympic rings, symbol of the Games.

The Summer Olympic Games, also known as the Games of the Olympiad, is a major international multi-sport event normally held once every four years. The inaugural Games took place in 1896 in Athens, Greece, and the most recent edition was held in Paris in 2024. The Summer Games are organised by the International Olympic Committee (IOC) and feature competitions in more than 30 sports.

History

The revival of the ancient Olympic Games was championed by Pierre de Coubertin, leading to the first modern Games in Athens in 1896 with 241 athletes from 14 nations. The Games grew steadily through the 20th century despite cancellation of three editions (1916, 1940, 1944) due to the world wars. Notable milestones include the admission of women's events (from 1900), the rise of global television audiences, the end of amateurism, and memorable editions such as Berlin 1936, Mexico City 1968, Munich 1972, Los Angeles 1984, Barcelona 1992, Sydney 2000, Beijing 2008, London 2012, Rio 2016, the pandemic-delayed Tokyo 2020 (held in 2021), and Paris 2024.

Programme and records

The programme centres on athletics, swimming, gymnastics and team sports, with sports added and removed over time; recent additions include skateboarding, sport climbing, surfing and breakdancing (Paris 2024). The United States leads the all-time medal table. The most decorated Olympian is swimmer Michael Phelps, with 28 medals, 23 of them gold. The next Summer Games are scheduled for Los Angeles in 2028 and Brisbane in 2032.
