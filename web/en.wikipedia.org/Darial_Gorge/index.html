The Darial Gorge (Georgian: დარიალის ხეობა; Russian: Дарьяльское ущелье; Ossetian: Арвыком) is a river gorge on the border between Russia and Georgia. It is at the east base of Mount Kazbek, south of the present-day Russian city of Vladikavkaz. The gorge was carved by the river Terek and stretches for about 13 kilometres. The steep granite walls of the gorge can be as much as 1,800 metres tall in places, and the road through it — the Georgian Military Highway — is flanked by cliffs on one side and the river on the other.

History

Known in antiquity as the Porta Caucasica (Caucasian Gates), the pass has been strategically vital since ancient times as one of only two crossings of the Greater Caucasus range — the other being the Derbent pass on the Caspian coast. Iberian and later Georgian kings, then Roman, Persian, Arab, Mongol, and Russian powers fortified it against nomadic invasions from the steppe. The name "Darial" derives from the Persian Dar-i Alān, "Gate of the Alans". A Russian fort at Darial guarded the southern end of the gorge in the imperial period.

The gorge captured the imagination of Russian Romantic writers. Alexander Pushkin described it in "The Journey to Arzrum", and Mikhail Lermontov, who served in the Caucasus, set scenes here and in his poem "The Demon" and novel A Hero of Our Time. The Georgian Military Road running through it became famous in Russian literature as one of the great scenic routes of the empire.

Modern importance

Today the gorge carries Georgia's only functioning road border crossing with Russia at Verkhni Lars–Kazbegi, a lifeline for trade between Russia, Georgia, and Armenia. The crossing is periodically closed by avalanches, mudslides, and severe weather, and long queues of trucks are common. A monastery and the ruins of Dariali castle overlook the defile near the border.
