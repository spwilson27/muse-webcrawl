Geometric distribution

In probability theory and statistics, the geometric distribution is either one of two discrete probability distributions:

— the probability distribution of the number X of Bernoulli trials needed to get one success, supported on the set {1, 2, 3, ...}; or

— the probability distribution of the number Y = X − 1 of failures before the first success, supported on the set {0, 1, 2, 3, ...}.

For the first version, if the probability of success on each trial is p, the probability that the k-th trial is the first success is Pr(X = k) = (1 − p)k−1 p. The distribution has mean 1/p and variance (1 − p)/p².

Memorylessness

The geometric distribution is the only discrete distribution with the memoryless property: given that no success has occurred in the first m trials, the distribution of the additional number of trials needed is the same as the original distribution. Formally, Pr(X > m + n | X > m) = Pr(X > n). Its continuous analogue is the exponential distribution, which is likewise memoryless.

Properties

The probability generating function of the first version is G(s) = ps / (1 − (1 − p)s). The cumulative distribution function is Pr(X ≤ k) = 1 − (1 − p)k. The entropy of the geometric distribution is maximised, among distributions on the non-negative integers with a fixed mean, by the geometric distribution itself; it is the discrete counterpart of the maximum-entropy property of the exponential distribution.

Applications

The geometric distribution models phenomena such as the number of coin tosses until the first head, the number of attempts needed to obtain a desired outcome in quality control, and waiting times in systems modelled by repeated independent trials. The sum of independent geometric random variables follows a negative binomial distribution.
