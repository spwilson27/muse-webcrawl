Random mating

Random mating is a term in population genetics describing a mating system in which the probability that two individuals mate is not influenced by their genotypes or phenotypes with respect to the trait under consideration. It is one of the assumptions of the Hardy–Weinberg principle.

Significance

Under random mating, allele frequencies remain unchanged across generations in the absence of selection, mutation, migration, and genetic drift, and genotype frequencies can be predicted from allele frequencies. Departures from random mating — such as assortative mating, inbreeding, or population structure — alter genotype frequencies and are studied as evolutionary forces in their own right.

Related concepts

Random mating in a subdivided population can produce the Wahlund effect, an apparent deficit of heterozygotes, when subpopulations with different allele frequencies are analysed as one.
