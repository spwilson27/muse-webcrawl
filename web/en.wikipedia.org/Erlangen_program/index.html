Erlangen program

The Erlangen program is a method of characterising geometries based on group theory and projective geometry. It was published by Felix Klein in 1872 as Vergleichende Betrachtungen über neuere geometrische Forschungen (A comparative review of recent researches in geometry), his inaugural address on taking the chair at the University of Erlangen.

Content

The program proposed that each geometry is defined by a group of transformations and by those properties (invariants) that remain unchanged under the group. For example:

  • Euclidean geometry is the geometry whose invariants are distances and angles — i.e., the properties preserved by the rigid motions (translations and rotations).
  • Projective geometry studies properties preserved by projective transformations, such as collinearity and cross-ratio.
  • Topology studies properties preserved under continuous deformations, including connectedness and compactness.

Influence

The Erlangen program gave mathematics a unifying framework for the proliferation of nineteenth-century geometries, and it made the concept of symmetry central to the subject. It profoundly influenced modern physics, where symmetry groups organise particle physics (the Standard Model), general relativity, and gauge theories. Élie Cartan and later workers extended the program's ideas to differential geometry, culminating in the modern theory of G-structures and Klein geometries.
