Rootstock

A rootstock is the lower part of a grafted plant — the stem with its root system — onto which the scion, a cutting or bud of a desired cultivar, is joined. The rootstock contributes the roots and lower stem while the scion forms the fruiting or flowering upper part.

Functions

Rootstocks are chosen to control the vigour and final size of the plant (dwarfing apple rootstocks such as M9 are a classic example), to confer resistance to soil pests and diseases, and to adapt a cultivar to particular soils or climates. In viticulture, European grape varieties are grafted onto American rootstocks resistant to the phylloxera louse, which devastated vineyards in the nineteenth century.

Common examples

Rootstocks are used extensively for apples, pears, citrus, stone fruits, roses, and tomatoes. The horticultural sense should not be confused with the botanical use of "rootstock" for a rhizome.
