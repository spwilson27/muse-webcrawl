Stone of Remembrance

DesignerSir Edwin Lutyens
Used inCommonwealth War Graves Commission cemeteries with 1,000 or more burials
Inscription"Their name liveth for evermore"

The Stone of Remembrance is a standard feature of Commonwealth War Graves Commission cemeteries commemorating the dead of the two World Wars. Designed by Sir Edwin Lutyens, it is a large monolithic altar-like stone, typically about 3.5 metres long, set on a stepped platform.

Design

Lutyens deliberately designed the stone to avoid overt religious symbolism so that it could serve all faiths and none; its subtle entasis and proportions derive from classical geometry. The inscription "Their name liveth for evermore", chosen by Rudyard Kipling from the Book of Ecclesiasticus, is carved on one face. Stones are installed in cemeteries containing a thousand or more burials, complementing the Cross of Sacrifice found in smaller cemeteries.
