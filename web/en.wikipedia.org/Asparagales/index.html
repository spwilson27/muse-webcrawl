Asparagales (asparagoid lilies) is an order of monocotyledonous flowering plants in the APG system of classification. It is the largest order of monocots, with roughly 40,000 species in 14 families, and includes many familiar and economically important plants: the orchids (Orchidaceae, the largest family of flowering plants), the asparagus family (Asparagaceae, including agaves, hyacinths, and hostas), and the amaryllis family (Amaryllidaceae, including onions, garlic, and daffodils).

Characteristics

Most Asparagales are herbaceous perennials arising from bulbs, corms, or rhizomes. Characteristic features include seeds with a dark, crust-like outer layer (phytomelan) in most families, simultaneous microsporogenesis, and an inferior ovary in many groups. Several lineages have independently evolved succulence (agaves, aloes and relatives) or mycoheterotrophy.

Classification

The APG IV system (2016) recognizes fourteen families, including Iridaceae (irises, crocuses, gladioli), Asphodelaceae (aloes, daylilies), Orchidaceae, Amaryllidaceae, and Asparagaceae. Molecular phylogenetics places the order as sister to the commelinid clade within the monocots.

Economic importance

Members of the order supply vegetables (asparagus, onion, garlic, leek), flavourings (vanilla, saffron, chives), ornamentals of major horticultural value (orchids, tulip relatives, irises, narcissi), and fibres and stimulants (sisal, agave syrup and spirits).
