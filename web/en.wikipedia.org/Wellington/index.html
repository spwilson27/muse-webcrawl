Wellington

Wellington (Māori: Te Whanganui-a-Tara) is the capital city of New Zealand. It is located at the south-western tip of the North Island, between Cook Strait and the Remutaka Range. Wellington is the third-largest city in New Zealand by metropolitan area, after Auckland and Christchurch, and is the country's political centre, housing the New Zealand Parliament, the head offices of all government ministries and departments, and the bulk of the foreign diplomatic missions.

History

The Wellington area was originally settled by Māori iwi including Te Ātiawa and Ngāti Toa. In Māori tradition, the explorer Kupe is said to have visited the harbour around the 10th century. The harbour, Te Whanganui-a-Tara ("the great harbour of Tara"), is named for Tara, son of the navigator Whātonga.

European settlement began in 1839 with the arrival of New Zealand Company settlers aboard the ship Tory, followed by the Aurora, the first settler ship, in January 1840. The settlement was named in honour of Arthur Wellesley, 1st Duke of Wellington, a supporter of the company. In 1865, Wellington replaced Auckland as the capital of New Zealand, chosen for its central location.

Geography

Wellington is situated on a hilly peninsula at the southern end of the North Island. Its natural harbour, Wellington Harbour (Port Nicholson), is one of the finest deep-water harbours in the world. The city is known for its strong winds funnelled through Cook Strait, earning it the nickname "Windy Wellington". The region is seismically active, lying on several fault lines including the Wellington Fault.

Government and politics

As the capital, Wellington hosts the New Zealand Parliament Buildings, including the distinctive Beehive executive wing, the Supreme Court, and Government House, the residence of the Governor-General. The Wellington City Council governs the city proper, while the wider region is administered by the Greater Wellington Regional Council.

Economy and culture

Government and public services form the backbone of Wellington's economy, alongside a significant film industry centred on the suburb of Miramar — home to Weta Workshop and Weta Digital, famous for The Lord of the Rings and Avatar films. The city is home to the Museum of New Zealand Te Papa Tongarewa, the New Zealand Symphony Orchestra, and Victoria University of Wellington.
