Letchworth

Letchworth Garden City, commonly known as Letchworth, is a town in Hertfordshire, England, noted for being the first garden city. It was founded in 1903 on the principles of Ebenezer Howard, whose ideas influenced town planning worldwide. The town has a population of about 33,000.

Country: England
County: Hertfordshire
Founded: 1903
Population: ~33,000

History

In his book To-morrow: A Peaceful Path to Real Reform (1898), Ebenezer Howard proposed self-contained "garden cities" combining the benefits of town and country, surrounded by agricultural green belt, with land held in trust for the community. First Garden City Ltd bought 3,800 acres near the village of Letchworth in 1903, and architects Barry Parker and Raymond Unwin laid out the town with low-density cottage housing, tree-lined avenues and generous open space.

Features

Letchworth pioneered features now standard in planning, including the green belt, zoning of industry, and the roundabout — the UK's first was built here in 1909. Later garden cities such as Welwyn Garden City (1920) and the post-war New Towns drew on its example. The town retains its garden city estate management under the Letchworth Garden City Heritage Foundation.
