Thomas Hart Benton (April 15, 1889 – January 19, 1975) was an American painter and muralist, a leading figure of the Regionalist movement alongside Grant Wood and John Steuart Curry. His fluid, sculptural depictions of everyday Midwestern life made him one of the most popular — and controversial — American artists of the 1930s.

Career

Benton studied at the Art Institute of Chicago and in Paris, where he experimented with modernist styles before rejecting European abstraction for a distinctly American subject matter. His ten-panel mural cycle America Today (1930–31), painted for the New School in New York, is now at the Metropolitan Museum of Art. His murals for the Missouri State Capitol (1936) remain famous for their unvarnished treatment of the state's history.

Teaching and legacy

Teaching at the Kansas City Art Institute, Benton mentored a young Jackson Pollock, who later repudiated Regionalism to lead Abstract Expressionism. Benton's populist stance and public feuds with the art establishment shaped debates about American art for decades.
