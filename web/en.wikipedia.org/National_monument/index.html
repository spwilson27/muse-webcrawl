National monument

A national monument is a monument constructed to commemorate a significant person or event in a nation's history, or designated as an officially protected area of cultural, historical or natural importance. The term is used in two principal senses: a great commemorative structure, and a category of protected land or site.

Commemorative structures

Many countries erect national monuments as symbols of national identity — examples include the Washington Monument in the United States, Indonesia's Monas in Jakarta, Pakistan Monument in Islamabad, and numerous national war memorials and tombs of unknown soldiers. Such monuments often anchor state ceremony and public remembrance.

Protected designations

In the United States, "National Monument" is a specific federal designation for protected lands administered by agencies such as the National Park Service and Bureau of Land Management. Other countries use similar terms: India designates "Monuments of National Importance", the Netherlands has rijksmonumenten, and many states maintain registers of protected monuments and heritage sites under national law.
