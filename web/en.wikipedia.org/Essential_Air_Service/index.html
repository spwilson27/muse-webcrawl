Essential Air Service (EAS) is a United States government program created by the Airline Deregulation Act of 1978 to guarantee that small communities served by certificated airlines before deregulation would maintain a minimal level of scheduled air service. The Department of Transportation subsidises airlines to serve communities that would otherwise lose all scheduled flights.

Operation

Under EAS, eligible communities typically receive two or three round trips per day to a hub airport, flown with small turboprop or regional aircraft under contracts awarded competitively for two- to four-year terms. Subsidy is paid per community, and reforms in 2012 capped eligibility criteria and per-passenger subsidy for communities near large hubs.

Criticism and support

Critics point to high per-passenger subsidies on lightly used routes; supporters argue the program sustains economic links and access for rural areas, particularly in Alaska, where many participating communities have no road access at all.
