Tom Harrisson

Tom Harnett Harrisson (26 September 1911 – 16 January 1976) was a British polymath — ornithologist, anthropologist, soldier, curator and social investigator — best known as a co-founder of Mass-Observation and for his wartime operations in Borneo.

Early expeditions and Mass-Observation

Educated at Harrow, Harrisson took part in ornithological expeditions to Lapland, Sarawak and the New Hebrides (now Vanuatu) before founding Mass-Observation in 1937 with Charles Madge and Humphrey Jennings. The project recruited volunteer observers to record everyday British life, producing studies such as the famous investigation of working-class culture in Bolton, the "Worktown" project.

War and Borneo

During the Second World War Harrisson parachuted into Japanese-occupied Sarawak in 1945 with Operation Semut, organising indigenous resistance from the longhouse of the Kelabit highlands. After the war he became curator of the Sarawak Museum in Kuching (1947–1966), modernising its collections, championing conservation of orangutans and turtles, and writing extensively on Borneo's peoples and prehistory, including excavations at the Niah Caves. He died in a road accident in Thailand in 1976.
