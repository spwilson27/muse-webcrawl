Kent

Kent is a county in South East England and one of the home counties. It borders Greater London to the northwest, Surrey to the west, East Sussex to the southwest, and the English Channel and North Sea. Its county town is Maidstone, and its cathedral city is Canterbury. Because of its abundance of orchards and hop gardens, Kent is known as the "Garden of England".

History

Kent was among the first parts of Britain settled by the Jutes after the Roman withdrawal and became one of the Anglo-Saxon kingdoms. In 597 St Augustine landed in Kent and founded the see of Canterbury, the centre of English Christianity. The county figured in the Peasants' Revolt, and its ports — the Cinque Ports of Dover, Sandwich, Hythe, Romney, and Hastings — long provided ships for the Crown. In the Second World War, Kent lay on the front line during the Battle of Britain.

Geography

The landscape is defined by the chalk North Downs, the Greensand ridge, and the low-lying Weald. The White Cliffs of Dover face France across the Strait of Dover, the narrowest part of the Channel; the Channel Tunnel links Folkestone with Coquelles. Principal rivers are the Medway, Stour, and Darent.

Economy and transport

Agriculture remains famous for hops, apples, and cherries, while services, logistics, and tourism now dominate. Kent is a principal gateway to continental Europe, with the Port of Dover, the Channel Tunnel, and high-speed HS1 rail services to London and Europe.
