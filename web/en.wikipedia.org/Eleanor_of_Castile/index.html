Eleanor of Castile

Eleanor of Castile
Born: 1241, Burgos, Castile
Died: 28 November 1290, Harby, Nottinghamshire
Spouse: Edward I of England
Burial: Westminster Abbey

Eleanor of Castile (1241 – 28 November 1290) was Queen of England as the first wife of King Edward I, whom she married in 1254. A daughter of Ferdinand III of Castile, she accompanied Edward on crusade to the Holy Land and was a shrewd acquirer of landed estates in England.

The Eleanor cross at Geddington, one of three surviving crosses.

Life

Eleanor married the future Edward I at the monastery of Las Huelgas in Burgos when she was about thirteen. The marriage brought England the claim to Gascony and proved personally close: Eleanor travelled with Edward on the Ninth Crusade, where legend says she saved his life after an assassination attempt. In England she built a large landed portfolio, whose methods of acquisition made her unpopular, though modern historians judge her an intelligent and cultured queen, a patron of literature and learning.

Death and the Eleanor crosses

Eleanor died at Harby in Nottinghamshire on 28 November 1290. Devastated, Edward ordered her body carried in procession to Westminster Abbey, and erected twelve ornate memorial crosses at the places where her funeral cortège rested overnight, from Lincoln to Charing Cross in London. Three crosses—at Geddington, Hardingstone, and Waltham—survive.

Issue

Of her many children, most died young; her surviving son became Edward II of England, and her daughters included Joan of Acre and Margaret, Duchess of Brabant.
