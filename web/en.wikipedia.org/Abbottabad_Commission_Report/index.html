Abbottabad Commission Report

CommissionedJune 2011
HeadJustice Javed Iqbal
SubmittedJanuary 2013
SubjectUS raid that killed Osama bin Laden

The Abbottabad Commission Report is the report of a judicial commission established by the Government of Pakistan to investigate the circumstances surrounding the presence of Osama bin Laden in Pakistan and the unilateral United States special forces raid of 2 May 2011 that killed him in Abbottabad. The commission was headed by Justice Javed Iqbal of the Supreme Court of Pakistan.

Background

Bin Laden had lived for years in a large compound in Bilal Town, Abbottabad, close to the Pakistan Military Academy, before being killed by US Navy SEALs in Operation Neptune Spear. The raid, conducted without informing Pakistan, raised acute questions about Pakistani sovereignty and about how the world's most wanted man had remained undetected.

Findings

The commission interviewed more than 200 witnesses and produced a report of some 336 pages. It concluded that bin Laden had entered Pakistan in 2002 and lived in several locations before settling in Abbottabad in 2005. It found a collective failure of state institutions — "governance failure" and incompetence at many levels — while noting it could not rule out the involvement of rogue elements. It also criticised the US raid as a violation of sovereignty and an act of war. The report was submitted in January 2013; the government did not officially release it, but a leaked copy was published by Al Jazeera in July 2013.
