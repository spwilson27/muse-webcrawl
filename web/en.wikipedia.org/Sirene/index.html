Sirene

Sirene Country of origin: Bulgaria
Region: Balkans
Source of milk: Sheep, goat, cow, buffalo
Texture: Crumbly to creamy
Aging time: Minimum 45–60 days in brine

Sirene (Bulgarian: сирене), also known as sirenje in North Macedonia, is a type of brined white cheese originating from Bulgaria and widely produced throughout the Balkans. It is traditionally made from the milk of sheep, goats, cows or buffalo, or from mixtures of these milks, and is matured and stored in brine, which gives it a characteristically salty taste and a crumbly to creamy texture.

Production

Sirene is produced by curdling milk with rennet, cutting and pressing the curd, and then immersing the resulting blocks in a salt brine for a minimum period of around 45 to 60 days. During this ripening the cheese develops its flavour and firm texture. Industrial and artisanal varieties differ in fat content and firmness; firmer sirene is preferred for grating and cooking, while softer versions are eaten fresh.

Culinary use

Sirene is a staple of Bulgarian and Balkan cuisine. It is eaten on its own, crumbled over salads — most famously in shopska salad together with tomatoes, cucumbers and peppers — grated over dishes such as banitsa, moussaka and French fries, and used as a filling for pastries and stuffed peppers. It is broadly comparable to Greek feta, which is protected under EU law, and sirene occupies a similar culinary niche across the region.

Protected status

In 2023 Bulgarian white brined cheese, known as Byalo salamureno sirene, was registered in the European Union as a Protected Designation of Origin product, recognising its traditional production methods and link to Bulgarian territory.
