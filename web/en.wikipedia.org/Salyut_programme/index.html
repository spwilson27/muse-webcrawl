The Salyut programme (Russian: Салют, "salute" or "fireworks") was the first space station programme, undertaken by the Soviet Union. It involved a series of four crewed scientific research space stations and two crewed military reconnaissance space stations over a period of 15 years, from 1971 to 1986. Two other Salyut launches failed. Salyut was intended as a civilian programme, while the parallel Almaz stations were flown under the Salyut name to conceal their military nature.

History

Salyut 1, launched on 19 April 1971, was the world's first space station. Its first crew, Soyuz 10, failed to dock properly, but the Soyuz 11 crew spent 23 days aboard before tragically dying during re-entry when a pressure valve failed — the only fatalities above the Kármán line. The station was deorbited after 175 days.

Second-generation stations (Salyut 6 and Salyut 7) added a second docking port, allowing uncrewed Progress resupply craft and longer expeditions. Salyut 7, the final station, operated until 1986 and hosted the first woman to perform a spacewalk, Svetlana Savitskaya. The programme's experience led directly to the modular Mir station and later to the Russian segment of the International Space Station.
