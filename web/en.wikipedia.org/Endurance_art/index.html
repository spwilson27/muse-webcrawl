Endurance art

Endurance art is a form of performance art in which the artist subjects the body to prolonged hardship, pain, deprivation, or extreme repetition, often over hours, days, or even years. Emerging in the 1960s and 1970s alongside body art and happenings, endurance works test the limits of the artist and implicate the audience as witness to real suffering or extraordinary discipline.

Notable works and artists

Chris Burden had himself shot in the arm (Shoot, 1971) and crucified on a Volkswagen (Trans-Fixed, 1974). Marina Abramović's early works, including Rhythm 0 (1974), in which the audience was invited to use objects on her passive body, explored endurance and trust; her later The Artist Is Present (2010) involved sitting silently for over 700 hours at MoMA. Tehching Hsieh's one-year performances (1978–1986) included living in a cage, punching a time clock every hour, and remaining outdoors for a year. In China, artists such as Zhang Huan developed endurance-based performances involving the body in extreme conditions.

Themes

Endurance art engages with time, mortality, the relationship between artist and audience, and the politics of the body. Critics note the risk of self-harm and raise questions about spectacle and authenticity, while defenders argue that genuine risk and duration distinguish the work from theatre: the body is not representing suffering but undergoing it.
