Kolmogorov–Smirnov test

In statistics, the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test (K–S test) is a nonparametric test of the equality of continuous probability distributions. It can be used to compare a sample with a reference distribution (one-sample test) or two samples with each other (two-sample test).

Statistic

The test statistic is the supremum (maximum) absolute difference between the empirical distribution function of the sample and the cumulative distribution function of the reference distribution — or between the two empirical distributions in the two-sample case. Its null distribution was derived from the work of Andrey Kolmogorov (1933) and Nikolai Smirnov (1939).

Properties

The test is distribution-free and sensitive to differences in both location and shape of distributions, though it is less powerful than specialized tests for normality and has limitations with discrete data and estimated parameters.
