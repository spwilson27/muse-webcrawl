Mount Ruapehu

Elevation2,797 m (9,177 ft)
LocationTongariro National Park, New Zealand
TypeActive stratovolcano
Last eruption2007

Mount Ruapehu is an active stratovolcano at the southern end of the Taupō Volcanic Zone on New Zealand's North Island and, at 2,797 metres, the island's highest point. It lies in Tongariro National Park and carries the North Island's only glaciers and its two largest ski areas, Whakapapa and Tūroa, on its northwestern and southwestern slopes.

Crater Lake and lahars

The summit plateau encloses a warm, acidic Crater Lake whose level and temperature fluctuate with volcanic activity. When the lake's rim of ash and ice fails, the outpouring water generates lahars — volcanic mudflows — down the mountain's rivers. The most devastating occurred on Christmas Eve 1953, when a lahar destroyed the Tangiwai railway bridge minutes before the Wellington–Auckland express arrived, killing 151 people in New Zealand's worst railway disaster. A monitored lahar warning system and a bund to protect the ski infrastructure followed the smaller 2007 lahar, which caused no injuries.

Eruptions

Major eruptions in 1945, 1969, 1975 and 1995–96 spread ash across the central North Island, closed the ski fields and disrupted air traffic; a brief eruption in September 2007 threw rocks that seriously injured a climber sheltering in the summit hut. The volcano is continuously monitored by GeoNet.
