J2000

J2000 is a standard epoch in astronomy, defined as noon Terrestrial Time on 1 January 2000 (Julian date 2451545.0 TT). The prefix "J" indicates it is a Julian epoch.

Use

Celestial coordinates, such as right ascension and declination in the International Celestial Reference System, are conventionally given for the J2000 epoch. Because the Earth's axis precesses, coordinates referred to J2000 must be transformed when high accuracy at other dates is required, using precession, nutation, and proper motion corrections. Earlier standard epochs include B1950.0 (Besselian) and J1900.

Related standards

The J2000 ecliptic and equinox define the reference frame used by most modern star catalogues and by the ephemerides of the Jet Propulsion Laboratory. In computer systems, the J2000 epoch is also used as a numerical time reference, for example in some telemetry and spacecraft systems counting seconds since J2000.
