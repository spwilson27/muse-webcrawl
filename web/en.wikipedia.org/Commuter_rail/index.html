Commuter rail

Commuter rail, or suburban rail, is a passenger rail transport service that primarily operates within a metropolitan area, connecting commuters to a central city from outlying suburbs and commuter towns. Generally, commuter rail systems are considered distinct from rapid transit (metro) and light rail, using heavier rolling stock, sharing tracks with freight or intercity trains in many systems, and operating at lower frequencies outside peak hours.

Characteristics

Commuter rail services usually run on existing mainline railway infrastructure, often with dedicated or loco-hauled trains, bilevel coaches, or electric/diesel multiple units. Service is typically oriented toward peak-period flows into the city centre in the morning and outward in the evening, though many networks now offer all-day, clock-face schedules. Fares are often integrated with other public transport through regional transport authorities.

Examples

Notable systems include the S-Bahn networks of Germany, Austria, and Switzerland; the RER in Paris; Cercanías in Spain; the suburban railways of London; and in North America, New York's Long Island Rail Road and Metro-North Railroad, Chicago's Metra, and Toronto's GO Transit. Australia's major cities operate extensive electric suburban networks, and Mumbai's suburban railway is among the busiest in the world.
