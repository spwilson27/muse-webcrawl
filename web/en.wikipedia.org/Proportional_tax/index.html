Proportional tax

A proportional tax is a tax imposed so that the tax rate is fixed, with no change as the taxable base amount increases or decreases. The amount of the tax is in proportion to the amount subject to taxation. It contrasts with a progressive tax, where the rate increases with the base, and a regressive tax, where the effective rate decreases.

Flat taxes

A proportional income tax is often called a flat tax. Several countries, particularly in Eastern Europe, adopted flat income taxes in the 1990s and 2000s. Consumption taxes such as a uniform sales tax or value-added tax are also proportional to the transaction amount, though their burden relative to income may be regressive if lower-income households consume a larger share of their earnings.

Debate

Supporters cite simplicity and uniform treatment; critics argue that equal rates imply unequal sacrifice, since the marginal utility of money declines with income.
