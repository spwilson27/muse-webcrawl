Pudong, officially the Pudong New Area, is a district of Shanghai, China, occupying the land east of the Huangpu River — its name literally means "the east bank of the Huangpu". Until its development was launched in 1990 the area was largely farmland; within three decades it became Shanghai's financial and commercial centre and a symbol of China's economic opening.

Development

In April 1990 the Chinese government announced the development and opening of Pudong as a special economic zone. Its centrepiece is Lujiazui, the finance and trade zone across the river from the Bund, where a cluster of supertall skyscrapers rose: the Oriental Pearl Tower (1994), the Jin Mao Tower (1999), the Shanghai World Financial Center (2008), and the Shanghai Tower (2015), at 632 metres China's tallest building. Pudong also hosts the Shanghai Stock Exchange, the Zhangjiang Hi-Tech Park, the Waigaoqiao Free Trade Zone, Shanghai Pudong International Airport (opened 1999, linked to the city by the world's first commercial maglev line), and Shanghai Disneyland (2016).

Administration

Pudong functions as a district of the Shanghai municipality with sub-provincial administrative powers; in 2009 it absorbed the former Nanhui District, roughly doubling its area. Its GDP and population are the largest of any Chinese district-level division.
