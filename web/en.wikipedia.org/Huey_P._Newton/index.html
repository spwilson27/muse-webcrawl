Huey Percy Newton (17 February 1942 – 22 August 1989) was an American political activist who co-founded the Black Panther Party with Bobby Seale in Oakland, California, in 1966. As the party's Minister of Defense, he articulated its Ten-Point Program and its strategy of armed self-defence patrols, community "survival programs" such as Free Breakfast for Children, and revolutionary internationalism.

Early life

Newton was born in Monroe, Louisiana, the youngest of seven children, and grew up in Oakland. Functionally illiterate at the end of high school, he taught himself to read using Plato's Republic, then studied at Merritt College and read law, Marx, Lenin, Frantz Fanon and Che Guevara.

The Black Panther Party

The Panthers combined Black nationalism with Marxist class analysis. Newton's 1967 arrest after a shootout in which police officer John Frey was killed made him an international symbol; convicted of voluntary manslaughter in 1968, he was freed after the conviction was overturned, and charges were dismissed in 1971. He developed the theory of "intercommunalism" and, after a period of exile in Cuba (1974–1977) to avoid further charges, earned a PhD in social philosophy from UC Santa Cruz in 1980 with a dissertation on the FBI's COINTELPRO campaign against the Panthers. His autobiography, Revolutionary Suicide, appeared in 1973.

Death

Newton was shot and killed in West Oakland in 1989 during a drug-related dispute; his killer, Tyrone Robinson, was convicted of first-degree murder.
