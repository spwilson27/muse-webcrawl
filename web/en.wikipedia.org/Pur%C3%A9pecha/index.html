Purépecha

Purépecha may refer to the Purépecha people, an indigenous people of the Mexican state of Michoacán, or to their language, also called Purépecha (or Tarascan). Before the Spanish conquest, the Purépecha built one of the great states of pre-Columbian Mesoamerica.

The Purépecha state

The Purépecha Empire, centred on the Lake Pátzcuaro basin with its capital at Tzintzuntzan, was the second-largest state in Mesoamerica at the time of the Spanish arrival. Skilled in metallurgy — working copper, bronze, gold, and silver — the Purépecha repeatedly resisted expansion by the Aztec (Mexica) Empire, whose armies they defeated in the late 15th century. The last ruler, Tangáxoan II, submitted to Spanish forces in 1530 and was executed in 1530s.

Language

The Purépecha language is a language isolate with no proven relatives. It is agglutinative, with a rich system of suffixes, and is spoken today by well over one hundred thousand people, mainly in the highlands of Michoacán. Efforts in bilingual education and broadcasting support its continued use.

Culture

Purépecha communities are known for crafts including copper work in Santa Clara del Cobre, pottery, textiles, and lacquerware, and for celebrations such as the Dance of the Old Men (Danza de los Viejitos) and elaborate Day of the Dead observances around Lake Pátzcuaro.
