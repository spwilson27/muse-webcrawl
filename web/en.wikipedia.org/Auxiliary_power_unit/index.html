Auxiliary power unit

An auxiliary power unit (APU) is a device on a vehicle that provides energy for functions other than propulsion. The term is most familiar from aviation, where an APU — usually a small gas turbine in the tail cone of an airliner — supplies electrical power and compressed air for air conditioning and for starting the main engines while the aircraft is on the ground, independent of ground power units.

Aircraft use

The Boeing 727 of 1963 was among the first airliners designed with a gas-turbine APU, freeing operations from ground equipment. On modern twins the APU also serves as a backup electrical source in flight and is certified as part of ETOPS arrangements. APUs burn jet fuel, so airlines increasingly restrict their use on the ground in favour of gate power to cut emissions and noise.

Other vehicles

APUs are also fitted to tanks (to run systems without the main engine), trucks (for cab heating and air conditioning during rest periods), spacecraft, and some locomotives. In the M1 Abrams tank, for example, an APU greatly reduces fuel consumption and engine wear during silent watch.
