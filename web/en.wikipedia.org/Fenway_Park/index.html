Fenway Park is a baseball stadium in Boston, Massachusetts, home of the Boston Red Sox of Major League Baseball since 1912. It is the oldest active ballpark in MLB and one of the best-known sports venues in the United States, nicknamed "America's Most Beloved Ballpark".

Features

Fenway is famous for its quirky dimensions, a product of its tight urban footprint. The most iconic feature is the Green Monster, a 37-foot-high (11.3 m) wall in left field, topped since 2003 by the "Monster seats". Other landmarks include the lone red seat in the right-field bleachers marking the longest home run hit in the park (by Ted Williams in 1946), "Pesky's Pole" down the right-field line, and the manual scoreboard at the base of the Monster.

History

The park opened on 20 April 1912 — the same week the Titanic sank — with an Osborn Engineering design in steel and concrete. Threatened with replacement in the late 1990s, Fenway was instead renovated extensively between 2002 and 2011 by the Red Sox' new ownership, adding seats and amenities while preserving its historic character. It was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 2012, its centennial year. Besides baseball, Fenway has hosted football, hockey (the 2010 NHL Winter Classic), soccer and concerts.
