Joseph Schumpeter

Joseph Alois Schumpeter
Born: 8 February 1883, Triesch, Moravia
Died: 8 January 1950, Taconic, Connecticut
Known for: Creative destruction, entrepreneurship theory

Joseph Alois Schumpeter (8 February 1883 – 8 January 1950) was an Austrian political economist. He later became a US citizen and served as a Harvard University professor. He popularised the term "creative destruction" in economics.

Ideas

In The Theory of Economic Development (1911), Schumpeter placed the entrepreneur at the centre of economic change, driving innovation in new combinations. In Capitalism, Socialism and Democracy (1942) he argued that capitalism's own success would undermine its institutions — while coining the enduring phrase "creative destruction" for the process by which innovation displaces existing firms and products.

Career

Schumpeter briefly served as Austria's finance minister in 1919, taught at Graz, Czernowitz and Bonn, and moved to Harvard in 1932, where he remained until his death. His posthumous History of Economic Analysis (1954) is a landmark survey of the field.
