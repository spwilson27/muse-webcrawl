Bimini

CountryThe Bahamas
LocationWesternmost district of the Bahamas, ~80 km east of Miami, Florida
Main islandsNorth Bimini, South Bimini

Bimini is the westernmost district of the Bahamas and comprises a chain of islands located about 80 kilometres (50 mi) east of Miami, Florida. The largest islands are North Bimini and South Bimini. Bimini is the Bahamian island group closest to the United States mainland.

History

The islands were inhabited by the Lucayan people before European contact. During Prohibition in the United States, Bimini became a centre for rum-running. Ernest Hemingway lived on Bimini during the 1930s and wrote about its big-game fishing; the islands' waters later feature in the legends surrounding the Bimini Road, an underwater rock formation some have claimed is man-made.

Tourism and fishing

Bimini is renowned for sport fishing, particularly for marlin and bonefish, as well as diving with sharks and dolphins. Alice Town on North Bimini is the district's administrative and commercial centre.
