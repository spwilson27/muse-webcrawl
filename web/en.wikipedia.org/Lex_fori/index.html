Lex fori

In conflict of laws (private international law), the lex fori (Latin: "the law of the forum") is the law of the jurisdiction in which a legal action is brought. It is contrasted with the lex causae, the law chosen by the forum's choice-of-law rules as applicable to the substance of the dispute.

Application

Traditionally, matters of procedure are governed by the lex fori, on the principle that a court applies its own procedural rules, while substantive matters are governed by the lex causae. The difficult borderline between substance and procedure — covering such questions as limitation periods, heads of damages, and rules of evidence — has generated extensive case law; many modern systems now treat limitation periods as substantive and therefore governed by the lex causae.

Related concepts

Related Latin tags include lex loci contractus (law of the place of the contract), lex loci delicti commissi (law of the place where the tort was committed), lex domicilii (law of domicile), and lex situs (law of the place where property is situated). Where the lex fori and a foreign law differ, the forum characterises the issue, applies its choice-of-law rules, and may refuse the foreign law on public policy grounds or where renvoi applies.
