Dimension

In physics and mathematics, the dimension of a mathematical space (or object) is informally defined as the minimum number of coordinates needed to specify any point within it. Thus, a line has a dimension of one (1D) because only one coordinate is needed to specify a point on it; a surface, such as the boundary of a cylinder or sphere, has a dimension of two (2D); and the inside of a cube, cylinder, or sphere is three-dimensional (3D).

From a point (0D) to a cube (3D).

In mathematics

The dimension of a vector space is the number of vectors in any basis for the space. More general notions include the Lebesgue covering dimension in topology, the Hausdorff dimension (which may be non-integer for fractals), and Krull dimension in algebra. The concept is central to linear algebra, geometry, and the study of manifolds.

In physics

Classical physics describes space as three-dimensional and time as a fourth dimension in spacetime. Some theories, such as string theory, postulate additional compactified spatial dimensions. Separately, dimensional analysis in physics concerns the physical dimensions of quantities—such as length, mass, and time—rather than spatial dimensionality.
