Canterbury, New Zealand

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Canterbury (Māori: Waitaha) is a region of New Zealand, located in the central-eastern South Island. The region covers an area of 44,503 square kilometres, making it the largest region in New Zealand by area. Its largest city, and the South Island's largest, is Christchurch.

Geography

Canterbury stretches from the Conway River in the north to the Waitaki River in the south, and from the Pacific Ocean coast to the Southern Alps, whose highest point, Aoraki / Mount Cook, lies on its western boundary. The broad alluvial Canterbury Plains, built by braided rivers such as the Waimakariri and Rakaia, dominate the lowland landscape.

History

The region was settled by Māori, principally the iwi Ngāi Tahu, from around the 14th century. Organised European settlement began in 1850 with the Canterbury Association's settlers, who founded Christchurch. The region was struck by a devastating earthquake in February 2011 that killed 185 people and caused widespread damage in Christchurch.

Economy

Agriculture, particularly dairy farming enabled by large-scale irrigation, is the mainstay of the regional economy, alongside tourism and the services centred on Christchurch.
