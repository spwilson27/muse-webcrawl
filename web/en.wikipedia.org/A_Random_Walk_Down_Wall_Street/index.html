A Random Walk Down Wall Street

AuthorBurton Malkiel
Published1973 (first edition)
SubjectInvesting, personal finance

A Random Walk Down Wall Street is a book by Princeton economist Burton Malkiel, first published in 1973 and revised in many subsequent editions. It is one of the most influential popular books on investing.

Thesis

Malkiel argues, drawing on the random walk hypothesis and the efficient-market hypothesis, that asset prices typically reflect all available information, so that short-term price movements cannot be reliably predicted and investors cannot consistently beat the market through stock selection or market timing. He reviews historical speculative manias, from tulipmania to the dot-com bubble, and critiques both technical analysis ("charting") and overreliance on fundamental analysis.

Recommendations

The practical prescription of the book is that most investors should hold a diversified portfolio of low-cost, broad-market index funds, rebalanced periodically and adjusted for age and risk tolerance. Malkiel's advocacy helped popularize index investing for individual investors.

Reception

The book has sold well over a million copies and remains a standard recommendation for beginning investors, although its strong efficiency claims continue to be debated by behavioral economists and active managers.
