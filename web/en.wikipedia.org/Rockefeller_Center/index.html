Rockefeller Center

Rockefeller Center is a large complex of commercial buildings in Midtown Manhattan, New York City, built during the Great Depression by the Rockefeller family. The original Art Deco complex of 14 buildings was constructed between 1931 and 1940 on land leased from Columbia University.

Features

The centrepiece is 30 Rockefeller Plaza ("30 Rock"), a 259 m skyscraper housing NBC Studios and the Top of the Rock observation deck. The complex includes Radio City Music Hall, the Channel Gardens, and the sunken Lower Plaza, famous for its ice rink and annual Christmas tree, overlooked by the gilded Prometheus statue. It was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1987.

History

Conceived by John D. Rockefeller Jr. after plans for a new Metropolitan Opera house collapsed in the 1929 crash, the project provided some 40,000 construction jobs during the Depression and set a model for integrated urban development combining offices, entertainment, art, and public space.
