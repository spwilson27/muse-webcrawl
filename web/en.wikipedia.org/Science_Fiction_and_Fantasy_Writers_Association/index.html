Science Fiction and Fantasy Writers Association

The Science Fiction and Fantasy Writers Association (SFWA, pronounced "sif-wah") is a nonprofit professional organization of science fiction and fantasy writers. Founded in 1965 by Damon Knight under the name Science Fiction Writers of America, SFWA has over 2,000 members worldwide. It administers the Nebula Awards, among the most prestigious honors in the field.

History

Damon Knight founded the organization in 1965 after his work with the Milford Writer's Workshop. In 1992 the name was changed to Science Fiction and Fantasy Writers of America to reflect the inclusion of fantasy writers, while keeping the SFWA abbreviation; in 2022 the name was changed again to Science Fiction and Fantasy Writers Association. The organization has long advocated for writers through sample contracts, its Writer Beware program against publishing scams, a legal fund, and an emergency medical fund for members.

Awards

The Nebula Awards are voted on by SFWA members and presented annually for works in categories including Novel, Novella, Novelette, Short Story, and dramatic presentation (the Ray Bradbury Award) and works for young readers (the Andre Norton Award). SFWA also names a Damon Knight Memorial Grand Master in recognition of lifetime achievement, and confers the Kevin O'Donnell Jr. Service to SFWA Award and the Kate Wilhelm Solstice Award.

Membership

Membership is open to professionally published authors of science fiction and fantasy who meet qualifying income or sales criteria, with additional tiers for associate and affiliate members such as writers with qualifying short fiction sales. The organization was incorporated as a 501(c)(3) nonprofit in 2014.
