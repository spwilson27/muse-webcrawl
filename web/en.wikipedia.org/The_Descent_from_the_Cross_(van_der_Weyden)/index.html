The Descent from the Cross (van der Weyden)


The Descent from the Cross (c. 1435) by Rogier van der Weyden, Museo del Prado, Madrid.

The Descent from the Cross (or Deposition of Christ) is a panel painting by the Early Netherlandish artist Rogier van der Weyden, painted around 1435 for the chapel of the Crossbowmen's Guild in Leuven. It depicts the lowering of the body of Christ from the Cross, attended by the Virgin Mary, Saint John, Joseph of Arimathea, Nicodemus, Mary Magdalene, and others. One of the most influential works of Northern Renaissance art, it is now in the Museo del Prado in Madrid.

Composition

Van der Weyden compresses ten figures into a shallow, gilded, box-like space resembling a carved altarpiece shrine. The body of Christ and the swooning Virgin are placed in parallel curves — her pose rhyming with his — so that her faint mirrors his death and emphasizes her role as co-sufferer. The enamel-like colours, meticulously rendered tears, and individualized faces convey intense, restrained grief. Small crossbows in the tracery at the upper corners identify the patrons, the Leuven guild of crossbowmen.

History

The painting stood in the guild's chapel in the church of Our Lady Outside the Walls in Leuven until it was acquired by Mary of Hungary, governor of the Habsburg Netherlands, in 1548 or 1549. It later entered the Spanish royal collection — a copy by Michiel Coxcie was substituted in Leuven — and hung in the hunting lodge of El Pardo and the Escorial before being transferred to the Prado. During the Spanish Civil War it was evacuated to Geneva with other Prado treasures and returned in 1939.

Reception

The work was copied and adapted across Europe throughout the 15th and 16th centuries and helped spread van der Weyden's fame to Spain and Italy. Art historians regard it as perhaps the most influential Netherlandish painting of Christ's Passion.
