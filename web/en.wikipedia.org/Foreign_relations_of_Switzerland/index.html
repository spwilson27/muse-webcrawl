Foreign relations of Switzerland

The foreign relations of Switzerland are shaped above all by the country's long-standing policy of permanent armed neutrality, recognised internationally at the Congress of Vienna in 1815. Switzerland has not participated in a foreign war since 1815 and maintains a foreign policy of neutrality, mediation and the promotion of international law.

Neutrality and diplomacy

Switzerland is a member of the United Nations (since 2002), the Council of Europe, the OSCE, the OECD, EFTA and the Schengen Area, and is a signatory to many international treaties, but it is not a member of the European Union or NATO; voters rejected EEA membership in 1992, and closer EU ties are governed by a network of bilateral agreements. Geneva hosts the European headquarters of the United Nations, the Red Cross movement (the ICRC was founded there), the WTO and many other international organisations, and Switzerland frequently acts as a protecting power and mediator — for example representing U.S. interests in Iran since 1980.

Good offices

Swiss "good offices" have included hosting peace talks and summits, providing protecting-power mandates, and humanitarian diplomacy. Swiss foreign policy priorities are typically grouped into peace and security, prosperity, sustainability and digital foreign policy.
