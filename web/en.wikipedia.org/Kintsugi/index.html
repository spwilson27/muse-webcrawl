Kintsugi (Japanese: 金緂ぎ, "golden joinery"), also known as kintsukuroi ("golden repair"), is the Japanese art of repairing broken pottery by mending the areas of breakage with urushi lacquer dusted or mixed with powdered gold, silver, or platinum. As a philosophy, it treats breakage and repair as part of the history of an object rather than something to disguise: the repaired piece's golden seams are emphasised rather than hidden.

Origins

The practice is traditionally dated to the late 15th century, when, according to legend, the shogun Ashikaga Yoshimasa sent a damaged Chinese tea bowl back to China for repair. It was returned held together with ugly metal staples, prompting Japanese craftsmen to develop a more elegant, aesthetic method of repair. Kintsugi is closely associated with the tea ceremony and the aesthetics influenced by Zen Buddhism, particularly wabi-sabi.

Technique

Broken fragments are cleaned and joined with urushi lacquer — the sap of the lacquer tree, which is also a strong natural adhesive — built up in several layers. The final layer is sprinkled with gold or other precious metal powder before it sets, then polished. Large missing areas may be reconstructed entirely from lacquer. A complete repair can take weeks because each lacquer layer must cure in warm, humid conditions.

Philosophy and modern popularity

Kintsugi embodies ideas central to Japanese aesthetics: wabi-sabi (beauty in imperfection and impermanence), mottainai (regret over waste), and the acceptance of change. In the 21st century the technique has been widely adopted internationally as a craft and embraced metaphorically — in popular psychology and self-help literature, the mended bowl serves as an image of resilience: that people, like the vessel, can become more beautiful for having been broken and repaired.

Related techniques

  • Yobitsugi – joining shards from different vessels into one patchwork piece.
  • Gintsugi – the same method using silver rather than gold.
  • Urushi-tsugi – repair with plain lacquer, without precious metals.
