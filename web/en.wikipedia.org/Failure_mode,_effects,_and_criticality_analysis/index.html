Failure mode, effects, and criticality analysis

Failure mode, effects and criticality analysis (FMECA) is an extension of failure mode and effects analysis (FMEA). In addition to the basic FMEA, it includes a criticality analysis, which is used to chart the probability of failure modes against the severity of their consequences.

Methodology

The analysis proceeds by identifying, for each component of a system, the ways in which it might fail (failure modes), the causes and effects of each failure, and the severity of those effects. A criticality rating is then assigned, often by combining severity classifications with failure probability levels, so that the most critical failure modes can be prioritised for corrective action.

History

FMECA was developed in the 1940s by the United States military, and was formalised in the military standard MIL-P-1629 in 1949. It was adopted by NASA for the Apollo programme and is now widely used in aerospace, defence, automotive, and medical device industries. The governing standard is MIL-STD-1629A.
