35P/Herschel–Rigollet is a periodic comet with an orbital period of about 155 years, placing it among the Halley-type comets. It is named for its discoverer, Caroline Herschel, and for Roger Rigollet, the French astronomer who recovered it at its next return 151 years later.

Discovery

Caroline Herschel discovered the comet on 21 December 1788 while sweeping the skies at Slough, England, with a small Newtonian telescope; it was the third of the eight comets she found. The comet was observed into early 1789, and several astronomers attempted to compute its orbit, but the short arc of observations made a reliable period determination impossible at the time.

Recovery

On 28 July 1939, Roger Rigollet at Lagny, France, discovered what he believed to be a new comet. Comparison of its orbit with that of Herschel's comet of 1788 showed the two objects were identical, with a period of roughly 155 years. The 1939 apparition was followed until early 1940 and confirmed the identification beyond doubt.

Orbit

The comet moves in a highly elliptical, retrograde orbit typical of Halley-type comets, with a perihelion distance of about 0.75 AU, inside the orbit of the Earth. Its returns are infrequent: after the apparitions of 1788–89 and 1939–40, the next perihelion passage is expected around the year 2092.
