Interpupillary distance

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Interpupillary distance (IPD) is the distance between the centres of the pupils of the two eyes, usually expressed in millimetres. It is an important measurement in optometry and ophthalmology for fitting eyeglasses, and in the design of binocular instruments, stereoscopic displays and virtual-reality headsets. IPD determines the lateral offset between the visual axes of the eyes and hence affects binocular vision and depth perception.

Typical values

In adults, IPD typically ranges from about 52 to 78 mm, with average values around 63–64 mm for men and 61–62 mm for women; children's values are smaller and increase with growth. Because the eyes converge when focusing on near objects, the effective IPD for near vision is slightly smaller — typically by 2–4 mm — than that for distance vision.

Measurement

IPD may be measured with a millimetre ruler held across the brow (anatomical PD), with a pupillometer that uses corneal reflections, or by digital facial scanning. Eyeglass prescriptions may record a binocular PD (one number for both eyes) or monocular PDs (from the centre of the nose to each pupil), which account for facial asymmetry and are especially important for progressive lenses.

Applications

  • Eyeglasses: lens optical centres are positioned in the frame to coincide with the wearer's pupils; a mismatch can induce unwanted prism and eyestrain.
  • Binoculars and microscopes: the separation of the eyepieces is adjustable — binocular barrels hinge — to match the user's IPD.
  • Stereoscopy and VR: rendering two viewpoints at the correct separation produces a natural sense of scale and depth; virtual and augmented-reality headsets offer physical or software IPD adjustment, and incorrect settings can cause discomfort.
  • Anthropometry and ergonomics: IPD statistics inform the design of head-mounted equipment and facial-recognition research.
