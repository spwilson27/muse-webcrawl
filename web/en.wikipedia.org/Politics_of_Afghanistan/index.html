The politics of Afghanistan have been shaped by decades of conflict and shifting regimes. Since the Taliban's return to power in August 2021, following the withdrawal of United States and NATO forces and the collapse of the Islamic Republic of Afghanistan, the country has been governed as the Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan, an unrecognised de facto government dominated by the Taliban movement.

Islamic Emirate (2021–present)

Authority in the Emirate is concentrated in the Supreme Leader (Amir al-Mu'minin), Hibatullah Akhundzada, based in Kandahar, who rules by decree with the advice of clerical councils. A cabinet headed by an acting prime minister administers ministries in Kabul. The 2004 constitution of the former republic was suspended, and no constitution for the Emirate had been formally promulgated; governance rests on the leadership's interpretation of Hanafi Islamic law. Women have been excluded from most public life, secondary education and many forms of employment.

Islamic Republic (2004–2021)

The republic established by the 2004 constitution was a presidential system with a directly elected president, a bicameral National Assembly (the Wolesi Jirga and Meshrano Jirga), and an elected judiciary, supported by extensive international aid and a foreign military presence. Presidents Hamid Karzai (2004–2014) and Ashraf Ghani (2014–2021) led the republic; its politics were characterised by weak formal parties, powerful regional strongmen, disputed elections, and a persistent Taliban insurgency.

Earlier history

Afghanistan was a monarchy under the Durrani and Barakzai dynasties until the 1973 coup that proclaimed a republic; the 1978 Saur Revolution brought a communist government, followed by the Soviet–Afghan War (1979–1989), civil war, and the first Taliban emirate (1996–2001), which was overthrown after the 2001 US-led invasion.
