Kitchen stove

A modern domestic range cooker with hob and oven.

A kitchen stove, also called a range or cooker, is a kitchen appliance designed for cooking food, combining a cooktop (hob) with burners or heating elements and usually an enclosed oven. It is the direct descendant of the closed fireboxes developed in the 18th century and replaced the open hearth as the centre of domestic cooking in the 19th century.

History

Early enclosed stoves included designs by Benjamin Thompson, Count Rumford, in the 1790s, whose "Rumford stove" confined the fire and directed its heat efficiently to several pots. Cast-iron wood- and coal-burning ranges became standard in the 1800s. The first practical gas stoves were patented in the 1820s–1850s and spread with urban gas networks; electric stoves followed from the 1890s and became common in the 20th century. In 1971 the first consumer induction cooktops appeared, using electromagnetic fields to heat the pan directly.

Types

  • Gas stoves burn natural gas or LPG through open flame burners, giving immediate visual control of heat.
  • Electric stoves use coiled elements, radiant ceramic glass, or halogen lamps.
  • Induction stoves heat ferromagnetic cookware by induction; they are fast, efficient and keep the surface relatively cool.
  • Solid-fuel ranges, such as the Aga cooker, burn wood or coal and often heat water or the room as well.
