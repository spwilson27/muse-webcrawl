Tenerife

Country: Spain
Autonomous community: Canary Islands
Province: Santa Cruz de Tenerife
Capital: Santa Cruz de Tenerife
Area: 2,034 km²
Highest point: Teide, 3,715 m

Tenerife is the largest and most populous island of the Canary Islands, an autonomous community of Spain in the Atlantic Ocean off the coast of northwest Africa. It has a population of about 970,000, making it also the most populous island of Spain. Its capital, Santa Cruz de Tenerife, is the seat of the island council (cabildo insular) and shares the status of capital of the Canary Islands with Las Palmas de Gran Canaria.

Geography

The island is dominated by Mount Teide, a dormant volcano that at 3,715 metres is the highest peak in Spain and the third-largest volcano in the world from its base. Teide National Park is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the most visited national parks in the world. Tenerife's climate is mild and spring-like year-round, with wet, forested northern slopes (including the ancient laurisilva forests of Anaga) contrasting with the sunny, arid south.

History and tourism

Before the Castilian conquest completed in 1496, Tenerife was inhabited by the indigenous Guanche people and divided into nine kingdoms (menceyatos). In 1797 Horatio Nelson lost his arm in a failed attack on Santa Cruz. In 1977 the island's Los Rodeos airport was the site of the deadliest accident in aviation history, when two Boeing 747s collided on the runway, killing 583 people. Today tourism is the economic mainstay, drawing around five to six million visitors a year to resorts such as Playa de las Américas and Los Cristianos, and the Carnival of Santa Cruz de Tenerife is one of the largest in the world.
