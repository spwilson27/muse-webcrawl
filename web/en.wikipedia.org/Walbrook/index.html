Walbrook is a subterranean river in the City of London, one of London's many lost rivers. It gives its name to a street in the City, running from the Bank junction area south to Cannon Street, and to Walbrook ward, one of the 25 wards of the City.

The river

The stream rose in the marshy ground of Moorfields (in the vicinity of modern Finsbury/Shoreditch) and flowed south through the Roman city of Londinium, dividing it roughly in two, before entering the Thames near Dowgate. In Roman times it was a significant waterway used for transport and industry; the Temple of Mithras stood on its eastern bank, and a major Roman port and quay have been excavated along its course. The name may derive from Old English weala broc, "stream of the foreigners (Britons)", or from the stream having run along the Roman city wall.

As London grew, the Walbrook was progressively culverted and built over; by the late medieval period it was largely covered, and today it flows entirely underground as part of the sewer system, reaching the Thames at Dowgate through a culvert.

The street and ward

Walbrook street, which follows part of the valley, is home to the Mansion House (the Lord Mayor's residence, at its northern end), the church of St Stephen Walbrook — a masterpiece of Christopher Wren — and the Bloomberg London building, beneath which the reconstructed London Mithraeum opened in 2017. The ward of Walbrook elects one alderman and several common councillors to the City of London Corporation.
