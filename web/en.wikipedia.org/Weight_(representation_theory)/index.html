Weight (representation theory)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In the mathematical field of representation theory, a weight of an algebra A over a field F is an algebra homomorphism from A to F, or equivalently a one-dimensional representation of A over F. It is the algebra analogue of a character of a group. In the representation theory of semisimple Lie algebras, weights play a central role: a representation decomposes into weight spaces, the simultaneous eigenspaces for a Cartan subalgebra, each labelled by a weight.

Weights of Lie algebra representations

Let g be a semisimple Lie algebra with Cartan subalgebra h. A weight is a linear functional λ on h. For a representation V of g, the weight space Vλ consists of vectors v such that H·v = λ(H)v for all H in h. The nonzero weights of the adjoint representation are the roots. Weights that take integer values on the coroots are called integral, and those pairing nonnegatively with all simple coroots are dominant.

Highest weight theory

Every finite-dimensional irreducible representation of a semisimple Lie algebra has a unique highest weight with respect to a chosen ordering, and it is determined up to isomorphism by that weight. The highest weights that occur are exactly the dominant integral weights. The dimensions of weight spaces and characters of representations are computed by formulas such as the Weyl character formula and the Kostant partition function.

Applications

Weight theory underlies the classification of representations of Lie groups and algebraic groups, and appears in physics in the eightfold way classification of hadrons, where the weights of representations of SU(3) organise particles into multiplets.
