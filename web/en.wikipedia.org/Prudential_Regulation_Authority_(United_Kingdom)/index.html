The Prudential Regulation Authority (PRA) is a part of the Bank of England and is responsible for the prudential regulation and supervision of banks, building societies, credit unions, insurers and major investment firms in the United Kingdom. It regulates around 1,500 institutions.

History

The PRA was created by the Financial Services Act 2012 as part of the post-financial-crisis restructuring of UK financial regulation, which abolished the Financial Services Authority. The "twin peaks" model split prudential supervision (PRA) from conduct regulation (the Financial Conduct Authority). The PRA formally launched on 1 April 2013 as a subsidiary of the Bank of England; since 2017 it has been an integral part of the Bank.

Objectives

The PRA's primary objective is to promote the safety and soundness of the firms it regulates, and it has a secondary objective relating to insurance policyholder protection, plus a secondary competition objective. It works closely with the Bank's Financial Policy Committee, which addresses systemic risks to financial stability.
