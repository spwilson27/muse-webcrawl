Pope Julius II

Pope Julius II (born Giuliano della Rovere; 5 December 1443 – 21 February 1513) was head of the Catholic Church and ruler of the Papal States from 1 November 1503 to his death in 1513. Nicknamed the Warrior Pope or the Fearsome Pope, he chose his papal name not in honour of Julius I but in emulation of Julius Caesar. He consolidated the Papal States as a major Italian power and was the foremost patron of the High Renaissance, commissioning Michelangelo's Sistine Chapel ceiling and beginning the rebuilding of St Peter's Basilica.

Early career

Giuliano was raised by his uncle, Pope Sixtus IV, who showered him with benefices and made him a cardinal in 1471. As a cardinal he amassed great wealth and influence, built the Palazzo della Cancelleria, and rivalled Rodrigo Borgia, whose election as Alexander VI drove him into exile in France, where he urged French intervention in Italy. After Alexander's death and the 26-day pontificate of Pius III, he was elected pope almost unanimously in 1503.

Pontificate

Julius personally led military campaigns to recover papal territories in the Romagna and Umbria, subduing Perugia and Bologna, and formed the League of Cambrai (1508) against Venice, then reversed alliances with the Holy League (1511) to drive the French from Italy. He convened the Fifth Lateran Council (1512) against a schismatic council backed by France. As patron, he demolished the ancient St Peter's and laid the foundation stone of the new basilica in 1506 (architect: Bramante), commissioned Michelangelo to paint the Sistine Chapel ceiling (1508–1512) and to carve his (never-completed) tomb, and employed Raphael to decorate the Vatican Stanze. He also founded the Swiss Guard in 1506.

Legacy

Julius II left the Papal States stronger and the papacy more monarchical, but his wars and building projects depended on revenues — including indulgences — that soon provoked Martin Luther's protest. Erasmus satirised him in the dialogue Julius Excluded from Heaven. His tomb by Michelangelo, completed in reduced form in San Pietro in Vincoli, includes the famous statue of Moses.
