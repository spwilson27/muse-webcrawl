Anthraquinone, also called anthracenedione or dioxoanthracene, is an aromatic organic compound with the formula C14H8O2. It is a derivative of anthracene in which two CH groups are replaced by carbonyl (C=O) groups. Anthraquinone is a yellow, highly crystalline solid that is poorly soluble in water but dissolves in hot organic solvents.

Synthesis

Anthraquinone is prepared industrially by several routes, including the oxidation of anthracene, the Friedel–Crafts reaction of benzene with phthalic anhydride in the presence of aluminium chloride, and the Diels–Alder reaction of naphthoquinone with butadiene followed by oxidation.

Applications

Anthraquinone derivatives are widely used as dyes and pigments, including alizarin, one of the oldest known natural dyes. Anthraquinone is also used as a catalyst in the production of hydrogen peroxide via the anthraquinone process, as a seed dressing, and as a bird repellent. In papermaking it serves as a pulping additive that increases yield.

Derivatives

Important derivatives include anthraquinone-2-sulfonic acid, hydroxyanthraquinones such as emodin and chrysophanol found in rhubarb and senna, and the anthracycline anticancer drugs such as doxorubicin, which contain a tetracyclic quinone structure.
