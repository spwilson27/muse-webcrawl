Cammell Laird is a British shipbuilding company based at Birkenhead on the River Mersey. One of the most famous names in British shipbuilding, the yard has built many notable Royal Navy vessels over nearly two centuries.

History

The firm traces its origin to 1824, when William Laird founded an ironworks at Birkenhead; his son John Laird built some of the earliest iron steamships and constructed the Confederate raider CSS Alabama in 1862, a cause célèbre of the American Civil War. In 1903 Laird Brothers merged with the steel and armaments firm of Charles Cammell & Co. to form Cammell Laird. The yard built battleships, carriers, and submarines in both world wars — including HMS Ark Royal (launched 1937), U-class submarines, and the troopship RMS Mauretania (1938).

Decline and revival

Nationalised in 1977 as part of British Shipbuilders, the yard was privatised in the 1980s but closed in 1993 after completing the submarine HMS Unicorn (SSN 19/S99). The site was revived under new ownership as a ship repair and conversion business from 2001, and the Cammell Laird name returned to shipbuilding in the 2010s, winning the contract for the polar research ship RRS Sir David Attenborough (launched 2018) and, with partners, work on the Royal Navy's Type 26 and Fleet Solid Support programmes.
