The Walton family is an American family based in Arkansas, best known as the heirs of Sam Walton (1918–1992), founder of the retail giant Walmart, and his wife Helen Walton (1919–2007). Collectively the family is regularly ranked among the wealthiest families in the world, with a fortune derived mainly from their stake in Walmart, the world's largest retailer by revenue.

Sam and Helen Walton

Sam Walton opened the first Walmart store in Rogers, Arkansas, in 1962, after earlier success operating Ben Franklin variety stores in Arkansas and Missouri. The company went public in 1970 and expanded rapidly by bringing discount retailing to small-town America. Helen Walton, a graduate of the University of Oklahoma, was a business partner in the ventures and later a prominent philanthropist. After Sam's death in 1992, his fortune was divided among Helen and their four children: Rob, John, Jim, and Alice.

The second generation

S. Robson "Rob" Walton (born 1944), the eldest son, served as chairman of Walmart's board from 1992 until 2015. John T. Walton (1946–2005), a decorated Vietnam War veteran and keen outdoorsman, died in a crash of an experimental aircraft in Wyoming in 2005. Jim Walton (born 1948) heads Arvest Bank, the family's banking business, and served on Walmart's board. Alice Walton (born 1949), the only daughter, is an art collector who founded the Crystal Bridges Museum of American Art in Bentonville, Arkansas, which opened in 2011, and for many years was ranked among the richest women in the world.

Wealth and holdings

The family controls roughly half of Walmart's shares through the holding company Walton Enterprises and a family trust, an arrangement designed to maintain family influence over the company across generations. Individual members regularly appear at or near the top of the Forbes list of billionaires. In 2015, Greg Penner, Rob Walton's son-in-law, succeeded him as Walmart chairman, marking the third generation of family leadership on the board.

Philanthropy

The Walton Family Foundation, founded in 1987, is one of the largest family foundations in the United States, giving in areas including K-12 education reform, environmental conservation (especially freshwater and ocean conservation), and community development in Arkansas and the Mississippi Delta. The family has also invested heavily in Bentonville, building museums, trails, and cultural amenities that transformed the small town into an unlikely centre of corporate and cultural gravity.

Criticism

The family's wealth and Walmart's business practices — including wages, labour relations, and effects on local businesses — have attracted sustained criticism from labour organizations and journalists, and the family's political spending has been a subject of public debate.
