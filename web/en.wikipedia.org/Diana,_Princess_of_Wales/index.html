Diana, Princess of Wales (born Diana Frances Spencer; 1 July 1961 – 31 August 1997), was a member of the British royal family. She was the first wife of Charles III (then Prince of Wales) and the mother of Princes William and Harry. Her activism and glamour made her an international icon, and she remains one of the most popular members of the royal family in history.

Life and marriage

Diana was born into the Spencer family, a branch of the British nobility. She grew up at Park House, Sandringham, and at Althorp, the family seat. Her engagement to Prince Charles was announced in February 1981, and their wedding at St Paul's Cathedral on 29 July 1981 was watched by a global television audience of some 750 million people. The marriage produced two sons but was troubled by incompatibility and extramarital affairs; the couple separated in 1992 and divorced in 1996.

Charity work and public role

As Princess of Wales, Diana undertook royal duties and championed causes including HIV/AIDS awareness — famously shaking hands with an AIDS patient in 1987 — and campaigned for the international ban on landmines, walking through a cleared minefield in Angola months before her death. Her candour about her struggles, including the 1995 Panorama interview, reshaped relations between the monarchy and the media.

Death and legacy

Diana died in a car crash in the Pont de l'Alma tunnel in Paris in the early hours of 31 August 1997, alongside Dodi Fayed and their driver Henri Paul. Her funeral at Westminster Abbey drew an estimated 2.5 billion television viewers. An inquest in 2008 returned a verdict of unlawful killing through grossly negligent driving. Memorials include the Diana Memorial Fountain in Hyde Park, and her sons continue much of her charitable work.
