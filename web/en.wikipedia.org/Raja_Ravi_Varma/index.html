Raja Ravi Varma

Born29 April 1848, Kilimanoor, Travancore
Died2 October 1906 (aged 58), Attingal, Travancore
Known forPainting, oleographs
Notable workShakuntala, Lady in the Moonlight, Hamsa Damayanti

Raja Ravi Varma (29 April 1848 – 2 October 1906) was an Indian painter and artist, considered among the greatest painters in the history of Indian art. His works are among the best examples of the fusion of European academic art with a purely Indian sensibility and iconography. He is particularly noted for making affordable lithographs of his paintings available to the public, which greatly enhanced his reach and influence as a painter and a public figure.

Early life

Ravi Varma was born at Kilimanoor Palace in the princely state of Travancore (present-day Kerala). He showed an early talent for drawing and was patronised by Ayilyam Thirunal, the Maharaja of Travancore, who arranged for him to study oil painting under the British artist Theodore Jenson. He also learned the techniques of watercolour from Rama Swamy Naidu.

Career

Varma won wide acclaim after winning an award for an exhibition of his paintings at Vienna in 1873. His paintings were also sent to the World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago in 1893, where he was awarded two gold medals. He travelled throughout India in search of subjects, frequently depicting Hindu goddesses and scenes from the Indian epics, including the Mahabharata and the Ramayana.

In 1894, Varma established the Ravi Varma Press in Bombay, which produced oleographs of Hindu deities and mythological scenes. These inexpensive prints brought art into ordinary Indian homes and helped shape the visual culture of Hindu devotion and, later, Indian calendar art.

Legacy

Ravi Varma is often criticised by modernists for his academic style, yet his influence on Indian popular culture is immense. His depictions of the goddesses Lakshmi and Saraswati became the standard images reproduced across the country. The Government of Kerala awards the Raja Ravi Varma Puraskaram in his honour for excellence in art.
