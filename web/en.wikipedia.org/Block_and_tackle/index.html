Block and tackle

A block and tackle or pulley system is a system of two or more pulleys (blocks) with a rope or cable threaded between them, used to lift or pull heavy loads with a mechanical advantage. The system trades the distance the hauling end is pulled against the force required, so a load can be raised with a fraction of the force it would otherwise need, at the cost of pulling proportionally more rope.

Mechanical advantage

In an ideal (frictionless) system, the mechanical advantage equals the number of rope segments supporting the moving block and its load. A "double tackle" with four supporting segments can, in principle, lift a load four times the applied force. In practice, friction in the sheaves reduces the effective advantage.

Terminology and use

The fixed block is attached to an anchor point, while the moving block is attached to the load. Tackles are named by their arrangement — gun tackle, luff tackle, double tackle — and have long been used on sailing ships for hoisting sails and cargo, in construction, in rescue operations, and in theatre rigging.
