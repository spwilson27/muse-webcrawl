The skeleton is the structural framework of an animal's body. In vertebrates it is composed of bone and cartilage, supports soft tissues, protects organs, enables movement with muscles, stores minerals such as calcium, and produces blood cells in bone marrow.

Types

Skeletons may be external (exoskeletons of arthropods and molluscs), internal (endoskeletons of vertebrates), or hydrostatic (fluid-based, in soft invertebrates). The human skeleton at birth has about 270 bones, many fusing to 206 in adulthood.

Human Skeleton

It divides into the axial skeleton (skull, vertebral column, ribs, sternum — 80 bones) and appendicular skeleton (limbs and girdles — 126 bones). Bones are living tissue remodelled throughout life by osteoblasts and osteoclasts.

Functions and Disorders

Beyond support and protection, the skeleton serves as a mineral reservoir and endocrine organ. Disorders include fractures, osteoporosis, arthritis, and scoliosis; a stroke or injury can also affect mobility of the skeleton's joints.
