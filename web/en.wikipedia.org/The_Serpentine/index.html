The Serpentine

The Serpentine is a 40-acre (16 ha) recreational lake in Hyde Park, London, England, created in 1730 at the order of Queen Caroline, wife of George II. It takes its name from its snakelike, curving shape. The part of the lake west of the Serpentine Bridge, in Kensington Gardens, is known as the Long Water.

History

Unlike the artificial, straight-sided lakes then fashionable, the Serpentine was among the first lakes designed to look natural. It was formed by damming the Westbourne river; since 2012 it has been supplied by boreholes into the chalk aquifer. The lake has hosted celebrated events, including the Great Exhibition's festivities in 1851 and the triathlon and marathon swimming events of the 2012 London Olympics.

Recreation

The Serpentine Lido, opened in 1930, offers open-water swimming; the Serpentine Swimming Club, founded in 1864, holds its famous Peter Pan Cup race every Christmas Day. Rowing boats and pedalos are available for hire, and the banks host the Serpentine Galleries and the Diana, Princess of Wales Memorial Fountain nearby. Solar-powered boats and a rich population of waterfowl make it one of central London's best-loved landscapes.
