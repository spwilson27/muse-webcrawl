Assault rifle

An assault rifle is a selective-fire rifle that uses an intermediate cartridge and a detachable magazine. Assault rifles were first put into mass production and accepted into widespread service during the final years of World War II and became the standard service rifles of most of the world's armies.

Characteristics

The defining features are a cartridge intermediate in power between pistol and full-power rifle rounds (such as 7.62×39mm or 5.56×45mm NATO), selective fire (semi-automatic and fully automatic or burst modes), an effective range of roughly 300–400 metres, and feeding from a detachable box magazine typically holding 20–30 rounds.

History

The German StG 44 of 1944 is generally regarded as the first assault rifle; its name Sturmgewehr — "storm (assault) rifle" — gave the class its name. The Soviet AK-47, designed by Mikhail Kalashnikov and adopted in 1949, became the most widely produced firearm in history. The American M16 family, firing the smaller 5.56×45mm cartridge, established the other major lineage, followed by designs such as the FAMAS, Steyr AUG and Heckler & Koch G36.

Terminology

The military term should be distinguished from "assault weapon", a legal and political term used in some jurisdictions, especially the United States, for certain semi-automatic firearms based on cosmetic or ergonomic features.
