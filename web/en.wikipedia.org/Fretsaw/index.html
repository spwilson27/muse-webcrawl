Fretsaw

The fretsaw is a bow saw used for intricate cutting work, particularly in wood, which often involves tight curves. The tool takes its name from its use in making fretwork — ornamental designs cut into wood or other materials — and is ultimately derived from the French fretter, meaning a lattice or grill.

Description

The fretsaw consists of a fine, narrow blade held under tension in a deep U-shaped or rectangular frame. The deep throat of the frame allows the user to cut well inside a workpiece, far from its edges, which distinguishes it from the shallower coping saw. The blade can be rotated in the frame so that curves can be cut in any direction, and it can be detached, passed through a pre-drilled hole in the work, and reattached to make enclosed cuts.

Use

Fretsaws are used by hand for marquetry, intarsia, jigsaw puzzles, model making, and delicate veneer work. The powered equivalent is the scroll saw, which uses a reciprocating fretsaw-type blade clamped in a machine frame. Blades are made in many tooth counts; finer blades give smoother cuts in thin material but break more easily.
