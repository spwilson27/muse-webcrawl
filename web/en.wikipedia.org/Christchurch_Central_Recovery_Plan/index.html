Christchurch Central Recovery Plan

The Christchurch Central Recovery Plan, often called the Blueprint, is the recovery plan for the central city of Christchurch, New Zealand, following the devastating earthquakes of 2010 and 2011, in particular the February 2011 earthquake that killed 185 people and destroyed much of the central business district.

Background

The Canterbury Earthquake Recovery Authority (CERA) was established by the government to lead the recovery. Christchurch City Council first produced a draft plan informed by the "Share an Idea" public consultation, and in April 2012 the minister directed CERA to produce the final recovery plan. The Blueprint was announced on 30 July 2012 by Prime Minister John Key.

Contents

The plan defined a more compact central city core framed by a new green "Frame" of open space, and identified anchor projects intended to catalyse private investment: a convention centre, a stadium, a metro sports facility, a central library (later built as Tūranga), a justice and emergency services precinct, an innovation precinct, and the Avon River precinct, along with a retail precinct and performing arts facilities. Land was acquired compulsorily where necessary to assemble sites.

Assessment

The Blueprint has been both praised for giving certainty to the rebuild and criticised for delays, cost overruns, and the slow delivery of some anchor projects such as the stadium and convention centre (Te Pae, opened 2021).
