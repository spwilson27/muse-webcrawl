Philip II, Duke of Burgundy (17 January 1342 – 27 April 1404), known as Philip the Bold, was a French prince of the Valois dynasty and the founder of the Burgundian branch of the House of Valois. The fourth son of King John II of France, he earned his epithet fighting beside his father at the Battle of Poitiers in 1356.

Duke of Burgundy

In 1363 his father granted him the Duchy of Burgundy, left vacant by the death of Philip of Rouvres. His marriage in 1369 to Margaret III, Countess of Flanders, brought Flanders, Artois and Franche-Comté under Burgundian control on her father's death in 1384, laying the foundations of the Burgundian state in the Low Countries.

Regency and patronage

Philip was one of the uncles who governed France during the minority and mental illness of Charles VI. A lavish patron of the arts, he founded the Chartreuse de Champmol near Dijon as a dynastic mausoleum, adorned with sculpture by Claus Sluter. He died in 1404 and was succeeded by his son John the Fearless.
