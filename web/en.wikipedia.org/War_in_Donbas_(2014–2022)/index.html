War in Donbas (2014–2022)

The war in Donbas was an armed conflict in the Donbas region of eastern Ukraine, part of the broader Russo-Ukrainian War, which began in April 2014 when armed Russian-backed separatists seized government buildings in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts following Russia's annexation of Crimea and Ukraine's Revolution of Dignity. It continued until Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022 subsumed it into a wider war.

Course of the war

Ukraine launched an "Anti-Terrorist Operation" to retake the seized cities. Fighting escalated in the summer of 2014, during which Malaysia Airlines Flight MH17 was shot down over the conflict zone, killing all 298 aboard. Regular Russian forces intervened decisively at Ilovaisk (August 2014) and Debaltseve (early 2015). The Minsk agreements produced a ceasefire line but never a settlement, and positional trench warfare with artillery exchanges and snipers continued for years while the OSCE Special Monitoring Mission documented repeated violations.

Humanitarian impact

According to the UN, more than 3,400 civilians were killed and some 1.5 million people internally displaced. On 21 February 2022 Russia recognised the DPR and LPR as independent; three days later it launched its full-scale invasion, ending the distinct Donbas phase of the war.
