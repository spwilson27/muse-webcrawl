Reichstag Fire Decree

The fire of 27 February 1933 was the decree's immediate pretext.

The Reichstag Fire Decree, formally the Decree of the Reich President for the Protection of People and State (Verordnung des Reichspräsidenten zum Schutz von Volk und Staat), was issued by President Paul von Hindenburg on 28 February 1933, the day after the Reichstag fire. Drafted by Adolf Hitler's cabinet and issued under Article 48 of the Weimar Constitution, it suspended most civil liberties guaranteed by that constitution for the remainder of the Nazi regime's existence.

Contents

Section 1 suspended, "until further notice", the constitutional articles guaranteeing personal liberty, freedom of expression and of the press, freedom of assembly and association, privacy of post and telephone communications, and protections against house searches and confiscation. Section 2 allowed the Reich government to take over the powers of state governments that failed to maintain order. The decree imposed the death penalty for a range of offences including arson of public buildings and high treason.

Consequences

The decree provided the legal basis for the arrest of some 4,000 Communist Party (KPD) functionaries and activists in its first days, the suppression of the Social Democratic and Communist press, and the establishment of the first concentration camps for "protective custody" (Schutzhaft) prisoners. It was never repealed: it remained in force for the twelve years of Nazi rule and is widely regarded by historians as a decisive legal step in the destruction of the Weimar Republic and the creation of the dictatorship, complemented three weeks later by the Enabling Act.
