Green River Formation

AgeEocene, c. 53.5–48.5 million years ago
TypeLake sediment (lacustrine) deposits
LocationColorado, Utah and Wyoming, US
Famous forExceptionally preserved fossils; oil shale
Key siteFossil Butte National Monument

The Green River Formation is a geological formation of Eocene age preserving the sediments of a system of ancient intermountain lakes in what is now Colorado, Utah and Wyoming. It is one of the world's great fossil Lagerstätten and also one of the largest oil-shale deposits on Earth.

The ancient lakes

Between about 53.5 and 48.5 million years ago, uplift during the Laramide orogeny created basins occupied by three long-lived lakes: Lake Gosiute in Wyoming, Lake Uinta in Utah and Colorado, and the smaller Fossil Lake in southwestern Wyoming. Their finely laminated mudstones, deposited in still, often oxygen-poor bottom waters, preserved organisms in exquisite detail.

Fossils

The formation is renowned for articulated fossil fish — Knightia, the Wyoming state fossil, the predatory Diplomystus and stingrays — by the thousands, recovered from Fossil Butte and commercial quarries. The fauna also includes early horses, primates, bats (including Icaronycteris and the clawed Onychonycteris), crocodilians, turtles, birds with preserved feathers, palm leaves and insects, documenting a subtropical ecosystem in the warm Eocene greenhouse climate.

Oil shale

The formation's kerogen-rich oil shales, particularly the Mahogany Zone, are estimated to contain on the order of a trillion barrels or more of shale oil in place — among the largest hydrocarbon resources known — though the kerogen is not petroleum and commercial extraction has repeatedly proven uneconomic and water-intensive, and no sustained production has been established.
