The Message (song)

"The Message" is a song by the American hip hop group Grandmaster Flash and the Furious Five, released on Sugar Hill Records in 1982. Widely regarded as one of the most important recordings in hip hop history, it brought social commentary — a stark portrait of inner-city poverty and despair — to a genre then dominated by party music.

Background and content

The song was largely written by Duke Bootee (Ed Fletcher) and Melle Mel, whose verses describe rats, roaches, broken glass, junkies, and the pressures of ghetto life, culminating in the famous refrain "Don't push me 'cause I'm close to the edge / I'm trying not to lose my head." Despite the group's initial reluctance to record it, the track became a hit, reaching the top five of the US R&B chart.

Legacy

"The Message" was the first hip hop recording added to the National Recording Registry of the Library of Congress, and the first hip hop song inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame. Rolling Stone ranked it among the greatest songs of all time, and it is credited with establishing rap as a vehicle for serious social and political expression.
