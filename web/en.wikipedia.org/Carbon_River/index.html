The Carbon River is a river in Pierce County, Washington, in the United States. It flows from the Carbon Glacier on the north flank of Mount Rainier northwestward to join the Puyallup River near Orting. The river is named for the coal deposits found along its banks.

Course and history

The upper valley forms the northwestern entrance area of Mount Rainier National Park, reached via the Carbon River Road. The valley was the site of coal mining and the town of Fairfax in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Flooding in 2006 washed out sections of the park road, which was converted to a trail for hikers and cyclists beyond the entrance.

Environment

The lower river supports salmon runs, while the upper valley preserves some of the finest inland temperate rainforest in the park, with old-growth Douglas-fir, cedar, and hemlock.
