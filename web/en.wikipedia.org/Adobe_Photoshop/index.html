Adobe Photoshop is a raster graphics editor developed and published by Adobe Inc. for Windows and macOS. It was created in 1987 by the American brothers Thomas and John Knoll, who initially developed it under the name Display while Thomas was a PhD student; Adobe licensed the software in 1988 and released Photoshop 1.0 in 1990 for Macintosh. It has since become the industry standard for digital image processing and editing, and its name is widely used as a verb ("to photoshop") for image manipulation.

Photoshop edits images in layers and supports masks, alpha compositing, numerous colour models (RGB, CMYK, Lab and others), spot colours and duotones. It includes tools for retouching, painting, selections, filters and transformations, and can import and export many graphics file formats; its native PSD format preserves layers and is supported across Adobe's Creative Cloud applications such as Illustrator, Premiere Pro and After Effects.

Distribution and features

Originally sold as a perpetual licence, Photoshop has been offered since 2013 by subscription through Adobe Creative Cloud, with versions for iPad and a web version following. Recent releases have added AI-driven features built on Adobe's Firefly models, including content-aware fill enhancements and Generative Fill, which can extend or alter images from text prompts.

Cultural impact

Photoshop transformed photography, advertising and publishing, enabling seamless composite imagery while also fuelling long-running debates about manipulated photographs in journalism and unrealistic beauty standards in media.
