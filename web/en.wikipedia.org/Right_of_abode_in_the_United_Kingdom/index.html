Legal basis: Immigration Act 1971, as amended
Effect: Freedom from immigration control; right to live and work in the UK

Right of abode in the United Kingdom

The right of abode in the United Kingdom is a statutory immigration status that gives a person complete freedom from UK immigration control: the holder may live, work, and enter and leave the country without restriction or leave to remain. It was created by the Immigration Act 1971 and took effect on 1 January 1973.

Who has it

All British citizens automatically have the right of abode. Certain Commonwealth citizens who held it before the British Nationality Act 1981 came into force on 1 January 1983 also retained it — principally those with a parent born in the UK, and Commonwealth citizen women married to a man with the right of abode. Since 1983 no new grants have been possible except through British citizenship.

Proof and certificates

Holders who are not British citizens may apply for a certificate of entitlement to the right of abode, placed in their passport as evidence. Unlike other statuses, the right of abode cannot generally be lost through absence from the UK, though certificates can be revoked in narrow circumstances such as deception or exclusion conducive to the public good.
