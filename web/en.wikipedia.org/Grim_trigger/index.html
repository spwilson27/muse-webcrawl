Grim trigger

FieldGame theory
Game typeRepeated (iterated) games
StrategyCooperate until the opponent defects once; then defect forever
Example gameRepeated prisoner's dilemma

Grim trigger (also known as the grim strategy or simply grim) is a trigger strategy in the theory of repeated games. A player using grim trigger cooperates in the first round and continues to cooperate as long as the other player has always cooperated — but if the opponent ever defects, the grim player defects in every subsequent round, permanently. The punishment is thus triggered once and never lifted.

In the repeated prisoner's dilemma

In the infinitely repeated prisoner's dilemma, mutual cooperation is sustainable as a subgame perfect equilibrium when both players use grim trigger and are sufficiently patient (that is, when the discount factor is high enough that the long-run loss of cooperation outweighs the one-shot gain from defecting). Because the punishment is the harshest possible — reversion to mutual defection forever — grim trigger supports cooperation for the widest range of discount factors among simple strategies, a result related to the folk theorem.

Limitations

Grim trigger is unforgiving and therefore fragile in practice. If defections can occur by mistake — through error, noise or misperception — a single accident collapses cooperation permanently, and the strategy performs poorly compared with forgiving strategies such as tit for tat or win-stay, lose-shift in noisy environments. It is also not renegotiation-proof: after a defection, both players would prefer to "renegotiate" back to cooperation rather than carry out the punishment, which can undermine its credibility. Variants with finite punishment periods ("contrite" or limited-trigger strategies) are studied as more robust alternatives.
