Doubling the cube

Doubling the cube: constructing a cube with twice the volume of a given cube

Doubling the cube, also known as the Delian problem, is an ancient geometric problem. Given the edge of a cube, the problem requires the construction of the edge of a second cube whose volume is double that of the first. As with the related problems of squaring the circle and trisecting the angle, doubling the cube is now known to be impossible to construct by using only a compass and straightedge, but even in ancient times solutions were known that employed other methods.

The Delian legend

According to legend, the citizens of Delos consulted the oracle of Apollo in order to learn how to defeat a plague sent by the gods. The oracle responded that they must double the size of the altar of Apollo, which was in the shape of a regular cube. The Delians doubled each side of the altar, but the plague continued, revealing that the oracle had meant they should double the altar's volume while keeping its shape. The problem was taken up by Plato and the geometers of his academy.

Mathematical formulation

If the original cube has edge length 1, its volume is 1. A cube of double volume has volume 2, so its edge length is the cube root of 2, approximately 1.2599. The problem reduces to constructing a segment of length ∛2 from a unit segment using only compass and unmarked straightedge.

Impossibility proof

In 1837 Pierre Wantzel proved that the cube root of 2 is not a constructible number. Constructible lengths must lie in field extensions of the rationals whose degree is a power of two; but ∛2 is a root of the irreducible polynomial x³ − 2, and so has degree 3, which is not a power of two. Hence the construction is impossible with the permitted tools.

Solutions with other tools

The problem can be solved using a conic section, as Menaechmus discovered by intersecting two parabolas, or using a marked ruler (neusis construction), or by origami, where the cube root can be obtained via Beloch's fold. Hippocrates of Chios reduced the problem to finding two mean proportionals between a segment and its double.
