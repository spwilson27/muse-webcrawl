Dené–Yeniseian languages

Dené–Yeniseian is a proposed language family linking the Yeniseian languages of central Siberia with the Na-Dené languages of North America. If valid, it would be the first well-demonstrated linguistic connection between the Old and New Worlds.

The proposal

The hypothesis was formally presented by linguist Edward J. Vajda of Western Washington University at a symposium in Fairbanks, Alaska, in February 2008. Vajda compared the surviving Yeniseian language Ket, and records of extinct relatives such as Yugh, Kott, and Pumpokol, with Na-Dené languages including Tlingit, Eyak, and the Athabaskan family.

Evidence

The evidence cited includes systematic correspondences in verb morphology — particularly the complex prefixing verb template both families share — along with cognate vocabulary items such as words built on roots for "stone" and other basic terms. Both families build verbs with a strikingly similar arrangement of positional prefixes, a feature unusual among the world's languages.

Reception

The proposal received a cautiously favorable reception from many specialists in Na-Dené and Yeniseian linguistics, though it remains unproven to the standard of a fully demonstrated family. It has been connected to archaeological and genetic research suggesting back-migrations between Siberia and North America across the Bering region.
