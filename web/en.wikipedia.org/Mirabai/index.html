Mirabai

Bornc. 1498, Kudki, Rajasthan
Diedc. 1547, Dwarka (traditional)
OccupationPoet, saint
TraditionBhakti movement; Krishna devotion

Mirabai (c. 1498 – c. 1547), also spelled Meera, was a Hindu mystic poet and devotee of Krishna, and one of the most celebrated saints of the Bhakti movement. Born into a Rathore Rajput royal family at Kudki in present-day Rajasthan, she is said to have regarded Krishna as her true husband from childhood. Married into the royal house of Mewar, she defied courtly expectations by devoting herself publicly to Krishna, singing and dancing in temples and keeping company with wandering devotees.

According to popular tradition, her devotion scandalised her in-laws, who persecuted her — legends tell of a poisoned cup that turned to nectar and a basket of flowers that concealed a snake. After her husband's death she left Mewar, travelling to Vrindavan and finally Dwarka, where tradition holds she merged into the image of Krishna.

Poetry and legacy

Hundreds of devotional songs (bhajans and pads) are attributed to Mirabai in Hindi, Rajasthani and Gujarati; because they circulated orally for centuries, few if any survive in her own autograph. Her verses express longing for Krishna with an intensity that crossed caste and gender boundaries, and she remains an enduring symbol of devotion and of resistance to social constraint. Her songs are still sung across India, and her life has inspired films, plays and scholarship.
