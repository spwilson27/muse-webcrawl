Digital India

Digital India is a campaign launched by the Government of India to ensure that government services are made available to citizens electronically by improved online infrastructure and by increasing Internet connectivity, and to make the country digitally empowered in the field of technology. The initiative was launched by Prime Minister Narendra Modi on 1 July 2015.

The programme is centred on three key vision areas: digital infrastructure as a core utility to every citizen, governance and services on demand, and digital empowerment of citizens.

Pillars

Digital India rests on nine pillars of growth areas: broadband highways, universal access to mobile connectivity, public internet access programmes, e-governance reform, e-Kranti (electronic delivery of services), information for all, electronics manufacturing, IT for jobs, and early harvest programmes.

Services and platforms

Major platforms associated with the initiative include DigiLocker, a cloud-based store for official documents; UMANG, a unified app for government services; the Unified Payments Interface (UPI) for instant bank-to-bank mobile payments; Aadhaar, the biometric identity system; and Common Service Centres delivering digital services in rural areas. Other schemes, such as the Pradhan Mantri Gramin Digital Saksharta Abhiyan, aim to spread digital literacy among rural households.

Reception and criticism

The programme has been credited with expanding mobile and internet penetration, catalysing a large digital-payments ecosystem and supporting a growing start-up sector. Critics have pointed to the persistent digital divide between urban and rural areas, concerns over data privacy and surveillance, and the exclusion risks faced by citizens who lack connectivity or digital skills when services move online.
