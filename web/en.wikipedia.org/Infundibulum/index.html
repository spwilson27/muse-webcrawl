Infundibulum

MeaningLatin for "funnel"
DomainAnatomy
ExamplesPituitary stalk, uterine tube opening, ethmoidal infundibulum, cardiac infundibulum

Infundibulum (from Latin infundibulum, a funnel) is an anatomical term for a funnel-shaped structure or passage in the body. Several distinct structures bear the name, in the brain, the heart, the female reproductive tract, the nasal cavity, the kidney, and the lungs.

Principal structures

The pituitary stalk, also called the infundibulum, connects the hypothalamus to the pituitary gland; it carries the hypothalamic–hypophyseal portal vessels and the axons whose hormones are released from the posterior pituitary. In the female reproductive system, the infundibulum is the funnel-shaped distal end of the uterine (fallopian) tube, fringed by fimbriae that sweep the ovulated oocyte into the tube. In the nose, the ethmoidal infundibulum is a curved channel into which the frontal and maxillary sinuses drain. In the heart, infundibulum (conus arteriosus) is the smooth outflow tract of the right ventricle leading to the pulmonary valve. The term also applies to the funnel-shaped calyceal recesses of the renal pelvis and to the stalk-like region where the gallbladder narrows toward the cystic duct.

Clinical significance

Compression or transection of the pituitary stalk — by tumours, trauma, or surgery — can cause hyperprolactinaemia and diabetes insipidus. Obstruction of the ethmoidal infundibulum is a key step in the development of chronic rhinosinusitis and is a target of functional endoscopic sinus surgery. In congenital heart disease, infundibular stenosis of the right ventricular outflow tract is one of the four components of tetralogy of Fallot.
