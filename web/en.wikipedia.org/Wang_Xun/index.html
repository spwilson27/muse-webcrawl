Wang Xun

Wang Xun (Chinese: 王恂; pinyin: Wáng Xùn; 1235–1281) was a Chinese mathematician and astronomer of the Yuan dynasty. He was a close collaborator of the astronomer Guo Shoujing and served as an official in the Astronomical Bureau under Kublai Khan. Wang is regarded as one of the leading mathematicians of medieval China.

Life

Wang Xun was born in Zhongshan (modern Dingzhou, Hebei). A child prodigy, he was presented to Kublai Khan and entered government service, where he rose to head the imperial astronomical establishment. Along with Guo Shoujing, Xu Heng, and others, he was charged with reforming the Chinese calendar.

Work

Wang played a leading mathematical role in creating the Shoushi calendar (授時曆, "Season-Granting Calendar"), promulgated in 1281, one of the most advanced calendars of its era, which fixed the tropical year at 365.2425 days — the same value later adopted by the Gregorian calendar. His mathematical work advanced the use of spherical trigonometry and finite-difference interpolation in Chinese astronomy.
