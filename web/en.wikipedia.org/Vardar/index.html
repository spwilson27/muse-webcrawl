Vardar

The Vardar (Macedonian: Вардар; Greek: Αξιός, Axios) is the longest river in North Macedonia and a major river of the southern Balkans. It is about 388 kilometres (241 mi) long, draining an area of roughly 20,500 km².

Course

The river rises at Vrutok, near Gostivar in western North Macedonia, and flows north and then southeast through the capital Skopje, past Veles and the Demir Kapija gorge, before crossing into Greece near Gevgelija. In Greece it takes the name Axios and empties into the Thermaic Gulf of the Aegean Sea west of Thessaloniki.

Significance

The Vardar valley forms the main north–south transport corridor of the region — the Morava–Vardar route linking Central Europe with the Aegean — and carries a motorway and railway. A strong northerly wind funnelling down the valley is known as the Vardaris. In Greek myth the Axios was associated with the region of Paeonia. The river's delta in Greece is an important wetland and bird habitat.
