Sir Michael Gideon Marmot (born 26 January 1945) is a British epidemiologist and Professor of Epidemiology and Public Health at University College London, where he directs the Institute of Health Equity. He is best known for his research demonstrating that social position and status are major determinants of health and longevity, and for the Whitehall Studies of British civil servants.

Research

The Whitehall Studies, launched in 1967 and followed by Whitehall II from 1985, followed thousands of civil servants and found a steep social gradient in health: mortality and illness increased progressively with each step down the employment hierarchy, even among people who were all employed and not poor. Marmot attributed much of the gradient to psychosocial factors such as control over one's work ("job strain") and status — the "status syndrome" explored in his book of that name (2004).

Policy work

Marmot chaired the World Health Organization's Commission on Social Determinants of Health (2005–2008), whose report Closing the Gap in a Generation influenced global health policy. In 2010 his Strategic Review of Health Inequalities in England, Fair Society, Healthy Lives (the Marmot Review), set out six policy objectives, including giving every child the best start in life and proportionate universalism — actions universal but scaled to disadvantage. A ten-year follow-up in 2020, Health Equity in England: The Marmot Review 10 Years On, reported stalling life expectancy and widening inequalities. He served as President of the World Medical Association (2015–16).
