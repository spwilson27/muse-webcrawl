Western clawed frog
Scientific name: Xenopus tropicalis
Family: Pipidae
Range: West Africa
Conservation status: Least Concern (IUCN)

The western clawed frog (Xenopus tropicalis) is a species of frog in the family Pipidae, found in the lowland rainforests and savanna wetlands of West Africa, from Senegal to Cameroon and south to Gabon. Like other pipids it is almost entirely aquatic, with a flattened body, eyes and nostrils on top of the head, and small black claws on the toes of its hind feet.

Description

Xenopus tropicalis is smaller than its better-known relative the African clawed frog (Xenopus laevis), reaching about 5 cm in length. It has smooth, mottled olive-brown skin, a lateral line system for detecting water movements, and no tongue or external ears. It feeds on aquatic invertebrates and small fish, using its clawed hind feet and sensitive fingers to shovel food into its mouth.

Model organism

The species has become an important model organism in developmental biology and genetics. Unlike X. laevis, which is allotetraploid, X. tropicalis is diploid, has a shorter generation time and a more compact genome, making it more suitable for genetic studies. Its genome was published in 2010, the first amphibian genome to be sequenced.
