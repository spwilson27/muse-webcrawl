Bayezid I (Ottoman Turkish: بايزيد اول; Turkish: I. Bayezid; c. 1360 – 8 March 1403), also known as Bayezid the Thunderbolt (Turkish: Yıldırım Bayezid), was the sultan of the Ottoman Empire from 1389 to 1402. He rapidly expanded Ottoman territory in the Balkans and Anatolia, but his reign ended in catastrophe at the Battle of Ankara, where he was defeated and captured by Timur.

Accession and Balkan conquests

Bayezid succeeded his father Murad I, who was killed at the Battle of Kosovo on 15 June 1389. Tradition holds that Bayezid, upon taking the throne on the battlefield, ordered the execution of his younger brother Yakub — often described as the first instance of royal fratricide later codified in Ottoman practice. He earned the epithet Yıldırım ("Thunderbolt") for the speed of his campaigns.

In the Balkans he reduced Serbia to vassalage, marrying Olivera Despina, daughter of the Serbian prince Lazar. In 1394 he began the first Ottoman blockade of Constantinople, which lasted intermittently until 1402. His greatest European victory came at the Battle of Nicopolis (25 September 1396), where he crushed a Crusader army of Hungarians, French, Burgundians, and others — the last large-scale crusade of the Middle Ages.

Anatolian campaigns

Turning east, Bayezid systematically annexed the Turkmen principalities (beyliks) of Anatolia — Saruhan, Aydın, Menteşe, Hamid, Germiyan, and Candar — alarming the region's remaining powers, including the Karamanids and the Mamluk Sultanate, and drawing the attention of Timur (Tamerlane), who claimed suzerainty over several dispossessed beys.

Battle of Ankara and death

After an exchange of insulting letters, Timur invaded Anatolia. At the Battle of Ankara on 20 July 1402, Timur's army decisively defeated the Ottomans; many of Bayezid's Anatolian troops defected to their former beys, and the sultan was captured. Later accounts that he was kept in an iron cage are generally regarded by historians as exaggeration, though he was treated harshly and died in captivity at Akşehir on 8 March 1403.

Legacy

Bayezid's capture plunged the empire into the Ottoman Interregnum (1402–1413), a civil war among his sons, from which Mehmed I emerged as reunifier. Bayezid is credited with creating the first standing Ottoman central army built around the Janissaries and sipahi cavalry supported by the timar system, with centralising provincial administration and court patronage, including the Bayezid I Mosque complex in Bursa.
