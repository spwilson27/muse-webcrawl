Cosplay

TypePerformance art / hobby
OriginsScience fiction conventions, United States and Japan
Term coined1984, by Nobuyuki Takahashi

Cosplay, a blend of "costume" and "play", is a performance art and hobby in which participants called cosplayers wear costumes and accessories to represent specific characters from anime, manga, video games, films, comic books, and other media. Cosplayers often interact to create a subculture, and the practice is a prominent feature of fan conventions worldwide.

History

Costuming at fan conventions dates to the first World Science Fiction Convention in 1939, where attendees wore futuristic costumes. The Japanese journalist Nobuyuki Takahashi coined the term "cosplay" after attending the 1984 Worldcon in Los Angeles, and the practice grew rapidly in Japan during the 1990s before spreading globally.

Costumes

Cosplay costumes vary greatly in complexity, from purchased outfits to elaborate hand-made garments involving sewing, armour fabrication with materials such as EVA foam and thermoplastics, wig styling, make-up, and props. Competitions at conventions judge craftsmanship and performance, and events such as the World Cosplay Summit in Nagoya draw international participants.

Culture

Cosplay is closely associated with anime and manga fandom but encompasses characters from Western media as well. Photography is a major part of the culture, with dedicated photographers and online communities sharing images. Debates within the community address topics such as accuracy, body image, consent in photography, and crossplay (portraying a character of a different gender).
