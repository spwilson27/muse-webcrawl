Le Vite
AuthorGiorgio Vasari
LanguageItalian
Published1550; enlarged 1568
SubjectArtists' biographies

The Lives of the Most Excellent Painters, Sculptors, and Architects (Italian: Le Vite de' più eccellenti pittori, scultori, e architettori), commonly called Vasari's Lives, is a series of biographies of Renaissance artists by the Italian painter and architect Giorgio Vasari. First published in 1550 and issued in a greatly expanded second edition in 1568, it is regarded as the founding work of art history.

Contents

The work traces Italian art from Cimabue and Giotto through three ages of progress to a culmination in Michelangelo, the only living artist given a full life in the first edition. It contains biographies of dozens of painters, sculptors and architects, combining anecdotes, descriptions of works, and Vasari's critical judgements.

Influence and accuracy

Vasari's biographies shaped the canon of Renaissance art and popularised the idea of a rebirth (rinascita) of art after medieval decline. His account favours Florentine and Roman artists and contains errors and inventions, so modern scholars use it critically, but it remains an irreplaceable primary source, written by a man who knew many of his subjects personally.
