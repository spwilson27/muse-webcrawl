Mac (computer)

Mac, short for Macintosh, is a family of personal computers designed, manufactured, and sold by Apple Inc. since January 1984. The original Macintosh was the first mass-market personal computer featuring a graphical user interface, built-in screen, and mouse, and the line remains Apple's flagship computer family, running the macOS operating system.

History

The Macintosh project began at Apple in the late 1970s under Jef Raskin and was reshaped by Steve Jobs, who drew inspiration from the Xerox Alto's graphical interface. The Macintosh 128k launched on 24 January 1984, introduced by Ridley Scott's famous "1984" Super Bowl advertisement. Early models used Motorola 68000-series processors; Apple migrated to PowerPC chips in 1994, to Intel x86 processors in 2006, and to its own Apple silicon (beginning with the M1 chip) in 2020, each transition delivered via emulation layers such as Rosetta.

Product line

The current Mac range includes the MacBook Air and MacBook Pro laptops, the all-in-one iMac, the compact Mac mini, the Mac Studio, and the tower Mac Pro. Macs are especially popular in creative industries such as design, music production, and video editing.

Software

Modern Macs run macOS, a Unix-based operating system descended from NeXTSTEP. The platform is known for tight hardware–software integration and for features such as Continuity with iPhone and iPad.
