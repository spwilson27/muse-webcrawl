Neufchâtel is a soft, slightly crumbly, mould-ripened cheese made in the Neufchâtel-en-Bray region of Normandy, France. One of the oldest cheeses of Normandy, it is believed to date back to the 6th century and was granted Appellation d'Origine Contrôlée (AOC) status in 1969.

Appearance and flavour

The cheese has a bloomy rind similar to that of Camembert or Brie, with a soft, creamy interior and a salty, sharp, slightly yeasty flavour. It is made from raw or pasteurised cow's milk and typically contains about 45% butterfat.

Shapes

Neufchâtel is produced in several shapes, most famously the heart shape (coeur), said to date from the Hundred Years' War, when French maidens presented heart-shaped cheeses to English soldiers. Other shapes include bricks, bungs (bondes) and squares.

American Neufchâtel

An American cheese also called Neufchâtel is a lower-fat version of cream cheese, developed in the 19th century; it is a distinct product from the French AOC cheese.
