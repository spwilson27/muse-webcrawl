San Pedro de Atacama is a Chilean town and commune in El Loa Province, Antofagasta Region, situated in an oasis of the Atacama Desert at about 2,407 metres elevation, near the Licancabur volcano and the Bolivian border. It is one of Chile's most popular tourist destinations.

History

The area has been inhabited for over 11,000 years and was a centre of the Atacameño (Likan Antay) culture, later incorporated into the Inca Empire and Spanish colonial trade routes. The town's adobe church dates to the colonial period, and the R. P. Gustavo Le Paige Archaeological Museum preserves the region's pre-Columbian heritage.

Tourism

Visitors use San Pedro as a base to explore the Valle de la Luna, the Salar de Atacama salt flat with its flamingo reserve, the El Tatio geysers, high-altitude lagoons and the Pukará de Quitor fortress. The exceptionally clear, dry skies also make the area a centre for astronomy, near the ALMA observatory on the Chajnantor plateau.
