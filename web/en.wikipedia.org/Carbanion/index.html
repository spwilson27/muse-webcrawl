Carbanion

A carbanion is an anion in which carbon carries a formal negative charge and an unshared pair of electrons, with three substituents (a trivalent carbon). Carbanions are key reactive intermediates and reagents in organic chemistry and act as nucleophiles and strong bases.

Structure and stability

Simple alkyl carbanions adopt a pyramidal geometry similar to amines and undergo rapid inversion. Stability is increased by anything that delocalizes or withdraws the negative charge: resonance (as in enolates and benzyl or allyl anions), adjacent electronegative groups, and s-character — sp-hybridized acetylide anions are far more stable (less reactive) than sp3 alkyl anions. Common stabilized carbanions include enolates, organolithium compounds, and Grignard reagents (which have substantial carbanion character).

Formation and reactions

Carbanions form by deprotonation with strong bases (the basis of enolate chemistry), by halogen–metal exchange, or by direct metalation. They attack electrophiles such as carbonyl compounds and alkyl halides, underpinning reactions like the aldol addition and alkylation of enolates.
