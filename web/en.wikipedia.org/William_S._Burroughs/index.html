William S. Burroughs

William Seward Burroughs II (February 5, 1914 – August 2, 1997) was an American writer and visual artist. He is widely considered a primary figure of the Beat Generation and a major postmodern author who influenced popular culture and literature.

Life

Born in St. Louis to a wealthy family (grandson of the founder of the Burroughs adding machine company), Burroughs studied at Harvard and drifted through jobs before gravitating to the New York circle of Jack Kerouac and Allen Ginsberg in the 1940s. Decades of drug addiction, life in Mexico City, Tangier, Paris and London, and the accidental 1951 shooting of his wife Joan Vollmer shaped his work.

Works

His first books were the confessional Junkie (1953) and Queer (written 1951–53). His third novel, Naked Lunch (1959), a fragmented, satirical indictment of addiction and control systems, became the subject of a landmark obscenity trial and established his fame. With Brion Gysin he developed the cut-up technique, producing the Nova trilogy — The Soft Machine (1961), The Ticket That Exploded (1962) and Nova Express (1964) — and later works such as The Wild Boys (1971) and the Red Night trilogy.

Legacy

Burroughs spent his final years in Lawrence, Kansas, collaborating with musicians and artists and becoming an unlikely icon of alternative culture. His deadpan voice and themes of control, language as virus, and addiction permeate modern literature, film and music.
