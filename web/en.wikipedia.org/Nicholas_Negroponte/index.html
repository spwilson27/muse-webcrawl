Nicholas Negroponte (born 1 December 1943) is a Greek American architect and computer scientist. He is best known as the founder and chairman emeritus of the Massachusetts Institute of Technology's Media Lab and as the founder of the One Laptop per Child Association (OLPC).

Career

Negroponte studied architecture at MIT, where he joined the faculty in 1966 and founded the Architecture Machine Group, a laboratory devoted to human–computer interaction. In 1985 he co-founded the MIT Media Lab, an interdisciplinary laboratory for new media and digital technologies, which he directed for its first fifteen years.

His book Being Digital (1995), a bestseller translated into many languages, popularised ideas about the shift from atoms to bits and predicted developments in personalised news and digital interfaces. He was a founding investor and long-time columnist for Wired magazine.

One Laptop per Child

In 2005 Negroponte launched the One Laptop per Child project, aiming to produce a laptop costing about $100 for children in developing countries. The resulting XO laptop, with its distinctive green-and-white design and low-power innovations, was distributed to millions of children, although the project fell short of its ambitious production goals and was debated by development experts.
