Reggae

Reggae is a music genre that originated in Jamaica in the late 1960s. The term also came to denote the modern popular music of Jamaica and its diaspora. It developed from the earlier genres ska and rocksteady, and is characterised by offbeat rhythms, prominent bass lines and, often, lyrics connected with Rastafari spirituality and social commentary.

Characteristics

Reggae emphasises the third beat and the "one drop" rhythm, in which the kick drum and rimshot land on the third beat of the bar while guitar and keyboards play offbeat chords ("skank"). Pioneering artists include Toots and the Maytals, who popularised the word in their 1968 song "Do the Reggay", The Wailers with Bob Marley, Peter Tosh and Bunny Wailer, and producer Lee "Scratch" Perry, whose studio innovations spawned dub.

Global influence

Marley's international success in the 1970s made reggae a worldwide phenomenon, influencing punk, hip hop and dance music. In 2018 UNESCO inscribed reggae music of Jamaica on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
