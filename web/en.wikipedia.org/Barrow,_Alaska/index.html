Barrow is the former name of the city now officially known as Utqiaġvik, the northernmost community in the United States and the seat of Alaska's North Slope Borough. Located on the Chukchi Sea coast at Point Barrow, the city sits roughly 320 nautical miles north of the Arctic Circle. Its name was changed to Utqiaġvik in 2016, reflecting the Iñupiaq name for the place, which refers to a spot for gathering wild roots.

History

The area around Point Barrow has been inhabited for well over a thousand years by the Iñupiat people, who have long hunted bowhead whales and other marine mammals from the ice. The town took the name Barrow from Point Barrow, named in 1826 for Sir John Barrow of the British Admiralty. A whaling station, trading posts, and later a military and scientific presence during the Cold War era shaped the modern settlement. The discovery of oil at nearby Prudhoe Bay in 1968 transformed the borough's economy.

Geography and climate

Utqiaġvik lies on a flat coastal plain of tundra underlain by permafrost. It experiences a polar climate with long, extremely cold winters and short, cool summers. The sun does not set for roughly 84 days in summer, while in winter the community endures a polar night of about two months. Average high temperatures remain below freezing for much of the year.

Demographics and culture

A majority of residents are Iñupiat Alaska Natives, and subsistence hunting — especially the spring and fall bowhead whale hunts — remains central to local culture and diet. The city is a regional hub for the North Slope villages, with services provided by the borough government. There are no roads connecting Utqiaġvik to the rest of Alaska; access is by air or by summer barge.
