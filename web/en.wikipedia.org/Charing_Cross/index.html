Charing Cross

Charing Cross is a junction in Westminster, central London, where Whitehall, the Strand, Cockspur Street and Trafalgar Square meet. It takes its name from the Eleanor cross erected there in the 1290s by Edward I in memory of his wife Eleanor of Castile, and is officially recognised as the centre of London: distances to the capital are measured from the statue of Charles I at the junction.

History

The hamlet of Charing grew up beside the Thames near a bend (Old English cierr, "turn") in the river. The original cross, the last of twelve marking the funeral procession of Queen Eleanor to Westminster Abbey, was destroyed by Parliamentarians in 1647. An ornate Victorian replica, the Queen Eleanor Memorial Cross, was erected in 1865 outside the railway station. The site was also a historic place of punishment, with a pillory and the stocks, and the whipping post stood nearby.

Transport

The area is served by Charing Cross railway station, a mainline terminus opened in 1864, and by the Charing Cross and Embankment Underground stations. The district is also famous for its concentration of second-hand and antiquarian bookshops, celebrated in the book and film 84 Charing Cross Road.
