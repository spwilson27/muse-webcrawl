Kurdish–Turkish conflict

The Kurdish–Turkish conflict is an armed conflict between the Republic of Turkey and various Kurdish insurgent groups, principally the Kurdistan Workers' Party (PKK), which have demanded separation from Turkey to create an independent Kurdistan, or autonomy and greater political and cultural rights for Kurds inside the Republic of Turkey.

Background

Kurds are the largest ethnic minority in Turkey, concentrated in the east and southeast. Following the founding of the Republic in 1923, Kurdish identity, language and political organisation were heavily restricted, and a series of Kurdish revolts were suppressed. The PKK, founded by Abdullah Öcalan in 1978, launched an armed insurgency on 15 August 1984.

Course of the conflict

The conflict intensified through the 1980s and 1990s, with tens of thousands killed and many villages evacuated. Öcalan was captured in Kenya in 1999. Periods of ceasefire and negotiation followed, including a peace process in 2013–2015; its collapse in 2015 led to renewed urban fighting in the southeast and to Turkish military operations against Kurdish groups in northern Syria and Iraq.
