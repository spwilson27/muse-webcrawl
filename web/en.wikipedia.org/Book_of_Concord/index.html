The Book of Concord or Concordia is the historic doctrinal standard of the Lutheran Church, consisting of ten confessional documents recognised as authoritative in Lutheranism since the 16th century. It was published on 25 June 1580, the fiftieth anniversary of the presentation of the Augsburg Confession.

Contents

  1. The three ecumenical creeds: Apostles', Nicene and Athanasian
  2. The Augsburg Confession (1530)
  3. The Apology of the Augsburg Confession (1531)
  4. The Smalcald Articles (1537)
  5. The Treatise on the Power and Primacy of the Pope (1537)
  6. Luther's Small Catechism (1529)
  7. Luther's Large Catechism (1529)
  8. The Epitome of the Formula of Concord (1577)
  9. The Solid Declaration of the Formula of Concord (1577)
  10. The Catalogue of Testimonies

History

After Martin Luther's death in 1546, controversies divided German Lutheranism. The Formula of Concord (1577), drafted chiefly by Jakob Andreae and Martin Chemnitz, settled the disputes. The Book of Concord gathered the Formula together with the earlier confessions; 86 estates of the Empire subscribed initially, and Lutheran churches have used it as their confessional standard ever since. Confessional Lutherans subscribe to it quia ("because") it agrees with Scripture.
