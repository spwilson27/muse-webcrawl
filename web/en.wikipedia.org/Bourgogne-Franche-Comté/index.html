Bourgogne-Franche-Comté is a region in eastern France created by the territorial reform of French regions in 2014, from a merger of Burgundy (Bourgogne) and Franche-Comté. Its capital is Dijon, historically the seat of the Dukes of Burgundy.

Geography

The region covers about 47,784 km² and borders Switzerland to the east. It includes the Jura Mountains along the Swiss frontier, the Saône plain, and the wine-growing hills of the Côte d'Or. Its eight departments are Côte-d'Or, Doubs, Haute-Saône, Jura, Nièvre, Saône-et-Loire, Territoire de Belfort, and Yonne.

Economy

The region is renowned for Burgundy wines, including those of the Côte de Nuits and Côte de Beaune, as well as Comté cheese and other Jura specialities. Industry includes watchmaking around Besançon and automotive manufacturing at Sochaux.

History

The two predecessor regions had distinct histories: Burgundy was a powerful medieval duchy, while Franche-Comté ("Free County") was long tied to the Holy Roman Empire before passing to France in the 17th century. The merged region took effect on 1 January 2016.
