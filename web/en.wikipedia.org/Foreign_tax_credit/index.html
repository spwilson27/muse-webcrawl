Foreign tax credit

A foreign tax credit (FTC) is a non-refundable tax credit for income taxes paid to a foreign government as a result of foreign-source income taxation. It is a system of relief from double taxation found in the tax law of many countries, allowing taxpayers to offset taxes paid abroad against tax owed at home on the same income.

Purpose

Without such a mechanism, income earned abroad could be taxed twice—once by the country where it was earned and again by the taxpayer's country of residence. The foreign tax credit reduces this double taxation while generally preserving the residence country's right to tax its residents' worldwide income.

United States rules

In the United States, the foreign tax credit is provided under sections 901–909 of the Internal Revenue Code. Taxpayers may choose to claim foreign taxes as a credit (generally more advantageous) or as an itemized deduction. The credit is limited to the proportion of U.S. tax attributable to foreign-source income, computed separately for categories ("baskets") of income, and only "creditable" foreign taxes—essentially taxes in the nature of an income tax—qualify.

Alternatives

Other methods of double-tax relief include the deduction method and the exemption method, under which certain foreign income is simply excluded from domestic tax. Bilateral tax treaties typically specify which methods apply between the treaty partners.
