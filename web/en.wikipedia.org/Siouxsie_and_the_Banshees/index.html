Siouxsie and the Banshees were an English rock band formed in London in 1976 by vocalist Siouxsie Sioux and bass guitarist Steven Severin. Emerging from the British punk scene, they became pioneers of post-punk and were a major influence on gothic rock, alternative rock and later artists including Joy Division, the Cure, U2, Radiohead and Massive Attack.

History

The band debuted with the single "Hong Kong Garden" in 1978, a UK top 10 hit, followed by the album The Scream (1978), acclaimed for its stark, abrasive sound. Personnel shifted repeatedly: guitarist John McKay and drummer Kenny Morris left in 1979; drummer Budgie (formerly of the Slits) joined permanently, and guitarist John McGeoch, formerly of Magazine, played on the albums Kaleidoscope (1980), Juju (1981) and A Kiss in the Dreamhouse (1982).

Robert Smith of the Cure served as guitarist in 1982–84. Later albums included Hyæna (1984), Tinderbox (1986) and Peepshow (1988), whose single "Peek-a-Boo" topped the US alternative chart. The band scored a US breakthrough with "Kiss Them for Me" from Superstition (1991) and performed on the first Lollapalooza tour. Their final studio album, The Rapture, appeared in 1995, and the band split in 1996. They briefly reunited for the Seven Year Itch tour in 2002.

Musical style and legacy

The Banshees' music combined Siouxsie's commanding vocals with experimental guitar textures, tribal drumming and dark, literate lyrics. Their early work is regarded as foundational to post-punk, while their flirtations with psychedelia, pop and world music broadened the palette of alternative rock. The Scream has frequently appeared on lists of the greatest debut albums.
