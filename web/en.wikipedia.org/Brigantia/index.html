Brigantia was the land of the Brigantes, a Celtic Brittonic tribe that occupied the largest territory of any tribe in Iron Age and Roman Britain, roughly corresponding to most of northern England between the rivers Tyne and Humber. The name is thought to derive from the goddess Brigantia, whose name means "high, elevated", possibly referring to the high ground of the Pennines.

History

The Brigantes are first mentioned in Roman sources in connection with the conquest of Britain in the 40s AD. Their queen, Cartimandua, maintained a loyal client relationship with Rome, handing over the rebel leader Caratacus in 51 AD. Her husband Venutius led revolts against her, and by around 69 AD Roman forces under Petillius Cerialis intervened directly, bringing Brigantia under Roman control in the early 70s AD.

Settlements

Major Brigantian centres included Isurium Brigantum (modern Aldborough), Eboracum (York), and the hillfort at Stanwick in North Yorkshire, which may have been an early tribal capital.

Religion

The goddess Brigantia was patron of the tribe and was equated by the Romans with Victory and Minerva; dedications to her are found across the north of England.
