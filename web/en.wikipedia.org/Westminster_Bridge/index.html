Westminster Bridge is a road-and-foot traffic bridge over the River Thames in London, linking Westminster on the west bank with Lambeth on the east bank. It stands beside the Palace of Westminster, connecting Parliament Square with County Hall and the London Eye. The bridge is painted predominantly green, the same colour as the leather seats in the House of Commons, whose chamber is on the Westminster side.

History

The first Westminster Bridge, built of Portland stone to a design by Charles Labelye, opened in 1750. For over a century it was only the second bridge across the Thames in London. William Wordsworth wrote his sonnet "Composed upon Westminster Bridge, September 3, 1802" from its vantage point, beginning "Earth has not anything to show more fair".

By the 1850s the old bridge was subsiding and expensive to maintain. The current bridge, designed by Thomas Page with detail work by Charles Barry, was opened on 24 May 1862. It is a seven-span wrought-iron bridge, 252 metres long, decorated with gothic detailing to match the Palace of Westminster, including coats of arms and the emblems of Queen Victoria, Prince Albert and the cities of London and Westminster.

Features

At the southern end of the bridge stands the statue of Boadicea (Boudica) and her daughters in a chariot, erected in 1902. At the northern end, in Bridge Street, is the statue of Oliver Cromwell opposite the Houses of Parliament. On the south bank beside the bridge is the South Bank Lion, carved in Coade stone, which once stood on the Lion Brewery.

The bridge forms part of the London Marathon course and is a popular viewpoint for photographing the Houses of Parliament and the London Eye. On 22 March 2017, the bridge was the site of a terrorist attack in which a vehicle was driven into pedestrians before the attacker stabbed a police officer at the Palace of Westminster; a memorial now commemorates the victims.
