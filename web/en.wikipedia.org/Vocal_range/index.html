Vocal range

Vocal range is the range of pitches that a human voice can produce. Its most common application is as a measure of the range a singer can reach, typically discussed within the context of singing, where it serves as one of the defining characteristics used to classify singing voices into voice types. A typical adult human can produce notes over about two octaves, while trained singers frequently span three octaves or more.

Voice classification

In classical music, voices are grouped into types based largely on range: for women, soprano, mezzo-soprano and contralto; for men, countertenor, tenor, baritone and bass. The typical soprano range runs roughly from C4 to C6, while a bass may sing from E2 to E4. Range alone does not determine voice type; tessitura, timbre and passagio are also considered.

Measurement

Vocal range is usually expressed in scientific pitch notation, from the lowest note the voice can phonate to the highest. Whistle register notes, vocal fry and falsetto may or may not be included depending on convention, which makes reported ranges for famous singers vary between sources.
