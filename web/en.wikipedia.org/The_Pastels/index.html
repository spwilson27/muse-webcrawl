The Pastels are an independent music group from Glasgow, Scotland, formed in 1981. Fronted by Stephen McRobbie (Stephen Pastel), the band has been a persistent presence in the British independent music scene for over four decades, their sound evolving from shambling indie pop to a delicate, orchestral style. They are considered part of the lineage associated with the C86 compilation era, though their career long outlasted it.

History

Early singles such as "Something Going On" and "Million Tears" appeared on independent labels including Creation and Rough Trade. Their debut album Up for a Bit with The Pastels arrived in 1987, followed by Sittin' Pretty (1989), Mobile Safari (1995), and Illumination (1997), the latter two reflecting a mellower, more reflective style. With Katrina Mitchell as co-vocalist and drummer, the band released the soundtrack The Last Great Wilderness (2003) and the collaborative album Two Sunsets (2009) with Japanese band Tenniscoats, before Slow Summits (2013), their first studio album in sixteen years. McRobbie also founded the influential Glasgow record shop Monorail Music and the Geographic label.
