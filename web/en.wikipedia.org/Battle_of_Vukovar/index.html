Date25 August – 18 November 1991
LocationVukovar, eastern Croatia
ResultYugoslav/Serb victory; Croatian defence exhausted

The Battle of Vukovar was an 87-day siege of the city of Vukovar in eastern Croatia by the Yugoslav People's Army (JNA) and Serb paramilitary forces, fought from 25 August to 18 November 1991 during the Croatian War of Independence.

A Croatian force of roughly 1,800 lightly armed defenders, commanded by Mile Dedaković and including the celebrated local commander Blago Zadro at Borovo Naselje, held out against a far larger force supported by armour and artillery. Dubbed the "Verdun of Croatia" and the "Croatian Stalingrad", the defenders inflicted heavy losses in ambushed armoured columns, including along the notorious Trpinja road, the "tank graveyard". Constant bombardment reduced most of the city to ruins.

Aftermath

When Vukovar fell on 18 November, surviving defenders and civilians were expelled, and some 260–264 prisoners and hospital patients taken from Vukovar hospital were murdered at Ovčara farm — a crime prosecuted at The Hague. An estimated 1,000 or more defenders and civilians died. Though a military defeat, the siege tied down the JNA for three months and became the defining symbol of Croatian resistance; Vukovar was peacefully reintegrated into Croatia in 1998.
