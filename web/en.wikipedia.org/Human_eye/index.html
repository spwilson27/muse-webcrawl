Human eye

Human eye
Organ of vision; a roughly spherical structure about 24 mm in diameter that detects light and converts it into electrochemical signals.

The human eye is a sensory organ of the visual system that reacts to visible light, allowing sight. It is a complex optical system that collects light from the surrounding environment, regulates its intensity through a diaphragm (the iris), focuses it through an adjustable lens, and converts it into electrical signals in the retina that the brain interprets as images.

Structure

The eyeball has three layers: the outer fibrous layer (sclera and cornea), the middle vascular layer (choroid, ciliary body and iris), and the inner neural layer (the retina). The cornea provides most of the eye's focusing power; the crystalline lens fine-tunes focus for near and distant objects in a process called accommodation. The coloured iris controls the size of the pupil and thus the amount of light entering the eye.

The retina

The retina contains photoreceptor cells: roughly 120 million rods, sensitive to low light, and about 6–7 million cones, responsible for colour vision and fine detail. Cones are concentrated in the fovea centralis, the region of sharpest vision. Signals leave the eye through the optic nerve; where the nerve exits there are no photoreceptors, producing the blind spot.

Movement and protection

Six extraocular muscles move each eye. The eyelids, eyelashes, tears and the bony orbit protect the eye, while the lacrimal glands keep its surface moist. Vision depends on an adequate oxygen supply to ocular tissues; severe oxygen deprivation can impair retinal function, a concern shared with other organs in hypoxia.
