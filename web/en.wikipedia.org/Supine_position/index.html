Supine position

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The supine position (from Latin supinus, "lying on the back") is a position of the body in which the person lies horizontally with the face and torso facing up. It is one of the standard anatomical and clinical positions, opposite to the prone position, in which the face is directed downward.

Clinical use

The supine position is the default position for physical examination, many surgical procedures (especially abdominal and cardiac surgery), cardiopulmonary resuscitation, and much diagnostic imaging. Variants include the semi-recumbent or Fowler position with the head raised, Trendelenburg position with the head lowered, and the supine position with legs in stirrups (lithotomy position) for pelvic procedures.

Considerations and risks

Prolonged supine immobility contributes to pressure injuries, while in late pregnancy the supine position can compress the inferior vena cava and reduce venous return (supine hypotensive syndrome), managed by tilting the patient. Supine sleeping in infants was historically associated with higher risk of sudden infant death syndrome; since the 1990s, public-health campaigns recommending that babies sleep on their backs have coincided with large falls in SIDS rates. In patients with impaired consciousness, the supine position risks airway obstruction and aspiration, so the recovery position is preferred.
