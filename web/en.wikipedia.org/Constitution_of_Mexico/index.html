Constitution of Mexico

The Political Constitution of the United Mexican States (Spanish: Constitución Política de los Estados Unidos Mexicanos) is the current constitution of Mexico. It was drafted in Santiago de Querétaro, in the State of Querétaro, by a constituent convention during the Mexican Revolution. It was approved by the Mexican Constitutional Congress on 5 February 1917, and remains in force today, making it one of the longest-lived constitutions in the world.

Background

The constitution emerged from the Mexican Revolution (1910–1920). Venustiano Carranza, head of the Constitutionalist Army, convened a congress in 1916 to revise the liberal Constitution of 1857; the delegates, influenced by radical and social currents within the revolution, went far beyond Carranza's draft and produced a markedly more socially progressive document. It is regarded as the first constitution in the world to enshrine social rights, predating the Russian and Weimar constitutions.

Key provisions

Famous articles include Article 3, establishing free, secular public education; Article 27, asserting national ownership of land and subsoil resources, restoring communal ejido lands and enabling the expropriation of the oil industry in 1938; Article 123, guaranteeing labour rights such as the eight-hour day, minimum wage, and the right to strike; and Article 130, imposing strict restrictions on the Catholic Church and clergy, whose enforcement helped trigger the Cristero War (1926–1929). The constitution establishes a federal presidential republic and prohibits presidential re-election.

Amendments

The constitution has been amended hundreds of times — by some counts over 700 individual reforms — covering electoral systems, human rights (a major reform in 2011), energy, education and judicial organisation, including a controversial 2024 judicial reform introducing popular election of judges. Despite the volume of amendment, its 1917 text and revolutionary principles remain the framework of Mexican public law. Constitution Day is celebrated on 5 February.
