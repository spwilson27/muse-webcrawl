Amidah

The Amidah (Hebrew: תפילת העמידה, "the standing prayer"), also called the Shemoneh Esreh ("eighteen"), is the central prayer of Jewish liturgy. It is recited standing, at each of the three daily prayer services — morning (Shacharit), afternoon (Mincha), and evening (Ma'ariv) — and consists of a series of blessings.

Structure

The weekday Amidah contains nineteen blessings (grown from an original eighteen): the first three praise God, the middle thirteen petition for needs such as wisdom, healing, sustenance, and redemption, and the last three give thanks. On Shabbat and festivals the middle petitions are replaced by a single blessing sanctifying the day, for a total of seven blessings. An additional Amidah, Musaf, is recited on Shabbat and holidays.

Practice

The prayer is said quietly while standing facing Jerusalem (or toward the ark in a synagogue), followed in public services by the prayer leader's repetition, which includes the Kedushah. Worshippers traditionally take three steps forward before beginning and three steps back at the conclusion.
