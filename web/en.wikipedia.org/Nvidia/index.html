Nvidia Corporation is an American multinational technology company headquartered in Santa Clara, California. Founded in 1993 by Jensen Huang, Chris Malachowsky, and Curtis Priem, it designs graphics processing units (GPUs), systems on chips, and the software platforms around them. Nvidia popularised the term "GPU" with the GeForce 256 in 1999 and became the dominant supplier of the accelerators used to train and run artificial-intelligence models; in 2024 it became one of the most valuable publicly traded companies in the world.

History

The founders, who met at a Denny's restaurant in San Jose, started Nvidia to build accelerators for 3D graphics. After the unsuccessful NV1, the RIVA 128 (1997) saved the company, and the GeForce line established it as the leader in PC gaming graphics. The 2006 launch of CUDA let developers program GPUs for general-purpose computing, which — once researchers showed in 2012 (AlexNet) that GPUs dramatically accelerated deep learning — positioned Nvidia at the centre of the AI boom. Its data-centre products (Tesla, A100, H100, and the Blackwell generation) became the standard hardware of AI supercomputing, complemented by the Mellanox acquisition (2020) in networking. A planned $40 billion acquisition of Arm was abandoned in 2022 amid regulatory opposition.

Products and markets

Nvidia's businesses span gaming (GeForce RTX graphics cards), professional visualisation, data centres (AI accelerators and DGX systems), automotive computing (DRIVE), and embedded platforms (Jetson). Software — CUDA, cuDNN, TensorRT, and the Omniverse simulation platform — is a central part of its competitive moat.
