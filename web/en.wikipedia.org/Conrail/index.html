Conrail

Consolidated Rail Corporation
Reporting mark: CR
Founded: April 1, 1976
Predecessors: Penn Central, Erie Lackawanna, Reading, Lehigh Valley, and others
Successors: CSX Transportation, Norfolk Southern Railway (1999)

Conrail, formally the Consolidated Rail Corporation, was a government-created railroad that operated freight service in the northeastern United States from 1976 to 1999. It was formed to take over the bankrupt rail networks of Penn Central and several other insolvent northeastern railroads whose collapse threatened the region's freight transportation.

Formation

After the Penn Central bankruptcy of 1970 — then the largest in American history — and the failures of the Erie Lackawanna, Reading, Lehigh Valley, Central Railroad of New Jersey, and others, Congress created the United States Railway Association to plan a rescue. The resulting Consolidated Rail Corporation began operations on April 1, 1976, inheriting some 17,000 miles of track and about $7 billion in federal investment for rebuilding.

Return to profitability

Initially unprofitable, Conrail turned around after deregulation under the Staggers Rail Act of 1980 and reforms in the Northeast Rail Service Act of 1981, which relieved it of commuter operations and unprofitable obligations. Under chairman L. Stanley Crane it reported its first profits in 1981. The federal government sold Conrail to the public in a 1987 initial public offering, then the largest in US history.

Division

In 1997 CSX and Norfolk Southern agreed to acquire Conrail jointly for about $10 billion, ending a bidding war. Conrail's lines were divided between the two railroads on June 1, 1999, with Norfolk Southern taking roughly 58 percent and CSX 42 percent. The Conrail name survives in Conrail Shared Assets Operations, which provides terminal switching services in jointly owned areas including northern New Jersey, Philadelphia, and Detroit.
