Theriac

Theriac was a medicinal confection originally developed by the ancient Greeks and used for more than a thousand years as an antidote to poison and a cure-all. The most famous version, theriaca Andromachi, was devised by Andromachus, physician to the emperor Nero, and refined by Galen.

Composition

Classical recipes contained dozens of ingredients — sometimes more than sixty — including viper flesh, opium, honey, wine and numerous herbs and spices. Venetian treacle ("Venice treacle") was especially prized and manufactured publicly under official supervision.

History

Theriac remained in European pharmacopoeias into the 19th century before falling from use as medical science rejected its panacea claims.
