The Tupolev Tu-142 (Russian: Τû-142; NATO reporting names: Bear-F and Bear-J) is a Soviet/Russian maritime patrol and anti-submarine warfare (ASW) aircraft, derived from the Tu-95 strategic bomber. It is the largest turboprop aircraft in service, powered by four Kuznetsov NK-12 engines driving contra-rotating propellers. The type entered service with the Soviet Naval Aviation in 1972 and remains in service with the Russian Navy and, from 1988, the Indian Navy.

Development

Conceived in the early 1960s to cover the growing oceanic range of American Polaris missile submarines, the Tu-142 stretched the Tu-95's fuselage by about 1.6 m, added a weapons bay for torpedoes, depth charges and sonobuoys, and fitted a magnetic anomaly detector boom, the "Berkut" search radar, and a surface-search system. The improved Tu-142M of the mid-1970s brought the "Korshun" (Kite) search-targeting suite, and later variants added the NK-12MP engines and longer endurance of over 12,000 km.

Variants and service

Main variants include the Tu-142M ASW aircraft, the Tu-142MZ with upgraded avionics, and the Tu-142MR ("Bear-J"), a specialised very-low-frequency communications relay platform that trails an 8.6 km wire antenna to contact submerged ballistic-missile submarines. The Indian Navy operated eight Tu-142MEs from Goa between 1988 and 2017 before replacing them with Boeing P-8I Neptunes. Russia continues to fly modernised Tu-142M3s from the Northern and Pacific fleets.
