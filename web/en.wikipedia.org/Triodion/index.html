Triodion

The Triodion (Greek: Triōdion; Slavonic: Triod) is the liturgical book used by the Eastern Orthodox Church and Byzantine-rite Eastern Catholic Churches containing the propers for the fast of Great Lent and the weeks preceding it. The name derives from the fact that during this season the canons sung at Matins contain only three odes (a "tri-ode") instead of the usual nine.

Contents and season

The season covered by the Triodion begins on the Sunday of the Publican and the Pharisee, the fourth Sunday before Lent, and extends through Holy Saturday. It includes the preparatory Sundays — of the Prodigal Son, of the Last Judgment (Meatfare) and of Forgiveness (Cheesefare) — followed by the six weeks of Great Lent and Holy Week. A companion volume, the Pentecostarion, covers the season from Pascha (Easter) to Pentecost and beyond.

History

The contents of the Triodion accumulated over centuries. Much of its hymnography comes from the monasteries of Constantinople and Jerusalem, including the Stoudion and the Lavra of St Sabbas. Notable composers whose works it preserves include Andrew of Crete, author of the Great Canon sung in the first and fifth weeks of Lent, John of Damascus and Kosmas of Maiuma. Printed editions appeared in Greek in Venice in the 16th century and in Slavonic soon after.

Themes

The hymns of the Triodion emphasise repentance, prayer, fasting and almsgiving, retelling biblical narratives from Adam's expulsion from Paradise through the Passion of Christ as a framework for the believer's own spiritual journey.
