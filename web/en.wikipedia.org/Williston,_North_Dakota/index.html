Williston is a city in and the county seat of Williams County, North Dakota, United States. With a population of 29,479 at the 2020 census, it is the sixth-most populous city in the state. Located in the northwest corner of North Dakota near the confluence of the Yellowstone and Missouri rivers, Williston became the centre of the state's oil boom in the Bakken Formation.

History

Williston was founded in 1887 and named for Daniel S. Willis, a friend of railroad magnate James J. Hill, whose Great Northern Railway reached the town that year. For most of the 20th century it was a modest agricultural and rail centre for wheat and cattle country, near Fort Union Trading Post National Historic Site and Fort Buford.

Oil boom

Oil was first produced in the area in 1951, but development of the Bakken Formation with horizontal drilling and hydraulic fracturing after 2006 transformed the city. At the boom's peak Williston was one of the fastest-growing small cities in the United States: the population roughly doubled between the 2010 and 2020 censuses, housing costs briefly ranked among the highest in the nation, and the city undertook major expansions of its airport (Williston Basin International Airport, opened 2019), schools, water treatment and roads. The economy remains closely tied to oil prices.

Geography and climate

Williston lies on the Missouri Plateau just east of Lake Sakakawea. It has a semi-arid continental climate with cold winters and warm summers.
