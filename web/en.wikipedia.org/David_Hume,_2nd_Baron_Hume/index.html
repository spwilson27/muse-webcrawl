David Hume, 2nd Baron Hume

David Hume, 2nd Baron Hume of Berwick (1752–1838) was a Scottish lawyer and legal scholar, nephew of the philosopher David Hume. He is remembered for his Commentaries on the Law of Scotland, Respecting Crimes, a foundational text of Scots criminal law that remained authoritative well into the modern era.

Hume was educated at the University of Edinburgh and admitted to the Faculty of Advocates in 1779. He served as Sheriff of Perthshire, and from 1786 he lectured on Scots law at Edinburgh, where his lectures formed the basis of his published works. He was appointed a Baron of the Exchequer in Scotland in 1822, from which his courtesy title derives — the Barons of Exchequer were judicial officers, and the style "Baron Hume" is conventional for him.

Works

The Commentaries on the Law of Scotland, Respecting Crimes were published in 1797 (with later editions in 1819 and posthumously in 1844). Together with his commentaries on trials for crimes, they systematized Scots criminal doctrine and are still cited by Scottish courts. Hume also edited and defended the literary legacy of his uncle, the philosopher, whose reputation he helped rehabilitate in Scotland.
