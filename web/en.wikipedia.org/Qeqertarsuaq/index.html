Qeqertarsuaq

Qeqertarsuaq (Danish: Godhavn) is a port and town in the Qeqertalik municipality, located on the south coast of Disko Island on the west coast of Greenland, in Disko Bay. Founded in 1773, the town is one of the oldest in Greenland. Its name in Greenlandic means "the big island", referring to Disko Island itself. With a population of roughly 800–850 inhabitants, it is the only town on the island.

History

The site was originally a summer hunting ground for Inuit. The whaler Svend Sandgreen established a whaling station in the area in the early 18th century, and in 1773 the settlement of Godhavn ("Good Harbour") was founded as a whaling and trading post. Because of its sheltered harbour it served as an important base for the northern inspectorate of Greenland and later hosted an arctic research station of the University of Copenhagen, founded in 1906, which still supports scientific work on the island's geology, glaciers and ecology.

Geography and economy

Qeqertarsuaq lies beneath the Lyngmarksfjeldet mountains, whose basalt formations and the Lyngmarksbræen glacier rise behind the town. The surrounding area is known for its rich marine life, including whales in Disko Bay, and for distinctive geological features such as hot springs. The local economy is based on fishing and hunting, public services, and growing tourism, with visitors arriving by ferry and cruise ship during the summer; the town is connected by helicopter to Ilulissat and Aasiaat. Dog sledding and hiking are popular activities, and the town's football pitch is among the northernmost in the world.
