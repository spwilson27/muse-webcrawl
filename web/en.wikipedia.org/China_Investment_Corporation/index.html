China Investment Corporation

China Investment Corporation (CIC) is a sovereign wealth fund responsible for managing part of the People's Republic of China's foreign exchange reserves. Founded on 29 September 2007 with approximately US$200 billion of assets, CIC became one of the largest sovereign wealth funds in the world, with total assets exceeding US$1 trillion by the early 2020s.

History

CIC was capitalized through special treasury bonds, whose proceeds purchased foreign exchange reserves from the People's Bank of China. Among its earliest transactions were a US$3 billion stake in the private equity firm Blackstone Group and a US$5.6 billion stake in Morgan Stanley in 2007, deals that drew attention during the run-up to the global financial crisis. CIC also absorbed Central Huijin Investment, which holds the state's controlling stakes in major Chinese banks and financial institutions.

Structure

The fund operates through subsidiaries including CIC International, created in 2011 to manage overseas investments, and CIC Capital (2015), focused on direct investments. Its portfolio spans public equities, fixed income, real estate, infrastructure, private equity, and hedge funds across developed and emerging markets.

Governance

CIC states that its investments are made on commercial grounds. It reports to the State Council and publishes annual reports disclosing returns and governance arrangements, and it participated in the Santiago Principles for sovereign wealth fund transparency.
