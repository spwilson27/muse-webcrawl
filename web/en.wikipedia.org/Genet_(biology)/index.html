Genet (biology)

In biology, a genet is a genetic individual: an organism, colony or group of interconnected structures that has developed from a single zygote or spore and is therefore genetically uniform (barring somatic mutation). Genet stands in contrast to ramet, the physiologically independent members — such as individual trunks, shoots or polyps — that together compose a clonal genet.

Clonal organisms

Many plants, fungi and colonial animals reproduce vegetatively, so that what appears to be a population of separate individuals is in fact a single genet. The term is essential for measuring the age, size and diversity of such organisms: a clonal colony may be ancient and enormous even though each ramet is short-lived.

Examples

The best-known example is Pando, a colony of quaking aspen (Populus tremuloides) in Utah: roughly 47,000 stems spread over about 43 hectares all belong to one genet originating from a single seed, with an estimated weight of some 6,000 tonnes and an age possibly in the thousands of years. Coral colonies, strawberry plants spreading by runners, and many grasses likewise form genets composed of numerous ramets.
