Battle of Maipú

Battle of Maipú
Date5 April 1818
LocationPlains of Maipú, near Santiago, Chile
ResultDecisive patriot victory; Chilean independence secured
BelligerentsArmy of the Andes and Chilean patriots; Spanish royalist army
CommandersJosé de San Martín, Bernardo O'Higgins; Mariano Osorio

The Battle of Maipú was fought on 5 April 1818 on the plains southwest of Santiago de Chile. The patriot Army of the Andes under José de San Martín defeated the Spanish royalist army of General Mariano Osorio, securing the independence of Chile.

After the patriot disaster at Cancha Rayada in March, San Martín rebuilt his army in little more than two weeks. At Maipú he deployed on high ground and opened with an artillery bombardment. When the royalist infantry advanced, the patriot cavalry under O'Higgins and the reserve division struck the Spanish flank and rear. The royalist army was destroyed, losing some 2,000 killed and over 2,000 captured, against far lighter patriot losses.

Legacy

Maipú is regarded as the decisive battle of the Chilean War of Independence. Before the battle San Martín and O'Higgins made a vow to build a church on the site; the Votive Temple of Maipú in Santiago commemorates the victory, and the famous embrace between the two generals on the battlefield — the "Abrazo de Maipú" — is a national symbol in Chile and Argentina.
