The German Centre Party (German: Deutsche Zentrumspartei), often called simply das Zentrum ("the Centre"), is a Christian democratic political party in Germany. It was most influential in the German Empire and the Weimar Republic, when it was the third-largest party in the Reichstag and participated in nearly every government coalition between 1919 and 1932, supplying several chancellors including Wilhelm Marx, Heinrich Brüning, and Franz von Papen.

History

Founded in 1870 to represent Germany's Catholic minority, the party was a principal target of Bismarck's Kulturkampf, the campaign against Catholic influence, under which it paradoxically grew stronger. Under leaders such as Ludwig Windthorst and later Matthias Erzberger, the Centre became a pivotal, pragmatic party defending parliamentary government, civil liberties, and social welfare policies rooted in Catholic social teaching.

After the Nazi seizure of power, the party voted for the Enabling Act of 1933 under duress and dissolved itself on 5 July 1933, days before the conclusion of the Reichskonkordat. Refounded after the Second World War, it was eclipsed by the interdenominational Christian Democratic Union, most of whose Catholic founders had been Centre members, and survives as a minor party.
