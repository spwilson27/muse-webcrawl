John Arbuthnot Fisher, 1st Baron Fisher, GCB, OM, GCVO (25 January 1841 – 10 July 1920), commonly known as Jacky Fisher, was a British Admiral of the Fleet who reshaped the Royal Navy in the years before the First World War, championing the dreadnought battleship and the battlecruiser.

Early career

Born in Ceylon, Fisher joined the Royal Navy aged thirteen and served in the Crimean War and the Second Opium War. He rose through the gunnery and torpedo branches, becoming a leading advocate of naval modernisation.

First Sea Lord

As First Sea Lord from 1904 to 1910, Fisher scrapped scores of obsolete warships, redistributed the fleet to home waters, introduced oil fuel and larger guns, and drove through the construction of HMS Dreadnought (1906), which made all existing battleships obsolete, and the fast battlecruiser type he conceived.

Return and resignation

Recalled as First Sea Lord in October 1914 after the outbreak of the First World War, Fisher worked closely with First Lord Winston Churchill on fleet construction and the projected Baltic operation, but resigned in May 1915 over disagreements about the Dardanelles campaign, contributing to the fall of the government.

Legacy

Energetic, controversial and quotable ("fear God and dread nought"), Fisher remains one of the most influential figures in the history of the Royal Navy.
