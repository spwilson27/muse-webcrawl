South Australian Maritime Museum

The South Australian Maritime Museum is a state museum in Port Adelaide, South Australia, devoted to the maritime history of South Australia and the wider story of migration, trade, and seafaring. It is administered by the History Trust of South Australia.

History and site

The museum opened in 1986 in the historic bond stores on Lipson Street in Port Adelaide's heritage precinct, near the Port River. Its campus includes the Port Adelaide Lighthouse (the Weman Martin lighthouse, originally on Warden Head) and the steam tug Yelta, and it has operated heritage vessels including the schooner Nelcebee and the police launch Archie Badenoch.

Collections

The collection of more than 20,000 objects covers the Flinders–Baudin encounter of 1802, the windjammer grain races from Port Victoria, the ketch trade of Spencer Gulf, migration to South Australia, naval history, and the state's shipwreck record. Exhibitions have addressed topics from the world's oldest seaworthy tug to the experiences of post-war migrants arriving by sea.
