Palm Sunday

Palm Sunday
Observed byChristians worldwide
SignificanceCommemorates Jesus's triumphal entry into Jerusalem
DateThe Sunday before Easter; begins Holy Week
LiturgyBlessing and procession of palms; reading of the Passion

Palm Sunday is a Christian feast falling on the Sunday before Easter, which commemorates Jesus's triumphal entry into Jerusalem, as described in all four Gospels. It is the first day of Holy Week, the final week of Lent, and is also known as Passion Sunday.

Biblical account

According to the Gospels, Jesus rode into Jerusalem on a donkey (or colt) in fulfilment, the evangelists say, of the prophecy in Zechariah. Crowds spread their cloaks and branches on the road before him and greeted him with cries of "Hosanna to the Son of David!" The Gospel of John specifies that the branches were palm branches, palm being a symbol of victory and triumph in the ancient world.

Observance

In many churches the day begins with the blessing of palm fronds (or, in cooler climates, branches of yew, willow, olive or other local trees) and a procession into the church. Worshippers traditionally take the blessed palms home; in the Roman Catholic and some other traditions they are later burned to make the ashes used on the following Ash Wednesday. Because the liturgy also anticipates the Crucifixion, the long account of the Passion is read at Mass or service, giving the day a dual character of triumph and sorrow.

Customs vary widely: in Spain and Latin America elaborate processions carry statues through the streets throughout Holy Week; Orthodox Christians observe the feast with palms or pussy willow; in parts of Europe woven palm crosses and decorated branches are traditional.
