Funen

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Funen (Danish: Fyn) is the third-largest island of Denmark, after Zealand and Vendsyssel-Thy, with an area of about 3,099 km². It lies in the centre of the country, between the mainland peninsula of Jutland to the west and the island of Zealand to the east, and belongs administratively to the Region of Southern Denmark. Its largest city is Odense, the birthplace of Hans Christian Andersen.

Geography

Funen is a gently rolling island of farmland, orchards, and small villages, connected to Jutland by the Little Belt bridges at Middelfart and to Zealand by the Great Belt Fixed Link (opened for rail in 1997 and road traffic in 1998), one of the world's longest suspension bridges. The surrounding South Funen Archipelago includes Ærø, Langeland, and Tåsinge. The island's mild climate and fertile soil have earned it the nickname "the garden of Denmark" (Danmarks have).

History and culture

Funen was settled in prehistory; the Ladby ship burial and the Kerteminde area preserve Viking Age remains. Odense, founded in the Viking Age, is associated with Saint Canute and with Andersen, whose childhood home and museum draw visitors. Egeskov Castle, a Renaissance water castle in the south of the island, is a major attraction. The Funen Village open-air museum in Odense recreates rural life of the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries.

Economy

Agriculture, food processing, greenhouses (around Bellinge), shipping (the former Maersk-owned Odense Steel Shipyard), and tourism are important. The University of Southern Denmark, headquartered in Odense, is a major employer.
