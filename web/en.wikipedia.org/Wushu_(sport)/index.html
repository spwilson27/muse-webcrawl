Wushu (simplified Chinese: 武术; traditional Chinese: 武術; pinyin: wǔshù), in its modern sporting sense, is a standardized performance and combat sport developed in China from the traditional Chinese martial arts. Governed internationally by the International Wushu Federation (IWUF), founded in 1990, it comprises two disciplines: taolu (forms) and sanda (sparring).

Taolu

Taolu competitors perform choreographed routines, judged on quality of movements, overall performance, and degree of difficulty, including acrobatic aerial techniques (nandu) such as jumping kicks and butterfly twists. Events include changquan (long fist), nanquan (southern fist), taijiquan, and weapons routines with the staff (gunshu), broadsword (daoshu), straight sword (jianshu), and spear (qiangshu), performed empty-handed or with weapons to set time limits on a carpeted arena.

Sanda

Sanda (also called sanshou) is full-contact fighting on a raised platform (leitai), combining punches, kicks, sweeps, and throws, with competitors able to win by pushing or throwing the opponent off the platform. Protective gear includes gloves, headgear, and a chest protector.

International competition

The World Wushu Championships have been held every two years since 1991. Wushu featured as a special exhibition event alongside the 2008 Beijing Olympics (the Beijing Wushu Tournament) and at the 2014 Nanjing Youth Olympics, but has not achieved full Olympic programme status; it is a medal sport at the Asian Games and was included as an invitational sport at the Dakar 2026 Youth Olympic Games programme. Modern wushu's emphasis on aesthetics and difficulty has drawn debate with traditional kung fu practitioners over its divergence from combat-oriented practice.
