Gheg (Albanian: gegë or gegnisht) is one of the two major dialect groups of the Albanian language, spoken in northern Albania north of the Shkumbin River and in Kosovo, northwestern North Macedonia, Montenegro and the Preševo Valley of southern Serbia. The traditional boundary between Gheg and Tosk Albanian runs along the Shkumbin.

Features

Gheg differs from Tosk in several characteristic ways: it retains nasal vowels; it preserves older consonant clusters such as mb, nd and ng; it has a distinctive infinitive formed with me plus the participle instead of the Tosk construction; and it does not show the Tosk rhotacism by which intervocalic n became r. Gheg itself divides into northwestern, northeastern, central and southern sub-dialects, with considerable local variation, especially between Albania proper and Kosovo.

Literary history and status

The earliest Albanian texts, including Gjon Buzuku's missal of 1555, were written in Gheg varieties, and much of Albania's early literature — including the works of the Franciscan tradition and Gjergj Fishta's epic The Highland Lute — is in Gheg. In 1972 the unified standard Albanian language adopted in Albania (and used officially in Kosovo) was based predominantly on Tosk, which reduced Gheg's role in education and media; since the 1990s and Kosovo's independence, Gheg has seen renewed literary and cultural use, though the standard remains Tosk-based.
