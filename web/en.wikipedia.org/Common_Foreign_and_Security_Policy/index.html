Common Foreign and Security Policy

The Common Foreign and Security Policy (CFSP) is the organised, agreed foreign policy of the European Union, covering diplomacy, conflict prevention, crisis management, and (through the Common Security and Defence Policy) military and civilian missions. It was established by the Treaty of Maastricht in 1993 as the second of the EU's three pillars, succeeding the looser European Political Co-operation of the 1970s.

Institutions

CFSP is intergovernmental: decisions in the Council of the European Union normally require unanimity among member states. The High Representative of the Union for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy, supported since 2011 by the European External Action Service, conducts the policy, represents the EU abroad, and chairs the Foreign Affairs Council.

Instruments

Its tools include joint actions and common positions, sanctions (restrictive measures), political dialogues with third countries, special representatives, and civilian and military missions abroad, from police training in the Balkans to anti-piracy operations off the Horn of Africa.

Challenges

The unanimity rule and differing national interests — exposed during the Iraq War of 2003 and debates over responses to Russia's invasion of Ukraine — limit CFSP's speed and coherence, fuelling recurring proposals for qualified majority voting in foreign policy.
