Gemini (constellation)

GenitiveGeminorum
SymbolismThe Twins (Castor and Pollux)
Brightest starsPollux (β Gem), Castor (α Gem)

ZodiacThird sign; Sun transits mid-June to mid-July

Gemini is one of the constellations of the zodiac, located in the northern celestial hemisphere. Its name is Latin for "twins", and it is associated in Greek mythology with the Dioscuri, Castor and Pollux, whose heads are marked by the constellation's two brightest stars. It was one of the 48 constellations listed by the 2nd-century astronomer Ptolemy and remains one of the 88 modern constellations.

Features

Pollux (β Geminorum) is the brightest star in Gemini, an orange giant about 34 light-years away with a known exoplanet. Castor (α Geminorum), though slightly fainter, is a remarkable multiple star system of six stars about 52 light-years away. Other notable objects include the planetary nebula NGC 2392 (the Eskimo Nebula), the open cluster Messier 35, and the neutron star Geminga. In mid-December the Geminids meteor shower radiates from a point near Castor and is among the richest showers of the year.

Mythology

In Greek myth, Castor and Pollux were twin half-brothers — Castor the mortal son of Tyndareus and Pollux the divine son of Zeus — famed for their brotherly devotion. When Castor was killed, Pollux begged Zeus to share his immortality with his brother, and the two were placed together in the sky. The constellation was also identified with twin deities in Babylonian and other traditions.
