The Industrial and Commercial Bank of China (ICBC) is a Chinese multinational banking and financial services corporation headquartered in Beijing. Founded in 1984, it is the largest bank in the world by total assets and one of China's "Big Four" state-owned commercial banks, alongside China Construction Bank, Agricultural Bank of China, and Bank of China.

History

ICBC was created on 1 January 1984 when the People's Bank of China spun off its commercial banking operations into a new institution as part of China's financial reforms. For its first two decades it operated as a policy-driven state bank; a major restructuring in 2005 converted it into a joint-stock company, with strategic investments from Goldman Sachs, Allianz, and American Express. Its initial public offering in October 2006, conducted simultaneously in Hong Kong and Shanghai, raised about US$22 billion and was at the time the largest IPO in history.

Operations

The bank serves hundreds of millions of personal customers and millions of corporate customers through an extensive branch network across China and offices in dozens of countries. Services span retail and corporate banking, asset management, insurance, and investment banking. ICBC consistently ranks as the world's largest bank by assets and among the largest by market capitalization and deposits, and the Financial Stability Board places it in the highest bucket of global systemically important banks.

Ownership

The Chinese state remains the controlling shareholder through Central Huijin Investment and the Ministry of Finance, together holding a majority of shares.
