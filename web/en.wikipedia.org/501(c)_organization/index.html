501(c) organization

A 501(c) organization is a nonprofit organization in the United States that is exempt from some federal income taxes under Section 501(c) of the Internal Revenue Code. The section enumerates 29 types of organizations, of which the best known is the 501(c)(3) — religious, charitable, scientific, and educational organizations to which contributions are generally tax-deductible for donors.

Major categories

501(c)(3) covers charities, churches, and educational institutions; these organizations face strict limits on lobbying and may not engage in partisan political campaigning. 501(c)(4) covers "social welfare" organizations, which may lobby more freely and engage in some political activity, though donations are not tax-deductible. 501(c)(5) covers labor and agricultural organizations, 501(c)(6) covers trade associations and chambers of commerce, and 501(c)(7) covers social and recreational clubs.

Requirements

To obtain and keep exemption, an organization must be organized and operated exclusively for exempt purposes, must not inure its net earnings to private individuals, and generally must apply to the IRS using Form 1023 or 1024 and file annual information returns (Form 990). Exemption can be revoked for prohibited political activity, excessive lobbying, or failure to file.

Political activity debates

The use of 501(c)(4) organizations as vehicles for political spending — sometimes called "dark money" because donor disclosure is limited — became a major controversy after the Supreme Court's 2010 Citizens United decision.
