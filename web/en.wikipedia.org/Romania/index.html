Romania (Romanian: România) is a country located at the crossroads of Central, Eastern, and Southeastern Europe. It borders Ukraine to the north and east, Hungary to the west, Serbia to the southwest, Bulgaria to the south, Moldova to the east, and the Black Sea to the southeast. It has a mainly temperate-continental climate. With a population of about 19 million, Romania is the sixth-most populous member of the European Union. Its capital and largest city is Bucharest.

The Romanian state was formed in 1859 through a personal union of the Danubian Principalities of Moldavia and Wallachia. The new state, officially named Romania since 1866, gained independence from the Ottoman Empire in 1877. During World War I, after declaring its neutrality in 1914, Romania fought together with the Allied Powers from 1916. In the aftermath of the war, Bukovina, Bessarabia, Transylvania, and parts of Banat, Crișana, and Maramureș became part of the Kingdom of Romania. In June–August 1940, as a consequence of the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact and Second Vienna Award, Romania was compelled to cede Bessarabia and Northern Bukovina to the Soviet Union and Northern Transylvania to Hungary. In November 1940, Romania signed the Tripartite Pact and, consequently, joined the Axis in June 1941, fighting against the Soviet Union until August 1944, when it joined the Allies and recovered Northern Transylvania.

History

Modern Romania's territory was inhabited in antiquity by the Dacians, who were conquered by the Roman Empire under Trajan in 106 AD, forming Roman Dacia. After Rome's withdrawal in 271, the region was subjected to successive waves of migration. Medieval principalities of Wallachia and Moldavia emerged in the 14th century, while Transylvania became part of the Kingdom of Hungary and later the Habsburg monarchy and the Austro-Hungarian Empire.

Following World War II, Romania became a socialist republic and a member of the Warsaw Pact. After the Romanian Revolution of December 1989, the country began a transition towards democracy and a capitalised market economy. Romania joined NATO on 29 March 2004 and the European Union on 1 January 2007.

Geography

With a total area of 238,391 square kilometres (92,043 sq mi), Romania is the twelfth-largest country in Europe and the sixth-most populous EU member state. The Carpathian Mountains, which cross Romania from the north to the southwest, include Moldoveanu Peak at an altitude of 2,544 m (8,346 ft). The River Danube forms much of Romania's southern border before flowing into the Black Sea through the Danube Delta, the second-largest and best-preserved delta in Europe.

Economy

Romania has a developing, high-income mixed economy, ranked as the world's 44th largest by nominal GDP. It has experienced rapid economic growth in the early 2000s; its economy is now predominantly based on services. Romania is a net exporter of cars and electric energy and has become a major regional centre for information technology, with a significant software industry centred on Bucharest and Cluj-Napoca.

Demographics

According to the 2021 census, Romania's population was 19,053,815 people. Ethnic Romanians make up about 89% of the population, with Hungarians (~6%) and Roma (~3.4%) the largest minorities. Romanian, a Romance language, is the official language and the native language of about 91% of the population. Most Romanians are Eastern Orthodox Christians.

Culture

Romania has a rich cultural heritage shaped by its position between Central, Eastern, and Balkan Europe. Notable Romanians include sculptor Constantin Brâncuși, playwright Eugène Ionesco, historian of religions Mircea Eliade, composer George Enescu, and gymnast Nadia Comăneci. Romanian sites on the UNESCO World Heritage List include the painted churches of Moldavia, the wooden churches of Maramureș, and the historic centre of Sighișoara.
