Operation Barbarossa (German: Unternehmen Barbarossa; Russian: Операция «Барбаросса», romanized: Operatsiya Barbarossa) was the invasion of the Soviet Union by Nazi Germany and many of its Axis allies, starting on Sunday, 22 June 1941, during World War II. It was the largest land offensive in human history, with over 10 million combatants taking part.

The operation, code-named after Frederick Barbarossa, the 12th-century Holy Roman emperor, put into action Nazi Germany's ideological goal of conquering the western Soviet Union to repopulate it with Germans. The German Generalplan Ost aimed to use some of the conquered territories' agricultural surplus while causing the deaths by starvation of tens of millions of Slavs.

Background

Despite the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact of 1939, Adolf Hitler regarded the Soviet Union as Germany's principal ideological enemy and coveted its territory as Lebensraum. Planning began in 1940; Directive No. 21, issued on 18 December 1940, ordered the Wehrmacht to "crush Soviet Russia in a quick campaign".

The invasion

Some 3.8 million Axis troops invaded along a 2,900 km (1,800 mi) front, organised into three army groups: North (toward Leningrad), Centre (toward Moscow) and South (toward Ukraine and Kyiv). Massive encirclements at Białystok-Minsk, Smolensk and Kyiv captured hundreds of thousands of Soviet soldiers. However, logistical overextension, the autumn rasputitsa mud, and fierce Soviet resistance delayed the advance on Moscow (Operation Typhoon). On 5 December 1941 the Soviet winter counteroffensive before Moscow halted the offensive and drove the Germans back — the first major strategic defeat of the Wehrmacht.

Consequences

Barbarossa opened the Eastern Front, the largest theatre of the war, where the great majority of German military deaths occurred. Behind the front, Einsatzgruppen and associated forces murdered around a million Jews and other civilians in mass shootings, and millions of Soviet prisoners of war died in German captivity. Germany's failure to defeat the Soviet Union in 1941 committed it to a war of attrition it was ultimately unable to win.
