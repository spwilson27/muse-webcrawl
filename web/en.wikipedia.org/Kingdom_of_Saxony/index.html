Kingdom of Saxony

The Kingdom of Saxony (German: Königreich Sachsen) was a German state that existed from 1806 to 1918. Created from the Electorate of Saxony when Napoleon raised its ruler to royal rank, it was a member of the Confederation of the Rhine, then of the German Confederation, sided with Austria in 1866, and afterwards joined the North German Confederation and the German Empire as a constituent kingdom, with Dresden as its capital and Leipzig as its great commercial city.

History

Elector Frederick Augustus I accepted a royal crown from Napoleon in 1806 under the Treaty of Posen and joined the Confederation of the Rhine, later ruling the Napoleonic Duchy of Warsaw. At the Congress of Vienna (1815) Saxony was punished for its loyalty to Napoleon, losing about three-fifths of its territory — including Wittenberg and Lower Lusatia — to Prussia. The reduced kingdom industrialised rapidly: textiles around Chemnitz ("the Saxon Manchester"), machine-building, Meissen porcelain, and Leipzig's book fairs and trade made it Germany's most densely populated and industrial state.

In the Austro-Prussian War of 1866 Saxony backed Austria; after Königgrätz it joined the Prussian-led North German Confederation and in 1871 the German Empire, retaining its own army, railways and postal service. King Frederick Augustus III abdicated on 13 November 1918 during the November Revolution, reputedly telling officials in dialect to "do their dirt themselves". The Free State of Saxony succeeded the kingdom in the Weimar Republic.
