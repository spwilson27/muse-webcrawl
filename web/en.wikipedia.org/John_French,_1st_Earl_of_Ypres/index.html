Field Marshal John Denton Pinkstone French, 1st Earl of Ypres (28 September 1852 – 22 May 1925) was a senior British Army officer. A cavalryman who made his reputation in the Second Boer War, he commanded the British Expeditionary Force (BEF) on the Western Front during the first year of the First World War before being replaced in December 1915. He later served as Commander-in-Chief, Home Forces, and as Lord Lieutenant of Ireland during the Irish War of Independence.

Early career

French joined the army in 1870 and served in the Sudan campaign and in India. In the Boer War he commanded the cavalry division that relieved Kimberley in February 1900 and went on to become one of Britain's most prominent cavalry commanders. He was Chief of the Imperial General Staff from 1912 to 1914, resigning over the Curragh incident.

First World War

In August 1914 French took the BEF to France. After the retreat from Mons and the stand at Le Cateau, he cooperated uneasily with his French allies and contemplated withdrawing the BEF out of the line; intervention by Lord Kitchener kept it in place for the Battle of the Marne. He commanded through First Ypres, Neuve Chapelle, Aubers Ridge and Loos, but his handling of reserves at Loos and his deteriorating relations with the French and with his own government led to his replacement by Douglas Haig in December 1915.

Ireland and later life

As Lord Lieutenant of Ireland from 1918 to 1921, French survived an assassination attempt by the Irish Republican Army in December 1919. He was created Earl of Ypres in 1922 and died at Deal Castle in 1925.
