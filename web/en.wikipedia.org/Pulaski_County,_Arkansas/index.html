Pulaski County, Arkansas

Pulaski County is a county in the U.S. state of Arkansas, located in the center of the state on the Arkansas River. Its county seat and largest city is Little Rock, which is also the state capital. With a population of roughly 400,000, it is the most populous county in Arkansas.

History

Pulaski County was formed on December 15, 1818, as one of the original counties of Arkansas Territory. It is named for Casimir Pulaski, the Polish nobleman and cavalry commander who fought for the American cause in the Revolutionary War and was killed at the Siege of Savannah in 1779. Little Rock was designated the territorial capital in 1821, cementing the county's role as the political center of Arkansas. During the Civil War, Union forces captured Little Rock in 1863 following the Battle of Bayou Fourche.

Geography

The county covers about 808 square miles, of which roughly 31 square miles are water. The Arkansas River bisects the county, separating Little Rock on the south bank from North Little Rock. The Ouachita Mountains approach the county's western edge, while the eastern part grades into the flat Mississippi Alluvial Plain. Interstate highways 30, 40, 430, 530, and 630 serve the county.

Communities

Cities and towns include Little Rock, North Little Rock, Jacksonville, Sherwood, Maumelle, Alexander, Cammack Village, and Wrightsville. The Little Rock Air Force Base, a major U.S. Air Force installation, is located in Jacksonville. The county is the core of the Little Rock–North Little Rock–Conway metropolitan statistical area.

Government and economy

The county is governed by a quorum court led by a county judge. As the seat of state government, the economy is anchored by public administration, health care (including the University of Arkansas for Medical Sciences), banking, and logistics along the river port.
