Physical oceanography

Physical oceanography is the study of physical conditions and processes within the ocean, including waves, tides, currents, circulation, and the distribution of temperature, salinity and density, as well as the ocean's interaction with the atmosphere.

Topics

Major subjects include wind-driven surface circulation and western boundary currents such as the Gulf Stream and Kuroshio; thermohaline circulation and deep-water formation; internal waves and mixing; coastal processes and upwelling; and the ocean's role in climate, including phenomena such as El Niño.

Methods

Observations come from research vessels, moored instruments, drifting floats (notably the Argo programme), tide gauges and satellite altimetry and radiometry. Numerical models simulate circulation from regional to global scales and are essential to weather and climate prediction.
