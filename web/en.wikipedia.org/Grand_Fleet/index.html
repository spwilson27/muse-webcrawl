Grand Fleet

Battleships of the Grand Fleet in line ahead formation

The Grand Fleet was the Royal Navy's main battle fleet during the First World War. It was formed in August 1914 from the First Fleet and elements of the Second Fleet of the Home Fleets, and it was based first at Scapa Flow in Orkney and later also at Rosyth on the Firth of Forth.

History

On the outbreak of war, Admiral Sir John Jellicoe took command. The fleet's strategy was to maintain a distant blockade of Germany, containing the German High Seas Fleet in the North Sea while avoiding decisive action in waters where mines and submarines posed a grave threat to Britain's irreplaceable dreadnoughts. Its only major fleet action was the Battle of Jutland on 31 May – 1 June 1916, in which the Grand Fleet's battlecruiser force under Vice-Admiral David Beatty suffered heavy losses but the main body preserved Britain's strategic command of the sea. In November 1916, Beatty succeeded Jellicoe as commander-in-chief.

Composition

At Jutland the Grand Fleet deployed 28 dreadnought battleships and 9 battlecruisers, alongside cruisers and destroyers — the largest concentration of capital ships ever put to sea by Britain. By 1918 it had grown still further with the addition of a squadron of American battleships, the Sixth Battle Squadron.

Disbandment

After the Armistice, the Grand Fleet received the surrender of the High Seas Fleet in November 1918. In April 1919 the Grand Fleet was disbanded and its ships redistributed to the newly formed Atlantic, Mediterranean, and Home Fleets.
