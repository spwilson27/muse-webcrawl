March West

PLA forces during the 1950 campaign
Date:1950
Location:Southwestern China
Part of:Chinese Civil War

The March West (Chinese: 向西进军) refers to the advance of the People's Liberation Army (PLA) into the southwest of China in the closing phase of the Chinese Civil War, in which Communist forces moved from their newly won positions in central and eastern China toward the remaining Nationalist-held provinces — Sichuan, Guizhou, Yunnan, and Xikang — in late 1949 and 1950.

Background

After the proclamation of the People's Republic of China on 1 October 1949, large Nationalist (Kuomintang) armies under generals such as Hu Zongnan and Bai Chongxi still held much of the south and southwest. The PLA high command planned a converging offensive: the Second Field Army under Liu Bocheng and Deng Xiaoping would thrust into Guizhou and Sichuan, while elements of the Fourth Field Army cut off retreat toward Indochina and Burma.

The campaign

PLA columns crossed into Guizhou in November 1949, capturing Guiyang and then advancing on Chengdu while coordinated forces entered Yunnan, whose governor Lu Han defected in December. Chongqing fell on 30 November 1949, and Chengdu, the last major Nationalist stronghold on the mainland, was taken on 27 December 1949 after the encirclement and collapse of Hu Zongnan's army group. Organized Nationalist resistance in the southwest ended in early 1950, although remnants fled into northern Burma, where they remained a cross-border irritant for a decade.

Significance

The westward advance completed the Communist conquest of mainland China except for Hainan and offshore islands, secured the approaches to Tibet — entered by the PLA later in 1950 — and brought the immense population and resources of Sichuan into the new state. The campaign is remembered in PRC historiography as the liberation of the southwest.
