Old Parliament House, known formerly as the Provisional Parliament House, was the seat of the Parliament of Australia from 1927 to 1988. Located in Parkes, Canberra, the building was opened on 9 May 1927 by the Duke of York (later King George VI).

History

Designed by chief Commonwealth architect John Smith Murdoch in the Stripped Classical style, the white-rendered building was intended as a temporary home for the federal parliament but served for 61 years. Every prime minister from Stanley Bruce to Bob Hawke sat in its chambers, and it witnessed events such as the 1975 dismissal of the Whitlam government, announced on its steps.

Museum

After parliament moved to the new Parliament House on Capital Hill in 1988, the building was preserved and reopened as the Museum of Australian Democracy in 2009. It is heritage-listed and retains its chambers, offices, and press gallery largely as they were.
