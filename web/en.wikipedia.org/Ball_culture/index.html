Ball culture

Ball culture, also known as ballroom culture or the house-ballroom community, is an African-American and Latino underground LGBTQ subculture that originated in New York City. Participants "walk" (compete) in balls in categories based on dance skills, appearance, and costumes, including voguing dance, drag performance, and modelling effects. Winners receive trophies, prizes, and glory.

Houses

Participants belong to "houses", which function as alternative families, often named after fashion labels or founders — famous houses include the House of Xtravaganza, the House of LaBeija, and the House of Ninja. Houses are led by "mothers" and "fathers" who mentor their "children". The scene developed from drag balls held as early as the 1920s and took its modern form in Harlem in the 1970s and 1980s, when Black and Latino queer people created their own scene in response to exclusion and racism in established drag pageantry.

Language and influence

Ball culture developed a distinctive vocabulary — "shade", "reading", "realness", "werk", "fierce", "mother" — much of which has entered mainstream slang. The 1990 documentary Paris Is Burning, directed by Jennie Livingston, brought the scene to wide attention, and Madonna's 1990 hit "Vogue" popularized voguing globally. The television series Pose (2018–2021) dramatized the ballroom scene of 1980s New York.
