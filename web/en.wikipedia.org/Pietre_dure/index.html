Pietre Dure

Pietre dure (Italian for "hard stones"), sometimes called Florentine mosaic or commesso, is a technique of inlaying pictures and patterns using highly polished, thinly sliced semiprecious and hard stones — such as lapis lazuli, jasper, agate, malachite, and various marbles — fitted together with virtually invisible joints.

History

The art descends from Roman opus sectile and Byzantine inlay work and flowered in Renaissance Florence, where Ferdinand I de' Medici founded the grand-ducal workshop (Opificio delle Pietre Dure) in 1588. Florentine craftsmen produced tabletops, cabinets, and panels depicting landscapes, flowers, and birds, exploiting the natural colors and veining of the stones as a painter uses pigments. The craft spread across Europe and influenced the pietra dura inlay of Mughal India seen in the Taj Mahal.
