Antonio Barluzzi (26 September 1884 – 14 December 1960) was an Italian architect who designed many of the Roman Catholic churches and sanctuaries built in the Holy Land in the first half of the 20th century, earning him the epithet "architect of the Holy Land".

Life and work

Barluzzi came from a family of architects associated with the Vatican. He went to Jerusalem in 1912 to work on the Italian hospital, and after service in the First World War he received commissions from the Franciscan Custody of the Holy Land. Over the following decades he designed or rebuilt a series of pilgrimage churches, each evoking the Gospel episode commemorated at its site.

Principal churches

His works include the Church of All Nations (Church of the Agony) at Gethsemane (1924), the Church of the Flagellation on the Via Dolorosa, the Church of Saint Lazarus at Bethany, the Church of the Transfiguration on Mount Tabor (1924), the Church of the Beatitudes overlooking the Sea of Galilee (1938), the Church of the Visitation at Ein Karem (1955), and the Shepherds' Field chapel near Bethlehem. His design for a monumental basilica of the Annunciation in Nazareth was not the one ultimately built.
