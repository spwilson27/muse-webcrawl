Civil engineering

Civil engineering spans bridges, dams, roads and water systems.

Civil engineering is a professional engineering discipline that deals with the design, construction and maintenance of the physical and naturally built environment, including public works such as roads, bridges, canals, dams, airports, sewage systems, pipelines, structural components of buildings and railways. It is one of the oldest engineering disciplines, after military engineering.

Sub-disciplines

  • Structural engineering — analysis and design of structures that support or resist loads, such as buildings, bridges and towers.
  • Geotechnical engineering — behaviour of soils and rock in foundations, tunnels and earthworks.
  • Transportation engineering — safe and efficient movement of people and goods by road, rail, air and water.
  • Water resources and environmental engineering — supply and treatment of water, flood control, wastewater and pollution management.
  • Construction engineering and management — planning, scheduling and execution of construction projects.

History

The term was coined by John Smeaton in the eighteenth century to distinguish civilian engineering from the military sort. The Institution of Civil Engineers, founded in London in 1818, was the first engineering society; its charter defines the field as "the art of directing the great sources of power in nature for the use and convenience of man." Professional licensure and accreditation (such as ABET in the United States) regulate practice in most countries.
