Off-roading

Off-roading is the activity of driving or riding in a vehicle on unpaved surfaces such as sand, gravel, riverbeds, mud, snow, rocks, and other natural terrain. Types of off-roading vary considerably, from casual trail driving in standard four-wheel-drive vehicles to competitive events such as the Dakar Rally and rock crawling competitions.

Vehicles

Off-road vehicles (ORVs) include sport utility vehicles, pickup trucks, all-terrain vehicles (ATVs), dirt bikes, dune buggies, and purpose-built rock crawlers. Key features for off-road capability include four-wheel drive with low-range gearing, high ground clearance, locking differentials, robust suspension, and all-terrain or mud-terrain tyres. Iconic off-road vehicles include the Jeep Wrangler, Land Rover Defender, and Toyota Land Cruiser.

Disciplines

  • Trail riding / green laning — recreational driving on unpaved roads and tracks.
  • Rock crawling — slow, technical traversal of boulder fields, demanding precise throttle and wheel placement.
  • Desert racing — high-speed competition such as the Baja 1000 and Dakar Rally.
  • Mudding — driving through deep mud courses.
  • Overlanding — long-distance self-reliant travel to remote destinations.

Environmental impact and regulation

Off-roading can cause soil erosion, habitat damage, and disturbance to wildlife, leading many jurisdictions to restrict ORV use to designated trails and areas. Advocacy groups promote responsible practices under principles such as "Tread Lightly!", which encourages staying on established trails and minimising impact.
