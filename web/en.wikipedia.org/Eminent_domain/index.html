Eminent domain

Eminent domain (United States, Philippines), land acquisition (India), compulsory purchase (United Kingdom, New Zealand, Ireland) or expropriation (France, Italy, Mexico, South Africa, Canada, Brazil) is the power of a state, provincial or national government to take private property for public use.

The power is generally conditioned on the payment of just compensation to the owner. In the United States, the Fifth Amendment to the Constitution provides that "nor shall private property be taken for public use, without just compensation." Condemning authorities typically use the power for roads, utilities, schools and other public infrastructure.

The scope of "public use" has been contentious. The U.S. Supreme Court's decision in Kelo v. City of New London (2005), which upheld a taking for economic development, provoked widespread legislative backlash, with many states restricting the practice. Comparable doctrines appear in many legal systems, descending from the concept of dominium eminens in Roman and early modern legal thought.
