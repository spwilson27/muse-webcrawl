Vulcan Centaur

Vulcan Centaur is a two-stage-to-orbit, heavy-lift launch vehicle developed by United Launch Alliance (ULA) to replace its Atlas V and Delta IV rockets. Announced in 2014, it combines a new first stage powered by two Blue Origin BE-4 engines burning liquid methane and oxygen with the proven Centaur upper stage. Its first flight on 8 January 2024 carried Astrobotic's Peregrine lunar lander, and it was certified for U.S. national security missions after a second flight later that year.

Development

Vulcan was driven by economics and geopolitics: the Atlas V depended on Russian RD-180 engines, whose use for military launches became politically untenable after 2014, while Delta IV was expensive to operate. ULA designed a single configurable system, with zero to six solid rocket boosters and two fairing sizes, to cover the combined manifest. Development was slowed by BE-4 engine delays and a solid-booster anomaly investigation during the second certification flight, in which a nozzle fell away but the mission still succeeded.

Design

The first stage is 5.4 metres in diameter and uses two BE-4 engines; the Centaur V upper stage, with two RL10 engines and larger tanks, provides high-energy performance for direct geostationary insertion. Depending on configuration, Vulcan can place up to about 27 tonnes in low Earth orbit. ULA has studied partial reuse of the first-stage engines (the "SMART" concept), though the vehicle initially flies expendable.

Flights

Vulcan is manifested for U.S. Space Force and NRO missions, NASA science payloads, and commercial launches including Kuiper broadband satellites for Amazon. It launches from Cape Canaveral in Florida and Vandenberg Space Force Base in California.
