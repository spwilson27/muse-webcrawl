Port of Seattle

The Port of Seattle is a United States government agency, operating as a port authority overseeing the seaport of Seattle, Washington, on Puget Sound, as well as Seattle–Tacoma International Airport (Sea-Tac). Founded in 1911 by voters of King County, it was among the first publicly owned ports created under Washington's Port District Act, a progressive-era reform intended to return waterfront development to public control.

Operations

The port's marine facilities include container terminals on Harbor Island and along the Duwamish Waterway, grain and breakbulk terminals, cruise ship terminals at Pier 66 and Pier 91 serving the Alaska cruise market, and Fishermen's Terminal, home port of the North Pacific fishing fleet. Since 2015, Seattle's container business has been operated jointly with the Port of Tacoma through the Northwest Seaport Alliance, a partnership designed to compete with other West Coast and Canadian gateways for Asia–North America trade.

Leadership and scope

The Port of Seattle is governed by five commissioners elected by King County voters to four-year terms. In addition to its maritime and aviation operations, it manages parks, marinas, and real estate, and plays a significant role in regional economic development, employing policies on environmental sustainability, including shore power for cruise ships and habitat restoration in the Duwamish River.
