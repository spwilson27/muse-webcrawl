Mersey Tunnels

The Mersey Tunnels are three road tunnels under the River Mersey in North West England, connecting Liverpool on the east bank with the Wirral Peninsula (Birkenhead and Wallasey) on the west. They are operated by Merseytravel and carry tolls.

The tunnels

The Queensway Tunnel (3.24 km, 2.01 mi), between Liverpool and Birkenhead, was opened on 18 July 1934 by King George V; at the time it was the longest underwater road tunnel in the world and employed thousands of men during the Great Depression. The Kingsway Tunnel (2.4 km), between Liverpool and Wallasey, consists of twin tubes opened in 1971 and 1974. Queensway carries two lanes in each direction in a single tube; Kingsway's twin bores each carry two lanes in one direction.

Operation

The tunnels are a vital transport link, carrying around 90,000 vehicles a day. Ventilation is provided by large ventilation stations on both banks. Along with the Merseyrail railway tunnel (1886) and the Mersey Gateway and Silver Jubilee bridges further upstream, they form the principal crossings of the lower Mersey.
