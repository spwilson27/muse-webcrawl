Inclisiran

Inclisiran, sold under the brand name Leqvio, is a medication used to lower low-density lipoprotein (LDL) cholesterol in adults with primary hypercholesterolemia or mixed dyslipidemia, including heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia and patients with atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease who need additional LDL lowering beyond statins. It is the first small interfering RNA (siRNA) therapy approved for cholesterol lowering.

Mechanism of action

Inclisiran is a double-stranded siRNA conjugated to a triantennary N-acetylgalactosamine (GalNAc) ligand, which directs it to hepatocytes in the liver. Once inside the cell, the siRNA is loaded into the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC), which binds and degrades the messenger RNA encoding PCSK9. With less PCSK9 produced, fewer LDL receptors are degraded, more receptors recycle to the hepatocyte surface, and more LDL cholesterol is cleared from the blood. Because RISC is recycled and the drug persists in the liver, the effect is long-lasting.

Clinical use and efficacy

Inclisiran is given by subcutaneous injection (284 mg) by a healthcare professional, with a second dose after three months and maintenance doses every six months — a twice-yearly schedule intended to improve adherence compared with daily pills or the biweekly/monthly self-injections of PCSK9 monoclonal antibodies. In the ORION-10 and ORION-11 phase 3 trials, inclisiran reduced LDL cholesterol by about 50% compared with placebo on top of maximally tolerated statin therapy. A dedicated cardiovascular outcomes trial (ORION-4) has been running to determine whether this translates into fewer heart attacks and strokes.

Approvals

The drug was developed by Alnylam Pharmaceuticals and licensed to Novartis. It was approved in the European Union in December 2020 and by the US Food and Drug Administration in December 2021; it became notable in England through a population-health agreement between Novartis and the NHS to offer it widely to eligible patients.

Side effects

The most common adverse effects are mild injection-site reactions (pain, redness, rash). Joint pain, urinary tract infection and bronchitis were also reported in trials at rates slightly above placebo. No major safety signals have emerged, though long-term data continue to accumulate.
