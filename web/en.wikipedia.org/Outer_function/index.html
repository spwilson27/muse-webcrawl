Outer function

In complex analysis, an outer function is a holomorphic function on the unit disc that plays a distinguished role in the theory of Hardy spaces. Outer functions are characterised by the fact that their values inside the disc can be recovered entirely from the modulus of their boundary values on the unit circle.

Specifically, a holomorphic function F on the open unit disc is called outer if it can be written in the form

F(z) = c · exp( (1/2π) ∫ (eiθ + z)/(eiθ − z) · log φ(eiθ) dθ )

for some unimodular constant c and some positive measurable function φ on the unit circle with log φ integrable. In that case φ agrees almost everywhere with the modulus of the radial boundary values of F.

Inner–outer factorisation

Every function f in a Hardy space Hp (for p ≥ 1) admits a canonical factorisation f = B · S · F, where B is a Blaschke product encoding the zeros of f, S is a singular inner function, and F is an outer function. The product B·S is called the inner function part, and the factorisation is unique up to unimodular constants.

Because the outer factor is determined by the boundary modulus, two functions in Hp with the same boundary modulus differ only in their inner factors. Outer functions have no zeros unless identically zero, and they are precisely the functions in the Hardy spaces that are cyclic vectors for the shift operator in the appropriate sense, a fact connected with Beurling's theorem on invariant subspaces.

Properties

  • An outer function is determined up to a unimodular constant by its boundary modulus.
  • Products and quotients of outer functions (when holomorphic) are outer.
  • A function in H∞ that is outer and bounded below in modulus is invertible in the algebra.
  • The outer factor of a function vanishing at a point is obtained by removing its Blaschke factors: dividing by a single Blaschke factor preserves outerness properties in the factorisation.

Applications

Inner–outer factorisation underlies the spectral theory of the unilateral shift, prediction theory for stationary stochastic processes (where the outer function is the Szegő–Kolmogorov spectral factor), and control theory, where outer (minimum-phase) transfer functions play a central role in H-infinity methods.
