Ban Chiang

Ban Chiang is an archaeological site in Nong Han District, Udon Thani Province, northeastern Thailand. It is famous for its distinctive red-painted pottery and for early bronze metallurgy, and is considered one of the most important prehistoric settlements in Southeast Asia.

Discovery and excavation

The site came to wide attention in 1966 when pottery was noticed eroding from village paths; excavations by the University of Pennsylvania and Thai archaeologists in the 1970s uncovered burials, bronze artefacts and evidence of rice agriculture spanning thousands of years. Early claims of extremely early bronze dates were later revised, and bronze working at the site is now generally placed in the second millennium BCE.

Recognition

Ban Chiang was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1992. A national museum displays the painted ceramics and bronze work, and the site became a prominent case in debates over looting and the repatriation of Thai antiquities held abroad.
