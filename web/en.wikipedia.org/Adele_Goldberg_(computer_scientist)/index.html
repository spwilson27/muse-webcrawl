Adele Goldberg (computer scientist)

Adele Goldberg
Born: 7 July 1945, Cleveland, Ohio, U.S.
Alma mater: University of Michigan; University of Chicago
Known for: Smalltalk, Xerox PARC
Fields: Computer science

Adele Goldberg (born 7 July 1945) is an American computer scientist who was one of the key developers of the Smalltalk programming language while working at Xerox PARC in the 1970s. Smalltalk and the graphical user interface environment built around it at PARC became foundational influences on modern personal computing, including the Macintosh interface.

Career

Goldberg received her bachelor's degree in mathematics from the University of Michigan and a master's degree and PhD in information science from the University of Chicago. She joined Xerox Palo Alto Research Center in 1973 as a research associate in Alan Kay's Learning Research Group, where she worked with Kay and Dan Ingalls on Smalltalk. She co-authored the landmark book Smalltalk-80: The Language and Its Implementation (1983, the "Blue Book") with David Robson, which defined the language for a generation of programmers.

Goldberg famously demonstrated the Alto-based Smalltalk system to Steve Jobs during his 1979 visits to PARC — visits that helped inspire the Apple Lisa and Macintosh. In 1988 she co-founded ParcPlace Systems to commercialize Smalltalk, serving as its president and CEO. She later founded the consulting firm Neometron and served on corporate boards including that of Ciena. She was president of the Association for Computing Machinery (ACM) from 1984 to 1986, and received the ACM Software System Award as part of the Smalltalk team.
