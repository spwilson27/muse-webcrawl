Mincemeat

Mincemeat is a mixture of chopped dried fruit, distilled spirits and spices, and historically (but now rarely) minced meat, traditionally used as a filling for mince pies at Christmas in Britain and other Commonwealth countries.

History

Medieval and early modern recipes combined meat — mutton or beef — with fruit and spices, a style influenced by Crusader-era contact with Middle Eastern cooking. Beef suet long remained a standard ingredient even as the meat content shrank. By the 20th century most recipes omitted meat entirely, though suet (or vegetable shortening) is still common.

Modern mincemeat

Typical ingredients are raisins, currants, sultanas, candied peel, chopped apples, brown sugar, suet, brandy or rum, and warm spices such as cinnamon, nutmeg, and mixed spice. The mixture is matured for several weeks before being baked into mince pies.
