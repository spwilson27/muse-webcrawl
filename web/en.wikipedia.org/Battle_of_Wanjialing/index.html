Battle of Wanjialing

DateAugust – October 1938
LocationWanjialing, Jiangxi, China
ResultChinese victory
BelligerentsRepublic of China vs Empire of Japan

The Battle of Wanjialing was a major engagement fought during the Battle of Wuhan in the Second Sino-Japanese War. In the autumn of 1938, Chinese forces under Xue Yue encircled and largely destroyed elements of the Japanese 27th Division in the mountainous Wanjialing region of Jiangxi province.

Background

After capturing Jiujiang in July 1938, Japanese forces advanced southwest along both banks of the Yangtze River toward Wuhan. The Japanese 106th Division pushed inland through difficult terrain, becoming separated from supporting units and vulnerable to envelopment by the much larger Chinese First Army Group.

The battle

Xue Yue concentrated roughly 100,000 troops against the isolated Japanese division. Fierce fighting raged around Wanjialing through late September and early October. Japanese attempts to relieve the encircled force, including the use of poison gas, failed to break the Chinese ring, though a portion of the Japanese force eventually escaped. The battle was one of China's few clear tactical victories of the war.

Aftermath

Although Wuhan fell to Japan later in October 1938, the victory at Wanjialing boosted Chinese morale and demonstrated that Japanese units could be defeated when cut off from support.
