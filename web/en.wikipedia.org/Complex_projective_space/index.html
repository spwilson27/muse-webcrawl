Complex projective space

In mathematics, complex projective space is the projective space with respect to the field of complex numbers. The complex projective space of dimension n, denoted CPn, is the space of all complex lines through the origin of Cn+1: it is the quotient of Cn+1 minus the origin by the equivalence relation identifying points that differ by a nonzero complex scalar multiple.

Homogeneous coordinates

Points of CPn are written in homogeneous coordinates [z0 : … : zn], not all zero, where rescaling all coordinates by the same nonzero complex number gives the same point. The standard affine charts, where one coordinate is nonzero and can be normalized to 1, cover the space and identify it as a complex manifold of complex dimension n (real dimension 2n).

Examples and topology

CP0 is a single point; CP1, the complex projective line, is homeomorphic to a 2-sphere and is the Riemann sphere. CP2 is a compact 4-manifold that cannot be embedded in Euclidean 3-space. Complex projective spaces are compact, orientable, and simply connected, with integral cohomology a truncated polynomial ring generated by the first Chern class of the tautological line bundle.

Role in geometry

CPn is a central example in algebraic geometry (a smooth projective variety), symplectic geometry (with the Fubini–Study metric), and mathematical physics, where CP1 models the Bloch sphere of a qubit and twistor theory uses CP3. Projective spaces are also the natural setting for homogeneous coordinates in computer graphics.
