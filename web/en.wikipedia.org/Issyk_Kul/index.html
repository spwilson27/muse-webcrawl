Issyk-Kul (also Ysyk-Köl; Kyrgyz for "warm lake") is an endorheic (closed-basin) lake in the Northern Tian Shan mountains of eastern Kyrgyzstan. It is the seventh-deepest lake in the world, the tenth-largest by volume, and the second-largest saline lake after the Caspian Sea. Despite lying at 1,607 metres above sea level amid snow-capped peaks, the lake never freezes — a result of its depth, mild salinity, and thermal springs — which gives it its name.

Geography

The lake stretches 182 km east to west and up to 60 km north to south, enclosed between the Kungey Alatau range to the north and the Terskey Alatau to the south. Some 118 rivers and streams flow into it, including the Jyrgalang and Tüp, but it has no outlet. Its water is slightly saline (about 0.6 percent) and its level has fluctuated over the centuries; medieval ruins submerged near its shores testify to lower or higher stands in past eras. The towns of Karakol at the eastern end and Cholpon-Ata on the northern shore are the main settlements.

History

Chinese sources from the Han dynasty onward knew the lake, and the Tang-dynasty monk Xuanzang described it in the 7th century. It lay near branches of the Silk Road, and medieval maps — notably the Catalan Atlas of 1375 — placed a Christian monastery on its shore said to hold relics of St Matthew. Under the Russian Empire and the USSR the lake's north shore became a resort area, and a Soviet naval facility near Karakol tested torpedoes in its waters.

Nature and tourism

Issyk-Kul is a Ramsar site of globally significant biodiversity and the centrepiece of a UNESCO biosphere reserve (2001). It is a vital stopover for migratory waterbirds and historically supported endemic fish species, several of which declined after predatory fish were introduced in the Soviet era. Today the lake is Kyrgyzstan's premier tourist destination, with beach resorts along the northern shore, petroglyph fields near Cholpon-Ata, and access to trekking and mountaineering in the surrounding ranges, including Khan Tengri and Jengish Chokusu on the region's borders.
