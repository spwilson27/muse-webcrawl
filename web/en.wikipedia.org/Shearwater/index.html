Shearwater

Shearwaters are medium-sized long-winged seabirds in the petrel family Procellariidae. The name refers to their habit of flying with stiff wings, shearing low across the surface of the water. There are more than 30 species, mostly placed in the genera Ardenna, Calonectris, and Puffinus.

Behaviour

Shearwaters are highly pelagic, coming ashore only to breed in burrows or rock crevices on islands, usually at night to avoid predators. They undertake some of the longest migrations known: the sooty shearwater travels between breeding grounds in New Zealand and the subantarctic and feeding grounds in the North Pacific and North Atlantic, a figure-of-eight journey of some 14,000 km. They feed on fish, squid, and krill, often following fishing vessels for offal.

Species and conservation

Well-known species include the Manx shearwater of the North Atlantic, the short-tailed shearwater of Australia (harvested traditionally as the Tasmanian muttonbird), and the critically endangered Balearic shearwater. Many species are threatened by introduced predators at breeding colonies, bycatch in fisheries, and plastic pollution.
