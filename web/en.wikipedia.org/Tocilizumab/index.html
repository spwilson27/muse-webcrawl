Tocilizumab

Tocilizumab, sold under the brand name Actemra among others, is an immunosuppressive monoclonal antibody drug used mainly to treat rheumatoid arthritis and other inflammatory conditions. It is a humanised antibody that blocks the receptor for interleukin-6 (IL-6).

Medical uses

Tocilizumab is approved for moderate to severe rheumatoid arthritis (including in combination with methotrexate), systemic and polyarticular juvenile idiopathic arthritis, giant cell arteritis, and cytokine release syndrome induced by CAR T-cell therapy. During the COVID-19 pandemic, large trials including RECOVERY and REMAP-CAP found benefit in severely ill hospitalised patients, leading to emergency authorisations and recommendations for its use alongside corticosteroids.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include upper respiratory tract infections, headache and hypertension. Serious risks include infections (including reactivation of tuberculosis), raised liver enzymes, neutropenia and gastrointestinal perforation. It was developed by Hoffmann–La Roche and Chugai Pharmaceutical, and first approved in Japan in 2005.
