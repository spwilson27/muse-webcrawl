The Monaco Grand Prix (French: Grand Prix de Monaco) is a Formula One motor racing event held annually on the Circuit de Monaco in late May or early June. First run in 1929, it is widely considered one of the most important and prestigious automobile races in the world, forming, with the Indianapolis 500 and the 24 Hours of Le Mans, the Triple Crown of Motorsport.

The circuit

The narrow streets of Monte Carlo and La Condamine make up the slowest and tightest circuit on the Formula One calendar. Its features include the climb to Casino Square, the Grand Hotel Hairpin — the slowest corner in Formula One — the tunnel, the harbour-front chicane, and the swimming-pool complex. Overtaking is notoriously difficult, so qualifying position is of exceptional importance.

History

Organised by the Automobile Club de Monaco under Antony Noghès, the race became part of the Formula One World Championship in 1950. Ayrton Senna won a record six times, including five consecutively from 1989 to 1993. Graham Hill, known as "Mr Monaco," won five times in the 1960s.
