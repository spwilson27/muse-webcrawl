Warli painting

Warli painting is a form of tribal art created by the Warli (Varli) people, an Adivasi community of the North Sahyadri range in Maharashtra, western India, especially in Palghar district and adjoining parts of Gujarat. The paintings are traditionally executed by women on the mud walls of village houses, using a white pigment made from rice paste and water, bound with gum, applied with a chewed bamboo stick on a background of red ochre (geru), cow dung, and mud.

Motifs and themes

Warli art is built from a restricted vocabulary of geometric shapes — the circle (sun and moon), the triangle (mountains and trees), and the square (the sacred enclosure or chauk). Human figures are formed from two joined triangles attached by the apex, depicted dancing, hunting, fishing, farming, and celebrating festivals. Central ritual paintings, such as the devchauk painted for weddings, honour the goddess Palaghata, a symbol of fertility; the most famous composition is the tarpa dance, in which figures circle endlessly around a musician playing the tarpa wind instrument.

Modern recognition

From the 1970s Warli painting moved from walls to paper and canvas and gained national and international recognition, notably through the artist Jivya Soma Mashe, who was honoured with the Padma Shri in 2011. The art form received a Geographical Indication tag in 2014, and Warli motifs are now widely used in design, textiles, and public art while remaining rooted in Warli community ritual life.
