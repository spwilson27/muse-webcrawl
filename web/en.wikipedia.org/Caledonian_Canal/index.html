The Caledonian Canal is a canal in Scotland that connects the east coast at Inverness with the west coast at Corpach near Fort William. It runs some 60 miles (97 km) from northeast to southwest along the Great Glen, the geological fault valley that cuts across the Scottish Highlands. Only about one third of its length is artificial: the route incorporates the natural waters of Loch Dochfour, Loch Ness, Loch Oich, and Loch Lochy, linked by excavated channels and 29 locks.

Construction

The canal was conceived to provide a safe passage for shipping between the North Sea and the Atlantic, avoiding the dangerous waters of the Pentland Firth and Cape Wrath, and to relieve unemployment and depopulation in the Highlands following the Clearances. Construction was authorized in 1803 and directed by the engineer Thomas Telford, with William Jessop as consulting engineer.

Work began in 1804 but proceeded slowly amid rising costs, labour shortages, and the difficulty of building in remote terrain. The canal opened to navigation in 1822, though parts were built to a shallower depth than designed; after storm damage and further works under James Walker, it was effectively completed in 1847 at a total cost of about £1.3 million — far beyond original estimates.

Features

The canal's most celebrated structure is Neptune's Staircase at Banavie, a flight of eight locks that raises vessels 19 metres (62 ft) over a quarter of a mile. Other notable features include the Muirtown lock flight at Inverness, the swing bridges that carry the A82 road, and the canal's summit level at Loch Oich, 32 metres (105 ft) above sea level.

Modern use

By the time the canal was finished, steamships could safely negotiate the northern route and commercial traffic never met expectations. Today the canal is managed by Scottish Canals and is used mainly by pleasure craft, including yachts transiting between the Moray Firth and the Hebrides, and by visitors drawn to Loch Ness and the scenery of the Great Glen. The towpath forms part of the Great Glen Way long-distance trail.
