Suspended animation

Suspended animation is the slowing or stopping of a living organism's life processes by external or internal means without termination of life. It occurs naturally in hibernation and cryptobiosis, and is a long-standing theme of science fiction and speculative medicine.

Natural forms

Many animals survive harsh conditions through hibernation, estivation, or diapause. Tardigrades and some brine shrimp can enter cryptobiosis, tolerating near-complete desiccation for years. Embryos of some fish survive in dried mud until rains return.

Medical research

Therapeutic hypothermia is used clinically after cardiac arrest and in some surgery. Researchers have explored emergency preservation and resuscitation (EPR), in which trauma patients are rapidly cooled to buy time for surgery, and hydrogen sulfide induced hypometabolism in laboratory animals.

In culture

Suspended animation is a staple device for interstellar travel in fiction, from 2001: A Space Odyssey to Alien and Futurama.
