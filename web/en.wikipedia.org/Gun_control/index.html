Gun control

Gun control (or firearms regulation) is the set of laws and policies that regulate the manufacture, sale, transfer, possession, modification and use of firearms by civilians. Approaches vary widely between countries, from permissive regimes with high rates of private ownership to strict licensing systems and near-total prohibitions.

Approaches worldwide

Most countries require some form of licence or permit, often involving background checks, safe-storage rules and registration. Japan and South Korea impose very strict controls; the United Kingdom largely banned handguns after the 1996 Dunblane massacre; Australia introduced licensing, registration and a buy-back scheme after the 1996 Port Arthur massacre. The United States, where the Second Amendment to the Constitution has been interpreted by the Supreme Court since District of Columbia v. Heller (2008) as protecting an individual right to keep and bear arms, has comparatively permissive federal law and the highest rate of civilian gun ownership in the world.

Debate

Debate centres on the relationship between gun availability, homicide, suicide and mass shootings, and on the balance between public safety and individual liberty. Researchers and advocates dispute how strongly particular laws — background checks, safe storage, assault-weapon restrictions — affect crime and death rates, and the topic is among the most contested in many countries' politics.
