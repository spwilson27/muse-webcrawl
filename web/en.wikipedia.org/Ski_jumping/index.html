Ski jumping


A jumper in the V-style position

Ski jumping is a winter sport in which competitors aim to achieve the longest jump while maintaining good form, launching from a specially designed ramp (take-off) on a ski jumping hill. It is a regular event at the Winter Olympic Games and is governed by the International Ski and Snowboard Federation (FIS). Points are awarded for distance and for style, assessed by judges.

Technique

The jumper descends the inrun in a crouch, reaches the take-off at speeds of up to 95 km/h on large hills, and launches into flight, leaning forward over the skis. Since the 1980s and 1990s the V-style — skis spread in a V, pioneered by Jan Boklöv — has replaced the parallel style, offering greater lift. Landing is ideally made in the Telemark position, with one foot ahead of the other and knees bent.

Hills and events

Hills are classified by their construction (K) point and hill size: normal hills (K90/HS106), large hills (K120/HS140), and ski flying hills (K185 and above), where jumps beyond 250 metres have been achieved. Major competitions include the World Cup, the Four Hills Tournament (held in Germany and Austria over New Year), the Nordic World Ski Championships, and the Olympics. The longest-standing format is two rounds, with the field cut after the first.

History

The sport originated in Norway in the 19th century, credited to Sondre Norheim of Telemark; the Holmenkollen festival in Oslo remains its spiritual home. Ski jumping has been on the Olympic programme since the first Winter Games in 1924. Women's ski jumping was added to the Olympics in 2014, and the first women's ski flying World Cup event was held in 2023.
