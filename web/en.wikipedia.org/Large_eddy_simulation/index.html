Large eddy simulation

Large eddy simulation (LES) is a mathematical model for turbulence used in computational fluid dynamics. It was initially proposed in 1963 by Joseph Smagorinsky to simulate atmospheric air currents and was explored further by James Deardorff in the 1970s. LES operates on the principle that the largest, energy-carrying scales of turbulence are resolved explicitly on the computational grid, while the effect of the smallest, unresolved scales is represented by a subgrid-scale (SGS) model.

Method

The governing Navier–Stokes equations are spatially filtered, separating resolved large eddies from subgrid motions. The unresolved stresses are typically closed with an eddy-viscosity model, most famously the Smagorinsky–Lilly model, in which the subgrid viscosity is proportional to the local strain rate and the square of the filter width. Dynamic procedures, introduced by Germano and co-workers, compute the model coefficient from the resolved field itself.

Applications

Because it resolves large-scale unsteadiness, LES is more accurate than Reynolds-averaged Navier–Stokes (RANS) for flows with separation, recirculation, combustion, aeroacoustics, and atmospheric boundary layers, at much lower cost than direct numerical simulation (DNS), which must resolve all scales. LES is widely used in meteorology, engine and gas-turbine simulation, aerodynamics, and environmental flows, and underlies hybrid RANS–LES methods such as detached eddy simulation.
