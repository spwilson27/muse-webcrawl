The F-22 is the world's first operational fifth-generation fighter.

The Lockheed Martin F-22 Raptor is an American single-seat, twin-engine, supersonic all-weather stealth fighter aircraft developed for the United States Air Force (USAF). As a product of the USAF's Advanced Tactical Fighter (ATF) programme, the aircraft was designed as an air superiority fighter, but also incorporates ground attack, electronic warfare, and signals intelligence capabilities. The prime contractor, Lockheed Martin, built most of the airframe and performed final assembly, while Boeing supplied the wings, aft fuselage, avionics integration, and training systems.

Development

The ATF programme began in 1981 to counter emerging Soviet fighters and surface-to-air missiles. Lockheed's YF-22 prototype was selected over Northrop's YF-23 in 1991. The F-22 first flew in 1997 and entered service in December 2005. Originally planned at 750 aircraft, production was repeatedly cut, and ended in 2011 after just 187 production aircraft, in part because of high cost, the lack of a peer air-to-air threat, and a US export ban intended to protect its technology. The final F-22 was delivered in 2012.

Design

The Raptor combines stealth shaping and radar-absorbent materials with supercruise — sustained supersonic flight without afterburners — and thrust-vectoring nozzles that deflect exhaust ±20 degrees for exceptional agility. Its AN/APG-77 AESA radar and integrated sensor fusion give the pilot a single picture of the battlespace. Armament is carried internally to preserve stealth: an M61A2 20 mm cannon, AIM-120 AMRAAM and AIM-9 Sidewinder missiles, and optionally JDAM precision bombs.

Operational history

F-22s are based principally at Joint Base Langley-Eustis, Joint Base Elmendorf-Richardson, and Joint Base Pearl Harbor-Hickam, with deployments worldwide. The Raptor flew its first combat missions in September 2014, striking Islamic State targets in Syria. In February 2023 an F-22 shot down a Chinese surveillance balloon off the US East Coast — the type's first air-to-air kill. The USAF plans to keep the upgraded F-22 in service into the 2030s alongside its NGAD successor programme.
