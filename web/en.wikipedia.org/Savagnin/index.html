Savagnin

Savagnin (or Savagnin blanc) is a variety of white wine grape with green-skinned berries, grown mostly in the Jura wine region of eastern France. It is best known as the sole grape of the region's distinctive vin jaune ("yellow wine"), and belongs to the ancient Traminer family of grapes: its aromatic pink-skinned mutation is Gewürztraminer.

Vin jaune

Savagnin reaches its apogee in vin jaune, made only in the Jura (most famously at Château-Chalon). After fermentation the wine is aged in oak barrels for at least six years and three months without topping up, so that a film of yeast (the voile, comparable to the flor of sherry) grows on the surface. The wine emerges deeply golden, intensely flavoured with walnuts, almonds, spices and dried fruit, and capable of ageing for many decades. It is bottled in the traditional 62-centilitre clavelin. Savagnin is also used for still dry white Jura wines, often blended with Chardonnay, and for the region's vin de paille.

Distribution and synonyms

Outside the Jura, the variety is found in small plantings elsewhere in France and in Australia, where vines long believed to be Albariño were identified in 2008–2009 by DNA testing as Savagnin, leading to relabelling of the wines. In Switzerland and the upper Rhine it has historically been known as Heida or Païen. Savagnin is genetically identical to Traminer, the unaromatic parent form of Gewürztraminer grown in South Tyrol (where the village of Tramin gives the family its name).
