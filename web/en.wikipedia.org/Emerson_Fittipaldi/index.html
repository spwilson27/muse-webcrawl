Emerson Fittipaldi

Emerson Fittipaldi (born 12 December 1946) is a Brazilian former racing driver who won the Formula One World Drivers' Championship in 1972 and 1974 and the Indianapolis 500 in 1989 and 1993. He was the youngest Formula One world champion at the time of his first title and opened the way for later Brazilian stars such as Nelson Piquet and Ayrton Senna.

Early career

Fittipaldi was born in São Paulo into a motorsport family — his father was a racing journalist and his brother Wilson also raced in Formula One. After success in karting and Brazilian Formula Vee, he moved to England in 1969, won the British Formula Three championship, and joined Team Lotus in Formula One in 1970.

Formula One career

Following the death of Jochen Rindt, Fittipaldi became Lotus's lead driver, winning the 1970 United States Grand Prix in only his fourth start. He took the 1972 championship with five wins, moved to McLaren in 1974 and won a second title, then left to join his brother's family team, Fittipaldi Automotive, whose cars were uncompetitive. He retired from Formula One in 1980 with 14 victories and 35 podiums from 144 starts.

IndyCar career

After a break, Fittipaldi relaunched his career in American CART racing in 1984. Driving for Patrick Racing and then Penske, he won the 1989 CART championship and the Indianapolis 500 in 1989 and 1993 — famously celebrating the second win by drinking orange juice to promote his family's citrus business. A serious crash at Michigan in 1996 ended his top-level career.

Later life

Fittipaldi has remained involved in Brazilian motorsport and business, and briefly in politics, standing in the 2022 Italian general election. His grandsons Pietro and Enzo Fittipaldi are also racing drivers.
