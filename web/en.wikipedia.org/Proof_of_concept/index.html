Proof of concept

A proof of concept (POC or PoC) or proof of principle is a realisation of a certain idea, method, or principle in order to demonstrate its feasibility, or a demonstration in principle with the aim of verifying that some concept or theory has practical potential. A proof of concept is usually small and may or may not be complete. The term originated in engineering and technology development and is now used across business, software, filmmaking, drug development, and computer security.

In engineering and business

In product development, a POC is a prototype built to test whether a design idea can be realised at all, before investing in a fully engineered product. In business, a proof of concept evaluates whether a proposed solution or venture is viable for a particular customer or market; technology vendors routinely run paid POC projects in which a product is trialled in a client's environment against agreed criteria. In software development, a POC (sometimes a "spike") explores the riskiest technical assumptions of a project. POCs differ from pilots, which test a near-complete product under real conditions, and from minimum viable products, which test market desirability.

In science and medicine

"Proof of principle" studies in pharmacology and medicine provide early evidence that a drug engages its target and produces the intended biological effect in humans, preceding large efficacy trials. In the physical sciences, proof-of-concept experiments demonstrate a new phenomenon or technique on a laboratory scale — for example, early demonstrations of quantum teleportation or laboratory fusion gain.

In computer security

In security research, a proof-of-concept exploit is code demonstrating that a vulnerability is genuinely exploitable. Security researchers publish POCs to substantiate vulnerability reports and motivate patching; the practice is controversial when code could be adapted for attacks, and norms of coordinated disclosure govern when full exploit code should be released.

In entertainment

Filmmakers create proof-of-concept shorts or trailers to pitch features to financiers; several major films began as POC shorts demonstrating a visual style or world.
