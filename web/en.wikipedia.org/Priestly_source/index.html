Priestly source

FieldBiblical studies; source criticism
CorpusPentateuch (Genesis–Deuteronomy)
Proposed dateExilic or post-exilic period (c. 6th–5th century BCE)
Other sourcesJahwist (J); Elohist (E); Deuteronomist (D)

The Priestly source (often abbreviated P) is one of the four sources of the Pentateuch identified by the documentary hypothesis. It is characterised by an interest in ritual, genealogy, chronology, priestly legislation, and the covenant, and by a formal, sometimes formulaic style.

Characteristics

Passages assigned to P include the first creation account in Genesis 1, the flood narrative in part, the covenant with Abraham in Genesis 17, much of Exodus concerning the construction of the Tabernacle, almost the whole of Leviticus, and substantial parts of Numbers. P refers to God as Elohim until the revelation of the name Yahweh to Moses in Exodus 6, and portrays God as majestic and transcendent rather than anthropomorphic. Its narrative is structured by genealogies (toledot) and precise dates and measurements.

Dating and authorship

Scholars traditionally attributed P to Aaronid priestly circles in Jerusalem. Julius Wellhausen placed it last among the sources, in the exilic or post-exilic period; some scholars, following Yehezkel Kaufmann, have argued for an earlier, pre-exilic date. The Holiness Code (Leviticus 17–26) is sometimes distinguished as a separate stratum (H) within or alongside P. While the classical documentary hypothesis has been modified and contested, P remains a standard analytical category in biblical scholarship.
