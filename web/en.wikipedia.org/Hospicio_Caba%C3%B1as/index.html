Hospicio Cabañas

LocationGuadalajara, Jalisco, Mexico
Built1805–1845
ArchitectManuel Tolsá
StyleNeoclassical
StatusUNESCO World Heritage Site (1997)

The Hospicio Cabañas in Guadalajara, Jalisco, Mexico, is one of the oldest and largest hospital complexes in the Americas. Founded by Bishop Juan Cruz Ruiz de Cabañas at the beginning of the 19th century, it was built to provide care for orphans, the elderly, the disabled, and the chronically ill. The neoclassical complex was designed by the architect Manuel Tolsá.

Architecture

The single-storey complex is organised around a series of courtyards and covers an entire city block. Its chapel has a Greek-cross plan surmounted by a dome. The restrained exterior hides an interior of remarkable scale and proportions.

Murals

Between 1938 and 1939, the artist José Clemente Orozco painted a celebrated cycle of frescoes in the chapel, the masterpiece of which is The Man of Fire (El Hombre en Llamas) in the dome, considered one of the masterpieces of Mexican muralism. The murals depict themes of the Conquest, pre-Hispanic ritual, and the Spanish colonial order.

Today

In 1997 UNESCO inscribed the Hospicio Cabañas on the World Heritage List. It now functions as the Instituto Cultural Cabañas, a museum and cultural centre hosting exhibitions and concerts.
