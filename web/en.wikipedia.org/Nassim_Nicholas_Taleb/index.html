Nassim Nicholas Taleb

Born: 12 September 1960, Amioun, Lebanon

Occupation: Essayist, scholar, statistician, former trader

Known for: The Black Swan, Antifragile, the Incerto series

Nassim Nicholas Taleb (born 12 September 1960) is a Lebanese-American essayist, mathematical statistician, former option trader, and risk analyst. His work concerns problems of randomness, probability, complexity and uncertainty. He is best known for his multivolume essay series the Incerto, which includes Fooled by Randomness (2001), The Black Swan (2007), The Bed of Procrustes (2010), Antifragile (2012), and Skin in the Game (2018).

Early life and career

Taleb was born in Amioun, Lebanon, into a prominent Greek Orthodox family. He studied at the University of Paris, where he received a bachelor's and a master's degree, earned an MBA from the Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania, and later a PhD in management science from the University of Paris (Dauphine). He worked as an options trader and derivatives specialist on Wall Street for about two decades, running the hedge fund Empirica Capital, before becoming a full-time writer and scholar. He has held academic posts including Distinguished Professor of Risk Engineering at New York University's Tandon School of Engineering.

Ideas

Taleb popularised the concept of a "black swan" event: a rare, unpredictable occurrence with extreme impact that is rationalised in hindsight. He argues that human knowledge is fragile to such events and that many statistical models, particularly Gaussian-based risk models, dangerously underestimate tail risk. In Antifragile he introduced the notion of systems that gain from disorder, contrasting them with fragile systems that are harmed by volatility. Other recurring themes include the "lindy effect", the importance of "skin in the game" (decision-makers bearing the consequences of their decisions), and scepticism toward experts he calls "intellectual yet idiots."

Criticism

Taleb's combative style and some of his claims have drawn criticism from statisticians and economists, though his warnings about systemic financial risk were widely credited after the 2008 financial crisis, during which funds advised by his methods reportedly profited substantially.
