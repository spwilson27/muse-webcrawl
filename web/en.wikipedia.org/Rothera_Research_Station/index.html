Rothera Research Station

The Rothera Research Station is a British Antarctic Survey (BAS) base on the Antarctic Peninsula, located at Rothera Point, Adelaide Island, off the west coast of Graham Land. It is one of the largest British research stations in Antarctica and serves as the principal logistics hub for BAS operations in the region, including support for the deep-field and airborne science programmes conducted from its gravel runway.

History

Rothera was established in 1975 to replace the station at Adelaide Island (Base T), which had become unusable because its skiway had deteriorated. The point is named after John Rothera, a Falkland Islands Dependencies Survey surveyor. The station grew steadily through the 1980s and 1990s, with the main building complex, the Bonner Laboratory for marine and terrestrial biology, and a 900-metre gravel runway that allows wheeled aircraft to operate.

Facilities and research

The station supports research in marine biology, terrestrial ecology, glaciology, geology and atmospheric science. The Bonner Laboratory, opened in 1997 (and rebuilt after a fire in 2001), includes aquaria and diving facilities; Rothera is noted for its scientific diving programme, which studies Antarctic marine ecosystems year-round. The nearby RRS Sir David Attenborough and the Dash 7 and Twin Otter aircraft based at Rothera link the station to the Falkland Islands and to BAS's other stations, including Halley and the deep-field sites served via Sky Blu.

Modernisation

The Antarctic Infrastructure Modernisation Programme has added the Discovery Building, a new operations and logistics facility named in part for the RRS Discovery, intended to consolidate Rothera's buildings and reduce its environmental footprint.
