Kingdom of Poland

The Kingdom of Poland (Polish: Królestwo Polskie) is the name borne by several Polish states across history. It most commonly denotes the kingdom established with the coronation of Bolesław I the Brave in 1025, restored in 1295 and again in 1320, which endured — later in union with Lithuania as the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth — until the partitions of the late 18th century. The name was revived for the Congress Kingdom of Poland (1815–1867) under the Russian Empire and for the German-sponsored Regency Kingdom of 1916–1918.

Medieval kingdom

Bolesław I was crowned the first king of Poland in 1025, shortly before his death. The royal title lapsed as the realm fragmented into duchies after 1138. Przemysł II restored the coronation in 1295, and Władysław I the Elbow-high secured lasting reunification in 1320. Under Casimir III the Great (r. 1333–1370) the kingdom codified its laws, founded the University of Kraków (1364) and expanded eastward.

Jagiellonian era and Commonwealth

From 1386 the Polish crown was joined with the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, first dynastically and then, by the Union of Lublin in 1569, in the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, a vast elective monarchy. The partitioned Commonwealth ceased to exist in 1795.

Later uses

The Congress of Vienna created a Kingdom of Poland in personal union with Russia in 1815; after the January Uprising of 1863–64 it was reduced to a province. In 1916 Germany and Austria-Hungary proclaimed a Regency Kingdom, a short-lived client state superseded by the Second Polish Republic in 1918.
