The Poet Laureate Consultant in Poetry to the Library of Congress, commonly known as the Poet Laureate of the United States, is appointed by the Librarian of Congress for a term of one or two years. The position evolved from the Consultant in Poetry to the Library of Congress, created in 1937; an Act of Congress in 1985 changed the title effective 1986, modelling it loosely on the Poet Laureate of the United Kingdom.

Duties

The Laureate's duties are deliberately kept to a minimum to allow time for personal writing projects. The incumbent gives an annual lecture and reading of his or her poetry, and usually plans a poetry-related project — for example, Billy Collins' "Poetry 180" for schools, Ted Kooser's newspaper column, Tracy K. Smith's travels to rural communities, and Joy Harjo's interactive map project. The position carries a modest stipend and an office in the Library of Congress's Thomas Jefferson Building.

History

The consultancy was funded initially by a gift from Archer M. Huntington. The first consultant was Joseph Auslander (1937–1941). Later holders include Robert Frost, William Carlos Williams (whose appointment was delayed amid political controversy), Elizabeth Bishop, Robert Lowell, Gwendolyn Brooks, Robert Penn Warren — who became the first to hold the redesigned laureate title in 1986 — and Rita Dove, the first African American laureate.

Notable laureates

Laureates include Robert Hass, Robert Pinsky (the first to serve three terms), Billy Collins, Louise Glück, Ted Kooser, Donald Hall, Charles Simic, Kay Ryan, W. S. Merwin, Philip Levine, Rita Dove, Natasha Trethewey, Juan Felipe Herrera, Tracy K. Smith, Joy Harjo (the first Native American laureate, serving three terms) and Ada Limón.
