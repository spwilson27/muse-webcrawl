Arcturus

Arcturus is the brightest star in the northern constellation of Boötes, and the brightest star in the northern celestial hemisphere. With an apparent visual magnitude of −0.05, it is the fourth-brightest star in the night sky, after Sirius, Canopus and Alpha Centauri. It is a red (orange) giant of spectral type K0 III, located about 36.7 light-years from the Sun.

Characteristics

Arcturus has expanded to roughly 25 times the Sun's diameter and radiates about 170 times the Sun's luminosity in visible and infrared light combined, though its surface is cooler, at around 4,300 K, giving it an orange hue. Its low metallicity and high proper motion suggest it is an old star, possibly belonging to a disrupted satellite galaxy accreted by the Milky Way. The name Arcturus derives from the Greek Arktouros, "guardian of the bear", referring to the neighbouring constellations Ursa Major and Ursa Minor.

Observation

Arcturus is easily found by following the arc of the handle of the Big Dipper ("arc to Arcturus"). In 1933, light from Arcturus, focused by telescopes onto photoelectric cells, was used to open the Chicago World's Fair, chosen because the star's light had left around the time of the previous Chicago fair in 1893.
