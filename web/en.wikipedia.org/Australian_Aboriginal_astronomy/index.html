Australian Aboriginal astronomy

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Emu in the Sky in the Milky Way

Australian Aboriginal astronomy encompasses the astronomy of Aboriginal Australians, whose traditions are among the oldest continuous astronomical cultures in the world. The stars, Sun, Moon, and Milky Way feature prominently in Dreaming stories, songlines, calendars, and navigation.

The Emu in the Sky

One of the most widespread constellations is the Emu in the Sky, formed not by stars but by the dark dust lanes of the Milky Way. Its position in the sky through the year signals seasonal activities, such as the time to collect emu eggs. Other notable asterisms include the Pleiades (often the Seven Sisters pursued by Orion's stars) and the Southern Cross.

Calendars and phenomena

Aboriginal groups developed seasonal calendars based on heliacal risings of stars and tracked the motions of the Sun and Moon; several traditions correctly associated tides with the Moon, and eclipses were interpreted within local cosmologies. Some researchers have proposed that stone arrangements such as Wurdi Youang in Victoria mark solstitial directions.

Research

European records of Aboriginal astronomy date from the 19th century (notably the work of William Edward Stanbridge among the Boorong), and the field has grown in recent decades, often called cultural astronomy or archaeoastronomy.
