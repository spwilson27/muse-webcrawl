Touch of Evil is a 1958 American film noir written and directed by Orson Welles, who also stars as the corrupt police captain Hank Quinlan. The film is often cited as one of the last and greatest examples of the classic noir period, and it was selected for preservation in the U.S. National Film Registry in 1993.

Plot

On the U.S.–Mexican border, Mexican narcotics investigator Miguel Vargas (Charlton Heston) witnesses a car bombing while honeymooning with his American wife Susan (Janet Leigh). The investigation brings him into conflict with Quinlan, a bloated, cane-carrying veteran detective whose uncanny record of convictions rests on planted evidence. As Vargas exposes Quinlan's methods, Quinlan conspires with a local crime family to frame Susan and destroy Vargas; the conflict ends on a bridge over an oil-slicked canal, recorded on a hidden wire, with Quinlan shot by his own devoted partner Menzies.

Production and versions

The film opens with a celebrated three-minute-plus continuous crane shot — among the most famous long takes in cinema — following the doomed car through a border town. Universal took the film away from Welles and re-edited it, adding scenes shot by Harry Keller; Welles responded with a 58-page memorandum of requested changes, largely ignored in his lifetime. A 1976 release of the longer preview version and, above all, Walter Murch's 1998 re-edit, reconstructed from the memo, restored the film's reputation. Marlene Dietrich's cameo as the brothel keeper Tana delivers its elegiac epitaph for Quinlan: "He was some kind of a man."
