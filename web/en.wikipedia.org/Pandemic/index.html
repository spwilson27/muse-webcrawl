Pandemic

Pandemic
DefinitionAn epidemic of an infectious disease spread across multiple continents or worldwide
Contrasts withEndemic (stable local presence); epidemic (regional outbreak)
Historical examplesBlack Death, 1918 influenza, HIV/AIDS, COVID-19
Key coordinating bodyWorld Health Organization (WHO)

A pandemic is an epidemic of an infectious disease that has spread across a large region — multiple continents or worldwide — affecting a substantial number of people. A widespread endemic disease that is stable in a population, such as malaria in many tropical regions, is not generally classed as a pandemic. The word derives from the Greek pan ("all") and demos ("people").

Notable pandemics

Recorded pandemics include the Plague of Justinian (541–549) and the Black Death (1346–1353), both caused by the bacterium Yersinia pestis, which killed tens of millions. The seven cholera pandemics of the nineteenth and twentieth centuries spread cholera from its home in the Ganges Delta around the world. Influenza pandemics recur periodically: the 1918 "Spanish flu" killed an estimated 50 million or more people; lesser pandemics followed in 1957, 1968 and 2009. HIV/AIDS, first recognised in 1981, has killed more than 40 million people. The COVID-19 pandemic, caused by the coronavirus SARS-CoV-2 and declared a pandemic by the WHO on 11 March 2020, caused at least seven million confirmed deaths and profound global social and economic disruption.

Preparedness and response

Pandemic risk is greatest when a novel pathogen — often a zoonotic virus to which humans have little immunity — acquires sustained human-to-human transmission. International surveillance, rapid diagnostics, vaccine development platforms, stockpiles and non-pharmaceutical interventions such as isolation, masks and travel measures are the principal tools of preparedness, coordinated internationally under the International Health Regulations. The WHO may declare a Public Health Emergency of International Concern, as it did for COVID-19 (2020), Ebola (2014, 2019) and mpox (2022, 2024).
