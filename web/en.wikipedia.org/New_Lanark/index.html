New Lanark is a village on the River Clyde, approximately 1.4 miles from Lanark, in Lanarkshire, Scotland. It was founded in 1785 by David Dale, who built cotton mills and housing for the mill workers, exploiting the water power of the Falls of Clyde. Dale sold the mills, lands, and village in 1799 to a partnership that included his son-in-law Robert Owen, with the price made attractive in order to secure Owen's vision.

Robert Owen's experiment

Under Owen (1800–1825), New Lanark became an early and celebrated example of enlightened industrial management and utopian socialism. Owen improved housing, sanitation, and the village store; refused to employ children under ten; established the Institute for the Formation of Character (1816); and founded what is regarded as Britain's first infant school, emphasizing education without corporal punishment. The village attracted visitors, reformers, and royalty from across Europe, and Owen's ideas influenced the cooperative movement and factory legislation.

Later history

Cotton spinning continued until 1968. After a period of decline, the New Lanark Conservation Trust (founded 1974) restored the village, which retains an inhabited community alongside a visitor centre, hotel in a former mill, and youth hostel. New Lanark was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2001, recognized for its pioneering social and industrial heritage.
