Gaudiya Vaishnavism

Gaudiya Vaishnavism (also known as Chaitanya Vaishnavism) is a Vaishnava Hindu religious movement inspired by Chaitanya Mahaprabhu (1486–1534) in India. "Gaudiya" refers to the Gauda region of Bengal (present-day Malda district of West Bengal), with Vaishnavism meaning "the worship of Vishnu". Its followers revere Krishna as the supreme form of God (svayam bhagavan) and emphasise congregational chanting of his names, especially the Hare Krishna mantra, as the principal means of spiritual realisation in the present age.

Theology

The movement's theology was systematised by the Six Goswamis of Vrindavan, direct disciples of Chaitanya, especially Rupa Goswami and Jiva Goswami. Its characteristic philosophy is acintya-bheda-abheda, "inconceivable simultaneous oneness and difference" between God and his energies, including the individual souls. Devotional service (bhakti) directed to Krishna and his consort Radha, as developed in the Bhagavata Purana, is held to culminate in pure love of God (prema).

History and spread

Chaitanya, regarded by followers as an incarnation of Krishna himself, spread ecstatic public chanting (nagara-kirtana) through Bengal and Odisha. The tradition was carried on by lineages of teachers and, in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, was revived and organised by Bhaktivinoda Thakura and Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, founder of the Gaudiya Math. His disciple A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada took the movement to the West in 1965, founding the International Society for Krishna Consciousness (ISKCON), through which Gaudiya Vaishnavism became a worldwide tradition.
