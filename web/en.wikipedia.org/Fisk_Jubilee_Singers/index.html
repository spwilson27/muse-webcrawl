Fisk Jubilee Singers

The Fisk Jubilee Singers are an African-American a cappella ensemble of students of Fisk University in Nashville, Tennessee. Founded in 1871, the group introduced the Negro spiritual to concert audiences around the world, touring to raise money for their struggling university and helping to preserve the songs of enslaved people as a recognised art form.

Founding and first tours

Fisk, a school for freedmen, was near bankruptcy in 1871 when its treasurer and music director, George L. White, assembled a choir of students — most of them formerly enslaved — to tour for funds. They took their name from the biblical year of Jubilee. Singing spirituals such as "Steal Away" and "Swing Low, Sweet Chariot" arranged for concert performance, the Singers achieved acclaim in the northern United States, and in 1873 sailed for England, where they performed before Queen Victoria. Their earnings built Jubilee Hall (1876), now a National Historic Landmark and the oldest academic building in continuous use for African-American education in the United States.

Legacy

The Singers' tours established the spiritual as a concert genre and funded Fisk's survival. An early recording of "Swing Low, Sweet Chariot" by the group in 1909 is in the United States National Recording Registry. The ensemble continues today with student singers from Fisk, received the National Medal of Arts in 2008, and won a Grammy Award in 2021 for the album Celebrating Fisk!.
