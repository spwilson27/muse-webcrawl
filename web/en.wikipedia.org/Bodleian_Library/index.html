Bodleian Library

The Bodleian Library is the main research library of the University of Oxford, and is one of the oldest libraries in Europe. It derives its name from its founder, Sir Thomas Bodley (1545–1613). With over 13 million printed items, it is the second-largest library in Britain after the British Library. Under the Legal Deposit Libraries Act 2003, it is one of six legal deposit libraries for works published in the United Kingdom, and it also holds collections of manuscripts, maps, and archives of international significance.

History

Oxford's first library, in the University Church of St Mary the Virgin, dates to the 14th century; Duke Humfrey's Library opened in 1488. The library declined after 1550, when its books were removed during the Reformation. Bodley, a retired diplomat, refounded it: his library opened on 8 November 1602 with around 2,000 books. In 1610 Bodley secured an agreement with the Stationers' Company of London that a copy of every book printed in England should be deposited at the Bodleian — the foundation of its legal deposit collection.

Buildings and collections

The central site includes Duke Humfrey's Library, the Divinity School, the Radcliffe Camera (opened 1749), and the Weston Library (reopened 2015), which houses special collections and exhibitions. Traditionally a reference-only library, the Bodleian long required readers to swear an oath not to "bring into the Library or kindle therein any fire or flame", and books were once chained to the shelves.

Use today

The Bodleian serves Oxford's students and scholars and admits external researchers. Its catalogues and digital collections (via Digital Bodleian) make millions of items searchable online.
