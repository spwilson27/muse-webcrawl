North Rhine-Westphalia (abbreviated NRW) is a state (Land) in western Germany. With a population of about 18 million, it is Germany's most populous state and its largest by economic output. Its capital is Düsseldorf and its largest city is Cologne; other major cities include Dortmund, Essen, Duisburg, Bochum, and Wuppertal in the Ruhr conurbation, and Bonn, the former capital of West Germany.

History

The state was created by the British military government in 1946 through the merger of the northern part of the Rhine Province with the province of Westphalia; the Free State of Lippe joined in 1947. The Ruhr area had been the industrial heartland of Germany since the nineteenth century, based on coal and steel.

Economy

North Rhine-Westphalia has the largest economy of any German state, accounting for roughly a fifth of national GDP. Although coal mining has ended (the last colliery closed in 2018), the state remains a centre of chemicals, machinery, logistics, media, and insurance, and hosts major trade fairs in Düsseldorf and Cologne. The Rhine-Ruhr metropolitan region is one of Europe's largest urban agglomerations.

Culture

The state is known for its carnival celebrations in Cologne, Düsseldorf, and Mainz's Rhineland neighbours, its Kölsch and Altbier beer traditions, and landmarks such as Cologne Cathedral and the Zollverein Coal Mine Industrial Complex, both UNESCO World Heritage Sites.
