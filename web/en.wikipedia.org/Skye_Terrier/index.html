The Skye Terrier is a Scottish breed of terrier named for the Isle of Skye in the Inner Hebrides, where it has been kept for at least 400 years. It is a long, low dog with a flowing double coat, bred to hunt foxes, badgers and otters among the rocky cairns and dens of the island.

Characteristics

The Skye is twice as long as it is tall, with short sturdy legs and a long coat that falls over the face and body. Ears are either pricked or drop. Colours include black, grey, silver and cream. Queen Victoria's fondness for the breed brought it great popularity in the nineteenth century. The most famous Skye is Greyfriars Bobby of Edinburgh, who, according to legend, kept vigil at his master's grave for fourteen years.

Status

Despite its history, the Skye Terrier is now among the rarest of British native breeds, with very low annual registrations, and it appears on the Kennel Club's list of vulnerable native breeds. It is known for loyalty, dignity and a somewhat reserved temperament with strangers.
