Gollum

Gollum is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium, appearing in The Hobbit (1937) and The Lord of the Rings (1954–55). He was originally a Stoor Hobbit named Sméagol, who was corrupted by the One Ring and transformed over centuries into a wretched, solitary creature. He is known for his distinctive hissing speech, his habit of referring to the Ring as "my precious," and his split personality.

History

Sméagol's friend Déagol found the One Ring in the river Anduin around the year 2463 of the Third Age. Sméagol murdered Déagol for it, was banished by his people, and retreated into a cave in the Misty Mountains. The Ring extended his life for nearly 500 years while twisting his body and mind; the gurgling noise he made in his throat earned him the name Gollum.

In 2941, Bilbo Baggins found the Ring in Gollum's cave and won his escape in a riddle contest. Years later Gollum left the mountains searching for "Baggins," was captured and interrogated by Sauron — revealing the Ring's location — and later followed Frodo Baggins's company. He guided Frodo and Samwise Gamgee to Mordor, betrayed them to the giant spider Shelob, and finally, at the Cracks of Doom, bit the Ring from Frodo's finger and fell with it into the fire, destroying the Ring and himself — an outcome Gandalf had foreshadowed in saying Gollum might yet have a part to play.

Portrayal

In Peter Jackson's film trilogies, Gollum was performed by Andy Serkis using pioneering motion-capture technology, a portrayal widely acclaimed. The character has become an enduring archetype of addiction and moral corruption in popular culture.
