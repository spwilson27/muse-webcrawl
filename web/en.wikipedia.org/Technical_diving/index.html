Technical diving

Technical diving (often shortened to tec diving) is a form of scuba diving that goes beyond the conventional limits of recreational diving, typically involving one or more of: depths greater than 40 metres, staged decompression obligations, overhead environments such as caves and wrecks, and breathing gases other than air or standard nitrox, such as trimix or pure oxygen for accelerated decompression.

Characteristics

Technical divers carry redundant equipment — multiple tanks, regulators, buoyancy devices and computers — because in an overhead environment or with a mandatory decompression ceiling they cannot ascend directly to the surface in an emergency. Dives are planned in detail in advance, including gas volumes, decompression schedules and contingency plans.

Gases

Deep technical dives use helium-based mixtures such as trimix (oxygen, nitrogen and helium) to reduce narcosis and oxygen toxicity, while decompression may be accelerated with nitrox and pure oxygen at shallow stops.

Training and history

The term "technical diving" was popularised in the early 1990s, notably by the magazine aquaCorps and its editor Michael Menduno, as experienced cave and wreck divers formalised procedures that recreational agencies then did not teach. Organisations such as TDI, IANTD, GUE and PADI's TecRec programme now offer technical training.
