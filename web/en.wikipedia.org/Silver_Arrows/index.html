Silver Arrows

Silver Arrows (German: Silberpfeile) is a nickname given to the dominant silver-painted Grand Prix motor racing cars of Germany between 1934 and 1939, built by Mercedes-Benz and Auto Union. The name was later revived for Mercedes-Benz's Formula One cars after the marque returned as a constructor in 2010, whose livery is likewise silver.

Origin of the name

According to the popular — and now disputed — legend, at the 1934 Eifelrennen at the Nürburgring the new Mercedes-Benz W25 weighed in at 751 kg, one kilogram over the 750 kg limit of the new formula. Team manager Alfred Neubauer supposedly ordered the white paint scraped off overnight, leaving the bare aluminium body gleaming silver; Manfred von Brauchitsch then won the race, and the "Silver Arrows" were born. Whatever the truth of the anecdote, both German teams raced in unpainted or silver-painted metal, silver having become Germany's national racing colour in place of white.

Dominance

Backed by state subsidies and advanced engineering — supercharged engines, mid-engined layouts in the Auto Union cars, and outputs eventually exceeding 500 horsepower — the Mercedes and Auto Union machines won nearly every major Grand Prix from 1934 until the outbreak of the Second World War, with drivers such as Rudolf Caracciola, Bernd Rosemeyer, Hermann Lang and Tazio Nuvolari (with Auto Union) at their wheels.
