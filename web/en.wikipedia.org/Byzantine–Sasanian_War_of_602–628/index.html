Byzantine–Sasanian War of 602–628

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Byzantine–Sasanian War of 602–628 was the last and most devastating of the many wars fought between the Byzantine (Eastern Roman) Empire and the Persian Sasanian Empire. It began when the Sasanian shah Khosrow II declared war to avenge the murdered emperor Maurice, and it ended, after a quarter-century of fighting across the Near East, with a Byzantine victory that restored the pre-war borders — but left both empires exhausted on the eve of the Arab conquests.

Course of the war

Persian armies initially swept all before them, conquering Mesopotamia, Syria, Palestine (Jerusalem fell in 614, and the True Cross was carried off to Ctesiphon), Egypt and much of Anatolia, reaching the Bosphorus opposite Constantinople by 626, where a joint Persian–Avar siege of the capital failed. Meanwhile the emperor Heraclius, having rebuilt the Byzantine army, launched a series of daring counter-offensives deep into Persian territory, campaigning in the Caucasus and Mesopotamia with Turkic allies.

End and aftermath

Heraclius's victory at the Battle of Nineveh in December 627 opened the road to Ctesiphon; Khosrow II was overthrown and killed by his son Kavad II, who made peace in 628, returning all conquered territory, prisoners and the True Cross. The restored status quo ante bellum concealed ruin: both empires were financially and militarily drained, and within a decade their southern provinces fell to the armies of the Rashidun Caliphate.
