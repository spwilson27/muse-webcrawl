Byzantine–Bulgarian wars

The Byzantine–Bulgarian wars were a series of conflicts fought between the Byzantines and Bulgarians which began after the Bulgars settled in the Balkan Peninsula in the 5th century and intensified with the expansion of the Bulgarian Empire to the south-west after 680 AD. The wars continued through much of the medieval period, punctuated by long periods of peace and alliance, until the fall of Bulgaria to the Ottomans in the late 14th and early 15th centuries.

Background

In 681, the First Bulgarian Empire was recognised by the Byzantine Empire after Khan Asparuh defeated Emperor Constantine IV. Despite a formal peace, hostilities soon resumed as both states competed for control of the northern Balkans, Thrace and the approaches to Constantinople.

Major conflicts

Notable episodes include Khan Krum's crushing victory at the Battle of Pliska in 811, in which Emperor Nikephoros I was killed; the long wars of Simeon I in the early 10th century, which brought Bulgaria to the height of its power; and the campaigns of Emperor Basil II, "the Bulgar-Slayer", whose victory at Kleidion in 1014 preceded the Byzantine annexation of Bulgaria in 1018. The Second Bulgarian Empire, restored in 1185, fought further wars against Byzantium until both states succumbed to Ottoman expansion.

Legacy

The centuries of warfare shaped the political geography, religious life and culture of south-eastern Europe, and the rivalry is a central theme of both Bulgarian and Byzantine historiography.
