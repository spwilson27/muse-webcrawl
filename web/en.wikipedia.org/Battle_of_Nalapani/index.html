Battle of Nalapani

Battle of Nalapani
Date31 October – 30 November 1814
LocationNalapani (Khalanga), near Dehradun, India
ResultBritish victory, at heavy cost
BelligerentsBritish East India Company; Kingdom of Nepal
CommandersRollo Gillespie (killed), John Mawbey; Balbhadra Kunwar

The Battle of Nalapani was the first battle of the Anglo-Nepalese War (1814–1816). It was fought between troops of the British East India Company and the Gorkha forces of the Kingdom of Nepal at the small fort of Nalapani, also called Khalanga, near Dehradun.

The fort was defended by Captain Balbhadra Kunwar with a garrison of only about 600 men, women and children, against a British force of several thousand. The first British assault on 31 October 1814 was repulsed with heavy losses, and the British commander, Major-General Sir Rollo Gillespie, was killed. Repeated attacks and eventually the cutting of the fort's water supply forced the issue; on the night of 30 November the surviving defenders, reduced to some seventy men, slipped away through the British lines rather than surrender.

Legacy

The stand at Nalapani became legendary in Nepal, and Balbhadra is celebrated as a national hero. The British, impressed by Gorkha bravery, later erected a memorial at the site honouring both Gillespie and "our brave adversary Balbhadra and his gallant men", and the battle helped establish the reputation that led to Gurkha recruitment into the British Army.
