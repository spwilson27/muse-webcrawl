One Canada Square is a skyscraper in Canary Wharf, London. It is among the tallest buildings in the United Kingdom and was the tallest building in the UK from 1990 until the completion of the Shard in 2010. The 50-storey tower is 235 metres (770 ft) tall and is clad in stainless steel. It is commonly known as the Canary Wharf Tower.

The building was designed by César Pelli's associate David Childs of the firm Skidmore, Owings and Merrill. It was constructed by Sir Robert McAlpine & Sons, and topped out in November 1990. Its pyramid roof contains a flashing aircraft warning light, a rare feature in London buildings.

History

The tower was the centrepiece of the Canary Wharf development by the Reichmann family's Olympia & York company, built on the derelict West India Docks in the London Docklands. The building struggled to attract tenants during the early-1990s property recession, contributing to Olympia & York's bankruptcy in 1992; the development was later bought back by a consortium including Paul Reichmann, and the tower subsequently filled with major financial tenants.

Design

The tower's stainless-steel cladding and obelisk-like form were inspired by earlier American skyscrapers and by Big Ben's silhouette. It has 50 floors of offices (the ground floor being level 0 in the numbering used), about 14,000 panes of glass, and is served by 32 passenger lifts. The lobby is clad in marble imported from Italy, Guatemala and Turkey.
