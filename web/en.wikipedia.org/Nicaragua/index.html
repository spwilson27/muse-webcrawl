Nicaragua, officially the Republic of Nicaragua, is the largest country in Central America, bordered by Honduras to the north, Costa Rica to the south, the Pacific Ocean to the west, and the Caribbean Sea to the east. Its capital and largest city is Managua. Nicaragua is known as the "land of lakes and volcanoes": Lake Nicaragua (Lake Cocibolca), the largest lake in Central America, contains the volcanic island of Ometepe and the only lake sharks historically reported in fresh water.

History

Indigenous peoples including the Nicarao inhabited the Pacific lowlands, while the Caribbean coast — the Mosquito Coast — came under British influence. Spain colonised the west from the 1520s, with León and Granada among the oldest Spanish cities in the Americas. Nicaragua gained independence in 1821, joined and then left the Federal Republic of Central America, and struggled through the 19th century with internal strife, the filibuster William Walker's brief rule, and US interventions. The US Marines occupied the country for much of 1912–1933, fighting Augusto César Sandino's guerrillas.

The Somoza family ruled from 1936 until the Sandinista (FSLN) revolution of 1979. The Sandinista government fought a US-backed insurgency (the Contras) through the 1980s — the conflict at the centre of the Iran–Contra affair — and lost power in the 1990 election to Violeta Chamorro. Daniel Ortega and the FSLN returned to power in 2007. Anti-government protests in 2018 were violently suppressed, drawing international condemnation and sanctions.

Geography and economy

A volcanic cordillera runs along the Pacific side; the east is lowland rainforest. Agriculture (coffee, beef, sugar, peanuts, bananas) dominates exports, with gold mining and tourism also important. Nicaragua remains one of the poorest countries in the Americas, and the San Juan River forms part of its border with Costa Rica.
