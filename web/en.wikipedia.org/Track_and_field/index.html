Track and field

Track and field (known as athletics in most of the world outside North America) is a sport that includes athletic contests based on running, jumping, and throwing skills. The name derives from the events' usual venue, a running track surrounding a field. It is one of the oldest sports, with roots in the ancient Olympic Games, and forms the centrepiece of the modern Summer Olympics. The world governing body is World Athletics (formerly the IAAF), founded in 1912.

Events

Track events include sprints (100 m, 200 m, 400 m), middle-distance (800 m, 1500 m) and long-distance races (5000 m, 10,000 m), hurdling, steeplechase, and relays, as well as road events such as the marathon and race walking. Field events comprise the jumps (long jump, triple jump, high jump, pole vault) and throws (shot put, discus, hammer, javelin). Combined events — the decathlon for men and heptathlon for women — test all-round ability over two days.

History

Competitive running and jumping appear in the festivals of ancient Greece from 776 BC, and in Irish and Scottish traditions. The modern sport took shape in nineteenth-century England and the United States through school, university, and club competitions. It was a founding sport of the modern Olympics in 1896; women's events were added in 1928. Standardization of rules, synthetic tracks from the 1960s, and electronic timing transformed performance, producing landmark feats such as Usain Bolt's 9.58-second 100 m world record in 2009.

Competitions

Major championships include the Olympic Games, the World Athletics Championships, the Diamond League circuit, and continental and national championships. Indoor athletics, held in winter on 200 m tracks, has its own world championships.
