The Coupe de France (officially Coupe de France de football) is the premier knockout cup competition in French football, organised by the French Football Federation. Founded in 1917 during the First World War and originally named the Coupe Charles Simon, after a sportsman killed in the war, it is open to every registered club in France and its overseas territories — regularly attracting more than 7,000 entries, making it one of the largest cup competitions in world football.

The Coupe de France trophy, awarded annually since 1918.

Format and character

The competition is a straight single-elimination knockout played over successive weekends from autumn to the final in late spring at the Stade de France. Its defining feature is openness: amateur village clubs host or visit Ligue 1 giants in single matches, producing celebrated giant-killings — such as Calais RUFC, a fourth-tier amateur side that reached the 2000 final, or US Quevilly's runs. Clubs from Guadeloupe, Martinique, French Guiana, Réunion, New Caledonia and Tahiti have also participated, sometimes travelling to mainland France for ties. The winner qualifies for the UEFA Europa League and traditionally receives the trophy from the President of the Republic.

History and records

Paris Saint-Germain is the most successful club with 15 titles (as of 2024), followed by Olympique de Marseille with 10 and AS Saint-Étienne and Lille with 6 each. Marseille won the first professional-era finals and the cup was dominated in the 1980s–2010s increasingly by PSG. The women's counterpart, the Coupe de France féminine, has been held since 2001 (as the Challenge de France originally).
