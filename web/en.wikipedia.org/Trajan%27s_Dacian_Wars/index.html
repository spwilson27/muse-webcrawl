Trajan's Dacian Wars

Trajan's Dacian Wars (101–102 and 105–106 AD) were two military campaigns fought between the Roman Empire and Dacia, during the reign of Emperor Trajan. The conquest of Dacia added a wealthy province rich in gold and silver mines north of the Danube and was commemorated by Trajan's Column in Rome.

Background

Under King Decebalus, Dacia had defeated a Roman expedition and imposed humiliating terms on Domitian. Trajan, after securing the Danube frontier, invaded in 101, defeating the Dacians at Tapae; Decebalus accepted peace in 102 but rearmed.

Second war

Trajan returned in 105 with a bridge built across the Danube by Apollodorus of Damascus. In 106 the Dacian capital Sarmizegetusa Regia fell; Decebalus took his own life, and Dacia became a Roman province guarded by legions and colonised by settlers from across the empire.
