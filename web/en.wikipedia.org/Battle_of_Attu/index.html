Battle of Attu

Date11–30 May 1943
LocationAttu Island, Aleutian Islands, Alaska
ResultAmerican victory
BelligerentsUnited States vs. Empire of Japan
CommandersGen. Eugene Landrum (US 7th Infantry Division) vs. Col. Yasuyo Yamasaki

The Battle of Attu, codenamed Operation Landcrab, took place from 11 to 30 May 1943 on Attu Island in Alaska's Aleutian Islands chain, during the Second World War. It was the only land battle of the war fought on territory that was part of an incorporated territory of the United States, and it ended with the annihilation of the Japanese garrison in one of the largest banzai charges of the Pacific War.

Background

Japanese forces had occupied Attu and the neighbouring island of Kiska in June 1942, during the Battle of Midway operations, partly as a diversion and partly to protect Japan's northern approaches. The United States, determined to recover the islands, first retook Amchitka and blockaded the garrisons; Kiska was to be invaded after Attu. The Japanese garrison on Attu, commanded by Colonel Yasuyo Yamasaki, numbered about 2,600 men dug into the mountainous, fog-bound terrain around Massacre Bay and Chichagof Harbor.

Battle

Elements of the US 7th Infantry Division landed on 11 May 1943. Progress was slow in bitter cold, fog and tundra mud, and General Albert E. Brown was replaced by General Eugene Landrum. American troops gradually compressed the Japanese into a pocket around Chichagof Harbor. On the night of 28–29 May, Yamasaki led the surviving Japanese force, perhaps 800 men, in a desperate banzai charge that overran American front-line positions and reached the rear areas before being destroyed. When organized resistance ended on 30 May, only 28 Japanese soldiers were taken prisoner; the rest were dead, many by suicide.

Aftermath

American casualties were heavy for the size of the operation: about 549 killed and more than 1,100 wounded, with thousands more suffering from exposure and disease. The battle provided a grim preview of the "fight to the last man" defence the United States would face across the Pacific. Kiska was evacuated by the Japanese in July 1943 before the Allied invasion. The native Unangax̑ (Aleut) villagers of Attu, who had been taken prisoner to Japan in 1942, were not permitted to return to their island after the war.
