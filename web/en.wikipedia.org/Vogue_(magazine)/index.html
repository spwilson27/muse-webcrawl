Vogue (magazine)

Categories: Fashion, lifestyle
First issue: 17 December 1892
Publisher: Condé Nast
Country: United States

Vogue is an American monthly fashion and lifestyle magazine that covers style news, haute couture fashion, beauty, culture, living, and runway. Founded in 1892 in New York City by Arthur Baldwin Turnure as a weekly newspaper, it was bought by Condé Nast in 1909 and transformed into the leading fashion magazine, today published in numerous international editions.

History

Under editors Edna Woolman Chase and later Diana Vreeland (1963–1971), Vogue shaped fashion photography and culture. Grace Mirabella led the magazine through the 1970s–80s, and Anna Wintour became editor-in-chief of American Vogue in 1988, revitalizing it with celebrity covers and a mix of high fashion and popular culture. British Vogue (launched 1916) and French Vogue (1920) were the first international editions; there are now more than two dozen.

Influence

Vogue is regarded as the world's most influential fashion magazine; its September issue is the largest and most commercially important of the year. The annual Met Gala, chaired by Wintour, is a major cultural and fundraising event. The magazine has also faced criticism over diversity, body image, and labour practices, prompting inclusivity initiatives in recent years.
