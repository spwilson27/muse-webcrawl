AMA Supercross Championship

The AMA Supercross Championship is an American motorcycle racing series sanctioned by the American Motorcyclist Association. It is the premier supercross competition in the world: races are held on purpose-built dirt tracks inside stadiums, with steep jumps, whoop sections, and tight turns, from January through May each year.

Format

The championship features a premier 450SX class and regional 250SX classes (East and West). Each round consists of heat races, last-chance qualifiers, and a main event, typically 20 minutes plus two laps for the 450 class. Since 2023 the season has concluded with the SuperMotocross World Championship playoffs, combining supercross and outdoor motocross points.

History and champions

The series began in 1974, growing out of the 1972 "Super Bowl of Motocross" at the Los Angeles Coliseum. Jeremy McGrath, dubbed the "King of Supercross," won seven championships in the 1990s and popularised the sport on television. Other dominant riders include Ricky Carmichael, Ryan Villopoto, Ryan Dungey, Eli Tomac, and Cooper Webb.
