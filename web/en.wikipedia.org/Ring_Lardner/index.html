Ringgold Wilmer Lardner (March 6, 1885 – September 25, 1933) was an American sports columnist and short story writer best known for his satirical writings on sports, marriage, and theatre. His stories, written in vernacular American English, influenced writers including Ernest Hemingway and J. D. Salinger.

Career

Lardner began as a sportswriter in Chicago, working for the Chicago Tribune, and covered the Chicago White Sox. His baseball stories featuring the naive rookie Jack Keefe, beginning with the serial "A Busher's Letters Home" in The Saturday Evening Post, were collected as You Know Me Al (1916). He famously covered the 1919 World Series and was one of the first journalists to suggest the "Black Sox" had thrown the Series.

Style and legacy

Lardner's best stories, such as "Haircut", "Some Like Them Cold" and "The Golden Honeymoon", combine comic vernacular narration with an often bleak view of human gullibility and self-deception. His son, Ring Lardner Jr., became a noted screenwriter and one of the Hollywood Ten.
