Water gas

Water gas is a mixture of carbon monoxide and hydrogen produced by passing steam over red-hot carbon fuel such as coke:

C + H2O → CO + H2

The reaction is endothermic, so in practice air is periodically blown through the fuel bed to reheat it, alternating with steam "runs" that produce the gas. Water gas is a form of synthesis gas.

Uses

Water gas was widely used in the 19th and early 20th centuries as a fuel gas for heating and lighting, either on its own or blended with other gases. Because it burns with a nearly non-luminous flame, it was often carburetted — enriched with vaporized oil — to produce carburetted water gas suitable for gas lighting. It also serves as a feedstock for chemical synthesis, and subjecting it to the water-gas shift reaction increases its hydrogen content for ammonia and hydrogen production.

Hazards

Water gas is highly toxic because of its carbon monoxide content, and its lack of odor contributed to accidental poisonings when domestic gas supplies contained it, before natural gas replaced manufactured gas in most distribution networks.
