Ljung–Box test

The Ljung–Box test (named for Greta M. Ljung and George E. P. Box) is a statistical test of whether any of a group of autocorrelations of a time series are different from zero. Instead of testing randomness at each distinct lag, it tests the "overall" randomness based on a number of lags, and is therefore a portmanteau test.

Definition

The test statistic is:

Q = n(n + 2) Σk=1h ρ̂²k / (n − k)

where n is the sample size, ρ̂k is the sample autocorrelation at lag k, and h is the number of lags tested. Under the null hypothesis that the data are independently distributed, Q approximately follows a chi-squared distribution with h degrees of freedom (adjusted downward by the number of fitted parameters when the test is applied to residuals of an ARMA model).

Use

The test is applied in econometrics and time-series modelling to check whether residuals from a fitted ARIMA model remain autocorrelated — evidence that the model has failed to capture the structure of the data. It is an improvement on the earlier Box–Pierce test, whose statistic lacked the (n + 2)/(n − k) correction and performed poorly in small samples. The choice of the number of lags h affects the power of the test; values around ln(n) or a fixed number relevant to seasonal periodicity are common in practice.
