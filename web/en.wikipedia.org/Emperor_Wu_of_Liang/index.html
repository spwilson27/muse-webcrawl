Emperor Wu of Liang (464 – 12 June 549), personal name Xiao Yan, was the founding emperor of the Chinese Liang dynasty of the Southern dynasties period. His reign, lasting nearly half a century, was one of the longest of early medieval China, and was initially a time of stability and cultural prosperity before ending in the chaos of the Hou Jing rebellion.

Rise and reign

Xiao Yan, an official of the Southern Qi, seized the throne in 502 and founded the Liang. He promoted scholarship, reformed administration, and was himself a talented poet and writer. Early in his reign the Liang successfully resisted Northern Wei invasions, notably at Zhongli in 507, one of the largest battles of the age.

Buddhism

Emperor Wu became a devout Buddhist, taking bodhisattva precepts, promoting vegetarianism in court ritual, and styling himself a wheel-turning Buddhist monarch. Several times he "gave himself" to a monastery, obliging his officials to ransom him back with huge donations. His devotion influenced Chinese Buddhism profoundly, though later historians criticised the expense. The monk-poet traditions around his court, and his legendary exchanges with Bodhidharma, founder of Chan Buddhism, are famous in Chinese culture.

Downfall

In 548 the Eastern Wei defector Hou Jing rebelled and besieged the capital Jiankang. The aged emperor starved to death in the besieged palace in 549, and the Liang state fragmented.
