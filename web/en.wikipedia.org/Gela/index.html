Gela is a city and comune in the Autonomous Region of Sicily, Italy, in the province of Caltanissetta, lying on Sicily's southern coast on the Gulf of Gela. With around 72,000 inhabitants it is one of the larger cities of southern Sicily, historically important as an ancient Greek colony and, in modern times, as a petrochemical centre.

History

Gela was founded around 689 BC by colonists from Rhodes and Crete led by Antiphamos and Entimos. It became one of the most powerful Greek cities in Sicily, founding Akragas (Agrigento) in turn, and reached its height under tyrants including Hippocrates and Gelon, who later ruled Syracuse. The playwright Aeschylus died here in 456 BC. The city declined after Carthaginian destruction around 405 BC and was refounded under the name Terranova in the 13th century by Frederick II.

In July 1943 Gela was one of the landing sites of the Allied invasion of Sicily (Operation Husky). In the postwar decades the construction of a large Eni petrochemical complex reshaped the local economy; the refinery was later converted toward "green" refining.

Archaeology

The acropolis area preserves Greek fortifications, including the well-preserved Capo Soprano walls (4th century BC), and the regional archaeological museum displays important finds, including ancient shipwrecks recovered from the sea off Gela.
