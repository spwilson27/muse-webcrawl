To the Lighthouse

To the Lighthouse
Author: Virginia Woolf
Published: 1927
Publisher: Hogarth Press

To the Lighthouse is a 1927 novel by Virginia Woolf. A landmark of high modernism, it follows the Ramsay family and their guests at a summer house on the Isle of Skye in the Hebrides, and centres on the deferred visit to a nearby lighthouse.

Structure

The novel is divided into three sections. "The Window" covers a single evening before the First World War, in which Mrs Ramsay soothes her husband and children, and the young James is promised a trip to the lighthouse if the weather is fine. "Time Passes", a brief, lyrical middle section, compresses ten years in which the house stands empty, Mrs Ramsay and two of her children die, and the war devastates Europe. "The Lighthouse" returns after the war: Mr Ramsay finally sails to the lighthouse with James and Cam, while the artist Lily Briscoe, a guest from the first section, completes the painting she began a decade earlier.

Themes and reception

Drawing on Woolf's memories of her parents and summers at St Ives in Cornwall, the novel explores perception, memory, grief, gender, and the passage of time through stream-of-consciousness narration. It is widely regarded as one of Woolf's finest works and one of the great English-language novels of the 20th century.
