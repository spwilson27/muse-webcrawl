Biomass

Biomass is plant or animal material, and in ecology the total mass of living organisms in a given area or ecosystem. As an energy source, biomass includes wood, agricultural crops and residues, animal wastes and organic municipal waste that can be burned, converted to biofuels, or digested to biogas.

Ecological meaning

Ecologists measure standing biomass (often as dry mass or carbon) to describe productivity and trophic structure. Global plant biomass dominates total living mass; bacteria, fungi and animals form smaller but functionally critical fractions. Biomass pyramids depict mass at each trophic level.

Energy use

Biomass supplied heat for most of human history and still provides a significant share of global primary energy, much of it traditional fuelwood. Modern uses include pellet boilers, co-firing in power plants, ethanol and biodiesel, and anaerobic digestion. Sustainability depends on regrowth, land-use change and lifecycle emissions; burning biomass is often treated as carbon-neutral only if carbon stocks are maintained.

Conversion technologies

Thermochemical routes (combustion, gasification, pyrolysis), biochemical routes (fermentation, anaerobic digestion) and chemical transesterification convert diverse feedstocks to heat, electricity, fuels and chemicals.
