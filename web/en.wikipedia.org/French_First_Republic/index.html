The French First Republic was proclaimed on 21 September 1792 during the French Revolution, the day after the monarchy was abolished and the National Convention first met. It lasted until the proclamation of the First French Empire in 1804.

National Convention

The Convention tried and executed Louis XVI (21 January 1793), declared war on much of Europe, and — under pressure of invasion and the Vendée revolt — created the Committee of Public Safety, whose dominance under Robespierre produced the Reign of Terror (1793–94), ended by the Thermidorian Reaction. Revolutionary armies nonetheless repelled the First Coalition and annexed Belgium and the Rhineland.

Directory and Consulate

The Constitution of 1795 created the five-man Directory, a regime plagued by coups, inflation and dependence on victorious generals. Napoleon Bonaparte, fresh from campaigns in Italy and Egypt, seized power in the coup of 18 Brumaire (9 November 1799) and became First Consul. The Consulate stabilised France with the Napoleonic Code, the Concordat of 1801 and administrative reform, before Napoleon crowned himself Emperor in 1804.

Legacy

The First Republic established enduring symbols and principles — the tricolour, the Marseillaise, "liberty, equality, fraternity", the metric system and secular citizenship — that later French republics claimed as their inheritance.
