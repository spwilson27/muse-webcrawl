Special administrative regions of China

CountryPeople's Republic of China
RegionsHong Kong, Macau
Established1997 (Hong Kong), 1999 (Macau)
PrincipleOne country, two systems

The special administrative regions (SARs) of China are a type of provincial-level administrative division that enjoys a high degree of autonomy under the principle of "one country, two systems". There are two SARs: Hong Kong, transferred from Britain in 1997, and Macau, transferred from Portugal in 1999.

Autonomy

Under their Basic Laws, the SARs maintain their own legal systems, currencies, customs territories, and immigration controls, while defence and foreign affairs are the responsibility of the central government in Beijing. Each is headed by a Chief Executive.

History

The arrangement was negotiated in the Sino-British Joint Declaration of 1984 and the Sino-Portuguese Joint Declaration of 1987, which guaranteed the regions' existing economic and social systems for fifty years after the handovers.
