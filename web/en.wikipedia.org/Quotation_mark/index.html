Quotation mark

Quotation marks are punctuation marks used in pairs in various writing systems to identify direct speech, a quotation, or a phrase. The pair consists of an opening quotation mark and a closing quotation mark, which may or may not be the same character. In English they appear as double marks ("…") or single marks ('…').

History

Quotation marks developed from marginal annotations in manuscripts; by the era of printing, inverted commas placed at the start of quoted lines evolved into the paired marks used today. Curved "smart quotes" (typographic quotation marks) differ from the straight "typewriter" quotes inherited from ASCII.

Conventions in English

American English generally uses double quotation marks for quotations, single marks for quotations within quotations, and places periods and commas inside the closing mark. British English style varies, often using single marks first and placing punctuation according to whether it belongs to the quoted material ("logical quotation"). Quotation marks also set off titles of short works, signal irony or nonstandard use ("scare quotes"), and mark words mentioned rather than used.

Other languages

Many languages use guillemets («…»), low-high marks („…"), or other forms. French typically pairs guillemets with spaces; German uses „…" with the opening mark at the baseline; Japanese uses corner brackets 「…」.
