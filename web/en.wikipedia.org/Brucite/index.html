Brucite

FormulaMg(OH)₂
Crystal systemTrigonal
ColourWhite, pale green, blue, grey; colourless in transmitted light
Mohs hardness2.5–3
LustreVitreous to pearly
Specific gravity2.37–2.43

Brucite is the mineral form of magnesium hydroxide (Mg(OH)2). It is a common alteration product of periclase in marble; a low-temperature hydrothermal vein mineral in metamorphosed limestones and chlorite schists; and formed during the serpentinisation of dunites and other ultramafic rocks. Brucite is named after the American mineralogist Archibald Bruce (1777–1818), who first described the mineral from Hoboken, New Jersey.

Properties and structure

Brucite crystallises in the trigonal crystal system and typically occurs as tabular crystals, foliated masses, or fibrous aggregates (the fibrous variety is called nemalite). It is soft (hardness 2.5–3), has perfect basal cleavage, and is white to pale green or bluish. Its structure consists of sheets of edge-sharing Mg(OH)6 octahedra held together by weak hydrogen bonding between layers, which accounts for the easy cleavage and the layered structure shared with related hydroxides such as portlandite, Ca(OH)2.

Occurrence and uses

Notable localities include the Kola Peninsula in Russia, the Asbestos region of Quebec, the Tilly Foster mine in New York, and deposits in Pakistan and South Africa that supply fine specimens to collectors. Commercially, brucite is mined as a source of magnesia and as a flame retardant: on heating it decomposes endothermically, releasing water that cools and dilutes flammable gases. It is also used in wastewater treatment and as an antacid feedstock.

Environmental significance

Brucite weathers readily to hydromagnesite and absorbs carbon dioxide from the atmosphere, a reaction studied in the context of carbon sequestration in mine tailings of ultramafic rocks. Dissolution of brucite in serpentinite-hosted hydrothermal systems, such as the Lost City hydrothermal field, helps generate the alkaline, hydrogen-rich fluids that support distinctive deep-sea ecosystems.
