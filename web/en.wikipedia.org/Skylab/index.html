Skylab was the United States' first space station, launched and operated by NASA from 1973 to 1979. Converted from a Saturn V upper stage, it remains the largest single-launch station of its era and hosted three crewed missions that conducted extensive solar astronomy, Earth-observation, and medical experiments.

Launch damage and repair

During launch on 14 May 1973, a micrometeoroid/sun shield tore away, taking one solar array with it and jamming the other. The first crew (Skylab 2, Conrad, Kerwin, and Weitz) saved the station by deploying a parasol sunshade through an airlock and freeing the stuck array during a spacewalk—one of the most celebrated repairs in spaceflight history.

Crewed missions

Three crews of three astronauts each occupied Skylab for 28, 59, and 84 days respectively, setting successive endurance records. The third crew's heavy workload led to a famous scheduling dispute with mission control, sometimes popularly described (with some exaggeration) as a "mutiny" or strike in space. Crews travelled to and from the station in Apollo command modules launched on Saturn IB rockets.

Re-entry

After the last crew left in February 1974, Skylab's orbit decayed faster than expected due to increased solar activity. NASA could only partially control its re-entry; on 11 July 1979 the station broke up over the Indian Ocean and sparsely populated Western Australia, where scattered debris was recovered near Esperance.
