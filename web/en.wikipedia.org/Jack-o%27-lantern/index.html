Jack-o'-lantern

A jack-o'-lantern is a carved lantern, most commonly made from a pumpkin, associated with the Halloween holiday. Its name comes from the phenomenon of strange lights flickering over peat bogs, called will-o'-the-wisps or jack-o'-lanterns.

History

In Ireland and Scotland, turnips and other root vegetables were carved with grotesque faces to ward off evil spirits at Samhain. Immigrants to North America adopted the native pumpkin, which is larger and easier to carve. The term became associated with the Irish folktale of Stingy Jack, a drunkard who bargained with the Devil and was doomed to roam the night with only a hollowed-out turnip to light his way.

Customs

Carving pumpkins into jack-o'-lanterns is a popular Halloween tradition; patterns range from simple faces to elaborate designs, lit by candles or electric lights. Communities hold pumpkin festivals and record attempts, and carved pumpkins are typically displayed on porches during trick-or-treating.
