Transparente

A transparente is a large banner carried in demonstrations, processions, and protest marches, most commonly in Spanish- and Portuguese-speaking countries. The term literally means "transparent" in Spanish and Portuguese, a reference to the thin, lightweight cloth or plastic sheeting from which such banners are typically made, which can be slightly translucent when held up against the light.

Description

Transparentes are usually wide horizontal banners held aloft by two or more people using poles or their hands. They bear slogans, the names of unions, student groups, political parties, or neighbourhood organisations, and are a characteristic visual element of marches in countries such as Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Spain, and Brazil. They often head a contingent within a larger march, identifying the group to spectators and the media.

Use

Because they are cheap and quick to produce from fabric or plastic sheeting and paint, transparentes are a staple of grassroots protest culture, appearing at labour demonstrations, student protests, feminist marches, and commemorations. The word is sometimes used in English-language reporting on Latin American protest movements without translation.
