Frederick Wiseman (January 1, 1930 – February 16, 2026) was an American documentary filmmaker. Over a career spanning more than five decades he made over forty films, most of them observational studies of American institutions — schools, hospitals, police forces, legislatures, libraries, and welfare offices — which together form a sweeping portrait of the United States.

Style

Wiseman's films use no narration, no interviews to camera, no music, and no on-screen titles beyond the opening. Working in the cinéma vérité tradition, he and a small crew recorded hundreds of hours of everyday life inside an institution, which he then edited into long, mosaic-like structures. He described his approach as an examination of "the relationship between people and institutions."

Major works

His debut, Titicut Follies (1967), filmed inside the Bridgewater State Hospital for the criminally insane in Massachusetts, was banned from general release by the courts for more than two decades. Subsequent landmarks include High School (1968), Hospital (1970), Welfare (1975), Public Housing (1997), At Berkeley (2013), In Jackson Heights (2015), Ex Libris: The New York Public Library (2017), and City Hall (2020). Later films turned to France, including Menus-plaisirs – Les Troisgros (2023).

Recognition

Wiseman received an Academy Honorary Award in 2016 and the Golden Lion for Lifetime Achievement at Venice in 2014. His films are distributed by his own company, Zipporah Films, and many were first broadcast on PBS.
