Lease

A lease is a contractual arrangement in which one party, the lessor, grants another party, the lessee, the right to use an asset for a specified period in exchange for periodic payments, usually rent. Leases are most commonly associated with real property, but vehicles, equipment and other assets are also leased.

Property leases

A leasehold estate gives the tenant exclusive possession of land or premises for a term of years. Leases typically specify the rent, duration, permitted use, maintenance obligations and conditions for renewal or termination. In common law systems, residential tenancies are heavily regulated by statute, while long leases of land (for example 99 or 999 years) function almost like ownership.

Finance and operating leases

In accounting, leases were traditionally divided into finance (capital) leases, which transfer substantially all risks and rewards of ownership, and operating leases; modern standards such as IFRS 16 place most leases on the lessee's balance sheet.
