The Chang'e program (Chinese Lunar Exploration Program, CLEP) is an ongoing series of robotic Moon missions by the China National Space Administration (CNSA). It is named after Chang'e, the Chinese goddess of the Moon. The program proceeds in phases: orbiting, landing, and sample return, followed by more advanced missions building toward a lunar research station.

Missions

Chang'e 1 (2007) and Chang'e 2 (2010) were orbiters that mapped the lunar surface. Chang'e 3 (2013) achieved China's first soft landing on the Moon, in Mare Imbrium, deploying the Yutu rover. Chang'e 4 (2018–19) made the first soft landing on the far side of the Moon, in Von Kármán crater within the South Pole–Aitken Basin, supported by the Queqiao relay satellite at the Earth–Moon L2 point, and deployed the Yutu-2 rover.

Chang'e 5 (2020) returned about 1.7 kg of lunar samples from Mons Rümker in Oceanus Procellarum — the first lunar sample return since 1976. Chang'e 6 (2024) landed in the Apollo basin on the far side and returned the first samples ever collected from the lunar far side. Chang'e 7 and Chang'e 8, planned for around 2026 and 2028, will target the south polar region, testing resource utilization and laying groundwork for the International Lunar Research Station (ILRS), which China plans to build with partners in the 2030s as a precursor to crewed Chinese lunar landings planned before 2030.

Technology and legacy

The program developed deep-space tracking (including stations in Argentina and Namibia), autonomous landing and hazard avoidance, lunar sample-return ascent and Earth re-entry at near second cosmic velocity, and far-side communications relay. Its lander and rover heritage feeds directly into China's planned crewed lunar program using the Mengzhou spacecraft and Lanyue lander.
