Tatort

Tatort (Crime scene) is a German-language police procedural television series that has run continuously since 1970, making it the longest-running German TV drama. The series is produced by the regional broadcasters of the ARD network in Germany, together with ORF in Austria and SRF in Switzerland. Each episode is about 90 minutes long and is produced by one of the regional teams, each with its own detectives, city, and production style.

Format

Unlike conventional series with a single cast, Tatort is a federation of regional productions. Teams of investigators work in cities such as Munich, Berlin, Hamburg, Cologne, Dortmund, Vienna, and Zurich, and episodes rotate among the teams on Sunday evenings on Das Erste. The famous opening sequence — featuring eyes, crosshairs, and running legs over a driving theme by Klaus Doldinger — has remained largely unchanged since 1970.

History

The first episode, Taxi nach Leipzig, aired on 29 November 1970 in West Germany. The series was conceived partly in response to East Germany's rival crime series Polizeiruf 110, which was later incorporated into the ARD's Sunday crime slot alongside Tatort. Well over 1,000 episodes have been produced. Long-running investigator teams, such as Batic and Leitmayr in Munich and Thiel and Boerne in Münster, have become fixtures of German popular culture, with the Münster episodes mixing crime with comedy.

Reception

Tatort regularly draws audiences of eight to ten million viewers in Germany and is a Sunday-evening institution, screened in pubs and public viewing events. Episodes frequently address contemporary social and political issues, including migration, extremism, and organized crime.
