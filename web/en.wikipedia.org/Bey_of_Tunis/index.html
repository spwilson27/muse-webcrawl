The Bey of Tunis was the hereditary ruler of Tunisia under the Husainid dynasty, from 1705 until the abolition of the monarchy in 1957. The title "bey" derived from an Ottoman Turkish honorific, and the beys ruled Tunisia first as Ottoman vassals and later under a French protectorate established in 1881.

History

The dynasty was founded by Al-Husayn ibn Ali in 1705. The beys modernised Tunisia during the nineteenth century, promulgating the Fundamental Pact of 1857 and the first Arab-world constitution in 1861, though mounting debt led to an international financial commission and then the French protectorate.

End of the monarchy

After Tunisian independence in 1956, the last bey, Muhammad VIII al-Amin, was deposed on 25 July 1957 by the Constituent Assembly, and Habib Bourguiba proclaimed the Republic of Tunisia.
