Orde Wingate

Orde Charles Wingate (26 February 1903 – 24 March 1944) was a British Army officer and creator of several unconventional military units during the Second World War. He is best known for founding the Chindits, long-range penetration brigades that operated behind Japanese lines in Burma, and for earlier service in Palestine and Ethiopia.

Early career

Born into a Plymouth Brethren family in India and educated at Charterhouse and Woolwich, Wingate served in Sudan and, from 1936, in Mandatory Palestine, where he organised the Special Night Squads — mixed British and Jewish counter-insurgency units — during the Arab revolt, becoming a controversial figure for his fierce Zionist sympathies.

Ethiopia and Burma

In 1941 Wingate led Gideon Force, a guerrilla formation that helped restore Emperor Haile Selassie during the East African campaign. In Burma he developed long-range penetration: self-contained brigades supplied by air, marching deep behind enemy lines to cut communications. The first Chindit expedition (Operation Longcloth, 1943) had mixed results but proved the concept; a much larger operation followed in 1944.

Death and legacy

Wingate was killed on 24 March 1944 when his B-25 bomber crashed in the hills of Manipur shortly after take-off. He remains a divisive figure — praised as an innovative, charismatic guerrilla theorist and criticised as erratic — but his influence on special forces and deep-penetration doctrine is widely acknowledged.
