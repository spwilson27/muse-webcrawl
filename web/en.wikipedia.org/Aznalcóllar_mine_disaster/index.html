The Aznalcóllar mine disaster, also known as the Doñana disaster, was an environmental catastrophe on 25 April 1998, when the tailings dam of the Los Frailes pyrite mine at Aznalcóllar, near Seville, Spain, burst, releasing several million cubic metres of toxic sludge and acidic water into the Agrio and Guadiamar rivers.

The spill

A section of the dam wall slid and failed in the early morning, sending a wave of black sludge laden with heavy metals — zinc, lead, copper, arsenic, cadmium — across some 4,600 hectares of farmland and wetlands. The spill threatened Doñana National Park, one of Europe's most important wetlands and a UNESCO World Heritage Site; an emergency barrier of earth and rock was hastily built to keep the toxic flow out of the park's core, and the most contaminated sludge was largely contained in the Entremuros channel.

Clean-up and consequences

The Spanish government mounted an enormous remediation programme, removing contaminated topsoil and creating the Guadiamar Green Corridor to reconnect Doñana with the Sierra Morena. Agricultural land along the river was expropriated and converted to parkland. The mine's operator, the Swedish-Canadian company Boliden, closed the mine, and litigation over the clean-up costs continued for many years. Studies found heavy fish and bird mortality in the immediate aftermath, though the feared ecological destruction of Doñana itself was largely averted.
