E-box

E-box
Consensus sequence: CANNTG
Canonical sequence: CACGTG
Bound by: Basic helix-loop-helix (bHLH) transcription factors

An E-box (enhancer box) is a DNA response element found in some eukaryotes that acts as a protein-binding site and has been found to regulate gene expression in neurons, muscles, and other tissues. Its specific DNA sequence, CANNTG (where N can be any nucleotide), is recognized by transcription factors, particularly those of the basic helix-loop-helix (bHLH) family.

A bHLH transcription factor dimer binding to an E-box sequence in DNA.

Role in gene regulation

E-boxes are present in the promoters and enhancers of many genes. bHLH proteins bind as dimers to the E-box to activate or repress transcription of downstream genes. The canonical E-box sequence CACGTG is a palindrome and is sometimes called the CACGTG box or G-box in plant biology contexts.

Circadian regulation

E-boxes play a central role in the circadian clock. The CLOCK and BMAL1 (ARNTL) proteins form a heterodimer that binds E-box elements in the promoters of the Period (Per) and Cryptochrome (Cry) genes, activating their transcription. The resulting PER and CRY proteins accumulate, enter the nucleus, and repress CLOCK-BMAL1 activity, closing a transcription-translation feedback loop that generates roughly 24-hour rhythms.

Muscle and neural development

Myogenic bHLH factors such as MyoD bind E-boxes in muscle-specific genes and are essential for skeletal muscle differentiation. Related factors, the neurogenins and other proneural proteins, use E-box binding in the development of neurons.
