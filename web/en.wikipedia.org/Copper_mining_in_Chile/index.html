Key minesEscondida, Chuquicamata, El Teniente

Copper mining in Chile is a mainstay of the Chilean economy. Chile is the world's largest copper producer, accounting for roughly a quarter to a third of global output, and holds the largest known copper reserves. Copper and related products make up a major share of the country's exports.

Major operations

Most production comes from giant porphyry copper deposits in the arid north. Escondida, majority-owned by BHP, is the world's largest copper mine. The state-owned company Codelco operates historic mines including Chuquicamata and El Teniente, the largest underground copper mine in the world.

History

Copper mining expanded in the early 20th century under foreign companies. In 1971 Chile nationalised its large copper mines under President Salvador Allende, and Codelco was created in 1976 to run them. Private investment returned from the 1990s onward, greatly expanding output.

Challenges

Declining ore grades, water scarcity in the Atacama Desert, energy costs and community and environmental pressures are central issues for the industry.
