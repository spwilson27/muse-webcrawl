Uilleann pipes

The uilleann pipes (from Irish píobaí uilleann, "elbow pipes") are the characteristic national bagpipes of Ireland. Unlike the Scottish Great Highland bagpipes, they are inflated by a bellows strapped to the player's elbow rather than by mouth, and are played seated, with the chanter's lower end resting on the thigh.

A full set consists of a chanter with a range of two octaves, three drones, and three regulators—keyed chanters laid across the player's lap that provide chordal accompaniment and are played with the heel of the lower hand. The instrument's sweet, comparatively quiet tone suits it to indoor music, and its dynamics and ornamentation, including rolls, crans, and staccato triplets, are central to Irish traditional music.

The pipes developed in their present form in the late 18th and early 19th centuries. Notable pipers include Seamus Ennis, Willie Clancy, Liam O'Flynn, and Paddy Keenan. In 2017 UNESCO inscribed uilleann piping on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
