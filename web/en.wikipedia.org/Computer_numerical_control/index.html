Computer numerical control

Computer numerical control (CNC) is the automated control of machine tools — such as drills, lathes, mills, grinders, routers, and 3D printers — by means of a computer. A CNC machine processes a piece of material to meet specifications by following coded programmed instructions, without a manual operator directly controlling the machining operation.

Operation

A part design is created in computer-aided design (CAD) software and translated by computer-aided manufacturing (CAM) software into a program of instructions, most commonly in G-code. The machine's controller executes the program, driving motors and servos to position the tool and workpiece, control spindle speed, and change tools automatically.

History

Numerical control began in the 1940s and 1950s with punched-tape systems developed for the United States Air Force by John T. Parsons and MIT. As computers became smaller and cheaper, direct computer control replaced hardwired controllers, and CNC became the standard for precision manufacturing.

Advantages

CNC machines offer high precision and repeatability, reduced setup labour, consistent quality across large production runs, and the ability to produce complex geometries that would be impractical to machine manually.
