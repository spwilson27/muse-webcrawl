Caymanian cuisine

Caymanian cuisine is the traditional food of the Cayman Islands, a British Overseas Territory in the Caribbean Sea. It reflects the islands' seafaring heritage and blends British, Jamaican, and broader Caribbean influences, drawing heavily on seafood, root vegetables, and coconut.

Traditional dishes

Signature preparations include turtle stew — historically the islands' most famous dish, now made from farmed turtle — fish rundown (fish simmered in coconut milk with vegetables), stewed conch, and Cayman-style beef, a slow-cooked seasoned beef served at Christmas. Heavy cake, a dense cake made from cassava, yam, or cornmeal with coconut milk and spices, is a beloved traditional dessert.

Ingredients

Local ingredients include conch, lobster, grouper, mahi-mahi, cassava, breadfruit, plantain, coconut, and Scotch bonnet peppers. Seasonings reflect the islands' history as a provisioning stop for ships, with jerk and curry dishes showing Jamaican influence.

Modern cuisine

The Cayman Islands — particularly Grand Cayman — market themselves as a "culinary capital of the Caribbean", hosting events such as the Cayman Cookout and a wide range of international restaurants alongside traditional fare.
