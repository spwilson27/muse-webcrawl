Pteridospermatophyta

KingdomPlantae
GroupSeed ferns (paraphyletic)
Temporal rangeDevonian – Cretaceous

The Pteridospermatophyta, commonly called seed ferns or pteridosperms, are a paraphyletic group of extinct seed plants known from the late Devonian through the Cretaceous. Despite their name, they are not ferns: they bore fern-like foliage but reproduced with true seeds, making them among the earliest seed plants.

History of study

In the early twentieth century, palaeobotanists demonstrated that characteristic Carboniferous fern-like fronds such as Lyginopteris bore ovules, overturning the assumption that all such foliage belonged to true ferns. This discovery helped establish that seed plants evolved from fern-like ancestors.

Groups

Recognised orders include the Lyginopteridales, Medullosales (with large seeds such as Pachytesta), Callistophytales, Caytoniales, Glossopteridales, Peltaspermales and Corystospermales. The glossopterids dominate Permian floras of Gondwana and were key evidence for continental drift.

Significance

Seed ferns were major components of Carboniferous coal forests and are central to debates about the origin of flowering plants, with groups such as the Caytoniales proposed as possible angiosperm relatives.
