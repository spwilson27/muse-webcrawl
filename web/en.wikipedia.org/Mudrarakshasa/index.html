Mudrarakshasa

AuthorViśākhadatta (Vishakhadatta)
LanguageSanskrit
GenreHistorical / political drama
SettingPataliputra, c. 4th–3rd century BCE

The Mudrarakshasa ("Rakshasa's Ring" or "The Signet of the Minister") is a Sanskrit historical drama by Vishakhadatta, generally dated between the 4th and 9th centuries CE. It dramatizes the rise to power of Chandragupta Maurya following the overthrow of the Nanda dynasty, centred on the political intrigues of the minister Chanakya.

Plot

The play opens after Chandragupta, guided by Chanakya, has seized the throne of Pataliputra. Rakshasa, the loyal minister of the deposed Nandas, plots with allies to restore his masters. Through forged letters, double agents and manipulation, Chanakya gradually wins Rakshasa over to serve Chandragupta, ending with Rakshasa accepting the ministership.

Significance

Unlike most Sanskrit drama, the play is a work of political intrigue without a central love story, and it preserves a detailed, if dramatised, account of early Indian statecraft and espionage.
