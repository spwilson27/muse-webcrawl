Sturgeon

Sturgeon is the common name for the 28 species of fish belonging to the family Acipenseridae. These large, long-lived fish are native to temperate waters of the Northern Hemisphere and are best known as the source of caviar from their roe. Many species are now critically endangered due to overfishing and habitat loss.

Sturgeon
Family: Acipenseridae
Order: Acipenseriformes
Range: Northern Hemisphere
Known for: Caviar

Description

Sturgeons have elongated bodies covered in rows of bony plates called scutes rather than scales, a heterocercal tail fin, and barbels near their protrusible mouths used to sense bottom-dwelling prey. The beluga sturgeon can exceed 7 metres in length and live for over a century, making it one of the largest freshwater fish.

Life cycle

Most sturgeons are anadromous, migrating from seas or lakes into rivers to spawn. Females mature slowly, sometimes not reproducing until 15–25 years of age, which makes populations especially vulnerable to exploitation.

Conservation

International trade in sturgeon products is regulated under CITES, and many species are bred in aquaculture both for caviar and for restocking wild populations.
