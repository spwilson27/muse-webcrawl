America First (policy)

America First is a foreign-policy position in the United States that emphasises American nationalism and unilateralism, prioritising domestic interests, and historically associated with non-interventionism and protectionism. The phrase has been used by politicians of both major parties since the early 20th century, including Woodrow Wilson, Warren G. Harding and Pat Buchanan, and was revived as a central slogan of Donald Trump's 2016 campaign and presidencies.

History

The slogan gained its most controversial early association with the America First Committee (1940–41), a mass-membership organisation of some 800,000 people that opposed American entry into World War II. Its most famous spokesman was aviator Charles Lindbergh; the committee dissolved days after the attack on Pearl Harbor in December 1941. Some members and rhetoric had been criticised as isolationist and, in Lindbergh's Des Moines speech, antisemitic — a charge his defenders disputed.

Modern usage

Trump's "America First" foreign policy framed trade deficits, alliance burden-sharing, immigration restriction and scepticism toward multilateral agreements (such as withdrawal from the Paris climate accord and the Iran nuclear deal) as putting American interests ahead of global commitments. Supporters describe it as a realist prioritisation of national sovereignty; critics argue it revives the discredited isolationism associated with the 1940 committee and undermines the liberal international order.
