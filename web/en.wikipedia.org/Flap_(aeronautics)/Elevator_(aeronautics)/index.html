Elevator (aeronautics)

In aeronautics, an elevator is a flight control surface, usually at the rear of an aircraft, which controls the aircraft's pitch, and therefore the angle of attack and the lift of the wing. The elevators are usually hinged to the tailplane or horizontal stabilizer. They may be the only pitch control surface present, sometimes located at the front (early airplanes and canards), or integrated into a movable tailplane known as a stabilator.

Function

By deflecting upward or downward, the elevator changes the camber of the tailplane and thus the lift it produces. An upward deflection (trailing edge up) pushes the tail down and pitches the nose up, increasing angle of attack; the reverse pitches the nose down. The pilot controls the elevator with fore-and-aft movement of the control stick or yoke.

Types

Some aircraft combine pitch and roll control in elevons (on delta-winged aircraft) or use a fully flying tailplane (stabilator), in which the entire horizontal stabilizer pivots to serve as the elevator.
