High-lift device

In aircraft design, a high-lift device is a component or mechanism on an aircraft's wing that increases the amount of lift produced by the wing. The device may be a fixed component, or a movable mechanism which is deployed when required. Common movable high-lift devices include flaps and slats. Fixed devices include leading-edge root extensions and boundary layer control systems.

Purpose

The size and lifting capacity of a fixed wing are often traded off against speed and efficiency. Deploying high-lift devices increases the wing's camber, area, or both, and energises the boundary layer, allowing the aircraft to generate the required lift at lower speeds — essential for take-off and landing, so that runway lengths can be shorter and approach speeds safer.

Types

Flaps are hinged surfaces on the trailing edge of the wing; types include plain, split, slotted, Fowler and blown flaps. Slats are aerodynamic surfaces on the leading edge that, when deployed, allow the wing to operate at a higher angle of attack. Other devices include Krueger flaps, leading-edge droops and cuffs, and vortex generators.
