Angle of attack

In fluid dynamics, the angle of attack (AOA, sometimes written α, the Greek letter alpha) is the angle between a reference line on a body (often the chord line of an airfoil) and the vector representing the relative motion between the body and the fluid through which it is moving. Angle of attack is the angle between the body's reference line and the oncoming flow.

Lift and stall

The lift coefficient of a fixed-wing aircraft varies directly with the angle of attack up to a point. As angle of attack increases, the flow over the upper surface of the wing eventually separates, causing a stall: lift drops sharply and drag rises. The angle at which this occurs is called the critical angle of attack, typically around 15 degrees for many airfoils.

Related concepts

Angle of attack must be distinguished from the pitch angle (the angle between the aircraft's longitudinal axis and the horizon) and the flight path angle. High-lift devices such as trailing-edge flaps and leading-edge slats allow an aircraft to fly safely at higher angles of attack and lower speeds.
