Aileron

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An aileron (French for "little wing" or "fin") is a hinged flight control surface, usually forming part of the trailing edge of each wing of a fixed-wing aircraft. Ailerons are used in pairs to control the aircraft in roll (rotation around the longitudinal axis): the aileron on one wing deflects upward, reducing lift, while the aileron on the other wing deflects downward, increasing lift, so the aircraft banks toward the side of the raised aileron.

History

The principle of lateral control by warping or moving the wingtips was contested territory among early aviators: the Wright brothers' 1903 patent covered wing-warping and was broadly interpreted to include ailerons, leading to prolonged litigation with Glenn Curtiss, who used separate hinged surfaces. French and British experimenters such as Robert Esnault-Pelterie and Matthew Piers Watt Boulton (who patented an aileron-like system in 1868) also developed the device. By the First World War the hinged aileron had become the standard means of roll control.

Operation and adverse yaw

When an aircraft banks, the down-going aileron increases drag on the rising wing, tending to yaw the nose away from the turn — an effect called adverse yaw. Designers counter it with differential ailerons (which deflect upward farther than downward), Frise-type ailerons whose nose protrudes into the airflow when raised, and aileron–rudder interconnects or yaw dampers. At high speeds, aileron reversal can occur on flexible wings, and large airliners supplement ailerons with roll spoilers; some aircraft combine aileron and flap functions in "flaperons," or aileron and elevator functions in "elevons" on tailless designs.

Types

Ailerons may be mass-balanced to prevent flutter and aerodynamically balanced with setback hinges or internal seals. Trim tabs on ailerons allow the pilot to relieve control forces in asymmetric flight. On most airliners, outboard ailerons are locked out at high speed to reduce wing loads, leaving inboard high-speed ailerons and spoilers to provide roll control.
