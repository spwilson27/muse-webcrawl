Spoiler (aeronautics)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In aeronautics, a spoiler (sometimes called a lift spoiler or lift dumper) is a device which intentionally reduces the lift component of an airfoil in a controlled way. Most often, spoilers are plates on the top surface of a wing that can be extended upward into the airflow to "spoil" the smooth flow over the wing, increasing drag and decreasing lift on that portion of the wing.

Uses

Spoilers serve several functions. Extended symmetrically in flight, as speed brakes, they allow an aircraft to descend steeply without gaining speed. Extended asymmetrically, as roll spoilers (spoilerons), they supplement or replace ailerons — particularly valuable at high speed and on wings where ailerons are small or locked out. On landing, full extension of ground spoilers dumps lift so that the aircraft's weight settles firmly on the wheels, making wheel braking effective and shortening the landing roll.

Spoilers versus air brakes

Unlike dedicated air brakes, whose primary purpose is to increase drag with minimal effect on lift, spoilers deliberately destroy lift; the two are often combined in spoiler panels that serve both roles. Gliders use spoilers or combined spoiler–airbrake systems (such as Schempp-Hirth airbrakes) to control glide path precisely on approach.

Incidents

Uncommanded or forgotten spoiler deployment has featured in several accidents: spoilers left extended after take-off reduce climb performance, and early jet transports required careful management of lift dumpers on approach. Modern systems interlock ground-spoiler deployment with weight-on-wheels sensors and throttle position to prevent in-flight extension on the ground setting.
