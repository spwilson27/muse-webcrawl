Nurse practitioner

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


An NP conducting a patient examination

A nurse practitioner (NP) is an advanced practice registered nurse who has completed additional graduate-level education and clinical training beyond that of a registered nurse. Nurse practitioners are qualified to assess patients, diagnose illnesses, order and interpret diagnostic tests, and initiate and manage treatment plans, in many jurisdictions including the prescription of medications.

Education and certification

In the United States, NPs typically hold a Master of Science in Nursing or a Doctor of Nursing Practice degree and must pass a national certification examination in a specialty population focus, such as family, adult-gerontology, paediatrics, or psychiatric-mental health. The first NP programme was established in 1965 at the University of Colorado by Loretta Ford and Henry Silver, initially to provide paediatric primary care in underserved areas.

Scope of practice

Scope of practice varies by country and, in the US, by state. In "full practice" states, NPs may evaluate patients, make diagnoses, and prescribe treatment independently; in others they work under collaborative agreements with physicians. NPs practise in hospitals, clinics, private offices, schools, and community health settings.

International equivalents

Similar advanced roles exist in Canada, Australia, the United Kingdom (advanced nurse practitioners), the Netherlands, and a growing number of other health systems, typically in response to physician shortages and the needs of rural and underserved populations.
