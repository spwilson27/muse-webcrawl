The Gaza Strip, or simply Gaza, is a small, densely populated Palestinian territory on the eastern coast of the Mediterranean Sea. It is bordered by Israel to the north and east, Egypt to the southwest, and the Mediterranean to the west. Together with the West Bank, it forms part of the State of Palestine claimed by the Palestinian people. Its population of over two million — a large majority of them refugees from the 1948 Arab–Israeli war and their descendants — makes it one of the most densely populated areas in the world.

History

Gaza, an ancient trading city occupied over millennia by Egyptians, Philistines, Romans, Byzantines, Arabs, Crusaders and Ottomans, came under British Mandate administration after the First World War. Following the 1948 war, the Strip was administered by Egypt; Israel captured it in 1967 and established settlements. Under the Oslo Accords of the 1990s, the Palestinian Authority gained partial control. Israel unilaterally withdrew its settlers and soldiers in 2005 but, with Egypt, imposed a blockade from 2007 after Hamas won the 2006 Palestinian legislative election and took control of Gaza in 2007, splitting it from the Fatah-led West Bank.

Conflicts

The territory has seen repeated wars and escalations between Israel and Hamas and other armed groups, notably in 2008–09, 2012, 2014 and 2021. On 7 October 2023, Hamas-led militants attacked southern Israel, killing around 1,200 people and taking some 250 hostages; Israel responded with a massive military campaign in Gaza that caused tens of thousands of deaths, widespread destruction of housing and infrastructure, and mass displacement, drawing intense international controversy and humanitarian alarm.

Geography and society

The Strip is divided into five governorates: North Gaza, Gaza, Deir al-Balah, Khan Yunis and Rafah. Its economy has been severely constrained by the blockade, with historically high unemployment and dependence on aid, particularly from UNRWA, the UN agency serving Palestinian refugees.
