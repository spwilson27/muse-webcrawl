BGP hijacking

BGP hijacking is the illegitimate takeover of groups of IP addresses by corrupting the Internet's global routing tables maintained through the Border Gateway Protocol (BGP). Because BGP was designed on the assumption that networks announce routes honestly, an autonomous system that announces prefixes it does not own can cause traffic for those prefixes to be routed to itself.

Mechanism

Routers prefer the most specific matching prefix and, among equal prefixes, often the apparently shortest path. An attacker — or a misconfigured router — that originates a victim's prefix, or a more specific sub-prefix of it, can therefore attract some or all of the traffic destined for that prefix. Depending on what the hijacker does with the traffic, the result may be a black hole (an outage), interception and surveillance, or impersonation of services.

Notable incidents

In 2008, Pakistan Telecom, acting on a government order to block YouTube domestically, announced a more specific route for YouTube's addresses that propagated worldwide and took the site offline for much of the Internet. Other widely reported incidents have involved large-scale route leaks and the redirection of traffic through unexpected countries.

Defences

Proposed and deployed defences include prefix filtering by providers, the Resource Public Key Infrastructure (RPKI), which lets address holders cryptographically authorise which networks may originate their prefixes, and route origin validation by receiving networks.
