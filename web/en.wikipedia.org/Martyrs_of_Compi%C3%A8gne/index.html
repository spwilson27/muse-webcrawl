Martyrs of Compiègne

The Martyrs of Compiègne were the 16 members of the Carmel of Compiègne, France, who were executed during the French Revolution's Reign of Terror. They were guillotined in Paris on 17 July 1794 and were beatified by Pope Pius X in 1906 and canonized by Pope Francis in December 2024.

Background

The community of Discalced Carmelite nuns at Compiègne was expelled from its convent in 1792 after refusing to abandon religious life under the Civil Constitution of the Clergy. Living in small groups in the town, the sisters continued their prayer and, according to tradition, offered themselves as victims for the restoration of peace and the Church in France.

Execution

Arrested in June 1794, the sisters were tried in Paris and condemned for "counter-revolutionary" activities. On the scaffold they renewed their vows and sang hymns, including the Salve Regina and the Veni Creator Spiritus, one by one falling silent as each was executed. The prioress, Mother Teresa of St. Augustine, was executed last. Ten days later Robespierre fell and the Terror ended. Their story inspired Gertrud von Le Fort's novella and Georges Bernanos's Dialogues of the Carmelites, later adapted into Francis Poulenc's opera of the same name.
