Neom

Neom (stylized NEOM; Arabic: نيوم, romanized: Niyūm) is a planned cross-border city and economic zone in northwestern Saudi Arabia, in the Tabuk Province near the Red Sea coast. Announced by Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman on 24 October 2017, the project covers an area of approximately 26,500 square kilometres (10,200 sq mi), extending along the Red Sea and across the borders into Egypt and Jordan. It is a central component of Saudi Vision 2030, the kingdom's programme to diversify its economy away from oil.

The name Neom is a portmanteau of the Greek word neo ("new") and the Arabic word mustaqbal ("future"). The project is administered by the Neom Company, a closed joint-stock company owned by the Public Investment Fund, and was marketed as a hub for innovation in energy, water, mobility, biotechnology, food, media and entertainment.

Projects

The best-known component of Neom is The Line, a proposed linear smart city 170 km (110 mi) long, 200 m wide and 500 m tall, designed to house nine million people with no cars, streets or carbon emissions. Other announced components include Oxagon, a floating industrial complex; Trojena, a mountain tourism resort in the Sarawat range intended to host the 2029 Asian Winter Games; Sindalah, a luxury island resort in the Red Sea; and Leyja, a tourism destination in a natural valley.

Criticism

The project has drawn criticism over its enormous cost — early estimates cited around US$500 billion, with later reports suggesting substantially higher figures — questions about its technical feasibility, and its human-rights implications. The displacement of members of the Howeitat tribe, on whose ancestral land much of Neom sits, has been widely reported, and several tribesmen were arrested after protesting evictions. In 2024, reports emerged that the scope of The Line's first phase had been significantly reduced, with only a few kilometres expected by 2030.
