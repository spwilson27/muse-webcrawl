Tribeca

Tribeca (sometimes styled TriBeCa) is a neighbourhood in Lower Manhattan, New York City. Its name is a syllabic abbreviation of "Triangle Below Canal Street" — although the area is more nearly trapezoidal — and it is bounded by Canal Street to the north, the Hudson River to the west, and roughly Chambers Street and Broadway to the south and east. It is known for its cast-iron and masonry warehouse buildings, converted loft residences, and the Tribeca Film Festival.

History

The area was among the first residential neighbourhoods developed in New York outside the colonial city, then became a centre of the dry-goods and textile trade in the 19th century, whose merchants built the store-and-loft buildings that define the district today. After commerce declined in the mid-20th century, artists took over the cheap loft spaces in the 1970s; several historic districts were designated to protect the building stock. From the 1990s the neighbourhood gentrified into one of the most expensive residential areas in the United States.

Film Festival and culture

The Tribeca Film Festival was founded in 2002 by Robert De Niro, Jane Rosenthal and Craig Hatkoff to revitalise Lower Manhattan after the September 11 attacks, which devastated the neighbourhood near the World Trade Center. The festival has since become a major annual event in the film calendar. Notable landmarks include the Washington Market Park, the former New York Mercantile Exchange building, and the "Ghostbusters" firehouse (Hook & Ladder Company 8).
