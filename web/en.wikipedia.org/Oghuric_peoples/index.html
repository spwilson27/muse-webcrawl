Oghuric peoples

The Oghuric peoples were a group of Turkic peoples who spoke Oghuric (Lir-Turkic) languages, a branch of the Turkic family distinguished by characteristic sound correspondences such as r where Common Turkic has z, and l where Common Turkic has š. They inhabited the Eurasian steppe in late antiquity and the early Middle Ages and were among the first Turkic groups to move westward toward Europe.

History

Oghuric-speaking groups recorded in Byzantine and other sources include the Onogurs, Saragurs, Kutrigurs, Utigurs, and the Bulgars. In the seventh century, Old Great Bulgaria was founded on the Pontic steppe under Kubrat; its successors included Danube Bulgaria in the Balkans and Volga Bulgaria on the middle Volga. The Chuvash people of the Volga region, whose language is the sole surviving Oghuric language, are generally regarded as descendants of the Volga Bulgars.

Language

The Oghuric branch is today represented only by Chuvash, spoken in Chuvashia in Russia. Extinct Oghuric languages are attested only fragmentarily in proper names, titles, and loanwords in neighbouring languages such as Hungarian, which absorbed many Bulgar-era borrowings.
