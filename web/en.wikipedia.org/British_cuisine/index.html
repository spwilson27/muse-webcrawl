British cuisine

A full English breakfast, one of the most recognisable British meals.

British cuisine is the specific set of cooking traditions and practices associated with the United Kingdom, comprising the regional cuisines of England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. Historically characterised by wholesome, hearty dishes based on local produce — beef, lamb, pork, potatoes and root vegetables — it has absorbed influences from Roman, Viking, Norman and, later, imperial and immigrant cultures, including South Asian and Chinese communities.

History

Medieval British cooking made extensive use of spices and herbs among the wealthy and featured elaborate court dishes; bread, cheese, ale and roasted meats formed the core of the common diet. The Columbian exchange brought potatoes, tomatoes and new spices. In the 18th and 19th centuries, industrialisation and rationing (especially during and after the Second World War) gave British food a reputation for plainness that persisted abroad, even as institutions such as afternoon tea, the Sunday roast and the pub meal flourished. Since the 1970s, commentators have described a revival driven by chefs, farmers' markets and the recovery of regional specialities.

Characteristic dishes

Well-known dishes include fish and chips, the full breakfast (full English, Scottish, Welsh variants), roast beef and Yorkshire pudding, shepherd's pie and cottage pie, steak and kidney pie, Cornish pasties, ploughman's lunch, bangers and mash, haggis in Scotland, Welsh rarebit, and desserts such as trifle, sticky toffee pudding, spotted dick and Bakewell tart. Sauces and condiments such as HP Sauce, Worcestershire sauce and Branston pickle are distinctive features. Anglo-Indian dishes, including chicken tikka masala — often said to have been invented in Glasgow — have become so popular that they are sometimes called a true British national dish.

Meals and customs

Traditional meal patterns include breakfast, lunch, afternoon tea and dinner (or "tea" in northern usage). The Sunday roast, eaten at midday or in the afternoon, remains a strong custom, typically roast meat with roast potatoes, vegetables, gravy and stuffing. Pub culture, tea drinking, and bakery items such as scones, crumpets and Eccles cakes are integral to British food culture.
