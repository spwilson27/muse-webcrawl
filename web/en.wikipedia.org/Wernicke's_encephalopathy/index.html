Wernicke's encephalopathy

Wernicke's encephalopathy (WE) is an acute neurological condition caused by a deficiency of thiamine (vitamin B1). It is characterised by a classical triad of symptoms: confusion, abnormal eye movements (ophthalmoplegia or nystagmus), and gait ataxia — although only a minority of patients present with all three. It is most commonly associated with chronic alcohol use disorder, but also occurs with malnutrition, hyperemesis gravidarum, dialysis and after bariatric surgery.

Diagnosis and treatment

The condition was described by Carl Wernicke in 1881. Diagnosis is clinical; MRI may show symmetric lesions around the third ventricle, aqueduct and mammillary bodies. Untreated, WE can progress to the chronic memory disorder Korsakoff syndrome or to coma and death. Treatment is a medical emergency: high-dose parenteral thiamine must be given promptly, before glucose, to avoid precipitating or worsening the disease.

Prognosis

Eye signs usually improve within hours to days of treatment, while confusion and ataxia resolve more slowly; many patients are left with residual memory impairment.
