Christiaan Snouck Hurgronje

Born8 February 1857, Oosterhout, Netherlands
Died26 June 1936, Leiden, Netherlands
OccupationOrientalist, colonial adviser

Christiaan Snouck Hurgronje (1857–1936) was a Dutch scholar of Oriental cultures and languages and a highly influential adviser on colonial affairs to the government of the Netherlands East Indies. He is considered one of the founders of the modern academic study of Islam.

Mecca and Aceh

Trained at Leiden University, Snouck Hurgronje travelled in 1884–85 to Jeddah and Mecca, where — presenting himself as a Muslim scholar — he lived for several months and produced one of the earliest detailed ethnographic accounts of Meccan society, Mekka (1888–89), illustrated with pioneering photographs. From 1889 he served in the East Indies, studying Islam in Aceh during the long Aceh War and advising the colonial government on policy toward Islam.

Policy and legacy

His advice distinguished between Islam as a religion, which he counselled tolerating, and political Islam, which he recommended suppressing — a framework that shaped Dutch colonial rule. His ethnographic work on Aceh and the Gayo lands remains foundational, though his dual role as scholar and colonial strategist continues to be debated.
