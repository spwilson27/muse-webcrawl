Carl Benz

Carl Friedrich Benz (25 November 1844 – 4 April 1929) was a German engine designer and automotive engineer, widely regarded as the inventor of the first practical automobile powered by an internal combustion engine. His Benz Patent-Motorwagen of 1885–1886 is generally recognised as the first automobile: a vehicle designed from the outset, rather than adapted from a horse-drawn carriage, to be propelled by its own engine.

[Image: The Benz Patent-Motorwagen, the first practical automobile]

Life and work

Born in Mühlburg (now part of Karlsruhe), Benz studied mechanical engineering at the Karlsruhe Polytechnic. After ventures in iron construction and engine manufacture, he founded Benz & Cie. in Mannheim in 1883 with financial backers, first building stationary two-stroke engines. He received the patent (DRP 37435) for his motor car in January 1886. The three-wheeled Patent-Motorwagen had a single-cylinder four-stroke engine of 954 cc producing about 0.75 horsepower, electric ignition, a water-cooled cylinder and a differential — many features of the modern car.

Bertha Benz and the first road trip

In August 1888, Benz's wife Bertha, without his knowledge, took the Motorwagen on a 106 km journey from Mannheim to Pforzheim with their sons — the first long-distance automobile trip — solving problems along the way (cleaning a blocked fuel line with a hatpin, buying ligroin fuel at a pharmacy) and demonstrating the car's practicality. Sales followed, and Benz & Cie. grew into the world's largest automobile factory by 1900. In 1926 the company merged with Daimler to form Daimler-Benz, maker of Mercedes-Benz cars. Carl Benz died in Ladenburg in 1929.
