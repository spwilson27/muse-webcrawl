The Ulster Unionist Party (UUP) is a unionist political party in Northern Ireland, favouring the continued union of Northern Ireland with Great Britain. Founded in 1905, it governed Northern Ireland from the state's creation in 1921 until the imposition of direct rule in 1972, and was historically affiliated with the Conservative Party.

History

Under leaders including Sir James Craig, Lord Brookeborough, Terence O'Neill, and Brian Faulkner, the UUP dominated unionist politics for most of the 20th century. Its leader David Trimble, alongside SDLP leader John Hume, received the Nobel Peace Prize for the 1998 Good Friday Agreement, which the UUP supported. From the late 1990s the party lost ground to the Democratic Unionist Party, which became the largest unionist party, though the UUP continues to hold seats in the Northern Ireland Assembly, local councils, and periodically at Westminster.
