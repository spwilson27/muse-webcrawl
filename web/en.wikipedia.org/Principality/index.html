Principality

A principality (sometimes also called a princedom) is a monarchical feudal state ruled by a hereditary titleholder, a prince or princess, or a sovereign state headed by a monarch styled as a prince. The term originated with the Roman emperor Augustus's principate and has been applied to a range of polities from medieval lordships to modern microstates.

Examples

Present-day principalities include Monaco, Andorra (a co-principality ruled jointly by the President of France and the Bishop of Urgell), and Liechtenstein. Historical examples include the principalities of Wales, Transylvania, Moldavia, Wallachia, and numerous states of the Holy Roman Empire ruled by princes.

Use of the term

Not every state with a prince as head is called a principality in its own language; the term is applied flexibly. Some entities, such as the Principality of Sealand, are unrecognized micronations claiming the status.
