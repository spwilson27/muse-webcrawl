Stephen Tyng Mather (July 4, 1867 – January 22, 1930) was an American industrialist and conservationist who was the first director of the National Park Service (NPS). A wealthy borax magnate, he used his fortune and publicity skills to build public and political support for America's national parks.

Early life and borax fortune

Mather graduated from the University of California, Berkeley in 1887 and made his fortune marketing 20 Mule Team Borax, turning borax into a household product.

Creating the Park Service

After a famous 1914 letter complaining about park conditions, Secretary of the Interior Franklin Lane invited Mather to Washington — "if you don't like the way they're being run, come down and run them yourself." Mather lobbied for the Organic Act of August 25, 1916, which created the NPS, and became its first director in 1917, assisted by Horace Albright.

Directorship

Mather championed new parks including Grand Canyon, Acadia, Bryce Canyon, and Great Smoky Mountains; promoted road building and concession quality; and founded the park ranger ideal. He suffered periodic bouts of severe depression (now understood as bipolar disorder) and retired in January 1929.

Legacy

Bronze Mather plaques mark park entrances nationwide. Places named for him include the Stephen Mather Wilderness in North Cascades, Mather Pass in the Sierra Nevada, and Mather Point at the Grand Canyon.
