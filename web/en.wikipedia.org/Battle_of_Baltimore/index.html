Battle of Baltimore

The Battle of Baltimore was a sea and land battle fought on 12–15 September 1814 during the War of 1812, in which American forces repelled a British attack on Baltimore, Maryland.

Course of the battle

After burning Washington, British forces landed at North Point, where their commander Major-General Robert Ross was killed. The Royal Navy bombarded Fort McHenry for 25 hours but failed to subdue it. Watching the bombardment from a truce ship, Francis Scott Key wrote "The Star-Spangled Banner", which became the national anthem of the United States. The failed attack marked a turning point in the war.
