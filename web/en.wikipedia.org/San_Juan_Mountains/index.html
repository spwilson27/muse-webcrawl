The San Juan Mountains are a high and rugged mountain range in the Rocky Mountains located in southwestern Colorado and northwestern New Mexico, United States. They form the largest mountain range in Colorado by area and contain some of the most extensive terrain above the timberline in the contiguous United States. The range is the source of the Rio Grande, which rises in its eastern part, and of the headwaters of the San Juan River.

Geology

Much of the range is of volcanic origin. In the mid-Tertiary period, enormous eruptions from a series of calderas — including the La Garita Caldera, site of one of the largest known explosive eruptions on Earth — deposited vast sheets of ash-flow tuff. Glaciation later carved the range's deep valleys and sharp peaks.

History and mining

The San Juans were part of the homeland of the Ute people. Gold and silver discoveries in the 1860s and 1870s led to mining boom towns such as Ouray, Silverton, Telluride, Lake City, and Creede, connected by Otto Mears's toll roads and narrow-gauge railroads, including the line now operated as the Durango and Silverton Narrow Gauge Railroad. The Million Dollar Highway (U.S. Route 550) between Ouray and Silverton follows one of the most dramatic mountain routes in America.

Peaks and recreation

Fourteeners in the range include Uncompahgre Peak (the highest at 14,309 ft), Mount Wilson, Mount Sneffels, and Handies Peak. The range is popular for hiking, climbing, skiing (Telluride and Purgatory resorts), and off-road touring on historic mining roads. Large areas are protected within the Weminuche Wilderness and other wilderness areas of the San Juan and Uncompahgre national forests.
