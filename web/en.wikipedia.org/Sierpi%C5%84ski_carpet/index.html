Sierpiński carpet

Type: Fractal
Named after: Wacław Sierpiński
Year: 1916
Hausdorff dimension: log 8 / log 3

The Sierpiński carpet is a plane fractal first described by the Polish mathematician Wacław Sierpiński in 1916. It is a two-dimensional generalisation of the Cantor set and a relative of the Sierpiński triangle.

Construction

Begin with a square, divide it into nine congruent subsquares in a 3×3 grid, and remove the central square. Repeat the procedure indefinitely on each of the remaining eight subsquares. The limiting set has zero area and Hausdorff dimension log 8 / log 3 ≈ 1.893.

Properties

The carpet is self-similar, nowhere dense yet connected, and serves as a universal curve for planar one-dimensional continua. Its three-dimensional analogue is the Menger sponge. It appears in designs such as fractal antennas and in the study of percolation and diffusion.
