World Organization of the Scout Movement

The World Organization of the Scout Movement (WOSM) is the largest international Scouting organisation, bringing together national Scout organisations from more than 170 countries and territories. It reports a membership of over 57 million young people and volunteers. WOSM is a non-governmental, non-political youth movement dedicated to the educational mission of Scouting as founded by Robert Baden-Powell in 1907.

History

International cooperation among Scout associations began soon after Scouting spread beyond Britain. In 1922, at the second International Scout Conference in Paris, an international committee and bureau were established — the nucleus of the present organisation. The body took the name World Organization of the Scout Movement in 1961. WOSM is a counterpart to the World Association of Girl Guides and Girl Scouts (WAGGGS), and the two organisations cooperate closely.

Structure

  • The World Scout Conference, meeting every three years, is the general assembly of member organisations and elects the World Scout Committee.
  • The World Scout Committee is the executive body between conferences.
  • The World Scout Bureau is the secretariat, with a global support centre in Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia, and regional offices serving Africa, the Arab world, Asia-Pacific, Eurasia, Europe and the Interamerican region.

Activities

WOSM organises the World Scout Jamboree, held roughly every four years, as well as the World Scout Moot for older members and JOTA-JOTI, the annual Jamboree on the Air and Internet. Its programmes include Messengers of Peace, Scouts of the World Award and environmental education initiatives. The organisation's purpose is to contribute to the education of young people through a value system based on the Scout Promise and Scout Law, helping build a better world where people are self-fulfilled as individuals and play a constructive role in society.
