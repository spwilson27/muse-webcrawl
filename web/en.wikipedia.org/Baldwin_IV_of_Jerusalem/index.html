Baldwin IV of Jerusalem

Baldwin IV

Reign: 1174–1185

Born: 1161

Died: 1185 (aged about 24)

Predecessor: Amalric I

Successor: Baldwin V

Baldwin IV (1161–1185), known as the Leper King, was king of Jerusalem from 1174 until his death. Despite suffering from leprosy, which progressively disabled him from childhood, he proved an energetic and effective ruler who repeatedly defended his kingdom against Saladin.

Early life

Baldwin was the son of King Amalric I and Agnes of Courtenay, and was educated by the historian William of Tyre, who noticed that the boy felt no pain when his companions pinched his arm during play — an early sign of leprosy. He succeeded his father at the age of thirteen, with regents ruling until he came of age in 1176.

Reign

Baldwin personally led campaigns against Saladin, most famously defeating him at the Battle of Montgisard in 1177 when only sixteen. Unable to marry or produce an heir, he arranged for his sister Sibylla's son, Baldwin V, to succeed, and spent his last years resisting the influence of Sibylla's husband, Guy of Lusignan, whose incompetence as regent he came to regret. Carried on campaign in a litter as his disease advanced, Baldwin died in the spring of 1185, two years before Saladin's victory at Hattin destroyed the kingdom's field army.

Legacy

Historians have praised Baldwin's determination and political skill under extraordinary physical suffering; William of Tyre and later chroniclers treated him as a tragic, heroic figure. His reign marked the last period in which the crusader kingdom of Jerusalem held its own against a united Muslim Near East.
