Mutual fund

Mutual fund
TypeCollective investment scheme
AssetsStocks, bonds, money market instruments
PricingNet asset value (NAV), once daily

A mutual fund is an investment vehicle that pools money from many investors to purchase a diversified portfolio of securities such as stocks, bonds, and money market instruments. Professional managers allocate the fund's assets according to its stated objectives. Investors own shares of the fund, and the value of those shares is based on the fund's net asset value (NAV), calculated at the end of each trading day.

History

The first modern investment funds appeared in the Netherlands in the eighteenth century. In the United States, the Massachusetts Investors Trust, launched in 1924, is considered the first modern open-end mutual fund. The industry grew rapidly after the Second World War and expanded enormously with the rise of retirement accounts such as the 401(k). In the 1970s John Bogle and the Vanguard Group popularised the index fund, which passively tracks a market benchmark at very low cost.

Structure and types

Open-end funds continuously issue and redeem shares at NAV; closed-end funds issue a fixed number of shares that trade on exchanges, sometimes at premiums or discounts. Funds are categorised by asset class (equity, bond, money market, hybrid) and by strategy (growth, value, sector, international). Index funds and actively managed funds differ sharply in fees: expense ratios range from a few basis points for index funds to over one percent for some active funds. Loads — front-end or back-end sales charges — have declined in importance as no-load funds and exchange-traded funds (ETFs) have grown.

Advantages and risks

Mutual funds offer diversification, professional management, and liquidity to small investors. However, returns are not guaranteed, management fees reduce performance, and actively managed funds on average underperform their benchmarks after costs. Taxation of capital-gains distributions can also affect after-tax returns in taxable accounts.
