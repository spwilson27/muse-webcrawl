Oskar Dirlewanger


Born: 26 September 1895, Würzburg, German Empire
Died: c. 7 June 1945 (aged 49), Altshausen, Germany
Allegiance: Nazi Germany
Branch: Waffen-SS
Unit: Dirlewanger Brigade

Oskar Paul Dirlewanger (26 September 1895 – c. 7 June 1945) was a German military officer and convicted child molester who served as commander of the SS penal unit known as the Dirlewanger Brigade during the Second World War. His unit, recruited from convicted criminals, poachers and later political prisoners and foreign volunteers, became notorious for extreme brutality against civilians, particularly in Belarus and Poland.

Early life and First World War

Dirlewanger was born in Würzburg in 1895. He served as a machine gunner in the First World War, was wounded several times and received the Iron Cross. After the war he joined Freikorps units fighting against communist uprisings and in Upper Silesia. He earned a doctorate in political science from the University of Frankfurt in 1922 and joined the Nazi Party, though his membership lapsed and he rejoined later. In 1934 he was convicted of sexually abusing a minor and imprisoned; a second conviction followed, and he was sent to a concentration camp.

The Dirlewanger unit

Through his acquaintance with Gottlob Berger, a senior SS official, Dirlewanger was released and given command in 1940 of a special unit initially formed from convicted poachers, chosen because of their fieldcraft. The unit expanded over the war to include criminals, concentration camp prisoners and defectors, growing from company to battalion, regiment and finally brigade size. Deployed mainly in anti-partisan warfare in occupied Belarus and later in the suppression of the Warsaw Uprising of 1944 and the Slovak National Uprising, the unit committed mass murders of civilians, mass rape and looting on an extraordinary scale; historians have linked it to the deaths of tens of thousands of people.

Death

Dirlewanger was wounded in February 1945 and removed from command. After the war ended he was arrested by French forces and died in custody at Altshausen in June 1945, reportedly beaten by Polish guards, though the exact circumstances remain disputed.
