Loch Torridon (Scottish Gaelic: Loch Thoirbheartan) is a sea loch on the west coast of Scotland in Wester Ross, in the Highland council area. Formed by glacial action during the last ice age, it is a fjord-like inlet of the North Atlantic, approximately 15 miles (24 km) long.

The loch is divided by the Ardheslaig peninsula into two parts: the outer loch, opening to the sea between Rubha Réidh and the island of Rona, and the inner Upper Loch Torridon. The village of Torridon lies at the head of the loch, and Shieldaig and Diabaig are situated along its shores.

Geography and geology

The surrounding landscape is dominated by the Torridon Hills, mountains of Torridonian sandstone — among the oldest sedimentary rocks in Britain, laid down around 1,000 million years ago — resting on even older Lewisian gneiss. Peaks around the loch include Beinn Alligin, Liathach and Beinn Eighe, the last giving its name to one of Britain's oldest nature reserves.

Economy and wildlife

The loch supports inshore fishing, notably for prawns (langoustines) and scallops, as well as salmon farming and tourism. The waters and shores host seals, otters, porpoises and sea eagles. The area around the loch forms part of the Wester Ross National Scenic Area.
