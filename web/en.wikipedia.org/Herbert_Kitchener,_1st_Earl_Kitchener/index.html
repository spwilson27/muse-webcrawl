Herbert Kitchener, 1st Earl Kitchener

Field Marshal Lord Kitchener

Born: 24 June 1850, Ballylongford, County Kerry, Ireland

Died: 5 June 1916 (aged 65), HMS Hampshire, off Orkney

Service: British Army

Rank: Field Marshal

Offices: Secretary of State for War, 1914–1916

"Lord Kitchener Wants You" — the iconic 1914 recruiting image

Field Marshal Horatio Herbert Kitchener, 1st Earl Kitchener (24 June 1850 – 5 June 1916) was an Anglo-Irish senior officer of the British Army and colonial administrator who won fame for his imperial campaigns, most notably in Sudan and South Africa, and who, as Secretary of State for War at the start of the First World War, organised the largest volunteer army Britain had ever fielded.

Imperial career

Commissioned in the Royal Engineers, Kitchener served in Palestine survey work, Cyprus, and Egypt, becoming Sirdar (commander) of the Egyptian Army. In 1898 he defeated the Mahdist forces at the Battle of Omdurman, avenging Gordon and reconquering Sudan, and confronted the French at Fashoda. As chief of staff and then commander-in-chief in the Second Boer War (1900–1902), he prosecuted a ruthless counter-guerrilla campaign involving farm burning and concentration camps that remain controversial. He was afterwards commander-in-chief in India and consul-general in Egypt.

First World War

Appointed Secretary of State for War in August 1914, Kitchener — almost alone among the cabinet — foresaw a long war requiring millions of men. His appeal for volunteers, immortalised by Alfred Leete's "Lord Kitchener Wants You" poster, raised the "Kitchener's Army" of Pals battalions. His influence waned after the Shell Crisis of 1915 and the creation of the Ministry of Munitions. On 5 June 1916, travelling to Russia aboard the cruiser HMS Hampshire, he was killed when the ship struck a German mine off Orkney.
