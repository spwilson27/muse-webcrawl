Crashgate

Crashgate, also known as Singapurgate, is a Formula One sporting scandal in which the Renault F1 team was found to have ordered its driver Nelson Piquet Jr. to deliberately crash during the 2008 Singapore Grand Prix, in order to bring out the safety car and thereby help his teammate Fernando Alonso, who went on to win the race. The scandal came to light in 2009, after Piquet was dismissed by the team and made allegations to the FIA.

Piquet's Renault at the inaugural Singapore Grand Prix, 2008

The race

The inaugural Singapore Grand Prix, Formula One's first night race, was held on 28 September 2008 at the Marina Bay Street Circuit. Alonso, who had started only 15th after a fuel problem in qualifying, made an early pit stop on lap 12 for fuel and tyres. Two laps later, Piquet crashed heavily into the wall at Turn 17, bringing out the safety car. Because most of the leading cars had not yet pitted, the deployment of the safety car allowed Alonso to move to the front of the field when others stopped; he won the race ahead of Nico Rosberg and Lewis Hamilton.

Allegations and investigation

Following his dismissal by Renault in mid-2009, Piquet told the FIA that he had been asked by team principal Flavio Briatore and engineering director Pat Symonds to crash at a specific corner in order to help Alonso. The FIA charged Renault with conspiring with Piquet to cause a deliberate crash in breach of the International Sporting Code. Radio transmissions and telemetry data were examined as part of the investigation.

Outcome

In September 2009, Renault announced that Briatore and Symonds had left the team and that it would not dispute the charges. The FIA World Motor Sport Council gave Renault a disqualification from Formula One suspended for two years; Briatore was banned from FIA-sanctioned events indefinitely, and Symonds for five years. Both bans were later overturned by a French court in 2010, though the two men agreed not to work in Formula One for a period thereafter. Piquet was granted immunity from individual sanction in exchange for his testimony. Alonso was cleared of any involvement or knowledge of the plan.

Reactions

The affair was described by former world champion Damon Hill and others as one of the worst examples of cheating in sporting history. It raised questions about safety — a deliberately induced crash endangered marshals, spectators, and other drivers — and prompted renewed scrutiny of race manipulation and team orders in Formula One.
