Rotor (mathematics)

A rotor is an object in geometric algebra (also called Clifford algebra) that rotates vectors in a plane or in space. Rotors are the natural generalisation of quaternions to arbitrary dimensions: whereas a quaternion encodes a rotation in three dimensions, a rotor encodes a rotation in a space of any number of dimensions, and a single formalism handles all of them.

Definition

In geometric algebra, a rotor R in a plane is the geometric product of two unit vectors, equivalently the exponential of a bivector: R = exp(−Bθ/2), where B is the unit bivector of the rotation plane and θ is the rotation angle. A vector x is rotated by the double-sided sandwich product x′ = R x R̃, where R̃ is the reverse of R. The double-sided action, with the half-angle, is exactly analogous to the action of unit quaternions on pure quaternions.

Properties

Rotors form a group under the geometric product — in three dimensions, the spin group Spin(3), which double-covers the rotation group SO(3). Composing rotations corresponds to multiplying rotors, and interpolation between rotors (analogous to quaternion slerp) is straightforward. Because the rotation plane is explicit (a bivector), rotors avoid gimbal lock and axis-angle parameterisation problems.

Applications

Rotors are used in computer graphics, robotics, and physics wherever rotations must be composed and interpolated robustly, and they extend naturally to "motors" — combinations of rotation and translation — in conformal geometric algebra, giving a uniform treatment of rigid-body motion.
