Gatwick Airport

Gatwick Airport (IATA: LGW, ICAO: EGKK) is a major international airport near Crawley, West Sussex, about 47 km south of central London. It is the United Kingdom's second-busiest airport after Heathrow and one of the busiest single-runway airports in the world.

History

The site began as an aerodrome in the late 1920s and was developed as a London airport in the 1930s, when its distinctive circular Beehive terminal opened. Rebuilt after the Second World War, Gatwick reopened in 1958 as London's second airport. The North Terminal opened in 1988, connected to the South Terminal by an automated people mover. Ownership passed from BAA to Global Infrastructure Partners in 2009, and Vinci Airports acquired a majority stake in 2019.

Operations

Gatwick operates two terminals and a single main runway (08L/26R) with a shorter standby runway. It serves over 200 destinations, with easyJet its largest airline, alongside British Airways, TUI, Ryanair, and others. Passenger numbers peaked at about 46.6 million in 2019 and have been recovering since the COVID-19 pandemic.

Transport links

Gatwick Airport railway station offers direct trains to London Victoria (including the Gatwick Express), London Bridge, Brighton, and other destinations. The airport lies on the M23 motorway and is served by long-distance coaches.

Expansion debate

Gatwick was shortlisted alongside Heathrow for additional runway capacity in the South East; the government chose Heathrow in 2016. Gatwick continues to pursue bringing its standby northern runway into routine use, a proposal examined through a development consent process in the 2020s.
