Pumpkin

A pumpkin is a cultivated winter squash in the genus Cucurbita, most commonly Cucurbita pepo, grown for its round, ribbed, typically deep-orange fruit with thick flesh and edible seeds. The name also applies to certain varieties of C. maxima, C. moschata, and related species. Pumpkins are native to North America and have been cultivated for at least 7,500 years.

Description and cultivation

Pumpkin plants are vigorous annual vines with large lobed leaves and separate male and female yellow flowers pollinated by bees, including specialist squash bees. Fruits range from a few hundred grams in ornamental types to record giants exceeding 1,200 kg (2,700 lb), bred from C. maxima 'Dill's Atlantic Giant'. Most commercial acreage is in the United States (notably Illinois), Mexico, China, and India.

Uses

Pumpkin flesh is used in pies, soups, breads, and purées; the great majority of the U.S. processing crop goes into canned pumpkin for pie filling. Roasted seeds (pepitas) are eaten as a snack and pressed for oil, a specialty of Styria in Austria. Pumpkin spice flavoring—cinnamon, nutmeg, ginger, and cloves—has become a pervasive seasonal flavor in coffee and baked goods.

Culture

Hollowed and carved pumpkins, called jack-o'-lanterns, are emblematic of Halloween, a tradition carried from Ireland (where turnips were originally used) to North America. Pumpkins also feature in the Cinderella fairy tale, in harvest festivals, and in competitive giant-pumpkin weigh-offs. In 2021 the heaviest pumpkin on record weighed about 1,226 kg, grown in Italy.
