Mesopotamian campaign
Date: 1914–1918
Location: Mesopotamia (modern Iraq)
Combatants: British Empire (mainly British Indian Army) vs Ottoman Empire
Result: Allied victory; Ottoman territories partitioned

The Mesopotamian campaign was a campaign of the First World War fought by British Empire forces, predominantly from the British Indian Army, against the Ottoman Empire in Mesopotamia — modern Iraq — between 1914 and 1918.

Course of the campaign

British forces landed at the head of the Persian Gulf in November 1914 to protect oil installations at Abadan and quickly occupied Basra. Advancing up the Tigris, General Townshend's 6th (Poona) Division won a string of victories in 1915, taking Amara and Kut and pressing on toward Baghdad before being checked at Ctesiphon. Retreating to Kut, Townshend's force endured a five-month siege and surrendered in April 1916 — some 13,000 men marched into captivity in one of Britain's worst defeats of the war.

Under General Maude the reorganised army retook Kut and captured Baghdad in March 1917. Further advances up the Tigris and Euphrates followed; after Maude's death from cholera, General Marshall continued operations, and in October 1918 British forces won the Battle of Sharqat and accepted the Ottoman surrender under the Armistice of Mudros, securing Mosul and its prospective oilfields at the war's end.

Consequences

The campaign cost roughly 85,000 British Empire battle casualties and many more from disease, against heavy Ottoman losses. Mesopotamia became a British mandate (the Mandate for Mesopotamia), whose imposed borders and Hashemite monarchy shaped the modern Iraqi state. Logistics — river transport, medical care and supply — were the campaign's defining challenge in a theatre of extreme heat, floods and poor infrastructure.
