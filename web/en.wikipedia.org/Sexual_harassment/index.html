Sexual harassment

Prevention awareness poster

Sexual harassment is a form of harassment involving unwelcome sexual advances, requests for sexual favours, and other verbal or physical conduct of a sexual nature. In legal contexts — especially employment and education — it generally falls into two categories: quid pro quo harassment, in which submission to such conduct is made a condition of employment or benefits, and hostile work environment harassment, in which the conduct is severe or pervasive enough to create an intimidating, hostile, or abusive environment.

History of the concept

The term was popularised in the 1970s by feminists at Cornell University and in Lin Farley's 1978 book Sexual Shakedown; legal scholar Catharine MacKinnon's 1979 Sexual Harassment of Working Women argued that it constituted sex discrimination. In the United States, the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission issued guidelines in 1980, and the Supreme Court recognised sexual harassment as a violation of Title VII of the Civil Rights Act in Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson (1986). The 1991 Clarence Thomas confirmation hearings, at which Anita Hill testified, brought the issue to national prominence.

Effects and prevalence

Research associates sexual harassment with psychological harm (anxiety, depression, post-traumatic stress), physical symptoms, job withdrawal, and lost earnings for victims, as well as productivity losses for organisations. Surveys in many countries find that a substantial share of women — and a smaller but significant share of men — report experiencing it, most often at work. Harassment also occurs in schools and universities (covered in the US by Title IX), in housing, in the military, and online.

Responses

Most jurisdictions now prohibit sexual harassment through employment discrimination law, and organisations commonly maintain policies, complaint procedures, and training programmes, though scholars debate the effectiveness of training and grievance systems. The #MeToo movement, which spread globally from 2017, led to high-profile dismissals, legal reforms in several countries, and restrictions on non-disclosure agreements covering harassment claims.
