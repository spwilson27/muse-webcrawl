The basal ganglia (or basal nuclei) are a group of subcortical nuclei of varied origin in the brains of vertebrates. In humans and other primates they sit deep within the cerebral hemispheres, connected with the cerebral cortex, thalamus, and brainstem. The basal ganglia are strongly associated with motor control — particularly the selection and initiation of voluntary movements — and also play central roles in procedural learning, habit formation, reward processing, and some cognitive and emotional functions.

Structure

The principal components are the striatum, divided into the dorsal striatum (caudate nucleus and putamen) and the ventral striatum (including the nucleus accumbens); the globus pallidus, with internal and external segments; the substantia nigra, divided into the dopamine-producing pars compacta and the pars reticulata; and the subthalamic nucleus. These nuclei are linked by parallel loops running from cortex through the basal ganglia and thalamus and back to cortex.

Function and circuitry

The classic model describes two opposing routes through the circuit: a direct pathway that facilitates movement by disinhibiting the thalamus, and an indirect pathway that suppresses movement, with dopamine from the substantia nigra pars compacta modulating both — exciting the direct pathway and inhibiting the indirect. The balance between the pathways is thought to underlie action selection, while reward-related dopamine signals help stamp in habits through learning in the striatum.

Clinical significance

Dysfunction of the basal ganglia produces characteristic disorders. Degeneration of dopamine neurons in Parkinson's disease causes slowness and paucity of movement, rigidity, and tremor; loss of striatal neurons in Huntington's disease causes involuntary chorea. Basal ganglia circuits are also implicated in Tourette syndrome, dystonia, obsessive–compulsive disorder, and addiction. Treatments include dopaminergic medication and deep brain stimulation of targets such as the subthalamic nucleus and globus pallidus.
