National universities (Japan)
Japanese: 国立大学 (kokuritsu daigaku)
Number: 86 (as of recent years)
Governing law: National University Corporation Act (2003)
Supervising ministry: MEXT

National universities (国立大学, kokuritsu daigaku) in Japan are universities established by the national government. Originally operated directly as organs of the Ministry of Education, they were converted in April 2004 into national university corporations (国立大学法人) under the National University Corporation Act of 2003, gaining greater administrative and financial autonomy while remaining publicly funded and supervised by the Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology (MEXT).

History

The system traces its origins to the imperial universities founded from 1877 onward, beginning with the University of Tokyo (1877), followed by Kyoto, Tohoku, Kyushu, Hokkaido, Osaka, and Nagoya. After the Second World War, the 1949 school system reform established at least one national university in each prefecture, largely through mergers of former higher schools, normal schools, and technical colleges.

Organization and funding

Under corporatization, each university is run by a president and executive board, with management councils including external members. The national government provides block operating grants, whose real value has declined over time, prompting universities to seek competitive research funding and industry collaboration. Tuition at national universities is standardized by regulation and is lower than at most private universities, though it has risen substantially since the 1970s.

Notable institutions

The former imperial universities, especially the University of Tokyo and Kyoto University, remain Japan's most prestigious research institutions. Other prominent national universities include Osaka University, Tohoku University, the Tokyo Institute of Technology (merged with Tokyo Medical and Dental University in 2024 to form the Institute of Science Tokyo), and Hitotsubashi University. National universities educate a minority of Japanese undergraduates — most students attend private universities — but dominate in research output and graduate education.
