Vishnukundina dynasty

The Vishnukundina dynasty (IAST: Viṣṇukuṇḍina) was an Indian dynasty based in the Deccan, which ruled parts of present-day Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Odisha and portions of Karnataka between the 5th and 7th centuries CE. The family's royal emblem was a lion. After the decline of the Vakataka dynasty, the Vishnukundinas became the dominant power in the eastern Deccan.

History

The dynasty's founder is generally identified as Indravarman (c. 420 CE). Its greatest ruler, Madhavavarman I (c. 440–460), performed numerous Vedic sacrifices including the Ashvamedha, and expanded the kingdom considerably. Later kings such as Govindavarman II, Madhavavarman II, and Vikramendravarman I ruled from capitals including Trivara, Amarapura, and Indrapalanagara. The dynasty waned in the early 7th century under pressure from the Pallavas and the rising Eastern Chalukyas of Pulakeshin II, who annexed their coastal territories.

Culture

The Vishnukundinas patronised Sanskrit learning and Brahmanical religion; their name reflects devotion to Vishnu. They are traditionally credited with initiating the excavation of rock-cut cave temples in the region, including the famous Undavalli Caves near Vijayawada with its large reclining Vishnu. Their inscriptions, issued in Sanskrit, record extensive land grants to Brahmins and the building of temples and tanks.

Legacy

The dynasty represents an important link in Deccan history between the Satavahanas and Ikshvakus on one side and the Chalukyas and Rashtrakutas on the other, shaping the political and temple culture of the Andhra country.
