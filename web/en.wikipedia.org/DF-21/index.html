The Dongfeng 21 (DF-21, NATO reporting name CSS-5) is a two-stage, solid-fuel, road-mobile medium-range ballistic missile developed by the People's Republic of China. Derived from the submarine-launched JL-1, it was China's first solid-fuelled land-based ballistic missile, entering service with the People's Liberation Army Rocket Force (formerly the Second Artillery Corps) in the early 1990s, replacing the liquid-fuelled DF-3.

Variants

The improved DF-21A introduced greater range and accuracy, while the DF-21C is a precision-strike variant with a terminally guided manoeuvring warhead intended for conventional attacks on fixed targets. The DF-21D, publicly revealed in the late 2000s and displayed at the 2015 Beijing victory parade, is widely described as the world's first anti-ship ballistic missile (ASBM): it is designed to target large warships, particularly aircraft carriers, at sea using a manoeuvring re-entry vehicle guided by radar and satellite updates. Its existence has been a focus of debate about naval operations in the Western Pacific and the concept of anti-access/area denial (A2/AD). Saudi Arabia is reported to have purchased DF-21 missiles, displayed in 2014.

Characteristics

Launched from a wheeled transporter-erector-launcher, the DF-21 can carry a single nuclear warhead (in its strategic variants) or conventional payloads, with later variants achieving circular error probable estimates in the tens of metres using satellite navigation and terminal seekers.
