Midland Line, New Zealand

Midland Line
Overview: Railway line in the South Island
Termini: Rolleston – Greymouth
Length: 212 km (132 mi)
Opened: 1923 (through route)

The Midland Line is a railway line in the South Island of New Zealand, running 212 kilometres from Rolleston, near Christchurch in Canterbury, westward through the Southern Alps via the Otira Tunnel to Greymouth on the West Coast. It is one of the most scenic railway journeys in the world and carries the TranzAlpine passenger train as well as heavy coal traffic from West Coast mines to the port of Lyttelton.

History

Construction began in the 1880s from both ends. The eastern section reached Springfield by 1880 and Cass by 1914, while the western section reached Otira by 1900. The two ends were finally linked on 4 August 1923 with the opening of the 8.5 km Otira Tunnel beneath Arthur's Pass, at the time one of the longest tunnels in the British Empire. The tunnel's steep 1-in-33 grade required electrification of the Otira–Arthur's Pass section, which operated from 1923 until dieselisation in 1997.

Route

From Rolleston the line crosses the Canterbury Plains to Springfield, then climbs through the Waimakariri Gorge, crossing the river on high viaducts including the Staircase Viaduct. It passes Cass — immortalised in Rita Angus's 1936 painting — and Waimakariri basin stations before reaching Arthur's Pass township. Beyond the Otira Tunnel it descends the Otira Gorge, follows the Taramakau River, and terminates at Greymouth on the Grey River.

Services

The TranzAlpine, operated by KiwiRail's Great Journeys New Zealand, runs daily between Christchurch and Greymouth and is regularly rated among the world's great rail journeys. Freight is dominated by export coal trains from the Buller and Grey districts to Lyttelton, along with timber, dairy products and containers.
