Automotive industry in China

The automotive industry in China is the largest in the world measured by annual production and sales. China has produced more vehicles than any other country since 2009, building over 30 million vehicles a year in recent years, and in 2023 it became the world's largest vehicle exporter.

History

The industry began in the 1950s with Soviet assistance — First Automobile Works (FAW) launched production in 1956 — and expanded slowly under state planning. Reform-era growth followed China's WTO accession and joint ventures between state firms such as SAIC, FAW and Dongfeng and foreign makers including Volkswagen and General Motors; for decades foreign brands could produce in China only through such partnerships. Private firms such as Geely and Great Wall emerged in the 1990s and 2000s.

Electric vehicles

Supported by industrial policy, subsidies and a dominant battery supply chain (CATL and BYD), China became the global centre of electric vehicle production. BYD overtook Tesla in global battery-electric sales in late 2023. Rapid export growth has also prompted trade responses, including additional EU and U.S. tariffs on Chinese-built EVs.
