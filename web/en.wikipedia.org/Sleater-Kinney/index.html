Sleater-Kinney is an American rock band formed in Olympia, Washington, in 1994 by Corin Tucker and Carrie Brownstein, veterans of the riot grrrl scene (Heavens to Betsy and Excuse 17 respectively). The band's name derives from Sleater Kinney Road in Lacey, Washington. Known for the interplay of Tucker and Brownstein's distinctly tuned guitars and vocals — without a bass player — and for drummer Janet Weiss's powerful playing, Sleater-Kinney became one of the most critically acclaimed American rock bands of the 1990s and 2000s.

History

After a self-titled debut (1995) and Call the Doctor (1996), the band achieved a breakthrough with Dig Me Out (1997), Weiss's first album with the group. The Hot Rock (1999), All Hands on the Bad One (2000) and One Beat (2002) consolidated their reputation; Rolling Stone critic Greil Marcus called them "America's best rock band". The Woods (2005), a heavier record produced by Dave Fridmann, preceded an indefinite hiatus announced in 2006, after which Brownstein co-created and starred in the comedy series Portlandia.

The band reunited in 2014 and released No Cities to Love (2015) to wide acclaim. The Center Won't Hold (2019), produced by St. Vincent, was followed by Weiss's departure. Tucker and Brownstein continued as a duo with Path of Wellness (2021) and Little Rope (2024).

Style and legacy

Sleater-Kinney's songs address feminism, politics, identity and relationships over angular, rhythmic guitar figures. Frequently cited among the greatest rock acts of their era, they influenced a generation of indie rock musicians, and their work is regularly included on lists of the best albums of the 1990s.
