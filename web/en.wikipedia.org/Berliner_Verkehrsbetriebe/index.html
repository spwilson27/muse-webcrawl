Berliner Verkehrsbetriebe


Illustration: Berliner Verkehrsbetriebe

The Berliner Verkehrsbetriebe (BVG) is the main public transport company of Berlin, Germany. It manages the city's U-Bahn (underground railway), tram, bus and ferry networks. Founded in 1928, the BVG is owned by the State of Berlin and is one of the largest municipal transport companies in Europe.

History

The BVG was formed in 1928 through the merger of several transport operators. After the division of Berlin in 1949, separate BVG administrations operated in West and East Berlin; the eastern company was renamed BVB in 1969. The two were reunified in 1992 following German reunification. The BVG is famous for its bright yellow livery and its humorous, self-deprecating advertising campaigns.

Network

The BVG operates ten U-Bahn lines, 22 tram lines and around 150 bus routes, as well as six ferry routes on Berlin's lakes and rivers. The S-Bahn, by contrast, is operated by Deutsche Bahn. See also ghost station for the closed stations of the divided city.
