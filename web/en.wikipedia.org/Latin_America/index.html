Latin America

Latin America is the portion of the Americas where Romance languages — principally Spanish and Portuguese, and to a lesser extent French — are spoken. It generally comprises Mexico, Central America, the Spanish- and French-speaking Caribbean, and South America.

Definition and history

The term was popularised in the 19th century, partly under Napoleon III's France, to emphasise common Latin heritage. The region was colonised by Spain and Portugal from 1492, reshaping Indigenous civilisations such as the Aztec, Maya and Inca. Independence movements in the early 1800s, led by figures such as Simón Bolívar and José de San Martín, created most modern states.

Geography and people

Latin America covers about 19 million km² with a population over 650 million. Brazil and Mexico are its largest countries. Its cultures blend Indigenous, European, African and Asian influences, and its economies range across agriculture, mining, manufacturing and services.
