WeWork

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

WeWork Inc. is an American provider of coworking spaces and flexible office services. Founded in 2010 by Adam Neumann and Miguel McKelvey in New York City, the company leases large office spaces on long-term contracts and sublets them, subdivided and furnished, to freelancers, startups and enterprises on short-term memberships.

Rise and failed IPO

WeWork expanded rapidly through the 2010s, backed by heavy venture funding, above all from Japan's SoftBank, which valued the company at $47 billion in early 2019. Its attempt at an initial public offering that year collapsed amid scrutiny of its losses, corporate governance and Neumann's behaviour and self-dealing. Neumann resigned as chief executive, the IPO was withdrawn, and SoftBank took control with a rescue package that slashed the company's valuation.

Public listing and bankruptcy

WeWork eventually went public in 2021 through a merger with a special-purpose acquisition company. Burdened by long-term lease obligations and weakened demand for office space following the COVID-19 pandemic, the company filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in the United States in November 2023. It emerged from bankruptcy in 2024 with a reduced footprint, having rejected hundreds of leases and restructured its debt.

Cultural impact

WeWork's rise and fall became a prominent symbol of the excesses of the late-2010s startup boom. It has been the subject of books, the documentary WeWork: Or the Making and Breaking of a $47 Billion Unicorn (2021), and the television drama WeCrashed (2022).
