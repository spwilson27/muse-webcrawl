Reykjanes

CountryIceland
RegionSouthern Peninsula (Suðurnes)
FeaturesLava fields, geothermal areas, Keflavík International Airport

Reykjanes is a peninsula in south-western Iceland, corresponding largely to the Southern Peninsula region (Suðurnes). It is characterised by young lava fields, volcanic fissures, hot springs and geothermal areas, and is home to towns including Keflavík, Njarðvík and Grindavík, as well as Keflavík International Airport, Iceland's main international gateway.

Geology

Reykjanes lies on the Mid-Atlantic Ridge, where the North American and Eurasian plates diverge; it is one of the most volcanically active parts of Iceland. After roughly 800 years of quiet, the peninsula entered a new eruptive period in 2021 with eruptions at Fagradalsfjall, followed by eruptions near Sundhnúkur from late 2023 that threatened Grindavík, prompting evacuations and the construction of protective barriers.

Attractions and infrastructure

The Blue Lagoon geothermal spa, the Svartsengi and Reykjanes power stations, the Bridge Between Continents, and the Reykjanesviti lighthouse are among the peninsula's landmarks. Much of the area is protected as the Reykjanes UNESCO Global Geopark.
