The Saturday Evening Post
Founded: 1821, Philadelphia
Publisher: Curtis Publishing Company (peak era)
Type: Magazine
Famed illustrators: Norman Rockwell, J. C. Leyendecker

The Saturday Evening Post is an American magazine, one of the oldest in the United States, currently published six times a year. Rooted in a newspaper first issued in 1821, it became a mass-circulation weekly under the Cyrus Curtis publishing empire in the early 20th century.

Golden age

Under editor George Horace Lorimer (1899–1936), the Post published work by authors such as F. Scott Fitzgerald, William Faulkner and Agatha Christie, and became famous for its cover illustrations. Norman Rockwell painted 323 covers for the magazine between 1916 and 1963, creating iconic images of American life, including the Four Freedoms series of 1943.

Decline and revival

Changing tastes and television competition collapsed the weekly's circulation in the 1960s; it ceased weekly publication in 1969 and was later revived as a quarterly and then bimonthly magazine by the Saturday Evening Post Society, emphasising general interest, health and nostalgia for its classic art.
