Patrick Joseph Buchanan (born 2 November 1938) is an American paleoconservative author, political commentator, and politician. He was a senior adviser and speechwriter to Presidents Richard Nixon and Gerald Ford, and White House Communications Director under Ronald Reagan (1985–1987). Buchanan sought the Republican presidential nomination in 1992 and 1996, and ran as the Reform Party candidate in the 2000 presidential election.

Early life and White House years

Born in Washington, D.C., to a family of Irish and German descent, Buchanan graduated from Georgetown University in 1961 and earned a master's degree in journalism from Columbia University. He joined the St. Louis Globe-Democrat as an editorial writer, then became an early aide on Nixon's comeback campaign in 1966. He served as a speechwriter in the Nixon White House, helping craft the administration's media and "Silent Majority" strategy, and remained through the Watergate period. Under Reagan he served as communications director.

Presidential campaigns

In 1992 Buchanan challenged the incumbent George H. W. Bush in the Republican primaries on a platform of economic nationalism, immigration restriction, and social conservatism, winning 37% in New Hampshire and about 23% of the primary vote overall; his "culture war" speech opened the 1992 Republican National Convention. In 1996 he won the New Hampshire primary and several other contests before losing the nomination to Bob Dole. In 2000 he took over Ross Perot's Reform Party, fought a factional battle for its ballot line, and won 0.4% of the national vote.

Views and commentary career

Buchanan is a leading voice of paleoconservatism: protectionist on trade, opposed to most post-Cold War military interventions, and restrictive on immigration. He co-founded The American Conservative magazine in 2002 and was a long-running panellist on The McLaughlin Group and co-host of CNN's Crossfire. His books include The Death of the West (2002), State of Emergency (2006), and Churchill, Hitler, and the Unnecessary War (2008). His career has also drawn sustained criticism over statements on race, immigration, and the Holocaust, disputes that contributed to his departure from MSNBC in 2012.
