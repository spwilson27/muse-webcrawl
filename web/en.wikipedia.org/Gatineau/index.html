Gatineau

Gatineau is a city in western Quebec, Canada, situated on the north bank of the Ottawa River directly opposite Ottawa, Ontario. It is the fourth-largest city in Quebec, with a population of roughly 290,000 (2021), and together with Ottawa forms Canada's National Capital Region. The present city was created in 2002 by the amalgamation of the former cities of Gatineau, Hull, Aylmer, Buckingham, and Masson-Angers.

History

The oldest part of the city is Hull, founded in 1800 by Philemon Wright, an American settler who established a timber-trading community at the Chaudière Falls; the area became a centre of the Ottawa valley lumber industry and later of pulp and paper manufacturing. The original city of Gatineau grew rapidly after the Second World War as a suburban community. In the later 20th century many federal government offices were built in Hull (now the downtown core), including Place du Portage and the Terrasses de la Chaudière, making the public service the city's dominant employer.

Culture and geography

Gatineau is predominantly French-speaking. It is home to the Canadian Museum of History, one of Canada's most visited museums, and adjoins Gatineau Park, a large conservation park in the Gatineau Hills that is a popular destination for hiking, skiing, and autumn colours. The Casino du Lac-Leamy and the Université du Québec en Outaouais are also located in the city. Five interprovincial bridges connect Gatineau with Ottawa.
