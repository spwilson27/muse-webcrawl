Netafim

Netafim is an Israeli manufacturer of irrigation equipment, best known as a pioneer and world leader in drip irrigation technology. The company was founded in 1965 at Kibbutz Hatzerim in the Negev desert, in collaboration with engineer Simcha Blass, whose observations of a tree growing vigorously beside a leaking water pipe inspired the development of the modern dripper. In 2017 the Mexican chemical company Mexichem (now Orbia) acquired an 80 percent stake in Netafim, valuing it at about US$1.9 billion; Kibbutz Hatzerim retained the remainder.

History

Netafim's first commercial drippers, released in the late 1960s, allowed water and dissolved fertiliser to be delivered slowly and precisely to the roots of each plant, dramatically reducing water use compared with flood or sprinkler irrigation — a crucial advantage in arid Israel. The company expanded internationally through the 1970s and 1980s, opening subsidiaries in the United States, Australia, India and elsewhere, and grew to employ several thousand people with factories in numerous countries.

Products and impact

Netafim's product range includes drippers and driplines, sprinklers and micro-emitters, filters, valves, and digital farming tools such as sensors and automated irrigation controllers. Its technologies are used in more than 100 countries on millions of hectares of farmland, in applications from open-field row crops and orchards to greenhouses and landscape irrigation. Drip irrigation is credited with major gains in water productivity ("more crop per drop"), particularly in water-scarce regions, though critics note concerns about plastic waste from discarded driplines and the cost barriers for smallholder farmers.
