Newsweek is an American weekly news magazine and digital publication. Founded as a weekly print magazine in 1933, it was widely distributed during the 20th century and had many international editions. For decades it was the second-largest weekly newsmagazine in the United States, trailing only its rival Time, and was owned for half a century by the Washington Post Company.

History

Newsweek was founded by Thomas J. C. Martyn, a former foreign editor of Time, with backing from investors including Paul Mellon. The first issue appeared on 17 February 1933, under the name News-Week, costing ten cents. After the Second World War the magazine built a global network of bureaus and foreign editions in several languages.

Decline and revival

Like other print newsmagazines, Newsweek suffered declining advertising and circulation in the 2000s. The Washington Post Company sold it in 2010 for $1 (plus liabilities) to Sidney Harman, who merged it with The Daily Beast. The print edition ceased at the end of 2012, making it all-digital, but print publication resumed in 2014 under new ownership (IBT Media, later Newsweek Media Group). Since 2018 it has been published by Newsweek Inc. as a digital news organisation with a smaller print magazine, and its business was later the subject of litigation between its co-owners.
