Dirichlet problem

In mathematics, a Dirichlet problem is the problem of finding a function which solves a specified partial differential equation (PDE) in the interior of a given region and takes prescribed values on the boundary of the region. It is named after the German mathematician Peter Gustav Lejeune Dirichlet.

Formulation

For a domain D with boundary ∂D, the Dirichlet problem for Laplace's equation asks for a function u harmonic in D (that is, Δu = 0) and equal to a given continuous function f on the boundary. The corresponding boundary conditions are called Dirichlet boundary conditions.

History and solution methods

Dirichlet believed the problem always had a solution, arguing from a variational principle (later called Dirichlet's principle) that minimises an energy integral. Weierstrass showed the argument had a gap, and a rigorous justification was only supplied by Hilbert around 1900. Solution methods include separation of variables, Green's functions, Perron's method using subharmonic functions, and, for irregular boundary points, criteria due to Wiener. Not every boundary point is "regular": Lebesgue exhibited a domain and boundary data for which the classical solution cannot attain the prescribed value at a certain boundary point.

Applications

The Dirichlet problem models steady-state temperature distribution, electrostatic potential, and incompressible potential flow when the quantity of interest is fixed on the boundary of the region.
