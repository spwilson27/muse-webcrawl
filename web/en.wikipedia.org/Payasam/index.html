Payasam

Payasam is a South Indian sweet dish of rice, vermicelli, lentils, or other grains slowly cooked in milk and sweetened with sugar or jaggery, then flavoured with cardamom, saffron, cashews, and raisins fried in ghee. In Kerala it is an essential element of the sadya feast, where it is traditionally served on a banana leaf and eaten by hand.

Varieties

  • Pal payasam — rice cooked in milk and sugar
  • Parippu payasam — moong dal with jaggery and coconut milk
  • Ada pradhaman — rice flakes (ada), jaggery, and coconut milk
  • Semiya payasam — vermicelli in milk
  • Elaneer payasam — made with tender coconut

Cultural role

Payasam is offered as prasadam in temples and prepared for festivals such as Onam, Vishu, and Pongal (where a similar dish is made). The name derives from the Sanskrit payasa, meaning a milk dish; cognates include North Indian kheer and payesh in Bengal.
