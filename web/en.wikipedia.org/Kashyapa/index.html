Kashyapa

Kashyapa (Sanskrit: कश्यप, IAST: Kaśyapa) is a revered Vedic sage in Hinduism, counted among the Saptarishis, the seven great rishis of the present manvantara. He is described in the Ramayana, Mahabharata, and the Puranas as the father of many beings — gods, demons, nagas, and animals — and thus a progenitor of much of creation.

Family and progeny

Kashyapa was the son of Marichi, one of the mind-born sons of Brahma. He married thirteen daughters of Daksha, including Aditi and Diti. By Aditi he fathered the Adityas, among them Vamana, an avatar of Vishnu; by Diti, the Daityas; by Danu, the Danavas; and by other wives, the nagas, Garuda and Aruna, the Gandharvas, Apsaras, and various classes of animals and birds.

Works and legacy

Tradition attributes to Kashyapa hymns in the Rigveda and treatises on medicine, law, and astrology. The Kashyapa Samhita is a classical text on Ayurvedic pediatrics and gynecology. The name of Kashmir is sometimes traditionally derived from "Kashyapa-mir" (Kashyapa's lake). Kashyapa is also the eponymous ancestor of the Kashyapa gotra, one of the principal Brahmin lineages.
