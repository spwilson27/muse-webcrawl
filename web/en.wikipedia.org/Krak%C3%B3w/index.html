Kraków is the second-largest and one of the oldest cities in Poland, situated on the Vistula River in the Lesser Poland region. It was the official capital of Poland until 1596 and remains one of the country's leading centres of academic, cultural and artistic life.

History

Settlement at Wawel Hill dates to the Stone Age; legend attributes the city's founding to the mythical ruler Krakus. Kraków became the seat of Polish kings in 1038 and was rebuilt after Mongol destruction in the 13th century around one of Europe's largest medieval market squares. The Jagiellonian University, founded in 1364, is one of the oldest universities in the world; Nicolaus Copernicus studied there. After the capital moved to Warsaw, Kraków later fell under Austrian rule in the partitions of Poland.

Second World War and after

Under German occupation (1939–1945) Kraków was the seat of the General Government; its Jewish population, centred in Kazimierz and confined to a ghetto in Podgórze, was largely murdered in the Holocaust, including at the nearby Płaszów and Auschwitz camps. Oskar Schindlers factory in the city became famous through the film Schindler's List. Unusually for a Polish city, Kraków's historic centre survived the war largely intact and was among the first sites inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1978.

Today

Landmarks include Wawel Castle and Cathedral, St Mary's Basilica with its hourly hejnał bugle call, the Cloth Hall, and the Barbican. Kraków is one of Poland's most visited tourist destinations and a major technology and services hub.
