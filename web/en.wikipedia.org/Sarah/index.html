Sarah (Hebrew: שָׂרָה, Śārā; originally named Sarai) is a biblical matriarch, the wife of Abraham and the mother of Isaac. She appears in the Book of Genesis and is revered in Judaism, Christianity and Islam as one of the great women of the patriarchal narratives. Her name means "princess" or "noblewoman", reflecting the divine promise that she would be "a mother of nations".

Biblical narrative

Sarah, Abraham's half-sister, accompanies him from Ur and Haran to Canaan and Egypt. Twice, fearing for his life, Abraham presents her as his sister — to Pharaoh in Egypt and to King Abimelech of Gerar. Long barren, Sarah gives her Egyptian slave Hagar to Abraham, who bears Ishmael; the ensuing tension leads to Hagar's exile. At the age of ninety, after the divine promise and the renaming of Sarai to Sarah, she bears Isaac — whose name ("he laughs") recalls her laughter at the announcement. Genesis reports her death at 127 in Hebron, where Abraham buys the Cave of Machpelah as her burial place, the first recorded land purchase in the Bible.

Religious significance

In Judaism Sarah is the first of the four matriarchs and a prophetess. The New Testament cites her as an example of faith (Hebrews 11) and of the "free woman" in Paul's allegory in Galatians. In Islam, Sarah (Sāra) is honored as the wife of Ibrahim and mother of Ishaq, though she is not named in the Quran itself and is developed in later tradition.
