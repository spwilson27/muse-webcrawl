Aufbau principle

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Order of filling of atomic orbitals

The Aufbau principle (from the German Aufbauprinzip, "building-up principle"), also called the Aufbau rule, states that in the ground state of an atom or ion, electrons fill subshells of the lowest available energy, then they fill subshells of higher energy. For example, the 1s subshell is filled before the 2s subshell is occupied.

The n + l rule

The order of filling follows the Madelung (or Klechkowski) rule: subshells are filled in order of increasing n + l, where n is the principal quantum number and l the azimuthal quantum number; ties are resolved in favour of the smaller n. This gives the familiar sequence 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d, 4p, and so on.

Exceptions

Roughly twenty elements, notably chromium and copper among the transition metals, have ground-state configurations that deviate from the simple rule, because half-filled and fully filled subshells confer extra stability. The principle works together with Hund's rule and the Pauli exclusion principle to determine electron configurations.

History

The principle emerged from the work of Niels Bohr in the early 1920s on the structure of the periodic table, and was formulated empirically by Erwin Madelung in 1936.
