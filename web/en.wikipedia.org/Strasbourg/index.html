Strasbourg is the prefecture and largest city of the Grand Est region of eastern France, lying on the river Ill near its confluence with the Rhine, on the border with Germany. A city that has passed repeatedly between French and German rule, it is today a symbol of European reconciliation and one of the continent's institutional capitals, hosting the official seat of the European Parliament, the Council of Europe and the European Court of Human Rights.

History

Founded as the Roman camp of Argentoratum, Strasbourg became a free imperial city of the Holy Roman Empire and a centre of printing — Gutenberg developed his press nearby — and of the Protestant Reformation. Its Gothic cathedral, completed in the fifteenth century, was the world's tallest building until the nineteenth century. France annexed the city in 1681; Germany took it in 1871, France recovered it in 1918, Germany again in 1940, and France finally in 1944–45.

The city today

The historic centre, the Grande Île, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, famed for the pink sandstone cathedral with its astronomical clock and for the half-timbered Petite France quarter of tanners and millers. The Neustadt (German imperial district) was added to the listing in 2017. Strasbourg is a major university city, a Rhine port, and a crossroads of high-speed rail (TGV Est and German connections). Its Christmas market, the Christkindelsmärik, founded in 1570, is among Europe's oldest.
