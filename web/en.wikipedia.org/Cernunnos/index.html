Cernunnos

Cernunnos is a Celtic god attested in Iron Age iconography, typically depicted as a horned or antlered figure seated cross-legged, often surrounded by animals and holding or wearing torcs. He is sometimes described as a god of nature, fertility, animals and the wild.


Cernunnos on the Gundestrup cauldron

Attestations

The name Cernunnos is known principally from the Pillar of the Boatmen, a Roman-era monument erected in Paris (ancient Lutetia) by a guild of sailors under Tiberius, where the inscription [C]ernunnos accompanies an image of a horned head with torcs hanging from the antlers. The famous image of an antlered figure on the Gundestrup cauldron is often identified with him, although the cauldron carries no inscription.

Interpretation

Scholars have interpreted Cernunnos variously as a lord of animals, a god of commerce and prosperity (he is sometimes shown with bags of coins or cornucopiae), and a figure mediating between civilization and wilderness. In modern neopaganism, especially Wicca, he is revered as the Horned God, consort of the Goddess.
