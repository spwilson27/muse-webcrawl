Price discovery

Price discovery is the process of determining the price of an asset in the marketplace through the interactions of buyers and sellers. As traders submit bids and offers based on their information and expectations, transactions converge on prices that aggregate dispersed knowledge about supply, demand, and future value.

Mechanisms

Price discovery occurs through several market structures: continuous double auctions on stock and futures exchanges, periodic call auctions that batch orders to establish an opening or closing price, dealer markets in which market makers quote bid and ask prices, and negotiated or over-the-counter trading. The economist Friedrich Hayek famously argued that prices act as signals that communicate information across an economy, allowing participants to coordinate without knowing the underlying circumstances.

Factors affecting quality

The speed and accuracy with which prices reflect new information—closely related to the concept of market efficiency—depend on liquidity, transparency, transaction costs, and the number and diversity of participants. In fragmented markets, researchers measure which venue leads in price discovery using econometric methods such as information share. Poor price discovery, by contrast, can produce bubbles, mispricing, and misallocation of capital.
