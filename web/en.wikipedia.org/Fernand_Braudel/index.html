Fernand Braudel

Born: 24 August 1902, Luméville-en-Ornois, France
Died: 27 November 1985, Cluses, France
Known for: The Mediterranean and the Mediterranean World; the longue durée

Fernand Paul Achille Braudel (24 August 1902 – 27 November 1985) was a French historian and leader of the Annales School. His scholarship focused on the material life of peoples, the structures of everyday existence, and the slow rhythms of history rather than on the deeds of kings and statesmen.

Life and career

Braudel taught in Algeria and at the University of São Paulo in Brazil before returning to Paris. During the Second World War he was held as a prisoner of war in Germany, where he drafted from memory much of his monumental doctoral thesis. After the war he became a central figure at the École pratique des hautes études and later directed the Maison des Sciences de l'Homme. He was elected to the Académie française in 1984.

Work

His masterpiece, The Mediterranean and the Mediterranean World in the Age of Philip II (1949), is organized in three layers of historical time: the almost timeless history of the environment and geography (the longue durée); the medium-term history of economies, societies, and states (conjonctures); and the rapid history of events (événements), which he treated as surface foam on deep tides. His later three-volume Civilization and Capitalism, 15th–18th Century (1979) extended this approach to the economic history of the early modern world, distinguishing the layered strata of material life, market economy, and capitalism proper. Braudel's concepts have influenced world-systems theory, historical geography, and social history worldwide.
