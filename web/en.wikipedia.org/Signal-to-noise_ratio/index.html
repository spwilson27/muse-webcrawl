Signal-to-noise ratio

Signal-to-noise ratio (SNR or S/N) is a measure used in science and engineering that compares the level of a desired signal to the level of background noise. It is defined as the ratio of signal power to noise power, often expressed in decibels:

SNRdB = 10 log10 (Psignal / Pnoise) = 20 log10 (Asignal / Anoise)

for power and amplitude quantities respectively. A ratio higher than 1:1 (greater than 0 dB) indicates more signal than noise.

Applications

SNR is a key specification for audio equipment, radio receivers, imaging systems and measurement instruments. In digital communications, the ratio of energy per bit to noise power spectral density (Eb/N0) determines the bit error rate. In imaging, SNR relates to the Rose criterion, which holds that an SNR of at least about 5 is needed to distinguish image features with certainty. In analytical chemistry and spectroscopy, the limit of detection is commonly taken as the concentration at which SNR is about 3.

Improving SNR

Common techniques include averaging repeated measurements (which improves SNR in proportion to the square root of the number of samples), filtering to restrict bandwidth to the signal band, shielding and careful grounding in electronics, cooling detectors to reduce thermal noise, and oversampling in analogue-to-digital conversion. The fundamental limits on communication over a noisy channel are described by the Shannon–Hartley theorem.
