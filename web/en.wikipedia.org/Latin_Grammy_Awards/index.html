Latin Grammy Awards

The Latin Grammy trophy

The Latin Grammy Awards (stylized as Latin GRAMMYs) are awards presented by the Latin Recording Academy to recognize outstanding achievement in the Latin music industry. They honor works recorded in Spanish, Portuguese, or languages and dialects of Ibero-America, and are considered the Latin music counterpart of the Grammy Awards.

History

The awards were established in 1999, and the first ceremony was broadcast by CBS on 13 September 2000 from the Staples Center in Los Angeles — the first primarily Spanish-language awards show broadcast on an English-speaking American network. The Latin Recording Academy was founded in 1997 as a division of the Recording Academy.

Ceremony and categories

The annual ceremony includes categories across many genres, including General Field awards such as Album of the Year, Record of the Year, Song of the Year, and Best New Artist, alongside fields for pop, urban, regional Mexican, tropical, rock, alternative, Brazilian, and traditional music. Most ceremonies have been held in Las Vegas; in 2023 the show moved outside the United States for the first time, to Seville, Spain.
