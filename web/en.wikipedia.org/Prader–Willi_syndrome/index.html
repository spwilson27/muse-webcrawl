Prader–Willi syndrome

Prader–Willi syndrome (PWS) is a genetic disorder caused by the loss of function of specific genes on chromosome 15 inherited from the father, most commonly by deletion, or by inheriting two copies of chromosome 15 from the mother (uniparental disomy). It affects roughly 1 in 10,000 to 30,000 births and is the most common genetic cause of life-threatening childhood obesity.

Signs and symptoms

In infancy PWS causes severe hypotonia and feeding difficulties with poor growth. From early childhood a hypothalamic dysfunction produces an insatiable appetite (hyperphagia) and absence of normal satiety, leading to obesity if food access is uncontrolled. Other features include short stature, small hands and feet, characteristic facial features, intellectual disability, behavioural problems including temper outbursts and compulsive traits, and hypogonadism.

Management

There is no cure. Management centres on strict environmental control of food, growth hormone therapy (which improves stature, body composition and strength), special education, behavioural support and treatment of complications such as sleep apnoea and scoliosis. With weight control, life expectancy has improved markedly.
