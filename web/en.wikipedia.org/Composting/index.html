Composting is a process by which organic matter — such as food scraps, leaves, and manures — is decomposed by micro-organisms under controlled, predominantly aerobic conditions into a soil-like material called compost. Compost is used as a fertiliser and soil conditioner, improving soil structure, moisture retention and nutrient content, and it diverts organic waste from landfill, where its anaerobic decay would produce methane.

Process

Composting proceeds through phases driven by successive communities of bacteria and fungi. In the initial mesophilic phase, microbes break down readily degradable compounds and the pile heats up. In the thermophilic phase, temperatures of 45–70 °C destroy many pathogens and weed seeds. Finally, during curing, temperatures fall and humus-like compounds form.

Successful composting requires a suitable carbon-to-nitrogen ratio (often cited as roughly 30:1), adequate moisture (about 40–60%), oxygen, and sufficient volume. Nitrogen-rich "greens" include food scraps, grass clippings and manure; carbon-rich "browns" include dry leaves, straw and wood chips.

Methods

Home composting

Garden compost bins and heaps are the commonest small-scale method. Turning the pile reintroduces oxygen and speeds decomposition. Vermicomposting uses worms, typically red wigglers (Eisenia fetida), to process food scraps in bins; the Japanese bokashi method instead ferments waste with inoculated bran before burial.

Industrial composting

Municipal facilities use windrows, aerated static piles, or in-vessel systems to process green waste and food waste at scale, with controlled aeration and temperature monitoring. Resulting compost is sold or given away for agriculture, landscaping and land restoration.

Uses and benefits

Compost improves soil fertility and structure, supports soil biodiversity, and can suppress some plant diseases. Composting also reduces the volume of organic waste sent to landfill and the associated methane emissions, though poorly managed anaerobic piles can themselves emit methane and nitrous oxide.
