Islamic marriage

The nikah contract formalises marriage under Islamic law.

In Islam, marriage (nikah) is a legal contract between a man and a woman intended to establish mutual consent to live together as spouses. Both the bridegroom and the bride must consent to the marriage of their own free will, verbally and in writing, and the contract is formalised before witnesses.

Requirements

Classical jurisprudence requires the presence of the bride's guardian (wali) — commonly her father — an offer and acceptance in a single sitting, two adult male witnesses (or equivalent arrangements in some schools), and the payment of a dower (mahr) by the husband to the wife, which becomes her property. The contract may stipulate conditions agreed by both parties.

Rights and duties

Islamic law assigns spouses reciprocal rights: maintenance by the husband according to his means, mahr and inheritance rights for the wife, and obligations of kindness and fidelity for both. The Quran describes marriage as a source of tranquillity, affection and mercy between spouses.

Prohibited relationships

Marriage is prohibited between close blood relatives, certain in-laws, and milk-kin (those related through wet-nursing). Rules for marriage with non-Muslims differ by school and by whether the Muslim party is the husband or the wife.
