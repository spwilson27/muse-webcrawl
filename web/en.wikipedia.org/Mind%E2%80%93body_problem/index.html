Mind–body problem

The mind–body problem is a long-standing debate in the philosophy of mind concerning the relationship between thought and consciousness in the human mind and the brain as part of the physical body. The problem asks how mental states, events, and properties can be related to physical states, events, and properties, and whether the mind and the body are distinct substances or aspects of one substance.

Dualism

René Descartes famously defended substance dualism, holding that mind (res cogitans) and body (res extensa) are fundamentally different substances, interacting in the pineal gland — a proposal widely criticised for failing to explain how interaction is possible. Later dualist forms include property dualism and epiphenomenalism.

Monism

Monist responses deny two separate substances. Physicalism identifies mental states with brain states; behaviourism analyses mental talk as talk about behaviour and dispositions; functionalism defines mental states by their causal roles. Idealism, by contrast, treats matter as dependent on mind.

Modern debate

Contemporary discussion focuses on consciousness and the "hard problem" articulated by David Chalmers: why physical processing is accompanied by subjective experience at all. Related notions include qualia, supervenience, and the explanatory gap.
