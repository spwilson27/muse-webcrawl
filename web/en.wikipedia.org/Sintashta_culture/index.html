The Sintashta culture (c. 2200–1900 BC) was a Bronze Age archaeological culture of the southern Ural steppe, in present-day Russia and northern Kazakhstan. It is widely associated with early Indo-Iranian-speaking peoples and is famous for its fortified settlements and the earliest known spoke-wheeled war chariots.

Settlements

More than twenty fortified settlements of the culture are known, arranged in a region sometimes called the "Country of Towns". Settlements such as Sintashta and Arkaim were planned, roughly circular or oval, enclosed by wooden palisades, earthen ramparts, and ditches, with houses arranged in concentric rings around a central plaza. Every house appears to have had facilities for copper smelting and bronze working.

Burials and chariots

Sintashta cemeteries contained elaborate burials under kurgans, with sacrificed horses and other animals. Several graves held the impressions of light, two-wheeled chariots drawn by paired horses—the earliest secure evidence for true war chariots anywhere in the world, dating to about 2000 BC.

Economy and origins

The economy combined stockbreeding (cattle, sheep, goats, and horses) with intensive copper metallurgy based on local ores. Genetic studies indicate the Sintashta population derived largely from Corded Ware-related steppe groups that migrated eastward, with admixture from local Uralic forager groups. The culture developed into the broader Andronovo horizon.
