Lead (horse)

The lead, in equine locomotion, is the leg that reaches furthest forward during the canter and gallop gaits. Horses naturally lead with one foreleg or the other, and the lead on which a horse travels has significant effects on its balance, turning ability, and soundness. The term also refers to the front legs being extended furthest during the stride; because the hind legs follow the same pattern, the lead is visible from both ends of the animal.

Correct and incorrect leads

A horse is said to be on the "correct lead" when, while travelling in a circle or turning, the inside foreleg is the leading leg. When the outside leg leads instead, the horse is "on the wrong lead" or counter-cantering. A rider can cue the horse to pick up a specific lead when departing into the canter, and trained horses perform flying lead changes, switching the lead mid-stride without breaking gait, a movement essential in dressage, show jumping and Western disciplines.

Cross-cantering

If a horse leads with one foreleg but the opposite hind leg, it is said to be cross-cantering or disunited. This is an unbalanced and uncomfortable gait that most horses will correct on their own; persistent disunited canter may indicate pain, weakness or training problems.

In racing

Racehorses typically switch leads in the stretch to combat fatigue, and in North America horses run on the left lead around turns and the right lead on the straightaways.
