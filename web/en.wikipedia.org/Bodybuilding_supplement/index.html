Bodybuilding supplement

Bodybuilding supplements are dietary supplements commonly used by those involved in bodybuilding, weightlifting, mixed martial arts, and athletics for the purpose of facilitating an increase in lean body mass. Bodybuilding supplements may be used to replace meals, enhance weight gain, promote weight loss, or improve athletic performance. Among the most widely used are high-protein drinks, pre-workout mixtures, branched-chain amino acids, glutamine, arginine, essential fatty acids, creatine, HMB, whey protein, beta-alanine, and fat burners.

History

Athletes in ancient Greece were advised to consume large quantities of meat and wine. Various herbal concoctions were used by strongmen through the nineteenth century. Modern supplement marketing expanded from the 1950s with the growth of competitive bodybuilding, as figures such as Joe Weider sold protein powders and weight-gain products.

Common types

Protein supplements, especially whey protein, are used to support muscle protein synthesis alongside resistance training. Creatine monohydrate is among the most studied ergogenic aids and is consistently shown to increase strength and lean mass when combined with resistance training. Pre-workout products typically combine caffeine, beta-alanine, and other stimulants or vasodilators.

Regulation and controversy

In the United States, dietary supplements are regulated under the Dietary Supplement Health and Education Act of 1994 and do not require pre-market proof of safety or efficacy. Some products have been found contaminated with undeclared stimulants or anabolic agents, leading to sanctions and health warnings from regulators such as the FDA.
