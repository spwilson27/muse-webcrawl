São Miguel Island

São Miguel Island (Portuguese for Saint Michael) is the largest and most populous island in the Portuguese archipelago of the Azores in the North Atlantic Ocean. Nicknamed "the Green Island" (Ilha Verde) for its lush pastures, it covers about 760 km² (293 sq mi) and has roughly 140,000 inhabitants, nearly half of the Azores' population. Its capital and largest city is Ponta Delgada.

The twin lakes of the Sete Cidades caldera, São Miguel.

Geography

São Miguel is about 90 km (56 mi) long and 8–15 km wide. It was formed by the joining of several volcanic massifs. The island is famous for its caldera lakes — the twin lakes of Sete Cidades in the west, Lagoa do Fogo in the centre, and the Furnas valley in the east, where geothermal springs and calderas (caldeiras) are used to cook the traditional cozido stew underground.

History

The island was discovered by Portuguese navigators in the 1420s–1430s and settled from 1439 under Gonçalo Velho Cabral. It developed through wheat, woad, orange and later pineapple and tea production. The Gorreana tea plantation, founded in 1883, is among the oldest operating tea factories in Europe. The island suffered major earthquakes and eruptions, including the 1563 eruption and the 1630 Furnas eruption.

Economy

Agriculture, dairy cattle ranching and tourism dominate the economy. São Miguel is the centre of Azorean dairy production, and whale watching, hiking and hot-spring bathing draw visitors year-round. João Paulo II Airport at Ponta Delgada is the Azores' main international gateway.
