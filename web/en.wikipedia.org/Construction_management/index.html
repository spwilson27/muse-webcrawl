Construction management

Construction management (CM) is a professional service that uses specialised project management techniques and software to oversee the planning, design, construction, and closeout of a project. The construction manager is responsible to the owner for delivering the project on time, within budget, and to the required quality.

Functions

Typical functions include specifying project objectives, preparing budgets and schedules, selecting contractors, administering contracts, managing cost and risk, coordinating communication among stakeholders, and monitoring progress and safety on site. Major responsibilities span the whole project life cycle: feasibility, pre-construction, procurement, construction, and handover.

Delivery models

In construction management at risk (CMAR), the manager also acts as contractor and guarantees a maximum price. In agency construction management, the manager acts purely as the owner's adviser while the owner holds the trade contracts directly. CM is often combined with design–build or integrated project delivery.

Software and standards

Modern construction management relies on building information modelling (BIM), critical path scheduling, earned value management, and common-data-environment platforms for document control. Professional bodies such as the Construction Management Association of America (CMAA) publish standards of practice and certification.
