Chalcedony

Chalcedony is a cryptocrystalline form of silica, composed of very fine intergrowths of quartz and moganite. It has a waxy lustre and may be semitransparent or translucent, occurring in a wide range of colours.

Varieties

Well-known varieties include agate (banded), onyx (parallel bands of black and white), carnelian (reddish-orange), chrysoprase (apple-green, coloured by nickel), bloodstone (dark green with red spots), and jasper (opaque).

History and use

Named after the ancient Greek town of Chalcedon in Asia Minor, chalcedony has been used since antiquity for seals, cameos, intaglios, beads, and tools. It remains a popular gemstone and ornamental material.
