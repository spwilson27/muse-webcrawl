Armero

Armero
Country: Colombia
Department: Tolima
Destroyed: 13 November 1985

Armero was a town in the department of Tolima, Colombia. On 13 November 1985 it was destroyed by lahars (volcanic mudflows) generated by the eruption of the Nevado del Ruiz volcano, in an event known as the Armero tragedy. Around 20,000 of the town's 29,000 inhabitants were killed, making it one of the deadliest volcanic disasters in recorded history.

Background

Armero was an important agricultural and cotton-processing centre, sometimes called the "white city" for its cotton production. It had previously been struck by lahars in 1595 and 1845, yet was rebuilt on the same debris deposits.

The disaster

The eruption melted snow and ice on the volcano's summit; the resulting lahars raced down river valleys at high speed and buried the town under metres of mud and debris late at night. Rescue was hampered by the mud's consistency. Thirteen-year-old Omayra Sánchez, trapped in debris for three days, became an enduring symbol of the tragedy through widely published photographs.

Aftermath

The site was declared a memorial and holy field (campo santo); survivors were resettled in nearby towns such as Lérida and the new settlement of Armero-Guayabal, which now administers the site. The disaster spurred major reforms in volcanic hazard monitoring in Colombia and worldwide.
