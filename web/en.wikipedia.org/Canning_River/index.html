Canning River

The Canning River is the name of several rivers, notably in Alaska and Western Australia.

Alaska

The Canning River of northern Alaska flows about 125 miles (200 km) north from the Philip Smith Mountains of the Brooks Range, across the Arctic National Wildlife Refuge, to Camden Bay on the Beaufort Sea. It forms part of the western boundary of the wildlife refuge's coastal plain and was named by the Franklin expedition in the 1820s for the British statesman George Canning.

Western Australia

The Canning River in Western Australia rises in the Darling Range and flows through Perth's southern suburbs to join the Swan River at Melville Water. It is dammed at the Canning Dam, a major water source for Perth, and its name honours George Canning as well.
