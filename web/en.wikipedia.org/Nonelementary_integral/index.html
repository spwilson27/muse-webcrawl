Nonelementary integral

In mathematics, a nonelementary antiderivative is an antiderivative of a given elementary function that is not itself an elementary function. A nonelementary integral is an integral whose value cannot be expressed in terms of elementary functions.

Examples and theory

Familiar examples include ∫e−x² dx (which defines the error function), ∫(sin x)/x dx (the sine integral), ∫dx/ln x (the logarithmic integral) and the integrals defining the elliptic integrals. Joseph Liouville proved in the 1830s that such antiderivatives cannot in general be elementary, founding the theory later developed into the Risch algorithm, which can decide whether an elementary function has an elementary antiderivative. Nonelementary integrals are handled by defining new special functions, by series expansions, or by numerical integration.
