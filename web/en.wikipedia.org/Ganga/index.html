The Ganga, usually called the Ganges in English, is a major transboundary river of Asia that flows through northern India and Bangladesh. Rising in the western Himalayas in the Indian state of Uttarakhand, it travels generally southeast across the Gangetic Plain before dividing into distributaries in the Ganges Delta and reaching the Bay of Bengal. It is sacred in Hinduism and supports one of the largest river-basin populations in the world.

Course

The Bhagirathi and Alaknanda meet at Devprayag to form the main stem of the Ganga. From the mountains the river passes Haridwar, Kanpur, Prayagraj, Varanasi, Patna and Kolkata. Major tributaries include the Yamuna, Gomti, Ghaghara, Gandak, Kosi and Son. In Bangladesh the main distributary continues as the Padma and joins the Brahmaputra and Meghna before entering the sea.

Religious significance

Hindus revere the river as the goddess Ganga. Ritual bathing is believed to cleanse sins, and cities such as Varanasi, Prayagraj and Haridwar host major festivals including Kumbh Mela. Ashes of the dead are often immersed in its waters.

Ecology and pollution

The basin contains fertile alluvial soils, wetlands, the Ganges river dolphin and many fish species. Dense settlement, industrial discharge, sewage and agricultural runoff have made pollution control a long-running public-policy issue; programmes such as Namami Gange have sought to improve water quality and riverfront management.
