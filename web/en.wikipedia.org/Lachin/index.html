Lachin (Azerbaijani: Laçın; Armenian: Բերձոր, romanized: Berdzor) is a town in Azerbaijan and the administrative centre of the Lachin District. It is located in a mountainous area on the road between Khankendi (Stepanakert) and Armenia, a corridor of strategic importance in the Nagorno-Karabakh conflict.

History

The town was known as Abdallyar in earlier times and was renamed Lachin in 1923. During the First Nagorno-Karabakh War, the town and the surrounding district were captured by Armenian forces in May 1992, giving the unrecognized Republic of Artsakh a land connection to Armenia; the Azerbaijani population fled or was expelled. Under the terms of the 2020 Nagorno-Karabakh ceasefire agreement, the Lachin District was returned to Azerbaijan, though the town itself and the Lachin corridor initially remained under the control of Russian peacekeepers.

Lachin corridor

The Lachin corridor, a roughly 5 km-wide mountain road passing through the town, was the only route connecting Nagorno-Karabakh with Armenia. From December 2022 the corridor was blockaded, and in August 2022 control of the town itself passed to Azerbaijan after a new southern route bypassing Lachin was opened. Following Azerbaijan's offensive of September 2023, the Armenian population of the region departed.
