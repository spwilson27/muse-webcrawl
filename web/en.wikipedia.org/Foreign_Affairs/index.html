Foreign Affairs

Foreign Affairs is an American magazine of international relations and U.S. foreign policy published by the Council on Foreign Relations (CFR), a nonprofit, nonpartisan, membership organization and think tank specializing in U.S. foreign policy and international affairs. It is published every two months, with six issues per year, and has been published continuously since 1922.

History

The Council on Foreign Relations was founded in 1921, and the following year it began publishing Foreign Affairs as its flagship publication. The journal's first editor was Archibald Cary Coolidge, a Harvard University professor of history. Coolidge intended the journal to cover not only the United States but the world, and he built a roster of contributors that included leading scholars, statesmen, and journalists.

Over the decades the magazine published landmark essays that shaped American foreign policy debates, including George F. Kennan's "The Sources of Soviet Conduct" (1947), published under the pseudonym "X," which laid out the doctrine of containment; Samuel P. Huntington's "The Clash of Civilizations?" (1993); and Francis Fukuyama's "The End of History?" (1989).

Content and influence

Foreign Affairs publishes essays by academics, government officials, and journalists on international politics, economics, and global issues. Because many of its contributors are prominent figures in the foreign policy establishment, the journal is often read as a bellwether of establishment thinking in Washington. Annual themed issues and a website carrying daily analysis supplement the print edition.

Editors

Notable editors have included Hamilton Fish Armstrong, who led the magazine for nearly five decades (1928–1972), and Gideon Rose, who became editor in 2010 after serving as managing editor under James F. Hoge Jr.
