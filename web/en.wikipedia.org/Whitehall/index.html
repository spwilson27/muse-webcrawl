Whitehall

Whitehall is a street in the City of Westminster, London, running from Trafalgar Square toward Parliament Square. It is lined with United Kingdom government departments and ministries, and its name is often used as a metonym for the British civil service and central government.

History

The street takes its name from the Palace of Whitehall, once a principal royal residence, largely destroyed by fire in 1698. The Banqueting House survives. In later centuries the area filled with offices for the Admiralty, War Office, Foreign Office, Treasury and other departments.

Landmarks

Whitehall includes the Cenotaph, Horse Guards, the entrance to Downing Street and many memorials. It remains both a working government district and a ceremonial route.
