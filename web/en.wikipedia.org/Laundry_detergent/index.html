Laundry detergent

Use: Laundering fabrics
Forms: Powder, liquid, pods
Key ingredients: Surfactants, builders, enzymes

Laundry detergent is a cleaning agent formulated for washing fabrics. It is sold as powders, liquids, and single-dose pods or tablets, and contains surfactants, builders, enzymes, and other additives that lift soil from cloth and keep it suspended in wash water.

Composition

Surfactants — typically anionic types such as alkylbenzene sulfonates — do the main cleaning. Builders such as zeolites or citrates soften water; enzymes (proteases, amylases, lipases, cellulases) break down protein, starch, and fat stains; oxygen bleaches and optical brighteners whiten fabrics. Phosphate builders, once common, were largely phased out over eutrophication concerns.

History

The first fully synthetic detergent, Germany's Fewa (1932), was followed by Dreft (1933) and Procter & Gamble's Tide (1946), the first heavy-duty synthetic laundry detergent. Liquid detergents grew from the 1970s, concentrated formulas and pre-measured pods (Tide Pods, 2012) later.
