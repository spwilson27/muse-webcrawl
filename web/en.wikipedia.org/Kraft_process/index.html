Kraft process

The kraft process (also known as kraft pulping or the sulfate process) is a process for converting wood into wood pulp, consisting of nearly pure cellulose fibers, the main component of paper.

Process

Wood chips are treated with a hot mixture of water, sodium hydroxide and sodium sulfide, known as white liquor, which breaks the bonds between lignin, hemicellulose and cellulose. The spent cooking liquor, black liquor, contains dissolved lignin and chemicals; it is concentrated and burned in a recovery boiler to regenerate the cooking chemicals and produce energy.

Properties and byproducts

Kraft pulp is stronger than pulp made by other processes — kraft is German and Swedish for "strength" — and the process can handle many wood species and resinous woods. Byproducts include tall oil, turpentine and lignin. Kraft paper and linerboard are major products.
