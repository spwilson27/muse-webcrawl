Guillaume de Machaut

Machaut supervised lavish collected editions of his own works, unusual for the Middle Ages.

Guillaume de Machaut (c. 1300 – April 1377) was a French composer and poet of the late medieval era, the leading figure of the ars nova style and the most famous composer of the 14th century. His output — sacred and secular, monophonic and polyphonic — survives in greater quantity than that of any earlier composer.

Life

Machaut was ordained and served as secretary to John I, Count of Luxembourg and King of Bohemia, travelling widely in his retinue. After John's death at Crécy in 1346 he was patronised by John's daughter Bonne of Luxembourg, Charles II of Navarre, and the future Charles V of France. He spent his later years as a canon of Reims Cathedral, surviving the Black Death and the siege of Reims.

Works

His Messe de Nostre Dame is the earliest known complete setting of the Mass Ordinary by a single composer. He wrote hundreds of poems and set many to music in the formes fixes — ballades, rondeaux and virelais — as well as motets and the narrative poem Le Voir Dit, based on his late correspondence with the young Péronne d'Armentières.
