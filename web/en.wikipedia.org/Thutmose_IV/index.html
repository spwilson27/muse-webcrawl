Thutmose IV

Thutmose IV (sometimes read as Thutmosis or Tuthmosis IV; died c. 1391 BC) was the eighth pharaoh of the Eighteenth Dynasty of ancient Egypt. He was the son of Amenhotep II and Tiaa. His reign is usually dated to about 1401–1391 BC, or 1397–1388 BC in the low chronology.

The Dream Stela

Thutmose IV is best known for the Dream Stela, erected between the paws of the Great Sphinx of Giza. According to its inscription, the young prince, resting in the shadow of the Sphinx during a hunting expedition, fell asleep and dreamed that the Sphinx — identified with the god Hor-em-akhet — promised him the kingship if he cleared away the sand that had engulfed its body. The stela suggests Thutmose may not have been the expected heir, and the restoration of the Sphinx was among the first recorded antiquarian projects of the New Kingdom.

Reign

Militarily, Thutmose IV campaigned in Nubia and Syria, but his reign is more notable for diplomacy: he concluded a peace with the kingdom of Mitanni, Egypt's former rival, sealing the alliance by marrying a Mitannian princess, Mutemwiya. She became the mother of his successor, Amenhotep III. Building works include additions at Karnak, where he erected a large alabaster barque shrine and repositioned an obelisk begun under Thutmose III — the "Lateran obelisk", now in Rome, the tallest standing ancient Egyptian obelisk.

Burial

Thutmose IV was buried in tomb KV43 in the Valley of the Kings, discovered in 1903 by Howard Carter. His mummy, later moved to the royal cache at Deir el-Bahari, shows he died relatively young, probably in his thirties.
