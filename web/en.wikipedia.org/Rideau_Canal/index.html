Rideau Canal

The Rideau Canal is a 202-kilometre (126 mi) canal in Ontario, Canada, linking Ottawa on the Ottawa River to Kingston on Lake Ontario via the Rideau and Cataraqui rivers. Opened in 1832, it is the oldest continuously operated canal system in North America.

History

The canal was built after the War of 1812 as a secure military supply route between Montreal and Kingston that avoided the St. Lawrence River's exposure to American attack. Under Lieutenant-Colonel John By of the Royal Engineers, construction (1826–1832) employed thousands of French Canadian and Irish labourers, many of whom died of malaria and accidents. The settlement that grew at its Ottawa terminus, Bytown, became the city of Ottawa.

The waterway

The canal includes 47 locks at 29 lockstations and uses slackwater sections created by dams across the rivers. It never served a major military purpose and soon lost commercial traffic to railways, but it became a celebrated recreational waterway.

Recognition

The Rideau Canal was designated a National Historic Site of Canada and, in 2007, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. In winter, a section through central Ottawa becomes the Rideau Canal Skateway, one of the world's largest skating rinks.
