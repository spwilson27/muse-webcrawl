Henry Draper Catalogue

The Henry Draper Catalogue (HD) is an astronomical star catalogue published between 1918 and 1924, giving spectroscopic classifications for 225,300 stars. It was compiled by Annie Jump Cannon and her colleagues at Harvard College Observatory under the direction of Edward Charles Pickering, and funded in memory of the physician and amateur astronomer Henry Draper by his widow, Anna Mary Draper.

Contents and classification

Each entry gives a star's position, photographic and visual magnitudes, and a spectral type in the Harvard classification system (O, B, A, F, G, K, M) refined by Cannon, who classified the vast majority of the stars herself at a rate of hundreds per day. An extension, the Henry Draper Extension, added some 133,782 stars in volumes published through 1949, bringing the total to more than 359,000 stars.

Legacy

The catalogue's HD numbers remain in wide use for stars lacking Bayer or Flamsteed designations, and the spectral classification scheme developed for it is still the basis of stellar classification today. The project was also a landmark in the history of women in science: the "Harvard Computers," including Cannon, Williamina Fleming, and Antonia Maury, performed the analysis.
