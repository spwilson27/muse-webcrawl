Inheritance

Inheritance is the practice of receiving private property, titles, debts, entitlements, privileges, rights, and obligations upon the death of an individual. The rules of inheritance (succession law) differ between societies and have changed over time. The transfer may be governed by the deceased's will (testate succession) or, in the absence of a valid will, by intestacy laws.

Legal framework

In most legal systems, a deceased person's estate is administered through a process such as probate, in which debts and taxes are settled before assets are distributed to heirs and beneficiaries. Many jurisdictions impose inheritance or estate taxes, though thresholds and rates vary widely, and several countries have abolished such taxes entirely.

Customs and systems

Historically, inheritance systems have included primogeniture (the eldest son — or eldest child — inherits the whole estate), which concentrated landholdings in aristocratic families; partible inheritance, in which property is divided among children; and ultimogeniture (inheritance by the youngest). Islamic law prescribes detailed fixed shares for relatives. In many societies, inheritance customs regulated the transmission not only of wealth but of occupation, status, and family names.

Social and economic significance

Inherited wealth is a major subject of debate in discussions of economic inequality. Economists such as Thomas Piketty have argued that as the rate of return on capital exceeds growth, inherited wealth becomes increasingly important relative to earned income, reviving interest in inheritance taxation as a policy lever.
