Horizontal coordinate system

Horizontal coordinate systemCoordinates: altitude (height) and azimuth
Reference: observer's local horizon
Also called: altazimuth system

The horizontal coordinate system is a celestial coordinate system that uses the observer's local horizon as the fundamental plane. The position of an object in the sky is given by two angles: its altitude (height above the horizon) and its azimuth (compass direction along the horizon).

Definitions

Altitude ranges from 0° at the horizon to +90° at the zenith directly overhead (and negative values below the horizon); its complement is the zenith distance. Azimuth is measured along the horizon, conventionally from north (0°) eastward through east (90°), south (180°) and west (270°), though some traditions measure from the south. The great circle passing through the zenith and the object is its vertical circle.

Use and conversion

Because the system is tied to the observer's location and the rotating Earth, an object's horizontal coordinates change continuously with time. Converting to the equatorial coordinate system requires knowledge of the observer's latitude and the local sidereal time. Altazimuth telescope mounts, however, naturally track in this system, and computerised mounts perform the conversion automatically.
