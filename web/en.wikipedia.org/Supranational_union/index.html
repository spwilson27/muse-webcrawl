Supranational union

A supranational union is a type of international organisation in which member states delegate sovereignty to shared institutions whose decisions can bind members, sometimes against a minority's preference. It sits between an intergovernmental organisation and a federation.

Examples

The European Union is the principal modern example: the European Commission proposes law, the Court of Justice rulings have direct effect, and the European Central Bank sets monetary policy for the eurozone. The term "supranational" first appeared in the 1951 Treaty of Paris establishing the Coal and Steel Community, a cornerstone of European integration.
