Edward IV

Reign: 4 March 1461 – 3 October 1470; 11 April 1471 – 9 April 1483

Born: 28 April 1442, Rouen, Normandy

Died: 9 April 1483, Westminster

Spouse: Elizabeth Woodville

House: York

Edward IV (28 April 1442 – 9 April 1483) was King of England from 4 March 1461 to 3 October 1470, and again from 11 April 1471 until his death in 1483. He was a central figure in the Wars of the Roses, a series of civil wars fought over control of the English throne, and the first Yorkist king.

Road to the throne

Edward was the eldest surviving son of Richard, Duke of York, and Cecily Neville. After his father's death at the Battle of Wakefield in 1460, Edward inherited the Yorkist claim to the throne. With the support of Richard Neville, Earl of Warwick — "the Kingmaker" — he defeated the Lancastrians at the Battle of Towton in 1461, the largest and bloodiest battle fought on English soil, and was crowned king.

First reign and deposition

Edward's secret marriage in 1464 to Elizabeth Woodville, a Lancastrian widow, alienated Warwick, who had been negotiating a French marriage. The Woodvilles' advancement at court drove Warwick into alliance with Edward's brother George, Duke of Clarence, and ultimately with the Lancastrians. In 1470 Warwick restored Henry VI to the throne, and Edward fled to Burgundy.

Second reign

Edward returned in 1471, defeated and killed Warwick at the Battle of Barnet, and crushed the remaining Lancastrians at Tewkesbury, where Henry VI's son Edward of Westminster was killed; Henry himself died in the Tower shortly afterwards. Edward's remaining reign was comparatively peaceful and financially successful: he made the crown solvent, encouraged trade, and concluded the Treaty of Picquigny with France in 1475. He died unexpectedly in 1483, aged forty, and was succeeded briefly by his young son Edward V, one of the Princes in the Tower.
