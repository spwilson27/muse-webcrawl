An Allegory with Venus and Cupid

ArtistBronzino (Agnolo di Cosimo)
Yearc. 1545
MediumOil on wood
LocationNational Gallery, London

An Allegory with Venus and Cupid is a painting by the Florentine Mannerist artist Agnolo Bronzino, dating to about 1545. It is an allegory of love, crowded with symbolic figures that have generated centuries of interpretation.

Description

Venus, holding the golden apple she won in the Judgement of Paris, is kissed by Cupid, who fondles her breast while a putto scatters rose petals. Around them appear personifications including Time (the bearded old man lifting a curtain), Oblivion, Jealousy or Syphilis (a tormented figure tearing her hair, with a woman's face and serpent's body), Fraud, and Folly.

History and interpretation

The painting was probably commissioned by Cosimo I de' Medici as a gift to King Francis I of France. Scholars read it as a moralising allegory on the dangers of syphilis, the vanity of earthly love, or the exposure of pleasure's deceits by Time and Truth. It is considered a masterpiece of Mannerist painting for its cold, enamel-like colours and complex, sensuous figures.
