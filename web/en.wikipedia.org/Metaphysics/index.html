Metaphysics is the branch of philosophy that investigates the fundamental nature of reality, including the relationship between mind and matter, substance and attribute, and potentiality and actuality. Traditionally, it asks what exists and what things are like at the most general level: questions of being (ontology), identity and change, space and time, causation, necessity and possibility, and the existence of God.

Name and origins

The name derives from the Greek ta meta ta physika — "the [books] after the Physics" — the title given by an editor (traditionally Andronicus of Rhodes, 1st century BCE) to Aristotle's treatise on "first philosophy", the study of "being qua being". Pre-Socratic thinkers such as Parmenides and Heraclitus had already debated whether reality is one and unchanging or many and in flux; Plato's theory of Forms and Aristotle's doctrines of substance, form and matter, and the four causes set the agenda for two millennia.

Central topics

  • Ontology — what exists: particulars and universals, objects and properties, abstract entities such as numbers.
  • Identity and change — how things persist through time (the Ship of Theseus; personal identity).
  • Time and space — whether they are substances, relations, or forms of intuition; presentism versus eternalism.
  • Causation and laws of nature — what it is for one event to cause another.
  • Modality — necessity, possibility, and possible worlds.
  • Mind and body — dualism, materialism, and the nature of consciousness.
  • Free will — its compatibility with determinism.

Critiques and revival

Metaphysics was challenged by Hume's empiricism and Kant's critical philosophy, which argued that speculative claims beyond possible experience are illegitimate; Kant replaced traditional metaphysics with an account of the structures of experience itself. In the twentieth century the logical positivists (e.g., Carnap) dismissed metaphysical statements as meaningless, but from the 1950s onward analytic philosophy saw a major revival of metaphysics in the work of Quine, Strawson, Kripke, and David Lewis. In continental philosophy, Heidegger recast the question of being, criticising the Western tradition for "forgetfulness of being".
