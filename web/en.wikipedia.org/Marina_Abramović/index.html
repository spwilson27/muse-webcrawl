Marina Abramović

Marina Abramović
Born: 30 November 1946, Belgrade, Yugoslavia (now Serbia)
Field: Performance art
Notable works: Rhythm series (1973–74), Balkan Baroque (1997), The Artist Is Present (2010)

Marina Abramović (born 30 November 1946) is a Serbian conceptual and performance artist. Her work explores body art, endurance art, the relationship between performer and audience, the limits of the body, and the possibilities of the mind. Active for more than five decades, she has been described as the "grandmother of performance art."

Early work

Born in Belgrade to Partisan parents in Tito's Yugoslavia, Abramović studied painting before turning to performance. In her Rhythm series of the early 1970s she subjected her body to pain, exhaustion and danger; in Rhythm 0 (1974) she stood passively for six hours beside 72 objects — from a rose to a loaded gun — inviting the audience to use them on her as they wished.

Collaboration with Ulay

From 1976 to 1988 she collaborated with the German artist Ulay, her partner in art and life; their works explored duality and endurance, culminating in The Lovers (1988), in which each walked from opposite ends of the Great Wall of China to meet and part in the middle.

Later career

Her Balkan Baroque (1997), a meditation on the Yugoslav wars, won the Golden Lion at the Venice Biennale. In 2010 her retrospective at New York's Museum of Modern Art included The Artist Is Present, in which she sat silently across from visitors for a total of 736 hours. She founded the Marina Abramović Institute to promote long-durational performance, and in 2023 became the first woman artist to receive a major solo retrospective at London's Royal Academy.
