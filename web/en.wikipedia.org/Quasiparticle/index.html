Quasiparticle

A quasiparticle is a concept used in condensed matter physics to describe the collective behaviour of a group of particles as if it were a single particle. Quasiparticles capture emergent excitations of a many-body system — for example, a "hole" left by a missing electron in a semiconductor, or a phonon, the quantized vibration of a crystal lattice.

Concept

Landau's Fermi liquid theory showed that interacting electrons in a metal can be described as weakly interacting quasiparticles with renormalized mass and charge. Other quasiparticles include magnons (spin waves), excitons (bound electron–hole pairs), polarons, plasmons and Cooper pairs; some, like Majorana zero modes, are sought for fault-tolerant quantum computing.

Quasiparticles vs elementary particles

Unlike elementary particles, quasiparticles exist only within the medium that supports them and typically have finite lifetimes, dispersing or decaying outside their host system.
