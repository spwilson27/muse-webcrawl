Browsing (herbivory)

Browsing is a type of herbivory in which a herbivore (or, more narrowly defined, a folivore) feeds on leaves, soft shoots, or fruits of high-growing, generally woody plants such as shrubs and trees. This is contrasted with grazing, usually associated with animals feeding on grass or other low vegetation. Examples of animals that are browsers include giraffes, which feed on the leaves of acacia trees; goats; deer; moose, which browse willow and aspen in winter; and many insects and primates.

Browser ecology

Browsers exploit a food resource — the foliage of woody plants — that is often protected by thorns, tough fibres, and chemical defences such as tannins. In response, browsers have evolved specialisations: the giraffe's long neck and tongue reach high canopy foliage and avoid thorns; ruminant browsers harbour gut microbes that detoxify plant compounds; and many browsers selectively feed on young growth, which is less defended and more nutritious. The "browse line" visible on trees and shrubs in areas with dense deer or livestock populations marks the height up to which foliage has been stripped.

Effects on vegetation

Browsing pressure profoundly shapes plant communities. Heavy browsing by overabundant deer can suppress tree regeneration, simplify forest understoreys, and alter species composition — a major concern in North American and European forestry. Conversely, moderate browsing can maintain open habitats and biodiversity, and domestic goats are deliberately used for brush control and wildfire fuel reduction. Safari ecologists distinguish browsers (such as giraffe, kudu, and black rhinoceros) from grazers (such as zebra, wildebeest, and white rhinoceros) and mixed feeders (such as elephants and impala), each with distinct effects on savanna structure.

In agriculture

"Browse" also denotes the woody forage itself — the leaves and twigs available to browsing animals — and its management is important in silvopasture and rangeland systems, where the availability and nutritional quality of browse sustain livestock and game through dry seasons when grass is scarce.
