Lutetium

SymbolLu
Atomic number71
Standard atomic weight174.967
GroupLanthanide
Melting point1925 K (1652 °C)
Density9.841 g/cm³

Lutetium is a chemical element; it has symbol Lu and atomic number 71. It is a silvery white metal, resistant to corrosion in dry air, and is the last element in the lanthanide series, traditionally counted among the rare earth elements.

Lutetium was independently discovered in 1907 by the French scientist Georges Urbain, the Austrian mineralogist Baron Carl Auer von Welsbach, and the American chemist Charles James, all working with impure samples of ytterbia. Urbain's name for the element, derived from Lutetia, the Latin name for Paris, was eventually adopted.

Properties

Lutetium is the hardest and densest of the lanthanides, a consequence of the lanthanide contraction. It has only one stable oxidation state, +3, and its compounds are generally colourless. The metal reacts slowly with water and dissolves readily in dilute acids.

Applications

Lutetium has few commercial uses because it is rare and expensive. The radioisotope lutetium-177 is used in targeted radionuclide therapy for neuroendocrine tumours and prostate cancer. Lutetium oxyorthosilicate (LSO) crystals doped with cerium are used as scintillators in positron emission tomography (PET) scanners. Lutetium aluminium garnet is used in high-refractive-index immersion lithography lenses.

Occurrence

Lutetium is found in almost all rare earth minerals, typically at concentrations of only a few parts per million, most commercially in monazite. It is among the least abundant of the rare earth elements.
