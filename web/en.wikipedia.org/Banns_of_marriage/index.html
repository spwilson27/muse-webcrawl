Banns of marriage

The banns of marriage, commonly known simply as the "banns", are the public announcement in a parish church of an intended marriage, made in advance so that anyone who knows a legal impediment to the union may raise it. The word derives from the Old English gebann, a proclamation.

History and practice

The requirement dates in the Western church to the Fourth Lateran Council of 1215. In the Church of England the banns are traditionally read on three consecutive Sundays before the wedding, and in the Roman Catholic Church a similar publication is required unless a dispensation is granted. The ceremony of objections is dramatised in the traditional marriage liturgy's charge to speak "now, or else hereafter for ever hold your peace".

Modern status

Civil marriage registration has displaced the banns in many jurisdictions, but they remain a legal preliminary to Church of England weddings in England and Wales, where couples marrying in an Anglican church may still have banns called instead of giving notice at a register office.
