Black Sabbath were an English heavy metal band formed in Birmingham in 1968 by guitarist Tony Iommi, bassist Geezer Butler, drummer Bill Ward, and vocalist Ozzy Osbourne. They are widely regarded as pioneers of heavy metal music, helping to define the genre with their self-titled debut album (1970), Paranoid (1970), and Master of Reality (1971), and their down-tuned, doom-laden sound influenced generations of metal bands.

History

The band took its name from a 1963 horror film and deliberately pursued a dark, heavy sound, in part shaped by Iommi's detuned guitar playing — adopted after he lost the tips of two fingers in an industrial accident and lowered his guitar's tuning to ease string tension. Early albums produced signature songs such as "Black Sabbath", "Iron Man", "Paranoid", and "War Pigs", which addressed war, addiction, and social unrest alongside occult imagery.

Osbourne was dismissed in 1979 and replaced by Ronnie James Dio, whose tenure produced Heaven and Hell (1980) and Mob Rules (1981). Numerous lineup changes followed over subsequent decades. The original lineup reunited in the late 1990s, released the chart-topping album 13 in 2013, and concluded their career with The End Tour, finishing in Birmingham in February 2017. The classic lineup reunited one final time for the "Back to the Beginning" farewell concert in Birmingham in July 2025.

Legacy

Black Sabbath have sold more than 70 million records worldwide and were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2006. MTV ranked them the "Greatest Metal Band" of all time, and their early work is regarded as foundational not only for heavy metal but also for doom metal, stoner rock, and sludge metal.
