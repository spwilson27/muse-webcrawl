Ürümqi is the capital of the Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region in northwestern China. Set in an oasis between the Tian Shan mountains and the Gurbantünggüt Desert, it is one of the world's most landlocked major cities — the place farthest from any ocean is near it.

History

Although not a major Silk Road city itself, Ürümqi (from Oirat Mongol for “fine pasture”) grew under Qing rule after the conquest of Dzungaria in the 1750s. It became capital of the new province of Xinjiang in 1884 under the name Dihua, renamed Ürümqi in 1954. Ethnic tensions erupted in riots in July 2009 between Uyghur and Han residents, after which security controls intensified.

Geography and economy

The city sits at about 800 metres elevation with a continental arid climate — hot summers, very cold winters. It is Xinjiang's political, industrial and transport hub, serving the region's Uyghur, Han, Hui, Kazakh and other peoples, with rail links west to Kazakhstan reviving Silk Road trade. Landmarks include Hong Shan (Red Mountain) park, the Xinjiang Regional Museum with its Tarim mummies, and the Grand Bazaar.
