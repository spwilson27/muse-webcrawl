The Uyghurs are a Turkic ethnic group native to the Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region in northwestern China, where they are recognised as one of the country's 56 ethnic groups. They are culturally and linguistically closely related to the peoples of Central Asia.

History

The Uyghur Khaganate ruled a steppe empire from Mongolia in the 8th–9th centuries. After its fall in 840, Uyghur groups migrated to the Tarim Basin and Turpan, where oasis kingdoms such as Qocho flourished along the Silk Road. Over centuries the population adopted Islam, largely complete by the 16th century under the Yarkent Khanate. The region came under Qing rule in the 18th century; the name Xinjiang (“new frontier”) was formalised in 1884.

Culture

Uyghur culture centres on oasis agriculture, music (the Twelve Muqam), dance, embroidery, and cuisine including laghman noodles, samsa and kebabs. The capital is Ürümqi; historic cities include Kashgar, Turpan and Hotan.

Contemporary issues

Since the 2010s, Chinese government policies in Xinjiang — including mass detention camps reported from about 2017, surveillance and labour programmes — have drawn international condemnation and allegations of human rights abuses, which Beijing denies, describing its measures as counter-terrorism and vocational training. The World Uyghur Congress advocates for Uyghur rights from exile.
