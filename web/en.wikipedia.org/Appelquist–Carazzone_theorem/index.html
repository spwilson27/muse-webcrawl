Appelquist–Carazzone theorem

The Appelquist–Carazzone theorem, proved by Thomas Appelquist and John Carazzone in 1975, is a result in quantum field theory concerning decoupling: heavy particles whose masses are much larger than the energy scale of a process have only small, suppressed effects on low-energy physics, which can be described by an effective field theory containing only the light particles.

Statement

In a renormalisable theory, the effects of a particle of mass M on processes at energy E ≪ M can be absorbed into renormalisations of the couplings and masses of the low-energy theory, up to corrections suppressed by powers of E/M. The heavy particle's influence thus "decouples" as its mass grows.

Exceptions and significance

Decoupling can fail when heavy particles couple through terms that grow with their mass — for example, particles whose masses arise from spontaneous symmetry breaking, such as the top quark's effect on the W and Z bosons, or the Higgs boson's non-decoupling in certain observables. The theorem underlies the modern effective-field-theory viewpoint, in which physics at each energy scale is described by the degrees of freedom relevant there, with heavy physics encoded in a few parameters.
