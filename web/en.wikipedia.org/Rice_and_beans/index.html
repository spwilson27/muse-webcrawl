Rice and beans


Rice and beans, a staple combination in many cuisines

Rice and beans, or beans and rice, is a category of dishes prepared from the combination of rice and beans as primary ingredients, found in many cultures around the world. The pairing is nutritionally complementary: rice is low in the amino acid lysine but rich in methionine, while beans are the reverse, so together they provide a complete protein. Inexpensive, filling and storable, the combination became a foundational staple of Latin American, Caribbean, Creole and many other cuisines.

Varieties

  • Feijão com arroz — Brazil's everyday staple, typically black or carioca beans served over white rice, the base of feijoada.
  • Moros y Cristianos and congrí — Cuban dishes of black or red beans cooked together with rice.
  • Arroz con habichuelas — Puerto Rican rice with beans, seasoned with sofrito.
  • Gallo pinto — Nicaragua and Costa Rica's breakfast dish of rice and beans fried together, often with Salsa Lizano in Costa Rica.
  • Red beans and rice — the Louisiana Creole classic, traditionally made on Mondays with ham hock or sausage.
  • Hoppin' John — black-eyed peas and rice of the American South, eaten on New Year's Day for luck.
  • Rice and peas — Jamaica's Sunday dish, usually kidney beans or gungo peas cooked with coconut milk.
  • Waakye — a Ghanaian street food of rice and black-eyed peas.
  • Rajma chawal — kidney bean curry with rice, popular in northern India.

Nutrition

Together, rice and beans supply complete protein, complex carbohydrates, fibre, iron, folate and other B vitamins, making the combination an important low-cost source of nutrition, especially in vegetarian diets.
