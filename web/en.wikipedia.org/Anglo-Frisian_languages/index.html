Anglo-Frisian languages

FamilyIndo-European → Germanic → West Germanic
BranchesAnglic (English, Scots); Frisian (West, North, Saterland)
RegionBritain; coastal Netherlands and Germany; worldwide (English)

The Anglo-Frisian languages are a proposed subgroup of the West Germanic languages comprising the Anglic languages (English and Scots) and the Frisian languages. The grouping is based on shared sound changes that distinguish these languages from neighbouring High and Low German varieties.

Shared features

Characteristic Anglo-Frisian innovations include the fronting and palatalization of Proto-Germanic *k before front vowels (compare English cheese and Frisian tsiis with German Käse), the loss of nasals before fricatives with compensatory lengthening (English five, us vs. German fünf, uns), and the Anglo-Frisian brightening of vowels. Such correspondences reflect the close contacts of Angles, Saxons, Jutes and Frisians around the North Sea coast in the early medieval period.

Languages

English, descended from Old English, has become a global lingua franca. Scots is spoken in Scotland. The living Frisian languages are West Frisian in the Dutch province of Friesland, North Frisian on the German North Sea coast and islands, and Saterland Frisian in Lower Saxony.
