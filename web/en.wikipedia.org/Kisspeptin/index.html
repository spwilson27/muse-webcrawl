Kisspeptin

Kisspeptin (formerly known as metastin) is a protein that in humans is encoded by the KISS1 gene. Kisspeptin is a G protein-coupled receptor ligand for GPR54. It plays a critical role in the regulation of the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal (HPG) axis and is considered a key gatekeeper of puberty onset and reproductive function.

History

The KISS1 gene was identified in 1996 by researchers in Hershey, Pennsylvania, as a metastasis suppressor gene in melanoma; the name is a play on Hershey's "Kisses" chocolates, with "SS" standing for suppressor sequence. Its peptide product, initially called metastin, was matched in 2001 to the orphan receptor GPR54 (now KISS1R). In 2003, two groups independently reported that inactivating mutations in GPR54 cause hypogonadotropic hypogonadism and failure of puberty in humans, revealing kisspeptin's central reproductive role.

Structure and processing

The KISS1 precursor is a 145-amino-acid peptide that is cleaved into shorter amidated fragments: kisspeptin-54, kisspeptin-14, kisspeptin-13 and kisspeptin-10. All share a C-terminal RF-amide motif (Arg-Phe-NH2) essential for binding and activating KISS1R.

Function

Kisspeptin neurons in the hypothalamus — principally in the arcuate nucleus and the anteroventral periventricular nucleus (AVPV) — stimulate gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) neurons. Arcuate kisspeptin neurons co-express neurokinin B and dynorphin and are known as KNDy neurons; they are thought to generate the pulsatile pattern of GnRH secretion. Sex steroids feed back on kisspeptin neurons, mediating both negative and positive feedback, including the pre-ovulatory LH surge in females.

Clinical significance

Loss-of-function mutations in KISS1 or KISS1R cause normosmic idiopathic hypogonadotropic hypogonadism, while activating mutations can cause central precocious puberty. Kisspeptin and its analogues are investigated as treatments for infertility, as triggers of egg maturation in IVF, and as diagnostic agents. Because KISS1 suppresses metastasis in several cancers, kisspeptin signalling is also studied in oncology.
