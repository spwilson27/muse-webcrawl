Franz Beckenbauer

Franz Beckenbauer (1945–2024), "Der Kaiser"

Franz Anton Beckenbauer (11 September 1945 – 7 January 2024) was a German professional football player, manager, and official. Nicknamed der Kaiser ("the Emperor") for his elegant style and leadership, he is widely regarded as one of the greatest players of all time and is credited with popularising the role of the attacking sweeper (libero).

Playing career

Born in Munich, Beckenbauer spent most of his career at Bayern Munich, winning four Bundesliga titles and three consecutive European Cups (1974–1976). With West Germany he won the European Championship in 1972 and the World Cup in 1974 as captain, having played the famous 1970 semi-final against Italy with a dislocated shoulder in a sling. He later played for the New York Cosmos alongside Pelé and finished his club career at Hamburger SV, winning another Bundesliga title.

Manager and administrator

As manager, Beckenbauer led West Germany to the 1986 World Cup final and to victory at Italia 90, becoming one of only three men to win the World Cup as both player and manager. He later served as Bayern Munich president and headed the organising committee of the 2006 World Cup in Germany. His later years were clouded by investigations into the 2006 bid, but his standing as the defining figure of German football endured. He died in Salzburg in January 2024.
