70 mm film

70 mm film (or 65 mm film, the gauge used in cameras) is a wide high-resolution film format for motion pictures. Its frame area is roughly four times that of standard 35 mm film, yielding much finer detail and less grain, and it was traditionally reserved for epic productions and special-venue exhibition.

History

Wide-film formats date to the earliest years of cinema, but 70 mm became prominent in the 1950s with processes such as Todd-AO, introduced with Oklahoma! (1955), and Super Panavision 70, used on films including Lawrence of Arabia (1962), 2001: A Space Odyssey (1968) and Ben-Hur (1959, shot in MGM Camera 65). Release prints were 70 mm wide because they added magnetic sound stripes to the 65 mm camera negative.

IMAX and revival

The IMAX format uses 70 mm film running horizontally through the camera, giving an even larger frame. After decades in which most "70 mm" releases were blow-ups from 35 mm, directors such as Quentin Tarantino (The Hateful Eight, 2015), Christopher Nolan (Dunkirk, Oppenheimer) and Paul Thomas Anderson championed shooting and projecting in the format, prompting restorations of vintage projectors and special roadshow engagements.
