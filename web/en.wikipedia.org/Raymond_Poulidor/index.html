Raymond Poulidor

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Raymond Poulidor (15 April 1936 – 13 November 2019) was a French professional racing cyclist, one of the most popular French sportsmen of the 20th century. Nicknamed "Poupou" and famously "the eternal second", he finished on the podium of the Tour de France a record eight times — three times second and five times third — without ever winning it or wearing the yellow jersey for a single day, largely because his career coincided with those of two of the greatest riders in history, Jacques Anquetil and Eddy Merckx. Despite this, his palmarès was outstanding: he won the Vuelta a España in 1964, Milan–San Remo, La Flèche Wallonne, Paris–Nice twice and the Critérium du Dauphiné Libéré twice, and was French national champion in 1961.

Career

Born to a poor farming family in the Creuse, Poulidor turned professional in 1960 with Mercier. His rivalry with Anquetil divided France in the 1960s: Anquetil, the cold, calculating time-trial specialist, won five Tours, while Poulidor, the attacking climber from peasant stock, embodied the popular underdog. Their closest battle came in 1964, including a legendary shoulder-to-shoulder duel on the Puy de Dôme, and Poulidor finished second by 55 seconds. Against Merckx in the following decade he again collected podium finishes, finishing second in 1974 and third in 1976 at the age of 40. In total he won seven Tour stages and contested fourteen Tours between 1962 and 1976. His third place in the 1962 Tour, achieved with his arm in plaster weeks earlier, began the podium record.

Legacy

Poulidor's failure to win the Tour paradoxically made him more beloved in France than many winners; "Poupoularité" became a byword for affectionate fame, and the phrase "a Poulidor" entered French usage for a perennial runner-up. He remained a fixture at the Tour as a commentator and ambassador into his eighties, and was the grandfather of the Dutch world champion cyclist Mathieu van der Poel. He died in November 2019 and was widely mourned across France.
