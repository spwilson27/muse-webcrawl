Tzatziki (Greek: τζατζίκι), also known as cacık in Turkish and similar names across the Balkans and Middle East, is a dip, soup, or sauce made of strained yogurt mixed with cucumbers, garlic, salt, olive oil, and herbs such as dill or mint. It is found in the cuisines of Southeast Europe and the Middle East, and is a staple of Greek cooking.

Preparation

Greek-style tzatziki uses thick strained yogurt — often sheep's or goat's milk yogurt — combined with grated or finely diced cucumber that has been salted and drained to remove excess water. Crushed garlic, olive oil, and sometimes lemon juice or vinegar are added, and the dip is served cold, commonly as part of a meze platter, with pita bread, or alongside grilled meats such as souvlaki and gyros.

Variations

Related dishes include Turkish cacık (thinner, sometimes served as a cold soup), Balkan tarator (with walnuts, sometimes as a soup), and Indian raita, a spiced yogurt-and-cucumber condiment. The name "tzatziki" derives from the Turkish cacık.
