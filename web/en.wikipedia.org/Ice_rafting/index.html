Ice rafting

Ice rafting is the transport of various materials by ice, a process of geomorphology and sedimentology in which floating ice — river ice, lake ice, sea ice, or icebergs — carries debris across or along bodies of water and deposits it elsewhere when the ice melts.

Mechanisms

Debris may become incorporated into ice when sediment-laden water freezes, when shore material is frozen into ice during winter, or when glaciers calve icebergs containing rock and soil. As the ice drifts with currents and wind, the material is transported, sometimes over long distances.

Geological significance

Ice-rafted debris found in marine and lake sediment cores is used as evidence of past glaciation and climate change. The dispersal of dropstones — isolated large rocks dropped from melting ice into fine-grained sediments — records the presence of floating ice in ancient environments, including during major glacial episodes such as the Heinrich events of the North Atlantic.
