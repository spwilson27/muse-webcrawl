Mikhail Vasilyevich Frunze (2 February 1885 – 31 October 1925) was a Soviet revolutionary, politician and Red Army commander during the Russian Civil War. One of the most successful Bolshevik military leaders, he succeeded Leon Trotsky as head of the Soviet military in 1925.

Revolution and Civil War

Born in Pishpek (now Bishkek, Kyrgyzstan) to a Romanian father and Russian mother, Frunze joined the Russian Social Democratic Labour Party in 1904 and became a professional revolutionary, surviving arrest, exile and a death sentence commuted to Siberian penal servitude. After the October Revolution he organised Red forces in Belarus and Ivanovo. As commander of the Eastern Front in 1919 he directed the counteroffensive that defeated Admiral Kolchak's White armies, and in 1920, commanding the Southern Front, he destroyed General Wrangel's forces in Crimea, the last major White stronghold in European Russia. He also campaigned in Central Asia against the Basmachi and negotiated with Turkey.

Reform and death

As People's Commissar for Military and Naval Affairs, Frunze pushed reforms creating a smaller regular army supported by territorial militia, and argued for a unified military doctrine. He died in October 1925 following stomach surgery, amid later speculation about the circumstances. His birthplace was renamed Frunze in his honour (it reverted to Bishkek in 1991), and the Frunze Military Academy in Moscow bore his name.
