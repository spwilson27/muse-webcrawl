Sidereal time

Sidereal time is a system of timekeeping that astronomers use to locate celestial objects. Using sidereal time, it is possible to determine the direction in which a telescope must point to see a given star in the night sky. A sidereal day is approximately 23 hours, 56 minutes and 4 seconds.

Definition

Sidereal time is measured by the rotation of the Earth relative to the distant stars rather than the Sun. Because the Earth moves along its orbit while rotating, it must turn slightly more than 360° for the Sun to return to the same meridian, making the solar day about four minutes longer than the sidereal day.

Local sidereal time

The local sidereal time is the right ascension of the objects currently crossing the observer's meridian. Greenwich Mean Sidereal Time is the corresponding value at the Greenwich meridian.

Uses

Observatories historically kept sidereal clocks so that the local sidereal time could be read directly when setting telescopes. It remains fundamental to the equatorial coordinate system and to astronomical navigation.
