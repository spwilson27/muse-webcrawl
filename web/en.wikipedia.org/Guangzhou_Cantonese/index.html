Guangzhou Cantonese

Guangzhou Cantonese (廣州話), also known as the Canton dialect or Guangzhou speech, is the prestige dialect of Yue Chinese (Cantonese), spoken in the city of Guangzhou (Canton) and the surrounding Pearl River Delta. It serves as the standard and reference form of Cantonese and is closely related to the variety spoken in Hong Kong.

Phonology

Guangzhou Cantonese is a tonal language with six tones in modern analysis (traditionally counted as nine when checked syllables are distinguished). It preserves features lost in Mandarin, including the entering tone with final stop consonants -p, -t and -k, and it has a relatively large inventory of syllable-final consonants. Compared with Hong Kong Cantonese, the Guangzhou variety is sometimes described as more conservative, though the two are fully mutually intelligible apart from vocabulary differences.

Status and use

The variety has historically dominated Cantonese-language media, education and commerce in the Pearl River Delta. Since the late twentieth century, promotion of Standard Mandarin (Putonghua) in schools and broadcasting has reduced the public space for Cantonese in Guangzhou, a trend that has prompted local cultural concern, notably during the 2010 controversy over proposals to increase Mandarin programming on local television.

Romanisation

Systems used to write Guangzhou Cantonese in the Latin alphabet include Yale romanisation, Jyutping (developed by the Linguistic Society of Hong Kong) and the Guangdong Romanization scheme used in mainland China.
