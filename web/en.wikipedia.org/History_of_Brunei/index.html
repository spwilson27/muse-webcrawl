The history of Brunei concerns the small sultanate on the north-west coast of Borneo. Once the centre of an empire controlling much of Borneo and parts of the Philippines, Brunei shrank under colonial pressure to a British-protected enclave, then regained full independence in 1984, enriched by oil discovered in 1929.

Early sultanate and empire

Chinese records mention a polity called Poli or Boni trading with China from the 6th century. By tradition the sultanate was founded in the 14th century; Alak Batatar became its first Muslim ruler as Sultan Muhammad Shah. Under Bolkiah (r. 1485–1524) Brunei's thalassocracy reached its height, spanning coastal Borneo and the Sulu archipelago toward Manila. Magellan's expedition visited in 1521; Pigafetta's account describes a wealthy court and the Kampong Ayer water city.

Decline and the British

Civil war, piracy, the Spanish attack of 1578 (the "Castilian War") and European rivals eroded Brunei's power. In the 19th century the cession of Sarawak to James Brooke (from 1841) and of North Borneo to a British company (1877–78) stripped most of its territory. The 1888 Protectorate Agreement made Brunei a British protectorate; a Resident system followed in 1906. Limbang's loss in 1890 split the sultanate in two — the source of its present geography.

Oil and independence

Oil at Seria (1929) transformed the sultanate's finances. Japanese occupation (1941–45) interrupted British rule. Sultan Omar Ali Saifuddien III negotiated the 1959 constitution and resisted joining Malaysia, then abdicated in 1967 in favour of his son Hassanal Bolkiah. Brunei resumed full independence on 1 January 1984 as an absolute monarchy, its oil and gas wealth funding a cradle-to-grave welfare state.
