The Saab JAS 39 Gripen (Swedish for "griffin") is a light single-engine supersonic multirole fighter aircraft manufactured by the Swedish aerospace and defence company Saab AB. The Gripen has a delta wing and canard configuration with relaxed stability design and fly-by-wire flight controls. It was designed to replace the Saab 35 Draken and 37 Viggen in the Swedish Air Force; the designation JAS stands for the Swedish words for air-to-air combat, air-to-surface attack and reconnaissance, reflecting its swing-role design.

The Gripen was designed for dispersed operations from short road bases.

Development and design

First flown in 1988 and introduced into service in 1996, the Gripen was built around Swedish Cold War doctrine: it can operate from short, improvised road runways with small ground crews and rapid turnaround. The original C/D models are powered by the Volvo RM12 engine; the enlarged Gripen E/F, with the more powerful General Electric F414G engine, a longer fuselage, greater fuel capacity and ten hardpoints, entered Swedish service development in the 2010s and was selected by Brazil as the F-39.

Operators

Besides Sweden, the Gripen has been operated or ordered by Brazil, the Czech Republic, Hungary, South Africa and Thailand, and has been a frequent competitor in fighter procurement contests worldwide, marketed on its comparatively low life-cycle cost.
