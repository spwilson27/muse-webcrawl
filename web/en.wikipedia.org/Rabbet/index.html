Rabbet


Placeholder image: Rabbet

A rabbet (American English) or rebate (British English) is a recess or groove cut into the edge of a piece of machinable material, usually wood. When viewed in cross-section, a rabbet is two-sided and open to the edge or end of the surface into which it is cut.

Uses

Rabbets are commonly used in cabinetmaking and joinery: to house the back panel of a cabinet, to receive glass in a picture frame or window sash, and as one half of a shiplap joint along board edges. A rabbet joint, often reinforced with glue, nails or screws, joins two pieces at a corner where one piece has a rabbet cut along its edge.

Tools

Rabbets can be cut with a table saw or router, with a dedicated rabbeting plane, or historically with moulding planes. A fillister plane is designed specifically for cutting rabbets across and along the grain.
