Samosir, or Samosir Island, is a large volcanic island in Lake Toba in North Sumatra, Indonesia. At roughly 630 km² it is often described as the largest island within an island (Sumatra) in the world. The island was formed by the uplift of the caldera floor — a resurgent dome — following the cataclysmic Toba eruption about 74,000 years ago, one of the largest volcanic eruptions of the past two million years.

Geography

Samosir is connected to the mainland by a narrow isthmus at Pangururan, cut by the Tano Ponggol canal in the late 19th century. The interior is a plateau of rolling grassland and pine forest rising to Pusuk Buhit, a mountain sacred in Batak tradition as the birthplace of the Batak people.

Culture

The island is the cultural heartland of the Toba Batak people. Villages such as Tomok and Ambarita preserve traditional rumah bolon houses with boat-shaped roofs, stone chairs and megalithic tombs, including the tomb of King Sidabutar. Ferries from Parapat land at Tomok, and the Tuk-Tuk peninsula is the main tourist area, with lakeside hotels and Batak museums.
