Necrosis

Necrosis (from Ancient Greek nékrōsis, "death") is a form of cell injury which results in the premature death of cells in living tissue by autolysis. Unlike apoptosis — programmed, orderly cell death — necrosis is uncontrolled and is typically caused by external factors or disease, such as trauma, infection, toxins, or loss of blood supply.

Patterns

Pathologists describe several morphological patterns: coagulative necrosis (typical of infarcts, preserving tissue outline), liquefactive necrosis (in bacterial abscesses and brain infarcts), caseous necrosis (characteristic of tuberculosis), fat necrosis, fibrinoid necrosis of blood vessels, and gangrenous necrosis of limbs.

Consequences and treatment

Necrotic cells release their contents, provoking inflammation in surrounding tissue and potentially systemic illness. Dead tissue cannot recover; management involves removing the cause (for example restoring blood flow), debridement or amputation of gangrenous tissue, and antibiotics where infection is present.
