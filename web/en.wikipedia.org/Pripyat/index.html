Pripyat

Pripyat (Ukrainian: Прип'ять) is an abandoned city in northern Ukraine, near the Ukraine–Belarus border. Founded on 4 February 1970 as a Soviet atomgrad — a closed city built to serve the nearby Chernobyl Nuclear Power Plant — it was evacuated on the afternoon of 27 April 1986, the day after the Chernobyl disaster.

History

Pripyat was one of nine atomgrads built by the Soviet Union to house nuclear plant workers and their families. By the time of the disaster its population was about 49,400, with an average age of just 26 years. The city had schools, a hospital, cultural and sports facilities, and an amusement park whose Ferris wheel has become an iconic image of the evacuation.

The disaster and evacuation

Reactor No. 4 of the Chernobyl plant exploded on 26 April 1986. Residents were initially told little; evacuation buses arrived the next day and the population left within hours, told they would be gone for only three days. They never returned. The city lies inside the Chernobyl Exclusion Zone.

Today

Pripyat is a ghost city, gradually being reclaimed by forest. Before the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine it was a destination for dark tourism, with guided tours visiting the amusement park, school, and swimming pool. Wildlife, including wolves, lynx, and reintroduced Przewalski's horses, thrives in the zone.
