Elizabeth Cady Stanton, a leading figure of the women's rights movement

Elizabeth Cady Stanton (née Cady; 12 November 1815 – 26 October 1902) was an American writer and activist who was a leader of the women's rights movement in the United States. Her Declaration of Sentiments, presented at the Seneca Falls Convention held in 1848 in Seneca Falls, New York, is often credited with initiating the first organised women's suffrage movement in the United States.

Early life and Seneca Falls

Born into a prominent family in Johnstown, New York, Elizabeth Cady received an unusually strong education at the Troy Female Seminary. In 1840 she married the abolitionist Henry Brewster Stanton, insisting that "obey" be omitted from the vows. On their honeymoon in London both were appalled that women delegates were excluded from the World Anti-Slavery Convention. With Lucretia Mott and others, Stanton organised the Seneca Falls Convention of July 1848; its Declaration of Sentiments, modelled on the Declaration of Independence, demanded women's suffrage — a demand many contemporaries considered radical.

Partnership with Susan B. Anthony

From 1851 Stanton worked in close partnership with Susan B. Anthony: Stanton wrote the speeches and arguments, Anthony organised. Together they founded the Women's Loyal National League during the Civil War, the American Equal Rights Association, and in 1869 the National Woman Suffrage Association, advocating a federal constitutional amendment for women's votes. They co-edited the newspaper The Revolution and the first volumes of the History of Woman Suffrage.

Later years

Stanton's interests broadened to divorce law, married women's property rights, employment and religion; her Woman's Bible (1895), questioning biblical passages used to subordinate women, was controversial even among suffragists. She died in 1902, eighteen years before American women won the vote with the Nineteenth Amendment.
