Vinca alkaloids are a class of naturally occurring and semi-synthetic alkaloids derived from the Madagascar periwinkle plant, Catharanthus roseus (formerly Vinca rosea). They are widely used as chemotherapy drugs in the treatment of cancers including leukaemias, lymphomas, and various solid tumours.

Discovery

In the 1950s, researchers at the University of Western Ontario and Eli Lilly investigating folk claims that periwinkle tea lowered blood sugar instead found that extracts suppressed white blood cell production. This led to the isolation of vinblastine and vincristine, introduced into clinical use in the early 1960s.

Mechanism of action

Vinca alkaloids bind to tubulin dimers and prevent their polymerisation into microtubules. This disrupts the mitotic spindle, arrests dividing cells in metaphase, and triggers apoptosis. Because they act on the microtubule system, they are classified as spindle poisons or antimitotic agents.

Clinical use and toxicity

Vincristine is a mainstay of regimens for acute lymphoblastic leukaemia and lymphomas; its dose-limiting toxicity is peripheral neuropathy, and it must never be given intrathecally. Vinblastine is used in Hodgkin lymphoma (as part of ABVD) and testicular cancer, with myelosuppression as its main toxicity. Later derivatives include vindesine, vinorelbine and vinflunine.
