Drain-waste-vent system

In modern plumbing, a drain-waste-vent system (or DWV) is the part of a plumbing system that removes sewage and greywater from a building and vents gases to the outside. It is distinct from the water-supply system that delivers potable water under pressure: the DWV works by gravity, so its pipes must slope downward, typically by about 1/4 inch per foot for small pipes.

Components

Drains carry waste from fixtures such as sinks, showers and water closets. Every fixture drain incorporates a trap — a U-, P- or S-shaped bend that retains a plug of water sealing the fixture against sewer gases. Drains join larger branch drains and the building's main stack, which discharges to the building drain and sewer.

Venting

Vents connect the drainage system to open air, usually through the roof. They admit air so that flowing water does not siphon traps dry, and they allow sewer and septic gases to escape safely above the building. Venting arrangements include individual vents, wet vents and circuit vents; air admittance valves may substitute for some through-roof vents where codes allow.

Materials and codes

DWV piping is commonly PVC, ABS, cast iron or copper. Because drainage is not pressurised, DWV pipe has thinner walls than supply pipe and uses fittings with smooth, sweeping bends. Installation is regulated by plumbing codes such as the Uniform Plumbing Code and International Plumbing Code in the United States.
