Jefferson Davis

Jefferson F. Davis (3 June 1808 – 6 December 1889) was an American politician who served as the first and only president of the Confederate States from 1861 to 1865. Before the Civil War he represented Mississippi in the US House and Senate and served as Secretary of War under President Franklin Pierce.

Early life and career

Davis was born in Kentucky and raised in Mississippi. He graduated from the US Military Academy at West Point in 1828 and served on the frontier. He married Sarah Knox Taylor, daughter of future president Zachary Taylor, who died of malaria only months after the wedding. Davis became a cotton planter at Brierfield, his brother's land on the Mississippi River.

National politics

Elected to the House of Representatives in 1845, Davis resigned to fight in the Mexican–American War, distinguishing himself at the Battle of Buena Vista. As Secretary of War (1853–1857) he modernised the army; in the Senate he was a leading spokesman for Southern rights and the expansion of slavery into the territories, though he warned that secession would mean a bloody war. When Mississippi seceded in January 1861 he resigned his seat in a celebrated farewell address.

Confederate president

The Montgomery convention unanimously elected Davis provisional president of the Confederate States; he was inaugurated on 18 February 1861 and later elected to a six-year term. As president he struggled with states'-rights governors, inflation, shortages and the rival claims of the eastern and western theatres, while pursuing a strategy that failed to win European recognition. He fled Richmond in April 1865 and was captured in Georgia on 10 May 1865.

Later life

Imprisoned at Fort Monroe for two years and indicted for treason, Davis was released on bail in 1867 and the charges were dropped in 1869. He retired to Beauvoir on the Mississippi Gulf Coast, where he wrote The Rise and Fall of the Confederate Government (1881). He died in New Orleans in 1889; his US citizenship was restored posthumously in 1978.
