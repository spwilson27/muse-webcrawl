Beinn Alligin

Beinn Alligin is one of the classic mountains of the Torridon region of Scotland. The name means the "mountain of beauty" or "jewelled mountain" in Scottish Gaelic. The mountain is composed of Torridonian sandstone and has two peaks of Munro status: Tom na Gruagaich (922 metres) to the south, and Sgurr Mhòr at 986 metres, the highest point.

Ascent

The usual route of ascent starts from the road near Torridon village and climbs Tom na Gruagaich before traversing the ridge to Sgurr Mhòr. The traverse continues over the Horns of Alligin, a series of rocky pinnacles that give a celebrated scramble. The mountain offers fine views over Upper Loch Torridon and to neighbouring peaks such as Liathach and Beinn Eighe.

Geology

Like the other Torridon hills, Beinn Alligin is formed from ancient Torridonian sandstone resting on Lewisian gneiss, sculpted by glaciation into steep terraces and corries.
