River Forth

The River Forth is a major river in central Scotland, 47 km (29 mi) long, which drains into the North Sea via the Firth of Forth. Its main tributaries emanate from near Loch Lomond and the Trossachs; from its source area below Ben Lomond it flows roughly eastward through Aberfoyle and Stirling. Below Stirling, the river becomes tidal and widens into the Firth of Forth.

Course and crossings

Historically, Stirling was the lowest bridging point of the river and thus a strategic prize: Stirling Bridge was the site of William Wallace's victory over the English in 1297. Today the Forth is crossed near its mouth by three famous bridges at Queensferry — the Forth Bridge (railway, opened 1890), the Forth Road Bridge (1964) and the Queensferry Crossing (2017) — as well as by the Kincardine and Clackmannanshire bridges further upstream.

Settlements and industry

Towns along the river and firth include Stirling, Alloa, Falkirk, Grangemouth (with its major port and petrochemical complex) and the communities of Fife and West Lothian facing each other across the firth. The Forth was historically important for shipping, coal export and shipbuilding.
