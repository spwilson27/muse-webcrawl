John Henry Holland

Born: 2 February 1929, Fort Wayne, Indiana, U.S.
Died: 9 August 2015 (aged 86), Ann Arbor, Michigan
Fields: Computer science, complex systems
Institutions: University of Michigan; Santa Fe Institute
Known for: Genetic algorithms; Holland's schema theorem

John Henry Holland (February 2, 1929 – August 9, 2015) was an American scientist and professor of psychology and electrical engineering and computer science at the University of Michigan. He was a pioneer of genetic algorithms and a leading figure in the study of complex adaptive systems.

Career

Holland studied physics at MIT and received the first computer science PhD awarded by the University of Michigan in 1959. In the 1960s he developed his theory of adaptive systems, summarised in his landmark book Adaptation in Natural and Artificial Systems (1975), which introduced genetic algorithms: search methods that evolve populations of candidate solutions using selection, crossover and mutation.

Contributions

His schema theorem explained how short, low-order patterns ("building blocks") receive exponentially increasing representation in evolving populations. Holland also created classifier systems that combined genetic search with rule-based learning, and the Echo model of artificial ecosystems. A co-founder of the Santa Fe Institute's research community, he wrote Hidden Order (1995) and Emergence (1998) on complex adaptive systems.

Recognition

Holland received a MacArthur Fellowship ("genius grant") in 1992 and was a fellow of the Association for Computing Machinery.
