Hells Canyon

Hells Canyon is a ten-mile-wide (16 km) canyon in the northwestern United States, located along the border of eastern Oregon, eastern Washington, and western Idaho. Carved by the Snake River, it is North America's deepest river gorge at 7,993 feet (2,436 m) — deeper than the Grand Canyon — measured from He Devil Peak in Idaho's Seven Devils Mountains down to the river. Much of the canyon is protected within the Hells Canyon National Recreation Area, established in 1975.

Geology and landscape

The canyon exposes basalt flows of the Columbia River Basalt Group and older volcanic and sedimentary rocks. Climate varies dramatically with elevation, from near-desert along the river — hot and dry in summer — to alpine conditions on the rims. The landscape supports bunchgrass slopes, ponderosa pine forest, and habitat for elk, bighorn sheep, mountain goats, black bears, and cougars.

History

Indigenous peoples, ancestors of the Nez Perce, lived in and travelled through the canyon for at least 15,000 years, leaving petroglyphs and pithouse villages. The Lewis and Clark expedition observed the canyon country in 1806 but did not descend into it. Miners, homesteaders, and steamboat operators followed in the nineteenth century; the canyon was also the site of the 1887 massacre of Chinese gold miners at Deep Creek, one of the worst anti-Chinese atrocities in American history. In the twentieth century three hydroelectric dams — Brownlee, Oxbow, and Hells Canyon — were built on the Snake below and within the gorge, while plans for further high dams were abandoned with the recreation area's creation.

Recreation

The canyon is popular for whitewater rafting and jet-boat tours on the Snake River, fishing, hunting, hiking, and the viewpoints at Hat Point and Hells Canyon Overlook.
