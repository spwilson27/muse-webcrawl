Bernard J. Baars (born 1946) is a Dutch-American cognitive neuroscientist known for developing global workspace theory, an influential theoretical framework for understanding consciousness. In his 1988 book A Cognitive Theory of Consciousness, Baars proposed that conscious contents are those broadcast widely throughout the brain from a limited-capacity "global workspace," while most processing remains unconscious and specialised.

Global workspace theory

The theory uses a theatre metaphor: consciousness is like a brightly lit stage spotlit by attention, on which actors compete for access, while a large audience of unconscious processors watches the broadcast. Stanislas Dehaene and colleagues later developed a neuronal version of the theory, associating the global workspace with long-distance cortical networks, especially in prefrontal and parietal regions.

Work

Baars has been affiliated with the Neurosciences Institute in San Diego and co-founded the journal Consciousness and Cognition. His research also covers volition, the psycholinguistics of speech errors, and the contrast between conscious and unconscious brain processes.
