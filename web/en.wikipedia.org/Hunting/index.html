Hunting

Hunting
Purposes: Food, recreation, wildlife management, trade
Methods: Trapping, shooting, coursing, falconry
Regulation: Licences, seasons, bag limits

Hunting is the human practice of seeking, pursuing, capturing or killing wildlife, typically for food, recreation, trade or wildlife management. Humans have hunted since prehistory; hunting was essential to the survival of early human groups and shaped tools, cooperation and culture. Today it is regulated in most countries through licensing, seasons and quotas.

History

Stone tools and cave art attest to hunting by early Homo sapiens and Neanderthals. The development of spears, bows, domesticated dogs and later firearms transformed efficiency. In medieval Europe hunting large game became a jealously guarded privilege of royalty and nobility, enforced by forest laws. Market hunting in the 19th century devastated species such as the American bison and passenger pigeon, prompting modern conservation laws.

Methods

Methods include still hunting and stalking, driven hunts, hunting with dogs, falconry, trapping and snaring, and bowhunting or rifle hunting. Subsistence hunting remains vital for many Indigenous and rural communities in the Arctic, Amazon and Africa.

Management and conservation

Regulated hunting funds conservation in countries such as the United States, where excise taxes on equipment and licence fees finance habitat protection under programmes like the Pittman–Robertson Act. Trophy hunting is controversial: supporters cite revenue for conservation and local communities, critics cite animal welfare and cases of corruption or unsustainable quotas.

Opposition

Animal rights movements oppose hunting on ethical grounds, and some forms — fox hunting with hounds, canned hunting, and poaching of endangered species — are widely banned or condemned. Illegal hunting, or poaching, is a major threat to elephants, rhinos and tigers.
