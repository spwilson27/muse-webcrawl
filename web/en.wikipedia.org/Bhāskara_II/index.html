Bhāskara II

Bornc. 1114, near Bijapur, India
Diedc. 1185
Known forSiddhānta Shiromani, Līlāvatī, chakravala method
FieldsMathematics, astronomy

Bhāskara II (c. 1114–1185), also known as Bhāskarācārya ("Bhāskara the Teacher"), was an Indian mathematician and astronomer of the 12th century, widely considered the greatest mathematician of medieval India. He headed the astronomical observatory at Ujjain, a leading centre of learning.

Works

His main work, the Siddhānta Shiromani ("Crown of Treatises", c. 1150), is divided into four parts: the Līlāvatī on arithmetic, the Bījagaṇita on algebra, and the Grahagaṇita and Golādhyāya on the mathematics of the planets and the sphere. The Līlāvatī, named — according to legend — after his daughter, became the standard arithmetic textbook in India for centuries, famous for its elegant verse problems.

Mathematical contributions

Bhāskara gave the general solution of Pell's equation by the chakravala (cyclic) method, solved indeterminate quadratic equations, worked with negative numbers and zero, stated an early form of Rolle's theorem, and computed π to several digits. In astronomy he calculated the sidereal year to within minutes of the modern value and described the apparent annual motion of the planets with refined trigonometric methods, including instantaneous velocity concepts anticipating differential calculus.

Legacy

His works were translated into Persian and later European languages, influencing mathematics across Asia. The Indian Space Research Organisation named its Bhaskara-I and Bhaskara-II satellites (1979, 1981) in his honour.
