Murti

In the Hindu tradition, a murti (Sanskrit: मूर्ति, romanized: mūrti, lit. "form, embodiment, or solid object") is a consecrated image, statue, or idol of a divinity or holy person in Indian culture. In Hindu temples, a murti is not considered a mere representation but, after consecration (prana pratishtha), a manifestation in which the deity is believed to be present and available for darshan (sacred sight) and worship.

Role in worship

Murtis serve as focal points for puja, in which devotees offer flowers, food, lamps, and incense, bathe and dress the image, and receive its blessing. They are found in temples, home shrines, and public festival installations such as the clay images of Ganesha and Durga made for annual festivals. Iconography — posture, gestures (mudras), attributes, mounts, and proportions — is governed by traditional treatises such as the Shilpa Shastras, so that each deity is visually recognizable.

Materials and consecration

Murtis are made of stone, metal (especially panchaloha bronze), wood, clay, or marble. The consecration ceremony of prana pratishtha ritually invites the deity to reside in the image, culminating in the opening of the eyes. Similar sacred images play roles in Jain and some Buddhist traditions, though theological interpretations differ.
