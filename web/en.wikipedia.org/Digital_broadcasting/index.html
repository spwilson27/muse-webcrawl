Digital broadcasting

Digital broadcasting is the practice of using digital signals rather than analogue signals for broadcasting over radio frequency bands. Digital television (DTV) and digital radio offer more channels in the same bandwidth, better picture and sound quality, and additional services such as electronic program guides, subtitles, and interactive features.

Television standards

The main terrestrial television standards are ATSC, used in North America and South Korea; DVB-T and its successor DVB-T2, used across Europe, much of Asia, Africa, and Oceania; ISDB, used in Japan and much of South America; and DTMB, used in China. Video and audio are typically compressed with MPEG standards, allowing several standard-definition channels, or one or more high-definition channels, in the space of a single analogue channel.

Radio and transition

Digital radio standards include DAB and DAB+, DRM for medium and short wave, and the in-band on-channel HD Radio system used in the United States. Countries have staged "digital switchover" programs to shut down analogue transmissions — the Netherlands and Finland were among the first to complete terrestrial television switchover in 2006–2007, and the United States ended full-power analogue television in 2009.
