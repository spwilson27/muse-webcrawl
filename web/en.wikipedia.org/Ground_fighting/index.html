Ground fighting

Ground fighting (also called ground work or ground game) is hand-to-hand combat that takes place while the combatants are on the ground. The term is commonly used in mixed martial arts (MMA) and other martial arts and combat sports for the phase of a fight that occurs after a takedown or throw. Ground fighting contrasts with stand-up fighting, and tactics include grappling and striking (the latter often called "ground and pound").

Principle of position

In most ground-fighting systems, success depends first on achieving and maintaining a dominant position, a hierarchy taught explicitly in Brazilian jiu-jitsu (BJJ): a fighter on top in the mount or back control can strike or submit the opponent with relative safety, while the fighter underneath seeks to escape, reverse ("sweep"), or attack from the guard. Key positions include the guard, half guard, side control, mount, and back mount. From these positions, practitioners apply joint locks (such as the armbar and kimura) and chokeholds (such as the rear-naked choke and triangle choke) to force a submission.

In martial arts

Ground fighting is the primary focus of Brazilian jiu-jitsu, which developed from Kodokan judo through the Gracie family and emphasizes leverage and technique so a smaller person can defeat a larger opponent. Judo's newaza, sambo, catch wrestling, and submission wrestling (grappling without the gi) all devote substantial training to ground technique. In modern MMA, proficiency on the ground is essential: wrestlers and BJJ practitioners transformed the sport in the 1990s, beginning with Royce Gracie's victories in the early Ultimate Fighting Championship events.

Striking on the ground

Within MMA rules, a fighter in a dominant top position can strike with punches, elbows, knees (where permitted), and hammerfists. Defending fighters use the guard to control posture and limit damage. Rules generally forbid certain attacks, such as strikes to the back of the head and, in many jurisdictions, soccer kicks to a downed opponent.
