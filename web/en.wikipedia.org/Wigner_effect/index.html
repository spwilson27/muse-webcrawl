Wigner effect

Named after: Eugene Wigner

Also called: Discomposition effect

Affects: Graphite moderators in nuclear reactors

The Wigner effect is the displacement of atoms in a solid by neutron radiation, particularly in the graphite moderator of early nuclear reactors. Displaced carbon atoms accumulate as interstitial defects, storing Wigner energy in the lattice.

Risks and annealing

If Wigner energy releases suddenly, graphite can heat sharply. Operators therefore anneal graphite periodically by controlled heating. A botched annealing procedure contributed to the 1957 Windscale fire in England, the worst nuclear accident in British history.

Significance

Understanding the effect, predicted by Eugene Wigner, was essential for graphite-moderated designs including early plutonium production reactors and the Soviet RBMK type involved in the Chernobyl disaster. Radiation damage remains a key concern for reactor materials science.
