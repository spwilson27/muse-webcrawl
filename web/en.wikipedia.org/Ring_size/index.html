Ring size

Ring size is a measurement used to denote the circumference (or sometimes the diameter) of jewellery rings and smart rings. Several incompatible measurement systems are in use around the world.

Standards

The international standard ISO 8653:2016 defines ring size in millimetres of internal circumference. The United States and Canada use a numerical scale in which whole sizes increase by increments of 2.55 mm of circumference; a size 6 ring has an inner circumference of about 51.9 mm. The United Kingdom, Ireland and Australia use an alphabetical scale (A to Z and beyond) in half-size steps. Much of continental Europe historically used the circumference minus 40, while Japan uses a scale related to diameter.

Measurement

Ring size is measured with a mandrel (a tapered graduated rod), a set of sample rings, or by measuring the internal diameter of an existing ring and multiplying by π to obtain circumference. Finger size varies with temperature, time of day and activity, so measurement is best taken when the finger is at normal temperature.

Resizing

Plain metal bands can usually be stretched or cut and re-soldered to change size, but rings with stones set around the full band (eternity rings) or made of materials such as tungsten or ceramic generally cannot be resized.
