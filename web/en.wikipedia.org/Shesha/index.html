Shesha

Shesha (Sanskrit: शेष, "that which remains"), also known as Ananta ("endless"), is a primordial serpent (naga) in Hindu mythology. Shesha is depicted as a giant many-headed serpent upon whose coils the god Vishnu rests in the cosmic ocean, and whose hoods form a canopy over the deity.

Mythology

Shesha is said to be the eldest of the nagas, son of the sage Kashyapa and Kadru. When the universe is dissolved at the end of a kalpa, Shesha remains — hence his name. Tradition holds that Shesha supports the world on his hoods and continually sings the glories of Vishnu from all his mouths. His incarnations include Lakshmana, brother of Rama, and Balarama, brother of Krishna, who in Vaishnava theology are avatars of Shesha himself.

Iconography

Images of Vishnu reclining on Ananta-Shesha (Anantasayana) are among the most celebrated in Indian art, notably the great rock-cut relief at the Dashavatara temple at Deogarh and the reclining Vishnu at Budhanilkantha in Nepal.
