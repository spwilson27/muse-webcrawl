Burseraceae

Burseraceae
Family of flowering plants
Order: Sapindales
Genera: ~19; Species: ~550–700
Common name: Torchwood family

The Burseraceae are a moderate-sized family of flowering plants, also known as the torchwood family. The family includes both trees and shrubs, and is rich in species producing fragrant resins.

Description

Members typically have alternate, pinnately compound leaves and resin ducts in the bark. The flowers are small and usually unisexual, and the fruit is a drupe. Many species exude aromatic oleoresins when the bark is cut.

Economically important genera

The genus Boswellia yields frankincense, and Commiphora yields myrrh — both resins prized since antiquity in incense, perfume and medicine. Bursera species (torchwoods) are characteristic trees of seasonally dry tropical forests in Mexico and Central America, and Canarium species produce edible nuts and elemi resin.

Distribution

The family is pantropical, with centres of diversity in the Neotropics, Africa and Asia. Fossil evidence suggests the group is of considerable antiquity.
