LED

An LED or light-emitting diode is a semiconductor device that emits light when an electric current flows through it, a phenomenon called electroluminescence. Electrons recombine with electron holes within the device, releasing energy as photons; the colour of the light is determined by the energy band gap of the semiconductor.

History

Electroluminescence was reported by H. J. Round in 1907. Practical visible-spectrum LEDs were developed from the 1960s, beginning with red devices, followed by green and yellow. The high-brightness blue LED, developed by Shuji Nakamura and others in the early 1990s, made white light (via a phosphor coating) and full-colour displays possible, and earned the 2014 Nobel Prize in Physics for its inventors.

Uses

LEDs are used as indicator lamps, in displays and televisions, in traffic signals, automotive lighting and as efficient general lighting, where their long life and low energy consumption have displaced incandescent and many fluorescent lamps.
