Marian apparition

A Marian apparition is a reported supernatural appearance by the Virgin Mary, to one or more persons, in a vision or locution. Such apparitions have been reported across many centuries and cultures and are a significant part of Catholic and Orthodox devotion.

History

Accounts of appearances of Mary date to the early centuries of Christianity. Among the most famous investigated apparitions are Our Lady of Guadalupe (1531, Mexico), Our Lady of Lourdes (1858, France) and Our Lady of Fátima (1917, Portugal), each associated with major pilgrimage shrines.

Church investigation

The Catholic Church investigates claimed apparitions through diocesan commissions and the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith, applying criteria such as the moral character of the visionaries, theological conformity of messages, and spiritual fruits. Approval signifies the claim is worthy of belief, though no apparition is required belief for Catholics. In 2024 the Vatican issued new norms emphasising prudential judgments over declarations of supernatural character.

Notable approved apparitions

Approved apparitions include Guadalupe, the Miraculous Medal (Paris, 1830), La Salette (1846), Lourdes, Pontmain (1871), Fátima, Beauraing and Banneux (1932–33), Akita (1973) and Kibeho (1981–89).

Criticism

Sceptics attribute reports to misperception, suggestion, fraud or psychological phenomena, and unapproved claims — such as the long-running Medjugorje case, authorised for pilgrimage without a ruling on supernatural authenticity — remain controversial.
