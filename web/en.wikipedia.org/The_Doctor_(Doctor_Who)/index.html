The Doctor (Doctor Who)

The Doctor is the protagonist of the BBC science fiction television programme Doctor Who. A Time Lord from the planet Gallifrey, the Doctor travels through time and space in the TARDIS, usually with companions, intervening against injustice across the universe. The character has been portrayed by more than a dozen lead actors since 1963.

Regeneration

When the actor playing the part changes — or the character is mortally wounded — the Doctor regenerates into a new body with a new personality, a device introduced in 1966 when William Hartnell left. Successive Doctors were played by Patrick Troughton, Jon Pertwee, Tom Baker (the longest-serving, 1974–81), Peter Davison, Colin Baker, Sylvester McCoy, Paul McGann, Christopher Eccleston, David Tennant, Matt Smith, Peter Capaldi, Jodie Whittaker (the first woman in the role), and Ncuti Gatwa, with John Hurt as the War Doctor in the 50th anniversary special.

Character

The Doctor is centuries old, brilliant, eccentric and compassionate, armed with a sonic screwdriver and an aversion to violence and authority. Later series developed the mythos of the Doctor as the "Timeless Child". The Doctor's greatest enemies include the Daleks, the Cybermen, the Master and the Weeping Angels.

Cultural impact

The Doctor is a British cultural icon; the role's recasting is a national media event, and the character's moral code — "never cruel or cowardly" — is frequently quoted. The programme itself holds the record as the longest-running science fiction television series.
