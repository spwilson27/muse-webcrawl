Grand Canyon National Park

Location: Arizona, United States
Area: 1,217,262 acres (4,926 km²)
Established: February 26, 1919
Governing body: National Park Service

Grand Canyon National Park is a national park of the United States located in northwestern Arizona. The park's central feature is the Grand Canyon, a gorge of the Colorado River, which is often considered one of the Wonders of the World. The park, which covers 1,217,262 acres (1,901.972 sq mi; 4,926.08 km²) of unincorporated area in Coconino and Mohave counties, received more than six million recreational visitors in 2017, second only to Great Smoky Mountains National Park.

The Grand Canyon was designated a national park in 1919 by President Woodrow Wilson, three years after the creation of the National Park Service. It was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1979. The park celebrated its 100th anniversary on February 26, 2019.

Geography

The Grand Canyon is a river valley in the Colorado Plateau that exposes uplifted Proterozoic and Paleozoic strata. The canyon is 277 miles (446 km) long, up to 18 miles (29 km) wide and attains a depth of over a mile (6,093 feet or 1,857 meters). Nearly two billion years of Earth's geological history have been exposed as the Colorado River and its tributaries cut their channels through layer after layer of rock while the Colorado Plateau was uplifted.

The park is divided into the more developed South Rim and the more remote North Rim, which is closed in winter. Elevations range from about 2,000 feet (610 m) along the river to over 8,000 feet (2,400 m) on the North Rim.

History

Native Americans have inhabited the Grand Canyon area for thousands of years; the Pueblo people considered the canyon a holy site and made pilgrimages to it. The first European known to have viewed the canyon was García López de Cárdenas, who arrived in 1540 as part of the Coronado expedition. In 1869 Major John Wesley Powell led the first recorded expedition through the canyon on the Colorado River.

President Theodore Roosevelt was a major proponent of preservation of the Grand Canyon area and visited it on numerous occasions to enjoy its scenery. He designated it a national monument in 1908, a status roughly equivalent to today's national parks.

Tourism and activities

The South Rim is open all year and is the destination of most visitors, who come to viewpoints such as Mather Point, Yavapai Observation Station, and Desert View. Popular activities include hiking the Bright Angel and South Kaibab trails, mule trips to Phantom Ranch at the canyon bottom, and whitewater rafting on the Colorado River. The North Rim receives only about ten percent of park visitors.
