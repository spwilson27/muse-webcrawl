Unitarian Universalist Association (UUA)
Founded: 1961
Headquarters: Boston, Massachusetts
Tradition: Unitarian Universalism

The Unitarian Universalist Association (UUA) is a liberal religious association in the United States, formed in 1961 by the consolidation of the American Unitarian Association and the Universalist Church of America. It brings together roughly a thousand congregations, primarily in North America, in a covenantal rather than creedal association.

Beliefs and practices

Unitarian Universalism draws on Jewish and Christian roots but embraces insights from many religious and humanist traditions. Members affirm shared principles — historically expressed as seven principles affirming the inherent worth and dignity of every person, justice, and the interdependent web of existence — rather than any common doctrine. Congregations are self-governing, and the association is known for progressive social witness, including early support for civil rights, LGBTQ equality, and environmental action.
