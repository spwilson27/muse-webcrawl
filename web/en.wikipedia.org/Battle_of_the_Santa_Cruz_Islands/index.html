The Battle of the Santa Cruz Islands, fought during October 25–27, 1942, was the fourth carrier battle of the Pacific campaign of World War II and the fourth major naval engagement fought between the United States Navy and the Imperial Japanese Navy during the long and strategically important Guadalcanal campaign.

Background

In late October 1942, Japanese ground forces on Guadalcanal attempted to recapture Henderson Field, supported by a large naval force built around the carriers Shōkaku, Zuikaku, Zuihō and Jun'yō. Admiral William Halsey, newly in command in the South Pacific, sent the carriers Enterprise and Hornet to intercept.

The battle

On October 26 the opposing carriers struck each other almost simultaneously. Japanese aircraft sank Hornet and damaged Enterprise, while American dive bombers badly damaged Shōkaku and the light carrier Zuihō. Japanese surface ships later finished off the abandoned Hornet.

Aftermath

The battle was a tactical victory for Japan, but the Japanese ground offensive on Guadalcanal failed anyway, and Japan's heavy losses among its experienced carrier aircrews could not be replaced. The damaged carriers were out of action for months, leaving Japan unable to contest the next American operations around Guadalcanal.
