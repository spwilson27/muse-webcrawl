Shoshone Falls

Shoshone Falls is a waterfall on the Snake River near Twin Falls, Idaho, United States.

Shoshone Falls (/ʃoʊˈʃoʊn/) is a waterfall on the Snake River in south-central Idaho, United States, located approximately 3 miles (4.8 km) northeast of the city of Twin Falls. Sometimes called the "Niagara of the West", Shoshone Falls is 212 feet (65 m) high — 36 feet (11 m) higher than Niagara Falls — and flows over a rim nearly 1,000 feet (300 m) wide.

Geology and flow

The falls were formed some 14,000–17,000 years ago during the catastrophic Bonneville Flood, when pluvial Lake Bonneville overflowed and scoured the Snake River plain, carving the Snake River Canyon and leaving the basalt ledge over which the river now drops. Today the flow over the falls varies greatly with the seasons and with upstream irrigation diversions and hydropower operations: flows are typically highest in spring and are often sharply reduced in late summer, when much of the river is diverted for agriculture in the Magic Valley.

Visiting

Shoshone Falls Park, operated by the city of Twin Falls, provides viewing platforms, trails and picnic areas overlooking the falls and the canyon. The falls are one of Idaho's best-known natural attractions, and the surrounding rim area includes the site of Evel Knievel's attempted 1974 rocket jump over the Snake River Canyon nearby.
