Solicitors Regulation Authority

Formation2007
PurposeRegulation of solicitors and law firms
JurisdictionEngland and Wales
Parent bodyThe Law Society (regulatory functions separated)

The Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA) is the regulatory body for solicitors and law firms in England and Wales. It is the independent regulatory arm of the Law Society of England and Wales, established following the Legal Services Act 2007, which required approved regulators to separate their representative and regulatory functions.

Functions

The SRA sets and enforces standards for more than 200,000 practising solicitors and around 10,000 regulated firms. Its work includes setting qualification and training requirements — since 2021 through the Solicitors Qualifying Examination (SQE) — authorising individuals and firms to practise, setting the SRA Principles and Codes of Conduct, investigating misconduct, and prosecuting cases before the independent Solicitors Disciplinary Tribunal, which can fine, suspend or strike off solicitors. The SRA also operates a compensation fund for clients who lose money through a solicitor's dishonesty or failure to account.

Reforms

The SRA's reforms of 2019 introduced separate codes for individuals and firms and permitted "freelance solicitors" to practise independently, while the SQE replaced the previous Legal Practice Course route to qualification with centralised assessments.
