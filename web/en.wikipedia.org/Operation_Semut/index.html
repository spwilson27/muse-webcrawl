Operation Semut

Operation Semut
Date: March – October 1945
Location: Sarawak, northern Borneo
Part of: Borneo campaign (1945)
Unit: Services Reconnaissance Department (Z Special Unit)

Operation Semut was a series of operations conducted in 1945 by the Services Reconnaissance Department (SRD), also known as Z Special Unit, an Allied special forces unit in the South West Pacific during the Second World War. The operations were conducted in Japanese-occupied Sarawak, in northern Borneo, to gather intelligence and train and arm the indigenous population in preparation for the Allied invasion of Borneo.

The parties

The operation was divided into four parties — Semut I, II, III, and IV — inserted by parachute from March 1945. Semut I, under Major Tom Harrisson, dropped into the Kelabit Highlands around Bario and recruited the Kelabit, Dayak, and other peoples; Semut II operated in the Baram valley under Major G. S. Carter; Semut III worked the Rejang River under Captain Eric Edmeades; and Semut IV operated on the coast. Together they built a guerrilla force of over a thousand local fighters.

Results

Semut forces provided intelligence for the Australian landings of Operation Oboe Six, harassed Japanese units retreating into the interior, and are credited with killing or capturing several hundred Japanese troops while rescuing downed Allied airmen and liberating prisoners and internees. Harrisson's postwar account, World Within, describes the campaign.
