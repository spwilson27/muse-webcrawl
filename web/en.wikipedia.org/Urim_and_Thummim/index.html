Urim and Thummim

TraditionAncient Israelite religion
Used byHigh Priest of Israel
PurposeDivination; obtaining divine decisions
Attested inExodus 28, Leviticus 8, Numbers 27, 1 Samuel

In the Hebrew Bible, the Urim and the Thummim (Hebrew: הָאוּרִים וְהַתֻּמִּים) are elements of the breastplate worn by the High Priest of Israel, associated with divination in general, and cleromancy — the casting of lots — in particular. Most scholars hold that the Urim and Thummim were a pair of objects used to obtain yes-or-no answers to questions put to God.

Biblical references

Exodus 28:30 directs that the Urim and Thummim be placed in the breastplate of judgment over Aaron's heart. In Numbers 27, Joshua is to stand before Eleazar the priest, "who shall inquire for him by the judgment of the Urim before the LORD". Saul seeks guidance through the Urim in 1 Samuel 28 but receives no answer. Ezra 2:63 records that returning exiles had to wait "until a priest with Urim and Thummim should arise" to settle questions of priestly descent, suggesting the objects were no longer available after the Babylonian exile.

Nature and meaning

The names are often interpreted as "lights" and "perfections", or, following older scholarship, "curse" and "innocence" — suggestive of lots used in judicial ordeals. What the objects physically were is unknown; proposals include two stones, a set of marked lots, or precious stones on the breastplate itself. Rabbinic tradition held that the letters of the tribal names engraved on the breastplate stones lit up to spell out God's answer.

Later use

The phrase "Urim and Thummim" became an emblem of divine guidance: Yale University bears the words in Hebrew on its seal alongside the Latin Lux et Veritas. In the Latter Day Saint tradition, Joseph Smith described using "interpreters", identified with the biblical Urim and Thummim, in connection with the translation of the Book of Mormon.
