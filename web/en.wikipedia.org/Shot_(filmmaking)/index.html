Shot (filmmaking)

Definition: Continuous unedited footage between cuts
Sizes: Extreme wide to extreme close-up
Movements: Pan, tilt, tracking, zoom

In filmmaking and video production, a shot is a series of frames recorded during an uninterrupted period of filming — the continuous footage between the camera starting and stopping, or, in the finished film, between two edits. Shots are the basic building blocks of film editing and cinematography.

Shot sizes

Shots are classified by how much of the subject and scene they frame: extreme long (or wide) shots establish locations, long shots show the full body, medium shots frame from about the waist up, close-ups concentrate on a face or detail, and extreme close-ups isolate a small feature. Terms such as cowboy shot and two-shot describe common variants.

Camera movement and angle

A shot may be static or involve movement: panning, tilting, tracking/dollying, crane moves or handheld motion, as well as zooming. Camera angle — high, low, eye-level, Dutch — and shot duration strongly affect the viewer's experience. The long take, or plan-séquence, extends a single shot to cover an entire scene.
