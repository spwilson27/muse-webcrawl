Aoraki / Mount Cook, the highest peak in New Zealand
Elevation3,724 m (12,218 ft)
RangeSouthern Alps
LocationSouth Island, New Zealand

Aoraki / Mount Cook is the highest mountain in New Zealand, at 3,724 metres (12,218 ft) above sea level. It stands in the Southern Alps, the mountain chain running the length of the South Island, within Aoraki / Mount Cook National Park in the Mackenzie District of Canterbury.

Geography

The mountain has three summits — Low Peak, Middle Peak and High Peak — arranged along a summit ridge. The Tasman Glacier lies to the east and the Hooker Glacier to the southwest. It is part of the Te Wahipounamu World Heritage Site. Sudden weather changes and avalanche-prone slopes make it a serious mountaineering objective; more than 200 climbers have died in the park since exploration began.

History

Aboriginal Māori knew the peak as Aoraki, an ancestor figure of Ngāi Tahu. Europeans sighted it from the coast in the 17th century (Tasman's expedition) and it was named Mount Cook for Captain James Cook. The first ascent was achieved by Tom Fyfe, Jack Clarke and George Graham on 25 December 1894, after near-successful attempts including that of Irishman William Spotswood Green in 1882. A 1991 rockfall from the summit reduced the mountain's height from 3,764 m; resurveying in 2014 set the current figure at 3,724 m.

The official dual name Aoraki / Mount Cook was adopted in 1998 under the Ngai Tahu Claims Settlement Act. Nearby Mount Cook Village, with the Hermitage Hotel, serves walkers and climbers, and the Hooker Valley Track is one of New Zealand's most popular short walks.
