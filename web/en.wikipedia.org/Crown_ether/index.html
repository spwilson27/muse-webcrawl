Crown ether

Illustration: Crown ether
Crown ethers
Class: cyclic chemical compounds
First reported: 1967
Discoverer: Charles J. Pedersen

Crown ethers are cyclic chemical compounds consisting of a ring of several ether groups, most commonly oligomers of ethylene oxide. The repeating unit is –CH2CH2O–, and important members include tetramers (4 oxygens), pentamers (5), and hexamers (6). They are named for their crown-like three-dimensional shape when complexed with a cation.

Binding

Crown ethers strongly bind certain metal cations, forming stable complexes: 18-crown-6 binds potassium ions especially well, 15-crown-5 suits sodium, and 12-crown-4 suits lithium. The cavity size relative to the ion diameter governs selectivity.

Discovery and applications

Charles J. Pedersen discovered crown ethers serendipitously in 1967 while working at DuPont, and shared the 1987 Nobel Prize in Chemistry with Donald Cram and Jean-Marie Lehn for work on host–guest chemistry. Crown ethers are used as phase-transfer catalysts, in ion-selective electrodes, and in research on molecular machines and supramolecular chemistry.
