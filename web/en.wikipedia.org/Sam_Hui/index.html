Samuel Hui Koon-kit (Chinese: 許冠傑; born 6 September 1948), usually known as Sam Hui, is a Hong Kong singer-songwriter and actor. He is widely credited with popularising Cantopop by incorporating colloquial Cantonese and the everyday experiences of working-class Hong Kong people into his lyrics, and is revered in Hong Kong as the “God of Song” (歌神).

Early life and education

Hui was born in Hong Kong into a family originally from Guangzhou. His father was a civil servant and his mother a Chinese opera singer. He is the fourth of five children; his elder brothers Michael Hui and Ricky Hui became famous comedians and actors. He attended the University of Hong Kong, graduating in 1971 with a degree in psychology.

In the late 1960s Hui was the lead singer of the band the Lotus, and became known for singing English-language pop songs on television. His early performances in English earned him local fame but little money, and he later switched to Cantonese, a decisive move in the development of modern Cantopop.

Music career

Hui’s breakthrough came with the theme songs he wrote for the Hui brothers’ comedy films. The title song of Games Gamblers Play (1974), written and sung in streetwise Cantonese, was a massive hit and demonstrated that songs in the local dialect could dominate the charts. The soundtrack album is considered a landmark of Cantopop.

Subsequent albums, including The Private Eyes (1976), The Contract (1978) and Security Unlimited (1981), paired Hui’s witty, often satirical lyrics with catchy melodies. Songs such as “Eiffel Tower Above the Clouds” (ⓜ塔凌雲), his very first Cantonese composition, “Tsim Sha Tsui Susanna”, and “Just for Fun” became standards. His lyrics captured the aspirations, grievances and humour of ordinary Hong Kong people during the city’s rapid economic rise.

Hui announced his retirement after a farewell concert series in 1991–92, but returned to the stage in 2003–04 following the deaths of fellow stars Leslie Cheung and Anita Mui, dedicating concerts to them and to the city during the SARS crisis. He has performed periodically since.

Film career

Alongside his brother Michael, Hui starred in a string of record-breaking Cantonese comedies in the 1970s: Games Gamblers Play (1974), The Last Message (1975), The Private Eyes (1976), The Contract (1978) and Security Unlimited (1981). He later headlined the Aces Go Places action-comedy series (1982 onwards) with Karl Maka and Sylvia Chang, playing the suave thief King Kong.

Legacy

Hui is regarded as the founding figure of Cantopop as a serious commercial and cultural force. By writing in vernacular Cantonese about daily life, he elevated the status of the language in popular music and paved the way for later stars such as Leslie Cheung, Alan Tam and Anita Mui.
