Isotretinoin, also known as 13-cis-retinoic acid and sold formerly under the brand name Accutane, is a medication used to treat severe acne that has not responded to other treatments. It is a retinoid — a derivative of vitamin A — and is regarded as the most effective acne therapy available, frequently producing long-term remission after a single course of several months. It is also used, less commonly, for certain skin cancers and other dermatological conditions.

Medical uses

Isotretinoin is indicated for severe nodular or cystic acne, acne at risk of permanent scarring, and moderate acne that fails to respond to oral antibiotics and topical therapy. Typical treatment is a daily oral dose of about 0.5–1 mg per kg of body weight for 15–20 weeks, often targeting a cumulative dose of 120–150 mg/kg. Unlike other acne treatments, it affects all four pathogenic factors of acne: it dramatically shrinks the sebaceous glands and reduces sebum production, normalizes follicular keratinization, reduces colonization by Cutibacterium acnes, and has anti-inflammatory effects.

Adverse effects

Common side effects mimic vitamin A excess: very dry skin and lips (cheilitis occurs in most patients), dry eyes and nose, nosebleeds, sun sensitivity, and muscle aches. Blood monitoring is used because the drug can raise triglycerides and liver enzymes.

Teratogenicity

Isotretinoin is one of the most potent known human teratogens: exposure during pregnancy carries a high risk of severe birth defects affecting the face, heart, brain, and thymus. Prescribing is therefore tightly controlled. In the United States the FDA's iPLEDGE programme (from 2006) requires registration of prescribers, pharmacies, and patients, negative pregnancy tests, and contraception commitments for patients who can become pregnant; similar pregnancy-prevention programmes operate in Europe and elsewhere.

Other controversies

Possible associations with depression and suicidal behaviour have been investigated for decades without a demonstrated causal link, though monitoring is advised. A link with inflammatory bowel disease was alleged in litigation and popular reporting, but large epidemiological studies have not established causation.

History

The compound was developed by Hoffmann-La Roche, building on research into retinoids by Werner Bollag and others. It was approved in the United States in 1982 under the name Accutane and became a landmark dermatology drug; Roche withdrew the brand in 2009 amid litigation costs, but generic versions remain widely used.
