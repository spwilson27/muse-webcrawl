Psalm 139

Psalm 139 is the 139th psalm of the Book of Psalms, beginning in English in the King James Version: "O LORD, thou hast searched me, and known me." In Latin it is known as "Domine probasti me et cognovisti me". The psalm is a hymn attributed to David and is part of the third section of the Hebrew Bible.

Content

The psalm celebrates God's omniscience and omnipresence: the speaker reflects that God knows his thoughts, words, and actions before they occur, and that there is nowhere — heaven, Sheol, or the far side of the sea — beyond God's presence. It includes the well-known passage on being "fearfully and wonderfully made", describing God's knowledge of the speaker in the womb, and closes with a plea for God to search and test the speaker's heart.

Use

Psalm 139 is recited in Jewish liturgy, including in some traditions before the High Holy Days, and appears in Christian lectionaries and in the Liturgy of the Hours. Its verses about life in the womb are frequently cited in debates about abortion, while its themes of divine intimacy make it popular in devotional settings and music.
