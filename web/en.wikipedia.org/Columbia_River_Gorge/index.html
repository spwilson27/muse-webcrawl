Columbia River Gorge

LengthAbout 80 miles (130 km)
DepthUp to 4,000 feet (1,200 m)
LocationOregon–Washington border, United States
DesignationNational Scenic Area (1986)

The Columbia River Gorge is a canyon of the Columbia River in the Pacific Northwest of the United States, cutting through the Cascade Range for about 80 miles between the Portland metropolitan area and The Dalles. It forms much of the border between Oregon and Washington and reaches depths of up to 4,000 feet.

Formation

The gorge was carved by the Columbia River through volcanic rocks of the Cascades, and its modern form was scoured by the Missoula floods, cataclysmic glacial outbursts at the end of the last ice age that swept through the canyon with enormous force.

Features

The western gorge is a temperate rainforest of steep cliffs and more than 70 waterfalls, including Multnomah Falls, Oregon's tallest at 620 feet. Eastward the climate dries into grassland and oak savanna. Persistent winds funnelling through the gorge make it one of the world's premier windsurfing and kiteboarding sites, centred on Hood River.

In 1986 the United States Congress designated the Columbia River Gorge National Scenic Area, the first of its kind, to protect its scenery, ecosystems, and cultural heritage while allowing the communities within it to continue developing.
