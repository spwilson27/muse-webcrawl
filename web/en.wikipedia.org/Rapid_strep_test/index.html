Rapid strep test

The rapid strep test (RST), or rapid antigen detection test (RADT), is a point-of-care diagnostic test used to detect group A streptococcal (GAS) antigens in a throat swab, most often to distinguish streptococcal pharyngitis ("strep throat") from viral sore throat.

Procedure and performance

A swab is rubbed over the tonsils and back of the throat, then treated with reagents that extract the group A carbohydrate antigen; immunologic methods such as lateral-flow immunoassay produce a result in about five to ten minutes. The test has high specificity (around 95%) but moderate sensitivity (roughly 70–90%), so negative results in children and adolescents are commonly confirmed by throat culture, the reference standard.

Use

Confirming GAS infection matters because antibiotic treatment shortens symptoms slightly and prevents complications such as acute rheumatic fever. Testing is generally recommended only when clinical features suggest streptococcal rather than viral infection, since carriers can test positive without true infection.
