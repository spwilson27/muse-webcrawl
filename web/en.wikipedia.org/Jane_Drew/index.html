Jane Drew

Dame Jane Beverly Drew (24 March 1911 – 27 July 1996) was an English modernist architect and town planner. She worked in London, West Africa, India and Iran, often in partnership with her husband Maxwell Fry.

Born24 March 1911, Thornton Heath, Surrey
Died27 July 1996, Cotherstone, County Durham
OccupationArchitect, town planner
SpouseMaxwell Fry

Early life and education

Drew studied at the Architectural Association School of Architecture in London, graduating in 1934. She became involved with the Modern Architectural Research Group (MARS).

Career

During the Second World War she worked on housing and planning in Britain. With Fry she undertook major projects in Ghana and Nigeria, including schools and the University of Ibadan. In India she collaborated with Le Corbusier and Pierre Jeanneret on the new capital of Chandigarh, designing housing, schools and health buildings suited to the climate. She also planned parts of Tehran and worked on the Open University campus at Milton Keynes.

Later life and honours

Drew was made a Dame Commander of the Order of the British Empire in 1996, shortly before her death. Her work is noted for adapting modernism to local climate and social needs.
