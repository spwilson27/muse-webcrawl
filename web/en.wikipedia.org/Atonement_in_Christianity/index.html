Atonement in Christianity

Atonement
Field: Christian theology
Central event: Crucifixion and resurrection of Jesus
Major theories: Christus Victor, satisfaction, penal substitution, moral influence, ransom

In Christian theology, atonement is the reconciliation of God and humankind through the sacrificial death of Jesus Christ. It is the central doctrine explaining how, through the life, suffering, death, and resurrection of Jesus, human beings can be forgiven and restored to a right relationship with God.

Theories of atonement

Christians have explained the mechanism of atonement in several ways. The oldest, the ransom theory and its development Christus Victor, picture Christ's death as defeating the powers of sin, death, and the devil and liberating humanity. Anselm of Canterbury's satisfaction theory (in Cur Deus Homo, c. 1098) argued that Christ's death made satisfaction for the debt owed to God's honour. Reformation theologians developed penal substitution, in which Christ bears the punishment due to sinners. Peter Abelard's moral influence theory emphasises that the cross demonstrates God's love and moves humans to repentance. Modern theology also includes governmental, scapegoat, and participatory models.

Biblical basis

The New Testament presents Jesus's death using a rich set of images: sacrificial (the Lamb of God), legal (justification), relational (reconciliation, as in 2 Corinthians 5), and victorious (Colossians 2:15). The word "atonement" in English, "at-one-ment," entered theological vocabulary through translations influenced by William Tyndale.
