Suggs

Graham McPherson (born 13 January 1961), known by the stage name Suggs, is an English singer-songwriter, musician, radio personality and actor. In a music career spanning over 40 years, he came to prominence in the late 1970s as the lead singer of the ska band Madness, which enjoyed many hits in the United Kingdom during the 1980s, including "One Step Beyond", "Baggy Trousers", "Our House", and "It Must Be Love".

Career

Madness were central to the late-1970s two-tone ska revival alongside bands such as The Specials. Suggs fronted the band through its initial run of chart success, its 1986 split, and its reformation in the 1990s; Madness have continued to record and perform, including appearances at the 2012 London Olympics closing ceremony and on the roof of Buckingham Palace for the Queen's Diamond Jubilee.

As a solo artist, Suggs released two albums in the 1990s, The Lone Ranger (1995) and The Three Pyramids Club (1998), and had a UK top-ten hit with a cover of Simon & Garfunkel's "Cecilia". He has also worked extensively in television and radio, presenting programmes including Night Fever and documentaries on London, and published an autobiography, Suggs and the City, in 2009, later followed by the memoir That Close.
