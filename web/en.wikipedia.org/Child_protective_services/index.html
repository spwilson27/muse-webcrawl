Child protective services

Child protective services (CPS) is the name of a government agency in many states of the United States responsible for providing child protection, which includes responding to reports of child abuse or neglect. Some states use other names, often attempting to reflect more family-centred (as opposed to child-centred) practices, such as "Department of Children and Family Services" (DCFS).

Functions

CPS agencies operate hotlines receiving reports of suspected maltreatment, investigate or assess allegations, and provide services intended to keep children safe. Where a child cannot remain safely at home, the agency may seek court-ordered placement in foster care or kinship care, with a statutory preference for reunification where possible. Most referrals do not result in removal; many families receive in-home services instead.

Legal framework

In the United States, child protection is primarily a state responsibility guided by federal laws including the Child Abuse Prevention and Treatment Act (CAPTA, 1974), the Adoption and Safe Families Act (1997), and the Family First Prevention Services Act (2018). Mandatory reporting laws require professionals such as teachers and physicians to report suspected abuse. Comparable systems exist in other countries under various names — for example, children's services departments in the United Kingdom and youth protection services in Canada.

Criticism and reform

Child protective systems are criticised from multiple directions: for intervening too slowly in cases that end in child deaths, and for removing children unnecessarily in other cases, with documented racial and socioeconomic disproportionality in investigations and foster placements. Reform efforts emphasise prevention services, kinship placements, and reducing entries into foster care.
