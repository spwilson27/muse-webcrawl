Coffee production in Colombia

Coffee production in Colombia has a reputation for producing mild, well-balanced coffee beans and is one of the country's best-known exports. Colombia is the world's third-largest coffee producer after Brazil and Vietnam (by some measures second in arabica), and is the largest producer of washed arabica. Coffee is grown largely by smallholders on steep Andean slopes in the Colombian coffee growing axis, a region inscribed by UNESCO as the Coffee Cultural Landscape of Colombia.

History

Coffee arrived in Colombia in the early 18th century, likely with Jesuit priests, and commercial exports began in the 19th century from the department of Santander before production spread to Antioquia and the central cordillera. In 1927 growers founded the National Federation of Coffee Growers of Colombia (FNC), which has since organized marketing, research, and extension services, and created the fictional character Juan Valdez in 1958 to promote Colombian coffee abroad. The collapse of the International Coffee Agreement quotas in 1989, outbreaks of coffee leaf rust, and climate pressures have repeatedly challenged the sector.

Cultivation

Colombian coffee is almost exclusively arabica, grown at elevations of roughly 1,200–1,800 metres in volcanic soils with two rainy seasons in many areas, which permit a main harvest and a smaller mid-crop (mitaca). Most farms are small — the great majority under five hectares — and cherries are typically hand-picked when ripe, wet-processed on the farm, and sun-dried. Varieties such as Caturra, Castillo, and Colombia were developed by the federation's research institute, Cenicafé, for yield and rust resistance.

Economy and culture

Coffee supports hundreds of thousands of rural families and remains a pillar of Colombia's agricultural exports and national identity. Regional tourism around the coffee landscape, specialty and single-origin marketing, and sustainability certifications have grown in importance as producers seek higher prices than the commodity market provides.
