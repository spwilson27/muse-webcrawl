MPEG-4

MPEG-4's object-based scene composition

Developed by: Moving Picture Experts Group (MPEG)
First published: 1998–1999 (ISO/IEC 14496)
Known parts: Part 2 (video), Part 10 (H.264/AVC), Part 14 (MP4 container)

MPEG-4 is a group of international standards for the compression and transmission of digital audio and visual data, primarily for video. It was introduced in late 1998 and designated ISO/IEC 14496 by the Moving Picture Experts Group. MPEG-4 expands on MPEG-1 and MPEG-2 by treating a scene as a composition of separate media objects that can be manipulated, transmitted, and rendered independently.

Parts

The standard is divided into many parts. Part 2 (Advanced Simple Profile) underpins codecs such as DivX and Xvid. Part 10, also standardised jointly as H.264/AVC, became the dominant video codec of the 2000s and 2010s, used in Blu-ray, streaming, and broadcasting. Part 14 defines the MP4 file format (.mp4), built on the ISO base media file format and Apple's QuickTime container. Part 3 covers audio, including AAC, the widely used audio codec.

Features and licensing

MPEG-4 introduced object-based coding, error resilience for mobile networks, scalable streams, and support for interactivity, 2D/3D graphics, and digital rights management hooks. Many MPEG-4 technologies are patent-encumbered and were licensed through patent pools managed by MPEG LA, a factor that spurred interest in royalty-free alternatives such as VP8/VP9 and AV1.
