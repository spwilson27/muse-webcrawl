Tubeless tire

A tubeless tire (also spelled tyre in British English) is a pneumatic tire that does not require a separate inner tube. Instead, the tire and the rim of the wheel together form an airtight seal, with the valve mounted directly on the rim. Tubeless tires are standard on modern automobiles, motorcycles, and increasingly on bicycles.

Design

The tire bead seats against a specially shaped rim flange so that air pressure pushes the bead outward, maintaining the seal. A liquid sealant may be used in bicycle applications to plug small punctures automatically. Because there is no inner tube, a puncture usually causes a slow leak rather than a sudden blowout, improving safety.

History

Several inventors patented tubeless designs in the early 20th century, but the design was first made commercially practical by the B.F. Goodrich Company in 1947, with production tires appearing on Packard automobiles in 1954. Tubeless tires largely replaced tube-type tires on passenger cars by the 1960s.

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages include reduced risk of pinch flats, lower weight, and the ability to run lower pressures on bicycles for better traction. Disadvantages include the need for airtight rims and more difficult roadside repair of major damage.
