Layering

Layering is a horticultural method of plant propagation in which a stem is induced to form roots while still attached to the parent plant, then detached as an independent plant. It exploits the natural rooting that occurs when stems touch moist soil, as in brambles and strawberries' runners.

Techniques

In simple layering a flexible low branch is bent, pinned in a shallow trench and covered with soil, tip exposed; after rooting it is severed. Air layering (marcotting), used for rubber plants and magnolias, wounds an aerial stem, wraps it in moist sphagnum and plastic until roots form. Other forms include mound (stool) layering for apple rootstocks, serpentine layering for vines, and tip layering in blackberries.

Advantages

Layering reliably clones cultivars that root poorly from cuttings, producing a larger new plant supported by the parent until established, at the cost of yielding few plants per parent.
