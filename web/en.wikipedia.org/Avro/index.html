Avro

Avro was a British aircraft manufacturer founded in 1910 by Alliott Verdon Roe in Manchester. Its full name was A.V. Roe and Company; it became one of Britain's major aircraft firms before merging into Hawker Siddeley Aviation in 1963.

Notable aircraft

The Avro 504 trainer of 1913 served for decades. In the Second World War Avro produced the Lancaster heavy bomber — one of the RAF's principal night bombers and the type used in the 1943 Dambusters raid — as well as the Anson and York. Post-war types included the Shackleton maritime patrol aircraft, the Vulcan delta-wing V-bomber, the Avro 748 turboprop airliner and the Tudor and Lancastrian airliners. Its Canadian subsidiary, Avro Canada, built the CF-105 Arrow interceptor, cancelled in 1959.

Legacy

After absorption into Hawker Siddeley and then British Aerospace, the Avro name was revived for the Avro RJ family of regional jets (developed from the BAe 146) produced into the early 2000s. The firm's founder had earlier built the Roe I Triplane, among the first British aircraft to fly.
