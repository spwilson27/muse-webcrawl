The Armed Forces Movement (Portuguese: Movimento das Forças Armadas, MFA) was a clandestine organization of junior and mid-ranking officers in the Portuguese armed forces that overthrew the authoritarian Estado Novo regime in the Carnation Revolution of 25 April 1974, ending nearly five decades of dictatorship and launching Portugal's transition to democracy and the decolonization of its African empire.

Origins

The movement grew out of professional grievances among career captains — over promotion rules that favoured militia officers — and widening disillusionment with the unwinnable colonial wars in Angola, Mozambique, and Guinea-Bissau, which had consumed the army since 1961. General António de Spínola's book Portugal and the Future (February 1974), arguing the wars could not be won militarily, emboldened the conspirators.

The revolution and its aftermath

In the early hours of 25 April 1974, MFA units seized strategic points in Lisbon; the signals for the operation were two songs broadcast on radio, the second being "Grândola, Vila Morena". Crowds placed red carnations in soldiers' rifle barrels, giving the revolution its name. The MFA then dominated the turbulent revolutionary period (PREC) of 1974–75, driving decolonization and land reform, before moderate officers prevailed in the crisis of 25 November 1975, clearing the way for the democratic constitution of 1976.
