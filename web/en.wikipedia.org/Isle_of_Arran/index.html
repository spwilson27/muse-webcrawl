Isle of Arran

Goat Fell and Brodick Bay
Location: Firth of Clyde, Scotland
Area: 432 km²
Highest point: Goat Fell (874 m)
Population: ~4,600

The Isle of Arran (Scottish Gaelic: Eilean Arainn) is an island off the west coast of Scotland, in the Firth of Clyde. It is the largest island in the firth and the seventh-largest Scottish island. Because it contains examples of nearly every geological formation found in Scotland, it is often described as "Scotland in miniature."

Geography

A fault line, the Highland Boundary Fault, crosses the island from northeast to southwest, dividing rugged mountainous terrain in the north — dominated by Goat Fell (874 m) — from gentler rolling farmland in the south. The main village and ferry port is Brodick, with other settlements including Lamlash, Whiting Bay, Lochranza and Blackwaterfoot.

History

Arran has been inhabited since the early Neolithic period; the Machrie Moor stone circles are among its best-known prehistoric remains. The island was part of the Norse-Gaelic world before passing to the Scottish crown after the Battle of Largs (1263) and the Treaty of Perth (1266). Brodick Castle was long a seat of the Dukes of Hamilton. In the 19th century the Highland Clearances removed many tenants to Canada.

Economy and wildlife

Tourism is the mainstay of the economy, supported by ferry links to Ardrossan and Claonaig. Arran is known for whisky distilleries at Lochranza and Lagg, and for wildlife including red deer, golden eagles, red squirrels and seals; the waters around Lamlash Bay include Scotland's first no-take marine reserve.
