Category 7 cable

Category 7 cable (Cat 7, ISO/IEC class F) is a shielded twisted pair cabling standard specified for frequencies up to 600 MHz. Each of the four pairs is individually foil-shielded, with an overall braid — an S/FTP construction — giving strong protection against crosstalk and external interference. It was designed to support 10 Gigabit Ethernet over 100 metres.

Connectors and adoption

Class F was originally specified with GG45 or TERA connectors rather than the familiar 8P8C (RJ45), though Cat 7 cable fitted with RJ45 connectors is widely sold. Category 7 was never recognised by the North American TIA/EIA standards, which moved instead to Category 6A for standardised 10GBASE-T. An enhanced Category 7A (class FA) extends bandwidth to 1,000 MHz and saw use in parts of Europe, including for carrying cable television signals.
