Personal genomics

A direct-to-consumer DNA testing kit.

Personal genomics or consumer genetics is the branch of genomics concerned with the sequencing, analysis, and interpretation of the genome of an individual person. Falling prices for DNA sequencing after the Human Genome Project made it possible to offer genetic testing directly to consumers, outside traditional clinical settings.

Methods

Consumer services typically begin with a saliva sample or cheek swab mailed to a laboratory. Most early services used genotyping microarrays that read several hundred thousand single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), from which ancestry composition, carrier status, and statistical health-risk estimates are inferred. Whole-genome and whole-exome sequencing have also become available to consumers, providing far more complete data.

Applications

  • Ancestry and genealogy — estimating ethnic composition and finding genetic relatives in company databases
  • Health risks — probabilistic estimates for conditions such as type 2 diabetes, macular degeneration, and late-onset Alzheimer's disease
  • Carrier screening — identifying carriers of recessive disorders such as cystic fibrosis
  • Traits and wellness — reports on caffeine metabolism, lactose tolerance, and other traits
  • Pharmacogenomics — variants affecting drug metabolism, such as those in CYP2C19

Criticism and regulation

Personal genomics has been criticized over the clinical validity of risk predictions, the risk of misinterpretation without genetic counseling, privacy concerns over large consumer DNA databases (including law-enforcement access, as in the Golden State Killer case), and gaps in anti-discrimination law — in the United States, GINA does not cover life, disability, or long-term-care insurance. In 2013 the U.S. Food and Drug Administration ordered 23andMe to stop marketing health reports until they were validated; the company later received authorization for a limited set of health risk and carrier reports. Other jurisdictions regulate consumer genetic testing to varying degrees.
