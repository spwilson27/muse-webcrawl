Novaculite

Novaculite
TypeMetamorphic / sedimentary rock (microcrystalline quartz)
Main sourceOuachita Mountains, Arkansas, USA
UsesWhetstones ("Arkansas stones"), abrasives

Novaculite is a dense, hard, fine-grained siliceous rock composed almost entirely of microcrystalline quartz. The name comes from the Latin novacula, "razor", reflecting its best-known use: as a whetstone for sharpening blades.

Occurrence

The classic source is the Devonian–Mississippian Arkansas Novaculite formation of the Ouachita Mountains in Arkansas and Oklahoma, where beds of white, grey, black and reddish novaculite were folded during the Ouachita orogeny. Native Americans quarried the stone for tools and weapons long before European settlement.

Uses

Graded novaculite is sold as "Arkansas stones" — soft, hard, black and translucent grades — prized by woodworkers, surgeons and jewellers for producing very fine edges. Ground novaculite (tripoli) is also used as a mild abrasive and filler.
