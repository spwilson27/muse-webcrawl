New Zealand Defence Force

The New Zealand Defence Force (NZDF) is the armed forces of New Zealand. It consists of three services: the Royal New Zealand Navy, the New Zealand Army and the Royal New Zealand Air Force, unified under a single command since 1990.

Role

The NZDF defends New Zealand's territory and interests, contributes to regional security in the South Pacific, and takes part in international peacekeeping and humanitarian operations. It also supports civil authorities in search and rescue, fisheries protection, Antarctic logistics and disaster relief, such as after the Canterbury earthquakes.

Organisation

The Chief of Defence Force commands all three services through Headquarters Joint Forces New Zealand at Trentham. The Governor-General is commander-in-chief, acting on ministerial advice. New Zealand's defence policy combines self-reliance with close cooperation with Australia and Five Eyes partners, though the country has maintained a nuclear-free policy since the 1980s, which ended the ANZUS treaty's application to New Zealand by the United States.
