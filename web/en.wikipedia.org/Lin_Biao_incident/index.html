Lin Biao incident

The Lin Biao incident (Chinese: 林彪事件), also known in China as the September 13 Incident, was the death of the Chinese marshal and defence minister Lin Biao in an aeroplane crash in Mongolia on 13 September 1971, and the political crisis surrounding his fall during the Cultural Revolution.

Background

Lin Biao, commander in the Chinese Civil War and a close ally of Mao Zedong, was named Mao's designated successor in the 1969 party constitution. Relations with Mao deteriorated after the 1970 Lushan plenum, amid disputes over state offices and the influence of Lin's supporters in the military.

The crash and official account

According to the official Chinese account, Lin, his wife Ye Qun, and his son Lin Liguo plotted to assassinate Mao (the alleged "Project 571" outline), and after the plot was discovered they fled by air toward the Soviet Union; their Hawker Siddeley Trident crashed near Öndörkhaan in Mongolia, reportedly after running out of fuel, killing all aboard. Key details remain disputed by historians, and alternative explanations have been proposed.

Aftermath

Lin was posthumously condemned as a traitor, his supporters in the People's Liberation Army were purged, and the incident shook public faith in the Cultural Revolution's leadership and propaganda.
