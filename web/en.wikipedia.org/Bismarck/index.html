Bismarck

Bismarck most commonly refers to Otto von Bismarck (1815–1898), the Prussian statesman who engineered the unification of Germany and served as the first Chancellor of the German Empire from 1871 to 1890. The name may also refer to the German battleship Bismarck, capital of North Dakota, or numerous other places, ships and people.

Otto von Bismarck

Bismarck, a conservative Junker landowner, became Minister President of Prussia in 1862 and provoked or exploited wars with Denmark (1864), Austria (1866) and France (1870–71) to unify the German states under Prussian leadership, excluding Austria. As Chancellor he pursued a policy of balance of power in Europe, constructed a web of alliances, and introduced pioneering social welfare legislation — health insurance, accident insurance and old-age pensions — partly to undercut the socialist movement he simultaneously repressed.

German battleship Bismarck

The battleship Bismarck, launched in 1939, was the first of two Bismarck-class battleships of Nazi Germany's Kriegsmarine. In May 1941 she sank the British battlecruiser HMS Hood in the Battle of the Denmark Strait, but was hunted down and sunk three days later by the Royal Navy.

Places

Bismarck is the capital city of the U.S. state of North Dakota and seat of Burleigh County. Other places include the Bismarck Archipelago and Bismarck Sea near Papua New Guinea, named in the Chancellor's honour.
