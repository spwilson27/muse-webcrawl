South Yemen

South Yemen, officially the People's Democratic Republic of Yemen (PDRY), was a state that existed from 1967 to 1990 in the southern and eastern portions of the present-day Republic of Yemen. Its capital was Aden.

History

South Yemen emerged from the British Aden Protectorate and the Federation of South Arabia. After a guerrilla campaign by the National Liberation Front, Britain withdrew in November 1967 and the People's Republic of South Yemen was proclaimed. In 1970 the country adopted a Marxist government and renamed itself the People's Democratic Republic of Yemen, becoming the only explicitly Marxist state in the Arab world.

The PDRY maintained close relations with the Soviet Union and other Eastern Bloc countries. Internal power struggles culminated in the 1986 South Yemen Civil War. Declining Soviet support and economic pressures led to unification talks with the Yemen Arab Republic (North Yemen).

Unification

On 22 May 1990, South Yemen united with North Yemen to form the Republic of Yemen. A brief civil war in 1994, when southern leaders attempted to secede, was defeated by northern forces. Southern grievances have persisted, and a southern separatist movement, the Southern Transitional Council, remains active.

Politics and society

The Yemeni Socialist Party was the sole legal party. The state implemented land reform, expanded education and healthcare, and advanced women's rights relative to the region, including family law reforms and women's suffrage.
