Anderton Boat Lift

LocationAnderton, Cheshire, England
Opened1875
Lift50 feet (15.2 m)
DesignerEdwin Clark

The Anderton Boat Lift is a two-caisson boat lift near the village of Anderton, Cheshire, in north-west England. It provides a 50-foot (15.2 m) vertical link between the River Weaver Navigation and the Trent and Mersey Canal, which sits at a higher level on the hillside above. Nicknamed the "Cathedral of the Canals", it is a scheduled monument and one of the surviving masterpieces of Victorian waterways engineering.

Design and history

Designed by Edwin Clark and opened in 1875, the lift originally used water-filled hydraulic rams: two enormous caissons, each holding about 250 tonnes of water, balanced one another so that a small amount of power raised or lowered boats between the two waterways. Salt traffic from the Cheshire salt works was its principal cargo. In 1908 the hydraulic system, corroded by salt water, was replaced by electric motors and a system of wire ropes and pulleys carried on a new steel superstructure of A-frames above the original masonry.

Restoration

The lift closed in 1983 because of corrosion and structural deterioration. After a long public campaign and a £7 million restoration project, it reopened to boat traffic in 2002 and now operates with a visitor centre run by the Canal & River Trust, offering trip boats and an exhibition on its history.
