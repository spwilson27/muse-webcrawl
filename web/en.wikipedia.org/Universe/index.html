The universe is all of space and time and their contents. It comprises all of existence, any fundamental interaction, physical process and physical constant, and therefore all forms of matter and energy, and the structures they form, from sub-atomic particles to entire galactic filaments.

Size and age

Since the Big Bang, the universe has expanded, and the observable universe spans about 93 billion light-years in diameter, even though the universe itself is about 13.8 billion years old — the discrepancy arises because space itself has expanded while the light was travelling to us. Space may be infinite in extent, in which case the observable universe is only a tiny portion of the whole.

Composition

According to the standard Lambda-CDM model of cosmology, the mass-energy content of the universe consists of about 5 percent ordinary (baryonic) matter, 27 percent dark matter, and 68 percent dark energy, which drives the accelerating expansion of the universe discovered in 1998 through observations of distant supernovae. Ordinary matter forms stars, galaxies, and the large-scale cosmic web of filaments and voids.

History and fate

The early universe was hot and dense, cooling as it expanded. Within the first fraction of a second, fundamental particles formed; within minutes, light atomic nuclei were synthesized. About 380,000 years after the Big Bang, atoms formed and the universe became transparent, releasing the cosmic microwave background radiation observed today. Gravity then drew matter into the first stars and galaxies. Depending on the nature of dark energy, the universe is expected to continue expanding and cooling indefinitely toward a state of maximum entropy.
