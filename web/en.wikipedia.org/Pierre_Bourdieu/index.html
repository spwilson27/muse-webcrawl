Pierre Bourdieu

Pierre Bourdieu (1 August 1930 – 23 January 2002) was a French sociologist and philosopher, one of the most influential social scientists of the 20th century. His work examined how power and social hierarchy are reproduced through culture, education and taste.

Key concepts

Bourdieu developed the concepts of habitus (embodied dispositions shaped by upbringing), capital in economic, cultural, social and symbolic forms, and field (structured social arenas with their own rules). In Distinction (1979), his best-known book, he showed empirically how taste in art, food and lifestyle marks and maintains class position in France.

Career

Born in Denguin in southwestern France, Bourdieu studied philosophy at the École Normale Supérieure, did fieldwork in Algeria during its war of independence, and held a chair at the Collège de France from 1981. In his later years he became a prominent public intellectual and critic of neoliberalism.
