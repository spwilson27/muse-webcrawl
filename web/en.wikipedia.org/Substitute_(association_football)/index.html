Substitute (association football)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A fourth official holds up an electronic board showing a substitution.

In association football, a substitute is a player who is brought onto the pitch during a match in exchange for an existing player, a procedure known as a substitution. Substitutions may be made for tactical reasons, to replace tired or injured players, or to waste time late in a match.

History

Substitutes were not permitted in the early game; the first recorded use in an international came in 1888, when an injured player was replaced by agreement. The Laws of the Game formally allowed substitutes for injured players in the 1950s–60s; England introduced them in 1965 (initially one, for injury only). Tactical substitutions followed, and the allowance grew over decades from two to three. In 2020, during the COVID-19 pandemic, the IFAB temporarily permitted five substitutes; the change was made permanent in the Laws in 2022, with changes allowed in three "substitution opportunities" plus half-time.

Rules

Substitutions are governed by Law 3. The referee must be informed; the substitute enters at the halfway line after the player being replaced has left, during a stoppage. A substituted player normally may not re-enter the match, though some youth and veterans' competitions allow return substitutions. Competitions name a bench of substitutes (commonly seven to twelve) from which changes must come.

Tactics

Substitutions are a major managerial tool: introducing fresh attackers, protecting a lead with defenders, or changing formation. A substitute who scores is often called a "super-sub" when the feat is repeated.
