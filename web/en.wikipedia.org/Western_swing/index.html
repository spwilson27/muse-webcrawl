Western swing is a subgenre of American country music that originated in the late 1920s in the Southwest and was popularized in the 1930s and 1940s, primarily in Texas and Oklahoma. It is an outgrowth of jazz, combining elements of country, blues, folk, and swing music with a strong dance beat. The genre, played by string bands augmented with drums, saxophones, pianos, and the steel guitar, became the signature sound of dance halls across the American Southwest.

History

Western swing developed from the string band tradition of the rural South and Southwest. Milton Brown and His Musical Brownies, formed in Fort Worth, Texas, in 1930, are often credited with founding the genre; Brown added a jazz-influenced vocal style and expanded instrumentation to the fiddle band format. After Brown's death in a car accident in 1936, Bob Wills and His Texas Playboys became the genre's dominant act, scoring hits such as "San Antonio Rose" and popularizing the style through radio, records, and tours.

During World War II, western swing bands such as Spade Cooley's orchestra drew huge crowds in California, where defense workers from Oklahoma and Texas had migrated. The genre's popularity faded in the 1950s with the rise of honky-tonk and rock and roll, but it influenced country music permanently — electric guitars, drums, and improvisation all entered mainstream country partly through western swing. A revival in the 1970s, led by Asleep at the Wheel and Merle Haggard's tribute album to Bob Wills, restored its reputation.

Characteristics

Western swing bands were larger than traditional country string bands, typically featuring twin fiddles, an electrically amplified steel guitar, lead electric guitar (often playing jazz-influenced single-note solos), piano, bass, drums, and sometimes a horn section. Repertoire mixed original songs, pop standards, jazz tunes, blues, and traditional fiddle breakdowns, all arranged for dancing in 2/4 or 4/4 time.

Legacy

Bob Wills was inducted into the Country Music Hall of Fame in 1968 and, with the Texas Playboys, into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1999 in the Early Influences category. Western swing remains a living tradition in Texas dance halls and at festivals such as the annual gatherings in Turkey, Texas, Wills's hometown.
