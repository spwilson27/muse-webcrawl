Encephalitis

Encephalitis is inflammation of the brain, usually caused by viral infection, though bacterial, parasitic, and autoimmune causes also occur. It is a medical emergency that can cause altered consciousness, seizures, and lasting neurological injury.

Signs and causes

Symptoms include fever, headache, confusion, personality change, seizures, and focal deficits. Herpes simplex virus is the most common identified cause of sporadic encephalitis in Western countries; others include arboviruses such as West Nile and Japanese encephalitis viruses, enteroviruses, and rabies. Autoimmune encephalitis, such as anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis, is increasingly recognized.

Diagnosis and treatment

Evaluation involves neuroimaging, lumbar puncture, EEG, and PCR testing of cerebrospinal fluid. Suspected herpes encephalitis is treated promptly with intravenous aciclovir. Prognosis varies; outcomes improve with early treatment.
