Firecracker

A firecracker (cracker, noise maker, banger) is a small explosive device primarily designed to produce a large amount of noise, especially in the form of a loud bang, usually for amusement. Similar to fireworks, they have fuses and are wrapped in a heavy paper casing to contain the explosive compound. Firecrackers, along with fireworks, originated in China.

History

The earliest firecrackers are thought to have been exploding bamboo stems, used in ancient China around 200 BC, when bamboo thrown into a fire would burst with a loud crack as the air inside its hollow segments expanded. After the invention of gunpowder in the Tang dynasty, paper tubes filled with powder replaced the bamboo. By the Song dynasty, firecrackers were widely manufactured and used in festivals, weddings and religious ceremonies to frighten away evil spirits.

Firecrackers spread along trade routes to India, the Middle East and Europe, where they became staples of celebrations such as Guy Fawkes Night, Diwali and the Fourth of July.

Construction

A typical firecracker consists of a cardboard or paper tube filled with flash powder — a mixture of an oxidizer such as potassium perchlorate and a metal fuel such as aluminium powder — sealed at both ends with a clay or paper plug. A short fuse protrudes from one end. When lit, the fuse burns to the charge, which deflagrates almost instantaneously, rupturing the casing with a sharp report. String firecrackers link dozens or hundreds of small crackers by their fuses so that they ignite in rapid sequence.

Regulation and safety

Because of injuries — particularly to hands and eyes — and noise complaints, many countries restrict or ban the sale of powerful firecrackers to the general public. In the United States, consumer firecrackers are limited by federal law to a small powder charge, and several states and cities ban them entirely. Large display crackers and military salutes are restricted to licensed professionals.

Cultural use

Firecrackers remain central to Lunar New Year celebrations across East and Southeast Asia, to Diwali in India, to Las Fallas in Valencia, Spain, and to numerous local festivals worldwide, where their noise is traditionally believed to drive off demons and bring good fortune.
