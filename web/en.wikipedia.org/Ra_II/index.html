Ra II, the papyrus vessel of Thor Heyerdahl's 1970 Atlantic crossing.

Ra II is a papyrus reed boat built by the Norwegian explorer and ethnographer Thor Heyerdahl and sailed across the Atlantic Ocean in 1970. The voyage, from Safi in Morocco to Barbados in the Caribbean, was undertaken to test Heyerdahl's theory that ancient Mediterranean mariners could have crossed the Atlantic in reed vessels and influenced the cultures of the pre-Columbian Americas.

Background

Heyerdahl had earlier crossed the Pacific on the balsa raft Kon-Tiki (1947). In 1969 his first papyrus boat, Ra I, built by Buduma craftsmen from Lake Chad, broke up short of Barbados after taking on water. Ra II was built by Aymara boat builders from Lake Titicaca in Bolivia, whose totora reed craft retained design features Heyerdahl believed matched ancient Egyptian depictions, including a correctly tensioned stern that kept the papyrus bundles from sagging.

The voyage

Ra II departed Safi on 17 May 1970 with an international crew of eight, flying the flag of the United Nations. The crew included Norman Baker (navigator), Carlo Mauri, Kei Ohara, Georges Sourial, Santiago Genovés, Yuri Senkevich and Madani Ait Ouhanni. The vessel crossed the Atlantic in 57 days, reaching Barbados on 12 July 1970 after travelling roughly 6,100 kilometres. Along the way the crew documented oil pollution in mid-ocean, observations that contributed to early international debate on marine pollution.

Legacy

The successful crossing demonstrated that a papyrus reed vessel of ancient design could survive an Atlantic passage, lending plausibility — though not proof — to the possibility of pre-Columbian trans-oceanic contact. Ra II is preserved at the Kon-Tiki Museum in Oslo, Norway.
