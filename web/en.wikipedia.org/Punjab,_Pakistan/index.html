Punjab (Punjabi/Urdu: پنجاب) is a province of Pakistan, the country's most populous, with about 128 million inhabitants — more than half of Pakistan's total population. It occupies the northeastern part of the country and is bordered by the Pakistani provinces of Sindh, Balochistan, and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, by Islamabad Capital Territory, and by the Indian states of Punjab and Rajasthan and Indian-administered Jammu and Kashmir. Its capital and largest city is Lahore.

Geography

The name Punjab means "land of five rivers," referring to the Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, and Sutlej, tributaries of the Indus that water the province's fertile alluvial plains. The interfluvial tracts, called doabs, form one of the most intensively irrigated agricultural regions in the world. The Thal and Cholistan deserts lie in the south and west, and the Potohar Plateau and Salt Range occupy the northwest.

History

The Punjab plains were a heartland of the Indus Valley Civilisation, with cities such as Harappa. The region was ruled in succession by Persian, Macedonian, Mauryan, Gupta, Arab, Ghaznavid, Mughal, and Sikh empires; the Sikh Empire of Maharaja Ranjit Singh, with its capital at Lahore, lasted until British annexation in 1849. The partition of British India in 1947 divided Punjab between India and Pakistan amid mass migration and violence. In Pakistan, the western portion became the province of West Punjab, renamed Punjab in 1955.

Economy

Punjab has the largest economy of any Pakistani province, contributing roughly half of national GDP. Irrigated agriculture produces wheat, cotton, rice, sugarcane, and citrus; the province is often called the breadbasket of Pakistan. Industry is concentrated in textiles, sports goods (Sialkot), surgical instruments, and food processing, centered on Lahore, Faisalabad, Gujranwala, and Multan.

Culture

Punjabi culture is expressed in the Punjabi language, Sufi poetry of saints such as Bulleh Shah and Waris Shah (author of the epic Heer Ranjha), bhangra music and dance, and festivals including Basant and the urs of Sufi shrines. Lahore is regarded as Pakistan's cultural capital, home to the Badshahi Mosque, Lahore Fort, and Shalimar Gardens.
