Lourdes is a market town in southwestern France, lying in the foothills of the Pyrenees. It is best known for the Sanctuary of Our Lady of Lourdes, a major Roman Catholic pilgrimage site. Each year, millions of visitors come to Lourdes, many of them sick or disabled people hoping for healing at the shrine.

History

Lourdes was a small unremarkable regional market town lying on a crossroad from France to Spain. In the mid-19th century it was at most a way station for visitors to the Pyrenean spas at Barèges, Cauterets, Luz-Saint-Sauveur and Bagnères-de-Bigorre.

The apparitions

In 1858, a 14-year-old local girl, Bernadette Soubirous, reported a series of apparitions of a lady, identified as the Virgin Mary, in the grotto of Massabielle, on the bank of the Gave de Pau river. During one apparition, Bernadette was directed to dig in the ground, where a spring of water appeared; the water of Lourdes has since been associated with reported cures.

The Catholic Church investigated the events and declared the apparitions worthy of belief in 1862. The Sanctuary was built around the grotto and includes the Basilica of Our Lady of the Rosary and the Upper Basilica. A medical bureau examines claims of miraculous cures.

Pilgrimage

Lourdes receives around three million pilgrims and tourists each year. Processions, including the nightly torchlight Marian procession and the Blessed Sacrament procession, are central features of the pilgrimage season. The town has the second-greatest number of hotel rooms in France after Paris.
