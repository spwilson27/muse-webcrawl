Critical infrastructure

Critical infrastructure refers to the systems and assets — physical and virtual — whose incapacity or destruction would have a debilitating effect on security, the economy, public health, or safety. The term became prominent in United States policy in the 1990s and is now used internationally.

Sectors

Designated sectors in the US include energy, water, transportation, communications, financial services, healthcare, food and agriculture, chemical facilities, dams, emergency services, and information technology. Most such infrastructure is privately owned, making public–private partnership central to protection policy.

Protection

Critical infrastructure protection addresses threats ranging from natural disasters and accidents to terrorism and cyberattack. Interdependence between sectors — for example, power failures cascading into water and communications — is a major focus of resilience planning. In the US, the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) coordinates national efforts.
