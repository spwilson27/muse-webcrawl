Hadar, Ethiopia

Hadar (also spelled Haddar) is a village and paleontological site in the Afar Region of northeastern Ethiopia, within the Afar Triangle (Afar Depression) along the lower Awash River valley. It is one of the most important fossil localities in the world for the study of human evolution.

Paleontology

The Hadar Formation's sediments, roughly 3.4 to 2.3 million years old, have yielded hundreds of hominin fossils, principally of Australopithecus afarensis. In 1974, Donald Johanson and Tom Gray discovered AL 288-1, the partial skeleton nicknamed "Lucy", about 40% complete and dated to about 3.2 million years ago. Other finds include the "First Family" collection of multiple individuals and some of the earliest stone tools and evidence associated with early Homo. The site is administered as part of the Lower Valley of the Awash, a UNESCO World Heritage Site inscribed in 1980.
