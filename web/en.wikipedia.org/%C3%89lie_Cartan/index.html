Élie Cartan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Born: 9 April 1869, Dolomieu, France
Died: 6 May 1951, Paris, France
Fields: Mathematics — Lie groups, differential geometry

Élie Joseph Cartan (9 April 1869 – 6 May 1951) was a French mathematician who did fundamental work in the theory of Lie groups, differential systems (the PDE system in exterior differential form), and differential geometry. His work has had lasting influence on modern geometry and mathematical physics, including the theory of spinors and general relativity.

Life

Cartan was born in Dolomieu, Isère, the son of a blacksmith. He studied at the École Normale Supérieure, where his doctoral thesis of 1894 concerned the structure of finite continuous groups. He taught at Montpellier, Lyon, Nancy and Paris, becoming a professor at the Sorbonne. His son Henri Cartan also became a distinguished mathematician.

Work

Cartan classified the simple Lie algebras, developed the theory of symmetric spaces, and created the method of moving frames (repère mobile). He introduced the concepts of exterior differential forms and the exterior derivative in their modern form, and his notion of connection (the Cartan connection) remains central to differential geometry. Although relatively neglected during his lifetime, his ideas were later recognised as foundational.
