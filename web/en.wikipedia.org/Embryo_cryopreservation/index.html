Embryo cryopreservation

Embryo cryopreservation is the process of preserving an embryo at sub-zero temperatures, generally at an embryogenesis stage corresponding to pre-implantation, that is, from fertilisation to the blastocyst stage. It is routinely used in assisted reproduction, particularly in vitro fertilisation (IVF), where surplus embryos produced in a treatment cycle are frozen for later transfer, or where transfer is deferred.

Techniques

Two main methods are used: slow programmable freezing and vitrification, an ultra-rapid cooling method that avoids ice crystal formation by converting the solution to a glass-like state. Vitrification has become the dominant technique because of higher survival rates after thawing. Embryos are stored in liquid nitrogen at −196 °C, where they can remain viable for many years; successful pregnancies have been reported from embryos stored for decades.

Ethical and legal issues

Cryopreservation raises questions about storage duration, disposition of unused embryos after divorce or death, and the legal status of embryos, which vary widely by jurisdiction.
