Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event

Timing
Date: ~66 million years ago
Severity: ~75% of all species extinct
Proposed cause: Chicxulub asteroid impact (10–15 km)
Notable victims: Non-avian dinosaurs, pterosaurs, ammonites, mosasaurs

The Cretaceous–Paleogene (K–Pg) extinction event, also known as the Cretaceous–Tertiary (K–T) extinction, was a sudden mass extinction of three-quarters of the plant and animal species on Earth, approximately 66 million years ago. The event caused the extinction of all non-avian dinosaurs. Most other tetrapods weighing more than 25 kilograms (55 lb) also became extinct, with the exceptions of some ectothermic species such as sea turtles and crocodilians. It marked the end of the Cretaceous period, and with it the Mesozoic era, while heralding the beginning of the current era, the Cenozoic.

Impact hypothesis

In 1980, a team led by Luis Walter Alvarez and his son Walter Alvarez discovered a thin worldwide layer of clay enriched in iridium at the K–Pg boundary and proposed that the extinction was caused by the impact of a large asteroid or comet. In 1991 the buried Chicxulub crater on Mexico's Yucatán Peninsula, about 180 km wide, was identified and dated to the boundary, providing strong confirmation.

Effects

The impact is thought to have caused an “impact winter”: ejected sulphur aerosols and dust blocked sunlight for years, collapsing photosynthesis and food webs. Marine groups such as ammonites, rudist bivalves and plesiosaurs perished, while survivors included birds (avian dinosaurs), mammals, crocodilians, turtles, frogs and many fish.

Other proposed causes

The Deccan Traps flood-basalt eruptions in India, which straddle the boundary, may have contributed through climate change; the relative roles of volcanism and the impact remain debated, although the scientific consensus holds the Chicxulub impact as the primary cause.
