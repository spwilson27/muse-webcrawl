Politics of Argentina

The politics of Argentina take place within a federal presidential representative democratic republic, established by the Constitution of 1853 as extensively amended in 1994. The President of Argentina is both head of state and head of government, and executive, legislative, and judicial powers are formally separated.

Executive branch

The president and vice president are elected together by popular vote for four-year terms, with one consecutive re-election permitted. Since 1995, election requires 45 percent of the vote, or 40 percent with a ten-point margin, failing which a runoff (ballotage) is held. The president appoints the cabinet, headed by the Chief of the Cabinet of Ministers.

Legislature

The bicameral National Congress consists of the Senate (72 seats, three per province and the City of Buenos Aires) and the Chamber of Deputies (257 seats, apportioned by population). Senators serve six-year terms and deputies four-year terms, with partial renewals every two years.

Political parties and history

Argentine politics has been dominated by Peronism, the movement founded by Juan Domingo Perón and embodied in the Justicialist Party, opposed historically by the Radical Civic Union and, more recently, by the center-right PRO and the libertarian La Libertad Avanza of Javier Milei, elected president in 2023. The twentieth century saw repeated military interventions, from 1930 through the last dictatorship of 1976–1983, since which democratic government has been continuous. Argentina is a federation of 23 provinces plus the autonomous City of Buenos Aires, each with its own constitution and governor.
