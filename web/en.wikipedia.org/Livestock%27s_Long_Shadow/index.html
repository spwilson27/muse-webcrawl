Livestock's Long Shadow: Environmental Issues and Options is a 2006 report published by the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO). It assessed the full environmental impact of the livestock sector and became one of the most widely cited publications on the subject.

Findings

The report famously estimated that livestock supply chains were responsible for about 18% of global anthropogenic greenhouse gas emissions measured in CO2 equivalents — a figure that included land-use change such as deforestation for pasture and feed crops. It also documented livestock's role in land degradation, water use and pollution, air pollution, and biodiversity loss, and proposed technical and policy options for mitigation.

Revisions and debate

The 18% figure attracted wide media coverage, sometimes simplified to the claim that livestock produced more emissions than transport. The FAO later refined its methodology, estimating the sector's share at about 14.5% in its 2013 GLEAM assessment. The report remains a landmark in debates over meat, climate change and sustainable food systems.
