March Engineering was a Formula One constructor and a manufacturer of customer racing cars from the United Kingdom. Founded in 1969 by Max Mosley, Alan Rees, Graham Coaker and Robin Herd—the initials forming the name MARCH—the company took a highly commercial approach, selling cars across Formula One, Two, Three, Can-Am, sports cars and Indycar racing.

The March 701, the firm's first Formula One car, sold to several teams in 1970.

History

March made an unprecedented debut in 1970, supplying F1 cars (the 701) to the works team, Ken Tyrrell's team for Jackie Stewart, and others; Jackie Stewart won the Spanish Grand Prix in a March. Ronnie Peterson took the works team's best results in 1971, finishing second in the championship. The works F1 effort faded in the mid-1970s, but March dominated customer racing: it became the leading supplier in Formula Two, won the Indianapolis 500 five consecutive times in the 1980s (1983–87) with its Indycars, and its BMW-powered cars won the European Formula Two championship repeatedly.

After financial troubles in the 1980s the company was sold to the Japanese Leyton House group; the F1 team raced as Leyton House Racing in 1990–91 and briefly revived the March name in 1992 before closing at the end of that season. The March name has been periodically revived by later owners for heritage and licensing purposes.
