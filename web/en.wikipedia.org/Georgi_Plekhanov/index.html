Georgi Valentinovich Plekhanov (December 11, 1856 – May 30, 1918) was a Russian revolutionary, philosopher, and Marxist theorist. He was a founder of the Social Democratic movement in Russia and is widely regarded as the father of Russian Marxism, having translated and popularised the works of Marx and Engels for a Russian audience.

Emancipation of Labour

After breaking with the populist movement, Plekhanov emigrated to Switzerland in 1880 and in 1883 founded the Emancipation of Labour group in Geneva, the first Russian Marxist organisation. His works Socialism and the Political Struggle (1883) and The Development of the Monist View of History (1895) argued that Russia would have to pass through capitalism before socialism. He helped found the Russian Social Democratic Labour Party and edited its paper Iskra alongside Vladimir Lenin.

Later years

At the party's 1903 congress Plekhanov sided with the Mensheviks against Lenin's Bolsheviks, a split he spent years trying to heal. He opposed the October Revolution of 1917, regarding it as premature, and died in Finland in 1918. Despite their political rupture, Lenin urged the study of Plekhanov's philosophical writings, and his works were canonised in the Soviet Union.
