The Iranian languages (also called Iranic languages) are a branch of the Indo-Iranian languages in the Indo-European language family that are spoken natively by the Iranian peoples, predominantly in the Iranian Plateau and neighbouring regions. Together with the Indo-Aryan languages, they form the Indo-Iranian group, which originated from Proto-Indo-Iranian speech of the late 3rd millennium BC.

History

Old Iranian languages (c. 1000–300 BC) include Avestan, the language of the Zoroastrian scriptures, and Old Persian, recorded in cuneiform inscriptions of the Achaemenid Empire. Middle Iranian (c. 300 BC–800 AD) is represented by Middle Persian (Pahlavi) in the west and by Bactrian, Sogdian, Khwarezmian and Khotanese in the east, as well as Parthian. New Iranian languages emerged after the Muslim conquest of Persia; New Persian, written in a modified Arabic script, became the great literary language of the eastern Islamic world.

Classification

Iranian languages are traditionally divided into Western and Eastern groups. Western languages include Persian (Farsi, Dari and Tajik), Kurdish, Balochi, Luri, Gilaki, Mazandarani and Talysh. Eastern languages include Pashto, Ossetian (descended from the language of the Alans), Yagnobi (a descendant of Sogdian), the Pamir languages, and Wakhi. Extinct members include Scythian and Sarmatian, once spoken across the Eurasian steppe.

Speakers

Persian is the largest Iranian language, with over 100 million speakers in total across Iran, Afghanistan and Tajikistan; Pashto and Kurdish each have tens of millions of speakers. Most Iranian languages use Arabic-derived scripts, except Tajik (Cyrillic) and Ossetian (Cyrillic).
