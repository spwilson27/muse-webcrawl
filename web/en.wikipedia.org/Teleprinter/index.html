Teleprinter

A teleprinter (or teletypewriter, TTY) is an electromechanical typewriter that sends and receives typed messages over a communications channel, such as a telegraph line or radio link. Teleprinters formed the basis of the worldwide Telex network and were a standard interface to early computers.

History

Printing telegraphs date to the 19th century, but practical teleprinters emerged in the early 1900s with machines from Creed, Kleinschmidt, Morkrum and the Teletype Corporation. Using the five-bit Baudot code (later ITA2), they exchanged messages at about 45–50 baud. The Telex network, launched in Germany in 1933, let subscribers dial each other directly and print messages automatically, remaining important in business and shipping into the fax and email era.

Computer use

Models such as the Teletype Model 33 were ubiquitous as computer terminals in the 1960s–70s, printing program output on fan-fold paper and reading punched paper tape. Their legacy survives in the "tty" device names of Unix-like operating systems and in text-telephone services for deaf users.
