Stratigraphy (archaeology)

Key principleLaw of superposition
Key toolHarris matrix
OriginAdapted from geology

In archaeology, stratigraphy is the study of the layered deposits (strata) that accumulate on a site through human and natural processes. It provides the primary means of establishing the relative chronological sequence of contexts, features and finds.

Principles

The law of superposition holds that in an undisturbed sequence, lower layers are older than those above them. Archaeologists record individual contexts — layers, cuts, fills and structures — and order them using the Harris matrix, a diagram devised by Edward Harris in 1973 that expresses the stratigraphic relationships of a site. Interfaces such as pits and ditches complicate simple layering and are central to excavation practice.
