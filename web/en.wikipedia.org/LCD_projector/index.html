LCD projector

An LCD projector is a type of video projector for displaying images, data or video onto a screen or flat surface. It works by passing light through three liquid crystal display panels — one each for the red, green and blue components of the signal — and recombining the modulated beams with a dichroic prism before projecting them through a lens.

History

The technology derives from experiments by Gene Dolgoff in the 1980s and was commercialised in the late 1980s and 1990s, notably by Epson and other manufacturers. The related 3LCD consortium promotes the three-panel design. LCD projection became the dominant technology for classroom and business presentation projectors.

Comparison with other technologies

LCD projectors compete with DLP projectors, which use a digital micromirror device. LCD systems historically avoided the "rainbow effect" of single-chip DLP but could suffer from a visible pixel grid ("screen door effect") and lower contrast ratios; both drawbacks have lessened with higher-resolution panels.
