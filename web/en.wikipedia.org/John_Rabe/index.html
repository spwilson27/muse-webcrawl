John Heinrich Detlev Rabe (23 November 1882 – 5 January 1950) was a German businessman and member of the Nazi Party who, as chairman of the International Committee for the Nanking Safety Zone, helped protect hundreds of thousands of Chinese civilians during the Nanjing Massacre of 1937–38.

Life in China

Rabe worked for Siemens in China from 1911, managing its operations in Nanjing (then Nanking). When Japanese forces approached the city in November 1937, most foreigners fled, but Rabe stayed and helped organise a demilitarised Safety Zone of about 3.8 km².

The Safety Zone

Using his party membership and the swastika flag to deter Japanese attacks, Rabe sheltered some 200,000 or more refugees in the zone, including about 600 in his own house and garden, and repeatedly intervened to stop atrocities. His diaries are a detailed eyewitness record of the massacre.

Later life and legacy

Recalled to Germany in 1938, Rabe was briefly arrested by the Gestapo after lecturing about the atrocities, and lived in poverty after the war; Nanjing residents sent him money and food in gratitude. His diaries were published in the 1990s, and his story was dramatised in the 2009 film John Rabe. He is sometimes called "the Oskar Schindler of China".
