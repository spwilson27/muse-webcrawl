Startup company

DefinitionA young company developing a scalable business model under uncertainty
FundingFounder capital, angel investors, venture capital
OutcomesAcquisition, IPO, failure, or maturation into an established company
Key hubsSilicon Valley, New York, London, Bangalore, Tel Aviv

A startup or startup company is a young company founded by one or more entrepreneurs to develop a unique product or service and bring it to market. Startups are typically characterised by high uncertainty, an emphasis on rapid growth and scalability, and reliance on external funding rather than initial profits.

Funding stages

Startups commonly raise capital in stages: pre-seed and seed rounds from founders, friends and family and angel investors; Series A and later venture capital rounds to fund growth; and eventually an exit through acquisition or an initial public offering, or failure. Business incubators and accelerators such as Y Combinator provide mentorship and early funding. A privately held startup valued above US$1 billion is called a "unicorn".

Methodology

The lean startup movement, popularised by Eric Ries in 2011, advocates building a minimum viable product, testing hypotheses with customers, and pivoting when evidence contradicts assumptions. Steve Blank's customer development framework similarly stresses searching for a repeatable business model before scaling — "a startup is a temporary organisation designed to search for a repeatable and scalable business model".

Risks and outcomes

Most startups fail; common causes include lack of market need, running out of cash, and team problems. Nevertheless, successful technology startups — from early Silicon Valley firms to today's platform companies — have reshaped industries, and startup ecosystems are promoted worldwide as engines of innovation and employment.
