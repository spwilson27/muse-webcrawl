The University of Coimbra (Portuguese: Universidade de Coimbra, abbreviated UC) is a public research university in Coimbra, Portugal. Founded in 1290 by King Denis, it is among the oldest universities in continuous operation in the world and the oldest university in Portugal. In 2013, UNESCO declared the university a World Heritage Site, noting its central role in the dissemination of scientific knowledge and its architecturally significant historic campus.

History

The university was established by the charter Scientiae thesaurus mirabilis, signed by King Denis on 1 March 1290. It began operating in Lisbon before being transferred to Coimbra in 1308, and oscillated between the two cities until 1537, when King John III definitively moved it to Coimbra and installed it in the royal palace on the hilltop overlooking the city. For centuries the university was the only one in Portugal, shaping the country's intellectual, legal and scientific life.

Major reforms were carried out by the Marquis of Pombal in 1772, modernising the teaching of the natural sciences, and again after the establishment of the Portuguese Republic in 1911, when new faculties were created and women were fully admitted. During the twentieth century the campus expanded with the monumental buildings of the Estado Novo era, and after 1974 the university grew rapidly in size and research output.

Organisation

The university is organised into eight faculties: Letters, Law, Medicine, Sciences and Technology, Pharmacy, Economics, Psychology and Education Sciences, and Sports Sciences and Physical Education. It also includes research institutes, the historic Joanina Library, the Science Museum, and the university stadium complex. The Joanina Library, built in the eighteenth century in the Baroque style, is one of the most famous libraries in Europe and houses some 60,000 volumes, preserved in part with the help of a resident colony of bats that protects the books from insects.

Campus and traditions

The historic campus, known as Alta Universitária, includes the Paço das Escolas courtyard, the university tower, the Chapel of São Miguel and the Via Latina colonnade. Students traditionally wear a black suit and cape (traje académico), and the university is home to distinctive traditions such as the Queima das Fitas festival, fado de Coimbra, and the academic praxe. The university's coat of arms features a crowned figure traditionally identified as King Denis.

Notable alumni

Alumni include the poet Luís de Camões (associated with the university), the statesman the Marquis of Pombal, the writer Eça de Queirós, the physician and Nobel laureate António Egas Moniz, and the dictator António de Oliveira Salazar, who taught economics there.
