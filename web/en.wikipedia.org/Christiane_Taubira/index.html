Christiane Taubira

Christiane Taubira (born 2 February 1952) is a French politician from French Guiana who served as Minister of Justice of France under President François Hollande from 2012 to 2016. She is best known as the author of the 2001 law recognising the Atlantic slave trade and slavery as crimes against humanity (the "Taubira law"), and as the minister who steered France's 2013 law legalising same-sex marriage through parliament.

Early life and career

Taubira was born in Cayenne, French Guiana, into a large, modest family. She studied economics in Paris, earned a postgraduate diploma in ethnology of Afro-American peoples and a diploma in the sociology of food industries, and worked in Guiana in agricultural economics and cultural affairs. In the 1970s she was involved in the Guianese independence movement before turning to institutional politics; in 1993 she founded the left-wing regional party Walwari and was elected to the National Assembly for French Guiana, a seat she held for nearly two decades.

Taubira law

As rapporteur, Taubira carried the law of 21 May 2001, which recognises the transatlantic and Indian Ocean slave trades and the resulting slavery as crimes against humanity, and requires that they be given a proper place in school curricula and research. France was the first (and remains the only) country to have made such a statutory declaration.

Minister of Justice

Appointed Keeper of the Seals in May 2012, Taubira piloted the law of 17 May 2013 opening marriage to same-sex couples — her eloquent parliamentary defence of the bill became famous — and a 2014 penal reform promoting individualised sentencing and alternatives to imprisonment. She resigned on 27 January 2016 in disagreement with the government's proposal, after the November 2015 Paris attacks, to strip dual-national terrorists of French citizenship. She later stood in the 2022 presidential election after winning a citizen-organised "People's Primary", but withdrew before the first round.

Works

Taubira has written several books, including L'esclavage raconté à ma fille (2002), Murmures à la jeunesse (2016) and the novel Nous habitons la Terre (2017).
