The Xbox Series X and Xbox Series S are home video game consoles developed by Microsoft. Released together on November 10, 2020, they form the fourth generation of the Xbox console family and the ninth generation of video game consoles overall, succeeding the Xbox One and competing with Sony's PlayStation 5.

Hardware

Both consoles use a custom AMD Zen 2 CPU and RDNA 2 GPU. The Series X targets native 4K rendering with a 12 teraflop GPU and 16 GB of GDDR6 memory, while the smaller, all-digital Series S targets 1440p upscaled to 4K with a 4 teraflop GPU and 10 GB of memory. Both use fast NVMe solid-state drives that, together with the Xbox Velocity Architecture, greatly reduce load times and enable the Quick Resume feature, which keeps multiple games suspended simultaneously.

Features

The consoles support hardware-accelerated ray tracing, variable refresh rates, and frame rates up to 120 Hz. "Smart Delivery" automatically provides the best version of a game for the console in use. The systems are backward compatible with nearly all Xbox One games as well as supported Xbox 360 and original Xbox titles, often with performance and resolution enhancements. The subscription service Xbox Game Pass is a central feature of the platform.

Reception and sales

The Series X was widely praised as a powerful, quiet machine, while the Series S was noted as a good-value entry point to the generation, though its limited storage was criticized. Combined sales of the two models were estimated at over 28 million units by mid-2024, also driven by Microsoft's increasing emphasis on Game Pass and multi-platform publishing.
