Godparent

In denominations of Christianity, a godparent (also known as a sponsor, or gossip) is someone who bears witness to a child's christening and later is willing to help in their catechesis, as well as their lifelong spiritual formation. In the past, in some places, a godparent's role included a legal responsibility for the child's care should the parents die or be unable to care for them; today the emphasis is usually on spiritual guidance and mentorship.

History and practice

The institution dates to early Christianity, when sponsors vouched for adult converts during persecutions. As infant baptism became standard, parents chose sponsors — traditionally one godfather and one godmother — who renounced evil on the child's behalf and promised Christian upbringing. The Roman Catholic Church requires at least one Catholic godparent in good standing; the Eastern Orthodox Church likewise requires an Orthodox sponsor. Many Lutheran, Anglican, and Reformed churches retain godparents at baptism, while Baptist and other traditions that practise believer's baptism generally do not.

Cultural roles

Beyond its religious function, godparenthood creates a bond of fictive kinship: in Latin American and Mediterranean cultures the relationship between parents and godparents (compadrazgo) is itself socially important, and godparents may act as mentors, gift-givers, and informal guardians. Secular "guideparents" are sometimes named in humanist naming ceremonies, with a similar expectation of a special relationship with the child.
