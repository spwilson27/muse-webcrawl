Kākāpō

The kākāpō (Strigops habroptilus), also known as the owl parrot, is a species of large, flightless, nocturnal parrot endemic to New Zealand. It is the world's heaviest parrot and the only flightless one, and is critically endangered, with every surviving individual known by name and intensively managed by the New Zealand Department of Conservation.

Biology

The kākāpō has finely blotched yellow-green plumage, a distinctive facial disc, and a sweet musty odour. It is nocturnal and herbivorous, climbing trees and "parachuting" down with its small wings. Males gather at lek arenas and produce deep booming calls that can carry for kilometres. Breeding is irregular and tied to the mast fruiting of rimu trees, occurring only every two to four years.

Conservation

Once widespread across New Zealand, the kākāpō was devastated by introduced predators such as stoats, rats and cats. By 1995 only 51 birds survived. The Kākāpō Recovery Programme moved all remaining birds to predator-free islands including Codfish Island / Whenua Hou, and through supplementary feeding, nest monitoring and artificial insemination the population has slowly climbed to over 200 individuals. The hand-reared male Sirocco became a famous ambassador for the species.
