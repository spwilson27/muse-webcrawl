Der Rosenkavalier

Der Rosenkavalier (The Knight of the Rose), Op. 59, is a comic opera in three acts by Richard Strauss to a libretto by Hugo von Hofmannsthal. It premiered at the Königliches Opernhaus in Dresden on 26 January 1911 and quickly became one of the most popular operas of the twentieth century.

Plot

Set in the Vienna of Maria Theresa, the opera centers on the Marschallin, Princess von Werdenberg, who is having an affair with the young Count Octavian. Octavian, disguised at first as a chambermaid and later serving as the "Knight of the Rose," presents a silver rose to Sophie von Faninal on behalf of her fiancé, the boorish Baron Ochs — and falls in love with her himself. The Marschallin, reflecting on the passage of time, gracefully renounces Octavian in the celebrated final trio.

Music

Although set in the eighteenth century, the score famously makes anachronistic use of the Viennese waltz. Its highlights include the orchestral prelude, the presentation of the rose in Act II, the Marschallin's Act I monologue on time, and the closing trio and duet, among the most admired ensembles in opera.

Reception

The opera was an immediate success across Europe and remains a cornerstone of the repertory, especially at the Vienna State Opera and the Salzburg Festival.
