Robert Tappan Morris

Robert Tappan Morris
Born: 8 November 1965, United States
Known for: Morris worm; Viaweb; Y Combinator

Robert Tappan Morris (born 1965) is an American computer scientist and entrepreneur, known for creating the Morris worm in 1988, considered the first computer worm distributed via the Internet, and for co-founding Viaweb and the startup accelerator Y Combinator.

The Morris worm

While a graduate student at Cornell University, Morris released a self-replicating program from MIT's network in November 1988, intending, he said, to gauge the size of the Internet. A design flaw caused it to reinfect machines repeatedly, disabling an estimated 6,000 computers — roughly a tenth of the network. Morris became the first person convicted in the United States under the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act of 1986; he received probation, community service and a fine.

Later career

Morris earned a PhD from Harvard, co-founded Viaweb (sold to Yahoo in 1998) and in 2005 co-founded Y Combinator with Paul Graham, Jessica Livingston and Trevor Blackwell. He later became a professor at MIT.
