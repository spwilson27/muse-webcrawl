Jochen Rindt

Karl Jochen Rindt (18 April 1942 – 5 September 1970) was a German-born racing driver who represented Austria during his career. He is the only driver to be awarded the Formula One World Drivers' Championship posthumously, having been killed in practice for the 1970 Italian Grand Prix at Monza while leading the championship standings by an unassailable margin.

Early life and career

Rindt was born in Mainz; his parents were killed in the bombing of Hamburg in 1943, and he was raised by his grandparents in Graz, Austria. He began racing in the early 1960s, won the 1965 24 Hours of Le Mans with Masten Gregory in a Ferrari 250LM, and reached Formula One with Cooper in 1964. After a difficult season with Brabham in 1968 he joined Lotus for 1969, winning his first Grand Prix at Watkins Glen.

1970 season and death

In Colin Chapman's revolutionary wedge-shaped Lotus 72, Rindt won five races in 1970, at Monaco, the Netherlands, France, Britain and Germany, building a commanding points lead. During practice at Monza on 5 September 1970 his car veered into the barriers at the Parabolica, probably after a brake shaft failure; he died of his injuries. No driver could overhaul his points total in the remaining races — his nearest rival, Jacky Ickx, needed to win them all and did not — and Rindt was declared world champion, the title accepted by his widow Nina.

Legacy

Rindt's death, one of many in that dangerous era, became a symbol of the risks drivers then accepted. He remains Austria's first Formula One world champion and the sport's only posthumous champion.
