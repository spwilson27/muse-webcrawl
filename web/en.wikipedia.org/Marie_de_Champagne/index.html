Marie de Champagne

Bornc. 1145
Died1198
SpouseHenry I, Count of Champagne
ParentsLouis VII of France; Eleanor of Aquitaine

Marie de Champagne (c. 1145 – 1198) was the eldest daughter of King Louis VII of France and Eleanor of Aquitaine. As Countess of Champagne by her marriage to Henry I, she became one of the most influential patrons of literature in twelfth-century France.

Life

Marie married Henry, Count of Champagne, in 1164. She served as regent of Champagne during her husband's absence on crusade and again after his death in 1181, ruling until her son Henry II came of age.

Patronage

At her court in Troyes, Marie patronised Chrétien de Troyes, whose romance Lancelot, the Knight of the Cart was composed, by Chrétien's own account, at her command and from material she supplied. She also commissioned a translation of Genesis and corresponded with leading churchmen and poets. Her court is associated with the culture of courtly love celebrated by Andreas Capellanus in De amore.

Descendants

Her children included Henry II, Count of Champagne and King of Jerusalem, and Theobald III. Through the marriages of her daughters she was connected to courts across France and the crusader states.
