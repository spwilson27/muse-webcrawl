Motorsport

Motorsport, also known as motor racing or auto racing, is a global term used to encompass the group of competitive sporting events involving the racing of motor vehicles — automobiles, motorcycles, powerboats, and aircraft in their analogous forms. The sport is governed internationally in its car and motorcycle branches principally by the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA) and the Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme (FIM).

History

Racing began soon after the invention of the internal combustion engine: the 1894 Paris–Rouen trial is usually counted the first motoring competition, and the first true race followed in 1895 (Paris–Bordeaux–Paris). The first Grand Prix was held in France in 1906, and the Indianapolis 500 began in 1911. After the Second World War the Formula One World Championship was inaugurated in 1950.

Categories

Motorsport divides into racing on closed circuits, rallies on public or private roads, and off-road disciplines, each with numerous vehicle classes from production-based cars to purpose-built prototypes.

Single-seater racing

Open-wheel single-seaters culminate in Formula One and, in North America, the IndyCar Series. Feeder series such as Formula 2 and Formula 3 develop young drivers.

Rallying

Rally cars compete against the clock on closed special stages of tarmac, gravel, or snow, linked by public-road sections. The World Rally Championship, including classics such as the Monte Carlo Rally, is its top level; rally raid events such as the Dakar Rally cross open desert terrain.

Stock car and touring car racing

NASCAR stock cars race mainly on American ovals, while touring cars — modified production saloons — compete worldwide, most famously in series such as the British Touring Car Championship and Supercars in Australia.

Endurance racing

Sports-prototype and GT cars contest long-distance races, headed by the 24 Hours of Le Mans and the FIA World Endurance Championship, sharing the "triple crown" of endurance with Daytona and Sebring.

Safety

Safety has transformed since the sport's lethal early decades: monocoque survival cells (see Monocoque), HANS devices, halo cockpit protection, and circuit design standards have drastically reduced fatalities.
