Alpine Club

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An alpine club is an organization devoted to mountaineering, climbing, and the appreciation and protection of mountain regions. Alpine clubs typically maintain mountain huts and trails, publish journals and guidebooks, organize expeditions and training, and advocate for access and conservation. The model spread from Europe in the nineteenth century, and national alpine clubs were founded in many countries.

History

The first alpine club, the Alpine Club of London, was founded in 1857 during the golden age of alpinism. It was followed by clubs in Austria (1862), Switzerland (1863), Italy (1863), Germany (1869), and France (1874), among others. These organizations played a central role in the exploration of the Alps and later of the world's greater ranges.

Activities

Modern alpine clubs run networks of huts and refuges, maintain paths, offer courses in climbing, skiing, and mountain safety, and publish periodicals such as the Alpine Journal. Many are members of the International Climbing and Mountaineering Federation (UIAA).
