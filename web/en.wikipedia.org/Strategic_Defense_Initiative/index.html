Strategic Defense Initiative

The Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI), nicknamed the "Star Wars" program, was a proposed missile defense system intended to protect the United States from attack by ballistic strategic nuclear weapons (intercontinental ballistic missiles and submarine-launched ballistic missiles). The concept was announced on March 23, 1983, by President Ronald Reagan, a vocal critic of the doctrine of mutually assured destruction (MAD), which he described as a "suicide pact". Reagan called upon American scientists and engineers to develop a system that would render nuclear weapons obsolete and meaningfully raise the threshold for their use.

The Strategic Defense Initiative Organization (SDIO) was set up in 1984 within the US Department of Defense to oversee development. A wide array of advanced weapon concepts was studied, including space-based and ground-based lasers, particle-beam weapons, and space-based missile systems, as well as the sensor, command and control, and high-performance computer systems that would be needed to control such a system, an effort projected to take multiple decades and cost hundreds of billions of dollars.

History

Reagan's announcement followed growing concern about the vulnerability of US land-based ICBMs and the influence of the nuclear freeze movement. Research drew on earlier work, including Edward Teller's advocacy of the X-ray laser concept known as Excalibur, pursued at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory. The program attracted intense criticism from scientists, including the Union of Concerned Scientists, who judged that key technologies were decades away and that the system could be defeated by countermeasures; the Soviet Union viewed SDI as a threat to the strategic balance and a possible enabler of a US first strike.

Following the end of the Cold War, priorities shifted from strategic defense against a large-scale Soviet attack toward theater missile defense against limited strikes. In 1993 the SDIO was reorganized as the Ballistic Missile Defense Organization (BMDO), and its successor, the Missile Defense Agency, continues work on systems such as ground-based midcourse defense, Aegis BMD, THAAD, and the Patriot system.

Technology

Concepts studied under SDI included the Brilliant Pebbles system of space-based kinetic interceptors, ground-launched interceptors such as the Exoatmospheric Reentry-vehicle Interception System (ERIS), chemical and free-electron lasers, the neutral particle beam, and nuclear-pumped X-ray lasers. Layered defense was a central idea: interceptors would engage attacking missiles during boost, post-boost, midcourse, and terminal phases.
