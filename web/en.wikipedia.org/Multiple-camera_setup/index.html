Multiple-camera setup

The multiple-camera setup, or multicam, is a method of television and video production in which several cameras are used simultaneously to record or broadcast a scene. Images from the cameras are typically cut together in real time by a vision mixer or assembled afterwards in editing.

The contrasting approach, the single-camera setup, dominates film-style production such as drama and feature films, while multicam is standard for sitcoms, soap operas, talk shows, news, sport and live events.

History

Pioneered for television in the early 1950s, the technique was famously systematised by Desilu Productions for the sitcom I Love Lucy (1951), which used three 35 mm film cameras before a live studio audience — an arrangement devised by cinematographer Karl Freund that preserved film quality for syndication while capturing continuous performances.

Production

In a multicam sitcom or soap, four cameras are commonly deployed, each assigned to particular shots or actors, with the director calling the running order from a gallery. Lighting must serve all camera angles at once, favouring flatter, brighter lighting than single-camera work, and sets are built open on one side — the 'fourth wall' — for the cameras and audience. Digital workflows have made live-switched multicam common for internet streaming as well.
