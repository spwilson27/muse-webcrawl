Orders, decorations, and medals of the United Kingdom

The British honours system comprises orders of chivalry, decorations, and medals awarded by the sovereign of the United Kingdom to reward personal bravery, achievement, and service. Honours are published in the London Gazette, principally in the New Year and Birthday Honours lists, and are presented at investitures at Buckingham Palace and other royal residences.


British orders, decorations, and medals

Principal orders

  • Order of the Garter (1348) — the most senior order of chivalry.
  • Order of the Thistle — the Scottish order of chivalry.
  • Order of the Bath (1725) — for senior military officers and civil servants.
  • Order of Merit (1902) — a limited order in the sovereign's personal gift.
  • Order of St Michael and St George (1818) — for diplomats and service overseas.
  • Royal Victorian Order (1896) — for personal service to the royal family.
  • Order of the British Empire (1917) — the most widely awarded order, with five classes from GBE to MBE.
  • Order of the Companions of Honour (1917) — for outstanding achievements in the arts, science, and public life.

Gallantry and service awards

The Victoria Cross (1856) is the highest award for gallantry in the face of the enemy, ranking with the George Cross (1940) for civilian and non-combatant gallantry. Below these stand decorations such as the Conspicuous Gallantry Cross, the Distinguished Service Order, and the Military Cross, together with campaign and long-service medals.

Administration

Most nominations pass through government departments and honours committees, with the Central Chancery of the Orders of Knighthood administering awards. The system has been periodically reformed — notably under John Major in 1993, which opened most awards to public nomination — and remains debated, particularly over its association with the former British Empire and political appointments.
