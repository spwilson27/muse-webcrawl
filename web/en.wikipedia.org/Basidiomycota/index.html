Basidiomycota

Basidiomycota
Kingdom: Fungi
Subkingdom: Dikarya
Division: Basidiomycota
Subdivisions: Agaricomycotina, Pucciniomycotina, Ustilaginomycotina

Basidiomycota is one of two large divisions (phyla) of fungi that, together with the Ascomycota, constitute the subkingdom Dikarya, often called the "higher fungi". Basidiomycetes are characterised by specialised club-shaped spore-producing cells called basidia, on which (typically four) basidiospores are borne externally. The division includes the familiar mushrooms, toadstools, puffballs, bracket fungi, jelly fungi, and the plant-pathogenic rusts and smuts. More than 40,000 species have been described.

Basidia line the gills of typical gilled mushrooms.

Life cycle

Basidiomycetes typically have a prolonged dikaryotic phase: after mating of compatible haploid mycelia, the resulting secondary mycelium contains two unfused nuclei per cell, maintained through divisions involving clamp connections. Karyogamy and meiosis occur only within the basidium, often in a fruiting body (basidiocarp) such as a mushroom. Rusts and smuts, by contrast, are obligate plant parasites with complex life cycles sometimes involving two hosts.

Ecology and importance

Many agaricomycetes are the principal decomposers of wood — white-rot and brown-rot fungi — and others form ectomycorrhizal partnerships with forest trees. The group includes prized edibles (porcini, chanterelles), deadly poisonous species such as the death cap (Amanita phalloides), and devastating crop pathogens such as wheat stem rust (Puccinia graminis).
