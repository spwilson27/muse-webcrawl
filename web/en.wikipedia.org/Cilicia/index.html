Cilicia

Cilicia is a geographical region in southern Anatolia, extending inland from the northeastern coasts of the Mediterranean Sea. It is bounded by the Taurus Mountains to the north and west and has historically served as a crossroads between Anatolia, Syria, and Mesopotamia. Its principal ancient cities included Tarsus and Adana.

Geography

Cilicia was traditionally divided into two parts: Cilicia Trachea ("rough Cilicia"), the mountainous western coast, and Cilicia Pedias ("flat Cilicia"), the fertile eastern plain watered by the Pyramus (Ceyhan) and Sarus (Seyhan) rivers. The Cilician Gates through the Taurus linked the plain to the Anatolian plateau.

History

The region was known in the Bronze Age as Kizzuwatna and was contested by the Hittites, Egypt, and Mitanni. It later formed part of the Achaemenid Empire, passed to Alexander the Great, and was fought over by the Seleucids and Ptolemies. Cilicia became a Roman province in the 1st century BC after campaigns against the Cilician pirates. In the Middle Ages it was the heartland of the Armenian Kingdom of Cilicia (1080–1375), an ally of the Crusader states. The region later came under Ottoman rule and today lies within the Republic of Turkey, roughly corresponding to the provinces of Adana, Mersin, Osmaniye, and parts of Hatay.
