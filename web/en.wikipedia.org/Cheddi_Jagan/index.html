Biography

Cheddi Berret Jagan (22 March 1918 – 6 March 1997) was a Guyanese politician and dentist who served as President of Guyana from 1992 until his death in 1997, and earlier as Chief Minister and Premier of British Guiana. He and his wife Janet Jagan founded the People's Progressive Party (PPP) in 1950, and he is regarded as a founding figure — the "Father of the Nation" — of independent Guyana.

Early political career

Born at Port Mourant to Indian indentured labourers, Jagan studied dentistry at Howard University and Northwestern University in the United States. Returning to British Guiana, he entered politics, and in 1953 the PPP won the colony's first election under universal suffrage. Britain suspended the constitution after 133 days, fearing Jagan's Marxist leanings; he returned to office as Premier in 1961, but covert US and British intervention and a changed electoral system helped bring Forbes Burnham to power in 1964, and Jagan spent 28 years in opposition.

Presidency and death

Jagan won Guyana's first free and fair elections in 1992. As president he moderated his programme, pursuing debt relief, foreign investment and environmental protection, including the Iwokrama rainforest conservation initiative. He died of a heart attack in Washington, D.C. in March 1997, while seeking medical treatment; his widow Janet succeeded him as president later that year.
