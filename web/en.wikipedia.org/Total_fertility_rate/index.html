Total fertility rate

The total fertility rate (TFR) of a population is the average number of children that are born to a woman over her lifetime if she were to experience the current age-specific fertility rates throughout her reproductive years and survive to the end of them. It is one of the most widely used indicators in demography.

Replacement level

A TFR of about 2.1 children per woman is called replacement-level fertility in developed countries — the rate at which a population exactly replaces itself from one generation to the next, allowing for child mortality and the sex ratio at birth. In countries with higher mortality, the replacement level may be as high as 3.3.

Global trends

The world TFR has fallen from roughly 5 children per woman in 1950 to about 2.3 in the early 2020s, driven by urbanisation, female education, contraception and declining child mortality — a process known as the demographic transition. Rates are lowest in East Asia and Europe — South Korea recorded a TFR below 0.8, the world's lowest — and highest in sub-Saharan Africa, where Niger's rate approaches 7. More than half of all countries now have below-replacement fertility.
