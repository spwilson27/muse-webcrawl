Carbon monoxide detector

A carbon monoxide detector or CO detector is a device that detects the presence of carbon monoxide gas to prevent carbon monoxide poisoning. Carbon monoxide is a colorless, odorless gas produced by incomplete combustion in furnaces, stoves, generators, and vehicle engines; a detector is the only reliable way to sense dangerous concentrations in a home.

Sensor types and standards

Detectors use biomimetic, metal oxide semiconductor, or electrochemical sensors; electrochemical types are the most common and accurate. Standards such as UL 2034 require alarms to sound based on concentration over time (e.g., within 60–240 minutes at 70 ppm, faster at higher levels) to mimic carboxyhemoglobin buildup while avoiding nuisance alarms. Many jurisdictions require CO alarms in residences with fuel-burning appliances or attached garages. Detectors typically have a service life of 5–10 years.

Placement

Alarms are recommended on each level of a home and outside sleeping areas. CO detectors are distinct from carbon dioxide (CO2) detectors used in industrial and indoor-air-quality settings.
