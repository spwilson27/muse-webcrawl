Lough Ennell

Lough Ennell
Location: County Westmeath, Ireland
Surface area: about 12 km² (1,200 ha)
Primary outflow: River Brosna

Lough Ennell (Irish: Loch Ainninn) is a lake near the village of Castletown Geoghegan in County Westmeath, Ireland, a short distance south of Mullingar. It is roughly 6.5 kilometres long and 2 kilometres wide, with a surface area of about 12 square kilometres, and is one of the group of lakes in the Irish midlands along the course of the River Brosna.

Ecology

The lough is a designated Special Protection Area and a Ramsar wetland of international importance. It is an important site for wildfowl, supporting populations of birds such as the pochard, tufted duck and coot. Historically the lake suffered from eutrophication caused by effluent from Mullingar, but water quality improved substantially following the diversion of treated sewage in the late twentieth century, and its once celebrated wild brown trout fishery partially recovered.

History

The lake contains several islands and is associated with local folklore, including traditions that it took its name from a chieftain named Ainninn. In the surrounding area stands Belvedere House and Gardens, an eighteenth-century estate famous for the Jealous Wall folly, built by Robert Rochfort, 1st Earl of Belvedere.
