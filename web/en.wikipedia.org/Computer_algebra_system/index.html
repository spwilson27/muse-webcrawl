Computer algebra system

A computer algebra system (CAS) is a mathematical software system with the ability to manipulate mathematical expressions in a way similar to the traditional manual computations of mathematicians and scientists. Unlike numerical software, a CAS works symbolically: it can simplify expressions, factor polynomials, solve equations exactly, differentiate and integrate in closed form, and compute with arbitrary-precision numbers and abstract algebraic structures.

Capabilities

Typical operations include simplification and expansion of expressions, substitution, partial fractions, exact solution of algebraic and differential equations, symbolic differentiation and integration, matrix operations over symbolic entries, series expansions, limits, and number-theoretic functions. Many systems combine symbolic computation with numerical evaluation, plotting, and a full programming language.

History and examples

The field began in the 1960s with systems such as Schoonschip, ALPAK, and early work driven by particle physics calculations. Influential general-purpose systems include Macsyma (from which the open-source Maxima descends), REDUCE, Maple (1980), Mathematica (1988), and the calculator-based Derive and TI systems used in education. Open-source systems such as SageMath, Singular, GAP, PARI/GP, and SymPy (a Python library) are widely used in research and teaching. Specialised systems excel in areas such as polynomial algebra, group theory, and number theory.

Algorithms

Core algorithms underlying a CAS include polynomial greatest-common-divisor computation, factorisation, Gröbner bases, the Risch algorithm for indefinite integration, and algorithms for solving linear systems over many domains. Expression swell — intermediate results far larger than either input or output — is a characteristic difficulty of symbolic computation.
