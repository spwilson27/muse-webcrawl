Mitchel Resnick

Mitchel Resnick (born June 12, 1956) is an American computer scientist and educator, the LEGO Papert Professor of Learning Research at the MIT Media Lab. He directs the Lifelong Kindergarten research group and is best known for leading the development of Scratch, the widely used block-based programming language and online community for children.

Career

Resnick earned a bachelor's degree in physics from Princeton University and master's and doctoral degrees in computer science from MIT, where his 1992 dissertation explored parallel construction kits and multi-agent modeling. Working in the tradition of his mentor Seymour Papert, his research focuses on how new technologies can expand opportunities for children to design, create, and learn through making.

Projects

  • Programmable bricks and LEGO Mindstorms — Resnick's group developed the computational toys that evolved, in collaboration with the LEGO Group, into the LEGO Mindstorms robotics kits (from 1998).
  • StarLogo — a programmable modeling environment for exploring decentralized systems and emergent phenomena.
  • Scratch (launched 2007) — a visual programming environment and online community where young people create and share interactive stories, games, and animations; it grew into one of the world's largest coding communities for children.
  • Computer Clubhouse — a network of after-school learning centers, co-founded with Natalie Rusk, where youth from underserved communities work with mentors on creative projects.

Ideas and writings

In his book Lifelong Kindergarten (2017), Resnick argues that the kindergarten model of learning — creating, playing, sharing, and reflecting in a spiral — should be extended to learners of all ages. He describes creative learning through the "four Ps": Projects, Passion, Peers, and Play.
