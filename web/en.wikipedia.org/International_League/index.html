The International League (IL) is a Minor League Baseball league that operates in the United States at the Triple-A level, which is one level below Major League Baseball. The league, founded in 1884, was long centred in the northeastern United States and eastern Canada, though it has since expanded across the Midwest and South. A league champion is crowned at the end of each season; historically the title was awarded through the Governors' Cup playoffs.

History

The International League traces its origins to 1884, when the Eastern League and the New York State League merged; it adopted the name International League in 1912, reflecting its Canadian franchises in cities such as Toronto and Montreal. For much of the 20th century the league included famous clubs such as the Baltimore Orioles (a minor league team of that era), Buffalo Bisons, Rochester Red Wings and Toronto Maple Leafs.

Modern league

Following the reorganisation of Minor League Baseball in 2021, the International League absorbed teams from the former Pacific Coast League's eastern side and operates with 20 teams, each affiliated with a Major League club. Well-known members include the Columbus Clippers, Durham Bulls, Indianapolis Indians, Lehigh Valley IronPigs, St. Paul Saints and Worcester Red Sox.
