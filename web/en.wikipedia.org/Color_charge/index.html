Color charge

Color charge is a property of quarks and gluons that is related to the particles' strong interactions in the theory of quantum chromodynamics (QCD). The color charge has no relation to visible colour; the name is an analogy, because the three kinds of charge combine to a neutral state much as red, green and blue light combine to form white light.

Red, green and blue

Quarks carry one of three color charges, conventionally named red, green and blue, while antiquarks carry anticolors: antired, antigreen and antiblue. Gluons, the carriers of the strong force, carry combinations of a color and an anticolor, which allows them to change the color charge of the quarks they interact with. Because gluons themselves carry color charge, the strong force behaves very differently from electromagnetism: gluons interact with each other, leading to the phenomena of color confinement and asymptotic freedom.

Confinement

Only color-neutral combinations of quarks and gluons are observed in nature: baryons made of three quarks (one of each color), antibaryons, and mesons made of a quark and an antiquark of matching anticolor. The total color charge of an isolated system must be zero, a rule known as color confinement, which explains why free quarks are never seen.
