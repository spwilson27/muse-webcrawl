Navaratri is a major Hindu festival held in honour of the divine feminine, principally the goddess Durga and her various forms. The word is Sanskrit for "nine nights": the festival spans nine nights and ten days, and is observed four times a year in different seasons, of which the most widely celebrated is Sharada Navaratri, in the bright half of the lunar month of Ashvin (September–October).

Theology and legends

Navaratri celebrates the victory of the goddess over the buffalo demon Mahishasura, who could be killed by no man or god. In different regions the same festival is linked to other legends: in the north, to Rama's victory over Ravana (celebrated at Dussehra on the tenth day, Vijayadashami), and in the east it forms the core of Durga Puja. The nine nights are often divided into three sets of three, honouring Durga (destroyer of evil), Lakshmi (giver of wealth) and Saraswati (giver of knowledge).

Celebrations

Regional observances vary greatly. In Gujarat, devotees perform the circular garba dance and the stick dance dandiya raas around a lamp-filled pot or image of the goddess. In West Bengal, elaborate clay images of Durga are installed in decorated pandals for Durga Puja and immersed in water at the festival's end. In Karnataka, Mysore's Dasara procession is famous, while in Tamil Nadu households arrange stepped displays of dolls called golu. Many observers fast or eat only certain foods, and young girls are honoured as embodiments of the goddess (Kanya Puja).
