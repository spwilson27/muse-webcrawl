Ostróda


Country: Poland
Voivodeship: Warmian-Masurian
German name: Osterode in Ostpreußen
Population: about 33,000

Ostróda is a town in northern Poland, in the Warmian-Masurian Voivodeship, with a population of around 33,000. It lies on the Drwęca River at the edge of Lake Drwęckie, in the western part of the Masurian Lake District, a region of forests and post-glacial lakes popular for sailing and tourism.

History

The settlement developed beside a castle of the Teutonic Order, built in the 13th–14th centuries on the site of an Old Prussian stronghold, and received town rights in 1329. Known in German as Osterode, the town lay successively in the Teutonic Order's state, the Duchy of Prussia and the Kingdom of Prussia, in the province of East Prussia. Napoleon stayed at the castle in 1807. The Elbląg Canal, a remarkable 19th-century waterway whose boats are carried overland on inclined planes, begins at Ostróda. Devastated in 1945, the town was transferred to Poland after the Second World War and its German population expelled.

Today

The rebuilt Teutonic castle now houses a museum, and the town is a centre for water tourism on the surrounding lakes and canals. Ostróda is also known for the Ostróda Reggae Festival, one of Poland's largest reggae events, held annually since 2001.
