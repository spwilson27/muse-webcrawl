Naivedya

Naivedya (Sanskrit: नैवेद्य, naivedya) is a Sanskrit word meaning "that which is offered", referring in Hinduism to food offered to a deity as part of a worship ritual (puja), before being distributed to devotees as prasada (blessed food). The offering expresses devotion and gratitude, and the food, having been sanctified, is regarded as a blessing returned from the deity.

Practice

Naivedya is one of the traditional sixteen services (shodashopachara) offered to a deity. Foods offered are typically vegetarian and prepared without onion or garlic in many traditions; common offerings include fruits, sweets such as laddoo and kheer, cooked rice, milk and tulsi leaves for Vishnu. In temples, elaborate cooked meals are offered at fixed times of day, after which the food is distributed to devotees. In the Jagannath temple at Puri, the offering is known as Mahaprasad; the Bhagavad Gita (9.26) speaks of accepting a leaf, a flower, a fruit or water offered with devotion.

Significance

The ritual embodies the idea that God provides all sustenance: the devotee offers food back to the deity first, and only then eats, transforming the meal into prasada. Naivedya is also offered in Jain and some Buddhist practices in adapted forms.
