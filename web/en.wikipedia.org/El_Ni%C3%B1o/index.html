PhenomenonWarm phase of ENSO
RegionEquatorial Pacific Ocean

RecurrenceEvery 2–7 years, lasting ~9–12 months

El Niño is the warm phase of the El Niño–Southern Oscillation (ENSO) and is associated with a band of warm ocean water that develops in the central and east-central equatorial Pacific, including the area off the Pacific coast of South America. The name, Spanish for "the boy", refers to the Christ child, because the warming was often noticed around Christmas by Peruvian fishermen.

Mechanism

Normally, trade winds push warm surface water westward, allowing cold water to upwell off South America. During El Niño the trade winds weaken, warm water spreads eastward, and upwelling is suppressed, altering weather patterns worldwide.

Effects

El Niño events bring increased rainfall to the southern United States and Peru, drought to Australia and Indonesia, changes in Atlantic hurricane activity, and major disruptions to fisheries as nutrient-rich cold water fails to surface.

Notable events

Particularly strong events occurred in 1982–83, 1997–98, and 2015–16, each causing billions of dollars in weather-related damage globally.
