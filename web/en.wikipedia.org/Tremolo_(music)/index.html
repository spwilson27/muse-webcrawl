Tremolo (music)

Tremolo (Italian for "trembling") is a musical effect consisting of a rapid repetition of a single note, a rapid alternation between two notes or chords, or a rapid fluctuation in volume. It is distinct from vibrato, which varies pitch rather than amplitude or articulation.

On instruments

On bowed string instruments, tremolo is produced by very short, rapid bow strokes on a single note. On plucked and keyboard instruments it is achieved by fast reiteration of the note, as in the flamenco and classical guitar technique that imitates the sustain of a bowed line, and in the mandolin's characteristic continuous tremolo picking. On wind instruments it may be played as a rapid repetition or, more slowly, as a measured alternation resembling a trill. Drummers produce a comparable effect with a roll.

Notation

Tremolo is written with one to three slashes through the stem of a note (or placed beside it). Three slashes conventionally mean the note is to be repeated as fast as possible; one or two slashes may indicate measured repetitions in eighth or sixteenth notes. A two-note tremolo is written as two notes joined by thick beams, with their combined value equal to the duration to be filled.

Electronic tremolo

In electronic music and guitar amplification, "tremolo" denotes amplitude modulation produced by a low-frequency oscillator controlling volume — famously built into many Fender amplifiers of the 1950s and 1960s (which Fender confusingly labelled "vibrato"). The floating bridge system on the Fender Stratocaster is likewise called a "synchronized tremolo", although it in fact produces vibrato.
