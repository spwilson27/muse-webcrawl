Hampton Court Palace

Hampton Court Palace is a Grade I listed royal palace in the London Borough of Richmond upon Thames, on the River Thames in Surrey. Originally built for Cardinal Thomas Wolsey from 1514, it was given to King Henry VIII, who greatly expanded it. In the late 17th century, William III and Mary II commissioned Christopher Wren to rebuild much of the palace in Baroque style, leaving Hampton Court with two contrasting architectural halves: Tudor and Baroque.

Features

The palace is noted for its Great Hall, Chapel Royal, Tudor kitchens, the astronomical clock in Clock Court, and its gardens, which include the Great Vine (planted in 1768), the Privy Garden, and the famous hedge Maze. The Mantegna paintings known as the Triumphs of Caesar are housed in the palace.

Later history

Monarchs ceased to reside at Hampton Court after George II. Queen Victoria opened the palace to the public in 1838. A fire in 1986 damaged the King's Apartments, which were restored by 1992. Today the palace is a major tourist attraction managed by Historic Royal Palaces, and hosts the annual Hampton Court Garden Festival.
