Iceland is a Nordic island country in the North Atlantic Ocean, situated between North America and Europe on the Mid-Atlantic Ridge. With a population of roughly 380,000 in an area of 103,000 km², it is one of the most sparsely populated countries in Europe. The capital and largest city is Reykjavík, home to about two-thirds of the population.

History

Iceland was settled by Norse seafarers from about 874 CE; the traditional first settler is Ingólfr Arnarson, who founded Reykjavík. In 930 the chieftains established the Althing, one of the world's oldest surviving parliaments. Iceland came under Norwegian and then Danish rule, gaining sovereignty in 1918 and full independence as a republic in 1944. It was a founding member of NATO (1949) despite having no standing army.

Geography and geology

Sitting astride a diverging plate boundary and a mantle hotspot, Iceland is volcanically and geologically active, with geysers, hot springs, lava fields and glaciers such as Vatnajökull, Europe's largest by volume. Eruptions, including Eyjafjallajökull in 2010 and the Reykjanes eruptions of the 2020s, periodically draw worldwide attention. Almost all electricity and heating comes from renewable hydroelectric and geothermal energy.

Economy and society

Fishing and fish processing were long the economy's backbone; tourism, aluminium smelting and technology have since grown in importance. A severe banking collapse in 2008 was followed by a notable recovery. Iceland consistently ranks among the world's highest countries for quality of life, gender equality and literacy, and its language has changed remarkably little from Old Norse.
