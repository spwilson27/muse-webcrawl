Maritime interdiction

A visit, board, search and seizure (VBSS) team approaching a vessel (image placeholder).

Maritime interdiction, also called maritime interception operations, is the naval activity of boarding, inspecting, diverting or seizing vessels at sea to enforce blockades, sanctions, counter-proliferation measures, counter-piracy, fisheries laws or anti-smuggling rules.

Practice

Interdictions are commonly carried out by VBSS (visit, board, search and seizure) teams delivered by boat or helicopter from a warship. Legal bases include flag-state consent, United Nations Security Council resolutions (such as those sanctioning Iraq or North Korea), the right of visit under the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea for stateless or piratical vessels, and bilateral agreements for drug interdiction in the Caribbean and eastern Pacific.

Examples

Notable sustained interdiction efforts include the Multinational Force interceptions of Iraqi shipping in the 1990s, NATO's Operation Active Endeavour in the Mediterranean, and Combined Task Force 150 operations around the Horn of Africa.
