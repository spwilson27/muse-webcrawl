Luxury hotel

A luxury hotel is a hotel that provides a high-end, premium accommodation experience, typically characterised by spacious and well-appointed rooms, attentive personalised service, fine dining, and extensive amenities such as spas, concierge services, and fitness centres. Luxury hotels are commonly rated with four or five stars under national or international classification schemes and charge premium room rates.

Characteristics

Defining features include a high staff-to-guest ratio, 24-hour room service, valet parking, turndown service, premium bedding and toiletries, and on-site restaurants led by notable chefs. Many luxury hotels belong to chains or marketing consortia such as Leading Hotels of the World, while others are independent landmark properties, including grand hotels in capital cities and resort hotels in destinations such as the Maldives and the French Riviera.

History

The modern luxury hotel emerged in the nineteenth century with railway-age grand hotels such as the Savoy in London (1889) and the Ritz in Paris (1898). César Ritz and Auguste Escoffier pioneered the combination of opulent interiors, private bathrooms, and haute cuisine that still defines the segment. In the twentieth century, brands such as Four Seasons, Ritz-Carlton, and Aman extended the concept worldwide.
