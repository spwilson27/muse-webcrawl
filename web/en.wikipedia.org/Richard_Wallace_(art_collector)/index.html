Richard Wallace (art collector)


Sir Richard Wallace, 1st Baronet (1818–1890)

Sir Richard Wallace, 1st Baronet (21 June 1818 – 20 July 1890) was an English art collector and philanthropist, famous as the heir to one of the greatest private art collections of the 19th century and as the donor of the Wallace drinking fountains that still supply water in Paris and elsewhere.

Life

Wallace was born in London, almost certainly the illegitimate son of Richard Seymour-Conway, 4th Marquess of Hertford, though this was never publicly acknowledged. He spent much of his life in Paris as the Marquess's secretary and agent, helping to assemble the Hertford collection of paintings, furniture, porcelain and arms and armour. On the Marquess's death in 1870, Wallace inherited the collection and the Paris properties, along with estates in Ireland.

During the Siege of Paris in the Franco-Prussian War he funded an ambulance service and food relief, earning wide popularity in France. Afterwards he moved the collection to London, displaying it at Bethnal Green before installing it at Hertford House in Manchester Square, and sat as Conservative MP for Lisburn (1873–1885). He was created a baronet in 1871.

The Wallace fountains

Having seen Parisians struggle for clean water after the siege, Wallace financed the elegant cast-iron drinking fountains designed by Charles-Auguste Lebourg that bear his name; about fifty were erected in Paris in the 1870s, and examples later spread worldwide. The green fountains, with their four caryatids, remain an iconic feature of the Paris streetscape.

Legacy

Wallace died at the Château de Bagatelle in Paris in 1890. His widow, Lady Wallace, bequeathed the Hertford House collection to the British nation in 1897; it opened to the public in 1900 as the Wallace Collection, one of the world's finest museums of French 18th-century art, old master paintings and arms and armour.
