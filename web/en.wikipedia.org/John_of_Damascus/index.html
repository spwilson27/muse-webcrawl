John of Damascus (Arabic: يوحنا الدمشقي; Greek: Ἰωάννης ὁ Δαμασκηνός, Latin: Ioannes Damascenus; born Yūḥanā ibn Manṣūr ibn Sarjūn, c. 675/676 – 4 December 749) was a Christian monk, priest, hymnographer and polymath from Damascus in Syria. Born and raised in Damascus under Umayyad Muslim rule, he served at the caliph's court before becoming a monk at Mar Saba monastery near Jerusalem. He is renowned for his spirited defence of icons during the Byzantine iconoclasm controversy and for his systematic exposition of the Christian faith. He is a saint and Doctor of the Church.

Life

John's family, the Mansur, were prominent Arab Christians who had administered Damascus's finances under Byzantine and then Umayyad rule; his father Sarjun ibn Mansur served Caliph Muawiyah I and his successors. John was educated, according to tradition, by a captured Sicilian monk named Cosmas, in arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy, rhetoric and theology. He succeeded his father at court but around the year 706, amid increasing pressure on Christian officials, withdrew to the monastery of Mar Saba in the Judaean desert, where he was ordained a priest and spent the rest of his life writing, teaching and composing hymns.

Works

John's three Treatises on the Divine Images (c. 726–730) defended the veneration of icons against Emperor Leo III's iconoclasm, arguing that because God had become matter in the Incarnation, matter could mediate grace. His masterpiece, The Fountain of Knowledge, culminates in An Exact Exposition of the Orthodox Faith (De Fide Orthodoxa), the first great summa of Byzantine theology, which organised Greek patristic doctrine systematically and influenced later scholasticism in the Latin West through translation. His liturgical hymns and canons, including the Easter canon, remain central to Orthodox worship, and he is credited with major contributions to the Octoechos.

Veneration

John was named a Doctor of the Church by Pope Leo XIII in 1890. His feast day is 4 December in both East and West. A famous legend tells how, falsely accused of treason to the caliph, his hand was cut off and miraculously restored after he prayed before an icon of the Theotokos, to whom he thereafter attached a silver hand — the origin of the "Three-Handed" (Tricherousa) icon.
