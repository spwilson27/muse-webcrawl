Fire door

A fire door is a door with a fire-resistance rating, used as part of a building's passive fire protection to slow the spread of fire and smoke between compartments. Ratings are expressed in minutes — common grades include FD30 and FD60 in the UK, and 20- to 90-minute labels in North America.

Construction

Fire doors are made from timber, steel or gypsum cores with intumescent seals that expand in heat to seal gaps, combined with fire-rated hinges, closers and glazing. The complete assembly — door, frame and hardware — is tested together, for example to BS 476 or UL 10C standards.

Regulation

Building codes require fire doors in flats, stairwells, corridors and plant rooms. They must be kept closed or fitted with automatic closers, and post-Grenfell reforms in the UK tightened inspection duties for fire doors in residential buildings.
