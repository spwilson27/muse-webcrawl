Very-high-bit-rate digital subscriber line (VDSL) and VDSL2 are digital subscriber line (DSL) technologies providing data transmission faster than ADSL over a single flat untwisted or twisted pair of copper wires. VDSL achieves high speeds over short distances, making it suitable for fibre-to-the-cabinet (FTTC) deployments.

Development

VDSL was standardized by the ITU-T as recommendation G.993.1 in 2001. Its successor, VDSL2 (G.993.2), approved in 2005, offered improved rates, longer reach, and compatibility for triple-play services including voice, video, and data.

Performance

Data rates fall as loop length increases: VDSL2 can deliver around 100 Mbit/s downstream at short ranges, dropping to tens of megabits per second over a kilometre. Later enhancements include vectoring, which cancels crosstalk between lines in a binder group, and profile 35b ("supervectoring"), which extends usable spectrum.

Deployment

VDSL2 is widely used in hybrid networks where fibre runs to a street cabinet and copper completes the last few hundred metres to the customer premises.
