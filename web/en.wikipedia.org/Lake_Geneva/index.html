Lake Geneva (French: Lac Léman) is a deep lake on the north side of the Alps, shared between Switzerland and France. It is one of the largest lakes in Western Europe and the largest in Switzerland and France combined. Growing from a former Roman name, Lacus Lemanus, the lake lies on the course of the Rhône.

The crescent-shaped lake is 73 km long and up to 14 km wide, with a surface area of about 580 km² and a maximum depth of 310 metres. Some 60% of it lies within Swiss territory (the cantons of Vaud, Valais, and Geneva) and 40% within France (the department of Haute-Savoie).

Geography

The Rhône enters the lake at its eastern end near Villeneuve and leaves at its western end at Geneva. The lake's level is controlled by the Seujet dam in Geneva. Its northern shore is lined with vineyards, including the terraced Lavaux wine region, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Cities on its shores include Geneva, Lausanne, Montreux, Vevey, and Évian-les-Bains.

History

Celtic and Roman settlements ringed the lake; the Romans knew Geneva (Genava) as a border town of the Allobroges. In the Middle Ages the lake was a vital trade route for the Republic of Bern and the House of Savoy. The Château de Chillon, an island castle near Montreux, inspired Lord Byron's poem The Prisoner of Chillon. In 1816, the "Year Without a Summer", Byron, Mary Shelley, and companions stayed at the Villa Diodati by the lake, where Shelley conceived Frankenstein.

Transport and tourism

Since 1823, paddle steamers have crossed the lake; today the Compagnie Générale de Navigation operates a fleet of historic Belle Époque steamers linking Swiss and French ports. The lake is famous for the Jet d'Eau fountain in Geneva, the Montreux Jazz Festival, and its lakeside resorts.
