Ventricular tachycardia

SpecialtyCardiology
Heart rateTypically >120 bpm
OriginVentricles of the heart
TreatmentCardioversion, antiarrhythmics, ICD

Ventricular tachycardia (VT or V-tach) is a rapid heart rhythm arising from abnormal electrical activity in the ventricles of the heart. It is defined as three or more consecutive ventricular beats at a rate above 100–120 beats per minute. VT can be a life-threatening arrhythmia because it may reduce cardiac output or degenerate into ventricular fibrillation.

Classification

VT is described as sustained if it lasts more than 30 seconds or requires intervention, and non-sustained if it stops spontaneously. Monomorphic VT has a uniform QRS shape on the electrocardiogram and usually arises from a fixed substrate such as scar tissue after myocardial infarction; polymorphic VT has varying QRS complexes and includes torsades de pointes, associated with a prolonged QT interval.

Causes and symptoms

Causes include coronary artery disease, cardiomyopathy, electrolyte disturbances, and inherited channelopathies. Symptoms range from none to palpitations, dizziness, breathlessness, chest pain, and collapse. Pulseless VT is a shockable rhythm in cardiac arrest protocols.

Treatment

Unstable patients require immediate electrical cardioversion or defibrillation. Stable VT may be treated with antiarrhythmic drugs such as amiodarone. Long-term management can include catheter ablation and implantable cardioverter-defibrillators (ICDs) for patients at risk of sudden cardiac death.
