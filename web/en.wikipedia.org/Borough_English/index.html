Borough English

Legal system: English customary law
Beneficiary: Youngest son
Abolished: 1925 (Administration of Estates Act)
Also called: Ultimogeniture

Borough English was a customary law in parts of England by which real property descended to the youngest son, in contrast to the more common rule of primogeniture, under which the eldest son inherited. The custom is an example of ultimogeniture.

Distribution

The custom applied in a number of English boroughs and manors, and was recognised by name in places such as Nottingham and in various copyhold tenures. Its name is thought to distinguish it from "French" (Norman) custom, under which the eldest son inherited.

History

Various explanations have been offered for the custom, including that the youngest son, having not yet been provided for, most needed the family holding, and that the eldest sons had left for military service. Borough English survived as a local custom for centuries, applied notably in cases of intestacy.

Abolition

The custom was abolished by the Administration of Estates Act 1925, which swept away the old rules of descent of real property in England and Wales.
