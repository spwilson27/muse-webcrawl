Pearl Harbor is a lagoon harbor on the island of Oahu, Hawaii, west of Honolulu. Much of the harbor and surrounding lands is a United States Navy deep-water naval base—today part of Joint Base Pearl Harbor–Hickam—and the headquarters of the U.S. Pacific Fleet. Pearl Harbor became world-famous as the target of the surprise Imperial Japanese Navy air attack of Sunday, 7 December 1941, which brought the United States into the Second World War.

History

Hawaiians called the harbor Wai Momi ("waters of pearl") for the pearl oysters once found there. After the United States gained exclusive rights to the harbor in the Reciprocity Treaty of 1875 and its 1887 renewal, it developed Pearl Harbor into a coaling and repair station and, by the early 20th century, the principal American naval base in the Pacific. In 1940 the U.S. Pacific Fleet moved its headquarters there from the West Coast, a deployment intended to deter Japanese expansion.

The attack of 7 December 1941

At 7:48 a.m. Hawaiian time on 7 December 1941, the first of two waves of Japanese carrier aircraft—353 planes in total from six aircraft carriers under Vice Admiral Chūichi Nagumo, planned by Admiral Isoroku Yamamoto—struck the fleet and nearby airfields. All eight U.S. battleships in the harbor were damaged, four of them sunk, including the USS Arizona, destroyed by a magazine explosion that killed 1,177 of her crew; in all, 2,403 Americans were killed and 1,178 wounded, and 188 aircraft destroyed. Japan lost 29 aircraft and five midget submarines. The fleet's aircraft carriers were at sea and escaped, and the attackers left the base's fuel depots, repair yards, and submarine base intact—omissions that let Pearl Harbor support the Pacific counteroffensive. The next day President Franklin D. Roosevelt addressed Congress, calling 7 December "a date which will live in infamy," and the United States declared war on Japan; Germany and Italy declared war on the United States three days later.

Today

Pearl Harbor remains an active naval base and a major historic site. The Pearl Harbor National Memorial, including the USS Arizona Memorial built over the sunken battleship—which still leaks oil and entombs most of her dead—along with the battleship USS Missouri, the submarine USS Bowfin, and the Pearl Harbor Aviation Museum, draws about two million visitors a year.
