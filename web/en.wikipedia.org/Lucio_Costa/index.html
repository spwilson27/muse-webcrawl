Lúcio Marçal Ferreira Ribeiro Lima Costa (1902–1998) was a Brazilian architect and urban planner best known for designing the Pilot Plan (Plano Piloto) for Brasília, Brazil's purpose-built capital.

Career

Costa was a pioneer of modernism in Brazil. He led the team, including a young Oscar Niemeyer, that designed the Ministry of Education and Health building in Rio de Janeiro (1936–43), with Le Corbusier as consultant. In 1957 he won the competition for the master plan of the new capital, Brasília, with a plan shaped like an airplane or bird, organizing the city along two crossing axes with monumental public buildings and modernist residential superblocks.
