Met Office

Met Office

Founded: 1 August 1854

Headquarters: Exeter, Devon, England

Founder: Robert FitzRoy

Parent: Department for Science, Innovation and Technology

The Met Office, until 2000 officially the Meteorological Office, is the United Kingdom's national weather service. It is an executive agency of the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology and provides weather forecasts, severe weather warnings, and climate science.

History

The office was founded in 1854 under Vice-Admiral Robert FitzRoy as a service to mariners, initially within the Board of Trade. It issued its first public weather forecast in 1861 and its first storm warnings for shipping. The Met Office later came under the Ministry of Defence for most of the 20th century before becoming a trading fund.

Work

From its Exeter headquarters, the Met Office runs numerical weather prediction models on some of the world's most powerful weather supercomputers and operates the Hadley Centre for climate prediction and research. It issues the shipping forecast areas' data used by the BBC and provides services to aviation, defence, and emergency planners worldwide.
