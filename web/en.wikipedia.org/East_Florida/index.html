East Florida
Period: 1763–1821
Capital: St. Augustine
Preceded by: Spanish Florida
Succeeded by: Florida Territory (U.S.)

East Florida (Spanish: Florida Oriental) was a colony of Great Britain from 1763 to 1784 and a province of Spain from 1784 to 1821. It was created by Britain from territory ceded by Spain at the end of the Seven Years' War and corresponded roughly to the eastern part of the modern U.S. state of Florida, with its capital at St. Augustine.

British period

Under the Treaty of Paris (1763), Spain ceded Florida to Britain in exchange for the return of Havana. Britain divided the territory into East Florida and West Florida, encouraging settlement with land grants. St. Augustine remained the capital of East Florida. During the American Revolutionary War, East Florida stayed loyal to Britain and became a refuge for Loyalists fleeing the rebellious colonies.

Spanish period and cession

The Treaty of Paris (1783) returned the Floridas to Spain. Spanish rule was weak, and the region became a haven for fugitive slaves and a base for cross-border raiding. By the Adams–Onís Treaty of 1819, Spain ceded Florida to the United States; the transfer took effect in 1821, and East and West Florida were combined into the Florida Territory.
