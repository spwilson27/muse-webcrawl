DJI (SZ DJI Technology Co., Ltd.) is a Chinese technology company headquartered in Shenzhen, Guangdong, and the world's dominant manufacturer of consumer and commercial unmanned aerial vehicles (drones). Founded in 2006 by Frank Wang, the company is estimated to hold around 70% or more of the global consumer drone market, and it also produces camera gimbals, action cameras, and robotic equipment.

History and products

Wang founded DJI while a student at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, initially selling flight controllers for model helicopters. The company's breakthrough came with the ready-to-fly Phantom quadcopter (2013), which brought aerial photography to a mass market. Successive product lines — the folding Mavic series (2016), the Spark, the sub-250 g Mini series, and the cinema-oriented Inspire — made DJI drones ubiquitous in photography, filmmaking, surveying, agriculture, and public safety. Its Osmo and Ronin stabilisers and Osmo Action cameras compete in adjacent imaging markets, and DJI took a majority stake in the Swedish camera maker Hasselblad.

Controversies and restrictions

DJI's market dominance and Chinese origins have drawn scrutiny in the United States: the U.S. Department of Defense designated it a "Chinese military company", several federal agencies restricted procurement of its drones, and it was added to the Commerce Department's Entity List in 2020. DJI has denied military ties and espionage allegations. DJI drones have also been widely used by both sides in the Russo-Ukrainian War for reconnaissance and strike roles, prompting the company to suspend business in both countries in 2022.
