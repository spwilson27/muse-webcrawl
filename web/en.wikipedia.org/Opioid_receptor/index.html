Opioid receptor

Opioid receptors are a group of inhibitory G protein-coupled receptors with opioids as ligands. The endogenous opioids are dynorphins, enkephalins, endorphins, endomorphins, and nociceptin. The opioid receptors are about 40% identical to somatostatin receptors and are widely distributed in the brain, spinal cord, peripheral neurons, and digestive tract.

Major subtypes

  • μ (mu) receptor — responsible for most analgesic effects of opioids, as well as respiratory depression, euphoria, sedation, and dependence; activated by morphine and β-endorphin.
  • κ (kappa) receptor — mediates analgesia, sedation, and dysphoria; binds dynorphins.
  • δ (delta) receptor — modulates analgesia, mood, and anxiety; binds enkephalins.
  • Nociceptin receptor (NOP) — related but insensitive to naloxone at typical doses; binds nociceptin/orphanin FQ.

Mechanism

Activation of opioid receptors inhibits adenylyl cyclase, reduces calcium influx, and increases potassium efflux, decreasing neuronal excitability and neurotransmitter release. Drugs acting at these receptors include agonists (morphine, fentanyl), partial agonists (buprenorphine), and antagonists (naloxone, naltrexone) used to reverse overdose and treat dependence.
