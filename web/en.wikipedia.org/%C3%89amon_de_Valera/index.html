Éamon de Valera (14 October 1882 – 29 August 1975) was an Irish statesman and political leader. He served several terms as head of government and head of state, and had a leading role in introducing the 1937 Constitution of Ireland.

Early life and revolution

Born in New York to an Irish mother and a Spanish father, de Valera was raised in County Limerick. He was a commandant in the 1916 Easter Rising and, as the senior surviving commander, escaped execution. He led Sinn Féin, opposed the Anglo-Irish Treaty, and later founded Fianna Fáil in 1926.

Leadership

As President of the Executive Council and then Taoiseach, de Valera directed Irish neutrality during the Second World War (the Emergency), steered the drafting of the 1937 constitution, and dominated Irish politics for decades. He served two terms as President of Ireland from 1959 to 1973.
