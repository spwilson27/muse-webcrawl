Prime Minister of India

The Prime Minister of India is the head of government of the Republic of India and the chief executive of the Union Government. The prime minister is appointed by the President of India and must command the confidence of the Lok Sabha, the lower house of Parliament. The prime minister leads the Council of Ministers, allocates portfolios, and is the principal channel of communication between the president and the cabinet.

The prime minister is India's chief executive and leader of the Lok Sabha majority.

History

Jawaharlal Nehru became India's first prime minister on independence in 1947 and served until 1964, the longest tenure in the office. Indira Gandhi, the only woman to hold the post, served 1966–1977 and 1980 until her assassination in 1984. Other notable holders include Lal Bahadur Shastri, Rajiv Gandhi, P. V. Narasimha Rao, who launched economic liberalisation in 1991, Atal Bihari Vajpayee, Manmohan Singh, and Narendra Modi, who took office in 2014 and won further terms in 2019 and 2024.

Powers and role

Under Articles 74 and 75 of the Constitution, the president acts on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers headed by the prime minister. The prime minister recommends ministerial appointments and dismissals, presides over cabinet meetings, oversees the civil service, and — through the Appointments Committee of the Cabinet — approves senior bureaucratic and military appointments. The prime minister also chairs bodies such as NITI Aayog and the National Security Council apparatus, and is India's principal spokesperson on national policy.

Residence and office

The official residence is 7, Lok Kalyan Marg in New Delhi, and the prime minister's office (PMO) is located in the South Block of the Secretariat Building on Raisina Hill.
