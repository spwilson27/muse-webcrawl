Gradient descent

Gradient descent is a first-order iterative optimisation algorithm for finding a local minimum of a differentiable function. The idea is to take repeated steps in the opposite direction of the gradient of the function at the current point, because this is the direction of steepest descent.

Algorithm

Starting from an initial guess x0, the update rule is xn+1 = xn − γ∇F(xn), where γ is the step size or learning rate. If γ is small enough and F is smooth, the iterates converge to a local minimum. Choosing γ too large can cause divergence; too small slows convergence.

Variants

Batch gradient descent uses the whole dataset for each step. Stochastic gradient descent (SGD) uses a single example or a mini-batch, making it practical for large-scale machine learning. Momentum, AdaGrad, RMSProp, and Adam add adaptive step sizes or acceleration and are standard in training neural networks, where gradients are computed by backpropagation.

History and applications

The method traces to Cauchy (1847) and was adapted to machine learning through the work of Robbins and Monro on stochastic approximation. It is the workhorse optimisation method of modern deep learning, used to fit models with billions of parameters.
