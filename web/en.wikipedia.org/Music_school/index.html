Music school

A music school is an educational institution specialised in the study, training and research of music. Such institutions may be independent conservatoires, faculties or departments within universities, or schools providing private and group tuition at elementary and secondary levels.

Types

A conservatoire (or conservatory) is a music school that offers professional training in performance, composition and conducting, typically at tertiary level; famous examples include the Paris Conservatoire, the Juilliard School in New York, the Royal Academy of Music in London and the Moscow Conservatory. Universities also host music departments offering academic degrees in musicology, theory and music education. At lower levels, community music schools and private studios offer instrumental and vocal lessons to children and adult learners.

History

The term derives from the Italian conservatorio, originally orphanages in Naples and Venice where children received musical training; the model of the professional conservatoire spread through Europe in the 19th century, notably after the founding of the Paris Conservatoire in 1795. Today music schools range from state-funded academies to specialist pre-college institutions such as the Yehudi Menuhin School.
