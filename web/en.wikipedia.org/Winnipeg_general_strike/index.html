Winnipeg general strike

The Winnipeg general strike of 1919 was one of the most famous and influential strikes in Canadian history. For six weeks, from 15 May to 26 June, more than 30,000 strikers brought economic activity to a standstill in Winnipeg, Manitoba, then Canada's third-largest city.

Background

Labour unrest had been building during the First World War as inflation rose faster than wages, working conditions remained poor, and workers demanded the right to collective bargaining. Inspired partly by the Russian Revolution, western Canadian labour leaders met in Calgary in March 1919 and resolved to form One Big Union.

The strike

When negotiations in the building and metal trades broke down, the Winnipeg Trades and Labour Council called a general strike. A Central Strike Committee coordinated essential services. On 21 June, "Bloody Saturday", a streetcar driven by strikebreakers was overturned and Royal Canadian Mounted Police charged into crowds of strikers; two men were killed and many injured.

Aftermath

Strike leaders were arrested and several convicted of seditious conspiracy. The strike nonetheless strengthened the Canadian labour movement and helped launch the political career of J. S. Woodsworth, one of its arrested leaders, who later founded the Co-operative Commonwealth Federation.
