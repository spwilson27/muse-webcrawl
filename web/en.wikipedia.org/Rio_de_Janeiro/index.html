Rio de Janeiro is the capital of the state of the same name and Brazil's second-largest city, famed for its dramatic landscape of granite peaks rising between the Atlantic Ocean and Guanabara Bay. It was Brazil's capital from 1763 until 1960, when it was succeeded by Brasília.

History

Portuguese sailors reached Guanabara Bay on 1 January 1502 and, mistaking it for a river mouth, named it Rio de Janeiro ("River of January"). The city was founded in 1565 by Estácio de Sá to expel French colonists. Rio grew rich on gold from Minas Gerais in the 18th century and on coffee in the 19th. In 1808 the Portuguese royal court, fleeing Napoleon, transferred to Rio, making it the capital of the Portuguese Empire; after independence in 1822 it remained the capital of the Empire and then the Republic of Brazil.

Geography and landmarks

The city occupies a narrow strip between mountains and sea. The statue of Christ the Redeemer (1931) crowns Corcovado Mountain; a cable car climbs Sugarloaf Mountain (Pão de Açúcar) at the bay's entrance. The curved beaches of Copacabana and Ipanema in the Zona Sul are internationally famous. Large informal settlements (favelas), including Rocinha, one of the largest, climb the hillsides beside wealthy districts.

Culture and events

Rio is the home of samba and of the Rio Carnival, the world's largest. It hosted the 2014 FIFA World Cup final and the 2016 Summer Olympics — the first Olympic Games held in South America. The Maracanã Stadium is one of football's most storied venues.
