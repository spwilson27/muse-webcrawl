The Rat Verlegh Stadion is a multi-purpose stadium in Breda, Netherlands, and the home ground of football club NAC Breda. It opened in 1996, replacing the club's former home, the NAC-stadion aan de Beatrixstraat. The stadium is named after Antoon "Rat" Verlegh, a celebrated former NAC player, coach and club figure of the early 20th century.

History

The stadium was inaugurated on 11 August 1996 with a friendly match between NAC Breda and Brazilian side Grêmio, which ended in a draw. At opening it was known as the Fujifilm Stadion for sponsorship reasons, and later as the Mycom Stadion; in 2006 it took its present name in honour of Verlegh. The original capacity of about 17,750 was later expanded to roughly 19,000 seats.

Features

The Rat Verlegh Stadion is an all-seater football stadium with covered stands on all four sides. It houses the NAC Museum, club offices, hospitality facilities and a supporters' home. The ground is noted in Dutch football for the intensity of its atmosphere, with NAC's home matches traditionally played on Saturday evenings, a fixture fans call the Avondje NAC ("an evening out at NAC").
