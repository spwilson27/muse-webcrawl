Space suit

TypePersonal life-support garment
Used forExtravehicular activity (EVA); launch and re-entry protection

A space suit is a garment worn to keep a human alive in the harsh environment of outer space — vacuum, extreme temperatures and radiation — and sometimes inside spacecraft during launch, docking and landing. A full extravehicular suit is effectively a miniature spacecraft: it supplies pressure, oxygen, cooling, power and communications to its wearer.

Requirements

In vacuum, a suit must press on the body to prevent ebullism (the formation of gas bubbles in body fluids). Suits maintain internal pressure — often pure oxygen at about 30 kPa in US suits — while staying flexible enough to work in. They must remove exhaled carbon dioxide, regulate temperature against sunlight and shadow, shield against micrometeoroids, provide drinking water, and support radio communication. Because a suit pressurised with pure oxygen is stiff, astronauts "pre-breathe" oxygen before spacewalks to purge nitrogen and avoid decompression sickness.

Types

  • Intravehicular (IVA) suits are lighter pressure suits worn inside the craft for launch and re-entry emergencies, such as the Russian Sokol suit and NASA's orange Advanced Crew Escape Suit ("pumpkin suit").
  • Extravehicular (EVA) suits, such as NASA's Extravehicular Mobility Unit (EMU) and Russia's Orlan, are self-contained with a backpack life-support system (PLSS) providing hours of independent operation.
  • Combined suits, like the Apollo A7L, served both roles on lunar missions, with extra layers against lunar dust and temperature extremes.

History

Space suits evolved from high-altitude pressure suits for aviators; Wiley Post wore an early full-pressure suit in 1934. Yuri Gagarin wore the SK-1 suit in 1961, and Alexei Leonov used a Berkut suit for the first spacewalk in 1965. Apollo suits enabled twelve astronauts to walk on the Moon between 1969 and 1972. The Shuttle-era EMU, introduced in 1981, still serves on the International Space Station alongside Orlan suits. New suits in development include NASA's xEMU for Artemis lunar missions — being developed with commercial partners — and commercial suits by SpaceX and others for private missions, such as the suit worn for the first commercial spacewalk in 2024.
