DECT (Digital Enhanced Cordless Telecommunications, originally Digital European Cordless Telecommunications) is a standard maintained by the European Telecommunications Standards Institute (ETSI) for short-range cordless telephony. It is the dominant standard for domestic cordless phones worldwide and is also widely used in enterprise telephone systems, wireless PBXs, and baby monitors.

Technical characteristics

DECT operates in dedicated spectrum around 1.9 GHz, separated from Wi-Fi and mobile phone bands, using TDMA with 10 radio channels divided into time slots that support 12 simultaneous voice connections per channel. Typical range is up to about 50 metres indoors and 300 metres outdoors. The Generic Access Profile (GAP) ensures interoperability between handsets and base stations from different manufacturers. Voice is traditionally carried with the G.726 ADPCM codec; newer CAT-iq profiles support wideband (HD) voice and data features.

Evolution

Standardised in 1992, DECT became the default cordless phone technology in Europe and most of the world outside North America, where a variant marketed as "DECT 6.0" operates in the 1920–1930 MHz UPCS band. The New Generation DECT (CAT-iq) profiles added internet-connected services and improved audio, and the DECT evolution continues with DECT-2020 NR, an ETSI standard recognised by the ITU as an IMT-2020 (5G) technology for massive machine-type communication and ultra-reliable low-latency links.
