Arta (Greek: Άρτα) is a city in northwestern Greece, capital of the regional unit of Arta in Epirus. It lies on the east bank of the Arachthos River near the Ambracian Gulf. In antiquity it was known as Ambracia, a colony of Corinth and later a capital of King Pyrrhus of Epirus.

History

Ambracia was founded in the 7th century BC and fought in the Peloponnesian War; Pyrrhus made it his capital in the 3rd century BC. Augustus relocated its population to Nicopolis after 31 BC, but the city revived in the Byzantine period. After 1204 Arta became the capital of the Despotate of Epirus, whose rulers built its Byzantine castle and churches such as the Panagia Parigoritissa (1282–89), notable for its unusual domed architecture. Arta passed to the Ottomans in 1449 and was ceded to Greece in 1881 by the Convention of Constantinople, briefly changing hands again during the war of 1897.

The Bridge of Arta

The city's most famous landmark is the stone arch Bridge of Arta over the Arachthos, built in its present form in the early 17th century. It is the subject of a well-known Greek folk ballad, in which the master builder's wife must be immured in the foundations for the bridge to stand — a motif found across Balkan folklore.

Economy

Arta is a centre for citrus fruit (oranges), olives, kiwifruit and livestock farming on the fertile plain of the Arachthos delta, and hosts a campus of the University of Ioannina.
