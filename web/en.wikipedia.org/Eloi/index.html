Eloi

The Eloi are one of the two fictional post-human races, along with the Morlocks, in H. G. Wells's 1895 novella The Time Machine. When the Time Traveller arrives in the year 802,701 AD, he finds humanity divided into the Eloi, who live a banal life of ease on the surface of the Earth in a state of apparent contentment, and the subterranean Morlocks, who maintain the machinery that supports this life.

Description

The Eloi are small, elegant, childlike adults — roughly four feet tall — who live together in large community halls, speak a simple language, eat only fruit, and show little curiosity or physical strength. Wells presents them as the descendants of the leisured classes, degenerated through generations without hardship, just as the Morlocks descend from the working classes. At night the cannibalistic Morlocks emerge to prey upon the Eloi.

Interpretation and legacy

The division of the Eloi and Morlocks is usually read as Wells's satire and warning about Victorian class inequality carried to its evolutionary extreme. The Eloi have since become a byword in popular culture for a soft, complacent elite or species.
