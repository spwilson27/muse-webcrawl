Leslie Cheung

Leslie Cheung Kwok-wing (12 September 1956 – 1 April 2003) was a Hong Kong singer and actor, regarded as one of the founding fathers of Cantopop and one of the most influential cultural icons in the Chinese-speaking world. Combining a chart-topping music career with acclaimed dramatic roles, he was affectionately known as "Gor Gor" ("elder brother").

Career

Cheung rose to fame in the 1980s with hits such as "Monica" and "Wild Wind", rivalling Alan Tam in the era's great Cantopop rivalry. As an actor he achieved international recognition in films including John Woo's A Better Tomorrow (1986), Stanley Kwan's Rouge (1988), Wong Kar-wai's Days of Being Wild (1990) — for which he won the Hong Kong Film Award for Best Actor — Ashes of Time (1994) and Happy Together (1997), and Chen Kaige's Farewell My Concubine (1993), the first Chinese-language film to win the Palme d'Or at Cannes.

Death and legacy

Cheung, who suffered from depression, died on 1 April 2003 after falling from the Mandarin Oriental hotel in Hong Kong. His death drew enormous public mourning; he is remembered as a pioneering, openly bisexual Asian superstar and a defining figure of Hong Kong cinema's golden age. In 2010 CNN named him one of the "Top 25 Asian Actors of All Time".
