Frank–Read source

A Frank–Read source is a mechanism proposed to explain the generation of multiple dislocations in specific well-spaced slip planes in crystals when they are deformed. When a crystal is deformed, slip must occur on the slip planes to allow the crystal to deform in a way that produces the least amount of lattice distortion. In the Frank–Read model, a dislocation line pinned at both ends by obstacles bows out under an applied shear stress and eventually pinches off, forming a closed dislocation loop together with a regenerated straight segment that can repeat the process.

Mechanism

The source consists of a dislocation segment pinned at two points, often by other dislocations, precipitates, or nodes in a dislocation network. Under an applied shear stress the segment bows out into an arc. When the arc reaches a semicircle, further stress causes the loop to expand unstably; the two ends of the expanding loop meet behind the pinning points, annihilate, and leave a complete dislocation loop plus a restored straight segment. Each cycle emits one loop, so a single source can generate many dislocations, accounting for the rapid multiplication of dislocations observed during plastic deformation and the phenomenon of slip bands.

History

The mechanism was proposed independently around 1950 by F. C. Frank and W. T. Read Jr. It provided a convincing answer to a central problem in early dislocation theory: the number of dislocations initially present in a crystal is far too small to account for the large strains observed in plastic flow, so some multiplication mechanism is required. Frank–Read sources were later observed directly in silicon crystals using X-ray topography, confirming the model.
