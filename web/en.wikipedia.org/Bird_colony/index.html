Bird colony

A bird colony is a large congregation of individuals of one or more species of bird that nest or roost in proximity to each other at a particular location. Many kinds of birds are known to congregate in large groups of varying size; a congregation of nesting birds is called a breeding colony.

Examples

Colonial nesting is characteristic of many seabirds — gannets, guillemots, puffins, penguins and albatrosses nest in colonies on cliffs and islands — and of herons and egrets in heronries, rooks in rookeries, and swallows and martins in mud-nest colonies. The sociable weaver of southern Africa builds enormous communal nest structures housing hundreds of birds.

Advantages and disadvantages

Coloniality can reduce predation through dilution, communal defence and many watchful eyes, and can help birds locate patchy food through information sharing. Against this, colonies concentrate competition for food and nest sites, facilitate the spread of parasites and disease, and attract predators. Historical colonies of the passenger pigeon were among the largest bird aggregations ever recorded.
