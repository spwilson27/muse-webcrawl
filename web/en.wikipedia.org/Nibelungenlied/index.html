Nibelungenlied

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A page of the poem's Handschrift C

The Nibelungenlied (German for "The Song of the Nibelungs") is an epic poem written around 1200 in Middle High German. Its author is unknown, though it is thought to have been composed in the Danube region of what is now Austria. The poem is based on an oral tradition that has some of its origins in historic events and individuals of the 5th and 6th centuries, preserved in Germanic heroic legend, especially in Old Norse parallels of the same story.

Plot

The poem tells of the dragon-slayer Siegfried, who wins the hand of the Burgundian princess Kriemhild; of his murder by the Burgundian courtier Hagen, who sinks Siegfried's treasure in the Rhine; and of Kriemhild's terrible revenge, years later, at the court of her second husband, Etzel (Attila), King of the Huns, which destroys the Burgundian kingdom. The cast also includes the Burgundian king Gunther and the Icelandic queen Brünhild.

Survival and influence

The poem was largely forgotten after the 16th century but rediscovered in the 18th, when it was hailed as the German Iliad. It became Germany's national epic, with mixed consequences: nineteenth-century nationalism elevated it, and the Nazis exploited its themes of loyalty. Richard Wagner drew on related sources for his opera cycle Der Ring des Nibelungen. Fritz Lang's two-part film Die Nibelungen (1924) is a celebrated adaptation. The manuscript is inscribed on UNESCO's Memory of the World register.

Its 39 chapters (âventiuren) comprise some 2,400 stanzas in the distinctive Nibelungen stanza form.
