Degania Bet (Hebrew: דְּגַנְיָה ב׳) is a kibbutz in northern Israel. Located south of the Sea of Galilee adjacent to its sister community Degania Alef, it falls under the jurisdiction of the Emek HaYarden Regional Council.

History

Degania Bet was founded in 1920 by Jewish immigrants of the Second Aliyah, a decade after Degania Alef — often described as the first kibbutz — was established nearby. Its name, like that of Degania Alef, derives from the Hebrew word dagan (grain) and possibly the village's abundant cornflowers. The two Deganias were among the earliest experiments in collective agricultural settlement in the region.

Economy

Historically based on agriculture — bananas, avocados, dates, field crops and dairy cattle — Degania Bet later developed industrial enterprises, including the Degania Sprayers company, a manufacturer of agricultural sprayers, and tourism centred on the nearby Sea of Galilee and the Jordan River baptismal site at Yardenit.

Notable residents

The kibbutz has been home to several figures in Israeli public life, and its cemetery, like that of Degania Alef, contains the graves of pioneers of the kibbutz movement.
