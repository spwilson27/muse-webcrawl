Group velocity dispersion

Group velocity dispersion (GVD) is a characteristic of a dispersive medium describing how the group velocity of a wave depends on its frequency or wavelength. Quantitatively, it is the derivative of the inverse group velocity with respect to angular frequency; in fibre optics it is commonly expressed through the dispersion parameter D, in units of ps/(nm·km), which describes the temporal spreading of an optical pulse per unit spectral width and unit propagation distance.

Physical origin

GVD arises from two contributions: material dispersion, the frequency dependence of the refractive index of the medium, and waveguide dispersion, which results from the dependence of the guided mode's confinement on wavelength. In standard single-mode telecommunications fibre, total dispersion crosses zero near 1310 nm and is anomalous (about +17 ps/(nm·km)) at the low-loss 1550 nm window.

Effects

Because different spectral components of a pulse travel at different group velocities, GVD causes pulses to broaden in time, limiting the bandwidth of optical communication links. In ultrafast optics, GVD stretches and chirps femtosecond pulses; it is compensated with prism pairs, grating pairs or chirped mirrors. Combined with the Kerr nonlinearity, anomalous GVD enables the formation of optical solitons, in which dispersion and self-phase modulation balance exactly.

Measurement

GVD is measured by techniques including white-light interferometry, frequency-resolved optical gating, and the modulation phase-shift method used for installed fibre spans.
