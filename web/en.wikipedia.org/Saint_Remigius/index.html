Saint Remigius

Bornc. 437, Cerny-en-Laonnois, Gaul
Died13 January 533, Reims
EpiscopateBishop of Reims (c. 459–533)
Feast1 October (translation); 13 January

Saint Remigius (c. 437–533) was Bishop of Reims for more than seventy years and is remembered as the "Apostle of the Franks" for baptising Clovis I, king of the Franks.

Life

Elected bishop at about twenty-two, Remigius became a leading churchman of post-Roman Gaul. Around 496 (dates vary) he baptised Clovis at Reims along with, according to Gregory of Tours, three thousand of his warriors — an event traditionally seen as foundational for the alliance between the Frankish monarchy and the Catholic Church, and recalled throughout French history.

Legacy

Remigius founded churches and bishoprics across northern Gaul. His remains were enshrined in the Abbey of Saint-Rémy at Reims, and the holy ampulla used in French coronations was linked by legend to Clovis's baptism.
