Tai'an

Tai'an (Chinese: 泰安; pinyin: Tài'ān) — a prefecture-level city in western Shandong province, China. Best known for Mount Tai (Tai Shan), which lies within its jurisdiction.

Tai'an (Chinese: 泰安; pinyin: Tài'ān) is a prefecture-level city in Shandong province of east-central China. It contains the famous Mount Tai, which is an important site of pilgrimage and one of the Five Great Mountains of China. The city administers several districts and county-level divisions and has a population of several million.

Geography

Tai'an lies on the southern slope of Mount Tai in the centre-west of Shandong. The Dawen River flows through the city. The metropolitan area is connected by high-speed rail on the Beijing–Shanghai corridor.

History and culture

The area was a centre of the Dawenkou culture in Neolithic times. For more than two millennia, emperors travelled to Mount Tai to perform the Feng and Shan sacrifices. The Dai Temple (Dai Miao) in the city centre, one of the largest ancient building complexes in China, served as the starting point for imperial pilgrimages up the mountain.

Economy

Tourism connected with Mount Tai dominates the service economy; industry includes mining, machinery, and building materials. The city is also a regional transport hub.
