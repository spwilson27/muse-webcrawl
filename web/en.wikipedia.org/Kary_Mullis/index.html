Kary Banks Mullis (28 December 1944 – 7 August 2019) was an American biochemist who invented the polymerase chain reaction (PCR), a technique that revolutionised molecular biology, medicine, and forensic science. He shared the 1993 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for the invention.

PCR

Working at the Cetus Corporation in Emeryville, California, Mullis conceived PCR in 1983 — he later described the insight arriving during a night drive along Highway 128 in Mendocino County. The method uses repeated cycles of heating and cooling with short primer sequences and a DNA polymerase to amplify a chosen segment of DNA exponentially; the later adoption of the heat-stable Taq polymerase from Thermus aquaticus made the process practical and automatable. PCR became indispensable for gene cloning, diagnostics, paternity and forensic testing, ancient DNA studies, and the detection of pathogens.

Life and controversies

Mullis earned his PhD in biochemistry from the University of California, Berkeley, in 1973, and after Cetus worked for several companies and founded his own ventures. A famously eccentric figure, he wrote the memoir Dancing Naked in the Mind Field (1998). Some of his later public positions — including scepticism about the link between HIV and AIDS and about aspects of climate science — were widely criticised by the scientific community. He died of pneumonia in 2019.
