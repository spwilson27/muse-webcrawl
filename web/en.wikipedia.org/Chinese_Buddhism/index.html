Chinese Buddhism

Chinese Buddhism
Chinese: 漢傳佛教
Introduced: Han dynasty, via the Silk Road
Main forms: Mahayana schools including Chan, Pure Land, Tiantai, Huayan

Chinese Buddhism (Han Chinese Buddhism) is the form of Buddhism that developed in China from its introduction during the Han dynasty, transmitted along the Silk Road from India and Central Asia. It is predominantly Mahayana and interacted deeply with Daoism and Confucianism.

History

Tradition dates Buddhism's arrival to the 1st century CE. Translators such as Kumārajīva (4th–5th century) and the pilgrim Xuanzang, who travelled to India in the 7th century, made Sanskrit texts available in Chinese, forming the Chinese Buddhist canon.

Schools

Distinctively Chinese schools emerged, including Tiantai, Huayan, Chan (known in Japan as Zen) and Pure Land, the latter two becoming the most widely practised. Chinese Buddhism also spread to Korea, Japan and Vietnam.

Modern period

After decline in the late Qing and disruption in the 20th century, Buddhism revived in mainland China, Taiwan and overseas Chinese communities, alongside humanistic Buddhism movements.
