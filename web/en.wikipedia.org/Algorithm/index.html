In mathematics and computer science, an algorithm is a finite sequence of rigorous, unambiguous instructions, typically used to solve a class of specific problems or to perform a computation. Algorithms are used as specifications for calculations, data processing, automated reasoning and decision-making. The word derives from the Latinised name of the 9th-century Persian mathematician Muḥammad ibn Mūsā al-Khwārizmī.

History

Step-by-step procedures are ancient: Babylonian clay tablets record algorithms for arithmetic, and Euclid's algorithm for the greatest common divisor (c. 300 BC) is still in use. Al-Khwārizmī's treatises transmitted systematic methods for solving equations. Ada Lovelace's notes on the Analytical Engine (1843) contain what is often called the first computer algorithm. In the 1930s the Church–Turing thesis gave the intuitive notion a precise formal meaning through Turing machines and the λ-calculus.

Analysis

Algorithms are analysed for correctness and for efficiency, measured by time and space complexity (commonly in big O notation) as a function of input size. Problems solvable in polynomial time form the class P; the famous P versus NP question asks whether problems whose solutions can be verified quickly can also be solved quickly. Design strategies include divide and conquer, dynamic programming, greedy methods and randomisation.

Applications

Algorithms underpin sorting and searching, cryptography, compression, networking, databases, graphics and machine learning. In wider society the word now also denotes the opaque decision procedures of search engines, recommendation systems and social-media feeds, raising questions of bias, transparency and accountability studied under the heading of algorithmic accountability.
