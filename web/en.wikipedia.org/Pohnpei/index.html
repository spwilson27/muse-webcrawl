Pohnpei (formerly known as Ponape) is an island in the Senyavin Islands, part of the Caroline Islands chain in the western Pacific Ocean. It is the largest and most populous island of the Federated States of Micronesia (FSM) and gives its name to Pohnpei State, one of the country's four states. The national capital, Palikir, is located on the island's northwestern coast, while Kolonia is its largest town.

Geography

Pohnpei is a high volcanic island surrounded by a barrier reef and lagoon with numerous small islets. Its rugged interior, rising to Mount Nanlaud (782 m), is among the wettest places on Earth, with annual rainfall in the mountains exceeding 7,600 mm (300 in). Lush jungle, mangrove coasts, and waterfalls characterize the landscape. The island's name means "upon a stone altar" in the Pohnpeian language.

Nan Madol

Off Pohnpei's eastern shore lies Nan Madol, a ruined city of some 92 artificial islets built of basalt logs and coral in a shallow lagoon, covering about 80 hectares. It was the ceremonial and political seat of the Saudeleur dynasty, which ruled Pohnpei from roughly the 12th to the 17th century, and remains one of the great archaeological enigmas of the Pacific. It was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2016 and simultaneously placed on the List of World Heritage in Danger.

History

Pohnpei was settled by Micronesian voyagers over two millennia ago. According to oral tradition, the Saudeleur dynasty unified the island before being overthrown by the culture hero Isokelekel, founder of the Nahnmwarki chiefly system that still structures Pohnpeian society. The island was claimed by Spain (as part of the Caroline Islands), sold to Germany in 1899, seized by Japan in 1914, and administered by the United States after the Second World War as part of the Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands, before the FSM became independent in free association with the United States in 1986.

Culture

Pohnpei is known throughout Micronesia for sakau (kava), pounded from pepper root and drunk ceremonially, and for its elaborate feasting culture involving yams, breadfruit, and pigs, bound up with the traditional title system. Pohnpeian is the main local language, alongside English as an official language.
