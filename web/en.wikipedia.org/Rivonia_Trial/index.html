The Rivonia Trial was a trial that took place in South Africa between 9 October 1963 and 12 June 1964. The apartheid government charged ten leaders of the African National Congress (ANC) with sabotage and conspiracy to overthrow the state, offences carrying the death penalty.

Arrests and charges

On 11 July 1963 police raided Liliesleaf Farm in Rivonia, a suburb of Johannesburg used as a secret headquarters by the ANC's armed wing, uMkhonto we Sizwe, arresting most of its high command. Nelson Mandela, already in prison, was added to the accused, along with Walter Sisulu, Govan Mbeki, Ahmed Kathrada, Denis Goldberg, and others. Documents seized at the farm, including plans for guerrilla warfare, formed the core of the prosecution case led by Percy Yutar; the defence team was led by Bram Fischer.

The trial and Mandela's speech

At the opening of the defence case on 20 April 1964, Mandela spoke from the dock for some four hours in the celebrated "I Am Prepared to Die" speech, explaining the ANC's turn to sabotage and ending by declaring that a democratic and free society was an ideal "for which I am prepared to die". On 11 June 1964 eight of the accused were convicted; the following day all eight were sentenced to life imprisonment rather than death, reportedly after international pressure. Goldberg went to Pretoria Central Prison; the others were sent to Robben Island.

Legacy

The trial removed the ANC's leadership for a generation and drove the liberation movement further underground and into exile. The convicted men were released between 1989 and 1990 as apartheid unravelled; Mandela walked free on 11 February 1990 and became South Africa's first democratically elected president in 1994. Liliesleaf is now a museum and heritage site.
