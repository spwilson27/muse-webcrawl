Information Age

Also calledComputer Age, Digital Age, New Media Age
Periodc. 1970s – present
Preceded byIndustrial Age
Key technologiesDigital computers, the Internet, mobile phones, social media

The Information Age (also known as the Computer Age, Digital Age, or New Media Age) is a historical period that began in the late 20th century. It is characterised by a rapid shift from traditional industries established by the Industrial Revolution to an economy and society centred on information technology — the digitisation of information, widespread computing, and near-instant global communication.

Origins

The foundations were laid by the transistor (1947), the integrated circuit (1958), and the development of digital computers and telecommunications networks. Claude Shannon's information theory (1948) provided the mathematical basis for digital communication. The creation of ARPANET in 1969, the personal computer in the late 1970s and 1980s, and Tim Berners-Lee's World Wide Web (1989–1991) brought networked computing into homes and businesses.

Characteristics

In the Information Age, the creation, distribution, and manipulation of information is a primary economic and cultural activity. Employment shifted toward knowledge work and services; commerce became global and electronic; and media were transformed by digitisation, search engines, and later by social media and smartphones, which placed networked computers in billions of pockets. Storage and processing costs collapsed following trends such as Moore's law, enabling big data and cloud computing.

Effects and criticism

Supporters emphasise the democratisation of knowledge, new forms of creativity and commerce, and gains in scientific collaboration. Critics point to misinformation, erosion of privacy, surveillance, attention-economy harms, digital divides between connected and unconnected populations, and the concentration of power in large technology platforms.
