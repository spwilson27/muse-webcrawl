Challenge for cause

A challenge for cause is a request that a prospective juror be dismissed because a specific reason exists — such as bias, prejudice, or a relationship to a party — suggesting the juror cannot be fair or impartial. It is a standard part of voir dire, the questioning of prospective jurors during jury selection in United States and other common-law courts.

Procedure

Unlike peremptory challenges, which are limited in number and usually require no stated reason, challenges for cause are unlimited in number. However, the challenging party must articulate grounds, and the judge decides whether the juror's views or circumstances would prevent or substantially impair the juror's ability to decide the case fairly. A juror who states they can set aside bias and decide on the evidence may be "rehabilitated" and the challenge denied.

Grounds

Typical grounds include a financial interest in the outcome, kinship or a close relationship with a party, witness, or counsel, prior knowledge of the case combined with a fixed opinion, or expressed bias for or against a side. In capital cases, jurors whose views on the death penalty would prevent them from following the law may be excused for cause (a Witherspoon/Wainwright v. Witt exclusion).

Appellate review

Erroneous grant or denial of a challenge for cause is a common ground of appeal, though trial courts are given substantial deference because they observe the juror's demeanour.
