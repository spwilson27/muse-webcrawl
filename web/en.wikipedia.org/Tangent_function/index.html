Tangent function

This article is about the trigonometric tangent function.

In mathematics, the tangent function, written tan, is a trigonometric function of an angle. The tangent of an acute angle in a right triangle is the ratio of the length of the opposite side to the length of the adjacent side: tan(θ) = opposite / adjacent. Equivalently, tangent is the ratio of sine to cosine: tan(θ) = sin(θ) / cos(θ).

Properties

The tangent function has period π, vertical asymptotes where cos(θ) = 0, and ranges over all real numbers. Its derivative is sec²(θ), and its inverse is the arctangent function. On the unit circle, tangent equals the slope of the terminal side of the angle.

History

The modern symbol "tan" was introduced by Albert Girard in the 17th century; the function itself was developed earlier by Islamic mathematicians including al-Battani.
