Istanbul Convention

The convention is the first legally binding instrument on violence against women in Europe.

The Council of Europe Convention on preventing and combating violence against women and domestic violence, better known as the Istanbul Convention, is a human rights treaty of the Council of Europe opposing violence against women and domestic violence, opened for signature on 11 May 2011 in Istanbul, Turkey.

Provisions

The convention obliges states to prevent violence, protect victims and prosecute offenders — the "three Ps". It criminalises psychological and physical violence, stalking, sexual violence including rape (defined by lack of consent), forced marriage, female genital mutilation, forced abortion and sterilisation, and so-called "honour" crimes. An independent expert group, GREVIO, monitors implementation.

Adoption and controversy

The treaty entered into force on 1 August 2014 and has been ratified by most Council of Europe member states, as well as by the European Union in 2023. Turkey, the first ratifier, withdrew in 2021 by presidential decree, a move widely protested. In several countries the convention has faced opposition from critics who object to its provisions on gender, generating ratification delays and political debate.
