Book of Tobit

Written: c. 3rd–2nd century BCE

Language: Aramaic (original), Greek

Status: Deuterocanonical

Chapters: 14

The Book of Tobit is a Jewish work of the Second Temple period, composed probably in the third or second century BCE. It tells the story of Tobit, a pious Israelite exiled in Nineveh who is blinded, and of his son Tobias, whose journey — guided unknowingly by the archangel Raphael — ends in marriage, the recovery of a family fortune, and the healing of his father's sight. The book is regarded as canonical by Catholic and Orthodox churches but as apocryphal by Protestants and is absent from the Hebrew Bible.

Story

Tobit, blinded by sparrow droppings after burying a murdered countryman, sends Tobias to Media to recover money deposited years before. Accompanied by Raphael in human guise, Tobias catches a great fish in the Tigris whose organs prove medicinal and protective; he marries Sarah, whose seven previous husbands had been killed by the demon Asmodeus, and drives the demon away with the burning fish heart and liver. Returning home, he restores Tobit's sight with the fish's gall.

Texts and themes

The book circulated in Greek, but fragments among the Dead Sea Scrolls confirmed an Aramaic original. Its themes — almsgiving, prayer, burying the dead, honoring parents, and marriage within the community — made it a favourite subject in Renaissance and Baroque art, where "Tobias and the Angel" became a standard motif.
