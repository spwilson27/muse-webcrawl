Utqiaġvik, Alaska

Utqiaġvik is the Iñupiaq spelling of the name of the city officially known as Utqiagvik, Alaska, the northernmost community in the United States. The name derives from utqiq, a word for wild roots gathered in the area, and the suffix -vik, "place where".

Spelling

The letter ġ (g with dot above) in Iñupiaq orthography represents a voiced uvular fricative. Official federal and state usage adopted the simplified spelling "Utqiagvik" when voters approved the name change from Barrow in 2016, though the fully diacritic spelling Utqiaġvik appears in linguistic and community contexts.

Overview

The city, borough seat of the North Slope Borough, lies on the Arctic coast of Alaska above the Arctic Circle. It is a centre of Iñupiaq culture and subsistence bowhead whaling, experiences about two months of polar night each winter, and is accessible only by air and seasonal barges.
