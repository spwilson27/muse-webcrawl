Mestizo

Mestizo (Spanish: mestizo, feminine mestiza) is a term used in Latin America and Spain for a person of mixed European and Indigenous American ancestry. It emerged under the Spanish colonial caste system, which classified people by parentage.

History

During the colonial period, mestizos occupied an intermediate position in the casta hierarchy, below peninsulares and criollos but above Indigenous people and enslaved Africans. As their numbers grew, mestizaje — the mixing of peoples and cultures — became the demographic foundation of most Latin American nations.

Modern usage

In many countries, including Mexico, Colombia, Ecuador, and Peru, mestizos form the majority or a large plurality of the population, and "mestizo" is often a broad cultural as well as ethnic identity. In the Philippines, the term mestizo historically referred to people of mixed Filipino and Spanish or Chinese ancestry. Modern scholarship and identity movements continue to debate the term's colonial origins and its role in national ideologies of mestizaje.
