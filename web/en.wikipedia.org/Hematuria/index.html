Hematuria

Hematuria or haematuria is defined as the presence of blood or red blood cells in the urine. "Gross hematuria" occurs when urine appears red, brown, or tea-colored due to the presence of blood. Hematuria may also be subtle and only detectable with a microscope or chemical test strips; this is called "microscopic hematuria". Hematuria can be painful or painless and is associated with a number of conditions ranging from minor to life-threatening.

Causes

Common causes of hematuria include urinary tract infections, kidney or bladder stones, benign prostatic hyperplasia in older men, vigorous exercise, and trauma. Glomerular causes include IgA nephropathy, thin basement membrane disease, and glomerulonephritis, which may present with dysmorphic red blood cells and proteinuria. Malignancies of the bladder, kidney, and prostate are important causes to exclude, particularly in older patients and smokers.

Diagnosis

Evaluation typically begins with urinalysis and urine microscopy to confirm red blood cells and look for casts, crystals, bacteria, and protein. Depending on the findings and risk factors, further workup may include urine culture, blood tests of kidney function, imaging such as CT urography or ultrasound, and cystoscopy to inspect the bladder. Guidelines recommend risk stratification, with high-risk patients receiving both cystoscopy and upper-tract imaging.

Treatment

Treatment is directed at the underlying cause: antibiotics for infection, procedures to remove or fragment stones, and specialist management for glomerular disease or malignancy. Gross hematuria with clots can occasionally cause urinary retention and may require catheter irrigation.
