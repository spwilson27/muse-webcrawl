Feluda, whose full name is Prodosh Chandra Mitter, is a fictional Bengali private investigator created by the Indian filmmaker and writer Satyajit Ray. The character debuted in the 1965 novella Feludar Goendagiri ("Feluda's Investigations"), published in the children's magazine Sandesh, and went on to star in 35 stories — novels and short stories — written by Ray between 1965 and 1992. Feluda is one of the most beloved characters in Bengali literature and popular culture.

Character

Feluda is a tall, athletic, sharp-witted detective in his late twenties, based in Kolkata, who solves cases with powers of observation and deduction modelled partly on Sherlock Holmes. His cases are narrated in the first person by his young cousin and assistant Tapesh Ranjan Mitter, nicknamed Topshe, who fulfils a Watson-like role. From the novel Joi Baba Felunath onward they are joined by Lalmohan Ganguly, pen name "Jatayu," a writer of popular thrillers who provides comic relief. Feluda's recurring adversary is the criminal mastermind Maganlal Meghraj.

Stories and adaptations

celebrated cases include Sonar Kella (The Golden Fortress), set around Jaisalmer; Joi Baba Felunath (The Elephant God), set in Varanasi; Baksho Rahashya; and Gangtokey Gondogol. Ray himself directed two Feluda films — Sonar Kella (1974) and Joi Baba Felunath (1979) — with Soumitra Chatterjee as Feluda; Ray's son Sandip Ray has directed many further film and television adaptations, with actors including Sabyasachi Chakrabarty and Abir Chatterjee in the title role.
