Govan

Population: c. 28,000 (approx.)

Council area: Glasgow City

Country: Scotland

Postcode district: G51

Govan is a district, parish, and former burgh now part of south-west Glasgow, Scotland. It is situated 2.5 miles (4.0 km) west of Glasgow city centre, on the south bank of the River Clyde, opposite the mouth of the River Kelvin and the district of Partick.

History

Govan is one of the oldest settlements in Scotland; its parish church preserves the Govan Stones, a remarkable collection of early medieval carved stones from the era of the Kingdom of Strathclyde, including the carved Govan Sarcophagus. In the 19th century Govan grew explosively from a weaving village into a world centre of shipbuilding, home to yards such as Fairfield Shipbuilding and Engineering. It became a burgh in 1864 and was absorbed into Glasgow in 1912.

Shipbuilding

The Govan shipyards built many famous vessels and gave the district a strong working-class and trade-union identity. Although much reduced from its peak, shipbuilding continues at the BAE Systems yard in Govan, which builds Royal Navy warships. The area's political history includes the 1971–72 Upper Clyde Shipbuilders work-in led by Jimmy Reid.

Transport and culture

Govan is served by Govan subway station, part of the Glasgow Subway, and by Govan bus station. The district is associated with the phrase "Govan humour" and is the birthplace of football manager Sir Alex Ferguson.
