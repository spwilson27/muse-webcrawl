Inari

Inari Ōkami (稲荷大神) is the Japanese kami of rice, agriculture, fertility, industry, and worldly success, and one of the most widely worshipped deities in Shinto. Inari's messengers and symbols are foxes (kitsune), which are regarded as sacred and are often depicted holding a key, a sheaf of rice, or a wish-granting jewel.

Worship

Inari shrines, numbering perhaps 30,000 across Japan — roughly a third of all Shinto shrines — are marked by vermilion torii gates and pairs of guardian fox statues. Offerings include rice, sake, and fried tofu (abura-age), said to be a fox favourite; the sushi dish inarizushi takes its name from the deity. The head shrine is Fushimi Inari-taisha in Kyoto, founded in 711 and famous for its thousands of torii covering Mount Inari.

Depictions

Inari has been variously depicted as male, female, or androgynous, and is closely associated with the Buddhist goddess Dakiniten and with Ukanomitama and other food and rice kami. Since the Edo period Inari worship has also been linked with prosperity for businesses, entertainers, and craftspeople, and many companies maintain small Inari shrines on their premises.
