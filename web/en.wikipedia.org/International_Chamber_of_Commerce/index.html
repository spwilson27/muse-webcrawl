The International Chamber of Commerce (ICC) is the world's largest business organization, representing companies and business associations in more than 170 countries. Founded in 1919 in the aftermath of the First World War, it promotes international trade, responsible business conduct, and a rules-based global economy.

Activities

The ICC is best known for widely used trade rules, including the Incoterms rules for delivery of goods, the Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (UCP 600) governing letters of credit, and model contracts. Its International Court of Arbitration, established in 1923 and based in Paris, is a leading institution for resolving international commercial disputes.

Structure

The ICC works through national committees in many countries and speaks for business in international forums; it was the first business organization granted general consultative status with the United Nations Economic and Social Council, and it holds observer status at the UN General Assembly.
