Andrew Wiles

Sir Andrew John Wiles (born 11 April 1953) is an English mathematician and a Royal Society Research Professor at the University of Oxford, specializing in number theory. He is best known for proving Fermat's Last Theorem — the assertion that no three positive integers a, b, c satisfy an + bn = cn for any integer n > 2 — which had resisted proof for over 350 years. His proof, completed in 1994 and published in 1995, established the modularity of semistable elliptic curves over the rationals, a special case of the modularity theorem whose full proof followed in 2001.

Early life and career

Wiles first encountered Fermat's Last Theorem at age ten in a library book and resolved to prove it. He took his bachelor's degree at Oxford in 1974 and his PhD at Cambridge in 1980 under John Coates, working on elliptic curves and Iwasawa theory; with Coates he proved results on the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture for curves with complex multiplication. After posts at the Institute for Advanced Study and Harvard, he became a professor at Princeton University in 1982.

The proof

The 1986 result of Ken Ribet (building on Gerhard Frey's curve and Jean-Pierre Serre's epsilon conjecture) showed that Fermat's Last Theorem would follow from the Taniyama–Shimura–Weil modularity conjecture for semistable elliptic curves. Wiles then worked in secrecy for nearly seven years at Princeton, telling almost no one. In June 1993 he announced a proof in three lectures at the Isaac Newton Institute in Cambridge. Review exposed a gap in one part of the argument (the Euler system bound); after a year of struggle, working with his former student Richard Taylor, Wiles circumvented the obstacle in September 1994 by returning to an earlier approach (the "3–5 trick" and a new construction). The papers "Modular elliptic curves and Fermat's Last Theorem" and, with Taylor, "Ring-theoretic properties of certain Hecke algebras," appeared in the Annals of Mathematics in May 1995.

Honours

Because Wiles was over 40 when the proof was published he was ineligible for the Fields Medal; the International Mathematical Union instead presented him a unique silver plaque in 1998. He was appointed KBE in 2000, received the Abel Prize in 2016 "for his stunning proof of Fermat's Last Theorem," and the Royal Society's Copley Medal in 2017. Oxford's Mathematical Institute building, opened in 2013, is named the Andrew Wiles Building. His story is told in Simon Singh's bestseller Fermat's Last Theorem and a widely seen BBC documentary.
