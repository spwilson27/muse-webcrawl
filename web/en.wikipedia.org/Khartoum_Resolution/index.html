Khartoum Resolution

The Khartoum Resolution of 1 September 1967 was issued at the conclusion of the Arab League summit held in Khartoum, Sudan, following the Six-Day War. The resolution is famous for its "three noes": no peace with Israel, no recognition of Israel, and no negotiations with Israel.

Background

In June 1967 Israel defeated Egypt, Jordan and Syria in six days, capturing the Sinai, Gaza, the West Bank, East Jerusalem and the Golan Heights. Arab leaders met in Khartoum from 29 August to 1 September 1967 to agree a common position. The summit was also notable for reconciling Egypt and Saudi Arabia over the Yemen war and for securing oil-state subsidies to Egypt and Jordan.

Content

The resolution affirmed Arab unity of action to eliminate the effects of the aggression and secure Israeli withdrawal from occupied Arab territories, within the framework of the principles of no peace, no recognition and no negotiation with Israel, and insistence on the rights of the Palestinian people. Crucially, it also endorsed political and diplomatic action at the international level — language interpreted by some diplomats as leaving room for an eventual political settlement, and which preceded UN Security Council Resolution 242 (November 1967) with its land-for-peace formula.

Interpretation and legacy

Israeli and Western commentary long treated the "three noes" as proof of Arab rejectionism; Arab commentary stressed that the text barred a separate, unconditional capitulation, not diplomacy itself. Egypt and Jordan accepted Resolution 242, Egypt signed a peace treaty with Israel in 1979, and the PLO recognised Israel in 1988 — developments that progressively overtook the Khartoum formula, though it remains a touchstone in debates about the Arab–Israeli conflict.
