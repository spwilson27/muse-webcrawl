Samuel Cunard

Sir Samuel Cunard, 1st Baronet (21 November 1787 – 28 April 1865) was a British shipping magnate, born in Halifax, Nova Scotia, who founded the Cunard Line, the first successful regular transatlantic steamship service. He is credited with establishing scheduled steam communication between Britain and North America and with shaping the ocean liner trade.

Early life and business

Cunard was the son of Abraham Cunard, a timber merchant and shipbuilder who had emigrated to Halifax after the American Revolution. Samuel built the family business into interests in shipping, timber, coal mining and the Shubenacadie Canal, and was a co-founder of the Halifax Banking Company. He acquired experience in mail packets and steam navigation, operating one of the first steamships in Canadian waters.

Cunard Line

In 1839 Cunard won an Admiralty contract to carry mail between Liverpool, Halifax and Boston, and with partners including Robert Napier founded the British and North American Royal Mail Steam Packet Company. Its first ship, RMS Britannia, sailed from Liverpool on 4 July 1840. Cunard insisted on safety and regularity over speed records — no ship of his line was lost with passengers under his management — and the "Cunarders" became bywords for reliability. He was created a baronet in 1859. The line he founded went on to operate famous liners including Lusitania, Queen Mary and Queen Elizabeth 2.
