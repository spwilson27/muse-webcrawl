The Burj Khalifa (known as the Burj Dubai prior to its inauguration) is a skyscraper in Dubai, United Arab Emirates. With a total height of 829.8 m (2,722 ft) including its spire, it has been the tallest structure and building in the world since its topping out in 2009, surpassing Taipei 101.

Design and construction

Designed by Adrian Smith of SOM with chief structural engineer Bill Baker, the tower uses a "buttressed core" system: a hexagonal central core supported by three wings in a Y-shaped plan inspired by the desert flower Hymenocallis, which also reduces wind forces. The structure steps back in a spiral as it rises. Construction began in January 2004 and consumed some 330,000 m³ of concrete and 39,000 tonnes of steel rebar; more than 12,000 workers were on site at peak. Renamed at opening for Sheikh Khalifa bin Zayed Al Nahyan, ruler of Abu Dhabi, whose government aided Dubai during the 2009 financial crisis, it cost about US$1.5 billion.

Features and records

The tower mixes residential apartments, corporate suites, the Armani Hotel, and observation decks ("At the Top" on levels 124–125 and 148, the latter at 555 m). It holds records for tallest building, most floors, highest occupied floor, and highest observation deck. Its elevators travel at up to 10 m/s. The building anchors the Downtown Dubai development, the Dubai Mall, and the Dubai Fountain.
