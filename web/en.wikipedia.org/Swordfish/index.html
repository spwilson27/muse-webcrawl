Swordfish

Swordfish
Scientific name: Xiphias gladius
Family: Xiphiidae (sole member)
Distribution: Tropical and temperate oceans worldwide
Conservation: Least Concern (globally)

The swordfish (Xiphias gladius) is a large, highly migratory predatory fish named for its long, flat, pointed bill, which resembles a sword. It is the only member of the family Xiphiidae and is found worldwide in tropical and temperate parts of the Atlantic, Pacific, and Indian Oceans.

Description

Adults commonly reach 3 metres in length, with reports of specimens over 4.5 metres and more than 600 kilograms. Unlike other billfish, adult swordfish lack scales and teeth. They are ectothermic but possess specialized eye and brain heating organs that improve vision and hunting ability in cold, deep water. Swordfish use their bills to slash and stun prey such as squid, mackerel, and other fish.

Fisheries

Swordfish are prized as a food fish and are taken mainly by longline and harpoon fisheries. Intensive fishing reduced Atlantic populations in the late twentieth century, prompting international quotas and a notable consumer campaign; North Atlantic stocks have since partially recovered. Because swordfish are apex predators, their flesh can carry elevated mercury levels, and consumption advisories apply in several countries.
