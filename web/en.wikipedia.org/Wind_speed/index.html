Wind speed

Wind speed is a fundamental atmospheric quantity caused by air moving from high to low pressure, usually due to changes in temperature. In modern usage, it also counts the speed of a moving vehicle relative to the air around it. Wind speeds affect weather forecasting, aviation and maritime operations, construction projects, growth and metabolism rates of plants, and many other implications.

Measurement

Wind speed is commonly measured with an anemometer, which consists of cups or vanes that rotate in the wind. It is reported in metres per second, kilometres per hour, miles per hour, or knots. Wind direction is typically reported by the direction from which it originates, for example a northerly wind blows from the north to the south.

Scales

The Beaufort scale, developed in 1805 by Admiral Francis Beaufort, relates wind speed to observed conditions at sea and on land, ranging from 0 (calm) to 12 (hurricane force). The highest wind gust ever recorded was 408 km/h (253 mph) on Barrow Island, Australia, during Tropical Cyclone Olivia in 1996.

Effects

Wind speeds affect the dispersal of seeds, the erosion of soils, and the operation of wind turbines used to generate electricity. Strong winds can cause structural damage and are a factor in the design of buildings in hurricane- and tornado-prone regions.
