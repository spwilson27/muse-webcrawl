Namco Limited was a Japanese multinational video game and entertainment company. Founded in 1955 by Masaya Nakamura as Nakamura Seisakusho, an operator of coin-operated children's rides, the company grew into one of the most influential arcade game manufacturers in the world before merging with Bandai in 2005 to form Bandai Namco Holdings.

History

Nakamura's company, renamed Nakamura Manufacturing and later Namco (from Nakamura Manufacturing Company), entered the arcade business in the 1970s, distributing Atari games in Japan and acquiring Atari's Japanese division. Namco achieved worldwide success with Galaxian (1979) and Toru Iwatani's Pac-Man (1980), which became the best-selling arcade game of all time and a cultural phenomenon.

Notable games

Beyond Pac-Man, Namco produced landmark series including Galaga, Dig Dug, Xevious, Ridge Racer, Tekken, Soulcalibur, Ace Combat, Tales, and Taiko no Tatsujin. The company also operated large amusement centres such as Namco Wonder Eggs and the Namja Town indoor theme park in Ikebukuro, Tokyo.

Legacy

In September 2005 Namco merged with toy maker Bandai; its home and arcade game operations were folded into Bandai Namco Entertainment (originally Namco Bandai Games) in 2006, while the Namco brand continued for arcade operations under Bandai Namco Amusement until 2018.
