The Xi River (Chinese: 西江, "West River") is the western tributary of the Pearl River in southern China. It is the longest of the Pearl River's tributaries and, by discharge, the second-largest river in China after the Yangtze.

Course

The river's headwaters rise in Yunnan province, from where the upper course is known successively as the Nanpan, Hongshui, Qian, Xun and finally the Xi proper. Flowing generally eastward through Guizhou and Guangxi, it receives the Yu and Li rivers and passes the city of Wuzhou before entering Guangdong, where it joins the Bei River near Sanshui and combines with the Pearl River network west of Guangzhou, eventually reaching the South China Sea through the Pearl River Delta.

Importance

The Xi River system drains an area of roughly 350,000 square kilometres and provides the principal inland waterway of southwestern China, linking the interior of Guangxi and Yunnan to the ports of the Pearl River Delta. Its delta is one of China's most densely populated and economically productive regions. The river is also a major source of hydroelectric power and, historically, of devastating floods along its lower reaches.
