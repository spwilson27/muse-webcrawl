Chhattisgarh

Chhattisgarh is a state in central India. It was formed on 1 November 2000 by partitioning ten Chhattisgarhi-speaking districts of Madhya Pradesh. Its capital is Raipur. The state borders Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, Jharkhand and Uttar Pradesh.

Geography

The Mahanadi River and its tributaries drain the central plain, one of India's major rice-growing regions, while forested hills cover the north and south, including the Bastar region. The state is rich in minerals — coal, iron ore, bauxite and dolomite — and in forest resources.

Economy and culture

Chhattisgarh is a major producer of electricity and steel, with large plants at Bhilai and Korba. About a third of its population is Scheduled Tribe, and the state is known for tribal crafts, the Chitrakote Falls on the Indravati River, and ancient temple sites such as Sirpur and Rajnandgaon.
