Songtsen Gampo


Songtsen Gampo, founder of the Tibetan Empire

Songtsen Gampo (Tibetan: སྲོང་བཙན་སྒམ་པོ, c. 569/605 – 649/650) was the 33rd king of the Yarlung dynasty and the founder of the Tibetan Empire. He united the Tibetan Plateau for the first time, moved the capital to Lhasa, and is traditionally credited with introducing Buddhism to Tibet and creating the Tibetan written script.

Reign

Songtsen Gampo succeeded his father Namri Songtsen, who had begun the unification of the plateau. He completed the conquest of rival kingdoms such as Zhangzhung and established an administrative system of districts and a legal code. His armies campaigned successfully against Nepal and against Tang China; after conflict over a marriage alliance, the Tang emperor Taizong granted him the princess Wencheng in marriage in 641. Songtsen Gampo also married the Nepalese princess Bhrikuti. Both princesses are said to have brought Buddhist images and scriptures to Tibet.

Cultural achievements

Tradition credits Songtsen Gampo with sending his minister Thonmi Sambhota to India to devise the Tibetan alphabet, based on Indic scripts, which enabled the translation of Buddhist texts. He is associated with the founding of the Jokhang Temple in Lhasa, which houses the Jowo statue brought by Wencheng and remains the most revered shrine in Tibetan Buddhism.

Legacy

In later Tibetan tradition, Songtsen Gampo came to be regarded as an emanation of the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, and together with Trisong Detsen and Ralpacan he is counted among the three Dharma Kings of Tibet.
