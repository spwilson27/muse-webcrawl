Human–computer interaction (HCI) is research in the design and use of computer technology, which focuses on the interfaces between people (users) and computers. HCI researchers observe how humans interact with computers and design technologies that allow humans to interact with computers in novel ways.

Scope

The field sits at the intersection of computer science, behavioral sciences, design, and media studies. It covers topics such as usability, user experience design, interaction techniques, accessibility, and ubiquitous computing.

History

Early milestones include Ivan Sutherland's Sketchpad (1963), Douglas Engelbart's 1968 demonstration of the mouse, windows, and hypertext, and the graphical user interfaces developed at Xerox PARC that inspired the Apple Macintosh. The discipline grew rapidly with personal computing in the 1980s.

Design principles

HCI emphasizes user-centered design: understanding users' goals, iterative prototyping, and empirical evaluation with usability testing and heuristics such as Nielsen's ten usability heuristics.
