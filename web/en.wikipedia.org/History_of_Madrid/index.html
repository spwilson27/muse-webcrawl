History of Madrid

The history of Madrid dates from the 9th century, when the Emir Muhammad I of Córdoba ordered the construction of a fortress (alcázar) on the site of the present Royal Palace, near the Manzanares River. The town's name derives from the Arabic Mayrit, referring to its waters.

From fortress town to capital

Madrid passed to the Christian kingdom of Castile in 1085 during the Reconquista and grew slowly as a modest town where the Cortes occasionally met. Its fortunes changed in 1561, when Philip II moved the royal court to Madrid, making it the de facto capital of the Spanish monarchy. Under the Habsburgs the city expanded around the Plaza Mayor (completed 1619) and the Buen Retiro Palace, attracting the artists of Spain's Golden Age — Velázquez, Lope de Vega, and Cervantes all lived and worked there.

Modern era

The Bourbon kings of the 18th century, especially Charles III, gave Madrid neoclassical landmarks such as the Prado Museum building, the Puerta de Alcalá, and the Cibeles Fountain. The city rose against French occupation on 2 May 1808, an event immortalised by Goya. In the 20th century Madrid endured a three-year siege during the Spanish Civil War (1936–1939), then grew into a metropolis of several million under and after the Franco regime; following the restoration of democracy it became the political and cultural centre of modern Spain.
