Battle of Borodino

The Battle of Borodino (Russian: Бородинское сражение) was fought on 7 September 1812 (26 August Old Style) near the village of Borodino, some 110 km west of Moscow, during the French invasion of Russia. It was the largest and bloodiest single-day battle of the Napoleonic Wars, involving around 250,000 troops and at least 70,000 casualties. Napoleon's Grande Armée attacked the Imperial Russian Army under General Mikhail Kutuzov, captured the main Russian positions — the Great Redoubt and the Bagration flèches — but failed to destroy the Russian army, which withdrew in good order. Napoleon entered an abandoned, soon-burning Moscow a week later.

Course of the battle

After the fight for the Shevardino Redoubt on 5 September, the main battle opened at dawn on 7 September. French assaults centred on the Bagration flèches in the south — where Prince Pyotr Bagration was mortally wounded — and the Great (Raevsky) Redoubt in the centre, which changed hands in desperate fighting involving massed artillery and cavalry charges. Kutuzov's army, though mauled, held a second position and retreated overnight towards Moscow, leaving the field to the French. Napoleon, ill and far from his supply bases, declined to commit the Imperial Guard. The battle is immortalised in Tolstoy's War and Peace and commemorated by the Borodino panorama museum and annual re-enactments.
