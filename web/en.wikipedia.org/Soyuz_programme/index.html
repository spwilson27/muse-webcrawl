Soyuz programme

A Soyuz spacecraft in orbit

The Soyuz programme (Russian: Союз, IPA: [sɐˈjus], meaning "Union") is a human spaceflight programme initiated by the Soviet Union in the early 1960s. The Soyuz spacecraft was originally part of a Moon landing project intended to put a Soviet cosmonaut on the Moon. It is the third human spaceflight programme organised by the Soviet Union, after the Vostok and Voskhod programmes.

Overview

The programme consists of the Soyuz spacecraft, launched atop Soyuz rockets from Baikonur Cosmodrome in Kazakhstan. The first crewed flight, Soyuz 1, in April 1967 ended in tragedy with the death of Vladimir Komarov when the parachute system failed during landing. The programme nevertheless matured into the longest-serving crewed spacecraft design in history.

Role in station operations

Soyuz ferried crews to the Salyut and Mir space stations, and since 1998 has been a mainstay of the International Space Station, serving both as crew transport and as a lifeboat. Successive versions include the Soyuz-T, Soyuz-TM, Soyuz-TMA, Soyuz-TMA-M and the current Soyuz-MS, introduced in 2016. Uncrewed Progress spacecraft are derived from the Soyuz design.

Continuation

After the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991, the programme was taken over by the Russian Federation and is operated by Roscosmos. Between the retirement of the US Space Shuttle in 2011 and the first crewed Commercial Crew flight in 2020, Soyuz was the sole means of transporting crews to the ISS.
