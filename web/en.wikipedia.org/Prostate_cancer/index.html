Prostate cancer

Prostate cancer is the uncontrolled growth of cells in the prostate, a gland in the male reproductive system below the bladder. Abnormal growth of prostate tissue is usually identified through screening tests, typically blood tests for prostate-specific antigen (PSA), and confirmed by biopsy. Most prostate cancers grow slowly; some grow relatively quickly and may spread (metastasise) to other parts of the body, particularly the bones and lymph nodes.

Epidemiology

Prostate cancer is among the most commonly diagnosed cancers in men worldwide and a leading cause of cancer death, with risk rising steeply with age. Many cases are indolent and would never cause harm in a man's lifetime, which makes screening decisions a matter of weighing early detection against overdiagnosis and overtreatment.

Staging and grading

Tumours are staged by extent (TNM system) and graded by the Gleason score (Grade Groups 1–5) based on the microscopic pattern of the cancer cells. Risk stratification combines stage, Gleason grade, and PSA level.

Treatment

Localised low-risk disease is often managed with active surveillance. Definitive treatments include radical prostatectomy and external-beam or brachytherapy radiotherapy. Androgen deprivation therapy is a mainstay for advanced disease; chemotherapy, newer hormonal agents (such as abiraterone and enzalutamide), and radioligand therapy are used for metastatic castration-resistant disease.
