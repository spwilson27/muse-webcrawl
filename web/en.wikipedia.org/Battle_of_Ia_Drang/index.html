Battle of Ia Drang

The Battle of Ia Drang was the first major battle between the United States Army and the People's Army of Vietnam (PAVN), as well as the first large-scale helicopter air assault of the Vietnam War. It took place from 14 to 18 November 1965 at two helicopter landing zones, X-Ray and Albany, in the Ia Drang valley near the Chu Pong Massif in South Vietnam's Central Highlands.

Landing Zone X-Ray

On 14 November 1965, the 1st Battalion, 7th Cavalry Regiment under Lieutenant Colonel Hal Moore was airlifted into LZ X-Ray by UH-1 Huey helicopters. The battalion of about 450 men was quickly engaged by some 2,000 PAVN soldiers and found itself surrounded. Over three days of intense close-quarters fighting, the Americans relied on massive artillery and air support, including B-52 strikes called in close to their positions. Captain Rick Rescorla and medic Joseph Marm (later a Medal of Honor recipient) were among those who distinguished themselves. A relief column from the 2nd Battalion, 5th Cavalry fought its way in from LZ Victor. US losses at X-Ray were 79 killed; PAVN losses were estimated in the hundreds.

Landing Zone Albany

On 17 November the exhausted 2nd Battalion, 7th Cavalry moved overland to LZ Albany and was ambushed while marching in column. In the confused fighting, napalm strikes killed many soldiers of both sides. The Albany fight produced 155 American dead—the worst single-day American loss of the war up to that point.

Significance

Ia Drang validated the US Army's new airmobile doctrine but also convinced North Vietnam that it could engage American forces and bleed them, setting the pattern of attrition for the years to come. General William Westmoreland cited the high enemy body count as vindication of search-and-destroy strategy. The X-Ray battle was chronicled in the book We Were Soldiers Once... And Young (1992) by Moore and journalist Joseph Galloway, and adapted into the 2002 film We Were Soldiers.
