Coherence theory of truth

The coherence theory of truth holds that a proposition is true if it coheres with a specified set of other propositions — if it is consistent with, and integrated into, a comprehensive system of beliefs. Versions of the theory were defended by idealist philosophers such as F. H. Bradley and Brand Blanshard, and it is associated with the holism of writers like Otto Neurath and W. V. O. Quine.

Overview

Where the correspondence theory defines truth as agreement with reality, coherentists argue that our only access to reality is through our beliefs, so truth must be judged by how well beliefs fit together — comprehensively, consistently and with explanatory integration. Stronger versions treat maximal coherence as constitutive of truth itself; weaker versions treat coherence mainly as a test or criterion of truth.

Objections

The classic objection is that indefinitely many mutually incompatible systems could each be internally coherent — a coherent fiction is still fiction — so coherence alone cannot distinguish truth from falsehood. A related problem asks which privileged set of beliefs the coherent set must be. Coherentists reply by appealing to the role of observation beliefs, the overall comprehensiveness of the system, or by combining coherence with other constraints. Coherence also plays a role, distinct from the theory itself, in reflective equilibrium in ethics and in confirmation holism in the philosophy of science.
