Meteorology

Meteorology is the branch of the atmospheric sciences concerned with the atmosphere and its phenomena, including temperature, air pressure, water vapour, and their interactions, with a primary focus on weather forecasting. The word derives from Aristotle's treatise Meteorologica.

Scope

Meteorology studies phenomena on timescales from minutes (thunderstorms, turbulence) to weeks, distinguishing it from climatology, which addresses long-term patterns. Subfields include synoptic, dynamical, physical and boundary-layer meteorology, as well as applied branches such as aviation, agricultural and marine meteorology.

Methods

Modern meteorology relies on a global observing system of surface stations, weather balloons, radar and satellites, and on numerical weather prediction: since the mid-twentieth century, supercomputers have solved the equations of atmospheric motion to produce forecasts, a practice pioneered by Lewis Fry Richardson and made practical by John von Neumann's group in 1950. The World Meteorological Organization coordinates international data exchange.

History

Systematic weather observation began with instruments such as the barometer and thermometer in the seventeenth century; the telegraph enabled synoptic weather maps in the nineteenth, and the Bergen school developed the polar front theory of cyclones in the early twentieth century.
