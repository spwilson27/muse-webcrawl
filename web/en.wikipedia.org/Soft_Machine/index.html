Soft Machine

Soft Machine are an English rock band from Canterbury, formed in 1966 by Robert Wyatt, Kevin Ayers, Daevid Allen, and Mike Ratledge. One of the central bands of the Canterbury scene, they were among the first British progressive rock groups and moved steadily toward jazz fusion over the course of the 1970s. The band took its name from the 1961 William S. Burroughs novel The Soft Machine.

History

After touring the United States supporting the Jimi Hendrix Experience in 1968, the band recorded its debut album; Ayers left and was replaced by Hugh Hopper. The albums Volume Two (1969) and Third (1970) — a double LP of four side-long compositions — marked a shift from psychedelic pop to extended jazz-rock. Wyatt departed in 1971 to form Matching Mole; later line-ups, including Karl Jenkins and Allan Holdsworth, produced the albums Six, Seven, and Bundles (1975).

Legacy and later activity

Soft Machine's original incarnation wound down by the early 1980s, though spin-off and successor bands (Soft Heap, Soft Works, and a revived Soft Machine from 2015, featuring John Etheridge and Roy Babbington) kept the name and repertoire alive, releasing albums such as Hidden Details (2018). The band influenced generations of progressive, jazz-rock, and experimental musicians.
