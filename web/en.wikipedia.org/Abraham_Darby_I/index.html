Abraham Darby I (1678–1717) was an English ironmaster and Quaker who, in 1709 at Coalbrookdale in Shropshire, became the first person to smelt iron ore successfully using coke rather than charcoal — a landmark step in the Industrial Revolution.

Career

Darby served an apprenticeship in Birmingham as a malt-mill maker before moving to Bristol, where he worked in brass founding and patented a method of casting iron cooking pots in sand, allowing cheaper, thinner vessels than rival methods. In 1708 he leased the derelict furnace at Coalbrookdale and in 1709 produced the first coke-smelted pig iron. Coke smelting freed iron production from shrinking charcoal supplies and made possible much larger furnaces, since coke could bear greater weight without crushing.

Legacy

Darby's descendants developed the Coalbrookdale works into a centre of the iron industry; his grandson Abraham Darby III built the world's first cast-iron bridge, the Iron Bridge over the Severn (1779). The Darby family remained leading Quaker industrialists for generations.
