TOEFL

The Test of English as a Foreign Language (TOEFL, pronounced "TOE-full") is a standardized test to measure the English language ability of non-native speakers wishing to enrol in English-speaking universities. The test is accepted by more than 13,000 institutions in over 160 countries, and is administered by the Educational Testing Service (ETS).

History and format

TOEFL was first administered in 1964 and has passed through paper-based (PBT), computer-based (CBT), and internet-based (iBT) formats; the iBT, introduced in 2005, became the dominant form. The TOEFL iBT tests four skills — reading, listening, speaking, and writing — in academic contexts. A major revision in July 2023 shortened the test to just under two hours, removing unscored trial questions and streamlining the writing section.

Scoring

Each of the four sections is scored from 0 to 30, giving a total score of 0–120. Scores are widely used for admissions and sometimes for immigration and professional licensing; universities commonly set minimum requirements between about 70 and 100. Results are reported online roughly a week after the test and remain valid for two years.
