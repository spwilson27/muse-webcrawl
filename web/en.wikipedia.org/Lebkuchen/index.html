Lebkuchen

Lebkuchen is a traditional German baked Christmas treat, similar to gingerbread. Made with honey, spices such as ginger, cinnamon, cloves, anise and cardamom, and often nuts, it ranges from soft, cake-like forms to harder varieties used for decorated hearts and houses.

History

Honey cakes were baked in medieval monasteries, and Lebkuchen took shape in Franconia, especially Nuremberg, which sat on medieval spice trade routes. Nuremberg Lebkuchen (Nürnberger Lebkuchen) is protected by geographical indication; the finest grade, Elisenlebkuchen, must contain at least 25% nuts and little or no flour, and is baked on a thin wafer (Oblate).

Forms

Common forms include glazed or chocolate-coated rounds, white Lebkuchen, gingerbread-style houses, and the large inscribed hearts sold at fairs and Oktoberfest. Lebkuchen is sold throughout Germany in the autumn and Christmas season.
