Magerøya is an island in Nordkapp Municipality in northern Norway, in the Barents Sea. Its name comes from Old Norse magr, "meagre", reflecting its barren, rocky terrain. The island's northern headland, the North Cape (Nordkapp), is traditionally regarded as the northernmost point of mainland-accessible Europe and is a major tourist destination.

Geography and settlement

The main settlement is the town of Honningsvåg, a port of call for the Hurtigruten coastal ships. Fishing villages such as Skarsvåg, Kamøyvær, Nordvågen and Gjesvær lie around the coast. The island is connected to the mainland by the undersea North Cape Tunnel, opened in 1999, with road access via the European route E69.

History

Magerøya was used for seasonal fishing and reindeer grazing for centuries. During the Second World War the population was forcibly evacuated in 1944 and the island's buildings burned during the German retreat; residents rebuilt after the war.
