Backburning

Backburning, also called backfiring, is a wildfire suppression technique in which a fire is deliberately lit along the inner edge of a control line in front of an advancing wildfire. The backburn consumes fuel in the fire's path so that, when the main fire arrives, it has nothing left to burn and dies out.

Technique

Firefighters ignite vegetation just ahead of the main fire, typically from a road, firebreak or natural barrier. Drawn toward the oncoming front by the convection and winds the main fire generates, the backburn burns inward against the prevailing wind, slowly and controllably.

Risks

If winds change unexpectedly, a backburn can escape and become a new wildfire front. The technique therefore requires careful planning, secure control lines and favourable weather, and is related to the broader practice of controlled burns and hazard-reduction burning.

Usage

Backburning is a standard tool in Australia, North America and other fire-prone regions, and has figured prominently in the management of major bushfire emergencies. See also Wildfire suppression.
