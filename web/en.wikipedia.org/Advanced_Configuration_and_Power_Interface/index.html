Advanced Configuration and Power Interface

The Advanced Configuration and Power Interface (ACPI) is an open standard for device configuration and power management by operating systems. First released in December 1996 by Intel, Microsoft, and Toshiba (later joined by HP and Phoenix), ACPI replaced earlier standards such as APM and the Plug and Play BIOS, bringing power management under operating-system control (OSPM).

Overview

Firmware provides ACPI tables (including the DSDT and SSDTs) written partly in a bytecode called ACPI Machine Language (AML), which the operating system interprets. The standard defines global states G0–G3 (working, sleeping, soft off, mechanical off) and sleep states S1–S5, as well as device and processor power states (D0–D3 and C0–C3). ACPI enables features such as sleep and hibernation, thermal management, battery monitoring, and responding to power-button and lid events.

History

ACPI 2.0 (2000) added 64-bit support; subsequent revisions extended multiprocessor and hardware-reduced platforms. Since 2013 the specification has been maintained by the UEFI Forum. ACPI remains the primary power-management interface on x86 PCs, though implementation quality in firmware varies widely.
