Antioxidant

An antioxidant is a molecule that inhibits the oxidation of other molecules. Oxidation reactions can produce free radicals, which start chain reactions that may damage cells; antioxidants terminate these reactions by being oxidised themselves, acting as reducing agents.

Biological role

Living organisms maintain complex networks of antioxidants — including the enzymes superoxide dismutase, catalase, and glutathione peroxidase, and low-molecular-weight compounds such as glutathione, uric acid, vitamin C (ascorbic acid), and vitamin E (tocopherols) — to control reactive oxygen species generated by metabolism. An imbalance between oxidants and antioxidants is termed oxidative stress and has been implicated in ageing and many diseases, though free radicals also serve useful signalling and immune functions.

Dietary antioxidants and health

Fruits and vegetables are rich in polyphenols and carotenoids, and diets rich in these foods are associated with better health outcomes. However, large randomised trials of high-dose antioxidant supplements (such as beta-carotene and vitamin E) generally failed to prevent disease, and some showed harm — beta-carotene increased lung cancer risk in smokers — suggesting that the benefits of plant-rich diets cannot be reduced to antioxidant content alone.

Industrial uses

Antioxidants such as BHA, BHT, and propyl gallate are added to foods, cosmetics, and fuels to prevent rancidity and oxidation; compounds such as ascorbic acid are used to prevent browning in processed foods.
