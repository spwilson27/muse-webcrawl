Pulitzer Prize for Fiction

The Pulitzer Prize for Fiction is one of the seven American Pulitzer Prizes awarded annually in the Letters, Drama and Music categories. It is given for distinguished fiction published in book form during the year by an American author, preferably dealing with American life. It was called the Pulitzer Prize for the Novel from 1917 to 1947.

History

The first award went in 1918 to Ernest Poole for His Family. In some years the jury's choice was overruled or no prize was awarded; in 1974 the trustees declined to award Thomas Pynchon's Gravity's Rainbow. Since 1980, finalists have also been announced.

Notable winners

Winners include Edith Wharton (The Age of Innocence), Harper Lee (To Kill a Mockingbird), Toni Morrison (Beloved), Colson Whitehead, who won twice for The Underground Railroad and The Nickel Boys, and Barbara Kingsolver for Demon Copperhead.
