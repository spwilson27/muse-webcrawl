Foreign relations of Canada

The foreign relations of Canada are conducted through Global Affairs Canada, the federal department responsible for the country's diplomatic and consular relations, international trade, and development assistance. Canada is a founding member of the United Nations, NATO, the Commonwealth of Nations, and la Francophonie, and is a member of the G7 and G20.

Relationship with the United States

Canada's most important relationship is with the United States, with which it shares the world's longest international land border, deeply integrated economies, and joint defence arrangements such as NORAD. Trade between the two countries is governed by the Canada–United States–Mexico Agreement (CUSMA), successor to NAFTA. Canada is also closely allied with the United Kingdom and France, its two founding colonial powers.

Multilateralism and peacekeeping

Canadian foreign policy has traditionally emphasized multilateralism, international law, and a "middle power" role. Canada played a prominent part in the creation of United Nations peacekeeping in 1956, an effort for which foreign minister Lester B. Pearson won the Nobel Peace Prize. Later initiatives included the Ottawa Treaty banning anti-personnel landmines (1997) and support for the International Criminal Court.

Arctic and Asia-Pacific

As an Arctic nation, Canada asserts sovereignty over its northern waters, including the Northwest Passage. In recent decades Canada has also deepened engagement with the Asia-Pacific region, concluding trade agreements such as the Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership (CPTPP).
