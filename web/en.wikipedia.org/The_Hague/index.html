The Hague (Dutch: Den Haag [dɛn ˈɦaːx], officially 's-Gravenhage) is a city on the western coast of the Netherlands on the North Sea. It is the capital of the province of South Holland and, though Amsterdam is the country's constitutional capital, The Hague is the seat of the Dutch government, parliament, Supreme Court, and Council of State, as well as the residence of the Dutch monarch. With a population of over half a million, it is the third-largest city in the Netherlands, after Amsterdam and Rotterdam.

International city of peace and justice

The Hague hosts a concentration of international courts and organisations unrivalled outside New York: the International Court of Justice (housed in the Peace Palace, opened 1913), the International Criminal Court, the Permanent Court of Arbitration, the International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia (closed 2017), Europol, and Eurojust, along with some 200 international organisations employing tens of thousands. The city's role grew from the Hague Peace Conferences of 1899 and 1907, which produced the Hague Conventions on the laws of war.

History and landmarks

The settlement grew around a hunting lodge of the Counts of Holland from the thirteenth century; the Binnenhof, the Gothic Ridderzaal (Hall of Knights), and the Hofvijver pond remain its medieval core. Landmarks include the Mauritshuis museum (home to Vermeer's Girl with a Pearl Earring), the Peace Palace, the seaside resort of Scheveningen with its pier, and the miniature city of Madurodam. Nearly all foreign embassies and government ministries are located in the city.
