Søren Kierkegaard

Died11 November 1855, Copenhagen
OccupationPhilosopher, theologian, author

Søren Aabye Kierkegaard (1813–1855) was a Danish philosopher, theologian and religious author, widely considered the first existentialist philosopher. His work explored individual existence, faith, anxiety, despair and the "leap of faith," in opposition to the systematic philosophy of Hegel and the established Danish church.

Life

Kierkegaard was born to a wealthy Copenhagen family and raised in a strict Pietist atmosphere. He broke off his engagement to Regine Olsen in 1841, an event that shadowed his writing. Living on his inheritance, he published a rapid stream of books, many under pseudonyms presenting contrasting viewpoints, and late in life launched a public attack on the Church of Denmark. He died in 1855, aged 42.

Thought

Key themes include the three stages of life — aesthetic, ethical and religious; the concept of anxiety as the dizziness of freedom; despair as the "sickness unto death"; and truth as subjectivity, the individual's passionate inward commitment. Fear and Trembling retells Abraham's near-sacrifice of Isaac as a paradox of faith.

Influence

Largely neglected outside Denmark in his lifetime, Kierkegaard was rediscovered in the early 20th century and became a central influence on existentialism, modern theology and thinkers including Heidegger, Sartre and Barth.
