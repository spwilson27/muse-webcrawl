Midwestern United States

The Midwestern United States, also referred to as the Midwest, is one of four census regions of the United States Census Bureau, occupying the northern central part of the country. It comprises twelve states: Illinois, Indiana, Michigan, Ohio, and Wisconsin in the East North Central division, and Iowa, Kansas, Minnesota, Missouri, Nebraska, North Dakota, and South Dakota in the West North Central division. Chicago is the region's largest city, followed by Columbus, Indianapolis, Detroit, and Milwaukee.

The region is characterised by the flat prairies and plains of the Great Lakes and Mississippi River basins, a humid continental climate, and an economy historically based on agriculture — the "Corn Belt" and "breadbasket" — and heavy manufacturing, including the automotive industry around Detroit. The Midwest is often regarded as politically competitive "swing" territory in national elections, and the term "Middle America" is sometimes used for its cultural identity.
