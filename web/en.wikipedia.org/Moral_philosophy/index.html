Moral philosophy

Moral philosophy, or ethics, is the branch of philosophy that involves systematising, defending, and recommending concepts of right and wrong behaviour. The field of ethics, along with aesthetics, concerns matters of value; these fields comprise the branch of philosophy called axiology.

Branch ofPhilosophy
SubfieldsNormative ethics, metaethics, applied ethics
Key figuresAristotle, Kant, Mill, Hume

Branches

Normative ethics

Normative ethics asks how one ought to act. Its major traditions are consequentialism, which judges acts by their outcomes (utilitarianism being the best-known version); deontology, associated with Immanuel Kant, which emphasises duties and rules; and virtue ethics, descending from Aristotle, which focuses on character.

Metaethics

Metaethics investigates the meaning, nature and justification of moral judgements themselves, including the debate between moral realism and anti-realism.

Applied ethics

Applied ethics addresses concrete issues such as medical ethics, business ethics, environmental ethics and the ethics of emerging technologies.

History

Ethical reflection appears in ancient Greek philosophy with Socrates, Plato and Aristotle, in Confucian and Buddhist traditions in Asia, and was reshaped in the Enlightenment by Hume and Kant, and in the 19th century by Mill's utilitarianism.
