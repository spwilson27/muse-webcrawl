Surgeon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In medicine, a surgeon is a medical doctor who performs surgery. Although there have been different traditions in different times and places, a modern surgeon is a medical professional who has also completed several years of general surgery training in a residency program and often continues training for several more years in a surgical speciality. The surgeon is the physician who leads the surgical team, which includes anaesthesiologists, nurses, and surgical technicians.

History

Surgery is among the oldest branches of medicine: trepanned skulls survive from prehistoric times, and ancient Egyptian, Indian (notably the Sushruta Samhita), Greek, and Roman texts describe operations such as cataract removal, lithotomy, and amputation. Through the Middle Ages and early modern period in Europe, surgery was largely practised separately from learned medicine by barber-surgeons and itinerant operators, while physicians considered manual work beneath them. The development of anaesthesia (1846) and antiseptic and later aseptic technique (from 1867) transformed surgery, and the discipline merged fully with medicine in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries.

Training

Surgeons first qualify as doctors, completing medical school and foundation or internship training. They then undertake residency training in surgery — typically five to seven years in general surgery in the United States, and comparable structured programmes elsewhere — followed in many cases by fellowship training of one to three years in a subspecialty. Board certification (for example by the American Board of Surgery) and continuing professional development are standard requirements.

Specialties

Recognised surgical specialties include:

  • General surgery
  • Cardiothoracic surgery
  • Neurosurgery
  • Orthopaedic surgery
  • Plastic and reconstructive surgery
  • Vascular surgery
  • Colorectal surgery
  • Trauma and surgical critical care
  • Transplant surgery
  • Paediatric surgery
  • Otolaryngology, ophthalmology, oral and maxillofacial surgery, and urology, which have surgical practices of their own

Practice

Modern surgical practice spans elective (planned) and emergency operations, open and minimally invasive (laparoscopic and robotic) techniques, and pre- and post-operative care. Surgeons also perform many procedures outside the operating theatre, including endoscopy and interventional techniques. In the United Kingdom and several Commonwealth countries, a qualified surgeon is traditionally addressed as "Mr", "Miss", "Mrs" or "Ms" rather than "Dr", a custom recalling the era before surgeons held medical degrees.
