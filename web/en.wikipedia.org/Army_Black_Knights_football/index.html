The Army Black Knights football team represents the United States Military Academy at West Point, New York, in NCAA Division I FBS college football. Founded in 1890, the program was one of the dominant forces in the sport in the first half of the 20th century.

History

Army won three consecutive national championships in 1944, 1945, and 1946 under coach Earl "Red" Blaik, led by Heisman Trophy winners Doc Blanchard (1945) and Glenn Davis (1946), known as "Mr. Inside" and "Mr. Outside". Pete Dawkins won the Heisman in 1958. After decades of struggle against scholarship competition, recent decades have seen periodic revivals, including successful seasons under coach Jeff Monken beginning in 2014.

Army–Navy

The annual Army–Navy Game, first played in 1890, is among the most storied rivalries in American sports. Army also competes with Air Force for the Commander-in-Chief's Trophy.

Home stadium

The Black Knights play at Michie Stadium, opened in 1924 on the banks of the Hudson River, named for Dennis Michie, founder of Army football and a casualty of the Spanish–American War.
