Cucurbitaceae

The Cucurbitaceae, also called the gourd family, is a plant family consisting of about 965 species in around 95 genera. The most important to humans are Cucurbita (squash, pumpkin, zucchini, and some gourds), Lagenaria (calabash), Citrullus (watermelon and others), Cucumis (cucumber, melons), and Luffa (loofah).

Characteristics

Most species are fast-growing annual vines, often with tendrils, palmately lobed leaves, and unisexual flowers that are typically yellow or white. The fruit is usually a fleshy berry with a leathery rind (a pepo). The family is distributed mainly in tropical and subtropical regions, with centres of diversity in Africa, Asia, and the Americas.

Uses

Cucurbits rank among the most widely cultivated vegetable families in the world. Members provide food (fruits, seeds, leaves, and shoots), oils, sponges (loofahs), and containers (bottle gourds). Bitter compounds called cucurbitacins occur in wild forms and are toxic in large doses.

Taxonomy

The family belongs to the order Cucurbitales, within the rosid clade of flowering plants, and is divided into subfamilies and tribes such as Benincaseae, Cucurbiteae, and Melothrieae.
