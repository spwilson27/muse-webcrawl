An Olympic medal table is a tabulation of the medals won by nations or delegations at an Olympic Games, ranked according to the number of gold, silver, and bronze medals achieved. Medal tables are produced for each edition of the Summer and Winter Games and in cumulative all-time form.

Ranking conventions

The International Olympic Committee does not officially publish or endorse a ranking of nations, but the convention used in most of the world ranks teams first by gold medals, with silver and then bronze as tie-breakers. Some media, historically in the United States, rank by total medals instead, which can produce different "winners" of a Games. Alternative systems weight medals (e.g., 3-2-1 or 5-3-1 points) or adjust per capita or by GDP.

The United States leads the all-time Summer table by a wide margin; Norway leads the all-time Winter table. Medal counts are occasionally revised years later when doping disqualifications reallocate results.

History

At the first modern Games in Athens in 1896, winners received silver medals and olive branches; the familiar gold–silver–bronze sequence was standardized from 1904. Team events count once in the table regardless of the number of athletes receiving medals.
