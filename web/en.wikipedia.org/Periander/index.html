Periander

Periander (died c. 585 BC) was the second tyrant of Corinth and one of the Seven Sages of Greece in some lists. Succeeding his father Cypselus around 627 BC, he ruled for about forty years and made Corinth one of the wealthiest and most powerful Greek city-states.

Periander
Reign: c. 627–585 BC
City: Corinth
Predecessor: Cypselus
Dynasty: Cypselids

Rule

Herodotus and Aristotle describe Periander as a harsher ruler than his father, though an effective administrator. He is credited with building projects, promoting trade and colonisation, and with the diolkos, the paved trackway across the Isthmus of Corinth for hauling ships. He maintained relations with Thrasybulus of Miletus and Alyattes of Lydia.

Legends

Stories told about him include the advice, relayed by a messenger, to cut down the tallest ears of grain — understood as eliminating prominent citizens — and tales concerning his wife Melissa. The saying "Practice is everything" is attributed to him.
