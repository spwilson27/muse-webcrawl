Codex Theodosianus

The Codex Theodosianus (Theodosian Code) is a compilation of the laws of the Roman Empire issued under Christian emperors since 312. It was commissioned by the Eastern emperor Theodosius II and promulgated in 438, taking effect in both the Eastern and Western empires.

Contents

The code contains over 2,500 constitutions arranged in 16 books by subject, covering administrative, criminal, and private law; the final book deals extensively with religious matters, including the legal position of Christianity, heresy, and pagan practice.

Legacy

The code was a principal source for the law of the successor kingdoms in the West — notably through the Breviary of Alaric (506) — and influenced the later Corpus Juris Civilis of Justinian I. Much of what survives does so through manuscripts of the Breviary and partial copies.
