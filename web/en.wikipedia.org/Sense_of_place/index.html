Sense of place

The term sense of place has been used in many ways. Generally it is the attachment and meaning that people assign to a particular geographic location, arising from the experiences, memories, and social interactions associated with it. It is studied in human geography, environmental psychology, architecture, and urban planning.

Concepts

Scholars often distinguish related notions: place attachment, the emotional bond between a person and a place; place identity, the extent to which a place becomes part of one's self-concept; and genius loci, the distinctive atmosphere or spirit of a place, a concept with roots in Roman religion and developed by architectural theorists such as Christian Norberg-Schulz.

The geographer Yi-Fu Tuan contrasted abstract "space" with "place" as space endowed with value through lived experience, while Edward Relph warned of "placelessness" produced by mass culture and standardised development.

Applications

Sense of place is invoked in heritage conservation, tourism, community development, and environmental management, where understanding residents' attachments can shape planning decisions and responses to change such as displacement or climate adaptation.
