Queen Charlotte Sound

Queen Charlotte Sound is a sound of the Pacific Ocean in British Columbia, Canada, lying between Vancouver Island in the south and Haida Gwaii in the north. It is part of the Inside Passage shipping route along the British Columbia Coast, connecting Queen Charlotte Strait in the southeast with Hecate Strait in the northwest, and opening westward to the open Pacific.


Queen Charlotte Sound, British Columbia

Geography

The sound is a structurally submerged extension of the Queen Charlotte Basin, bounded on the east by the mainland coast with its fjords, including Rivers Inlet and Knight Inlet. Islands within and around the sound include Calvert Island and the Goose Group. The waters are an important habitat for salmon, halibut and other fisheries species, and for marine mammals including humpback whales and orcas.

History

The sound was named in 1786 by the fur trader James Strange in honour of Queen Charlotte, consort of King George III. It should not be confused with the Queen Charlotte Sound in New Zealand, the strait between the North and South Islands named by James Cook in 1770, also for the queen. In 2010 the neighbouring archipelago formerly known as the Queen Charlotte Islands was restored to its Haida name, Haida Gwaii, under the Haida Gwaii Reconciliation Act, though the sound retains its colonial name.
