The Sistine Madonna, also called the Madonna di San Sisto, is an oil painting by the Italian artist Raphael. Commissioned in 1512 by Pope Julius II, it was intended for the church of San Sisto in Piacenza. It is widely regarded as one of the finest works of the High Renaissance and among the most famous paintings of the Madonna in Western art.

Description

The painting shows the Virgin Mary holding the Christ Child, flanked by Saint Sixtus and Saint Barbara. A pair of green curtains, drawn back, frame the scene, giving the impression of a vision revealed to the viewer. At the bottom of the canvas rest two putti — the famous contemplative cherubs — who lean on a ledge and gaze upward. These putti have become cultural icons in their own right, reproduced on countless prints, posters and household items since the nineteenth century.

History

Raphael painted the work near the end of his life, and it is considered his last completed Madonna. After more than two centuries in Piacenza, the painting was sold in 1754 to Augustus III of Poland, Elector of Saxony, and moved to Dresden. During the Second World War the painting was taken by the Soviet Union and displayed in Moscow before being returned to Dresden in 1955.

Legacy

The Sistine Madonna has been praised by generations of artists, writers and philosophers. Its two cherubs at the bottom edge are among the most reproduced details in art history. The painting remains a centerpiece of the Gemäldegalerie Alte Meister in Dresden.
