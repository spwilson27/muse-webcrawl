The 1964 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the XVIII Olympiad, were held from 10 to 24 October 1964 in Tokyo, Japan. They were the first Olympic Games held in Asia. Tokyo had originally been awarded the 1940 Summer Olympics, which were cancelled because of the Second World War.

The Games symbolised Japan's recovery and reintegration into the international community less than twenty years after the war. The final torchbearer, Yoshinori Sakai, was born in Hiroshima on 6 August 1945, the day of the atomic bombing. The Games spurred massive infrastructure investment, including the Tōkaidō Shinkansen bullet train, which opened days before the opening ceremony.

Highlights

Judo and women's volleyball made their Olympic debuts as sports in which the host nation excelled. Ethiopian runner Abebe Bikila became the first athlete to defend the Olympic marathon title, winning barely six weeks after an appendectomy. American swimmer Don Schollander won four gold medals; Soviet gymnast Larisa Latynina won six medals to finish her career with a record 18 Olympic medals. Hungarian water polo player Dezső Gyarmati completed a run of five consecutive medals. The United States topped the medal table with 36 golds, ahead of the Soviet Union's 30, while Japan won 16 golds, its best result to that date.

The 1964 Games were the first to be broadcast internationally by satellite to the United States. South Africa was banned from the Games because of apartheid, beginning an exclusion that lasted until 1992. Judoka Anton Geesink's heavyweight gold over Akio Kaminaga was one of the defining moments of the Games for the host nation.
