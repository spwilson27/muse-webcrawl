Belaying

Belaying
ActivityRock climbing, mountaineering
EquipmentBelay device, climbing rope, harness, carabiners
PurposeControlling the rope to arrest a climber's fall

A belayer managing the rope for a lead climber.

Belaying is the technique by which a climbing partner (the belayer) controls a rope attached to a climber so that a fall is arrested with minimal force and distance. The belayer feeds out or takes in rope as the climber moves, and locks the rope in a friction-enhancing belay device if the climber falls.

Technique

In top-rope belaying the rope runs from the belayer up through an anchor above the climber; in lead belaying the climber clips the rope into protection while ascending, and the belayer must pay out slack while remaining ready to catch a longer fall. Communication calls such as "on belay", "climbing" and "take" coordinate the pair.

Devices

Modern belay devices include tubular devices such as the ATC, assisted-braking devices such as the Grigri, and traditional methods like the hip belay and Munter hitch. The nautical term "belay"—to secure a rope to a pin or cleat—gave the climbing technique its name.
