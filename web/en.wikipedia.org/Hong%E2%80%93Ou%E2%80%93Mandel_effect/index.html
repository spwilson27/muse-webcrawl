Hong–Ou–Mandel effect

Hong–Ou–Mandel effectField: quantum optics
Demonstrated: 1987 (Hong, Ou, Mandel)
Phenomenon: two-photon interference dip

The Hong–Ou–Mandel (HOM) effect is a two-photon interference phenomenon in quantum optics. When two identical photons enter the two input ports of a 50:50 beam splitter simultaneously, they always exit together from the same output port, never one from each. Consequently, as the relative delay between the photons is scanned, the rate of coincidences between detectors at the two outputs drops to zero — the characteristic "HOM dip".

Explanation

The effect arises from destructive interference between the two indistinguishable two-photon amplitudes: both photons transmitted, and both reflected. These amplitudes cancel exactly for identical photons, leaving only the bunched outcomes. It has no classical analogue at full visibility and is a direct signature of photon indistinguishability and bosonic statistics.

Applications

The HOM effect is used to characterise single-photon sources, synchronise photons in quantum networks, and implement operations in linear-optical quantum computing. The width of the dip measures the photons' coherence time and spectral bandwidth.
