Árni Magnússon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Born: 13 November 1663, Kvennabrekka, Iceland
Died: 7 January 1730, Copenhagen, Denmark
Occupation: Scholar, collector of manuscripts

Árni Magnússon (13 November 1663 – 7 January 1730) was an Icelandic scholar and collector of manuscripts. He assembled the Árni Magnússon Collection (the Arnamagnæan Manuscript Collection), one of the most important collections of medieval Icelandic and Scandinavian manuscripts, now divided between institutes in Reykjavík and Copenhagen.

Life

Árni was born at Kvennabrekka in western Iceland and educated in Iceland and at the University of Copenhagen. He became a professor at the University of Copenhagen and travelled extensively in Iceland, where he carried out a major census and land register (the Jarðabók) with Páll Vídalín, and systematically collected vellum manuscripts that might otherwise have been lost.

The collection

Many of Árni's books and papers were destroyed in the Copenhagen fire of 1728, though most of the vellum manuscripts were saved. After a long dispute between Denmark and Iceland, the manuscripts began to be returned to Iceland following legislation in 1961; the transfer was completed in 1997. The collection was inscribed on UNESCO's Memory of the World Register in 2009. Árni is also remembered as the model for the character Arnas Arnæus in the novel Iceland's Bell by Halldór Laxness.
