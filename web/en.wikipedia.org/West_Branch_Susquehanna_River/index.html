The West Branch Susquehanna River is one of the two principal branches of the Susquehanna River in Pennsylvania, United States. It rises in the Allegheny Mountains near Cherry Tree in Indiana County and flows about 243 miles (391 km) generally eastward and then south-east, joining the North Branch at Northumberland, near Sunbury, to form the main Susquehanna.

Course

The river meanders through a deep, winding valley past Clearfield, Renovo, Lock Haven and Williamsport — the largest city on its banks — carving water gaps through the folded ridges of the Allegheny Plateau. Its valley is noted for scenery, including the "Grand Canyon of Pennsylvania" region along its tributary Pine Creek.

History

In the 19th century the West Branch valley was a centre of the lumber industry: log drives floated timber to sawmills at Williamsport, once called the "Lumber Capital of the World", and a canal and later railroads followed the river. The valley suffered catastrophic floods, notably in 1889 and in 1972 from Hurricane Agnes.

Environment

Acid mine drainage from abandoned coal mines in its upper basin long impaired the river, leaving stretches nearly lifeless; decades of remediation have gradually restored water quality, and the West Branch now supports recovering fisheries and recreation.
