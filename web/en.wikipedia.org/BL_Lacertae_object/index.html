BL Lacertae object

A BL Lacertae object or BL Lac object is a type of active galactic nucleus (AGN) known for rapid and large-amplitude variations in brightness and high polarization, together with an almost featureless optical spectrum lacking strong emission lines. The class is named after its prototype, BL Lacertae, in the constellation Lacerta, which was originally catalogued as a variable star.

Properties

BL Lac objects are understood as active galaxies whose relativistic jets point almost directly toward the observer, so that the jet's beamed emission dominates the light we see. This orientation explains their violent variability, strong polarization, and weak spectral lines, which are swamped by the beamed continuum. Along with flat-spectrum radio quasars, BL Lac objects form the class of blazars.

BL Lacertae

The prototype was discovered in 1929 by Cuno Hoffmeister and taken to be an irregular variable star. Only in 1968 was it identified with a radio source, and in 1974 its host galaxy was detected, revealing its extragalactic nature at a redshift of about 0.07 — roughly 900 million light-years away.
