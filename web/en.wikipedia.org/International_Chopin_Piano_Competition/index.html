The International Chopin Piano Competition (Polish: Międzynarodowy Konkurs Pianistyczny im. Fryderyka Chopina) is one of the oldest and most prestigious classical-music competitions in the world. Held in Warsaw every five years and devoted entirely to the music of Frédéric Chopin, it is widely regarded as a supreme test of pianistic artistry and a launchpad for major careers.

Victory in Warsaw has traditionally guaranteed the winner an international concert career.

History

The competition was founded by the Polish pianist and pedagogue Jerzy Żurawlew and first held in January 1927, when the Soviet pianist Lev Oborin won first prize. Early editions were held in 1932 (won by Alexander Uninsky) and 1937 (Yakov Zak). The Second World War interrupted the cycle; the competition resumed in 1949 and has since run quinquennially, missing its rhythm only when the 2020 edition was postponed to 2021 because of the COVID-19 pandemic.

Format

Competitors, generally aged 16 to 30, pass through a preliminary round judged from recordings and then three solo stages in Warsaw plus a final in which they perform one of Chopin's two piano concertos with the Warsaw Philharmonic. Programmes are prescribed from Chopin's genres — études, nocturnes, scherzos, ballades, polonaises, mazurkas, and sonatas. An international jury awards ranked prizes; in some years, such as 1995, no first prize at the top level has been shared or the field has produced joint placements, and in 1980 the elimination of Ivo Pogorelich before the final provoked a famous scandal in which juror Martha Argerich resigned in protest.

Significance

Winners including Maurizio Pollini, Martha Argerich, Garrick Ohlsson (the first American winner, 1970), Krystian Zimerman, Dang Thai Son (1980, the first Asian winner), Yundi Li, Rafał Blechacz, Yulianna Avdeeva, Seong-Jin Cho, and Bruce Liu have gone on to leading international careers. The competition is streamed worldwide and draws enormous interest in East Asia, where Chopin performance is intensely followed.
