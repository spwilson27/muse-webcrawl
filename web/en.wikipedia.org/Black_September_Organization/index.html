The Black September Organization (BSO) was a Palestinian militant organization founded in 1970. It is best known for the Munich massacre, in which eleven members of the Israeli Olympic team were taken hostage and killed during the 1972 Summer Olympics in Munich, West Germany.

The group took its name from the Black September conflict of 1970–71, when the Jordanian army under King Hussein expelled the Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO) from Jordan after a brief civil war. Black September was widely regarded as a front or deniable arm of Fatah, the largest faction within the PLO, led by Yasser Arafat. Key figures associated with the group included Ali Hassan Salameh, known as the "Red Prince."

History

Following the expulsion of Palestinian fedayeen from Jordan in 1970–71, elements within Fatah concluded that conventional guerrilla operations from neighbouring Arab states had failed. Black September was established as a secret apparatus to carry out attacks abroad, targeting Israeli and Jordanian interests while maintaining plausible deniability for Fatah's leadership.

Major operations

  • November 1971: Assassination of Jordanian prime minister Wasfi Tal in Cairo, Egypt.
  • May 1972: Hijacking of Sabena Flight 571, freed by Israeli commandos in Operation Isotope.
  • September 1972: Munich massacre at the Olympic Games; eleven Israeli athletes and coaches and a West German police officer were killed, along with five of the eight gunmen.
  • March 1973: Attack on the Saudi embassy in Khartoum, Sudan, in which three diplomats, including the U.S. ambassador Cleo Noel, were killed.

Aftermath

Israel responded to Munich with Operation Wrath of God, a long-running campaign by Mossad to assassinate those involved in planning the attack. Ali Hassan Salameh was killed by a car bomb in Beirut in 1979. The BSO largely ceased major operations after 1973, and by the late 1980s it was defunct, though the extent of its formal dissolution remains debated.
