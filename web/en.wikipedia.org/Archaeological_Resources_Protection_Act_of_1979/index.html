Archaeological Resources Protection Act of 1979

Archaeological Resources Protection Act of 1979
EnactedOctober 31, 1979
Public law96-95
Codified16 U.S.C. §§ 470aa–470mm
Signed byPresident Jimmy Carter

The Archaeological Resources Protection Act of 1979 (ARPA) is a United States federal law that protects archaeological resources on public lands and Native American lands. It was enacted to curb the widespread looting and unauthorized excavation of archaeological sites on federal land, which earlier legislation such as the Antiquities Act of 1906 had failed to prevent.

Provisions

The Act prohibits the excavation, removal, damage, or trafficking of archaeological resources that are more than 100 years old and located on public or Indian lands, except under a permit issued by the relevant federal land manager. It establishes criminal penalties, including fines of up to $20,000 and imprisonment, with enhanced penalties for repeat offenses and for trafficking in resources removed in violation of state or local law.

Permits and enforcement

Permits may be issued to qualified applicants for scientific research, and artifacts recovered under permit generally remain the property of the United States. The Act also requires federal agencies to inventory archaeological resources and to consult with Native American tribes regarding resources of traditional or religious significance.

Legacy

ARPA, together with the Native American Graves Protection and Repatriation Act of 1990, forms the core of modern federal protection for cultural heritage in the United States.
