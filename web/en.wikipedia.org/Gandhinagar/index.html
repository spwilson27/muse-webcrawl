Gandhinagar

Gandhinagar
State: Gujarat, India
Named after: Mahatma Gandhi
Population (2011): ~292,000
Area: ~326 km²

Gandhinagar is the capital of the state of Gujarat in India. It lies on the west bank of the Sabarmati River, about 23 km north of Ahmedabad, and is one of India's planned cities, laid out in numbered sectors with abundant greenery. It is named after Mahatma Gandhi, who was born in Gujarat.

History

When Bombay State was divided in 1960 into Gujarat and Maharashtra, a new capital was planned for Gujarat. Chief architect H. K. Mewada and his deputy Prakash M. Apte designed the city, influenced by Le Corbusier's planning of Chandigarh. Government offices moved from Ahmedabad in the 1970s as the secretariat and assembly buildings were completed.

Features

Gandhinagar hosts the Gujarat Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha) at the Vitthalbhai Patel Bhavan, the Akshardham Temple (Swaminarayan), the Mahatma Mandir convention centre, and institutions including the Indian Institute of Technology Gandhinagar (at nearby Palaj) and Gujarat National Law University. Nearby GIFT City (Gujarat International Finance Tec-City) is being developed as a financial and technology hub. Indroda Nature Park, with fossil exhibits and a zoo, is a popular attraction.

Climate and transport

The city has a hot semi-arid climate with summer temperatures exceeding 40°C. It is connected by road to Ahmedabad and served by Ahmedabad's Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel International Airport and railway links from Ahmedabad and Gandhinagar Capital station.
