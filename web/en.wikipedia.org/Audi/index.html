Audi

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The four rings of Audi

Audi AG is a German automotive manufacturer of luxury vehicles headquartered in Ingolstadt, Bavaria, Germany. A subsidiary of the Volkswagen Group, Audi produces vehicles in nine production facilities worldwide. The company's name is based on the Latin translation of the surname of its founder, August Horch: horch, meaning "listen", becomes audi in Latin.

History

August Horch founded Audi Automobilwerke in Zwickau in 1910 after leaving his earlier company, Horch. In 1932, Audi merged with Horch, DKW, and Wanderer to form Auto Union, whose four interlocking rings survive as Audi's emblem. After the Second World War, Auto Union was re-established in Ingolstadt in West Germany and was acquired by Volkswagen in the 1960s; the Audi name was revived with the 1965 F103 series and the 1968 Audi 100.

Quattro and motorsport

The 1980 Audi Quattro introduced the company's permanent all-wheel-drive system to rallying, dominating the World Rally Championship in the early 1980s. Audi later achieved major success in endurance racing, winning the 24 Hours of Le Mans 13 times between 2000 and 2014, and has competed in Formula E.

Models and slogan

Audi's model range includes the A1 through A8 passenger cars, Q-series SUVs, the TT and R8 sports cars, and the e-tron electric vehicles. Its slogan is Vorsprung durch Technik ("Progress through Technology").
