Ohio State University

Ohio State University (Ohio State or OSU) is a public land-grant research university in Columbus, Ohio, United States. Founded in 1870 under the Morrill Act, it is the flagship institution of the University System of Ohio and one of the largest universities by enrolment in the United States, with around 60,000 students on its Columbus campus alone.

History

The university was chartered in 1870 as the Ohio Agricultural and Mechanical College and opened in 1873 on a farm north of Columbus. It was renamed Ohio State University in 1878 as its curriculum broadened. Over the 20th century it grew into a comprehensive research university with a major academic medical center (the Wexner Medical Center) and regional campuses at Lima, Mansfield, Marion, Newark, and Wooster.

Academics

Ohio State offers more than 200 undergraduate majors through colleges including Arts and Sciences, Engineering, the Fisher College of Business, Education and Human Ecology, and the Moritz College of Law. It is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities — Very high research activity" and is a member of the Association of American Universities.

Campus and student life

The Columbus campus is anchored by the Oval, a large green surrounded by historic buildings including Orton Hall and Thompson Library. Student traditions include the Mirror Lake jump, the rivalry with the University of Michigan, and the marching band's "Script Ohio". The athletic teams, the Buckeyes, compete in the Big Ten Conference.

Notable people

Alumni and faculty include Nobel laureates, heads of state, and prominent figures in business, science, and sport. The university's alumni association is one of the largest in the world.
