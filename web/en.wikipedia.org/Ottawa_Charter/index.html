Ottawa Charter for Health Promotion


Adopted: 21 November 1986, Ottawa, Canada
Organisation: World Health Organization
Event: First International Conference on Health Promotion

The Ottawa Charter for Health Promotion is the name of an international agreement signed at the First International Conference on Health Promotion, organised by the World Health Organization and held in Ottawa, Canada, in November 1986. It remains a foundational document of the modern health promotion movement, defining health promotion as "the process of enabling people to increase control over, and to improve, their health".

Action areas and strategies

The Charter identifies five action areas: building healthy public policy, creating supportive environments, strengthening community action, developing personal skills, and reorienting health services away from purely clinical care toward prevention and well-being. It proposes three basic strategies — advocate, enable and mediate — and lists prerequisites for health including peace, shelter, education, food, income, a stable ecosystem and social justice. Its winged logo, depicting a circle with three wings, is widely reproduced in public health literature.

Legacy

The Charter launched the settings approach to health promotion (healthy cities, schools and workplaces) and was followed by a series of WHO global conferences, from Adelaide (1988) to Shanghai (2016), which reaffirmed and extended its principles.
