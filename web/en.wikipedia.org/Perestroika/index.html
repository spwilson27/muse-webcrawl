Literal meaning"Restructuring"
Period1985–1991
LeaderMikhail Gorbachev
CountrySoviet Union

Perestroika (Russian: перестройка, "restructuring") was a political reform movement within the Communist Party of the Soviet Union during the late 1980s, widely associated with CPSU General Secretary Mikhail Gorbachev and his policy of glasnost ("openness"). It aimed to revitalise the stagnant Soviet economy through decentralisation, limited market mechanisms and greater political openness.

Reforms

Gorbachev, who came to power in March 1985, initially promoted uskoreniye ("acceleration") and an anti-alcohol campaign. From 1987 the reform programme deepened: the Law on State Enterprise granted managers more autonomy, the Law on Cooperatives (1988) legalised forms of small private business, and joint ventures with foreign firms were permitted. Politically, perestroika brought contested elections to a new Congress of People's Deputies in 1989, the abolition of the Communist Party's constitutional monopoly in 1990, and the creation of an executive presidency.

Consequences

Instead of reviving the system, partial reform disrupted the planned economy without replacing it: shortages worsened, the budget deficit grew, and republics began asserting sovereignty. Combined with glasnost's exposure of past crimes and present failings, perestroika unleashed nationalist movements and political pluralism that culminated in the failed August 1991 coup and the dissolution of the Soviet Union in December 1991. Historians continue to debate whether perestroika failed because it went too far or not far enough.
