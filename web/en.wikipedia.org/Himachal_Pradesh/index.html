Himachal Pradesh

Himachal Pradesh is a state in the northern part of India, situated in the Western Himalayas. It is bordered by Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh to the north, Punjab to the west, Haryana to the southwest, Uttarakhand to the southeast, and shares an international border with China to the east. The capital is Shimla, and the state covers an area of about 55,673 square kilometres.

Geography

The state is characterised by an extreme landscape of lofty snow-clad peaks, deep gorges, and valleys carved by rivers including the Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Sutlej, and Yamuna. Elevations range from about 350 metres to more than 7,000 metres above sea level.

History and economy

Himachal Pradesh was created after Indian independence, initially as a chief commissioner's province in 1948, and became a full state on 25 January 1971. Its economy rests on agriculture, horticulture — the state is famous for its apples — hydroelectric power, and tourism, with hill stations such as Shimla, Manali, Dharamshala, and Kullu attracting visitors year-round.
