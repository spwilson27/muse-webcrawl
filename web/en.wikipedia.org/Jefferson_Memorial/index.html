Jefferson Memorial

Location: West Potomac Park, Washington, D.C.

Architect: John Russell Pope

Built: 1939–1943

Dedicated: 13 April 1943

The Thomas Jefferson Memorial is a presidential memorial in Washington, D.C., dedicated to Thomas Jefferson, third president of the United States and principal author of the Declaration of Independence. Standing on the shore of the Tidal Basin in West Potomac Park, its neoclassical rotunda is one of the capital's most familiar landmarks.

Design and construction

Designed by John Russell Pope in the style of the Roman Pantheon — an homage to Jefferson's own architectural tastes — the memorial consists of a circular marble temple with a shallow dome, encircled by Ionic columns. Construction began in 1939 and the memorial was dedicated by President Franklin D. Roosevelt on 13 April 1943, the bicentennial of Jefferson's birth. Inside stands a 19-foot bronze statue of Jefferson by sculptor Rudulph Evans, installed in 1947; excerpts from Jefferson's writings are carved into the interior walls.

Setting

The memorial is framed each spring by Washington's famous Japanese cherry trees around the Tidal Basin, making it a centrepiece of the National Cherry Blossom Festival. It is administered by the National Park Service as part of the National Mall and Memorial Parks.
