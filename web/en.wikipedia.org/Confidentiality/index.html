Confidentiality

Confidentiality involves a set of rules or a promise that limits access to, or places restrictions on, certain types of information. It is a core obligation in many professional relationships — medicine, law, psychotherapy, journalism and the clergy — where clients or patients must be able to speak freely in the assurance that what they reveal will not be disclosed without their consent.

Legal and professional duties

Confidentiality may arise from contract, from professional codes of ethics, from statute (such as health-privacy laws) or from equitable doctrines protecting trade secrets. Attorney–client privilege and doctor–patient confidentiality are well-known examples; journalists may also protect their sources. Duties of confidentiality have recognised exceptions, for example where disclosure is needed to prevent serious harm to others.

In information security

In information security, confidentiality is one leg of the "CIA triad" (confidentiality, integrity, availability): ensuring that information is accessible only to those authorised to have access, typically through encryption, access control and classification systems.
