Fraternities and sororities, also referred to collectively as Greek life or the Greek system, are social organisations at colleges and universities, predominantly in the United States and Canada. A form of fraternal social organisation, fraternities for men and sororities for women typically have their names composed of two or three Greek letters, a process dating back to the 18th century. They generally offer housing, social activities, philanthropy, networking, and lifelong membership bonds.

History

The first Greek-letter society, Phi Beta Kappa, was founded at the College of William & Mary in 1776 as a literary and social society (it later became an academic honour society). The modern social fraternity system began with the "Union Triad" at Union College in Schenectady, New York: the Kappa Alpha Society (1825), Sigma Phi (1827), and Delta Phi (1827), followed by the "Miami Triad" at Miami University in Ohio. The term "sorority" was coined for Gamma Phi Beta (founded 1874), though women's fraternities such as Alpha Delta Pi (1851) and Kappa Alpha Theta (1870) preceded it. In the early 20th century, Black students, excluded from existing organisations, founded their own fraternities and sororities, nine of which form the National Pan-Hellenic Council, known as the Divine Nine.

Organisation and activities

Local chapters are chartered by national or international organisations, which set standards and own trademarks. Membership is selective, proceeding through "rush" or recruitment, a pledging or new-member period, and a secret initiation ritual. Chapters commonly maintain a chapter house, hold formal events and mixers, organise charitable service and fundraising, and promote academic achievement and leadership. Umbrella bodies include the North American Interfraternity Conference (NIC), the National Panhellenic Conference (NPC), the National Pan-Hellenic Council (NPHC), and multicultural and professional councils.

Criticism

Greek life has long been criticised for hazing (sometimes resulting in deaths and consequent bans and prosecutions), alcohol abuse, exclusivity and elitism, sexual assault risks, and racial and socioeconomic homogeneity. Supporters counter with statistics on leadership, graduation rates, alumni networks, and charitable work. Many campuses have reformed or abolished the system at various times, and some elite institutions have banned single-sex Greek organisations outright.
