Prolonged grief disorder

Prolonged grief disorder
SpecialtyPsychiatry, psychology
SymptomsIntense yearning, preoccupation with the deceased, functional impairment

Prolonged grief disorder (PGD), also called complicated grief, is a mental disorder consisting of a distinct set of symptoms following the death of a loved one: intense yearning and longing for the deceased, preoccupation with the loss, and difficulty accepting the death, persisting far beyond expected social and cultural norms and causing significant impairment.

Diagnosis

Prolonged grief disorder was included in the ICD-11 (effective 2022) and added to the DSM-5-TR in 2022. The DSM-5-TR requires, for adults, that the death occurred at least 12 months earlier, with grief responses nearly every day for at least the last month, plus additional symptoms such as identity disruption, disbelief, avoidance of reminders, intense emotional pain, or loneliness. It is distinguished from normal grief, major depression, and post-traumatic stress disorder.

Treatment

Targeted psychotherapies — such as prolonged grief disorder therapy (complicated grief treatment) developed by Katherine Shear — have the strongest evidence; generic grief counselling and antidepressants alone show limited benefit for core PGD symptoms.
