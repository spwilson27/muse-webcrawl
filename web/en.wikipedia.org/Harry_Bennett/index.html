Harry Bennett

Harry Herbert Bennett (January 17, 1892 – January 4, 1979) was an American executive at the Ford Motor Company. As head of Ford's Service Department, Bennett was Henry Ford's chief enforcer and one of the most powerful and feared men in the American automobile industry during the 1930s and early 1940s.

Role at Ford

Bennett joined Ford in 1916 after serving in the United States Navy. Rising through the personnel department, he built the Ford Service Department into a private security force of thousands of men, including ex-boxers, former athletes and underworld figures, tasked with spying on workers and suppressing union organizing. His men were implicated in violent attacks on union activists, most notoriously the Battle of the Overpass in 1937, when United Auto Workers organizers including Walter Reuther were beaten outside Ford's River Rouge Plant.

Bennett's influence waned after Henry Ford's death in 1945-era succession struggles, and he was fired by Henry Ford II in 1945. He retired to California and later published a memoir, We Never Called Him Henry (1951).
