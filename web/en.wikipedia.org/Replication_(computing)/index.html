Replication (computing)

Replication in computing involves sharing information so as to ensure consistency between redundant resources, such as software or hardware components, to improve reliability, fault-tolerance, or accessibility.

Types

Replication may be synchronous, in which updates are applied to all replicas before a transaction completes, or asynchronous, in which changes propagate with some lag. Common models include primary–backup (leader–follower) replication and multi-primary replication, in which any replica may accept writes.

Challenges

Replicated systems must manage consistency, availability, and partition tolerance — tensions summarised by the CAP theorem. Techniques such as consensus protocols (Paxos, Raft), quorum reads and writes, and conflict resolution strategies are used to keep replicas in agreement.
