Hebrew University of Jerusalem

The Hebrew University of Jerusalem (Hebrew: האוניברסיטה העברית בירושלים, abbreviated HUJI) is a public research university based in Jerusalem, Israel. Founded in 1918, some thirty years before the establishment of the State of Israel, it officially opened on 1 April 1925 on Mount Scopus. It is the second-oldest Israeli university after the Technion and is consistently ranked among the world's top 100 universities.

History

The idea of a Jewish university in the Land of Israel was advanced by Zionist thinkers and formally endorsed at the 1913 Zionist Congress. The cornerstone was laid in 1918, and the opening ceremony in 1925 was attended by Lord Balfour, Chaim Weizmann, and Albert Einstein, who sat on the first Board of Governors alongside Sigmund Freud and Martin Buber. After the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, the Mount Scopus campus was cut off inside a Jordanian-held enclave, and the university rebuilt on a new campus at Givat Ram. Mount Scopus was reclaimed and restored after 1967.

Campuses and academics

The university operates four campuses in Jerusalem — Mount Scopus, Givat Ram (Edmond J. Safra), Ein Kerem (medicine and dentistry, adjacent to Hadassah Medical Center), and a campus in Rehovot hosting agriculture, food and environment. It enrolls more than 23,000 students and has produced eight Nobel Prize laureates affiliated with it, including Daniel Kahneman, as well as Fields Medalists. The Jewish National Library of Israel is located on the Givat Ram campus.
