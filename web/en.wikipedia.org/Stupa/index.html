Stupa

Stupa
ReligionBuddhism (also Jain and Hindu use)
FunctionRelic shrine, meditation, pilgrimage
Famous examplesSanchi, Borobudur, Boudhanath, Shwedagon

A stupa is a mound-like or hemispherical structure containing relics, typically of Buddhist monks, nuns, or the Buddha himself, used as a place of meditation and pilgrimage. Stupas evolved from pre-Buddhist burial mounds and became central monuments of Buddhism as it spread across Asia.

History

Tradition holds that after the Buddha's death his ashes were divided into eight (later more) portions enshrined in stupas. Emperor Ashoka in the 3rd century BCE is said to have redistributed relics into 84,000 stupas. The Great Stupa at Sanchi, begun under Ashoka, is among the oldest stone structures in India.

Symbolism and Forms

The dome (anda) represents the Buddha and the cosmos; the harmika and parasol (chattra) crown it, and devotees circumambulate clockwise. Regional forms include Tibetan chörten, Sri Lankan dagoba, Burmese paya, and — by evolution of the form — the East Asian pagoda. Vast examples include Borobudur in Java, built under the influence of the world that also saw Srivijaya, and Boudhanath in Kathmandu.
