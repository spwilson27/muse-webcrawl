Steller's sea cow

Steller's sea cow
Scientific name: Hydrodamalis gigas
Order: Sirenia
Length: up to 8–9 m
Extinct: 1768

Steller's sea cow (Hydrodamalis gigas) was a large sirenian mammal that lived around the Commander Islands in the Bering Sea. It was discovered by Europeans in 1741 during Vitus Bering's expedition and was described by the naturalist Georg Wilhelm Steller, after whom it is named.

Description

Reaching at least 8 metres in length and weighing several tonnes, it was far larger than the surviving manatees and dugong. It fed on kelp near the surface, had a small head, toothless mouth with horny plates, and a thick bark-like skin that protected it from cold water and ice.

Extinction

The population, probably only about 1,500–2,000 animals when discovered, was hunted for meat by fur traders and shipwrecked crews. The last confirmed animal was killed in 1768 — only 27 years after its discovery — making it a classic early example of human-caused extinction of a large mammal.
