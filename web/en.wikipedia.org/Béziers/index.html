Béziers

Country: France

Region: Occitanie

Department: Hérault

Population: approx. 78,000 (city proper)

Béziers (Occitan: Besièrs) is a commune in the Hérault department in the Occitanie region of southern France. Situated on a bluff above the river Orb, about 10 km from the Mediterranean coast, it is one of the oldest towns in France, with origins as a Neolithic and then Greek and Roman settlement (Baeterrae). Béziers lies on the Canal du Midi, the 17th-century canal linking the Atlantic to the Mediterranean.

History

Roman Baeterrae stood on the Via Domitia and prospered on the wine trade. The town is chiefly infamous for the Massacre at Béziers on July 22, 1209, during the Albigensian Crusade, when crusader forces stormed the city and slaughtered much of its population, Catholics and Cathars alike — the episode that gave rise to the (possibly apocryphal) order attributed to the papal legate Arnaud Amalric: "Kill them all; God will know his own."

The Canal du Midi, engineered by the Biterrois-born Pierre-Paul Riquet and opened in 1681, passes the city; its Fonseranes Locks, a staircase of eight chambers, are among the canal's most famous structures. Béziers became a major centre of the Languedoc wine trade in the 19th century, and was a focus of the 1907 winegrowers' revolt.

Culture

The city's Gothic Saint-Nazaire Cathedral overlooks the Orb from the old town. Béziers is renowned for the Feria de Béziers, a five-day festival around August 15 centred on bullfighting at its arena, drawing hundreds of thousands of visitors, and for its rugby union club AS Béziers Hérault, one of France's historically successful teams.
