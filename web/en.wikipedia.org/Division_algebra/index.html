Division algebra

In the field of mathematics called abstract algebra, a division algebra is, roughly speaking, an algebra over a field in which division, except by zero, is always possible. Formally, it is a nonzero algebra over a field together with a notion of multiplication such that every nonzero element has a multiplicative inverse under the algebra's multiplication, or equivalently the algebra has no zero divisors.

Definitions

An algebra A over a field F is a division algebra if for every element a in A and every nonzero element b in A there exists precisely one element x in A such that a = bx and precisely one element y in A such that a = yb. For finite-dimensional algebras, these conditions are equivalent to the algebra having no zero divisors.

Examples

The most familiar examples of associative division algebras over the real numbers are the real numbers themselves, the complex numbers, and the quaternions. By the Frobenius theorem, these are the only finite-dimensional associative division algebras over the reals. If associativity is dropped, the octonions provide an eight-dimensional normed division algebra.

Finite fields

By Wedderburn's little theorem, every finite division ring is commutative and therefore a field, so finite-dimensional division algebras over finite fields are simply field extensions.
