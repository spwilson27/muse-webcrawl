Shemini Atzeret

Shemini Atzeret (Hebrew: שְׁמִינִי עֲצֶרֶת, "Eighth [Day] of Assembly") is a Jewish holiday celebrated on the 22nd day of Tishrei, immediately following the seven-day festival of Sukkot. Though closely associated with Sukkot, the rabbis regarded it as a distinct festival in its own right.

Observances

In Israel, Shemini Atzeret is combined with Simchat Torah, celebrating the completion and restarting of the annual Torah reading cycle; in the Diaspora, Simchat Torah is observed on the following day, the 23rd of Tishrei. On Shemini Atzeret the prayer for rain (Tefillat Geshem) is recited, formally beginning the rainy season in the liturgy, and the memorial service Yizkor is held in many communities. Meals are still traditionally eaten in the sukkah in some Diaspora customs, though without the blessings associated with Sukkot itself.

The holiday's name reflects its place as an eighth day added after Sukkot's seven, described in Leviticus 23 and Numbers 29 as a solemn assembly with cessation from work.
