Amazon Mechanical Turk

Amazon Mechanical Turk (MTurk) is a crowdsourcing marketplace operated by Amazon that enables individuals and businesses ("requesters") to coordinate the use of human intelligence to perform tasks that computers are currently unable to do. Launched in 2005, it is one of the suites of Amazon Web Services.

How it works

Requesters post microtasks known as Human Intelligence Tasks (HITs) — such as identifying objects in photographs, transcribing receipts, moderating content, or completing surveys — and workers ("Turkers") complete them for small payments, often a few cents per task. The service's name alludes to the 18th-century "Mechanical Turk", a chess-playing automaton that concealed a human operator; Amazon described the platform as "artificial artificial intelligence".

Uses and criticism

MTurk became a major tool for social-science experiments and for labelling training data for machine learning. It has also drawn criticism over low effective wages, lack of worker protections and the absence of a direct relationship between workers and requesters; researchers have likewise raised questions about data quality and worker use of automated tools. Amazon takes a commission on each payment.
