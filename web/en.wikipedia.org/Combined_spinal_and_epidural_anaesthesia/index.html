Combined spinal and epidural anaesthesia

SpecialtyAnaesthesiology
UsesSurgery, labour analgesia
ApproachNeuraxial (lumbar)

Combined spinal and epidural anaesthesia (CSE) is a regional anaesthetic technique in which an epidural catheter and a spinal block are combined in a single procedure. The technique offers the rapid onset and dense block of a spinal anaesthetic together with the ability to prolong or supplement anaesthesia through an indwelling epidural catheter.

Technique

The most common approach is the "needle-through-needle" technique. An epidural (Tuohy) needle is first advanced into the epidural space, identified by loss of resistance to saline or air. A long, fine spinal needle is then passed through the Tuohy needle, piercing the dura mater so that its tip lies in the subarachnoid space; free flow of cerebrospinal fluid confirms placement. A small dose of local anaesthetic, usually hyperbaric bupivacaine, often with an opioid such as fentanyl, is injected intrathecally. The spinal needle is withdrawn and an epidural catheter is threaded through the Tuohy needle and left in place.

An alternative is the "separate needle" technique, in which the spinal and epidural are performed at different interspaces.

Advantages

CSE combines the fast, reliable onset of a spinal (typically within minutes) with the flexibility of an epidural: the block can be extended in duration, topped up for prolonged surgery, or used for postoperative analgesia. Because the spinal dose can be kept small, CSE is popular in obstetric anaesthesia for Caesarean section and in the "walking epidural" technique for labour, where a low-dose intrathecal injection gives rapid pain relief while preserving motor function.

Risks and complications

Complications are those of neuraxial blockade generally: hypotension, post-dural-puncture headache, inadequate or failed block, infection, epidural haematoma, and, rarely, neurological injury. A specific concern is that the epidural catheter cannot be tested before the spinal component takes effect, and there is a small risk of the catheter migrating through the dural puncture. High or total spinal block is a rare but serious complication if a full epidural dose is given through a misplaced catheter.
