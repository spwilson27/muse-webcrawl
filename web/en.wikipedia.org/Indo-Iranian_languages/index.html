Indo-Iranian languages

Geographic distributionSouth, Central, and Western Asia
SpeakersOver 1.5 billion
BranchesIndo-Aryan, Iranian, Nuristani
Proto-languageProto-Indo-Iranian

The Indo-Iranian languages (also Indo-Iranic languages) are the largest and southeasternmost extant branch of the Indo-European language family, spoken by more than 1.5 billion people. They comprise three principal groups: the Indo-Aryan languages (including Hindi, Bengali, Punjabi, Marathi, Urdu, and historically Sanskrit), the Iranian languages (including Persian, Pashto, Kurdish, and historically Avestan and Old Persian), and the small Nuristani group of the Hindu Kush.

Distribution of Indo-Iranian languages across Asia.

History

The languages descend from Proto-Indo-Iranian, spoken in the late 3rd and early 2nd millennia BCE, associated with the Sintashta and Andronovo cultures of the Eurasian steppe. The oldest attested Indo-Iranian languages are Vedic Sanskrit (in the Rigveda) and Avestan, whose striking similarities point to a recent common origin. Indo-Iranian speakers migrated into Iran and northern India during the 2nd millennium BCE, bringing chariot technology and the roots of Vedic and Zoroastrian religion.

Features

Shared innovations include the merger of PIE voiced aspirates, the Ruki sound law, and the satem palatalisation shared with neighbouring branches. Both early Sanskrit and Avestan preserved the Indo-European ablaut system and eight-case noun declension to a notable degree.
