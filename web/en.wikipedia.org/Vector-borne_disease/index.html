Vector-borne disease

Vector-borne diseases are human illnesses caused by parasites, viruses and bacteria that are transmitted by vectors — living organisms, usually blood-feeding arthropods such as mosquitoes, ticks, fleas, lice, sandflies and triatomine bugs, that carry infectious pathogens between humans, or from animals to humans.

Major diseases

According to the World Health Organization, vector-borne diseases account for more than 17% of all infectious diseases and cause over 700,000 deaths annually. The most important include malaria (transmitted by Anopheles mosquitoes), dengue, Zika, chikungunya and yellow fever (by Aedes mosquitoes), Lyme disease and tick-borne encephalitis (by ticks), plague (by fleas), leishmaniasis (by sandflies) and Chagas disease (by triatomine bugs).

Transmission and control

Vectors acquire pathogens when feeding on an infected host and transmit them in later feeds, either biologically (after the pathogen develops or multiplies in the vector) or mechanically. Control relies on reducing vector populations and contact — insecticide-treated bed nets, indoor residual spraying, environmental management, repellents — together with vaccination where available, surveillance, and treatment of cases. Climate change, travel, trade and urbanisation are altering the geographic range of many vectors.
