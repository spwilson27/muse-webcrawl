Alpheratz

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Alpheratz, designated Alpha Andromedae (α Andromedae), is the brightest star in the constellation of Andromeda. It lies about 97 light-years from the Sun and marks the northeastern corner of the Great Square of Pegasus; historically it was shared between the two constellations and also bore the designation Delta Pegasi.

Properties

Alpheratz is a binary star system. The primary is a blue-white subgiant of spectral type B8, notable as the brightest of the chemically peculiar mercury-manganese stars, with unusually high abundances of mercury and manganese in its atmosphere. The companion is a fainter A-type star. The system has an apparent magnitude of about 2.06.

Nomenclature

The traditional name Alpheratz derives from the Arabic surrat al-faras, "navel of the mare", reflecting its older association with Pegasus. In 2016 the International Astronomical Union formally approved the name Alpheratz for the primary star.
