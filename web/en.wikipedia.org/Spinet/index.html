Spinet

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A bent-side spinet harpsichord

A spinet is a smaller type of harpsichord or piano. In the harpsichord sense, a spinet is a compact instrument in which the strings run at an angle to the keyboard — typically in a triangular or bent-side case — rather than perpendicular to it as in a virginal or parallel as in a full harpsichord. Spinets were popular domestic instruments from the 17th to the 18th century, particularly in England, where makers such as Stephen Keene and the Hitchcock family produced them in large numbers.

Spinet pianos

The term was later applied to the smallest upright pianos, spinet pianos, marketed from the 1930s onwards; these use a dropped action in which the keys act on the hammers indirectly via rods, allowing a case only about 36–38 inches tall. While affordable and space-saving, spinet pianos are generally considered inferior in tone and touch to taller uprights and are no longer manufactured in significant numbers.

Etymology

The name may derive from the Italian spinetta, possibly from the thorns (Italian spina) used as plectra, or from the Venetian instrument maker Giovanni Spinetti.
