Julius Wellhausen

Julius Wellhausen (17 May 1844 – 7 January 1918) was a German biblical scholar and orientalist, noted particularly for his Prolegomena zur Geschichte Israels (1878), which formulated the documentary hypothesis of the composition of the Pentateuch.

Career and work

Born in Hamelin, the son of a Lutheran pastor, Wellhausen studied theology at Göttingen and taught at Greifswald, Halle, Marburg, and Göttingen. In his Prolegomena he gave the documentary hypothesis its classic form: the first five books of the Bible were woven together from four originally independent sources — the Yahwist (J), Elohist (E), Deuteronomist (D), and Priestly source (P) — composed in that order between roughly the 10th and 5th centuries BC.

Convinced that the law was later than the prophets, he resigned his theology chair at Greifswald in 1882, writing that he had become a burden to his students' faith, and thereafter taught Oriental languages. His later scholarship extended to the New Testament, early Islam (notably The Arab Kingdom and its Fall), and Arabic poetry.

Influence

The documentary hypothesis dominated Pentateuchal scholarship for a century and, though modified and contested by later scholars, remains a landmark of historical-critical biblical study.
