Michael Collins (Irish leader)

Michael Collins (Irish: Mícheál Ó Coileáin; 16 October 1890 – 22 August 1922) was an Irish revolutionary, soldier and politician who was a leading figure in the early-20th-century struggle for Irish independence. He served as Minister for Finance of the Irish Republic, Director of Intelligence for the IRA, and Chairman of the Provisional Government of the Irish Free State.

Early life and Easter Rising

Born near Clonakilty, County Cork, Collins worked in London before returning to Ireland, where he fought in the General Post Office during the Easter Rising of 1916. After internment at Frongoch in Wales, he became a central organiser in Sinn Féin and the Irish Volunteers.

War of Independence

During the Irish War of Independence (1919–1921), Collins built an intelligence network that penetrated Dublin Castle, organised the "Squad" that assassinated British intelligence agents, and financed the revolutionary government through a national loan. His guerrilla warfare strategy made him Britain's most wanted man in Ireland.

Treaty and death

Collins signed the Anglo-Irish Treaty of December 1921, which created the Irish Free State while partitioning Ireland — a compromise he called a "stepping stone" to full freedom. The Treaty split the movement, leading to the Irish Civil War. As commander-in-chief of the National Army, Collins was shot and killed in an ambush at Béal na Bláth, County Cork, on 22 August 1922, aged 31.
