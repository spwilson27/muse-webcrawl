Issyk-Kul

Issyk-Kul (Kyrgyz: Ысык-Көл, "warm lake") is an endorheic lake in the Northern Tien Shan mountains in eastern Kyrgyzstan. It is the seventh-deepest lake in the world, the tenth-largest by volume, and the second-largest saline lake after the Caspian Sea. Although surrounded by snow-capped peaks, it never freezes, owing to its slight salinity and depth.

Geography

The lake is about 182 km long and up to 60 km wide, enclosed by the Küngöy Ala-Too range to the north and the Terskey Ala-Too to the south. Roughly 118 rivers and streams flow into it; it has no outlet. The towns of Karakol (at its eastern end) and Cholpon-Ata (on the northern shore) are its principal settlements.

History

The lake lay on a branch of the Silk Road; Chinese sources of the Han dynasty knew it as a "hot sea". In the Middle Ages it was associated with legends of submerged Armenian monasteries and treasures, and underwater archaeology has found remains of settlements along its shores, evidence that the water level was once much lower. During the Soviet era the northern shore became a resort area, and a closed naval test facility operated at the lake's eastern end.

Environment and tourism

The lake and its wetlands are a Ramsar site and the core of a UNESCO biosphere reserve, important for wintering waterfowl. Sandy beaches on the north shore make it Central Asia's leading domestic beach resort destination in summer.
