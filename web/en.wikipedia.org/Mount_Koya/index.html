Mount Koya

Mount Koya (Kōya-san) is a large temple settlement in Wakayama Prefecture, Japan, and the centre of Shingon Buddhism, founded in 816 by the monk Kūkai (Kōbō Daishi), who is believed by followers to rest in eternal meditation in the Okunoin mausoleum.

Sites

The settlement, set in a valley at about 800 m among eight peaks said to resemble a lotus, includes the Danjo Garan sacred precinct, the head temple Kongōbu-ji, and Okunoin, Japan's largest cemetery, where some 200,000 tombs line the approach through ancient cedars. Many of the town's temples offer lodging (shukubō) to pilgrims and visitors. Mount Koya is a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the Sacred Sites and Pilgrimage Routes in the Kii Mountain Range.
