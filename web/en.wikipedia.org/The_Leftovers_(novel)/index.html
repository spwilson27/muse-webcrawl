The Leftovers (novel)

The Leftovers
Author: Tom Perrotta
Language: English
Genre: Dystopian fiction
Publisher: St. Martin's Press
Publication date: August 30, 2011

The Leftovers is a 2011 novel by American author Tom Perrotta. It tells the story of the residents of the fictional town of Mapleton, three years after the "Sudden Departure," a rapture-like event in which 2% of the world's population inexplicably vanished at the same moment. Rather than focusing on the event itself, the novel examines how ordinary people — the "leftovers" — cope with grief, faith, and the collapse of meaning in its aftermath.

Plot summary

Kevin Garvey, Mapleton's mayor, tries to restore normal life while his own family has fractured: his wife Laurie has joined the Guilty Remnant, a silent, chain-smoking cult that stalks residents to remind them of the Departure; his son Tom has dropped out of college to follow a self-styled prophet called Holy Wayne; and his daughter Jill struggles with grief and rebellion. Nora Durst, who lost her husband and both children in the Departure, becomes the town's emblem of suffering. The novel follows these characters as they move, painfully, toward a fragile new equilibrium.

Themes

Perrotta explores grief, religious mania, eschatology, and suburban anomie. The Sudden Departure functions as a metaphor for sudden loss, and the novel contrasts the competing strategies of remembrance, denial, and reinvention adopted by its characters.

Reception and adaptation

The novel received warm reviews, with Stephen King calling it the best Twilight Zone episode never filmed. HBO adapted it as the television series The Leftovers (2014–2017), co-created by Perrotta and Damon Lindelof, which used the novel's material primarily in its first season before expanding beyond it to widespread critical acclaim.
