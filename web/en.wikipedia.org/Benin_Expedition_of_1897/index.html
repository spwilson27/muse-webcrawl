The Benin Expedition of 1897 was a British punitive expedition against the Kingdom of Benin, in present-day southern Nigeria. In February 1897 a force of about 1,200 British marines, sailors and Niger Coast Protectorate troops captured and burned Benin City, deposed and exiled the Oba (king), Ovonramwen, and seized several thousand royal artworks — the celebrated Benin Bronzes — which were dispersed to museums and collectors in Europe and America.

Background

Britain sought greater control over the palm-oil and rubber trade of the Niger Delta hinterland. Benin's Oba, however, maintained a royal monopoly and restricted trade. In January 1897 an unarmed British mission led by Acting Consul-General James Phillips set out for Benin City against the Oba's request to delay (a major ritual season was under way); the party was ambushed and almost all of its members killed — the "Benin massacre" that provided the pretext for retaliation.

The expedition

A naval force under Rear-Admiral Harry Rawson landed at the Benin River and advanced on the capital, reaching it on 18 February 1897 after fierce fighting. Benin City was looted and burned, and large areas, including the Oba's palace complex, were destroyed. Ovonramwen surrendered in August and was exiled to Calabar, where he died in 1914. The kingdom was absorbed into British colonial Nigeria, and the monarchy was restored only in 1914 under Eweka II.

The Benin Bronzes and restitution

The looted works — brass and bronze plaques, heads and sculptures, ivory carvings, some dating back centuries — were sold to defray the expedition's costs and entered collections such as the British Museum, the Ethnologisches Museum in Berlin, and the Metropolitan Museum of Art. They transformed Western appreciation of African art. Since the late twentieth century Nigeria has pressed for their return, and in the 2020s significant restitutions began: institutions in Germany, the United States, Britain and elsewhere transferred ownership of hundreds of bronzes to Nigeria, with further returns under negotiation.
