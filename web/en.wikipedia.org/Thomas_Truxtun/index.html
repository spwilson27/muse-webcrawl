Commodore Thomas Truxtun

Thomas Truxtun (17 February 1755 – 5 May 1822) was an American naval officer, privateer and merchant captain. He achieved fame as commander of the frigate USS Constellation during the Quasi-War with France, winning two celebrated single-ship victories.

Early life and privateering

Truxtun went to sea at age twelve and served in the Royal Navy and the merchant service. During the American Revolutionary War he commanded several privateers and letter-of-marque vessels, capturing numerous British prizes. After the war he traded to China and became a prominent Philadelphia merchant and navigational writer, publishing Remarks, Instructions and Examples Relating to Latitude and Longitude (1794).

Naval service

When the United States Navy was re-established in 1794, Truxtun was appointed one of its first six captains and supervised the construction of Constellation at Baltimore. During the Quasi-War he captured the French frigate L'Insurgente on 9 February 1799, the first major American naval victory over a French warship. On 1 February 1800 he fought the heavier French frigate La Vengeance in a hard-fought night action; though La Vengeance escaped, Truxtun was hailed as a hero and received a gold medal from Congress.

Later life

Truxtun resigned his commission in 1802 after a dispute over seniority with Richard Dale and the assignment of a flag captain. He later held civic posts in Philadelphia, including sheriff of Philadelphia County. Two U.S. Navy destroyers and the cruiser USS Truxtun (CGN-35) have borne his name.
