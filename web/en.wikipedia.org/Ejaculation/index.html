Ejaculation

Anatomy of the male reproductive system

Ejaculation is the discharge of semen (the ejaculate, normally containing sperm) from the male reproductive tract. It is the final stage of, and a natural objective of, male sexual stimulation, and an essential component of natural conception. Ejaculation usually accompanies orgasm, though the two are distinct physiological events.

Physiology

The process occurs in two phases. In the emission phase, the vas deferens, seminal vesicles, and prostate gland contract to move semen into the posterior urethra, while the bladder neck closes to prevent retrograde flow. In the expulsion phase, rhythmic contractions of the bulbospongiosus and pelvic floor muscles expel the semen through the urethra. The process is controlled by a spinal reflex coordinated by the sympathetic nervous system and a spinal ejaculatory generator in the lumbar cord.

Variations and disorders

Ejaculation first becomes possible at puberty. Nocturnal emissions ("wet dreams") are a normal occurrence. Disorders include premature ejaculation, delayed ejaculation, anejaculation, and retrograde ejaculation, in which semen enters the bladder instead; these may have neurological, pharmacological, or psychological causes and are studied within sexual medicine and urology.
