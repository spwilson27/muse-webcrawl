Court system of Canada

The court system of Canada is the system of courts that interprets and applies Canadian law. At its apex is the Supreme Court of Canada. Below it are federal courts and provincial and territorial courts.

Structure

Provincial superior courts handle serious civil and criminal matters; provincial courts handle most criminal, family and small claims cases. The Federal Court and Federal Court of Appeal deal with federal matters such as immigration, intellectual property and maritime law. Military courts and tax court are specialised tribunals.

Supreme Court

Established in 1875, the Supreme Court has nine justices appointed by the governor general on advice of the prime minister. It is the final court of appeal and hears constitutional references.
