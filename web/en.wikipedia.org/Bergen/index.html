Bergen

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Bergen (Norwegian pronunciation: [ˈbæ̀rɡn̩]) is a city and municipality in Vestland county on the west coast of Norway. As of 2022, its population was roughly 290,000, making it the second-most populous city in Norway after the capital, Oslo. The city centre and northern neighbourhoods are located on Byfjorden, "the city fjord", and the city is surrounded by mountains — Bergen is known as "the city of seven mountains".

History

Bergen is traditionally thought to have been founded in 1070 by King Olaf III, though research in the 21st century suggests the trading settlement may have originated earlier. In the 13th century Bergen was Norway's capital and its most important port. From the late 13th century, the German Hanseatic League established one of its four foreign offices (Kontore) at Bryggen, dominating the trade in stockfish from northern Norway for centuries. Bryggen's Hanseatic wharf is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Geography and economy

Bergen is a centre for shipping, offshore petroleum, aquaculture and tourism, and is a gateway to the fjords of Norway. Despite its northern latitude it has a relatively mild climate, but it is famously rainy, with around 2,250 mm of annual precipitation. The University of Bergen and the Norwegian School of Economics are based in the city, and composer Edvard Grieg's home, Troldhaugen, is a popular attraction.
