Localization

Localization (often abbreviated L10n) is the process of adapting a product, service, document or software application for a specific region or language — a locale — by translating text and adjusting cultural references, formats and conventions. It goes beyond literal translation to encompass date and number formats, currencies, units of measurement, colours and imagery, legal requirements, keyboard layouts and even product names.

Localization versus internationalization

Localization is the second step of a two-part process. Internationalization (i18n) is the design and engineering work that makes a product adaptable in the first place — for example, separating translatable text from program code, supporting Unicode and bidirectional text, and avoiding hard-coded formats. Localization then produces each locale-specific version. The abbreviation L10n counts the letters between the initial "l" and final "n" of "localization".

Scope

The language industry localises software, websites, video games, films and television (through dubbing and subtitling), marketing materials and technical documentation. In video games, localization may involve re-recording dialogue, altering graphics containing text, and sometimes censoring or changing content to comply with regional laws and sensibilities. Translation management systems, translation memories and glossaries are used to keep terminology consistent across versions.

Economics

Effective localization is a major driver of international sales: users strongly prefer products in their own language, and poor localization can cause offence or legal liability. At the same time each additional locale adds cost, leading companies to prioritise markets by expected return.
