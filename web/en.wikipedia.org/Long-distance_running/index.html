Long-distance running

Long-distance running, or endurance running, is a form of continuous running over distances of at least 3 kilometres (1.9 mi). Physiologically, it is largely an aerobic activity and requires stamina as well as mental strength. In competitive athletics, long-distance track events are those of 3,000 metres and above, while on the roads the best-known distances include the 10K, half marathon and marathon.

Competitive events

Standard long-distance events at the Olympic Games and World Athletics Championships include the 5000 metres, 10,000 metres and the marathon (42.195 km), along with the 3000 metres steeplechase. Road running has its own championships and elite circuits, such as the World Marathon Majors in Tokyo, Boston, London, Berlin, Chicago and New York City. Longer distances fall under ultrarunning, and cross country running and trail running are closely related disciplines.

Physiology and training

Performance depends mainly on maximal oxygen uptake (VO2 max), lactate threshold and running economy. Training typically combines high volumes of easy mileage with long runs, tempo sessions and interval work, alongside tapering before major races. East African runners, particularly from Kenya and Ethiopia, have dominated elite distance running since the 1960s, a success variously attributed to altitude, diet, culture and running economy.

Health

Recreational distance running is associated with improved cardiovascular fitness and reduced risk of many chronic diseases, though it carries risks of overuse injury such as stress fractures, tendinopathy and runner's knee.
