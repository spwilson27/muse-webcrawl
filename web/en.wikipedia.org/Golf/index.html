Golf

Golf is a club-and-ball sport in which players use various clubs to hit a ball into a series of holes on a course in as few strokes as possible. Most courses have 18 holes. Unlike most ball games, golf is played without a standardised playing area and without direct opposition interference; players compete against the course and their own scores.

History

The modern game originated in 15th-century Scotland; the earliest known written reference is an Act of the Scottish Parliament of 1457 banning golf and football as distractions from archery practice. The Old Course at St Andrews, founded in 1552, is regarded as the game's spiritual home. The rules were first drawn up in Edinburgh in 1744. Golf spread through the British Empire and the United States in the 19th century, and the standards of 18 holes and the modern equipment evolved thereafter.

Play and organisation

The two principal formats are stroke play, counted by total strokes, and match play, counted by holes won. The game is governed internationally by The R&A and the USGA, which maintain the Rules of Golf and equipment standards. Professional golf centres on tours such as the PGA Tour and DP World Tour and four men's major championships — the Masters, PGA Championship, U.S. Open, and The Open Championship — and five women's majors. Golf returned to the Olympic Games in 2016.
