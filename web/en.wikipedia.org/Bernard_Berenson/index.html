Bernard Berenson

Bernard Berenson (June 26, 1865 – October 6, 1959) was an American art historian specialising in the Italian Renaissance. Born Bernhard Valvrojenski in Butrimonys, Lithuania, then part of the Russian Empire, he emigrated with his family to Boston as a child. Berenson became the most influential connoisseur of early Italian painting of his generation: his attributions shaped the collections of major American museums and the art market alike.

Career

Educated at Harvard, Berenson travelled in Europe before settling in Italy, where, with his wife Mary, he developed a method of attribution combining close visual scrutiny — of ears, hands, drapery, and landscape details — with archival research, influenced by the method of Giovanni Morelli. His books, including The Venetian Painters of the Renaissance (1894), Lorenzo Lotto (1895), The Florentine Painters of the Renaissance (1896), and The Central Italian Painters of the Renaissance (1897), established his reputation, and his lists of works by the old masters became standard references.

Berenson advised collectors such as Isabella Stewart Gardner and worked for many years with the dealer Joseph Duveen, an arrangement that made him wealthy but also drew criticism, since his certificates of authenticity carried great commercial weight. Some of his attributions have since been revised by later scholarship.

Villa I Tatti

From 1900 Berenson lived at the Villa I Tatti outside Florence, building a celebrated library and photographic archive of Renaissance art. He bequeathed the villa, library, and collection to Harvard University, which administers it as the Harvard University Center for Italian Renaissance Studies.
