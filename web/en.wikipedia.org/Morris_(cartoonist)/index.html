Morris (cartoonist)

Morris

Born: Maurice De Bevere, 1 December 1923, Kortrijk, Belgium
Died: 16 July 2001, Brussels, Belgium
Nationality: Belgian
Known for: Lucky Luke

Morris, the pen name of Maurice De Bevere (1 December 1923 – 16 July 2001), was a Belgian comics artist widely regarded as one of the masters of Franco-Belgian comics. He is best known as the creator of the long-running Western series Lucky Luke, featuring "the man who shoots faster than his own shadow".

Career

Born in Kortrijk, Morris began his career in animation before joining the magazine Spirou, where Lucky Luke debuted in 1946. In the late 1940s he travelled to the United States with fellow artists Jijé and André Franquin, absorbing American Western culture and comics. From 1955, writer René Goscinny scripted Lucky Luke, and their partnership produced the series' classic period, introducing recurring villains such as the Dalton brothers—whom Morris had originally modelled on the historical Dalton Gang—and the talking horse Jolly Jumper and dog Rantanplan.

Style and legacy

Morris's dynamic, caricatural drawing style was influenced by American animation and by Milton Caniff. After Goscinny's death in 1977, Morris worked with other writers but continued drawing Lucky Luke until his own death in 2001, following a fall. The series has sold hundreds of millions of albums worldwide and has been adapted into films and animated series. Morris received numerous honours, including the Grand Prix of the Angoulême International Comics Festival in 1992.
