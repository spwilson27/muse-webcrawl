The Germany national football team represents Germany in men's international football and is governed by the German Football Association. It is one of the most successful national teams in history, having won the FIFA World Cup four times and the UEFA European Championship three times.

History

Germany played its first official match in 1908. After the Second World War the country was represented by separate West and East German teams; the West German side won the 1954 World Cup in Bern — the "Miracle of Bern" — and again as hosts in 1974. Following reunification in 1990, the unified team continued the West German record. Franz Beckenbauer, who captained the 1974 side and coached the 1990 winners, is one of only a few men to win the World Cup as both player and manager.

Recent era

Under Joachim Löw, Germany reached at least the semi-finals of every major tournament from 2006 to 2016, culminating in the 2014 World Cup in Brazil, where a 7–1 semi-final defeat of the hosts preceded victory over Argentina in the final, Mario Götze scoring the extra-time winner. The team then suffered group-stage exits at the 2018 and 2022 World Cups. Julian Nagelsmann became head coach in 2023, leading Germany at the home Euro 2024.

Records

Miroslav Klose is the team's all-time top scorer with 71 goals and also holds the World Cup scoring record with 16. Lothar Matthäus is the most-capped player, with 150 appearances.
