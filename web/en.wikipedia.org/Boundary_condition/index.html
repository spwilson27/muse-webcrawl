Boundary condition

In the study of differential equations, a boundary condition is a constraint that a solution must satisfy on the boundary of its domain. Together with the equation itself, boundary conditions form a boundary value problem, which arises throughout physics and engineering — in heat conduction, fluid flow, electromagnetism, structural mechanics, and quantum mechanics. A well-posed problem requires enough conditions (and of the right type) for a unique solution to exist.

Types

  • Dirichlet boundary condition: specifies the value of the solution itself on the boundary (e.g., a fixed temperature).
  • Neumann boundary condition: specifies the normal derivative of the solution (e.g., heat flux through a surface).
  • Robin (mixed) boundary condition: specifies a combination of the value and its derivative, modelling, for example, convective heat exchange.
  • Cauchy boundary condition: specifies both the value and the derivative, as in some wave problems.
  • Periodic boundary condition: requires the solution to match at opposite ends of the domain, common in simulations of extended or cyclic systems.

Boundary vs. initial conditions

Boundary conditions constrain a solution in space, while initial conditions constrain it in time; time-dependent problems in a bounded region typically need both. Solution methods include separation of variables, Green's functions, and numerical schemes such as the finite element and finite difference methods, in which boundary conditions are embedded directly into the discrete equations.
