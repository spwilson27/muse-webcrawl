Conditional mood

The conditional mood (abbreviated cond) is a grammatical mood used in statements such as English "I would go" or French j'irais, in which the action or state is seen as being dependent upon a condition, mostly counterfactual (hypothetical). The conditional mood is found in many languages and is often used in the main clause of a conditional sentence.

Conditional forms in several Indo-European languages

Formation

In Romance languages, the conditional is an inflected verb form historically derived from the infinitive plus forms of the verb "to have" — the same origin as the future tense. In English, the conditional is expressed periphrastically with the modal auxiliary would (historically the past tense of will), as in "she would travel". German uses würde plus the infinitive or the subjunctive II forms. Finnish and Turkish possess dedicated conditional suffixes.

Uses

  • Hypothetical consequences — "If you had asked, I would have come."
  • Politeness — "Would you pass the salt?"
  • Future in the past in reported speech — "He said he would come."
  • Wishes and suppositions — "I would love to see it."

Conditional sentences

In conditional sentences the conditional mood typically appears in the main clause (apodosis), while the subordinate clause (protasis) uses the subjunctive or a past-tense form: "If I were rich, I would travel." Linguists debate whether the conditional is a mood in its own right or a tense-mood combination, since in many languages it also serves as a future-in-the-past tense.
