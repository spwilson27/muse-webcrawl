Dysphemism

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A dysphemism is an expression with connotations that are derogatory, offensive, or disparaging about the subject, where the speaker or writer intends the expression to be seen as such. It is the opposite of a euphemism, which substitutes an agreeable or inoffensive expression for one that might offend. Using a rough or coarse word where a neutral one exists — for example, calling a politician a "windbag" or referring to junk food as "grease bombs" — is dysphemistic.

Usage

Dysphemisms are used for comic effect, for shock value, to express anger or contempt, or as loaded language in political argument — for instance, describing the same policy as "tax relief" or as a "handout to the rich" depending on one's stance. Many dysphemisms begin as neutral terms that acquire negative connotations over time; conversely, euphemisms can become soiled by association with their referent, a process called the "euphemism treadmill".

Categories

Common types include synecdoche-based reductions (referring to a person by a single trait), animal or disease metaphors, profanity and terms drawn from taboo subjects, and derogatory nicknames or ethnophaulisms directed at groups.
