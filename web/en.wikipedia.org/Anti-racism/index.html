Anti-racism

Anti-racism encompasses a range of ideas and political actions that aim to counter racial prejudice, systemic racism, and the oppression of specific racial groups. Anti-racism promotes changes in personal beliefs as well as public policies, and includes active opposition to racism rather than merely the absence of racial prejudice.

History

Anti-racist activism has roots in abolitionism, the movements against colonialism, and the American civil rights movement. In the postwar period, anti-racism informed struggles against apartheid in South Africa and segregation in the United States. Scholars such as Franz Boas challenged scientific racism in the early 20th century, and UNESCO issued statements on race beginning in 1950 rejecting biological hierarchies of races.

Approaches

Modern anti-racism includes anti-racist education, affirmative action and diversity policies, and efforts to address structural inequality in housing, employment, and criminal justice. Ibram X. Kendi's 2019 book How to Be an Antiracist popularized the view that policies are either racist or antiracist in their effects, and that neutrality is not a meaningful position. Anti-racist organizations range from community groups to bodies such as the Anti-Defamation League and the Southern Poverty Law Center.

Criticism

Critics of particular anti-racist frameworks argue that some approaches place excessive emphasis on race or are ineffective at reducing disparities, while supporters maintain that explicit attention to race is necessary to remedy entrenched inequality.
