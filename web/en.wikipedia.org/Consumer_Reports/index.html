Consumer Reports

Consumer Reports tests products in its own laboratories in Yonkers, New York.

Consumer Reports (CR) is an American nonprofit consumer organisation dedicated to independent product testing, investigative journalism, and consumer advocacy. Founded in 1936 as Consumers Union, it publishes the magazine Consumer Reports and a widely used website rating thousands of products and services, from cars and appliances to food and financial services.

Principles and testing

Consumer Reports accepts no advertising and buys every product it tests anonymously at retail, in order to remain independent of manufacturers. Its main testing centre in Yonkers, New York, operates dozens of specialised laboratories, and it maintains an automobile test track in Connecticut. Ratings are based on standardised laboratory tests and large reader surveys of reliability and satisfaction.

History and influence

The organisation was founded after the collapse of an earlier testing group, Consumers' Research, amid a labour dispute; its first magazine appeared in May 1936. Its reporting has influenced major public policy, including automobile safety (its criticism of unsafe cars preceded federal safety standards), the 1964 Surgeon General's report on smoking, and recalls of defective products. It campaigned for the creation of the United States Consumer Product Safety Commission and remains active on issues such as data privacy, food safety, and healthcare costs.

The organisation's name was shortened from Consumers Union to Consumer Reports in 2016. Its magazine has millions of subscribers, and its ratings strongly influence manufacturers' design decisions.
