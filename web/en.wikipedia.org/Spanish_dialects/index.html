Spanish dialects and varieties

The Spanish dialects and varieties are the regional spoken forms of the Spanish language used throughout the Spanish-speaking world. Despite wide geographic spread, Spanish dialects remain mutually intelligible, but they differ noticeably in pronunciation, vocabulary and some points of grammar, notably the use of vos instead of tú in much of Latin America (voseo) and the retention of vosotros in most of Spain.

Peninsular Spanish

In Spain the major division is between northern varieties (Castilian proper), which traditionally distinguish the sounds /s/ and /θ/ (spelled c before e/i and z) — a feature called distinción — and southern varieties, including Andalusian and Canarian Spanish, which merge them (seseo or ceceo). Andalusian Spanish is noted for weakening syllable-final consonants and aspirating or dropping final /s/. Peninsular varieties also use vosotros for the informal plural "you".

Latin American Spanish

All Latin American varieties practise seseo. Broad groupings include Mexican and Central American Spanish; Caribbean Spanish (Cuba, Puerto Rico, coastal Venezuela and Colombia), marked by strong weakening of syllable-final /s/; Andean Spanish, influenced by Quechua and Aymara; Chilean Spanish, with rapid speech and heavy aspiration; and Rioplatense Spanish (Argentina and Uruguay), famous for voseo and for the pronunciation of y/ll as [ʃ] or [ʒ] (sheísmo/zheísmo). Philippine Spanish, now nearly extinct, preserved some archaic features; Equatoguinean Spanish is the main African variety.

Vocabulary and grammar differences

Everyday words vary widely: a car is coche in Spain but carro or auto in America; a computer is an ordenador in Spain and a computadora in America. The preterite and present perfect are used differently — in much of America the preterite serves for recent past events where Spain would use the perfect. Standard written Spanish, overseen by the Association of Spanish Language Academies, remains broadly uniform across regions.
