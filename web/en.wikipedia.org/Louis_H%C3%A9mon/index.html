Louis Hémon

Louis Hémon (12 October 1880 – 8 July 1913) was a French writer best known for the novel Maria Chapdelaine, a classic of Quebec literature set among settler families in the Saguenay–Lac-Saint-Jean region of Canada. He wrote the novel after working on a farm near Péribonka in 1912.

Life

Born in Brest, Brittany, Hémon studied law in Paris before moving to London, where he lived from 1903, working as a sports journalist and writing fiction in his spare time. Restless, he emigrated to Canada in 1911, worked on farms in Quebec, and in 1912 settled for a time with the Bédard family near Péribonka on Lake Saint-Jean, gathering the material for Maria Chapdelaine. He was killed by a train near Chapleau, Ontario, in July 1913 while travelling west.

Maria Chapdelaine

The novel, published in serial form in Paris in 1914 and as a book in Montreal in 1916, follows a young habitant woman courted by three suitors representing three destinies: emigration to the United States, the adventurous life of the woods, and the hard, rooted life of clearing land. Its portrayal of pioneer endurance and French-Canadian traditions made it an international success, translated into many languages and adapted several times for film, though later generations of Quebec writers sometimes treated it as a symbol of the conservative "roman du terroir" (novel of the soil) tradition they sought to escape.
