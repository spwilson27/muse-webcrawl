Boston is the capital and most populous city of the U.S. state of Massachusetts, and the county seat of Suffolk County. Founded in 1630 by Puritan settlers from Boston, Lincolnshire, it is one of the oldest municipalities in the United States and was the scene of several key events of the American Revolution, including the Boston Massacre, the Boston Tea Party, and the Battle of Bunker Hill.

History

Boston was founded on the Shawmut Peninsula in 1630 by Puritan settlers from England. During the American Revolutionary War, it was the site of the Siege of Boston (1775–1776). After independence, the city became a major port and manufacturing centre, and a centre of education and culture.

Education and culture

The Greater Boston area is home to more than 100 colleges and universities, including Harvard University and the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in neighbouring Cambridge. The city is renowned for medicine, higher education, and professional sports teams including the Red Sox, Celtics, Bruins, and Patriots (based in Foxborough). The Boston Marathon, first run in 1897, is the world's oldest annual marathon.

Geography

Boston is located on Massachusetts Bay, at the mouth of the Charles River. Much of the city, including Back Bay and South Boston, is built on landfill. Its distinctive neighbourhoods include Beacon Hill, the North End, Charlestown, Dorchester, Roxbury, and Jamaica Plain.
