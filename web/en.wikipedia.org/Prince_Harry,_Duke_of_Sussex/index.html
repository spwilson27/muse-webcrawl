Prince Harry, Duke of Sussex

Prince Harry, Duke of Sussex (Henry Charles Albert David; born 15 September 1984), is a member of the British royal family. He is the younger son of King Charles III and Diana, Princess of Wales, and is fifth in the line of succession to the British throne.

Early life and education

Harry was born in the Lindo Wing of St Mary's Hospital in Paddington, London, the second child of the then Prince of Wales and Diana, Princess of Wales. He was educated at Wetherby School, Ludgrove School, and Eton College. His mother died in a car crash in Paris in 1997, when he was twelve. After school he took a gap year in Australia and Lesotho, then entered the Royal Military Academy Sandhurst.

Military service

Commissioned into the Blues and Royals, Harry served two tours of duty in Afghanistan: in 2007–08 as a forward air controller in Helmand Province (cut short when his presence was publicised), and in 2012–13 as an Apache attack helicopter co-pilot with the Army Air Corps. He left the army in 2015 with the rank of captain.

Charitable work

In 2014 he founded the Invictus Games, an international sporting event for wounded, injured and sick servicemen and women. He co-founded the charity Sentebale in Lesotho in 2006 to support children affected by HIV/AIDS, and has campaigned on mental health awareness, including through the Heads Together initiative.

Marriage and step back from royal duties

Harry married American actress Meghan Markle at St George's Chapel, Windsor Castle, on 19 May 2018, and was created Duke of Sussex shortly before the wedding. In January 2020 the couple announced their intention to step back as senior working members of the royal family; they subsequently settled in California, where they founded the Archewell organisation. His memoir, Spare, was published in January 2023.
