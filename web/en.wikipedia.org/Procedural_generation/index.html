Procedural generation

In computing, procedural generation (sometimes shortened to procgen) is a method of creating data algorithmically as opposed to manually, typically through a combination of human-generated content and algorithms coupled with computer-generated randomness and processing power. It is most commonly used in video games to generate levels, terrain, items, and quests, but also in film, music, and simulation.

History

Early examples include the roguelike games of the 1980s, such as Rogue (1980), which generated dungeons randomly each playthrough, and Elite (1984), which generated a galaxy of star systems from a seed. The demoscene and titles such as .kkrieger (2004) demonstrated generating entire games in tiny file sizes.

Modern use

Games such as Minecraft use noise functions (notably Perlin and simplex noise) to generate vast terrain, while No Man's Sky (2016) famously generated a universe of over 18 quintillion planets. Techniques include L-systems for vegetation, cellular automata for caves, wave function collapse for layouts, and grammar-based generation. Procedural generation trades authorial control for scale and replayability.
