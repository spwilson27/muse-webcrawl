Snow leopard

A snow leopard in its mountain habitat

The snow leopard (Panthera uncia), also known as the ounce, is a species of large cat in the genus Panthera native to the mountain ranges of Central and South Asia. It is listed as Vulnerable on the IUCN Red List, with a wild population estimated at roughly 4,000–6,500 individuals. Snow leopards live in alpine and subalpine zones at elevations of about 3,000–4,500 m, from the Hindu Kush and Himalayas to the Altai and the mountains of Siberia.

Adaptations

The snow leopard's smoky-grey, rosette-patterned coat provides camouflage among rocks, and its dense fur, small rounded ears, wide fur-covered paws and stocky build suit it to extreme cold and steep terrain. Its exceptionally long, thick tail aids balance and can be wrapped around the body for warmth. Unlike the other big cats it cannot roar, lacking a fully ossified hyoid structure suited to it; it communicates by chuffing, growling and mewing.

Behaviour and conservation

Snow leopards are solitary, crepuscular hunters of wild sheep and goats such as bharal and ibex, as well as marmots and other smaller prey. They are threatened by poaching for fur and bones, retaliatory killing by herders after livestock depredation, and the decline of wild prey and habitat under climate change. Conservation programmes work with local communities on predator-proof corrals and livestock insurance, and the species is the national animal of Afghanistan and a state symbol in parts of Central Asia.
