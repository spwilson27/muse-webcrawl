Japan–Russia relations

Japan–Russia relations are the bilateral relations between Japan and the Russian Federation. They are complicated by the unresolved Kuril Islands dispute, which has prevented the two countries from signing a formal peace treaty since the Second World War.

History

Contacts began in the 18th century as Russian explorers and traders reached Japan's northern frontier. The Treaty of Shimoda (1855) established formal relations and a border in the Kurils; the Treaty of Saint Petersburg (1875) gave Japan the whole Kuril chain in exchange for Sakhalin. Rivalry over Korea and Manchuria led to the Russo-Japanese War of 1904–05, which Japan won — the first victory of an Asian power over a European great power in modern warfare. Japan intervened in Siberia during the Russian Civil War. The Soviet–Japanese Neutrality Pact of 1941 held until August 1945, when the USSR declared war and seized southern Sakhalin and the Kuril Islands, expelling the Japanese population.

The Kuril dispute

In the 1956 Joint Declaration the USSR agreed to transfer the Habomai islets and Shikotan to Japan after a peace treaty, but no treaty followed. Japan claims four islands — Etorofu (Iturup), Kunashiri, Shikotan and the Habomai group — as its Northern Territories. Negotiations continued intermittently, including talks between Prime Minister Shinzo Abe and President Vladimir Putin, but stalled after Japan joined sanctions against Russia following the 2022 invasion of Ukraine, after which Russia suspended peace-treaty talks.

Trade

Trade centres on Russian energy exports, including liquefied natural gas from Sakhalin, and Japanese machinery and vehicles, though volumes declined under sanctions.
