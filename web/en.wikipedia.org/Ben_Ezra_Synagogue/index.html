Interior of the Ben Ezra Synagogue in Old Cairo.

The Ben Ezra Synagogue (Hebrew: בית כנסת בן עזרא) in Fustat, now part of Old Cairo, Egypt, is one of the oldest and most famous synagogues in the Jewish world. According to local tradition the site is where the infant Moses was found in the reeds, and the building—originally a Coptic church sold to the Jewish community in the late 9th century—is associated with the scholar Abraham ibn Ezra.

The Cairo Geniza

The synagogue is renowned for its geniza, a storeroom where worn-out sacred texts and communal documents were deposited for centuries. The Cairo Geniza preserved some 300,000 manuscript fragments, brought to scholarly attention in 1896–97 by Solomon Schechter, who transported the bulk to Cambridge University. The documents illuminate a millennium of Jewish and Mediterranean life, commerce and scholarship, including letters of Maimonides, who worshipped in Fustat.

Today

Rebuilt in the 1890s in a basilica-like form and restored in the 1980s–90s, the synagogue now functions mainly as a museum and tourist site, with a small caretaker community.
