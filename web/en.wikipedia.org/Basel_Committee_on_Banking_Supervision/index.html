Basel Committee on Banking Supervision

Founded1974
HeadquartersBasel, Switzerland (at the BIS)
PurposeInternational banking regulation and supervision standards

The Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS) is a committee of banking supervisory authorities established by the central bank governors of the Group of Ten countries in 1974, following disturbances in international currency and banking markets, notably the failure of Bankhaus Herstatt. It provides a forum for regular cooperation on banking supervisory matters and sets global standards for the prudential regulation of banks.

The Committee's best-known outputs are the Basel Accords: Basel I (1988), which introduced minimum capital requirements; Basel II (2004), which refined risk measurement; and Basel III, developed in response to the financial crisis of 2007–2009, which strengthened capital and liquidity standards. The Committee's standards are not legally binding but are implemented by member jurisdictions through national law. Its secretariat is hosted by the Bank for International Settlements in Basel, Switzerland.
