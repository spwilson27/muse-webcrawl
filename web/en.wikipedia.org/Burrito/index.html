A burrito (Spanish: [buˈrito], lit. "little donkey") is a dish in Mexican and Tex-Mex cuisine that consists of a flour tortilla wrapped into a sealed cylindrical shape around various fillings. The tortilla is sometimes lightly grilled or steamed to soften it, make it more pliable, and allow it to adhere to itself. In Mexico, burritos are traditionally filled only with meat and refried beans, while in the United States they typically include a combination of ingredients such as Mexican-style rice, beans, lettuce, salsa, meat, guacamole, cheese and sour cream.

Etymology

The word burrito means "little donkey" in Spanish, the diminutive of burro ("donkey"). Why the dish acquired this name is not certain; popular explanations include a resemblance to a donkey's ears, to the packs and bedrolls donkeys carried, or an association with a particular street vendor whose food was transported by donkey. The term appears in print by the early 20th century in connection with the border region of northern Mexico.

History

The burrito's origins lie in the cuisine of northern Mexico, particularly the state of Chihuahua and the border city of Ciudad Juárez, where wheat flour tortillas are common. It spread into the southwestern United States, where regional styles developed: the Mission burrito of San Francisco, known for its large size and inclusion of rice; the smothered burrito of Colorado and New Mexico, covered in chile sauce and cheese; and the breakfast burrito of New Mexico, filled with eggs, potatoes and chile. Korean-Mexican fusion versions, such as those popularised in Los Angeles food trucks, added ingredients like kimchi and bulgogi.

Preparation

A large flour tortilla is warmed, fillings are arranged in the centre, and the sides are folded in before the tortilla is rolled tightly around them. Burritos may be served "wet" — smothered in sauce — or grilled (sometimes called chimichanga when deep-fried). Common fillings include carne asada, carnitas, chicken, al pastor, chorizo, beans, rice, cheese, pico de gallo, guacamole and salsa.

Variations

  • Breakfast burrito – filled with eggs, potatoes, cheese and breakfast meats.
  • Chimichanga – a deep-fried burrito, popular in Arizona and Sonora.
  • Mission burrito – an extra-large burrito from San Francisco's Mission District, including rice and wrapped in foil.
  • Smothered burrito – covered with chile sauce and melted cheese, eaten with knife and fork.
