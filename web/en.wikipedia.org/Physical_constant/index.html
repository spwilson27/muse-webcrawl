Physical constant

A physical constant, sometimes called a fundamental physical constant or universal constant, is a physical quantity that is generally believed to be both universal in nature and constant in time. It is distinct from a mathematical constant, which is a fixed numerical value but does not directly involve any physical measurement.

Examples include the speed of light in vacuum (c), the gravitational constant (G), the Planck constant (h), the electric constant (ε0), and the elementary charge (e). Some constants, such as the fine-structure constant (α), are dimensionless.

SI redefinition

Since the 2019 redefinition of the SI base units, the SI is defined by fixing the numerical values of seven constants, including the caesium hyperfine frequency, the speed of light, the Planck constant, the elementary charge, the Boltzmann constant, the Avogadro constant, and the luminous efficacy of monochromatic radiation.
