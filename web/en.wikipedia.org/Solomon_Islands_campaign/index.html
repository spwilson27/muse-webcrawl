Solomon Islands campaign

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


US Marines on Guadalcanal

The Solomon Islands campaign was a major campaign of the Pacific War of World War II. The campaign began with Japanese landings and occupation of several areas in the British Solomon Islands and Bougainville, in the Territory of New Guinea, during the first six months of 1942. The Allies countered with the landings on Guadalcanal in August 1942, the first major Allied offensive of the Pacific War.

Course of the campaign

Fighting centred on Guadalcanal (1942–43), where American forces seized the airfield (Henderson Field) and withstood Japanese counterattacks in battles on land, at sea (including the Naval Battle of Guadalcanal), and in the air. The Allies then advanced up the Solomons chain — New Georgia, Bougainville, and other islands — under the strategy of bypassing strongpoints such as Rabaul, which was neutralised rather than captured.

Significance

The campaign marked the turning of the tide in the Pacific alongside Midway and the New Guinea campaigns: Japan lost irreplaceable ships, aircraft, and trained aircrews, while Allied material superiority grew overwhelming. The campaign ended in 1945 with Australian forces still fighting on Bougainville and New Britain.
