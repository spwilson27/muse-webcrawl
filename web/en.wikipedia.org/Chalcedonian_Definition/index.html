Chalcedonian Definition

The Chalcedonian Definition (also called the Chalcedonian Creed) is the statement of faith produced by the Council of Chalcedon in AD 451. It declares that Jesus Christ is one person (hypostasis) in two natures, divine and human, united "without confusion, change, division or separation".


The Acts of Chalcedon

Content

The Definition affirms Christ as "perfect in Godhead and also perfect in manhood; truly God and truly man", consubstantial with the Father in divinity and with humanity in manhood, born of the Virgin Mary, the Theotokos. Its four adverbs — inconfusedly, unchangeably, indivisibly, inseparably — were aimed simultaneously at Nestorian division of Christ into two persons and at the Eutychian (Monophysite) blending of the natures into one.

Reception

The Definition remains the standard Christological confession of the Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox and most Protestant churches, collectively called Chalcedonian. The Oriental Orthodox churches rejected it, judging it too Nestorian, and accepted instead the miaphysite formula of Cyril of Alexandria — a schism that persists despite modern agreed statements on Christology.
