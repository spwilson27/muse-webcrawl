Punic Wars

The Punic Wars were a series of wars between 264 and 146 BC fought between the Roman Republic and Carthage. Three wars took place, on land and at sea, over control of the western Mediterranean. The term Punic comes from the Latin Punicus — "Carthaginian," referring to the Carthaginians' Phoenician ancestry. At the end of the Third Punic War, Rome destroyed Carthage and emerged as the dominant power of the Mediterranean.

First Punic War (264–241 BC)

Beginning over the Sicilian city of Messana, the first war was largely naval. Rome, previously a land power, built a fleet using a captured Carthaginian quinquereme as a model and the corvus boarding device. Roman victory at the Battle of the Aegates Islands (241 BC) forced Carthage to cede Sicily and pay a large indemnity; Rome soon also took Sardinia and Corsica.

Second Punic War (218–201 BC)

The Barcid family built a Carthaginian empire in Spain; Hannibal's attack on Saguntum triggered the second war. Hannibal famously crossed the Pyrenees and the Alps with war elephants and inflicted catastrophic defeats on Rome at Trebia, Lake Trasimene, and Cannae (216 BC). Rome avoided further pitched battle and wore Carthage down; Scipio Africanus conquered Spain and defeated Hannibal at Zama (202 BC), ending the war on Roman terms.

Third Punic War (149–146 BC)

Pressed by Rome's ally Numidia and urged on by Cato the Elder's refrain that "Carthage must be destroyed," Rome declared war in 149 BC. After a long siege, Scipio Aemilianus stormed and systematically destroyed the city in 146 BC; its territory became the Roman province of Africa.
