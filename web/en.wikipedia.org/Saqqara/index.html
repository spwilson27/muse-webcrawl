Saqqara (Arabic: سقارة) is an Egyptian village in the Giza Governorate, about 30 km (19 mi) south of Cairo, which contains a vast ancient burial ground of Egyptian royalty and nobles. The necropolis served the ancient Egyptian capital of Memphis and was in use from the Early Dynastic Period (c. 3100 BC) through the Ptolemaic and Roman periods. Its most famous monument is the Step Pyramid of Djoser, the oldest large-scale cut-stone construction in Egypt.

The Step Pyramid of Djoser, built in the 27th century BC.

Monuments

The Step Pyramid complex was designed by the architect Imhotep for Pharaoh Djoser of the Third Dynasty (c. 2670 BC), rising in six steps to a height of about 62 metres. Saqqara also contains numerous other royal pyramids, including those of Userkaf, Unas — whose burial chamber bears the earliest Pyramid Texts — Teti, Pepi I, and Pepi II, as well as hundreds of mastaba tombs of officials. The Serapeum, a long subterranean gallery, held the burials of the sacred Apis bulls.

Discoveries

Excavations at Saqqara continue to yield major finds. In recent years Egyptian missions have announced the discovery of large caches of sealed coffins and bronze statues from the Late Period, a vast catacomb containing animal mummies, and the tomb of the Fifth Dynasty official Wahtye, notable for its remarkably preserved painted reliefs. The site forms part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing for Memphis and its Necropolis, inscribed in 1979.
