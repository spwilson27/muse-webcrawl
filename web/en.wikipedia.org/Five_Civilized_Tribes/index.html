Five Civilized Tribes

The term Five Civilized Tribes derives from the colonial and early federal period in the history of the United States. It refers to five Native American nations in the Southeastern United States: the Cherokee, Chickasaw, Choctaw, Creek (Muscogee), and Seminole.

Origin of the term

White settlers used "civilized" because these nations adopted aspects of colonial culture, including Christianity, centralised governments, literacy (notably Sequoyah's Cherokee syllabary), market participation and plantation slavery. The term is now considered outdated and loaded.

Removal

Under the Indian Removal Act (1830), the five nations were forced west to Indian Territory (present-day Oklahoma) on the Trail of Tears, on which thousands died.

In Indian Territory

They rebuilt republics with capitals such as Tahlequah. Allotment under the Dawes Act and Oklahoma statehood (1907) dissolved much tribal land; the nations remain sovereign tribal governments today.
