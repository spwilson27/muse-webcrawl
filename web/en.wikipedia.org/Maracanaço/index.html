Maracanaço

Event: 1950 FIFA World Cup, final round
Date: 16 July 1950
Venue: Maracanã Stadium, Rio de Janeiro
Result: Uruguay 2–1 Brazil

The Maracanaço (Portuguese: Maracanaço, "the Maracanã blow") is the name given in Brazil to the national football team's defeat by Uruguay in the deciding match of the 1950 FIFA World Cup, played at the Maracanã Stadium in Rio de Janeiro on 16 July 1950. It is regarded as one of the greatest upsets in football history and a lasting national trauma in Brazil.

The match

The 1950 tournament ended with a final round-robin group rather than a single final; Brazil needed only a draw against Uruguay to become champions, having beaten Sweden 7–1 and Spain 6–1, and were overwhelming favourites before a crowd of some 200,000. Friaça put Brazil ahead early in the second half, but Juan Alberto Schiaffino equalised and Alcides Ghiggia scored the winner in the 79th minute, silencing the stadium. Uruguay took their second World Cup title.

Aftermath

The defeat prompted national mourning and soul-searching. Brazil's white kit was blamed and replaced after a newspaper competition by the now-iconic yellow shirt with green trim. Goalkeeper Moacir Barbosa was scapegoated for the winning goal for the rest of his life, an injustice often cited in discussions of racism in Brazilian football. Ghiggia's goal remains legendary in Uruguay; he later remarked that only three people had silenced the Maracanã — the Pope, Frank Sinatra, and himself.
