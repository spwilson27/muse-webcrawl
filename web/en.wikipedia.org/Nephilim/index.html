Nephilim

The Nephilim (Hebrew: נְפִילִים) are mysterious beings or people in the Hebrew Bible described as being large and strong. The word is conventionally translated as “giants” in most translations of the Hebrew Bible, although some scholars render it “the fallen ones”.

Biblical references

The Nephilim are mentioned in Genesis 6:1–4, immediately before the account of Noah's flood: the “sons of God” took wives from the “daughters of men”, and their offspring were the Nephilim, described as “the heroes of old, men of renown”. They appear again in Numbers 13:32–33, where the spies sent into Canaan report that the inhabitants include descendants of Anak who come from the Nephilim, beside whom the spies seemed like grasshoppers.

Interpretations

Interpretations of the passage vary widely. In the Second Temple period, works such as the Book of Enoch expanded the story into an account of fallen angels — the Watchers — whose union with human women produced giants. Later Jewish and Christian commentators variously understood the “sons of God” as angels, as the righteous line of Seth, or as rulers and nobles. Some modern fringe theories associate the Nephilim with ancient astronauts, though this is not supported by mainstream scholarship.
