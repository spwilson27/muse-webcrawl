Burgundian Wars

The Burgundian Wars (1474–1477) were a series of conflicts between the Burgundian State under Duke Charles the Bold and a coalition led by the Old Swiss Confederacy and the Duchy of Lorraine, backed financially by Louis XI of France. They ended with Charles's death at Nancy and the partition of the Burgundian lands between France and the Habsburgs.

Causes

Charles the Bold sought to weld his territories — Burgundy, Franche-Comté and the wealthy Low Countries — into a middle kingdom between France and the Empire, expanding down the Rhine. His seizure of Upper Alsace and pressure on the Swiss cantons and their ally Mulhouse provoked war; the Swiss had additional grievances after Charles's governor Peter von Hagenbach was tried and executed by an Alsatian tribunal in 1474.

Major battles

  • Héricourt (1474) — Swiss victory over a Burgundian relief force.
  • Grandson (March 1476) — Charles's army panicked and abandoned its artillery and fabulous treasure, though casualties were light.
  • Morat (Murten) (June 1476) — the Swiss annihilated the Burgundian army besieging the town; thousands were killed.
  • Nancy (5 January 1477) — Charles was killed fighting the army of René II of Lorraine, his frozen body found days later.

Consequences

Charles's heir Mary of Burgundy married Maximilian of Austria, carrying the Low Countries and Franche-Comté to the Habsburgs, while France annexed the Duchy of Burgundy and Picardy — a partition settled by the Treaty of Arras (1482). The wars established the Swiss pikeman's reputation as Europe's finest infantry and marked the emergence of the Confederacy as a military power.
