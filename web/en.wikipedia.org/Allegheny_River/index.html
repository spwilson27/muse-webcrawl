Allegheny River

The Allegheny River is a river in the eastern United States, one of the two principal tributaries — along with the Monongahela River — that join at Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, to form the Ohio River. It is about 325 miles (523 km) long, rising in north-central Pennsylvania near the town of Coudersport in Potter County and flowing northwest into New York, then southwest through western Pennsylvania to Pittsburgh.

The name Allegheny is commonly said to derive from a Lenape word meaning "fine or fair waters". The river drains a rugged plateau area of the Allegheny Plateau, much of it forested, including the Allegheny National Forest.

Course and navigation

From its headwaters near the New York state line, the river winds through the Southern Tier of New York, where the Kinzua Dam (completed in 1965) impounds the Allegheny Reservoir. It re-enters Pennsylvania and flows past Oil City, Franklin, and Kittanning before joining the Monongahela at the Golden Triangle in downtown Pittsburgh. A series of locks and dams, maintained by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, makes the lower 72 miles navigable for commercial barge traffic.

History

The valley was long inhabited by Indigenous peoples, including the Seneca, and was contested during the French and Indian War; Fort Duquesne was built by the French at the forks of the Ohio in 1754. After the American Civil War, the upper valley became a centre of the early petroleum industry, following the 1859 Drake Well strike near Titusville on Oil Creek, a tributary of the Allegheny. In 1992 a 87-mile segment of the river was designated a National Wild and Scenic River.
