Menu

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In a restaurant, a menu is a list of food and beverages offered to customers, together with their prices. Menus may be printed on paper, displayed on boards, or presented digitally, and they often include descriptions of dishes, notes on ingredients and allergens, and information about the restaurant.

Types

An à la carte menu prices each item separately, while a table d'hôte or prix fixe menu offers a set meal at a fixed price. Tasting menus present a sequence of small courses chosen by the chef. Separate menus may exist for drinks, desserts, children, or dietary requirements.

History

The modern restaurant menu developed in Paris in the late 18th century, as restaurants proliferated and diners came to expect a written bill of fare rather than eating whatever the house served. Printed menus became works of design in the 19th and 20th centuries, and elaborate banquet menus are now collected as ephemera illustrating social and culinary history.

Menu engineering

Restaurateurs analyze the popularity and profitability of dishes — a practice known as menu engineering — and use layout, ordering, and description to influence customer choices. In computing, the word "menu" was borrowed for a list of options presented by a program, now a standard element of user interfaces.
