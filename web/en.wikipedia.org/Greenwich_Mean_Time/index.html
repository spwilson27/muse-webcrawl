Greenwich Mean Time

Greenwich Mean Time (GMT) is the mean solar time at the Royal Observatory in Greenwich, London, counted from midnight. Historically it served as the world's primary time standard: the time in every time zone was expressed as an offset from GMT, and the prime meridian at Greenwich was adopted as the reference for longitude in 1884. For scientific and technical purposes GMT has been superseded since 1972 by Coordinated Universal Time (UTC), which is based on atomic time but kept within 0.9 seconds of Earth's rotation by leap seconds.

History and use

GMT spread through the 19th century with the railways and telegraph: British railways adopted "railway time" based on GMT in 1847, and it became Britain's legal standard time in 1880. The BBC World Service long announced times in GMT, and the term remains in everyday British usage for the winter time zone (UTC+00:00), as distinct from British Summer Time. Because "GMT" can refer ambiguously to slightly different astronomical and civil conventions, precise work uses UTC; the zone designation "Z" (Zulu Time) in aviation and the military corresponds to UTC+00:00.
