The Russian famine of 1921–22, also known as the Povolzhye famine, was a severe famine in the Russian Soviet Federative Socialist Republic that killed an estimated five million people. It struck principally the Volga and Ural river regions, including Samara, Saratov, and the Tatar and Bashkir republics, following the devastation of the Russian Civil War.

Causes

A severe drought in 1921 destroyed harvests across the Volga basin on top of years of disruption: the First World War, revolution, and civil war had wrecked transport and agriculture, while the Bolshevik policy of prodrazverstka — forced grain requisitioning under War Communism — left peasants with little seed or food reserve. Livestock numbers had collapsed, and epidemics of typhus and cholera compounded the hunger.

Relief efforts

The Soviet government appealed for international aid in mid-1921. The American Relief Administration, directed by Herbert Hoover, mounted the largest operation, feeding more than ten million people daily at its peak, alongside efforts by Fridtjof Nansen's organization, the Quakers, and other charities. Aid wound down in 1923 as harvests recovered. The famine prompted the regime to retreat from War Communism to the New Economic Policy.
