Thomas Spence (2 July 1750 – 8 September 1814) was an English radical pamphleteer and advocate of the common ownership of land. His "Plan" proposed that all land in each parish be vested in the community, with rents funding public services and a basic dividend for every inhabitant — an early vision of common land ownership with social income.

Life and ideas

Spence, a Newcastle schoolteacher turned London bookseller, first presented his plan in a 1775 lecture and spread it through cheap pamphlets, ballads, and even stamped tokens. He was repeatedly imprisoned for seditious libel, including for publishing works arguing that private landed property was the root of poverty and tyranny.

Influence

After his death, his followers — the Spencean Philanthropists — formed one of the most radical underground movements of the Regency era, and some were implicated in the Spa Fields riots and the Cato Street conspiracy. Spence is also remembered for devising a phonetic spelling system and publishing some of the earliest dictionaries of reformed English spelling.
