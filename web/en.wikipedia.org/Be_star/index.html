Be star

A Be star is a hot B-type star whose spectrum shows emission lines, especially the hydrogen Balmer lines — the "e" denotes emission. The phenomenon was first noted by Angelo Secchi in 1866 in the star Gamma Cassiopeiae.

Cause

Be stars rotate extremely rapidly, close to their critical breakup velocity. Material shed from the equator forms a viscous circumstellar disc, and it is this disc that produces the emission lines. The disc can grow, dissipate, and reform over years, causing the star's spectral appearance to vary.

Examples

Bright examples include Achernar — the flattest known star due to its rapid spin — Gamma Cassiopeiae, and Pleione in the Pleiades cluster. A related, more extreme class, the gamma-ray binary systems, pairs a Be star with a neutron star or black hole.
