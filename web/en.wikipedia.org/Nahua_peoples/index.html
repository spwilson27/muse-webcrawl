Nahua peoples

The Nahua are a group of Indigenous peoples of Mexico, El Salvador, Honduras and Nicaragua, united by their use of the Nahuatl language (or historically of one of the Nahuan languages of the Uto-Aztecan family). They are the largest Indigenous group in Mexico, numbering well over a million Nahuatl speakers.

History

Nahuan-speaking peoples migrated into central Mexico from the north, probably beginning around the 5th–6th centuries CE. Nahua groups included the Toltecs and, most famously, the Mexica, who founded Tenochtitlan and built the Aztec Empire. Other Nahua peoples included the Tlaxcalteca, the Totonac neighbours' rivals, and the Pipil of El Salvador.

Contemporary Nahua

Today Nahua communities are concentrated in the states of Puebla, Veracruz, Hidalgo, Guerrero, San Luis Potosí and Morelos. Community life often centres on the village, its patron-saint fiestas and the cargo system of rotating civil-religious offices, blending pre-Hispanic and Catholic traditions. Nahuatl literature and language revitalisation efforts continue across the Nahua regions.
