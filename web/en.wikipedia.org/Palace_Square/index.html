Palace Square

Palace Square (Russian: Дворцовая площадь, Dvortsovaya ploshchad) is the central city square of Saint Petersburg, Russia, connecting Nevsky Prospekt with Palace Bridge leading to Vasilievsky Island. It is overlooked by the Winter Palace, the former imperial residence, and was the setting of many events of worldwide significance, including the Bloody Sunday massacre of 1905 and the storming of the Winter Palace during the October Revolution of 1917.

Description

The square is bounded on its northern side by the baroque Winter Palace (now part of the Hermitage Museum), and on its southern side by the semicircular General Staff Building, designed by Carlo Rossi and completed in 1829, whose centre is pierced by a triumphal arch crowned with the Chariot of Victory. In the centre of the square stands the Alexander Column, erected in 1834 to commemorate Russia's victory over Napoleonic France; at 47.5 metres, it is the tallest monument of its kind in the world, carved from a single piece of red granite.

Historical events

On 22 January 1905 (Bloody Sunday), troops fired on a peaceful workers' procession marching to present a petition to Nicholas II at the Winter Palace, an event that helped ignite the 1905 Revolution. During the October Revolution, Bolshevik forces occupied the square and took the Winter Palace on the night of 25–26 October 1917 (Old Style). In the Soviet and post-Soviet eras the square has hosted military parades, demonstrations, concerts, and New Year celebrations.
