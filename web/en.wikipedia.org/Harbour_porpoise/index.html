Harbour porpoise

Harbour porpoise
Phocoena phocoena
Family: Phocoenidae (porpoises)

The harbour porpoise (Phocoena phocoena) is one of the smallest and most widely distributed porpoise species, found in cool coastal waters of the North Atlantic, North Pacific, and Black Sea. It averages about 1.4–1.9 m in length and 60–75 kg in weight, with a small triangular dorsal fin and a blunt, beakless head.

Behaviour and diet

Shy and usually seen alone or in small groups, harbour porpoises feed on small schooling fish such as herring, sprat, and sand eels, as well as squid. They use high-frequency echolocation clicks and must feed almost continuously because of their high metabolic rate. Females typically give birth to a single calf each year after a gestation of about 10–11 months.

Conservation

Although the species as a whole is listed as Least Concern, several populations are endangered — notably the Baltic Sea population and the Black Sea subspecies. The main threats are incidental capture in gillnets, chemical pollution, and underwater noise. The harbour porpoise is protected under European law and agreements such as ASCOBANS, and is a familiar sight in harbours and estuaries around northern Europe.
