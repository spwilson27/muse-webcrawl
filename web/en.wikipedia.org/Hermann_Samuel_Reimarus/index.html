Hermann Samuel Reimarus (22 December 1694 – 1 March 1768) was a German philosopher and writer of the Enlightenment, a professor of Oriental languages at the Academic Gymnasium in Hamburg. He is best known posthumously for the Wolfenbüttel Fragments, the published portions of his radical critique of biblical Christianity, which helped inaugurate the historical quest for Jesus.

Life and published work

Reimarus studied at Jena and Wittenberg and taught at Hamburg from 1728 until his death. In his lifetime he published respectable works on natural theology, logic, and animal instinct, including The Principal Truths of Natural Religion (1754), which defended a rational deism while stopping short of attacking revealed Christianity in public.

The Fragments

His large manuscript Apology for the Rational Worshippers of God was too dangerous to publish in his lifetime. After his death, Gotthold Ephraim Lessing, librarian at Wolfenbüttel, issued excerpts anonymously between 1774 and 1778 as the Fragments of an Unknown Author, provoking the fierce Fragment Controversy with orthodox theologians such as Johann Melchior Goeze.

Influence

Reimarus argued that the historical Jesus was an apocalyptic preacher whose message must be sharply distinguished from the Christ proclaimed by the apostles — a distinction that shaped all later historical-Jesus research, as Albert Schweitzer's The Quest of the Historical Jesus (1906) acknowledged by beginning its survey with Reimarus.
