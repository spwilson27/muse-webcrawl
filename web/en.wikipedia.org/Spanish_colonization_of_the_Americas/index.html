Spanish colonization of the Americas


Extent of the Spanish Empire in the Americas

The Spanish colonization of the Americas began with the arrival of Christopher Columbus in the Caribbean in 1492 and continued for more than three centuries. Spain established the first sustained European empire in the New World, encompassing the Caribbean, Mexico, Central America, much of South America, and large parts of what is now the southern and western United States, together with the Philippines across the Pacific.

Conquest

After initial settlement of Hispaniola and Cuba, Spanish conquistadors overthrew the great indigenous empires: Hernán Cortés conquered the Aztec Empire in Mexico (1519–1521), and Francisco Pizarro conquered the Inca Empire in Peru (1532–1533), exploiting civil wars, steel weapons, horses, and — decisively — epidemic diseases such as smallpox, which devastated Native American populations. The destruction and disease of the conquest era caused a demographic catastrophe, with indigenous populations falling by as much as 90 percent in some regions.

Administration and society

The colonies were governed as viceroyalties — New Spain (1535) and Peru (1542) first, later New Granada and the Río de la Plata — under the Council of the Indies in Madrid. A rigid caste system distinguished peninsulares (Spanish-born), criollos (American-born Spaniards), mestizos (mixed Spanish and indigenous), indigenous peoples, and enslaved Africans. The encomienda and later repartimiento systems extracted indigenous labour; vast silver mines such as Potosí and Zacatecas flooded world markets with bullion. The Catholic Church, through missions and orders such as the Franciscans, Dominicans, and Jesuits, evangelized the population and shaped colonial culture.

Independence

The Spanish American wars of independence (1808–1833), triggered by Napoleon's invasion of Spain, dismantled the mainland empire; by 1825 only Cuba and Puerto Rico remained, held until 1898. The colonial legacy endures in the Spanish language, Catholicism, urban patterns, and legal traditions of Latin America.
