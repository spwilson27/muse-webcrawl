Undulator

Undulator
Type: Insertion device
Use: Synchrotron light sources, free-electron lasers
Field: Periodic permanent-magnet or electromagnet array

An undulator is an insertion device used in particle accelerators, consisting of a periodic structure of dipole magnets that forces a beam of relativistic charged particles to undergo oscillations and thereby radiate intense electromagnetic energy. The radiation produced is concentrated in narrow energy bands and is far brighter than that from ordinary bending magnets.

Principle

As particles traverse the undulator they follow a sinusoidal path. Radiation emitted at each bend interferes constructively at wavelengths determined by the undulator period, the magnetic field strength, and the particle energy. The dimensionless parameter K distinguishes undulators (K of order 1, producing coherent, narrowband radiation) from wigglers (large K, producing broadband radiation).

Applications

Undulators are key components of third- and fourth-generation light sources and free-electron lasers, supplying tunable X-rays for crystallography, materials science, and imaging.
