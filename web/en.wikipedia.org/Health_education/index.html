Health education

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Health education is a profession and field of practice that educates people about health. The World Health Organization defines health education as any combination of learning experiences designed to help individuals and communities improve their health, by increasing their knowledge or influencing their attitudes. It encompasses education about physical, mental, emotional, and social health, delivered in schools, workplaces, clinics, and communities.

Areas and settings

Health education covers topics such as nutrition, physical activity, hygiene, sexual and reproductive health, substance-use prevention, mental health, injury prevention, and management of chronic disease. In schools it is delivered as part of health curricula; in healthcare settings it includes patient education; in public health it includes mass-media and social-marketing campaigns. Certified professionals (in the US, the CHES — Certified Health Education Specialist) plan programmes using behaviour-change theories such as the health belief model, the transtheoretical model, and social cognitive theory, and evaluate outcomes.

History

The field professionalised in the early twentieth century with school hygiene and public-health nursing movements, expanded after the Second World War with government health agencies, and gained new prominence through the health promotion movement crystallised in the 1986 Ottawa Charter. Modern practice increasingly uses digital media, and a major current concern is combating health misinformation online.
