Eburones

Eburones
Region: Between the Meuse and the Rhine (modern Belgium/Germany)
Period: 1st century BC
Leaders: Ambiorix, Cativolcus
Fate: Destroyed by Julius Caesar, 54–53 BC

The Eburones were a Gallic-Germanic tribe inhabiting the region between the Meuse and Rhine rivers, in what is now northeastern Belgium and the neighbouring parts of Germany and the Netherlands, at the time of Julius Caesar's Gallic Wars.

Revolt against Rome

In the winter of 54 BC, under their leaders Ambiorix and Cativolcus, the Eburones rose against the Romans, destroying a legion and a half — about 6,000 men under Sabinus and Cotta — in one of Rome's worst defeats of the war, and unsuccessfully besieging the camp of Quintus Cicero. Ambiorix became a celebrated figure of resistance.

Destruction

Caesar retaliated in 53 BC with a campaign of annihilation: the territory of the Eburones was systematically devastated and their lands thrown open to plunder by neighbouring tribes. The Eburones disappear from history as a people; Cativolcus poisoned himself, while Ambiorix escaped across the Rhine and was never captured. Their territory was later occupied by the Tungri, whose name survives in Tongeren.
