Battle of the Overpass

Date26 May 1937
LocationMiller Road overpass, Ford River Rouge Complex, Dearborn, Michigan, US
PartiesUnited Automobile Workers (UAW) organizers vs. Ford Motor Company Service Department
OutcomeUnion members beaten; photographs galvanized public opinion and aided the UAW

The Battle of the Overpass was a confrontation on 26 May 1937 in which security personnel of the Ford Motor Company attacked United Automobile Workers (UAW) union organizers outside the Ford River Rouge Complex in Dearborn, Michigan, United States. The assault, carried out in full view of press photographers, became one of the most famous episodes of American labour history; the resulting photographs turned public opinion against Ford's violent anti-union campaign and are credited with helping the union eventually organize the company.

Events

As part of a leafleting campaign ("Unionism, Not Fordism") timed to a shift change, UAW organizers led by Walter Reuther and Richard Frankensteen obtained a permit to distribute handbills on the Miller Road pedestrian overpass, which crossed from public property toward Gate 4 of the Rouge plant. As the organizers posed for newspaper photographers on the bridge, members of Harry Bennett's Ford Service Department — the company's internal security force, notorious for its strong-arm methods — attacked them. Frankensteen's coat was pulled over his head and he was beaten and kicked; Reuther was thrown down the overpass stairs repeatedly. Women organizers were also assaulted, and several photographers had their cameras smashed, though Detroit News photographer James R. (Scotty) Kilpatrick hid his plates and got the images out.

Aftermath

Published nationwide, Kilpatrick's photographs won attention (and later recognition as among the century's iconic news images) and contradicted Ford's account of the incident. The National Labor Relations Board investigated Ford's labour practices, and the company was found to have violated the National Labor Relations Act. Ford, the last of the major automakers to resist the UAW, finally recognized the union in 1941 after a strike shut down the Rouge plant, signing a contract that June. The overpass itself still stands in Dearborn, and the site is commemorated with a historical marker.
