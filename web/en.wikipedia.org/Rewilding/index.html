Rewilding

Rewilding is a form of ecological restoration that seeks to restore self-sustaining ecosystems by protecting core wild areas, reconnecting habitats, and reinstating natural processes and missing species, especially apex predators and other keystone organisms. It generally emphasises ecological autonomy and reduced continuing human control.

Principles

The influential “cores, corridors, and carnivores” model proposes large protected areas linked by habitat corridors and populated by wide-ranging predators whose effects cascade through food webs. Rewilding projects may allow rivers to flood, dead wood to accumulate, scavengers to consume carcasses, and herbivores to shape vegetation through grazing and browsing.

Approaches

Pleistocene rewilding proposes ecological proxies for extinct megafauna, a controversial idea illustrated by proposals involving large herbivores or predators outside their recent ranges. European rewilding often uses cattle and horse breeds as proxies for aurochs and wild horses and may involve reintroducing bison, beavers, lynx, or wolves. “Passive rewilding” occurs when farmland abandonment allows vegetation and wildlife to return with limited intervention.

Benefits and risks

Supporters argue that rewilding can restore food-web interactions, increase habitat heterogeneity, store carbon, reduce management costs, and create nature-based livelihoods. Critics point to uncertain historical baselines, disease and invasive-species risks, conflicts with farmers and residents, animal-welfare questions for introduced populations, and the danger that charismatic projects distract from protecting intact ecosystems.

Examples

Yellowstone's wolf reintroduction is frequently cited, although the size and mechanism of its ecosystem effects remain debated. The Oostvaardersplassen in the Netherlands, Knepp Wildland in England, Iberá in Argentina, and large landscape programmes in the Carpathians and Danube delta each apply different combinations of species introduction, land purchase, and process restoration.
