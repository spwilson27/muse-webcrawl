Deutsche Reichspost

The Deutsche Reichspost was the German imperial postal service, established at the founding of the German Empire in 1871 and continuing through the Weimar Republic and Nazi Germany until the end of the Second World War in 1945.

History

Following German unification, the postal services of the North German Confederation and the southern German states were consolidated under an imperial postal administration headed for many years by Heinrich von Stephan, who reorganised the service, introduced the postcard and helped found the Universal Postal Union in 1874.

The Reichspost expanded into telegraphy and telephony, becoming one of the largest employers in the Empire. After 1918 it operated under the Weimar Republic as a state enterprise of the Reich Ministry of Posts, and from 1934 it also administered the postal services of the Saar and, after 1938, Austria.

Dissolution

With Germany's defeat in 1945, the Reichspost ceased to exist as a unified service; postal operations were taken over by the Allied occupation authorities and subsequently by the Deutsche Bundespost in the Federal Republic and the Deutsche Post of the GDR in the east.
