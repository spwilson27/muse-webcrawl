Telepresence

Telepresence refers to a set of technologies which allow a person to feel as if they were present, to give the appearance or effect of being present, or to have an effect, at a place other than their true location. Telepresence requires that the users' senses be provided with stimuli giving the feeling of being in that other location; users may also manipulate objects in the remote environment, for example through a telepresence robot or telerobotics.

Applications

The best-known applications are high-end videoconferencing systems — immersive rooms with life-size, high-definition displays and spatial audio designed to simulate a shared meeting table. Telepresence robots, consisting of a screen and camera on a mobile base, let remote users move through offices, hospitals and classrooms. In medicine, telerobotic surgery enables specialists to operate at a distance. Similar principles underlie the remote operation of deep-sea vehicles, bomb-disposal robots and spacecraft rovers.

History

The term was popularised in 1980 by cognitive scientist Marvin Minsky, who envisioned remote work through advanced teleoperation. Commercial telepresence systems emerged in the 1990s and 2000s from vendors including Polycom and Cisco, while cheaper consumer videoconferencing has since incorporated many of the same ideas.
