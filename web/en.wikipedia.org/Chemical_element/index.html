Chemical element

A chemical element is a pure substance consisting only of atoms that all have the same number of protons in their nuclei. Unlike chemical compounds, elements cannot be broken down into simpler substances by chemical reactions.

Characteristics

Each element is distinguished by its atomic number (proton count) and is represented by a one- or two-letter chemical symbol, such as H for hydrogen, O for oxygen and Fe for iron. Atoms of an element with different neutron numbers are isotopes of that element.

The periodic table

As of 2024, 118 elements have been identified, the first 94 occurring naturally on Earth. They are organised in the periodic table by atomic number and recurring chemical properties into groups including metals, metalloids and nonmetals, noble gases and halogens.

History

The modern concept derives from Robert Boyle and Antoine Lavoisier, whose 1789 list contained 33 elements. Dmitri Mendeleev's periodic table of 1869 predicted undiscovered elements, and Moseley's work in 1913 established atomic number as the ordering principle.
