Ljubljana Castle

Ljubljana Castle (Slovene: Ljubljanski grad) is a castle complex standing on Castle Hill above the old town of Ljubljana, Slovenia. A medieval fortress — built in the eleventh or twelfth century and largely rebuilt in the fifteenth after an earthquake, with the pentagonal Viewing Tower added in 1848 — it is the city's dominant landmark.

History

The castle guarded the town and the river crossing, served as a seat of the provincial lords of Carniola, and later as a barracks, arsenal and prison, and in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries as poor housing. The city bought it in 1905, intending a municipal museum. Major renovation from the 1960s onward converted it into a cultural venue.

Today

Reached on foot or by funicular (opened 2006), the castle hosts exhibitions on Slovenian history, a puppet museum, concerts, weddings and restaurants, and its tower offers panoramic views over the city to the Alps.
