Vestibulocochlear nerve

Cranial nerveCN VIII
InnervatesCochlea; vestibular apparatus
FunctionHearing and balance

The vestibulocochlear nerve, also known as the auditory vestibular nerve or the eighth cranial nerve (CN VIII), transmits sound and balance information from the inner ear to the brain. It consists of two components: the cochlear nerve, carrying auditory signals from the cochlea, and the vestibular nerve, carrying signals about head position and motion from the semicircular canals, utricle, and saccule.

Pathway

The two divisions unite within the temporal bone and travel together through the internal acoustic meatus alongside the facial nerve, entering the brainstem at the junction of the pons and medulla. Cochlear fibres terminate in the cochlear nuclei, while vestibular fibres project to the vestibular nuclei and cerebellum, supporting balance reflexes such as the vestibulo-ocular reflex.

Clinical significance

Damage to the nerve causes hearing loss, tinnitus, vertigo, and imbalance. The most common tumour affecting it is the vestibular schwannoma (acoustic neuroma), a benign growth arising from the Schwann cells of the vestibular division, typically presenting with one-sided hearing loss and ringing in the ear. The nerve is unusual among cranial nerves in being myelinated by oligodendrocytes, like central nervous tissue, for part of its course.
