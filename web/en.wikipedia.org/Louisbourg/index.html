Louisbourg

Louisbourg is a community in Nova Scotia, Canada, on the southeastern coast of Cape Breton Island. It is best known as the site of the Fortress of Louisbourg, the great French fortified port founded in 1713 and now a National Historic Site of Canada, partially reconstructed as the largest historical reconstruction project in North America.

History

France founded Louisbourg after the Treaty of Utrecht (1713) ceded mainland Acadia and Newfoundland to Britain, building a fortified harbour to guard the entrance to the Gulf of St. Lawrence, protect the cod fishery, and anchor French power in the region. Named for Louis XIV, the fortress town became a major commercial port. It was captured by New England colonial forces with British naval support in 1745, returned to France by treaty in 1748, then besieged and taken again by Britain in 1758 during the Seven Years' War; its fortifications were demolished in 1760.

Today

The modern town is a fishing port. The fortress site, about a quarter of the original town reconstructed beginning in the 1960s, operates as a living-history museum with costumed interpreters. The Louisbourg Lighthouse marks the site of Canada's first lighthouse, built in 1734.
