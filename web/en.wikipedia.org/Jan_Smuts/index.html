Jan Smuts

Jan Christiaan Smuts (24 May 1870 – 11 September 1950) was a South African statesman, military leader and philosopher. He served as Prime Minister of the Union of South Africa from 1919 to 1924 and again from 1939 to 1948, and was the only person to sign both the Treaty of Versailles and the Charter of the United Nations — he drafted the preamble to the UN Charter.

Born: 24 May 1870, Bovenplaats, Cape Colony

Died: 11 September 1950, Doornkloof, South Africa

Office: Prime Minister of South Africa (1919–1924, 1939–1948)

Life

Smuts studied law at Christ's College, Cambridge, after winning the Ebden scholarship, and was called to the Cape Bar. During the Second Boer War he led a commando raid deep into the Cape Colony. Afterwards he became a key architect of the Union of South Africa (1910) alongside Louis Botha. In the First World War he commanded South African forces in German South-West Africa and East Africa, and sat in the Imperial War Cabinet in London, where he helped found the Royal Air Force.

As prime minister after 1919 he pursued reconciliation between Afrikaners and English speakers while enforcing racial segregation policies at home. He brought South Africa into the Second World War on the Allied side and was promoted to field marshal. His United Party lost the 1948 election to the National Party, which went on to institutionalise apartheid.

Philosophy

Smuts coined the term "holism" in his 1926 book Holism and Evolution, arguing that wholes are more than the sum of their parts — an idea that influenced later systems thinking.
