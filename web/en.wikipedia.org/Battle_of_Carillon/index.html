Battle of Carillon

Battle of Carillon
Date: 8 July 1758
Location: Fort Carillon, present-day Ticonderoga, New York
Result: French victory
Part of the French and Indian War

The Battle of Carillon was fought on 8 July 1758 during the French and Indian War. A French army of about 3,600 men under the Marquis de Montcalm, defending entrenched positions in front of Fort Carillon (later Fort Ticonderoga), repulsed frontal assaults by a British force of some 16,000 regulars and provincials under General James Abercrombie, inflicting heavy casualties.

Background

Abercrombie advanced up Lake George as part of the 1758 British offensive against New France. Montcalm, heavily outnumbered and expecting no reinforcements, built a log breastwork across the heights before the fort, fronted by an abatis of felled trees. After the death of Brigadier Lord Howe in a preliminary skirmish deprived the British of their most respected officer, Abercrombie received an engineer's optimistic report that the works could be stormed before French reinforcements arrived.

The battle

Instead of waiting for his artillery, Abercrombie ordered repeated frontal infantry assaults. British regiments, including the 42nd Highlanders (the Black Watch), struggled through the abatis under devastating French musket fire for hours and were thrown back with some 2,000 casualties — the bloodiest day of the war. The French lost only a few hundred men. Abercrombie retreated to Lake George and was soon replaced by Jeffery Amherst.

Aftermath

Carillon was Montcalm's finest defensive victory, but the fort was abandoned to Amherst the following summer as French resources dwindled. The site, renamed Ticonderoga, became famous again in the American Revolution.
