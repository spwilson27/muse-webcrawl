Amun

Name in hieroglyphsimn (Amun, "the Hidden One")
Major cult centreThebes (Karnak)
SymbolTwo tall plumes, ram, goose
ConsortMut (also Amunet)
OffspringKhonsu

Amun was a major ancient Egyptian deity whose name means "the hidden one". He was attested from the Old Kingdom as a member of the Ogdoad of Hermopolis, but rose to national prominence with the Theban dynasties of the Middle and New Kingdoms, becoming eventually the chief god of the Egyptian pantheon as Amun-Ra, king of the gods.

Rise to prominence

When Thebes became the capital of a reunified Egypt under the Eleventh and Twelfth Dynasties, its local god Amun grew in importance. Under the Eighteenth Dynasty he was fused with the sun god Ra as Amun-Ra, and his temple at Karnak became the largest religious complex of the ancient world, enriched by pharaohs' war spoils and offerings. His priesthood accumulated such wealth and influence that it at times rivalled the crown.

Theology

Amun's hiddenness was central to his nature: he was the unknowable creator whose true form no god or human could perceive. Hymns such as the Leiden Hymns to Amun portray him as transcendent yet immanent, a sole god of whom all other gods are manifestations.

Family

At Thebes, Amun formed a triad with his consort Mut and their son Khonsu, the moon god. His earlier consort in the Ogdoad tradition was Amunet.

Later history

The pharaoh Akhenaten attempted to suppress Amun's cult during the Amarna period, but it was restored under Tutankhamun. An oracle of Amun at the Siwa Oasis famously confirmed Alexander the Great as son of the god in 331 BC. The cult declined with the rise of Christianity in Egypt.
