Vampire bats are bats whose food source is blood, a dietary trait called hematophagy. Three extant species feed solely on blood: the common vampire bat (Desmodus rotundus), the hairy-legged vampire bat (Diphylla ecaudata), and the white-winged vampire bat (Diaemus youngi). All three species are native to the Americas, ranging from Mexico to Brazil, Chile, Uruguay, and Argentina.

Feeding

Vampire bats hunt only when it is dark. Like fruit bats, they use echolocation and olfaction to identify prey; they are also able to sense the heat of blood vessels near the skin using infrared-sensitive pits on their noses. A feeding bat lands near its prey, approaches on the ground, makes a small incision with its razor-sharp front teeth, and laps up the blood. An anticoagulant in the saliva, called draculin, prevents the blood from clotting. A typical meal lasts about 20 to 30 minutes, and the bat may ingest so much blood that it has difficulty taking off.

Social behaviour

Common vampire bats roost in colonies of up to a few hundred individuals in caves, hollow trees, and abandoned buildings. They are highly social and display reciprocal altruism: a bat that fails to find food may receive regurgitated blood from a roost-mate, and bats remember which individuals have helped them, favouring those partners in the future. Females adopt orphaned young, another unusual trait among bats.

Relationship with humans

Vampire bats occasionally bite sleeping humans and livestock; they are a vector of rabies in Latin America, and their bites can cause economic losses in cattle ranching. Nevertheless, attacks on humans are rare, and draculin and other salivary compounds have been studied for possible medical use as anticoagulants.
