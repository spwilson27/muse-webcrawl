Yunus Emre (c. 1238–1328) was a Turkish folk poet and Sufi mystic who greatly influenced Turkish culture. Writing in the spoken Turkish of Anatolia rather than the Persian or Arabic favoured at court, he is regarded as a founder of Turkish literature and a pioneer of Turkish humanism.

Life

Yunus Emre lived during the decline of the Seljuk Sultanate of Rum and the Mongol invasions of Anatolia. Tradition places his home at Sarıköy near Sivrihisar, where his tomb is located, and describes him as a disciple of the dervish Haji Bektash Veli and of Taptuk Emre. For many years he is said to have carried wood to his master's lodge before turning to poetry.

Works

His collected poems, the Divan, and his didactic mesnevi Risaleten Nushiyye ("Treatise of Advice", 1307) expound Sufi themes: divine and human love, humility, tolerance, and the unity of creation. His famous maxim — "We love the created for the sake of the Creator" (Yaradılanı severiz, Yaradan'dan ötürü) — summarises his outlook.

Legacy

His verses are still sung as hymns (ilahi) in Turkey. UNESCO proclaimed 1991 the International Yunus Emre Year, and 2021, marking 700 years since his death, was likewise dedicated to his memory in Turkey and internationally.
