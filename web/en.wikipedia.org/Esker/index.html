Esker

An esker, eskar, eschar, or os, sometimes called an asar or serpent kame, is a long, winding ridge of stratified sand and gravel associated with glaciers and the ice sheets that formerly covered northern Europe and North America.

Formation

Eskers form when sediment carried by meltwater streams flowing within or beneath a glacier is deposited as the stream slows. When the ice melts away, the channel deposits are left as a raised ridge that may extend for tens or hundreds of kilometres.

Examples

Notable eskers include the Great Esker Park in Massachusetts, the Katahdin Woods eskers of Maine, and the long esker systems of Finland, Sweden, Ireland, and Canada, where they often serve as sources of sand and gravel and as dry routes across wetlands.
