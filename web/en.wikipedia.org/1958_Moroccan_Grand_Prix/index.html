The 1958 Moroccan Grand Prix was a Formula One motor race held at Ain-Diab near Casablanca on 19 October 1958. It was the eleventh and final race of the 1958 World Championship of Drivers and the only World Championship Grand Prix ever held in Morocco.

Championship decider

The title was contested between Vanwall's Stirling Moss and Ferrari's Mike Hawthorn. Moss won the race, but Hawthorn's second place — aided by team-mate Phil Hill slowing to wave him through — gave Hawthorn the championship by a single point, making him Britain's first Formula One World Champion.

Fatal accident

The race was marred by the death of Vanwall driver Stuart Lewis-Evans. His car suffered an engine seizure, crashed, and caught fire; he died of his burns six days later in an English hospital. The tragedy contributed to Vanwall owner Tony Vandervell's decision to withdraw his team from racing at the end of the season, despite Vanwall winning the inaugural Constructors' Championship.

Hawthorn retired from racing as champion and was killed in a road accident in January 1959.
