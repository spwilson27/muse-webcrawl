Laudato si'

Laudato si' (Praise Be to You) is the second encyclical of Pope Francis, promulgated on 18 June 2015 and dated 24 May 2015. Subtitled "on care for our common home", it is the Pope's critique of consumerism and irresponsible development, his lament on environmental degradation and global warming, and his call to all people of the world to take "swift and unified global action". The encyclical's title is Umbrian for "Praise be to you, my Lord", the refrain of Saint Francis of Assisi's 13th-century "Canticle of the Sun".

Content

The encyclical, divided into six chapters, opens by surveying "what is happening to our common home": pollution, climate change as "one of the principal challenges facing humanity", water scarcity, loss of biodiversity and global inequality. Francis affirms the scientific consensus that climate change is largely human-caused, and insists that environmental and social problems are inseparable — a theme he names "integral ecology". He critiques the "technocratic paradigm" and "throwaway culture", calls for a transition away from fossil fuels, international agreements on climate and the protection of ecosystems such as the Amazon and Congo basins, and proposes an "ecological conversion" and simpler lifestyles.

Sources and drafting

The document draws on the teaching of previous popes (John Paul II, Benedict XVI) and, unusually, on the Ecumenical Patriarch Bartholomew I of the Eastern Orthodox Church. The Pontifical Council for Justice and Peace under Cardinal Peter Turkson led the drafting, consulting climate scientists including members of the Pontifical Academy of Sciences.

Reception

Laudato si' was widely praised by environmental organizations, scientists and many political and religious leaders as a landmark intervention ahead of the 2015 Paris climate conference (COP21); UN Secretary-General Ban Ki-moon welcomed it. It also drew criticism from some conservative and business commentators who objected to its critique of markets and fossil fuels. The encyclical helped spur Catholic and interfaith climate movements and an annual "Laudato Si' Week"; in 2023 Francis issued a follow-up apostolic exhortation, Laudate Deum, noting insufficient progress since 2015.

Influence

The encyclical is credited with influencing Catholic institutional divestment from fossil fuels, the framing of "integral ecology" in policy debates, and faith-based environmental advocacy worldwide. It is one of the most widely read papal documents in history, having been published online in numerous languages.
