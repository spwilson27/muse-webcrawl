4th Armored Division

The 4th Armored Division was an armored division of the United States Army that earned distinction in the European Theater of the Second World War. Activated on 15 April 1941 at Pine Camp, New York, it was unusual among American armored divisions in having no official nickname — its men preferred the motto "They shall be known by their deeds alone", though it was widely called the "Breakthrough Division".

Second World War

The division landed in Normandy in July 1944 and spearheaded Operation Cobra, the breakout from the Normandy beachhead, racing across Brittany and then France as the advance guard of George S. Patton's Third Army. It fought in the Lorraine campaign in the autumn of 1944 and, during the Battle of the Bulge, famously drove to relieve the besieged 101st Airborne Division at Bastogne in December 1944, with the 37th Tank Battalion under Creighton Abrams among the first units to break through. In March 1945 the division liberated the Ohrdruf concentration camp, the first such camp reached by American forces, and ended the war in Czechoslovakia.

Postwar

After occupation duty the division returned to the United States and was inactivated in 1946. It was reactivated in 1954 and stationed in West Germany during the Cold War, before being inactivated again in 1971; its colours were later used in the lineage of successor formations.
