The Lockheed P-3 Orion is a four-engined, turboprop anti-submarine and maritime surveillance aircraft developed for the United States Navy and introduced in the 1960s. Derived from the L-188 Electra airliner, it has served for over six decades with the U.S. Navy and the forces of many other nations.

Design and mission

The P-3 is distinguished by its long tail stinger housing a magnetic anomaly detector used to detect submerged submarines. Equipped with sonobuoys, radar, and electro-optical sensors, it conducts anti-submarine warfare, anti-surface warfare, and intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance missions. It can carry torpedoes, depth charges, mines, and anti-ship missiles.

Operators and variants

More than 750 P-3s were built, including licence production in Japan. Variants include the EP-3 signals-intelligence aircraft and the NP-3 test aircraft. In U.S. service the Orion is being replaced by the jet-powered Boeing P-8 Poseidon, though upgraded P-3s remain in use with numerous operators worldwide.
