World Series of Poker

The World Series of Poker (WSOP) is a series of poker tournaments held annually in Las Vegas, Nevada, widely regarded as the most prestigious in the game. The series culminates in the Main Event, a US$10,000 buy-in no-limit Texas hold 'em tournament whose winner is considered the world champion of poker for that year and receives a gold bracelet and a multi-million-dollar prize.

History

The WSOP began in 1970 at Binion's Horseshoe casino, where owner Benny Binion invited a small group of elite players to compete; the first champion, Johnny Moss, was elected by his peers. In 1971 the event adopted a freezeout tournament format, won by Moss again. Stu Ungar famously won the Main Event three times (1980, 1981, 1997). The 2003 victory of amateur Chris Moneymaker, who had qualified through a low-cost online satellite, helped trigger the global "poker boom" of the 2000s, swelling Main Event fields into the thousands.

Ownership and venues

Harrah's Entertainment (later Caesars Entertainment) acquired the WSOP in 2004 and moved it to the Rio All-Suite Hotel and Casino in 2005. Since 2022 the series has been staged at the Horseshoe and Paris casinos on the Las Vegas Strip. In 2024, Caesars announced the sale of the WSOP brand to NSUS Group, the operator of GGPoker, while retaining the right to host the live series in Las Vegas.

Events and bracelets

The modern WSOP comprises around one hundred bracelet events across poker variants including hold 'em, Omaha, seven-card stud and mixed games, with buy-ins from a few hundred dollars to the US$250,000 Super High Roller. Gold bracelets, first awarded in 1976, are the most coveted non-monetary prize in poker. Phil Hellmuth holds the record for most bracelets, with 17. The WSOP has also expanded internationally through WSOP Europe, first held in 2007, and online bracelet events.
