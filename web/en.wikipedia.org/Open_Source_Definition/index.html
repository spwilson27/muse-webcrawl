Open Source Definition

The Open Source Definition (OSD) is a document published by the Open Source Initiative (OSI) to determine whether a software licence can be labelled open source. It was derived from the Debian Free Software Guidelines, written by Bruce Perens in 1997 and adapted in 1998 when the term open source was coined.

Criteria

The definition requires free redistribution; availability of source code; permission for derived works under the same licence name's integrity rules; no discrimination against persons, groups or fields of endeavour; distribution of the licence without needing an additional one; and technology neutrality, among ten provisions. Licences such as Apache, BSD, GPL and MIT comply.

The OSI maintains a licence review process and list of approved licences. The definition is distinct from, though closely related to, the Free Software Foundation's definition of free software.
