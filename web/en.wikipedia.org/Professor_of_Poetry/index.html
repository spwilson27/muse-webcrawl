Professor of Poetry

The Professor of Poetry is an academic appointment at the University of Oxford, one of the most prestigious chairs in English literature. The professorship was established in 1708 by Henry Birkhead, a former fellow of All Souls College, who left an endowment for a lectureship in Latin poetry. Holders are elected by members of the university's Congregation for a term of four years and deliver public lectures, including a Creweian Oration in praise of benefactors at the university's Encaenia ceremony.

History

The first holder, Joseph Trapp, took up the post in 1708. The lectures were originally delivered in Latin; over time the chair became associated with English poetry and criticism. Distinguished holders have included Matthew Arnold (1857–1867), the first to lecture in English rather than Latin; W. H. Auden (1956–1961); Robert Graves (1961–1966); Seamus Heaney (1989–1994); Paul Muldoon (1999–2004); Christopher Ricks (2004–2009); Geoffrey Hill (2010–2015); and Simon Armitage (2015–2019). In 2019 Ruth Padel was elected but resigned days later amid controversy surrounding the election campaign; Marcus du Sautoy was subsequently elected, followed by A. E. Stallings in 2023.

Election

Uniquely among Oxford chairs, the Professor of Poetry is elected by a vote of Congregation — the university's academic community — rather than appointed by a selection committee, which has occasionally made elections spirited public contests between celebrated poets. Candidates need not hold a university post, and the role is part-time in practice, requiring a small number of lectures per year.

Significance

Because its holders are chosen from among the leading poets and critics of the day, the chair's history serves as a partial record of English literary life over three centuries. The Creweian Oration, delivered in Latin verse-turned-prose tradition, remains a distinctive ceremonial duty of the office.
