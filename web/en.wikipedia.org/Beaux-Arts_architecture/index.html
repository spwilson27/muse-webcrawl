Beaux-Arts architecture

Beaux-Arts architecture was the academic architectural style taught at the École des Beaux-Arts in Paris, particularly from the 1830s to the end of the 19th century. It drew upon principles of French neoclassicism but also incorporated Renaissance and Baroque elements.

Characteristics

Beaux-Arts buildings are characterised by symmetry, hierarchy of spaces, classical details such as columns and pediments, raised first stories, and elaborate ornamentation. Examples include the Opéra Garnier in Paris and Grand Central Terminal in New York.

In the United States

American architects trained in Paris brought the style home, where it flourished for civic buildings, museums, railway stations and mansions during the Gilded Age and the City Beautiful movement.

Legacy

The style fell from favour after the 1920s as modernism rose, but its monuments remain landmarks. See Neoclassical architecture.
