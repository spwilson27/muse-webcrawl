Emperor Ming of Han (28–75 AD), born Liu Zhuang, was the second emperor of the Chinese Eastern Han dynasty. His reign (57–75 AD), together with that of his son Emperor Zhang, is traditionally regarded as the golden age of the Eastern Han, known as the "Rule of Ming and Zhang".

Reign

Liu Zhuang was the son of Emperor Guangwu, the dynasty's founder. As emperor he was diligent and hardworking, personally supervising the administration and strictly controlling corruption among officials and imperial in-laws. He expanded the empire's reach in Central Asia, sending the general Ban Chao westward in 73 AD, and re-established Chinese influence along the Silk Road.

Buddhism

Traditional accounts link Emperor Ming with the introduction of Buddhism to China: according to legend, the emperor dreamed of a golden man, interpreted as the Buddha, and dispatched envoys to India who returned with sutras and monks, leading to the founding of the White Horse Temple near Luoyang, traditionally dated to 68 AD. Historians treat the dream story as legend, though Buddhism was indeed entering China around this period.

Death and succession

Emperor Ming died in 75 AD and was succeeded by his son, Emperor Zhang. His mausoleum, the Xianjie Mausoleum, lies in the imperial necropolis southeast of Luoyang.
