Telephone exchange

A telephone exchange, or central office, is a facility in the public switched telephone network where subscriber lines are connected and calls between them are switched. Early exchanges used human operators with cord switchboards; modern exchanges are computer-controlled digital switches, increasingly implemented in software.

History

The first commercial telephone exchange opened in New Haven, Connecticut, in January 1878, serving 21 subscribers. Operators connected calls manually by plugging cords into jacks until automatic switching arrived: the Strowger (step-by-step) switch, crossbar switches in the mid-20th century, and stored-program-control electronic exchanges from the 1960s.

Function

An exchange provides dial tone, interprets dialled digits, routes calls to the correct line or onward to other exchanges, supplies ringing current, and records billing information. Subscriber lines terminate on line cards in the exchange building, and exchanges are interconnected by trunk circuits forming a hierarchy from local to international level.
