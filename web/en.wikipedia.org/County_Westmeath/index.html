County Westmeath (Irish: Contae na hIarmhí) is a county in Ireland, in the province of Leinster. Its county town is Mullingar, while Athlone, on the River Shannon, is its other major town. The county is known as the "Lake County" for its many lakes, including Lough Ennell, Lough Owel, Lough Derravaragh, and Lough Ree.

Geography

Westmeath lies in the Irish Midlands, a landscape of drumlins, eskers, lakes, and bogs. The Hill of Uisneach, near the centre of the county, was in Irish mythology the symbolic centre of Ireland and a site of the Beltane fire festival. The River Shannon and Lough Ree mark the western boundary; the Royal Canal and the Dublin–Sligo railway cross the county through Mullingar.

History

The county derives from the old kingdom of Mide (Meath); it was separated from County Meath in 1543 and named Westmeath to distinguish it. The area is rich in early Christian and medieval sites, including Fore Abbey and the monastic site at Multyfarnham. Athlone's strategic Shannon crossing was besieged in 1690–91 during the Williamite War.

Culture

Westmeath is associated with the tenor John McCormack, born in Athlone, and the singer Joe Dolan, from Mullingar. Belvedere House and Gardens, Tullynally Castle, and Kilbeggan Distillery are major visitor attractions. The county's Gaelic football team won the Leinster Senior Football Championship for the first time in 2004.
