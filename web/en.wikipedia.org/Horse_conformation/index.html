Horse conformation

Equine conformation evaluates a horse's bone structure, musculature, and body proportions in relation to each other, as these determine the animal's athletic ability, soundness, and suitability for a particular purpose. Evaluating a horse's conformation combines an objective knowledge of anatomy with an understanding of how form affects function, and opinions vary between breeds and disciplines.

Principles

Judges assess balance (the horse's body should be roughly divisible into equal thirds of shoulder, barrel, and hindquarters), structural correctness of the legs viewed from the front, side, and rear, and the quality of the head, neck, topline, and hindquarters. Straight legs with correct angles at the hock and pastern are able to withstand concussion and transmit power efficiently, whereas deviations predispose the horse to lameness and gait faults.

Common faults

Recognised conformation faults include being "over at the knee" or "back at the knee" in the forelegs, sickle hocks and cow hocks behind, base-narrow or base-wide stance, pigeon toes or splayed feet, a ewe neck, a sway back, and upright ("straight") pasterns or shoulders. Such faults are assessed for severity and for their likely effect on the discipline for which the horse is intended.

Use

Conformation judging is central to halter and in-hand showing classes, to breed inspections, and to the practical selection of horses for racing, sport, and breeding, where systematic evaluation helps predict durability and performance.
