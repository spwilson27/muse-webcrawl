Jogging

Jogging is a form of trotting or running at a slow or leisurely pace. The main intention is to increase physical fitness with less stress on the body than from faster running but more than walking, or to maintain a steady recumbent speed for longer periods of time. It is a form of aerobic exercise.

History

Jogging as mass exercise was popularized in the 1960s and 1970s. New Zealand coach Arthur Lydiard is credited with developing and promoting jogging for fitness, and American writers such as Kenneth H. Cooper (with Aerobics, 1968) and Jim Fixx (with The Complete Book of Running, 1977) helped turn recreational running and jogging into a worldwide movement.

Health effects

Regular jogging improves cardiorespiratory fitness and is associated with reduced risk of cardiovascular disease and improved mental health. Studies of recreational runners suggest modest regular jogging is linked to increased life expectancy, while very high volumes offer diminishing returns. Common injuries include shin splints and runner's knee, often from abrupt increases in training load.
