Zwarte Cross

The Zwarte Cross ("Black Cross") is the largest music and motorsport festival in the Netherlands and one of the largest outdoor festivals in Europe. Held annually near Lichtenvoorde in the Achterhoek region of Gelderland, it combines motocross racing with dozens of stages of music, theatre, comedy, and stunts, attracting around 200,000–230,000 visitors over its four days.

History

The festival grew out of a motocross event organized in 1997 by friends including members of the Achterhoek rock band Jovink en de Voederbietels — whose frontman Gijs Jolink and manager Hendrik Jan Lovink became the festival's driving forces. The first Zwarte Cross drew about 150 motocross participants and 1,000 spectators. Its name and cross motif come from the black cross associated with the local motocross tradition.

The festival

Uniquely, most motocross classes are open to anyone with a bike, and festival-goers race in comic vehicle classes — modified mopeds, sidecars, combine harvesters, and home-built machines — alongside serious motocross competition. Around the racing, the festival runs over thirty stages covering rock, pop, metal, dance, Dutch-language music, theatre, stand-up comedy, poetry, and children's programming, with an atmosphere of Achterhoek humour and surreal stunts. The organizing company, De Feestfabriek, is credited with making the once-modest local event a national phenomenon while retaining its regional dialect-flavoured identity. The festival was cancelled in 2020 and 2021 due to the COVID-19 pandemic and returned in 2022.
