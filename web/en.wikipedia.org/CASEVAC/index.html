CASEVAC

Casualty evacuation, known in military terminology as CASEVAC, is the movement of casualties from the battlefield to a medical treatment facility using whatever transport is available, without the dedicated medical personnel and equipment that distinguish medical evacuation (MEDEVAC).

Distinction from MEDEVAC

In United States and NATO usage, MEDEVAC is performed by dedicated medical platforms — marked ambulances or helicopters staffed by medical personnel — protected under the Geneva Conventions when unarmed and displaying the red cross. CASEVAC uses non-medical vehicles, which may be armed and are not so protected, but which may be faster or the only option available.

Practice

CASEVAC is requested using standard formats such as the 9-line brief. Ground vehicles, boats and aircraft of opportunity have all been used, from jeeps in the Second World War to helicopters in Korea, Vietnam, Iraq and Afghanistan. Care during CASEVAC is usually limited to first aid and Tactical Combat Casualty Care provided by the casualty's unit.

Timely evacuation within the "golden hour" after wounding is associated with improved survival.
