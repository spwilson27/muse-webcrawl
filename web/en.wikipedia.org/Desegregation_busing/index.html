Desegregation busing

Desegregation busing in the United States was the practice of assigning and transporting students to schools beyond their neighbourhoods in order to achieve racial integration following Brown v. Board of Education (1954).

History

The Supreme Court endorsed busing as a remedy in Swann v. Charlotte-Mecklenburg (1971). Programs in cities such as Boston and Louisville provoked fierce protests during the 1970s, while supporters credited busing with reducing segregation.

Decline

Court orders were progressively lifted from the 1990s, and many districts returned to neighbourhood schooling, accompanied by renewed racial segregation in numerous metropolitan areas.
