Dunedin (Māori: Ōtepoti) is the second-largest city in the South Island of New Zealand and the principal city of the Otago region. Located at the head of Otago Harbour on the south-east coast, it is known for its Scottish heritage, its Victorian and Edwardian architecture, and its large student population.

History

The area was originally settled by Māori, and the modern city was founded in 1848 by Scottish settlers of the Free Church of Scotland. Its name is the Gaelic form of Edinburgh. The discovery of gold inland in 1861 made Dunedin, for a time, New Zealand's largest and wealthiest city, funding grand buildings such as the University of Otago (founded 1869, the country's oldest university) and the Dunedin Railway Station.

Geography and landmarks

The city sprawls across hills around Otago Harbour and the Otago Peninsula, noted for wildlife including royal albatrosses and yellow-eyed penguins. Landmarks include Baldwin Street, once recognised as the world's steepest residential street; Larnach Castle; the Octagon city centre; and the Forsyth Barr Stadium. Nearby Aramoana and Kaikorai are among its coastal settlements.

Culture and education

Dunedin is a UNESCO City of Literature. The University of Otago and Otago Polytechnic give the city a youthful character; students are colloquially known as "Scarfies". In the 1980s the city was the centre of the "Dunedin sound", an influential jangle-pop music scene associated with Flying Nun Records bands such as The Chills and The Clean.
