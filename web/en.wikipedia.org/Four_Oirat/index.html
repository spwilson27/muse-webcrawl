Four Oirat

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Four Oirat (Mongolian: Дөрвөн Ойрад, Dörvön Oirad) was a confederation of the four principal Oirat Mongol tribes — the Khoshut, Torghut, Dzungar (Choros) and Dörbet — which dominated the western Mongolian steppe and large parts of Central Asia from the fifteenth to the eighteenth century.

History

The Oirats, forest peoples of western Mongolia, rose to power after the fall of the Yuan dynasty. By about 1450 their leader Esen Tayisi briefly united the Mongols and captured the Ming emperor at Tumu in 1449. After Esen's death the confederation fragmented but the four-tribe structure endured as the organizing framework of Oirat society.

In the seventeenth century the confederation transformed: the Khoshut migrated to the Kokonor (Qinghai) region and intervened in Tibet, where Güshi Khan's conquest of 1642 installed the Fifth Dalai Lama's government; the Torghut migrated west to the Volga steppe, forming the Kalmyk Khanate; and the remaining Choros and Dörbet built the Dzungar Khanate in the Ili valley, which became the last great nomadic empire of the steppe.

Decline

The Dzungar Khanate fought a long series of wars with the Qing dynasty. After the death of Galdan Tseren in 1745 and a succession crisis, the Qianlong Emperor's campaigns of 1755–1758 destroyed the khanate and killed or dispersed much of the Dzungar population. The Kalmyks, descendants of the Torghut, remain in Russia's Republic of Kalmykia; many Torghuts returned east in the famous migration of 1771.

Legacy

Oirat groups today include the Kalmyks of Russia, the Oirats of western Mongolia, and communities in Xinjiang and Qinghai. Their written tradition used the Clear Script (todo bichig), devised by Zaya Pandita in 1648.
