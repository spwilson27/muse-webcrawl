The Encyclopædia Britannica is a general-knowledge English-language encyclopedia, for more than two centuries one of the most authoritative reference works in the world. It was first published in Edinburgh, Scotland, in instalments between 1768 and 1771, and grew from three volumes into a vast multi-volume set produced with contributions from leading scholars. After 244 years in print, the final print edition appeared in 2010; the Britannica continues as an online encyclopedia.

The first edition (1768–1771) was published in Edinburgh

History

The Britannica was conceived by printer Colin Macfarquhar and engraver Andrew Bell and edited by the young scholar William Smellie, who wrote much of the first edition. Published in 100 weekly parts, it reflected the spirit of the Scottish Enlightenment. Successive editions expanded dramatically: the ninth edition (1875–1889), the "Scholar's Edition", was famed for its authoritative articles by figures such as James Clerk Maxwell and Lord Kelvin; the eleventh edition (1910–1911), produced with Cambridge University, is still celebrated for its literary quality. In the twentieth century ownership passed to American hands, and the fifteenth edition (1974) reorganised the work into the Micropædia, Macropædia, and Propædia.

Contributors and reputation

Britannica articles were traditionally written by invited experts — over 4,000 contributors for the fifteenth edition, including Nobel laureates. The work's reputation for reliability was tested in a 2005 Nature study comparing it with Wikipedia, which found comparable error rates in science articles (a conclusion Britannica disputed). Door-to-door sales of the print sets became a cultural fixture in the English-speaking world.

Digital transition

Britannica launched CD-ROM and online versions in the 1990s as print sales declined against cheap digital references and, later, Wikipedia. In 2012 the company announced it would cease print publication after the 2010 edition. Encyclopædia Britannica, Inc. now focuses on the Britannica website, educational products, and reference services.
