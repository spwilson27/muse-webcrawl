Lily Dale is a hamlet and gated community in the town of Pomfret, Chautauqua County, New York, United States. It is home to the Lily Dale Assembly, a Spiritualist organisation founded in 1879, and is frequently described as the world's largest centre for the religion of Spiritualism — the belief that the spirits of the dead can communicate with the living, often through mediums.

History

The community began as a camp meeting ground of the Free Religious Association of Cassadaga Lake, taking its name from the water lilies on the lake; it was incorporated as the Cassadaga Lake Free Association in 1879 and renamed the Lily Dale Assembly in 1906. In the movement's heyday, tens of thousands of visitors arrived each summer by train and steamer for lectures, séances and healing services. Notable visitors over the years included Susan B. Anthony, who spoke there in connection with the women's suffrage movement.

The community today

Lily Dale's Victorian cottages are largely owned by registered mediums, and residency requires membership of the Assembly. During the summer season (late June to early September) the Assembly runs a programme of workshops, public mediumship demonstrations at sites such as Inspiration Stump and the Forest Temple, healing services and lectures; admission is by a gate fee. The grounds include the Marion Skidmore Library — one of the largest collections of Spiritualist literature — a museum, hotels and the Leolyn Woods, a stand of old-growth forest.

In popular culture

Lily Dale has been the subject of documentaries and books, including Christine Wicker's Lily Dale: The Town That Talks to the Dead (2003), and featured in the HBO documentary No One Dies in Lily Dale (2010).
