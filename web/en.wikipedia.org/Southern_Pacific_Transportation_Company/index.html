Southern Pacific Transportation Company

The Southern Pacific Transportation Company (reporting mark SP), earlier Southern Pacific Railroad and Southern Pacific Company, was an American Class I railroad network that operated in the Western United States. Founded in 1865, it grew from land grants and acquisitions — most famously absorbing the Central Pacific Railroad, builder of the western half of the first transcontinental railroad — into a system of some 13,000 miles of track across California, Oregon, Nevada, Arizona, Texas, Louisiana, and other states.

History

Controlled for decades by the "Big Four" associates of the Central Pacific and later by E. H. Harriman, Southern Pacific dominated West Coast transport and famously wielded great political influence in California. Its passenger trains — the Daylight, the Sunset Limited, and the Lark — were celebrated streamliners. Freight operations included extensive refrigerator-car produce traffic from California's valleys.

After a failed merger with the Santa Fe in the 1980s, Southern Pacific was sold to Rio Grande Industries, owner of the Denver and Rio Grande Western, in 1988. In 1996 the combined system was acquired by Union Pacific, and the Southern Pacific name disappeared into its successor.
