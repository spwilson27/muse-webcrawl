MediSave is a national medical savings scheme in Singapore, introduced in 1984. Under the scheme, working Singaporeans and permanent residents set aside a portion of their income in a personal MediSave account, held within the Central Provident Fund (CPF), to pay for their own and their approved dependants' healthcare expenses.

How it works

Employees and their employers contribute monthly to the CPF, and the portion allocated to the MediSave Account varies by age, subject to the Basic Healthcare Sum. Balances earn interest and can be used only for approved purposes: hospitalisation and day surgery at approved institutions, certain outpatient treatments such as chronic disease management under the Chronic Disease Management Programme, approved vaccinations and health screenings, and premiums for MediShield Life and approved Integrated Shield Plans.

Role in the system

MediSave forms one of Singapore's "3M" healthcare financing pillars, alongside MediShield Life, a national health insurance scheme, and MediFund, a safety-net endowment fund for those unable to pay. The design emphasizes individual and family responsibility and co-payment, with withdrawal limits intended to preserve savings for old age and discourage over-consumption of healthcare.
