Perceptron

The perceptron is an algorithm for supervised learning of binary classifiers, and a type of artificial neuron. It was invented in 1957 by Frank Rosenblatt at the Cornell Aeronautical Laboratory, funded by the United States Office of Naval Research. The perceptron computes a weighted sum of its inputs and outputs 1 if the sum exceeds a threshold, and 0 otherwise.

History

Rosenblatt's Mark I Perceptron machine was one of the first artificial neural networks, generating great excitement and predictions of imminent machine intelligence. In 1969, Marvin Minsky and Seymour Papert's book Perceptrons showed that single-layer perceptrons cannot compute functions such as XOR, which cooled research funding and contributed to the first AI winter.

Learning rule

The perceptron learning algorithm updates weights whenever an example is misclassified, and is guaranteed to converge to a separating solution if the data are linearly separable. Multilayer networks of perceptron-like units, trained by backpropagation, later overcame the single-layer limitations and underpin modern deep learning.
