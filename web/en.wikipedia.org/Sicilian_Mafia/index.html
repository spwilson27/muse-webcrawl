Sicilian Mafia

The Sicilian Mafia, also known as Cosa Nostra, is a criminal society and criminal organization originating on the island of Sicily and dates to the 19th century. It is an association of criminal groups that share a common organizational structure and code of silence, known as omertà.

Origins

The Mafia emerged in western Sicily during the island's transition from feudalism to capitalism, as armed guards and estate managers filled the vacuum left by a weak state. After Italian unification in 1861, the organization consolidated its role as a provider of private protection and intermediary between landowners, peasants and the state.

Structure

The basic unit is the "family" (famiglia), which controls a territory, usually a town or neighbourhood. Families are coordinated regionally, and a provincial commission oversees disputes. Members swear an oath of silence and loyalty.

Activities and crackdowns

Historically involved in protection rackets, smuggling, construction and drug trafficking, the Sicilian Mafia was weakened by the Maxi Trial of 1986–87 and the state response to the 1992 assassinations of judges Giovanni Falcone and Paolo Borsellino. Salvatore Riina, head of the Corleonesi faction, was arrested in 1993; his successor Bernardo Provenzano was captured in 2006.
