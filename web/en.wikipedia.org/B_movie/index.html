B movie

A B movie or B film is a low-budget commercial motion picture. In its original usage during the Golden Age of Hollywood, the term more precisely identified films intended for distribution as the less-publicized bottom half of a double feature.

History

During the 1930s and 1940s, major studios and Poverty Row independents produced short, inexpensive genre films — westerns, crime pictures, horrors and serials — to fill double bills. Studios such as Republic and Monogram specialised in them.

Later usage

After the double feature faded, the term broadened to cover low-budget exploitation and genre films generally. Some B movies, including works by directors such as Roger Corman, gained cult followings, and the term now often carries affectionate connotations of unpretentious entertainment.
