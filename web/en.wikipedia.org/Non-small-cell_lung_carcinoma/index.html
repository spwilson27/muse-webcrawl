Non-small-cell lung carcinoma

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Non-small-cell lung carcinoma (NSCLC) is any type of epithelial lung cancer other than small-cell lung carcinoma. NSCLC accounts for about 85% of all lung cancers. The main subtypes are adenocarcinoma, squamous-cell carcinoma, and large-cell carcinoma. Compared with small-cell carcinoma, NSCLC generally grows more slowly but is less sensitive to chemotherapy.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms include persistent cough, coughing up blood, shortness of breath, chest pain, weight loss, and fatigue. Many cases are diagnosed at an advanced stage, when the cancer has spread beyond the lung.

Causes

Tobacco smoking is the dominant risk factor, though a substantial minority of cases — particularly adenocarcinomas — occur in never-smokers and are associated with genetic mutations such as EGFR, ALK rearrangements, ROS1, KRAS, and MET alterations. Radon exposure, asbestos, and air pollution also contribute.

Treatment

Early-stage disease may be cured by surgical resection, sometimes combined with adjuvant chemotherapy, targeted therapy, or immunotherapy. Advanced disease is treated according to molecular profile: EGFR inhibitors such as osimertinib, ALK inhibitors, and immune checkpoint inhibitors targeting PD-1/PD-L1 have substantially improved survival for subsets of patients.
