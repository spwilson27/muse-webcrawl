Wari

The Wari (also spelled Huari) were a Middle Horizon civilisation that flourished in the south-central Andes and coastal area of modern-day Peru from about 500 to 1000 AD. The Wari built one of the earliest empires in the Andes, predating the Inca Empire by several centuries.

History

The Wari capital, also called Wari (Huari), lay near modern Ayacucho. From there the Wari expanded through military conquest and colonisation, establishing administrative centres such as Pikillaqta near Cusco and Viracochapampa in the north, connected by roads.

Culture

Wari art is noted for abstract, highly stylised designs on ceramics and textiles, including large tunics (unku) woven with complex geometric patterns derived from Tiwanaku-influenced iconography. They practised terrace agriculture and built extensive storage facilities.

Decline

The empire fragmented around 1000 AD, possibly owing to prolonged drought. Administrative centres were abandoned or deliberately burned, and regional cultures re-emerged in the Late Intermediate Period.
