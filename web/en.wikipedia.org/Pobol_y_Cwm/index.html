Pobol y Cwm

Pobol y Cwm (Welsh for "People of the Valley") is a Welsh-language soap opera produced by the BBC and broadcast on S4C since 1982, having previously run on BBC Wales from its start in October 1974. It is the longest-running television soap opera produced by the BBC and one of the longest-running in Britain. The series is set in the fictional village of Cwmderi in the Gwendraeth Valley, Carmarthenshire, in south-west Wales.

History

Created by John Hefin and Gwenlyn Parry, the programme began on BBC Wales in 1974 and was shown across the BBC network in the 1970s–80s with English subtitles—at its peak attracting audiences in the millions and becoming one of the few Welsh-language programmes widely watched outside Wales. It transferred to the new Welsh channel S4C at its launch in 1982, where it became a flagship programme broadcast several times a week.

Setting and characters

Stories revolve around the inhabitants of Cwmderi, their families, farms, businesses, chapel, and pub, The Deri Arms. The show has tackled major social issues—including rural decline, alcoholism, LGBTQ storylines, and mental health—while remaining rooted in the comedy and drama of close-knit village life. Long-running characters and cast members, such as Harri Parri and Mark Jones, have become fixtures of Welsh popular culture.

Production

The series was filmed for many years at the BBC studios in Llandaff, Cardiff, with a purpose-built outdoor set, and production later moved to the BBC's Roath Lock studios in Cardiff Bay. It is produced by BBC Studios for S4C.
