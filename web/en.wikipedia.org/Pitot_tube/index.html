Pitot tube

A pitot tube is a pressure instrument used to measure fluid flow velocity. Invented by the French engineer Henri Pitot in the early 18th century, it consists of a tube pointing directly into the flow; the pressure at its tip (total or stagnation pressure) compared with the static pressure of the fluid gives the velocity through Bernoulli's principle.

Principle

The velocity is calculated from v = √(2(pt − ps)/ρ), where pt is total pressure, ps static pressure and ρ the fluid density. Combined pitot-static tubes concentrate both openings in a single probe.

Applications

Pitot tubes are standard equipment on aircraft, where pitot-static systems drive the airspeed indicator, altimeter and vertical speed indicator; blockage of pitot tubes by ice or insects has contributed to several accidents. They are also used in wind tunnels, industrial flow measurement, and motor racing.
