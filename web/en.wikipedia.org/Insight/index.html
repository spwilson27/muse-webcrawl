Insight

Insight is the understanding of a specific cause and effect within a particular context. The term has several related meanings: a piece of information, the act or result of understanding the inner nature of things, or seeing intuitively. It is considered a sudden comprehension after a trial-and-error learning process, and is often described as an "aha moment".

Psychology

In psychology, insight occurs when a solution to a problem presents itself quickly and without warning. Wolfgang Köhler's experiments with chimpanzees in the 1910s and 1920s, including the famous Sultan experiments, demonstrated that animals could solve problems through sudden restructuring of perception rather than incremental trial and error. Gestalt psychologists argued that insight involves reorganizing the elements of a problem into a new whole.

Problem solving

Insight problem solving is typically preceded by a period of incubation, during which conscious attention is withdrawn from the problem. Researchers such as Graham Wallas described stages of preparation, incubation, illumination, and verification. Neuroimaging studies have associated insight solutions with activity in the right anterior superior temporal gyrus and bursts of gamma-band activity.

Psychiatry

In psychiatry, insight refers to a patient's awareness of their own mental illness, its symptoms, and the need for treatment. Lack of insight, sometimes termed anosognosia, is common in schizophrenia and can affect treatment adherence and prognosis. Clinicians assess insight along dimensions such as awareness of illness, recognition of symptoms, and acceptance of treatment.
