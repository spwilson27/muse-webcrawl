Azerbaijani (also called Azeri), sometimes officially Azerbaijani Turkic or Azerbaijani Turkish, is a Turkic language of the Oghuz branch spoken primarily by the Azerbaijani people, who live mainly in the Republic of Azerbaijan and in northwestern Iran (Iranian Azerbaijan). It is the official language of the Republic of Azerbaijan and one of the official languages of Dagestan in Russia.

Varieties

Two main varieties are recognised: North Azerbaijani, spoken in the Republic of Azerbaijan and Russia and based on the Baku dialect, and South Azerbaijani, spoken in Iran. They differ somewhat in phonology, lexicon and syntax, and use different scripts: Latin in the north and a modified Perso-Arabic script in the south. The varieties remain largely mutually intelligible, as is Azerbaijani with Turkish to a considerable degree.

History

Azerbaijani developed from the eastern branch of Oghuz Turkic, spread into the Caucasus and northwestern Persia from the 11th century onwards, and became a literary language under the Safavids and later dynasties; the poet Fuzuli wrote in it in the 16th century. Its vocabulary has been strongly influenced by Persian and Arabic, and in the north also by Russian.

Alphabets

The language has changed script several times in the 20th century: from Arabic to Latin in 1929, to Cyrillic in 1939 under Soviet rule, and back to a Latin alphabet after independence in 1991, a transition completed by decree in 2001.
