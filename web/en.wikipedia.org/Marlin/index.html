Marlin
FamilyIstiophoridae (billfishes)
GeneraIstiompax, Makaira, Tetrapturus
HabitatTropical and subtropical oceans
DietSquid, tuna, mackerel and other fish

Marlins are fish of the family Istiophoridae, characterised by an elongated body, a spear-like snout or bill, and a long, rigid dorsal fin that can form a crest. They are fast-swimming predators of the open ocean and among the most prized game fish in the world.

Species

Recognised marlins include the blue marlin, black marlin, striped marlin and white marlin. The largest species can exceed 5 metres in length and 800 kilograms in weight. Females grow considerably larger than males.

Behaviour

Marlins are apex predators that feed on squid, tuna, mackerel and other schooling fish, using their bills to stun or slash prey. They are migratory, following warm currents over great distances, and are capable of bursts of speed estimated above 80 km/h, making them among the fastest fish in the sea.

Fishing

Marlin are celebrated in sport fishing and famously featured in Ernest Hemingway's novel The Old Man and the Sea. Several populations have declined under commercial fishing pressure, and catch-and-release practices are widely promoted in recreational fisheries.
