CAF Champions League

The CAF Champions League, known for sponsorship purposes as the TotalEnergies CAF Champions League, is an annual club football competition organised by the Confederation of African Football (CAF) and contested by top-division African clubs. It is the most prestigious club competition in African football.

History

The tournament began in 1964 as the African Cup of Champions Clubs. In 1997 it was rebranded as the CAF Champions League, adopting a group stage and increased prize money modelled partly on the UEFA Champions League.

Format

Clubs qualify through their domestic leagues; national champions and runners-up from stronger leagues enter preliminary rounds before a group stage of 16 teams, followed by knockout rounds and a final. The winner qualifies for the FIFA Club World Cup and plays the CAF Confederation Cup winner in the CAF Super Cup. Egypt's Al Ahly is the most successful club, with a record number of titles.
