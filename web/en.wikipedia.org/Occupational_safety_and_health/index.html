Occupational safety and health (OSH), also known as occupational health and safety (OHS), is a multidisciplinary field concerned with the safety, health, and welfare of people at work (i.e. while performing duties required from one's occupation). OSH is related to the fields of occupational medicine and occupational hygiene and aligns with workplace health promotion, and with environmental health in its concern for the effects of work on surrounding communities. The International Labour Organization (ILO) estimates that nearly 3 million people die each year from work-related accidents and diseases, and that hundreds of millions suffer non-fatal injuries.

History

Bernardino Ramazzini's De Morbis Artificum Diatriba (1700) founded occupational medicine by documenting the diseases of trades. Industrialisation brought factory legislation: Britain's Factory Acts (from 1833) created inspectorates, and coal mines, railways and chemical works generated the modern safety movement. Landmark disasters — the Triangle Shirtwaist fire (1911), the Hawks Nest Tunnel disaster (1930s), Bhopal (1984), and Rana Plaza (2013) — repeatedly drove reform. The US Occupational Safety and Health Act of 1970 created OSHA and NIOSH; the ILO adopted its core conventions, and in 2022 the International Labour Conference recognised a safe and healthy working environment as a fundamental principle and right at work.

Hazards and controls

Workplace hazards are conventionally grouped as safety hazards (falls, machinery, electricity, vehicles), biological (infections, moulds), physical (noise, radiation, heat, vibration), chemical (dusts such as silica and asbestos, solvents, gases), ergonomic, and psychosocial (workload, violence, bullying, shift work). The hierarchy of controls ranks interventions from most to least effective: elimination, substitution, engineering controls (e.g. ventilation, guarding), administrative controls (procedures, rotation), and finally personal protective equipment. Occupational exposure limits such as the ACGIH Threshold Limit Values guide control of airborne substances; management systems are formalised in ISO 45001.

Regulation and practice

Most jurisdictions place a duty on employers to provide a workplace free from recognised hazards, risk assessments, information and training, with enforcement by inspectorates and penalties, and compensation systems (often no-fault workers' compensation) for injured workers. Modern OSH practice emphasises worker participation, "safety culture", psychosocial risk management, and the extension of protection to informal, gig and migrant workers, where the burden of occupational harm is greatest and least recorded.
