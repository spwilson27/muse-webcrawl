Halo (safety device)

The halo is a driver crash-protection system used in open-wheel racing series, consisting of a curved titanium bar placed around the driver's head and connected to the monocoque at three points. It is designed to protect the driver's head from large flying debris, other vehicles and trackside objects while preserving an open cockpit.

Development

Following the deaths of Henry Surtees in 2009 and Justin Wilson in 2015, both killed by debris striking the head, and the fatal injuries sustained by Jules Bianchi in 2014, the FIA accelerated research into additional frontal head protection. The halo was tested extensively from 2016 and, after evaluation against alternatives such as the Red Bull Aeroscreen and the later Shield canopy concept, was mandated for Formula One and Formula 2 from the 2018 season, and subsequently adopted across most FIA open-wheel series.

Design

The device is made from grade 5 titanium and consists of a frontal hoop, a central front strut and rear mountings. It must withstand large static and impact loads — the FIA's tests required it to survive forces equivalent to the weight of a London bus resting on it. Aerodynamic fairings are permitted within limits.

Notable incidents

The halo has been credited with protecting drivers in several serious accidents, including Charles Leclerc at the 2018 Belgian Grand Prix, when Fernando Alonso's car rode over his; Romain Grosjean's fiery 2020 Bahrain crash, in which the halo helped deflect the barrier; the 2021 Italian Grand Prix collision in which Max Verstappen's car landed on Lewis Hamilton's; and Zhou Guanyu's roll at the 2022 British Grand Prix.
