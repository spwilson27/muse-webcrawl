Velocity

The velocity of an object is the rate of change of its position with respect to a frame of reference, and is a function of time. Velocity is equivalent to a specification of an object's speed and direction of motion: it is a vector quantity, whereas speed is a scalar. Velocity is a fundamental concept in kinematics, the branch of classical mechanics that describes the motion of bodies.

Definition

Average velocity is defined as displacement divided by the time interval: v = Δx/Δt. Instantaneous velocity is the limit as the interval approaches zero, i.e. the derivative of position with respect to time, v = dx/dt. The SI unit of velocity is the metre per second (m/s). The rate of change of velocity is acceleration.

Relative velocity

Velocity is always measured relative to a reference frame. The relative velocity of one object with respect to another is the vector difference of their velocities in a common frame. In special relativity, velocities combine according to the relativistic velocity-addition formula rather than simple addition, so that no velocity exceeds the speed of light.

Related concepts

Special cases include constant velocity (no acceleration), terminal velocity in a fluid, escape velocity, and angular velocity for rotating bodies. In fluid dynamics, the velocity field describes the motion of a fluid at every point.
