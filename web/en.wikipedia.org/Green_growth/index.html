Green growth

Green growth is an approach to economic development that aims to foster economic growth while ensuring that natural assets continue to provide the resources and environmental services on which human well-being depends. The concept was promoted internationally by the OECD, the World Bank and the United Nations following the 2008 financial crisis and was a central theme of the Rio+20 conference in 2012. It rests on the idea that environmental degradation is a market failure that can be corrected through pricing, regulation, innovation policy and investment in green infrastructure.

Renewable energy investment is a common green growth strategy

Policies and debate

Green growth strategies typically include carbon pricing, removal of fossil-fuel subsidies, support for renewable energy and energy efficiency, and natural capital accounting. Supporters argue that such measures can drive innovation and create jobs while decoupling output from emissions and resource use. Critics, including advocates of degrowth, argue that absolute decoupling at the required scale and speed lacks empirical support, and that efficiency gains are partly offset by rebound effects. Some countries, notably South Korea with its 2008 Low Carbon, Green Growth strategy, adopted the framework explicitly in national policy.
