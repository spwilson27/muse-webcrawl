Middle-distance running

Middle-distance running events are track races longer than sprints, up to 3000 metres. The standard middle distances are the 800 metres, 1500 metres, the mile (1,609.34 m), and the 3000 metres. Middle-distance events demand a combination of speed and endurance: runners must sustain a fast pace while managing aerobic and anaerobic energy systems, and tactical positioning and a finishing kick are often decisive.

The 800 metres is run in two laps of a standard track and the 1500 metres is the premier championship "metric mile". Historically, notable middle-distance runners include Sebastian Coe, Steve Ovett and Steve Cram in the British golden age of the early 1980s, Hicham El Guerrouj of Morocco, who set enduring 1500 m and mile world records in the late 1990s, and Faith Kipyegon of Kenya, a multiple Olympic and world champion at 1500 m.

Training

Middle-distance training blends interval work at race pace, longer tempo runs, hill work, and strength training, with periodised plans peaking for championship season. Races below 800 metres such as the 600 metres, and longer "long middle distance" events such as 2000 metres, are contested less frequently, often indoors or in schools competition.
