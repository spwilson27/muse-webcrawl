Dialect continuum

A dialect continuum or dialect chain is a series of language varieties spoken across a geographical area such that neighbouring varieties are mutually intelligible, while varieties at the extremes of the range may not be intelligible to each other at all.

Examples

Classic examples include the West Germanic continuum, in which local dialects once shaded gradually from the Netherlands through Germany; the North Slavic continuum; the Arabic dialect continuum across North Africa and the Middle East; and the Romance continuum linking varieties from Portugal to Italy. The continuum between Hindi and Urdu in northern India is another well-known case.

Implications

Continua complicate the distinction between "language" and "dialect": boundaries drawn through a continuum are often political and historical rather than linguistic. Standardisation, education, and mass media can erode continua by levelling local varieties toward national standard languages, while political borders may harden dialect differences into separate standard languages, as with Serbian, Croatian, and Bosnian.
