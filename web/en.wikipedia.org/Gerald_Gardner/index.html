Gerald Brosseau Gardner (13 June 1884 – 12 February 1964), also known by the craft name Scire, was an English civil servant, amateur anthropologist and occultist who published some of the first books about the neopagan religion of Wicca. He is widely regarded as the founder of modern Wicca; the tradition descending from his coven is called Gardnerian Wicca.

Gardner promoted Wicca publicly after the repeal of the Witchcraft Act in 1951.

Life

Born into a wealthy timber-importing family, Gardner was plagued by asthma and spent much of his career in the Far East — Ceylon, Borneo and Malaya — as a tea and rubber planter and later a customs official. He pursued interests in archaeology, local magic and the kris dagger (his first book was Keris and Other Malay Weapons, 1936). Retiring to England in 1936, he settled near the New Forest and joined occult circles, including the Rosicrucian Order Crotona Fellowship.

Founding of Wicca

Gardner claimed that in 1939 he was initiated into a surviving witch coven in the New Forest led by "Old Dorothy" Clutterbuck, which he said practised a pre-Christian religion — claims historians regard with scepticism, concluding that Gardner largely created the rituals himself, drawing on ceremonial magic, Freemasonry, Margaret Murray's witch-cult hypothesis, Charles Leland's Aradia and the works of Aleister Crowley (whom he met in 1947). After the Witchcraft Act was repealed in 1951, Gardner published Witchcraft Today (1954) presenting "Wica" as an ancient nature religion, and founded his own coven at Bricket Wood. He worked with Doreen Valiente, who rewrote much of his Book of Shadows in more graceful form. Gardner also founded a witchcraft museum on the Isle of Man. He died in 1964 aboard ship returning from Lebanon.

Legacy

Though his historical claims are not accepted by scholars, Gardner's synthesis launched a worldwide religious movement; Wicca and derived traditions have hundreds of thousands of adherents, and "Gardnerian" remains the benchmark initiatory lineage within modern witchcraft.
