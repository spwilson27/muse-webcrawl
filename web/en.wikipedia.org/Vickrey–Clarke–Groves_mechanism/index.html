Vickrey–Clarke–Groves mechanism

VCG mechanism
Field: mechanism design, game theory
Named after: William Vickrey, Edward H. Clarke, Theodore Groves
Property: dominant-strategy incentive compatible

The Vickrey–Clarke–Groves (VCG) mechanism is a generalisation of the Vickrey auction to settings with many goods and arbitrary allocations. It is a truthful mechanism: each participant maximises their own utility by reporting their true valuation, whatever the others do.

Definition

Each agent i reports a valuation function. The mechanism chooses the allocation that maximises the sum of the reported valuations. Agent i then pays the externality they impose on the others: the difference between the maximum total welfare of the others in i's absence and their welfare under the chosen allocation. Because an agent's payment does not depend on their own report except through the allocation chosen, misreporting can only hurt them.

Applications and limits

VCG mechanisms underlie spectrum auctions, online advertising allocation, and scheduling problems. Their drawbacks include vulnerability to collusion, the possibility of low or zero revenue, computational difficulty of the winner-determination problem, and the fact that payments may not cover costs in exchange settings.
