Logo (programming language)

Logo is an educational programming language, designed in 1967 by Wally Feurzeig, Seymour Papert and Cynthia Solomon. Its most famous feature is turtle graphics, in which commands direct an on-screen (or floor-roving mechanical) "turtle" to move and draw, making programming concepts tangible for children. Logo is a dialect of Lisp adapted for education, with a philosophy of "low threshold, high ceiling" and no threshold for beginners.

History and philosophy

Logo was developed at Bolt, Beranek and Newman (BBN) in Cambridge, Massachusetts. Seymour Papert, who had worked with developmental psychologist Jean Piaget, promoted the language as part of his theory of constructionism — the idea that children learn best by making things — set out in his 1980 book Mindstorms. Logo spread widely in the 1980s through versions for home computers such as the Apple II, Commodore 64 and BBC Micro, and through implementations like Apple Logo and LogoWriter.

Features

Logo commands such as FORWARD 100 and RIGHT 90, abbreviated FD and RT, move the turtle, while REPEAT loops and user-defined procedures (introduced with TO) teach iteration and abstraction. Despite its reputation as a children's language, Logo supports lists, recursion, file handling and other serious programming structures. Descendant systems include the multi-agent environments StarLogo and NetLogo, and the block-based language Scratch grew out of the same educational tradition.
