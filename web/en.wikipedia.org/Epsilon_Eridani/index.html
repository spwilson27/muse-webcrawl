Epsilon Eridani

Epsilon Eridani (ε Eridani) is a star in the southern constellation of Eridanus. At a distance of about 10.5 light-years, it is one of the Sun's nearest neighbors and the third-closest star (or star system) visible to the naked eye. It is a main-sequence star of spectral class K2, somewhat smaller, cooler, and less luminous than the Sun, with an estimated age of roughly 400–800 million years.

Planetary system and debris disks

Epsilon Eridani is surrounded by an extensive system of dust and debris disks, including an asteroid-belt analogue and outer belts reminiscent of the Kuiper belt, indicating ongoing planet formation processes. A Jupiter-like exoplanet, Epsilon Eridani b (also called AEgir), orbiting at about 3.4 AU with a period of roughly 7 years, was inferred from radial velocity observations; its existence and properties have been debated, and it has not been directly imaged. The star's youth and proximity make the system a prime target for direct imaging searches for planets.

In culture

As one of the nearest Sun-like stars, Epsilon Eridani was one of the two targets of Project Ozma, the first modern search for extraterrestrial intelligence, in 1960. It appears frequently in science fiction as the location of fictional inhabited planets.
