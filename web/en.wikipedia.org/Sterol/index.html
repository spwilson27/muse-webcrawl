Sterol

Sterol
TypeSteroid alcohol (lipid)
ExamplesCholesterol, ergosterol, sitosterol, stigmasterol

Sterols are a subgroup of steroids: organic molecules with a characteristic four-ring structure and a hydroxyl group at the 3-position. They are essential components of cell membranes in eukaryotes, regulating membrane fluidity and serving as precursors to hormones and vitamins.

Types

Cholesterol is the principal sterol in animals (a zoosterol). Plants contain phytosterols such as β-sitosterol, campesterol, and stigmasterol; fungi contain ergosterol, the target of many antifungal drugs and the precursor of vitamin D2. Cholesterol is the precursor of all steroid hormones, bile acids, and vitamin D.

Function and Health

Sterols insert among phospholipids in membranes, buffering fluidity across temperatures. Plant sterols compete with cholesterol absorption in the gut and are added to some foods to lower LDL cholesterol.
