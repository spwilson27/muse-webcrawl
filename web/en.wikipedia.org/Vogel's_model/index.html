Vogel's model

Vogel's model is a mathematical model of the arrangement of florets in the head of a flower, such as a sunflower, and of other spiral patterns in plants (phyllotaxis). It was proposed by the physicist Helmut Vogel in 1979. In the model, the nth floret is placed in polar coordinates at a fixed divergence angle from the previous one and at a radius growing with the square root of n, producing the familiar interlocking spirals of seeds.

The model

The position of the nth floret is given by

  • r = c√n
  • θ = n × 137.508°

where c is a constant scaling factor. The angle 137.508° is the golden angle, derived from the golden ratio φ: it is 360° × (1 − 1/φ), approximately. Because the golden ratio is the "most irrational" number, successive florets never line up in radial rows, and the packing is optimally dense and uniform. The resulting pattern displays families of spirals (parastichies) whose counts are consecutive Fibonacci numbers — the numbers of spirals visible in real sunflower heads.

Significance

Vogel's simple two-equation model showed that complex botanical patterns need no genetic blueprint for each floret: a constant divergence angle at the growing tip suffices. It is widely used in computer graphics to synthesize realistic flowers and seed heads, and it connects phyllotaxis to number theory, continued fractions and optimal packing.
