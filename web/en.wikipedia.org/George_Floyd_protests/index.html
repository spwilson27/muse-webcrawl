The George Floyd protests were a series of protests and civil unrest that began in Minneapolis, Minnesota, on May 26, 2020, following the murder of George Floyd by Minneapolis police officer Derek Chauvin during an arrest the previous day. Demonstrations quickly spread to more than 2,000 cities and towns in the United States and to over 60 countries, in support of the Black Lives Matter movement.

Course of the protests

Protests in Minneapolis turned to widespread unrest in late May 2020, including the burning of the Minneapolis Police Department's 3rd Precinct building. Governors in many states activated the National Guard, and numerous cities imposed curfews; at one point some 62,000 Guard members were deployed across 30 states. Polls estimated between 15 and 26 million people participated in the United States, making the movement among the largest protest movements in American history.

Demands and impact

Protesters demanded prosecution of the officers involved, police reform, and broader action against systemic racism. Slogans included "Black Lives Matter", "I can't breathe", and "Defund the police". The protests led to police reforms in various jurisdictions, removal of Confederate monuments, changes in corporate and media practices, and the introduction of the George Floyd Justice in Policing Act in Congress. Internationally, solidarity protests addressed local histories of racism and colonial monuments. While most protests were peaceful, some involved property destruction and clashes with police, and at least 25 deaths were linked to the unrest.
