Erik Thorvaldsson (Old Norse: Eiríkr Þorvaldsson; c. 950 – c. 1003), known as Erik the Red, was a Norse explorer remembered in medieval Icelandic sagas as the founder of the first European settlement in Greenland. His nickname is usually explained by his red hair and beard, though some accounts connect it to his temper or to killings in which he was involved.

Life

Erik was born in Rogaland, Norway, the son of Thorvald Asvaldsson, who was exiled to Iceland for manslaughter. In Iceland, Erik settled at Haukadalur but was himself outlawed for three years around 982 after killings arising from feuds. During his exile he sailed west and explored the coast of a land sighted earlier by Gunnbjörn Ulfsson, spending his exile exploring its fjords.

Settlement of Greenland

Returning to Iceland in 985, Erik promoted the new land — which he named Greenland, according to the sagas, because "men would be more readily persuaded thither if the land had a good name" — and led a colonising fleet of 25 ships, of which 14 arrived. He established the Eastern Settlement and his own estate, Brattahlíð, in present-day Qassiarsuk, where he became the leading chieftain of Norse Greenland. The colony eventually comprised the Eastern and Western Settlements and lasted for nearly 500 years.

Family and death

Erik's wife was Thjodhild, who converted to Christianity after their son Leif Erikson returned from Norway with a priest; Erik himself remained a pagan. Leif's voyage to Vinland in North America, around the year 1000, followed his father's example of western exploration. Erik died in an epidemic in the winter of about 1003–1004, shortly after Leif's return.

Sources

The main sources are the Saga of Erik the Red and the Saga of the Greenlanders, written down centuries after the events but preserving older traditions; ruins at Qassiarsuk are identified with Brattahlíð.
