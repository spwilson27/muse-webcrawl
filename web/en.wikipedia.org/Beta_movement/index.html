Beta movement

The beta movement is an optical illusion of apparent motion, first described by Max Wertheimer in 1912, in which a series of still images shown in rapid succession is perceived as smooth continuous movement.

Description

When two or more stimuli are presented in sequence at different positions with suitable timing, observers perceive a single object moving from the first position to the second, rather than two separate flashes. Beta movement occurs at longer intervals between stimuli than the related phi phenomenon, in which motion is perceived without an object appearing to move.

Applications

The effect underlies motion perception in film, animation and LED signage, where frame rates of around 10–12 images per second or more are sufficient for convincing apparent motion.
