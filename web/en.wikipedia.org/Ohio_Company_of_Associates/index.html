Ohio Company of Associates

The Ohio Company of Associates, also known as the Ohio Associate Company, was a land company formed on March 3, 1786, by General Rufus Putnam, Benjamin Tupper, Samuel Holden Parsons and Manasseh Cutler, Revolutionary War veterans, for the purpose of settling the Ohio Country. In 1787 the company purchased roughly 1.5 million acres from the United States Congress under the terms negotiated by Cutler, and in 1788 its settlers founded Marietta, the first permanent American settlement in the Northwest Territory.

Purchase

Manasseh Cutler lobbied Congress in New York in the summer of 1787. The resulting contract, signed in October 1787, granted the company an option on up to 5 million acres, of which it initially took about 1.5 million acres at roughly two-thirds of a dollar per acre, payable in depreciated government securities. The negotiations coincided with, and by some accounts influenced, the passage of the Northwest Ordinance of 1787, which established government for the territory and prohibited slavery there. The purchase reserved sections for schools, a university (which became Ohio University), religion, and future sale.

Settlement

In December 1787 an advance party of 48 men under Rufus Putnam left Massachusetts and Connecticut, travelling overland to Sumrill's Ferry on the Youghiogheny River, where they built the flatboat Adventure Galley (later called the Mayflower). They arrived at the mouth of the Muskingum River on 7 April 1788 and founded Marietta, named in honour of Queen Marie Antoinette of France. The company built Campus Martius, a fortified enclosure, and laid out farms in the surrounding "Donation Tract" after the Big Bottom massacre of 1791 during the Northwest Indian War.

Legacy

Financial difficulties, title problems in the eastern portion of the purchase, and the depreciation of its paper left the company unable to complete payments on the full grant; Congress adjusted the arrangement in 1792. The company wound up its affairs over the following decades, but its settlement at Marietta set the pattern for the organised settlement of the Old Northwest.
