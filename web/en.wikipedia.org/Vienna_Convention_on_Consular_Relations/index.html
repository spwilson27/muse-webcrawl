Vienna Convention on Consular Relations

Signed24 April 1963, Vienna, Austria
Effective19 March 1967
Parties182 states

The Vienna Convention on Consular Relations is an international treaty that defines a framework for consular relations between sovereign states. Adopted in 1963 and in force since 1967, it codifies many consular practices that originated in custom: the functions of consulates, the privileges and immunities of consular officers and premises, and the rights of nationals abroad.

Consular notification

Article 36, providing that arrested or detained foreign nationals must be informed without delay of their right to have their consulate notified and to communicate with consular officers, has been litigated before the International Court of Justice in cases including LaGrand (Germany v. United States) and Avena (Mexico v. United States), arising from death-row cases in the US.
