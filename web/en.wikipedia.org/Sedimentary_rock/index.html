Sedimentary rocks are types of rock formed by the accumulation or deposition of mineral or organic particles at Earth's surface, followed by cementation. Sedimentation is the collective name for processes that cause these particles to settle in place. Sedimentary rocks cover between 73 and 75 percent of Earth's current land surface, although they make up only a small fraction of the crust by volume. Because they form at or near the surface, they record the history of surface environments, climates, and life — including nearly all known fossils.

Formation

The particles that form a sedimentary rock are called sediment, and may be composed of geological detritus (minerals) or biological detritus (organic matter). The geological detritus originated from the weathering and erosion of existing rocks, or from the solidification of molten rock fragments. Sediment is transported by water, ice (glaciers), or wind, and deposited in layers. Burial under further sediment subjects it to increasing pressure and temperature: compaction squeezes out water and cementation precipitates minerals such as calcite, silica, or iron oxide between the grains. Together these processes, called diagenesis or lithification, convert loose sediment into solid rock.

Classification

Clastic sedimentary rocks, such as sandstone, shale, siltstone, and conglomerate, are composed of rock and mineral fragments classified by grain size on the Wentworth scale. Chemical sedimentary rocks, such as rock salt (halite) and some limestones, form when minerals precipitate directly from solution, often by evaporation. Organic or biochemical rocks, such as most limestones, chalk, and coal, form from the accumulated remains of organisms — shells, coral, plant matter — or by biologically mediated precipitation.

Importance

Sedimentary rocks contain the fossil record used to reconstruct the evolution of life and past environments, and their layered structure (stratigraphy) established the relative geological time scale. They are economically vital: they host most of the world's oil, natural gas, and groundwater reservoirs, and provide coal, limestone for cement, sand and gravel for construction, and ores such as ironstone and phosphorite. Famous sedimentary landscapes include the Grand Canyon, whose walls expose nearly two billion years of strata.
