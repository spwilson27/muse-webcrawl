Bartolomeu Dias (c. 1450 – 29 May 1500) was a Portuguese nobleman and explorer. In 1488 he became the first European navigator to round the southern tip of Africa, proving that the Atlantic and Indian oceans were connected and opening the sea route from Europe to India.

Voyage of 1487–1488

King John II of Portugal appointed Dias to search for a sea route to India and for the fabled Christian kingdom of Prester John. Dias sailed from Lisbon in August 1487 with two caravels and a supply ship. Following the African coast past the farthest point reached by Diogo Cão, he was driven south by a great storm and, after thirteen days out of sight of land, found the coast trending east and then north — he had rounded the Cape without seeing it.

Dias explored eastward as far as the Great Fish River in modern South Africa before his crews compelled him to turn back. On the return voyage he sighted the Cape itself, which he named the "Cape of Storms" (Cabo das Tormentas); King John renamed it the Cape of Good Hope, for the promise it held of a route to India. Dias erected stone pillars (padrões) along the coast to mark Portuguese claims.

Later career and death

Dias later superintended the building of the ships São Gabriel and São Rafael used by Vasco da Gama, whose 1497 expedition he accompanied as far as the Cape Verde Islands. In 1500 he sailed with Pedro Álvares Cabral's fleet, which reached Brazil, but on the onward voyage his ship was lost with all hands in a storm in the South Atlantic on 29 May 1500, not far from the Cape he had discovered.

Legacy

Dias's voyage was a landmark of the Age of Discovery: it demonstrated the feasibility of the sea route to India that Vasco da Gama completed a decade later. Dias is commemorated in Luís de Camões's epic Os Lusíadas and by place names such as Dias Point in Namibia.
