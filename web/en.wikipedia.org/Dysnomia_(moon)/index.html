Dysnomia (moon)

Discovery10 September 2005; Michael E. Brown and the Keck Observatory team
OrbitsEris (dwarf planet)
Orbital period15.774 days
Mean radiusapprox. 350 km
Designation(136199) Eris I Dysnomia; provisional S/2005 (136199) 1

Dysnomia, formal designation (136199) Eris I Dysnomia, is the only known moon of the dwarf planet Eris and one of the largest moons of a dwarf planet in the Solar System. It was discovered on 10 September 2005 by Michael E. Brown and colleagues using the laser guide star adaptive optics system at the W. M. Keck Observatory in Hawaii, a few months after Eris itself was announced.

Name

Before receiving its official name, Dysnomia was nicknamed "Gabrielle" by its discoverers, after the companion of Xena — the working nickname for Eris. The official name, approved by the International Astronomical Union in 2006, refers to Dysnomia, the Greek goddess (daemon) of lawlessness and daughter of Eris, the goddess of strife. The name also alludes to actress Lucy Lawless, who played Xena: Warrior Princess.

Properties

Dysnomia is estimated to be roughly 700 km in diameter, though measurements of its thermal emission suggest it could be smaller — in the range of several hundred kilometres — and is much darker than Eris, with an albedo considerably lower than Eris's highly reflective methane-ice surface. It orbits Eris in a nearly circular orbit at a distance of about 37,300 km with a period of 15.774 days. Observations of this orbit allowed astronomers to determine Eris's mass precisely, showing Eris to be about 27% more massive than Pluto — a measurement central to the debate that led to the IAU's 2006 definition of a planet and the reclassification of Eris and Pluto as dwarf planets.

Formation

Dysnomia is thought, like the moons of Pluto and Haumea, to have formed from debris generated by a giant collision between Eris and another Kuiper Belt object early in the Solar System's history.
