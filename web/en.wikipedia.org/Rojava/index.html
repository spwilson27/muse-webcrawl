Rojava

Rojava, officially the Autonomous Administration of North and East Syria (AANES), is a de facto autonomous region in northeastern Syria. Its name derives from the Kurdish word for "west," denoting western Kurdistan. Established amid the Syrian civil war, the administration governs a multi-ethnic territory of Kurds, Arabs, Assyrians, and others and organises society on the basis of democratic confederalism, the political philosophy of Abdullah Öcalan.

History

As Syrian government forces withdrew from northern areas in 2012, Kurdish parties led by the Democratic Union Party (PYD) assumed control. Cantons were declared in 2014, and after the defence of Kobanî against the Islamic State (2014–15), backed by a U.S.-led coalition, the multi-ethnic Syrian Democratic Forces (SDF) expanded the territory, capturing Raqqa in 2017. Turkey, which views the administration's dominant militia, the YPG, as linked to the PKK, launched cross-border operations in 2018 and 2019, occupying Afrin and a border strip.

Politics and society

The administration emphasises grassroots communes, co-presidency by women and men in public offices, ethnic quotas, and ecology, influenced by Murray Bookchin's libertarian municipalism. The Women's Protection Units (YPJ) became internationally known during the fight against the Islamic State. Critics have raised concerns about conscription and the dominance of the PYD, while supporters hail the region as a model of secular, pluralist governance in the Middle East.
