Walk-off home run

A walk-off home run is a home run hit by the home team in the bottom of the ninth inning or in the bottom of an extra inning that gives the home team the lead and thereby ends the game. It is called a "walk-off" because the teams can walk off the field immediately after the run scores.

The term "walk-off" was attributed to Oakland Athletics pitcher Dennis Eckersley in the late 1980s, though the phrase gained wider currency after Kirk Gibson's famous walk-off home run off Eckersley in Game 1 of the 1988 World Series.

Rules and conditions

A walk-off home run can only occur when the home team is batting in the final inning of a game that is tied or in which it trails by no more than three runs (so that the home run itself can put the team ahead). If the home team is already ahead, the game ends after the top half of the inning and no bottom half is played.

Under Major League Baseball rules, all runs driven in by a walk-off home run count, even if the winning run scores before the batter completes his circuit of the bases — a rule clarified after Robin Ventura's "Grand Slam Single" in the 1999 National League Championship Series, when Ventura was mobbed by teammates between first and second base and only the winning run was credited.

Notable examples

  • Bill Mazeroski's home run in Game 7 of the 1960 World Series — the only walk-off home run in a World Series Game 7.
  • Kirk Gibson's pinch-hit home run, Game 1 of the 1988 World Series.
  • Joe Carter's home run in Game 6 of the 1993 World Series.
  • Kirby Puckett's home run in Game 6 of the 1991 World Series.

Records

Walk-off home runs to end a postseason series are rare. Jim Leyritz, David Ortiz, and others have hit famous postseason walk-offs, while in regular-season play players such as Jim Thome hold records for career walk-off home runs.
