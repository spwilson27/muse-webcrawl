Mount Washington is the highest peak in the Northeastern United States at 1,917 metres (6,288 ft) and the most topographically prominent mountain east of the Mississippi River. It lies in the Presidential Range of the White Mountains of New Hampshire.

Weather

The summit is famous for exceptionally severe weather. On 12 April 1934 the Mount Washington Observatory recorded a wind speed of 372 km/h (231 mph), a world record for a surface station that stood for decades. The summit averages hurricane-force winds more than 100 days a year, with a climate classified as subarctic/alpine tundra.

Access

The Mount Washington Cog Railway, opened in 1869, climbs the western slope, while the Mount Washington Auto Road (1861) ascends from the east. The Appalachian Trail crosses the summit, and the Tuckerman Ravine trail is a classic spring skiing venue. The summit buildings form Mount Washington State Park.
