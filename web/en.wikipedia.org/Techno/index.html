Techno is a genre of electronic dance music that emerged in Detroit, Michigan, in the mid-1980s. It is characterised by a repetitive instrumental groove, usually built around a four-on-the-floor beat at roughly 120–150 beats per minute, with heavy use of drum machines — most famously the Roland TR-909 and TR-808 — synthesisers, and sequencers. The term was popularised in 1988 by the compilation Techno! The New Dance Sound of Detroit.

Origins

The genre was pioneered by the so-called "Belleville Three" — Juan Atkins, Derrick May, and Kevin Saunderson — school friends from Belleville, Michigan, who combined the machine funk of Parliament-Funkadelic and Kraftwerk's European electronic music with Chicago house. Atkins's Cybotron project and his Model 500 releases, May's "Strings of Life" (as Rhythim Is Rhythim), and Saunderson's Inner City project defined the first wave.

Spread and development

Techno found an especially devoted audience in Europe after the late-1980s acid house explosion. Following the fall of the Berlin Wall, a second wave of Detroit artists and a new generation of European producers — particularly in Berlin, home to the Tresor club and record label — developed harder and more minimal variants. Subgenres that followed include minimal techno, acid techno, dub techno, industrial techno, and melodic techno. Detroit's annual Movement festival and Berlin venues such as Berghain remain central institutions of the culture.

Characteristics

Techno tracks are usually instrumental, constructed in a digital audio workstation or with hardware sequencers, and built from loops and gradual layering rather than verse-chorus song structure. Its aesthetic has often been described as futuristic or machine-like, reflecting Detroit's industrial landscape and the Afrofuturist themes in the work of its originators.
