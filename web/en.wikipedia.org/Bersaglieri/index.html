Bersaglieri

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Bersaglieri (Italian for "sharpshooters") are a troop of marksmen in the Italian Army's infantry corps. They were originally created by General Alessandro Ferrero La Marmora on 18 June 1836 to serve in the Army of the Kingdom of Sardinia, later becoming part of the Royal Italian Army. They have always been a high-mobility light infantry unit, and can still be recognized by the distinctive wide-brimmed hat with black capercaillie feathers worn with the uniform.

History

The Bersaglieri were designed as fast-moving skirmishers; in the nineteenth century they famously marched at a running pace, the passo di corsa, a tradition maintained at parades today. They distinguished themselves in the Crimean War, the Italian wars of independence — including the breach of Porta Pia in 1870 that completed Italian unification — and both world wars. During the First World War several battalions served as cyclist troops; in the Second World War Bersaglieri regiments fought in North Africa and on the Eastern Front.

Today

Modern Bersaglieri regiments are mechanized infantry units of the Italian Army, including the 1st, 6th, 7th, 8th and 11th Bersaglieri Regiments and the Garibaldi Brigade. Their hat plumes and fanfare bands marching at a jog remain iconic symbols of the Italian military.
