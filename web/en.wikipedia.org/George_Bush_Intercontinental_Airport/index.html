George Bush Intercontinental Airport (IATA: IAH) is an international airport in Houston, Texas, United States, serving the Greater Houston metropolitan area. Located about 23 miles (37 km) north of Downtown Houston, it is one of the busiest airports in the United States and the largest hub for United Airlines.

Aerial view of the airport and its five terminals.

History

The airport opened in June 1969 as Houston Intercontinental Airport, built to replace the increasingly constrained William P. Hobby Airport as the city's principal airport. It was renamed in 1997 in honour of George H. W. Bush, the 41st President of the United States, who maintained a home in Houston. The airport served as a major hub for Continental Airlines until that carrier's merger with United in 2010–2012.

Facilities

The airport covers about 10,000 acres and has five runways and five terminals (A through E), connected by an underground train and an above-ground Skyway. Terminal E handles many international arrivals, and the airport offers nonstop service to destinations across North America, Latin America, Europe, Asia, and Oceania. It is consistently ranked among the busiest airports in the United States by international passenger traffic.
