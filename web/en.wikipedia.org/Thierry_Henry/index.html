Thierry Daniel Henry (born 17 August 1977) is a French professional football coach, pundit, and former player. Considered one of the greatest strikers of all time and one of the greatest players in Premier League history, he was known for his pace, finishing, and elegance on the ball.

Career

Henry began his career at AS Monaco under Arsène Wenger, winning Ligue 1 in 1997. After a brief, unhappy spell at Juventus playing on the wing, he rejoined Wenger at Arsenal in 1999 for a club-record fee. Converted into a central striker, he became Arsenal's all-time top scorer with 228 goals, winning two Premier League titles (including the unbeaten "Invincibles" season of 2003–04) and two FA Cups, and finishing runner-up for the Ballon d'Or in 2003.

In 2007 he moved to Barcelona, where he was part of the historic treble-winning side of 2008–09 under Pep Guardiola, alongside Lionel Messi and Samuel Eto'o. He later played for the New York Red Bulls in Major League Soccer before retiring in 2014. With France, Henry won the 1998 FIFA World Cup and UEFA Euro 2000, and remains one of his country's most-capped players and record scorers.

Later career

After retirement Henry became a television pundit and coach, serving as an assistant coach of Belgium at the 2018 World Cup and as head coach of Monaco and the Montreal Impact, and subsequently coaching France's under-21 and Olympic teams, winning silver at the Paris 2024 Olympics.
