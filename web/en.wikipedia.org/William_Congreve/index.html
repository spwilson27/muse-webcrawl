William Congreve

Born24 January 1670, Bardsey, Yorkshire, England
Died19 January 1729 (aged 58), London, England
OccupationPlaywright, poet
Notable worksThe Way of the World, Love for Love, The Mourning Bride

William Congreve (24 January 1670 – 19 January 1729) was an English playwright and poet of the Restoration period, celebrated for his witty comedies of manners, above all The Way of the World (1700), widely regarded as one of the greatest English comedies.

Life and works

Educated at Trinity College, Dublin, Congreve came to London and published the novel Incognita (1692) before turning to the stage. His first play, The Old Bachelor (1693), was a triumph, followed by The Double Dealer (1693), Love for Love (1695), and the tragedy The Mourning Bride (1697), source of the famous line "Music has charms to soothe a savage breast." The Way of the World initially met a cool reception, and Congreve largely withdrew from the theatre, living on government posts and literary reputation.

Legacy

Congreve's dialogue, especially the proviso scene between Mirabell and Millamant, remains the high-water mark of Restoration comedy.
