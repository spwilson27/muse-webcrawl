Afrika Korps

Afrika Korps
Active: 1941–1943
Country: Nazi Germany
Branch: German Army (Heer)
Role: Expeditionary armoured force
Engagements: North African campaign

The Afrika Korps (German: Deutsches Afrikakorps, DAK) was the German expeditionary force in Africa during the North African campaign of the Second World War. First sent as a holding force to shore up Italy's defence of its African colonies, the formation fought on in Africa from March 1941 until its surrender in Tunisia in May 1943.

History

The unit's first commander was Erwin Rommel, whose aggressive leadership earned him the nickname "the Desert Fox". The Afrika Korps landed at Tripoli in February 1941 as part of Unternehmen Sonnenblume (Operation Sunflower). Rommel immediately went on the offensive, driving British forces back across Cyrenaica.

Through 1941 and 1942 the corps seesawed across Libya and Egypt, winning notable victories at Gazala and capturing Tobruk in June 1942. Its advance was finally halted at the First Battle of El Alamein, and after the Second Battle of El Alamein in October–November 1942 the Axis forces retreated westward. Following the Allied landings of Operation Torch, the remnants of the Afrika Korps were trapped in Tunisia and surrendered on 13 May 1943.

Composition

The corps was built around the 15th and 21st Panzer Divisions and the 90th Light Infantry Division, supported by Italian formations under the broader Panzer Army Africa. Its reputation for professionalism and relatively correct conduct contributed to the "war without hate" characterisation of the desert war.

Legacy

The Afrika Korps remains one of the most famous German formations of the war, and its commander Rommel became a central figure in the mythology of the North African campaign.
