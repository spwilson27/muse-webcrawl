Influencer marketing

Influencer marketing is a form of social media marketing involving endorsements and product placement from influencers — people and organisations who have a purported expert level of knowledge or social influence in their field. Brands partner with influencers to reach their audiences in a way that feels more authentic and targeted than traditional advertising.

The practice grew rapidly during the 2010s as platforms such as Instagram, YouTube, and later TikTok matured. By the early 2020s the global influencer marketing industry was valued in the tens of billions of dollars annually.

History

Influencer marketing descends from celebrity endorsement and word-of-mouth marketing. The spread of blogs in the 2000s, followed by image- and video-based social platforms, allowed non-celebrity creators to build large, loyal audiences. Early campaigns often took the form of product seeding — sending free products in the hope of coverage — before evolving into paid, contracted campaigns with deliverables, exclusivity clauses, and performance metrics.

Strategies

Common strategies include sponsored posts and stories, unboxing and review videos, affiliate links and discount codes, brand ambassadorships, account takeovers, and co-created product lines. Campaigns may be managed directly by brands, through agencies, or via influencer marketplaces that match creators with advertisers. Compensation ranges from free products for small creators to six- or seven-figure fees for major celebrities.

Regulation

Advertising regulators, including the U.S. Federal Trade Commission and the UK Advertising Standards Authority, require influencers to disclose material connections with brands clearly and conspicuously. Enforcement actions and updated guidelines in the late 2010s led to widespread adoption of disclosures such as "#ad" and "paid partnership" labels.

Effectiveness and criticism

Supporters argue influencer marketing achieves higher engagement and trust than display advertising, particularly with younger consumers who use ad blockers and skip television commercials. Critics cite fraud (fake followers and engagement bots), measurement difficulties, brand-safety risks, and saturation of sponsored content as significant problems.
