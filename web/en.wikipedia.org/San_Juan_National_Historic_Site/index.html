San Juan National Historic Site (Spanish: Sitio Histórico Nacional de San Juan) in the Old San Juan section of San Juan, Puerto Rico, is a National Park Service-managed historic site preserving Spanish colonial military architecture dating from the 16th century. It comprises the fortresses of Castillo San Felipe del Morro and Castillo San Cristóbal, most of the city walls, the San Juan Gate, and Fort San Juan de la Cruz (El Cañuelo) across the bay on Isla de Cabras.

History

Spain began fortifying the entrance to San Juan Bay in 1539, recognising the island's strategic position as the first major port of call for ships crossing from Europe. El Morro, begun that year, grew over 250 years into a six-level citadel. San Cristóbal, built between 1634 and 1790 to guard against land attacks, became the largest fortification Spain built in the Americas, covering 27 acres.

The fortifications repelled attacks by Sir Francis Drake in 1595 and the Dutch in 1625. In 1898, during the Spanish–American War, United States naval forces bombarded the castles; Puerto Rico passed to the United States under the Treaty of Paris. The forts remained military installations through the Second World War, when new bunkers were added, before transfer to the National Park Service in 1961. Together with La Fortaleza, the site was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983.

Features

Visitors walk the city walls along the Paseo del Morro trail, explore the tiered ramparts and garitas — the sentry boxes that have become a symbol of Puerto Rico — and visit exhibits on five centuries of military history. The broad esplanade before El Morro is a popular kite-flying ground.
