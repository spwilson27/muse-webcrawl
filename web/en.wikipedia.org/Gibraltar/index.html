Gibraltar is a British Overseas Territory at the southern tip of the Iberian Peninsula, overlooking the strait where the Atlantic Ocean meets the Mediterranean Sea. Dominated by the Rock of Gibraltar, a 426-metre limestone promontory, it has been a prized naval base for three centuries, and its sovereignty remains disputed by Spain.

History

Known in antiquity as one of the Pillars of Hercules, the Rock takes its name from the Umayyad general Tariq ibn Ziyad — Jabal Tariq, "Tariq's mountain" — who landed nearby in 711. An Anglo-Dutch force captured Gibraltar in 1704 during the War of the Spanish Succession, and Spain ceded it to Britain "in perpetuity" under the Treaty of Utrecht (1713). It withstood the Great Siege of 1779–1783 and served as a key base in the Napoleonic Wars, both World Wars, and as a gateway for convoys. In referendums in 1967 and 2002 Gibraltarians overwhelmingly rejected Spanish sovereignty and shared sovereignty respectively; Spain continues to press its claim, and the territory's status was complicated by Brexit, since Gibraltar voted 96% to remain in the EU.

Geography and life

The territory is densely populated for its size, with an economy based on financial services, online gambling, shipping and tourism. It is famous for its Barbary macaques — Europe's only wild monkey population — and for the caves of Gorham's Cave complex, a UNESCO World Heritage Site associated with Neanderthals. Its airport runway famously crosses the main road into Spain.
