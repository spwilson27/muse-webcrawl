Environmental impact of meat production

The environmental impact of meat production encompasses the effects of livestock farming on greenhouse gas emissions, land use, water consumption, deforestation, biodiversity loss and pollution. Because animals convert feed into meat inefficiently, meat — especially beef — generally has a much larger environmental footprint per calorie or gram of protein than plant foods.

Greenhouse gas emissions

Livestock supply chains emit methane from enteric fermentation in ruminants, nitrous oxide from manure and fertilised feed crops, and carbon dioxide from land-use change and energy use. The FAO report Livestock's Long Shadow (2006) estimated that livestock accounted for about 18% of anthropogenic greenhouse gas emissions (in CO2 equivalents), a figure later revised by the FAO's GLEAM assessment to around 14.5%.

Land and water use

Livestock production uses the great majority of agricultural land, including grazing land and a large share of cropland devoted to feed. Expansion of pasture and feed cropping is a leading driver of deforestation in regions such as the Amazon. Producing a kilogram of beef typically requires many thousands of litres of water when feed production is included, far more than most plant foods.

Other effects

Concentrated animal feeding operations produce large volumes of manure, which can pollute waterways and contribute to eutrophication and antibiotic resistance. Overgrazing can degrade soils. Assessments such as the 2018 Oxford study by Poore and Nemecek found wide variation among producers and concluded that dietary shifts away from meat and dairy are among the most effective ways for individuals to reduce their food-related footprint.
