International Electrotechnical Commission

The International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) is an international standards organization that prepares and publishes international standards for all electrical, electronic and related technologies — collectively known as electrotechnology. Founded in 1906, it is one of the oldest international standards bodies, and works in coordination with the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) and the International Telecommunication Union (ITU). Its headquarters are in Geneva, Switzerland.

Standards

IEC standards are numbered in the 60000 series and beyond (for example, IEC 60320 for appliance couplers and IEC 60529, the IP Code for ingress protection). Joint standards developed with ISO carry the ISO/IEC prefix, such as ISO/IEC 27001 for information security management. The IEC also manages the International Electrotechnical Vocabulary (IEC 60050), published online as Electropedia, and conformity assessment systems including IECEE for product testing and certification, IECEx for equipment in explosive atmospheres, IECQ for electronic components and IECRE for renewable energy.

Membership and structure

Members are national committees, one per country, representing manufacturers, regulators, users and academia in that country. Standards are drafted by technical committees and subcommittees made up of experts appointed by the national committees, and published in English and French. The IEC's membership covers more than 170 countries, with over 20,000 experts participating in its work.

History

The IEC grew out of resolutions passed at the International Electrical Congress held in St. Louis in 1904, and held its inaugural meeting in London in 1906, with the British physicist Lord Kelvin as its first president. In 1938 it published the first edition of its multilingual vocabulary. The IEC moved to Geneva in 1948, where an offer of affiliation with the newly formed ISO was declined so that it could remain an independent body, while a joint committee, ISO/IEC JTC 1, handles information technology standards.
