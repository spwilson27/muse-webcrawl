Usage share of web browsers

The usage share of web browsers is the proportion of visitors to websites who use a particular web browser, usually estimated by analytics firms such as StatCounter and Net Applications from page views across large networks of sites.

History

Mosaic and Netscape Navigator dominated the early web; Microsoft's Internet Explorer, bundled with Windows, won the "browser wars" of the late 1990s and peaked above 90% share in the early 2000s. Mozilla Firefox eroded that lead from 2004, and Google Chrome, released in 2008, rose rapidly to become the majority browser by the mid-2010s across desktop and mobile combined.

Measurement issues

Estimates differ because of differing site samples, geographic weighting, counting of in-app and automated traffic, and the rise of mobile browsing, where Safari and Chrome dominate through default installations on iOS and Android.
