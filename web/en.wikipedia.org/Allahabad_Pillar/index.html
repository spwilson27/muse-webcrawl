Allahabad Pillar

LocationAllahabad Fort, Prayagraj, Uttar Pradesh, India
MaterialSandstone
Height~10.7 m (35 ft)
InscriptionsAshokan edicts; Samudragupta prashasti; Jahangir inscription

The Allahabad Pillar is a stambha (column) in the Allahabad Fort, at Prayagraj in Uttar Pradesh, India. It is one of the Pillars of Ashoka, erected in the 3rd century BCE, and is remarkable for bearing inscriptions from three different eras spanning nearly two millennia.

Inscriptions

The pillar carries Ashoka's Major Pillar Edicts I–VI in Brahmi script, along with the Schism Edict and the Queen's Edict. Around the 4th century CE, the Gupta emperor Samudragupta's court poet Harishena composed a lengthy Sanskrit eulogy (prashasti) on the pillar, a key source for Gupta history. A third inscription, in Persian, records the accession of the Mughal emperor Jahangir in 1605.

History

The pillar probably originally stood at Kaushambi and was moved to Allahabad, possibly by Akbar when he built the fort in 1583. It was re-erected in the 19th century under British supervision after having been toppled, and its inscriptions were crucial to James Prinsep's decipherment of Brahmi.
