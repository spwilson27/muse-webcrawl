A desert is a landscape where little precipitation occurs and, consequently, living conditions create unique biomes and ecosystems. The lack of vegetation exposes the unprotected surface of the ground to denudation. About one-third of the land surface of the Earth is arid or semi-arid. This includes much of the polar regions, where little precipitation occurs, which are sometimes called polar deserts or "cold deserts".

Classification

Deserts can be classified by the amount of precipitation that falls, by the temperature that prevails, by the causes of desertification, or by geographical location. Hot deserts such as the Sahara lie under subtropical high-pressure belts where air descends and warms; rain-shadow deserts form on the lee side of mountain ranges, such as the Mojave behind the Sierra Nevada; coastal deserts like the Atacama and Namib are cooled by cold ocean currents; and continental-interior deserts, such as the Gobi, are simply far from any ocean moisture.

Features

Wind is the dominant agent of erosion and transport in most deserts, building dune fields (ergs), carving yardangs and polishing ventifacts. Rocky deserts (hamada) and gravel plains (reg) are actually more extensive than sand seas. Ephemeral streams carve wadis and arroyos, and rare cloudbursts cause flash floods. Evaporation leaves salt pans — playas and chotts — at the lowest points of closed basins.

Life in the desert

Desert plants, called xerophytes, conserve water with small or absent leaves, thick cuticles, deep or widespread roots, and in cacti and euphorbs, water-storing stems. Many desert animals are nocturnal, avoiding the daytime heat, and some — kangaroo rats, fennec foxes, camels — are famed for surviving with little or no free water. Microbial crusts of cyanobacteria stabilize desert soils.

Human use and desertification

Some 500 million people live in desert regions, practicing pastoralism, oasis agriculture, or mining in deserts rich in minerals and solar energy. Desertification — the degradation of drylands through climate change and overgrazing, deforestation and poor irrigation — threatens livelihoods across the Sahel and Central Asia and is the subject of the UN Convention to Combat Desertification.
