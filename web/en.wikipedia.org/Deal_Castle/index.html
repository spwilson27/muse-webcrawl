Deal Castle

LocationDeal, Kent, England
Built1539–1540
Built byHenry VIII
Managed byEnglish Heritage

Deal Castle is an artillery fort constructed by Henry VIII in Deal, Kent, between 1539 and 1540. It formed part of the King's Device programme to protect England's southern coast against invasion from France and the Holy Roman Empire, guarding the strategic anchorage known as the Downs.

Architecture

The castle has a distinctive plan shaped like a Tudor rose: a central circular keep surrounded by six inner bastions and six larger outer bastions, all rounded to deflect artillery fire. Moats and 145 embrasures for guns and hand weapons covered every approach. Together with nearby Walmer and Sandown castles, it defended the Downs.

History

Deal Castle saw action during the English Civil War, when it was held for Parliament, besieged by Royalists in 1648, and retaken. It remained garrisoned through the Napoleonic Wars and served as the residence of the Captain of the Cinque Ports. German bombing damaged it in the Second World War.

Today

Restored in the 1950s and now managed by English Heritage, the castle is open to visitors as a museum exploring Tudor coastal defence.
