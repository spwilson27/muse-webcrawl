SPDY

SPDY (pronounced "speedy") is a deprecated open networking protocol that was developed primarily at Google for transporting web content. SPDY manipulates HTTP traffic, with particular goals of reducing web page load latency and improving web security. SPDY achieved reduced latency through compression, multiplexing, and prioritization, allowing many concurrent requests to share a single connection. It required the use of TLS encryption for security.

SPDY multiplexed many streams over a single TCP connection.

History

Google announced SPDY in November 2009 as part of its "Let's Make the Web Faster" initiative, and deployed it in the Chrome browser and across Google's own services. The protocol was widely implemented in browsers and servers, and its design formed the basis for the HTTP/2 specification developed by the IETF HTTP Working Group; the first draft of HTTP/2 used SPDY as its starting point. After HTTP/2 was published as RFC 7540 in May 2015, support for SPDY was progressively removed: Google announced its deprecation in 2015 and removed it from Chrome in 2016, and the last major servers followed.

Features

SPDY's key techniques included multiplexed streams, request prioritization, header compression, and server push — most of which were carried into HTTP/2 — making it one of the most influential experimental protocols in the history of the web despite its short life.
