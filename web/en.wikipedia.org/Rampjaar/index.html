Rampjaar

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Rampjaar (Dutch for "disaster year") is the name given in Dutch historiography to the year 1672, when the Dutch Republic was simultaneously attacked by France, England, and the German prince-bishoprics of Münster and Cologne, and plunged into political crisis at home. A contemporary saying held that the people were redeloos (irrational), the government radeloos (desperate) and the country reddeloos (beyond saving).

The invasion

Angered by Dutch obstruction of his designs on the Spanish Netherlands, Louis XIV of France invaded in April 1672 at the head of some 120,000 men, opening the Franco-Dutch War; England joined in the Third Anglo-Dutch War, while Münster and Cologne attacked from the east. French troops swept across the Rhine and occupied Utrecht, Gelderland and Overijssel within weeks. The Republic was saved by opening the sluices and flooding the Dutch Waterline, a belt of inundations that halted the French advance before Amsterdam.

Political crisis

The catastrophe destroyed the regime of Grand Pensionary Johan de Witt, leader of the "True Freedom" republican government. Blamed for neglecting the army, De Witt resigned in August; on 20 August 1672 he and his brother Cornelis were lynched by an Orangist mob in The Hague, their bodies mutilated — one of the most notorious episodes in Dutch political history. William III of Orange was appointed stadtholder of Holland and Zeeland in July, beginning an ascendancy that would dominate the Republic for thirty years.

Aftermath

Under William III the Dutch stabilised the front, defeated the English at sea under Michiel de Ruyter, and built a European coalition against France. The wars ended with the Treaties of Nijmegen (1678) and Westminster (1674), leaving the Republic intact but permanently marked by the trauma of 1672.
