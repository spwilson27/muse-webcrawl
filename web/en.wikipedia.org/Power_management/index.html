Power management

Scope: Energy use of electronic systems

Standards: ACPI, APM, Energy Star

Techniques: Sleep states, DVFS, clock gating

Power management is a feature of electrical and electronic equipment, especially computers and battery-powered devices, that turns off or reduces the performance of components when they are not needed, in order to save energy, extend battery life and reduce heat. Modern systems spend much of their time in low-power idle states and wake components on demand.

Techniques

Key techniques include system sleep and hibernation states (standardised by ACPI), dynamic voltage and frequency scaling (DVFS), in which processors run slower at lower voltage when the workload is light, clock gating and power gating of unused circuits, and display dimming. Operating systems expose these through power plans and drivers that monitor activity and device usage.

Why it matters

In portable devices, power management directly determines battery life. In data centres, where electricity and cooling dominate operating costs, power management underpins metrics such as power usage effectiveness and strategies like server consolidation and capping. At the grid scale, demand management and smart charging shift consumption away from peak periods.

Standards

Programmes such as Energy Star set efficiency requirements for equipment, and standards from ACPI to USB Power Delivery govern how devices report and negotiate their power states and supplies.
