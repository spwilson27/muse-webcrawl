In particle physics, a boson is a subatomic particle whose spin quantum number has an integer value (0, 1, 2, ...). Bosons form one of the two fundamental classes of subatomic particle, the other being fermions, which have half-integer spin. Every observed subatomic particle is either a boson or a fermion. Paul Dirac named the class after the Indian physicist Satyendra Nath Bose.

Properties

Multiple bosons may occupy the same quantum state, because they obey Bose–Einstein statistics and are not subject to the Pauli exclusion principle. This underlies lasers, superfluidity and Bose–Einstein condensation. Bosons are the carriers of fundamental forces: photons mediate electromagnetism, gluons the strong interaction, W and Z bosons the weak interaction; the Higgs boson, discovered at CERN in 2012, arises from the Higgs field.

Elementary and composite bosons

The elementary bosons of the Standard Model are the gauge bosons (photon, gluons, W±, Z) and the scalar Higgs boson. Composite particles made of an even number of fermions — such as mesons, helium-4 atoms and deuterons — also behave as bosons.
