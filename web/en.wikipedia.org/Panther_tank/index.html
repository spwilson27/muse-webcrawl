The Panther (official designation Panzerkampfwagen V Panther, Sd.Kfz. 171) was a German medium tank of the Second World War, in service from 1943 until the end of the war in Europe in 1945. It was designed in response to the Soviet T-34, which had outclassed German tanks encountered during the invasion of the Soviet Union in 1941, and it combined sloped armour, a long high-velocity 7.5 cm gun and good mobility.

Design and development

Daimler-Benz and MAN submitted competing designs; the MAN design was selected and production began in early 1943. The Panther's glacis plate of 80 mm, sloped at 55 degrees, gave effective protection greater than its nominal thickness, and its KwK 42 L/70 gun was among the most powerful tank guns of the war. At about 44.8 tonnes, however, it was heavy for a "medium" tank, and its Maybach engine and final drives were prone to failure, especially in early production.

Combat history

The Panther made its combat debut at the Battle of Kursk in July 1943, where early reliability problems caused many losses before mechanical teething troubles were partly resolved. It went on to serve on the Eastern Front, in Italy and in Normandy and north-west Europe, where it was regarded by Allied crews as a formidable opponent, superior in a frontal engagement to the Sherman and roughly comparable in influence to the heavier Tiger, at far lower cost. Variants included the Jagdpanther tank destroyer and the Bergepanther recovery vehicle. After the war, captured Panthers were briefly used by France and other countries, and the design influenced post-war tank development.
