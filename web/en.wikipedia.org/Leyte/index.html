Leyte


Leyte is an island in the Visayas group of the Philippines. It is the eighth largest island in the country, with an area of about 7,368 km². Population (2020): about 2.6 million.

Leyte is an island in the Visayas group of islands in the Philippines. It is the eighth-largest island in the Philippines by land area.

The island is divided into two provinces: Leyte and Southern Leyte. It is connected to the island of Samar across the San Juanico Strait by the San Juanico Bridge, the longest bridge in the Philippines.

History

Historically, the site of one of the largest naval battles in history, the Battle of Leyte Gulf, occurred in the waters off the island. On 20 October 1944, General Douglas MacArthur landed on Leyte, fulfilling his promise to return to the Philippines during World War II. The landing is commemorated by a memorial in Palo.

Geography

Leyte is mountainous with forests in the interior, and fertile coastal plains in the northeast where most of the population lives. The main city is Tacloban, the regional center of Eastern Visayas.
