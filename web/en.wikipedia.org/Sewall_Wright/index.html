Sewall Green Wright (December 21, 1889 – March 3, 1988) was an American geneticist known for his influential work on evolutionary theory and for developing path analysis. With Ronald Fisher and J. B. S. Haldane, he founded the theoretical field of population genetics that underpinned the modern synthesis.

Career and contributions

Wright studied zoology at Harvard and worked for the US Department of Agriculture on guinea pig inbreeding before joining the University of Chicago (1926–1955) and then the University of Wisconsin–Madison. His inbreeding experiments led to the coefficient of inbreeding and F-statistics, tools for measuring population structure. He emphasised random genetic drift in small populations and proposed the shifting balance theory, in which drift lets a population cross a valley on an "adaptive landscape" — a metaphor he introduced — before selection carries it up a new peak; his emphasis on drift and epistasis put him at odds with Fisher's selection-centred view. His four-volume treatise Evolution and the Genetics of Populations (1968–1978) summarised a lifetime's work.
