Emma Lazarus (July 22, 1849 – November 19, 1887) was an American author of poetry, prose, and translations, and an activist for Jewish causes. She is best known for the sonnet "The New Colossus" (1883), written to raise money for the construction of a pedestal for the Statue of Liberty (Liberty Enlightening the World). In 1903, lines from the poem were engraved on a bronze plaque and mounted inside the pedestal's lower level.

Early life and career

Lazarus was born in New York City to a wealthy Sephardic Jewish family of Portuguese descent. Educated privately, she published her first book of poems at eighteen and attracted the mentorship of Ralph Waldo Emerson. She wrote poetry, a novel (Alide), translations of Heine and other German poets, and essays for journals including The Century and Lippincott's.

Activism

Moved by reports of pogroms against Jews in Russia in the early 1880s, Lazarus became an outspoken advocate for Jewish refugees arriving in the United States, volunteered at the Hebrew Emigrant Aid Society, and wrote essays arguing for a Jewish homeland in Palestine. Her Songs of a Semite (1882) collected her Jewish-themed verse.

"The New Colossus"

Donated to an 1883 auction for the Statue of Liberty's pedestal fund, the sonnet famously contrasts the ancient Colossus of Rhodes with "Mother of Exiles," ending with the lines "Give me your tired, your poor, / Your huddled masses yearning to breathe free." Largely forgotten at her early death, the poem was revived by a campaign led by her friend Georgina Schuyler and has since become closely identified with American ideals of immigration and refuge.
