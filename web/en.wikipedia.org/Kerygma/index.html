Kerygma

An early Christian mosaic depicting the preaching of the apostles.

Kerygma (from Ancient Greek κῆρυγμα, kḗrygma, "proclamation" or "herald's announcement") is a term used in New Testament scholarship for the earliest preaching about Jesus Christ by the apostles — the core gospel message proclaimed by the early church, as distinct from didache, the detailed teaching given to converts.

Content in the New Testament

The sermons in the Acts of the Apostles are usually taken as models of the kerygma. Scholars such as C. H. Dodd summarized its content as: the prophecies are fulfilled in Jesus; Jesus was born of David's line, died according to the Scriptures, was buried, and rose on the third day; he is exalted as Lord at God's right hand; he will come again; therefore repentance, forgiveness, and the gift of the Holy Spirit are offered. Paul's summary in 1 Corinthians 15:3–5 similarly preserves an early creed-like statement.

Theological discussion

The concept became prominent in 20th-century theology. Rudolf Bultmann argued that the kerygma is an existential address demanding a decision of faith rather than a set of historical claims. The term has also been adopted in Roman Catholic catechetics, where a "kerygmatic" renewal emphasized proclamation of the basic gospel before systematic doctrine, influencing approaches to evangelization.
