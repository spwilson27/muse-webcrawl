Irish Naval Service

Founded1946 (origins in the Coastal and Marine Service, 1923)
HeadquartersHaulbowline, County Cork
RoleMaritime defence, fishery protection, search and rescue support
ParentIrish Defence Forces

The Irish Naval Service (Irish: An tSeirbhís Chabhlaigh) is the maritime component of the Defence Forces of Ireland, headquartered at Haulbowline in Cork Harbour. One of the smallest navies in Europe, it patrols Ireland's extensive exclusive economic zone — many times the country's land area.

History

A naval force was first established as the Coastal and Marine Service in 1923 and, after disbandment and wartime Marine Service, was constituted as the Naval Service in 1946. Its peacetime workload has centred on fishery protection within Irish waters under the EU Common Fisheries Policy, drug interdiction, and maritime safety.

Fleet and operations

The fleet is built around offshore patrol vessels, including the Samuel Beckett-class ships delivered from 2014, named for Irish writers, and inshore patrol vessels acquired from New Zealand in the 2020s. Between 2015 and 2018 Irish ships deployed to the Mediterranean in Operation Pontus, rescuing more than 18,000 migrants. Personnel shortages in recent years have at times reduced the number of ships available for patrol.
