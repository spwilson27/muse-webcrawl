The Upper Deck Company is an American company that produces trading cards and other collectibles. Founded in 1988 and based in Carlsbad, California, Upper Deck first rose to prominence with its premium-priced 1989 baseball card set, which featured the sought-after rookie card of Ken Griffey Jr. and introduced innovations such as anti-counterfeiting holograms, tamper-proof packaging and higher-quality card stock.

History

Upper Deck obtained a licence from Major League Baseball in 1988 and released its first set in 1989, selling out its production run. The company expanded into the other major North American sports leagues in the early 1990s, signing an exclusive autograph deal with Michael Jordan, and grew rapidly during the sports-card boom, before the market contracted in the mid-1990s.

Products and licences

In addition to sports cards, Upper Deck produced the Yu-Gi-Oh! Trading Card Game for Konami in North America and Europe from 2002 until litigation ended the relationship in 2008–2009. The company lost its licences with Major League Baseball, the NBA and the NFL in 2009–2010, but retained an exclusive licence with the National Hockey League and agreements with the NHL Players' Association, and has continued to hold exclusive autograph rights for athletes including Michael Jordan, LeBron James and Tiger Woods through its Upper Deck Authenticated memorabilia business.

Entertainment and games

Upper Deck also publishes entertainment trading cards under licence from Marvel and other properties, and tabletop games including the Legendary deck-building game series and the Vs. System 2PCG. Its collectible lines include Goodwin Champions and the Fleer and SkyBox brands it acquired.
