Manhattan is the most densely populated of the five boroughs of New York City and coextensive with New York County. It consists mainly of Manhattan Island, bounded by the Hudson, East, and Harlem rivers, and is the historical and commercial heart of the city, home to Wall Street, Broadway, Times Square, and Central Park.

History

The island was inhabited by the Lenape people before Europeans arrived. In 1626 the Dutch West India Company's director Peter Minuit is said to have purchased Manhattan from the Lenape — traditionally for goods worth 60 guilders — and founded New Amsterdam at its southern tip. The English seized the colony in 1664 and renamed it New York. Manhattan was the scene of battles in the American Revolutionary War and briefly served as the new nation's capital in 1785–1790; George Washington was inaugurated at Federal Hall in 1789. The Commissioners' Plan of 1811 imposed the famous street grid, and the opening of the Erie Canal in 1825 made the island America's great port and commercial centre. Waves of immigration, the skyscraper boom, and the rise of finance and media made 20th-century Manhattan a global symbol of the modern city.

Geography and neighbourhoods

Manhattan stretches about 21.6 km from Marble Hill (attached to the Bronx) to the Battery at its southern tip. Its neighbourhoods run from Lower Manhattan's financial district, through Chinatown, Greenwich Village, Chelsea, and Midtown, to Harlem and Washington Heights uptown. Central Park, an 843-acre landscaped park designed by Frederick Law Olmsted and Calvert Vaux, occupies the island's centre. The island is joined to the other boroughs and New Jersey by dozens of bridges and tunnels.

Economy and culture

Lower Manhattan and Midtown form two of the world's largest central business districts. The borough hosts the New York Stock Exchange and Nasdaq, the headquarters of major banks, media companies, and the United Nations, as well as Broadway theatres, museums such as the Metropolitan Museum of Art and MoMA, and universities including Columbia and NYU.
