Pushing hands

Pushing hands, known in Mandarin as tuishou (推手) and in Cantonese as po sau, is a two-person training exercise practised in tai chi and other Chinese internal martial arts. Partners stand facing each other, maintain continuous contact with the hands and forearms, and move in coordinated circular patterns while each attempts to sense, neutralize, and unbalance the other. It is intended to develop sensitivity (ting jin, "listening energy"), balance, yielding, and the ability to redirect force, bridging the gap between solo form practice and free sparring.

Practice

Training begins with fixed-step, single-hand patterns in which partners rock back and forth in bow stances, exchanging weight while rolling their joined forearms through the four basic energies of tai chi: ward off (peng), roll back (lu), press (ji), and push (an). Double-hand patterns add stepping, first in straight lines and then freely ("moving step" push hands). Advanced practice may become competitive, with each player trying to cause the other to lose balance or step out, and push hands competitions are held internationally under various rule sets, in fixed-step and moving-step divisions.

Principles

Pushing hands embodies central tai chi principles: relaxation (song), sticking and following the partner's movement without resisting or collapsing, yielding to incoming force rather than opposing it directly, and issuing power from the legs and waist rather than the arms. The exercise trains practitioners to detect the direction and quality of a partner's force through touch — described in the tai chi classics as understanding oneself and the opponent — and to respond with neutralization followed by control or uprooting of the partner's centre of gravity.

Styles and variations

Each major tai chi style (Chen, Yang, Wu, Wu (Hao), and Sun) maintains its own push hands methods and pattern sets. Related exercises exist in other arts: chi sao ("sticking hands") in Wing Chun and analogous sensitivity drills in baguazhang and xingyiquan. The name is also that of Ang Lee's 1991 film Pushing Hands, the first of his "Father Knows Best" trilogy.
