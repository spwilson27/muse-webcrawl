Jakobshavn Glacier (Greenlandic: Sermeq Kujalleq; Danish: Jakobshavn Isbræ) is a major outlet glacier of the Greenland ice sheet in western Greenland, draining into the Ilulissat Icefjord near the town of Ilulissat (formerly Jakobshavn). It is one of the fastest-moving and most productive glaciers in the world.

Characteristics

The glacier drains about 6.5% of the Greenland ice sheet and produces roughly 35 billion tonnes of icebergs per year — around 10% of all Greenland icebergs. Its flow speeds have exceeded 40 metres per day. Icebergs calved from Jakobshavn enter Disko Bay and can drift into the Atlantic; the iceberg believed to have sunk RMS Titanic in 1912 is often said to have originated here.

Retreat and research

The glacier's floating ice tongue largely disintegrated between the late 1990s and early 2000s, after which its speed roughly doubled and its contribution to sea-level rise increased — estimated at about 4% of twentieth-century global sea-level rise by itself. Subsequent thickening and slowing were observed in the late 2010s, attributed to cooler ocean waters. The Ilulissat Icefjord was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2004, and the glacier is intensively monitored as a key indicator of ice-sheet response to climate change.
