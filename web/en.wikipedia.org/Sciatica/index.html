Sciatica

Sciatica is pain going down the leg from the lower back, following the distribution of the sciatic nerve, the largest nerve in the body. The pain typically affects only one side, radiating from the buttock down the back of the thigh and calf, sometimes into the foot. It may be accompanied by numbness, tingling or weakness in the leg. Sciatica is a symptom rather than a diagnosis in itself.

Causes

In about 90 percent of cases, sciatica is caused by a herniated (slipped) intervertebral disc pressing on one of the lumbar or sacral nerve roots that form the sciatic nerve. Other causes include spinal stenosis (narrowing of the spinal canal), spondylolisthesis, degenerative disc disease, and, less commonly, piriformis syndrome, tumours, infection or trauma. Risk factors include age, obesity, occupations involving heavy lifting or prolonged sitting, and diabetes.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is usually clinical, based on the characteristic radiating pain and examination findings such as the straight-leg-raise test, which reproduces the pain when the leg is lifted while the patient lies on their back. Imaging with MRI is generally reserved for cases with "red flag" features — such as bladder or bowel dysfunction suggesting cauda equina syndrome, progressive weakness, fever, or a history of cancer — or when symptoms persist beyond several weeks and surgery is being considered.

Treatment

Most cases resolve within a few weeks with conservative management: remaining active, simple analgesics such as paracetamol or non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, and sometimes physiotherapy. Bed rest is not recommended. Medications such as gabapentinoids and opioids have limited evidence of benefit. Epidural corticosteroid injections may give short-term relief. Surgery, typically a microdiscectomy to remove the herniated disc material, is considered when pain is severe and persistent, or when there is significant or progressive neurological deficit; it tends to relieve leg pain faster than conservative care, though long-term outcomes converge.

Sciatica affects a substantial minority of adults at some point in their lives, with lifetime prevalence estimates commonly in the range of 10 to 40 percent.
