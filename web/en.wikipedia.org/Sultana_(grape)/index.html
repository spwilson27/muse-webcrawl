Sultana (grape)

The sultana is a white, oval, seedless grape variety also called the Thompson Seedless in the United States. It is believed to originate in the Ottoman Empire or Persia; the name "sultana" derives from sultan. Dried, it produces the golden-brown dried fruit likewise called a sultana, one of the world's most important raisin types.

History

The variety was introduced to California in the 1870s by William Thompson, whose name attached to it in American viticulture. It became the dominant raisin grape of California's San Joaquin Valley and is also grown extensively in Turkey and Australia.

Uses

Sultanas are eaten fresh as a table grape, dried into raisins, and used in winemaking as a neutral base for bulk wines and spirits. Because the grape is seedless and thin-skinned, it dries readily; commercial sultanas are often treated with an alkaline emulsion and sulphur to produce the familiar light golden colour.
