Ellen Browning Scripps

Ellen Browning Scripps (October 18, 1836 – August 3, 1932) was an American journalist and philanthropist who was the founding donor of several major institutions in Southern California. She was the younger sister of newspaper magnate Edward W. Scripps.

Born in London and raised in Illinois, she worked as a teacher and later as a proofreader and editor for her brother's newspapers. After inheriting a substantial fortune, she devoted herself to philanthropy, funding schools, libraries, hospitals and scientific research.

Her gifts founded Scripps College in Claremont (1926), the Scripps Institution of Oceanography in La Jolla, and the Scripps Memorial Hospital. She also helped establish the La Jolla community, including its library, children's pool and the Birch Aquarium's predecessor. She died in La Jolla at the age of 95.
