Charminar

The Charminar (1591), emblem of Hyderabad.

The Charminar (Urdu: چار مینار, "Four Towers") is a monument and mosque in Hyderabad, Telangana, India, and the most recognised symbol of the city. Built in 1591 by Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah, the fifth ruler of the Qutb Shahi dynasty, it marked the founding of Hyderabad and — by the best-known tradition — commemorated the end of a devastating plague. Its name derives from its four grand minarets.

History

When Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah shifted his capital from Golconda to the new city of Hyderabad on the Musi River in 1591, the Charminar was raised at the centre of the city's planned grid, at the crossing of its two main bazaar streets. Tradition relates that the sultan vowed to build a mosque on the spot if the plague ravaging his people abated; a mosque occupies the monument's upper floor. The surrounding Laad Bazaar and markets grew up around it, and the grand Mecca Masjid stands nearby.

Architecture

The Charminar is a square structure, each side about 20 metres, built of granite and lime mortar in the Indo-Islamic style with Persian influences. At each corner rises a four-storey minaret roughly 56 metres high, crowned with a double balcony and bulbous dome; spiral staircases of 149 steps climb within. Four grand arches, each about 11 metres wide and 20 metres high, open to the cardinal directions. Stucco ornament, delicate balustrades, and a clock added in 1889 complete the structure, which functions both as monument and mosque — worship continues on its top floor to this day.

Today

Maintained by the Archaeological Survey of India, the Charminar appears on the emblem of the state of Telangana. The surrounding old city remains a busy commercial district famed for bangles and pearls, and the monument is floodlit at night and anchors the city's identity much as the Eiffel Tower does Paris.
