Auto sacramental

An auto sacramental is a form of Spanish religious drama, a one-act allegorical play celebrating the Eucharist, traditionally performed during the feast of Corpus Christi. The genre flourished during the Spanish Golden Age of the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries.

Characteristics

Autos sacramentales feature personified abstractions such as Faith, Sin, Grace, and Death, alongside biblical and mythological figures, in plots dramatizing theological concepts like redemption and transubstantiation. They were staged outdoors in public squares on large wheeled carts and scaffolding with elaborate machinery and music.

Major authors

The form reached its highest development in the works of Pedro Calderón de la Barca, who wrote around eighty autos, including El gran teatro del mundo (The Great Theatre of the World). Earlier contributors included Lope de Vega and José de Valdivielso; the genre's roots lie in medieval Eucharistic plays.

Decline

In 1765 the performance of autos sacramentales was prohibited by royal order under Charles III, as Enlightenment-era officials considered them irreverent and outdated. The genre has since been occasionally revived.
