Modena (Italian: [ˈmɔːdena]) is a city and comune (municipality) on the south side of the Po Valley, in the Province of Modena, in the Emilia-Romagna region of northern Italy. It is known for its car industry — Ferrari, Maserati, Lamborghini and Pagani have roots in or near the city — and for culinary products such as traditional balsamic vinegar and Parmigiano-Reggiano cheese.

History

The city, ancient Mutina, was an Etruscan and then Roman settlement, lying on the Via Aemilia. In 43 BC it was the scene of the Battle of Mutina between Mark Antony and the forces of the Senate. From 1598 to 1859 Modena was the capital of the Duchy of Modena, ruled by the Este family, whose court made it a centre of art and learning. The Romanesque cathedral, begun in 1099 and sculpted in part by Wiligelmo, together with the leaning Ghirlandina bell tower and the Piazza Grande, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Culture and economy

Modena is the birthplace of tenor Luciano Pavarotti and of Enzo Ferrari; the Museo Enzo Ferrari now occupies his childhood home. The University of Modena and Reggio Emilia, founded in 1175, is among Italy's oldest. The city's traditional balsamic vinegar (Aceto Balsamico Tradizionale di Modena) is aged in wooden casks for a minimum of twelve years and enjoys protected status. Osteria Francescana, chef Massimo Bottura's restaurant, has been ranked among the world's best.
