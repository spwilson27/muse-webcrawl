Juukan Gorge

Juukan Gorge is a gorge in the Hamersley Range in the Pilbara region of Western Australia, on the traditional lands of the Puutu Kunti Kurrama and Pinikura (PKKP) peoples. It contained rock shelters showing evidence of human occupation dating back some 46,000 years, including through the last Ice Age.

Destruction

On 24 May 2020, mining company Rio Tinto blasted the gorge to expand its Brockman 4 iron ore mine. The destruction was legal under a ministerial consent issued in 2013 under Section 18 of Western Australia's Aboriginal Heritage Act 1972, but it proceeded despite archaeological findings of exceptional significance — including a 4,000-year-old plaited human hair belt with DNA linking it to present-day PKKP people — and despite the traditional owners' opposition.

Aftermath

The destruction caused national and international outrage. A federal parliamentary inquiry ("Never Again" report) condemned the act and the weakness of heritage laws. Rio Tinto's chief executive Jean-Sébastien Jacques and two senior executives resigned, the chairman Simon Thompson later stepped down, and the company apologized and undertook to rebuild its relationship with the PKKP. Western Australia's heritage legislation was subsequently reformed.
