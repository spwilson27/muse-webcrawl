Straße des 17. Juni

The Straße des 17. Juni (German pronunciation: [ˈʃtʁaːsə dɛs ˈziːpt͡seːntn̩ ˈjuːni], "17 June Street") is a street in central Berlin, the capital of Germany. It runs from the Brandenburg Gate through the Tiergarten to the Ernst-Reuter-Platz in Charlottenburg, forming the western continuation of Unter den Linden and part of the historic east–west axis of the city. The Berlin Victory Column (Siegessäule) stands on the street at the Großer Stern roundabout.

History

The road began as a causeway through the Tiergarten laid out in the 18th century and was long known as the Charlottenburger Chaussee. Under Nazi planning for the capital "Germania", Albert Speer widened it as part of the grand East–West Axis and moved the Victory Column to its present position in 1938–39. After the Second World War the street lay in West Berlin; it was renamed in 1953 to commemorate the East German uprising of 17 June 1953, which began with workers' protests in East Berlin.

Events

The broad, straight street is a traditional venue for mass gatherings, including the Love Parade in the 1990s, fan miles for international football tournaments, New Year celebrations near the Brandenburg Gate, and public festivals and demonstrations. The TU Berlin (Technical University of Berlin) campus fronts the street at its western end.
