Greville Maynard Wynne (19 March 1919 – 28 February 1990) was a British businessman and electrical engineer recruited by MI6 as a courier in its operation with the Soviet intelligence officer Oleg Penkovsky. His arrest in 1962 and show trial in Moscow, alongside Penkovsky, became one of the most publicised espionage episodes of the Cold War.

Background and recruitment

Wynne trained as an electrical engineer and founded companies selling electrical equipment in Eastern Europe, travelling frequently behind the Iron Curtain. In 1960 British intelligence recruited him to make contact with Oleg Penkovsky, a colonel in Soviet military intelligence (GRU) who had offered to spy for the West. Over the following two years Wynne carried messages and film between Penkovsky in Moscow and his handlers in London and Paris. Penkovsky's intelligence — especially on Soviet missile capabilities — was of major importance during the Cuban Missile Crisis of October 1962.

Arrest, trial and exchange

Wynne was arrested in Budapest on 2 November 1962 and taken to Moscow. In May 1963 he and Penkovsky were tried together; Penkovsky was sentenced to death and executed, while Wynne received eight years' imprisonment. He served time in harsh conditions in Vladimir Prison, and his health deteriorated badly. In April 1964 he was exchanged at the Heerstraße checkpoint in Berlin for the Soviet intelligence officer Konon Molody (known in the West as Gordon Lonsdale).

Later life

Wynne wrote accounts of his experiences, including The Man from Moscow (1967) and The Man from Odessa (1981), though official sources later disputed elements of his version of events and the degree of his foreknowledge of the operation. His story was dramatised in the BBC serial Wynne and Penkovsky (1985) and in the film The Courier (2020), in which he was played by Benedict Cumberbatch. He died of throat cancer in London in 1990.
