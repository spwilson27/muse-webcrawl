Telescope Array Project

Telescope Array
LocationMillard County, Utah, US
Area~700 km² surface array
Operating2008–present
PurposeUltra-high-energy cosmic rays

The Telescope Array Project is an international cosmic-ray observatory in the high desert of Millard County, Utah, designed to detect ultra-high-energy cosmic rays — subatomic particles arriving from space with energies above 1018 electronvolts. It is a collaboration between institutions in Japan, the United States, and other countries, led by the University of Utah and the University of Tokyo.

Design

The observatory combines 507 surface scintillator detectors spread over about 700 km², which sample the particle showers produced when a cosmic ray strikes the atmosphere, with three fluorescence telescope stations that observe the faint ultraviolet glow of the showers on clear, moonless nights. This hybrid technique, inherited from the earlier Fly's Eye and AGASA experiments, gives accurate energy and direction measurements.

Results

Telescope Array has confirmed the suppression of the cosmic-ray flux at the highest energies and reported a statistically significant "hotspot" of events above 57 EeV in the northern sky, suggesting a nearby source. Its TAx4 expansion quadruples the surface array area to study this anisotropy.
