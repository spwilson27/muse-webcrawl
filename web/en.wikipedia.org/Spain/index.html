Spain, officially the Kingdom of Spain, is a country in southwestern Europe occupying most of the Iberian Peninsula, together with the Balearic Islands, the Canary Islands and the North African cities of Ceuta and Melilla. It is bordered by France, Andorra and Portugal, and by the Mediterranean Sea and the Atlantic Ocean.

History

The peninsula was successively inhabited by Iberians and Celts, colonised by Phoenicians, Greeks and Carthaginians, and ruled by Rome as Hispania. After Visigothic rule, most of Iberia came under Muslim rule from 711, while Christian kingdoms in the north slowly expanded during the Reconquista, completed in 1492 with the fall of Granada. The dynastic union of Castile and Aragon under the Catholic Monarchs, and Columbus's voyage of 1492, launched a global Spanish Empire. Spain declined as a great power in the 18th and 19th centuries, endured civil war (1936–39) and the Franco dictatorship (1939–75), and became a parliamentary democracy under the 1978 constitution.

Politics and regions

Spain is a constitutional monarchy with a parliamentary system; executive power lies with the prime minister (President of the Government). The country is divided into 17 autonomous communities and two autonomous cities, with Catalonia, the Basque Country and Galicia among those recognising co-official regional languages. The Inquisition and the Golden Age remain touchstones of its cultural memory, and its modern culture spans literature, cinema and sport.

Economy

Spain has one of the largest economies in the European Union, with major sectors including tourism (it is among the world's most-visited countries), agriculture, automotive manufacturing, and renewable energy.
