Permian

The Permian is a geologic period and stratigraphic system which spans 47 million years from the end of the Carboniferous period 298.9 million years ago to the beginning of the Triassic period 251.9 million years ago. It is the last period of the Paleozoic Era. The Permian was named in 1841 by geologist Sir Roderick Murchison after the region of Perm in Russia, where Permian strata are extensively exposed.

Life

During the Permian the continents were assembled into the supercontinent Pangaea, surrounded by the global ocean Panthalassa. On land, the early amniotes diversified into synapsids — including the sail-backed Dimetrodon and the therapsid ancestors of mammals — and early reptiles such as the pareiasaurs. Ferns gave way in drier interiors to gymnosperms and conifers; the giant clubmoss forests of the Carboniferous declined.

The end-Permian extinction

The period closed with the most severe mass extinction in Earth's history, the Permian–Triassic extinction event, sometimes called the "Great Dying". About 251.9 million years ago, an estimated 90 percent or more of marine species and some 70 percent of terrestrial vertebrate species became extinct. The leading cause is thought to be massive volcanism in the Siberian Traps, which drove rapid global warming, ocean acidification and anoxia. Recovery of ecosystems took millions of years into the Triassic.
