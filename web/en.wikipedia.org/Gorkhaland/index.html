Gorkhaland

Region: Northern West Bengal, India

Main centre: Darjeeling

Population group: Indian Gorkhas (Nepali speakers)

Gorkhaland is a proposed state in India demanded by the Nepali-speaking Gorkha ethnic group in the Darjeeling Hills and the Dooars in the northern part of the Indian state of West Bengal. The movement for a separate state seeks recognition of the distinct cultural and linguistic identity of the Indian Gorkhas.

History

The demand for a separate administrative unit dates back to the early 20th century, including memoranda submitted during the British colonial period. The movement turned violent in the 1980s under Subhash Ghisingh and the Gorkha National Liberation Front (GNLF), with an agitation in 1986–88 that cost over a thousand lives. It ended with the creation of the Darjeeling Gorkha Hill Council in 1988.

The demand was revived in 2007 by Bimal Gurung's Gorkha Janmukti Morcha (GJM). In 2011, a tripartite agreement created the Gorkhaland Territorial Administration (GTA), a semi-autonomous body. A renewed agitation in 2017, sparked by fears that Bengali would be imposed in schools, shut down the hills for 104 days before being called off.

Rationale

Supporters argue that the Gorkhas of India face an identity crisis, being frequently mistaken for citizens of Nepal, and that a separate state would bring political recognition and focused development to the hill region.
