Eraser

An eraser (in some places known as a rubber) is an article of stationery that is used for removing marks from paper or skin (such as parchment or vellum). Erasers have a rubbery consistency and come in a variety of shapes, sizes, and colours. Some pencils have an eraser on one end. Less expensive erasers are made from synthetic rubber, but more expensive or specialized erasers can be made of vinyl, plastic, or gum-like materials.

History

Before rubber erasers, writers used wax, sandstone, or pieces of soft bread to remove graphite marks; the Japanese used soft bread in place of erasers throughout the Meiji and Taishō periods. In 1770 the English engineer Edward Nairne is credited with developing the first widely marketed rubber eraser, having picked up a piece of rubber by mistake instead of breadcrumbs and found it effective. He sold natural rubber erasers for the high price of three shillings per half-inch cube. Joseph Priestley also described the material's usefulness in 1770, giving rise to the British name "rubber". In 1839 Charles Goodyear discovered vulcanization, which made rubber more durable and the eraser ubiquitous. The eraser attached to a pencil was patented in 1858 by Hymen Lipman of Philadelphia.

Types

Common types include the block or bar eraser, the cap eraser fitted over a pencil end, the kneaded eraser (a soft, putty-like eraser that can be moulded and lifts graphite without crumbs), the vinyl or plastic eraser, the art gum eraser, and the ink eraser, which contains abrasive material to remove ink by abrading the paper surface. Electric erasers, driven by a small motor, are used in technical drawing. Erasers remove pencil marks because the polymers in the eraser have a greater affinity for graphite particles than the paper fibres do; friction also heats and softens the eraser, which sheds rolled-up crumbs carrying the graphite away.
