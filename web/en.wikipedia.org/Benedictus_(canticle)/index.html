Benedictus (canticle)

The Benedictus (Latin for "blessed"), also called the Song of Zechariah or the Canticle of Zachary, is one of the three canticles in the first two chapters of the Gospel of Luke, the other two being the Magnificat and the Nunc dimittis. The Benedictus was the song of thanksgiving uttered by Zechariah on the occasion of the circumcision of his son, John the Baptist (Luke 1:57–79).

Content

The canticle has two parts. The first (verses 68–75) is a song of thanksgiving for the realisation of the Messianic hopes of the Jewish nation, acknowledging God's fidelity to his promises to Abraham and the house of David. The second part (verses 76–79) is an address by Zechariah to his own son, who would "go before the Lord to prepare his ways" and give knowledge of salvation by the remission of sins, concluding with the image of the "dayspring from on high" visiting those who sit in darkness.

Liturgical use

The Benedictus is used daily in the Roman Catholic Liturgy of the Hours at Lauds (Morning Prayer) and in the Anglican service of Morning Prayer, where it may be sung or said. It has been set to music by many composers within chant and polyphonic traditions.
