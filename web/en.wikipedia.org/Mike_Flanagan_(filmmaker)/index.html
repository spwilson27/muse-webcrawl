Mike Flanagan (filmmaker)

BornMay 20, 1978, Salem, Massachusetts, U.S.
OccupationFilm director, screenwriter, editor
Known forHorror films and series

Mike Flanagan (born May 20, 1978) is an American filmmaker best known for his work in the horror genre, which he frequently writes, directs and edits himself. His films include Absentia (2011), Oculus (2013), Hush (2016), Gerald's Game (2017) and Doctor Sleep (2019), a sequel to The Shining. For Netflix he created the acclaimed limited series The Haunting of Hill House (2018), The Haunting of Bly Manor (2020), Midnight Mass (2021) and The Fall of the House of Usher (2023).

Career

Flanagan grew up in Maryland and studied film at Towson University. After early work directing reality television and documentaries, he raised funds on Kickstarter for the micro-budget feature Absentia, which won festival awards and launched his horror career. Oculus, expanded from his short film, was a commercial success, and Hush, co-written with his wife, actress Kate Siegel, premiered on Netflix to critical praise.

Doctor Sleep required Flanagan to reconcile Stephen King's novel with Stanley Kubrick's film of The Shining, and earned King's endorsement. His television work is noted for long takes, ensemble casts of recurring collaborators, and horror grounded in grief and family trauma. He later adapted King's novella The Life of Chuck (2024), which won the People's Choice Award at the Toronto International Film Festival.
