Cotonou is the largest city and economic capital of Benin, in West Africa, and the seat of the country's government, although the official capital is Porto-Novo. It lies on the coastal strip between Lake Nokoué and the Atlantic Ocean (Gulf of Guinea), cut by a channel dug in 1885 that made it a deep-water port. Most government ministries, foreign embassies, and Benin's principal port and industry are concentrated here.

History

The name derives from the Fon phrase Ku-Tonu, "by the river of death", recalling the region's history in the slave trade era under the Kingdom of Dahomey. In the later 19th century France established itself at Cotonou, receiving formal cession from King Ghezo's successors, and built the wharf and railway that made it French Dahomey's principal port. It grew rapidly after Benin's independence in 1960.

Economy and transport

The Autonomous Port of Cotonou handles most of Benin's trade and serves as a transit hub for landlocked Niger and for re-export trade with Nigeria; imported used cars ("tokunbo") form a famous sector. The Dantokpa Market, sprawling over some 20 hectares near the lagoon, is among the largest open-air markets in West Africa. Motorcycle taxis known as zémidjans, in their yellow shirts, dominate local transport. Cadjehoun Airport is Benin's international airport, and the city is the terminus of the railway north to Parakou.

Geography

The city spreads east–west along the narrow barrier coast; seasonal flooding and coastal erosion threaten low-lying quarters. The fishing village of Ganvié, built on stilts in Lake Nokoué, lies just north of the city. Landmarks include the red-and-white striped Cotonou Cathedral, the Fondation Zinsou museum, and the 30-metre statue of the Amazone (a Dahomey woman warrior) inaugurated in 2022 near the presidency.
