Gottorf Castle

Gottorf Castle (German: Schloss Gottorf, Danish: Gottorp Slot) is a castle and estate in the city of Schleswig in the German state of Schleswig-Holstein. It was the residence of the dukes of Schleswig-Holstein-Gottorp and is one of the most important secular buildings in the state. Today it houses the Schleswig-Holstein State Museums.

Location: Schleswig, Schleswig-Holstein, Germany

Founded: First mentioned 1161

Former residents: Dukes of Schleswig-Holstein-Gottorp

Current use: State museums

History

The estate was first mentioned in 1161 as the residence of the Bishop of Schleswig. It passed to the Danish crown and then to the Counts of Holstein. In 1544 it became the seat of Duke Adolf, founder of the House of Schleswig-Holstein-Gottorp, and over the following century it was rebuilt as a Renaissance and then Baroque palace. Under Duke Frederick III it became a notable cultural and scientific court. The famous Gottorf Globe — a giant hollow globe-planetarium built for the duke in the 1650s–1660s — was later given to Tsar Peter the Great and is now in St. Petersburg; a modern replica stands in the castle's globe house.

After the Great Northern War, the Danish king annexed the Gottorp lands in Schleswig in 1713/1721, and the castle served as a Danish residence and later as a barracks. Baroque gardens at the site, the Neuwerk garden, contain a giant ceramic globe pavilion and were among the first Baroque gardens in northern Europe.

Museums

The castle today hosts the State Museum of Art and Cultural History and the State Archaeological Museum. The archaeological collections include the Nydam boat, a large oak rowing boat from the 4th century AD recovered from the Nydam bog, and the famous bog bodies and finds of the region, including the Thorsberg moor finds.
