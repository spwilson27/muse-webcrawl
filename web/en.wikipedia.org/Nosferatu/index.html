Nosferatu: A Symphony of Horror (German: Nosferatu – Eine Symphonie des Grauens) is a 1922 German Expressionist silent horror film directed by F. W. Murnau and starring Max Schreck as the vampire Count Orlok.

Production and lawsuit

The film was an unauthorised adaptation of Bram Stoker's 1897 novel Dracula, with names and details changed — the Count Dracula became Count Orlok, and "vampire" became "Nosferatu". Stoker's widow sued the production company Prana Film, and a court ordered all copies destroyed; some prints survived, securing the film's legacy.

Synopsis

Estate agent Hutter travels to Transylvania to sell a house to Count Orlok, who becomes obsessed with Hutter's wife Ellen. Orlok journeys by ship to the town of Wisborg, bringing plague with him, and Ellen ultimately sacrifices herself to destroy him at dawn.

Legacy

Nosferatu is regarded as a masterpiece of Expressionist cinema, famed for Schreck's gaunt, rat-like Orlok and its use of shadow and negative imagery. Werner Herzog remade it in 1979 as Nosferatu the Vampyre, and Robert Eggers directed a further remake released in 2024.
