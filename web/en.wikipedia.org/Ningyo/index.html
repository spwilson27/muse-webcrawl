Ningyo

Ningyo (Japanese: 人魚, literally "human fish") is a creature from Japanese folklore, generally described as a fish-like being — in some accounts resembling a mermaid, in others a fish with a human head, or an ape-like creature with a fish's body. Sightings of ningyo were recorded in chronicles such as the Nihon Shoki, and preserved "mermaid mummies" were displayed at sideshows and kept in temples during the Edo period.

Folklore

Unlike the seductive mermaids of Western tradition, the ningyo was often considered an omen of storms, war, or disaster, and catching one was thought to bring misfortune. Its flesh was said to grant longevity or eternal youth and beauty to anyone who ate it. The best-known legend is that of Yao Bikuni ("the eight-hundred-year nun"), a girl who unknowingly ate ningyo flesh and remained young for centuries, eventually becoming a wandering Buddhist nun.

Mummified ningyo

Numerous mummified ningyo survive in Japanese temples and museums. Scientific examinations have shown many to be composite fabrications made from fish, monkey parts, cloth, and other materials, crafted during the Edo and Meiji periods when such curiosities were prized attractions.
