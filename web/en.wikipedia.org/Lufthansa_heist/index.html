Lufthansa heist

The Lufthansa heist was a robbery at John F. Kennedy International Airport in New York City carried out in the early hours of 11 December 1978. An estimated $5.875 million (about $5 million in cash and $875,000 in jewellery) was stolen from the Lufthansa cargo terminal — at the time the largest cash robbery committed on American soil.

The robbery

The heist was masterminded by James Burke ("Jimmy the Gent"), an associate of the Lucchese crime family, and executed by a crew including Angelo Sepe, Louis Cafora, Thomas DeSimone and others, using inside information from Lufthansa employee Louis Werner, who owed gambling debts and told bookmaker Martin Krugman about the cash flown in from Germany. The robbers subdued the terminal staff without firing a shot. The cash was largely untraceable "American money" that had circulated among servicemen and tourists in West Germany.

Aftermath

Burke, fearing exposure, is believed to have ordered the murder of most of the participants and intermediaries in the months that followed; several bodies were found, others disappeared. Almost none of the money was recovered. Werner was the only person convicted in direct connection with the heist itself; decades later, in 2014, mobster Vincent Asaro was charged in relation to the robbery but acquitted in 2015. The story was popularised by Nicholas Pileggi's book Wiseguy and Martin Scorsese's film Goodfellas (1990), in which Burke appears as "Jimmy Conway" and Henry Hill — a Burke associate — narrates.
