Marguerite de Navarre (1492–1549), also known as Marguerite d'Angoulême, was Queen of Navarre as the wife of Henry II of Navarre and the sister of King Francis I of France. A leading figure of the French Renaissance, she was a patron of humanists and reformers, a diplomat, and one of the most influential women of her century.

Life and work

Marguerite played a key diplomatic role in negotiating the release of Francis I after his capture at Pavia in 1525. Her court at Nérac sheltered religious moderates and evangelical reformers, though she remained within the Catholic Church. Her best-known literary work, the Heptaméron — a collection of 72 framed tales modelled on Boccaccio's Decameron — was published posthumously in 1558–1559; she also wrote religious poetry including the Miroir de l'âme pécheresse.

Family

Her daughter Jeanne d'Albret became Queen of Navarre, and her grandson reigned as Henry IV of France.
