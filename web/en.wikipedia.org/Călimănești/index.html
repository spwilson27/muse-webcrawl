Călimănești

Călimănești, often known as Călimănești-Căciulata, is a town in Vâlcea County, Oltenia, Romania, situated on the Olt River at the foot of the Southern Carpathians. It is a well-known spa resort, valued since Roman times for its sulphurous and mineral springs used in treating rheumatic, kidney and nervous disorders.

Geography and tourism

The town lies at the southern end of the Olt defile through the Carpathians, near the Cozia National Park. The resort area of Căciulata, administered by the town, hosts hotels and treatment centres. Nearby is the Cozia Monastery, founded in 1388 by Mircea the Elder, who is buried there.

History

Legend attributes the discovery of the springs to a shepherd named Căliman. The town developed as a balneary resort in the 19th century, and its waters were exhibited internationally. The Turnu and Cozia monasteries in the area date to the 14th century.
