United States Space Force

The United States Space Force (USSF) is the space service branch of the United States Armed Forces, one of the eight US uniformed services, and the smallest armed service. Established as an independent service on 20 December 2019, it was formed from Air Force Space Command, whose personnel and missions it inherited; like the Marine Corps' relationship to the Navy, the Space Force is a separate service within the Department of the Air Force, under the Secretary of the Air Force. Its members are called Guardians, a name drawn from Air Force Space Command's 1982 motto "Guardians of the High Frontier."

Creation

Calls for a separate space service grew with anxiety over Chinese and Russian counterspace weapons and after the 2001 Space Commission warned of a "Space Pearl Harbor." President Donald Trump directed the creation of a Space Force in 2018; the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2020, signed on 20 December 2019, established it as the sixth armed service. General John "Jay" Raymond became the first Chief of Space Operations and the first Space Force member of the Joint Chiefs of Staff.

Missions and organisation

The Space Force organises, trains and equips forces for space operations: satellite communications; positioning, navigation and timing (the GPS constellation); missile warning and tracking; space domain awareness; and space electronic warfare and launch operations (the Eastern and Western Ranges at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station and Vandenberg Space Force Base). Its field commands are Space Operations Command, Space Systems Command (acquisitions) and Space Training and Readiness Command (STARCOM). Most Guardians are transferred Air Force personnel; the service uses Army-style rank names for enlisted members ("Specialist," "Sergeant") and Air Force-style officer ranks.
