Laws of the Indies

The Laws of the Indies (Leyes de las Indias) are the entire body of laws issued by the Spanish Crown for its American and Philippine possessions, from the Laws of Burgos (1512) through the great compilation Recopilación de las Leyes de los Reinos de las Indias (1680), which organised some 6,400 laws into nine books.

Content

They governed colonial administration — viceroyalties, audiencias, the Council of the Indies — trade, the Church, and the status and (nominal) protection of Indigenous peoples, including rules on encomienda labour. Philip II's 1573 ordinances on town planning prescribed grid plans, central plazas and uniform building rules that shaped hundreds of cities from Mexico City to Manila.

Legacy

The famous formula obedézcase pero no se cumpla ("let it be obeyed but not carried out") reflected the gap between metropolitan law and colonial practice. The Laws remain a foundation for studying Spanish colonial urbanism and law.
