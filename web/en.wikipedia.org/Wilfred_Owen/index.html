Wilfred Edward Salter Owen MC (18 March 1893 – 4 November 1918) was an English poet and soldier. He was one of the leading poets of the First World War. His war poetry on the horrors of trenches and gas warfare stood in stark contrast to the public perception of war at the time and to the confidently patriotic verse written by earlier war poets such as Rupert Brooke. Some of his best-known works — most of which were published posthumously — include "Dulce et Decorum est", "Insensibility", "Anthem for Doomed Youth", "Futility" and "Strange Meeting".

Life

Owen was educated at the Birkenhead Institute and Shrewsbury Technical School, and worked as a lay assistant and private tutor in Bordeaux before enlisting in the Artists' Rifles in 1915. Commissioned into the Manchester Regiment, he fought on the Somme and was hospitalised with shell shock at Craiglockhart War Hospital in Edinburgh in 1917. There he met Siegfried Sassoon, who read his drafts, influenced his poetic development, and introduced him to literary circles including Robert Graves.

Poetry and death

Owen's mature poems, written between August 1917 and September 1918, pioneered the use of pararhyme and unflinching realism to convey the experience of industrial warfare. He returned to active service in France in 1918, won the Military Cross for gallantry at Joncourt, and was killed in action on 4 November 1918 during the crossing of the Sambre–Oise Canal at Ors, exactly one week before the signing of the Armistice. His mother received the telegram of his death on Armistice Day, as the church bells of Shrewsbury rang out in celebration.

Legacy

Only five of Owen's poems were published in his lifetime. Posthumous editions edited by Sassoon (1920) and Edmund Blunden (1931) established his reputation; Benjamin Britten incorporated nine of his poems into the War Requiem (1962). Owen is buried at Ors Communal Cemetery.
