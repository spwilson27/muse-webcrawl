Ferdinando Gorges

Sir Ferdinando Gorges
Bornc. 1565–1568, Somerset, England
Died24 May 1647, Long Ashton, England
Known forColonization of Maine and New England

Sir Ferdinando Gorges (c. 1565/68 – 1647) was an English military commander and colonial entrepreneur, often called the "Father of English colonization in North America" for his central role in founding the Province of Maine. Although he never visited America himself, he promoted and financed numerous expeditions and settlements from his base as governor of Plymouth, England.

Career

Gorges served in the wars in the Netherlands and was involved in the Essex Rebellion of 1601, later testifying against the Earl of Essex. As a shareholder in the Plymouth Company, he sponsored the ill-fated Popham Colony (1607–08) in present-day Maine. Through the Council for New England, he and John Mason received grants dividing the territory that became Maine and New Hampshire; in 1639 he obtained a royal charter as Lord Proprietor of the Province of Maine.

Legacy

Gorges envisioned orderly, hierarchical colonies under proprietary rule, in contrast to the Puritan settlements of Massachusetts Bay, with which he clashed in a series of legal and political disputes. His province was gradually absorbed by Massachusetts after his death, but his claims were eventually sold by his heirs to Massachusetts in 1677. The town of Gorges in Maine bears his family name (as does Gorgeana, the early name for York, Maine).
