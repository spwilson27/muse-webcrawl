Yahwist

The Yahwist, or Jahwist, often abbreviated J, is the most prominent of the sources identified in the documentary hypothesis for the composition of the Torah (or Pentateuch), the first five books of the Hebrew Bible. The others are the Elohist (E), the Deuteronomist (D), and the Priestly source (P).

The Yahwist source is identified by its use of the name YHWH and its vivid narrative style.

Characteristics

The Yahwist text is identified by its consistent use of the Tetragrammaton, YHWH, for God from the beginning of Genesis; by its anthropomorphic portrayal of God, who walks in the Garden of Eden and speaks face to face; and by its focus on the southern kingdom of Judah and on themes of promise, blessing, and land. It includes the creation account of Genesis 2, the flood narrative in part, the patriarchal stories, and much of Exodus and Numbers.

Date

Classical scholarship dated J to the tenth century BCE, the time of Solomon, but many modern scholars place its composition or final shaping in the exilic or post-exilic period (sixth–fifth centuries BCE). Its dating remains one of the most debated issues in biblical studies.
