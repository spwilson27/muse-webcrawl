Christmas and holiday season

The Christmas and holiday season is an annually recurring period near the end of the year, centered on Christmas Day and typically encompassing other festivals such as Hanukkah, Kwanzaa, and New Year's Day. In the United States and other countries it generally runs from late November (around Thanksgiving) through the New Year, and is characterized by peak retail activity, travel, charitable giving, school holidays, and seasonal media.

Decorations, shopping, and travel define the Christmas and holiday season

Scope and timing

The season is often considered to begin with Thanksgiving in the United States (the fourth Thursday of November) and to end after New Year's Day, sometimes extending to Epiphany (January 6). Retailers mark its start with Black Friday and Cyber Monday. Related seasonal periods in other cultures include Advent in Western Christianity, the pre-Lenten Carnival in some traditions, and winter festivals worldwide that share customs of feasting, gift-giving, and light in the darkest part of the year.

Economic significance

The season is the most important period of the year for many retailers; the National Retail Federation regularly reports that a substantial share of annual retail sales occurs in November and December, and many businesses rely on holiday surges in staffing and sales. It is also the busiest travel period of the year in countries such as the United States, and a peak season for charitable donations and postal and parcel volumes.

Cultural aspects

The inclusive phrase "holiday season" and greetings such as "Happy Holidays" are widely used in the United States to acknowledge the multiple religious and secular observances in the period, a practice that has periodically drawn public debate. Seasonal traditions include Christmas music and films, office parties, greeting cards, and televised specials and sporting events.
