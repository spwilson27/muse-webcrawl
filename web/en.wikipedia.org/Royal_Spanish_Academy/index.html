The Royal Spanish Academy (Spanish: Real Academia Española, RAE) is Spain's official royal institution with a mission to ensure the stability of the Spanish language. Founded in 1713 on the initiative of Juan Manuel Fernández Pacheco, 8th Marquis of Villena, it was modelled on the Académie française and granted royal assent by Philip V in 1714.

Work

The Academy's principal product is the Diccionario de la lengua española, first published in 1780 and regularly revised; it also produces authoritative grammars and orthographies. Since the late twentieth century the RAE has worked in a pan-Hispanic framework with the Association of Spanish Language Academies, which groups the academies of all Spanish-speaking countries, so that its reference works reflect the unity and diversity of Spanish worldwide.

Membership

The Academy comprises 46 members elected for life, each occupying a chair identified by a letter of the alphabet. Members have included many of Spain's most notable writers and scholars.
