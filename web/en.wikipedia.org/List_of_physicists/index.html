List of physicists

This is a list of physicists, organised by era, of notable contributors to the science of physics, from the founders of classical mechanics to the architects of quantum theory, relativity and modern particle physics.

Founders of classical physics (16th–18th centuries)

  • Galileo Galilei (1564–1642) — founder of modern experimental science; laws of falling bodies and pendulum motion.
  • Johannes Kepler (1571–1630) — three laws of planetary motion.
  • René Descartes (1596–1650) — analytic geometry; conservation of momentum.
  • Christiaan Huygens (1629–1695) — wave theory of light; pendulum clock.
  • Isaac Newton (1643–1727) — laws of motion and universal gravitation; co-inventor of calculus.
  • Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz (1646–1716) — calculus; vis viva (kinetic energy) concept.
  • Daniel Bernoulli (1700–1782) — fluid dynamics; Bernoulli's principle.
  • Leonhard Euler (1707–1783) — mechanics, optics and mathematical physics.
  • Benjamin Franklin (1706–1790) — electrical charge, lightning rod.
  • Joseph-Louis Lagrange (1736–1813) — analytical (Lagrangian) mechanics.
  • Alessandro Volta (1745–1827) — the electric battery (voltaic pile).
  • John Dalton (1766–1844) — atomic theory of matter.
  • Thomas Young (1773–1829) — double-slit experiment; wave theory of light.
  • André-Marie Ampère (1775–1836) — electrodynamics; the ampere is named for him.
  • Joseph Fourier (1768–1830) — heat conduction; Fourier analysis.
  • William Rowan Hamilton (1805–1865) — Hamiltonian mechanics.

19th century

  • Michael Faraday (1791–1867) — electromagnetic induction; laws of electrolysis.
  • Sadi Carnot (1796–1832) — founder of thermodynamics.
  • Georg Ohm (1789–1854) — Ohm's law of electrical resistance.
  • James Prescott Joule (1818–1889) — mechanical equivalent of heat.
  • Lord Kelvin (William Thomson) (1824–1907) — absolute temperature scale; thermodynamics.
  • Rudolf Clausius (1822–1888) — entropy; second law of thermodynamics.
  • James Clerk Maxwell (1831–1879) — Maxwell's equations unifying electricity, magnetism and light; statistical mechanics.
  • Ludwig Boltzmann (1844–1906) — statistical mechanics; Boltzmann constant.
  • Josiah Willard Gibbs (1839–1903) — chemical thermodynamics; phase rule.
  • Heinrich Hertz (1857–1894) — experimental proof of electromagnetic waves.
  • J. J. Thomson (1856–1940) — discovery of the electron (1897).
  • Wilhelm Röntgen (1845–1923) — discovery of X-rays; first Nobel Prize in Physics (1901).
  • Henri Becquerel (1852–1908) — discovery of radioactivity.
  • Marie Curie (1867–1934) — pioneering research on radioactivity; Nobel Prizes in Physics and Chemistry.
  • Pierre Curie (1859–1906) — magnetism (Curie's law); piezoelectricity.
  • Max Planck (1858–1947) — founder of quantum theory; Planck's constant.
  • Hendrik Lorentz (1853–1928) — Lorentz transformations; electron theory.
  • Henri Poincaré (1854–1912) — foundations of relativity and chaos theory.

Founders of modern physics (1900–1950)

  • Albert Einstein (1879–1955) — special and general relativity; photoelectric effect; E = mc².
  • Ernest Rutherford (1871–1937) — nuclear model of the atom; discovered the proton.
  • Niels Bohr (1885–1962) — Bohr model of the atom; complementarity.
  • Arnold Sommerfeld (1868–1951) — atomic structure; teacher of a generation of theorists.
  • Werner Heisenberg (1901–1976) — matrix mechanics; uncertainty principle.
  • Erwin Schrödinger (1887–1961) — wave mechanics; Schrödinger equation.
  • Paul Dirac (1902–1984) — quantum electrodynamics foundations; predicted antimatter.
  • Wolfgang Pauli (1900–1958) — exclusion principle; proposed the neutrino.
  • Max Born (1882–1970) — probabilistic interpretation of the wave function.
  • Louis de Broglie (1892–1987) — matter waves (wave–particle duality).
  • Satyendra Nath Bose (1894–1974) — Bose–Einstein statistics; bosons named for him.
  • Enrico Fermi (1901–1954) — nuclear physics; first nuclear reactor; Fermi statistics.
  • J. Robert Oppenheimer (1904–1967) — scientific director of the Manhattan Project.
  • Lise Meitner (1878–1968) — co-discoverer of nuclear fission.
  • Otto Hahn (1879–1968) — discovery of nuclear fission.
  • Victor Weisskopf (1908–2002) — quantum electrodynamics; CERN director-general.
  • Eugene Wigner (1902–1995) — symmetry in quantum mechanics.
  • John von Neumann (1903–1957) — mathematical foundations of quantum mechanics.
  • Emmy Noether (1882–1935) — Noether's theorem linking symmetry and conservation laws.
  • Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar (1910–1995) — stellar structure; Chandrasekhar limit.
  • Lev Landau (1908–1968) — condensed matter and theoretical physics; Landau levels.
  • Hideki Yukawa (1907–1981) — predicted the meson; first Japanese Nobel laureate in physics.
  • Cécile DeWitt-Morette and Chien-Shiung Wu (1912–1997) — Wu experimentally disproved parity conservation.
  • Abdus Salam (1926–1996) — electroweak unification (later work).

Post-war and contemporary physics

  • Richard Feynman (1918–1988) — quantum electrodynamics; Feynman diagrams; path integrals.
  • Julian Schwinger (1918–1994) and Shin'ichirō Tomonaga (1906–1979) — co-founders of QED.
  • Murray Gell-Mann (1929–2019) — the quark model; quantum chromodynamics foundations.
  • George Zweig (b. 1937) — independently proposed quarks ("aces").
  • Sheldon Glashow (b. 1932), Steven Weinberg (1933–2021), Abdus Salam — electroweak unification.
  • Peter Higgs (1929–2024) — the Higgs mechanism; co-predicted the Higgs boson.
  • François Englert (b. 1932) and Robert Brout (1928–2011) — co-founders of the Higgs mechanism.
  • Yoichiro Nambu (1921–2015) — spontaneous symmetry breaking; string theory pioneer.
  • David Gross (b. 1941), Frank Wilczek (b. 1951), David Politzer (b. 1949) — asymptotic freedom in QCD.
  • Gerard 't Hooft (b. 1946) and Martinus Veltman (1931–2021) — renormalisation of gauge theories.
  • Stephen Hawking (1942–2018) — black hole radiation (Hawking radiation); cosmology.
  • Roger Penrose (b. 1931) — singularity theorems; Penrose diagrams.
  • Kip Thorne (b. 1940) — gravitational waves; LIGO co-founder.
  • Rainer Weiss (1932–2025) and Barry Barish (b. 1936) — LIGO and the first detection of gravitational waves (2015).
  • Alan Guth (b. 1947) — cosmic inflation.
  • Edward Witten (b. 1951) — string theory and M-theory.
  • John Bardeen (1908–1991) — co-inventor of the transistor; BCS theory of superconductivity; only person to win two Nobel Prizes in Physics.
  • Philip W. Anderson (1923–2020) — condensed matter theory; localisation.
  • Leo Esaki (b. 1925) and Ivar Giaever (1929–2025) — tunnelling phenomena.
  • Donna Strickland (b. 1959) — chirped pulse amplification for lasers.
  • Andrea Ghez (b. 1965) and Reinhard Genzel (b. 1952) — the supermassive black hole at the Galactic Centre.
  • Takaaki Kajita (b. 1959) and Arthur B. McDonald (b. 1943) — neutrino oscillations.
  • Michel Mayor (b. 1942) and Didier Queloz (b. 1966) — first exoplanet around a sun-like star.
