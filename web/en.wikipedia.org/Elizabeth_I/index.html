Elizabeth I

Elizabeth I (7 September 1533 – 24 March 1603) was Queen of England and Ireland from 17 November 1558 until her death. The daughter of Henry VIII and his second wife, Anne Boleyn, she was the last monarch of the House of Tudor. Her 44-year reign, known as the Elizabethan era, is famed for the flourishing of English drama, the seafaring expansion of explorers such as Francis Drake, and the defeat of the Spanish Armada.

Early life

Elizabeth was declared illegitimate after her mother's execution in 1536 and spent much of her youth in a precarious position at court. During the reign of her Catholic half-sister Mary I, she was imprisoned in the Tower of London on suspicion of involvement in Wyatt's rebellion, though later released to house arrest at Woodstock.

Reign

On accession she established a moderate religious settlement, re-creating the Church of England with the monarch as supreme governor. She never married, despite many suitors, and used the prospect of marriage as a diplomatic tool. Her reign saw the execution of Mary, Queen of Scots, in 1587, and the defeat of the Spanish Armada in 1588, after which she delivered her famous speech at Tilbury.

Government and culture

Elizabeth governed with the counsel of advisers such as William Cecil, Lord Burghley, and maintained an elaborate cult of royal imagery. The era produced the works of William Shakespeare, Christopher Marlowe and Edmund Spenser, and saw the beginnings of English colonization in the Americas.

Death and succession

Elizabeth died in 1603 without children and was succeeded by James VI of Scotland, uniting the crowns of England and Scotland. Her reign is often regarded as a golden age in English history.
