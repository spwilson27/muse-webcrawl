Dartmoor pony

Dartmoor pony
Country of origin: England (Devon)
Height: up to 12.2 hands (127 cm)
Colour: Bay, brown, black, grey, chestnut, roan
Status: Rare breed

The Dartmoor pony is a breed of pony that lives on Dartmoor in Devon, England, where ponies have grazed the moor for centuries. It is a hardy, sure-footed breed of good temperament, traditionally used for riding, driving and work in tin mines, and today valued as a children's riding and show pony.

Characteristics

Dartmoor ponies stand no more than 12.2 hands (about 127 cm). They have a small, refined head, strong body, and dense coat adapted to the harsh moorland climate. Most solid colours are accepted, but piebald and skewbald markings are not permitted in the registered breed.

History and status

A breed society was founded in 1899 and a studbook established in the early 20th century. Numbers of semi-feral ponies on the moor have declined sharply, from tens of thousands in the 1930s to only a few thousand, and the registered Dartmoor is classed as a rare breed by the Rare Breeds Survival Trust. Moorland herds play an important role in conservation grazing.
