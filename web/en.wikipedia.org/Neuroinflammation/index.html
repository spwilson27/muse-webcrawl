Neuroinflammation

Neuroinflammation is inflammation of the nervous tissue of the brain and spinal cord. It may be initiated in response to infection, traumatic brain injury, toxic metabolites, or autoimmunity, and is mediated chiefly by microglia and astrocytes, the resident immune cells of the central nervous system.

Mechanisms

Activated microglia and astrocytes release cytokines, chemokines, complement proteins, and reactive oxygen species. Acute neuroinflammation is usually protective, clearing debris and pathogens, but chronic activation can damage neurons and synapses and is implicated in the progression of neurodegenerative diseases.

Role in disease

Neuroinflammatory processes feature in multiple sclerosis, Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's disease, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, and in responses to stroke and trauma. Inflammation can also follow systemic insults when the blood–brain barrier is disrupted. Understanding and modulating neuroinflammation is an active area of therapeutic research.
