Lockheed Martin F-35 Lightning II

The Lockheed Martin F-35 Lightning II is an American family of single-seat, single-engine, stealth multirole combat aircraft designed for both air superiority and strike missions. It also provides electronic warfare and intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance capabilities. The aircraft is built by Lockheed Martin with principal partners Northrop Grumman and BAE Systems, and comes in three variants: the conventional takeoff and landing F-35A, the short take-off and vertical-landing F-35B, and the carrier-based F-35C. The F-35 was the product of the Joint Strike Fighter programme, the largest and most expensive military weapons programme in history, and first flew in 2006.

Development

The F-35 descended from the Lockheed Martin X-35, which in 2001 won the Joint Strike Fighter competition against the Boeing X-32. Designed around stealth shaping, an internal weapons bay, the F135 engine, and an advanced sensor suite centred on the AN/APG-81 AESA radar and electro-optical systems, the aircraft was intended to replace a wide range of US and allied types including the F-16, A-10, F/A-18 and AV-8B Harrier II.

Service

The F-35B entered service with the US Marine Corps in 2015, the F-35A with the US Air Force in 2016, and the F-35C with the US Navy in 2019. The aircraft is operated by numerous allied air forces, including those of the United Kingdom, Italy, the Netherlands, Australia, Japan, Norway, Israel — which first used it in combat in 2018 — and others. Well over a thousand aircraft have been delivered.

Criticism

The programme has been criticised for cost overruns, delays, high operating costs and technical issues, including problems with the helmet-mounted display, ejection seat, and the ALIS logistics system later replaced by ODIN. Supporters argue the F-35's sensor fusion and low observability give it unmatched situational awareness and survivability.
