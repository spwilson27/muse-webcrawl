Academy Award for Best Director

The Academy Award for Best Director (officially known as the Academy Award of Merit for Directing) is an award presented annually by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences (AMPAS). It is given in honour of a film director who has exhibited outstanding directing while working in the film industry.

History

The award was first presented at the 1st Academy Awards in 1929, when it was split into two categories — Best Director (Dramatic Picture), won by Frank Borzage for 7th Heaven, and Best Director (Comedy Picture), won by Lewis Milestone for Two Arabian Knights. The categories were merged from the following ceremony.

The winner is traditionally presented the award by the previous year's winner. In most years the Best Director winner has also directed the Best Picture winner, though the two awards have diverged more frequently in recent decades.

Records

John Ford holds the record with four wins, for The Informer (1935), The Grapes of Wrath (1940), How Green Was My Valley (1941) and The Quiet Man (1952). Frank Capra and William Wyler each won three times; Wyler also holds the record for nominations, with twelve. Kathryn Bigelow became the first woman to win, for The Hurt Locker (2009); Chloé Zhao (Nomadland) and Jane Campion (The Power of the Dog) followed. The youngest winner is Damien Chazelle, aged 32, for La La Land (2016).
