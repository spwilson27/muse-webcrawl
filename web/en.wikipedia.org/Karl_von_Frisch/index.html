Karl von Frisch

Karl Ritter von Frisch (20 November 1886 – 12 June 1982) was an Austrian ethologist who received the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1973, along with Nikolaas Tinbergen and Konrad Lorenz. His work centred on investigations of the sensory perceptions of the honey bee, and he was one of the first to translate the meaning of the waggle dance.

Life

Von Frisch was born in Vienna, the son of the surgeon and urologist Anton Ritter von Frisch and his wife Marie, née Exner. He studied zoology at the University of Vienna and later in Munich under Richard von Hertwig. He completed his doctorate in 1910 and worked at the Zoological Institute of the University of Munich, where he became professor of zoology.

During the First World War he worked in a military hospital in Vienna. After the war he returned to Munich, and in 1925 he moved to the University of Munich permanently, where he founded the Institute of Zoology. The institute was largely destroyed by bombing in the Second World War, but was rebuilt after 1945.

Work on bees

Von Frisch showed that bees can distinguish various blossoming plants by their scent, and that they possess a sense of taste. He demonstrated that honey bees communicate the direction and distance of food sources to other bees in the hive through the waggle dance, a figure-eight movement performed on the vertical comb. He also studied the bees' internal clock, their orientation by the sun and polarised light, and their colour vision — proving that bees see ultraviolet light but are blind to red.

His popular book The Dancing Bees (1927) made his findings widely known. His theory of the dance language was initially controversial and was later confirmed by other researchers, notably through the work of Adrian Wenner and later experiments using robotic bees.

Honours

Von Frisch shared the 1973 Nobel Prize "for their discoveries concerning organization and elicitation of individual and social behaviour patterns." He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society and received numerous other distinctions, including the Balzan Prize in 1962.
