Périgord

Périgord is a natural region and historical province in south-western France, roughly corresponding to the modern Dordogne department in Nouvelle-Aquitaine. Its capital is Périgueux, which preserves the name of the Gallic Petrocorii tribe.

Sub-regions

The region is traditionally divided into four areas named by colour: the Périgord Vert (green) in the north, the Périgord Blanc (white, limestone) in the centre around Périgueux, the Périgord Noir (black, dark oak forests) in the south-east around Sarlat-la-Canéda, and the Périgord Pourpre (purple, vineyards) around Bergerac in the south-west.

History and heritage

Périgord is celebrated for its prehistoric sites in the Vézère valley, including the painted caves of Lascaux, and for its medieval castles and bastide towns. It was a battleground in the Hundred Years' War between England and France.

Gastronomy

The region is famed for foie gras, black truffles (the "black diamonds" of the Périgord), walnuts, duck confit, and Bergerac wines.
