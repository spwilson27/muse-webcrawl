Chilpancingo


Cathedral of Santa María de la Asunción

Chilpancingo de los Bravo (commonly shortened to Chilpancingo) is the capital and second-largest city of the Mexican state of Guerrero, after Acapulco. It lies in the Sierra Madre del Sur mountains at an elevation of about 1,250 metres (4,100 ft), on the federal highway connecting Mexico City with Acapulco. The municipality had a population of roughly 240,000 at the 2020 census, with the city proper accounting for most of that number.

History

The name derives from Nahuatl Chilpantzinco, meaning "place of the little red wasp" or "place of the red flags". In pre-Hispanic times the area was inhabited by Nahua and other peoples and was later tributary to the Aztec Empire. During the Mexican War of Independence, Chilpancingo achieved lasting historical importance as the site of the Congress of Chilpancingo in 1813, convened by José María Morelos. There, in the church of Santa María de la Asunción, Morelos presented the Sentimientos de la Nación ("Feelings of the Nation"), and the congress formally declared Mexico's independence from Spain on 6 November 1813. The suffix "de los Bravo" honours the Bravo family of insurgents from nearby Chichihualco.

Geography and climate

Chilpancingo occupies a valley in the Sierra Madre del Sur, drained by the Huacapa River. Its climate is warm subhumid, with a rainy season from June to September and mild, dry winters. The city is the seat of the Universidad Autónoma de Guerrero and serves as an administrative and commercial centre for the central region of the state.
