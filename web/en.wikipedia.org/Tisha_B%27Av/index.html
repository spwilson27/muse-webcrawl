Tisha B'Av

Tisha B'Av (Hebrew: תשעה באב, "the Ninth of Av") is an annual fast day in Judaism, one of the most solemn days of the Jewish calendar. It primarily commemorates the destruction of the First and Second Temples in Jerusalem, traditionally held to have occurred on the same date.

Observances

The fast lasts from sunset on the eighth of Av until nightfall on the ninth, roughly 25 hours, during which eating and drinking are prohibited. Additional restrictions, modelled on mourning practices, include refraining from bathing, wearing leather shoes, and marital relations. In synagogue, the Book of Lamentations is read in the evening, and kinot (dirges) are recited. Worshippers often sit on low stools or the floor as a sign of mourning.

Events commemorated

According to tradition, five calamities befell the Jewish people on the Ninth of Av: the decree after the report of the spies that the generation of the Exodus would die in the desert; the destruction of the First Temple (586 BCE); the destruction of the Second Temple (70 CE); the fall of Betar in the Bar Kokhba revolt (135 CE); and the ploughing over of Jerusalem by the Romans. Later tragedies, including expulsions from England (1290) and Spain (1492), also became associated with the date.

The Three Weeks

Tisha B'Av culminates a period of mourning beginning on the fast of the 17th of Tammuz, when the walls of Jerusalem were breached, and intensifies during the final nine days of the period.
