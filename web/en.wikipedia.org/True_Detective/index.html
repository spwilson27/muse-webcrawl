True Detective is an American crime drama television series created by Nic Pizzolatto for HBO. The series is an anthology: each season follows a new cast of detectives investigating a murder, often across multiple timelines, in a distinct American setting.

Seasons

The first season (2014), set in coastal Louisiana, starred Matthew McConaughey and Woody Harrelson as detectives Rust Cohle and Marty Hart, whose hunt for a ritualistic serial killer spans seventeen years. Directed entirely by Cary Joji Fukunaga, it received widespread critical acclaim and numerous award nominations.

The second season (2015), set in California, starred Colin Farrell, Rachel McAdams, Taylor Kitsch and Vince Vaughn, and received a more mixed response. The third season (2019), set in the Ozarks and starring Mahershala Ali, returned to a multi-decade structure and was better received. The fourth season, True Detective: Night Country (2024), was set in Alaska, starred Jodie Foster and Kali Reis, and was the first season developed by a new showrunner, Issa López.

Reception

The series has been praised for its writing, cinematography and performances, and it is credited with helping popularise the modern television anthology format. Its first season in particular is frequently ranked among the finest seasons of television drama.
