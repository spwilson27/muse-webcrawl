Dromaeosauridae is a family of feathered theropod dinosaurs, commonly known as raptors. They were small to medium-sized carnivores that flourished in the Cretaceous Period. The name Dromaeosauridae means "running lizards", from Greek dromeus ("runner") and sauros ("lizard"). Dromaeosaurids include the famous genera Deinonychus and Velociraptor, and are among the closest known relatives of birds.

Description

Dromaeosaurids were small to medium-sized dinosaurs, ranging from about 0.5 metres (1.6 ft) in length for the microraptorines to over 6 metres (20 ft) for large forms such as Utahraptor and Dakotaraptor. Their most distinctive feature was an enlarged, strongly curved "sickle claw" on the second toe of each foot, held off the ground while walking. They had stiffened tails that acted as dynamic stabilizers, long arms, and relatively large brains for dinosaurs, indicating keen senses and possibly complex behaviour.

Feathers

Evidence for feathers in dromaeosaurids is extensive. Quill knobs have been found on the forearm of Velociraptor, and fully feathered specimens of Microraptor, Sinornithosaurus and Zhenyuanlong have been recovered from the Jehol Biota of China. Microraptor possessed long flight feathers on both its arms and legs, making it a "four-winged" dinosaur.

Classification

Dromaeosauridae was first named by William Diller Matthew and Barnum Brown in 1922. The family is divided into several subfamilies, including Microraptorinae, Unenlagiinae, Halszkaraptorinae, Velociraptorinae, Dromaeosaurinae and Saurornitholestinae. Dromaeosaurids, together with troodontids, form the clade Deinonychosauria, which is the sister group to Avialae (birds and their closest relatives).

Palaeobiology

Dromaeosaurids were active predators. Studies of the sickle claw suggest it was used to restrain or puncture prey rather than to slash. Some evidence, including trackways and bonebeds, suggests that certain dromaeosaurids may have hunted in groups, though this remains debated.
