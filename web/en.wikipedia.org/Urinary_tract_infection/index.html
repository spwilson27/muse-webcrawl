Urinary tract infection
Specialty: Urology, infectious disease
Symptoms: Painful urination, frequent urination, pelvic pain
Causes: Bacterial infection (often Escherichia coli)

A urinary tract infection (UTI) is an infection affecting part of the urinary system, including the bladder, urethra, ureters, or kidneys. UTIs are among the most common bacterial infections worldwide, affecting women much more frequently than men because of their shorter urethra.

Signs and symptoms

Typical symptoms include a burning sensation when urinating, frequent and urgent urination, cloudy or strong-smelling urine, and pelvic pain. Infections spreading to the kidneys (pyelonephritis) may cause fever, flank pain, nausea, and vomiting.

Causes and diagnosis

Most UTIs are caused by bacteria, especially E. coli from the gastrointestinal tract. Diagnosis is generally based on symptoms and confirmed by urinalysis or urine culture. Uncomplicated cases are usually treated with oral antibiotics such as nitrofurantoin or trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole.

Prevention

Staying hydrated and urinating after sexual intercourse may lower risk. Recurrent infections sometimes warrant low-dose preventive antibiotics.
