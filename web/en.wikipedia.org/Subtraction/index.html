Subtraction

Subtraction is one of the four basic arithmetic operations, the inverse of addition. It represents the removal of objects from a collection, or the difference between two quantities. The result of subtracting the subtrahend from the minuend is called the difference, and the operation is denoted by the minus sign (−).

Subtraction
Field: Arithmetic
Symbol: −
Terms: Minuend − subtrahend = difference
Inverse: Addition

Basic properties

Subtraction is neither commutative nor associative: a − b differs from b − a, and grouping matters. Within the natural numbers, subtraction is only defined when the minuend is at least as large as the subtrahend; extending to integers allows negative results. Subtracting zero leaves a number unchanged, and subtracting a negative number equals adding its positive counterpart.

Algorithms

In primary education, subtraction of multi-digit numbers is taught with methods such as decomposition and borrowing (regrouping), in which a unit is taken from a higher place value when a digit of the subtrahend exceeds the corresponding digit of the minuend.

Beyond numbers

The operation generalizes to vectors, matrices, polynomials, and sets, where set difference removes the elements of one set from another. In computing, subtraction is implemented by adding the two's complement of the subtrahend.
