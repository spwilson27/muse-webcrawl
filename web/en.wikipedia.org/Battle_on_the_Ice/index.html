The Battle on the Ice, also known as the Battle of Lake Peipus, was fought on 5 April 1242 between the forces of the Novgorod Republic, led by Prince Alexander Nevsky, and a coalition of the Livonian branch of the Teutonic Knights and their allies, largely on the frozen surface of Lake Peipus.

Background

During the Northern Crusades, the Livonian Order sought to expand eastward at Novgorod's expense and had captured Pskov. Alexander Nevsky retook the frontier fortresses in the winter of 1241–42 and advanced to meet the crusader army in its own territory; the two sides met on the ice near the eastern shore of the lake.

The battle

The crusader knights charged in their customary wedge formation and broke into the Novgorodian centre, but were enveloped by the stronger wings of Alexander's army. After hard fighting the crusader force broke and fled across the ice, pursued for several kilometres. Later tradition holds that many knights drowned when the ice gave way, though this detail is not in the earliest chronicles.

Aftermath

The defeat halted the eastward expansion of the Teutonic Order at Novgorod's frontier and secured the border for decades. Alexander Nevsky became a central hero of Russian historical memory, later canonized as a saint, and the battle was famously dramatized in Sergei Eisenstein's 1938 film Alexander Nevsky.
