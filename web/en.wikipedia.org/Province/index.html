Province

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A province is almost always an administrative division within a country or state. The term derives from the ancient Roman provincia, which was the major territorial and administrative unit of the Roman Empire's territorial possessions outside Italy. The term "province" has since been adopted by many countries. In some countries with no actual provinces, "the provinces" is a metaphorical term meaning "outside the capital city".

While some provinces were produced artificially by colonial powers, others were formed around long-established local groups with their own ethnic identities. Many have their own powers independent of central or federal authority, especially in Canada and Pakistan. In other countries, like China or France, provinces are the creation of central government, with very little autonomy.

Etymology and history

The English word "province" is attested from about 1330 and derives from the 13th-century Old French province, from the Latin word provincia, which referred to a magistrate's sphere of authority and later to a territory administered by a provincial governor. In Roman law, a provincia was originally a task assigned to a magistrate; under the republic and empire it became the standard term for an overseas territory governed by a proconsul or propraetor, such as Sicily, Gaul, or Asia.

Modern usage

Today, provinces are the first-level administrative divisions of countries including Argentina, Canada, China, Indonesia, Pakistan, the Philippines, South Africa, Spain (provincias), and Vietnam, among others. Canada's ten provinces, for example, are constituent units of the federation with constitutionally entrenched powers comparable in many areas to the federal government. China's 23 provinces (including Taiwan, claimed by the People's Republic of China) are governed by provincial governments subordinate to the central government in the country's unitary system.

In some languages, equivalents of "province" also denote ecclesiastical provinces — groupings of dioceses under an archbishop in the Roman Catholic, Anglican, and other churches — and the term survives in religious orders as the designation of regional groupings of houses.

"The provinces"

In countries such as the United Kingdom and France, "the provinces" refers to anywhere outside the capital — a usage that can carry overtones of the capital's cultural dominance and the supposed backwardness or charm of provincial life. The adjective "provincial" derives from this contrast and can mean unsophisticated or narrow in outlook, as well as simply relating to a province.
