The Caribbean Court of Justice (CCJ) is the judicial institution of the Caribbean Community (CARICOM). Established in 2005 and seated in Port of Spain, Trinidad and Tobago, it is the Caribbean's regional court, exercising both an original jurisdiction over the Revised Treaty of Chaguaramas and, for states that have acceded to it, an appellate jurisdiction as their final court of appeal.

The headquarters of the Caribbean Court of Justice in Port of Spain.

History

The Agreement Establishing the Caribbean Court of Justice was signed in Bridgetown, Barbados, on 14 February 2001. The Court was inaugurated on 16 April 2005. It was designed to replace the Judicial Committee of the Privy Council in London as the final appellate court for Caribbean states, completing the region's judicial independence.

Jurisdiction

In its original jurisdiction, the CCJ interprets and applies the Revised Treaty of Chaguaramas, which established the CARICOM Single Market and Economy; all member states are subject to this jurisdiction, and its decisions are compulsory. In its appellate jurisdiction, the Court hears appeals from states that have amended their constitutions to make it their highest court. Barbados and Guyana were the first to do so in 2005, followed by Belize (2010) and Dominica (2015).

Judges and administration

The Court's judges are appointed by the Regional Judicial and Legal Services Commission, an independent body, insulating appointments from political influence. The Court's financing is secured through a trust fund administered by the Caribbean Development Bank, intended to guarantee its independence. The Court is also notable for its use of technology, including electronic filing and video-conferenced hearings across the region bordering the Caribbean Sea.
