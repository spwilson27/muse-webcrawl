Wave

In physics, mathematics, engineering, and related fields, a wave is a propagating dynamic disturbance (change from equilibrium) of one or more quantities. Periodic waves oscillate repeatedly about an equilibrium (resting) value at some frequency. When the entire waveform moves in one direction, it is said to be a travelling wave; by contrast, a pair of superimposed periodic waves travelling in opposite directions makes a standing wave. In a standing wave, the amplitude of vibration has nulls at some positions where the wave amplitude appears smaller or even zero.

Characteristics

Waves are described by properties including wavelength (the distance between repeating points), amplitude (maximum displacement from equilibrium), period and frequency, and speed. The fundamental relation v = fλ connects wave speed, frequency, and wavelength. Waves exhibit reflection, refraction, diffraction, and interference.

Types of waves

Mechanical waves require a material medium: sound waves in air, water waves on a surface, and seismic waves in the Earth. In transverse waves the disturbance is perpendicular to the direction of travel (as in a vibrating string); in longitudinal waves it is parallel (as in sound). Electromagnetic waves — light, radio waves, X-rays — consist of oscillating electric and magnetic fields and need no medium. In quantum mechanics, matter also exhibits wave behaviour described by a wave function.

Mathematical description

The behaviour of many waves is governed by the wave equation, a second-order linear partial differential equation relating the second derivatives of the disturbance with respect to time and space. Its simplest solutions are sinusoids, and arbitrary waveforms can be built from sinusoids by superposition (Fourier analysis).
