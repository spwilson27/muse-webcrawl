Queen (chess)

The queen (♕, ♛) is the most powerful piece in the game of chess. It can move any number of squares vertically, horizontally, or diagonally, combining the moves of the rook and bishop. Each player starts the game with one queen, placed on the square of her own colour (the white queen on d1, the black queen on d8). In algebraic notation the queen is abbreviated Q.

History

In chess's ancestor shatranj the corresponding piece, the vizier (ferz), was weak, moving a single square diagonally. In late 15th-century Europe — first documented in the Valencian poem Scachs d'amor (c. 1475) — the queen gained her modern movement, a change nicknamed "mad queen chess" (scacchi alla rabiosa) that dramatically quickened the game. Historians have associated the powerful queen's rise with strong female rulers of the era, notably Isabella I of Castile, though the connection is debated.

Value and play

The queen is conventionally valued at about nine pawns — slightly more than a rook and bishop combined. Because of her power, players usually avoid exchanging queens early unless it gains an advantage, and promotion of a pawn is almost always to a queen ("queening"). Sacrificing the queen can be a spectacular winning device, as in famous "queen sacrifice" combinations.
