Parshall, North Dakota

Parshall
County: Mountrail
State: North Dakota

Parshall is a city in Mountrail County, North Dakota, United States. It lies within the Fort Berthold Indian Reservation and is closely associated with the Mandan, Hidatsa and Arikara Nation.

History

Parshall was founded in 1914 and named for George Parshall. The community developed as an agricultural and trading centre on the northern plains. The nearby construction of Garrison Dam and the creation of Lake Sakakawea reshaped the region's geography and economy in the mid-twentieth century.

Geography

The city sits on the Fort Berthold Indian Reservation near the shores of Lake Sakakawea, one of the largest reservoirs in the United States. The surrounding country is rolling prairie used for farming and ranching.

Demographics

The population has numbered under one thousand in recent censuses, with a substantial share of residents identifying as Native American.

Economy

Agriculture, tribal enterprises, and oil development in the Bakken Formation have all influenced the local economy.
