Balloon payment

A balloon payment is a large, lump-sum payment due at the end of a loan term, after a series of smaller periodic payments. Balloon loans are common in commercial real estate and some mortgages, where payments are calculated as if amortized over a long period but the loan matures much sooner.

Structure

For example, a loan may have payments based on a 30-year amortization schedule but a term of only five or seven years, at which point the remaining principal — the balloon — must be paid, refinanced, or the property sold.

Risks

Borrowers face refinancing risk: if credit conditions tighten or property values fall, they may be unable to refinance the balloon amount, leading to default. Consumer balloon mortgages drew regulatory scrutiny after the 2008 financial crisis.
