Nizamuddin Auliya

Nizamuddin Auliya (1238 – 3 April 1325), also known as Hazrat Nizamuddin, was an Indian Sufi saint of the Chishti Order and one of the most famous Sufis of the Indian subcontinent. He was a spiritual successor of Khwaja Moinuddin Chishti of Ajmer through the lineage of Qutbuddin Bakhtiar Kaki and Baba Farid.

Life

Born in Badayun, he settled in Delhi, where his khanqah in the village of Ghiyaspur — now the Nizamuddin locality — became a centre of devotion visited by rich and poor alike. He stressed love as the means of realising God, renunciation of the world, service to humanity, and tolerance toward other faiths.

Legacy

His disciples included the poet and musician Amir Khusrau and the saint Nasiruddin Chiragh Dehlavi. His tomb, the Nizamuddin Dargah in Delhi, remains one of India's most visited Sufi shrines, famous for qawwali performances on Thursday evenings, and the surrounding neighbourhood bears his name.
