Guadalcanal campaign

The Guadalcanal campaign, also known as the Battle of Guadalcanal and codenamed Operation Watchtower, was a military campaign fought between 7 August 1942 and 9 February 1943 on and around the island of Guadalcanal in the Pacific theatre of the Second World War. It was the first major land offensive by Allied forces against the Empire of Japan.

Background

Japanese forces had occupied Tulagi and begun building an airfield on Guadalcanal that threatened Allied supply routes between the United States and Australia. On 7 August 1942, US Marines landed on Guadalcanal and Tulagi, quickly capturing the unfinished airstrip, which they completed and named Henderson Field.

The fighting

For six months the campaign hung in the balance. Japanese forces repeatedly tried to retake Henderson Field in battles including the Tenaru, Bloody Ridge, and the actions along the Matanikau, while bitter naval battles raged in the surrounding waters — including Savo Island, the Eastern Solomons, Cape Esperance, the Naval Battle of Guadalcanal and Tassafaronga. The area off the island became known as "Ironbottom Sound" for the many ships sunk there. Japanese troops suffered heavily from combat, disease and starvation; in February 1943 Japan evacuated its surviving forces, conceding the island.

Significance

Guadalcanal marked the transition by the Allies from defensive operations to the strategic offensive in the Pacific, combined with the victory at Midway and the fighting in New Guinea.
