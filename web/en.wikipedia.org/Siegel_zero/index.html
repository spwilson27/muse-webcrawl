Siegel zero

In mathematics, a Siegel zero or Landau–Siegel zero, named after Edmund Landau and Carl Ludwig Siegel, is a hypothetical type of potential counterexample to the generalized Riemann hypothesis concerning the zeros of Dirichlet L-functions.

Definition

A Siegel zero would be a real zero of a Dirichlet L-function associated to a real primitive character, lying exceptionally close to s = 1 — closer than the classical zero-free regions allow for all other characters. No such zero is known to exist.

Consequences

Siegel's theorem implies that any such zero must satisfy a lower bound depending ineffectively on the modulus, an ineffectivity that propagates into results such as lower bounds for class numbers of imaginary quadratic fields. Paradoxically, if a Siegel zero existed, some conjectures — for example on the distribution of prime gaps and twin primes — would become easier to prove, so the hypothetical zero features in the hypotheses of several theorems.

Status

Extensive results rule out Siegel zeros for all sufficiently small moduli, and many modern arguments proceed by showing that either such a zero exists (with useful consequences) or a stronger theorem holds without it.
