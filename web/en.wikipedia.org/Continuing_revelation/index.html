Continuing revelation

Continuing revelation, or ongoing revelation, is a theological belief shared by several religious denominations that God continues to reveal divine principles or commandments to humanity, rather than having confined revelation to a closed canon of scripture.

Latter Day Saint movement

The doctrine is most prominent in the Latter Day Saint movement, whose ninth Article of Faith states: "We believe all that God has revealed, all that He does now reveal, and we believe that He will yet reveal many great and important things pertaining to the Kingdom of God." In the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, the president of the church is regarded as a prophet who may receive revelation for the whole church, including declarations that have ended practices such as plural marriage (1890) and the priesthood restriction on men of Black African descent (1978).

Other traditions

Related ideas appear in Quakerism (continuing revelation through the Inner Light), the Baháʼí Faith (progressive revelation through successive messengers), charismatic and Pentecostal Christianity (prophecy and words of knowledge), and Seventh-day Adventist belief in the prophetic ministry of Ellen G. White. Many other Christian traditions hold that public revelation closed with the apostolic age.
