Police state

Term originGerman Polizeistaat
CharacteristicsSecret police, surveillance, suppression of dissent
Related conceptsTotalitarianism, authoritarianism

A police state is a state in which the government exercises rigid and repressive controls over the social, economic, and political life of the population, typically through a secret police force that operates above or outside the law. Civil liberties and freedom of movement, speech, and association are heavily restricted.

Origin of the term

The term derives from the German Polizeistaat, which in the 18th and 19th centuries referred more neutrally to a well-ordered, regulated state. Its pejorative meaning developed in the 20th century, applied to regimes such as Nazi Germany, with its Gestapo, and the Soviet Union, with the NKVD and KGB.

Characteristics

Common features include pervasive surveillance of citizens, censorship, arbitrary arrest and detention, show trials, internal exile or restrictions on emigration (as with exit visas), and police powers exercised without independent judicial oversight. George Orwell's novel Nineteen Eighty-Four is the best-known literary depiction of a police state.
