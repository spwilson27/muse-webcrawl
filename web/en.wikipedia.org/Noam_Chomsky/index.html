Noam Chomsky

Avram Noam Chomsky
Born: December 7, 1928, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, U.S.
Known for: Generative grammar, Chomsky hierarchy, universal grammar, propaganda model

Avram Noam Chomsky (born December 7, 1928) is an American professor and public intellectual known for his work in linguistics, political activism, and social criticism. Sometimes called "the father of modern linguistics", Chomsky is also a major figure in analytic philosophy and one of the founders of the field of cognitive science. He is a laureate professor of linguistics at the University of Arizona and an institute professor emeritus at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT).

Linguistics

Chomsky's 1957 book Syntactic Structures and his review of B. F. Skinner's Verbal Behavior launched the cognitive revolution against behaviourism. He proposed that human language is underpinned by an innate universal grammar and described grammars with the Chomsky hierarchy. His framework evolved through transformational grammar, government and binding theory, and the minimalist program.

Political activism

A lifelong critic of United States foreign policy, capitalism and corporate power, Chomsky co-authored Manufacturing Consent (1988) with Edward S. Herman, setting out the propaganda model of mass media. He identifies as an anarcho-syndicalist and libertarian socialist, and has written extensively on the Vietnam War, the Israeli–Palestinian conflict, and other topics.
