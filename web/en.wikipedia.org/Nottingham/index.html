Nottingham

Nottingham is a cathedral city and unitary authority area in Nottinghamshire, East Midlands, England. It is known for its links to the legend of Robin Hood, for Nottingham Castle, and for its historic lace-making, bicycle and tobacco industries. The city has two large universities, the University of Nottingham and Nottingham Trent University, and one of the largest student populations in the country.

History

The settlement began as the Anglo-Saxon town of Snotingaham, "the homestead of Snot's people". Nottingham Castle was built by William the Conqueror in 1068 on a sandstone outcrop above the River Leen, near the Trent. In the Middle Ages the town became known for alabaster carving and, later, for framework knitting and lace. Beneath the city lies a network of over 800 man-made caves carved into the soft sandstone, used over centuries as dwellings, tanneries, cellars and air-raid shelters.

Charles I raised his standard at Nottingham in 1642, beginning the English Civil War. In the industrial era the city grew rapidly around textiles, Raleigh bicycles and Boots pharmaceuticals. In 1831 rioting over the Reform Bill saw the castle burned by a mob.

Culture and sport

Nottingham is associated with the outlaw Robin Hood and his adversary the Sheriff of Nottingham. Its literary figures include Lord Byron, D. H. Lawrence and Alan Sillitoe. The city hosts two of England's oldest professional football clubs, Notts County (founded 1862) and Nottingham Forest, European Cup winners in 1979 and 1980, as well as Trent Bridge cricket ground.
