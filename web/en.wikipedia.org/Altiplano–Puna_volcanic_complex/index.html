Altiplano–Puna volcanic complex

The Altiplano–Puna volcanic complex, also known as the APVC, is a complex of volcanic systems in the Central Andes of Bolivia, Argentina and Chile, between 21° and 24° S latitude. It forms part of the Central Volcanic Zone of the Andes and sits atop the Altiplano–Puna magma body, one of the largest known zones of partial melt in Earth's continental crust.

Altiplano–Puna volcanic complex
Location: Bolivia / Argentina / Chile
Area: ~50,000 km²
Activity: Miocene–present, ignimbrite flare-up ~10–1 Ma

Geology

Between about 10 and 1 million years ago the complex produced at least 15 major ignimbrite eruptions totalling over 13,000 km³ of material, including the Vilama, Guacha, La Pacana and Atana ignimbrites, several from calderas tens of kilometres wide — among the largest known eruptions on Earth. Seismic imaging reveals a vast low-velocity magma body at 15–20 km depth covering about 500,000 km³.

Present activity

Modern activity includes stratovolcanoes such as Lascar and Uturunku, which is inflating over a wide area, plus extensive geothermal fields such as Sol de Mañana and El Tatio. Satellite geodesy shows broad uplift and subsidence (the "sombrero" pattern) above the magma body.
