James Baird Weaver (June 12, 1833 – February 6, 1912) was an American politician in Iowa who was a member of the United States House of Representatives and two-time candidate for President of the United States. He is best remembered as the presidential nominee of the Greenback Party in 1880 and of the People's Party (Populists) in 1892.

James B. Weaver, Populist presidential candidate in 1892

Early life and military service

Weaver was born in Dayton, Ohio, and moved with his family to Iowa as a boy. He studied law and was admitted to the bar. When the American Civil War began, he enlisted in the Union Army, rose to the rank of colonel of the 2nd Iowa Infantry, and was brevetted brigadier general. He was wounded at Fort Donelson and fought at Shiloh and Corinth.

Congressional career

Originally a Republican, Weaver grew dissatisfied with the party's monetary policies and joined the Greenback Party, which advocated an expanded paper currency. He was elected to Congress in 1878 and again in 1884 and 1886, championing farmers' interests, railroad regulation, and currency expansion.

Presidential campaigns

In 1880 Weaver ran for president on the Greenback ticket, winning over 300,000 votes. In 1892 he was nominated by the newly formed People's Party on the Omaha Platform, calling for free silver, a graduated income tax, government ownership of railroads, and direct election of senators. He won more than a million popular votes and 22 electoral votes, carrying several western states — one of the strongest third-party showings in American history. In 1896 he supported the Democratic nominee William Jennings Bryan.

Later life

Weaver later served as mayor of Colfax, Iowa, and wrote a history of Davis County. He died in Des Moines in 1912.
