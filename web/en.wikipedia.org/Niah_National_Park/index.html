Niah National Park

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Niah National Park, located in Miri Division, Sarawak, Malaysia, is the site of the Niah Caves, a limestone cave complex of outstanding archaeological importance. The park covers about 31 square kilometres of rainforest surrounding the limestone massif of Gunung Subis. In 2024, the archaeological heritage of the Niah Caves complex was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

The caves

The Great Cave (Lubang Batu Niah) is one of the largest cave entrances in the world, and its enormous chamber is famous for the traditional harvest of edible swiftlet nests, collected by local Penan and other communities using tall poles and ladders. The Painted Cave contains prehistoric wall paintings and small boat-shaped burial coffins known as "death ships".

Archaeology

Excavations led by Tom Harrisson of the Sarawak Museum in the 1950s and 1960s uncovered the "Deep Skull", a human cranium dated to roughly 40,000 years old — for decades regarded as the oldest evidence of modern humans in Southeast Asia. Later research, including the Niah Cave Project of the early 2000s, refined the chronology and showed evidence of human activity in and around the caves extending back some 50,000 years, including early rainforest foraging. The cave deposits contain a long sequence of burials and occupation layers spanning the Palaeolithic to the Neolithic and into recent centuries.

Nature

Beyond the caves, the park protects lowland rainforest with wildlife including hornbills, long-tailed macaques, bats — which pour out of the cave at dusk — and swiftlets. Boardwalk trails cross the swamp forest to the cave entrances.
