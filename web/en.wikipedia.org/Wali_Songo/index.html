The Wali Songo (Indonesian: Wali Sanga), meaning "Nine Saints", are revered saints of Islam in Indonesia, in particular on the island of Java, because of their historic role in the spread of Islam in Indonesia. The word wali is Arabic for "custodian", "friend" or "holy man"; songo is Javanese for nine.

The nine saints

Although lists vary between sources, the nine are commonly identified as:

  • Sunan Gresik (Maulana Malik Ibrahim) — regarded as the earliest of the nine, based at Gresik, East Java (d. 1419).
  • Sunan Ampel (Raden Rahmat) — founder of the Ampel Denta pesantren in Surabaya.
  • Sunan Bonang (Raden Makhdum Ibrahim) — son of Sunan Ampel, associated with gamelan music and the bonang instrument.
  • Sunan Drajat (Raden Qasim) — younger brother of Sunan Bonang, known for social welfare teaching.
  • Sunan Kalijaga (Raden Said) — famed for using Javanese arts such as wayang puppetry and gamelan to convey Islamic teaching.
  • Sunan Kudus (Ja'far Shadiq) — founder of Kudus, noted for tolerance toward Hindu traditions.
  • Sunan Muria (Raden Umar Said) — son of Sunan Kalijaga, based on Mount Muria.
  • Sunan Gunung Jati (Syarif Hidayatullah) — founder of Cirebon and Banten in West Java.
  • Sunan Giri (Raden Paku) — founder of the influential Giri pesantren near Gresik.

Legacy

The Wali Songo are credited with adapting Islamic teaching to Javanese culture, founding pesantren (Islamic boarding schools) and mosques — including the Great Mosque of Demak, traditionally linked to the saints — and supporting the first Muslim kingdoms of Java such as Demak. Their tombs remain major pilgrimage (ziarah) sites, and their stories are preserved in Javanese chronicles such as the Babad Tanah Jawi. The details of their lives blend documented history with legend, and scholars treat many traditional accounts as hagiography.
