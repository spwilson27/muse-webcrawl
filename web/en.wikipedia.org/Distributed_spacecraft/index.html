Distributed spacecraft

A distributed spacecraft system is a mission architecture in which the functions traditionally performed by a single large spacecraft are divided among two or more separate spacecraft that cooperate in flight. The cooperating elements may fly in formation, operate as a constellation, or dock and separate during the mission, sharing resources such as data, power or propulsion through inter-spacecraft links.

Motivation

Dividing a mission among several smaller spacecraft can reduce risk — the loss of one element need not end the mission — and can enable measurements that a single vehicle cannot make, such as simultaneous observations from widely separated points, interferometry with long baselines, or assembly of very large apertures from multiple mirrors. Distributed systems can also be launched incrementally and upgraded over time by replacing individual elements.

Types

  • Constellations — many spacecraft distributed over an orbital plane or shell, providing continuous coverage, as in navigation and communications systems.
  • Formation flying — two or more spacecraft maintaining precise relative positions, used for interferometry, magnetospheric studies and rendezvous demonstrations.
  • Fractionated spacecraft — subsystems such as power, communications and payload hosted on separate free-flying modules that share services wirelessly.
  • Swarms — large numbers of very small, loosely coordinated satellites.

Examples

The Magnetospheric Multiscale Mission flies four identical spacecraft in a tetrahedral formation to study magnetic reconnection. The European Space Agency's Proba-3 mission demonstrates precise formation flying with two spacecraft creating artificial solar eclipses for coronagraphy. Gravity Recovery and Climate Experiment (GRACE) and its follow-on measured Earth's gravity field using the changing distance between two co-orbiting satellites. Proposed distributed telescopes have included interferometers made of free-flying collectors combined on the ground or in space.

Challenges

Distributed architectures require autonomous navigation, precise relative positioning, inter-satellite communication and coordinated control, often with limited ground contact. Relative dynamics in orbit, collision avoidance and the allocation of mission functions among elements are active research topics in astrodynamics and systems engineering.
