The Lord of the Rings (film series)

The Lord of the Rings
Directed by: Peter Jackson
Screenplay by: Fran Walsh, Philippa Boyens, Peter Jackson
Based on: The Lord of the Rings by J. R. R. Tolkien
Starring: Elijah Wood, Ian McKellen, Viggo Mortensen, Sean Astin, Andy Serkis
Box office: $2.9 billion

The Lord of the Rings is a trilogy of epic fantasy adventure films directed by Peter Jackson, based on the novel by J. R. R. Tolkien. The films are titled identically to the three volumes of the novel: The Fellowship of the Ring (2001), The Two Towers (2002), and The Return of the King (2003). Produced by New Line Cinema, the films were shot simultaneously in New Zealand over eight years and are widely regarded as one of the greatest and most influential film series ever made.

Plot

The trilogy follows the hobbit Frodo Baggins and the Fellowship as they quest to destroy the One Ring and defeat the Dark Lord Sauron. The ensemble cast includes Elijah Wood as Frodo, Ian McKellen as Gandalf, Viggo Mortensen as Aragorn, Sean Astin as Samwise Gamgee, and Andy Serkis as Gollum, a landmark motion-capture performance.

Production

Jackson, Fran Walsh, and Philippa Boyens adapted Tolkien's work with a focus on its emotional core, using extensive miniatures, prosthetic makeup, location shooting across New Zealand, and pioneering digital effects by Weta Digital, including the MASSIVE crowd-simulation software for battle scenes. Howard Shore composed the acclaimed scores, incorporating leitmotifs for the cultures of Middle-earth.

Reception

The films grossed nearly $3 billion worldwide and won 17 Academy Awards from 30 nominations. The Return of the King won all 11 of its nominations, including Best Picture, tying the record for the most Oscars won by a single film. The trilogy is credited with reviving the fantasy genre in cinema and transforming New Zealand's film and tourism industries. Extended editions, released on home media, are widely beloved.
