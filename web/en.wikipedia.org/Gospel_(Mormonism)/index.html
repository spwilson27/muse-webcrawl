Gospel (Mormonism)

In Mormonism — principally in the theology of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (LDS Church) — the gospel refers to the "good news" of Jesus Christ as understood within the framework of a restored, full gospel: the doctrines, principles, ordinances, and priesthood authority believed to have been lost in a Great Apostasy after the apostolic era and restored through the prophet Joseph Smith beginning in 1830.

[ Image placeholder: The angel Moroni statue atop a Latter-day Saint temple ]

Latter-day Saints often speak of the "restored gospel" and of the "fulness of the gospel" contained in the Book of Mormon. The fourth Article of Faith summarises its first principles and ordinances: faith in Jesus Christ, repentance, baptism by immersion for the remission of sins, and the laying on of hands for the gift of the Holy Ghost.

Content of the gospel

Within LDS teaching, the gospel is centred on the Atonement of Jesus Christ and on the plan of salvation: premortal existence, the Fall, mortal life as a time of probation, and the possibility of exaltation — eternal life with God and one's family — through obedience to gospel laws and ordinances. Essential ordinances include baptism, confirmation, the sacrament (communion), priesthood ordination for men, and temple ordinances such as the endowment and sealing of marriages and families, including vicarious ordinances for the dead.

Scriptures

The gospel is taught from the Latter-day Saint canon: the Bible, the Book of Mormon, the Doctrine and Covenants, and the Pearl of Great Price, supplemented by the teachings of living prophets and apostles, who are believed to lead the church by continuing revelation. The church sends tens of thousands of missionaries worldwide to preach the gospel.
