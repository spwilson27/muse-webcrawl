The Zulu people (Zulu: amaZulu) are a Nguni ethnic group native to Southern Africa. The Zulu are the largest ethnic group and nation in South Africa, numbering roughly 14 to 15 million people, most of them living in the province of KwaZulu-Natal. Their language, isiZulu, is the most widely spoken home language in South Africa.

History

The Zulu were originally a minor clan founded about 1709 by Zulu kaMalandela. In the early nineteenth century, under Shaka kaSenzangakhona, the Zulu built a powerful kingdom through military innovation and the incorporation of neighbouring peoples during the period known as the Mfecane. The kingdom later fought the Voortrekkers at the Battle of Blood River (1838) and the British in the Anglo-Zulu War of 1879, famous for the battles of Isandlwana and Rorke's Drift. After defeat, Zululand was annexed by Britain and eventually incorporated into the Union of South Africa.

Culture

Zulu culture is noted for its beadwork, basketry, beer ceremonies, and ceremonies such as the Umkhosi womHlanga (Reed Dance) and the first-fruits festival Umkhosi woMama. Traditional religion centres on the creator uNkulunkulu and ancestral spirits (amaDlozi). The Zulu monarchy continues today; Misuzulu Zulu became king in 2021, following the death of his father Goodwill Zwelithini, who had reigned for five decades.
