The Proms

The Proms, formally the BBC Proms and originally the Henry Wood Promenade Concerts, is an eight-week summer season of daily orchestral classical music concerts and other events held annually, predominantly in the Royal Albert Hall in central London.

Founded in 1895 by impresario Robert Newman with conductor Henry Wood, the Proms aimed to bring classical music to a wider audience at low prices, with informal "promenading" standing areas.

History

The first Proms season ran at the Queen's Hall in 1895. The BBC took over in 1927; after the Queen's Hall was destroyed by bombing in 1941, the festival moved to the Royal Albert Hall. Henry Wood conducted for nearly fifty years.

The Last Night

The Last Night of the Proms is famous for patriotic works including Elgar's "Land of Hope and Glory", Parry's "Jerusalem" and "Rule, Britannia!", with enthusiastic audience participation.

Promming and broadcasts

Standing tickets in the Arena and Gallery remain comparatively cheap, with "Prommers" a distinctive part of the audience. All concerts are broadcast by the BBC on radio and many on television, and Proms in the Park and chamber Proms extend the season beyond London.
