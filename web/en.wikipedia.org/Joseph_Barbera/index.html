Joseph Barbera

Joseph Barbera
Born: 24 March 1911, New York City
Died: 18 December 2006, Los Angeles
Occupation: Animator, producer, director

Joseph Roland Barbera (24 March 1911 – 18 December 2006) was an American animator and cartoon producer. Through his work with William Hanna, he co-founded Hanna-Barbera, which became one of the most successful television animation studios.

Career

Barbera joined MGM's cartoon studio in 1937, partnering with Hanna to create Tom and Jerry, which won seven Academy Awards. After MGM closed its animation unit in 1957, the pair founded Hanna-Barbera Productions.

Hanna-Barbera

The studio produced iconic series including Huckleberry Hound, The Flintstones, Yogi Bear, The Jetsons, Scooby-Doo and The Smurfs, pioneering limited animation techniques for television.

Legacy

Barbera received multiple Emmy Awards and a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame, and is regarded as one of the most influential figures in television animation.
