Valles Marineris

Valles Marineris stretching across the face of Mars.

Valles Marineris (Latin for "Mariner Valleys," named after the Mariner 9 spacecraft that discovered it) is a vast system of canyons, or rift valleys, that runs along the equator of Mars, just east of the Tharsis region. At more than 4,000 km long, up to 200 km wide, and as much as 7 km deep, it is one of the largest canyon systems in the Solar System, dwarfing Earth's Grand Canyon.

Formation

Most researchers believe the system began as tectonic cracks formed as the Tharsis region rose and stressed the Martian crust; the fractures were later widened and deepened by erosion, landslides, and possibly flowing water. The system is divided into regions including Noctis Labyrinthus in the west, the long central chasmata of Melas, Candor and Ophir, and Coprates Chasma, ending in chaotic terrain and outflow channels to the east.

Evidence of water

Layered interior deposits, hydrated minerals detected from orbit, and features resembling landslide and flood deposits suggest that liquid water once ponded in or flowed through parts of the canyon system, making Valles Marineris a key site in the search for Mars's watery past.
