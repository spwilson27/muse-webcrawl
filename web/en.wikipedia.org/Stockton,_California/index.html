Stockton is a city in and the county seat of San Joaquin County in the Central Valley of California. Founded during the California Gold Rush and named after Robert F. Stockton, it grew as an inland port on the San Joaquin River and remains one of California's largest inland ports, connected to San Francisco Bay by a deepwater channel.

History

Stockton was founded by Carlos Maria Weber in 1849 and incorporated in 1850, making it one of California's earliest cities. During the Gold Rush it served as a supply hub for miners heading to the southern mines. Agriculture, manufacturing and shipping drove its economy through the twentieth century. The city filed for bankruptcy in 2012, then the largest American city to do so, and emerged in 2015.

Geography and economy

Situated in the northern San Joaquin Valley, Stockton is surrounded by rich agricultural land and a network of waterways forming part of the Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta. The Port of Stockton handles bulk cargo, and the city hosts the University of the Pacific, founded in 1851.
