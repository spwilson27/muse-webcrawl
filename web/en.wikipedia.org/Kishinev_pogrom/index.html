The Kishinev pogrom was an anti-Jewish riot that took place in Kishinev (now Chișinău, Moldova) in the Russian Empire on 19–21 April 1903. Forty-nine Jews were killed, hundreds were injured, and some 1,500 homes and shops were damaged or destroyed, while police and troops failed to intervene for two days.

Background

Jews made up nearly half of Kishinev's population of about 110,000. Antisemitic agitation, promoted by the newspaper Bessarabets under Pavolachi Krushevan, blamed Jews for economic hardship. A blood libel — the false claim that Jews had murdered a Christian boy in nearby Dubăsarii Vechi for ritual purposes — and the suicide of a young woman in a Jewish hospital inflamed tensions before Easter.

The pogrom

Violence erupted after Easter services on 19 April 1903 (6 April Old Style). Mobs looted and burned Jewish property and attacked residents. Authorities, including the governor, stood aside; order was restored only on the third day. A second, smaller pogrom followed in October 1905.

Response and legacy

The pogrom drew worldwide condemnation and fundraising for victims in Europe and America. It spurred Jewish emigration to the United States and Palestine, strengthened Zionist and Jewish self-defence movements, and was commemorated in Hayim Nahman Bialik's poem "In the City of Slaughter". Trials punished few perpetrators and none of the instigators.
