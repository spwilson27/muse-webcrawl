Oka (river)

The Oka (Russian: Ока́) is a river in European Russia, the largest right tributary of the Volga. It flows through the regions of Oryol, Tula, Kaluga, Moscow, Ryazan, Vladimir and Nizhny Novgorod oblasts, and is more than 1,500 kilometres (930 mi) long. The city of Nizhny Novgorod stands at its confluence with the Volga.

Course

The Oka rises in the Central Russian Upland near the village of Aleksandrovka in Oryol Oblast. It flows north through Oryol and Kaluga, then eastward past Serpukhov, Kolomna (where it receives the Moskva River), Ryazan, and Kasimov, before turning north-east to join the Volga at Nizhny Novgorod. Its principal tributaries include the Moskva, Klyazma, Moksha, Upa, and Pronya rivers.

History

The Oka basin was a heartland of early Russian history. Trade routes along the river connected the forest zone with the Volga trade routes, and towns such as Ryazan and Murom grew up along its banks. In the era of the Mongol invasions and the rise of Moscow, the Oka formed a defensive frontier — the "Oka line" of fortified towns guarding the southern approaches to Moscow against steppe raids.

Navigation and ecology

The river is navigable for much of its lower course, with locks near Kolomna and elsewhere. It supports commercial fisheries and supplies water to several large cities, though industrial pollution has been a recurring concern. Floodplains along the middle Oka are noted habitats for waterbirds.
