Intercity Express

The Intercity Express (abbreviated ICE; German: Intercity-Express) is a system of high-speed trains predominantly running in Germany. It is the highest service and fare category of rail service of Deutsche Bahn, with trains reaching speeds of up to 300 km/h (186 mph) on dedicated high-speed lines.

History

ICE service began in 1991 with the ICE 1 on the Hanover–Würzburg and Mannheim–Stuttgart high-speed lines. Successive generations — ICE 2, the tilting ICE T, and the distributed-traction ICE 3 and ICE 4 — extended services across Germany and into the Netherlands, Belgium, France, Switzerland, Austria, and Denmark. The network is centered on clock-face timetables linking major cities roughly every hour.

Rolling stock and network

ICE trains are recognizable by their white livery with a red stripe. Long-distance ICE services are complemented by the lower-category Intercity and EuroCity trains. The system has carried more than a billion passengers and is a central element of German transport policy, though it has faced criticism over punctuality and capacity constraints.
