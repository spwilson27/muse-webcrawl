Arches National Park is a national park in eastern Utah, United States, adjacent to the Colorado River near Moab. The park preserves more than 2,000 natural sandstone arches — the highest density anywhere in the world — including the world-famous Delicate Arch, a symbol of Utah that appears on state license plates.

Geology

The arches formed from Entrada Sandstone eroded over millions of years. Underground salt beds, deposited when a sea covered the region some 300 million years ago, dissolved and collapsed, fracturing the sandstone into fins that wind and water then sculpted into arches, spires, and balanced rocks.

Features

Notable formations include Landscape Arch, one of the longest natural arches in the world, Double Arch, Balanced Rock, and the Windows Section. The park was proclaimed a national monument in 1929 and redesignated a national park in 1971.
