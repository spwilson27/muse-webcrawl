Thrust stage

In theatre, a thrust stage (also known as a platform stage or open stage) is one that extends into the audience on three sides and is connected to the backstage area by its fourth side. It is also sometimes called an apron stage, although "apron" more specifically refers to the portion of a proscenium stage extending in front of the curtain.

Though some may also consider an arena stage (theatre in the round) a variant of thrust staging, a thrust stage has supporters (audience) surrounding it on three sides, whereas an arena stage encloses the stage on all sides. The back wall of a thrust stage functions much like the backdrop of a proscenium stage.

History

The thrust stage is one of the oldest stage forms. The ancient Greek theatres had a roughly circular orchestra surrounded by audience seating on more than half its circumference. Elizabethan playhouses such as the Globe Theatre in London used a platform stage jutting into the yard, surrounded on three sides by standing spectators ("groundlings") with galleries above.

In the 20th century, the thrust stage was revived as an alternative to the dominant proscenium arch. Notable modern thrust theatres include the Festival Theatre at Stratford, Ontario (opened 1957), the Guthrie Theater in Minneapolis, and the Olivier Theatre at the National Theatre in London, modelled on the ancient Greek theatre at Epidaurus.

Advantages and disadvantages

A thrust stage offers greater intimacy between performers and audience than a proscenium stage, since audience members are closer to the action and surround it. The format also allows a large seating capacity in a compact space. A "vomitorium" (vom) entrance under the seating allows actors to enter through the audience.

Disadvantages include the inability to hide stage machinery or large scenery within the audience's view, restrictions on scenic backdrops (which would block some spectators' sightlines), and the challenge for actors of playing to audience members on three sides—when an actor faces one section, their back is to another.
