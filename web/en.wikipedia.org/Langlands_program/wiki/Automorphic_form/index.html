Automorphic form

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In harmonic analysis and number theory, an automorphic form is a well-behaved function from a topological group G to the complex numbers (or complex vector space) which is invariant under the action of a discrete subgroup of G. Automorphic forms are a generalization of the idea of periodic functions in Euclidean space to general topological groups, and they include modular forms as a special case.

Definition and properties

In the classical setting, a modular form is a holomorphic function on the upper half-plane invariant (up to a factor of automorphy) under the modular group. General automorphic forms are functions on a locally symmetric space satisfying invariance under an arithmetic subgroup, a growth condition, and eigendifferential equations given by the centre of the universal enveloping algebra.

Role in the Langlands program

Automorphic forms and their associated L-functions are central objects of the Langlands program, which predicts deep connections between automorphic representations of reductive groups and Galois representations. Poincaré first discovered automorphic functions in the late nineteenth century; the modern theory was developed by, among others, Harish-Chandra, Gelfand, and Langlands.
