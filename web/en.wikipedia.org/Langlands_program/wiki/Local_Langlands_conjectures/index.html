Local Langlands conjectures

In mathematics, the local Langlands conjectures are part of the Langlands program, an extensive network of influential conjectures connecting number theory and representation theory. They were proposed by Robert Langlands in 1970, in the form of a correspondence between complex representations of a reductive algebraic group over a local field and representations of the local Weil group into the L-group of the group.

The local Langlands correspondence describes a deep relationship between two apparently unrelated kinds of objects: on one side, the n-dimensional complex representations of the local Weil group (or Weil–Deligne group), and on the other side, the smooth irreducible representations of a reductive group G over a local field F, such as the real numbers or the p-adic numbers.

Statement

The conjectures predict that the irreducible admissible representations of G(F) are partitioned into finite sets called L-packets, which are indexed by certain parameters: continuous homomorphisms from the Weil–Deligne group of F into the complex L-group of G. Each member of an L-packet should share the same L-function and epsilon factor, and the internal structure of a packet is governed by the component group of the centralizer of the parameter.

History and known cases

Langlands formulated the local conjectures in a letter and subsequent work around 1970, building on the earlier local class field theory, which gives a correspondence for one-dimensional representations of the multiplicative group — the case of the general linear group GL(1). The case of GL(2) over the p-adic fields was established by work of Jacquet and Langlands and by Kutzko, who developed the theory of types. The full correspondence for GL(n) over p-adic fields was proved independently by Michael Harris and Richard Taylor, and by Guy Henniart, around 2000, using very different methods: Harris and Taylor used the geometry of Shimura varieties, while Henniart gave a more elementary numerical proof based on the uniqueness of the correspondence characterized by L- and epsilon-factors of pairs.

For real groups, the local Langlands correspondence was established through the work of Langlands himself and Shelstad, and is closely related to the classification of representations of real reductive groups completed in the Langlands classification. For classical groups over p-adic fields, major progress was made by James Arthur (for orthogonal and symplectic groups) and by Colette Mœglin. The case of function fields was treated within the global theory by Laurent Lafforgue and others.

Relation to the global conjectures

The local correspondence is expected to be compatible with the global Langlands correspondence: an automorphic representation of a global group decomposes into a restricted tensor product of local representations, and the local parameters at each place should come from restricting a global Galois parameter. This compatibility is a key constraint that pins down the form of the local correspondence in many cases.
