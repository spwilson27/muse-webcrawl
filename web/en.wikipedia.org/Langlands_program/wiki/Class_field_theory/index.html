Class field theory

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Class field theory is the field of algebraic number theory concerned with describing the abelian extensions of a number field (or local field) in terms of the arithmetic of the field itself. Its central result, the existence theorem and the Artin reciprocity law, establishes a correspondence between abelian extensions and certain subgroups of the idele class group.

Main statements

For a number field K, the Artin map gives a canonical isomorphism between the Galois group of the maximal abelian extension of K and the profinite completion of the idele class group of K. Finite abelian extensions correspond bijectively to open subgroups of finite index — the norm groups. A landmark special case is the description of the Hilbert class field, the maximal unramified abelian extension, whose Galois group is the ideal class group.

History

The theory grew out of results of Gauss on quadratic reciprocity and work of Weber, Hilbert, Takagi, Artin, and Hasse in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. Class field theory is regarded as the abelian case of the Langlands program, which seeks non-abelian generalizations.
