Langlands functoriality

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Functoriality is a central conjecture of the Langlands program, proposed by Robert Langlands. It predicts that any homomorphism between the L-groups of two reductive groups over a number field should induce a transfer of automorphic representations from one group to the other, compatible with their L-functions and local parameters.

Statement and examples

Given reductive groups G and H and an L-homomorphism from the L-group of G to that of H, functoriality asserts that automorphic representations of G can be "lifted" to automorphic representations of H. Important special cases include base change, symmetric power lifts for GL(2), and the transfer between classical groups and general linear groups.

Progress

Many cases have been proved using the trace formula and the theory of endoscopy; a far-reaching approach, "beyond endoscopy," was proposed by Langlands to attack the general conjecture. Functoriality, combined with reciprocity, would imply major results such as the Sato–Tate conjecture, which was proved using partial cases of these ideas.
