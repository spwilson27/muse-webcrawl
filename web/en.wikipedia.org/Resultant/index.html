In mathematics, the resultant of two polynomials is a polynomial expression of their coefficients that is equal to zero if and only if the polynomials have a common root (possibly in a field extension), or, equivalently, a common factor (over their coefficient field). It is also equal, up to a factor, to a product of differences of the roots of the two polynomials.

The resultant is widely used in number theory, either directly or through the discriminant, which is essentially the resultant of a polynomial and its derivative. The resultant of two polynomials with rational or integer coefficients can be computed efficiently and is a basic tool of elimination theory and computer algebra: solving a system of polynomial equations can be reduced to computing resultants that eliminate variables one at a time.

The resultant is the determinant of the Sylvester matrix formed from the coefficients of the two polynomials.

Definition

Let f and g be polynomials of degrees m and n with leading coefficients a and b, and roots α1, …, αm and β1, …, βn (in an algebraic closure). Then

Res(f, g) = an bm ∏i,j (αi − βj) = an ∏i g(αi) = (−1)mn bm ∏j f(βj).

This product is the determinant of the Sylvester matrix, an (m + n) × (m + n) matrix built from the coefficients of f and g, which gives a formula involving only the coefficients.

Properties

  • Res(f, g) = 0 if and only if f and g have a common root in an algebraically closed field, or a common factor of positive degree.
  • Res(f, g) = (−1)mn Res(g, f).
  • The resultant belongs to the elimination ideal generated by f and g: there exist polynomials u, v with Res(f, g) = uf + vg.
  • It is multiplicative in each argument: Res(f, g1g2) = Res(f, g1)Res(f, g2).

Applications

Resultants are used to eliminate variables from systems of polynomial equations, to compute discriminants, to find intersections of algebraic curves, and in control theory and robotics for solving inverse kinematics. The subresultant algorithm computes resultants efficiently while controlling coefficient growth.
