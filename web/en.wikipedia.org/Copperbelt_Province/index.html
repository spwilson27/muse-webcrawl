Copperbelt Province

Copperbelt Province is one of the ten provinces of Zambia, named for the mineral-rich Copperbelt, the copper- and cobalt-mining region it shares with the adjoining Haut-Katanga province of the Democratic Republic of the Congo. It is the most urbanised province of Zambia, and its capital is Ndola. Major towns include Kitwe, Chingola, Mufulira, Luanshya and Chililabombwe.

History

Commercial mining began in the late 1920s under British colonial rule in Northern Rhodesia, when the rich orebodies of the region were developed by the Rhodesia Selection Trust and Anglo American. Mining drew large-scale labour migration and created company towns and one of Africa's most organised workforces; Copperbelt miners and trade unions played a central role in the independence movement and in Zambian politics after independence in 1964.

Economy

The province's economy rests on copper and cobalt mining and smelting. After nationalisation under Zambia Consolidated Copper Mines and a long decline through the 1980s and 1990s, the mines were privatised in the late 1990s and 2000s, and production revived with rising copper prices and new investment, notably at Kansanshi (in neighbouring North-Western Province) and within the Copperbelt's own mines and smelters. Mining's dominance leaves the province vulnerable to swings in the world copper price, and agriculture, manufacturing and cross-border trade with the Congo supplement incomes.

Geography and demography

The province covers about 31,000 km² of plateau at roughly 1,200 m elevation, drained by the Kafue River. Its population exceeds two million, predominantly Bemba-speaking, with high urbanisation concentrated in the chain of mining towns strung along the rail and road corridor to Lusaka.
