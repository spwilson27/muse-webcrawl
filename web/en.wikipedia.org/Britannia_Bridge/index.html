Britannia Bridge (Welsh: Pont Britannia) is a bridge across the Menai Strait between the island of Anglesey and the mainland of Wales. It was originally designed and built by the noted railway engineer Robert Stephenson as a tubular bridge of wrought-iron rectangular box-section spans, and opened in 1850 to carry the Chester and Holyhead Railway. Following a catastrophic fire in 1970 it was rebuilt as a two-tier steel truss arch bridge; it now carries both rail and the A55 dual carriageway.

Original bridge

Stephenson's design carried trains through two parallel rectangular wrought-iron tubes, each 460 m long, supported by masonry towers. The tubes were famously tested at model scale with the help of William Fairbairn and Eaton Hodgkinson. Pairs of monumental stone lions, carved by John Thomas, guard the entrances. The bridge's completion gave the railway its continuous link from London to the ferry port of Holyhead for Ireland.

Fire and rebuilding

On 23 May 1970 boys playing inside the bridge dropped a burning torch, igniting the tubes; the fire distorted the wrought iron beyond repair. The bridge was redesigned by Husband & Co. with steel arches supporting both a new rail deck and, above it, a road deck built so that Anglesey gained its first fixed road crossing in this location. Rail traffic resumed in 1972 and the road deck opened in 1980. The original masonry towers and the stone lions were retained.

Today

The bridge is a Grade II listed structure and forms a vital transport link, carrying the North Wales Coast Line and the A55 North Wales Expressway, part of Euroroute E22.
