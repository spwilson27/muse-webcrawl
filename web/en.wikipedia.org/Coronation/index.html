Coronation
A symbolic ceremony marking the formal investiture of a monarch with royal power, typically involving the placing of a crown upon the head.

A coronation is a ceremony marking the formal investiture of a monarch or their consort with regal power. In general, the term also includes any ceremony in which a person is crowned, but in common use it refers to the accession rituals of kings, queens and emperors. Coronations remain central to the monarchies of the United Kingdom, Tonga, Thailand, Bhutan and several other states.

History

Coronations originated in ancient Egypt, Persia and Rome and were Christianised in medieval Europe, where anointing with holy oil gave the rite a sacramental character. The British coronation at Westminster Abbey retains medieval elements, including anointing, investiture with regalia, enthronement and homage.

Modern coronations

The coronation of Charles III and Camilla took place on 6 May 2023 at Westminster Abbey, the first British coronation in 70 years. Many European monarchies have replaced coronations with simpler inauguration or blessing ceremonies.
