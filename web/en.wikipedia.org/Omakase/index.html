Omakase

Japaneseお任せ
Literal meaning"I leave it up to you"
SettingJapanese restaurants, especially sushi counters

Omakase (Japanese: お任せ, from makaseru, "to entrust") is a style of Japanese dining in which the guest leaves the selection of dishes entirely to the chef. It is most associated with sushi restaurants, where diners seated at the counter receive a progression of courses chosen according to the season, the day's best ingredients, and the chef's reading of the guest's reactions.

Experience

An omakase meal typically unfolds course by course, often beginning with lighter fare and appetizers, moving through sashimi and a sequence of nigiri sushi served one piece at a time — traditionally eaten promptly and sometimes with the hands — and concluding with items such as tamago, soup, and dessert. The format presumes trust: guests generally refrain from special requests, strong perfume, and other behaviour that would disturb the chef's choreography.

Spread

Popularized in Japan from the 1990s and then exported worldwide, omakase has come to denote high-end tasting-counter experiences generally, with prices ranging from moderate to several hundred dollars at celebrated restaurants. Variants include okonomi (ordering by preference) and tokusen selections; the term is also used beyond restaurants for curated, chef's- or expert's-choice services.
