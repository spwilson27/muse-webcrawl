MariaDB is a free and open-source relational database management system, created as a community-developed fork of MySQL by its original developers. It was founded by Michael "Monty" Widenius after Oracle Corporation acquired MySQL through its purchase of Sun Microsystems, amid concerns about MySQL's future licensing and development. The name combines "Maria," one of Widenius's daughters, with "DB."

Features

MariaDB aims to remain a drop-in replacement for corresponding MySQL versions, with matching commands, APIs and file formats, while adding its own storage engines (such as Aria, its crash-safe replacement for MyISAM), improved replication, virtual columns, dynamic columns, and performance work in the optimiser. The Galera-based MariaDB Cluster provides synchronous multi-master replication. Development is coordinated by the non-profit MariaDB Foundation, which holds the trademark; commercial support and enterprise products are offered by MariaDB plc.

Adoption

MariaDB is the default database in many Linux distributions and is used by large services and content management systems. It diverged more substantially from MySQL from version 10 onward, so compatibility is maintained at the level of most common usage rather than complete feature parity.
