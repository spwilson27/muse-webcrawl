The Battle of Culloden was the final confrontation of the Jacobite rising of 1745, fought on 16 April 1746 on Drummossie Moor near Inverness in the Scottish Highlands. The Jacobite army of Charles Edward Stuart ("Bonnie Prince Charlie") was decisively defeated by government forces under the Duke of Cumberland.

Background

The rising of 1745 aimed to restore the exiled Stuart line to the thrones of Great Britain. After early victories at Prestonpans and Falkirk, the Jacobites invaded England as far as Derby before retreating into Scotland, short of supplies, money, and the expected French support. Cumberland's army pursued them north through the winter.

The battle

An exhausted Jacobite attempt at a night attack on Cumberland's camp failed, and the next morning the Jacobites, hungry and outnumbered roughly 7,000 to 9,000, faced the government army on open, marshy moorland ill-suited to the Highland charge. Government artillery and musketry tore gaps in the Jacobite lines; the clan regiments that reached the government ranks fought briefly and bloodily before the line broke. The fighting lasted about an hour. Around 1,500–2,000 Jacobites were killed or wounded, against roughly 300 government casualties.

Aftermath

Charles fled, eventually escaping to France. The government conducted harsh reprisals in the Highlands, and Parliament passed measures — the Disarming Act and the abolition of heritable jurisdictions — aimed at destroying the clan system. Culloden was the last pitched battle fought on British soil, and it ended Jacobitism as a serious military threat.
