Guangxi

Guangxi, officially the Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region, is an autonomous region of the People's Republic of China in South China, bordering Vietnam and the Gulf of Tonkin. Its capital and largest city is Nanning. Guangxi is home to the Zhuang people, China's largest ethnic minority, and is noted for the dramatic karst landscapes of Guilin and Yangshuo along the Li River.

Geography

The region is largely hilly and mountainous, with limestone karst peaks in the north around Guilin, river basins of the Xi River system in the centre, and a short coastline on the Gulf of Tonkin in the south at Beihai, Qinzhou and Fangchenggang. Its position bordering Vietnam and facing Southeast Asia has made it a gateway for trade between China and ASEAN.

History

The area was incorporated into imperial China under the Qin dynasty and was long administered with neighbouring Guangdong (the two names share the element Guang, "expanse"). Guangxi was a centre of the Taiping Rebellion's origins in the mid-19th century, and during the Republican era it was dominated by the New Guangxi clique. It became an autonomous region for the Zhuang in 1958.

Economy

Agriculture, including sugarcane, rice and subtropical fruit, remains important, alongside aluminium and nonferrous metals, machinery, and a growing role in China–ASEAN trade through its ports and the annual China-ASEAN Expo in Nanning.
