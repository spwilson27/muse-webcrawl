Game Developers Choice Awards

The Game Developers Choice Awards are awards presented annually at the Game Developers Conference (GDC) in San Francisco, honoring outstanding video games and developers of the previous year. Established in 2001, they are notable as peer-based awards: nominations and voting are conducted by game developers themselves through the International Choice Awards Network, and the ceremony is held alongside the Independent Games Festival awards.

Categories

The centerpiece is Game of the Year, accompanied by awards for Best Debut, Best Design, Innovation, Best Narrative, Best Audio, Best Technology, Best Visual Art, Best Mobile Game, Best VR/AR Game, and the Audience Award. Special honors include the Lifetime Achievement Award — past recipients include Shigeru Miyamoto, John Carmack, Hideo Kojima, and Yuji Horii — and the Ambassador Award, recognizing figures who have advanced the medium or the development community.

Notable winners

Game of the Year winners have included The Sims, Grand Theft Auto III, Half-Life 2, Shadow of the Colossus, Portal, Red Dead Redemption, Journey, The Last of Us, The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt, The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild, God of War (2018), Hades, Elden Ring, and Baldur's Gate 3. Because the voters are working developers, the awards are regarded as an insider's counterpart to more commercial honors.
