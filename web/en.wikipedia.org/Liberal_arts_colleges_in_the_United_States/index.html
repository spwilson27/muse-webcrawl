Liberal arts colleges in the United States

Liberal arts colleges in the United States are undergraduate institutions emphasising a broad liberal arts education — in the humanities, social sciences, natural sciences and arts — rather than vocational or specialised professional training. There are roughly 200 such colleges in the United States.

Characteristics

These colleges are typically small (often fewer than 2,500 students), residential, and focused on undergraduate teaching with small classes and close faculty contact. Many are private and selective; well-known examples include Williams, Amherst, Swarthmore, Wellesley, Oberlin, Carleton, Bowdoin and Pomona Colleges.

History and challenges

The model descends from the colonial colleges founded before the American Revolution. In recent decades many liberal arts colleges have faced financial pressure from rising costs, demographic decline and competition from large universities, leading some to close or broaden their curricula while others defend the residential liberal arts model.
