Tactics

Tactics — the art and science of arranging and manoeuvring forces in combat to achieve objectives; the specific methods used to accomplish a short-term goal, subordinate to strategy.

Tactics in the military sphere comprise the science and art of organizing a military force, and techniques for using weapons and military units in combination for engaging and defeating an enemy in battle. Changes in philosophy and technology have been reflected in changes to military tactics. In contemporary military science, tactics is the lowest of three levels of warfighting, the higher levels being the strategic and operational levels. Throughout history, there has been a shifting balance between the four kinds of tactics: offensive, defensive, and the evolving influence of technology and morale.

Tactics versus strategy

A common saying holds that "strategy without tactics is the slowest route to victory, while tactics without strategy is the noise before defeat." Strategy determines the overall objectives and the allocation of resources; tactics concern the concrete actions taken in an engagement. The distinction is one of scope and time horizon rather than a sharp boundary, and the modern "operational level" sits between them.

Applications beyond the military

The term is used more broadly for any short-term plan of action used to achieve a goal — in business (negotiation tactics), sport (team tactics), politics, and emergency services such as firefighting and policing, where tactical units handle hostage, barricade, and high-risk arrest situations.

Historical development

From the phalanx of hoplite warfare, through the combined-arms legions of Rome and the mounted shock tactics of the medieval knight, to gunpowder infantry squares, Napoleonic columns, and modern combined-arms and network-centric warfare, tactical doctrine has continually adapted to weapons and communications technology.
