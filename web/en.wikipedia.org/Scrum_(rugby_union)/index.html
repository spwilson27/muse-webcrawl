Scrum (rugby union)

A scrum (short for scrummage) is a method of restarting play in rugby union that involves players packing closely together with their heads down and attempting to gain possession of the ball. A scrum is formed in the field of play when each team assembles its forwards in a tight formation; the ball is then thrown into the middle of the tunnel between the two front rows by the scrum-half of the team awarded the scrum.

Formation

Each scrum consists of eight forwards: a front row of two props and a hooker, a second row of two locks, and a back row of two flankers and a number eight. The players bind together and engage with the opposition under the referee's calls of "crouch, bind, set". Once the scrum is set, the scrum-half feeds the ball into the tunnel, and the hookers compete to strike it back toward their own side while both packs drive against each other.

Purpose and awards

Scrums are awarded for minor infringements such as a knock-on or a forward pass. A dominant scrum can win possession against the feed, earn penalties, and provide a platform for attacking plays by the backs once the ball emerges.

Safety

Because of the large forces involved, the scrum is one of the most dangerous phases of the game, and collapsed or improperly formed scrums can cause serious neck and spinal injuries. Law changes have progressively depowered the engagement, and in amateur and age-grade rugby scrums may be uncontested if suitably trained front-row players are unavailable.
