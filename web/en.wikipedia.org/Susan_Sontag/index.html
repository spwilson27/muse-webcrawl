Susan Sontag (born Susan Rosenblatt; January 16, 1933 – December 28, 2004) was an American writer, filmmaker, philosopher, and political activist. She is best known for her essays on modern culture, photography, illness, and the interpretation of art, and she became one of the most influential American intellectuals of the second half of the twentieth century.

Early life and education

Sontag was born in New York City to Jewish parents and grew up partly in Tucson, Arizona, after her father died of tuberculosis in China, where he had run a fur-trading business. A precocious student, she graduated from high school at fifteen and attended the University of California, Berkeley before transferring to the University of Chicago, where she earned a degree in philosophy, ancient history, and literature in 1951. She went on to graduate study at Harvard University, earning master's degrees in English and philosophy, and later studied at the University of Oxford and the Sorbonne in Paris.

Career

Sontag taught philosophy and religion at several American universities before abandoning academia in 1966. Her essay collection Against Interpretation (1966) established her reputation; its title essay argued against the over-intellectualisation of art and in favour of an "erotics of art." On Photography (1977) examined how photographs shape the way people see and remember, and Illness as Metaphor (1978), written after her own treatment for breast cancer, attacked the punitive metaphors surrounding disease. Her later essay Regarding the Pain of Others (2003) returned to the ethics of images of suffering. Sontag also directed four films and wrote novels including The Benefactor (1963), Death Kit (1967), The Volcano Lover (1992), and In America (1999), which won the National Book Award.

Activism and later life

Sontag was an outspoken critic of the Vietnam War and later of the Iraq War. During the Siege of Sarajevo she staged a production of Samuel Beckett's Waiting for Godot in the besieged city in 1993, an act for which she was made an honorary citizen of Sarajevo. She died of complications of myelodysplastic syndrome in New York in 2004 and is buried in Montparnasse Cemetery in Paris.
