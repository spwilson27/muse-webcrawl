CC0

CC0 is a public dedication tool created by Creative Commons and released in 2009, which enables creators to waive all their copyright and related rights in a work, effectively placing it in the public domain worldwide.

Structure

CC0 works in three layers: a waiver of rights to the fullest extent permitted by law; a fallback public licence if the waiver is ineffective in some jurisdiction; and a non-assertion covenant. Unlike the Creative Commons licences proper, CC0 does not require attribution and does not cover patents or trademarks.

Use

CC0 is widely used for data, scientific research outputs, photographs and cultural-heritage material, for example by museums releasing images of public-domain works. Software projects generally use dedicated licences such as the Unlicense or MIT-0 instead, and Creative Commons does not recommend CC0 for software.
