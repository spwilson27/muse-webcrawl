Yacyretá Dam

The Yacyretá Dam is a hydroelectric dam on the Paraná River between Argentina (Corrientes Province) and Paraguay (Misiones Department), near the Paraguayan city of Ayolas and the Argentine town of Ituzaingó. Its name comes from Guaraní jasy reta, "land of the moon." The powerhouse has an installed capacity of about 3,200 megawatts, making it one of South America's largest hydroelectric plants; a second powerhouse at Aña Cuá has been under development to expand capacity.

History and construction

Argentina and Paraguay signed the treaty creating the binational entity Entidad Binacional Yacyretá in 1973, but construction did not begin until 1983 and was repeatedly delayed by financing problems and political disputes. The first turbines entered service in 1994, and the plant was formally inaugurated in 1998. For years the reservoir operated below its design level (76 m instead of 83 m) to avoid flooding additional land, limiting output, until the water level was finally raised to full height in 2011.

Environmental and social impact

At full pool the reservoir covers roughly 1,600 km², flooding wetlands of the Iberá region's margins and displacing tens of thousands of people, particularly in the Paraguayan city of Encarnación and the Argentine city of Posadas. The project has been persistently criticised — including in a famous remark by Argentine president Carlos Menem calling it a "monument to corruption" — for cost overruns, its impact on fish migration in the Paraná, and the slow completion of resettlement and mitigation works.

Operation

Yacyretá supplies a substantial share of Argentina's electricity consumption and a majority of Paraguay's, with Paraguay selling most of its share of the energy to Argentina. The dam includes navigation locks, fish elevators and two spillways, and the combined crest length of its structures is among the longest of any dam in the world.
