Jury stability criterion

The Jury stability criterion is a method in control systems theory, developed by Eliahu I. Jury, for determining the stability of a discrete-time linear system by analysing the characteristic polynomial of the system in a tabular array, analogous to the Routh–Hurwitz criterion for continuous-time systems.

Background

A discrete-time system is stable when all roots of its characteristic polynomial lie strictly inside the unit circle in the complex z-plane. The Jury test arranges the polynomial coefficients in a table (the Jury array) and imposes a set of inequality conditions. Satisfaction of all conditions is necessary and sufficient for stability, avoiding the need to compute the roots directly.

The test

For a polynomial F(z) = anzn + an-1zn-1 + … + a0, the basic conditions include F(1) > 0, (−1)nF(−1) > 0, and |a0| < an, together with a sequence of determinant-like constraints generated from successive rows of the Jury table. If any condition fails, the system has roots on or outside the unit circle and is unstable.

Applications

The criterion is widely used in digital control and signal processing to check the stability of sampled-data systems and recursive digital filters, particularly for low-order systems where the inequalities remain manageable.
