Centerfire ammunition

Centerfire ammunition is a type of metallic cartridge used in firearms, where the primer is located at the center of the base of its casing. Unlike rimfire cartridges, the primer of a centerfire round is a separate, replaceable component seated in a pocket in the middle of the case head.

Characteristics

Centerfire cartridges are used in most modern rifles, shotguns, and handguns above small calibres. Because their cases are thicker and stronger than rimfire cases, they can withstand much higher pressures, and the cases can be reloaded — the spent primer is punched out and replaced — which makes centerfire ammunition popular among handloaders.

Primer types

Two main primer systems exist. The Boxer primer, common in the United States, has a single central flash hole and an integral anvil, making cases easy to reload. The Berdan primer, common in Europe, uses two off-center flash holes with the anvil formed in the case itself. Early centerfire designs of the mid-19th century, developed by inventors including Edward Boxer and Hiram Berdan, replaced earlier pinfire and rimfire systems in military service.
