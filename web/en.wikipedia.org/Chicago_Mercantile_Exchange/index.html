Chicago Mercantile Exchange

The Chicago Mercantile Exchange (CME), often called "the Merc", is an American financial and commodity derivatives exchange based in Chicago. Founded in 1898 as the Chicago Butter and Egg Board, it was renamed in 1919. Today it is part of CME Group, the world's largest derivatives marketplace, which also includes the Chicago Board of Trade (CBOT), NYMEX and COMEX.

Products

The CME trades futures and options on interest rates, equity indexes (such as the E-mini S&P 500), foreign exchange, agricultural commodities, energy and metals. In 1972 it pioneered financial futures with currency contracts through its International Monetary Market division, an innovation championed by chairman Leo Melamed. Most trading now occurs electronically on the CME Globex platform, though the exchange's history is famed for open-outcry pit trading.

History

The exchange demutualised in 2000 and became a publicly traded company in 2002, the first US exchange to do so. In 2007 it merged with the Chicago Board of Trade to form CME Group, and subsequently acquired NYMEX (2008) and the Kansas City Board of Trade (2012).

Clearing

CME Clearing acts as central counterparty to trades, guaranteeing performance and managing risk for trillions of dollars in annual notional value.
