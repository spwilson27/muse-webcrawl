Petrophysics

Petrophysics is the study of the physical and chemical rock properties of reservoirs and their interactions with fluids, particularly through the drilling of exploration and production wells. It is a key discipline in petroleum geology and reservoir engineering, used to determine whether a rock formation can produce hydrocarbons commercially.

Key properties

Petrophysicists measure and interpret properties such as porosity (the fraction of rock volume that can hold fluids), permeability (the ability of fluids to flow through the rock), water saturation, and lithology. Data come mainly from well logging — instruments lowered into boreholes that record electrical resistivity, density, neutron porosity, and acoustic properties — supplemented by core samples analysed in laboratories.

Applications

Analysis of well logs allows estimation of hydrocarbon volumes in place and guides decisions on completing and producing wells. Petrophysical methods are also applied to groundwater studies, carbon sequestration, and geothermal energy.
