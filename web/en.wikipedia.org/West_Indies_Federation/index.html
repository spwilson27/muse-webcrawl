West Indies Federation

The West Indies Federation, also known as the West Indian Federation or the Federation of the West Indies, was a short-lived political union that existed from 3 January 1958 to 31 May 1962. It was a federation of ten territories of the British Caribbean: Antigua and Barbuda, Barbados, Dominica, Grenada, Jamaica, Montserrat, Saint Kitts and Nevis, Saint Lucia, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, and Trinidad and Tobago.

History

The federation was established by the British Caribbean Federation Act 1956 with the aim of creating a single independent state from Britain's Caribbean colonies. Its capital was Port of Spain, Trinidad and Tobago. The first Governor-General was Patrick Buchan-Hepburn, Lord Hailes, and the first (and only) Prime Minister was Grantley Herbert Adams of Barbados, leader of the Federal Labour Party, which won the federal elections of 1958.

The union was undermined by disputes over taxation, freedom of movement, customs union and the relative contributions of the larger members. In a referendum in September 1961, Jamaica voted to leave the federation, prompting its leader Norman Manley to remark on the costs Jamaica bore; Trinidad and Tobago's Eric Williams then declared that "one from ten leaves nought", and Trinidad also withdrew. The federation was dissolved on 31 May 1962. Jamaica and Trinidad and Tobago became independent later in 1962, and most of the smaller territories later formed the Caribbean Community (CARICOM) structures instead.
