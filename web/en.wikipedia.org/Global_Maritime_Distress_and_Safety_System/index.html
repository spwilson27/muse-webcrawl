Global Maritime Distress and Safety System

The Global Maritime Distress and Safety System (GMDSS) is an internationally agreed set of safety procedures, equipment, and communication protocols, established under the SOLAS convention of the International Maritime Organization, that increases safety and makes it easier to rescue distressed ships, boats, and aircraft. Fully implemented on 1 February 1999, it replaced Morse-code distress procedures that had been in use since the early 20th century.

Components

GMDSS combines satellite and terrestrial radio systems: Emergency Position-Indicating Radio Beacons (EPIRBs) alert rescue authorities via the Cospas-Sarsat satellite system; Digital Selective Calling (DSC) sends automated distress alerts on VHF, MF, and HF bands; NAVTEX broadcasts navigational and meteorological warnings; and satellite communication providers such as Inmarsat and Iridium provide ship-to-shore alerting and voice links. Search and Rescue Transponders (SARTs) help rescuers home in on survival craft.

Sea areas and requirements

Required equipment depends on the sea area in which a vessel operates, from Area A1 (within VHF coast-station range) to Area A4 (polar regions). SOLAS ships must carry qualified GMDSS radio operators and maintain continuous radio watches, ensuring a distress alert can be sent and received anywhere in the world.
