Imperial Russian Navy

The Imperial Russian Navy (Российский императорский флот) was the navy of the Russian Empire from its establishment by Peter the Great in 1696 until the February Revolution of 1917. At its height in the nineteenth century it ranked among the largest navies in the world.

Origins

Peter I founded the regular navy during the Second Azov campaign and built the Baltic Fleet during the Great Northern War against Sweden, famously declaring that a ruler with land forces alone has "only one hand." The victory at Gangut (1714) established Russia as a Baltic naval power.

Nineteenth century

Russian fleets operated in the Baltic, Black Sea, and Pacific. Notable episodes include the victories of Chesma (1770) and Navarino (1827), the defense of Sevastopol during the Crimean War, and the circumnavigations of Adam Johann von Krusenstern. By the 1880s Russia possessed the third-largest battle fleet in the world.

Russo-Japanese War and decline

The navy suffered catastrophic defeat in the Russo-Japanese War of 1904–05, losing the Port Arthur squadron and the Baltic Fleet at the Battle of Tsushima. A rebuilding program was under way when the First World War began; after the revolutions of 1917 the Imperial Navy dissolved, its remnants forming parts of the Soviet Navy and White fleets.
