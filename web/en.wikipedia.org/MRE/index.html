MRE

Contents of a US MRE

Full name: Meal, Ready-to-Eat
Introduced: 1981 (US Armed Forces)
Energy: ~1,250 kcal per meal
Shelf life: ~3 years at 27 °C (80 °F)

The Meal, Ready-to-Eat (MRE) is a self-contained individual field ration in lightweight packaging procured by the United States Department of Defense for its service members to use in combat or field conditions where organised food facilities are unavailable. Each sealed bag contains a full meal — an entrée, side dishes, crackers or bread, a spread, dessert, drink mixes, and an accessory pack — totalling about 1,250 calories.

History

The MRE was adopted in 1975 and first issued in 1981, replacing the canned MCI (Meal, Combat, Individual) rations that had descended from Second World War C-rations. Continuous feedback programmes ("From the Warfighter" surveys) have expanded the menu list to 24 entrées, including vegetarian and religious (halal and kosher) options, and menus rotate regularly.

Heating and packaging

Food is packaged in retort pouches that can be eaten cold or warmed; since 1993 each MRE has included a flameless ration heater, a magnesium-iron pad that reacts exothermically with a small amount of water. The packaging is designed to survive a drop by parachute and storage for about three years at 27 °C, or shorter periods at higher temperatures.

Other uses

MREs are also used in disaster relief — they were distributed after Hurricane Katrina and other emergencies — and similar ready-to-eat ration systems have been developed by many other armed forces, including the French RCIR and the British Operational Ration Pack. Civilian-produced equivalents are sold for camping and emergency preparedness.
