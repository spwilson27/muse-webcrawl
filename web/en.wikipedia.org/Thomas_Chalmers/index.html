Thomas Chalmers

Thomas Chalmers (17 March 1780 – 31 May 1847) was a Scottish minister, professor of theology, political economist, and a leader of both the Church of Scotland and of the Free Church of Scotland. He has been called "Scotland's greatest nineteenth-century churchman".

Born in Anstruther, Fife, Chalmers studied at the University of St Andrews, showing early brilliance in mathematics. He was licensed to preach in 1799 and became minister of Kilmany in 1803. After a period of personal religious crisis and evangelical conversion, he moved to Glasgow in 1815, where his preaching drew enormous crowds at the Tron Church and later St John's parish.

In Glasgow, Chalmers pioneered a parish-based system of poor relief intended to replace statutory poor rates with voluntary charity administered locally — an experiment that made him famous across Britain. He became professor of moral philosophy at St Andrews in 1823 and professor of divinity at the University of Edinburgh in 1828.

Chalmers wrote extensively on political economy, including On Political Economy in Connexion with the Moral State and Moral Prospects of Society (1832), and his Bridgewater Treatise on natural theology (1833). He defended the establishment principle — that the national church should have a minister in every parish funded by the state.

When the conflict over patronage and spiritual independence came to a head, Chalmers led the Disruption of 1843, walking out of the General Assembly with over 450 ministers to form the Free Church of Scotland, of which he became the first Moderator and principal of its New College in Edinburgh. He died in Edinburgh in 1847 and was buried in Grange Cemetery.
