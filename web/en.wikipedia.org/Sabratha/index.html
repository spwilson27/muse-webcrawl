Sabratha (Arabic: صبراتة, Ṣabrāta) is a city on the Mediterranean coast of northwestern Libya, about 70 km west of Tripoli. Founded by the Phoenicians around 500 BC as a trading post, it formed — with Oea (Tripoli) and Leptis Magna — the three cities of ancient Tripolis. Its remarkably preserved Roman ruins, dominated by a great theatre, were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1982.

The theatre of Sabratha, reconstructed by Italian archaeologists in the 1930s.

History

Sabratha prospered under Carthaginian influence and, after the Roman conquest of North Africa, as an outlet for trans-Saharan trade in ivory, gold and wild animals for the arena. Granted colonial status under the Antonines or Severans, it was lavishly rebuilt in the 2nd and 3rd centuries AD with a new forum, temples, baths and a theatre seating some 5,000 spectators, whose stage building was reconstructed by Italian excavators under Mussolini. The city declined after earthquake damage in AD 365, Vandal and Byzantine occupations, and the Arab conquest of the 7th century, after which the site was largely abandoned.

Archaeology

Excavations, begun by the British in the 1920s and continued by Italian teams, revealed Punic tombs, Roman houses with mosaics, early Christian basilicas and a museum. Since 2016 the site has been listed as World Heritage in Danger owing to the Libyan civil conflict.
