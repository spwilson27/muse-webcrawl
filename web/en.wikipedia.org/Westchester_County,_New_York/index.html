Westchester County is a county located in the U.S. state of New York. It is situated immediately north of New York City, bordering the Bronx, and is part of the New York metropolitan area. With a population of about one million, it is known for its affluent suburban communities, extensive parkways and parkland, and its role as a commuter region for New York City.

The county seat is White Plains, a major retail and office centre. Yonkers, on the Hudson River, is the county's most populous city and one of the largest cities in New York State. Other cities include New Rochelle, Mount Vernon, Peekskill and Rye.

History

One of New York's original counties, Westchester was established in 1683. The area saw significant fighting during the American Revolutionary War, including the Battle of White Plains in 1776, and much of it lay in the contested "Neutral Ground." In the 19th and early 20th centuries, the development of railroads and later the parkway system of Robert Moses transformed it into a suburban retreat for wealthy New Yorkers.

Geography

Westchester is bounded by the Hudson River to the west and Long Island Sound to the southeast. Its terrain ranges from flat coastal areas in the south to rolling hills and reservoirs in the north, where the Croton watershed supplies New York City's drinking water.

Transportation

Metro-North Railroad lines (Hudson, Harlem and New Haven) connect the county to Grand Central Terminal. Major highways include Interstates 87, 95, 287 and 684, and parkways such as the Bronx River, Saw Mill River, Hutchinson River and Taconic State Parkways. Westchester County Airport lies near White Plains.
