Gluon condensate


The gluon field of the QCD vacuum.

In quantum chromodynamics (QCD), the gluon condensate is a non-zero vacuum expectation value of the gluon field strength tensor, conventionally written ⟨(αs/π) Gaμν Gaμν⟩. It is one of the most important non-perturbative parameters characterising the QCD vacuum.

Significance

Unlike a classical empty vacuum, the QCD vacuum is thought to contain condensates of quark–antiquark pairs (chiral condensate) and of gluons. The gluon condensate gives the vacuum a non-trivial structure that contributes to the masses and properties of hadrons. Its value is estimated from QCD sum rules, a method developed by Shifman, Vainshtein and Zakharov, by matching theoretical calculations of current correlators with experimental data on resonances.

Value

Phenomenological estimates typically give ⟨(αs/π) G²⟩ ≈ 0.012 GeV⁴, though with considerable uncertainty and some analyses favouring larger values. The gluon condensate enters the operator product expansion as the leading power correction (dimension four) to perturbative calculations, and it also appears in the trace anomaly of the energy–momentum tensor, connecting it to the origin of hadron mass.
