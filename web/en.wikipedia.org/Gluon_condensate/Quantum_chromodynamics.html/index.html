Quantum chromodynamics

Quantum chromodynamics (QCD) is the quantum field theory of the strong interaction, describing the interactions of quarks and gluons, the fundamental particles that make up hadrons such as protons and neutrons. Quarks carry a property called colour charge — red, green or blue — and interact by exchanging gluons, which themselves carry colour.

QCD has two distinctive properties: confinement, by which quarks and gluons are never observed in isolation, and asymptotic freedom, by which the coupling becomes weak at very short distances, a discovery recognised with the 2004 Nobel Prize in Physics. Nonperturbative features of the QCD vacuum are described by condensates such as the gluon condensate and the chiral condensate; at low energies QCD is studied numerically by lattice QCD.
