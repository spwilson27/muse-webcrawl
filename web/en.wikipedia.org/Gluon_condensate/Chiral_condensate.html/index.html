Chiral condensate

In quantum chromodynamics, the chiral condensate is the vacuum expectation value of the quark bilinear operator, ⟨q̄q⟩, which is nonzero (about −(250 MeV)³) and serves as an order parameter for the spontaneous breaking of chiral symmetry. Because the up and down quarks are nearly massless, the QCD Lagrangian has an approximate chiral symmetry; the condensate breaks this symmetry, giving rise to the pion as a pseudo-Goldstone boson and generating most of the mass of the nucleon.

The chiral condensate is closely related to other QCD vacuum condensates, such as the gluon condensate, and appears in QCD sum rules connecting hadronic properties to the structure of the vacuum.
