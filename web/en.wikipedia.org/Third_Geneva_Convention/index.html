Signed12 August 1949, Geneva
SubjectTreatment of prisoners of war

The Third Geneva Convention, formally the Geneva Convention relative to the Treatment of Prisoners of War, adopted in 1949, is one of the four treaties of the Geneva Conventions. It defines humanitarian protections for persons taken prisoner during armed conflict and requires that prisoners of war be treated humanely at all times.

Key provisions

The Convention requires that prisoners of war be protected against violence, insults, and public curiosity; that they receive adequate food, shelter, and medical care; and that they may correspond with their families and receive relief shipments. It guarantees the role of the International Committee of the Red Cross in visiting prisoners and monitoring conditions, and it prohibits reprisals against prisoners.

Definitions and application

Article 4 defines which categories of persons qualify as prisoners of war, including members of armed forces and certain militias and resistance movements. Common Article 3 extends minimum humane-treatment standards to non-international armed conflicts. The Convention replaced the 1929 Geneva Convention on prisoners of war and has been ratified by 196 states, making it universally binding.
