Jubal Early

Jubal Anderson Early
Born: November 3, 1816, Franklin County, Virginia, U.S.
Died: March 2, 1894, Lynchburg, Virginia, U.S.
Allegiance: United States; Confederate States
Rank: Lieutenant general (Confederate)

Jubal Anderson Early (November 3, 1816 – March 2, 1894) was an American lawyer and Confederate general in the American Civil War. Known for his aggressive temperament and acid tongue, he became one of Robert E. Lee's most trusted subordinates, and after the war he was an influential promoter of the "Lost Cause" interpretation of the conflict.

Early life and career

Early graduated from the United States Military Academy at West Point in 1837 and fought in the Seminole Wars before resigning his commission to practice law in Virginia. Elected to the Virginia House of Delegates, he opposed secession at the 1861 state convention but accepted his state's decision and joined the Confederate Army.

Civil War service

Early commanded a brigade at First Bull Run and was wounded at Williamsburg in 1862. Promoted to major general, he fought at Antietam, Fredericksburg, and Chancellorsville. At Gettysburg his division advanced into the town itself. In 1864 Lee gave him command of the Army of the Valley and sent him down the Shenandoah Valley; Early marched through the valley, crossed the Potomac, defeated Union forces at Monocacy, and reached the outskirts of Washington, D.C., in July 1864 — the closest the Confederacy came to capturing the Union capital. His army was subsequently destroyed by Philip Sheridan at Third Winchester, Fisher's Hill, and Cedar Creek, and Lee relieved him in March 1865.

Postwar

Early fled briefly to Mexico and Cuba before settling in Canada; he returned to Virginia after receiving a pardon in 1869, though he famously described himself as an "unrepentant rebel." He practiced law in Lynchburg and worked tirelessly to shape the historical memory of the war, defending Lee's record and his own, and serving as president of the Southern Historical Society. His memoirs, A Memoir of the Last Year of the War for Independence, appeared in 1866.
