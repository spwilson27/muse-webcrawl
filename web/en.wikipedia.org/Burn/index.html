Burn

Burn
CausesHeat (flame, scald), electricity, chemicals, friction, radiation

A burn is an injury to the skin or other tissue caused primarily by heat, electricity, chemicals, friction or radiation. Burns range from minor superficial injuries to life-threatening emergencies involving large areas of the body. Most burns are thermal, caused by flame, hot liquids (scalds), steam or contact with hot objects.

Classification

Burns are classified by depth. Superficial (first-degree) burns affect only the epidermis, causing redness and pain without blistering, as in mild sunburn. Partial-thickness (second-degree) burns extend into the dermis, producing blisters, swelling and severe pain. Full-thickness (third-degree) burns destroy the entire skin thickness and may appear white, charred or leathery, with loss of sensation. Severity also depends on the total body surface area affected, estimated with the "rule of nines", and on the site of injury and the patient's age.

Treatment

First aid consists of stopping the burning process, cooling the burn with cool running water, removing constricting items, and covering with a sterile dressing. Major burns require hospital care: airway management, large-volume intravenous fluid resuscitation (commonly calculated with the Parkland formula), wound care, infection control, nutritional support, and excision and skin grafting of deep burns. Complications include shock, infection, sepsis, hypothermia and scarring with contractures.

Epidemiology

Burns are a significant global cause of injury, particularly in low- and middle-income countries, where open fires and unsafe cooking arrangements are common. Children under five and older adults are at highest risk. Prevention measures include smoke alarms, safe stove design, hot-water temperature limits and fire-safety education.
