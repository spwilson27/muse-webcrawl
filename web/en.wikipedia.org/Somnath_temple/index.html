Somnath temple

DeityShiva (Somnath)
LocationPrabhas Patan, Veraval, Gujarat, India
SignificanceFirst of the twelve Jyotirlingas
Present templeConsecrated 1951

The Somnath temple, also called Somanātha temple or Deo Patan, is a Hindu temple located in Prabhas Patan, Veraval in Gujarat, India. It is one of the most sacred pilgrimage sites for Hindus and is the first among the twelve Jyotirlinga shrines of Shiva. The temple's name means "Lord of the Moon", from a legend in which the moon god Chandra, cursed to lose his lustre, was restored after worshipping Shiva here.

History

The site is ancient and associated with the confluence of three rivers and with the place where Krishna is said to have ended his earthly life. The temple was repeatedly destroyed by invaders and rebuilt by devotees: famously raided by Mahmud of Ghazni in 1026, who carried off its wealth; destroyed again under the Delhi Sultanate and later by Aurangzeb's orders in 1702. Each destruction was followed by reconstruction, most notably by the Maratha queen Ahilyabai Holkar, who built a temple nearby in 1783.

Modern reconstruction

After Indian independence, Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel championed the temple's rebuilding. The present temple, in the Māru-Gurjara (Chaulukya) style of temple architecture, was completed and consecrated on 11 May 1951 in the presence of President Rajendra Prasad. It stands on the shore of the Arabian Sea and remains one of India's most visited Shiva temples.
