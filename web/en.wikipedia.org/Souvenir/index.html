Souvenir

A souvenir (from French, meaning "a remembrance or memory") is an object a person acquires for the memories the owner associates with it. A souvenir can be any object that can be collected or purchased and transported home by the traveler as a memento of a visit. The object itself may have intrinsic value, or be a symbol of experience; without the owner's input, the symbolic value is inconsequential.

History and types

Travellers have carried home mementos since ancient times — medieval pilgrims collected badges and relics, and Grand Tour travellers brought back paintings and antiquities. Common modern souvenirs include postcards, snow globes, refrigerator magnets, key rings, miniature landmarks (such as Eiffel Tower models), T-shirts, and local handicrafts and foodstuffs. In Japan, the practice of buying omiyage — souvenirs, typically sweets, bought for colleagues and family — is a formalized social custom.

Cultural significance

Souvenirs function as memory anchors and as social proof of travel, and the souvenir trade is an important source of income in tourist destinations. Scholars of tourism distinguish authentic handcrafted objects from mass-produced imports, a tension that has grown with globalization; collectors also prize ephemera such as ticket stubs and programmes as personal souvenirs.
