Perth Agreement

The Perth Agreement is an agreement reached at the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting held in Perth, Australia, in October 2011, under which the sixteen Commonwealth realms agreed to change the rules of succession to their thrones. Announced by British prime minister David Cameron, the reforms introduced absolute primogeniture for persons born after 28 October 2011, removed the disqualification of persons who marry Roman Catholics, and limited the requirement for the monarch's consent to marriage to the first six people in line to the throne.

Implementation

Because the realms share a single monarch, the changes required legislation in each country. In the United Kingdom they were enacted by the Succession to the Crown Act 2013, with coordinated laws elsewhere, including Australia's Succession to the Crown Act 2015 following referral of powers by its states. The changes came into force simultaneously across the realms on 26 March 2015.

Effects

The reforms ended the system of male-preference primogeniture under which a younger son displaced an elder daughter in the line of succession, and removed the bar arising under the Act of Settlement 1701 on marrying a Catholic, although a Catholic still cannot become monarch. The first persons affected in the order of succession included Princess Charlotte, who retained her place ahead of her younger brother Prince Louis.
