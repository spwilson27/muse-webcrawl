Omega Speedmaster

ManufacturerOmega SA
Introduced1957
TypeChronograph wristwatch
Nickname"Moonwatch"

The Omega Speedmaster is a line of chronograph wristwatches produced by Omega SA since 1957. Originally designed as a sports and racing chronograph, it became famous as the first watch worn on the Moon: after rigorous testing, NASA qualified the Speedmaster for all manned space missions, and Buzz Aldrin wore one when he stepped onto the lunar surface during Apollo 11 in July 1969. (Neil Armstrong had left his inside the Lunar Module as a backup timer.)

NASA qualification

In 1964 NASA sought a watch for its astronauts and subjected chronographs from several brands to severe tests of temperature, shock, vibration, vacuum, and humidity. Only the Speedmaster completed all tests, and it was declared "flight qualified for all manned space missions" in March 1965. It was first worn in space by Wally Schirra and later by Ed White during the first American spacewalk on Gemini 4.

Apollo 13

During the Apollo 13 emergency in 1970, astronaut Jack Swigert used his Speedmaster to time a critical 14-second engine burn needed to correct the spacecraft's course, a role in the crew's safe return that earned Omega NASA's Snoopy Award.

Models

The classic "Moonwatch" retains a hand-wound movement (descended from calibre 321 and later 861/1861), a black dial with three subdials, and a tachymeter bezel. Numerous variants, limited editions, and modern Master Chronometer versions have followed, while the Speedmaster remains flight-qualified for NASA extravehicular activity.
