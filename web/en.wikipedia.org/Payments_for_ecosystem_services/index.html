Payments for ecosystem services


Forest protected under a watershed payment scheme

Payments for ecosystem services (PES), also known as payments for environmental services, are incentives offered to farmers, forest owners, or other landholders in exchange for managing their land to provide an ecological service — such as watershed protection, carbon sequestration, biodiversity conservation, or scenic beauty. PES schemes create a market-like transaction between the beneficiaries of a service and those whose land use supplies it.

Definition and principles

A widely used definition (Wunder, 2005) describes PES as a voluntary transaction in which a well-defined environmental service is bought by at least one buyer from at least one provider, conditional on the provider actually delivering the service. In practice many programs relax these criteria. Conditionality — paying only on verified delivery — is considered the feature distinguishing PES from general conservation subsidies.

Examples

Costa Rica's Pago por Servicios Ambientales program, launched in 1997, pays landowners for reforestation and forest protection and is credited with helping reverse the country's deforestation. New York City's watershed agreements with farmers in the Catskills protected drinking-water quality at a fraction of the cost of a filtration plant. Carbon forestry projects under mechanisms such as REDD+ apply the same logic at international scale.

Criticism

Debates concern the additionality of payments (whether the service would have been provided anyway), the risk of crowding out intrinsic conservation motives, effects on poor land users who lack formal title, and the commodification of nature.
