Roald Engelbregt Gravning Amundsen (16 July 1872 – c. 18 June 1928) was a Norwegian explorer of the polar regions. He was a key figure of the Heroic Age of Antarctic Exploration. He led the first expedition to successfully traverse the Northwest Passage (1903–1906) by ship and, on 14 December 1911, led the first expedition to reach the South Pole, beating Robert Falcon Scott's party by five weeks.

Northwest Passage

Between 1903 and 1906 Amundsen led the first expedition to cross the Northwest Passage between the Atlantic and Pacific oceans, aboard the small sloop Gjøa with a crew of six. During the journey he learned Arctic survival skills, including dog sledging and the use of animal skins, from the local Netsilik Inuit — knowledge that proved decisive in Antarctica.

South Pole expedition

Amundsen's plan to reach the North Pole was abandoned after Frederick Cook's and Robert Peary's competing claims of 1908–09. He secretly redirected his ship Fram south. From a base at the Bay of Whales on the Ross Ice Shelf, Amundsen and four companions (Olav Bjaaland, Helmer Hanssen, Sverre Hassel and Oscar Wisting) reached the South Pole on 14 December 1911, using skis and dog sleds, and returned safely with all men and 11 dogs.

Later years and death

In 1918–1920 Amundsen traversed the Northeast Passage aboard Maud. In 1926 he flew over the North Pole in the airship Norge with Umberto Nobile and Lincoln Ellsworth. In June 1928 he disappeared while flying on a rescue mission for Nobile's crashed airship Italia; wreckage of his Latham 47 flying boat was later found near Bear Island, and he is presumed to have died in the crash.
