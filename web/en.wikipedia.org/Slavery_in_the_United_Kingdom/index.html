Slavery in the United Kingdom

Slavery in the United Kingdom existed in various forms from Roman Britain through the medieval period and, in the era of the British Empire, Britain became a leading participant in the transatlantic slave trade, transporting an estimated 3.1 million Africans to the Americas. Slavery had no basis in English common law, a position affirmed in Somerset v Stewart (1772).

History

Slavery existed in Anglo-Saxon and Norman England (the Domesday Book records slaves as roughly a tenth of the population in 1086) before fading into serfdom. From the 16th century Britain's involvement in Atlantic slavery grew through chartered companies such as the Royal African Company, and ports including Liverpool, Bristol and London prospered from the trade.

Abolition

A mass abolitionist movement — Granville Sharp, William Wilberforce, Olaudah Equiano and popular petitioning — secured the Slave Trade Act 1807 and the Slavery Abolition Act 1833, effective across most of the empire in 1834 with compensation paid to slave-owners. The legacy of British slavery, and of the compensation loan finally repaid in 2015, remains a subject of public debate and research, including the UCL Legacies of British Slavery project.
