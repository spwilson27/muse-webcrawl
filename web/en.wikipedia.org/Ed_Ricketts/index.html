Ed Ricketts

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ricketts' Pacific Biological Laboratories on Cannery Row, Monterey, California.

Edward Flanders Robb Ricketts (May 14, 1897 – May 11, 1948) was an American marine biologist, ecologist and philosopher. He is best known for Between Pacific Tides (1939), a pioneering study of intertidal ecology organised by habitat rather than taxonomy, and as the model for the character Doc in John Steinbeck's novels Cannery Row (1945) and Sweet Thursday (1954).

Life and work

Ricketts studied at the University of Chicago, where he was influenced by the ecologist Warder Clyde Allee, but left without a degree. In 1923 he co-founded Pacific Biological Laboratories in Pacific Grove, later moved to Ocean View Avenue in Monterey — the street Steinbeck immortalised as Cannery Row — supplying preserved specimens to schools, universities and aquariums.

Collaboration with Steinbeck

Ricketts became close friends with John Steinbeck; their 1940 collecting expedition to the Gulf of California produced Sea of Cortez (1941), part travel narrative, part species catalogue, with Ricketts' philosophical essay "Essay on Non-Teleological Thinking" embedded in its narrative chapters. The two men profoundly influenced each other's ecological and holistic worldview.

Death and legacy

On May 8, 1948, Ricketts' car was struck by the Del Monte Express train at a crossing in Monterey; he died three days later, shortly before his 51st birthday. Pacific Biological Laboratories survives as a preserved building, and Ricketts is honoured as a founder of modern intertidal ecology.
