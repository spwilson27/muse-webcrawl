1933 double eagle

The 1933 double eagle is a United States 20-dollar gold coin designed by sculptor Augustus Saint-Gaudens. Although 445,500 were minted in 1933, none were legally released into circulation, and nearly all were melted down after the United States abandoned the gold standard. A handful of survivors became among the most famous and valuable coins in the world.

History

The double eagle had been struck since 1849, and the Saint-Gaudens design — Liberty striding forward on the obverse, a flying eagle on the reverse — had been in use since 1907 and is widely considered the most beautiful in American coinage. The 1933 issue was minted in the last days of the gold standard, but President Franklin D. Roosevelt's executive order prohibiting the hoarding of gold and the Gold Reserve Act of 1934 ended circulation of gold coins; the entire 1933 mintage was ordered melted, apart from two specimens sent to the Smithsonian Institution.

Surviving specimens and legal battles

A number of coins escaped the Mint, apparently through a cashier, and passed into collectors' hands; the Secret Service seized most of them in the 1940s and 1950s. One coin was exported with a licence to King Farouk of Egypt, and after decades of dispute the United States government and its later owner agreed to sell it at auction and split the proceeds. On 30 July 2002 that coin sold for $7.59 million, then a world record for a coin. A cache of ten more coins surfaced in the family of a Philadelphia jeweller; after lengthy litigation the courts ruled they remained property of the United States. In June 2021 the Farouk specimen — the only one that may be legally owned privately — was sold again for $18.9 million, once more setting the record for the world's most valuable coin.
