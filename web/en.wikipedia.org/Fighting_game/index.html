Fighting game

A fighting game is a video game genre that centers on close combat between a limited number of characters, in which players fight in individual rounds using martial arts moves, special attacks, blocking, throws, and combos. Fighting games typically emphasize one-on-one matches in an arena, though some support tag teams or multiple simultaneous fighters.

History

The genre has roots in Sega's Heavyweight Champ (1976) and Data East's Karate Champ (1984). Capcom's original Street Fighter (1987) introduced special moves; its sequel, Street Fighter II (1991), was a global phenomenon that defined the genre's conventions — distinct characters with unique move sets, six-button controls, combos, and competitive versus play — and ignited an arcade boom. Midway's Mortal Kombat (1992) added digitized actors and graphic "Fatalities," helping prompt the creation of the ESRB ratings board. SNK's The King of Fighters and series like Tekken, Virtua Fighter, and Soulcalibur brought 3D movement, while Nintendo's Super Smash Bros. created the platform-fighter subgenre.

Gameplay concepts

Core concepts include health bars and round timers, light/medium/heavy attacks, special moves performed with joystick motions (such as the quarter-circle "fireball" motion), blocking and throws forming a rock-paper-scissors dynamic, combos and juggling, meter management for super moves, and mind games such as footsies, zoning, and okizeme (attacking a rising opponent). Frame data — the startup, active, and recovery frames of each move — underpins high-level play.

Community and esports

Fighting games sustain one of gaming's oldest competitive communities (the FGC), organized around local arcades historically and now tournaments such as EVO (Evolution Championship Series), founded from the Battle by the Bay events of the 1990s. Modern titles including Street Fighter 6, Tekken 8, and Guilty Gear Strive support rollback netcode, crossplay, and large official circuits.
