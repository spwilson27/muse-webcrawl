Seville Cathedral

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Cathedral of Saint Mary of the See (Spanish: Catedral de Santa María de la Sede), better known as Seville Cathedral, is a Catholic cathedral in Seville, Andalusia, Spain. It is the largest Gothic cathedral in the world and was registered in 1987 by UNESCO as a World Heritage Site, along with the adjoining Alcázar palace complex and the General Archive of the Indies.

History

Construction began in 1401 on the site of the city's Almohad mosque, which had been converted to Christian use after the reconquest of Seville by Ferdinand III in 1248. According to tradition, the chapter resolved to build a church "so great and of such quality that those who see it finished will think we are mad." The cathedral was completed in 1506–1507, replacing Hagia Sophia as the largest Christian church by volume, a position the latter had held for a thousand years.

The builders retained elements of the mosque: the sahn courtyard (the Patio de los Naranjos), and the great minaret, converted into the bell tower known as La Giralda, crowned by a Renaissance belfry and the bronze weathervane statue called El Giraldillo.

Description

The cathedral has the longest nave of any cathedral in Spain, rising 42 metres (138 ft). Its vast interior houses an immense gilded main altarpiece, the Retablo Mayor, carved over the course of the 15th and 16th centuries, depicting scenes from the life of Christ. The building contains numerous chapels, notable paintings by Murillo and other Spanish masters, and the largest and richest cathedral museum collection in Spain.

Tomb of Christopher Columbus

The cathedral holds the tomb of Christopher Columbus, whose remains, after many journeys between Spain and the Caribbean, were brought to Seville in 1899. The monument depicts four kings of the Spanish kingdoms bearing the coffin. DNA analysis published in 2006 supported the identification of the Seville remains with Columbus, though claims persist elsewhere.
