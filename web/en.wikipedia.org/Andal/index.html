Andal

Andal (Tamil: ஆண்டாள், romanized: Āṇḍāḷ, lit. 'the girl who ruled over'), also known as Godadevi, Nāchiyār and Kothai, was the only female Alvar among the twelve Alvar saints of South India. She is traditionally dated to the 8th century CE and is revered in the Sri Vaishnava tradition as an incarnation of Bhudevi, the consort of the god Vishnu.

Andal
Tradition: Sri Vaishnavism / Alvar saints
Works: Tiruppavai, Nachiyar Tirumoli
Birthplace (trad.): Srivilliputhur, Tamil Nadu

Life

According to tradition, Andal was found as an infant under a tulasi plant in the temple garden at Srivilliputhur by the saint Periyalvar, who raised her. Devoted to Vishnu (as Ranganatha/Krishna), she refused earthly marriage and is said to have merged with the deity at Srirangam.

Works

Andal composed two works in Tamil that form part of the Alvar canon (Divya Prabandham): the Tiruppavai, thirty hymns sung during the Tamil month of Margazhi, and the Nachiyar Tirumoli, 143 verses expressing her longing for Krishna. Her verses are central to South Indian devotional practice, and her shrine at Srivilliputhur is a major pilgrimage site.
