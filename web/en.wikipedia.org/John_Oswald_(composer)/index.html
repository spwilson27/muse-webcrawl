John Oswald (composer)

Born: 30 May 1953, Kitchener, Ontario, Canada
Occupation: Composer, saxophonist, media artist
Known for: Coining "plunderphonics"; the Plunderphonics recordings
Awards: Governor General's Award in Visual and Media Arts (2004)

John Oswald (born May 30, 1953) is a Canadian composer, saxophonist and media artist best known for coining the term "plunderphonics" to describe his practice of building new compositions entirely from samples of existing recordings.

Plunderphonics

In his 1985 essay "Plunderphonics, or Audio Piracy as a Compositional Prerogative", Oswald argued that sampling and recontextualisation were legitimate compositional tools. His 1989 album Plunderphonic, distributed free on condition it not be sold, transformed recordings by artists from the Beatles to Michael Jackson; legal pressure from the Canadian Recording Industry Association led to the destruction of remaining copies.

Other work

Oswald's Grayfolded (1994), commissioned with the approval of the Grateful Dead, folds more than a hundred performances of "Dark Star" into a single composition. He directed the Mystery Tapes laboratory, created the Chronophotic photographic works, and since 2000 has been associated with research and performances of "chronotopic" audio. He also choreographed and composed for dance and founded the collective that became CCMC in Toronto in the 1970s.
