Great Horde

Existed: c. 1466–1502

Capital: Sarai

Preceded by: Golden Horde

Succeeded by: Crimean Khanate, Astrakhan Khanate

The Great Horde (Tatar: Oluğ Urda) was a Tatar-Mongol khanate that existed from about 1466 to 1502. It was the central remnant of the Golden Horde, centred on the lower Volga around the former capital Sarai, after other successor states — the khanates of Kazan, Crimea, Astrakhan, Qasim and Siberia, and the Nogai Horde — had broken away.

History

The Great Horde emerged under Khan Mahmud bin Kuchuk and his son Ahmed Khan bin Küchük after the disintegration of the Golden Horde in the mid-15th century. Its khans claimed primacy over the other successor states and continued to demand tribute from the Russian principalities.

Ahmed Khan attempted to reassert control over Muscovy, culminating in the Great Stand on the Ugra River in 1480, traditionally regarded as the end of the "Mongol-Tatar yoke" over Russia: Ahmed's forces faced Ivan III's army across the Ugra but withdrew without a decisive battle. Ahmed was killed the following year by the Siberian and Nogai Tatars.

Under Ahmed's sons, the Great Horde weakened through internal conflict and pressure from the Crimean Khanate. In 1502 the Crimean khan Meñli I Giray sacked Sarai and destroyed the Great Horde, absorbing its remaining territories and its prestige as the principal heir of the Golden Horde.
