Internal energy

The internal energy of a thermodynamic system, usually denoted U, is the total energy contained within the system — the sum of the kinetic and potential energies of its microscopic constituents. It is a state function: its change depends only on the initial and final states of the system.

First law of thermodynamics

The change in internal energy equals the heat transferred into the system plus the work done on the system: ΔU = Q + W (with sign conventions varying by discipline). In an isolated system, internal energy remains constant.

Microscopic interpretation

In statistical mechanics, internal energy comprises translational, rotational and vibrational motion of molecules plus intermolecular forces. For an ideal gas it depends only on temperature.
