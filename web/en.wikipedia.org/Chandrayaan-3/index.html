Chandrayaan-3

Mission
OperatorIndian Space Research Organisation (ISRO)
Launch14 July 2023, Satish Dhawan Space Centre, on LVM3
Landing23 August 2023, near the lunar south pole
ComponentsVikram lander, Pragyan rover, propulsion module
PredecessorChandrayaan-2 (2019)

Chandrayaan-3 is the third mission in the Chandrayaan programme of the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO), and the first mission ever to achieve a soft landing near the lunar south pole. Launching on 14 July 2023 aboard an LVM3 rocket, the spacecraft's Vikram lander touched down on 23 August 2023, making India the fourth country — after the Soviet Union, the United States, and China — to land successfully on the Moon, and the first to do so in the south polar region.

Surface operations

The six-wheeled Pragyan rover rolled down the lander's ramp and drove on the surface during one lunar day (about 14 Earth days), analysing soil with its Alpha Particle X-ray Spectrometer and Laser-Induced Breakdown Spectroscope, which confirmed the presence of sulfur and other elements in the polar regolith. Experiments on the lander measured thermal properties of the topsoil and near-surface plasma density, and recorded a possible seismic event. On 3 September the rover was put into sleep mode; attempts to reawaken it after the lunar night were unsuccessful, and the mission's surface phase was declared complete.

The landing site was named Statio Shiv Shakti by Prime Minister Narendra Modi, and 23 August was declared India's National Space Day. The propulsion module later performed manoeuvres returning it to a high Earth orbit.
