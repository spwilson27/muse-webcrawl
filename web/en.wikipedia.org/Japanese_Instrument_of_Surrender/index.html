Japanese Instrument of Surrender

The Japanese Instrument of Surrender was the written agreement that formalised the surrender of the Empire of Japan, ending the Second World War. It was signed by representatives of Japan and the Allied powers aboard the battleship USS Missouri in Tokyo Bay on 2 September 1945.

Signed: 2 September 1945

Location: USS Missouri, Tokyo Bay

Effect: Formal end of World War II hostilities

The ceremony

The document proclaimed the unconditional surrender of the Imperial Japanese Headquarters and all Japanese armed forces, accepting the terms of the Potsdam Declaration. Foreign Minister Mamoru Shigemitsu signed for the Japanese government and General Yoshijirō Umezu for the armed forces. General of the Army Douglas MacArthur signed as Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, followed by representatives of the United States, China, the United Kingdom, the Soviet Union, Australia, Canada, France, the Netherlands and New Zealand. A famous mishap occurred when the Canadian signatory, Colonel Lawrence Cosgrave, signed on the wrong line, obliging MacArthur's chief of staff to correct the document by hand.

Aftermath

The signing began the Allied occupation of Japan under MacArthur. The original document is held by the United States National Archives; a copy is displayed aboard the museum ship Missouri at Pearl Harbor.
