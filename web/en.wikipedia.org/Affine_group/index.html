Affine group

In mathematics, the affine group or general affine group of any affine space is the group of all invertible affine transformations from the space into itself. It is a Lie group if the underlying field is the real or complex numbers. The affine group of an n-dimensional affine space over a field K is often denoted Aff(n, K).

Definition

An affine transformation can be written in the form x ↦ Ax + b, where A is an invertible linear map (an element of the general linear group GL(n, K)) and b is a translation vector. Consequently the affine group is a semidirect product of the group of translations by the general linear group: Aff(n, K) = Kn ⋊ GL(n, K).

Matrix representation

Affine transformations can be represented as (n+1)×(n+1) matrices acting on homogeneous coordinates, combining the linear part A and translation b into a single block matrix. This representation is the basis of homogeneous coordinates in computer graphics and robotics.

Subgroups and related groups

The Euclidean group of rotations, reflections, and translations is a subgroup of the affine group, as is the special affine group of volume-preserving affine transformations (those with det A = 1). In Felix Klein's Erlangen program, affine geometry is the study of properties invariant under the affine group: parallelism, ratios of lengths on a line, and ratios of areas are preserved, while distances and angles are not.
