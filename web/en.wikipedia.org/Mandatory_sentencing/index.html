Mandatory sentencing

Mandatory sentencing requires that offenders serve a predefined term of imprisonment for certain crimes, removing or limiting the discretion of judges to impose a shorter or alternative sentence. Mandatory minimum laws set the lowest term a court may impose.

Use

Mandatory sentences are common for serious offences such as murder (for example, mandatory life imprisonment in many jurisdictions) and were widely adopted for drug offences in the United States during the 1980s. "Three-strikes" laws impose long mandatory terms on repeat offenders.

Debate

Supporters argue that mandatory sentences ensure consistency, deter crime, and keep dangerous offenders off the streets. Critics argue that they produce disproportionate punishments, shift discretion to prosecutors, contribute to mass incarceration, and prevent courts from considering individual circumstances.
