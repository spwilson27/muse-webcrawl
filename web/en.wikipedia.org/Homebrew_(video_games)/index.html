Homebrew (video games)

In video games, homebrew refers to software produced by hobbyist consumers for proprietary video game consoles or handheld systems, rather than by licensed commercial developers. Homebrew ranges from original games and demos to emulators, utilities, and fan translations.

Development

Because console makers restrict official development kits, homebrew creators rely on reverse engineering, unofficial toolchains, flash cartridges, exploits, or modchips to run unsigned code. Communities formed around systems such as the Game Boy Advance, Nintendo DS, Wii, PSP, and Dreamcast, the latter notable for running homebrew from ordinary CDs.

Legal issues

Circumventing console protections can violate laws such as the U.S. Digital Millennium Copyright Act and console makers' terms, though developing original software itself is generally lawful. Manufacturers have alternated between fighting homebrew and, on some platforms, officially supporting hobbyist development.
