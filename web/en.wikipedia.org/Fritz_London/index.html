Fritz London

Fritz Wolfgang London (7 March 1900 – 30 March 1954) was a German-born American theoretical physicist known for his contributions to quantum mechanics, superconductivity, and superfluidity, much of it in collaboration with his younger brother Heinz London.

Life

London was born in Breslau, Germany (now Wrocław, Poland), into an academic family; his father Franz London was a professor of mathematics. After studying philosophy and then physics, he worked with Erwin Schrödinger in Berlin and Stuttgart. With Walter Heitler in 1927 he published the first quantum-mechanical treatment of the covalent chemical bond in the hydrogen molecule. Fleeing Nazi Germany in 1933, he worked in Oxford and Paris before emigrating to the United States in 1939, becoming a professor at Duke University in North Carolina and a U.S. citizen in 1945.

Work

London's name survives in several concepts: the London dispersion forces between atoms and molecules, explained by him in 1930 through quantum fluctuations; the London equations (1935, with Heinz London), which phenomenologically describe superconductors, including the Meissner effect; and the London penetration depth. He proposed that superfluid helium II could be understood through Bose–Einstein condensation and wrote the influential two-volume monograph Superfluids. He was nominated repeatedly for the Nobel Prize but never received it; he died of a heart ailment in Durham, North Carolina, in 1954.
