Restriction fragment length polymorphism

In molecular biology, restriction fragment length polymorphism (RFLP) is a technique that exploits variations in homologous DNA sequences, known as polymorphisms, in order to distinguish individuals, populations, or species or to pinpoint the locations of genes within a sequence. The term may refer to a polymorphism itself, as detected through the differing locations of restriction enzyme sites, or to the related laboratory technique by which such differences can be illustrated.

In RFLP analysis, a DNA sample is digested into fragments by one or more restriction enzymes, and the resulting restriction fragments are then separated by size through gel electrophoresis and transferred to a membrane via Southern blotting. Hybridization of the membrane to a labelled DNA probe then determines the length of the fragments which are complementary to the probe. A polymorphism is present — and an RFLP occurs — when the length of a detected fragment varies between individuals.

Differences in restriction fragment banding patterns between individuals reveal a polymorphism.

History

RFLP analysis was among the first widely used DNA profiling techniques. It was applied to the construction of the first genetic linkage map of the human genome in the 1980s, to the mapping of genes for inherited diseases such as Huntington's disease and cystic fibrosis, and to early forensic DNA fingerprinting developed by Alec Jeffreys. The technique required relatively large amounts of undegraded DNA.

Applications

  • Genetic mapping: RFLPs served as markers for building linkage maps and locating disease genes.
  • Forensics and paternity testing: Variable number tandem repeat (VNTR) loci detected by RFLP produced highly individual-specific profiles.
  • Ecology and taxonomy: RFLP patterns of ribosomal DNA or mitochondrial DNA are used to distinguish species and populations.
  • PCR-RFLP: A faster variant digests a PCR product, and remains in common use for genotyping known mutations.

Decline and replacements

Although RFLP analysis was once a key technique, it has largely been replaced by cheaper, faster methods based on the polymerase chain reaction (PCR), such as short tandem repeat (STR) profiling, single-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) genotyping, and direct DNA sequencing. It remains historically important and is still used in some plant, microbial and low-resource settings.
