Brabant Revolution

The Brabant Revolution (1789–1790) was an uprising in the Austrian Netherlands (modern Belgium) against the rule of Holy Roman Emperor Joseph II, whose centralising and enlightened reforms had alienated the region's traditional estates, clergy, and guilds.

Led by figures such as Jan Frans Vonck and Hendrik Van der Noot, patriot forces defeated the Austrians at the Battle of Turnhout on 27 October 1789. In January 1790 the provinces proclaimed the United Belgian States, a confederation modelled partly on the Dutch Republic and the United States.

Divided between conservative Statists and democratic Vonckists, the new state was short-lived: Austrian forces under Emperor Leopold II reconquered the territory by December 1790. The revolution is nevertheless regarded as a precursor of Belgian independence in 1830.
