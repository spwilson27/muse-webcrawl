The Battle of Dunkirk took place in Dunkirk, northern France, during the Second World War. The battle was fought between the Allies and Nazi Germany. As part of the Battle of France on the Western Front, the Battle of Dunkirk was the defence and evacuation of British and other Allied forces to Britain from 26 May to 4 June 1940.

Background

After the Phoney War, the Battle of France began in earnest on 10 May 1940. To the east, the German Army Group B had invaded the Netherlands and advanced westward. The British Expeditionary Force (BEF) advanced to the River Dyle in Belgium, but the German breakthrough at Sedan and the drive to the Channel coast threatened to trap the Allied forces in northern France and Belgium.

The evacuation

Large numbers of troops were able to reach Dunkirk, and the Royal Navy and a flotilla of hundreds of merchant marine boats, fishing boats, pleasure craft and lifeboats — the "Little Ships of Dunkirk" — crossed the English Channel to rescue them. In total, 338,226 soldiers were evacuated, including 139,997 French, Polish and Belgian troops, together with a small number of Dutch soldiers, aboard 861 vessels.

Aftermath

Although the evacuation was hailed as a deliverance in Britain — Winston Churchill called it a "miracle of deliverance" in his "We shall fight on the beaches" speech on 4 June — the British Army was forced to leave behind virtually all of its heavy equipment. The battle ended with a German victory and the occupation of the town, but the rescued troops formed the core around which the British Army would be rebuilt.
