Portugal national football team

The Portugal national football team (Portuguese: Seleção Portuguesa de Futebol) represents Portugal in men's international football and is controlled by the Portuguese Football Federation. Nicknamed the Seleção das Quinas, the team plays in red and green.

History

Portugal played its first match in 1921 against Spain. For decades the team achieved little, though it finished third at the 1966 World Cup in England, inspired by Eusébio, the tournament's top scorer. A "golden generation" led by Luís Figo reached the Euro 2004 final on home soil, losing to Greece, and the semi-finals of the 2006 World Cup.

The team's greatest triumph came at Euro 2016 in France, where, captained by Cristiano Ronaldo — the most capped men's international player and the all-time top scorer in men's international football — Portugal beat the hosts in the final through Éder's extra-time goal. Portugal also won the inaugural UEFA Nations League in 2019, again on home ground. Ronaldo's longevity has defined the modern era, with appearances at five World Cups.

Stadiums and rivalry

Portugal has no fixed home stadium, rotating matches among venues such as the Estádio da Luz and Estádio José Alvalade in Lisbon and the Estádio do Dragão in Porto. Its keenest rivalry is with neighbouring Spain.
