Mullah Omar

BornMohammed Omar, c. 1960, near Kandahar, Afghanistan
Died23 April 2013 (aged ~53), Zabul Province, Afghanistan (announced 2015)
OfficeSupreme Leader of Afghanistan, 1996–2001
Known forFounder and leader of the Taliban

Mohammed Omar (c. 1960 – 2013), commonly known as Mullah Omar, was an Afghan religious scholar and militant who founded the Taliban and served as Afghanistan's head of state from 1996 to 2001, ruling as "Commander of the Faithful" (Amir al-Mu'minin) of the Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan.

Rise of the Taliban

An ethnic Pashtun from the Kandahar region, Omar fought with the mujahideen against the Soviet occupation, losing an eye to shrapnel. In 1994, amid the anarchy of the Afghan civil war, he gathered a movement of religious students (taliban) pledged to end warlord corruption and impose sharia law. Taliban forces swept the country, taking Kabul in 1996. His regime enforced an exceptionally strict interpretation of Islamic law, severely restricting women and minorities, destroying the Bamiyan Buddhas in 2001, and harbouring Osama bin Laden and al-Qaeda.

Overthrow and death

After refusing to hand over bin Laden following the September 11 attacks, Omar's government was overthrown by the U.S.-led invasion in late 2001. He fled and led the Taliban insurgency from hiding, with a $10 million U.S. bounty on his head. In 2015 the Afghan government and the Taliban confirmed he had died of illness (reported as tuberculosis/hepatitis complications) in April 2013 — a fact the Taliban had concealed for two years while issuing statements in his name. He was succeeded by Akhtar Mansour and later Hibatullah Akhundzada.
