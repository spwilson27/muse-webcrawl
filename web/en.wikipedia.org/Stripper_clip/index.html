Stripper clip

A stripper clip (also known as a charger or charger clip, particularly in Commonwealth militaries) is a speedloader that holds several cartridges (usually between five and ten) together in a single unit for easier and faster loading of a firearm's magazine. Unlike an en bloc clip, a stripper clip is not inserted into the weapon itself: it is used to push the cartridges into the magazine and is then removed and (in military use) discarded or retained for reuse.

Design and use

The typical stripper clip is a strip of stamped metal whose edges fold over the rims or extractor grooves of the cartridges, holding them in a row. To load, the shooter seats the clip in a guide — machined into the receiver of many bolt-action military rifles, or an add-on guide on a detachable magazine — and presses the cartridges down into the magazine with the thumb. Famous examples include the 5-round clips of the German Mauser Karabiner 98k, the 10-round clips of the Soviet SKS and the British Lee–Enfield's 5-round chargers.

History

Stripper clips became standard with the smokeless-powder bolt-action service rifles adopted from the 1890s onward, enabling soldiers to reload an internal magazine in seconds. Although most modern service rifles use detachable box magazines, stripper clips remain in use for loading magazines quickly (for example, 5.56 mm ammunition is commonly supplied on 10-round stripper clips) and for clip-fed legacy weapons.
