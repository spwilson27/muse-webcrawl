The American-British-Dutch-Australian (ABDA) Command, or ABDACOM, was the first combined major Allied military command of World War II, created in January 1942 to coordinate land, air and naval forces resisting the Japanese advance in South-East Asia and the South West Pacific.

Formation

ABDACOM was established on 3 January 1942 following the Arcadia Conference in Washington. Its supreme commander was British General Sir Archibald Wavell, with headquarters at Lembang, near Bandung, in the Dutch East Indies. Its area covered Burma, Malaya, the Netherlands East Indies and the Philippines, and its mission was to hold the "Malay Barrier" against Japanese expansion.

Operations and dissolution

The command faced overwhelming Japanese air and naval superiority. Allied forces under ABDA suffered a string of defeats, including the fall of Singapore (15 February 1942) and the Battle of the Java Sea (27 February 1942). Wavell dissolved the command on 25 February 1942, handing control back to local commanders shortly before the surrender of Java. Despite its failure, ABDACOM set the precedent for later combined Allied commands such as South East Asia Command and South West Pacific Area.
