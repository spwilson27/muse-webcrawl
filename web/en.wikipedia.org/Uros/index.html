Uros

Uros floating islands on Lake Titicaca, Peru.

The Uros (or Uru) are an Indigenous people of Peru and Bolivia. A community of about 1,200 Uros live on an archipelago of artificial floating islands which they fashion from totora reeds in Lake Titicaca, near Puno, Peru. The Uros also regard themselves, in their oral traditions, as among the oldest peoples of the Andes, predating the Inca civilisation.

The floating islands

The islands are built from buoyant totora reeds, which grow abundantly in the shallows of the lake. Blocks of reed roots are lashed together and covered with layers of cut reeds, which rot from below and must continually be replenished from above. Houses, watchtowers, boats (balsas), and even a traditional school are likewise made of reeds. Historically the islands offered protection from hostile neighbours such as the Colla and the Inca; today tourism is an important source of income alongside fishing and handicrafts.

Language

The original Uru language has largely been lost; most Uros now speak Aymara, Quechua, and Spanish, though efforts at language revival exist.
