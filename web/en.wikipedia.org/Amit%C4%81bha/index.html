Amitābha ("Immeasurable Light"), also known as Amituofo in Chinese and Amida in Japanese, is a celestial Buddha in Mahayana Buddhism. He is the principal Buddha of Pure Land Buddhism and presides over Sukhavati, the Western Paradise.

Doctrine

According to the Larger Sutra of Immeasurable Life, Amitābha was once a monk named Dharmākara who made forty-eight vows, the eighteenth of which promised that beings who sincerely call upon his name would be reborn in his Pure Land. Devotees practice nianfo (nembutsu in Japanese) — the recitation of his name — as a path to rebirth in Sukhavati, where enlightenment is assured.

Iconography

Amitābha is usually depicted seated in meditation, often reddish in color, sometimes flanked by the bodhisattvas Avalokiteśvara and Mahāsthāmaprāpta. He is one of the Five Tathagatas in Vajrayana Buddhism, associated with the west.

Influence

Pure Land practice centered on Amitābha became one of the most widespread forms of Buddhism in East Asia, shaping the religious life of China, Japan, Korea, and Vietnam.
