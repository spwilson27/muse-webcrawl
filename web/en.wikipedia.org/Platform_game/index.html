A platform game (or platformer) is a subgenre of action video games in which the core objective is to move the player character between points in an environment. Platform games are characterized by levels with uneven terrain and suspended platforms of different heights that require jumping and climbing to traverse. Other acrobatic maneuvers may factor into the gameplay, such as swinging from objects like vines or grappling hooks, jumping off walls, air-dashing, gliding through the air, and being shot from cannons.

History

The genre emerged in the early 1980s. Universal's Space Panic (1980) placed a character on platforms and ladders but without jumping; Nintendo's Donkey Kong (1981), designed by Shigeru Miyamoto, added the jump and became the template for the genre. The side-scrolling platformer arrived with games such as Jump Bug and Pitfall!, and was defined for the home market by Nintendo's Super Mario Bros. (1985), one of the best-selling video games of all time.

2D to 3D

During the 16-bit era, series such as Sonic the Hedgehog emphasized speed, while the transition to 3D graphics in the mid-1990s produced landmarks including Super Mario 64 (1996), which established free-roaming 3D movement and camera control, and the Crash Bandicoot and Spyro series. In 3D platformers, goals often shift toward exploration and collecting items across open levels.

Subgenres and related forms

Puzzle-platformers combine jumping with problem-solving (e.g., Braid, Portal). Run-and-gun games emphasize shooting while moving (e.g., Contra, Metal Slug). Cinematic platformers such as Prince of Persia and Another World use realistic movement and rotoscoped animation. The Metroidvania blends platforming with exploration gated by acquired abilities. From the late 2000s, independent developers revived 2D platforming with titles such as Super Meat Boy, Celeste and Hollow Knight.

Design elements

Common elements include jumping between platforms, avoiding hazards and enemies, collecting items, power-ups that grant new abilities, checkpoints, boss encounters, and — in many modern games — carefully tuned jump physics such as variable jump height, "coyote time" and input buffering that make movement feel responsive.
