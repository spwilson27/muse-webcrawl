Vicuña

Vicuña (Vicugna vicugna)
Family: Camelidae
Habitat: High Andes of Peru, Bolivia, Argentina, Chile
Conservation status: Least Concern (recovered)

The vicuña is one of the two wild South American camelids, the other being the guanaco, and a relative of the llama and alpaca. It lives in the high alpine areas of the Andes above about 3,200 metres.

Characteristics

Vicuñas are slender animals weighing 35–65 kg, with a soft tawny coat and a white bib of long hair on the chest. Their fleece is among the finest animal fibres in the world — each animal yields only about 200 grams of wool per shearing — and their teeth grow continuously, an adaptation to grazing tough highland grasses.

Conservation

Prized since Inca times, vicuñas were hunted almost to extinction for their wool, falling to perhaps 6,000 animals in the 1960s. International protection under CITES and community-based shearing (the traditional chaccu) have restored the population to several hundred thousand, making the vicuña a celebrated conservation success. It appears on the coat of arms of Peru.
