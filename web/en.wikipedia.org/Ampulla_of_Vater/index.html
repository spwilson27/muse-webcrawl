The ampulla of Vater, also known as the hepatopancreatic ampulla or hepatopancreatic duct, is the common duct that is usually formed by the union of the common bile duct and the pancreatic duct within the wall of the duodenum. This common duct usually features a dilation ("ampulla") and opens into the descending part of the duodenum at the major duodenal papilla, about 7–10 cm from the pylorus. The structure is named after the German anatomist Abraham Vater, who first described it in 1720.

Structure

The ampulla is formed where the distal common bile duct and the main pancreatic duct (of Wirsung) join, usually within the duodenal wall. Its opening is regulated by the sphincter of Oddi, a muscular valve that controls the flow of bile and pancreatic juice into the duodenum and prevents reflux of duodenal contents. In some people the two ducts open separately, and accessory pancreatic drainage may occur through the minor duodenal papilla.

Function

Through the ampulla, bile produced by the liver and stored in the gallbladder, together with enzyme-rich pancreatic secretions, enters the duodenum to aid digestion of fats and other nutrients. The sphincter of Oddi relaxes in response to the hormone cholecystokinin (CCK) after meals.

Clinical significance

Gallstones may lodge in the ampulla, obstructing both ducts and causing obstructive jaundice, cholangitis, or acute pancreatitis ("gallstone pancreatitis"). Tumours arising here, called ampullary cancers, are a distinct type of periampullary cancer and often present early with painless jaundice. The ampulla is examined and treated endoscopically via ERCP (endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography), including sphincterotomy and stent placement; surgical removal may involve a Whipple procedure (pancreaticoduodenectomy).
