Axel Honneth (born 18 July 1949) is a German philosopher and sociologist associated with the third generation of the Frankfurt School. From 2001 to 2018 he directed the Institute for Social Research in Frankfurt, succeeding Jürgen Habermas's tradition, and he has also taught at Columbia University.

Theory of recognition

Honneth's best-known work, The Struggle for Recognition (1992), develops a theory of society grounded in the concept of recognition (Anerkennung). Drawing on Hegel's early writings and George Herbert Mead's social psychology, he argues that human identity and self-realization depend on intersubjective recognition in three spheres: love, rights (legal recognition), and solidarity (social esteem). Experiences of disrespect are, for Honneth, the moral engine of social conflict and progress.

Later works

His subsequent books include Reification: A New Look at an Old Idea (2005), Freedom's Right (2011), which reconstructs justice as realized in modern institutions, and The Idea of Socialism (2015). Honneth has engaged in prominent debates on recognition and redistribution with Nancy Fraser.
