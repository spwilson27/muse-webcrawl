Canal de Garonne

Canal de Garonne
Location: southwestern France
Length: 193 km (120 mi)
Locks: 53
Opened: 1856

The Canal de Garonne (French: Canal latéral à la Garonne) is a French canal connecting Toulouse to Castets-en-Dorthe. Opened in 1856, it forms, together with the Canal du Midi, the two-seas link between the Atlantic Ocean and the Mediterranean Sea.

Route

The canal runs for 193 kilometres alongside the river Garonne, passing through Agen, where it crosses the river on the Agen aqueduct, one of the longest canal bridges in France.

History

Construction began in 1838 and the canal was completed in 1856. It was built to extend the Canal du Midi (opened 1681) so that barges could travel from Bordeaux to Sète without navigating the shifting channels of the Garonne.
