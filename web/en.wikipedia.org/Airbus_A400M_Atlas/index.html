Airbus A400M Atlas

Type: Military transport aircraft

Manufacturer: Airbus Military / Airbus Defence and Space

First flight: 11 December 2009

Introduction: 2013 with the French Air Force

Status: In production, in service

The Airbus A400M Atlas is a European four-engine turboprop military transport aircraft. It was designed by Airbus Military (now Airbus Defence and Space) as a tactical airlifter with strategic capabilities, intended to replace older transport aircraft such as the Transall C-160 and the Lockheed C-130 Hercules.

Development

The program grew out of the Future Large Aircraft requirement shared by several European nations. The launch customers — France, Germany, Spain, the United Kingdom, Turkey, Belgium, and Luxembourg — signed for 180 aircraft in 2003, with Malaysia later joining as an export customer. Development of the Europrop TP400-D6 turboprop, the most powerful turboprop engine in the Western world, caused significant delays and cost growth. The A400M first flew in December 2009 from Seville, Spain, and the first production aircraft was delivered to France in 2013.

Capabilities

The A400M can carry up to 37 tonnes of cargo, including helicopters, armoured vehicles, and palletized freight, and can operate from short, unpaved airstrips. It can also perform aerial refuelling, medical evacuation, and paratroop delivery. A fatal crash of a test aircraft near Seville in May 2015, caused by incorrect engine software calibration data, led to temporary fleet restrictions.

Operators

Operators include the air forces of France, Germany, Spain, the United Kingdom, Turkey, Belgium, Luxembourg, Malaysia, Indonesia, and Kazakhstan.
