RRS Discovery (2012)

RRS Discovery
Operator: National Oceanography Centre / Natural Environment Research Council
Launched: 2012; delivered 2013
Length: 99.7 m
Home port: Southampton

RRS Discovery is a Royal Research Ship operated by the United Kingdom's National Oceanography Centre. Built in Spain and delivered in 2013, she replaced the previous RRS Discovery (1962) as one of Britain's two global-class oceanographic research vessels, alongside RRS James Cook.

Design

The ship is about 99.7 metres long with a displacement of roughly 6,000 tonnes and accommodation for around 52 people, including 28 scientists. She is fitted with dynamic positioning, a large aft working deck, hangars, and winches for deploying equipment such as CTD rosettes, corers, trawls, and remotely operated or autonomous underwater vehicles to depths of 6,000 metres and beyond.

Service

Discovery undertakes multidisciplinary ocean science worldwide, including physical oceanography, marine biology, and geophysics cruises in the Atlantic and other oceans. She is the fourth vessel to bear the name, continuing a lineage that includes the famous 1901 Antarctic expedition ship preserved at Dundee.
