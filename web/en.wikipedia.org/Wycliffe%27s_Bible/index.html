Wycliffe's Bible

LanguageMiddle English
Publishedc. 1382–1395
Source textLatin Vulgate
Associated withJohn Wycliffe and the Lollards

Wycliffe's Bible is the name given to a group of Bible translations into Middle English made under the direction of, or associated with, the theologian John Wycliffe (c. 1328–1384). Translated from the Latin Vulgate, these were the first complete translations of the Bible into English and appeared between about 1382 and 1395.

Versions

Two main versions survive: an earlier, more literal translation, and a later revision (associated with John Purvey) in more idiomatic English. About 250 manuscripts of Wycliffe's Bible survive in whole or part — more than for any other Middle English work — indicating wide circulation despite official opposition.

Opposition and legacy

The translation was linked to the Lollard movement, which held that scripture should be accessible to laypeople. In 1409 Archbishop Arundel's Constitutions restricted unauthorized Bible translation and possession in England. Wycliffe's Bible nonetheless paved the way for later English translations, including Tyndale's in the 16th century.
