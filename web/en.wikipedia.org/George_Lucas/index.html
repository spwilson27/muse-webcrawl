George Lucas

George Walton Lucas Jr. (born May 14, 1944) is an American filmmaker and philanthropist. He created the Star Wars and Indiana Jones franchises and founded Lucasfilm, LucasArts, Industrial Light & Magic, and THX. Lucas is among the most financially successful filmmakers of all time.

Career

After studying at the USC School of Cinematic Arts, Lucas directed THX 1138 (1971) and American Graffiti (1973), a major hit. Star Wars (1977) became the highest-grossing film of its era and reshaped the film industry with its merchandising and special effects. Lucas wrote and produced the sequels, executive-produced the Indiana Jones films directed by Steven Spielberg, and directed the Star Wars prequel trilogy (1999–2005). In 2012 he sold Lucasfilm to Disney for over $4 billion.

Philanthropy

Lucas has pledged much of his fortune to philanthropy, particularly education, through the George Lucas Educational Foundation (Edutopia), and is building the Lucas Museum of Narrative Art in Los Angeles.
