Samba

Samba is a Brazilian music genre and dance style with roots in Afro-Brazilian communities of Rio de Janeiro in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Characterised by syncopated 2/4 rhythms and large percussion ensembles, it is internationally recognised as a symbol of Brazil and the centrepiece of the Rio Carnival.

Origins

Samba grew from the cultural life of Afro-Brazilian migrants from Bahia who settled in Rio after abolition (1888), gathering in neighbourhoods such as Praça Onze and the homes of the tias baianas ("Bahian aunties"), notably Tia Ciata. It fused African rhythms and circle dances (semba, lundu) with Brazilian maxixe and marcha. "Pelo Telefone" (1916), registered by Donga, is conventionally called the first recorded samba.

Styles

Samba schools (escolas de samba) — beginning with Deixa Falar (1928) — organised neighbourhood ensembles whose hundreds of drummers (baterias) play surdo, caixa, tamborim and agogô in Carnival parades, formalised at the Sambadrome from 1984. Other forms include samba-canção (melodic, romantic), samba de roda of Bahia (recognised by UNESCO in 2005), partido-alto, pagode, samba-rock and samba-reggae. Bossa nova emerged in the late 1950s as a quieter, harmonically sophisticated offshoot.

Carnival

At Carnival, Rio's top samba schools parade through the Sambadrome with thousands of costumed dancers, floats and a theme song (samba-enredo), judged competitively. Samba is celebrated on Brazil's National Samba Day, 2 December.
