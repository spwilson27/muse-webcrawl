Sailing ship

PropulsionWind on sails
EraAntiquity to early 20th century (commercial peak 19th century)
Typical rigsFull-rigged ship, barque, brig, schooner

A sailing ship is a sea-going vessel that uses sails mounted on masts to harness the power of the wind. Sailing ships carried world trade, navies and migration from antiquity until steam propulsion displaced them in the late nineteenth century.

Development

Early sailing craft included Egyptian and Phoenician ships, Chinese junks and the dhows of the Indian Ocean. European square-rigged ships of the Age of Sail — carracks, galleons and ships of the line — gave way to the fast clippers of the 1800s and finally to iron- and steel-hulled windjammers on bulk trades.

Decline and survival

Steamships, more reliable and eventually cheaper, took over passenger and cargo routes. A few commercial sailing vessels operated into the twentieth century. Today sailing ships survive as naval training ships, museum vessels, tall-ship festival participants and cruise vessels.
