Cyril Ramaphosa

Matamela Cyril Ramaphosa, fifth president of post-apartheid South Africa.

Matamela Cyril Ramaphosa (born 17 November 1952) is a South African businessman and politician who has served as the fifth president of South Africa since 15 February 2018. A former anti-apartheid activist, trade union leader and negotiator of the country's democratic transition, he is also president of the African National Congress (ANC).

Early life and union career

Ramaphosa was born in Soweto to Venda parents and studied law at the University of the North and later the University of South Africa. Detained twice in the 1970s for anti-apartheid organising, he came to prominence as founding general secretary of the National Union of Mineworkers (NUM) in 1982, building it into one of the most powerful unions in the country and leading the landmark 1987 mineworkers' strike. He was a senior figure in the United Democratic Front and the internal resistance that made South Africa ungovernable in the late 1980s.

Negotiations and business career

After the ANC was unbanned in 1990, Ramaphosa became its secretary-general and chief negotiator in the talks that ended apartheid, facing Roelf Meyer of the National Party government. He chaired the Constitutional Assembly that produced the 1996 constitution. Passed over as Mandela's deputy in favour of Thabo Mbeki, he left politics for business in 1996, building the Shanduka Group and large stakes in mining, telecoms and fast food; he served on the boards of Lonmin and MTN. His business career drew controversy after the 2012 Marikana massacre, when emails he sent as a Lonmin director urging action against striking miners were publicly criticised, though the Farlam Commission did not find him responsible for the killings.

Presidency

Ramaphosa returned to politics as ANC deputy president in 2012 and became deputy president of the country under Jacob Zuma in 2014. Elected ANC president narrowly in December 2017, he became president of South Africa when Zuma resigned in February 2018. His administration promised a "new dawn" of anti-corruption reform and economic renewal, confronted the COVID-19 pandemic, electricity shortages at Eskom, and the July 2021 unrest. He won a full term in 2019; after the ANC lost its parliamentary majority in the 2024 general election, he formed a government of national unity with the Democratic Alliance and other parties and was re-elected president by the National Assembly.
