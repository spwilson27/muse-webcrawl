Lombardy (Italian: Lombardia) is one of the twenty administrative regions of Italy, in the northwest of the country, with an area of about 23,800 square kilometres. It is the most populous region of Italy, with roughly ten million inhabitants — a sixth of the country's population — and its capital and largest city is Milan.

Geography

The region stretches from the Alps and Prealps in the north — including the lakes Como, Garda, Maggiore, and Iseo — across the fertile Po Valley plain to the Po River in the south. Its provinces include Bergamo, Brescia, Como, Cremona, Lecco, Lodi, Mantua, Monza and Brianza, Pavia, Sondrio, and Varese.

History and economy

Lombardy takes its name from the Lombards, the Germanic people who ruled much of Italy after 568 AD. In the Middle Ages its cities were among the centres of the Lombard League that resisted the Holy Roman Emperors. Today Lombardy is Italy's economic engine, a hub of industry, finance, fashion, and agriculture, generating about a fifth of national GDP.
