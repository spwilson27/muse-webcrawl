Mount Meru (Tanzania)

Mount Meru is a dormant stratovolcano in Arusha Region, northern Tanzania, about 70 kilometres west of Mount Kilimanjaro. At 4,562.13 metres it is the second-highest mountain in Tanzania and the fifth-highest in Africa.

The mountain lies entirely within Arusha National Park and dominates the skyline of the city of Arusha.

Geology and eruption history

Mount Meru began forming through volcanic activity in the East African Rift roughly two million years ago. A major eruption about 7,800 years ago collapsed the eastern flank, creating the distinctive horseshoe-shaped summit crater; a secondary ash cone rises inside the breach. The last significant eruption was a minor one in 1910, though minor ash emission was reported later in the century.

Climbing and wildlife

The standard climb follows the Momella route on the northern side, usually over three to four days, from Momella Gate through fig-tree forest to Miriakamba and Saddle huts, and on to Socialist Peak (4,562 m). The route is frequently used to acclimatise for Kilimanjaro.

The lower slopes support rich wildlife, including giraffes, buffalo, elephants, colobus monkeys and flamingos around the Momella lakes, and armed rangers accompany hikers because of the animals.
