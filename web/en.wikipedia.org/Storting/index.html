The Storting is the supreme legislature of Norway, established by the Constitution of 1814. It consists of 169 members elected for four-year terms by proportional representation from 19 constituencies. Unlike most parliaments, the Storting cannot be dissolved early; it serves its full fixed term.

Organisation

Members sit in twelve standing committees covering areas such as finance, foreign affairs and defence, and justice. The Storting's presidium is led by its President. Historically the Storting divided itself into two chambers, the Lagting and Odelsting, for legislative purposes, but this arrangement was abolished with effect from 2009, leaving a formally unicameral body. It also elects members of the Norwegian Nobel Committee.
