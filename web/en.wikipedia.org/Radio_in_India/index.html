Radio in India

Radio broadcasting in India began in the early 1920s, with amateur radio clubs in Bombay and Calcutta making the first transmissions in 1923. Organised broadcasting started in 1927 with the Indian Broadcasting Company, and after its liquidation the colonial government took over, eventually establishing All India Radio (AIR) in 1936. Today, radio in India comprises the public broadcaster All India Radio (Akashvani), hundreds of private FM stations, and a growing community radio sector.

History

The Radio Club of Bombay made the first broadcast in India in June 1923, followed by the Calcutta Radio Club in November of the same year. The Indian Broadcasting Company (IBC) began regular services from Bombay and Calcutta in July 1927 but went into liquidation in 1930. The government then set up the Indian State Broadcasting Service, renamed All India Radio on 8 June 1936. After independence in 1947, AIR expanded rapidly, and was renamed Akashvani in 1957. Vividh Bharati, a popular entertainment service, launched in 1957 and became hugely successful with film music programming.

All India Radio

All India Radio, a division of Prasar Bharati, is one of the largest radio networks in the world, operating hundreds of broadcasting centres covering nearly the entire population of India. It broadcasts in numerous languages and dialects, offering news, music, drama, sports commentary and educational programming. Its external service broadcasts to audiences abroad in several foreign languages.

Private FM radio

Private FM broadcasting was opened in phases beginning in 1999–2000. Phase I auctions brought stations such as Radio Mirchi, Radio City and Red FM to major metros. Subsequent phases (II in 2005 and III from 2015) extended FM to hundreds of smaller cities. Private FM stations in India are generally not permitted to broadcast their own news, carrying only AIR news bulletins under certain arrangements.

Community radio

Community radio licensing began in 2002 for educational institutions and was extended in 2006 to non-profit organisations, agricultural universities and NGOs. Hundreds of community stations now serve local audiences with programming in regional languages on agriculture, health and local culture.
