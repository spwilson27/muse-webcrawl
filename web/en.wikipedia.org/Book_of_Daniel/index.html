The Book of Daniel is a biblical apocalypse set in the 6th century BCE Babylonian exile, following Daniel, a young Israelite taken to Babylon, who serves successive kings while receiving visions of the future of empires. In the Hebrew Bible it sits among the Writings (Ketuvim); in Christian Bibles it is counted among the Major Prophets.

The book divides into two halves: court tales (chapters 1–6) — the fiery furnace, Nebuchadnezzar's dreams and madness, Belshazzar's feast with the writing on the wall, and Daniel in the lions' den — and apocalyptic visions (chapters 7–12), including the four beasts, the ram and the goat, the seventy weeks, and a detailed prophecy of wars between the "king of the North" and the "king of the South".

Composition

Most modern scholars date the visions to the Maccabean crisis of the 160s BCE, with the book reaching its final form around 164 BCE; the court tales may be older. The text switches between Hebrew and Imperial Aramaic. Catholic and Orthodox Bibles include the deuterocanonical additions: the Prayer of Azariah and Song of the Three Holy Children, Susanna, and Bel and the Dragon.

Influence

Daniel's "Son of Man" vision and its resurrection teaching profoundly influenced Second Temple Judaism and early Christianity, and its statue of four kingdoms has been reinterpreted by interpreters for two millennia.
