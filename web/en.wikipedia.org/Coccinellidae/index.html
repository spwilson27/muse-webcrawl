Coccinellidae

Kingdom: Animalia

Phylum: Arthropoda

Class: Insecta

Order: Coleoptera

Family: Coccinellidae

Coccinellidae is a widespread family of small beetles, commonly known as ladybugs in North America and ladybirds in the United Kingdom and other English-speaking countries. The family includes more than 6,000 described species worldwide. They range in size from about 0.8 to 18 mm (0.03 to 0.71 in).

Description

Ladybirds typically have a hemispherical or oval body shape with short legs and antennae. Many species are brightly coloured in red, orange, or yellow with black spots — a warning coloration (aposematism) advertising their distasteful defensive chemicals, which include alkaloids that they can exude from leg joints when threatened. Other species are black, brown, or patterned differently.

Ecology

Most coccinellids are beneficial predators of aphids, scale insects, mealybugs, and other plant pests, and both larvae and adults feed voraciously, making the family important in biological pest control in agriculture and gardens. A minority of species, such as those in the subfamily Epilachninae, feed on plants and can themselves be pests. The harlequin ladybird (Harmonia axyridis), introduced widely for pest control, has become an invasive species in many regions.

Cultural significance

Ladybirds are widely regarded as lucky in folklore and are among the most popular and beloved of insects, featuring in nursery rhymes such as "Ladybird, ladybird, fly away home".
