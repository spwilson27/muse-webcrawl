Nalanda

Nalanda Mahavihara
Location: Bihar, India
Founded: 5th century CE (Gupta era)
Flourished: c. 5th–12th century CE
Type: Buddhist monastic university
Status: Ruins; UNESCO World Heritage Site (2016)

Nalanda was a renowned Buddhist mahavihara (great monastery) and center of learning in ancient Magadha, in present-day Bihar, India. Operating from roughly the 5th century CE until its destruction around 1200, it is often described as one of the world's first residential universities, drawing thousands of students and teachers from across Asia.

History

Nalanda was patronized by the Gupta emperors, by Harsha of Kannauj, and later by the Pala dynasty. Chinese pilgrims Xuanzang and Yijing studied there in the 7th century and left detailed accounts of its rigorous curriculum, entrance examinations, and vast library, the Dharmaganja, said to occupy three great buildings. Subjects included the Buddhist canon, logic, grammar, medicine, astronomy, and the Vedas.

Decline and legacy

The complex was sacked around 1193–1200 during the campaigns of Bakhtiyar Khalji's forces; Tibetan sources describe the burning of its libraries. Excavations by the Archaeological Survey of India revealed rows of monasteries and temples. The site was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2016, and a new Nalanda University, established by an Act of Parliament with support from East Asian Summit countries, opened near the ruins in 2014.
