Jacques Lemercier

Jacques Lemercier
Born: c. 1585, Pontoise, France
Died: 13 January 1654, Paris
Occupation: Architect

Jacques Lemercier (c. 1585 – 13 January 1654) was a French architect and engineer, one of the founders of French classical architecture and, with Salomon de Brosse and François Mansart, one of the three great architects of the reign of Louis XIII.

Life and career

Born into a family of masons and builders, Lemercier studied in Rome from about 1607 to 1612, absorbing the lessons of Italian Baroque and Renaissance architecture. On his return he came to the attention of Cardinal Richelieu, becoming his favoured architect. He was appointed first architect to King Louis XIII and was responsible for the royal works at the Louvre and elsewhere.

Major works

His masterpiece is the chapel of the Sorbonne in Paris (begun 1635), whose dome was the first of the great domes of Paris and which houses Richelieu's tomb. For Richelieu he designed the Palais Cardinal (later the Palais Royal) and planned the entire new town of Richelieu in the Loire valley, one of the earliest regularly planned towns in France. He also began the church of Saint-Eustache in its classical form, worked on the Louvre's Pavillon de l'Horloge and western extension, and designed the church of Val-de-Grâce project initially, though it was completed by others.

Style

Lemercier's style combined Roman Baroque motifs with French restraint, using superimposed classical orders and clear geometry that set the pattern for the French classical tradition continued by Louis Le Vau and others.
