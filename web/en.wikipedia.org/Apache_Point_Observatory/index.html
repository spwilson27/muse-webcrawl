Apache Point Observatory

Location: Sunspot, New Mexico, United States
Altitude: 2,788 m (9,147 ft)
Established: 1990s

The Apache Point Observatory (APO) is an astronomical observatory located in the Sacramento Mountains in New Mexico, United States, near the community of Sunspot and the National Solar Observatory. It sits at an elevation of about 2,788 metres (9,147 ft), where dry, clear skies make it well suited to optical and infrared astronomy.

Telescopes

The observatory is best known as the home of the 2.5-metre telescope used by the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS), which has mapped large portions of the sky and produced catalogues of hundreds of millions of celestial objects. The site also hosts a 3.5-metre telescope operated by the Astrophysical Research Consortium, a 1.0-metre telescope for New Mexico State University, and the 0.5-metre ARCSAT telescope used for photometric monitoring.

Laser ranging

APO is the site of the Apache Point Observatory Lunar Laser-ranging Operation (APOLLO), which uses the 3.5-metre telescope to bounce laser pulses off retroreflectors left on the Moon by the Apollo missions. The project measures the Earth–Moon distance with millimetre precision and tests aspects of general relativity.
