JumpSTART triage

JumpSTART (Jump Simple Triage And Rapid Treatment) is a mass-casualty triage system designed specifically for infants and children. It was developed in the mid-1990s by Dr. Lou Romig, a paediatric emergency physician, at Jackson Memorial Hospital in Miami, Florida, as a modification of the adult START system, whose physiological criteria and assumptions fit children poorly.

How it differs from START

Children differ from adults in ways that matter in triage: apnoea in children is more often a primary respiratory event that is potentially reversible (whereas in adults it usually follows cardiac arrest), and normal respiratory rates vary widely by age. JumpSTART therefore (1) applies to children roughly between 1 and 8 years of age, or those who appear to be children; (2) uses a respiratory rate band of 15–45 breaths per minute rather than the single adult threshold of 30; and (3) allows rescue breathing during primary triage: an apnoeic child with a pulse receives five rescue breaths, and if breathing resumes the child is tagged immediate (red). Perfusion is assessed by palpable peripheral pulse rather than capillary refill, and the mental-status check uses the AVPU scale (alert, responds to voice, responds to pain, unresponsive).

Categories

As in START, patients are sorted within about a minute each into four colour-coded categories: green (minor — the "walking wounded," including children carried in who meet minor criteria), yellow (delayed), red (immediate), and black/grey (deceased or expectant — apnoeic children who fail to breathe after five rescue breaths and children with no pulse). Infants under one year are assessed with the same algorithm, with extra caution because they cannot ambulate.

Use

JumpSTART is widely taught in the United States and internationally alongside START in disaster and emergency-medical-services training. Together they are sometimes promoted as a paired system: "START for adults, JumpSTART for kids."
