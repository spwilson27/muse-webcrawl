The Habsburg monarchy, also known as the Habsburg Empire, was the collection of empires, kingdoms, duchies, counties and other polities that were ruled by the House of Habsburg. From the 18th century it is also referred to as the Austrian monarchy or simply Austria.

The history of the Habsburg monarchy can be traced to Rudolf I of Germany, who in 1282 invested his sons with the duchies of Austria and Styria. Through advantageous marriages — famously summarized by the saying that others wage war while fortunate Austria marries — the dynasty acquired the crowns of Bohemia and Hungary (1526), the Burgundian Netherlands, and, through a separate Spanish line, Spain and its empire.

Development

The Austrian Habsburgs, frequently Holy Roman Emperors, defended Central Europe against Ottoman expansion, notably at the sieges of Vienna (1529 and 1683), and contended with France and Prussia for predominance in Europe. Under Maria Theresa and Joseph II in the 18th century the monarchy was centralized and modernized through administrative, military and educational reforms. In 1804, in response to Napoleon, Francis II proclaimed the Austrian Empire, and in 1806 the Holy Roman Empire was dissolved.

Dissolution

The Compromise of 1867 transformed the empire into the dual monarchy of Austria-Hungary. Defeat in the First World War brought dissolution in 1918: Emperor Charles I renounced participation in state affairs in November 1918, and the Habsburg lands fragmented into the successor states of Austria, Hungary, Czechoslovakia, and parts of Poland, Yugoslavia, Romania and Italy.
