UGM-27 Polaris

The UGM-27 Polaris missile was a two-stage, solid-fuel, nuclear-armed submarine-launched ballistic missile (SLBM) developed by the United States Navy during the Cold War. First launched from the submarine USS George Washington on July 20, 1960 — while submerged — Polaris gave the United States a survivable sea-based nuclear deterrent that formed one leg of the nuclear triad.

Development

The program was developed under the Navy's Special Projects Office, led by Rear Admiral William F. Raborn, using the PERT management method. Polaris was a remarkable feat of miniaturization: advances in warhead design, solid propellants, and inertial guidance allowed a ballistic missile small enough to fit inside a submarine hull. The first deterrent patrol began in November 1960.

Variants and service

Three main variants were produced: the A-1, with a range of about 1,000 nautical miles (1,900 km); the A-2, at about 1,500 nautical miles; and the A-3, at about 2,500 nautical miles, carrying multiple reentry bodies. Forty-one Polaris submarines — the "41 for Freedom" — were built. The United States replaced Polaris with the longer-range Poseidon missile in the 1970s. Under the 1962 Nassau Agreement, the United Kingdom adopted Polaris for its Resolution-class submarines, carrying it (later with the Chevaline upgrade) from 1968 until it was replaced by Trident in the 1990s.
