The Crown

For the Netflix historical drama series, see The Crown (TV series). For other uses, see Crown (disambiguation).


The Imperial State Crown, part of the Crown Jewels of the United Kingdom.

The Crown is the legal embodiment of the state in various, particularly Commonwealth, countries. Developed in the United Kingdom, the concept of the Crown as a corporation sole — a legal person distinct from the physical person of the monarch — allows the state to own property, enter contracts, and exercise authority in the monarch's name in perpetuity, regardless of the individual reigning. In modern constitutional terms, references to "the Crown" in legislation, litigation (for example, The Crown v. … in criminal prosecutions), landholding ("Crown land"), and public employment are references to the state acting through its executive government.

Development of the concept

The distinction between the monarch's personal and public capacities has roots in medieval English law and was articulated in the Tudor period, notably in the doctrine of the "King's two bodies" discussed by the jurist Edmund Plowden: the body natural, subject to age and death, and the body politic, which never dies. Over the centuries the Crown's functions were progressively exercised by ministers responsible to Parliament, a process consolidated after the Glorious Revolution of 1688 and the Bill of Rights 1689. The term came to stand for the whole machinery of executive government.

The Crown in Commonwealth realms

With the development of the British Empire into the Commonwealth, the Crown became "divisible": legally distinct Crowns exist in right of each realm — the Crown in right of the United Kingdom, of Canada, of Australia, and so on, as well as in right of provinces and states within federal realms — while remaining united in the person of the monarch. The Crown in each jurisdiction is advised by that jurisdiction's ministers. Crown corporations, Crown prosecutors, and Crown lands are familiar institutional expressions of the concept.

Related senses

"The Crown" is also used informally to refer to the monarchy itself or to the reigning sovereign, and in several European monarchies — historically, Spain, and in a different legal tradition, other kingdoms — the institution of the Crown (la Corona) is defined by constitution as the symbol of the state's unity and permanence. Physical crowns and regalia, such as the Crown Jewels of the United Kingdom, are held in right of the Crown rather than as the monarch's private property.
