Operation Rah-e-Nijat

Operation Rah-e-Nijat ("Path to Salvation")
Date: 16 June 2009 – 12 December 2009 (ground offensive from 17 October)
Location: South Waziristan, FATA, Pakistan
Result: Pakistani victory; TTP stronghold captured

The Operation Rah-e-Nijat ("Path to Salvation") was a strategic offensive military operation by the unified command of Pakistan Armed Forces against the Tehrik-i-Taliban Pakistan (TTP) and their allied Islamist extremist militias in South Waziristan, part of the Federally Administered Tribal Areas of Pakistan. It followed the successful Operation Rah-e-Rast in Swat, and came after months of blockade and preparatory air and artillery strikes.

The offensive

The ground offensive began on 17 October 2009, with some 28,000–30,000 soldiers advancing from three directions against an estimated 10,000 Taliban militants, including foreign fighters. Major towns such as Kotkai, Sararogha, Makeen, and Kaniguram — strongholds associated with the Mehsud network and leaders including Baitullah Mehsud (killed in a drone strike that August) and Hakimullah Mehsud — were captured after heavy fighting.

Aftermath

By December 2009 the Pakistan Army declared the main objectives secured, having killed several hundred militants at the cost of over a hundred soldiers. The operation displaced hundreds of thousands of civilians, whose return and the region's reconstruction remained challenges in the following years. Many TTP fighters escaped into North Waziristan and Afghanistan, where the insurgency continued.
