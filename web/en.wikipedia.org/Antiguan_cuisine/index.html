Antiguan cuisine

Fungee and pepperpot, the national dish

Antiguan cuisine refers to the culinary traditions of the Caribbean islands of Antigua and Barbuda. It blends Amerindian, African, British and broader West Indian influences and makes extensive use of local seafood, root vegetables and tropical produce.

National dish

The national dish is fungee and pepperpot. Fungee is a cornmeal-based pudding similar to polenta, traditionally turned with a "fungee stick" until firm; pepperpot is a rich stew of meats (often salt beef, pig snout or salted pork) and vegetables such as spinach, okra, squash and eggplant, quite unlike the pepperpot of Guyana. The dish reflects the resourceful cooking of enslaved Africans who combined available ingredients.

Other dishes

Other local favourites include ducana (a sweet potato and coconut dumpling, served with saltfish), seasoned rice, goat water (a spicy goat stew), roti, jerk chicken, and "rice and peas". Seafood — lobster, conch, red snapper and wahoo — is abundant. Antigua is also known for its black pineapple, reputed to be among the sweetest in the world, and for English Harbour rum.

Beverages

Local drinks include mauby, tamarind and sorrel drinks, ginger beer, seamoss, and rum punches. Wadadli is the local lager.
