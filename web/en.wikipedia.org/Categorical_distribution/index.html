Categorical distribution

In probability theory and statistics, a categorical distribution is a discrete probability distribution that describes the possible results of a random variable that can take on one of K possible categories, with the probability of each category separately specified.

Definition

A categorical distribution is specified by a vector of probabilities p = (p1, …, pK) with each pi ≥ 0 and the pi summing to 1. A single draw yields category i with probability pi. The categorical distribution generalises the Bernoulli distribution (which is the case K = 2) and is the single-trial counterpart of the multinomial distribution.

Bayesian statistics

The Dirichlet distribution is the conjugate prior of the categorical distribution, making it fundamental in Bayesian models such as latent Dirichlet allocation and Bayesian networks with discrete variables. In machine learning, classifier outputs over classes are routinely modelled as categorical, and the cross-entropy loss corresponds to maximum likelihood under this distribution.
