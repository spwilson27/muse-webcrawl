Bounce music

Origin: New Orleans, Louisiana
Emerged: Late 1980s–early 1990s
Genre: Hip hop
hallmarks: "Triggerman" beat, call-and-response, twerking culture

Bounce music is a style of hip hop that originated in New Orleans, Louisiana, characterised by energetic, party-oriented call-and-response lyrics, heavy use of the "Triggerman" beat sample, and chants drawn from Mardi Gras Indian traditions. It is built for dancing, most famously twerking.

History

Bounce developed in New Orleans housing projects and clubs in the late 1980s and early 1990s, with pioneers such as DJ Jimi, Juvenile, Magnolia Shorty, Big Freedia and Katey Red. The genre's rhythmic backbone, the Triggerman beat, derives from "Drag Rap" by the Showboys (1986), layered with the break from Cameron Paul's "Brown Beats".

Reach

Long a local phenomenon sustained by block parties and second lines, bounce influenced mainstream pop and hip hop in the 2010s: Big Freedia became its ambassador, and bounce elements surfaced in hits by artists such as Beyoncé and Drake.
