The Volkswagen Group, legally Volkswagen Aktiengesellschaft (abbreviated VW AG), is a German multinational automotive manufacturer headquartered in Wolfsburg, Lower Saxony. It is one of the world's largest carmakers by sales and Europe's largest, selling passenger and commercial vehicles under brands including Volkswagen, Audi, Porsche, Škoda, SEAT, Cupra, Bentley, Lamborghini, Ducati, MAN, Scania and Navistar. The company operates manufacturing plants in more than 25 countries and sells vehicles in over 150.

History

Founding and the Beetle

Volkswagen was founded on 28 May 1937 by the German Labour Front, the Nazi labour organisation, to build a "people's car" (Volkswagen) designed by Ferdinand Porsche. Production was interrupted by the Second World War, during which the Wolfsburg plant produced military vehicles, and resumed under British military supervision after 1945 under Major Ivan Hirst. The Volkswagen Beetle became a global icon of the postwar era, eventually surpassing 21 million units and becoming the best-selling single-platform car of all time.

Expansion and brand acquisitions

The group expanded through acquisition: Auto Union (the predecessor of today's Audi) in 1964, SEAT in 1986–1990, Škoda in 1991–2000, and Bentley, Bugatti and Lamborghini in 1998. In 2009–2012 Volkswagen acquired a controlling stake in Porsche's automobile business after a dramatic takeover battle in which Porsche had attempted to acquire Volkswagen itself. Commercial vehicles were consolidated with majority stakes in MAN and Scania.

Emissions scandal

In September 2015 the United States Environmental Protection Agency found that Volkswagen had installed "defeat device" software in millions of diesel vehicles to cheat emissions tests. The scandal, known as Dieselgate, cost the company more than €35 billion in fines, settlements and refits, led to criminal charges against executives, and accelerated the group's strategic pivot towards electric vehicles.

Brands and products

The group's passenger-car brands span the mass market (Volkswagen, Škoda, SEAT and Cupra), the premium segment (Audi, Volkswagen Commercial Vehicles) and the luxury and sports segments (Porsche, Bentley, Lamborghini and the formerly held Bugatti, transferred in 2021 to a joint venture with Rimac). Motorcycles are sold under the Ducati name, and heavy trucks and buses under MAN, Scania and Navistar. China is the group's largest single market, operated largely through joint ventures with FAW and SAIC.

Electric transition

Since 2019 the group has rolled out dedicated electric-vehicle architectures — the MEB platform for volume brands and PPE for premium models — under models such as the Volkswagen ID. series and Audi e-tron range. Volkswagen has committed tens of billions of euros to electrification, battery plants through its PowerCo subsidiary, and software development through Cariad.

Corporate structure

Volkswagen AG is listed on the Frankfurt Stock Exchange. Its voting capital is controlled by Porsche SE, the holding company of the Porsche–Piëch families, with the State of Lower Saxony and Qatar Holding holding major stakes; Lower Saxony retains a special blocking right under the "Volkswagen Law".
