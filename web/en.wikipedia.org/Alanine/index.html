Alanine

Abbreviations: Ala, A

Chemical formula: C3H7NO2

Codons: GCU, GCC, GCA, GCG

Essential: No (non-essential in humans)

Alanine (symbol Ala or A) is an α-amino acid used in the biosynthesis of proteins. It contains an amino group, a carboxylic acid group, and a methyl side chain, making it a nonpolar, aliphatic amino acid. It is encoded by the codons GCU, GCC, GCA, and GCG, and is among the most common amino acids in proteins.

Metabolism

Alanine is non-essential in humans and is readily synthesized from pyruvate by transamination. It plays a central role in the glucose–alanine cycle (Cahill cycle), in which muscle exports nitrogen to the liver as alanine; the liver converts the carbon skeleton to glucose, which returns to muscle, while the nitrogen enters the urea cycle. Alanine aminotransferase (ALT) is a clinically important enzyme whose elevation in blood indicates liver damage.

Properties and uses

L-Alanine has a slightly sweet taste and is used as a food additive and in dietary supplements. The related compound β-alanine, with the amino group on the β-carbon, is a component of the dipeptide carnosine and of pantothenic acid (vitamin B5), and is popular as a sports supplement. In protein secondary structure, alanine strongly favours the alpha helix conformation, and alanine scanning is a mutagenesis technique used to probe the function of individual residues.
