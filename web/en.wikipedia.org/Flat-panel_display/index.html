Flat-panel display

A flat-panel display (FPD) is an electronic display device used to present video and images in a thin, lightweight panel, in contrast to the deep cathode-ray tube (CRT) displays it replaced. Flat panels are used in televisions, computer monitors, laptops, smartphones, tablets, digital signage, vehicle dashboards and instrument panels.

Technologies

Liquid-crystal displays (LCDs) dominate the market. An LCD panel modulates light from a backlight — now almost always LEDs — through a layer of liquid crystals between polarising filters; thin-film transistor (TFT) active-matrix backplanes switch each pixel. Variants include TN, IPS and VA panels, and quantum-dot enhancement films (marketed as QLED) that improve colour.

OLED displays use organic light-emitting diodes in which each pixel emits its own light, enabling very thin, flexible panels with true blacks and high contrast; AMOLED is the common active-matrix form used in phones and televisions.

Other technologies include plasma display panels (once popular for large televisions, now discontinued), microLED (inorganic self-emissive, emerging in large displays), electronic paper (e-ink, used in e-readers), and electroluminescent and field-emission displays for niche applications.

Characteristics

Flat panels are specified by diagonal size, resolution (pixel count), refresh rate, brightness in nits, contrast ratio, colour gamut and viewing angle. Because they are digital devices with a fixed native resolution, images at other resolutions must be scaled. Modern panels commonly support high dynamic range (HDR) standards such as HDR10 and Dolby Vision, and resolutions from HD through 4K and 8K in television.

History

Early flat-panel research dates to the 1960s, with plasma panels invented in 1964 at the University of Illinois and practical LCDs developed in the late 1960s and 1970s. LCDs first appeared in calculators and watches, then in laptop screens in the 1980s–90s. Around the mid-2000s, LCD televisions and monitors outsold CRTs worldwide, completing the transition to flat-panel displays in consumer electronics.
