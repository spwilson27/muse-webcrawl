Emperor Shun

Shun, traditionally regarded as a legendary Chinese sovereign, was one of the Three Sovereigns and Five Emperors of ancient China. Tradition dates his reign to the 23rd–22nd centuries BC, after Emperor Yao, whom he succeeded because of his virtue and filial piety rather than heredity.

Legend

Stories describe Shun as a potter and farmer who remained devoted despite mistreatment by his father, stepmother, and half-brother Xiang. Yao tested him, married his two daughters to him, and passed the throne to him. Shun appointed Yu the Great to control flooding; Yu later succeeded Shun, founding the pattern of abdication to the worthy that Confucian tradition idealized.

Legacy

Shun is a paragon of filial piety in Confucian texts such as the Classic of History and Mencius. Historic scholars debate how much, if any, historical kernel lies behind the legend.
