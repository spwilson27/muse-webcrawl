Michelangelo di Lodovico Buonarroti Simoni (6 March 1475 – 18 February 1564), known mononymously as Michelangelo, was an Italian sculptor, painter, architect, and poet of the High Renaissance. Born in the Republic of Florence, his work was inspired by models from classical antiquity and had a lasting influence on Western art. Michelangelo's creative abilities and mastery in a range of artistic arenas define him as an archetypal Renaissance man, along with his rival and elder contemporary, Leonardo da Vinci. He is widely considered one of the greatest artists of all time.

Early life

Michelangelo was born in Caprese, in Tuscany, where his father was a judicial administrator. The family returned to Florence while he was still an infant; after his mother's death in 1481 he was raised in Settignano by a stonecutter's family, an upbringing he later credited with his affinity for the chisel. Apprenticed at thirteen to the painter Domenico Ghirlandaio, he soon attracted the patronage of Lorenzo de' Medici, in whose household he studied classical sculpture under Bertoldo di Giovanni.

Sculpture: Pietà and David

In Rome, Michelangelo carved the Pietà (1498–1499) for St Peter's Basilica before he was twenty-five; it remains the only work he ever signed. Returning to Florence, he produced the colossal marble David (1501–1504) from a block other sculptors had abandoned. Standing 5.17 metres (17 ft) tall, the David became a symbol of Florentine republican virtue and is regarded as a supreme masterpiece of Renaissance sculpture.

Sistine Chapel

Summoned to Rome by Pope Julius II, Michelangelo was commissioned, reluctantly, to paint the ceiling of the Sistine Chapel (1508–1512). The vast fresco, covering some 500 square metres with over 300 figures, depicts nine scenes from Genesis — most famously The Creation of Adam — framed by prophets, sibyls, and ignudi. Two decades later, Pope Paul III commissioned The Last Judgment (1536–1541) on the chapel's altar wall, a turbulent vision that influenced the development of Mannerism.

Architecture and later work

In Florence Michelangelo designed the Medici Chapel and the Laurentian Library, whose vestibule staircase anticipated Mannerist architecture. In Rome he redesigned the Capitoline Hill and, from 1546, served as architect of St Peter's Basilica, completing its great dome — the model for domes across the Western world — though it was finished after his death. He also wrote more than 300 sonnets and madrigals, published posthumously, and continued working nearly until his death in Rome at the age of 88, days short of his 89th birthday. His body was returned to Florence and buried in the Basilica of Santa Croce.
