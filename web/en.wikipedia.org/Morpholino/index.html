Morpholino

A Morpholino, also known as a morpholino oligomer and as a phosphorodiamidate morpholino oligomer (PMO), is a type of oligomer molecule (colloquially, an oligonucleotide) used in molecular biology to modify gene expression. Its molecular structure contains a nucleic acid base attached to a morpholine ring, and the rings are linked through phosphorodiamidate groups instead of the phosphate groups found in natural nucleic acids. Because of this unusual backbone, morpholinos are not recognized as substrates by nucleases and are therefore not readily degraded in cells.

Morpholinos are commonly used as antisense tools to block translation of messenger RNA or to prevent splicing of pre-mRNA. They are widely used in developmental biology, for example in zebrafish and Xenopus embryos, where they are injected into eggs or early embryos to knock down the function of specific genes.

Structure

Morpholino oligos are usually about 25 bases in length. The backbone consists of methylenemorpholine rings linked by non-ionic phosphorodiamidate groups. Unlike DNA or RNA, morpholinos are uncharged, which affects how they interact with proteins and how they must be delivered into cells.

Function

A morpholino binds to a complementary sequence of RNA via standard nucleic acid base pairing. Because the binding does not rely on degradation of the target, morpholinos act by steric blocking: a translation-blocking morpholino bound near the start codon prevents the ribosome from initiating protein synthesis, while a splice-blocking morpholino bound at an exon–intron junction redirects splicing of the pre-mRNA.

History and delivery

Morpholinos were developed by James Summerton and colleagues at Gene Tools. Delivery into cultured cells can be challenging because the neutral backbone does not cross cell membranes readily; methods include microinjection, electroporation, and the use of special delivery reagents. Derivatives such as peptide-conjugated PMOs (PPMOs) have improved uptake and have been investigated as therapeutics for diseases such as Duchenne muscular dystrophy.
