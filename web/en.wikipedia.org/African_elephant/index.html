African elephant

GenusLoxodonta
SpeciesAfrican bush elephant (L. africana); African forest elephant (L. cyclotis)
StatusEndangered / Critically Endangered (IUCN)

African elephants are members of the genus Loxodonta, comprising two living species: the African bush elephant and the African forest elephant. They are the largest living terrestrial animals, with bulls standing up to about 3.3 metres (11 ft) at the shoulder and weighing up to 6 tonnes. African elephants are distinguished from Asian elephants by their larger ears—said to resemble the shape of Africa—wrinkled skin, and two finger-like projections on the trunk.

Behaviour

Elephants live in matriarchal family herds, communicate through infrasound and seismic signals, and show complex social behaviour including apparent empathy and mourning. They are herbivores that consume up to 150 kg of vegetation a day and act as ecosystem engineers.

Conservation

Poaching for ivory and habitat loss have drastically reduced populations; the bush elephant is listed as Endangered and the forest elephant as Critically Endangered on the IUCN Red List. The international ivory trade has been banned under CITES since 1989.
