The presidency of Barack Obama began at noon EST on January 20, 2009, when Barack Obama was inaugurated as the 44th president of the United States, and ended on January 20, 2017. Obama, a Democrat from Illinois, took office following his victory over Republican nominee John McCain in the 2008 presidential election. Four years later, he won re-election over Mitt Romney in the 2012 election. He was succeeded by Republican Donald Trump, who won the 2016 election.

Obama's presidency took place during the Great Recession, and his first major act was signing the American Recovery and Reinvestment Act of 2009, a stimulus package intended to counter the economic downturn. His administration also passed the Dodd–Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act and the Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act (often called "Obamacare"), a landmark health care reform law.

Domestic policy

In foreign policy, Obama ordered the military operation that resulted in the death of Osama bin Laden in 2011, negotiated the Iran nuclear deal (the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action) in 2015, and normalized relations with Cuba. His administration oversaw the drawdown of American combat troops in Iraq and a temporary surge in Afghanistan. Domestically, Obama appointed Sonia Sotomayor and Elena Kagan to the Supreme Court, supported the legalization of same-sex marriage, which the Supreme Court affirmed in Obergefell v. Hodges (2015), and took executive action on immigration through the Deferred Action for Childhood Arrivals (DACA) program.

Legacy

Historians and political scientists generally rank Obama in the upper tier of American presidents. His presidency was notable as the first held by an African American, and his two terms reshaped American health care, financial regulation, and climate policy, including United States participation in the Paris Agreement on climate change.
