Gradec, Zagreb


St Mark's Church and square, heart of Gradec.

Gradec (pronounced [ɡrǎdet͡s]), also known as Grič, is the old Upper Town (Gornji Grad) of Zagreb, Croatia, and one of the two historical nuclei of the city, the other being the ecclesiastical settlement of Kaptol across the Medveščak stream. Gradec was proclaimed a free royal city by the Golden Bull of King Béla IV in 1242, after the Mongol invasion, and was fortified with walls and towers of which the Lotrščak Tower and the Stone Gate survive.

History

For centuries Gradec and Kaptol were rivals — occasionally violently, as in clashes on the bridge over the stream between them, remembered in the street name Krvavi most ("Bloody Bridge"). The two towns were formally united, together with surrounding settlements, into the single city of Zagreb in 1850 under Ban Josip Jelačić. St Mark's Square, dominated by the church of St Mark with its colourful tiled roof bearing the coats of arms of Zagreb and the Triune Kingdom, became the political centre of Croatia.

Today

Gradec's steep, gas-lit streets now house the Croatian Parliament (Sabor), the Government building (Banski dvori), the Constitutional Court, Zagreb City Assembly, museums and galleries including the Museum of Broken Relationships. The Lotrščak Tower's cannon has fired a noon shot daily since 1877, and the surviving Stone Gate chapel is a major Marian shrine. Together with Kaptol it forms the protected historic core of the capital.
