Catharine MacKinnon

Catharine A. MacKinnon
Born: October 7, 1946, Minneapolis, Minnesota
Occupation: Legal scholar, feminist theorist
Known for: Sexual Harassment of Working Women (1979); Toward a Feminist Theory of the State (1989)

Catharine Alice MacKinnon (born October 7, 1946) is an American radical feminist legal scholar, activist, and author. She is the Elizabeth A. Long Professor of Law at the University of Michigan Law School and has been a leading figure in feminist legal theory.

Work

MacKinnon's 1979 book Sexual Harassment of Working Women argued that sexual harassment in the workplace is a form of sex discrimination under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 — an argument adopted by the U.S. Supreme Court in Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson (1986). With Andrea Dworkin she drafted civil-rights ordinances treating pornography as sex discrimination, an approach struck down in Indianapolis by the courts but influential in Canada. She also worked on international law, representing Bosnian and Croatian survivors and contributing to the recognition of rape as an act of genocide in the Kadic v. Karadžić litigation.

Criticism and influence

Her dominance theory of gender inequality has been both highly influential and contested within feminism and legal scholarship, drawing responses from liberal and sex-positive feminists. MacKinnon's writings include Feminism Unmodified (1987) and Butterfly Politics (2017).
