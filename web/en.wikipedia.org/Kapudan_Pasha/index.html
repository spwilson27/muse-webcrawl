Kapudan Pasha

The Kapudan Pasha (Ottoman Turkish: قپودان پاشا, Kapudan Paşa; modern Turkish: Kaptan Paşa) was the grand admiral of the navy of the Ottoman Empire. The title, derived from the Italian capitano, was held by the commander-in-chief of the Ottoman fleet and ranked, from the 16th century, as a vizier of the highest grade, sitting in the Imperial Council (divan).

History

The office developed from the earlier title of derya bey ("bey of the sea") as the Ottoman navy grew under Mehmed II, conqueror of Constantinople. It reached its zenith under Hayreddin Barbarossa, who became Kapudan Pasha in 1534 and made the Ottomans the dominant naval power in the Mediterranean. Other notable holders include Piri Reis (briefly, as a senior admiral), Uluç Ali Reis, and Cezayirli Gazi Hasan Pasha, the reforming admiral of the late 18th century.

Duties and domain

The Kapudan Pasha commanded the imperial arsenal (Tersane-i Amire) at the Golden Horn and administered the maritime province of the Archipelago (Cezayir-i Bahr-i Sefid), which comprised the Aegean islands and coasts, Cyprus, and at times territories in North Africa. He led the fleet on its annual summer expedition and collected the taxes of his province in return.

Abolition

The office declined in importance during the 19th-century Tanzimat reforms, as naval administration passed to a Ministry of the Navy, and was finally abolished in 1867.
