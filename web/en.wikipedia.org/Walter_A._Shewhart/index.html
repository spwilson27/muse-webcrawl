Walter Andrew Shewhart (March 18, 1891 – March 11, 1967) was an American physicist, engineer, and statistician, sometimes called the father of statistical quality control and of the Shewhart cycle.

Career

Shewhart earned his bachelor's and master's degrees from the University of Illinois and a doctorate in physics from the University of California, Berkeley, in 1917. He joined Western Electric in 1918 and moved to Bell Telephone Laboratories when it was founded in 1925, remaining there until his retirement in 1956.

Contributions

On May 16, 1924, Shewhart drew the first control chart in a brief internal memorandum — the foundational tool of statistical process control, distinguishing variation due to assignable causes from variation inherent in a process. His ideas were set out in Economic Control of Quality of Manufactured Product (1931), regarded as a landmark in the philosophy of science as much as in manufacturing.

Shewhart also described the cycle of specification–production–inspection, which his protégé W. Edwards Deming adapted into the PDCA (plan-do-check-act) cycle, often called the Deming cycle. Shewhart's distinction between chance causes and assignable causes of variation underpins modern quality management and Six Sigma.
