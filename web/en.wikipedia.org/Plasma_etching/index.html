Plasma etching

Plasma etching is a form of plasma processing used in semiconductor manufacturing to remove material from a substrate, such as a silicon wafer, using a plasma of reactive gases. It is a key step in patterning the microscopic features of integrated circuits.

Process

A gas such as a fluorocarbon or chlorine compound is ionised in a vacuum chamber by a radio-frequency field. Reactive ions and radicals attack exposed areas of the wafer, while a photoresist mask protects the areas to be retained. Reactive-ion etching adds directional ion bombardment to produce near-vertical sidewalls.

Advantages

Compared with wet chemical etching, plasma etching gives finer, more anisotropic profiles needed for modern device geometries, though it can cause charging and radiation damage that must be controlled.
