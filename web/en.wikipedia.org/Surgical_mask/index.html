Surgical mask

A surgical mask, also known as a medical face mask, is a loose-fitting, disposable barrier worn over the mouth and nose, typically made of non-woven fabric (often polypropylene) with several layers. It is intended to protect the wearer from splashes and sprays and to reduce the spread of respiratory droplets from the wearer to others (source control).

History and design

Face coverings in surgery date from the late 19th century, following the development of germ theory; cloth masks were promoted by surgeons such as Jan Mikulicz-Radecki in 1897. Modern disposable masks, developed in the 1960s, typically consist of three layers: an outer fluid-repellent layer, a middle melt-blown filtration layer, and an inner absorbent layer, held by ear loops or ties and shaped by a nose wire. Standards such as ASTM F2100 grade masks by bacterial filtration efficiency and fluid resistance.

Use

Surgical masks are standard in operating rooms and patient care to protect sterile fields and reduce droplet transmission. During the COVID-19 pandemic they were widely adopted by the general public. They differ from respirators such as N95 masks, which seal tightly to the face and filter airborne particles; surgical masks fit loosely and are not designed to protect against fine aerosols entirely, though they reduce exposure.
