Molière

BornJean-Baptiste Poquelin, baptised 15 January 1622, Paris, France
Died17 February 1673 (aged 51), Paris
OccupationPlaywright, actor, stage director
Notable worksTartuffe, The Misanthrope, The School for Wives, The Imaginary Invalid

Jean-Baptiste Poquelin (1622–1673), known by his stage name Molière, was a French playwright, actor, and poet, widely regarded as one of the greatest writers in French literature and the master of comedy in Western theatre. His plays satirised the hypocrisies of 17th-century French society — religious pretence, social climbing, quack medicine, and misanthropy.

Life

Born into a prosperous Parisian family, Poquelin renounced a career in law and the family upholstery trade to found the Illustre Théâtre in 1643, adopting the name Molière. After years of touring the provinces, his troupe won the patronage of Louis XIV in 1658 and settled in Paris. Tartuffe was banned under pressure from religious authorities from 1664 to 1669, though the king privately supported him.

Works and death

His major comedies include The School for Wives (1662), Tartuffe (1664), Don Juan (1665), The Misanthrope (1666), The Miser (1668), The Bourgeois Gentleman (1670), and The Imaginary Invalid (1673). Molière collapsed on stage while playing the hypochondriac Argan in the last of these and died the same night.

Legacy

French is sometimes called "the language of Molière". His name is honoured by the Molière Awards, France's national theatre prizes, and the Comédie-Française remains known as the "House of Molière".
