Tuplet

A triplet: three notes played in the time of two

In music, a tuplet is any rhythm that involves a division of the beat into a different number of equal subdivisions from that usually permitted by the time signature — for example, three notes played in the time normally occupied by two. The most common tuplet is the triplet.

Types

  • Duplet — two notes in the time of three, common in compound time.
  • Triplet — three notes in the time of two (simple time) or four (compound contexts vary).
  • Quadruplet — four notes in the time of three.
  • Quintuplet — five notes in the time of four (or three).
  • Sextuplet — six notes in the time of four.
  • Septuplet — seven notes in the time of six or four.

Notation

Tuplets are marked with a number (or ratio, such as 3:2) placed above or below the group, often with a bracket or slur. In modern notation a ratio may be used to show precisely how many notes are to be played in the time of how many. Nested tuplets — tuplets within tuplets — occur in rhythmically complex music.

Use

Triplets are ubiquitous in many styles, from Classical music to jazz and blues shuffles. Complex tuplets are a hallmark of virtuoso piano music (Chopin, Liszt) and of 20th- and 21st-century composition, where polyrhythms built from simultaneous tuplets can create intricate rhythmic textures.
