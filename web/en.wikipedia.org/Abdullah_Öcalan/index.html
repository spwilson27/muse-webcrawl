Abdullah Öcalan

Born: 4 April 1949, Ömerli, Şanlıurfa, Turkey

Known for: Founding leader of the Kurdistan Workers' Party (PKK)

Abdullah Öcalan (born 4 April 1949), also referred to as Apo (short for Abdullah in Turkish; Kurdish for "uncle"), is a Kurdish political activist and the founding leader of the Kurdistan Workers' Party (PKK), an organisation that fought an armed insurgency against the Turkish state from 1984. He has been imprisoned on İmralı Island in Turkey since his capture in 1999.

Early life

Öcalan was born in Ömerli, a village in the Halfeti district of Şanlıurfa Province in southeastern Turkey, to a Kurdish mother and a father of mixed Kurdish and Turkish background. He studied political science at Ankara University but left before completing his degree, becoming involved in leftist and Kurdish nationalist activism during the turbulent 1970s.

Founding of the PKK

In 1978, Öcalan and a group of associates founded the PKK in the village of Fis near Lice, declaring the goal of an independent Kurdish state. The party launched its armed campaign on 15 August 1984 with attacks in Eruh and Şemdinli. Öcalan directed the movement from exile, mainly in Syria and Lebanon, until Syria expelled him in 1998 under Turkish pressure.

Capture and imprisonment

After leaving Syria, Öcalan sought asylum in several countries before being captured by Turkish intelligence officers in Nairobi, Kenya, on 15 February 1999. He was tried on İmralı Island and sentenced to death for treason; the sentence was commuted to life imprisonment when Turkey abolished the death penalty in 2002. From prison he has authored numerous books developing his ideology of "democratic confederalism," influenced by the American anarchist thinker Murray Bookchin, and he has repeatedly called for negotiated settlements to the Kurdish question.
