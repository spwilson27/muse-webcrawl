Canadian Corps

The Canadian Corps was a World War I corps formed from the Canadian Expeditionary Force in September 1915 after the arrival of the 2nd Canadian Division in France. The corps was expanded by the addition of the 3rd Canadian Division in December 1915 and the 4th Canadian Division in August 1916. It was commanded for much of the war first by Lieutenant-General Sir Edwin Alderson, then by Sir Julian Byng, and finally by Sir Arthur Currie.

Service

The four divisions of the corps fought together for the first time at the Battle of Vimy Ridge in April 1917, a victory often cited as a defining moment in Canada's emergence as a nation. The corps later fought at Hill 70, Passchendaele, and in the Hundred Days Offensive of 1918, including Amiens, Arras, and the crossing of the Canal du Nord — a period in which it earned a reputation as an elite assault formation.

Composition and casualties

At full strength the corps numbered over 100,000 men. The Canadian Expeditionary Force suffered approximately 172,000 casualties during the war, including about 60,000 killed. The corps was demobilized in 1919.

Legacy

In the Second World War Canada fielded two corps, I Canadian Corps and II Canadian Corps. Canadian formations in both wars are commemorated at memorials across France and Belgium, including the Canadian National Vimy Memorial.
