Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress

The Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress is an award presented annually by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences (AMPAS). It is given to an actress who has delivered an outstanding performance in a supporting role in a film released that year. The award is traditionally presented by the previous year's Best Supporting Actor winner.

The supporting categories were introduced at the 9th Academy Awards, held in 1937 for films of 1936. The first winner was Gale Sondergaard for her performance in Anthony Adverse. For the first several ceremonies, winners received plaques rather than statuettes.

Records

Shelley Winters and Dianne Wiest share the record for the most wins in this category, with two each. Thelma Ritter holds the record for the most nominations, with six, without a win. The youngest winner is Tatum O'Neal, who won at age 10 for Paper Moon (1973), also making her the youngest competitive Oscar winner overall; the oldest winner is Peggy Ashcroft, who won at age 77 for A Passage to India (1984). Hattie McDaniel, the first winner for Gone with the Wind (1939), was the first African American to win an Academy Award.

Selection

Currently, nominees are determined by single transferable vote within the actors branch of AMPAS, and winners are selected by a plurality vote of the Academy's full eligible voting membership. The category is the female counterpart of the Best Supporting Actor award and complements the leading categories, the Best Actor and Best Actress awards.
