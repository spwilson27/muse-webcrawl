Acqua alta

Acqua alta
LocationVenice and the northern Adriatic coast, Italy
DefinitionHigh water exceeding +80 cm above the mareographic zero (Punta della Salute)
Peak periodAutumn and winter, especially October–January
Record level+194 cm (4 November 1966)
MitigationMOSE mobile flood barrier system

Acqua alta (Italian for "high water") is the term used in Veneto for the exceptional tide peaks that occur periodically in the northern Adriatic Sea. The peaks reach their maximum in the Venetian Lagoon, where they cause partial flooding of Venice and Chioggia. The phenomenon occurs mainly between autumn and spring, when seasonal astronomical tides are reinforced by strong sirocco winds driving water toward the lagoon, or by the Adriatic seiche.

Causes

Acqua alta results from the coincidence of several factors: the astronomical tide, low atmospheric pressure, the seiche oscillation of the Adriatic basin, and sirocco winds blowing along the sea's long axis, sometimes worsened by bora winds and rainfall draining from the lagoon's catchment. Venice's situation is aggravated by subsidence — the city sank several centimetres during the 20th century, partly from groundwater extraction at Marghera — and by global sea-level rise.

Thresholds and effects

Water levels are measured relative to the mareographic zero at Punta della Salute (1897 average sea level). Levels above +80 cm flood the lowest parts of the city, including St Mark's Square; at +100 cm about 5% of the city is affected, rising to roughly a third at +140 cm. Raised walkways (passerelle) are laid out, sirens warn residents, and a forecasting centre (Centro Previsioni e Segnalazioni Maree) issues alerts.

Notable events

The highest recorded acqua alta struck on 4 November 1966, reaching +194 cm amid a storm that devastated much of Italy. On 12 November 2019 a tide of +187 cm, the second highest, flooded more than 80% of Venice, damaging St Mark's Basilica and prompting a state of emergency. Since October 2020 the MOSE system of mobile barriers at the three lagoon inlets has been raised to protect the city from tides up to 3 metres.
