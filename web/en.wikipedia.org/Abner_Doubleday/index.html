Abner Doubleday

Born: 26 June 1819, Ballston Spa, New York

Died: 26 January 1893, Mendham, New Jersey

Service: United States Army (1842–1873)

Rank: Major general

Abner Doubleday (26 June 1819 – 26 January 1893) was a career United States Army officer and Union general in the American Civil War. He fired the first shot in defence of Fort Sumter in 1861 and fought at Antietam and Gettysburg. In popular culture he is long credited with inventing baseball, a claim historians now regard as a myth.

Civil War service

A graduate of West Point, Doubleday served in the Mexican–American War and against the Seminoles before being stationed at Fort Sumter, where as second-in-command he aimed the first Union cannon fired in reply to the Confederate bombardment on 12 April 1861. He later commanded a division in the Army of the Potomac, fighting at Second Bull Run, Antietam and Fredericksburg. At Gettysburg, after General John Reynolds was killed on 1 July 1863, Doubleday briefly commanded I Corps before being replaced, a slight that embittered him.

The baseball myth

In 1907 the Mills Commission, convened to determine baseball's origins, concluded on the basis of a single letter that Doubleday had devised the game at Cooperstown, New York, in 1839. Doubleday himself never claimed the honour, and modern scholarship shows the story to be unfounded; nevertheless Doubleday Field at Cooperstown and his popular association with baseball endure.

Later life

After the war Doubleday served in Texas and California, patented a cable-car system in San Francisco and became president of the Theosophical Society in America. He died in Mendham, New Jersey, in 1893.
