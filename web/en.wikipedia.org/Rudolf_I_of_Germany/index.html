Rudolf I (1 May 1218 – 15 July 1291) was the first King of Germany of the House of Habsburg, reigning from 1273 until his death. His election ended the Great Interregnum, the period of turmoil following the fall of the Hohenstaufen dynasty. Though never crowned Holy Roman Emperor, Rudolf succeeded in re-establishing royal authority in Germany and, most significantly, secured the Duchies of Austria and Styria for his family, laying the foundations of the Habsburg dynasty's centuries of power.

Early life

Rudolf was born at Limburg Castle near Sasbach am Kaiserstuhl in the Breisgau region, the son of Count Albert IV of Habsburg and Hedwig of Kyburg. He inherited extensive family lands in Alsace, the Aargau and elsewhere, and increased them by inheritance and marriage — his marriage in 1253 to Gertrude of Hohenberg brought further Swabian possessions. He served as a loyal supporter of the Hohenstaufen emperors and gained a reputation as an energetic and capable lord.

Reign

Following the double election of rival kings and the death of Richard of Cornwall, the prince-electors chose Rudolf as King of the Romans on 1 October 1273; he was crowned at Aachen later that month. Rudolf set about restoring imperial estates alienated during the Interregnum (revindication) and curbing the robber barons. His great rival was King Ottokar II of Bohemia, who refused to recognise the election. Rudolf defeated Ottokar at the Battle on the Marchfeld in August 1278, where Ottokar was killed. Rudolf then enfeoffed his sons Albert and Rudolf with Austria and Styria in 1282, anchoring Habsburg power in the east.

Death and legacy

Rudolf died at Speyer in 1291 and was buried in Speyer Cathedral, the traditional resting place of German kings. Although he never obtained the imperial crown from the Pope, his reign is generally regarded as restorative: he reasserted royal authority, enforced the public peace (Landfrieden), and founded the territorial base from which the Habsburgs rose to dominate European politics.
