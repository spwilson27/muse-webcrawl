King's Highway 400, commonly called Highway 400, is a 400-series controlled-access highway in Ontario, Canada, running north from Toronto through Barrie to Parry Sound. It is the principal route from the Greater Toronto Area to cottage country in Muskoka and Georgian Bay and forms part of the corridor toward Sudbury and Northern Ontario via Highway 69.

History

The highway opened in stages beginning in the late 1940s; the Toronto–Barrie section was completed in the early 1950s as one of Ontario's first freeways. Extensions pushed northward over following decades: to Coldwater and Orillia-area junctions, and by the early 2000s the freeway was extended to Parry Sound, bypassing the older Highway 69 alignment. Long-term plans envision the freeway extending to Sudbury, with Highway 69 progressively widened to four lanes.

Characteristics

Within Toronto and York Region the highway is among the widest and busiest in Canada, carrying heavy commuter traffic; summer weekends bring extreme volumes bound for cottage country. Service centres (ONroute) serve the route. North of Barrie, the highway splits traffic toward Highway 11 and the Muskoka region, then traverses Canadian Shield terrain of rock cuts and lakes toward Georgian Bay.
