Corus Entertainment

Corus Entertainment Inc. is a Canadian mass media and television production company. Headquartered in Toronto, Ontario, at Corus Quay on the city's waterfront, the company was formed in 1999 as a spin-off of the media assets of Shaw Communications. Corus is one of Canada's largest media companies, operating the Global Television Network, numerous specialty television channels, a portfolio of radio stations, and the Nelvana animation studio.

Corus Quay, the company's headquarters in Toronto.

History

Corus began in 1987 as part of Shaw Communications' expansion into broadcasting; Shaw acquired radio and television properties through the 1990s, including the stations of Western International Communications (WIC) in a complicated 1999–2000 transaction. To satisfy regulators, Shaw spun off its programming and broadcast holdings into a separately traded company, Corus Entertainment, which began trading on the Toronto Stock Exchange in 1999. The Shaw family retained effective control through multiple-voting shares.

The company grew through acquisitions, buying the animation studio Nelvana in 2000 and expanding its specialty channel portfolio with services such as YTV, Treehouse TV, Teletoon, W Network, Showcase, and History in Canada. In 2016, Corus acquired Shaw Media — including the Global Television Network and Shaw's specialty channels — from its parent Shaw Communications for approximately C$2.65 billion, transforming Corus into one of the country's dominant broadcasters. The deal was structured so that Shaw Communications became Corus's controlling shareholder.

Operations

Corus's television business centres on Global, one of Canada's three main English-language broadcast networks, and a large suite of specialty channels. Through licensing agreements, Corus operates Canadian versions of American brands such as HGTV Canada, Food Network Canada, National Geographic, and Cartoon Network, alongside wholly owned brands including W Network, Showcase, Slice, and History. Its radio division operates stations in major markets under brands such as CFRB's rivals in Toronto (Q107 and CFIQ among them) and news-talk and music stations in Vancouver, Calgary, Edmonton, Winnipeg, and elsewhere.

Nelvana, Corus's animation arm based in Toronto, is one of Canada's best-known producers of children's programming, known for series such as Babar, Franklin, and The Fairly OddParents (co-produced), and for distributing international children's content. Corus also operates Kids Can Press, a children's book publisher.

Challenges

Like other traditional broadcasters, Corus has faced pressure from cord-cutting, declining television advertising, and competition from global streaming services. The loss of key output and branding arrangements — notably the 2024 transfer of rights to several Rogers Sports & Media channels carrying brands Corus had long operated — and the end of its longstanding Warner Bros. Discovery lifestyle brand agreements, together with a heavy debt load from the Shaw Media acquisition, strained the company's finances in the mid-2020s, prompting asset sales and restructuring efforts.
