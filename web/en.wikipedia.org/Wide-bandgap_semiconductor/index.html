Wide-bandgap semiconductor

Wide-bandgap semiconductors (WBGSs or WBG semiconductors) are semiconductor materials with an electronic band gap substantially larger than that of silicon (1.1 eV). The principal materials are silicon carbide (SiC, about 3.3 eV) and gallium nitride (GaN, about 3.4 eV), along with aluminium nitride, boron nitride and diamond.

Properties and applications

The wide bandgap gives a much higher critical breakdown electric field than silicon, allowing devices that are smaller, faster-switching and able to operate at higher voltages and temperatures with lower losses. SiC and GaN power devices are used in electric-vehicle inverters and chargers, solar inverters, fast USB chargers, data-centre power supplies and 5G radio-frequency amplifiers. GaN- and InGaN-based devices also underpin blue and white LEDs.

Challenges

Wide-bandgap materials are harder and more expensive to grow as large, defect-free crystals than silicon, though substrate costs have fallen rapidly and larger SiC wafers (150 mm and 200 mm) have entered production, driving adoption in power electronics.
