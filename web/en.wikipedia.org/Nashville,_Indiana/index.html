Nashville is a town in Washington Township, Brown County, Indiana, United States. It is the county seat of Brown County and, despite a population of only around a thousand, serves as the centre of one of Indiana's best-known artist colonies and tourist destinations. The town was founded in 1836 and named after Nashville, Tennessee.

Artist colony and tourism

In the early twentieth century, artists of the Hoosier Group — most famously T. C. Steele, who built his "House of the Singing Winds" nearby in 1907 — were drawn to the forested hills of Brown County, establishing the Brown County Art Colony. Nashville's streets are now lined with galleries, craft shops, studios and restaurants, and the town draws large crowds each autumn for the fall foliage of the surrounding hills and nearby Brown County State Park, the largest state park in Indiana. The Brown County Art Gallery and the T. C. Steele State Historic Site preserve the colony's heritage.

Geography

Nashville lies in south-central Indiana, about 65 km south of Indianapolis, in rugged, heavily wooded hill country unlike the flat farmland typical of most of the state.
