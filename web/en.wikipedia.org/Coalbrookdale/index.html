Coalbrookdale

The Upper Works at Coalbrookdale, where Abraham Darby first smelted iron with coke in 1709

Coalbrookdale is a village in the Ironbridge Gorge in Shropshire, England, containing a settlement of great significance in the history of iron ore smelting. It lies within the civil parish of the Gorge, Telford and Wrekin borough.

History

In the early eighteenth century, Abraham Darby I perfected the smelting of iron ore using coke in the Coalbrookdale furnaces, first achieving it in 1709. Until this time, iron had largely been smelted with charcoal, which limited production. Coke smelting made large-scale, cheaper cast iron production possible and is regarded as one of the enabling innovations of the Industrial Revolution.

The Darby family developed the Coalbrookdale works over successive generations. Castings produced there included early steam engine cylinders, rails, and the iron ribs of the world's first cast iron bridge, the Iron Bridge over the River Severn, cast by Abraham Darby III and opened in 1781. The settlement became a community of ironworkers' housing, Quaker meeting houses and schools built by the family.

Today

The Ironbridge Gorge was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1986, recognized as a symbol of the Industrial Revolution. The former Coalbrookdale works now host the Coalbrookdale Museum of Iron, part of the Ironbridge Gorge Museum Trust's group of museums, and the restored Old Furnace where Darby smelted with coke can still be seen. The region's coal, ironstone and clay resources — dug from ancient strata containing fungal and plant fossils of Carboniferous age — underpinned the industry.
