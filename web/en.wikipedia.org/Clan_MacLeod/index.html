Clan MacLeod (/məˈklaʊd/; Scottish Gaelic: Clann Mhic Leòid; [kʰl̪ˠãũn̪ˠ viçkʲ ˈʎɔːtʲ]) is a Highland Scottish clan associated with the Isle of Skye. There are two main branches of the clan: the MacLeods of Dunvegan and Harris (Clan MacLeod), and the MacLeods of the Lewes, known in Gaelic as Sìol Torcaill. Both branches claim descent from Leòd, who lived in the 13th century.

History

Leòd, the clan's eponymous founder, is traditionally held to have been a son of Olaf the Black, King of Mann and the Isles, reflecting the clan's Norse-Gaelic origins in the Kingdom of the Isles. His sons founded the two branches: Tormod (Norman) became ancestor of the MacLeods of Dunvegan and Harris, and Torcall (Torquil) of the MacLeods of Lewis.

The MacLeods of Dunvegan have occupied Dunvegan Castle on Skye for some 800 years — it is the oldest continuously inhabited castle in Scotland — and possess celebrated heirlooms including the Fairy Flag (Bratach Sìth), said to bring victory in battle, the Dunvegan Cup, and Rory Mor's Horn. The clan fought in the Wars of Scottish Independence and feuded with neighbouring clans, notably the MacDonalds of Sleat and the Mackenzies; the MacLeods of Lewis eventually lost their lands in the early 17th century. Alasdair Crotach, 8th chief, founded the MacCrimmon piping dynasty at Borreraig, whose pibroch became synonymous with Highland piping.

Today

The chief of Clan MacLeod is Hugh Magnus MacLeod of MacLeod, 30th chief (as of the modern era), recognised by the Court of the Lord Lyon. The Associated Clan MacLeod Societies coordinate clan gatherings worldwide, and the chief's tartan — the loud yellow-and-black "MacLeod of Lewis" or dress tartan — is among the most recognisable in Scotland.
