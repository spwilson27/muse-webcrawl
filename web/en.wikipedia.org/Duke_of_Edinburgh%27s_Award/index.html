The Duke of Edinburgh's Award

Participants on an expedition section hike (placeholder image)

The Duke of Edinburgh's Award (commonly abbreviated to DofE) is a youth awards programme founded in the United Kingdom in 1956 by Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh, together with Kurt Hahn, headmaster of Gordonstoun School, and Lord Hunt. It has since expanded to more than 140 countries and territories around the world, operating as The Duke of Edinburgh's International Award outside the UK.

Programme structure

The Award is available to young people aged 14 to 24 and has three progressive levels: Bronze, Silver and Gold. Each level requires completion of sections covering Volunteering, Physical activity, Skills, and an Expedition; Gold participants additionally undertake a Residential project away from home. The programme is non-competitive: participants set personal goals and progress at their own pace.

History and influence

The scheme grew out of Hahn's educational ideas and the wartime Moray Badge scheme. Launched for boys in 1956, it was extended to girls in 1958. John Hunt, leader of the first successful Everest expedition, was its first director. Over a million young people participate annually worldwide, and the award is widely recognized by schools, universities and employers as evidence of commitment and teamwork.

Gold Award presentations

In the UK, Gold Awards are traditionally presented at royal palaces such as Buckingham Palace and St James's Palace at ceremonies hosted by members of the royal family, continuing the founder's personal association with the programme until his death in 2021.
