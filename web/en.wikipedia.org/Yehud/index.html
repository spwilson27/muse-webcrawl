Yehud (Aramaic: 𐤉𐤄𐤃, YHD) was a province of the Achaemenid Persian Empire in the region of the former Kingdom of Judah, established after Cyrus the Great conquered Babylon in 539 BC and permitted the return of Jewish exiles. Its capital was Jerusalem, where the Second Temple was completed around 516/515 BC.

History

Yehud was a small, largely rural province considerably smaller than the former kingdom, encompassing the hill country around Jerusalem and perhaps some 30,000 inhabitants at its height. It was administered by governors (peḥah), several of whom are known from the Bible and from seals and coins, including Zerubbabel, and the figures of Ezra and Nehemiah under Artaxerxes I. The province minted small silver coins inscribed "Yehud", among the earliest coinage of the region.

Significance

The Persian period in Yehud was formative for Judaism: the Torah was redacted and promulgated, the Second Temple cult was established, and Jerusalem became the religious centre of the Jewish people. After Alexander the Great's conquest in 332 BC, Yehud passed into the Hellenistic world as part of Coele-Syria.
