Lunokhod programme

Lunokhod programme
Country: Soviet Union
Rovers: Lunokhod 1 (1970), Lunokhod 2 (1973)
Carriers: Luna 17, Luna 21
First rovers on another world

The Lunokhod programme (Russian for "Moonwalker") was a series of Soviet robotic lunar rovers landed on the Moon between 1970 and 1973 as part of the Luna programme. They were the first roving remote-controlled robots to land on another world.

Lunokhod 1

Carried by Luna 17, Lunokhod 1 landed in Mare Imbrium on 17 November 1970. Driven by a five-person team on Earth, the eight-wheeled rover travelled 10.5 km over nearly eleven months, sending back thousands of television images and soil analyses.

Lunokhod 2

Luna 21 delivered Lunokhod 2 to Le Monnier crater in January 1973. In about four months it travelled some 39 km — a distance record for off-Earth roving that stood until 2014 — before dust on its radiators caused it to overheat.

Legacy

The Lunokhod design, developed under Georgy Babakin at the Lavochkin bureau, demonstrated teleoperated surface exploration and informed later rover missions.
