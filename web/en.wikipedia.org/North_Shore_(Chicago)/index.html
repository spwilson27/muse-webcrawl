North Shore (Chicago)

The North Shore is an affluent suburban region north of Chicago, Illinois, comprising communities along the western shore of Lake Michigan in Cook and Lake counties. Traditionally the term refers to the suburbs of Evanston, Wilmette, Kenilworth, Winnetka, Glencoe, Highland Park, Lake Forest, and Lake Bluff, sometimes extended to other nearby communities such as Glenview, Northbrook, Deerfield, and Bannockburn.

History

The North Shore developed in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries as wealthy Chicago families built country estates along the lake, commuting by rail into the city. The Chicago North Shore and Milwaukee Railroad's "North Shore Line" and the Chicago and North Western Railway tied the suburbs to the Loop, and the area became a byword for established wealth and elite education.

Character

The region is known for its large homes, lakefront parks and beaches, ravines and wooded bluffs, and highly rated public schools such as New Trier High School. Cultural institutions include the Ravinia Festival in Highland Park, the summer home of the Chicago Symphony Orchestra, and Northwestern University in Evanston.

The North Shore has featured prominently in American popular culture, serving as the setting or filming location for films such as Home Alone, Ferris Bueller's Day Off, and Risky Business, and for John Hughes's portraits of suburban adolescence.
