Fontanelle

A fontanelle (or fontanel) is an anatomical feature of the infant human skull comprising soft membranous gaps (sutures) between the cranial bones that make up the calvaria of a fetus or infant, allowing the skull to flex during birth and to accommodate the rapid growth of the brain in the first years of life.

Types

The two largest and most clinically important fontanelles are the anterior fontanelle, a diamond-shaped gap at the junction of the frontal and parietal bones (the "soft spot" on top of a baby's head), which typically closes between 12 and 18 months of age, and the smaller posterior fontanelle, at the junction of the parietal and occipital bones, which usually closes within the first two to three months. There are also smaller sphenoidal and mastoid fontanelles.

Clinical significance

Examination of the fontanelles is part of routine infant assessment: a sunken anterior fontanelle may indicate dehydration, a bulging one may indicate raised intracranial pressure, and abnormally early or late closure can signal developmental disorders. The anterior fontanelle also provides a window for cranial ultrasound.
