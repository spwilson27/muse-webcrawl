Stephen Fuller Austin (1793–1836) was an American empresario who founded the first legal Anglo-American colony in Mexican Texas and is remembered as the "Father of Texas."

Colonization

Austin inherited a land grant originally awarded to his father, Moses Austin, by Spanish authorities. After Mexican independence he confirmed the grant and from 1821 began settling the "Old Three Hundred" families along the Brazos and Colorado rivers. As empresario he held extensive civil and military authority, balanced loyalty to Mexico with settlers' interests, and founded San Felipe de Austin as his colony's capital.

Texas Revolution

Imprisoned in Mexico City in 1834 on suspicion of treason after advocating Texas statehood, Austin returned convinced that war was inevitable. He commanded Texian forces at the Siege of Béxar, then served as commissioner to the United States. After independence he lost the Republic's first presidential election to Sam Houston and served briefly as secretary of state before dying of pneumonia in 1836. The city of Austin is named for him.
