Defendu

Defendu is a modern martial art developed by William E. Fairbairn in the 1920s while he served with the Shanghai Municipal Police. Drawing on jujutsu, judo, boxing, savate, kung fu and street-fighting experience, Fairbairn designed the system for police work in one of the world's most violent ports, and later taught it to Allied commandos and special agents during the Second World War.

Development

Fairbairn set out his method in the 1926 book Defendu and refined it in later manuals including Scientific Self-Defence (1931), Get Tough! (1942) and Shooting to Live (1942, with Eric Sykes). The wartime version taught to British Commandos, SOE operatives and the American OSS was often called "gutter fighting".

Techniques

Defendu emphasises simple, aggressive techniques usable under stress: palm-heel and hammer-fist strikes, edge-of-hand blows, low kicks, throws, joint locks and escapes from grabs, together with stick, knife and pistol work. With Sykes, Fairbairn also designed the Fairbairn–Sykes fighting knife, still iconic of British commandos.

Legacy

Defendu heavily influenced later military combatives programmes and modern reality-based self-defence systems.
