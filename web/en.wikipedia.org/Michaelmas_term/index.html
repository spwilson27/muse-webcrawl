Michaelmas term is the first academic term of the year at a number of British and Irish universities, and was formerly one of the four terms of the legal year in England and Wales. It runs from September or October to December and takes its name from the Feast of St Michael and All Angels, which falls on 29 September.

Universities

At the University of Oxford, Michaelmas term lasts from October to December and is one of three terms, the others being Hilary term and Trinity term; full term, during which teaching takes place, lasts eight weeks. The University of Cambridge likewise calls its autumn term Michaelmas, running from October to December. The name is also used at Durham University, King's College London, the London School of Economics, and many other institutions, as well as at numerous independent schools.

Legal year

In the courts of England and Wales, Michaelmas was historically one of the four sittings of the legal year, alongside Hilary, Easter and Trinity terms. Although the terminology has largely been replaced by the modern system of court sittings and terms of business, the names survive in some contexts.

Other uses

Michaelmas was traditionally one of the quarter days on which rents were due and accounts were settled in England, Ireland and Wales. The Michaelmas daisy, a late-flowering aster, takes its name from blooming around the feast day.
