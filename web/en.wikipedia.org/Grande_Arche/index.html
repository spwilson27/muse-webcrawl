Grande Arche

The Grande Arche de la Défense, officially the Grande Arche de la Fraternité, is a monument and office building in the La Défense business district west of Paris, France. Completed in 1989 for the bicentenary of the French Revolution, it forms the western terminus of Paris's historical axis (Axe historique), aligned with the Arc de Triomphe and the Champs-Élysées.

Location: La Défense, Paris, France

Architect: Johann Otto von Spreckelsen; completed by Paul Andreu

Construction: 1985–1989

Height: About 110 m (a hollow cube of roughly 110 × 108 × 112 m)

Design and construction

President François Mitterrand launched an international competition in 1982 as part of his Grands Projets. The winner, Danish architect Johann Otto von Spreckelsen, designed a pure geometric form: a hollow cube — a three-dimensional projection of a tesseract — faced in white Carrara marble and glass, with a Teflon-coated fibreglass "cloud" canopy suspended in its opening. Spreckelsen resigned in 1986 and died in 1987; the work was completed under Paul Andreu. Notre-Dame Cathedral could fit within the arch's opening. The monument was inaugurated in July 1989 with the G7 summit and bicentennial celebrations.

Use

The two vertical pillars contain government offices (including transport ministry agencies), and the roof — providing panoramic views — was closed to the public in 2010 after an elevator incident but reopened in 2017 following renovation, with an exhibition space and restaurant. The arch sits slightly askew (about 6.5°) from the historical axis, partly to accommodate transport infrastructure below.
