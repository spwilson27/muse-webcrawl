Comacchio is a town in the province of Ferrara, in the Emilia-Romagna region of northern Italy, set on the edge of a lagoon at the mouth of the Po Delta. It is built on thirteen islets joined by bridges and canals, and is sometimes called the "little Venice".

History

Comacchio grew rich in the early Middle Ages on salt production and trade, rivalling Venice, which sacked the town in 932. It later passed to the Este family of Ferrara and then to the Papal States. The town's most famous monument, the Trepponti bridge of 1638, spans the junction of five canals and was designed by Giovanni Battista Aleotti.

Economy and culture

The surrounding Valli di Comacchio lagoon is famed for eel fishing, conducted with traditional V-shaped traps called lavorieri; the annual Sagra dell'Anguilla celebrates the catch. The wetlands form part of the Po Delta regional park, a noted refuge for flamingos, herons, and other water birds, and the nearby Lidi di Comacchio are popular Adriatic beach resorts.
