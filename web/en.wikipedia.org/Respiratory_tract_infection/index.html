Respiratory tract infection

A respiratory tract infection (RTI) is an infection involving the respiratory tract. RTIs are conventionally divided into upper respiratory tract infections (URTIs), affecting the nose, sinuses, pharynx and larynx, and lower respiratory tract infections (LRTIs), affecting the trachea, bronchi and lungs.

Upper respiratory tract infections

Common URTIs include the common cold (rhinitis), sinusitis, pharyngitis and tonsillitis, laryngitis, and influenza-like illnesses. Most are caused by viruses such as rhinoviruses and coronaviruses, and resolve without antibiotics.

Lower respiratory tract infections

LRTIs include bronchitis, bronchiolitis and pneumonia. They are a leading cause of illness and death worldwide, particularly in young children, the elderly, and people with chronic lung disease or weakened immune systems. Causes include bacteria such as Streptococcus pneumoniae and viruses including influenza and respiratory syncytial virus (RSV).

Prevention and treatment

Hand hygiene, vaccination (influenza, pneumococcal, COVID-19), and avoiding smoke exposure reduce risk. Treatment is usually supportive; antibiotics are reserved for bacterial infections.
