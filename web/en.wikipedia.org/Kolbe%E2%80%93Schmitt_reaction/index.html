Kolbe–Schmitt reaction

The Kolbe–Schmitt reaction is a carboxylation chemical reaction in which sodium phenoxide is heated with carbon dioxide under pressure (around 100 atmospheres) and then treated with acid, yielding an aromatic hydroxy acid — principally salicylic acid.

Process

Developed by Hermann Kolbe in 1860 and improved by Rudolf Schmitt in 1885, the reaction proceeds through nucleophilic addition of the phenoxide to CO₂. It is the industrial route to salicylic acid, the precursor of aspirin.

Variants

Using potassium instead of sodium phenoxide favours the para-hydroxybenzoic acid product (the basis of parabens), and the related Marasse procedure extends the reaction to other phenols.
