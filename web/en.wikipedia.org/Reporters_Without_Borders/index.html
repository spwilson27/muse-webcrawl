Reporters Without Borders

Reporters Without Borders (French: Reporters sans frontières, RSF) is an international non-profit and non-governmental organisation with the stated aim of safeguarding the right to freedom of information. Its head office is in Paris, France. Founded in 1985 by four journalists including Robert Ménard, RSF is best known for publishing the annual World Press Freedom Index.

Activities

RSF monitors attacks on journalists worldwide, runs assistance desks providing legal aid, insurance and protective equipment to reporters in danger, and campaigns against censorship and restrictive media laws. It has consultative status at the United Nations and UNESCO. The organisation also operates "Collateral Freedom" mirror sites to unblock censored media in restrictive countries.

World Press Freedom Index

Published annually since 2002, the Index ranks 180 countries and territories according to the freedom available to journalists, scoring five contextual indicators: political, economic, legislative, social and safety. Nordic countries typically top the ranking; Eritrea, North Korea, China, Myanmar and, in recent editions, Russia have ranked near the bottom. The Index is widely cited, though its methodology has attracted criticism from governments and some researchers.

History and criticism

Co-founder Robert Ménard led RSF until 2008; successors include Jean-François Julliard and Christophe Deloire (2012–2024). RSF has faced controversy over funding from sources such as the U.S. National Endowment for Democracy and over campaigns some governments called biased. It awards the annual RSF Press Freedom Prize to journalists and media outlets.
