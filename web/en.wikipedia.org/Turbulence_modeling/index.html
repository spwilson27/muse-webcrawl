Turbulence modeling

FieldFluid dynamics, CFD
Key approachesRANS, LES, DNS
Governing eqs.Navier–Stokes equations

Turbulence modeling is the construction and use of mathematical models to predict the effects of turbulence in fluid flows. Because turbulent flows contain eddies spanning an enormous range of scales, directly resolving them is usually impractical, so computational fluid dynamics (CFD) relies on models that represent the influence of the unresolved motion.

The closure problem

Averaging the Navier–Stokes equations (Reynolds averaging) introduces unknown Reynolds stresses that must be expressed in terms of known mean quantities — the closure problem. Most models assume an eddy viscosity, analogous to molecular viscosity, following Boussinesq's hypothesis.

Model families

RANS models (Reynolds-averaged Navier–Stokes) include the one-equation Spalart–Allmaras model and two-equation models such as k–ε and k–ω SST, widely used in industry. Large eddy simulation (LES) resolves the large energy-containing eddies and models only the smaller subgrid scales, at much greater cost. Direct numerical simulation (DNS) resolves everything but is limited to simple geometries and modest Reynolds numbers. Hybrid RANS–LES methods, such as detached eddy simulation, bridge the two.

Applications

Turbulence models underpin the design of aircraft, vehicles, turbomachinery, buildings and weather and climate codes, where their accuracy limits the reliability of predictions.
