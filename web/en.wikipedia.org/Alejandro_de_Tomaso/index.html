Alejandro de Tomaso

Born: 10 July 1928, Buenos Aires, Argentina
Died: 21 May 2003, Modena, Italy
Occupation: Racing driver, car manufacturer
Known for: Founding De Tomaso Automobili

Alejandro de Tomaso (10 July 1928 – 21 May 2003) was an Argentine racing driver and businessman who founded the Italian sports car company De Tomaso in Modena in 1959. The company produced cars including the Vallelunga, Mangusta, and the Ford-powered Pantera, and de Tomaso later controlled marques including Maserati, Innocenti, and Moto Guzzi.

Racing career

Born into a wealthy Argentine family — his father was a politician who had served as a government minister — de Tomaso began racing in Argentina in the 1950s. He left the country in 1955 amid political turmoil under Juan Perón and settled in Italy, where he raced for OSCA and competed in two Formula One World Championship Grands Prix, for Ferrari in 1957 and Cooper in 1959, without scoring points.

De Tomaso Automobili

With financial backing from his American wife, Isabelle Haskell, de Tomaso founded his company in Modena in 1959, initially building racing cars. The road-car business began with the Vallelunga (1964), followed by the Mangusta (1967) and the Pantera (1971), which was sold through Ford's Lincoln-Mercury dealers in the United States and became the marque's best-known product. In the 1970s, with support from the Italian state holding company GEPI, de Tomaso acquired Maserati (1976) and Innocenti, rescuing Maserati with the Biturbo range.

Later years

Financial difficulties in the early 1990s led de Tomaso to sell most of his holdings, though he retained the De Tomaso marque and presented the Guará. He died in Modena in 2003, aged 74.
