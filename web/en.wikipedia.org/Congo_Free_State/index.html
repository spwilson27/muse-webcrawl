Congo Free State

The Congo Free State was a large state in Central Africa from 1885 to 1908, ruled as the personal property of King Leopold II of Belgium. Granted to Leopold at the Berlin Conference of 1884–85 under the guise of a humanitarian and free-trade association, the territory — roughly the modern Democratic Republic of the Congo — was administered for the extraction of ivory and, above all, wild rubber.

Atrocities

To enforce rubber quotas, Leopold's administration and concession companies used the Force Publique, hostage-taking, mutilation and massacre. Demographic estimates of the resulting population decline vary widely, from about one million to figures as high as fifteen million, with around ten million frequently cited. Reports by the British consul Roger Casement (1904) and campaigning by E. D. Morel and the Congo Reform Association made the abuses an international scandal.

Annexation

In 1908, under international pressure, the Belgian parliament annexed the territory, ending Leopold's personal rule; it became the Belgian Congo, which remained a colony until independence in 1960. The period remains a defining symbol of colonial exploitation.
