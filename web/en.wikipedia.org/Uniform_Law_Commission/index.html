The Uniform Law Commission (ULC), also known as the National Conference of Commissioners on Uniform State Laws (NCCUSL), is a nonprofit, unincorporated association of the United States. Founded in 1892, it consists of commissioners appointed by each state, the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, and the U.S. Virgin Islands. Its purpose is to promote uniformity in state law on subjects where uniformity is desirable and practical.

Work

The ULC drafts model and uniform acts — such as the Uniform Commercial Code (drafted jointly with the American Law Institute), the Uniform Probate Code, and the Uniform Transfers to Minors Act — which state legislatures may then enact, with or without modification. Because the ULC has no power to impose laws, a uniform act becomes law only where a state legislature adopts it.

History

The first meeting of the conference took place in 1892 at Saratoga Springs, New York, with commissioners from seven states. By the mid-twentieth century, nearly every state participated. The UCC, first promulgated in 1952, was eventually enacted in some form in every state except Louisiana (which adopted most of it), and remains the commission's most influential product.
