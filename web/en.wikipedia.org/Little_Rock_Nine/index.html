Little Rock Nine

The Little Rock Nine were a group of nine African American students enrolled in Little Rock Central High School in 1957, in Little Rock, Arkansas. Their enrolment was followed by the Little Rock Crisis, in which the students were initially prevented from entering the racially segregated school by Orval Faubus, the Governor of Arkansas. They then attended after the intervention of President Dwight D. Eisenhower, who federalised the Arkansas National Guard and deployed the 101st Airborne Division to enforce the Supreme Court's desegregation ruling in Brown v. Board of Education (1954).

Students entering Central High under military escort, 1957

The nine students

  • Ernest Green (b. 1941) — the first African American to graduate from Central High, in May 1958.
  • Elizabeth Eckford (b. 1941) — famously photographed facing a hostile crowd alone on the first day, 4 September 1957.
  • Jefferson Thomas (1942–2010)
  • Terrence Roberts (b. 1941)
  • Carlotta Walls LaNier (b. 1942) — the youngest of the nine.
  • Minnijean Brown (b. 1941) — expelled in February 1958 after repeated harassment.
  • Gloria Ray Karlmark (b. 1942)
  • Thelma Mothershed (1940–2024)
  • Melba Pattillo Beals (b. 1941) — author of the memoir Warriors Don't Cry (1994).

The crisis

The Little Rock school board had adopted a gradual desegregation plan, with Central High first. On 4 September 1957, Governor Faubus deployed the Arkansas National Guard "to preserve the peace," and the Guard turned the nine students away. Elizabeth Eckford, whose family had no telephone and had not received word of a change of plans, arrived alone and was confronted by an angry mob — an encounter captured in Will Counts's iconic photograph.

After a federal court ordered the Guard removed, the students entered on 23 September but were evacuated amid rioting. On 24 September, President Eisenhower issued Executive Order 10730, federalising the Arkansas National Guard and sending 1,000 paratroopers of the 101st Airborne Division to Little Rock. The students entered under escort on 25 September and endured a year of sustained abuse despite military protection.

Aftermath

Faubus closed all Little Rock public high schools for the 1958–59 school year — the "Lost Year" — rather than accept integration. In 1999, President Bill Clinton presented the nine with the Congressional Gold Medal. Little Rock Central High School remains a functioning school and a National Historic Site administered by the National Park Service.
