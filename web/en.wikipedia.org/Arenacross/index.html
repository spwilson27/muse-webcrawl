Arenacross

Arenacross is a form of motorcycle racing held on small, tight indoor dirt tracks built inside arenas and stadiums. It is closely related to supercross, but uses considerably more compact tracks, typically constructed on the floor of hockey or basketball arenas, with tighter corners, shorter straights, and obstacles packed into a smaller space. The close confines produce bar-to-bar racing and frequent contact between riders.

Format

Arenacross events are typically held during the winter months, serving as an off-season complement to outdoor motocross and the larger stadium supercross series. A standard event features heat races, semi-finals, last-chance qualifiers, and main events in professional classes (historically the 250cc and 450cc classes, later reorganised into AX Pro and support classes) as well as amateur classes for younger riders.

History

Indoor arena racing grew out of the success of supercross in the 1970s and 1980s, bringing motorcycle racing to smaller markets that lacked large stadiums. In the United States, the AMA Arenacross Championship became the premier professional series. Promoters such as Feld Entertainment organised the national series, which visited mid-sized cities across the country. The series helped develop young riders who later progressed to supercross and motocross competition.
