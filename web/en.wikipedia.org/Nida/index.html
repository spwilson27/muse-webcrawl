Nida (German: Nidden) is a resort town in Lithuania, the administrative centre of Neringa Municipality on the Curonian Spit, a narrow sand peninsula separating the Curonian Lagoon from the Baltic Sea. It lies close to the border with Russia's Kaliningrad Oblast and is one of Lithuania's most popular seaside resorts.

Nida is famous for its enormous shifting sand dunes, especially the Parnidis Dune, one of the highest in Europe, and for its historic fishermen's cottages with carved weather vanes. In the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries the village hosted a noted artists' colony that attracted Expressionist painters including members of Die Brücke.

Sights

The writer Thomas Mann built a summer house in Nida in 1930, now a museum; he wrote parts of Joseph and His Brothers there before fleeing Germany in 1933. Other sights include the Amber Gallery-Museum, the old cemetery with its carved wooden grave markers (krikštai), and the sundial on Parnidis Dune. The whole Curonian Spit is a UNESCO World Heritage Site shared between Lithuania and Russia.
