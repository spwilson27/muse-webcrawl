Beechcraft Bonanza

The Beechcraft Bonanza is an American general aviation aircraft introduced in 1947 by the Beech Aircraft Corporation (now part of Textron Aviation). It has been in continuous production longer than any other aircraft in history. The original Model 35 was famous for its distinctive V-tail; later variants adopted a conventional tail. The modern G36 Bonanza is a six-seat, single-engine, retractable-gear aircraft aimed at private and business owners.

History

Designed by a team led by Ralph Harmon, the Bonanza was a fast, all-metal, low-wing monoplane that brought airline-style performance to the post-war private market. The V-tail Model 35 remained in production until 1982; concerns about its structural strength after a series of in-flight breakups — usually traced to weather encounters and overstressing by pilots — led to reinforcement programmes and airworthiness directives. A conventional-tail version, the Model 33 Debonair (later Bonanza), appeared in 1960, and the stretched six-seat Model 36 followed in 1968.

Notable events

A V-tail Bonanza was the aircraft that crashed on 3 February 1959 near Clear Lake, Iowa, killing musicians Buddy Holly, Ritchie Valens and J. P. "The Big Bopper" Richardson — "the Day the Music Died". In 1967 Thor Heyerdahl's contemporary, pilot Sheila Scott and others set records in Bonanzas; in 1949 a Bonanza set a long-distance record flying from Honolulu to New Jersey.
