Common knowledge (logic)

Common knowledge
Field: epistemic logic, game theory
Definition: everyone knows it, everyone knows that everyone knows it, ad infinitum
Key texts: Lewis (1969); Aumann (1976)

Common knowledge is a special kind of knowledge for a group of agents. A proposition is common knowledge when everyone knows it, everyone knows that everyone knows it, and so on without end. It is stronger than mutual knowledge, in which the hierarchy of "knowing that others know" stops at some finite level.

Formalisation

In epistemic logic, common knowledge of p for a group G is defined as the infinite conjunction of "everyone in G knows that" applied repeatedly to p. Robert Aumann's set-theoretic formulation (1976) defines it via the meet of the agents' information partitions.

Applications

David Lewis used common knowledge to analyse convention. In game theory it underpins coordinated action: the muddy children puzzle and the two generals' problem show how behaviour changes when information is, or cannot be, common knowledge. Public announcements and public rituals are classic generators of common knowledge.

Agreement theorem

Aumann's "agreeing to disagree" result shows that rational agents with common knowledge of each other's posteriors cannot maintain different probabilities for the same event.
