Disjoint union

In mathematics, the term disjoint union denotes the union of a family of mutually disjoint sets, together with the related construction that forces sets to be disjoint by tagging their elements with the index of the set they came from. For sets A and B, the disjoint union A ⊔ B can be formed as (A × {0}) ∪ (B × {1}), keeping identical elements from the two sets distinct.

In topology

The disjoint union of topological spaces carries the disjoint union (sum) topology: a subset is open exactly when its intersection with each summand is open. With this topology the disjoint union is the coproduct in the category of topological spaces. Analogous sum constructions exist in measure theory, where the measure of a set in the sum is the sum of the measures of its pieces.

Set theory and category theory

In set theory the disjoint union of an indexed family (Ai)i∈I is ∪i∈I (Ai × {i}); its cardinality is the sum of the cardinalities of the parts. In type theory and programming, the corresponding notion is the tagged or discriminated union (sum type).
