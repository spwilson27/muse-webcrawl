Eye movement

Six extraocular muscles control eye movement.

Eye movement includes the voluntary or involuntary motion of the eyes, helping organisms direct the fovea—the high-resolution region of the retina—toward objects of interest. Eye movements are controlled by six extraocular muscles innervated by the oculomotor, trochlear, and abducens cranial nerves.

Types

Saccades are rapid, ballistic jumps between fixation points, used when scanning a scene or reading; during them, vision is largely suppressed. Smooth pursuit movements track moving objects. Vergence movements rotate the eyes in opposite directions to fixate near or far targets. The vestibulo-ocular reflex stabilizes gaze during head movement, while the optokinetic response follows large moving visual fields. Even during fixation, tiny involuntary movements—tremor, drift, and microsaccades—prevent the retinal image from fading.

Reading and research

During reading, the eyes alternate between fixations of roughly 200–250 milliseconds and saccades spanning about 7–9 characters, with occasional regressions. Eye tracking is used in psychology, neuroscience, marketing, and human–computer interaction to infer attention and cognitive processing, and in the clinic to detect neurological and psychiatric disorders affecting gaze control.
