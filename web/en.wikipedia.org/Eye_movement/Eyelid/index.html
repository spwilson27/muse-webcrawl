Eyelid

An eyelid is a thin fold of skin that covers and protects the eye. The levator palpebrae superioris muscle retracts the eyelid, exposing the cornea to light and giving the human its characteristic appearance. The eyelids close by contraction of the orbicularis oculi muscle.

Function

Eyelids protect the eye from foreign bodies and excessive light, and their regular blinking spreads tears over the corneal surface, keeping it moist and washing away debris. During sleep the closed lids protect the eye and maintain moisture. The eyelashes and the blink reflex help keep particles out.

Structure

Each eyelid contains skin, muscle, the fibrous tarsal plate, and conjunctiva lining its inner surface. Embedded in the tarsal plates are the meibomian glands, which secrete an oily layer of the tear film; along the lid margins are the eyelashes (cilia) with their associated glands of Zeis and Moll. The upper eyelid is larger and more mobile than the lower.

Clinical significance

Common eyelid conditions include blepharitis (inflammation of the lid margin), styes (hordeola), chalazia (blocked meibomian glands), ptosis (drooping of the upper lid), entropion and ectropion (inward or outward turning of the lid), and basal cell carcinoma, the most common eyelid malignancy. Many animals, unlike humans, possess a third eyelid, the nictitating membrane.
