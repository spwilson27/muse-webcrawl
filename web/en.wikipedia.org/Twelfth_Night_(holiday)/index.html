Twelfth Night (holiday)

Observed byChristians
TypeChristian festival
Date5 or 6 January
Related toChristmas, Epiphany, Twelvetide

Twelfth Night (also known as Epiphany Eve) is a Christian festival on the last night of the Twelve Days of Christmas, marking the coming of the Epiphany. Different traditions mark the date of Twelfth Night as either 5 January or 6 January, depending on whether the counting begins on Christmas Day or Boxing Day (26 December).

Traditions

Twelfth Night traditions vary around the world. In many cultures, a special cake — such as the French galette des rois, the Spanish roscón de reyes, or the English Twelfth cake — is baked with a bean or small figure hidden inside; whoever finds it in their slice is crowned king or queen of the feast. In England, Twelfth Night was traditionally a night of revelry, misrule, and wassailing, with a Lord of Misrule overseeing the festivities. The custom of removing Christmas decorations on Twelfth Night persists in some households, where leaving them up longer is considered unlucky.

Literary references

William Shakespeare's comedy Twelfth Night, or What You Will was written to be performed as part of Twelfth Night entertainment, and takes its festive, topsy-turvy spirit from the holiday's traditions of disguise and inversion of social order.
