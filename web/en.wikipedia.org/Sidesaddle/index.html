Sidesaddle

Sidesaddle riding is a form of equestrianism in which the rider sits aside, rather than astride, the horse, with both legs on the same side — traditionally the left, or near side.

History

Riding aside developed in medieval Europe, partly so women in long skirts could ride with decorum. Anne of Bohemia is credited with popularising it in England in the 14th century. The modern sidesaddle, with a leaping head (a second pommel) developed in the 19th century, notably under Jules Pellier, allowed women to ride, jump and hunt securely.

Technique

The rider's right leg rests over a pommel while the left foot uses a stirrup, giving control comparable to astride riding when fitted and taught correctly. Sidesaddle competition and hunting persist today, supported by associations such as the Side Saddle Association in Britain.
