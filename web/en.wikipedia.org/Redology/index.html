Redology

Redology (Chinese: 紅學, Hóngxué) is the study of the Chinese classic novel Dream of the Red Chamber (also known as The Story of the Stone), written by Cao Xueqin in the 18th century. One of the most prolific fields in Chinese literary scholarship, Redology investigates the novel's text and its many versions, the life of its author, the identity of the continuing author of its final forty chapters, and the novel's themes, structure and symbolism. Devotees are called Redologists.

History

Commentary began during Cao's lifetime in the remarks of Zhiyanzhai (the "Red Inkstone"), whose annotated manuscripts preserve early, differing recensions of the first eighty chapters. The field took its name in the late Qing and Republican eras; Hu Shih's textual studies of 1921 applied modern evidential methods (kaozheng) to date the novel and establish Cao's authorship and autobiographical reading, while Cai Yuanpei championed allegorical-political interpretations. Yu Pingbo's 1923 studies, influenced by Hu, became landmarks — and later targets.

Schools and debates

Major approaches include the autobiographical school, the "索隱" (index-and-allusion) school seeking hidden political meanings, textual criticism comparing the manuscript ("Zhi") versions with the 1791–92 Cheng–Gao printed edition, and "探佚" scholarship attempting to reconstruct Cao's lost original ending from clues within the text. During the Cultural Revolution Redology was politically charged: Mao Zedong praised the novel as a portrayal of class struggle, and a 1974 campaign criticised Yu Pingbo. Redology remains immensely popular in China, with dedicated journals, societies, television lectures and amateur communities.
