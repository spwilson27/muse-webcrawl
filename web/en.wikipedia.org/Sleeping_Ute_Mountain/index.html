Sleeping Ute Mountain is a peak in Montezuma County in southwestern Colorado, rising to 9,979 feet (3,042 m) near the town of Towaoc and west of Cortez. Viewed from the east, the mountain's ridge profile famously resembles a reclining Ute chief lying on his back with arms folded across his chest, his headdress feathers at the northern end — a landmark visible across the Four Corners region and from Mesa Verde.

Geology

Like the nearby La Plata Mountains, Sleeping Ute Mountain is a laccolith: a blister-like intrusion of magma that domed up the overlying sedimentary strata (including the Mancos Shale and Mesaverde Group sandstones) around 70 million years ago, without reaching the surface as a volcano. Erosion has since exposed parts of the igneous core.

Ute legend

In Ute Mountain Ute tradition, the mountain is the form of a great warrior god who fell asleep while resting after a battle with evil spirits; clouds gathering on the peak are said to be his breath or his blanket, and rain is a sign of his changing mood. Out of respect for the tribe, whose reservation includes the summit, the peak is not generally open to public climbing.
