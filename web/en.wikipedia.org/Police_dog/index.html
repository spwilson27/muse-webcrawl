Police dog

A police dog, also known as a K-9 (a homophone of canine), is a dog that is trained to assist police and other law enforcement officers in their work. A popular dog breed used for this work is the German Shepherd, although Belgian Malinois have become increasingly common for patrol work, and spaniels and Labradors are often used for detection roles.

Roles

Police dogs are used for a variety of purposes, both single-purpose and dual-purpose (trained in two functions). These include:

  • Tracking and patrol: pursuing and apprehending suspects, searching buildings, and protecting their handler.
  • Detection: sniffing out narcotics, explosives, firearms, currency, or other contraband, exploiting the dog's powerful sense of smell.
  • Search and rescue: locating missing persons, disaster victims, or human remains (cadaver dogs).
  • Public order: crowd control at public events.

Training and legal status

Police dogs and their handlers form K-9 units and undergo months of training together; the dog usually lives with its handler. In many jurisdictions, harming a police dog is a specific criminal offence, and retired police dogs are often adopted by their handlers. Dogs may be trained to alert passively (sitting by a find) or actively (scratching or barking), depending on the role.

History

Dogs have been used by law enforcement since the Middle Ages, but formal police dog programmes began in Europe in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, notably in Ghent, Belgium, in 1899, and later spread to Germany, Britain and the United States. Bloodhounds were famously used for tracking, while shepherd breeds came to dominate general-purpose police work.
