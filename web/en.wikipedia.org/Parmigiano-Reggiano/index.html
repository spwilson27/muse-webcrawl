Parmigiano-Reggiano

Parmigiano-Reggiano is an Italian hard, granular cheese made from cow's milk and aged 12–36 months. It is produced in the provinces of Parma, Reggio Emilia, Modena, Bologna (west of the Reno river), and Mantua (south of the Po) in the Emilia-Romagna and Lombardy regions, and it is protected by Italian DOP and European PDO status. Outside Europe it is often sold simply as "Parmesan", a name that within the European Union is legally reserved for the genuine product.

The cheese is made by a method essentially unchanged for centuries, traditionally attributed to Benedictine and Cistercian monks of the Middle Ages. Part-skimmed evening milk is mixed with whole morning milk, warmed in copper vats, and coagulated with calf rennet. The curd is cut into rice-sized grains, cooked, pressed into great wheels of 30–45 kilograms, brined, and aged for a minimum of 12 months. Wheels are hammer-tested by inspectors of the Consorzio del Formaggio Parmigiano-Reggiano; only those that pass are fire-branded with the name in dotted letters around the rind.

Characteristics and uses

Long ageing gives Parmigiano-Reggiano a complex fruity, nutty flavour and a slightly gritty texture caused by crunchy tyrosine crystals. It is grated over pasta, soups, and risottos, shaved into salads, and eaten in chunks, classically with balsamic vinegar from nearby Modena. A single wheel requires about 550 litres of milk to produce, and total annual production is around four million wheels.
