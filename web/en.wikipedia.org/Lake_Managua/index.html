Lake Managua (Spanish: Lago de Managua, also known by its indigenous name Lake Xolotlán) is a lake in Nicaragua. At 65 kilometres (40 mi) long and 25 kilometres (16 mi) wide, it is the country's second-largest lake after Lake Nicaragua, and lies immediately north of the capital city, Managua, which stands on its southern shore.

Geography

The lake has an average depth of about 9.5 metres. Its basin is volcanic, and the still-active Momotombo volcano rises on its northern shore; the island of Momotombito lies offshore. The lake once connected seasonally to the larger Lake Nicaragua via the Tipitapa River at times of high water.

Pollution and recovery

From the 1920s, Managua discharged untreated sewage into the lake, and industrial effluent — including mercury from a chlor-alkali plant on the southern shore — heavily contaminated its water and fish. For decades the lake was effectively written off as a dead body of water. A German-financed wastewater treatment plant inaugurated in 2009 began treating the capital's sewage, after which water quality and fish populations improved markedly.

Fauna

The lake historically supported freshwater sharks (now considered extremely rare or extirpated), sawfish, and many cichlid species shared with Lake Nicaragua. It is connected faunistically to the larger lake, though pollution greatly reduced its diversity during the 20th century.
