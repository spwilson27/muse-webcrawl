Fitbit

Fitbit is a line of wireless-enabled wearable technology devices — activity trackers, smartwatches and smart scales — marketed by Google. The devices measure data such as the number of steps walked, heart rate, quality of sleep, steps climbed, and other personal metrics involved in fitness. The brand originated with Fitbit, Inc., an American consumer electronics company founded in San Francisco in 2007 by James Park and Eric Friedman.

History

The company's first product, the Fitbit Tracker, was announced in 2008 and released in late 2009; it clipped to clothing and counted steps, distance and calories burned. Later models moved to the wrist and added features such as heart-rate monitoring, sleep tracking, GPS and smartphone notifications. The product line came to include the Flex, Charge, Alta, Versa and Sense families of trackers and smartwatches, as well as the Aria smart scale. Fitbit went public on the New York Stock Exchange in June 2015.

Acquisition by Google

In November 2019, Google announced it would acquire Fitbit for approximately $2.1 billion. The deal closed in January 2021 after regulatory scrutiny in the United States and Europe, where Google made commitments regarding the handling of health data and Android API access. Fitbit's products were subsequently integrated into Google's hardware division, and the Pixel Watch line launched in 2022 with Fitbit health and fitness software.

Products and features

Fitbit devices sync with the Fitbit mobile app and web dashboard, presenting activity, exercise, sleep and heart-health data. A subscription service, Fitbit Premium, adds advanced analytics, guided programs and a Daily Readiness Score. Health features on newer devices include electrocardiogram (ECG) readings, blood oxygen (SpO2) estimates, skin-temperature variation and stress management tools.

Criticism and controversies

Fitbit has faced criticism over the accuracy of its heart-rate and calorie measurements, resulting in class-action lawsuits, and several product recalls — including the 2014 voluntary recall of the Fitbit Force over skin irritation and a 2022 recall of Ionic smartwatches over a battery overheating burn hazard. Privacy advocates have repeatedly raised concerns about the collection and secondary use of users' health data, concerns that intensified around the Google acquisition.
