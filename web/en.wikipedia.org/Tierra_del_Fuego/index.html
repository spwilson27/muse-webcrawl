Tierra del Fuego

LocationSouthern tip of South America
Separated byStrait of Magellan
CountriesArgentina, Chile

Tierra del Fuego is an archipelago off the southernmost tip of the South American mainland, across the Strait of Magellan. The archipelago consists of the main island, Isla Grande de Tierra del Fuego, and a group of smaller islands including Cape Horn. The territory is divided between Argentina and Chile.

Geography

The western part of the main island is mountainous, covered by the southern extension of the Andes, while the northeast is a steppe-like plain. The climate is subpolar oceanic, with strong winds, cool summers and frequent precipitation. The Beagle Channel separates the main island from the southern islands.

History

The archipelago was inhabited for thousands of years by indigenous peoples including the Selk'nam, Yaghan, Kawésqar and Haush. Ferdinand Magellan's expedition passed through in 1520, and the name, Spanish for "Land of Fire", refers to the many fires lit by the native inhabitants that were seen from the sea. The Argentine city of Ushuaia, on the Beagle Channel, is often described as the southernmost city in the world.
