Poznań is a city on the River Warta in west-central Poland, within the Greater Poland region. The city is an important cultural and business centre, and one of Poland's most populous regions with many regional customs such as Saint John's Fair (Jarmark Świętojański), traditional saint's breads (rogale świętomarcińskie), and local dialects.

Its population is approximately 530,000, while the overall Poznań metropolitan area, including suburban areas and other towns in the region such as Swarzędz, Luboń, Mosina, and Murowana Goślina, is inhabited by over 1.029 million people.

History

Poznań is one of the oldest cities in Poland. It was founded in the 8th and 9th centuries as a fortified settlement on an island in the Warta River, between the river's banks. Poznań Cathedral, on Ostrów Tumski island, was built in the 10th century and is the oldest Polish cathedral, containing the tombs of the first Polish rulers, Duke Mieszko I and King Bolesław I the Brave. In June 1956, workers' protests in Poznań were the first of several massive protests against the communist government of the Polish People's Republic.

Economy and education

Poznań is a centre of trade, sport, education, technology and tourism. It is an important academic site, with about 130,000 students and Adam Mickiewicz University, the third largest Polish university. The Poznań International Fair is the largest and oldest trade fair in Poland, first held in 1921.
