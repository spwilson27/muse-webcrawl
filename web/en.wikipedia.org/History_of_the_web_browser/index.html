History of the web browser

The history of the web browser begins with Tim Berners-Lee's WorldWideWeb browser at CERN in 1990–1991 and continues through the browser wars of the 1990s, the rise of open-source engines, and the dominance of Google Chrome in the 2010s and 2020s.

Early browsers

Berners-Lee wrote the first browser-editor, WorldWideWeb (later Nexus), on a NeXT computer in 1990. The line-mode browser made the Web portable; ViolaWWW, Erwise, and Midas followed in 1992. NCSA Mosaic (1993), developed by Marc Andreessen and Eric Bina, popularized the Web with inline images and an easy-to-use interface for Windows and Macintosh.

The browser wars

Andreessen co-founded Netscape, whose Navigator browser dominated by 1995. Microsoft responded with Internet Explorer, bundled free with Windows; by the early 2000s IE held over 90% share — a dominance that triggered the United States v. Microsoft antitrust case. Netscape open-sourced its code, leading to the Mozilla project and Firefox (2004), which revived competition and standards compliance. Apple's Safari launched in 2003 using the WebKit engine.

The Chrome era and mobile

Google Chrome (2008), built on WebKit and later its Blink fork, overtook rivals with speed and frequent updates, passing 60% global share by the late 2010s; its Chromium engine also powers Microsoft Edge (from 2020), Opera, and others. Mobile browsing grew dominant after the iPhone (2007) and Android, while privacy-focused browsers such as Brave and DuckDuckGo carved niches amid debates over tracking and engine diversity.
