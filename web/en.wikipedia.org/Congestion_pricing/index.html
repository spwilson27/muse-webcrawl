Congestion pricing

Congestion pricing or congestion charges is a system of surcharging users of public goods that are subject to congestion — most often road space in city centres — in order to reduce traffic demand to manageable levels. Economists argue that pricing scarce road capacity at peak times discourages low-value trips, speeds buses and freight, and raises revenue that can be invested in public transport.

Schemes in operation

Singapore introduced the first scheme in 1975 and later moved to electronic road pricing with gantries. London's congestion charge began in 2003, Stockholm's in 2006 (after a trial and referendum), and Milan and Gothenburg operate their own schemes. In the United States, high-occupancy toll lanes are widespread, and New York City's central business district tolling programme — the first full cordon scheme in the country — began in January 2025.

Effects and debate

Studies of London and Stockholm find traffic reductions of roughly 15–30 percent within the charged zone, faster journeys and lower emissions. Critics raise concerns about equity for low-income drivers and effects on businesses; supporters respond with discounts, exemptions and investment of revenues in transit.
