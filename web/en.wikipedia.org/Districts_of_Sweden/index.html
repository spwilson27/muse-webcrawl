Districts of Sweden

Districts of Sweden

Swedish: distrikt

Number: About 2,500

Established: 1 January 2016

Based on: Historical parishes (församlingar)

The districts of Sweden (Swedish: distrikt) are geographical subdivisions introduced on 1 January 2016 as a basis for population registration. There are roughly 2,500 districts, and every person registered in Sweden is recorded as living in one of them. The districts are based on the parishes of the Church of Sweden as they existed on 31 December 1999.

Purpose

When the Church of Sweden was separated from the state in 2000, civil registration could no longer be based on church parishes. For a time, population registration was tied only to municipalities and properties, which made long-term statistical comparison difficult. The district division restores a stable small-area geography for statistics, research, and administration, broadly consistent with the historical parish map.

Administration

The districts are maintained under the responsibility of the Swedish Tax Agency (Skatteverket), which keeps the population register, while Lantmäteriet, the national land survey authority, maintains the geographical boundaries. Districts may cross municipal boundaries in a few cases, reflecting older parish geography.

Relationship to other divisions

Sweden's main administrative divisions remain its 21 counties (län) and 290 municipalities (kommuner). Districts sit below the municipal level and are distinct from the Church of Sweden's current parishes, which have changed through amalgamations since 2000 while district boundaries remain fixed.
