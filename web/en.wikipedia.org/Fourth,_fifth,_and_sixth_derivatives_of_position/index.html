Fourth, fifth, and sixth derivatives of position

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In physics, the fourth, fifth and sixth derivatives of position are derivatives of position with respect to time with the fourth, fifth, and sixth orders. Unlike the first three derivatives — position, velocity, and acceleration — and the fourth, jerk, the fifth and sixth derivatives are not in widespread use and have no universally agreed names, though the terms snap (or jounce), crackle, and pop are used informally, following the mascot names Snap, Crackle and Pop of a well-known breakfast cereal.

Definitions

  • Snap (fourth derivative) is the rate of change of jerk; the term jounce is also used. Units: metres per second to the fourth power (m/s⁴).
  • Crackle (fifth derivative) is the rate of change of snap. Units: m/s⁵.
  • Pop (sixth derivative) is the rate of change of crackle. Units: m/s⁶.

Applications

Snap is of practical interest in engineering and motion planning: minimising jerk and snap improves ride comfort in transport systems, reduces vibration and wear in machinery and cam design, and produces smoother trajectories for robotics and roller-coaster design. In physics, the Abraham–Lorentz force on an accelerating charged particle depends on the third derivative of position (jerk), making higher derivatives relevant in classical electrodynamics. Higher derivatives also appear in some theories of cosmology and in the analysis of physical systems near equilibrium.

Notation

No standard symbols exist beyond writing successive time derivatives with dots or superscripts; snap is sometimes denoted s, crackle c, and pop p. Even these names are largely whimsical and the quantities are rarely computed outside trajectory optimisation.
