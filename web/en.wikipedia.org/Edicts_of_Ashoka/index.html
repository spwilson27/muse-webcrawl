Edicts of Ashoka

The Edicts of Ashoka are a collection of more than thirty inscriptions on pillars, large stones, and cave walls, made by Emperor Ashoka of the Mauryan Empire during his reign from about 268 to 232 BCE. They were scattered throughout the areas of modern-day India, Nepal, Pakistan, and Afghanistan, and provide the first tangible evidence of Buddhism. The edicts, written in local languages (primarily Prakrits in the Brahmi and Kharoshthi scripts, with Greek and Aramaic versions in the northwest), carry the messages of the emperor to his people regarding his policies of Dhamma.

Content

The edicts proclaim Ashoka's devotion to a policy he called Dhamma (from Sanskrit dharma), promoting social and animal welfare, religious tolerance, respect for elders and servants, generosity, and non-violence. In Major Rock Edict XIII, Ashoka expresses remorse for the conquest of Kalinga, in which, he records, 100,000 were killed and many more died, and renounces conquest by violence in favor of "conquest by Dhamma." Other edicts record the planting of trees and digging of wells along roads, the provision of medical treatment for humans and animals, and the appointment of Dhamma officers (dhamma-mahamattas) to promote welfare and justice.

Classification

The inscriptions are conventionally divided into the Minor Rock Edicts, the Major Rock Edicts (fourteen edicts and two separate Kalinga edicts), the Minor Pillar Edicts, and the Major Pillar Edicts (seven edicts inscribed on the great polished sandstone pillars of Ashoka). The Minor Rock Edict at Maski, discovered in 1915, was the first to name "Devanampriya Ashoka" explicitly, confirming that the royal titles used elsewhere referred to Ashoka.

Decipherment and significance

The Brahmi script was deciphered in the 1830s by James Prinsep, unlocking the edicts for modern scholarship. They constitute the earliest decipherable Indian inscriptions and the primary contemporary source for Ashoka's reign, anchoring early Indian chronology. The lion capital from the Ashoka pillar at Sarnath was adopted as the national emblem of India.
