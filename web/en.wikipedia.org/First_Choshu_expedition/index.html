The First Chōshū expedition (第一次長州征討, Daiichiji Chōshū seitō) was a punitive military expedition by the Tokugawa shogunate against the Chōshū Domain in 1864. It followed Chōshū's defeat at the Forbidden Gates Incident (the Kinmon incident) in Kyoto, where Chōshū forces had attempted to seize control of the imperial court, and the bombardment of Chōshū's batteries at Shimonoseki by Western fleets after Chōshū fired on foreign shipping.

Background

Chōshū was a leading centre of the sonnō jōi ("Revere the Emperor, expel the barbarians") movement. After Chōshū troops fired on foreign vessels in the Shimonoseki Straits in 1863 and Chōshū radicals attempted a coup in Kyoto in August 1864, the shogunate obtained an imperial edict authorising a punitive campaign. A large shogunal army, commanded by Tokugawa Yoshikatsu with Saigō Takamori of Satsuma playing a key mediating role, advanced on Chōshū's borders.

Outcome

Faced with overwhelming force, the conservative faction within Chōshū gained the upper hand: the domain submitted without a full-scale invasion, executed officials held responsible for the Kyoto affair, and apologised to the shogunate. The settlement humiliated Chōshū's reformists but proved temporary — reformers under Takasugi Shinsaku soon seized power in a brief internal struggle, and when the shogunate launched the Second Chōshū expedition in 1866, Chōshū's modernised forces defeated it, fatally weakening Tokugawa authority and hastening the Meiji Restoration.
