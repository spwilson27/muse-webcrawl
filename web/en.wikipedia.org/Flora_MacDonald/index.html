Flora MacDonald

Flora MacDonald (Gaelic: Fionnghal nic Dhòmhnaill; 1722 – 5 March 1790) was a Scottish heroine of the Jacobite rising of 1745, famed for helping Charles Edward Stuart — Bonnie Prince Charlie — escape government forces after his defeat at the Battle of Culloden. Her voyage with the disguised prince "over the sea to Skye" became one of the most celebrated episodes in Scottish folklore, immortalised in the Skye Boat Song.

The escape to Skye

In June 1746 Charles was hiding on the island of Benbecula in the Outer Hebrides, with government militia searching for him and a reward of £30,000 on his head. Flora MacDonald, then 24, agreed to help him cross to Skye. She obtained passes for herself, a manservant and an Irish spinning maid named "Betty Burke" — the prince in disguise. On 27 June they rowed across the Little Minch to Skye, narrowly escaping detection, and Charles was passed on to safety at Portree before eventually escaping to France.

Later life

Flora was arrested and held for about a year, partly in the Tower of London, before being released under an indemnity act in 1747. She became a celebrity in London society and was received by Frederick, Prince of Wales. In 1750 she married Allan MacDonald of Kingsburgh; in 1774 the couple emigrated to North Carolina, where her husband fought as a Loyalist in the American Revolutionary War and was captured. Flora later returned to Scotland and died on Skye in 1790. Samuel Johnson, who met her during his 1773 tour of the Hebrides, famously called her "a name that will be mentioned in history, and if courage and fidelity be virtues, mentioned with honour".
