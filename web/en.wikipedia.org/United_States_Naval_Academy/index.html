United States Naval Academy

The United States Naval Academy (USNA) is an undergraduate college in Annapolis, Maryland, for the education of officers of the United States Navy and the United States Marine Corps. Founded in 1845 under Secretary of the Navy George Bancroft, it is the second-oldest of the five U.S. service academies.

Candidates for admission generally must apply directly to the academy and apply separately for a nomination, usually from a member of Congress. Students are officers-in-training known as midshipmen; tuition is fully funded by the Navy in exchange for an active-duty service obligation after graduation. The student body, known as the Brigade of Midshipmen, numbers about 4,500.

Campus and curriculum

The 338-acre campus, known as "the Yard," sits at the mouth of the Severn River where it meets the Chesapeake Bay. Its centerpiece is Bancroft Hall, one of the largest dormitory buildings in the world. The academy's curriculum emphasizes science, engineering, and mathematics alongside naval science and leadership, and graduates earn a Bachelor of Science degree and a commission as an ensign in the Navy or a second lieutenant in the Marine Corps.

Traditions

Notable traditions include the Herndon Monument climb by plebes at the end of their first year, the rivalry with the United States Military Academy (highlighted by the annual Army–Navy football game), and the academy's motto, Ex Scientia Tridens ("From Knowledge, Seapower").
