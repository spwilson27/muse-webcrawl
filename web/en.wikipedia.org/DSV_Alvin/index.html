DSV Alvin is a crewed deep-ocean research submersible owned by the United States Navy and operated by the Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution (WHOI) in Massachusetts. Commissioned in 1964, it is one of the world's first deep-ocean submersibles able to carry a crew, and over more than five thousand dives it has become the most productive human-occupied vehicle in deep-sea science.

Design

Alvin carries a pilot and two scientists inside a titanium personnel sphere about 2.1 metres in diameter, equipped with viewports, external lights, cameras, and two manipulator arms for collecting samples. It is launched and recovered from its support ship, R/V Atlantis, and can remain submerged for six to ten hours.

Notable missions

In 1966 Alvin helped locate a hydrogen bomb lost in the Mediterranean off Palomares, Spain. In 1977 its dives at the Galápagos Rift led to the discovery of hydrothermal vents and their chemosynthesis-based ecosystems — the first known food chain independent of sunlight. In 1986 it made the first crewed exploration of the wreck of RMS Titanic, launching the small robot Jason Jr. into the ship's interior. Alvin has also surveyed the wreck of the German battleship Bismarck and countless mid-ocean ridge sites.

Upgrades

The submersible has been essentially rebuilt several times. A multi-year overhaul completed in 2013 added a larger sphere, improved imaging, and a 4,500 m rating; a further upgrade completed in 2022 certified it to 6,500 m, giving access to about 99 percent of the ocean floor.
