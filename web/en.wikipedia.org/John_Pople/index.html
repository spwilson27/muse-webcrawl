John Pople

Born31 October 1925, Burnham-on-Sea, Somerset, England
Died15 March 2004 (aged 78), Sarasota, Florida, U.S.
Known forComputational chemistry, Gaussian program, Pople basis sets
AwardsNobel Prize in Chemistry (1998), Copley Medal (2002)

Sir John Anthony Pople FRS (31 October 1925 – 15 March 2004) was a British theoretical chemist who shared the 1998 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for his development of computational methods in quantum chemistry, work embodied in the widely used Gaussian program.

Career

Pople studied mathematics at Trinity College, Cambridge, and later led the basic physics division of the National Physical Laboratory before moving to the United States in 1964 as professor at Carnegie Mellon University in Pittsburgh.

Contributions

He developed approximate molecular orbital methods (CNDO, INDO), standard basis sets such as 6-31G, and model chemistries (the Gn theories) that made accurate quantum-chemical calculations routine. His Gaussian software, first released in 1970, became the standard tool of computational chemists.

Honours

Pople shared the 1998 Nobel Prize with Walter Kohn, was knighted in 2003, and received the Copley Medal of the Royal Society in 2002.
