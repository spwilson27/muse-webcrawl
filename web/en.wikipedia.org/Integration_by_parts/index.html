Integration by parts

Integration by parts
Field: Calculus
Formula: ∫ u dv = uv − ∫ v du
Origin: Product rule for differentiation

In calculus, and more generally in mathematical analysis, integration by parts is a process that finds the integral of a product of functions in terms of the integral of the product of their derivative and antiderivative. It is frequently used to transform the antiderivative of a product of functions into an antiderivative for which a solution can be more easily found.

Formula

The rule can be derived from the product rule of differentiation. If u and v are differentiable functions, then ∫ u dv = uv − ∫ v du. A common strategy is to choose u to be a function that becomes simpler when differentiated (following the heuristic order: logarithmic, inverse trigonometric, algebraic, trigonometric, exponential) and dv to be a function that is easy to integrate.

Applications

The method evaluates integrals such as ∫ x ex dx and ∫ ln x dx, and repeated application yields reduction formulas, for example for powers of sine and cosine. Integration by parts is also foundational in analysis: it underlies the weak formulation of differential equations, the definition of weak derivatives, and proofs of the divergence theorem and Green's identities.

History

The formula was known to Isaac Newton and Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz in essence, and was developed further by Brook Taylor, who published a version in 1715. See also Integral and Product rule.
