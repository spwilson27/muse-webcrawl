Casino

A casino gaming floor with slot machines and table games

A casino is a facility for certain types of gambling. Casinos are often built near or combined with hotels, resorts, restaurants, retail shopping, cruise ships, and other tourist attractions. Some casinos are also known for hosting live entertainment, such as stand-up comedy, concerts, and sporting events.

Games

Casinos typically offer a mix of table games and gaming machines. Common table games include blackjack, roulette, baccarat, craps, and casino poker variants such as Texas hold 'em. Gaming machines include slot machines and video poker. In most casino games the establishment retains a statistical advantage — the house edge — which guarantees the casino a profit from players over the long run, while individual players may win or lose in the short term.

History

The word casino is of Italian origin, the diminutive of casa ("house"). The first known European gambling house, the Ridotto, was opened in Venice in 1638 to provide controlled gambling during carnival season. In the twentieth century, casino gaming became strongly associated with Las Vegas and Atlantic City in the United States, Monte Carlo in Monaco, and later with Macau, which surpassed Las Vegas in gambling revenue in the 2000s, and Singapore.

Regulation and customers

Casinos are subject to extensive government licensing and regulation, and generally restrict entry to patrons above a minimum age (usually 18 or 21). Many casinos use surveillance systems ("eye in the sky") and security procedures to deter cheating, card counting, and theft. Casino design and perks such as complimentary drinks and "comps" are used to encourage patrons to remain and gamble.
