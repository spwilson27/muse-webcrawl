Defensible space (fire control)

Defensible space, in the context of fire control, is the natural and landscaped area around a structure that has been maintained and designed to reduce fire danger. The concept is central to protecting buildings in the wildland–urban interface from wildfires: fuel is removed, thinned or converted into less flammable vegetation in concentric zones around the home.

Zones

Guidance such as California's commonly describes an ember-resistant zone within about 5 feet of the structure, a "lean, clean and green" zone out to 30 feet, and a reduced-fuel zone extending to 100 feet or the property line. Within these zones residents remove dead plants and debris, space trees and shrubs, prune branches away from roofs, and keep gutters and vents clear of embers.

Effectiveness and regulation

Research after major fires shows that the condition of the immediate surroundings of a house strongly affects whether it survives. Several US states and local authorities legally require defensible space for properties in high fire-hazard areas, with inspections and clearance deadlines before fire season.
