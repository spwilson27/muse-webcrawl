Syncretism

Syncretism
Field: Religious studies, anthropology
Definition: Blending of distinct traditions
Examples: Santería, Greco-Buddhism, Sikh/Hindu festivals

Syncretism is the practice of combining or reconciling different beliefs and schools of thought, especially in religion, where elements of distinct traditions merge into new forms. The term derives from the Greek synkrētismos, used by Plutarch for the way Cretans set aside their differences against common enemies.

Religious syncretism

Syncretism is prominent wherever cultures meet: in Afro-Caribbean religions such as Santería and Vodou, which blend West African religion with Catholic saints; in Greco-Buddhist art of ancient Central Asia; in the fusion of indigenous American and Christian practices; and in the historical identification of foreign gods with Roman or Greek counterparts (the interpretatio romana). Some traditions welcome blending, while others denounce it as corruption of pure doctrine, making "syncretism" sometimes a pejorative term in theology.

Beyond religion

The concept extends to linguistics (merger of word forms), politics (combinations of left- and right-wing positions), and culture generally, describing creative fusion rather than simple borrowing.
