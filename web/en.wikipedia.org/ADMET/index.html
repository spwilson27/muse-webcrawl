ADMET

ADMET is an acronym in pharmacokinetics and pharmacology standing for absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion, and toxicity. It describes the disposition of a pharmaceutical compound within an organism. Together with efficacy, ADMET properties largely determine whether a drug candidate will succeed in development.

Components

  • Absorption — how a drug enters the bloodstream, e.g. across the intestinal wall.
  • Distribution — how the drug spreads through tissues, including plasma protein binding and crossing barriers such as the blood–brain barrier.
  • Metabolism — chemical modification of the drug, mainly by liver enzymes such as the cytochrome P450 family.
  • Excretion — elimination of the drug and its metabolites, chiefly via the kidneys and bile.
  • Toxicity — adverse effects, including hepatotoxicity and cardiotoxicity such as hERG channel blockade.

Role in drug discovery

Poor pharmacokinetics was historically a leading cause of failure in drug development. Modern discovery pipelines therefore screen ADMET properties early, using in vitro assays and computational (in silico) prediction models, to eliminate unsuitable compounds before costly clinical trials. The related acronym ADME omits the toxicity component.
