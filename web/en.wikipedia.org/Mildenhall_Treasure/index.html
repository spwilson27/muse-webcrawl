Mildenhall Treasure

The Mildenhall Treasure is a major hoard of highly decorated Roman silver tableware from the fourth century AD, buried at Mildenhall in Suffolk, England. It consists of 34 items, including the Great Dish, one of the most famous pieces of Roman silver ever found.

Discovery

The hoard was discovered in January 1942 by farmer Gordon Butcher while ploughing, and was retrieved with the help of his employer Sydney Ford. It was not declared to the authorities until 1946, an episode that provoked controversy; the treasure was ultimately declared treasure trove and acquired by the British Museum, where it is now displayed.

Contents

The hoard includes large serving dishes decorated with Bacchic imagery, flanged bowls, goblets, ladles, and spoons — some of the spoons bearing Christian chi-rho symbols, mixing pagan and Christian iconography in a single wealthy household's plate.
