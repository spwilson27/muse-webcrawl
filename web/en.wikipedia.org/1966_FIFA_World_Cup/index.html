The 1966 FIFA World Cup was the eighth FIFA World Cup, the quadrennial football tournament for the men's national teams of FIFA member countries. It was played in England from 11 to 30 July 1966. Hosts England won the tournament, defeating West Germany 4–2 in the final at Wembley Stadium after extra time — England's first and only World Cup title.

Final

The final is famous for Geoff Hurst's hat-trick, the only one in a World Cup final until 2022, and for his disputed second goal, awarded by the linesman after the ball bounced off the crossbar. The match ended with Kenneth Wolstenholme's famous commentary line, "They think it's all over... it is now!" The Jules Rimet Trophy itself had been stolen before the tournament and was recovered by a dog named Pickles.

Notable events

North Korea were the surprise team, reaching the quarter-finals after beating Italy 1–0. Portugal, led by Eusébio, finished third. African nations boycotted the tournament in protest at FIFA's allocation of qualifying places, a stance that led to the modern allocation system.
