Remote work

Remote work (also called telecommuting, telework or working from home) is the practice of an employee performing their official job duties from a remote location, particularly the employee's home, rather than from a centralised workplace such as an office building. It is enabled by information technologies including email, videoconferencing, cloud computing and collaboration software.

Also calledTelecommuting, telework, WFH
Term coined1973, by Jack Nilles
EnablersBroadband internet, VPNs, video conferencing, cloud services

History

The term "telecommuting" was popularised in the 1970s by NASA engineer Jack Nilles. Remote work grew slowly with personal computing and the internet, concentrated in technology and professional services. The COVID-19 pandemic of 2020 forced a sudden global experiment: at its peak, a large share of employees in advanced economies worked from home full-time.

Effects

Studies find remote workers report higher job satisfaction and avoid commuting time, while employers can reduce office costs and hire from wider labour markets. Countervailing findings include risks of isolation, blurred work–life boundaries, weaker informal collaboration and challenges in training junior staff. Many organisations have settled on hybrid models mixing office and home days.

Technology

Remote work depends on reliable broadband, virtual private networks, and tools such as video conferencing, instant messaging, shared documents and project management platforms. The distributed-work model also produced the "digital nomad" phenomenon and companies with no headquarters at all.
