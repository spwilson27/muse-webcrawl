Cor pulmonale

SpecialtyCardiology, pulmonology
CauseLung disease causing pulmonary hypertension
EffectRight ventricular enlargement and failure

Cor pulmonale is the enlargement and failure of the right ventricle of the heart as a response to increased vascular resistance or high blood pressure in the lungs (pulmonary hypertension), usually caused by chronic lung disease.

Causes

The most common cause is chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD). Other causes include interstitial lung disease, sleep apnea, pulmonary embolism (which can cause acute cor pulmonale), and conditions restricting chest movement. Long-standing low oxygen levels cause the pulmonary arteries to constrict and remodel, raising the pressure against which the right ventricle must pump.

Signs and symptoms

Signs include breathlessness, fatigue, swelling of the legs and ankles, distended neck veins and enlargement of the liver. Diagnosis is supported by echocardiography, electrocardiography and imaging showing right-heart enlargement.

Treatment

Treatment targets the underlying lung disease: smoking cessation, bronchodilators, long-term oxygen therapy and diuretics for fluid retention. Managing pulmonary hypertension and preventing blood clots are also important.
