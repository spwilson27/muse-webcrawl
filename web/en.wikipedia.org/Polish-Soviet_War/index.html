Polish–Soviet War

Date: 1919 – March 1921

Location: Central and Eastern Europe

Result: Treaty of Riga

Combatants: Second Polish Republic vs. Russian SFSR and Ukrainian SSR

Polish troops at the Battle of Warsaw, August 1920.

The Polish–Soviet War (1919–1921) was an armed conflict between the Second Polish Republic and Soviet Russia (along with Soviet Ukraine) over the borderlands of present-day Ukraine and Belarus, in the aftermath of the First World War and the Russian Revolution. Poland, led by Józef Piłsudski, sought to secure its eastern frontier and build a federation of allied states (the Intermarium concept), while the Bolsheviks aimed to carry the revolution westward into Europe.

Course of the war

Fighting began in 1919 as both sides moved into territories vacated by German forces. In April 1920 Poland, allied with the Ukrainian leader Symon Petliura, launched the Kiev offensive and briefly took Kiev. A massive Soviet counteroffensive under Mikhail Tukhachevsky drove the Poles back to the gates of Warsaw.

At the Battle of Warsaw in August 1920 — remembered in Poland as the "Miracle on the Vistula" — Polish forces executed a daring counterstroke from the Wieprz River that shattered the Soviet advance. A further victory at the Battle of the Niemen River followed, and an armistice was signed in October 1920.

Outcome

The Treaty of Riga (March 1921) divided the disputed territories between Poland and the Soviet republics, fixing Poland's eastern border for the interwar period. The Soviet failure halted the westward spread of the revolution in Europe, while the settlement left both sides dissatisfied and millions of Ukrainians and Belarusians divided between the two states.
