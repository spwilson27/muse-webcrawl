Literature of Spain

[Image: Early edition of Don Quixote]
Don Quixote (1605–1615) by Miguel de Cervantes.

The literature of Spain comprises works written in Spanish (Castilian) and in the other languages of Spain, including Catalan, Galician and Basque. It spans more than a thousand years, from the medieval epic Poema de mio Cid to the experimental fiction of the present day.

Medieval literature

The earliest surviving work of Spanish literature is the epic poem Cantar de mio Cid (c. 1140), celebrating the Castilian hero Rodrigo Díaz de Vivar. Medieval highlights include the Galician-Portuguese cantigas, the learned verse of the mester de clerecía, Alfonso X's prose histories and law codes, and Juan Ruiz's playful Libro de buen amor.

The Golden Age

The Spanish Golden Age (roughly 1492–1681) produced Miguel de Cervantes, whose Don Quixote is often called the first modern novel; the dramatists Lope de Vega and Calderón de la Barca; the poets Garcilaso de la Vega, Luis de Góngora and Francisco de Quevedo; and the mystics Teresa of Ávila and John of the Cross. The picaresque novel flourished with Lazarillo de Tormes (1554) and Mateo Alemán's Guzmán de Alfarache.

Nineteenth and twentieth centuries

Nineteenth-century realism is represented by Benito Pérez Galdós and Leopoldo Alas ("Clarín"). The Generation of '98 — including Miguel de Unamuno, Antonio Machado and Pío Baroja — responded to Spain's imperial decline, while the Generation of '27 produced poets such as Federico García Lorca. After the Civil War, notable novelists included Camilo José Cela (Nobel laureate, 1989) and Carmen Laforet.

Contemporary literature

Spain's literature today is written in several languages: Javier Marías, Carlos Ruiz Zafón and Arturo Pérez-Reverte are internationally read Spanish-language authors, while Catalan writers such as Jaume Cabré and Basque writers such as Bernardo Atxaga reach global audiences in translation.
