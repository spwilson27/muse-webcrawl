Messier 82

Messier 82 (Cigar Galaxy, NGC 3034)
Constellation: Ursa Major
Distance: approx. 12 million light-years
Type: starburst galaxy
Discovered: 1774, Johann Elert Bode

Messier 82 (also known as the Cigar Galaxy or NGC 3034) is a starburst galaxy approximately 12 million light-years away in the constellation Ursa Major. A member of the M81 Group, it forms a famous pair with the spiral galaxy M81, whose gravitational interaction with M82 over the past several hundred million years triggered its extraordinary rate of star formation.

Starburst and superwind

M82's core manufactures stars at roughly ten times the rate of the Milky Way, concentrated in brilliant super star clusters. Radiation and supernova explosions from these young stars drive a galactic superwind — filaments of glowing hydrogen gas streaming thousands of light-years perpendicular to the disc, imaged dramatically by the Hubble Space Telescope. The galaxy is five times more luminous than the Milky Way and exceptionally bright in the infrared, radio and X-ray bands.

Centre and supernovae

Near the galaxy's heart lies a bright X-ray source (M82 X-1) attributed to an intermediate-mass black hole, and in 2014 astronomers discovered M82 X-2, an ultraluminous X-ray pulsar — a neutron star accreting beyond the classical Eddington limit. In January 2014 the bright Type Ia supernova SN 2014J appeared in M82, the closest such explosion in decades, and was studied across the spectrum despite heavy dust obscuration.
