IntroducedMarch 1, 2004

The National Incident Management System (NIMS) is a standardized approach to incident management developed by the United States Department of Homeland Security. Released in 2004 under Homeland Security Presidential Directive 5, NIMS provides a consistent framework enabling government, nongovernmental and private-sector partners to work together to prevent, protect against, respond to and recover from incidents.

Components

NIMS is built around standardized management characteristics, including common terminology, modular organization and manageable span of control. Its major components include the Incident Command System (ICS), resource management, and command and coordination structures such as Emergency Operations Centers and the Multiagency Coordination System. Preparedness activities — planning, training and exercises — support the system.

Adoption

Federal agencies are required to use NIMS, and state, local, tribal and territorial governments must adopt it to receive federal preparedness grants. The doctrine was revised in 2008 and again in 2017, when a refreshed NIMS document reorganized the material around three major components: resource management, command and coordination, and communications and information management.
