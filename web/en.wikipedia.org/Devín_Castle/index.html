Devín Castle

Devín Castle (Slovak: hrad Devín) is a ruined castle in Devín, a borough of Bratislava, Slovakia. It stands on a high cliff above the confluence of the Danube and Morava rivers, at the border with Austria, and is one of the oldest and most symbolically important fortified sites in Slovakia.

History

The site was occupied from prehistoric times and fortified by the Celts and Romans; it formed part of the Roman Limes. In the 9th century it was a stronghold of Great Moravia, associated with Prince Rastislav. A medieval castle developed from the 13th century onward and passed through the hands of several noble families. In 1809, during the Napoleonic Wars, French troops blew up the castle, leaving it in ruins.

Significance

In the 19th century Devín became a powerful symbol of Slovak national awakening; Ľudovít Štúr and his followers made a celebrated excursion there in 1836. During the Cold War the area lay within the restricted Iron Curtain border zone; today the castle is a museum and a memorial near the site commemorates victims shot while attempting to cross the border.

Today

The castle grounds are open to visitors, with exhibitions on its history in the upper castle caves and reconstructed sections. The Maiden Tower, a slender watchtower on a rock at the river confluence, is the most photographed part of the site.
