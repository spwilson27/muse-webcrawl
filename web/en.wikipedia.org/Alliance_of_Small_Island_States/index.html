Alliance of Small Island States

Abbreviation: AOSIS
Founded: 1990
Members: 39 small island and low-lying coastal states
Focus: Climate change and sustainable development

The Alliance of Small Island States (AOSIS) is an intergovernmental organisation of low-lying coastal and small island countries established in 1990 to consolidate the voices of Small Island Developing States (SIDS) in the United Nations system. Its members are drawn principally from the Caribbean, Pacific, and the Atlantic, Indian Ocean, Mediterranean and South China Sea (AIMS) regions.

Climate advocacy

AOSIS is best known for its role in international climate negotiations. It was central to the adoption of the 1.5 °C temperature goal in the Paris Agreement, campaigning under the slogan "1.5 to stay alive", and has long advocated for action on loss and damage, leading to the agreement at COP27 in 2022 to establish a dedicated fund. Member states face existential threats from sea-level rise and ocean warming despite contributing only a tiny fraction of global greenhouse gas emissions.

Organisation

AOSIS works mainly as a negotiating bloc within the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change and the UN General Assembly. The chair rotates among the regions; recent chairs include Samoa, Belize, and Antigua and Barbuda. The alliance has no formal treaty basis and operates through consensus among its members.
