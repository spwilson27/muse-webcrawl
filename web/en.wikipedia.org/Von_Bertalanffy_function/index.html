Von Bertalanffy function


Named for: Ludwig von Bertalanffy
Type: Growth curve
Used in: Fisheries science, oncology, ecology

The von Bertalanffy function is a type of growth curve model for a time series, named after Austrian biologist Ludwig von Bertalanffy, who proposed it in work beginning in 1934 and developed it further in 1957. It describes the growth of an individual organism: growth is rapid early in life and slows as the organism approaches an asymptotic maximum size.

Formulation

The length of an organism at age t is commonly expressed as:

L(t) = L∞ (1 − e−K(t − t0))

where L∞ is the asymptotic average length, K is a growth coefficient describing how quickly the maximum is approached, and t0 is the hypothetical age at which length would be zero. A corresponding equation in weight replaces length with mass; in its generalized form an exponent allows the curve to fit a wider family of growth patterns.

Derivation

Von Bertalanffy derived the curve from physiological principles: he assumed that growth is proportional to the difference between anabolism (building up, scaling with surface area or nutrient intake) and catabolism (breakdown, scaling with body mass). The resulting differential equation yields the characteristic asymptotic curve.

Applications

The function is a cornerstone of fisheries science, where it is fitted to age-and-length data (often from otolith readings) to estimate stock productivity and set catch limits. Variants of the model have been applied to tumour growth in oncology, tree growth in forestry, and other biological growth phenomena.

Limitations

The model assumes growth continues throughout life toward a fixed asymptote and does not capture seasonal growth oscillations, abrupt changes at maturity, or shrinkage. Seasonal and generalized extensions of the function have been developed to handle some of these cases.
