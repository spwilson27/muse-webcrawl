Neoproterozoic

The Neoproterozoic Era is the last of the three eras of the Proterozoic Eon, spanning from 1 billion to 538.8 million years ago, immediately before the Phanerozoic Eon. It is divided into three periods: the Tonian, the Cryogenian, and the Ediacaran. The Neoproterozoic records some of the most dramatic events in Earth history: the break-up of the supercontinent Rodinia, the most severe ice ages known, and the first appearance of complex multicellular life.

Geology and climate

During the Cryogenian Period, Earth experienced at least two global or near-global glaciations — the Sturtian (c. 717–660 Ma) and Marinoan (c. 645–635 Ma) events — in which ice may have reached the equator, the so-called "Snowball Earth" episodes. Thick cap carbonate deposits overlying glacial sediments worldwide record the abrupt ends of these glaciations, likely driven by volcanic CO2 build-up under the ice.

Life

The fossil record of the Neoproterozoic shows rising biological complexity. Acritarchs diversified, and by the Ediacaran Period the first large, complex organisms appear: the Ediacaran biota, including forms such as Dickinsonia, Charnia, and the mobile Kimberella, a probable early mollusc-like grazer. Near the era's close, the first skeletal animals (e.g. Cloudina) appeared, setting the stage for the Cambrian explosion that opens the Phanerozoic.
