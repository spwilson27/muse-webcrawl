The Hôtel Negresco is a famous luxury hotel on the Promenade des Anglais in Nice, France, overlooking the Baie des Anges. Built in the Belle Époque style and opened in 1913, it is recognisable by its pink dome and ornate façade, and is classified as a historic monument. It houses an extensive private art collection and is a member of The Leading Hotels of the World.

History

The hotel was founded by Henri Negresco, a Romanian immigrant who had risen through the restaurant trade, and designed by the Dutch-born architect Édouard Niermans, renowned for Parisian theatres and grand hotels. It opened on 4 January 1913 and quickly attracted royalty and wealthy visitors to the French Riviera. During the First World War it was converted into a hospital, and Negresco died in 1920 with the hotel in financial difficulty. In 1957 the building was bought by the Augier family; Jeanne Augier rebuilt its reputation, filling it with an eclectic museum-grade collection of French art from the 17th century to the present, including works associated with Salvador Dalí and the sculptor Niki de Saint Phalle.

The building and collection

The hotel's celebrated Salon Royal is crowned by a glass dome designed, according to tradition, in the workshop of Gustave Eiffel. Interiors range from period rooms in Louis XVI style to contemporary suites, and the hotel's restaurant Le Chantecler holds Michelin stars. The building was listed as a historic monument in 2003. On 14 July 2016 the hotel stood on the route of the Bastille Day truck attack on the Promenade des Anglais and served as a refuge and triage point for the injured.
