A space capsule is a type of crewed spacecraft that uses a simple, mostly unguided blunt-body reentry shape and has no wings. Capsules were the first crewed spacecraft and remain the standard design for human spaceflight, prized for their structural simplicity, stability during atmospheric entry, and reliability.

Design

A capsule's blunt shape creates a shock wave that keeps most of the heat of reentry away from its surface, protected by an ablative or reusable heat shield. Inside, the crew sits in a pressurised cabin with life support, controls and usually a docking mechanism; an attached service module may provide propulsion, power and consumables and is discarded before landing. Recovery is by parachutes, ending in an ocean splashdown or, for Soyuz, Shenzhou and Starliner, a touchdown on land, sometimes cushioned by retrorockets or airbags.

History

The Soviet Vostok carried Yuri Gagarin on the first human spaceflight in 1961, followed by the American Mercury, Gemini and Apollo capsules — the Apollo command module remains the only crewed capsule to travel to the Moon. The Russian Soyuz, first flown in 1967, is the longest-serving crewed spacecraft design. In the twenty-first century, capsules returned to prominence with China's Shenzhou, SpaceX's Crew Dragon (first crewed flight 2020), Boeing's Starliner and NASA's Orion for Artemis lunar missions.
