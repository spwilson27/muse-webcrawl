Transnistria

Pridnestrovian Moldavian Republic
Capital: Tiraspol
Languages: Russian, Romanian (Moldovan), Ukrainian
Status: Unrecognized breakaway state; internationally recognized as part of Moldova

Transnistria, officially the Pridnestrovian Moldavian Republic (PMR), is a breakaway state internationally recognized as part of Moldova. It controls a narrow strip of territory on the eastern bank of the Dniester River, as well as the city of Bender and its surrounding localities on the western bank. Transnistria declared independence from Moldova in 1990 and, after the Transnistria War of 1992, has operated as a de facto independent state, though it is not recognized by any United Nations member state.

History

The territory became part of the Moldavian Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic in 1924 and was joined to Bessarabia in 1940 to form the Moldavian SSR. As the Soviet Union dissolved, fears in the Russophone east that Moldova would unite with Romania led to the declaration of the PMR in September 1990. Armed conflict culminated in 1992, ending with a ceasefire brokered by Russia, whose troops — the Operational Group of Russian Forces — remain stationed in the region, alongside a large ammunition depot at Cobasna.

Politics and economy

Transnistria has its own president, parliament (the Supreme Council), army, police, and currency, the Transnistrian ruble. Its economy combines heavy industry, steel production at Rîbnița, and textiles, and has been shaped by the conglomerate Sheriff, which operates across much of the private sector. Settlement efforts through the 5+2 format have not resolved the region's status.
