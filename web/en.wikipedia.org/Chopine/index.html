Chopine

A chopine is a type of women's platform shoe that was popular in the 15th, 16th, and 17th centuries, especially in Italy and Spain. Chopines consisted of a high platform — sometimes exceeding 40 cm (16 inches) — made of wood or cork, covered in leather or fabric, worn beneath or attached to a slipper-like upper shoe.

Use and symbolism

Originally practical for keeping dresses and feet above muddy streets, chopines became conspicuous status symbols: the taller the platform, the higher the wearer's social rank appeared, literally and figuratively. In Venice they were favoured by patrician women and by courtesans, and wearers often needed attendants to walk steadily. Sumptuary discussions of the era debated their extravagance.

Origins

The style is thought to derive from the pattens and wooden bath clogs of the Ottoman world, transmitted through Mediterranean trade. Related high platform footwear existed in Spain (chapines) and influenced later European fashions for raised heels.
