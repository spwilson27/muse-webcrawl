Clip (firearms)

A clip is a device that is used to store multiple rounds of ammunition together as a unit for later insertion into the internal magazine or cylinder of a firearm. This speeds up the process of loading and reloading the firearm as several rounds can be loaded at once, rather than one round being loaded at a time.

Types

A stripper clip (or charger) holds cartridges by their rims so they can be pressed into a magazine in one motion; the clip itself is then discarded or pocketed, and does not enter the weapon. An en bloc clip is inserted into the firearm together with its cartridges and forms part of the feeding mechanism until the last round is fired, when it is ejected — famously in the M1 Garand rifle, whose eight-round clip is ejected with a distinctive metallic "ping". Revolver moon clips hold rimless cartridges by their extraction grooves and are loaded and ejected with the rounds as a unit.

Clip vs. magazine

The terms are often conflated in popular usage, but a magazine is the (fixed or detachable) device that stores and feeds ammunition within or to the firearm, whereas a clip merely holds rounds together for loading into a magazine. Firearms historians and manufacturers insist on the distinction; most modern self-loading rifles use detachable box magazines, sometimes filled with the help of stripper clips and a guide.
