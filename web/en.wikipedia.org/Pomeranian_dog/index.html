Pomeranian dog

The Pomeranian (often known as a Pom) is a breed of dog of the Spitz type that is named for the Pomerania region in north-west Poland and north-east Germany in Central Europe. Classed as a toy dog breed because of its small size, the Pomeranian is descended from larger Spitz-type dogs, specifically the German Spitz.

History

Pomeranians were brought to notice internationally when Queen Victoria returned from a holiday in Florence, Italy, in 1888 with a small red Pomeranian named Marco; she became a serious breeder and exhibitor, and is credited with reducing the breed's size from around 14 kg to the toy size popular today. The breed was recognised by the American Kennel Club in 1900. Two Pomeranians were among the three dogs to survive the sinking of the Titanic in 1912.

Appearance

Pomeranians are small dogs weighing 1.95–3.5 kilograms and standing 13–28 centimetres high at the withers. They have a distinctive wedge-shaped head, prick ears, a curled tail carried flat over the back, and a profuse double coat with a frill of fur around the neck. Recognised colours include orange, red, black, white, cream, blue, sable, chocolate, and parti-colour combinations; traditional breed standards list as many as two dozen colours and patterns.

Temperament and health

The breed is lively, intelligent, extroverted and often bold despite its size; Pomeranians are alert watchdogs but can be territorial and bark-prone, and require early socialisation. Health concerns include luxating patella, tracheal collapse, hypothyroidism, and the skin condition alopecia X ("black skin disease"). With proper care they commonly live 12 to 16 years.
