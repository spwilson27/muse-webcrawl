Florence

Florence (Italian: Firenze) is the capital city of the Italian region of Tuscany. It is the most populous city in Tuscany, with about 380,000 inhabitants in the city proper and over 1,520,000 in its metropolitan area. Florence was a centre of medieval European trade and finance and one of the wealthiest cities of that era. It is considered by many academics to have been the birthplace of the Renaissance, becoming a major artistic, cultural, commercial, political, economic and financial center.

History

Florence originated as a Roman city, Florentia, founded in 59 BCE. It grew into a major banking and cloth-manufacturing centre in the Middle Ages; its gold coin, the florin, became a standard currency of European trade. From 1434 the city was dominated by the Medici family, whose patronage funded artists including Botticelli, Leonardo da Vinci and Michelangelo. The republic was repeatedly restored and overthrown before Florence became capital of the Duchy and then Grand Duchy of Tuscany. Between 1865 and 1871 Florence served as capital of the newly unified Kingdom of Italy.

Art and architecture

The historic centre is dominated by Florence Cathedral (the Duomo) with Brunelleschi's great dome, completed in 1436, and Giotto's campanile. Other landmarks include the Palazzo Vecchio, the Palazzo Pitti, the Basilica of Santa Croce, and the Ponte Vecchio over the Arno. The Uffizi Gallery holds one of the world's greatest collections of Renaissance painting, and the Galleria dell'Accademia houses Michelangelo's David.

Economy and tourism

Tourism is the most significant industry in central Florence, with millions of visitors each year. The city is also known for fashion — houses such as Gucci and Ferragamo were founded there — and for leather goods, wine from the surrounding Chianti region, and art restoration.
