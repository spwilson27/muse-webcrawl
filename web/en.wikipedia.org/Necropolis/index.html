Necropolis

A necropolis (from the Ancient Greek νεκρόπολις, "city of the dead") is a large, designed cemetery with elaborate tomb monuments. The term usually implies a separate burial site near an ancient city and is most often used for sites of the ancient world.

History

Famous necropolises include the Theban Necropolis opposite Luxor in Egypt, which contains the Valley of the Kings; the Etruscan necropolises of Cerveteri and Tarquinia in Italy; and the Kerameikos in Athens. Necropolises often had temples, roads and facilities for funerary rites and commemorative feasts.

Modern usage

The word is sometimes applied to large modern cemeteries, such as the Glasgow Necropolis, a Victorian garden cemetery in Scotland.
