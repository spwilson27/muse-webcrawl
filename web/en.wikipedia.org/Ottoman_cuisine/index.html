Ottoman cuisine

RegionOttoman Empire; modern Turkey, Balkans, Levant, Egypt
CentrePalace kitchens of Istanbul (Topkapı)

Ottoman cuisine is the cuisine of the Ottoman Empire and its successor regions, blending Central Asian Turkic traditions with the ingredients and techniques of Anatolia, the Balkans, the Levant, Egypt, and the palace kitchens of Istanbul. It is the foundation of modern Turkish cuisine and strongly influenced the cooking of Greece, the Middle East, and southeastern Europe.

Palace kitchen

The kitchens of Topkapı Palace employed hundreds of cooks organised into specialised sections for kebabs, pilavs, pastries, helva, and sherbets. Palace demand drove refinement of dishes such as stuffed vegetables (dolma), kebabs, börek, baklava, and rice pilavs, and spread them throughout the empire.

Characteristics

Characteristic elements include kebabs and köfte; soups; olive-oil vegetable dishes (zeytinyağlı); yoghurt as a sauce and drink (ayran); syrup-soaked pastries; and, from the sixteenth century onward, coffee culture centred on the coffeehouse. Ingredients vary by region: seafood on the coasts, lamb and grains inland, and aubergines, tomatoes, and peppers after their arrival from the Americas.

Legacy

Many dishes now claimed by various national cuisines — kebab, baklava, stuffed vine leaves, Turkish delight — descend from the shared Ottoman table, and their naming and origin remain culturally contested.
