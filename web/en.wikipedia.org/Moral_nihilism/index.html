Moral nihilism

Moral nihilism (also known as ethical nihilism) is the metaethical view that nothing is morally right or wrong, good or bad. It holds that there are no objective moral facts, values, duties or virtues. Moral nihilism is distinct from moral relativism, which allows moral claims to be true or false relative to a culture or individual.

FieldMetaethics
Related toError theory, nihilism
ProponentsJ. L. Mackie, Richard Joyce

Error theory

The most influential form of moral nihilism is error theory, developed by J. L. Mackie in Ethics: Inventing Right and Wrong (1977). Mackie argued that ordinary moral discourse presupposes objectively prescriptive values, but since nothing in the world answers to that description, moral claims are all false.

Other forms

Some moral nihilists adopt a non-cognitivist or fictionalist stance: they continue using moral language while treating it as a useful fiction rather than a description of reality.

Criticism

Critics object that moral nihilism has counterintuitive practical consequences, undermines blame and responsibility, and relies on controversial arguments such as the argument from queerness.
