The Tour of Qinghai Lake is an annual professional road bicycle racing stage race held in Qinghai Province, China, since 2002. The race is named after Qinghai Lake, China's largest lake, around which much of the course runs. It is part of the UCI Asia Tour and has been rated at the highest level (2.HC, later UCI ProSeries) for a race in Asia.

Course and conditions

The race is notable for its extreme altitude: stages take place on the Tibetan Plateau at elevations generally between 3,000 and 4,000 metres (around 3,200 m at the lake itself), making it one of the highest-altitude races in professional cycling. The thin air, harsh sun, and strong winds pose unusual demands on riders, and teams typically arrive early to acclimatize. Editions have usually comprised around nine to thirteen stages totalling well over 1,000 kilometres, frequently beginning in the provincial capital, Xining.

History

The event was created to promote tourism and sport in Qinghai province, coinciding with China's growing investment in cycling. It has attracted UCI WorldTour and Professional Continental teams alongside Asian continental squads, and sprinters have often dominated its flat lakeside stages, while high mountain stages cross passes above 3,800 metres. The race did not run in 2020–2022 owing to the COVID-19 pandemic before returning to the calendar.
