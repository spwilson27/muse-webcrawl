Location: North West England
Between: River Mersey and River Dee
Main town: Birkenhead

Wirral Peninsula

The Wirral Peninsula, known locally as the Wirral, is a roughly rectangular peninsula in North West England, about 15 miles (24 km) long and 7 miles (11 km) wide. It is bounded by the River Dee to the west, the River Mersey to the east, and the Irish Sea to the north. Most of the peninsula forms the Metropolitan Borough of Wirral in Merseyside, with southern areas in Cheshire West and Chester.

Geography and settlements

The largest town is Birkenhead, a historic shipbuilding and port town facing Liverpool across the Mersey, connected by the Mersey railway and road tunnels. Other towns include Wallasey, Wirral's coastal resorts of New Brighton and West Kirby, the planned village of Port Sunlight, and Heswall and Neston on the Dee side. Hilbre Island, off West Kirby, is a tidal island and nature reserve. A ridge of sandstone runs along the peninsula, reaching its highest point on Poll Hill in Heswall.

History

The name Wirral derives from Old English Wirheal, "myrtle corner." The area was settled by Norse Vikings from Ireland in the 10th century, whose presence survives in place names such as Thingwall (from þingvǫllr, assembly field) and Meols. Birkenhead Priory was founded around 1150. In the 19th century the peninsula changed rapidly with shipbuilding at Cammell Laird, the docks, and suburban growth as Liverpool merchants moved across the river.
