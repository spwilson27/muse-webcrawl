Sierra de Guadarrama

The Sierra de Guadarrama (Guadarrama Mountains) is a mountain range forming the main eastern section of the Sistema Central, the system of mountain ranges at the centre of the Iberian Peninsula. It is located between the systems Sierra de Gredos to the southwest and Sierra de Ayllón to the northeast, in the Spanish provinces of Segovia, Ávila, and the Community of Madrid.

The range runs southwest–northeast, extends approximately 80 kilometres (50 mi), and its highest peak, Peñalara, reaches 2,428 metres (7,966 ft). The Guadarrama forms the watershed between the basins of the Tagus to the south and the Duero to the north, and provides Madrid with much of its water supply and a nearby zone of summer recreation, skiing, and mountaineering.

Geology and landscape

The range is composed principally of granite and gneiss raised during the Alpine orogeny. Glacial action during the Pleistocene sculpted cirques and small lagoons on the northern face of Peñalara, the best preserved glacial remains in the Sistema Central. Passes such as Navacerrada and Somosierra have historically linked the two Meseta plateaus.

Flora and fauna

Slopes are covered with Scots pine forests—among them the celebrated pinewoods of Valsaín—melting into high-mountain scrub above about 1,900 m. The range hosts Spanish ibex (reintroduced), roe deer, wild boar, Spanish imperial eagles, and griffon and cinereous vultures.

Protected areas

In 2013, much of the range was declared the Sierra de Guadarrama National Park, the fifteenth and one of the largest national parks in Spain, protecting about 34,000 hectares across Madrid and Castile and León. The Peñalara massif had earlier been protected as a natural park.
