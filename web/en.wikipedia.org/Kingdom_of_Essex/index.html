Kingdom of Essex

The Kingdom of Essex, or Kingdom of the East Saxons, was one of the seven traditional kingdoms of the Anglo-Saxon Heptarchy. Founded in the 6th century, it covered territory roughly matching the later county of Essex together with Middlesex, Hertfordshire and for a period Surrey, with London as an important centre.

History

Its first recorded king was Æscwine, dated to around 527. King Sæberht converted to Christianity in the early 7th century under the influence of his uncle Æthelberht of Kent, and St Paul's in London was founded in this period. Essex later fell under the overlordship of Mercia and then Wessex, and by the 9th century it had effectively been absorbed into the expanding West Saxon kingdom.

Kings

The king list, reconstructed from Bede, charters and later chronicles, includes Sledd, Sæberht, Sigeberht the Good and Sigeheard. Many reigns are obscure, and co-kingship appears to have been common.
