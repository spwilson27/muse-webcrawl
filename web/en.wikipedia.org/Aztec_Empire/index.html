The Aztec Empire, also known as the Triple Alliance, was an alliance of three Nahua city-states — Tenochtitlan, Texcoco and Tlacopan — that dominated the Valley of Mexico and much of Mesoamerica from 1428 until its conquest by Spanish forces under Hernán Cortés in 1521. The Mexica of Tenochtitlan were the senior partners, and the empire's ruler, the tlatoani, resided there.

Rise

The Mexica founded Tenochtitlan on an island in Lake Texcoco in 1325 (by tradition). In 1428 the ruler Itzcoatl allied with Texcoco and Tlacopan to overthrow the Tepanec empire of Azcapotzalco, founding the Triple Alliance. Successive tlatoani — Moctezuma I, Ahuitzotl, Moctezuma II — expanded the empire through warfare, extracting tribute in goods, warriors and sacrificial victims from subject provinces stretching from the Gulf Coast to the Pacific.

Society

Tenochtitlan, with perhaps 200,000 inhabitants, was among the largest cities in the world, laid out on causeways and canals with the great pyramid of the Templo Mayor at its centre. Society was stratified between nobles (pipiltin) and commoners (macehualtin); agriculture relied on highly productive chinampa raised beds, and markets such as Tlatelolco drew tens of thousands daily. The Aztecs used a pictorial writing system and an accurate calendar.

Conquest

Cortés landed in 1519 and, allying with the Tlaxcalteca and other enemies of the empire, reached Tenochtitlan, where Moctezuma II died in contested circumstances in 1520. After the Spanish expulsion of La Noche Triste and a devastating smallpox epidemic, Cortés besieged the city; it fell on 13 August 1521 with the capture of the last tlatoani, Cuauhtémoc.
