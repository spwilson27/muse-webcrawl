Computational geometry

Computational geometry is a branch of computer science devoted to the study of algorithms that can be stated in terms of geometry. Some purely geometrical problems arise from the study of computational geometric algorithms, and such problems are also considered part of computational geometry.

The field emerged in the 1970s as geometric problems from computer graphics, robotics, geographic information systems, and computer-aided design demanded efficient algorithmic solutions.

Core problems

Classical problems include computing the convex hull of a set of points, finding intersections among line segments, constructing Voronoi diagrams and Delaunay triangulations, nearest-neighbour search, point location, and polygon triangulation. Many of these admit optimal algorithms running in O(n log n) time, and sweeping techniques such as the plane sweep are characteristic tools of the field.

Applications

Computational geometry underlies computer graphics and ray tracing, robotics motion planning, geographic information systems, integrated circuit design, and molecular modelling. Libraries such as CGAL provide robust implementations of geometric algorithms for practical use.
