Beaver Wars

Datec. 1609–1701
LocationGreat Lakes and Ohio Country, North America
CombatantsIroquois Confederacy vs. Algonquian peoples and other nations, with French involvement
Ended byGreat Peace of Montreal, 1701

The Beaver Wars, also known as the Iroquois Wars or the French and Iroquois Wars, were a series of conflicts fought intermittently during the 17th century in eastern North America. Encouraged and armed by their Dutch and later English trading partners, the peoples of the Iroquois Confederacy (Haudenosaunee) sought to expand their territory and monopolise the fur trade, particularly in beaver pelts, at the expense of neighbouring Indigenous nations allied with the French.

Course of the wars

By the 1640s the Iroquois had depleted beaver in their own homeland south of Lake Ontario and needed new hunting grounds. Armed with Dutch firearms from Fort Orange, Mohawk, Seneca and allied war parties destroyed the Huron (Wendat) Confederacy in 1649, dispersing its people, and went on to defeat the Neutral Nation, the Erie and the Susquehannock. The wars also had a "mourning war" dimension: captives were taken to replace population lost to disease and conflict. French royal troops intervened in the 1660s, and fighting shifted to raids on settlements in New France and Iroquois country.

Consequences

The wars reshaped the human geography of the Great Lakes and Ohio Valley, depopulating large areas that became contested hunting grounds. The Great Peace of Montreal in 1701, negotiated between New France, the Iroquois and some forty Indigenous nations, ended the conflict and established Iroquois neutrality in later Anglo-French wars.
