The Fourth International (FI) is an international organisation of Trotskyist political parties and groups, founded in 1938 by Leon Trotsky and his supporters as an alternative to the Communist International (Comintern), which they regarded as having degenerated under the leadership of Joseph Stalin. Its founding programme, the Transitional Programme, was drafted by Trotsky and adopted at the founding congress held near Paris.

The organisation grew out of the International Left Opposition, formed in 1930 by Trotskyists expelled from or opposed to the Communist parties aligned with Moscow. Trotsky argued that the Comintern had become an instrument of the Soviet bureaucracy and was incapable of leading the working class to power, pointing to its failure to prevent the rise of Adolf Hitler in Germany.

Founding

The founding congress of the Fourth International took place on 3 September 1938 at the home of Alfred Rosmer in Périgny, France. Delegates represented organisations from eleven countries, though many leading figures were absent: several had been murdered during the Great Purge, and Trotsky himself, exiled in Mexico, was represented by an empty chair. The congress adopted the Transitional Programme, formally titled The Death Agony of Capitalism and the Tasks of the Fourth International.

History

During the Second World War the International was weakened by internal disputes, repression and the assassination of Trotsky in August 1940 by the NKVD agent Ramón Mercader. After the war, its leadership was centred on figures such as Michel Pablo and Ernest Mandel. In 1953 the organisation split over questions of strategy, including "entrism sui generis" in Communist parties, producing the International Secretariat of the Fourth International (ISFI) and the International Committee of the Fourth International (ICFI).

In 1963 the majority currents reunified to form the United Secretariat of the Fourth International, which remains the largest body claiming the FI name and has been associated with figures such as Tariq Ali and with parties including the French Ligue communiste révolutionnaire and its successor tradition, the Nouveau Parti anticapitaliste. Other currents, including the Lambertist and Morenoist traditions and the International Workers League, maintain separate organisations that also claim continuity with the Fourth International.

Programme

The Transitional Programme combines immediate demands—such as a sliding scale of wages and hours, workers' control of production and the expropriation of major industries—with the goal of establishing workers' governments. Trotskyists characterise their method as permanent revolution: the theory that democratic tasks in less developed countries can only be completed through socialist revolution led by the working class.
