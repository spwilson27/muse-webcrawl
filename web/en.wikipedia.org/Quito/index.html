Quito, officially San Francisco de Quito, is the capital and second-most populous city of Ecuador, and the capital of Pichincha Province. Located high in the Andes at an elevation of 2,850 metres on the slopes of the Pichincha volcano, it is the second-highest capital city in the world and lies just south of the equator.

History

The area was an important centre of the Quitu and later Inca worlds. Sebastián de Benalcázar founded the Spanish city on 6 December 1534. Quito became a major colonial administrative and artistic centre; its churches and monasteries fostered the Quito School of art. On 10 August 1809 the city issued one of the first calls for independence in Spanish America, and Ecuador's independence was sealed at the Battle of Pichincha in 1822.

Historic centre

Quito's Old Town, with its churches of San Francisco, La Compañía, and Santo Domingo and its preserved colonial streetscape, was among the first places inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1978.

Geography and climate

Despite its equatorial location, altitude gives Quito a mild spring-like climate year-round. The city stretches along a narrow valley beneath Pichincha; landmarks include El Panecillo hill, the Basílica del Voto Nacional, and the TelefériQo cable car. The modern Mariscal Sucre International Airport opened in 2013 east of the city.
