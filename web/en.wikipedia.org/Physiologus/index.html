Physiologus

The Physiologus is an early Christian didactic text, probably compiled in Alexandria in the 2nd century AD, describing the characteristics of animals, birds, and fantastic creatures, each followed by a moral or allegorical Christian interpretation. It is the ancestor of the medieval bestiaries.

Contents

The text treats some fifty creatures and phenomena, including the lion, the pelican, the phoenix, the unicorn (monoceros), the antelope and the caladrius. Each chapter typically gives a brief account of the animal's supposed nature and then draws a theological lesson; thus the pelican, said to revive its young with its own blood, becomes a figure of Christ.

Influence

Translated into Latin, Syriac, Armenian, Ethiopic, Arabic and many European vernaculars, the Physiologus shaped medieval natural lore and iconography for more than a thousand years. Its descriptions underpin the animal symbolism of Romanesque and Gothic art and the later illustrated bestiaries of the 12th and 13th centuries.
