Arrow–Debreu model

The Arrow–Debreu model is a mathematical model of a market economy in general equilibrium theory, developed by Kenneth Arrow and Gérard Debreu in 1954. It provides conditions under which a competitive equilibrium exists for an economy with many goods, dates, and states of the world, and it underlies the two fundamental theorems of welfare economics.

Overview

In the model, each commodity is distinguished by its physical properties, location, date, and the state of nature in which it is delivered. Consumers maximise utility subject to budget constraints, firms maximise profit, and prices adjust so that all markets clear simultaneously. Arrow and Debreu proved existence of such an equilibrium using fixed-point theorems, assuming convex preferences and production sets, no externalities, and complete markets.

Significance

The model gave rigorous form to the idea of Adam Smith's "invisible hand": under its assumptions, competitive equilibria are Pareto efficient. It also highlighted, through the Sonnenschein–Mantel–Debreu results, that aggregate excess demand functions are largely unrestricted, limiting the model's predictions about uniqueness and stability of equilibrium.
