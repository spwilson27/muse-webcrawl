Great Sioux War of 1876

DateFebruary 1876 – May 1877
LocationMontana, Dakota and Wyoming Territories
ResultUnited States victory; seizure of the Black Hills
BelligerentsUnited States vs. Lakota Sioux, Northern Cheyenne, Arapaho

The Great Sioux War of 1876, also known as the Black Hills War, was a series of battles and negotiations that occurred in 1876 and 1877 in an alliance of Lakota Sioux and Northern Cheyenne against the United States. The cause of the war was the desire of the U.S. government to obtain ownership of the Black Hills, where gold had been discovered, and its ultimatum ordering all Sioux bands onto reservations by January 31, 1876 — a deadline impossible to meet in midwinter.

Course of the war

Large encampments of "non-treaty" Indians gathered under leaders including Sitting Bull and Crazy Horse in the unceded territory of Montana. The army mounted converging campaigns in the spring and summer of 1876. On June 17, Crazy Horse fought General George Crook to a standstill at the Rosebud; on June 25–26, Lieutenant Colonel George Armstrong Custer and five companies of the 7th Cavalry were annihilated at the Battle of the Little Bighorn, the war's most famous engagement and a stunning Indian victory.

Defeat and surrender

The defeat shocked the American public and brought massive reinforcements. Through the autumn and winter the army relentlessly pursued the bands, destroying villages and food stores, as at the Dull Knife Fight in November. Congress cut off rations to force cession of the Black Hills. Starving and fragmented, most bands surrendered in the spring of 1877; Crazy Horse surrendered in May and was killed that September, while Sitting Bull escaped to Canada before returning in 1881.

Legacy

The war ended organized Lakota resistance in the northern plains and secured the Black Hills for the United States, a taking the Supreme Court a century later ruled unconstitutional, in a case the Sioux have never settled.
