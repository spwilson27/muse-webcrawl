Identity of indiscernibles

The identity of indiscernibles is an ontological principle associated with Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz, stating that there cannot be separate objects or entities that have all their properties in common. If two things share every property, they are in fact one and the same thing.

Formulation

The principle is often expressed as: for any objects x and y, if x and y share all properties, then x is identical to y. Its converse, the indiscernibility of identicals — that identical things share all their properties — is generally accepted and underlies Leibniz's law in logic.

Discussion

Philosophers have debated whether purely qualitative properties suffice for the principle, or whether spatial and temporal properties may be included. Max Black's thought experiment of a universe containing only two exactly similar spheres is a well-known challenge to the principle.
