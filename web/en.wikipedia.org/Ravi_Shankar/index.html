Ravi Shankar

Pandit Ravi Shankar
Born: 7 April 1920, Benares (Varanasi)
Died: 11 December 2012, San Diego, California
Instrument: Sitar
Awards: Bharat Ratna, five Grammy Awards

Ravi Shankar (born Robindro Shaunkor Chowdhury; 7 April 1920 – 11 December 2012) was an Indian sitar virtuoso and composer of Hindustani classical music. He was the best-known ambassador of Indian music to the Western world, influencing musicians from Yehudi Menuhin to George Harrison, and is often called the "godfather of world music".

Early life and training

Shankar was born in Benares into a Bengali Brahmin family. As a boy he toured Europe and America with his elder brother Uday Shankar's dance troupe, then gave up dancing in 1938 to study sitar intensively under the court musician Allauddin Khan at Maihar, living with his guru for seven years of rigorous training. He composed music for All India Radio and for Satyajit Ray's Apu Trilogy films, and founded the Kinnara School of Music.

International career

Shankar's collaborations made him famous in the West: recordings with violinist Yehudi Menuhin (West Meets East), the Byrds and especially George Harrison of the Beatles, who studied sitar with him in 1966. He performed at the Monterey Pop Festival (1967), Woodstock (1969) and the Concert for Bangladesh (1971), which he co-organised with Harrison. He later composed concertos for sitar and orchestra, an opera, and scores for films including Gandhi.

Honours and legacy

India awarded him its highest civilian honour, the Bharat Ratna, in 1999. He won five Grammy Awards, including a posthumous Lifetime Achievement Award, served in India's Rajya Sabha (1986–1992), and founded the Ravi Shankar Centre in New Delhi. His daughters Norah Jones and Anoushka Shankar are both acclaimed musicians.
