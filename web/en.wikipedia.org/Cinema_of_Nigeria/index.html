Cinema of Nigeria

The cinema of Nigeria, often referred to informally as Nollywood, is one of the largest film industries in the world by volume of output, producing around 2,500 films a year. Nigeria's film industry, which emerged in its modern form in the early 1990s with direct-to-video productions, surpassed Hollywood in output volume by 2009 and ranks behind only India's Bollywood in the number of annual film productions.

History

Filmmaking in Nigeria dates to the colonial era, when the Colonial Film Unit produced newsreels and documentaries. After independence in 1960, directors such as Ola Balogun, Eddie Ugbomah, and Hubert Ogunde made celebrated celluloid films in the 1970s and early 1980s, but economic crisis and the collapse of cinema infrastructure stalled the industry.

The video-film boom

The 1992 release of Living in Bondage, a straight-to-VHS Igbo-language thriller produced by Kenneth Nnebue, inaugurated the Nollywood model: films shot on video in a matter of weeks on small budgets and sold directly to consumers through markets in Lagos, Onitsha, and Aba. Hausa-language productions from the north developed their own industry, called Kannywood, centered on Kano.

New Nollywood

From the late 2000s, a "New Nollywood" movement raised production values, returned films to cinemas, and won international festival recognition. Streaming platforms such as Netflix and Amazon Prime began commissioning Nigerian originals, while directors like Kunle Afolayan, Genevieve Nnaji, and Kemi Adetiba gained global profiles. Nigeria submitted films for Academy Award consideration beginning in 2019.
