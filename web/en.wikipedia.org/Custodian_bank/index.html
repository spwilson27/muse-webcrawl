Custodian bank

A custodian bank, or simply custodian, is a specialised financial institution responsible for safeguarding the financial assets of its customers to minimise the risk of theft or loss. Custodians hold assets such as stocks, bonds, and commodities in electronic or physical form on behalf of institutional investors, asset managers, pension funds, and other clients.

Services

Beyond safekeeping, custodians settle securities trades, collect dividends and interest, handle tax withholding and reclaims, process corporate actions such as stock splits and mergers, and provide foreign exchange and cash management. Global custodians operate through networks of sub-custodians in foreign markets so clients can hold assets in many countries through a single relationship.

Role in the financial system

The largest custodian banks, such as the Bank of New York Mellon and State Street, each hold tens of trillions of dollars in assets under custody. Custody assets are held off the custodian's balance sheet and belong to the clients, so they are generally protected if the custodian fails. Custodians also provide fund administration services including accounting and net asset value calculation for mutual funds and hedge funds.

Regulation

In the United States, registered investment advisers are generally required to hold client assets with a qualified custodian under the Investment Advisers Act. Custody of cryptocurrency and other digital assets has become a growing business line, with regulators setting rules for how such assets must be segregated and secured.
