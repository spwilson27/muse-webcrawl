Shipyard

Purpose: Ship construction and repair
Facilities: Dry docks, slipways, cranes, workshops
Major centres: South Korea, China, Japan, Europe

A shipyard, also called a dockyard, is a place where ships are built and repaired. Shipyards are located on coasts or major rivers near deep water, and range from huge industrial complexes building supertankers and aircraft carriers to small yards servicing local fishing fleets.

Facilities

A modern shipyard centres on dry docks or slipways where hulls are assembled, large gantry cranes for lifting prefabricated blocks, and workshops for steel cutting, welding, pipework, electrical fitting and outfitting. Repair yards add facilities for cleaning, inspection and refit.

Construction process

Contemporary shipbuilding assembles a vessel from prefabricated steel sections or "blocks", which are welded together in the dock before the ship is floated out, outfitted with machinery and systems, and completed through sea trials.

Industry

In the twenty-first century most large merchant shipbuilding is concentrated in East Asia, particularly China, South Korea and Japan, while European yards specialise in cruise ships, ferries and naval vessels. Naval dockyards also maintain fleets for national navies.
