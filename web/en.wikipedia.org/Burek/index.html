Burek

TypeFilled pastry
RegionBalkans, Turkey, Middle East, Central Asia
Main ingredientsFilo (yufka) dough; ground meat, cheese, spinach or potato
VariationsBörek, burek, byrek, boureka, brik

Burek is a family of baked or fried filled pastries made of thin flaky dough such as filo (yufka), found throughout the Balkans, Turkey, the Levant, North Africa and Central Asia. It derives from the Turkish börek, and its spread is closely tied to the cuisine of the Ottoman Empire, which carried the dish across its territories.

Varieties

Fillings include seasoned ground meat, white cheese, spinach, potato and, in sweet versions, custard or fruit. In Bosnia and Herzegovina, the name burek is reserved strictly for the meat-filled spiral, while cheese, spinach and potato versions are called sirnica, zeljanica and krompiruša — a distinction that is a matter of lively local pride. Related forms include Albanian byrek, Bulgarian banitsa (made with whisked eggs and yoghurt), Greek boureki and tiropita, Sephardic bourekas, Armenian byorek and the fried North African brik.

Preparation

The dough is stretched or rolled until paper-thin, brushed with oil or butter, spread with filling, then rolled into spirals, coils or cigars, or layered in a large round pan and cut into wedges after baking. Burek is eaten at any time of day, sold by bakeries and dedicated burek shops (buregdžinica) across the Balkans, and traditionally accompanied by drinking yoghurt. See also: Filo, Ottoman cuisine.
