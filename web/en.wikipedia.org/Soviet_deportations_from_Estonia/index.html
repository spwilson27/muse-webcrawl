Soviet deportations from Estonia


Memorial to deportation victims

Soviet deportations from Estonia were a series of mass deportations carried out by the Soviet Union from Estonia in 1941 and 1949, following the Soviet occupation of the Baltic states under the terms of the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact. Tens of thousands of Estonian citizens were forcibly removed to remote regions of the Soviet Union, principally Siberia and Kazakhstan.

June deportation of 1941

After Estonia was occupied and annexed by the USSR in 1940, Soviet security organs compiled lists of "anti-Soviet elements" — officials of the former republic, military officers, police, landowners, business owners, and members of voluntary organizations. In the night of 13–14 June 1941, more than 10,000 people were arrested and loaded onto freight trains; men were generally sent to labour camps in the Gulag, while women and children were exiled to remote settlements in Siberia. Many died of starvation, cold, disease, and overwork.

Operation Priboi, 1949

The second great wave, code-named Operation Priboi, took place across all three Baltic states on 25 March 1949. Roughly 20,700 people from Estonia — about two percent of the population, predominantly farming families resisting collectivization — were deported to Siberia and Kazakhstan. The operation was intended to break resistance to the collectivization of agriculture and to support Sovietization.

Aftermath

The deportations, together with emigration, war losses, and executions, cost Estonia a substantial share of its population. Survivors were permitted to return gradually after Stalin's death in 1953, but many remained in exile for decades. In independent Estonia, 14 June and 25 March are observed as days of mourning and remembrance.
