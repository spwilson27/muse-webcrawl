Bench (sports)

In sports, the bench is the seating area, usually at the side of the playing field or court, where substitute players and team staff sit during a game. By extension, "the bench" refers collectively to a team's reserve players — those not currently in the starting lineup — and a player's role may be described as "coming off the bench" when they enter a game as a substitute.

Usage by sport

In association football, substitutes and coaching staff occupy a technical area and bench (often with individual moulded seats in modern stadiums) beside the pitch; the number of named substitutes varies by competition. In ice hockey, players sit on benches inside the boards, entering and leaving the ice "on the fly," and a separate penalty bench — the penalty box — holds players serving penalties. In basketball, the bench sits along the sideline, and "bench points" (scoring by non-starters) is a standard statistic; the NBA's Sixth Man of the Year Award honours the best reserve player. In baseball, reserves typically occupy the dugout rather than a bench proper, though bullpen pitchers warm up away from it.

Related terms

To "bench" a player is to remove them from participation, whether as a tactical decision, for disciplinary reasons, or because of poor form. A "bench warmer" is a pejorative term for a player who rarely plays. In some sports, notably ice hockey, collective fights involving players leaving the bench are called "bench-clearing brawls" and are heavily penalized. The term is also used figuratively outside sport, for example for reserve judges.
