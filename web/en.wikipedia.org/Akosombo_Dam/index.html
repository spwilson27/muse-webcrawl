Akosombo Dam

The Akosombo Dam, also known as the Akosombo Hydroelectric Project, is a hydroelectric dam on the Volta River in southeastern Ghana. Completed in 1965, the rock-fill embankment dam created Lake Volta, one of the largest artificial lakes in the world by surface area. The dam's power station has an installed capacity of about 1,020 megawatts and supplies electricity to Ghana and neighbouring countries.

History

The idea of damming the Volta River dated to the colonial era, but the project was realised under Ghana's first president, Kwame Nkrumah, as the centrepiece of the Volta River Project. Construction began in 1961, carried out by an Italian consortium (Impregilo), and the dam was completed in 1965. Financing came from Ghana, the World Bank, the United States and the United Kingdom.

Power generation

The power station originally housed four 227 MW Francis turbine generators; two additional units were later added, raising capacity. Much of the electricity was committed to the Volta Aluminum Company (VALCO) smelter at Tema, with the remainder supplying Ghana's national grid and exports to Togo, Benin and Burkina Faso.

Impact

Lake Volta covers roughly 8,500 km² and displaced about 80,000 people from some 740 villages. The lake transformed transport and fishing in Ghana but also brought ecological change and the spread of waterborne diseases such as schistosomiasis near its shores.
