Sir Michael Francis Atiyah (22 April 1929 – 11 January 2019) was a British-Lebanese mathematician specialising in geometry. One of the most influential mathematicians of the 20th century, he is best known for the Atiyah–Singer index theorem and for deepening the connections between mathematics and theoretical physics.

Life and career

Atiyah was born in London to a Lebanese father, the writer Edward Atiyah, and a Scottish mother. He was educated in Egypt and at Manchester Grammar School, then studied at Trinity College, Cambridge, completing his doctorate under William Hodge. After posts at Cambridge, Oxford (where he held the Savilian Chair of Geometry) and the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton, he became Master of Trinity College, Cambridge, president of the Royal Society (1990–1995), and later an honorary professor at the University of Edinburgh.

Work

With Isadore Singer he proved the Atiyah–Singer index theorem (1963), linking analysis, geometry and topology and opening new bridges to quantum field theory — work for which they shared the 2004 Abel Prize. Atiyah helped develop K-theory with Friedrich Hirzebruch, worked on instantons and monopoles with Nigel Hitchin and others, and influenced string theory and gauge theory. In 1966 he received the Fields Medal. In 2018 he attracted widespread attention for a claimed proof of the Riemann hypothesis that was not accepted by the mathematical community.
