Catuvellauni

The Catuvellauni were a Celtic tribe or state of southeastern Britain before the Roman conquest, attested from the late 2nd century BC. Their territory lay in the modern counties of Hertfordshire, Bedfordshire and southern Cambridgeshire, north of the River Thames, and their lands bordered those of the Trinovantes to the east.

History

The Catuvellauni are first mentioned by Cassius Dio in connection with resistance to Julius Caesar's invasion of Britain in 54 BC, when they were led by Cassivellaunus. Under Tasciovanus (c. 20 BC – AD 10) they established a capital at Verlamion, later the Roman city of Verulamium near modern St Albans, and began minting inscribed coins. Tasciovanus's son Cunobelinus expanded Catuvellaunian power, conquering the Trinovantes and making Camulodunum (Colchester) a major centre; Shakespeare later immortalised him as Cymbeline.

Roman conquest

Expansion under Cunobelinus's sons Togodumnus and Caratacus, including the annexation of territory of the Atrebates, provided a pretext for the Roman invasion of AD 43 under Claudius. After defeat, Caratacus fled west and continued resistance in Wales. The tribal territory became part of the Roman province of Britannia, and the Catuvellauni were administered as a civitas with its capital at Verulamium.

Coinage

The Catuvellauni produced some of the most elaborate coinages of Iron Age Britain, including gold staters and inscribed issues naming rulers such as Tasciovanus, Cunobelinus, and Epaticcus.
