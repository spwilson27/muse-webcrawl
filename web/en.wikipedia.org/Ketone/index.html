Ketone

In organic chemistry, a ketone is an organic compound with the structure R−C(=O)−R′, where R and R′ are carbon-containing substituents. Ketones contain a carbonyl group (C=O) bonded to two carbon atoms, distinguishing them from aldehydes, in which the carbonyl is bonded to at least one hydrogen.

Nomenclature and examples

Ketones are named with the suffix -one (e.g. propanone). The simplest ketone is acetone (propanone), a common solvent. Other important ketones include butanone (methyl ethyl ketone), cyclohexanone (a precursor to nylon), and the ketone bodies produced in metabolism.

Properties and synthesis

Ketones are polar, can act as hydrogen-bond acceptors, and undergo characteristic reactions such as nucleophilic addition, reduction to secondary alcohols, and enolate formation at the α-carbon. They are commonly prepared by oxidation of secondary alcohols, Friedel–Crafts acylation, and ozonolysis of alkenes. Ketones are widely used as solvents and as intermediates in the chemical and pharmaceutical industries.
