Adventures of Huckleberry Finn

Adventures of Huckleberry Finn is a novel by American author Mark Twain, first published in the United Kingdom in December 1884 and in the United States in February 1885. Narrated in the vernacular first-person voice of its teenage protagonist, Huck Finn, it is a sequel to The Adventures of Tom Sawyer and is widely regarded as one of the Great American Novels.

Plot

Huck, chafing under civilisation in St. Petersburg, Missouri, and fleeing his violent alcoholic father, fakes his own death and runs away to Jackson's Island, where he joins Jim, an enslaved man fleeing his owner Miss Watson. Together they raft down the Mississippi River toward the free states, meeting frauds (the "Duke" and the "King"), a deadly family feud, and a scheme to steal an inheritance. The novel's moral climax comes when Huck decides to help free Jim even believing it will damn him: "All right, then, I'll go to hell." The ending, in which Tom Sawyer elaborately "frees" the already-freed Jim, has long been debated by critics.

Reception

Ernest Hemingway wrote that "all modern American literature comes from one book by Mark Twain called Huckleberry Finn." The book has also been persistently controversial — banned in some schools from its own time onward, and frequently challenged in recent decades for its racial language and portrayal of race, even as scholars defend its anti-racist satire.
