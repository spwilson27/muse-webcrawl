Ismailia

Ismailia (Arabic: al-Ismāʿīliyya) is a city in north-eastern Egypt, situated on the west bank of the Suez Canal at Lake Timsah, roughly midway between Port Said and Suez. It is the capital of the Ismailia Governorate and has a population of several hundred thousand.

History

The city was founded in 1863 by the Khedive Ismail Pasha, after whom it is named, as a headquarters settlement during the construction of the Suez Canal; Ferdinand de Lesseps, the canal's builder, had his house there. Ismailia's French-influenced planning produced wide, tree-lined boulevards and gardens, earning it the nickname "the bride of the Canal".

Later history

Ismailia was a base for British forces guarding the Canal Zone, and Egyptian nationalist resistance there — including a 1952 clash between Egyptian police and British troops — helped precipitate the Egyptian revolution of that year. The city was evacuated during the wars with Israel in 1967 and 1973 and later rebuilt. It hosts Suez Canal University and the headquarters of the Suez Canal Authority.
