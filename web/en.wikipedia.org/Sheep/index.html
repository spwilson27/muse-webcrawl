Sheep (Ovis aries) are domesticated ruminant mammals typically kept as livestock. One of the earliest animals domesticated for agricultural purposes, sheep were tamed from the wild mouflon of Southwest Asia roughly 11,000 to 9,000 years ago and have since spread worldwide, with over 1 billion alive at any time and more than a thousand recognised breeds. They are raised for their wool, meat, and milk, and figure prominently in the agriculture, culture, and symbolism of many societies.

Characteristics

Sheep are relatively small ruminants, usually with a crimped coat of hair called wool and, in many breeds, spiralling horns. Adult males are rams, females are ewes, and young are lambs. As ruminants they digest coarse forage through a four-chambered stomach. Sheep have excellent peripheral vision, a strong flocking instinct, and can recognise individual faces — of other sheep and of humans — for years. Lifespan is typically 10–12 years.

History and domestication

Genetic evidence points to domestication in the Fertile Crescent from the Asiatic mouflon; sheep remains appear at early farming villages in modern-day Turkey, Iran, and Iraq. Initially kept for meat, sheep were selectively bred over millennia for woolly fleeces (wild sheep have mostly hair) and became foundational to economies from Mesopotamia to medieval England, where wool exports funded churches and wars. Spanish Merino sheep, protected by royal monopoly, set the standard for fine wool; European colonisation carried sheep to the Americas, Australia, and New Zealand, where Australia became the world's leading wool exporter.

Husbandry and products

Raised under systems ranging from intensive lowland farming to transhumant pastoralism, sheep supply wool (about 2% of world textile fibre), lamb and mutton, sheep's milk for cheeses such as Roquefort, pecorino, and feta, and lanolin for cosmetics. Management includes shearing, usually annually, docking and drenching, and guarding flocks with shepherds and dogs. Sheep diseases of economic importance include scrapie (a prion disease), foot rot, and parasitic worms. In science, sheep were the first mammal successfully cloned from an adult cell — Dolly (born 1996) — and are used as model organisms in physiology and veterinary research.

Sheep in culture

Sheep symbolise gentleness, innocence, and sacrifice in the Abrahamic religions: the Passover lamb, the binding of Isaac, and Christian imagery of Christ as the Lamb of God and the Good Shepherd. "Sheep" appears in idioms for conformity ("to follow like sheep") and for the outcast ("black sheep"). Sheep are one of the twelve animals of the Chinese zodiac, and sheep husbandry shapes festivals and folklore in pastoral cultures worldwide.
