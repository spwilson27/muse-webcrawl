The Tokyo Game Show (東京ゲームショウ, TGS) is an annual video game trade fair held in Chiba, Japan, at the Makuhari Messe convention centre. Organised by the Computer Entertainment Supplier's Association (CESA), it is one of the world's largest gaming exhibitions, alongside events such as Gamescom. The show typically runs for four days, the first two reserved for industry and press and the last two open to the public, and it serves as a showcase for Japanese and international game developers, hardware makers and indie studios.

History

The first Tokyo Game Show was held in 1996; the event was initially staged twice a year (spring and autumn) before settling into an annual autumn schedule from 2002. Attendance grew steadily, peaking at around 270,000 visitors in the mid-2010s and reaching some 298,000 in 2018. During the COVID-19 pandemic the show moved online in 2020 and 2021, returning to a physical event in 2022. The 2023 show filled the entire Makuhari Messe for the first time in four years.

Format

Exhibitors include the major Japanese publishers — Nintendo has traditionally been a notable absentee from the show floor, while Sony, Square Enix, Capcom, Bandai Namco, Sega and Konami have been regular presences — together with platform holders, mobile and PC game companies, gaming schools and overseas pavilions. The show hosts the Japan Game Awards, including the prestigious Minister of Economy, Trade and Industry Award and the Future Division honours for upcoming titles. Indie games, esports, VR/AR and cosplay are prominent features of the public days.

Significance

As the flagship event of the Japanese games industry, TGS is a traditional venue for major announcements aimed at the domestic market and a barometer of trends in console, mobile and arcade gaming. Its September timing placed it near the start of the industry's peak release season.
