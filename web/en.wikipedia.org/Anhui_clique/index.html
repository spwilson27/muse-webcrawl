The Anhui clique (Chinese: 皖系軍閥) was one of the major military-political factions of the Warlord Era in the Republic of China. It was led by Duan Qirui and took its name from Duan's home province of Anhui. The clique dominated the government in Beijing in the late 1910s.

History

Duan Qirui, a leading general of Yuan Shikai's Beiyang Army, became premier and effectively head of government after Yuan's death in 1916. His Anhui clique controlled the Beijing administration and, relying on Japanese loans (notably the Nishihara Loans), built up a "War Participation Army" nominally raised for the First World War. Duan's refusal to restore the old constitution helped provoke rival factions and the Constitution Protection Movement in the south.

Downfall

The clique's defeat by the Zhili clique in the Zhili–Anhui War of July 1920 shattered its power; Duan briefly returned as head of state in 1924–1926 under a compromise arrangement but never regained real authority, and the clique dissolved as its generals were absorbed or defeated by rivals.
