Francesco I Sforza

Francesco I Sforza (1401–1466), founder of the Sforza dynasty of Milan.

Francesco I Sforza (23 July 1401 – 8 March 1466) was an Italian condottiero (mercenary captain) who founded the Sforza dynasty and became Duke of Milan in 1450. One of the most successful soldiers of fortune of the Renaissance, he rose from modest origins to rule one of Italy's leading states.

Early life and career

Francesco was the son of Muzio Attendolo Sforza, a famous condottiero from Romagna, from whom he inherited both the surname "Sforza" ("the Forcer") and a seasoned company of soldiers. After his father's death in 1424, Francesco served in turn the Kingdom of Naples, the Papacy, Florence, Venice and the Duchy of Milan under Filippo Maria Visconti, whose illegitimate daughter Bianca Maria he married in 1441, giving him a claim to Milan.

Duke of Milan

On Filippo Maria's death in 1447, Milan briefly became the Ambrosian Republic, which employed Sforza as its captain-general against Venice. In 1450 he turned on the republic, starved Milan into submission and was proclaimed duke. His diplomacy culminated in the Peace of Lodi (1454) with Venice and the Italic League, which stabilised the Italian peninsula for a generation.

As duke, Sforza proved an able and relatively popular ruler. He rebuilt the Castello Sforzesco, began the great hospital (Ca' Granda, now the University of Milan), reformed taxation and administration, and patronised artists and humanists. He was succeeded by his son Galeazzo Maria Sforza.
