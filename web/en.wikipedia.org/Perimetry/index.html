Perimetry (visual field test)

Perimetry is the systematic measurement of the visual field, and a visual field test maps the differential light sensitivity at points across the retina and visual pathways. It is a key examination in ophthalmology and neurology, particularly for detecting and monitoring glaucoma.

Types

In kinetic perimetry, such as the Goldmann perimeter, a stimulus of fixed intensity is moved from the periphery toward the centre until seen, tracing isopters of equal sensitivity. In static perimetry, stimuli are presented at fixed locations with varying brightness to determine threshold sensitivity; automated static perimeters such as the Humphrey Field Analyzer are now standard.

Patterns of loss

Characteristic defects include arcuate scotomas and nasal steps in glaucoma, bitemporal hemianopia from pituitary lesions compressing the optic chiasm, and homonymous hemianopia from retrochiasmal lesions of the brain. Confrontation testing at the bedside is a crude screening counterpart.
