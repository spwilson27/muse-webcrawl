New jack swing is a fusion genre of rhythm and blues and hip hop that emerged in the mid-1980s and dominated popular music from the late 1980s into the early 1990s. Characterised by swung, programmed drum-machine rhythms—often with a distinctive snapping snare—combined with soulful vocals, rapped verses, and samples, the sound was pioneered by producer Teddy Riley and the Minneapolis production team of Jimmy Jam and Terry Lewis.

History

The term "new jack swing" was coined by writer Barry Michael Cooper in a 1987 Village Voice profile of Teddy Riley. Early landmarks include Janet Jackson's Control (1986), produced by Jam and Lewis, and Keith Sweat's Make It Last Forever (1987). Riley's group Guy (1988) and his productions for Bobby Brown's Don't Be Cruel (1988) defined the genre's commercial peak. The soundtrack to the 1991 film New Jack City cemented the term in popular culture.

Characteristics

New jack swing tracks typically run at 90–110 beats per minute, use Roland TR-808 and sampled drums with swing quantization, and blend sung choruses with rap verses. Fashion and dance were integral: high-top fades, bright colours, and choreographed routines became hallmarks of acts such as Bell Biv DeVoe and Boyz II Men.

Legacy

By the mid-1990s new jack swing gave way to hip hop soul and contemporary R&B, but its fusion of rapping and singing became the template for mainstream pop and R&B for decades. Its influence resurfaced in the 2010s, notably in Bruno Mars and Anderson .Paak's An Evening with Silk Sonic and in K-pop.
