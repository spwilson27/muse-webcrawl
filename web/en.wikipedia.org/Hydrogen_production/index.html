Hydrogen production

Hydrogen production is the industrial production of molecular hydrogen (H2), an important feedstock for petroleum refining, ammonia synthesis (via the Haber process), methanol manufacture, and food processing, and a potential low-carbon fuel and energy carrier. Global production is on the order of 95 million tonnes per year, overwhelmingly derived from fossil fuels.

Methods

  • Steam methane reforming (SMR): the dominant method. Natural gas reacts with high-temperature steam over a nickel catalyst to yield hydrogen and carbon monoxide, followed by the water–gas shift reaction to produce additional hydrogen and carbon dioxide. Without carbon capture this emits roughly 9–12 kg of CO2 per kg of hydrogen; with capture it is classed as "blue hydrogen".
  • Coal gasification and partial oxidation of heavy hydrocarbons supply significant volumes, especially in China.
  • Electrolysis: splitting water with electricity in alkaline, PEM, or solid-oxide electrolysers. When powered by renewable electricity the product is "green hydrogen" — currently a small share of supply but the focus of most expansion plans.
  • Other routes: biomass gasification, thermochemical cycles, photoelectrochemical water splitting, methane pyrolysis ("turquoise hydrogen", yielding solid carbon), and biological production remain developmental.

Colour terminology

Industry shorthand classifies hydrogen by production route and emissions: grey (unabated fossil), blue (fossil with carbon capture), green (renewable electrolysis), pink (nuclear electricity), and turquoise (pyrolysis). The conventions are informal and increasingly supplemented by carbon-intensity standards.
