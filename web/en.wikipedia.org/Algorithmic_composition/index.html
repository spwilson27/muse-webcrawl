Algorithmic composition

Algorithmic composition is the technique of using algorithms to create music. Algorithms (or, at the very least, formal sets of rules) have been used to compose music for centuries; the procedures used to plot voice leading in Western counterpoint, for example, can often be reduced to algorithmic determinacy.

History

Early examples include the musical dice games attributed to the 18th century, in which pre-composed measures were assembled by chance. In the 1950s Lejaren Hiller and Leonard Isaacson produced the Illiac Suite (1957), the first score composed by a computer. Iannis Xenakis used stochastic processes derived from probability theory in works such as Metastaseis.

Techniques

Common approaches include stochastic methods and Markov chains, generative grammars, cellular automata, evolutionary and genetic algorithms, data-driven machine-learning models, and constraint-based systems. Modern work increasingly uses neural networks trained on corpora of existing music.

Related fields

Algorithmic composition overlaps with computer music, generative art, and live coding practices such as algorave, in which performers write and modify compositional algorithms in real time.
