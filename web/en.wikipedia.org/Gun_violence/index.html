Gun violence

Gun violence is violence committed with firearms, including homicide, suicide, assault, robbery and unintentional shootings. It is a major public health and criminal justice issue, with rates varying enormously between countries.

Scale

Globally, an estimated quarter of a million people die from firearm injuries each year. The United States has by far the highest firearm death rate among high-income countries — roughly 40,000 to 48,000 deaths annually in recent years, of which slightly more than half are suicides, followed by homicides; mass shootings account for a small share of deaths but a large share of public attention. Other high-burden countries include Brazil, Mexico and other Latin American states affected by organised crime and drug trafficking.

Causes and responses

Research links gun violence rates to firearm availability, poverty, inequality, illicit drug markets and the strength of social institutions. Policy responses include gun control laws, focused-deterrence policing, community violence-interruption programmes, safe-storage campaigns and suicide-prevention measures such as waiting periods and extreme risk protection orders. Gun violence is concentrated demographically and geographically, disproportionately affecting young men and disadvantaged urban neighbourhoods.
