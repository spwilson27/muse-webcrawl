Terrace (stadium)

A terrace or terracing in a stadium is a sloping bank of shallow steps on which spectators stand to watch a sporting event. Terraces were the dominant form of spectator accommodation in football grounds in the United Kingdom and much of Europe through most of the twentieth century, and remain common in lower-league grounds and in some other sports.

Design

A terrace consists of a series of wide, shallow steps rising away from the pitch, often built on an earth bank or concrete structure. Steel crush barriers are installed at intervals down the slope to prevent dangerous forward surges of the crowd. Terraces typically stand behind the goals, where the cheapest tickets were sold, and developed a distinctive, densely packed, vocal supporter culture.

History

Early football grounds relied on simple earth banks; by the early 1900s large grounds such as those in Glasgow and London had terraces holding tens of thousands. The record British crowd, at Hampden Park in 1937, stood largely on terracing. Famous terraces include the Kop at Anfield and the Stretford End at Old Trafford.

Safety and decline

A series of disasters focused attention on terrace safety, culminating in the Hillsborough disaster of 1989, in which 97 Liverpool supporters died in a crush on the Leppings Lane terrace. The resulting Taylor Report required all-seater stadiums in the top two divisions of English football, and top-flight terraces were converted to seating in the 1990s.

Safe standing

Since the 2010s, "safe standing" areas using rail seats — seats with a continuous barrier rail that can be locked upright — have been introduced in Germany (where large terraces such as Borussia Dortmund's Yellow Wall survived) and, from 2022, as licensed standing areas in the English Premier League and Championship.
