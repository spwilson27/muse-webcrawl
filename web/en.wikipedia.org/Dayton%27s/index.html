Dayton's was an American department store chain founded in Minneapolis, Minnesota, in 1902 by George Dayton. It was the flagship division of the Dayton Hudson Corporation, the company that later became the Target Corporation. The downtown Minneapolis store on Nicollet Mall was an Upper Midwest retail landmark for nearly a century.

History

George Draper Dayton, a banker and real estate investor from Worthington, Minnesota, built a six-storey building in downtown Minneapolis in 1901 and persuaded the Goodfellows dry goods store to move into it. In 1902 he took over the store himself, renaming it the Dayton Dry Goods Company; it became Dayton's in 1911. The store was famous for its Oval Room, its eighth-floor auditorium concerts, and its elaborate Christmas displays.

Expansion and Target

After George Dayton's death in 1938, the company passed through generations of the Dayton family. It opened Southdale Center in Edina in 1956 — the first fully enclosed, climate-controlled shopping mall in the United States — and in 1962 launched the Target discount chain. The parent company was renamed the Dayton Hudson Corporation in 1969 and acquired chains including Marshall Field's and Mervyn's.

End of the name

In 2000 the corporation renamed itself Target Corporation, and in 2001 the Dayton's stores were rebranded as Marshall Field's. Following the sale of the department store division to the May Department Stores Company in 2004, the former Dayton's stores became Macy's in 2006. The historic downtown Minneapolis store closed in 2017 and was later redeveloped as the Dayton's Project.
