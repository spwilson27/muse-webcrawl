Faculty of Advocates

The Faculty of Advocates is the professional body for advocates — the Scottish equivalent of barristers — in Scotland. Founded in 1532 with the establishment of the College of Justice, it is an independent body of lawyers who have been admitted to practise as counsel before the superior courts of Scotland, principally the Court of Session and the High Court of Justiciary.

History

The Faculty emerged when the Court of Session was founded by James V in 1532, formalising a body of pleaders before the new court. Its library, the Advocates Library, founded in 1682, grew into one of the great libraries of Europe and was a copyright deposit library until its collections were largely transferred to the National Library of Scotland in 1925. The Faculty is housed in Parliament House in Edinburgh, beside the courts.

Admission and organisation

To become an advocate, a candidate (called a "devil" during pupillage) must be a qualified solicitor or hold a law degree, pass the Faculty's examinations, and complete a period of devilling (training) under a devilmaster. Advocates are self-employed, grouped in "stables" administered by practice managers (clerks), and are headed by the Dean of Faculty, elected by the membership. Senior advocates may be appointed King's Counsel. Advocates have rights of audience in the supreme courts of Scotland and, by extension, in cases going to the UK Supreme Court on Scottish matters.
