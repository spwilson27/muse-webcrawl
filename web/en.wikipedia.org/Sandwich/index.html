A sandwich is a dish typically consisting of vegetables, sliced cheese or meat, placed between slices of bread, or more generally any dish in which bread serves as a container or wrapper for another food type. The sandwich began as a portable, finger food in the Western world, though over time it has become prevalent worldwide.

Etymology and legend

The word is popularly said to derive from John Montagu, 4th Earl of Sandwich (1718–1792), an aristocrat who supposedly ordered meat between bread so he could eat without leaving the gaming table or using cutlery. The story appears in print in the 1760s–70s; the actual usage "sandwich" for the food is recorded from 1762. Whether or not the Earl invented it, the name stuck and the form spread through British society.

Varieties

Sandwiches range from the British tea sandwich and bacon butty to the American club, BLT, Reuben and submarine sandwich; the French croque-monsieur; the Vietnamese bánh mì; the Middle Eastern shawarma wrap; and open-faced Scandinavian smørrebrød. Fillings and breads vary endlessly, and the boundary questions — whether a hot dog, burrito or burger qualifies — are a perennial popular debate.
