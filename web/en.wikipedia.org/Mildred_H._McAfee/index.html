Mildred Helen McAfee Horton (12 May 1900 – 2 September 1994) was an American academic administrator and naval officer. As president of Wellesley College from 1936 to 1949, she took leave during World War II to become the first director of the WAVES (Women Accepted for Volunteer Emergency Service), the U.S. Navy's women's reserve, holding the rank of captain — the first woman commissioned in the United States Naval Reserve.

Early life and education

McAfee was born in Parkville, Missouri, the daughter of a Presbyterian minister. She graduated from Vassar College in 1920 and earned a master's degree in sociology from the University of Chicago, later serving as dean of women at Centre College and dean of college women at Oberlin before her appointment at Wellesley at age 36.

WAVES directorship

Commissioned in August 1942, McAfee organized the recruitment, training, and administration of some 100,000 WAVES, who served in shore billets across the United States, freeing men for sea duty. She navigated institutional resistance to women in uniform with notable diplomacy and was awarded the Distinguished Service Medal.

Later life

Returning to Wellesley after the war, she married theologian Douglas Horton in 1945 and resigned the presidency in 1949. She remained active on corporate and educational boards, including RCA and NBC, and served as a delegate to UNESCO conferences. McAfee Hall at Wellesley is named in her honor.
