DEF CON

DEF CON is one of the world's largest and best-known hacker conventions, held annually in Las Vegas, Nevada. Founded in 1993 by Jeff Moss, it draws tens of thousands of attendees including computer security professionals, hackers, journalists, lawyers, government employees, and hobbyists. Together with the Black Hat Briefings, which Moss also founded, it forms part of "Hacker Summer Camp".

History

DEF CON began in 1993 as a farewell party for a Canadian bulletin-board community and grew into a major convention. The first event drew about 100 people; by the 2010s attendance exceeded 20,000, and DEF CON 27 (2019) reported around 30,000 attendees. DEF CON China was held in Beijing in 2018–2019.

Events and culture

The convention features talks, workshops, and themed "villages" devoted to voting-machine security, car hacking, aviation, biohacking, lockpicking, and social engineering. Notable traditions include Capture the Flag competitions, the Badge Challenge (electronic badges are themselves hackable puzzles), the unofficial game "Spot the Fed", and fundraisers for the Electronic Frontier Foundation. The Wall of Sheep publicly lists credentials captured on the conference network as a security-awareness demonstration.

Notable incidents

DEF CON talks have disclosed widely reported vulnerabilities, from ATM skimming and vehicle keyless-entry flaws to vulnerabilities in voting machines demonstrated at the Voting Village, informing election-security policy debates.
