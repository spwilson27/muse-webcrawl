Occupy Wall Street (OWS) was a protest movement against economic inequality and the perceived influence of corporations and the financial sector on government, which began on 17 September 2011 with an encampment in Zuccotti Park in New York City's Financial District. Its slogan, "We are the 99%", contrasted the wealth of the richest one percent with the rest of the population.

Background

The protest was prompted by a call from the Canadian magazine Adbusters for an occupation of Wall Street, in the wake of the 2008 financial crisis, bank bailouts, high unemployment and rising inequality. Early organising drew on the example of the Arab Spring and the Spanish anti-austerity (15-M) movement, including the use of consensus-based general assemblies and the "human microphone" to amplify speakers.

The movement

Protesters maintained a round-the-clock encampment with a kitchen, library and media centre, governed by the New York City General Assembly. The movement spread to hundreds of cities in the United States and abroad under the wider "Occupy" banner. It made no single list of formal demands, instead articulating grievances about student debt, foreclosures, campaign finance and corporate personhood. Labour unions, celebrities and some politicians expressed support, while critics questioned the movement's lack of leadership and specific goals.

Eviction and legacy

On 15 November 2011, New York City police cleared the Zuccotti Park encampment. Subsequent attempts to re-establish camps largely failed, though Occupy networks later organised campaigns such as Occupy Sandy relief efforts after Hurricane Sandy and debt-resistance projects. The movement is widely credited with shifting public debate toward income inequality and popularising the "1% versus 99%" framing in political discourse.
