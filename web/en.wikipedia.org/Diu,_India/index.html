Diu is a town and headquarter of the Diu district in the union territory of Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, in western India. Situated at the eastern end of Diu Island on the southern coast of Gujarat's Kathiawar peninsula, separated from the mainland by a tidal creek, Diu was a Portuguese colony from the 16th century until 1961 and remains notable for its fort, churches and beaches.

History

Diu was an important trading port in the medieval period, contested by Gujarati sultans, the Ottoman Empire and the Portuguese. After the Battle of Diu in 1509, a naval engagement in which a Portuguese fleet defeated a combined Mamluk, Ottoman, Venetian and Gujarati fleet, Portugal gained ascendancy in the Indian Ocean. The Portuguese built the massive Diu Fort in 1535–36 after an alliance with Bahadur Shah of Gujarat, and repelled famous sieges in 1538 and 1546. Diu remained under Portuguese rule for over 450 years, administered as part of Portuguese India along with Goa and Daman, until it was annexed by India on 19 December 1961 during Operation Vijay.

Geography and climate

The island lies at sea level and has a warm, humid climate moderated by the Arabian Sea. The town is connected to the mainland village of Ghoghla by a bridge. Agriculture and fishing are local occupations, and the island's sandy beaches—including Nagoa Beach—draw domestic tourists.

Landmarks

Diu Fort, built at the mouth of the creek, encloses lighthouses, bastions and a former prison. Within the town stand three Portuguese churches: St. Paul's Church (begun 1601, a notable example of Baroque architecture), the Church of St. Francis of Assisi, and the Church of St. Thomas, now a museum. The Zampa Gateway and the sea-facing Gangeshwar Mahadev Temple are other points of interest.
