Oroville Dam

Construction began1961
Opening date1968

Oroville Dam is an earthfill embankment dam on the Feather River east of the city of Oroville, California, in the Sierra Nevada foothills. At 235 metres (770 ft) high, it is the tallest earthen dam in the United States. Completed in 1968, it impounds Lake Oroville as the keystone facility of the California State Water Project, providing water storage, hydroelectric power and flood control.

Facilities

The dam's Edward Hyatt Powerplant, housed in an underground cavern, has a capacity of about 819 MW. Water releases are controlled by a gated service spillway and, in extreme floods, an ungated emergency spillway weir. The reservoir stores about 4.36 cubic kilometres of water and supplies irrigation and municipal water across California.

2017 spillway crisis

In February 2017, during heavy storms, the main spillway chute fractured and eroded, and water flowed over the emergency spillway for the first time in the dam's history, eroding the hillside below. Fearing a collapse of the emergency weir, authorities ordered the evacuation of some 188,000 people downstream. The lake level was drawn down and no catastrophic failure occurred. Reconstruction of both spillways cost about $1.1 billion and was completed in 2019; an independent forensic report attributed the failure to long-term systemic design, construction and maintenance shortcomings.
