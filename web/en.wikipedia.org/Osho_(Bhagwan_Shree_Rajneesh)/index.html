Osho (Bhagwan Shree Rajneesh)

Osho

Born: Chandra Mohan Jain, 11 December 1931, Kuchwada, British India

Died: 19 January 1990 (aged 58), Pune, India

Known for: Spiritual teaching, Rajneesh movement

Philosophy: Neo-sannyas, dynamic meditation

Rajneesh (born Chandra Mohan Jain; 11 December 1931 – 19 January 1990), also known as Acharya Rajneesh, Bhagwan Shree Rajneesh, and later as Osho, was an Indian godman, mystic and founder of the Rajneesh movement. He was a controversial figure who attracted an international following with his teachings on meditation, sexuality, and the rejection of institutional religion, blending Eastern mysticism with Western philosophy and psychology.

During his lifetime, he was viewed as a controversial new religious movement leader and mystic. In the 1960s, he travelled throughout India as a public speaker, earning a reputation as a charismatic critic of socialism, Mahatma Gandhi, and orthodox religion. In 1970, he settled in Bombay (now Mumbai) and began initiating followers known as "neo-sannyasins", wearing orange robes and mala necklaces.

Life

Rajneesh was born in Kuchwada, a small village in Madhya Pradesh, the eldest of eleven children of a Jain cloth merchant. He studied philosophy at Jabalpur University, graduating with distinction, and worked as a lecturer in philosophy, first at Raipur Sanskrit College and later at Jabalpur University, before resigning in 1966 to teach full time. In 1974 he established an ashram in Koregaon Park, Pune, which grew into a major centre attracting Western seekers with therapies drawn from the Human Potential Movement alongside his discourses and "dynamic meditation" techniques.

Rajneeshpuram

In 1981, facing conflict with Indian authorities, Rajneesh moved to the United States, where his followers established the intentional community of Rajneeshpuram on a ranch in Wasco County, Oregon. The commune grew rapidly but became embroiled in conflict with local residents and the state government. In 1984–85, a group within the commune's leadership committed a series of crimes, including a bioterror attack in The Dalles — the largest biological attack in United States history — and a plot to assassinate a United States Attorney. Rajneesh was arrested in 1985, pleaded guilty to immigration fraud in an Alford plea, and was deported.

Final years and legacy

After travelling to and being refused entry by numerous countries, Rajneesh returned to Pune in 1987, where he revived his ashram and adopted the name Osho in 1989. He died there in January 1990, officially of heart failure. The Pune centre survives as the Osho International Meditation Resort. His books — compilations of his recorded discourses, numbering in the hundreds — remain widely read, and his meditation techniques are practised worldwide, though his reputation remains polarised between devoted followers and critics of the movement's Oregon period.
