Temple

A temple (from Latin templum) is a building reserved for spiritual rituals and activities such as prayer and sacrifice. Religions that erect temples include Christianity (whose temples are typically called churches, though the term is used by some denominations such as the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints), Hinduism (called mandira), Buddhism (often wat or vihara), Sikhism (gurdwara), Jainism, Judaism, Islam (mosques), and ancient religions such as the Egyptian, Greek and Roman religions.

Ancient temples

Some of the earliest temples were the ziggurats of Mesopotamia and the mortuary temples of ancient Egypt, such as those at Karnak and Luxor. Greek temples, typically built on an elevated site with a colonnade and an inner cella housing a cult statue, became models for Roman temple architecture and later neoclassical buildings. Temples in ancient Rome were places of public cult and civic ceremony as much as private devotion.

Temples in living religions

Hindu temples are dedicated to particular deities and serve as houses of god, centres of festivals and pilgrimage destinations; the great temple complexes of South India feature monumental gateway towers (gopurams). Buddhist temples house images of the Buddha and spaces for meditation and monastic life. In Judaism, "the Temple" refers specifically to the destroyed Temple in Jerusalem, while synagogues serve as houses of prayer. For Latter-day Saints, temples are distinct from meetinghouses and are regarded as houses of the Lord reserved for special ordinances.
