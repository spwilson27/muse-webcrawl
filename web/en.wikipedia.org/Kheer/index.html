Kheer

A bowl of kheer garnished with almonds and pistachios.

Kheer (Hindi: खीर), also known as payasam in South India and by many regional names, is a sweet dish of the Indian subcontinent made by boiling milk and sugar (or jaggery) with rice, broken wheat, vermicelli, tapioca, or other grains, and flavoured with cardamom, saffron, raisins, and nuts such as almonds and pistachios. It is a type of wet pudding traditionally served as dessert.

History and names

Milk-and-rice puddings are ancient in Indian cooking, mentioned in early Sanskrit literature, and the Hindi word kheer derives from the Sanskrit kṣīra, meaning milk. In Tamil Nadu, Kerala, and Andhra Pradesh the dish is payasam, an essential part of festival meals and temple offerings; in Bengal a slow-cooked version made with fragrant gobindobhog rice is called payesh. Firni, popular in the north and in Pakistan, uses ground rice for a smoother texture and is set in earthen bowls.

Preparation and occasions

Kheer is prepared for festivals such as Eid, Diwali, and Raksha Bandhan, for weddings, and as a religious offering (prasad). It may be served warm or chilled, sometimes garnished with edible silver leaf (varak). Special variants include seviyan kheer (vermicelli), sabudana kheer (tapioca pearls, eaten during fasts), and carrot or bottle-gourd kheer.
