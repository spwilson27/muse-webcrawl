Kheda Satyagraha of 1918

Gandhi and his co-workers during the Kheda Satyagraha.

The Kheda Satyagraha of 1918 was a nonviolent resistance campaign organised by Mohandas Gandhi in the Kheda district of Gujarat, India, in support of peasants whose crops had failed and who demanded remission of the land revenue tax. Together with the Champaran Satyagraha of 1917 and the Ahmedabad mill strike of 1918, it established Gandhi's method of satyagraha in India and launched the political careers of leaders including Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel.

Background

In 1917 floods and crop failure struck Kheda. Under the revenue code, cultivators were entitled to a remission if the yield fell below a quarter of normal, but the Bombay government disputed their estimates and refused full relief, and began seizing property of peasants who could not pay. Local leaders Mohanlal Pandya and Shankarlal Parikh appealed to Gandhi and the Gujarat Sabha, who after their own crop survey endorsed the peasants' claim.

The campaign

On 22 March 1918, at Nadiad, Gandhi advised the peasants to take a pledge not to pay the revenue — including those who could afford it, in solidarity — and to accept confiscation and imprisonment without violence or resentment. Vallabhbhai Patel, Indulal Yagnik, Mahadev Desai and others toured the villages sustaining morale as officials attached lands, cattle and household goods. Wealthier Patidar peasants who paid early were socially boycotted; the campaign held together despite these pressures.

Outcome

In June 1918 the government reached a compromise: payment would be demanded from those well able to pay, while the poor would be exempted, and confiscated property was to be returned. The settlement was less than a complete victory, but the satyagraha demonstrated the power of organised nonviolent non-cooperation, drew the Gujarati peasantry into the national movement, and confirmed Patel's standing as Gandhi's lieutenant in Gujarat.
