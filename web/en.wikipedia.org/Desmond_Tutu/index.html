Desmond Tutu

Desmond Mpilo Tutu
Born: 7 October 1931, Klerksdorp, South Africa
Died: 26 December 2021, Cape Town, South Africa
Office: Archbishop of Cape Town (1986–1996)
Award: Nobel Peace Prize (1984)

Desmond Mpilo Tutu (7 October 1931 – 26 December 2021) was a South African Anglican bishop and theologian, known for his work as an anti-apartheid and human rights activist. He was the first black African Archbishop of Cape Town and a recipient of the Nobel Peace Prize in 1984.

Early life and ministry

Tutu was born in Klerksdorp and trained first as a teacher before studying theology; he was ordained an Anglican priest in 1960 and studied in London. After serving as Bishop of Lesotho, he became general secretary of the South African Council of Churches in 1978, from which platform he became the most prominent internal voice calling for an end to apartheid and for international economic pressure on the regime.

Anti-apartheid leadership

As Archbishop of Cape Town from 1986, Tutu advocated non-violent resistance, famously mediating between angry crowds and authorities and preaching reconciliation even at the height of state violence. The award of the Nobel Peace Prize in 1984 recognised his role as a unifying figure in the campaign to resolve South Africa's racial conflict.

Truth and Reconciliation Commission

After the first democratic elections in 1994, President Nelson Mandela appointed Tutu to chair the Truth and Reconciliation Commission (1995–1998), which investigated human rights abuses under apartheid and granted amnesty in exchange for full disclosure. Tutu popularised the image of South Africa as the "Rainbow Nation" and articulated a theology of forgiveness summarised in his phrase "no future without forgiveness".

Later life

Tutu remained an outspoken moral commentator on issues from HIV/AIDS and climate change to the rights of Palestinians and LGBT people. He died in Cape Town on 26 December 2021, aged 90, and was given a state funeral at St George's Cathedral.
