Peter Sellers

Peter Sellers CBE (born Richard Henry Sellers; 8 September 1925 – 24 July 1980) was an English actor and comedian. One of the most versatile screen performers of his generation, he first came to prominence in the BBC radio comedy series The Goon Show and became an international film star through roles including the bumbling Inspector Clouseau in The Pink Panther series and three contrasting characters in Stanley Kubrick's Dr. Strangelove (1964).

Career

After wartime service in the RAF, Sellers joined Spike Milligan and Harry Secombe in The Goon Show (1951–1960). Film success came with The Ladykillers (1955) and I'm All Right Jack (1959). Kubrick cast him in Lolita (1962) and then in the triple role of Group Captain Mandrake, President Muffley and Dr. Strangelove, earning an Academy Award nomination. As Clouseau, beginning with The Pink Panther (1963) and A Shot in the Dark (1964), he created one of cinema's enduring comic figures, revived in several sequels. His final acclaimed performance was the simple gardener Chance in Being There (1979), which brought a second Oscar nomination.

Personal life

Sellers' private life was turbulent, marked by four marriages, struggles with depression and serious heart disease from the 1960s; he died of a heart attack in 1980, aged 54.
