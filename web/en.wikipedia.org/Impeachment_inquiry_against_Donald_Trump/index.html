Impeachment inquiry against Donald Trump

The impeachment inquiry against Donald Trump was an investigation initiated on September 24, 2019, by Speaker of the House Nancy Pelosi into whether President Donald Trump should be impeached. The inquiry followed a whistleblower complaint alleging that Trump had pressured Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelenskyy during a July 25, 2019 telephone call to investigate former vice president Joe Biden and his son Hunter Biden, while withholding military aid to Ukraine.

Background

An anonymous intelligence-community whistleblower filed a complaint in August 2019 concerning the call. A rough transcript released by the White House confirmed that Trump had asked Zelenskyy to "do us a favor" and look into matters related to the Bidens and to a conspiracy theory about the 2016 election. Acting Director of National Intelligence Joseph Maguire initially withheld the complaint from Congress, prompting a confrontation that led Pelosi to announce the formal inquiry.

Proceedings

Six House committees participated in the investigation. Closed-door depositions were followed by public hearings in November 2019, featuring testimony from officials including Ambassador Gordon Sondland, Lt. Col. Alexander Vindman, Marie Yovanovitch, and Fiona Hill. On December 3, the House Intelligence Committee released its report, and on December 10 the Judiciary Committee introduced two articles of impeachment: abuse of power and obstruction of Congress.

Outcome

The House voted on December 18, 2019, to impeach Trump on both articles, largely along party lines. The Senate trial in January and February 2020 ended in acquittal. A second impeachment followed in 2021 after the January 6 Capitol attack.
