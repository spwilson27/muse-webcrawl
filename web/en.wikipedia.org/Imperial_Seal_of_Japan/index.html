Imperial Seal of Japan

The Imperial Seal of Japan or National Seal of Japan, also called the Chrysanthemum Seal (kikumon), is the emblem (mon) of the Emperor of Japan. It consists of a stylized chrysanthemum blossom with sixteen petals at the front and sixteen tips of another chrysanthemum visible behind it — a "sixteen-petaled double chrysanthemum."

History

The chrysanthemum emblem came into imperial use during the Kamakura period; Emperor Go-Toba was particularly fond of the flower and adopted it as his personal emblem. During the Meiji period, use of the seal was legally restricted to the imperial family and imperial institutions; in 1869 and 1871, regulations standardized the design and limited who could use the sixteen-petal form.

Usage

The Imperial Seal appears on the cover of Japanese passports (in a simplified form), on the Order of the Chrysanthemum (Japan's highest order), on imperial properties and shrines associated with the imperial family, and on the emperor's standard. Members of the imperial family use modified versions, such as a fourteen-petal variant. Although Japan has no legally designated national emblem, the Imperial Seal is widely treated as a de facto national emblem.

The seal and the Chrysanthemum Throne are closely associated: the term "Chrysanthemum Throne" is used metonymically for the Japanese imperial institution itself.
