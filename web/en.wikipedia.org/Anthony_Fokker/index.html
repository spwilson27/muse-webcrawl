Anton Herman Gerard "Anthony" Fokker (6 April 1890 – 23 December 1939) was a Dutch aviation pioneer and aircraft manufacturer. Born in the Dutch East Indies and raised in Haarlem, he built his first aeroplane, the Spin ("Spider"), in Germany in 1910 and founded the company that became Fokker.

First World War

Based in Germany during the First World War, Fokker supplied the German air service with aircraft including the Eindecker monoplanes — the first fighters fitted with a synchronisation gear allowing a machine gun to fire through the propeller arc — and later the famous Dr.I triplane flown by Manfred von Richthofen, the "Red Baron", and the D.VII biplane, regarded by many as the war's finest fighter.

Later career

After the war Fokker smuggled much of his operation back to the Netherlands and founded a Dutch factory; he later emigrated to the United States, where his Atlantic Aircraft Corporation built successful airliners such as the Fokker F.VII trimotor, used for pioneering polar and transoceanic flights. He died in New York in 1939, aged 49.
