Fines herbes

The classic fines herbes: parsley, chives, tarragon, and chervil.

Fines herbes (French: [fin.z‿ɛʁb]) designates an important combination of herbs that forms a mainstay of French cuisine. The ingredients of fines herbes are fresh parsley, chives, tarragon and chervil. These "fine" herbs are not the pungent and resinous herbs that appear in a bouquet garni, which, unlike fines herbes, release their flavour in long cooking. Fines herbes are usually added towards the end of cooking, or used raw, so that their delicate flavour is preserved.

Composition

The classic mixture, as codified by Auguste Escoffier, consists of equal parts of finely chopped fresh parsley, chives, tarragon, and chervil. Marjoram, thyme, or rosemary are sometimes included in modern variations, although purists limit the blend to the four traditional herbs. Unlike dried herb mixtures, fines herbes are generally prepared fresh, though dried versions are also sold.

Uses

Fines herbes are a hallmark of French cooking and are used in omelettes — the classic omelette aux fines herbes — as well as in salads, light sauces, soups, fish and poultry dishes, and sprinkled over vegetables. Because the herbs are delicate, they are added at the end of cooking or used as a garnish.
