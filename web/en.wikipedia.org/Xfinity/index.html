Xfinity

Xfinity is the trade name of Comcast Cable Communications, LLC, a subsidiary of Comcast Corporation, used to market consumer cable television, broadband internet, telephone and wireless services in the United States. Introduced in 2010, Xfinity is the largest provider of cable broadband in the country, serving tens of millions of customers.

History

Comcast announced the Xfinity brand in 2009 and launched it during the 2010 Winter Olympics, rebranding its existing cable services — previously sold as Comcast Digital Cable, Comcast High-Speed Internet and Comcast Digital Voice — under a single consumer-facing name while the parent company retained the Comcast corporate identity. The brand was widely understood partly as an attempt to improve consumer perceptions of the company.

Services

Xfinity's flagship television platform, the X1 Entertainment Operating System, introduced cloud DVR, a voice remote and integrated streaming apps; the Flex (later Xumo-compatible) streaming box serves broadband-only customers. Internet service is delivered over hybrid fibre-coaxial DOCSIS networks, with multi-gigabit tiers rolled out under DOCSIS 3.1 and 4.0 plans. Xfinity Mobile, launched in 2017, operates as an MVNO on Verizon's network supplemented by millions of Xfinity WiFi hotspots. Home telephone (Xfinity Voice) and home security/automation (Xfinity Home) complete the bundle.

Criticism

Like its parent, Xfinity has repeatedly placed near the bottom of American Customer Satisfaction Index rankings for subscription television and internet providers, with data caps, pricing and customer service common sources of complaint; it has also faced state investigations over billing fees. Comcast has countered with investments in network capacity and a redesigned retail and app experience.
