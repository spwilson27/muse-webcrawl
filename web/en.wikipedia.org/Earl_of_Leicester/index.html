Earl of Leicester

Earl of Leicester is a title that has been created seven times. The first creation, in the Peerage of England, was in 1107 for Robert de Beaumont. The title is currently held by the Coke family, Earls of Leicester of Holkham Hall, Norfolk, in the seventh creation (Peerage of the United Kingdom, 1837).

Notable holders

Simon de Montfort, 5th Earl of Leicester (c. 1208–1265), leader of the baronial opposition to Henry III, summoned the parliament of 1265 that included representatives of the boroughs — often regarded as the origin of the House of Commons — and died at the Battle of Evesham. In the Elizabethan age, Robert Dudley, 1st Earl of Leicester (1532–1588), was the great favourite of Queen Elizabeth I and commander of the English forces sent to the Netherlands in 1585.

The fifth creation (1618) was for Robert Sidney, and the sixth (1744) for Thomas Coke of Holkham, builder of Holkham Hall; the current line descends from his great-nephew, Thomas Coke, the agricultural reformer "Coke of Norfolk", created Earl of Leicester in 1837.
