Oratory (worship)

An oratory is a place of worship other than a parish church, set aside for prayer by a particular community.

In Catholic canon law, an oratory is a place which is set aside by permission of an ordinary for divine worship, for the convenience of some community or group of the faithful who gather there, but to which other members of the faithful may also have access with the consent of the competent superior. Oratories are distinguished from churches, which are open to all the faithful, and from private chapels.

Types

Canon law recognises public and semi-public oratories. Houses of religious institutes typically contain oratories where the community celebrates Mass and the Liturgy of the Hours. Seminaries, schools, hospitals, and prisons may also have oratories for those who live or work there.

History

Oratories developed from the house churches and martyrs' shrines of early Christianity. In modern usage the word is also associated with the Oratorians, the Congregation of the Oratory founded by Saint Philip Neri in Rome in the sixteenth century, and with buildings such as the Brompton Oratory in London.

Other traditions

In Judaism, small prayer rooms, and in Islam dedicated prayer facilities, are sometimes described in English with similar terms, though "oratory" is chiefly a Christian usage.
