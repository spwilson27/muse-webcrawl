The Seminole Wars (also known as the Florida Wars) were three military conflicts in Florida between the United States and the Seminole, a Native American people formed of Creek and other groups, together with many African Americans who had escaped slavery. They are often described as among the longest and costliest of the American Indian Wars.

First Seminole War (1816–1819)

Clashes along the Georgia–Spanish Florida border, including the destruction of the "Negro Fort" on the Apalachicola River in 1816, escalated into a full invasion in 1818 led by General Andrew Jackson, who seized Spanish settlements at St. Marks and Pensacola. Spain subsequently ceded Florida to the United States under the Adams–Onís Treaty (1819).

Second Seminole War (1835–1842)

The longest and most expensive of the three wars arose from the federal policy of Indian removal. After the Treaty of Payne's Landing (1832) required the Seminole to move west, resistance under leaders including Osceola led to war in December 1835, beginning with the ambush known as the Dade Massacre. Guerrilla warfare in the Florida swamps frustrated U.S. forces for seven years; Osceola was captured under a flag of truce in 1837 and died at Fort Moultrie. Some 4,000 Seminole were eventually removed to Indian Territory, though several hundred remained in the Everglades.

Third Seminole War (1855–1858)

The final conflict, fought against the remaining Seminole led by Billy Bowlegs, ended with the removal of most of the holdouts; a small community — ancestors of today's Seminole Tribe of Florida and Miccosukee Tribe — never surrendered.
