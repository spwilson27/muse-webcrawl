The Battle of Ascalon took place on 12 August 1099 and is considered the last engagement of the First Crusade. The Crusader army, led by Godfrey of Bouillon, defeated a numerically superior Fatimid Egyptian army commanded by the vizier al-Afdal Shahanshah, securing the Crusaders' capture of Jerusalem a month earlier.

Background

After taking Jerusalem on 15 July 1099 and electing Godfrey as its ruler, the Crusaders learned that a large Fatimid relief army had landed at Ascalon (Ashkelon), the great fortress-port on the coast road from Egypt. The Crusader leaders — Godfrey, Raymond of Toulouse, Robert of Normandy, Robert of Flanders, Tancred, and Eustace of Boulogne — marched south with perhaps 1,200 knights and 9,000 infantry to meet it, leaving Jerusalem almost undefended.

Battle

Marching through the night, the Crusaders surprised the Fatimid camp at dawn. The Fatimid army — likely 20,000–30,000 men, including Armenian and Sudanese infantry and Arab and Berber cavalry — was largely unprepared. The Crusader wings attacked simultaneously; Godfrey held against pressure on one flank while the other divisions drove the Fatimids toward the sea and the orchards around Ascalon. Al-Afdal escaped by ship, abandoning his camp and its rich booty.

Aftermath

Despite the victory, quarrels between Godfrey and Raymond prevented the Crusaders from occupying Ascalon itself, which remained a Fatimid stronghold and a base for raids on the new Kingdom of Jerusalem until Baldwin III finally captured it in 1153, after the Siege of Ascalon. With the Fatimid threat removed, most Crusaders considered their pilgrimage vows fulfilled and returned home.
