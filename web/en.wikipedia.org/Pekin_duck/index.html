Pekin duck

The Pekin or White Pekin is an American breed of domestic duck, raised primarily for meat. It derives from birds brought to the United States from China in the nineteenth century and is now found in many countries. It is the most popular commercial meat duck breed worldwide.

Characteristics

The Pekin is a large, heavy duck with creamy white plumage, yellow-orange bill, and orange feet. It grows rapidly, reaching market weight in about seven weeks, and is known for a calm temperament. Hens lay white eggs and may lay 150–200 per year, though they rarely brood.

History

The breed descends from mallards domesticated in China, where duck husbandry dates back more than two thousand years. In 1873, fifteen white ducks were shipped from China to Long Island, New York, founding the American population. The name derives from Peking, the former romanization of Beijing.

Use

Pekins dominate commercial duck-meat production, and the breed is also familiar as the model for the classic rubber duck and characters such as Donald Duck.
