Jaguar D-Type

The Jaguar D-Type is a sports racing car produced by Jaguar Cars between 1954 and 1957. Designed by Malcolm Sayer, it succeeded the C-Type and is famous for its monocoque construction, aerodynamic bodywork and the vertical stabilising fin behind the driver's head.

Design

The D-Type's central tub was a monocoque of aluminium alloy, with tubular subframes carrying the engine and front suspension — construction techniques borrowed from aircraft engineering. Power came from the 3.4-litre XK straight-six engine, later enlarged to 3.8 litres. Disc brakes, pioneered on the C-Type, were standard. Its slippery shape gave a top speed of around 170 mph (274 km/h).

Racing record

D-Types won the 24 Hours of Le Mans three times consecutively, in 1955, 1956 and 1957. The 1957 victory was a privateer triumph for Scotland's Ecurie Ecosse team, whose cars finished first and second. The 1955 win was overshadowed by the Le Mans disaster, in which a Mercedes-Benz crashed into the crowd, killing more than 80 spectators.

After Jaguar withdrew from racing in 1956, unsold D-Type chassis were converted into road-going XKSS models; only 16 were completed before a factory fire destroyed the remaining cars in 1957. In 2018 Jaguar Classic announced it would build the remaining 25 cars of the original production run to period specification.

A 1955 D-Type sold at auction in 2016 for $21.78 million, then a record for a British car. See also: Jaguar Cars, 24 Hours of Le Mans.
