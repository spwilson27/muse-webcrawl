Radionuclide

Decay scheme diagram
Also calledRadioactive nuclide, radioisotope
PropertyUnstable atomic nucleus; undergoes radioactive decay
ExamplesCarbon-14, uranium-238, technetium-99m

A radionuclide (or radioactive nuclide) is a nuclide whose nucleus is unstable and which decays by emitting radiation, transforming into another nuclide. A radionuclide that is an isotope of an element is often called a radioisotope. More than 3,000 radionuclides are known; only about 84 occur naturally on Earth in appreciable amounts, the rest being produced artificially in reactors and accelerators.

Decay

Radionuclides decay by modes including alpha decay, beta decay, gamma emission, electron capture and spontaneous fission, each characterised by a half-life ranging from fractions of a second to billions of years (e.g. uranium-238, 4.47 billion years). Decay chains link parent radionuclides to stable end products such as lead-208.

Applications

Radionuclides are used in nuclear medicine (technetium-99m imaging, iodine-131 therapy), radiotherapy, industrial radiography, smoke detectors (americium-241), radiometric dating (carbon-14, uranium–lead), and as heat sources in radioisotope thermoelectric generators for space probes.
