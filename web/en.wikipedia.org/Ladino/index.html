Ladino, also known as Judaeo-Spanish (judeoespañol), is a Romance language derived from Old Spanish and spoken by Sephardic Jews. After the expulsion of the Jews from Spain in 1492, exiles carried their language across the Ottoman Empire — to Salonica, Constantinople, Izmir, the Balkans and North Africa — where it developed in relative isolation, preserving 15th-century Spanish features while absorbing loanwords from Hebrew, Turkish, Greek and other languages.

History and status

Ladino developed a rich literature, including the Me'am Lo'ez Bible commentary begun in 1730, newspapers, romansas (ballads) and koplas. The Holocaust destroyed its largest community, Salonica, and assimilation, emigration to Israel and language shift have left it seriously endangered. Revival efforts include the Autoridad Nasionala del Ladino in Israel, university programmes, and online communities.

Characteristics

Ladino retains archaic Spanish traits, such as initial f- (e.g., favlar, "to speak") and the old sibilants spelled x and j. It is traditionally written in Hebrew characters (Rashi script) but today usually in a phonetic Latin orthography promoted by the journal Aki Yerushalayim.
