Hermite

Hermite most commonly refers to Charles Hermite (1822–1901), a French mathematician who made major contributions to number theory, algebra, and analysis. In 1873 he proved that the mathematical constant e is a transcendental number — the first such proof for a naturally occurring constant — a method later extended by Ferdinand von Lindemann to prove the transcendence of π.

Named after Hermite

Many mathematical objects bear his name, including Hermitian matrices and Hermitian operators (central to quantum mechanics), Hermite polynomials, Hermitian manifolds, Hermite interpolation, and the Hermite normal form for integer matrices. The lunar crater Hermite, the coldest recorded crater in the Solar System, is also named for him.

Disambiguation

"Hermite" may also appear as a French surname borne by other figures, and in older texts as a variant spelling associated with hermits. See also Hermitian manifold and the disambiguation pages for related terms.
