Real analytic Eisenstein series

The real analytic Eisenstein series E(z, s) is a function of a point z in the upper half-plane and a complex parameter s.

In mathematics, a real analytic Eisenstein series (after Gotthold Eisenstein), also called a non-holomorphic or Maass–Eisenstein series, is a function of a complex variable z = x + iy in the upper half-plane and a complex parameter s, defined for Re(s) > 1 by the series

E(z, s) = ½ ∑(m,n)≠(0,0) ys / |mz + n|2s

where the sum runs over all pairs of coprime integers (m, n) — equivalently, over all non-zero pairs divided by their greatest common divisor. Unlike the classical holomorphic Eisenstein series, E(z, s) is real analytic but not holomorphic in z.

Properties

The series is invariant under the modular group SL(2, Z), acting on z by fractional linear transformations, so it descends to a function on the modular curve. It is an eigenfunction of the hyperbolic Laplace operator with eigenvalue s(1 − s). For Re(s) > 1 the defining series converges absolutely and uniformly on compact sets; E(z, s) admits a meromorphic continuation to all s, with a single simple pole at s = 1 whose residue is 3/π.

Fourier expansion

Because E(z, s) is periodic in x with period 1, it has a Fourier expansion whose constant term involves the completed Riemann zeta function:

E(z, s) = ys + φ(s) y1−s + (higher Fourier terms involving K-Bessel functions),

where φ(s) = π−s Γ(s) ζ(2s) / (π−(1−s) Γ(1−s) ζ(2−2s)) is the scattering function. The non-constant terms decay exponentially as y → ∞ and involve divisor sums and modified Bessel functions of the second kind.

Applications

Real analytic Eisenstein series are fundamental in the spectral theory of automorphic forms: they provide the continuous spectrum of the Laplacian on modular surfaces, play a central role in the work of Selberg and in the Langlands program, and appear in the Kronecker limit formula. Hans Maass introduced closely related non-holomorphic modular forms, and Robert Langlands generalized Eisenstein series to arbitrary reductive groups.
