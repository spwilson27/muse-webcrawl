Rates (tax)

A historical rates demand

Rates are a type of property tax system in the United Kingdom, and in places with systems based on the British model, used as a means of raising revenue for local government. Rates were levied according to the rental value of a property, assessed by reference to the rent for which the property might reasonably be expected to be let from year to year.

History in England and Wales

Local rates have their origin in the Poor Relief Act 1601, which required parishes to levy a rate to support the poor. Over subsequent centuries the system was extended to finance other local services, and assessments were standardised by the Union Assessment Committee Act 1862 and later legislation. Domestic rates in England, Scotland and Wales were abolished by the Local Government Finance Act 1988, which introduced the Community Charge (the "poll tax") from 1989–1990; that tax was in turn replaced by the Council Tax in 1993. Non-domestic (business) rates survive as the business rates system.

Northern Ireland

Domestic rates continue to exist in Northern Ireland, where they were retained and reformed rather than abolished. The domestic rate there combines a district rate set by councils and a regional rate set by the Northern Ireland Assembly, based on capital values assessed in 2005.

Other jurisdictions

Rates systems modelled on the British example persist in several countries, including Australia and New Zealand, where local councils levy "rates" on land or property values to fund local services, and in Hong Kong, where rates are charged as a percentage of rateable (rental) value.
