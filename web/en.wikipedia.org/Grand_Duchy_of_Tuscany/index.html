Grand Duchy of Tuscany

The Grand Duchy of Tuscany (Italian: Granducato di Toscana) was a state in central Italy that existed, with interruptions, from 1569 to 1860. It was created when Pope Pius V granted Cosimo I de' Medici, already Duke of Florence, the title of Grand Duke — the first use of that rank in Italy. Under the Medici and their Habsburg-Lorraine successors, Tuscany was one of the more prosperous and, in the 18th century, most reform-minded states of Italy, before being annexed to the rising Kingdom of Sardinia during Italian unification.

Medici era

Cosimo I (r. 1537–1574) built a centralised state, conquered Siena (1559), founded the navy of the Order of Saint Stephen to fight Barbary corsairs, and developed Livorno as a free port. His successors Francesco I and Ferdinand I patronised the arts and sciences; the Medici court supported Galileo Galilei, and Florence remained a cultural centre even as its banking and cloth industries declined. The dynasty decayed under Cosimo III and the last grand duke, Gian Gastone, who died childless in 1737.

Habsburg-Lorraine era

By the European settlement of 1735–1738, Tuscany passed to Francis Stephen of Lorraine, husband of Maria Theresa of Austria. His son Peter Leopold (r. 1765–1790) made Tuscany a laboratory of Enlightenment reform: he rationalised taxation and administration, promoted free trade in grain, reformed criminal law and, in 1786, promulgated a penal code that made Tuscany the first modern state to abolish the death penalty and torture. Leopold left in 1790 to become Holy Roman Emperor.

End of the grand duchy

French armies occupied Tuscany repeatedly during the Revolutionary and Napoleonic wars, when it was briefly the Kingdom of Etruria and then annexed to France. Restored in 1814, the grand duchy under Ferdinand III and Leopold II remained comparatively liberal; Leopold granted a constitution in 1848. During the Second Italian War of Independence in 1859, Leopold II abdicated and his son Ferdinand IV was deposed by a provisional government; a plebiscite in March 1860 voted overwhelmingly for annexation to Sardinia, and Tuscany became part of the Kingdom of Italy proclaimed in 1861.
