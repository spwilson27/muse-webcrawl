Mercedes-Benz 300 SLR

The Mercedes-Benz 300 SLR (W196S) is a two-seat sports racing car built for the 1955 World Sportscar Championship season. It was based on the chassis and drivetrain of the W196 Formula One car, but with a larger, 3.0-litre straight-eight engine derived from the 2.5-litre F1 unit, mounted at an angle to lower the hood line. "SLR" stands for Sport Leicht Rennen ("Sport Light Racing").

Racing record

Driven by Juan Manuel Fangio and Stirling Moss, the 300 SLR won the Mille Miglia (Moss, with navigator Denis Jenkinson, set a record average speed of nearly 98 mph that was never beaten), the RAC Tourist Trophy, the Targa Florio, and the Eifelrennen, securing the 1955 World Sportscar Championship.

Le Mans disaster and withdrawal

At the 1955 24 Hours of Le Mans, Pierre Levegh's 300 SLR was launched into the crowd after contact with Lance Macklin's Austin-Healey, killing Levegh and more than 80 spectators in the worst accident in motorsport history. Mercedes withdrew from the race and, at the end of the season, from motorsport entirely, returning only in the late 1980s.

The Uhlenhaut Coupé

Chief engineer Rudolf Uhlenhaut built two closed coupé versions for 1956, which never raced. In May 2022 one of them was sold at auction for €135 million, making it the most valuable car ever sold.
