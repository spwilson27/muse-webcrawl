Military supply-chain management

Military supply-chain management is the planning, sourcing, movement, storage and distribution of materiel — fuel, food, ammunition, spare parts, medical supplies and equipment — needed to sustain armed forces in garrison and on operations. It is the operational core of military logistics, applying commercial supply-chain techniques (demand forecasting, inventory optimisation, vendor management, track-and-trace) to the military problem of delivering the right supplies to deployed forces under time pressure and enemy threat.

Functions

The chain runs from national procurement and defence industry suppliers through strategic transport (sealift and airlift), theatre depots and distribution networks, to the "last tactical mile" reaching units in contact. Key processes include requirements forecasting by consumption rates, pre-positioning of war stocks, configuration management of spare parts, reverse logistics for repair and disposal, and contracting with private logistics providers — the U.S. LOGCAP programme being the largest example.

Modernisation

Forces have adopted enterprise resource planning systems (such as the U.S. Army's GCSS-Army), RFID and barcode asset visibility ("in-transit visibility"), and just-in-time "sense and respond" concepts to cut the huge inventories of earlier eras. The tension between leanness and resilience — exposed by high-intensity warfare since 2022, which devours artillery ammunition and spares — has pushed militaries back toward larger stockpiles and diversified suppliers.
