The State Duma (Russian: Государственная дума, Gosudarstvennaya duma) was the lower house of the legislature of the Russian Empire from 1906 to 1917. It was created by the October Manifesto of 1905, issued by Tsar Nicholas II during the Russian Revolution of 1905, and met in the Tauride Palace in St Petersburg.

The four Dumas

Four Dumas were elected. The First Duma (April–July 1906), dominated by the liberal Constitutional Democrats (Kadets), was dissolved by the Tsar after 72 days over demands for land reform. The Second Duma (1907) was even more radical and was also dissolved, after which Prime Minister Pyotr Stolypin changed the electoral law (the "coup of 3 June 1907") to weight voting towards landowners and the wealthy. The Third Duma (1907–1912), dominated by the conservative Octobrists, was the only one to serve a full five-year term. The Fourth Duma (1912–1917) played a key role in the events of 1917.

Powers

The Duma shared legislative power with the appointed State Council, and the Tsar retained an absolute veto, control over ministers, foreign policy and the military, and the power to dissolve the Duma. It could approve or reject the budget and legislation but could not compel the government to resign.

1917 and abolition

During the February Revolution of 1917, Duma deputies formed the Provisional Committee of the State Duma, which became the nucleus of the Russian Provisional Government. The Duma was formally dissolved in October 1917.
