Simon Fraser (20 May 1776 – 18 August 1862) was a Canadian fur trader and explorer of Scottish descent who charted much of what is now the province of British Columbia. Working for the Montreal-based North West Company, he established the first European settlements west of the Rocky Mountains in the region and in 1808 led the first recorded descent of the river that now bears his name, the Fraser River, from its headwaters to the sea.

Early life and the fur trade

Fraser was born in New York State to Loyalist parents of Scottish origin; after his father's death during the American Revolutionary War, the family settled in Canada. At fourteen he was apprenticed to the North West Company and by 1801 had become a partner. In 1805 he was assigned to extend the company's trade beyond the Rockies, following routes pioneered by Alexander Mackenzie.

New Caledonia

Fraser established a chain of posts in the interior district he named New Caledonia, including Fort McLeod (1805), Fort St. James and Fort Fraser (1806), and Fort George (1807, now Prince George). These were the first permanent European settlements in present-day British Columbia, supplied over difficult portage routes from the east.

Descent of the Fraser River, 1808

In the spring of 1808 Fraser set out with 24 men in four canoes to trace the great river flowing past his posts, believing it to be the Columbia. The descent through the Fraser Canyon was extraordinarily hazardous, requiring portages over cliffs and passages in which canoes were smashed and supplies lost; Fraser famously described stretches where the party had to cling to the rocks above the rapids. Reaching the river's mouth near present-day Vancouver at the beginning of July, he realised the river was not the Columbia — its latitude was wrong — and after hostilities with the Musqueam people the party retreated upstream, completing the return journey in just over a month.

Later life and legacy

Fraser continued in the fur trade, was arrested (and released) in connection with the violence surrounding the Red River Colony, and retired after the North West Company's merger with the Hudson's Bay Company in 1821. He died in Ontario in 1862. Simon Fraser University in Burnaby, British Columbia, founded in 1965, is named in his honour, as are numerous schools, bridges and the river itself.
