Freedom of speech

Freedom of speech
ConceptHuman right / civil liberty
Key instrumentsUDHR Art. 19; ICCPR Art. 19; First Amendment (US); ECHR Art. 10
Key thinkersJohn Milton, John Stuart Mill, Voltaire (attributed)

Freedom of speech is a principle that supports the freedom of an individual or a community to articulate their opinions and ideas without fear of retaliation, censorship, or legal sanction. The right to freedom of expression has been recognised as a human right in the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and international human rights law by the United Nations, and many countries have constitutional law that protects free speech.

History

The concept has roots in ancient Athens' parrhesia and in the free-speech clauses of early modern republics. John Milton's Areopagitica (1644) argued against press licensing, and John Stuart Mill's On Liberty (1859) gave the classic liberal defence: suppressing an opinion robs humanity of the chance to exchange error for truth. The First Amendment to the United States Constitution (1791) prohibits Congress from abridging the freedom of speech or of the press.

Limits

Most legal systems limit freedom of speech in areas such as incitement to violence, defamation, obscenity, perjury, threats and the disclosure of classified information. Hate speech laws in many democracies restrict expression intended to incite hatred against protected groups. The boundary between protected and prohibited speech remains one of the most contested questions in law and public policy, now extended to online platforms and social media.
