José de la Cruz Porfirio Díaz Mori (15 September 1830 – 2 July 1915) was a Mexican general and politician who served as President of Mexico for a total of about 31 years, a period known as the Porfiriato. A hero of the Reform War and the fight against the French intervention, he seized power under the Plan de Tuxtepec in 1876.

The Porfiriato

Díaz's regime brought political stability and rapid economic modernization: railroads, telegraphs, mining, and foreign investment expanded dramatically. Order was enforced by the Rurales and by a system of co-opted local bosses. Growth, however, came with land concentration, repression of labor, and the disenfranchisement of indigenous communities.

Fall

In a 1908 interview Díaz claimed Mexico was ready for democracy and that he would not seek re-election, encouraging opposition led by Francisco I. Madero. When Díaz ran again and jailed Madero, Madero's Plan de San Luis Potosí ignited the Mexican Revolution. Díaz resigned in May 1911 and died in exile in Paris.
