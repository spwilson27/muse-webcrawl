Physical Comedy

Physical comedy is a form of comedy focused on manipulation of the body for humorous effect, including slapstick, pratfalls, clowning, mime, and comic violence. It relies on action and visual gags rather than dialogue, making it readily accessible across language barriers.

History

Roots of physical comedy lie in ancient theatre, the commedia dell'arte of Renaissance Italy, and the clowning of music hall and vaudeville. Silent film made it a global art: Charlie Chaplin, Buster Keaton, Harold Lloyd, and the Marx Brothers built elaborate routines from falls, chases, and improbable stunts. Later masters include Lucille Ball, Jacques Tati, Rowan Atkinson (as Mr. Bean), and Jim Carrey.

Techniques

Common devices include the pratfall, the double take, comic timing, exaggerated reactions, and precisely choreographed "business" with props. Because convincing falls and fights require training, physical comedians draw on acrobatics, dance, and stage combat.
