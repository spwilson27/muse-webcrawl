Training and development

Training and development involves improving the effectiveness of organizations and the individuals and teams within them. Training may be viewed as related to immediate changes in organizational effectiveness via organized instruction, while development is related to the progress of longer-term organizational and employee goals. While training and development technically have differing definitions, the two terms are often used interchangeably and/or together. Training and development has historically been a topic within applied psychology but has since become closely associated with human resources management, talent management, human resources development, instructional design, human factors, and knowledge management.

Practice

The systematic approach to training typically proceeds through needs assessment, instructional design (frameworks such as ADDIE), delivery — classroom, on-the-job, simulation, or e-learning — and evaluation. Kirkpatrick's four levels of evaluation (reaction, learning, behaviour, and results) are widely used to assess effectiveness.

Common activities include onboarding and orientation, technical and compliance training, leadership development, coaching and mentoring, job rotation, and succession planning. Organizations often manage these through learning management systems (LMS) and formal talent-development strategies.

History

Organized training has roots in apprenticeship systems and gained prominence with the mass production demands of the World Wars, when programs such as Training Within Industry taught supervisors to instruct workers rapidly. After the Second World War the field professionalized, and in recent decades digital learning, microlearning, and skills-based talent models have reshaped practice.
