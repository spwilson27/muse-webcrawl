Prediction market

Prediction markets, also known as betting markets, information markets, decision markets, idea futures, or event derivatives, are open markets that enable the prediction of specific outcomes using financial incentives. They are exchange-traded markets established for trading contracts whose payoffs are tied to a future event, such as an election result or a sales figure.

How they work

In a typical prediction market, a contract that pays $1 if an event occurs trades at a price between $0 and $1, which can be interpreted as the crowd's estimated probability of the event. Traders with private information profit by correcting the price, so the market aggregates dispersed knowledge — an idea related to the efficient-market hypothesis and to Friedrich Hayek's account of prices as information.

History and examples

The Iowa Electronic Markets, run by the University of Iowa since 1988, pioneered academic election forecasting markets and have often outperformed opinion polls. Commercial and play-money markets such as Intrade (closed 2013), PredictIt, and Kalshi have covered elections, economics, and current events. Companies have also run internal prediction markets to forecast product launches and project deadlines.

Accuracy and criticism

Research generally finds prediction market prices to be well calibrated and competitive with expert forecasts, though thin markets can be noisy or manipulated, and regulatory classification as gambling or derivatives trading has limited their operation in some jurisdictions.
