Sidoarjo mud flow

Location: Sidoarjo, East Java, Indonesia
Began: 29 May 2006
Type: Mud volcano eruption

The Sidoarjo mud flow (commonly called Lusi, a contraction of Indonesian Lumpur Sidoarjo, or the Lapindo mud disaster) is an erupting mud volcano in Sidoarjo Regency, East Java, Indonesia, that began on 29 May 2006 and has continued for many years.

Cause

The eruption began days after drilling at the Banjar Panji-1 gas exploration well operated by Lapindo Brantas. Debate continues over whether drilling triggered the flow or whether the Yogyakarta earthquake two days earlier reactivated faults. Most peer-reviewed studies have favoured the drilling hypothesis; the company has disputed responsibility.

Impact

Hot mud inundated villages, factories, roads and rice fields, eventually covering several square kilometres despite levees. Tens of thousands of people were displaced, and compensation disputes lasted for years. Peak discharge reached roughly 180,000 cubic metres per day before gradually declining.
