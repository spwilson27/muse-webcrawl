Law of agency

The principal–agent relationship

The law of agency is an area of commercial law dealing with contractual and quasi-contractual relationships in which a person, the agent, is authorized to act on behalf of another, the principal, to create legal relations with a third party.

Creation of agency

Agency may be created by express agreement, by implication from conduct, by ratification of acts already performed, or by operation of law, as in cases of necessity. An agent's authority may be actual (express or implied) or apparent (ostensible), the latter binding the principal when third parties reasonably rely on the principal's representations.

Duties

Agents owe their principals fiduciary duties, including loyalty, obedience, care, and the duty to account and not to make secret profits. Principals in turn must remunerate the agent, reimburse expenses, and indemnify the agent for liabilities properly incurred.

Applications

Agency principles underpin much of commercial life: employees acting for employers, brokers, estate agents, company directors acting for their companies, and powers of attorney. An undisclosed principal may in many circumstances sue and be sued on contracts made by the agent.
