Roger Joseph Ebert (June 18, 1942 – April 4, 2013) was an American film critic, film historian, journalist, essayist, screenwriter, and author. He was a film critic for the Chicago Sun-Times from 1967 until his death in 2013. In 1975, Ebert became the first film critic to win the Pulitzer Prize for Criticism. Neil Steinberg of the Chicago Sun-Times said Ebert was "without question the nation's most prominent and influential film critic", and Kenneth Turan of the Los Angeles Times called him "the best-known film critic in America".

Television career

Ebert and Chicago Tribune critic Gene Siskel co-hosted a series of popular film-review television programs, beginning with Sneak Previews on PBS in 1975 and continuing with At the Movies and Siskel & Ebert & the Movies. Their "thumbs up / thumbs down" verdicts became a widely recognized shorthand for film quality. After Siskel's death in 1999, Ebert continued the program with Richard Roeper as Ebert & Roeper until 2007.

Writing and views

Ebert wrote more than twenty books, including annual movie guides, the Great Movies series of essays on classic films, a memoir (Life Itself, 2011), and the screenplay for Russ Meyer's Beyond the Valley of the Dolls (1970). He was an early and enthusiastic adopter of the internet, and his website, rogerebert.com, became a major archive of his reviews. He was a recovering alcoholic, a member of Alcoholics Anonymous, and wrote candidly about addiction, faith, and science; he described himself as an agnostic with a strong appreciation for the mystery of existence.

Illness and death

Diagnosed with thyroid cancer in 2002 and salivary-gland cancer thereafter, Ebert underwent surgeries that cost him his lower jaw and the ability to speak, eat, or drink in 2006. He continued writing prolifically using text-to-speech technology and became an active blogger and social-media presence. He died on April 4, 2013, two days after announcing a "leave of presence" to deal with a recurrence of cancer. The documentary Life Itself (2014), directed by Steve James, chronicles his life and final months.

Legacy

Ebert received a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame in 2005. The Ebertfest film festival, held annually in his hometown of Champaign-Urbana, Illinois, was founded in his honor. His four-star review scale and championing of filmmakers from Martin Scorsese to emerging independent directors shaped generations of moviegoers.
