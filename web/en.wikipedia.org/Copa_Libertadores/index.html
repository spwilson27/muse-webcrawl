Copa Libertadores

The Copa Libertadores de América is an annual international club football competition organised by CONMEBOL since 1960. It is the highest level of competition in South American club football, analogous to the UEFA Champions League in Europe. The tournament is named in honour of the Libertadores, the leaders of the Latin American wars of independence.

History

The competition began in 1960 with seven champions; Uruguay's Peñarol won the first two editions. Argentine club Independiente dominated the 1970s, winning a record seven titles including four consecutively (1972–75). Mexican clubs participated as guests from 1998 to 2016. Since 2019 the final has been played as a single match at a pre-selected venue, replacing the traditional two-legged final.

Format

The modern tournament includes qualifying stages, a group stage of eight groups of four, and two-legged knockout rounds through the semi-finals. The champion qualifies for the FIFA Club World Cup (and its successor format) and contests the Recopa Sudamericana against the Copa Sudamericana winner.

Notable finals and records

The 2018 final between Argentine rivals Boca Juniors and River Plate — the "Superclásico final" — was moved to Madrid after crowd violence in Buenos Aires, a unique episode in the tournament's history. Alberto Spencer of Peñarol and Barcelona SC remains the all-time top scorer with 54 goals.
