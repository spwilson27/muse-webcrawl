Villain

A villain (also known in the feminine as a villainess) is a stock character, whether based on a historical narrative or one of literary fiction. Villains are a type of character, or perhaps an agent of certain kinds of narrative, who are evil or whose intent is to harm others. Random House Unabridged Dictionary defines such a character as "a cruelly malicious person who is involved in or devoted to wickedness or crime; scoundrel; or a character in a play, novel, or the like, who constitutes an important evil agency in the plot".

Etymology

The word derives from the Late Latin villanus, a farmhand or peasant attached to a villa (country estate). In medieval usage it simply denoted a low-born serf; over time, through class prejudice, it acquired associations with rusticity, dishonesty and finally criminal wickedness.

Role in narrative

The villain typically serves as the antagonist of the hero or protagonist, providing the conflict that drives the story. Classic literary villains include Iago in Othello, Professor Moriarty in the Sherlock Holmes stories, and Count Dracula in Bram Stoker's novel. In film and comics, the supervillain is an exaggerated counterpart to the superhero. Modern storytelling often complicates the role, giving villains sympathetic motives or making them antiheroes, and genres such as the picaresque centre the villain as protagonist.
