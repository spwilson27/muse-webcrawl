NeXT, Inc. (later NeXT Software, Inc.) was an American computer hardware and software company founded in 1985 by Apple co-founder Steve Jobs after his resignation from Apple. Based in Redwood City, California, NeXT developed and manufactured a series of computer workstations intended for the higher-education and business markets, including the NeXT Computer (1988), NeXTcube (1990) and NeXTstation (1990).

History

Jobs founded NeXT with several former Apple colleagues, taking the idea of a powerful, easy-to-use machine for academia. The NeXT Computer was unveiled in October 1988; its US$6,500 price limited sales — about 50,000 machines were sold in total — but the systems were admired for their object-oriented operating system, NeXTSTEP, the Motorola 68040 processor, a built-in DSP, and an innovative magneto-optical drive. Tim Berners-Lee wrote the first web browser and web server on a NeXTcube at CERN in 1990.

Software and WebObjects

In 1993 NeXT abandoned hardware manufacturing and became a software company, porting NeXTSTEP to Intel and other architectures. In 1996 it released WebObjects, an application server and development framework that became successful for building e-commerce sites (Dell's online store was a notable early user).

Acquisition by Apple

On 20 December 1996, Apple announced it would acquire NeXT for about US$429 million in cash and stock. The deal closed in February 1997 and brought Jobs back to Apple, first as adviser and soon as interim CEO. NeXTSTEP's technology — its Mach kernel and BSD layers, the Cocoa frameworks and Objective-C — became the foundation of Mac OS X (now macOS) and later iOS.
