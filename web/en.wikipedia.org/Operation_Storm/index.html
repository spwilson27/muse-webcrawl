Operation Storm

Operation Storm (Croatian: Operacija Oluja)
Date: 4–7 August 1995
Location: Croatia
Part of: Croatian War of Independence
Result: Decisive Croatian victory; end of the Republic of Serbian Krajina

Operation Storm (Serbo-Croatian: Operacija Oluja) was the last major battle of the Croatian War of Independence and a decisive factor in the outcome of the Bosnian War. It was a decisive victory for the Croatian Army (HV), which attacked across a 630-kilometre front against the self-declared proto-state Republic of Serbian Krajina and, in conjunction with the Army of the Republic of Bosnia and Herzegovina, ended the Siege of Bihać.

The offensive

The operation began at dawn on 4 August 1995 with some 130,000 Croatian troops. Knin, the Krajina capital, fell on 5 August, and within 84 hours the bulk of the territory seized by Serb forces in 1991 had been retaken. The offensive was supported by earlier cooperation under the Split Agreement between Croatia and Bosnia and Herzegovina and by years of Croatian Army training and re-equipment.

Aftermath

Between 150,000 and 200,000 Serbs fled or were expelled from the region, and war crimes against Serb civilians and prisoners were documented during and after the operation, leading to proceedings at the ICTY in The Hague; Croatian generals Ante Gotovina and Mladen Markač were convicted at trial but acquitted on appeal in 2012. The victory is commemorated in Croatia on 5 August as Victory and Homeland Thanksgiving Day, while in Serbia the operation is mourned as an exodus. Strategically, Storm shifted the balance of power in Bosnia and helped precipitate the Dayton peace negotiations.
