Max-flow min-cut theorem

In computer science and optimization theory, the max-flow min-cut theorem states that in a flow network, the maximum amount of flow passing from the source to the sink is equal to the total weight of the edges in a minimum cut, that is, the smallest total weight of the edges which if removed would disconnect the source from the sink. The theorem is a special case of linear programming duality and underlies algorithms such as Ford–Fulkerson and Edmonds–Karp.

Max-flow min-cut theorem
FieldGraph theory, optimization
Proved1950s (Elias, Feinstein & Shannon; Ford & Fulkerson)
GeneralizesMenger's theorem, König's theorem

Statement

Given a directed graph with edge capacities, a source s and a sink t, the value of a maximum s–t flow equals the capacity of a minimum s–t cut. Because capacities may be integral, the theorem also implies that a maximum flow can be chosen integral when capacities are integers.

Applications

The theorem is used in network design, transportation, bipartite matching, image segmentation, and project selection problems. It implies Menger's theorem on disjoint paths and connects flow theory to linear programming duality.
