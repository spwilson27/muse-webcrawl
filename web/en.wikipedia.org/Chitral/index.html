Chitral

Chitral is a town and district in the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province of northern Pakistan, set in a high valley of the Hindu Kush along the Chitral River (known in its lower course as the Kunar River). Dominated by Tirich Mir (7,708 m), the highest peak of the Hindu Kush, Chitral is renowned for its mountain scenery, polo tradition, and distinctive cultures, including the Kalash valleys.

History

Chitral was historically a princely state ruled by the Katoor dynasty, whose ruler bore the title Mehtar. Its strategic position among passes leading to the Wakhan Corridor and Central Asia made it a pawn in the Great Game between Britain and Russia; in 1895 British and Chitrali forces endured the famous Siege of Chitral Fort. The state acceded to Pakistan in 1947 and was fully merged into Pakistan in 1969.

Culture

The majority speak Khowar, an Indo-Aryan language. The Kalash people of the Bumburet, Rumbur, and Birir valleys preserve a unique polytheistic religion and festival calendar that draws anthropologists and tourists. Polo, played in a freestyle form, is the region's signature sport; the annual Shandur Polo Festival, held on the world's highest polo ground, pits Chitral against Gilgit-Baltistan.

Access

Chitral is connected by the Lowari Tunnel (opened in stages from 2017) and by seasonal passes, as well as by flights to Chitral Airport, weather permitting.
