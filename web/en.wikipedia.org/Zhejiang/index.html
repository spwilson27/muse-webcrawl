Zhejiang (Chinese: 浙江) is a coastal province in East China, on the East China Sea south of the Yangtze River Delta. Its capital and largest city is Hangzhou. One of China's wealthiest and most economically dynamic provinces, Zhejiang is known for its private enterprise, e-commerce sector, and scenic landscapes.

Geography

The province takes its name from the Zhe River, the old name of the Qiantang River, famous for the world's largest tidal bore. Northern Zhejiang is a flat, canal-laced plain that was the heartland of the Neolithic Liangzhu culture; the south and west are hilly and mountainous. The coast is indented, with the Zhoushan Archipelago — China's largest — offshore, and the port of Ningbo-Zhoushan is among the world's busiest by cargo tonnage.

Economy and culture

Since reform and opening up, Zhejiang has pioneered market-oriented development: cities such as Wenzhou became models of private business, and Yiwu hosts the world's largest small-commodities market. Hangzhou is the home of Alibaba Group and a centre of China's digital economy. Cultural landmarks include West Lake in Hangzhou, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, the Grand Canal, Longjing tea, and Jinhua ham and Shaoxing wine among its culinary products. The dominant language is Wu Chinese.
