The Battle of Nicopolis took place on 25 September 1396 near the Danube fortress of Nicopolis in present-day Bulgaria. It was the climax of the Crusade of Nicopolis, the last large-scale crusade of the Middle Ages, and ended in a crushing victory for the Ottoman Sultan Bayezid I over a crusading army led by King Sigismund of Hungary and a Franco-Burgundian contingent under John the Fearless.

Background

Responding to Ottoman expansion into the Balkans, a coalition army — including Hungarian, French, Burgundian, German, Hospitaller, Wallachian, and other troops — assembled in Hungary in 1396 and marched down the Danube, besieging Nicopolis after failing to take the smaller fortresses along the way.

The battle

The proud French knights, rejecting Sigismund's cautious plan, charged first. They cut through the Ottoman light infantry and cavalry and fought up a hill into the sipahi cavalry, but exhausted and disordered, they were then struck by Bayezid's reserves, including fresh Serbian heavy cavalry under Stefan Lazarević. The crusader army disintegrated; Sigismund escaped by boat down the Danube, while thousands of crusaders were killed or drowned. Many captured nobles were held for enormous ransoms, and Bayezid had large numbers of common prisoners executed in reprisal for the crusaders' earlier massacre of prisoners.

Aftermath

The defeat ended serious Western attempts to relieve the Balkans for decades and secured Ottoman dominance in south-eastern Europe. Bayezid tightened his grip on the region until his own defeat at the Battle of Ankara in 1402.
