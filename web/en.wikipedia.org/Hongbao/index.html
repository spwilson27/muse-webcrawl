Red envelope (hongbao)

Red envelopeChinese: 紅包 (hóngbāo)
Contents: money
Occasions: Lunar New Year, weddings, births
Given by: elders and married couples

A red envelope (Mandarin: hóngbāo; Cantonese: lai see) is a monetary gift enclosed in a decorative red packet, given during holidays and special occasions in China and other East and Southeast Asian societies. Red symbolises good luck, prosperity and protection from evil spirits.

Customs

During Lunar New Year, married elders give hongbao to children and unmarried juniors, traditionally in exchange for kneeling and offering New Year greetings — a ritual called ya sui qian ("suppressing Sui money"), after the legend of a demon named Sui frightened away by the noise of coins. Red envelopes are also given at weddings, birthdays (especially of elders), births and graduations.

Digital hongbao

In 2014, Tencent's WeChat launched electronic red envelopes, allowing users to send money gifts through the app; the feature exploded in popularity, with billions of digital hongbao exchanged over Lunar New Year periods and group "red envelope rain" games becoming a social phenomenon. Alipay and other platforms run similar promotions.
