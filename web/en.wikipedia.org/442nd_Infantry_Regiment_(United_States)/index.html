442nd Infantry Regiment (United States)

The 442nd Infantry Regiment was an infantry regiment of the United States Army, composed almost entirely of second-generation Japanese American soldiers (Nisei). Organized in 1943 as the 442nd Regimental Combat Team, it fought in Europe during World War II and became, for its size and length of service, the most decorated unit in American military history.

Formation

After the attack on Pearl Harbor, Japanese Americans were barred from enlistment, and some 120,000 people of Japanese ancestry were forcibly relocated to internment camps. In 1943 the Army reversed course and called for volunteers; thousands enlisted from Hawaii and from the mainland camps. The unit's motto, "Go for Broke," came from Hawaiian gamblers' slang.

Combat in Europe

The 442nd fought in Italy and France, including the rescue of the "Lost Battalion" of the 141st Infantry in the Vosges Mountains in October 1944, an action in which it suffered heavy casualties. Together with the 100th Infantry Battalion, it helped liberate towns in France and Italy and reached the Gothic Line in 1945.

Decorations and legacy

Members earned more than 18,000 individual decorations, including 21 Medals of Honor, and the unit received eight Presidential Unit Citations. In 2010 the regiment's veterans were awarded the Congressional Gold Medal. The 442nd's record is widely credited with helping to shift American public opinion toward Japanese Americans in the postwar era.
