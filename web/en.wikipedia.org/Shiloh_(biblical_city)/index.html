Shiloh (biblical city)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Tel Shiloh excavation site]

Shiloh (Hebrew: שִׁילֹה, Šīlō) was an ancient city and sanctuary in Samaria, in the hill country of Ephraim. According to the Hebrew Bible, Shiloh was the principal Israelite cultic centre before the construction of the First Temple in Jerusalem. It was the resting place of the Tabernacle and the Ark of the Covenant, and the site where the tribal allotments of the Promised Land were drawn by lot under Joshua.

Shiloh features prominently in the books of Joshua, Judges, and 1 Samuel. It was the home of the high priest Eli and the place where the prophet Samuel was raised in service at the sanctuary. The annual festival at Shiloh, mentioned in Judges 21, included dancing in the vineyards.

Decline and archaeology

The Bible implies that Shiloh fell into ruin after the Philistines captured the Ark (c. 1050 BCE); Jeremiah later cited its destruction as a warning to Jerusalem. The site, identified with modern Khirbet Seilun (Tel Shiloh), has been excavated by Danish expeditions in the 1920s and 1930s and by Israeli teams since the 1980s, uncovering Bronze Age and Iron Age remains, fortifications, and storage buildings.

Today Tel Shiloh is an archaeological park adjacent to the modern Israeli settlement of Shilo in the West Bank.
