The Tragical History of the Life and Death of Doctor Faustus

AuthorChristopher Marlowe
Writtenc. 1588–1593
First published1604 (A-text); 1616 (B-text)
GenreTragedy

The Tragical History of the Life and Death of Doctor Faustus, commonly referred to simply as Doctor Faustus, is a play by Christopher Marlowe, believed to have been first performed around 1588–1593. Based on the German Faustbuch, it tells the story of John Faustus, a brilliant scholar of Wittenberg who, dissatisfied with the limits of traditional knowledge, conjures the devil Mephistopheles and sells his soul in exchange for twenty-four years of service.

Synopsis

Doctor Faustus, having mastered law, medicine, divinity, philosophy and astronomy, grows dissatisfied with the limits of human knowledge and turns to necromancy. He conjures Mephistopheles, a prince of Hell, and agrees to a contract: twenty-four years of Mephistopheles's service in exchange for his soul. During the intervening years, Faustus travels widely and uses his powers for feats of magic—summoning Helen of Troy, entertaining the Duke and Duchess of Vanholt, and conjuring grapes for the Pope's feast—while repeatedly vacillating between repentance and despair.

The A-text and B-text

The play survives in two major versions: the shorter "A-text" (1604) and the longer "B-text" (1616), which includes additional scenes likely by other hands. Scholarly debate about which text is more authoritative has continued for centuries. The play is regarded as a landmark of English Renaissance drama and the source of the term "Faustian bargain".

Themes

Themes include sin and damnation, the conflict between medieval and Renaissance values, ambition, and the limits of human knowledge. Faustus's final soliloquy—beginning "O Faustus, now hast thou but one bare hour to live"—is among the most famous passages in English literature.
