Ulster Cycle

The Ulster Cycle (Irish: an Rúraíocht), one of the four great cycles of Irish mythology, is a large body of prose and verse set in and around the reign of King Conchobar mac Nessa of Ulster, around the 1st century BC/AD. Its central hero is Conchobar's nephew Cú Chulainn, and its best-known tale is the epic Táin Bó Cúailnge ("The Cattle Raid of Cooley").

Setting and characters

The tales are set at Emain Macha (Navan Fort, near Armagh), Conchobar's royal court, and concern the wars between Ulster and Connacht under Queen Medb and King Ailill. Other major figures include the warriors Fergus mac Róich, Conall Cernach and the tragic Deirdre of the Sorrows. The cycle depicts a heroic warrior society of chariot warfare, single combat, cattle raiding and feasting.

Texts

The tales survive in manuscripts such as the Book of Leinster, Lebor na hUidre and the Yellow Book of Lecan. The Táin exists in several recensions. Shorter tales are traditionally classed by type — conceptions and births (comperta), cattle raids (tána), wooings (tochmarca), battles (catha) and tragedies (aideda).

Influence

The cycle was a major source for the Irish Literary Revival, inspiring works by W. B. Yeats and Lady Gregory, and remains central to the study of early Irish literature and Celtic mythology.
