Poisoning of Alexander Litvinenko

Event
Date: Poisoned 1 November 2006; died 23 November 2006
Location: London, United Kingdom
Victim: Alexander Litvinenko
Agent: Polonium-210
UK inquiry finding (2016): assassination probably approved at the highest level of the Russian state

The poisoning of Alexander Litvinenko was the murder of the naturalised British former Russian Federal Security Service (FSB) officer in London in November 2006, using the radioactive isotope polonium-210. Litvinenko fell ill on 1 November 2006 after meeting two former Russian agents, Andrey Lugovoy and Dmitry Kovtun, at the Millennium Hotel in Mayfair, and died of acute radiation syndrome on 23 November — the first confirmed case of polonium-210-induced acute radiation syndrome.

Investigation

British investigators traced a trail of polonium contamination across London locations — hotels, restaurants, offices, and aircraft — left before and after the poisoning. Traces at the Itsu sushi restaurant and Litvinenko's home helped reconstruct the events; the source was traced to a teapot at the Pine Bar of the Millennium Hotel.

Aftermath and inquiry

The Crown Prosecution Service charged Lugovoy with murder in 2007, but Russia refused extradition, causing a major diplomatic rift. A public inquiry chaired by Sir Robert Owen reported in January 2016, concluding that Lugovoy and Kovtun had poisoned Litvinenko and that the operation was probably approved by the FSB leadership and President Vladimir Putin. In 2021 the European Court of Human Rights also found Russia responsible for the assassination.
