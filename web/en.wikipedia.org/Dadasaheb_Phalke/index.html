Dadasaheb Phalke

Dhundiraj Govind Phalke (30 April 1870 – 16 February 1944), popularly known as Dadasaheb Phalke, was an Indian producer-director-screenwriter, known as "the Father of Indian cinema". His debut film, Raja Harishchandra (1913), was the first Indian-made feature film, and marked the beginning of the Indian film industry. Phalke made 95 feature-length films and 27 short films over a career spanning 19 years, until 1937.

Early life

Phalke was born in Trimbak, near Nashik, into a Marathi-speaking Brahmin family. He studied sculpture at Mumbai's Sir J. J. School of Art and later at the Maharaja Sayajirao University in Baroda, training in painting, drawing, theatre and photography. He worked as a portrait painter and photographer, then as a draftsman for the Archaeological Survey of India, before setting up a printing press in Lonavala. Watching the French film The Life of Christ in 1910 inspired him to make moving pictures with Indian mythological subjects.

Career

Phalke travelled to London in 1912 to buy equipment and learn filmmaking, trained briefly at Cabourn's studio, and returned with a Williamson camera. Mortgaging his possessions and enduring widespread scepticism, he shot Raja Harishchandra, casting men in female roles and his own family in the production. Released on 3 May 1913 in Bombay, it was a commercial success. His subsequent mythologicals — Mohini Bhasmasur (1913), Satyavan Savitri (1914), Lanka Dahan (1917) and Shri Krishna Janma (1918) — established his company, the Hindustan Cinema Films Company. The advent of sound in the 1930s left his silent-era style behind; his last film was Gangavataran (1937). He died in Nashik in 1944, largely forgotten by the industry he founded.

Legacy

The Dadasaheb Phalke Award, instituted by the Government of India in 1969 on his birth centenary, is Indian cinema's highest honour, presented annually at the National Film Awards for lifetime contribution. A postage stamp was issued in his honour in 1971, and the 2009 Marathi film Harishchandrachi Factory dramatised the making of his first film.
