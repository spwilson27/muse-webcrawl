Siege of Kazan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The siege of Kazan in 1552 was the final battle of the Russo-Kazan Wars, in which the forces of Tsar Ivan IV of Russia ("Ivan the Terrible") conquered the Khanate of Kazan. The fall of Kazan marked a turning point in Russian expansion, opening the Volga region to Russian settlement and beginning the transformation of Russia into a multi-ethnic empire.

Background

The Khanate of Kazan, a successor state of the Golden Horde, had contested control of the middle Volga with Moscow for over a century. Ivan IV built the fortress of Sviyazhsk upstream from Kazan in 1551 as a staging base, secured the allegiance of the Mountain Cheremis (Mari) on the west bank, and assembled a large army — often estimated at around 150,000 men with 150 cannon.

The siege

The Russian army invested Kazan in late August 1552. The city's defenders, led by Khan Yadegar Mokhammad, numbered perhaps 30,000–50,000, including Nogai and Crimean auxiliaries. The Russians built siege towers, batteries and trenches, cut off the city's water supply, and dug mines under the walls, with the help of foreign engineers. After repeated assaults and the detonation of a great mine, Russian troops stormed the city on 2 October 1552. The khan was captured, and much of the male population was massacred; the city was looted and its mosques destroyed.

Aftermath

The khanate was annexed to Russia. Guerrilla resistance in the countryside continued for several years, and Kazan was repopulated with Russian settlers. The conquest was followed in 1556 by the fall of Astrakhan, giving Russia control of the entire Volga. St Basil's Cathedral in Moscow was commissioned to commemorate the victory. The siege remains a sensitive episode in Tatar historical memory, commemorated annually in Tatarstan.
