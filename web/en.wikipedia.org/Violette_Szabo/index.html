Violette Reine Elizabeth Szabo, GC (née Bushell; 26 June 1921 – c. 5 February 1945) was a British-French Special Operations Executive (SOE) agent during the Second World War and a posthumous recipient of the George Cross. Captured after a gun battle during her second mission to France in June 1944, she was interrogated, deported to Germany and executed at Ravensbrück concentration camp.

Early life

Szabo was born in Paris to an English father and French mother and raised in London. In 1940 she married Étienne Szabo, a French Foreign Legion officer, who was killed at the Second Battle of El Alamein in 1942. Fluent in French, she joined the Auxiliary Territorial Service and was recruited by SOE in 1943, training as a field agent despite having a young daughter.

Missions to France

Her first mission, in April 1944, assessed damage to the Salesman circuit in Rouen after arrests. On her second mission, parachuted into France just after D-Day in June 1944, her party was ambushed near Salon-la-Tour; Szabo covered her companions' escape with a Sten gun until her ammunition ran out and she was captured. She was tortured by the Gestapo but revealed nothing.

Death and honours

Deported to Ravensbrück, she endured hard labour and was executed, probably on 5 February 1945, together with fellow agents Denise Bloch and Lilian Rolfe. She was posthumously awarded the George Cross in 1946, presented to her young daughter Tania. Her story was told in the book and 1958 film Carve Her Name with Pride, and she is commemorated by a museum in Wormelow, Herefordshire, and a memorial in Lambeth, London.
