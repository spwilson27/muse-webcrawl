Quantum gravity

Quantum gravity (QG) is a field of theoretical physics that seeks to describe gravity in accordance with the principles of quantum mechanics. It deals with environments in which neither gravitational nor quantum effects can be ignored, such as in the vicinity of black holes or similar compact astrophysical objects, as well as in the early universe shortly after the Big Bang.

Three of the four fundamental forces are described within quantum field theory, but gravity is described by general relativity, which treats spacetime as a smooth, dynamic geometry. Attempts to quantise gravity as an ordinary field theory fail because general relativity is perturbatively non-renormalisable, and conceptually the two frameworks conflict over the nature of time, background independence and the meaning of spacetime at the Planck scale.

Approaches

The two most developed approaches are string theory, in which particles are extended one-dimensional strings and gravity emerges from closed-string states, and loop quantum gravity, which quantises spacetime geometry directly and predicts discrete spectra for area and volume. Other programmes include causal dynamical triangulations, asymptotic safety, causal set theory and twistor theory. Indirect evidence and constraints come from black hole thermodynamics — the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy formula suggests that a finite region of space contains a finite amount of information — and from the holographic principle and the AdS/CFT correspondence, which provide concrete but special models of quantum gravity in anti-de Sitter space.

Experimental status

Direct experiments probing the Planck energy (around 1019 GeV) are far beyond present technology, so quantum gravity remains primarily theoretical, with possible observational windows in cosmology, gamma-ray burst timing, and gravitational-wave astronomy. There is currently no complete, universally accepted theory of quantum gravity.
