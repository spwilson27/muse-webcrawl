Auguste and Louis Lumière

Auguste (1862–1954) and Louis Lumière (1864–1948), the Lumière brothers, were French manufacturers and pioneers of cinema. Working in their father's photographic business in Lyon, they patented the Cinématographe in 1895, a device that recorded, developed and projected motion pictures.

First screenings

Their first public paying screening on 28 December 1895 at the Grand Café in Paris is widely regarded as the birth of cinema. Early films included Workers Leaving the Lumière Factory and L'Arroseur arrosé.

Other work

The brothers also developed the Autochrome, the first commercially successful colour photography process, marketed from 1907.
