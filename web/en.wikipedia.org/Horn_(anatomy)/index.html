Horn (anatomy)

The permanent, unbranched horns of a goat

A horn is a permanent pointed projection on the head of various animals that consists of a covering of keratin and other proteins surrounding a core of live bone. Horns are distinct from antlers, which are not permanent, are made solely of bone, and are typically shed and regrown each year.

Occurrence and structure

True horns occur in the family Bovidae — cattle, sheep, goats, antelopes and their relatives. A horn grows from the frontal bone of the skull, is usually present in both sexes (though often larger in males), and grows throughout the animal's life. Other animals bear horn-like structures, such as the keratin horn of the rhinoceros, which lacks a bony core, and the ossicones of giraffes.

Function

Horns are used in defence against predators, in contests between males for mating rights, and in establishing dominance within herds. Their size and shape vary enormously, from the short spikes of duikers to the massive curled horns of the bighorn sheep and the lyre-shaped horns of the kudu.

Human uses

Horn has historically been worked into cups, combs, buttons, spoons and powder flasks, and musical instruments such as the shofar and alphorn take their names from the material. Demand for rhinoceros horn in traditional medicine and as a status symbol has driven poaching that threatens several rhino species.
