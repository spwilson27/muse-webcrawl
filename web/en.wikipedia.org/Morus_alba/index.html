Morus alba, known as white mulberry, is a fast-growing, small to medium-sized mulberry tree that grows to roughly 10–20 metres tall. It is native to China and is widely cultivated and naturalized elsewhere.

Ecology and uses

The leaves of the white mulberry are the preferred food of the silkworm (Bombyx mori), and the species has been cultivated for thousands of years in support of sericulture. The tree was introduced to many regions along with silk production. Its fruit, ripening from white to pink or dark purple, is edible though generally less flavourful than that of the black mulberry (Morus nigra).

The tree is a prolific pollen producer and is considered a significant allergen in some cities; in a few places, such as parts of the southwestern United States, planting male white mulberries has been restricted by pollen-control ordinances. The species can become invasive where it hybridizes with the native red mulberry (Morus rubra) in North America.

Cultivation

Morus alba is tolerant of drought, poor soils, and urban conditions, which has made it a common street and shade tree, though its fruit can stain pavements.
