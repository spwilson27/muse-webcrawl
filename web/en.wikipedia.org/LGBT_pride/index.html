LGBT pride

LGBT pride (also known as gay pride or simply pride) is the promotion of self-affirmation, dignity, equality and increased visibility of lesbian, gay, bisexual and transgender (LGBT) people as a social group. Pride, as opposed to shame and social stigma, is the predominant outlook that bolsters most LGBT rights movements.

Origins

The modern pride movement grew out of the Stonewall riots of June 1969 in New York City and the commemorative marches held a year later, in 1970, in New York, Los Angeles, San Francisco and Chicago. June is widely observed as Pride Month, and events range from parades and parties to political rallies and memorials.

Symbols

The rainbow flag, designed by Gilbert Baker in 1978, is the best-known symbol of LGBT pride; later designs include the Progress Pride flag, which adds chevrons representing trans people and marginalised communities of colour. Pride events are now held in cities worldwide, some drawing millions of participants.
