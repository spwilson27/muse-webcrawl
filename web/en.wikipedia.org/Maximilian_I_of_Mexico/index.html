Maximilian I (Spanish: Fernando Maximiliano José María de Habsburgo-Lorena; 6 July 1832 – 19 June 1867) was an Austrian archduke who reigned as the only emperor of the Second Mexican Empire from 1864 to 1867. A younger brother of Emperor Franz Joseph I of Austria, he accepted the Mexican crown offered by Napoleon III of France and Mexican conservatives, and was executed after the republican forces of Benito Juárez prevailed.

Early life

Maximilian served in the Austrian navy and as Governor-General of Lombardy–Venetia (1857–1859), where his liberal administration and conciliatory policies won praise. With his wife Charlotte of Belgium he built Miramare Castle near Trieste.

The Mexican adventure

France, which had invaded Mexico in 1861 under the pretext of collecting debts, installed an empire to create a Catholic, French-aligned monarchy in the Americas. Maximilian, having renounced his Austrian succession rights, accepted the throne after a stage-managed plebiscite and arrived in Mexico in May 1864. A liberal by temperament, he disappointed his conservative sponsors by retaining many of Juárez's reform laws and seeking reconciliation, while the republican government, backed increasingly by the United States after its Civil War, never ceased to resist. Under American pressure and facing unrest at home, Napoleon III withdrew French troops in 1866–67; Maximilian refused to abdicate.

Fall and execution

Abandoned by France, Maximilian's forces were overwhelmed. He was captured after the siege of Querétaro and, despite international pleas for clemency, was executed by firing squad on the Cerro de las Campanas on 19 June 1867 with his generals Miramón and Mejía. His death inspired Édouard Manet's famous paintings of the execution. Empress Carlota, who had travelled to Europe to plead for support, descended into mental illness and lived in seclusion in Belgium until 1927.
