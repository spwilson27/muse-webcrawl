Error budget

An error budget quantifies how much unreliability a service may incur over a period while still meeting its service-level objective.

An error budget is the amount of error or unreliability that a system or service is permitted to incur within a given period while still meeting its service-level objective (SLO). The term is most associated with site reliability engineering (SRE) as practised and popularised by Google, but has earlier analogues in engineering error analysis, where an error budget allocates the total allowable error among the contributing sources of measurement or design uncertainty.

In site reliability engineering

If a service has an SLO of 99.9% availability over 30 days, its error budget is the remaining 0.1% — roughly 43 minutes of downtime in that window. The budget is measured from a service-level indicator (SLI), such as the proportion of successful requests. Error budgets are used as a decision-making tool: while budget remains, development teams may release new features and take risks; if the budget is exhausted (or projected to be exhausted), changes are frozen and effort is redirected to reliability until the budget recovers. This is intended to balance innovation against stability with data rather than opinion.

Error budget burn rate

The burn rate is the rate at which the budget is being consumed relative to the expected rate: a burn rate of 1 means the budget will be exactly exhausted at the end of the window; higher rates trigger alerts proportional to the speed of exhaustion. Multi-window, multi-burn-rate alerting on error budgets has largely replaced simple uptime-threshold alerting in SRE practice.

In engineering analysis

In precision engineering and metrology, an error budget tabulates the expected contributions of each error source (thermal expansion, vibration, calibration uncertainty, and so on) to the total error of an instrument or system, often combining independent contributions by root-sum-square. Such budgets guide design margins, for example in telescope and semiconductor lithography engineering.
