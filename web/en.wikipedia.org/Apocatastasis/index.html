Apocatastasis

Apocatastasis (from Greek apokatastasis, "restoration" or "return") is a theological doctrine concerning the ultimate restoration of all things. In early Christian thought it came to denote the belief that, in the end, all rational creatures — including sinners and, in some versions, even the Devil — will be reconciled to God and restored to a state of blessedness, so that punishment is ultimately remedial rather than eternal.

Origins

The term appears in the New Testament in Acts 3:21, speaking of the "times of restoration of all things". It had earlier uses in Stoic cosmology, where it described the periodic return of the cosmos to its original state after a great conflagration.

Early Christian teaching

The doctrine is most associated with Origen of Alexandria and Gregory of Nyssa, who taught the eventual salvation of all souls through purification. Aspects of the teaching attributed to Origen were condemned in the sixth century, and apocatastasis in its universalist form has remained controversial, though some modern theologians have revived interest in it as a hope, if not a dogma.
