Bernard Arnault
Born5 March 1949, Roubaix, France
OccupationChairman and CEO of LVMH

Bernard Jean Étienne Arnault (born 5 March 1949) is a French businessman and the co-founder, chairman, and chief executive of LVMH Moët Hennessy Louis Vuitton, the world's largest luxury goods company. Through decades of acquisitions he assembled a group of more than 75 brands including Louis Vuitton, Dior, Tiffany & Co., and Moët & Chandon, and he has repeatedly ranked among the wealthiest people in the world.

Career

Arnault trained as an engineer at the École Polytechnique and worked in his family's construction firm, Ferret-Savinel, before moving into property development in the United States. In 1984 he acquired the ailing Boussac textile group, principally to obtain Christian Dior. In 1987 he co-founded LVMH and, after a celebrated battle with the Guinness group and the Louis Vuitton family interests, gained control of the group by 1990.

Art and philanthropy

Arnault is a major art collector and patron; he commissioned the Fondation Louis Vuitton museum in Paris, designed by Frank Gehry and opened in 2014. His family pledged substantial funds toward the restoration of Notre-Dame Cathedral after the 2019 fire.
