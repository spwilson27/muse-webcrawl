White River (Indiana)

White River
Location: Indiana, United States
Length: about 580 km (362 mi) including forks
Mouth: Wabash River
Major city: Indianapolis

The White River is an American two-forked river that flows through central and southern Indiana and is a principal tributary of the Wabash River. The West Fork, 312 miles (502 km) long, is regarded as the main stem; it rises in Randolph County and flows southwest through Muncie, Anderson and Indianapolis. The East Fork begins near Columbus and joins the West Fork in Daviess County.

Course and history

The river's name derives from the Lenape name Wapahani, "white sands". Indianapolis was selected as Indiana's capital in part because of its position on the river, which early promoters wrongly believed was navigable. The river supported mills, fisheries and later industry, but suffered severe pollution during the 20th century.

1999 fish kill

In December 1999, an industrial discharge from a Guide Corporation plant in Anderson released chemicals that killed an estimated 4.6 million fish along some 50 miles of the river, one of the worst ecological disasters in Indiana history. Stocking and cleanup efforts followed, and water quality has since improved substantially.
