The Battle of Saule (Lithuanian: Saulės mūšis) was fought on 22 September 1236 between the Livonian Brothers of the Sword, a German military order crusading in Livonia, and a coalition of pagan Samogitians and Semigallians. The exact location is debated, with candidates near modern Šiauliai in Lithuania and Vecsaule in Latvia.

Returning from a raid into Samogitia, the crusader force under Master Volkwin, accompanied by troops from Pskov and crusaders from Holstein, found its retreat blocked. The Baltic forces attacked the heavily armoured knights on ground — marshy terrain crossed by streams — where their horses were useless. The Order was annihilated: Volkwin and several dozen brothers were killed, and the survivors were cut down in flight.

Consequences

The defeat destroyed the Sword Brothers as an independent power. In 1237 the remnants were absorbed into the Teutonic Order as its Livonian branch. The victory checked the crusade's expansion southward for a generation and is commemorated in Lithuania and Latvia; 22 September is marked as the Day of Baltic Unity, and the battle is traditionally linked to the founding of Šiauliai, the "Sun City".
