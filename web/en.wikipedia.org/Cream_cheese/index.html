Cream cheese

Cream cheese is a soft, usually mild-tasting fresh cheese made from milk and cream. It is not naturally matured and is meant to be consumed fresh, which distinguishes it from other soft cheeses such as Brie and Neufchâtel. Stabilizers such as carob bean gum and carrageenan are often added in industrial production.

History

Cream cheese originated in the United States. In 1873, William A. Lawrence, a dairyman in Chester, New York, began mass-producing an unripened cheese made from cream and whole milk. In 1880 the product began to be distributed under the brand name "Philadelphia" by the Phenix Cheese Company of South Edmeston, New York, because Philadelphia was associated with high-quality food at the time.

Uses

Cream cheese is a key ingredient in cheesecake and is widely spread on bagels, used in frostings, dips, and as a base for sandwiches and canapés. It also appears in dishes such as cream cheese wontons (crab Rangoon) and as a filling in sushi rolls.

Regulation

In the United States, the Food and Drug Administration defines cream cheese as containing at least 33 percent milk fat and no more than 55 percent moisture.
