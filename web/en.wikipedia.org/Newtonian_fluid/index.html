Newtonian fluid

A Newtonian fluid is a fluid in which the viscous stresses are directly proportional to the local strain rate: its viscosity remains constant regardless of the rate of shear. The relationship, named after Isaac Newton, describes common fluids such as water, air and most gases.

Definition

Newton's law of viscosity states that shear stress is proportional to the velocity gradient perpendicular to the direction of shear, with the constant of proportionality being the dynamic viscosity. In a Newtonian fluid this coefficient does not depend on the stress or flow history.

Examples and contrasts

Water, mineral oils, alcohol and air at ordinary conditions behave nearly Newtonian. Non-Newtonian fluids, by contrast, change apparent viscosity under shear: ketchup and blood are shear-thinning, cornstarch suspensions (oobleck) are shear-thickening, and toothpaste exhibits a yield stress like a Bingham plastic.

Applications

The assumption of Newtonian behaviour underlies the Navier-Stokes equations as applied in most classical fluid dynamics, hydraulics and aerodynamics calculations.
