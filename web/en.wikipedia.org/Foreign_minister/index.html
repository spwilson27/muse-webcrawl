Foreign minister

A foreign minister or minister of foreign affairs (sometimes also minister for foreign affairs) is the head of the government ministry responsible for a sovereign state's foreign policy and relations. The position is among the most senior in a cabinet, and the minister works together with the head of government and other ministries to formulate and execute foreign policy.

Role

The foreign minister oversees the diplomatic service, embassies and consulates abroad, treaty negotiation, representation at international organizations such as the United Nations, and the protection of citizens overseas. In many countries the office is titled differently: in the United States the equivalent role is the Secretary of State; in the United Kingdom it is the Foreign Secretary; in some Commonwealth countries the post is the Minister of External Affairs.

History

Specialized foreign ministries emerged in Europe in the early modern period as diplomacy professionalized; France under Cardinal Richelieu is often credited with pioneering a centralized foreign ministry in the 1620s. By the nineteenth century, foreign ministers had become central figures of great-power politics, as exemplified by figures such as Klemens von Metternich of Austria and Charles Maurice de Talleyrand of France.
