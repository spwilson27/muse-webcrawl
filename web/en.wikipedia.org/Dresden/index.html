Dresden is the capital city of the German state of Saxony and its second most populous city after Leipzig. It stands on the River Elbe near the Czech border and is renowned for its Baroque and Rococo architecture, its art collections, and its cultural heritage, earning it the nickname "Florence on the Elbe".

History

First mentioned in 1206, Dresden became the residence of the Saxon electors and later kings. Under Augustus the Strong in the early eighteenth century it was rebuilt in splendour: the Zwinger palace, the Hofkirche cathedral, and the Frauenkirche (completed 1743) date from this era. The city was a centre of art, music and porcelain — the nearby Meissen manufactory produced Europe's first hard-paste porcelain after 1708.

Bombing and reconstruction

On 13–15 February 1945, Allied bombing created a firestorm that destroyed the historic centre and killed an estimated 22,700 to 25,000 people. The attack remains one of the most controversial of the Second World War and forms the setting of Kurt Vonnegut's novel Slaughterhouse-Five. In the GDR era some ruins were rebuilt and others cleared; the Frauenkirche ruin was kept as a war memorial. After German reunification, the Frauenkirche was reconstructed (1994–2005) from old stones and international donations and reconsecrated as a symbol of reconciliation.

Culture and economy

Dresden's collections include the Green Vault treasury, the Old Masters Picture Gallery with Raphael's Sistine Madonna, and the Semperoper opera house. The Dresden Elbe Valley was a UNESCO World Heritage Site from 2004 until 2009, when it was delisted over the construction of the Waldschlösschen Bridge — only the second site ever removed. Today Dresden is a major centre for microelectronics ("Silicon Saxony"), research institutes and the Technical University of Dresden.
