Mania

Mania, also known as a manic syndrome or manic episode, is a state of abnormally elevated arousal, affect, and energy level, accompanied by changes in behaviour that impair functioning. Mania is a defining feature of bipolar I disorder, in which it alternates with periods of depression, and it may also arise from medical conditions or substances.

Signs and symptoms

Features include an expansive, euphoric, or irritable mood; inflated self-esteem or grandiosity; decreased need for sleep; pressured speech; racing thoughts and flight of ideas; distractibility; increased goal-directed activity; and excessive involvement in risky activities such as spending sprees or sexual indiscretion. Severe episodes may involve psychotic symptoms and require hospitalisation. Hypomania is a milder form that does not cause marked impairment or psychosis and distinguishes bipolar II disorder.

Causes and treatment

Mania is associated with disturbances in neurotransmitter systems and circadian rhythms; it can be triggered by sleep deprivation, antidepressants, corticosteroids, and recreational drugs, as well as by conditions such as hyperthyroidism and multiple sclerosis. Acute treatment commonly uses mood stabilisers such as lithium and valproate and atypical antipsychotics; long-term management aims to prevent recurrence.
