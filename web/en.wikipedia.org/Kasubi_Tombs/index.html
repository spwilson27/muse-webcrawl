Kasubi Tombs

The Kasubi Tombs in Kampala, Uganda, are the burial grounds of the kings (kabakas) of the Kingdom of Buganda. The site spans about 30 hectares on Kasubi hill and is a major spiritual and cultural centre for the Baganda people. It was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2001.

Description

The main building, the Muzibu Azaala Mpanga, is a vast circular structure with a domed thatched roof, originally built in 1882 as a palace for Kabaka Muteesa I and later converted into the royal burial ground. Inside lie the tombs of four kabakas: Muteesa I, Mwanga II, Daudi Chwa II and Muteesa II. The buildings are constructed entirely of organic materials — wood, thatch, reed and wattle and daub — and are maintained by clan caretakers, including the widows of the kings.

Fire and reconstruction

On 16 March 2010 the main tomb building was almost completely destroyed by a fire of unknown cause, prompting unrest in Kampala. UNESCO placed the site on the List of World Heritage in Danger. Reconstruction, supported by UNESCO and the government of Japan, was completed and the restored tombs were officially reopened; the site was removed from the danger list in 2023.
