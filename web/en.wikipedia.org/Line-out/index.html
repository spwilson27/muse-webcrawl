Line-out

A line-out or lineout is a means of restarting play in rugby union after the ball has gone into touch (out of the field of play at the side). It is also the term, in audio engineering, for a line-level output signal — but this article concerns the rugby restart.

Formation and play

Players of both teams line up in two parallel lines, perpendicular to the touchline and at least a metre apart, between the 5-metre line and the 15-metre line. The ball is thrown in straight down the middle by the team that did not last touch it (with exceptions, for example when a kick goes directly into touch from outside the 22). Designated jumpers, often supported and lifted by teammates, contest the throw; the catching team commonly forms a driving maul or moves the ball to the backs.

Laws

The throw must be straight, players must not close the gap or leave the line-out early, and lifting supports must grip the jumper legally. Numbers in the line-out are set by the team throwing in, and the opposition must match or use fewer. Infringements result in a free kick or a scrum option.
