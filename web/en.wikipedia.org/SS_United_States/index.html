SS United States is a retired American ocean liner built between 1949 and 1951 for the United States Lines. She was the fastest ocean liner ever built, capturing the Blue Riband on her maiden voyage in 1952 with an average speed of over 35 knots, a transatlantic record that still stands for a passenger liner.

Design

Designed by William Francis Gibbs, the ship was built at Newport News, Virginia, partly with government funding so she could be converted into a troopship in wartime. Extensive use of aluminium reduced weight, and fire-resistant materials were used throughout — famously, the only wood aboard was said to be the grand piano and the butcher's blocks.

Service and retirement

The liner carried presidents, celebrities and immigrants between New York and Europe until jet aircraft undermined the trade; she was withdrawn in 1969. After decades of failed redevelopment schemes and lay-up in Philadelphia, plans were announced in the 2020s to reef the stripped hull as an artificial reef off Florida.
