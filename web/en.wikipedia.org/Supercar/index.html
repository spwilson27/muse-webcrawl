Supercar

A supercar – also called an exotic car – is a high-performance sports car, typically characterised by exceptional speed, power, handling and design, and by limited production and high price. The term is loosely defined and has evolved with automotive technology; cars once considered supercars have been surpassed by later generations, giving rise to the informal category of the "hypercar".

History

The 1966 Lamborghini Miura, with its transverse mid-mounted V12, is widely credited with establishing the supercar formula. Earlier candidates include the Mercedes-Benz 300SL of the 1950s. The 1970s and 1980s brought the Lamborghini Countach, Ferrari 288 GTO and F40, and the Porsche 959, which introduced advanced all-wheel drive and twin-turbocharging. The McLaren F1 (1992) set a naturally aspirated production-car speed record of 240 mph that stood for years.

Modern era

In the 2000s the Bugatti Veyron pushed production-car performance past 1,000 hp and 400 km/h, popularising the term "hypercar" for the tier above supercars, later exemplified by the hybrid "holy trinity" of the McLaren P1, Ferrari LaFerrari and Porsche 918 Spyder, and by fully electric cars such as the Rimac Nevera. Modern supercars make extensive use of carbon-fibre monocoques, active aerodynamics, dual-clutch gearboxes and hybrid or electric assistance.

Definition debates

There is no agreed threshold for power, speed or price. Motoring journalists variously emphasise performance figures, rarity, price, design and the impracticality of the car for everyday use. Electric drivetrains, which deliver supercar acceleration in relatively mainstream vehicles, have further blurred the category.
