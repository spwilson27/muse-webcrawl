Roman festivals

Roman festivals were religious celebrations (feriae) held throughout the year in ancient Rome, honouring gods, marking seasons and commemorating historical events. By the late Republic the calendar contained well over a hundred festival days, combining solemn rites, games (ludi), processions and feasting.

Major festivals

The Saturnalia (17–23 December), honouring Saturn, featured gift-giving, feasting and temporary social inversion. The Lupercalia (15 February) was a purification and fertility rite at the Lupercal cave. Parilia (21 April) celebrated Rome's founding. Others included the Floralia, Vestalia, Lemuria for the spirits of the dead, and the great games: Ludi Romani in September and Ludi Apollinares in July.

Organisation

Festivals were supervised by priestly colleges — pontifices, augurs and the quindecimviri — and funded publicly or by magistrates seeking popularity. Ovid's poem Fasti describes the festivals of the first six months of the year and is a principal source. See also Roman calendar and religion in ancient Rome.
