John Garand

Born: Jean Cantius Garand, 1 January 1888, St. Rémi, Quebec, Canada
Died: 16 February 1974 (aged 86), Springfield, Massachusetts, U.S.
Occupation: Firearms designer
Known for: Designing the M1 Garand rifle

John Cantius Garand (January 1, 1888 – February 16, 1974) was a Canadian-American designer of firearms best known for creating the M1 Garand, a semi-automatic rifle that the United States armed forces used in the Second World War and the Korean War.

Early life

Born Jean Cantius Garand in St. Rémi, Quebec, he moved with his family to Connecticut as a boy. A machinist by trade, he became fascinated by firearms design and built a light machine gun prototype during the First World War that brought him to the attention of the U.S. Bureau of Standards.

Springfield Armory

In 1919 Garand began work at Springfield Armory in Massachusetts, where he spent his career developing a self-loading service rifle. After years of trials and competition with designs such as John Pedersen's, Garand's gas-operated rifle chambered for .30-06 was adopted on 9 January 1936 as the U.S. Rifle, Caliber .30, M1. More than five million were produced.

Legacy

General George S. Patton called the M1 "the greatest battle implement ever devised." Garand never received royalties, having assigned his patents to the government, but was awarded the Medal for Meritorious Service in 1941 and the Presidential Medal for Merit in 1944. He continued improving the design, work that fed into the later M14 rifle.
