Enteric fever

Enteric fever is a systemic bacterial illness caused mainly by Salmonella enterica serotypes Typhi and Paratyphi. The diseases are known respectively as typhoid fever and paratyphoid fever and spread through food or water contaminated with human feces.

Symptoms

After an incubation period of about one to two weeks, patients develop sustained fever, headache, malaise, abdominal pain, and constipation or diarrhea. Some develop a faint rash of rose spots. Severe disease can cause intestinal perforation, bleeding, confusion, or shock.

Diagnosis and treatment

Blood culture is the classic diagnostic test, though sensitivity varies and prior antibiotics reduce yield. Treatment uses antibiotics chosen according to local resistance patterns; extensively drug-resistant typhoid has made susceptibility testing and vaccine prevention increasingly important.

Prevention

Safe water, sanitation, hand hygiene, and food safety are central controls. Typhoid conjugate vaccines are recommended in many endemic settings and for some travelers. See also typhoid fever and Salmonella.
