Working memory

Working memory is a cognitive system with a limited capacity that temporarily holds information available for processing. It is important for reasoning, comprehension, and the guidance of decision-making and behaviour, and it differs from short-term memory in emphasising manipulation of information, not merely storage.

Baddeley's model

Alan Baddeley and Graham Hitch proposed a multi-component model in 1974 comprising a central executive that directs attention, a phonological loop handling speech-based information, and a visuospatial sketchpad for visual and spatial material; an episodic buffer was added later.

Capacity

Working memory capacity is limited — often estimated at around four chunks of information in adults — and varies between individuals. Capacity measures correlate with reading comprehension, fluid intelligence, and scholastic achievement, and decline in conditions such as ADHD and some forms of dementia.
