Photic zone

The photic zone, also called the euphotic zone or sunlight zone, is the uppermost layer of a body of water that receives sunlight, allowing photosynthesis to occur. In the ocean it extends from the surface down to a depth where light intensity falls to about 1% of the surface value, typically around 200 metres in clear water.

Ecology

Nearly all primary production in the ocean takes place in the photic zone, where phytoplankton convert sunlight, carbon dioxide and nutrients into organic matter. This production supports almost the entire marine food web.

Zones below

Beneath the photic zone lie the mesopelagic (twilight) zone and the aphotic zone, where no sunlight penetrates and organisms depend on food sinking from above.
