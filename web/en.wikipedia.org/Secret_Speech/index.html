Secret Speech

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Khrushchev delivered the speech at a closed session of the 20th Party Congress in February 1956.

The "Secret Speech" — formally, On the Cult of Personality and Its Consequences — was a report delivered by Soviet leader Nikita Khrushchev to the 20th Congress of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union on 25 February 1956. In it, Khrushchev denounced the crimes of his predecessor Joseph Stalin, particularly the Great Purge, the deportations of whole nationalities, and the cult of personality surrounding Stalin.

Content

Speaking for about four hours to a closed session, Khrushchev accused Stalin of violating Leninist norms of party democracy, ordering mass repressions against loyal communists and military commanders, and mismanaging the early period of the Second World War through his purges of the officer corps and his refusal to heed warnings of the German invasion. The speech drew on the findings of a party commission and sought to shift blame onto Stalin and security chief Lavrentiy Beria while shielding the party itself.

Publication and impact

Although delivered in secret, the text was read to party organizations across the USSR and leaked abroad; the U.S. State Department released a version in June 1956. The speech shocked communists worldwide, discrediting Stalinism and triggering the process of de-Stalinization and the political relaxation known as the Khrushchev Thaw. It contributed to crises in the Eastern Bloc, including the Polish October and the Hungarian Revolution of 1956, and deepened the emerging Sino-Soviet split, as Mao Zedong criticized Khrushchev's handling of Stalin's legacy.
