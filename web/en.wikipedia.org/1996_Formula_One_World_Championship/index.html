The 1996 Formula One World Championship was the 50th season of FIA Formula One motor racing, contested over sixteen Grands Prix from 10 March to 13 October 1996. Damon Hill of Williams-Renault won the Drivers' Championship, becoming the first son of a World Champion — his father Graham Hill — to win the title himself. Williams also won the Constructors' Championship by a wide margin.

Season summary

The defending champion Michael Schumacher had moved to Ferrari, and Hill's new team-mate was the rookie Jacques Villeneuve, the 1995 Indianapolis 500 winner and son of Gilles Villeneuve. Villeneuve won on only his fourth start, at the Nürburgring, and took the title fight to the final round. Hill won eight races, including the opening round in Melbourne, and built a commanding points lead.

The season's most memorable race was the Monaco Grand Prix, a wet-dry event of attrition in which only three cars finished and Olivier Panis scored his only Formula One victory for Ligier. Schumacher took three wins for Ferrari late in the season, including a celebrated wet-weather victory in Spain. At the finale in Suzuka, Villeneuve lost a wheel and retired, while Hill won the race and the championship. Despite his success, Hill was dropped by Williams for 1997 and moved to Arrows. The season also introduced the 107% qualifying rule and compulsory cockpit head protection changes following the safety reforms of 1994.
