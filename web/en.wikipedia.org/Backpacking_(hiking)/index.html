Backpacking (hiking)

Backpacking is the outdoor recreation activity of carrying gear on one's back while hiking for more than a day. It combines hiking and camping, allowing access to remote areas beyond the reach of day trips, and its participants typically carry food, water, shelter and clothing in a backpack.

Equipment

Core gear includes a frame backpack, a tent or other shelter, a sleeping bag and pad, a camp stove, water treatment, and layered clothing. The "Ten Essentials" list codifies items considered necessary for safety. Ultralight backpacking is a sub-discipline devoted to minimising pack weight, sometimes below 4.5 kg (10 lb) base weight.

Practice

Popular backpacking destinations include long-distance trails such as the Appalachian Trail, the Pacific Crest Trail and the John Muir Trail in the United States, and multi-day hut and trail routes worldwide. Leave No Trace principles govern waste disposal, camp siting and wildlife protection.

Risks

Hazards include weather exposure, dehydration, waterborne illness, wildlife encounters and getting lost; preparation, route planning and carrying navigation and emergency equipment mitigate them.
