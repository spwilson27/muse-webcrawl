Svante Pääbo

Svante Pääbo founded the field of paleogenomics.

Svante Pääbo (born 20 April 1955) is a Swedish geneticist and Nobel Laureate who specialises in evolutionary genetics. As one of the founders of paleogenetics, he has worked extensively on the Neanderthal genome. In 1997, he became founding director of the Department of Genetics at the Max Planck Institute for Evolutionary Anthropology in Leipzig, Germany. Since 1999, he has been an honorary professor at Leipzig University; he also teaches as an adjunct professor at Okinawa Institute of Science and Technology, Japan.

Research

Pääbo's team published the first draft sequence of the Neanderthal genome in 2010, showing that Neanderthals interbred with early modern humans and contributed 1–4% of the genomes of present-day people outside Africa. His group also identified the Denisovans from a finger bone genome and developed methods for extracting ancient DNA from fossils.

Nobel Prize

In 2022 he was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine "for his discoveries concerning the genomes of extinct hominins and human evolution." His father, Sune Bergström, had also won the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, in 1982.
