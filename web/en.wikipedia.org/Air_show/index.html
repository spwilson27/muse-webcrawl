Air show

An air show (or airshow) is a public event at which aviators display their flying skills and the capabilities of their aircraft to spectators, usually at an airfield over one or more days. Displays typically include aerobatics, formation flying by military demonstration teams, parachuting, wing walking, and static exhibitions of aircraft on the ground. Trade-oriented events such as the Paris Air Show and Farnborough International Airshow combine flying displays with industry exhibitions and aircraft sales announcements.

History

Public flying exhibitions began in the earliest days of aviation; the 1909 Reims meeting in France, the Grande Semaine d'Aviation, drew crowds of hundreds of thousands. Barnstormers toured the United States in the 1920s, and after the Second World War military jet teams such as the U.S. Navy Blue Angels (founded 1946), the U.S. Air Force Thunderbirds, and Britain's Red Arrows became headline acts. The EAA AirVenture Oshkosh in Wisconsin grew into one of the largest aviation gatherings in the world.

Safety

Air shows are tightly regulated following serious accidents, including the 1988 Ramstein disaster in Germany, in which three Italian jets collided and killed 70 people, and the 2015 Shoreham accident in England, after which the UK sharply tightened rules on ex-military jet displays over land. Modern rules mandate minimum distances and heights for manoeuvres relative to crowd lines and prohibit aerobatics directed toward spectators.
