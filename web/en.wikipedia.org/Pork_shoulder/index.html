Pork shoulder

Pork shoulder is a cut of meat from the shoulder of a pig. It comes from the area above the front leg and includes part of the blade bone. Pork shoulder is a relatively tough and inexpensive cut with a generous amount of intramuscular fat and connective tissue, which makes it well suited to long, slow cooking methods that render it tender.

Cuts

In American butchery, the whole shoulder is commonly divided into two subcuts: the upper Boston butt (or Boston shoulder), which comes from the top of the shoulder and contains the blade bone, and the lower picnic shoulder (or picnic ham), which extends down toward the foreleg and is often sold with the skin on. The name "Boston butt" derives from the barrels, called butts, in which the cut was packed in colonial New England. In British butchery, the corresponding cut is generally called hand and spring or simply shoulder.

Cooking

Pork shoulder is the classic cut for slow-smoked barbecue in the American South, cooked low and slow over wood smoke until it can be shredded into pulled pork, the signature dish of North Carolina and other regional barbecue traditions. It is also used for carnitas in Mexican cooking, char siu in Chinese cooking, braises, stews, sausages, and ground pork. Its collagen converts to gelatin during slow cooking, keeping the meat moist despite long cooking times.
