Reign of Terror

The Reign of Terror (French: la Terreur) was a period of the French Revolution when, following the creation of the First Republic, a series of massacres and numerous public executions took place in response to revolutionary fervour, anti-clerical sentiment, and accusations of treason by the Committee of Public Safety. It lasted from September 1793 until the fall of Maximilien Robespierre on 27 July 1794 (9 Thermidor, Year II).

Causes

France in 1793 faced war with most of Europe, civil war in the Vendée, federalist revolts, economic crisis, and food shortages. The National Convention, dominated by the Mountain, created the Committee of Public Safety and the Revolutionary Tribunals, declared terror "the order of the day", and under the Law of Suspects authorized mass arrests of alleged enemies of the Revolution.

The Terror

Victims included Queen Marie Antoinette (executed 16 October 1793), the Girondin deputies, the Hébertists, and then the Dantonists, before the Law of 22 Prairial (10 June 1794) stripped the accused of defence counsel and accelerated the Great Terror — over 1,500 executions in Paris in seven weeks. In the provinces, repression was bloodier still, notably the drownings at Nantes and shootings at Lyon, while the war in the Vendée cost hundreds of thousands of lives.

End and historiography

Fearing for their own safety, deputies of the Convention arrested Robespierre and his allies on 9 Thermidor; he was guillotined the next day. The Thermidorian Reaction dismantled the Terror's machinery. Historians continue to debate whether the Terror was a contingent response to war and civil war or an outcome inherent in revolutionary ideology.
