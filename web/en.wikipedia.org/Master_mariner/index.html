Master mariner

A master mariner is a professional seafarer holding the highest grade of maritime qualification: a licence (certificate of competency) permitting command of a merchant vessel of any size, anywhere in the world. The modern qualification is the "Master" certificate issued under the International Convention on Standards of Training, Certification and Watchkeeping for Seafarers (STCW), such as the United Kingdom's Master (Unlimited) certificate or the United States Coast Guard's master of vessels of any gross tons upon oceans.

A master mariner holds the senior licence to command a merchant ship without limits of tonnage or trade area.

Certification

Under STCW, certification as a master requires prior sea service as an officer in charge of a navigational watch and as chief mate, examinations in navigation, seamanship, ship handling, cargo operations, maritime law, and emergency procedures, and medical and ancillary safety qualifications. Certificates are graded by tonnage and operating area; the unlimited master certificate is the top grade. In the United States, the equivalent credential is the National Master of Self-Propelled Vessels of Any Gross Tons license issued by the Coast Guard.

Role and responsibility

The master has overriding authority on board and ultimate legal responsibility for the vessel, its crew, passengers, and cargo, for compliance with maritime and environmental law, and for the safety of the ship. Masters also commonly serve ashore as marine superintendents, pilots, shipping-company managers, harbour masters, and expert witnesses in maritime litigation, where the master mariner qualification is a standard mark of expertise.

History

Formal examinations for masters in the British merchant service were introduced in the mid-nineteenth century under the Board of Trade, and "extra master" certificates recognised the highest attainments. The title itself is centuries older: the master was historically the officer responsible for navigation and sailing the ship, distinct from a captain appointed to command in war; in merchant practice the two roles merged into the modern shipmaster.
