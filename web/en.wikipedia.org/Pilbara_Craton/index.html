Pilbara Craton

The Pilbara Craton is an ancient and stable portion of the continental lithosphere located in the Pilbara region of Western Australia. Along with the Kaapvaal Craton of southern Africa, it is one of only two places on Earth where pristine crust dating from 3.6 to 2.7 billion years ago (the Paleoarchean and Mesoarchean eras) is preserved at the surface.

Ancient terrain of the Pilbara, Western Australia.

Geology

The craton comprises the East Pilbara Terrane, the West Pilbara Superterrane, and intervening terranes, assembled between about 3.53 and 3.17 billion years ago. Its geology features the classic granite-greenstone pattern of Archaean cratons: domes of granitic rock surrounded by belts of metamorphosed volcanic and sedimentary rock. Overlying it are the sedimentary and volcanic rocks of the Hamersley Basin, which host enormous deposits of banded iron formation.

Early life

The Pilbara is famous for some of the earliest evidence of life on Earth. Stromatolites in the ~3.48-billion-year-old Dresser Formation and structures in the ~3.4-billion-year-old Strelley Pool Formation are among the oldest widely accepted traces of microbial life. Claims of microfossils in the ~3.46-billion-year-old Apex Chert have been debated for decades.

Economic importance

The Pilbara region is one of the world's richest mining provinces, producing a large share of the world's seaborne iron ore, as well as gold, lithium, and other minerals. The mineral wealth derives largely from the ancient craton and its overlying basins.
