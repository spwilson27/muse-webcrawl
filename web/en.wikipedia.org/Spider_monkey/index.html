Spider monkey

Spider monkeys are New World monkeys belonging to the genus Ateles, part of the subfamily Atelinae, family Atelidae. Like other atelines, they are found in tropical forests of Central and South America, from southern Mexico to Brazil. The genus contains seven species, all of which are under threat; the black-headed spider monkey and brown spider monkey are critically endangered.

Anatomy

Spider monkeys have long, lanky arms and prehensile tails that can support their entire weight, allowing them to swing from branch to branch by brachiation. Unusually, they lack external thumbs. Adults weigh about 6 kg and measure up to 66 cm in body length, with tails up to 90 cm.

Behaviour and diet

They live in loosely organised groups of up to 30 individuals that split into smaller sub-groups to forage (fission–fusion society). Their diet consists mainly of ripe fruit, making them important seed dispersers, supplemented by leaves, nuts, and occasionally insects.

Conservation

Habitat loss to deforestation and hunting for bushmeat have sharply reduced populations; several species are listed as endangered or critically endangered by the IUCN.
