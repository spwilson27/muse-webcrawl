Michael E. Brown (born 5 June 1965) is an American astronomer and professor of planetary astronomy at the California Institute of Technology (Caltech). He is best known for leading the team that discovered Eris, a dwarf planet more massive than Pluto, a discovery that precipitated the International Astronomical Union's 2006 reclassification of Pluto as a dwarf planet — earning Brown the joking moniker "the man who killed Pluto."

Discoveries

Brown's survey work at Palomar Observatory produced many of the largest known trans-Neptunian objects, including Haumea, Makemake, Sedna, Orcus, and Quaoar. The 2005 announcement of Eris (temporarily nicknamed "Xena") forced the debate over the definition of a planet that culminated in the IAU's 2006 resolution.

Planet Nine

In 2016, Brown and colleague Konstantin Batygin proposed the existence of a massive undiscovered "Planet Nine" in the outer solar system, inferred from the clustered orbits of distant Kuiper belt objects. The hypothesis remains under investigation by observational surveys.

Writing and outreach

Brown wrote the memoir How I Killed Pluto and Why It Had It Coming (2010). He shared the 2012 Kavli Prize in Astrophysics for work on the Kuiper belt and its largest members.
