Soviet deportations from Latvia


Wagons of the type used to transport deportees

Soviet deportations from Latvia were a series of mass deportations by the Soviet Union from the Latvian Soviet Socialist Republic in 1941 and 1949. They formed part of the Soviet policy of political repression in the occupied Baltic states, in which more than 60,000 Latvian inhabitants were forcibly removed to distant regions of the USSR.

June deportation of 1941

Following the Soviet occupation of Latvia in 1940, lists of persons considered enemies of the regime were drawn up. In the night of 13–14 June 1941, more than 15,000 people — including former government ministers, army officers, policemen, members of the Aizsargi home guard, industrialists, and their families — were seized and deported. Men were sent to Gulag camps such as Vyatlag and Usollag, where most perished; women and children were exiled to settlements in the Krasnoyarsk and Novosibirsk regions.

Operation Priboi, 1949

On 25 March 1949, in the operation code-named Priboi, some 42,000 people from Latvia — over two percent of the population — were deported in a single action. Most were farming families labelled "kulaks" or accused of supporting the Forest Brothers resistance, and the deportation was designed to force the collectivization of agriculture. In Latvia the March 1949 deportation is remembered as the largest single episode of Soviet repression in the country.

Commemoration

Many deportees died in exile; survivors were allowed to return only after the mid-1950s, and many found their homes occupied. In modern Latvia, 14 June and 25 March are official remembrance days for the victims of communist terror.
