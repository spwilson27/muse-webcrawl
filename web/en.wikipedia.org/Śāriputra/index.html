Śāriputra (Sanskrit: शारिपुत्र; Pali: Sāriputta) was one of the two chief male disciples of the Buddha, together with Maudgalyāyana (Moggallāna). Among the Buddha's followers he was praised as foremost in wisdom, and in later tradition he is associated with the systematisation of the Abhidharma teachings.

Life

Born Upatissa in a Brahmin family near Rājagṛha, he wandered as a seeker with his friend Maudgalyāyana under the teacher Sañjaya. Encountering the monk Aśvajit, Śāriputra heard the famous verse of dependent origination — "Of those phenomena that arise from a cause, the Tathāgata has stated the cause, and also their cessation" — and immediately attained the first stage of awakening. The two friends then brought their own followers to the Buddha and became his chief disciples.

In the sutras Śāriputra frequently expounds doctrine at the Buddha's request, analyses meditation attainments, and instructs younger monks; the Mahāvedalla and Cūḷavedalla discourses show his probing question-and-answer style. He is depicted as modest and forgiving, once enduring a brahmin's blow without anger. He died shortly before the Buddha, returning to his birth village and converting his mother on her deathbed.

In Mahāyāna literature

In Mahāyāna sutras Śāriputra often plays the role of the intelligent but limited śrāvaka interlocutor whose questions prompt the Buddha's deeper teaching — most famously in the Vimalakīrti Sutra, the Heart Sutra (addressed to him), and the Lotus Sutra, where he receives a prediction of future buddhahood. Relics attributed to him, excavated at Sanchi and Satdhara in the nineteenth century, are now enshrined in temples in Sri Lanka and Myanmar.
