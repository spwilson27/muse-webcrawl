An endangered species is a species that is very likely to become extinct in the near future, either worldwide or in a particular political jurisdiction. On the IUCN Red List, "Endangered" (EN) is the second-most severe conservation status for wild populations, ranking below "Critically Endangered" (CR) and above "Vulnerable" (VU). A taxon is classified as Endangered when it meets quantitative criteria indicating a very high risk of extinction, such as a population reduction of at least 70 percent over ten years or three generations, a severely restricted range, or a population of fewer than 2,500 mature individuals with continuing decline.

Causes of endangerment

The main drivers of endangerment are habitat loss and fragmentation (from agriculture, logging and urbanisation), overexploitation through hunting and fishing, invasive species, pollution, disease, and climate change. Small, isolated populations are additionally vulnerable to inbreeding and random environmental events.

Conservation

Protection measures include legal instruments such as the US Endangered Species Act of 1973 and the international CITES treaty regulating trade in wild species; protected areas; captive breeding and reintroduction programmes, which have recovered species such as the California condor, the Arabian oryx and the black-footed ferret; and habitat restoration. The IUCN Red List, maintained since 1964, has assessed well over 150,000 species, of which more than a quarter are threatened with extinction.

Examples

  • Amur leopard (Panthera pardus orientalis) — critically endangered
  • Mountain gorilla (Gorilla beringei beringei) — endangered, improved from critically endangered in 2018
  • Blue whale (Balaenoptera musculus) — endangered
  • Asian elephant (Elephas maximus) — endangered
  • Snow leopard (Panthera uncia) — vulnerable (downlisted from endangered in 2017)
