Onyx is a banded variety of chalcedony, a cryptocrystalline form of quartz. The bands of onyx are parallel and straight — in contrast to agate, whose bands are curved — and range in colour from white to almost every colour. Commonly, specimens contain bands of black and/or white. The name comes from the Greek ὄνυξ (onyx), meaning "claw" or "fingernail," because stones with flesh-coloured and white bands were said to resemble a fingernail.

Varieties

Sardonyx is the variant in which the bands are sard (reddish-brown chalcedony) rather than black. Black onyx — chalcedony that is solid black or nearly so — is the most famous variety, though genuinely black material is rare and much commercial black onyx is grey chalcedony that has been dyed or treated with sugar and acid. Cornelian onyx and niccolo (a thin greyish layer over black) are other historical trade varieties.

Formation

Like other chalcedonies, onyx forms from silica-rich waters depositing microscopic quartz crystals in cavities and fissures of volcanic rock, especially basalt flows; distinct bands record successive episodes of deposition with slightly different impurity contents. Major sources have included India, Brazil, Madagascar, Pakistan and Mexico.

Uses

Onyx has been carved since antiquity: the layered structure makes it ideal for cameos and intaglios, in which the design is cut through upper layers of one colour to reveal another beneath — the Roman Gemma Augustea is a celebrated example in sardonyx. It is also used in jewellery, knife handles, chessmen, and as a polished architectural stone; much "onyx marble" sold for tabletops and lamps is actually banded travertine (a form of calcite), not true quartz onyx.

History and lore

Onyx was among the stones of the high priest's breastplate listed in the Book of Exodus, and Pliny the Elder described its varieties and sources. In folklore it was variously credited with absorbing negative energy, protecting travellers and — in some traditions — causing discord, a reputation that made it a favoured stone for carving seals and amulets.
