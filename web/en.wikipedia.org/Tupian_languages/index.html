Tupian languages

The Tupian languages (or Tupi–Guarani in the broad sense) form one of the largest language families of South America, comprising about 70 languages spoken in Brazil, Paraguay, Bolivia, Peru, and French Guiana. The best known members are Guarani, an official language of Paraguay, and Old Tupi, once a lingua franca of colonial Brazil.

Classification

The family is usually divided into several branches, including Tupi–Guarani, Tupari, Mondé, Munduruku, Arikém, Juruna, Puruborá–Ramaráma, and the divergent Awetï and Mawé. The homeland of Proto-Tupian is generally placed in the Madeira River basin of Rondônia.

History

Tupi-speaking peoples expanded widely before European contact. In colonial Brazil, a Tupi-based língua geral was used by settlers and missionaries, and thousands of Brazilian place names and loanwords derive from Tupi.
