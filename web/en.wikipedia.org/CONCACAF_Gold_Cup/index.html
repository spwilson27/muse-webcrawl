CONCACAF Gold Cup

The Concacaf Gold Cup (Spanish: Copa Oro de la Concacaf) is the main association football competition of the men's national football teams governed by Concacaf, determining the continental champion of North America, Central America and the Caribbean.

History

The tournament grew out of the CONCACAF Championship, held from 1963 to 1989. The Gold Cup was established in 1991 and is generally held every two years, mostly in the United States. Since 2019 it has featured 16 teams qualifying principally through the Concacaf Nations League.

Results

Mexico is the most successful team, with a record number of titles, followed by the United States; Canada won in 2000. Guest teams from other confederations, such as Brazil, Colombia and Qatar, have occasionally been invited.
