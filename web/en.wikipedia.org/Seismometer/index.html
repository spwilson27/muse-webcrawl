Seismometer

A seismometer is an instrument that responds to ground motions, such as those caused by earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, and explosions. Seismometers are usually combined with a timing and recording device to form a seismograph, whose output record is a seismogram.

Principle

The basic design suspends an inertial mass from a frame fixed to the ground; when the ground moves, the relative motion between mass and frame is measured. Early instruments recorded on smoked paper or photographic film with a stylus or light beam; modern broadband seismometers use electronic feedback sensors and digital recording, detecting motions across a wide range of frequencies and amplitudes.

History

An early seismoscope was described by the Chinese scholar Zhang Heng in AD 132. The first modern seismographs were developed in Japan in the 1880s by John Milne and his colleagues, and global networks of instruments were established during the twentieth century.

Uses

Seismometer networks locate earthquakes, monitor volcanic activity, verify nuclear test ban treaties, and probe the interiors of Earth, the Moon, and Mars.
