Larry Ellison

Lawrence Joseph Ellison (born August 17, 1944) is an American businessman and entrepreneur who co-founded the software company Oracle Corporation. He was Oracle's chief executive officer from its founding in 1977 until 2014, and subsequently served as chairman and chief technology officer. Ellison is among the wealthiest people in the world, with a fortune derived principally from his stake in Oracle.

Early life

Ellison was born in New York City and adopted as an infant by relatives in Chicago. He attended the University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign and the University of Chicago but left both without a degree, moving to California where he worked as a programmer, including at Ampex, where he worked on a database project for the CIA codenamed "Oracle".

Oracle

In 1977 Ellison co-founded Software Development Laboratories with Bob Miner and Ed Oates. Inspired by Edgar F. Codd's papers on the relational model, the company released Oracle Database, one of the first commercially available relational database management systems, and later renamed itself Oracle. Under Ellison, Oracle grew through aggressive marketing and a long series of acquisitions, including PeopleSoft, Siebel Systems, BEA Systems, and Sun Microsystems. In 2014 Ellison stepped down as CEO, succeeded by Safra Catz and Mark Hurd; he remained executive chairman and CTO.

Other interests

Ellison has been a prominent figure in competitive sailing, founding the Oracle Team USA syndicate, which won the America's Cup in 2010 and 2013, and co-founding the SailGP racing league. In 2012 he purchased most of the Hawaiian island of Lānaʻi. He has also invested in companies including Tesla and signed the Giving Pledge.
