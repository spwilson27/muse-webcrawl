Arte Povera

Arte Povera (literally "poor art") was a radical Italian art movement of the late 1960s and early 1970s whose artists explored a wide range of unconventional materials and processes, rejecting the polished, industrial aesthetic associated with American Minimalism and the commercial art market.

Origins

The term was coined by the Italian art critic Germano Celant, who organised the first Arte Povera exhibition in Genoa in 1967 and published a manifesto calling for a "guerrilla warfare" against established culture. The movement was centred in Turin and Rome and emerged amid the social and political upheavals of Italy in the late 1960s.

Characteristics

Arte Povera artists used humble, everyday "poor" materials — soil, rags, twigs, rope, neon, glass, vegetables and even live animals — to collapse the distance between art and life and to emphasise natural processes, energy and change. Works often took the form of installations or ephemeral actions rather than durable, saleable objects.

Artists

Key figures include Giovanni Anselmo, Alighiero Boetti, Pier Paolo Calzolari, Luciano Fabro, Jannis Kounellis, Mario Merz, Marisa Merz, Giulio Paolini, Pino Pascali, Giuseppe Penone, Michelangelo Pistoletto and Gilberto Zorio.

Legacy

Although the loose grouping had largely dissolved by the early 1970s, Arte Povera profoundly influenced later installation art, process art and land art, and its works are held in major museum collections worldwide.
