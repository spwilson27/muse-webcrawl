The 1998 FIFA World Cup was the 16th FIFA World Cup and was held in France from 10 June to 12 July 1998. It was the first World Cup to feature 32 teams, expanded from 24. Hosts France won the tournament, their first World Cup title, defeating defending champions Brazil 3–0 in the final at the Stade de France in Saint-Denis.

Final

The final was overshadowed by the controversy surrounding Brazil striker Ronaldo, who was initially left off the team sheet before being restored shortly before kick-off, reportedly after a convulsive fit the night before. Zinedine Zidane scored two first-half headers from corners, and Emmanuel Petit added a third in stoppage time, to give France a famous victory with a team celebrated as a symbol of multicultural France — Black, blanc, beur.

Notable events

Croatia, playing in their first World Cup as an independent nation, finished third, with Davor Šuker winning the Golden Boot. The tournament introduced the golden goal rule in the knockout stage. Total attendance of nearly 2.8 million set a record for the expanded format, and the tournament is remembered for matches such as Argentina's penalty shootout win over England and France's semi-final comeback against Croatia.
