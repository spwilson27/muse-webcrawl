BRCA1

BRCA1 (breast cancer type 1 susceptibility protein) is a human protein encoded by the BRCA1 gene on chromosome 17. It is a tumour suppressor that plays a central role in repairing damaged DNA, particularly double-strand breaks repaired by homologous recombination, and in regulating the cell cycle and transcription.

Function

The BRCA1 protein contains an N-terminal RING finger domain with E3 ubiquitin ligase activity and C-terminal BRCT domains that bind phosphorylated proteins involved in the DNA-damage response. Working with partners including BRCA2, PALB2 and RAD51, BRCA1 helps ensure high-fidelity repair of DNA breaks; when it fails, cells accumulate mutations.

Cancer risk

Inherited pathogenic variants in BRCA1 greatly increase the lifetime risk of breast cancer and ovarian cancer, and also raise the risk of several other cancers. The gene was localised by Mary-Claire King's team in 1990 and cloned in 1994. Genetic testing for BRCA1 and BRCA2 mutations informs decisions about enhanced screening and preventive surgery; tumours with BRCA defects are also sensitive to PARP inhibitor drugs.
