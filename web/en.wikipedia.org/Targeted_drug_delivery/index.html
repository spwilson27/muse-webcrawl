Targeted drug delivery

Targeted drug delivery, sometimes called smart drug delivery, is a method of delivering medication to a patient in a manner that increases the concentration of the medication in some parts of the body relative to others. The goal is to improve therapeutic efficacy while reducing side effects on healthy tissue.

Approaches

Strategies include passive targeting, which exploits the enhanced permeability and retention effect in tumour tissue, and active targeting, in which a carrier such as a liposome, nanoparticle or polymer conjugate is decorated with ligands (for example antibodies or folate) that bind receptors overexpressed on target cells. Antibody–drug conjugates deliver cytotoxic payloads directly to antigen-bearing cells.

Applications

Targeted delivery is most developed in oncology, where examples include liposomal doxorubicin and the antibody–drug conjugates trastuzumab emtansine and brentuximab vedotin. Magnetic targeting, ultrasound-triggered release and implantable depots are also studied.
