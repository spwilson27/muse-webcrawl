Sadi Carnot (statesman)

Marie François Sadi Carnot (11 August 1837 – 25 June 1894) was a French statesman who served as President of the French Republic from 1887 until his assassination in 1894. A member of the distinguished Carnot family — grandson of Lazare Carnot, the "Organiser of Victory" of the French Revolution, and nephew of the physicist Sadi Carnot — he was a civil engineer by training and a moderate republican politician.

Early life and career

Born in Limoges, Carnot studied at the École Polytechnique and the École des Ponts et Chaussées, becoming an engineer in the state corps of bridges and roads. Elected to the National Assembly in 1871, he aligned with the moderate republicans. He served as Minister of Public Works and twice as Minister of Finance (1885–1886), earning a reputation for integrity during a period of financial scandals.

Presidency

Carnot was elected President in December 1887 after the resignation of Jules Grévy amid a decorations-selling scandal involving Grévy's son-in-law. His presidency weathered the Boulanger crisis, the collapse of the Panama Company, and the 1889 Paris Exposition with its new Eiffel Tower. He was a popular, dignified head of state who travelled widely in France, and during his term the Franco-Russian Alliance was concluded.

Assassination

On 24 June 1894, while attending a banquet in Lyon, Carnot was stabbed by the Italian anarchist Sante Geronimo Caserio in retaliation for the execution of anarchist bombers and for Carnot's refusal to pardon them. He died in the early hours of 25 June. Carnot received a state funeral and was buried in the Panthéon beside his grandfather.
