Dhanush (Sanskrit for "bow") is a variant of the Prithvi II short-range ballistic missile developed by India's Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) for launch from surface ships of the Indian Navy. It forms part of India's sea-based nuclear deterrent, being capable of carrying both conventional and nuclear warheads.

Development

The Dhanush was developed under India's Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme as a navalised Prithvi, a single-stage liquid-fuelled missile fitted with a stabilised launch platform that compensates for the motion of the ship. It was first test-fired in 2000, with launches conducted from specially modified offshore patrol vessels, notably INS Subhadra and INS Suvarna, fitted with helipad-style launch pads on their decks.

Characteristics

The missile is about 8.5 metres long, carries a payload of roughly 500 kilograms, and has a reported range of about 350 kilometres. During a series of successful trials in the 2000s and 2010s it was fired from ships stationed in the Bay of Bengal against land and sea targets.

Role

The Dhanush gave India an interim sea-based strike capability while longer-range submarine-launched ballistic missiles of the K family were under development for the Arihant-class submarines. Commentators have noted the operational awkwardness of launching a liquid-fuelled ballistic missile from a surface ship, and the system's role has been described as that of a technology demonstrator and stopgap within India's nuclear triad.
