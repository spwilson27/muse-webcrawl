Suryavansha

Suryavansha (Sanskrit: सूर्यवंश, "Solar dynasty") is a legendary dynasty of ancient India, said to descend from Surya, the Hindu sun god, through his son Vaivasvata Manu and Manu's son Ikshvaku, who founded its royal line at Ayodhya. Together with the lunar Chandravansha, it is one of the two great lineages from which most royal families of classical Indian tradition claimed descent.

Legendary kings

The dynastic lists preserved in the Puranas and epics name many celebrated kings of the line: Ikshvaku; Sagara, whose sons were destroyed by the sage Kapila; Bhagiratha, whose austerities brought the river Ganga down from heaven; the emperor Raghu, ancestor celebrated in Kalidasa's poem Raghuvamsha; and Dasharatha of Ayodhya, father of Rama, the hero of the Ramayana and seventh avatar of Vishnu. The Buddha, Siddhartha Gautama, was also traditionally said to belong to the Shakya clan of the Ikshvaku line.

Historical claims

Several historical royal houses claimed Suryavanshi descent to legitimise their rule, including Rajput clans such as the Sisodias of Mewar — often regarded as the senior Suryavanshi house — as well as dynasties in Odisha, Nepal and elsewhere. Jain and Buddhist texts preserve parallel traditions of the line's kings.

Sources

The genealogy is set out in the Ramayana, the Mahabharata, the Harivamsha and the Puranas, notably the Vishnu and Bhagavata Puranas, with variant lists across texts. Modern historians treat the early portions as mythical while debating possible kernels of historical memory in the Kosala kingdom centred on Ayodhya.
