Gospel Oak is an inner urban area of north London in the London Borough of Camden, immediately south of Hampstead Heath. The name derives from an oak tree under which, according to local tradition, the Gospel was read during the annual beating of the bounds of the parish of St Pancras; the tree stood near the junction of present-day Mansfield Road and Southampton Road and was last recorded in the early 19th century.

Historically a rural fringe of the parish of St Pancras, the area was developed in the mid-19th century with the arrival of the railways. Gospel Oak station opened in 1860 and is today the eastern terminus of the Gospel Oak to Barking line of the London Overground (the "Goblin"). Much of the district's Victorian housing stock was cleared in the mid-20th century and replaced by Camden Council estates, including the distinctive low-rise, high-density housing of the late 1960s and 1970s around Fleet Road and Lismore Circus.

Geography and local life

Gospel Oak borders Kentish Town to the east, Chalk Farm and Belsize Park to the west, and Hampstead Heath — with its Parliament Hill viewpoint and bathing ponds — to the north. Queen's Crescent Market, held since the 19th century, is one of London's oldest street markets. The area is also the name of an electoral ward of Camden Council.
