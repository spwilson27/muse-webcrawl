Full nameIsraeli–Palestinian Interim Agreement on the West Bank and the Gaza Strip
Signed28 September 1995, Washington, D.C. (initialled at Taba, Egypt, 24 September)

The Oslo II Accord, formally the Israeli–Palestinian Interim Agreement on the West Bank and the Gaza Strip, was signed in 1995 as the central implementing agreement of the Oslo peace process. It greatly expanded Palestinian self-rule in the West Bank, provided for the first Palestinian national elections, and divided the West Bank into the patchwork of Areas A, B and C that — intended as a temporary arrangement pending a final-status agreement — still structures the territory's governance decades later.

Provisions

The agreement, running to several hundred pages with annexes on security, legal affairs, economics and water, provided for the redeployment of Israeli forces from Palestinian cities and the transfer of powers to the Palestinian Authority. It established an elected Palestinian Legislative Council and an elected president (Ra'is) of the Palestinian Authority; Yasser Arafat won the presidency and his Fatah movement the council majority in January 1996. Israeli forces withdrew from six West Bank cities and later, under the 1997 Hebron Protocol, from most of Hebron.

Areas A, B and C

The West Bank was divided into three categories of control: Area A (initially about 3%, eventually ~18%) under full Palestinian civil and security control, comprising the main cities; Area B (~22%) under Palestinian civil control and joint security arrangements, comprising most villages; and Area C (~60%) under full Israeli control, containing the Israeli settlements, military areas and most of the land reserves. The division was to be transitional over 18 months; instead it hardened into permanence as final-status negotiations stalled.

Aftermath

Israeli prime minister Yitzhak Rabin, who signed the accord, was assassinated in November 1995 by a Jewish extremist opposed to the peace process. Implementation faltered amid suicide bombings, settlement expansion and political change in Israel, and the framework collapsed as a negotiating process with the outbreak of the Second Intifada in 2000 — although the Palestinian Authority and the A/B/C geography created by Oslo II endure.
