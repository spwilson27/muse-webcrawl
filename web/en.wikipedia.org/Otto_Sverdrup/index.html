Otto Neumann Knoph Sverdrup (31 October 1854 – 26 November 1930) was a Norwegian sailor and Arctic explorer. He was a central figure in the golden age of Norwegian polar exploration alongside Fridtjof Nansen and Roald Amundsen, and is regarded as one of the most accomplished polar travellers of his era, though his achievements were often overshadowed by those of his more famous contemporaries.

Early life

Sverdrup was born on the farm Haarstad in Bindalen in Nordland county, the son of a farmer. He went to sea at seventeen, gaining experience in Norwegian and foreign waters, and passed his mate's examination in 1875. He later commanded vessels in the merchant service.

Expeditions

In 1888 Sverdrup joined Nansen's first crossing of Greenland on skis. He then served as captain of the specially built polar ship Fram on Nansen's 1893–1896 drift expedition across the Arctic Ocean, taking command of the ship when Nansen left by sledge in an attempt on the North Pole, and bringing the Fram safely home.

From 1898 to 1902 Sverdrup led his own expedition aboard the Fram to northern Greenland and the Canadian Arctic Archipelago. The expedition charted and claimed for Norway a group of islands west of Ellesmere Island — named the Sverdrup Islands — and mapped some 260,000 square kilometres of previously unknown territory, one of the largest mapping achievements of the heroic age of polar exploration. The account of the voyage was published as New Land (1903).

Later life

Sverdrup undertook further voyages, including rescue and exploration work in the Russian Arctic in 1914–1915, where he freed the icebound ships of the Russian hydrographic expedition. Canada's later purchase of Norway's claims to the Sverdrup Islands (agreed in 1930, shortly before his death) included a lifetime pension for Sverdrup. He died at Sandvika, near Oslo, on 26 November 1930.
