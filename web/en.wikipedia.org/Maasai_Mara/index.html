Maasai Mara

The Maasai Mara, officially the Maasai Mara National Reserve, is a large game reserve in Narok County, Kenya, contiguous with the Serengeti National Park in Tanzania. Named in honour of the Maasai people who inhabit the region, and for the Mara River that crosses it, the reserve covers about 1,510 km² (580 sq mi) of gently rolling savanna grassland in the Great Rift Valley area.

Wildlife

The Mara is renowned for its exceptional populations of lions, leopards, cheetahs, and African bush elephants, and for the annual Great Migration, in which roughly 1.5 million wildebeest, zebra, and gazelle move between the Serengeti and the Mara, typically arriving around July and departing by October. The Mara River crossings, where animals brave crocodile-infested waters, are among the most famous wildlife spectacles on Earth. All members of the "Big Five" are present year-round.

History and management

The area was first established as a wildlife sanctuary in 1961 and enlarged over time. Part of the reserve, the Mara Triangle, is managed by the non-profit Mara Conservancy; the remainder is administered by the Narok County Council. Surrounding the reserve are community conservancies leased from Maasai landowners, which extend wildlife habitat and share tourism revenue with local communities.

Tourism

The Maasai Mara is one of Africa's most popular safari destinations, known for hot-air balloon flights over the plains and high densities of big cats made famous by nature documentaries such as the BBC's Big Cat Diary.
