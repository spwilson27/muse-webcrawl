John Tyler (29 March 1790 – 18 January 1862) was the tenth president of the United States, serving from 1841 to 1845. Elected vice president on the Whig ticket with William Henry Harrison in 1840, he became the first vice president to succeed to the presidency on the death of a president, setting the precedent — later codified in the Twenty-fifth Amendment — that a successor serves as president in full, not merely in an acting capacity.

Early political career

Born to a Virginia planter family, Tyler studied at the College of William & Mary, practised law, and served in the Virginia House of Delegates, the U.S. House of Representatives, as governor of Virginia (1825–27) and in the U.S. Senate (1827–36), resigning rather than obey instructions to vote to expunge censure of Andrew Jackson. A strict constructionist and states' rights advocate, he broke with the Democrats and joined the Whigs, balancing the 1840 ticket ("Tippecanoe and Tyler Too").

Presidency

Harrison died a month after inauguration in April 1841. Tyler vetoed Whig legislation to re-charter a national bank, whereupon his entire cabinet except Daniel Webster resigned and the Whigs expelled him from the party — leaving him a "president without a party". His administration concluded the Webster–Ashburton Treaty (1842) settling the Maine boundary, ended the Seminole War in Florida, and in its final days secured the annexation of Texas by joint resolution (1845), a step toward the Mexican–American War. Tyler's wife Letitia died in the White House in 1842; in 1844 he married Julia Gardiner, 30 years his junior, with whom he had seven children (he fathered 15 in total, more than any president).

Later life

Retiring to his Sherwood Forest Plantation in Virginia, Tyler re-entered public life in 1861 as a mediator, chairing the failed Washington Peace Conference, then supported secession and was elected to the Confederate House of Representatives, dying in Richmond in January 1862 before taking his seat. Remarkably, through his son Lyon, Tyler had grandsons (including Lyon Gardiner Tyler Jr. and Harrison Ruffin Tyler) living into the 21st century; Harrison Tyler died in 2025.
