Isadore Singer

Born: Isadore Manuel Singer, May 3, 1924, Detroit, Michigan

Died: February 11, 2021 (aged 96), Boxborough, Massachusetts

Known for: Atiyah–Singer index theorem

Awards: National Medal of Science (1983), Abel Prize (2004)

Institutions: MIT

Isadore Manuel Singer (May 3, 1924 – February 11, 2021) was an American mathematician. He was an Institute Professor in the Department of Mathematics at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology and a professor emeritus at the University of California, Berkeley.

The index theorem

Singer is noted for his work with Michael Atiyah proving the Atiyah–Singer index theorem in 1962. The theorem relates the analytic index of an elliptic differential operator on a compact manifold to a topological index computed from characteristic classes, unifying analysis, geometry and topology. It opened new avenues in mathematics and later found deep applications in theoretical physics, including gauge theory and string theory. In recognition of this work, Singer and Atiyah jointly received the Abel Prize in 2004.

Career and other work

Singer studied physics at the University of Michigan before switching to mathematics, earning his PhD from the University of Chicago in 1950 under Irving Segal. He spent most of his career at MIT, with periods at UC Berkeley, where he founded the Mathematical Sciences Research Institute (MSRI) with Shiing-Shen Chern and Calvin Moore in 1982. His other contributions include the Kadison–Singer problem (posed with Richard Kadison in 1959 and solved in 2013), work on torsion with Henry McKean, and influential papers connecting spectral theory and geometry. He received the National Medal of Science in 1983 and the Steele Prize for Lifetime Achievement in 2000.
