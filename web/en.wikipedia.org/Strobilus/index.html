Strobilus
Plural: Strobili
Found in: Conifers, lycophytes, horsetails

A strobilus (plural strobili) is a structure present on many land plant species consisting of sporangia-bearing structures densely aggregated along a stem. Strobili are often called cones, most familiarly the woody seed cones of conifers.

Types

Conifer strobili include pollen cones (male) and seed cones (female). Lycophytes such as clubmosses and spikemosses bear spores in strobili at stem tips, and horsetails reproduce through strobili as well. In cycads and some other gymnosperms the cones can be very large.

Structure

A typical strobilus has a central axis surrounded by modified leaves — sporophylls or cone scales — each bearing sporangia or ovules. After fertilisation, seed cones protect developing seeds until maturity and dispersal.
