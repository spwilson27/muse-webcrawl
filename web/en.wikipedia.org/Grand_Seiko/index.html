Grand Seiko

Grand Seiko is a line of luxury watches manufactured by the Japanese company Seiko. Launched in 1960 with the goal of creating "the ideal watch", Grand Seiko is known for its high-precision movements, restrained design and distinctive hand-finishing, including the mirror-like Zaratsu polishing of its cases.

History

The first Grand Seiko, the model 3180, was produced by Seiko's Suwa factory and exceeded Swiss chronometer standards of its day. Two Seiko factories — Suwa Seikosha and Daini Seikosha — competed to make the best Grand Seiko watches, an internal rivalry that drove rapid improvement. In the late 1960s Grand Seiko mechanical movements dominated Japanese observatory trials and performed strongly in Swiss competitions at Neuchâtel and Geneva. Production of mechanical Grand Seiko models ceased in the mid-1970s during the quartz crisis, before the line was revived in 1988 with quartz models and in 1998 with new mechanical calibres.

Movements

Grand Seiko produces three families of movements: high-beat mechanical calibres (the 9S series, oscillating at 36,000 vibrations per hour), high-accuracy quartz calibres (the 9F series), and Spring Drive movements (the 9R series), which combine a mainspring with an electronic regulator for a claimed accuracy of about one second per day and a perfectly smooth-gliding seconds hand.

Design

The brand follows a design code known as the "Grammar of Design", established by designer Taro Tanaka in the 1960s, emphasizing flat, distortion-free polished surfaces, sharp hour markers and interplay of light and shadow. Grand Seiko became an independent brand, separate from Seiko, in 2017.
