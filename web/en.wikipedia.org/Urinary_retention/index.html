Urinary retention

SpecialtyUrology, emergency medicine
SymptomsInability to urinate, suprapubic pain, bladder distension
TypesAcute, chronic
TreatmentCatheterization, treatment of underlying cause

Urinary retention is an inability to completely empty the urinary bladder. It may be acute — a sudden inability to urinate at all, which is a medical emergency — or chronic, in which a person can urinate but incompletely empties the bladder, leaving a persistently elevated post-void residual volume.

Causes

Causes fall into several categories. Obstructive causes include benign prostatic hyperplasia (the most common cause in older men), prostate cancer, urethral strictures, bladder stones, severe constipation, and pelvic organ prolapse. Neurological causes include spinal cord injury, multiple sclerosis, diabetes-related bladder dysfunction, and stroke. Pharmacological causes include anticholinergic drugs, sympathomimetics in cold remedies, opioids, and anaesthesia. Postoperative retention is common, particularly after pelvic or perineal surgery.

Signs and diagnosis

Acute retention presents with suprapubic pain, a palpable distended bladder, and an urgent but futile desire to void. Diagnosis is confirmed by bladder ultrasound or by catheterization measuring a large residual volume; a post-void residual persistently above about 300 mL is a common diagnostic threshold for significant retention.

Treatment

Acute retention is relieved by urinary catheterization, usually with a urethral (Foley) catheter; a suprapubic catheter is used when urethral access fails. Definitive treatment addresses the underlying cause: alpha-blockers or surgery (such as transurethral resection of the prostate) for prostatic obstruction, discontinuation of offending medications, or intermittent self-catheterization for neurogenic bladder. Untreated retention can cause bladder overdistension injury, urinary tract infection, bladder stones, and hydronephrosis with kidney damage.
