The Alexander Archipelago is a group of about 1,100 islands off the south-eastern coast of Alaska, in the Alaska Panhandle. The islands are the drowned tops of a submerged section of the Coast Mountains, separated from the mainland and from each other by deep channels and fjords; the sheltered waterways between them form much of Alaska's Inside Passage.

Geography and ecology

The archipelago stretches some 500 km and is covered largely by the temperate rainforest of the Tongass National Forest, with dense stands of Sitka spruce and western hemlock. Wildlife includes brown bears — Admiralty Island has one of the world's densest populations — Sitka black-tailed deer, bald eagles, and the genetically distinct Alexander Archipelago wolf. Communities such as Ketchikan, Sitka, Juneau (on the adjacent mainland) and Petersburg depend on fishing, timber and tourism.

History

The islands are the homeland of the Tlingit, Haida and Tsimshian peoples. Russian fur traders colonised the area in the late eighteenth century, making Sitka (New Archangel) the capital of Russian America, before Alaska was sold to the United States in 1867. The archipelago was named in honour of Tsar Alexander II.
