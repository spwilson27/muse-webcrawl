Tony Awards

Tony Awards
Awarded for: Excellence in Broadway theatre
Country: United States
First awarded: 1947
Presented by: American Theatre Wing and The Broadway League

The Tony Awards, formally the Antoinette Perry Award for Excellence in Broadway Theatre, are annual awards recognising excellence in live Broadway theatre. Presented by the American Theatre Wing and The Broadway League, the awards are widely regarded as the highest honour in American theatre — the theatre equivalent of the Academy Awards for film.

History

The awards were founded in 1947 by the American Theatre Wing and named for Antoinette "Tony" Perry, an actress, director and co-founder of the Wing who had died the previous year. The first ceremony was held at the Waldorf Astoria Hotel in New York City. The famous medallion — a mask of comedy and tragedy on a swivelling mount — was introduced in 1949.

Categories and eligibility

Awards are given in competitive categories for plays and musicals, including Best Play, Best Musical, and performance awards in leading and featured roles, together with honours for direction, design and score. Eligibility is limited to productions mounted in the 41 designated Broadway theatres in New York City during the season. The ceremony is traditionally held in June and broadcast nationally on CBS.

Notable records

Hamilton received a record 16 nominations in 2016, winning 11. Harold Prince holds the record for the most Tonys won by an individual, with 21. Audra McDonald has won six acting awards, the most of any performer.
