2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami

The 2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami occurred at 14:46 JST (05:46 UTC) on Friday, 11 March 2011. The undersea megathrust earthquake had a magnitude of 9.0–9.1 and an epicentre approximately 72 km east of the Oshika Peninsula of Tōhoku, Japan. It was the most powerful earthquake ever recorded in Japan and the fourth most powerful in the world since modern seismography began.

Earthquake

The earthquake was caused by subduction of the Pacific Plate beneath the Okhotsk Plate along the Japan Trench. It shifted the Earth's axis and shortened the length of a day slightly. Honshu was moved 2.4 m east by the quake.

Tsunami

The earthquake triggered powerful tsunami waves that reached heights of up to 40.5 m in Miyako, Iwate Prefecture, and travelled up to 10 km inland in the Sendai area. Coastal towns in Iwate, Miyagi and Fukushima prefectures were devastated.

Casualties and damage

The National Police Agency confirmed more than 15,000 deaths, with thousands more injured or missing. Over 100,000 buildings were destroyed. The World Bank estimated economic damage of about US$235 billion, making it the costliest natural disaster in history.

Fukushima Daiichi nuclear disaster

The tsunami flooded the Fukushima Daiichi Nuclear Power Plant, disabling cooling systems and causing meltdowns in three reactors. Hydrogen explosions damaged the reactor buildings, and radioactive material was released, prompting large-scale evacuations.
