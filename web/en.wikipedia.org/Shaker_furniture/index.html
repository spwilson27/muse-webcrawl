Shaker furniture is a distinctive style of furniture developed by the United Society of Believers in Christ's Second Appearing, commonly known as the Shakers, a religious community founded in England in the eighteenth century and established in the United States in the 1780s. Shaker furniture is widely admired for its simplicity, utility and craftsmanship, and it embodies the guiding principles of the Shaker faith: honesty, humility, utility and perfection.

Design principles

The Shakers believed that making something well was in itself an act of prayer, and that ornamentation was prideful and unnecessary. Their furniture therefore avoided carving, veneer, inlay and other decoration, relying instead on fine proportions, visible joinery and carefully chosen local woods such as maple, pine and cherry. Pieces were designed for the communal life of the villages: built-in cupboards in dwellings, long dining tables, sewing desks, oval boxes, and the famous ladder-back rocking chairs. Peg rails ran around Shaker rooms so that chairs, clocks and clothing could be hung to keep floors clear.

Production and influence

Shakers sold furniture and oval boxes to the outside world, and the chair industry at Mount Lebanon, New York, became their most famous commercial enterprise. As Shaker membership declined in the twentieth century, original pieces became highly collectible, and the Shaker style strongly influenced modern design movements, including the Danish modern furniture tradition and contemporary minimalist design.
