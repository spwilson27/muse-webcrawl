The Andaman Sea (historically also known as the Burma Sea) is a marginal sea of the northeastern Indian Ocean, bounded by the coastlines of Myanmar and Thailand along the Malay Peninsula to the east, the Andaman and Nicobar Islands of India to the west, and the Indonesian island of Sumatra to the south. It covers about 600,000 square kilometres and connects to the Bay of Bengal in the northwest and, through the Strait of Malacca, to the South China Sea in the southeast.

Geology

The Andaman Sea lies over an active plate boundary where the Indian Plate subducts beneath the Burma Plate, producing deep trenches, submarine volcanoes — including Barren Island, India's only active volcano — and frequent earthquakes. Rupture of the megathrust off Sumatra on 26 December 2004 generated the catastrophic Indian Ocean tsunami that devastated coastlines around the sea and the wider ocean basin.

Oceanography and ecology

The sea receives huge freshwater inflows from the Irrawaddy and Salween rivers, lowering surface salinity especially in the north. Coral reefs around the Andaman Islands, the Mergui Archipelago, and Thailand's Similan Islands support rich marine biodiversity, and the sea sustains major fisheries, including of anchovies and other pelagic fish.

Trade and history

The Andaman Sea has been a trade route between India and Southeast Asia since ancient times. The Strait of Malacca at its southern outlet remains one of the world's busiest shipping lanes. Port Blair in the Andaman Islands is the principal Indian port on the sea.
