Genetically modified food

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Genetically modified foods (GM foods) are foods produced from organisms whose genetic material has been altered using genetic engineering techniques, rather than through conventional selective breeding alone. Genetic modification allows precise introduction of traits such as insect resistance, herbicide tolerance, disease resistance, delayed ripening or improved nutritional content.

History

The first commercialised GM food was the Flavr Savr tomato, approved in the United States in 1994 and engineered for longer shelf life. From 1996, large-scale planting began of herbicide-tolerant soybean and insect-resistant (Bt) maize and cotton; the United States, Brazil, Argentina, Canada and India became the largest growers. Golden Rice, engineered to produce beta-carotene to combat vitamin A deficiency, became a prominent and debated example of a nutritionally enhanced crop.

Regulation and safety assessment

GM foods are assessed before marketing by regulators such as the FDA, EFSA and national authorities, which evaluate toxicity, allergenicity and nutritional equivalence. There is a broad scientific consensus — expressed by bodies including the World Health Organization and national academies of science — that approved GM foods are no riskier to human health than conventional foods, while each new product is assessed case by case.

Controversy

Public debate continues over labelling, corporate control of seed markets, environmental effects such as gene flow and herbicide-resistant weeds, and the appropriate role of precaution. The European Union applies stricter authorisation and mandatory labelling, and cultivation there remains limited, while the Americas have adopted GM crops widely.
