Bicycle-sharing system

A bicycle-sharing system, also called a public bicycle system or bike share, is a shared transport service in which bicycles are made available for shared use on a short-term basis for a price or free. Users pick up a bicycle at one docking station (or anywhere, in dockless systems) and return it to another location, making the systems well suited to one-way urban trips.

History

The earliest well-known programme was Amsterdam's "White Bicycle" plan of 1965. Coin-deposit systems followed in Denmark in the 1990s, and smartcard-based systems such as Paris's Vélib' (2007) made large-scale bike share practical in hundreds of cities. From about 2015, dockless systems using GPS locks and smartphone apps spread rapidly, especially in China, where operators such as Mobike and Ofo launched millions of bicycles.

Operation

Operators redistribute bicycles between stations to balance supply, maintain the fleet, and increasingly offer e-bikes. Systems are usually run by or for city governments, funded by subscriptions, per-ride fees, advertising, and sponsorship.

Effects

Studies find bike share increases cycling and provides "last mile" connections to public transport, though dockless parking clutter and short bicycle lifespans have raised regulatory and sustainability concerns.
