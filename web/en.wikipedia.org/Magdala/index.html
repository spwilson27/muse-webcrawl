Magdala

Magdala (Aramaic: Migdal, "tower") was an ancient Jewish town on the western shore of the Sea of Galilee, in present-day Israel, near the modern town of Migdal. It is best known as the hometown of Mary Magdalene — "Mary of Magdala" — a prominent follower of Jesus.

History

In the first century CE, Magdala was a prosperous fishing and fish-processing centre; its Greek name, Tarichaeae ("pickling vats"), reflects the salted-fish trade for which it was famous across the Roman world. The historian Josephus, who fortified the town during the First Jewish–Roman War, describes its wealth and population. Roman forces retook it after a naval battle on the lake in 67 CE, and the town declined thereafter, though occupation continued into the Byzantine period.

Archaeology

Excavations since 2006 have uncovered a first-century synagogue — among the oldest known in Israel — containing the carved "Magdala stone", decorated with a depiction of a menorah and other Temple imagery. Finds also include ritual baths, a marketplace, harbour installations, and well-preserved residential quarters, providing rare evidence for a Galilean town of the time of Jesus.

Modern site

The archaeological park at Magdala, together with the modern pilgrimage centre and the nearby Arab village of al-Majdal (depopulated in 1948), keeps the ancient name alive in the landscape of the Galilee.
