Andrey Kolmogorov

Andrey Nikolaevich Kolmogorov (25 April 1903 – 20 October 1987) was a Soviet mathematician who made fundamental contributions to probability theory, topology, turbulence, information theory, and the theory of algorithms. He is regarded as one of the greatest mathematicians of the 20th century.

Work

Kolmogorov's 1933 monograph Foundations of the Theory of Probability gave probability its modern axiomatic basis, built on measure theory. With his student Vladimir Arnold and Jürgen Moser he developed KAM theory on the stability of Hamiltonian systems. In fluid mechanics he proposed the statistical theory of turbulence, including the famous energy spectrum law. His definition of algorithmic (Kolmogorov) complexity, developed independently of Solomonoff and Chaitin, founded algorithmic information theory. He also contributed to stochastic processes, harmonic analysis, set theory, and mathematical logic.

Life

Born in Tambov, Kolmogorov studied at Moscow State University, where he spent his career and founded a major school of mathematics. He reformed Soviet school mathematics education and co-founded a boarding school for gifted students. He received the Wolf Prize in 1980 among many honours.
