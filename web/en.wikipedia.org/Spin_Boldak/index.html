Spin Boldak

Spin Boldak (Pashto: سپین بولدک) is a border town and the headquarters of Spin Boldak District in the southern part of Kandahar Province, Afghanistan, at the border with Pakistan. It is linked by a highway with Kandahar to the north and with Chaman in Pakistan's Balochistan province to the south. The Wesh–Chaman border crossing at Spin Boldak is one of the busiest official crossings between the two countries and a major trade route.

Geography and population

The town sits at an elevation of about 1,200 metres in arid country near the foothills south of Kandahar. Its population is predominantly Pashtun, principally of the Achakzai and Noorzai tribes. Spin Boldak serves as a market and transit hub for goods moving between Kandahar and Quetta, and for Afghan transit trade through Karachi.

History

During the Soviet–Afghan War and the subsequent civil war, Spin Boldak was a supply point for mujahideen moving between Pakistan and Kandahar. The area saw fighting during the Taliban's rise in the 1990s and after the 2001 US-led invasion; an airstrike incident near Spin Boldak in 2002 drew attention to the border region's volatility. The Taliban retook the town in July 2021 during their nationwide offensive, capturing the border crossing, which has since been the scene of periodic closures, clashes, and disputes over the Durand Line frontier. Smuggling and the cross-border movement of people remain important to the local economy.
