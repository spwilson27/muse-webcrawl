Tijuana is the largest city in the Mexican state of Baja California and sits on the Pacific coast immediately south of the United States border, across from San Diego, California. It is one of Mexico's fastest-growing and most economically dynamic cities, and forms a single binational metropolitan area with San Diego.

History

The area was inhabited by the Kumeyaay people and explored by Spaniards in the 16th century; a ranch called Rancho Tía Juana ("Aunt Jane") appeared on maps by 1829. The town was founded in 1889 after the 1848 Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo fixed the international border nearby. During U.S. Prohibition (1920–1933), Americans flocked to Tijuana's bars, racetracks and casinos — including the luxurious Agua Caliente resort — building its reputation as an entertainment city. Later growth was driven by tourism, the bracero programme and, from the 1960s, the maquiladora export-manufacturing industry.

Economy and culture

Tijuana is a major manufacturing centre for electronics, medical devices and aerospace components, employing tens of thousands in maquiladoras. The San Ysidro Port of Entry is among the busiest land border crossings on Earth. The city is credited with inventing the Caesar salad (at Caesar's restaurant in 1924) and is known for Baja Med cuisine, its cultural centre CECUT, the Tijuana Cultural Center's iconic "La Bola", and Club Tijuana (Xolos) football team.

Challenges

The city has endured severe violence from cartel conflict, notably around 2008–2010 and again in the late 2010s, while also receiving large numbers of migrants and deportees. Despite this, Tijuana remains a gateway for trade and migration between the United States and Mexico.
