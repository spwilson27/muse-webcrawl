Insurgency in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa

The insurgency in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (also known as the War in North-West Pakistan) is an ongoing armed conflict between the government of Pakistan and Islamist militant groups — most prominently the Pakistani Taliban (Tehreek-e-Taliban Pakistan, TTP) and allied factions — concentrated in the northwestern province of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and the former Federally Administered Tribal Areas (FATA), along the border with Afghanistan.

Background

The conflict has roots in the Soviet–Afghan War and the aftermath of the 2001 US-led invasion of Afghanistan, after which al-Qaeda and Afghan Taliban fighters took refuge in Pakistan's tribal belt. Pakistan's alliance with the United States in the "War on Terror" and military operations in the tribal areas from 2002 onward brought it into open conflict with local and foreign militants. In December 2007, dozens of militant commanders united under Baitullah Mehsud to form the TTP, dedicated to fighting the Pakistani state and imposing its interpretation of Sharia law.

Course of the conflict

The insurgency spread beyond the tribal agencies into settled districts including Swat, Buner and Dir, and militants carried out waves of bombings across Pakistan's cities. Major Pakistani military offensives included Operation Rah-e-Rast in Swat (2009), Operation Rah-e-Nijat in South Waziristan (2009), and Operation Zarb-e-Azb in North Waziristan (2014), launched after the attack on Karachi's Jinnah International Airport. In December 2014, TTP gunmen attacked the Army Public School in Peshawar, killing around 150 people, most of them children — an atrocity that prompted the National Action Plan and a broad crackdown.

Merger of FATA

In May 2018 the tribal areas were constitutionally merged into Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province, ending the colonial-era Frontier Crimes Regulation regime and extending Pakistan's courts and administration to the region.

Resurgence

Violence declined sharply after 2015, but attacks rose again following the Taliban's return to power in Afghanistan in 2021 and the collapse of peace talks and a ceasefire with the TTP in late 2022. The insurgency has killed tens of thousands of Pakistani civilians and security personnel since 2004 and displaced millions of people at its peak.
