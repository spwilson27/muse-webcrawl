Jezreel Valley

The Jezreel Valley (Hebrew: עמק יזרעאל, Emek Yizre'el; Arabic: مرج ابن عامر, Marj Ibn ʿĀmir), often called simply the Valley (HaEmek), is a large, fertile plain and inland valley in northern Israel, lying between the Galilee hills to the north and the mountains of Samaria and Mount Gilboa to the south, with Mount Carmel to the west. It forms the eastern and largest part of the Esdraelon plain, drained in part by the Kishon River toward Haifa Bay and in part eastward to the Jordan Valley. Long the crossroads of the ancient Via Maris trade route, the valley has been a battleground from Pharaonic times to the twentieth century, and in modern times a heartland of Jewish agricultural settlement — the moshav Nahalal and many kibbutzim were founded there.

Geography

The valley is a roughly triangular graben plain some 380 km² in area within the larger Esdraelon plain, bounded by fault scarps: Nazareth and the Lower Galilee hills to the north, the Samarian hills and Mount Gilboa to the south, and the Menashe heights to the southwest. The Kishon River drains its western part to the Mediterranean near Haifa, while the Harod Stream drains the eastern Beit She'an valley toward the Jordan River. Its rich, heavy alluvial soils — once marshy and malarial — became, after drainage in the 1920s and 1930s, some of Israel's most productive agricultural land, producing field crops, cotton, orchards, fish ponds, and dairy farming.

History

The valley's position on the Via Maris, the great route between Egypt and Mesopotamia, made it a perennial theatre of war. At Megiddo, Pharaoh Thutmose III defeated a Canaanite coalition in 1457 BCE in the first battle in history recorded in detail; Josiah of Judah died fighting Pharaoh Necho II there in 609 BCE. In biblical narrative, Deborah and Barak defeated Sisera near the Kishon, Gideon fought the Midianites by the spring of Harod, Saul fell on Mount Gilboa, and the royal city of Jezreel gave the valley its name — associated with Naboth's vineyard and the fall of Ahab's house. In Christian eschatology, nearby Armageddon (Har Megiddo) is the prophesied site of the final battle. The valley later saw battles in the Crusader period, Napoleon's campaign of 1799 (Battle of Mount Tabor), and in 1918 Allenby's decisive Megiddo offensive against the Ottomans.

Jewish settlement and modern development

From 1911 and especially in the 1920s, the Jewish National Fund purchased large tracts, much of it from the absentee Sursock family of Beirut — purchases that became politically contentious as tenant farmers were displaced. Drainage of the swamps eliminated malaria, and the valley became a showpiece of Zionist agricultural pioneering: Nahalal (1921), the first moshav ovdim, was laid out in a celebrated circular plan by Richard Kauffmann; kibbutzim such as Ein Harod, Geva, and Beit Alfa followed, along with the town of Afula (founded 1925). The Jezreel Valley railway (Haifa–Deraa, originally part of the Hejaz Railway) crossed the valley from 1905 until 1948; a new standard-gauge Valley Railway between Haifa and Beit She'an opened in 2016 broadly following its route.

Today

The valley remains predominantly agricultural, dotted with kibbutzim, moshavim, community settlements, and the Arab town of Zarzir at its edge, administered largely by the Jezreel Valley Regional Council. It hosts the Max Stern Yezreel Valley College near Afula, the HaEmek Medical Center, and the Megiddo Regional airfield proposals that have periodically stirred controversy. Tel Megiddo and Beit She'arim are UNESCO World Heritage Sites, drawing visitors to the valley's layered history.
