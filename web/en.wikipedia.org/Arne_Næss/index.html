Arne Næss

Arne Dekke Eide Næss (27 January 1912 – 12 January 2009) was a Norwegian philosopher who coined the term "deep ecology" and was an important figure in the environmental movement of the late 20th century. He held the chair of philosophy at the University of Oslo from 1939 to 1970 and was also a noted mountaineer, leading the first ascent of Tirich Mir in 1950.

Philosophy

Næss's early work concerned the philosophy of science, semantics and the interpretation of language, influenced by logical empiricism and by Spinoza and Gandhi. In a 1973 article he distinguished the "shallow" environmental movement, concerned mainly with pollution and resource management in wealthy countries, from a "deep" ecology movement that questions the fundamental premises of industrial society and affirms the intrinsic value of all living beings — the doctrine of biospherical egalitarianism. His personal philosophy, which he called "Ecosophy T" after his mountain cabin Tvergastein, emphasised self-realisation and identification with nature.

Activism and legacy

Næss took part in non-violent direct action, including protests against hydroelectric development at Mardalsfossen in 1970, and helped found the Norwegian Green Party's intellectual milieu. The deep ecology platform he drafted with George Sessions in 1984 became a founding statement of the movement. He received numerous honours, including the Swedish Academy's Nordic Prize.
