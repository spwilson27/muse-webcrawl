Turoyo

RegionTur Abdin (SE Turkey), Syria, diaspora
FamilyAfro-Asiatic → Semitic → Aramaic → Neo-Aramaic
ScriptSyriac (Serto), Latin

Turoyo is a Central Neo-Aramaic language spoken traditionally by Syriac Christians in the Tur Abdin region of southeastern Turkey and northeastern Syria. It is closely related to, but distinct from, the literary Classical Syriac language used in church liturgy.

Dialects and speakers

Dialects are named after villages such as Midyat (the basis of the standard), Midin, and Kfarze. Large speaker communities now live in the diaspora, especially in Germany, Sweden, and the Netherlands, following emigration and the upheavals of the 20th century, including the Sayfo genocide of 1915.

Features

Turoyo preserves the Aramaic emphatic state system in modified form and has borrowed vocabulary from Turkish, Kurdish, and Arabic. Efforts to teach and standardize a written form using Syriac script have grown in the diaspora.
