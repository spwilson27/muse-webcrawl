EV-DO (Evolution-Data Optimized, officially CDMA2000 1xEV-DO) is a telecommunications standard for the wireless transmission of data through radio signals, developed by Qualcomm and standardised by the 3GPP2 as part of the CDMA2000 family. It provided mobile broadband data services on CDMA networks, which previously carried data only at low rates.

Revisions

The original release, Revision 0, offered peak forward-link rates of about 2.4 Mbit/s. Revision A (2006) raised peaks to 3.1 Mbit/s downlink and 1.8 Mbit/s uplink and added quality-of-service support suited to voice over IP. Revision B allowed carriers to bond multiple channels for higher rates, though it saw limited deployment as operators moved to LTE.

Deployment and decline

SK Telecom in South Korea launched the first commercial EV-DO service in 2002; large networks followed at Verizon Wireless and Sprint in the United States and operators in Japan, Australia and elsewhere. EV-DO carried early smartphone and laptop data traffic through the 2000s before LTE displaced it. The remaining major networks were shut down in the early 2020s as part of the global 3G sunset.
