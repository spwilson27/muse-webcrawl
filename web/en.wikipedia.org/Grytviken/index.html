Grytviken

Grytviken is a settlement on South Georgia in the South Atlantic. Founded in 1904 as a whaling station, it was the first and most famous of the shore whaling stations on the island. Although now abandoned as a working station, Grytviken is the island's best-known site and a popular stop for Antarctic cruise ships visiting South Georgia. The administrative centre of the territory of South Georgia and the South Sandwich Islands is at nearby King Edward Point.

History

Grytviken was established on 16 November 1904 by the Norwegian sea captain Carl Anton Larsen and his Compañía Argentina de Pesca, backed by Argentine capital. The name is Swedish for "the pot cove", referring to sealers' try-pots found there. The station operated for six decades, processing tens of thousands of whales, and at its height housed several hundred workers. Whaling ended and the station closed in the mid-1960s as whale stocks and the industry declined.

Ernest Shackleton died aboard the Quest at Grytviken in January 1922 and is buried in the whalers' cemetery there; the ashes of his right-hand man Frank Wild were interred beside him in 2011.

Today

During the Falklands War in 1982, Argentine forces occupied Grytviken before being retaken by British troops in Operation Paraquet. Since the 1990s the rusting station has been cleaned up (including asbestos and fuel removal) and the South Georgia Museum, housed in the former station manager's villa, opened in 1992. The restored Norwegian whalers' church (1913) and the cemetery remain focal points for visitors. A small number of museum staff and officials live seasonally at King Edward Point, a short walk away.
