Oirat language

Oirat (Clear Script: ᡆᡕᡅᠷᠠᡑ ᡘᡄᠯᡄᠨ; Mongolian script: ᠣᠶᠢᠷᠠᠳ ᠬᠡᠯᠡᠨ, Oyirad kelen) is a Mongolic language spoken by the Oirat Mongols in Mongolia, western China (Xinjiang and Qinghai), and Russia's Republic of Kalmykia, where its variety is known as Kalmyk. Oirat is often regarded as a dialect cluster of Mongolian or as a separate language closely related to Khalkha Mongolian.

Distribution and varieties

Varieties include Dörbet, Torgut, Khoshut, and Ölöt in Mongolia and Xinjiang; the Khoshut and Torgut varieties of Qinghai (the Deed Mongols); and Kalmyk in the Caspian steppe, descended from Oirats who migrated west in the 17th century. Kalmyk is officially recognised in Kalmykia and written in Cyrillic, while Oirat in China has been written in the Clear Script.

Writing

In 1648 the Oirat Buddhist monk Zaya Pandita created the Clear Script (todo bichig, "clear writing"), modifying the Mongolian script to represent Oirat sounds unambiguously. It was used for an extensive literature of Buddhist translations, chronicles, and folk tales.

Status

Oirat is considered endangered in many of its homelands: in Mongolia it is overshadowed by Khalkha Mongolian, and in China by Khalkha and Mandarin, though revitalisation and documentation efforts continue.
