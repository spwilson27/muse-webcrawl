The Galleria dell'Accademia di Firenze, or "Gallery of the Academy of Florence", is an art museum in Florence, Italy. It is best known as the home of Michelangelo's sculpture David. It also has other sculptures by Michelangelo and a large collection of paintings by Florentine artists, mostly from the period 1300–1600, the Trecento to the Late Renaissance.

History

The gallery was founded in 1784 by Grand Duke Pietro Leopoldo of Tuscany alongside the Accademia di Belle Arti, Florence's fine arts academy, so that students could study exemplary works of art. In 1873 Michelangelo's David was moved here from Piazza della Signoria to protect it from the weather, and a tribune was built to display it, completed in 1882.

Collections

Alongside David, the museum holds Michelangelo's unfinished Prisoners (or Slaves), the Saint Matthew, and the Palestrina Pietà (attribution disputed). Other galleries contain Florentine Gothic painting, works by Botticelli and Ghirlandaio, a collection of historic musical instruments from the Luigi Cherubini Conservatory, and one of the world's most important collections of early stringed instruments, including Stradivari violins.
