Emission spectrum

The emission spectrum of a chemical element or compound is the spectrum of frequencies of electromagnetic radiation emitted when an electron transitions from a high-energy state to a lower-energy state. Each element produces a unique set of spectral lines, making emission spectra a "fingerprint" used to identify substances.

When atoms are excited — by heating, electrical discharge or other energy input — their electrons move to higher energy levels and then fall back, emitting photons with energies equal to the differences between levels. For hydrogen, the visible lines form the Balmer series, described by the Rydberg formula.

Emission spectroscopy underpins techniques such as flame tests, atomic emission spectrometry and astronomical spectroscopy, by which the composition of stars and nebulae is determined. Emission spectra contrast with absorption spectra, which show dark lines where radiation has been absorbed.
