Robert Mueller

Robert Swan Mueller III
Born: August 7, 1944, New York City, New York, U.S.
Occupation: Attorney, law enforcement official
Known for: Director of the FBI (2001–2013); Special Counsel investigation (2017–2019)

Robert Swan Mueller III (born August 7, 1944) is an American lawyer and government official who served as the sixth director of the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) from 2001 to 2013. A graduate of Princeton University and the University of Virginia School of Law, Mueller served as a Marine Corps officer during the Vietnam War, receiving the Bronze Star and Purple Heart. In May 2017 he was appointed special counsel by Deputy Attorney General Rod Rosenstein to investigate Russian interference in the 2016 presidential election and related matters.

Early life and military service

Mueller was born in New York City and raised in Princeton, New Jersey. He attended St. Paul's School in New Hampshire and Princeton University, graduating in 1966. He joined the United States Marine Corps and served in Vietnam as a platoon commander in the 3rd Marine Division. He was wounded in action and was awarded the Bronze Star with Combat "V" for heroism, the Purple Heart, two Navy Commendation Medals, and the Combat Action Ribbon.

Legal career

After earning his law degree in 1973, Mueller worked in private practice and then as a federal prosecutor. He served as United States Attorney for the Northern District of California, worked in the Department of Justice under the George H. W. Bush administration, and headed the Criminal Division from 1990 to 1993, where he oversaw prosecutions including the Lockerbie bombing case against Pan Am and the case against Panamanian dictator Manuel Noriega. He also served as United States Attorney for the District of Massachusetts in an acting capacity.

Director of the FBI

President George W. Bush nominated Mueller as FBI director in 2001; he was confirmed by the Senate 98–0 and took office on September 4, 2001, one week before the September 11 attacks. He oversaw the bureau's transformation toward counterterrorism and intelligence work. In 2011, President Barack Obama asked him to serve two additional years beyond the statutory ten-year term, and Congress passed special legislation allowing the extension. He stepped down in 2013 after twelve years and was succeeded by James Comey. Mueller is the second-longest-serving FBI director in history, after J. Edgar Hoover.

Special counsel

On May 17, 2017, following President Donald Trump's dismissal of Comey, Rosenstein appointed Mueller special counsel. The investigation examined Russian interference in the 2016 election, possible coordination with the Trump campaign, and obstruction of justice. The final report, submitted in March 2019, documented extensive Russian interference and ten potential instances of obstruction but did not establish a criminal conspiracy between the campaign and Russia, and made no traditional prosecutorial judgment on obstruction. The investigation produced indictments of numerous individuals, including Paul Manafort and Michael Flynn, as well as Russian nationals and organizations.

Later life

After the investigation, Mueller generally withdrew from public life, occasionally teaching and speaking about public service.
