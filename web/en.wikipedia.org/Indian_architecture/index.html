Indian architecture

Indian architecture is the architecture of the Indian subcontinent, a tradition spanning more than four millennia, from the planned cities of the Indus Valley civilisation to the skyscrapers of modern Mumbai. Rooted in the subcontinent's religions and cultures, it absorbed and transformed influences from Central Asia, Persia, and Europe while retaining distinctive continuities, especially in temple design governed by the treatises of Vastu shastra.

Early periods

The cities of Mohenjo-daro and Harappa (c. 2600–1900 BCE) show grid planning, baked brick, and sophisticated drainage. From the Mauryan period (3rd century BCE) survive rock-cut architecture and Ashokan pillars; the great stupa at Sanchi and the rock-cut chaitya halls and viharas of Ajanta and Ellora developed under Buddhist patronage.

Hindu temple architecture

Two great classical styles crystallized: the Nagara style of northern India, with its curvilinear shikhara tower (as at Khajuraho and Konark), and the Dravidian style of the south, with pyramidal vimana towers and monumental gateway gopurams (as at Thanjavur and Madurai). The Vesara style of the Deccan blends features of both, seen in Chalukya and Hoysala temples such as those at Halebidu.

Islamic and colonial eras

From the 12th century, Indo-Islamic architecture produced mosques, tombs, and forts culminating in Mughal masterpieces: Humayun's Tomb, the Taj Mahal at Agra, and Fatehpur Sikri. British rule added Indo-Saracenic and Gothic Revival public buildings, and Edwin Lutyens and Herbert Baker's New Delhi. After 1947, modernists such as Le Corbusier (Chandigarh), Louis Kahn, Charles Correa, and B. V. Doshi reshaped Indian building.
