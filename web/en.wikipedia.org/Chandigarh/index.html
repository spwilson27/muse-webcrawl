Chandigarh is a city and union territory in northern India, serving as the shared capital of the states of Punjab and Haryana. It was one of the first planned cities of independent India, built in the 1950s as a new capital for Punjab after the Partition of 1947 left Lahore in Pakistan. Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru championed it as a symbol of modernity, "unfettered by the traditions of the past".

Design

After the death of planner Matthew Nowicki in 1950, the Swiss-French architect Le Corbusier took over the master plan, aided by his cousin Pierre Jeanneret and the British architects Maxwell Fry and Jane Drew. The city is laid out in a grid of self-sufficient neighbourhood "sectors", framed by the Shivalik Hills, with landmark modernist buildings including the Capitol Complex — the Secretariat, Palace of Assembly, and High Court — inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2016 as part of Le Corbusier's architectural work. Other attractions include Nek Chand's Rock Garden of recycled materials and the artificial Sukhna Lake.

Administration

Chandigarh became a union territory in 1966 when Haryana was carved out of Punjab, with both states retaining it as their capital. It is administered by an administrator appointed by the President of India, and consistently ranks among India's wealthiest and cleanest cities, with high literacy and per-capita income.
