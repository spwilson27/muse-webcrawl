The Valle dei Templi ("Valley of the Temples") is an archaeological site in Agrigento, Sicily, containing some of the best-preserved ancient Greek temples in the world. It preserves the remains of the Doric temples of Akragas, one of the leading cities of Magna Graecia.

Temples

The ridge holds seven temples in Doric style dating to the 5th century BCE, including the remarkably intact Temple of Concordia, the Temple of Juno, the Temple of Heracles, and the colossal but unfinished Temple of Olympian Zeus, among the largest Doric temples ever built.

History and preservation

Akragas was founded around 582 BCE and flourished until its sack by Carthage in 406 BCE. Excavations began in the early 19th century; the valley was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1997 and forms one of Sicily's most visited attractions.
