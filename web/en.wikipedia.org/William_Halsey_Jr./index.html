William Halsey Jr.

William Frederick "Bull" Halsey Jr. (October 30, 1882 – August 16, 1959) was an American Navy admiral during World War II. He is one of only four officers to have attained the rank of fleet admiral of the United States Navy, and commanded the Third Fleet through the final campaigns against Japan.

World War II

Halsey was at sea commanding carrier forces when Pearl Harbor was attacked, and he led early-war carrier raids against Japanese-held islands. As commander of the South Pacific Area in late 1942 he reinvigorated the Guadalcanal campaign, famously pledging to "hit hard, hit fast, hit often." From 1944 he alternated command of the fast carrier force with Raymond Spruance (as Third Fleet and Fifth Fleet respectively), commanding at the Battle of Leyte Gulf, where his decision to chase a decoy carrier force — prompting the famous query, "Where is, repeat, where is Task Force Thirty Four?" — remains debated. His fleet twice sailed through destructive typhoons (December 1944 and June 1945), and the Japanese surrender was signed aboard his flagship, USS Missouri, in September 1945.

Promoted to fleet admiral that December, Halsey retired in 1947 and died in 1959. He is buried in Arlington National Cemetery.
