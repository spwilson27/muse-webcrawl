Canada–European Union relations

Canada–European Union relations are the bilateral relations between Canada and the European Union (EU). Canada's ties with Europe are deep-rooted, and the EU is one of Canada's largest trading partners and closest political partners. Formal relations with the European Economic Community began in the 1950s, and the EU opened a delegation in Ottawa in 1976.

History

Canada was among the first countries to establish diplomatic links with the predecessor organisations of the EU. The 1976 Framework Agreement for Commercial and Economic Cooperation was the Community's first formal agreement with an industrialised third country. Relations are led by regular summits between the Canadian prime minister and the presidents of the European Council and the European Commission.

Agreements

The two major agreements underpinning the modern relationship are the Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement (CETA), a free-trade agreement provisionally applied since 21 September 2017 that eliminated duties on most goods traded between the parties, and the Strategic Partnership Agreement (SPA), which provides a framework for political dialogue and cooperation on foreign policy, security, and other issues and has also been provisionally applied since 2017.

Trade

The EU is among Canada's largest export markets and a major source of foreign direct investment. Canadian exports to Europe include mineral fuels, precious metals and stones, machinery, and agri-food products, while imports include pharmaceuticals, vehicles, and machinery.
