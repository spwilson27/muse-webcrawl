Remote sensing

Remote sensing is the acquisition of information about an object or phenomenon without making physical contact with it, typically using sensors mounted on aircraft or Earth-observation satellites. In modern usage the term chiefly refers to detecting and measuring electromagnetic radiation reflected or emitted by the Earth's surface and atmosphere in order to monitor physical, chemical and biological properties at a distance.

Techniques

Passive sensors measure natural radiation, chiefly reflected sunlight and thermal infrared emission. Multispectral and hyperspectral imagers record many wavelength bands, allowing land cover, vegetation health, water quality and mineralogy to be inferred from spectral signatures. Active sensors emit their own energy and time the return signal: radar (including synthetic-aperture radar, which images through cloud and darkness), lidar (laser scanning, used for elevation models and forestry) and sonar for underwater mapping.

Platforms and data

Key satellite programmes include Landsat (operating since 1972), the European Space Agency's Sentinel satellites under Copernicus, MODIS instruments on Terra and Aqua, and commercial high-resolution constellations. Images are characterised by spatial, spectral, temporal and radiometric resolution, and are processed with geographic information systems (GIS) for analysis.

Applications

  • Agriculture: crop condition, yield forecasting, irrigation management
  • Environment: deforestation, land-use change, glacier retreat, ocean colour
  • Weather and climate: cloud, temperature and greenhouse-gas monitoring
  • Disasters: flood, wildfire and earthquake damage mapping
  • Urban planning, archaeology and defence reconnaissance

History

Remote sensing began with aerial photography from balloons in the 19th century and expanded rapidly with military reconnaissance aircraft in the two world wars. The launch of TIROS-1 (1960) began weather observation from space, and Landsat 1 (1972) opened systematic civilian Earth observation, transforming geography, geology and environmental science.
