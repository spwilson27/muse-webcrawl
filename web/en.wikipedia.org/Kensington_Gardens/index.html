Kensington Gardens

Kensington Gardens is one of the Royal Parks of London, covering 265 acres immediately west of Hyde Park, from which it is separated by the Serpentine Bridge and West Carriage Drive. Once the private gardens of Kensington Palace, they were opened to the public by Queen Victoria.

Landscaped in the 18th century for Queen Caroline, with the Long Water and Round Pond, the gardens contain the Albert Memorial, the Peter Pan statue (1912), the Diana, Princess of Wales Memorial Playground, the Italian Gardens, and the Serpentine Galleries. J. M. Barrie set Peter Pan's early adventures here. Kensington Palace, at the western edge, remains a royal residence.
