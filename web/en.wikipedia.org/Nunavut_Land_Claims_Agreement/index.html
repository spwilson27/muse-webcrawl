Nunavut Land Claims Agreement

The Nunavut Land Claims Agreement (Inuktitut: Nunavut Nunatarutit Nalunaijainiq) is a land claim and treaty between the Inuit of the Nunavut Settlement Area and the Crown in Right of Canada. Signed on 25 May 1993 in Iqaluit by representatives of the Tunngavik Federation of Nunavut and the Government of Canada, it is the largest Indigenous land claim in Canadian history, covering some 2 million square kilometres. The Agreement provided for the creation of the territory of Nunavut, which came into being on 1 April 1999.

The Nunavut Settlement Area covers about one-fifth of Canada's land mass.

Terms

Under the Agreement, Inuit exchanged Aboriginal title to the lands and waters of the Nunavut Settlement Area for defined rights and benefits. These included title to about 356,000 km² of land — of which roughly 37,000 km² includes mineral (subsurface) rights — a capital transfer of $1.173 billion paid over fourteen years, a share of resource royalties, guaranteed rights to harvest wildlife, and participation in co-management boards for wildlife, land use planning, impact assessment and water regulation. The Agreement also committed the federal government to recommend legislation establishing the new territory of Nunavut.

Nunavut Tunngavik Incorporated

Nunavut Tunngavik Incorporated (NTI), successor to the Tunngavik Federation of Nunavut, was created to hold and administer the settlement funds and to monitor implementation of the Agreement on behalf of Inuit beneficiaries.

Implementation disputes

NTI has repeatedly argued that the federal government failed to implement key provisions — notably obligations on Inuit employment in government — and launched a major lawsuit in 2006, settled in 2015 for $255 million, part of which funded an Inuit training organisation. Devolution of control over Crown lands and resources to the Government of Nunavut was advanced by a devolution agreement signed in January 2024, with transfer targeted for 2027.
