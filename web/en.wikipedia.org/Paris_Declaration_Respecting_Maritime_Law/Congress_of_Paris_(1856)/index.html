Congress of Paris (1856)

Congress of Paris
Date: February–March 1856
Location: Paris, France
Outcome: Treaty of Paris (1856)

The Congress of Paris of 1856 was a diplomatic conference held in Paris after the Crimean War to negotiate peace between Russia and the allied powers — France, Britain, the Ottoman Empire, and Sardinia. It produced the Treaty of Paris, signed on 30 March 1856.

Terms

The Treaty neutralized the Black Sea, demilitarizing it and ending Russia's naval presence there; it guaranteed the integrity of the Ottoman Empire; it gave southern Bessarabia to Moldavia, pushing Russia back from the Danube; and it confirmed free navigation of the Danube. The Åland Islands were demilitarized.

Declaration on Maritime Law

Attached to the congress was the Paris Declaration Respecting Maritime Law of 16 April 1856, the first multilateral treaty on the laws of naval warfare, which abolished privateering and protected neutral goods.
