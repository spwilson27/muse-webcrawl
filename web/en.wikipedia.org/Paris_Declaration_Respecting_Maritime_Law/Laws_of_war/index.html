Laws of war
Also called: International humanitarian law
Key treaties: Geneva Conventions; Hague Conventions; Paris Declaration (1856)

The laws of war, or international humanitarian law, are the rules of international law that govern the conduct of armed conflict: how wars may be fought (jus in bello) and the protection of persons not taking part in hostilities.

Development

Modern codification began with the Lieber Code of 1863 and the first Geneva Convention of 1864. The Paris Declaration of 1856 regulated naval warfare, abolishing privateering. The Hague Conventions of 1899 and 1907 set rules on weapons and the treatment of prisoners, while the four Geneva Conventions of 1949 and their Additional Protocols form the core of present-day law.

Principles

Central principles include distinction between combatants and civilians, proportionality, military necessity, and humane treatment of prisoners of war and the wounded. Grave breaches constitute war crimes, prosecutable before international tribunals.
