Crimean War
Date: October 1853 – February 1856
Location: Crimea, Black Sea, Caucasus, Baltic
Result: Allied victory; Treaty of Paris

The Crimean War (1853–1856) was fought between the Russian Empire and an alliance of France, Britain, the Ottoman Empire and Sardinia, mainly in the Crimean Peninsula. It is remembered for the Siege of Sevastopol, the Charge of the Light Brigade, and Florence Nightingale's pioneering nursing work.

Causes

The war grew out of disputes over the rights of Christian minorities in the Ottoman-controlled Holy Land and Russian ambitions to expand at Ottoman expense. Russia's occupation of the Danubian principalities prompted Britain and France to declare war in March 1854.

Course

Allied forces landed in Crimea in September 1854, won the battles of Alma, Balaclava and Inkerman, and besieged Sevastopol for a year until its fall in September 1855. Fighting also occurred in the Baltic, Caucasus and Pacific. Disease caused the majority of deaths on all sides.

Peace

The war ended with the Congress and Treaty of Paris in 1856, which neutralized the Black Sea and checked Russian expansion.
