Privateer
Authorization: Letter of marque
Abolished (Europe): Paris Declaration, 1856

A privateer was a privately owned ship (and its crew) authorized by a government during wartime, through a letter of marque, to attack and capture enemy shipping. Captured vessels, called prizes, were sold and the proceeds shared among the owners, captain and crew.

History

Privateering flourished from the 16th to the 19th centuries. Famous privateers included Francis Drake, whose raids on Spanish shipping were sanctioned by Elizabeth I, and the French corsairs of Saint-Malo. During the American Revolutionary War and the War of 1812, American privateers captured hundreds of British merchant ships.

Abolition

The Paris Declaration Respecting Maritime Law (1856) abolished privateering among its signatories, which eventually included nearly all maritime powers.
