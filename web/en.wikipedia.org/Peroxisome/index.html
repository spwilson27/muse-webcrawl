Peroxisome

A peroxisome is a membrane-bound organelle found in the cytoplasm of virtually all eukaryotic cells. Peroxisomes contain oxidative enzymes and are involved in the catabolism of very long chain fatty acids, branched chain fatty acids, bile acid intermediates, and in the detoxification of reactive oxygen species.

Structure and discovery

Peroxisomes are roughly spherical, bounded by a single membrane, and often contain a crystalline core of urate oxidase in animal cells. They were first observed by Johannes Rhodin in 1954 and characterised biochemically by Christian de Duve, who coined the name because of their role in producing and scavenging hydrogen peroxide.

Functions

Peroxisomal oxidases transfer hydrogen from substrates to oxygen, forming hydrogen peroxide, which the enzyme catalase converts to water and oxygen. Peroxisomes carry out beta-oxidation of very long chain fatty acids (later completed in mitochondria), synthesise plasmalogen ether lipids important for myelin, and in plants house the glyoxylate cycle (glyoxysomes) and photorespiration enzymes.

Disorders

Defects in peroxisome biogenesis cause Zellweger syndrome spectrum disorders; defects of single enzymes include X-linked adrenoleukodystrophy, dramatised in the film Lorenzo's Oil.
