The Zollverein Coal Mine Industrial Complex (German: Zeche Zollverein) is a large former industrial site in the city of Essen, in the Ruhr region of Germany. Once the largest and most productive coal mine in Europe, it has been inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List since 2001 and is a landmark of European industrial heritage.

History

The first Zollverein shaft was sunk in 1847 by the industrialist Franz Haniel, who named the mine after the German customs union, the Zollverein. The mine grew steadily with the Ruhr's coal and steel industry. Its most famous component, Shaft XII, opened in 1932, was designed by architects Fritz Schupp and Martin Kremmer in the Bauhaus-influenced New Objectivity style and was celebrated as the "most beautiful coal mine in the world", its symmetrical red-brick and steel-truss buildings a deliberate work of industrial architecture.

Closure and reuse

Coal extraction ended in 1986 and the coking plant closed in 1993. Rather than being demolished, the site was preserved and redeveloped as a cultural and creative centre housing the Ruhr Museum, the Red Dot Design Museum and event spaces, and serving as an anchor point of the European Route of Industrial Heritage. The site was a focal point of Essen's year as European Capital of Culture in 2010.
