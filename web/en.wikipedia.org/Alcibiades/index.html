Alcibiades

Alcibiades (c. 450 – 404 BC) was a prominent Athenian statesman, orator and general, the last of the Alcmaeonid family. A ward of Pericles and a companion of Socrates, he played a major and dramatic role in the second half of the Peloponnesian War as a strategic adviser, military commander and political intriguer, changing his allegiance among Athens, Sparta and Persia several times.

Early life

Born into the aristocratic Alcmaeonid clan, Alcibiades was raised after his father's death in the household of his guardian Pericles. He fought at Potidaea and Delium, where Socrates reportedly saved his life. Famous for his beauty, charm, wealth and ambition, he entered politics as a flamboyant leader of the democratic faction.

Peloponnesian War

Alcibiades championed the Sicilian Expedition of 415 BC but was recalled to face charges connected with the mutilation of the Hermae; he escaped to Sparta, where he advised Athens's enemies, including the fortification of Decelea. Falling foul of the Spartans, he defected to the Persian satrap Tissaphernes, then engineered his recall to Athens, winning victories including Cyzicus (410 BC). After the defeat at Notium he withdrew into exile in Thrace and Phrygia, where he was assassinated in 404 BC, probably at Spartan instigation after Athens's fall.

Legacy

Alcibiades appears in Plato's dialogues, notably the Symposium, and in Thucydides and Plutarch as a figure of dazzling talent and dangerous instability.
