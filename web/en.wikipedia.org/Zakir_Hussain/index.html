Zakir Hussain

Zakir Hussain (9 March 1951 – 15 December 2024) was an Indian tabla player, composer, percussionist, music producer, and film actor. The eldest son of the legendary tabla maestro Alla Rakha, he was widely regarded as one of the greatest tabla players of all time, and was central to bringing Indian classical percussion to a global audience through Hindustani classical performance and cross-cultural collaborations.

Early life and training

Hussain was born in Bombay (now Mumbai) and trained from early childhood by his father, Ustad Alla Rakha, a close accompanist of sitar maestro Ravi Shankar. A child prodigy, he was touring by the age of twelve. He moved to the United States in 1970, beginning an international career that spanned more than five decades.

Career

Hussain performed as an accompanist to virtually every major Hindustani classical musician of his era, and as a soloist and bandleader. He was a founding member of the Indo-jazz fusion group Shakti with guitarist John McLaughlin, and collaborated with artists including George Harrison, Van Morrison, Yo-Yo Ma, Béla Fleck, Edgar Meyer, Charles Lloyd, and Mickey Hart of the Grateful Dead, with whom he made the Grammy-winning world-music albums Planet Drum and Global Drum Project. In 2024 the album This Moment by the revived Shakti won the Grammy Award for Best Global Music Album, and Hussain won further Grammys for his work with Fleck, Meyer, and Rakesh Chaurasia.

He composed music for films and dance, taught tabla widely, and was a beloved mentor to generations of percussionists. He died in San Francisco in December 2024 of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis.

Honours

The Government of India awarded him the Padma Shri in 1988, the Padma Bhushan in 2002, and the Padma Vibhushan in 2023. He also received the Sangeet Natak Akademi Award and, in the United States, the National Heritage Fellowship from the National Endowment for the Arts.
