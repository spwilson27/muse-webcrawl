The Renault–Nissan–Mitsubishi Alliance is a strategic partnership between the automobile manufacturers Renault (France), Nissan (Japan), and Mitsubishi Motors (Japan). Formed in 1999 as the Renault–Nissan Alliance, it is not a merger: the companies remain legally independent, linked by cross-shareholdings and joint management structures. Mitsubishi joined in 2016 after Nissan acquired a controlling 34% stake in it. At its peak, the Alliance sold more than one in nine cars worldwide and was a leader in electric vehicles with the Nissan Leaf and Renault Zoe.

History

On 27 March 1999 Renault took a 36.8% stake in the heavily indebted Nissan for about $5.4 billion. Under Carlos Ghosn, Nissan's "Revival Plan" returned it to profitability, and the two companies developed shared platforms, joint purchasing (through RNPO), and common powertrains. In 2005 Ghosn became CEO of both companies. In 2016 Nissan acquired its stake in scandal-hit Mitsubishi Motors, bringing the third member into the Alliance.

Crisis and restructuring

The alliance was shaken in November 2018 when Ghosn was arrested in Japan on allegations of financial misconduct; he later fled to Lebanon in December 2019 while awaiting trial. Relations between Renault and Nissan deteriorated over the imbalance in their cross-shareholdings (Renault held about 43% of Nissan, while Nissan held 15% of Renault without voting rights) and the French state's stake in Renault. In 2023 the partners agreed to rebalance: Renault reduced its stake in Nissan toward 15%, and the companies refocused cooperation on regional projects, with Nissan investing in Renault's electric vehicle arm, Ampere.

Joint activities

Members share vehicle architectures (such as the CMF family of platforms), cooperate in purchasing, logistics, batteries, and autonomous driving research, and operate joint plants in several countries.
