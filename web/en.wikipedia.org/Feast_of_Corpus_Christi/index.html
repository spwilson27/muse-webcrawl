Feast of Corpus Christi

The Feast of Corpus Christi (Latin: Dies Sanctissimi Corporis et Sanguinis Domini Iesu Christi, "Day of the Most Holy Body and Blood of Jesus Christ the Lord"), also known as the Solemnity of the Most Holy Body and Blood of Christ, is a Christian liturgical solemnity celebrating the Real Presence of the Body and Blood, Soul and Divinity of Jesus in the elements of the Eucharist. It is observed by the Latin Church, in addition to certain Western Orthodox, Lutheran, and Anglican churches.

Observed byRoman Catholics, some Lutherans and Anglicans
TypeChristian feast
DateThursday after Trinity Sunday (or the following Sunday)

History

The feast was proposed by Saint Juliana of Liège, a Norbertine canoness from modern-day Belgium, who reported visions of Christ asking for a feast day in honour of the Blessed Sacrament. After Juliana's death, the feast was instituted by Pope Urban IV, who issued the papal bull Transiturus de hoc mundo on 11 August 1264, and commissioned Thomas Aquinas to compose the office for the feast, including the hymns Pange lingua and Lauda Sion.

Celebration

The feast is traditionally marked by Eucharistic processions in which the consecrated host, carried in a monstrance, is borne through the streets, often over carpets of flowers. In many countries the feast is a public holiday. In the Roman Rite it is celebrated on the Thursday after Trinity Sunday, though in many places it is transferred to the following Sunday.
