The brown pelican (Pelecanus occidentalis) is a North and South American species of pelican, one of three pelican species found in the Americas and one of two that feed by diving into the water. It lives on the Atlantic, Gulf and Pacific coasts of the Americas and is the state bird of Louisiana and the national bird of several Caribbean nations.

Description

The brown pelican is the smallest of the pelicans on average, though still a large bird, with a grey-brown body, white head and neck in non-breeding plumage, and a long bill with a large throat pouch. Breeding adults develop a dark brown neck and yellow head. Juveniles are largely grey-brown.

Feeding and behaviour

It feeds mainly on fish such as menhaden, anchovies and sardines, which it catches by spectacular plunge-diving from heights of up to 20 metres, using the pouch as a scoop. It breeds in colonies on coastal islands, building nests in trees, shrubs or on the ground, and lays two to three eggs.

Conservation

Populations crashed in the mid-20th century because the pesticide DDT caused eggshell thinning. The species was listed under the US Endangered Species Act in 1970; following the DDT ban it recovered strongly and was delisted in 2009.
