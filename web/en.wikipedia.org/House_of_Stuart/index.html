House of Stuart

The Stuarts ruled Scotland from 1371 and England and Ireland from 1603 until 1714.

The House of Stuart, originally Stewart, was a royal house of Scotland, England, Ireland, and later Great Britain. The family traces its descent to Walter FitzAlan, a Breton-descended steward of Scotland in the 12th century, from whose office the surname Stewart derives.

Kings of Scotland

Robert II, grandson of Robert the Bruce, became the first Stewart king of Scots in 1371. The line continued through the tumultuous reigns of James I through James V, and Mary, Queen of Scots, whose son James VI inherited the English throne in 1603 as James I of England, uniting the crowns.

The Stuarts in England

James I's son Charles I was executed in 1649 after the English Civil War, and the monarchy was abolished during the Commonwealth. The Restoration of 1660 brought Charles II to the throne, but his brother James II's Catholicism provoked the Glorious Revolution of 1688, which replaced him with William III and Mary II.

End of the line

The last reigning Stuart, Queen Anne, died in 1714 without surviving children; under the Act of Settlement 1701 the crown passed to the House of Hanover. Jacobite supporters of the exiled line launched risings in 1715 and 1745, the latter led by Charles Edward Stuart, "Bonnie Prince Charlie." The direct line ended with the death of Henry Benedict Stuart in 1807.
