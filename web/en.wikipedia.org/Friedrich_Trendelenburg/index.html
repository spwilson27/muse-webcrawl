Friedrich Trendelenburg

Friedrich Trendelenburg (24 May 1844 – 15 December 1924) was a German surgeon. He was a son of the philosopher Friedrich Adolf Trendelenburg and became one of the prominent surgeons of his era, holding chairs at Rostock, Bonn, and Leipzig.

Career

Trendelenburg studied medicine in Scotland and Germany and served as a military surgeon. He introduced several lasting contributions to surgery. The Trendelenburg position, in which the patient lies supine on an inclined plane with the head lower than the pelvis, is used in abdominal and pelvic surgery and for improving venous return. He described the Trendelenburg test for assessing the competence of the valves of the saphenous vein in patients with varicose veins, and he performed early operations for pulmonary embolism (the Trendelenburg operation), laying groundwork that later surgeons developed successfully. He also developed the Trendelenburg gait sign associated with weakness of the hip abductors, and a tracheal cannula used in anaesthesia.

Legacy

Trendelenburg founded the German Surgical Society's journal traditions and trained many students, including members of a family that produced several further generations of physicians. His name remains attached to numerous eponymous terms in surgery and clinical examination.
