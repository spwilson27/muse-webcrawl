Julie Andrews

Dame Julie Andrews (born Julia Elizabeth Wells; 1 October 1935) is an English actress, singer, and author. With a career spanning more than seven decades, she is best known for her starring roles in the film musicals Mary Poppins (1964) and The Sound of Music (1965).

Career

A child prodigy with a remarkable four-octave voice, Andrews performed in British music halls before making her Broadway debut in The Boy Friend (1954). She originated the roles of Eliza Doolittle in My Fair Lady (1956) and Queen Guinevere in Camelot (1960). Passed over for the film of My Fair Lady, she won the Academy Award for Best Actress for her film debut in Walt Disney's Mary Poppins, and was nominated again as Maria von Trapp in The Sound of Music, one of the highest-grossing films of all time.

Later work

Later films include Thoroughly Modern Millie (1967), Victor/Victoria (1982), directed by her husband Blake Edwards, and The Princess Diaries (2001). After botched vocal surgery in 1997 ended her singing career, she focused on writing children's books with her daughter Emma Walton Hamilton and on voice roles, including Queen Lillian in Shrek and the narrator of Enchanted. She was made a Dame Commander of the Order of the British Empire in 2000.
