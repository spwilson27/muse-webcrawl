The Cajuns (French: les Cadjins), also known as Louisiana Acadians (French: les Acadiens), are a Louisiana French ethnic group mainly found in the U.S. state of Louisiana and the surrounding Gulf Coast region. While Cajuns are usually described as descendants of Acadian exiles who settled the region, their ancestry is not solely Acadian: through intermarriage and assimilation they incorporated French, Spanish, German, Anglo-American, and Native American settlers who adopted the developing Cajun culture.

History

The Acadians were French settlers of Acadia, in what is now the Canadian Maritime provinces. Between 1755 and 1764, during the French and Indian War, British colonial officers deported more than 11,500 Acadians in the event known as Le Grand Dérangement (the Great Expulsion). Many exiles eventually made their way to Spanish-controlled Louisiana, where the first group arrived in 1765 under the leadership of Joseph Broussard, called Beausoleil. They settled first along the Mississippi River and then in the prairies and bayous of southwestern Louisiana, a region now called Acadiana.

In the early 20th century, Louisiana's 1921 constitution effectively banned French instruction in public schools, and generations of Cajun children were punished for speaking French, driving a sharp decline in the language. A cultural revival beginning in the 1960s — led by organizations such as CODOFIL (the Council for the Development of French in Louisiana, founded 1968) — restored pride in Cajun identity and promoted French-language education.

Culture

Cajun culture is renowned for its cuisine, including gumbo, jambalaya, boudin, cracklins, and crawfish boils, characterized by the "holy trinity" of onion, bell pepper, and celery and liberal use of cayenne and filé powder. Cajun music, played on accordion, fiddle, and triangle ('tit fer), and its close cousin zydeco — developed by Louisiana Creoles of African descent — remain central to community life, as do the fais do-do dance parties and the rural Mardi Gras run (courir de Mardi Gras).

The United States Census recognized Cajuns as an ethnic group, and in 1980 the federal court in Roach v. Dresser recognized Acadians as a protected class under the Civil Rights Act of 1964.
