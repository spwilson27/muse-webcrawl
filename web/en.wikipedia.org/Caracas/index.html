Caracas, officially Santiago de León de Caracas, is the capital and largest city of Venezuela, located in a valley of the Venezuelan Coastal Range in the north of the country, separated from the Caribbean coast by the steep Cerro El Ávila. Founded in 1567, it is Venezuela's political, economic, and cultural centre.

History

Caracas is the birthplace of Simón Bolívar, liberator of much of South America, born there in 1783. The city was damaged by major earthquakes in 1812 and 1967. Oil wealth drove rapid growth and modernist construction in the mid-20th century, including the University City designed by Carlos Raúl Villanueva.

Recent decades

Since the 2010s Venezuela's economic and political crisis has deeply affected the city, with shortages, emigration, and deteriorating infrastructure, though Caracas remains the seat of government and the country's principal metropolis.
