Human height
The distance from the bottom of the feet to the top of the head in a standing human, measured with a stadiometer.

Human height is the distance from the bottom of the feet to the top of the head of a person standing erect. It is one of the most studied anthropometric traits: a product of both genetics and environment, it correlates with health, nutrition and living standards across populations and history.

Determinants

Studies of twins and families suggest that about 80% of variation in height within a well-nourished population is genetic, with hundreds of gene variants identified. The remaining variation reflects childhood nutrition, disease burden and socioeconomic conditions. Growth is driven by growth hormone acting through the hypothalamic–pituitary axis and related endocrine pathways.

Historical trends

Average heights fell during early agricultural transitions and again in early industrial cities, then rose markedly in the 19th and 20th centuries with improved diet and sanitation — a phenomenon called the secular trend. The Netherlands, once among the shortest nations in Europe, became the tallest in the world, with average male height around 183 cm. Growth has plateaued in some wealthy countries, while rapid gains continued recently in parts of East Asia.
