The Intellivision is a home video game console released by Mattel Electronics in 1979. The name is a portmanteau of "intelligent television". Development began in 1977, the same year Atari introduced the Atari VCS (later the 2600). In an attempt to compete with Atari, Mattel entered the video game market with a console featuring more capable graphics and sound, a distinctive controller with a 12-button keypad and directional disc, and a library of roughly 125 games, many of them licensed sports titles.

History

The console was developed at Mattel's design and development department with hardware by APh Technological Consulting and chips from General Instrument. It was test-marketed in Fresno, California in 1979 and released across the United States in 1980 at a price of US$299. George Plimpton starred in a famous series of television commercials contrasting Intellivision graphics with the Atari 2600's.

Hardware and accessories

The Intellivision is built around the 16-bit CP1610 microprocessor, unusual at a time when rival systems used 8-bit parts. Controllers combine a flat 16-direction disc, four side action buttons, and a numeric keypad accepting game-specific overlays. Accessories included the Intellivoice voice synthesis module (1982), which added speech to supporting games, and the Entertainment Computer System (ECS) keyboard add-on. A keyboard component originally promised at launch was delayed and became the subject of an FTC complaint and fines before limited release.

Legacy

Over three million units were sold before Mattel Electronics was closed in 1984 during the video game crash of 1983. The Intellivision library has been repeatedly revived through compilations such as Intellivision Lives!, the Intellivision Flashback plug-and-play console (2014), and the (long-delayed, ultimately cancelled) Intellivision Amico project. In 2024 Atari SA acquired the Intellivision brand and game library.
