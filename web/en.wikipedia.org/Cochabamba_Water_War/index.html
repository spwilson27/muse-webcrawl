Cochabamba Water War

The Cochabamba Water War was a series of protests that took place in Cochabamba, Bolivia, between late 1999 and April 2000, against the privatisation of the city's municipal water supply.

Background

Following pressure for market reforms, Bolivia granted a concession to Aguas del Tunari, a consortium led by the U.S. company Bechtel. A new water law and sharp tariff increases — in some cases doubling or tripling bills — provoked mass mobilisation led by the Coordinadora de Defensa del Agua y de la Vida, with figures such as Óscar Olivera.

Outcome

Protests escalated into a general strike and clashes in which one protester was killed. In April 2000 the government cancelled the contract and the concession was withdrawn, making the Water War an emblematic case in global debates over water privatisation and resource rights.
