Specificity (linguistics)

FieldSemantics, syntax
DefinitionWhether a noun phrase refers to a particular identifiable entity
RelatedDefiniteness, reference, scope

In linguistics, specificity is a semantic feature of noun phrases that distinguishes whether the speaker has a particular, identifiable referent in mind. A specific noun phrase picks out an individual the speaker could in principle identify, while a nonspecific one refers to any member of a class.

Examples

The sentence "I want to marry a Norwegian" is ambiguous: on the specific reading there is a particular Norwegian the speaker has in mind; on the nonspecific reading, any Norwegian would do. Many languages mark the distinction grammatically, for example through case marking on objects in Turkish, where accusative case signals specific direct objects.

Related notions

Specificity interacts with definiteness, animacy, and scope, and figured prominently in theories of differential object marking and in semantic analyses by scholars such as Klaus von Heusinger.
