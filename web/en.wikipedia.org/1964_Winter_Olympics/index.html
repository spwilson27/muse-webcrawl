The 1964 Winter Olympics, officially known as the IX Olympic Winter Games, were a winter multi-sport event held in Innsbruck, Austria, from 29 January to 9 February 1964. Innsbruck had been the runner-up for the 1960 Games and was awarded the 1964 edition ahead of Calgary and Lahti.

Highlights

A warm Föhn wind caused a severe lack of snow, and the Austrian army famously carried snow from higher elevations to the courses. The Soviet Union topped the medal table, led by speed skater Lidiya Skoblikova, who won all four women's speed skating events, and cross-country skier Klavdiya Boyarskikh, who won all three women's cross-country events.

Luge made its Olympic debut at these Games, though the sport was overshadowed by the death of British luger Kazimierz Kay-Skrzypecki during a pre-Games practice run; Australian skier Ross Milne also died in a training accident. The Games were the first Winter Olympics to be broadcast internationally on a large scale and were the first to use computerised results.

Medal table

The Soviet Union won 25 medals, including 11 golds. Host nation Austria finished fourth with 12 medals. Germany competed as a single United Team of Germany for the last time at these Games.
