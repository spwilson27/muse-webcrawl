Seventh chord

A dominant seventh chord (G7) in root position

A seventh chord is a chord consisting of a triad plus a note forming an interval of a seventh above the chord's root. When not otherwise specified, a "seventh chord" usually means a dominant seventh chord: a major triad together with a minor seventh. Seventh chords are fundamental to Western harmony and are standard building blocks in classical music, jazz, blues and popular music.

Types

Combining major and minor triads with major and minor sevenths gives the main types: the dominant seventh (major triad, minor seventh), major seventh (major triad, major seventh), minor seventh (minor triad, minor seventh), half-diminished seventh (diminished triad, minor seventh) and fully diminished seventh (diminished triad, diminished seventh). Other combinations, such as minor-major seventh and augmented seventh chords, are also used.

Function

In common-practice harmony the dominant seventh chord (V7) has a strong tendency to resolve to the tonic chord, driven by the resolution of its chordal seventh downward and its third — the leading tone — upward. In blues, dominant sevenths are used as stable tonic and subdominant chords. In jazz, seventh chords are the basic harmonic unit, frequently extended with added ninths, elevenths and thirteenths and altered tones.

Notation

Seventh chords are written with a superscript 7 after the chord root in popular-music notation (e.g. C7, Cmaj7, Cm7) and with figured bass numerals in classical analysis. Inversions are indicated by additional figures such as 6/5, 4/3 and 2.
