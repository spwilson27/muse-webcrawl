Ultimate fate of the universe

The ultimate fate of the universe is a topic in physical cosmology concerned with the long-term evolution and eventual end state of the universe. Competing scenarios depend mainly on the geometry of space, the density of matter and energy, and the nature of dark energy, whose discovery in 1998 showed that the expansion of the universe is accelerating.

Role of dark energy

If dark energy is a cosmological constant, expansion accelerates forever and the universe cools toward a state of maximum entropy. If dark energy strengthens over time (phantom energy), acceleration could tear apart all structures in a "Big Rip." If dark energy decays or reverses sign, expansion could halt and reverse into a "Big Crunch," possibly followed by a bounce. Observations currently favour a cosmological-constant-like behaviour, making eternal accelerated expansion the leading scenario.

Big Freeze (heat death)

In the Big Freeze, expansion continues indefinitely. Star formation ceases within roughly 100 trillion years as gas is exhausted; existing stars burn out, galaxies' remnants fall into central black holes or are ejected, and matter decays. On timescales of 1040 years, if protons decay, atoms disintegrate; black holes evaporate by Hawking radiation over up to ~10100 years, leaving a cold, dark universe of photons, electrons, positrons and neutrinos at near absolute zero — the heat death.

Big Rip

If the dark energy equation-of-state parameter w stays below −1, its density grows as space expands, and the acceleration reaches a finite-time singularity: first galaxy clusters, then galaxies, solar systems, planets and finally atoms themselves are torn apart.

Big Crunch and Big Bounce

If the mean density is high enough, or if dark energy weakens, gravity could reverse the expansion. The universe would contract, heat up and end in a Big Crunch resembling the Big Bang in reverse. Some cyclic models propose a subsequent Big Bounce beginning a new expansion.

Vacuum decay

If the Higgs field occupies a metastable false vacuum, a quantum tunnelling event could nucleate a bubble of true vacuum expanding at nearly light speed, destroying all structures within it without warning. Whether the vacuum is metastable remains an open question in particle physics.

Timescales

The far-future timeline contemplates epochs: the Stelliferous Era of active stars (to ~1014 years), the Degenerate Era of stellar remnants, the Black Hole Era, and the Dark Era. These timescales dwarf the universe's present age of 13.8 billion years.
