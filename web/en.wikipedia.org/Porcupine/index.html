Porcupine

Porcupines
KingdomAnimalia
OrderRodentia
FamiliesHystricidae (Old World), Erethizontidae (New World)

Porcupines are large rodents with coats of sharp spines, or quills, that protect them against predators. The term covers two families: the Old World porcupines (Hystricidae) of Africa and Asia, and the New World porcupines (Erethizontidae) of the Americas. They are the third-largest rodents, after the capybara and the beaver.

Quills

Quills are modified hairs coated with thick plates of keratin. Contrary to popular belief, porcupines cannot shoot their quills; instead, quills detach easily when touched and, in New World species, are barbed so they work deeper into a predator's skin. Lost quills regrow. A single porcupine may have more than 30,000 quills.

Diet and behavior

Porcupines are herbivores, eating leaves, bark, twigs, and fruit. Many are nocturnal and sleep in dens or hollow trees during the day. New World species are skilled climbers; Old World species are mainly terrestrial and can grow to 30 kilograms or more, as in the crested porcupine.
