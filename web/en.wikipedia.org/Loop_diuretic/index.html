Loop diuretic

Loop diuretics are a class of diuretic medicines that act on the ascending limb of the loop of Henle in the kidney. They are among the most powerful diuretics, used mainly to treat hypertension and oedema due to heart failure, liver cirrhosis, and kidney disease.

Mechanism

They inhibit the sodium–potassium–chloride cotransporter (NKCC2) in the thick ascending limb, reducing reabsorption of sodium, chloride and potassium and thereby increasing urine output. Examples include furosemide, bumetanide, torsemide and ethacrynic acid.

Adverse effects

Common adverse effects include low blood potassium (hypokalaemia), low sodium, dehydration, low blood pressure, metabolic alkalosis, increased uric acid (which can provoke gout), and, at high doses, hearing damage (ototoxicity), especially with rapid intravenous administration.
