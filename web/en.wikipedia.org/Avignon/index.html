Avignon (Provençal: Avinhon) is a commune and the prefecture of the Vaucluse department in the Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur region of southeastern France, on the left bank of the Rhône. It is world-famous as the seat of the papacy during the 14th century, when seven successive popes resided in the city — the period known as the Avignon Papacy — leaving it the immense fortress-palace of the Palais des Papes, the largest Gothic palace in Europe.

History

The site was settled by the Cavares tribe and became the Roman town of Avennio. After centuries within the orbit of Provence's rulers, Avignon's history was transformed in 1309, when Pope Clement V, a Frenchman unwilling to face the anarchy of Rome, settled in Avignon, then technically a possession of the Angevin counts of Provence under the suzerainty of the Holy Roman Empire. His successors built the papal palace and court; the papacy purchased the city outright in 1348 from Joanna I of Naples. Avignon and the surrounding Comtat Venaissin remained papal territory until annexed by revolutionary France in 1791 — an enclave within France for over four centuries.

The Avignon Papacy and Schism

Seven popes reigned from Avignon (1309–1376): Clement V, John XXII, Benedict XII, Clement VI, Innocent VI, Urban V, and Gregory XI, who returned the court to Rome in 1377. His successor's contested election triggered the Western Schism (1378–1417), during which rival "antipopes" again held Avignon against Rome. Though denounced by critics such as Petrarch as a "Babylonian captivity" and a period of corruption and French influence, the Avignon court was also a glittering centre of administration, finance, and early Renaissance culture, and its bureaucracy modernised the medieval Church.

Landmarks

  • Palais des Papes — the Popes' Palace, built mainly under Benedict XII and Clement VI (1335–52), with painted chapels by Matteo Giovannetti.
  • Pont Saint-Bénézet — the "Pont d'Avignon" of the famous children's song Sur le pont d'Avignon; of its original 22 arches (built 1177–85), only four survive, the rest swept away by Rhône floods.
  • The city walls — 4.3 km of 14th-century ramparts ring the old town, unusually intact.
  • Avignon Cathedral (Notre Dame des Doms) and the Rocher des Doms gardens above the river.

Culture

Every July the Festival d'Avignon, founded by Jean Vilar in 1947, turns the city into one of the world's great theatre festivals, with official productions in the palace's Cour d'Honneur and hundreds of fringe ("Off") shows. Avignon is also a gateway to Provence's wine country, near Châteauneuf-du-Pape — a name recalling the Avignon popes' summer residence — and is served by a TGV station on the Paris–Marseille high-speed line.
