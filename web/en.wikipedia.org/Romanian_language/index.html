Romanian (limba română) is an Eastern Romance language belonging to the Indo-European language family. It is the official and national language of both Romania and Moldova, and one of the official languages of the European Union. Romanian is spoken by roughly 24 million people as a first language, mostly in Romania and Moldova, with significant communities in Ukraine, Serbia, Hungary and the worldwide diaspora.

History

Romanian descends from the Vulgar Latin spoken in the Roman provinces of Dacia and Moesia. After Rome withdrew from Dacia in 271 AD, the Latin of the region developed in relative isolation, absorbing influences from neighbouring Slavic languages, Hungarian, Greek and Turkish. The oldest surviving Romanian text is Neacșu's letter of 1521, written in the Cyrillic alphabet, which Romanian used until the 19th century, when a Latin-based alphabet was adopted as part of a deliberate "re-Latinisation" that also brought in many French and Italian loanwords.

Features

Romanian preserves several archaic Latin features lost elsewhere in Romance, including a reduced case system (nominative–accusative, genitive–dative, vocative) and a neuter gender that behaves as masculine in the singular and feminine in the plural. The definite article is suffixed to the noun (băiat "boy", băiatul "the boy"). The alphabet includes the distinctive letters ă, â, î, ș and ț. Vocabulary is predominantly Latin in origin, with a substantial Slavic layer (estimated around 10–15% of the lexicon).

Varieties

Closely related Eastern Romance varieties include Aromanian, Megleno-Romanian and Istro-Romanian, spoken in the Balkans and Croatia. In Moldova the language was long officially called "Moldovan" and written in Cyrillic during the Soviet period; since 2023 Romanian law and the Moldovan constitution recognise it as Romanian.
