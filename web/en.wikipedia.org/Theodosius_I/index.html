Theodosius I

Theodosius I (January 11, 347 – January 17, 395), also called Theodosius the Great, was Roman emperor from 379 to 395. He was the last emperor to rule both the eastern and western halves of the empire.

Reign

Appointed to the East after the disaster at Adrianople, Theodosius made peace with the Goths, settling them as foederati. With Gratian and Valentinian II he issued the Edict of Thessalonica (380), making Nicene Christianity the state religion of Rome; he suppressed pagan cults and presided over the First Council of Constantinople in 381.

Legacy

After his death the empire was divided permanently between his sons Arcadius (East) and Honorius (West). His religious settlement defined the Christian Roman state, though his penance before Ambrose of Milan after the massacre at Thessalonica also illustrated the church's growing moral authority over emperors.
