Milan Kundera

Milan Kundera (1 April 1929 – 11 July 2023) was a Czech and French novelist, short story writer, playwright, and essayist. He went into exile in France in 1975, became a naturalized French citizen in 1981, and had his Czech citizenship revoked in 1979 (it was restored in 2019).

Milan Kundera
Born: 1 April 1929, Brno, Czechoslovakia
Died: 11 July 2023, Paris, France
Occupation: Novelist, essayist, playwright
Notable works: The Unbearable Lightness of Being, The Joke, The Book of Laughter and Forgetting

Life

Kundera was born in Brno, the son of a musicologist. He studied in Prague and joined the Communist Party, from which he was expelled twice. After the Warsaw Pact invasion of Czechoslovakia in 1968, his works were banned and he emigrated to France in 1975.

Works

His first novel, The Joke (1967), satirized life under communism. The Book of Laughter and Forgetting (1979) and The Unbearable Lightness of Being (1984), set during and after the Prague Spring, brought him international fame; the latter was adapted into a 1988 film. From the 1990s he wrote in French, producing novels such as Immortality, Slowness, and The Festival of Insignificance.
