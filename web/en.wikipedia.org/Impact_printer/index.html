Impact printer

An impact printer is a type of computer printer that creates an image by striking an inked ribbon against paper with a print head, in a manner similar to a typewriter. Impact printers were the dominant form of computer printing in the 1970s and 1980s before being largely displaced by non-impact technologies such as inkjet and laser printers.

Types

  • Dot matrix printers use a print head containing a column of pins (commonly 9 or 24) that strike the ribbon to form characters and graphics as patterns of dots. They were widely used for home and office computing.
  • Daisy wheel printers use a wheel with fully formed characters embossed on petals, producing letter-quality output comparable to a typewriter but no graphics capability.
  • Line printers print an entire line at once using chains, bands, or drums of character slugs, and were used for high-volume data processing.
  • Drum and band printers are variants of line printers used in mainframe environments.

Advantages and disadvantages

Impact printers can print on multipart forms using carbon copies or carbonless copy paper, because the physical strike transfers the image through several layers — a capability non-impact printers lack. For this reason they survive in niche applications such as invoices, receipts, and log printing. Their disadvantages include noise, slow speed, and lower print quality compared with modern technologies.
