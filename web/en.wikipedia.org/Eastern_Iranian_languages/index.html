Eastern Iranian languages

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Eastern Iranian languages are a subgroup of the Iranian languages, within the Indo-Iranian branch of Indo-European. They were historically spoken across a vast area of Central Asia and the Eurasian steppe, from the Caucasus to western China, and are generally divided into Northeastern and Southeastern branches.

History

Middle Iranian Eastern languages are attested in documents and inscriptions: Bactrian, written in Greek script in northern Afghanistan; Sogdian, the lingua franca of the Silk Road, preserved in letters, Buddhist, Manichaean and Christian texts; Khotanese (Saka) from the Tarim Basin oases of Khotan and Tumshuq; Chorasmian from south of the Aral Sea; and Scythian and Sarmatian, known from names and glosses in Greek sources. Avestan, the language of the Zoroastrian scriptures, is usually classified as Old Eastern Iranian.

Modern languages

Surviving Eastern Iranian languages are mostly spoken in mountainous pockets of Central Asia: Pashto, the largest, in Afghanistan and Pakistan; Ossetian in the Caucasus, descended from the Alans; Yaghnobi in Tajikistan, a direct descendant of Sogdian; the Pamir languages (Shughni, Wakhi, Sarikoli and others) of the Badakhshan region; and Ormuri and Parachi, which are near extinction. Many others, including Khwarezmian and Bactrian, are extinct.

Features

Eastern Iranian languages tend to preserve certain Old Iranian consonant clusters lost in Western Iranian, and they show characteristic developments such as the retention of the initial clusters *fr- and the treatment of voiced stops as fricatives. Classification within the group remains debated because of long contact and convergence among neighbouring varieties.
