Transport Scotland is the executive agency of the Scottish Government responsible for the country's national transport networks. Established in 2006, it manages the Scottish trunk road and rail networks, oversees ferry and aviation services to the islands, sets national transport strategy, and funds active travel and decarbonisation programmes.

Remit

Transport Scotland is responsible for the trunk road network (including the A9 and A82 dualling and safety programmes), the franchised ScotRail train operator, major rail infrastructure such as the Borders Railway and the Airdrie–Bathgate line, lifeline ferry services to the Hebrides and other islands through Caledonian MacBrayne and NorthLink, and Highlands and Islands airports. It publishes the National Transport Strategy, with commitments to reduce car kilometres and decarbonise transport.

Major projects

  • Queensferry Crossing — opened 2017, replacing the Forth Road Bridge for most traffic.
  • Borders Railway — opened 2015, the longest new domestic railway in Britain for a century.
  • Dualling the A9 — long-term programme to upgrade the Perth–Inverness trunk road.
  • Levenmouth rail link — reopened stations at Leven and Cameron Bridge in 2024.

Ferry controversies

The agency has faced scrutiny over the procurement of two large dual-fuel ferries at Ferguson Marine (the Glen Sannox and Glen Rosa), delivered years late and far over budget, triggering a parliamentary inquiry into procurement practices. Island ferry reliability remains a politically charged issue in Scotland.
