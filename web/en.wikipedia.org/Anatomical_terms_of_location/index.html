Anatomical terms of location

Anatomical terms of location are standardised words used by anatomists, zoologists, and health professionals to describe precisely where structures lie in relation to other structures or to the body as a whole. Because the same word would otherwise shift meaning with posture, these terms are defined relative to the standard anatomical position: standing upright, facing forward, arms at the sides with palms facing forward.

Main directional terms

Anterior (ventral) describes the front of the body, while posterior (dorsal) describes the back. Superior (cranial) refers to a position above or higher than another part, and inferior (caudal) to a position below. Medial means nearer the midline of the body; lateral means farther from the midline. Structures may also be proximal, closer to the trunk or point of origin, or distal, farther away.

Terms for limbs and surfaces

On the limbs, palmar and plantar name the grasping surfaces of the hand and foot, opposed to the dorsal surfaces. Superficial and deep describe distance from the body surface, while visceral and parietal distinguish the coverings of organs from the walls of the cavities containing them.

Planes and axes

Three principal planes divide the body: the sagittal plane (left–right), the coronal or frontal plane (front–back), and the transverse or axial plane (top–bottom). Movements such as flexion, extension, abduction, and rotation are described about the axes perpendicular to these planes.

Use in other animals

In quadrupeds the terms dorsal and ventral align with the back and belly, and orientation may be described by rostral (toward the nose) and caudal (toward the tail). Comparative anatomy adapts the human scheme to each body plan.
