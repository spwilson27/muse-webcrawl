Hamstring

In human anatomy, a hamstring is any one of the three posterior thigh muscles between the hip and the knee: the semitendinosus and semimembranosus medially, and the biceps femoris laterally. The name derives from "ham", the back of the knee, and "string", referring to the prominent tendons felt there.

Structure and function

The hamstrings originate largely on the ischial tuberosity of the pelvis and cross both the hip and knee joints. They extend the hip and flex the knee, and are innervated mainly by the tibial division of the sciatic nerve (the short head of biceps femoris by the common fibular division). They are essential for walking, running, jumping and posture.

Injury

Hamstring strains — tears of the muscle or tendon — are among the most common injuries in sprinting and field sports. They typically occur during high-speed running and have high recurrence rates; prevention programmes emphasise eccentric strengthening such as Nordic hamstring exercises. Hamstring tendons are also used as grafts in anterior cruciate ligament reconstruction.
