Hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis

The hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis (HPG axis) is a neuroendocrine system in which the hypothalamus, pituitary gland, and gonads interact to regulate reproduction and sexual development. It controls the synthesis and release of gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH), the gonadotropins luteinizing hormone (LH) and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), and the gonadal sex steroids such as testosterone, estradiol, and progesterone.

Function

Neurons in the hypothalamus secrete GnRH in pulses into the hypophyseal portal circulation. GnRH stimulates the anterior pituitary to release LH and FSH, which act on the ovaries or testes to drive gametogenesis and steroid production. Sex steroids and inhibin feed back on the hypothalamus and pituitary to regulate the system, producing the menstrual cycle in females and supporting spermatogenesis and testosterone production in males.

Development

The axis is active in infancy (sometimes called "mini-puberty"), becomes quiescent during childhood, and is reactivated at puberty. Kisspeptin signaling is a key driver of the pubertal increase in GnRH secretion.

Suppression and clinical relevance

The axis can be suppressed by stress, severe energy deficit, and hyperprolactinemia, and by drugs such as GnRH analogues and hormonal contraceptives. Disorders of the axis cause hypogonadotropic hypogonadism, precocious puberty, and various forms of infertility.
