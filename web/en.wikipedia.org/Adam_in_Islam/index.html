Adam in Islam

Adam (Arabic: آدم, ʾĀdam) is believed to have been the first human being on Earth and the first prophet in Islam. Adam's role as the father of the human race is looked upon by Muslims with reverence. Muslims also refer to his wife, Ḥawwāʾ (Eve), as the "mother of mankind".

Quranic account

The Quran relates that God created Adam from clay, breathed life into him, and taught him "the names of all things", commanding the angels to prostrate before him. Iblis (Satan) refused out of pride and was expelled. Adam and Eve lived in Paradise but ate from the forbidden tree; unlike the Christian doctrine of original sin, Islam teaches that they repented and were forgiven, then sent down to Earth. Adam is regarded as the first prophet and, in later tradition, associated with constructing the Kaaba.

In tradition

Hadith and histories add details: Adam was of great stature, lived for about a thousand years, and his sons included Abel (Hābīl), Cain (Qābīl), and Seth (Shīth), the latter regarded as a prophet and Adam's successor.
