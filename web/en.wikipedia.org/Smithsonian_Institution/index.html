The Smithsonian Institution is a group of museums, education and research centres, the largest such complex in the world, created by the United States federal government to be an educational and research organisation devoted to public education, national service and scholarship in the arts, sciences and history. It is named after the British scientist James Smithson, whose unexpected bequest of £100,000 founded the institution.

History

Smithson, who never visited the United States, left his fortune to found an establishment "for the increase and diffusion of knowledge". After years of debate, Congress passed the act establishing the Smithsonian on 10 August 1846. The Institution is governed by a Board of Regents that includes the Chief Justice and Vice President of the United States. Its first secretary, physicist Joseph Henry, set its research character; the Smithsonian Institution Building, the "Castle", was completed in 1855.

Museums and research

The Smithsonian operates 21 museums, most in Washington, D.C. along the National Mall, including the National Museum of American History, the National Air and Space Museum, the National Museum of Natural History, the National Museum of African American History and Culture, and the National Zoo. Admission to most museums is free. Its research centres include the Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory and the Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute in Panama.
