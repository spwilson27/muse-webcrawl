Bengaluru (formerly Bangalore) is the capital and largest city of the southern Indian state of Karnataka. Sitting on the Deccan Plateau at over 900 metres elevation, it enjoys a moderate climate year-round. Bengaluru is India's leading technology hub — headquarters or major home to firms such as Infosys and Wipro, the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO), and thousands of startups — earning it the nickname "Silicon Valley of India".

History

The modern city was founded in 1537 by Kempe Gowda I, a chieftain under the Vijayanagara Empire, who built a mud fort and laid out the Pete (market) districts. The city later passed to the Marathas, Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan of Mysore, and, after the Fourth Anglo-Mysore War (1799), to British administration as a cantonment town alongside the older native city. A plague in 1898 spurred modern sanitation and new planned extensions. After Indian independence, Bengaluru became capital of Mysore State (later Karnataka), and from the 1980s its public-sector electronics and aerospace industries gave way to a booming software and services sector, centred on Electronic City and Whitefield. The city was officially renamed Bengaluru in 2014.

Geography and climate

Bengaluru occupies a ridge between the Arkavathi and Dakshina Pinakini (South Pennar) basins; unusually for a large Indian city it has no major perennial river and depends on water pumped from the Kaveri, over 100 km away. Its many man-made lakes, parks (Lalbagh, Cubbon Park) and avenues of rain trees underpin its older "Garden City" identity, though rapid growth has strained lakes, traffic and water supplies.

Economy and transport

Besides IT — which makes the city one of India's largest export earners — Bengaluru hosts aerospace (HAL), biotechnology, and research institutions including the Indian Institute of Science (founded 1909). Kempegowda International Airport is among India's busiest; the Namma Metro, opened in 2011, is expanding across the city.
