A Munro (listen) is a mountain in Scotland with a height over 3,000 feet (914.4 metres) that is on the Scottish Mountaineering Club's (SMC) list of such mountains. There is no explicit prominence threshold; the SMC designates as separate Munros those peaks it considers to be separate mountains, while subsidiary summits over 3,000 feet are classed as Munro Tops.

History

The list was first compiled by Sir Hugh Munro, who published his tables in 1891. The list has been revised several times, most notably as surveying improved; the current list contains 282 Munros and 226 Munro Tops. The highest Munro is Ben Nevis.

Munro bagging

Completing a round of all the Munros, known as "Munro bagging", is a popular challenge among hillwalkers. Those who complete the list are called Munroists or Compleatists. The first recorded completion was by the Reverend A. E. Robertson in 1901. Rounds have since been completed in winter, continuously, and in record times of under 32 days.

Related lists

Similar classifications elsewhere in Britain and Ireland include Corbetts (Scottish peaks between 2,500 and 3,000 feet), Grahams, Marilyns, and the Furths — mountains in England, Wales and Ireland over 3,000 feet.
