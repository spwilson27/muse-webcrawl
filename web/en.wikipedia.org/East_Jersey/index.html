East Jersey

Period: 1674–1702
Capital: Perth Amboy
Status: Proprietary colony of England
Today part of: New Jersey, United States

The Province of East Jersey, together with the Province of West Jersey, was a jointly owned colony of the Kingdom of England that was subsequently a jointly owned colony of the Kingdom of Great Britain. The two provinces were separated by the Keith Line and were merged in 1702 to form the Province of New Jersey.

History

After the English conquest of New Netherland in 1664, the Duke of York granted New Jersey to Lord Berkeley and Sir George Carteret. In 1676 the Quintipartite Deed divided the colony into East Jersey, centred on Carteret's interest, and West Jersey. East Jersey was later sold to a group of proprietors, expanded to twenty-four, many of them Scots; the Scottish proprietors promoted substantial Scottish settlement, and Perth Amboy became the capital in 1686.

Government

The province was governed by a governor and council appointed by the proprietors, together with an elected assembly. Disputes over land titles and governance, and the proprietors' difficulties administering the colony, led both Jerseys to surrender their rights of government to the Crown in 1702, after which they were united as a royal colony under the governor of New York until New Jersey received its own royal governor in 1738.
