Fosaprepitant, sold under the brand name Emend (for injection) among others, is an antiemetic medication used to prevent nausea and vomiting caused by chemotherapy. It is a prodrug of aprepitant: after intravenous administration it is rapidly converted in the body to aprepitant, which blocks the neurokinin-1 (NK1) receptor for substance P in the brain's vomiting centre.

Medical use

Fosaprepitant is used, in combination with other antiemetics such as a 5-HT3 antagonist (for example ondansetron) and a corticosteroid (such as dexamethasone), for the prevention of acute and delayed nausea and vomiting associated with highly and moderately emetogenic cancer chemotherapy, including high-dose cisplatin regimens. It is given as a single intravenous infusion on the first day of chemotherapy, which is convenient for patients unable to swallow capsules.

Pharmacology

Fosaprepitant is the phosphorylated prodrug of aprepitant, designed to be water-soluble for intravenous use. Plasma phosphatases convert it to aprepitant within about 30 minutes. Aprepitant is metabolised mainly by the liver enzyme CYP3A4 and moderately inhibits that enzyme, so doses of co-administered drugs metabolised by CYP3A4—including dexamethasone and some chemotherapy agents—may need adjustment.

Side effects

Common side effects include fatigue, diarrhoea, hiccups, and infusion-site reactions such as pain, redness, and thrombophlebitis, particularly when given with vesicant chemotherapy through a peripheral vein. Hypersensitivity reactions, including rare anaphylaxis, have been reported.
