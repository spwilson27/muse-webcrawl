AIM-9 Sidewinder

The AIM-9 Sidewinder is a short-range air-to-air missile developed by the United States Navy at China Lake, California. Using infrared homing to track the heat of an aircraft's engines, it entered service in 1956 and became the most widely used air-to-air missile in the world, carried by most Western fighter aircraft.

Design and development

The missile takes its name from the sidewinder rattlesnake, which senses its prey's heat. Early versions could engage only from behind, where engine exhaust was hottest, but later models — notably the AIM-9L of the late 1970s — gained all-aspect capability. The current AIM-9X uses an imaging infrared seeker, thrust vectoring, and high off-boresight cueing for extreme manoeuvrability. The missile is about 2.85 m long, weighs roughly 85 kg, and reaches speeds above Mach 2.5.

Combat history

The Sidewinder's first combat use was in 1958 during the Second Taiwan Strait Crisis. It has since been used in Vietnam, the Falklands War, the Gulf War, and many other conflicts, with hundreds of aircraft kills attributed to it. More than 110,000 missiles have been produced for over 40 nations.
