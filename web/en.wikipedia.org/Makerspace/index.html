Makerspace

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A makerspace workshop with 3D printers and workbenches]

A makerspace (also referred to as a hackerspace or hacklab) is a community-operated, often nonprofit workspace where people with common interests—typically in computers, machining, technology, science, digital art or electronic art—can meet, socialize and collaborate. Makerspaces are closely related to the maker movement and frequently provide shared access to tools such as 3D printers, laser cutters, CNC machines, soldering stations and sewing equipment.

History

The concept descends from the hackerspaces that emerged in Germany in the 1990s, such as c-base in Berlin. The term "makerspace" gained popularity in the 2000s with the rise of Make magazine (founded 2005) and Maker Faire. Libraries, schools and universities subsequently adopted makerspaces as venues for hands-on STEM learning.

Activities

Typical activities include electronics prototyping, robotics, woodworking, metalworking, coding instruction, and workshops for beginners. Many spaces operate on membership or pay-per-use models and host open nights for the public.

Education

Educational makerspaces promote learning through constructionism—the idea that people learn effectively by building public artefacts—and are used to teach design thinking, engineering and digital literacy.
