Beverley McLachlin

Beverley Marian McLachlin (born 7 September 1943) is a Canadian jurist who served as the 17th chief justice of Canada from 2000 to 2017. She was the first woman and the longest-serving chief justice in Canadian history, sitting on the Supreme Court of Canada for over 28 years.

Career

Born in Pincher Creek, Alberta, McLachlin studied at the University of Alberta and taught law at the University of British Columbia before her appointment to the bench in 1981. She rose through the British Columbia courts to become Chief Justice of the Supreme Court of British Columbia in 1988, was appointed to the Supreme Court of Canada in 1989, and became Chief Justice on 7 January 2000.

Tenure and legacy

As Chief Justice, McLachlin presided over major Charter decisions on same-sex marriage, assisted dying (Carter v Canada), prostitution laws (Bedford), and Aboriginal title (Tsilhqot'in Nation). She was known for building consensus and for frequent unanimous judgments. After retiring in December 2017, she was appointed a non-permanent judge of the Hong Kong Court of Final Appeal in 2018; she published a memoir in 2019 and a novel, Full Disclosure, in 2018.
