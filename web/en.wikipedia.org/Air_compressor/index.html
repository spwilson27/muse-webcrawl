Air compressor

An air compressor is a pneumatic device that converts power (using an electric motor, diesel or gasoline engine, etc.) into potential energy stored in pressurized air (i.e., compressed air). By one of several methods, an air compressor forces more and more air into a storage tank, increasing the pressure. When the tank's pressure reaches its engineered upper limit, the air compressor shuts off. The compressed air is then used, on demand, to power tools and equipment.

Types

Compressors may be classified by the method used to compress air. Positive displacement compressors include reciprocating piston compressors, rotary screw compressors, rotary vane compressors and scroll compressors. Dynamic compressors, such as centrifugal and axial compressors, use rotating impellers to impart velocity that is converted to pressure. Small portable units serve workshops and garages, while large industrial compressors supply factories and process plants.

Applications

Compressed air powers pneumatic tools, paint sprayers, sandblasters, tire inflation, air brakes on vehicles, refrigeration compressors, diving-air supplies, and industrial automation. Compressors are rated by pressure (in bar or psi) and free air delivery or flow.
