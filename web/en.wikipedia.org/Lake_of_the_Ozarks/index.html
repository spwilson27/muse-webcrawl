The Lake of the Ozarks is a large reservoir in central Missouri, created by impounding the Osage River with Bagnell Dam, completed in 1931. Its serpentine shape — often nicknamed "The Magic Dragon" — gives it an exceptionally long shoreline and makes it one of the largest man-made lakes in the United States.

History

Construction of Bagnell Dam by the Union Electric Company began in 1929, providing a major source of employment during the Great Depression as well as hydroelectric power for St. Louis. The project flooded numerous towns and farmland, and it created a landmark of Depression-era engineering.

Recreation

The lake is one of the Midwest's premier recreation destinations, known for boating, fishing for bass and crappie, and lakeside resorts. Communities such as Osage Beach, Lake Ozark, and Sunrise Beach line its arms, and Lake of the Ozarks State Park, Missouri's largest state park, preserves undeveloped shoreline and the Ozark Caverns.
