Enhanced Fujita scale

The Enhanced Fujita scale, usually abbreviated EF scale, rates the intensity of tornadoes in the United States and Canada by the damage they cause. It replaced the original Fujita scale for operational use in the United States in 2007 and in Canada in 2013.

Scale and damage indicators

Ratings run from EF0, for light damage, to EF5, for incredible damage. Surveyors examine damage indicators such as houses, barns, schools, trees, and mobile homes, then consider degrees of damage for each indicator. Estimated wind ranges are associated with the ratings, with EF5 corresponding to winds greater than 200 miles per hour.

Development

The scale was developed by meteorologists and engineers after experience with the Fujita scale showed that its wind estimates were often too high for the damage observed. The enhanced version kept the familiar 0-to-5 categories while tying them more closely to documented structural damage.

Use in warnings and records

After a tornado, National Weather Service teams survey the path and assign the highest supported rating. The rating describes damage at the worst affected locations, not the size of the tornado or its duration. Related topics include tornadoes and the original Fujita scale.
