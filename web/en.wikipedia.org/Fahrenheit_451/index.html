Fahrenheit 451

Author: Ray Bradbury
Published: 19 October 1953
Publisher: Ballantine Books
Genre: Dystopian fiction

Fahrenheit 451 is a 1953 dystopian novel by American writer Ray Bradbury. It presents a future American society where books are outlawed and "firemen" burn any that are found. The title refers to the supposed temperature at which book paper catches fire and burns. The novel grew out of Bradbury's short story "The Fireman", published in 1951.

Plot

Guy Montag is a fireman who burns books for a living. Encounters with his eccentric young neighbour Clarisse McClellan and a woman who chooses to burn with her books lead him to question his life. After stealing and reading books, Montag is denounced — in part by his wife Mildred — and ordered by Captain Beatty to burn his own house. Montag kills Beatty, flees the city, and joins a group of exiled book-lovers who memorize books to preserve them. The city is then destroyed in a nuclear war, and the exiles return to rebuild.

Themes

The novel explores censorship, state control, and the displacement of books by mass entertainment — Bradbury later insisted its central subject was television's destruction of interest in literature rather than government censorship per se. Ironically, the book itself has repeatedly been subject to censorship and expurgation challenges in schools. It won the Retro Hugo Award for Best Novel and has been adapted for film (1966, 2018), theatre and radio.
