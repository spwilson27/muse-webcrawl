Fabius Maximus

Quintus Fabius Maximus Verrucosus (c. 280 – 203 BC), surnamed Cunctator ("the Delayer"), was a Roman statesman and general of the third century BC. As consul and dictator during the early stages of the Second Punic War, he devised the Fabian strategy of avoiding pitched battle with Hannibal while harassing his supply lines.

Second Punic War

Fabius was appointed dictator after the Roman disaster at Lake Trasimene in 217 BC. Believing the Roman army unable to defeat Hannibal in open battle, he pursued a war of attrition. Though unpopular at first — his cognomen Cunctator was initially a term of abuse — his strategy was vindicated and he was later honoured as the shield of Rome.

Legacy

The Fabian strategy gave its name to the modern term for gradualist tactics, and to the British Fabian Society, which adopted his name and motto of gradual social reform.
