Nikol Vovaevich Pashinyan (born 1 June 1975) is an Armenian politician and former journalist serving as the prime minister of Armenia since 8 May 2018. He came to power leading the 2018 Armenian Velvet Revolution, a wave of peaceful protests that forced the resignation of Serzh Sargsyan and ended the long dominance of the Republican Party.

Early life and journalism

Pashinyan was born in Ijevan and studied journalism at Yerevan State University. He edited the opposition newspaper Haykakan Zhamanak and became one of the most prominent critics of Armenia's post-Soviet governments. After the disputed 2008 presidential election and the ensuing crackdown, he went into hiding, later surrendered, and was imprisoned before being released under a 2011 amnesty. He was elected to the National Assembly in 2012.

Velvet Revolution and premiership

In April 2018 Pashinyan led a protest march from Gyumri to Yerevan against Sargsyan's move from the presidency to the premiership. Mass civil disobedience brought the country to a standstill; Sargsyan resigned, and parliament elected Pashinyan prime minister. His My Step Alliance won a landslide in snap elections in December 2018. His government pursued anti-corruption and economic reforms.

Armenia's defeat in the 2020 Nagorno-Karabakh war against Azerbaijan triggered a major political crisis and calls for his resignation, but Pashinyan's Civil Contract party won the snap parliamentary election of June 2021. Following Azerbaijan's September 2023 offensive, Nagorno-Karabakh's Armenian population fled to Armenia and the unrecognized republic was dissolved; Pashinyan has since pursued a peace agreement with Azerbaijan and a reorientation of Armenia's foreign policy away from exclusive reliance on Russia.
