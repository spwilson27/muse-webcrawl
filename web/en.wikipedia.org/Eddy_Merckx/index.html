Édouard Louis Joseph, Baron Merckx (born 17 June 1945), better known as Eddy Merckx, is a Belgian former professional road and track bicycle racer, widely regarded as the most successful and greatest rider in the history of competitive cycling. His eleven Grand Tour victories, twenty-eight classic race wins and three World Championships head a record of achievement unmatched in the sport. His insatiable appetite for victory earned him the nickname "The Cannibal".

Career

Merckx turned professional in 1965 and won the first of his five Tour de France titles in 1969, a race he also won in 1970, 1971, 1972 and 1974. He won the Giro d'Italia five times (1968, 1970, 1972, 1973, 1974) and the Vuelta a España once, in 1973, making him one of only a handful of riders to have won all three Grand Tours. In 1974 he became one of the few riders to achieve the Triple Crown of Cycling, winning the Giro, the Tour and the World Championship road race in a single season.

Merckx is the only rider to have won all five of cycling's Monuments multiple times: Milan–San Remo (seven times), Paris–Roubaix (three), Liège–Bastogne–Liège (five), the Tour of Flanders (two) and the Giro di Lombardia (two). In 1972 he set a world hour record of 49.431 km in Mexico City. He won a total of 525 races during his career.

Riding style and rivalry

Unlike many champions who conserved their strength for the biggest objectives, Merckx raced to win throughout the season, a dominance that demoralised opponents and provoked resentment as well as admiration. His principal rivals included Roger De Vlaeminck, Freddy Maertens and Felice Gimondi. His career was also marked by three positive doping tests for stimulants, common in the era, which he disputed; he was expelled from the 1969 Giro d'Italia while leading, in circumstances that remain controversial.

Later life

Merckx retired in 1978 and founded a successful bicycle company. He was made a baron by King Albert II of Belgium in 1996. His son Axel Merckx also became a professional cyclist. In 2019 a Brussels metro station and numerous monuments honoured his legacy, and he remains the standard against whom all-round cyclists are measured.
