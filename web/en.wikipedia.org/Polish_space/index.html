Polish space

In mathematics, a Polish space is a separable completely metrizable topological space — that is, a space homeomorphic to a complete metric space that has a countable dense subset. The name honours the Polish mathematicians, including Kazimierz Kuratowski and Wacław Sierpiński, who developed the theory.

Examples

Examples include the real line, Euclidean spaces, the Cantor set, the Baire space (the space of irrational numbers with its usual topology), and every compact metric space. Every Polish space has the cardinality of the continuum or is countable.

Importance

Polish spaces are the standard setting of descriptive set theory, where they provide the natural framework for studying Borel and analytic sets, and they underpin much of modern probability theory, since the spaces of measures on them have convenient topological structure. A non-empty Polish space with no isolated points is homeomorphic to the Cantor set if it is zero-dimensional, and every Polish space is a continuous image of the Baire space.
