Field Marshal Edmund Henry Hynman Allenby, 1st Viscount Allenby (23 April 1861 – 14 May 1936) was a British soldier and administrator. He fought in the Second Boer War and in the First World War, in which he led the British Empire's Egyptian Expeditionary Force during the Sinai and Palestine campaign against the Ottoman Empire.

First World War

After commanding the Third Army at Arras on the Western Front, Allenby took command in Egypt in June 1917. He reorganised the force, won the Third Battle of Gaza with the flanking stroke at Beersheba, and entered Jerusalem on foot on 11 December 1917 — the first Christian conqueror of the city in centuries, and a symbolic Christmas gift to war-weary Britain. In September 1918 his offensive at Megiddo, using infantry, cavalry, aircraft, and deception (with Arab forces under Faisal and T. E. Lawrence on his flank), destroyed three Ottoman armies and led to the capture of Damascus and the Armistice of Mudros.

Egypt and later life

As High Commissioner of Egypt (1919–1925), Allenby handled nationalist unrest and pressed London to grant Egypt nominal independence in 1922. He was raised to the peerage as Viscount Allenby of Megiddo and Felixstowe. He retired in 1925 and died in London in 1936.
