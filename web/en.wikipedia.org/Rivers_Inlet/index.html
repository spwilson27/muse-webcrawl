Rivers Inlet is a fjord in the Central Coast region of British Columbia, Canada, opening into Fitz Hugh Sound. It extends about 45 kilometres (28 mi) inland to the mouth of the Wannock River, which drains Owikeno Lake. The inlet lies within the traditional territory of the Wuikinuxv (Owikeno) Nation, whose main community is at Rivers Inlet village.

History

The inlet was named by Captain George Vancouver in 1793 for George Pitt, 1st Baron Rivers. In the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries it supported a major salmon cannery industry, with canneries around Owikeno Lake and the inlet employing many Indigenous and immigrant workers.

Geography and fishing

The Rivers Inlet sockeye salmon run was historically one of the largest in British Columbia, though it declined sharply in the late twentieth century. Today the area is known for sport fishing lodges and for large Chinook (king) salmon, alongside efforts to rebuild sockeye stocks.
