Agroecology

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Agroecology is an academic discipline and set of practices that applies ecological principles to the design and management of agricultural systems, as well as a social movement advocating for sustainable and equitable food systems. It seeks to optimise interactions between plants, animals, humans and the environment while taking into account social aspects of food production.

Principles

The Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) identifies ten elements of agroecology, including diversity, co-creation and sharing of knowledge, synergies, efficiency, recycling, resilience, human and social values, culture and food traditions, responsible governance, and circular and solidarity economy. Common practices include intercropping and polyculture, agroforestry, crop–livestock integration, composting and biological pest control, cover crops and minimal reliance on synthetic inputs.

History

The term appeared in scientific literature in the late 1920s and 1930s, initially for the ecology of agricultural systems. In the 1970s–1990s it broadened from the field scale to the whole food system, drawing on the traditional knowledge of peasant and indigenous farmers, particularly in Latin America, where movements such as La Via Campesina linked agroecology to food sovereignty.

Agroecology as science, practice and movement

Scholars commonly describe agroecology as simultaneously a science (applying ecology to agriculture), a set of farming practices, and a social movement for transforming food systems. Critics argue about scalability and yields relative to conventional intensive farming, while supporters point to benefits for resilience, biodiversity, soil health and rural livelihoods.
