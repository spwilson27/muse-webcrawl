Southern metal

Southern metal
Stylistic originsHeavy metal, southern rock, blues, sludge metal, hardcore punk
Cultural origins1980s–1990s, Southern United States (esp. New Orleans)
Key actsPantera, Down, Corrosion of Conformity, Crowbar, Eyehategod, Black Label Society

Southern metal is a fusion genre of heavy metal that combines metal's aggression with the blues-based grooves, slide guitar and swagger of Southern rock. It is closely associated with the New Orleans scene and with bands that blend sludge metal, hardcore punk and classic rock influences.

Characteristics

Southern metal typically features thick, down-tuned guitar riffs, bluesy lead playing, pounding mid-tempo grooves and gruff, shouted vocals. Lyrics and imagery often draw on Southern working-class themes, rebellion and regional identity, and the music pays homage to acts such as Lynyrd Skynyrd, ZZ Top and Black Sabbath.

History

The style crystallised in the early 1990s. Pantera, from Texas, incorporated southern grooves into their influential groove metal sound. In New Orleans, bands like Eyehategod and Crowbar pioneered sludge metal, while supergroup Down — featuring members of Pantera, Corrosion of Conformity and Crowbar — issued the landmark album NOLA in 1995. Corrosion of Conformity's later work, and bands such as Black Label Society, Alabama Thunderpussy and Maylene and the Sons of Disaster, carried the genre forward.
