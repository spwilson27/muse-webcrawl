Muhammad Kudarat

Muhammad Dipatuan Kudarat (1581–1671), often called Sultan Kudarat, was the seventh sultan of the Maguindanao Sultanate in Mindanao, in the southern Philippines, reigning from about 1619 to 1671. He is regarded as one of the most powerful and successful Muslim rulers in Philippine history and a national hero for his resistance to Spanish colonisation.

The Philippine province of Sultan Kudarat and several municipalities are named after him.

Reign and resistance

Kudarat consolidated the Maguindanao sultanate at his capital in Lamitan/Bacat (near present-day Cotabato), building alliances with the sultanates of Sulu and Buayan and with the Dutch, while raiding Spanish-held areas in the Visayas and Luzon. In 1637 the Spanish conquistador Sebastián Hurtado de Corcuera defeated his forces and temporarily occupied Lamitan, but Kudarat escaped to the interior, resumed resistance, and eventually forced Spain to withdraw.

In 1645 he concluded a treaty with the Spanish that effectively recognised Maguindanao's independence and defined spheres of control; despite renewed fighting in the 1650s, the Spanish never conquered Maguindanao.

Legacy

Sultan Kudarat is celebrated for preserving the sovereignty and Islamic identity of the Moro peoples. He died in 1671 and was succeeded by his son, Dundang Tidulay.
