Chymosin

Chymosin or rennin is a protease enzyme found in rennet, produced in the stomachs of ruminant animals. It is an aspartic protease that curdles milk by cleaving κ-casein, allowing milk to be retained in the stomach of young animals so it can be properly digested.

Action

Chymosin is secreted as the inactive precursor prochymosin, which is activated in the acidic environment of the stomach. The enzyme cleaves a single specific bond in κ-casein (between residues 105 and 106 in the bovine protein), destabilising the casein micelles so that the milk proteins aggregate and form a curd. Because of this highly specific action, chymosin is the key agent in cheesemaking: it is the principal enzyme in calf rennet.

Commercial production

Traditionally obtained from the stomach lining (abomasum) of suckling calves, chymosin is now mostly produced by fermentation: fermentation-produced chymosin (FPC), made by fungi such as Aspergillus niger or the yeast Kluyveromyces lactis engineered to carry the bovine (or camel) chymosin gene, accounts for a large share of the cheese made in many countries. Camel chymosin is favoured for some applications because of its higher clotting activity relative to general proteolysis. Chymosin was one of the first genetically engineered enzymes approved for use in food.
