Alamanni

The Alamanni (also spelled Alemanni) were a confederation of Germanic tribes on the upper Rhine River, first mentioned by Roman authors in the 3rd century CE. Their name is commonly interpreted as "all men," reflecting their formation from multiple groups.

History

The Alamanni appeared on Rome's Rhine frontier in the 3rd century, invading the Agri Decumates region and raiding into Gaul and Italy; they were famously defeated by the emperor Julian at the Battle of Strasbourg in 357 CE. After the collapse of Roman control on the Rhine in the 5th century, they expanded into what is now southwestern Germany, Alsace, and northern Switzerland — the region known as Alemannia.

In 496 CE (or 506, by some accounts) they were defeated by the Frankish king Clovis I at the Battle of Tolbiac and incorporated into the Frankish realm, thereafter forming the stem duchy of Alemannia (later Swabia) within the Frankish and Holy Roman empires. The Alamanni gradually converted to Christianity and received a written law code, the Lex Alamannorum, in the early medieval period.

Legacy

The Alemannic dialects of German are still spoken in Baden-Württemberg, Alsace, Switzerland, Liechtenstein, and Austria's Vorarlberg. The name survives in the French word for Germany (Allemagne), Spanish (Alemania), Portuguese (Alemanha), and Turkish (Almanya).
