Monotone subsequence

In mathematics, a monotone subsequence is a subsequence of a sequence of real numbers that is either non-decreasing or non-increasing. A fundamental result states that every infinite sequence of real numbers contains an infinite monotone subsequence — a fact often proved by considering "peak" indices.

Erdős–Szekeres theorem

The finite form, proved by Erdős and Szekeres in 1935, states that any sequence of (r−1)(s−1)+1 distinct real numbers contains a monotone increasing subsequence of length r or a monotone decreasing subsequence of length s.

Applications

The result underlies the Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem — every bounded sequence has a convergent subsequence, obtained by first extracting a monotone subsequence — and appears throughout combinatorics and Ramsey theory.
