Atom transfer radical polymerization

Atom transfer radical polymerization (ATRP)
Field: Polymer chemistry
Introduced: 1995 (Wang & Matyjaszewski; Sawamoto group)
Type: Reversible-deactivation radical polymerization

Atom transfer radical polymerization (ATRP) is a method of controlled ("living") radical polymerization introduced independently in 1995 by the groups of Krzysztof Matyjaszewski and Mitsuo Sawamoto. It allows polymers to be synthesized with predetermined molecular weights, narrow molecular weight distributions, and complex architectures such as block copolymers, stars, and brushes.

Mechanism

ATRP rests on a dynamic equilibrium between dormant alkyl halide chain ends and a very low concentration of active radicals. A transition-metal catalyst, classically a copper(I) complex with an amine ligand, abstracts the halogen atom from the dormant chain, generating a radical and a copper(II) deactivator; rapid deactivation returns most chains to the dormant state. Because radicals are scarce and short-lived, termination is suppressed while chains grow steadily and uniformly.

Variants and applications

Variants such as ARGET, ICAR, and electrochemically mediated ATRP (eATRP) reduce catalyst loadings to parts per million and improve tolerance of oxygen. ATRP has been used for coatings, adhesives, biomaterials, and surface modification, and it is one of the most widely studied controlled polymerization techniques.
