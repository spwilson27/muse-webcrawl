René Goscinny

Goscinny in 1971, at the height of his career in Franco-Belgian comics.

René Goscinny (French: [ʁəne ɡosini]; 14 August 1926 – 5 November 1977) was a French comic editor and writer, who created the Asterix comic book series with illustrator Albert Uderzo. Raised largely in Buenos Aires, Argentina, he worked in New York in the late 1940s and early 1950s, where he was associated with the early staff of Mad magazine, before returning to France and becoming one of the defining figures of Franco-Belgian comics.

Career

With Jean-Michel Charlier, Goscinny co-founded the magazine Pilote in 1959, in which Asterix the Gaul first appeared. The series, about a village of indomitable Gauls resisting Roman occupation, became one of the best-selling comics of all time. Goscinny also wrote the cowboy series Lucky Luke for Morris from 1955, the adventures of the Ottoman courtier Iznogoud with Jean Tabary, and, with illustrator Jean-Jacques Sempé, the beloved children's books about Le Petit Nicolas.

Legacy

Goscinny died suddenly of a heart attack during a stress test in 1977, aged 51. His scripts are celebrated for their wordplay, puns, and satire of contemporary French life transposed into historical settings; his name has become nearly synonymous with the golden age of French comics writing, and the René Goscinny Prize is awarded annually to comics writers.
