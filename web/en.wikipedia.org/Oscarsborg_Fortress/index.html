Oscarsborg Fortress

LocationDrøbak Sound, Oslofjord, Norway
Built1846–1855
Named forKing Oscar I
Famous actionSinking of the cruiser Blücher, 9 April 1940

Oscarsborg Fortress is a coastal fortress in the Oslofjord near Drøbak, Norway, built on two small islands (Nordre and Søndre Kaholmen) guarding the narrow Drøbak Sound, the innermost approach to Oslo. Construction took place between 1846 and 1855, and the fortress was named after King Oscar I, who visited in 1855.

The sinking of the Blücher

Oscarsborg is famous for the action of 9 April 1940, the first day of the German invasion of Norway. Under Colonel Birger Eriksen, the fortress's elderly armament — 28 cm Krupp guns of 1893 named Moses, Aron and Josva, and a hidden torpedo battery of 1901-vintage torpedoes — engaged the German heavy cruiser Blücher at close range in the dark. Hit by shells and two torpedoes, Blücher sank with heavy loss of life. The delay allowed the King, government and gold reserves to escape Oslo. Eriksen's order — "Either I will be decorated, or I will be court-martialled. Fire!" — is a celebrated episode in Norwegian history.

Later history

The fortress operated as a coastal artillery installation and training school through the Cold War and was decommissioned as a defensive site in the 1990s–2000s. Today it is a museum, hotel and cultural venue, accessible by ferry from Drøbak, and stages an annual opera and events; the wreck of the Blücher remains on the fjord floor as a war grave.
