John DeLorean

BornJanuary 6, 1925, Detroit, Michigan, U.S.
DiedMarch 19, 2005 (aged 80), Summit, New Jersey, U.S.
OccupationAutomotive engineer and executive
Known forPontiac GTO; DeLorean Motor Company; DMC DeLorean

John Zachary DeLorean (January 6, 1925 – March 19, 2005) was an American engineer, inventor and executive in the U.S. automobile industry. He is best known for founding the DeLorean Motor Company, whose DMC DeLorean sports car — with its stainless-steel body and gull-wing doors — achieved lasting fame as the time machine in the Back to the Future film trilogy.

General Motors career

DeLorean earned engineering and business degrees in Detroit and joined Packard before moving to General Motors in 1956. At Pontiac he rose rapidly, overseeing the development of the Pontiac GTO, widely regarded as the first muscle car, and became the youngest division head in GM history. He later headed Chevrolet and was tipped as a future GM president, but his flamboyant lifestyle and clashes with management led to his resignation in 1973.

DeLorean Motor Company

In 1975 DeLorean founded his own company and, with substantial investment from the British government, built a factory at Dunmurry in Northern Ireland. The DMC DeLorean entered production in 1981 but suffered from quality problems, a weak engine and a high price. The company collapsed into receivership in 1982. In October 1982 DeLorean was arrested in Los Angeles on charges of trafficking cocaine to raise funds; he was acquitted in 1984 on the grounds of entrapment by federal agents.

Later life

DeLorean spent his later years fighting legal battles and promoting new ventures, none of which matched his earlier fame. He died in 2005 from complications of a stroke.
