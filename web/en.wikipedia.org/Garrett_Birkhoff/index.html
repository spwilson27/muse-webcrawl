Garrett Birkhoff (January 19, 1911 – November 22, 1996) was an American mathematician best known for his work in lattice theory. He was the son of the mathematician George David Birkhoff, with whom he is often confused.

Life and career

Birkhoff studied at Harvard University, graduating in 1932, and pursued further study at Cambridge University. After a fellowship, he joined the Harvard faculty in 1936 and spent his entire career there, becoming George Putnam Professor of Pure and Applied Mathematics. During and after the Second World War he worked on applied problems, including hydrodynamics, and became an early advocate of the use of computers in numerical computation.

Work

His book Lattice Theory (1940) established lattice theory as a major branch of abstract algebra and remained a standard reference through successive editions. He proved the Birkhoff representation theorem, which characterises finite distributive lattices, and the Birkhoff–von Neumann theorem on doubly stochastic matrices. With Saunders Mac Lane, he wrote A Survey of Modern Algebra (1941), an influential undergraduate textbook that introduced generations of students to modern algebra, and later Algebra (1967). Birkhoff also made contributions to universal algebra — including the HSP theorem on varieties — to fluid dynamics (Hydrodynamics: A Study in Logic, Fact, and Similitude, 1950), and to numerical methods for partial differential equations.
