Weak topology

In mathematics, the weak topology on a vector space is the coarsest topology that makes all continuous linear functionals continuous. For a normed space X, it is the initial topology induced by the continuous dual space X*; a net converges weakly precisely when every continuous linear functional converges on it.

Properties

The weak topology is generally strictly weaker (coarser) than the norm topology in infinite dimensions, so it has fewer open sets and more convergent nets — and crucially, more compact sets. The Banach–Alaoglu theorem states that the closed unit ball of a dual space is compact in the related weak-* topology, a cornerstone result in functional analysis. By Mazur's theorem, a convex set is closed in the norm topology if and only if it is weakly closed.

Weak convergence

A sequence that converges in the weak topology but not in norm is said to converge weakly. Weak convergence is central to the calculus of variations and partial differential equations, where minimizing sequences are often shown to converge weakly to solutions.
