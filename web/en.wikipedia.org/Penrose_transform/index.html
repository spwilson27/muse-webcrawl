Penrose transform

In theoretical physics and mathematics, the Penrose transform, introduced by Roger Penrose, is a correspondence between fields on (complexified, compactified) Minkowski spacetime and cohomology classes on twistor space. It is a central tool of twistor theory.

Description

Under the twistor correspondence, each point of twistor space corresponds to a light ray (more precisely, an alpha-plane) in complexified Minkowski space, and vice versa. The Penrose transform maps a massless free field of helicity h satisfying the wave (or Maxwell or linearised Einstein) equations on spacetime to an element of the first sheaf cohomology group of twistor space with values in a suitable line bundle, whose power depends on the helicity. Differentiating representatives on twistor space reproduces the field equations automatically, making the transform especially useful in the study of self-dual gauge fields and integrable systems, and in the description of scattering amplitudes.
