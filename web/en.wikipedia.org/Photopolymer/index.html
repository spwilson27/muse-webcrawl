Photopolymer

A photopolymer or light-activated resin is a polymer that changes its properties when exposed to light, usually ultraviolet or visible light within a specific region of the electromagnetic spectrum. These changes are often manifested in the curing, hardening or cross-linking of a liquid resin into a solid material when it is exposed to radiation of suitable wavelength in the presence of a photoinitiator.

Mechanism

Photopolymerisation begins when photoinitiator molecules absorb photons and generate reactive species such as free radicals or cations. These species attack monomer and oligomer double bonds, starting chain reactions that rapidly link the liquid into a three-dimensional network. The reaction typically stops or slows greatly once the light is removed, giving fine spatial control over where solid material forms.

Applications

Photopolymers are widely used in dentistry (composite fillings and adhesives), printing (photopolymer plates for flexography), coatings and inks, adhesives, and microelectronics (photoresists). They are central to additive manufacturing processes such as stereolithography and digital light processing, in which an object is built layer by layer by selectively curing resin.
