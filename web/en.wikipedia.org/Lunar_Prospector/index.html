Lunar Prospector

Lunar Prospector
Operator: NASA
Launch: 7 January 1998
End of mission: 31 July 1999 (impact)
Target: The Moon

Lunar Prospector was the third mission selected by NASA for full development and construction as part of the Discovery Program. The small spin-stabilised orbiter mapped the Moon's surface composition, magnetic and gravity fields, and searched for polar water ice.

Mission

Launched aboard an Athena II rocket from Cape Canaveral on 7 January 1998, the spacecraft entered lunar orbit four days later and began a year-long mapping mission from a polar orbit about 100 km above the surface, later extended at lower altitude.

Findings

Its neutron spectrometer detected enhanced hydrogen at the lunar poles, interpreted as evidence for water ice in permanently shadowed craters. The mission ended on 31 July 1999 with a controlled impact into Shoemaker crater near the south pole, an experiment that failed to detect the expected plume of water.

Legacy

Lunar Prospector produced the first global maps of the Moon's elemental composition and refined models of the lunar crust and core.
