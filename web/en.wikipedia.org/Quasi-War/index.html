Quasi-War
Date7 July 1798 – 30 September 1800
LocationAtlantic Ocean, Caribbean Sea, Mediterranean
BelligerentsUnited States (with British support) vs French Republic
ResultConvention of 1800; end of French seizures and of the Franco-American alliance of 1778

The Quasi-War was an undeclared war fought from 1798 to 1800 between the United States and the French Republic, primarily at sea in the Atlantic and Caribbean. It arose after France, angered by the Jay Treaty between the US and Britain and by American refusal to repay revolutionary-era debts to the French Republic, authorised privateers to seize American merchant ships, capturing hundreds of them.

Course of the conflict

In response, President John Adams rebuilt the United States Navy — creating the Department of the Navy in 1798 — and Congress authorised the capture of French armed vessels. American frigates such as USS Constellation, which defeated L'Insurgente in February 1799 and fought La Vengeance in 1800, and a fleet of converted merchantmen convoyed trade and suppressed French privateers based in the West Indies, in loose cooperation with the British Royal Navy. The crisis was entangled with the XYZ Affair, which had inflamed American opinion against France, and with domestic controversy over the Alien and Sedition Acts.

Resolution

Adams, against much of his own Federalist Party, pursued negotiations; the Convention of 1800 (Treaty of Mortefontaine) ended the fighting, abrogated the 1778 alliance and restored commercial relations. The Quasi-War established the young US Navy's reputation and remains a classic example of a limited, undeclared naval war.
