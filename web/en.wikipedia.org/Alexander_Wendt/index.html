Alexander Wendt

Alexander Wendt (born 12 June 1958) is an American political scientist and one of the most influential scholars of international relations theory. He is best known as a founder of social constructivism in international relations, arguing that the structures of world politics are made of shared ideas rather than material forces alone. His 1992 article "Anarchy is What States Make of It" and his 1999 book Social Theory of International Politics are canonical texts of the discipline.

Career

Wendt was born in Mainz, West Germany, and raised in Minnesota. He studied at Macalester College and the University of Minnesota, and has taught at Yale University, Dartmouth College, the University of Chicago, and Ohio State University, where he holds the Mershon Professor of International Security chair.

Constructivism

Wendt argues that phenomena such as power politics and anarchy are not natural givens but products of social practice: "anarchy is what states make of it". Cultures of anarchy — Hobbesian (enmity), Lockean (rivalry), and Kantian (friendship) — arise from shared understandings, and states' identities and interests are constructed through interaction rather than fixed by material structure. His later work, including Quantum Mind and Social Science (2015), explores quantum foundations for social theory.
