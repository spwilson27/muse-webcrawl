History of Poland (1918–1939)

The history of Poland from 1918 to 1939 covers the Second Polish Republic, restored to independence after 123 years of partition, from its re-establishment at the end of the First World War to the invasion of Poland in September 1939 that began the Second World War.

Rebirth and border wars

Poland regained independence in November 1918, with Józef Piłsudski as Chief of State. Its frontiers were settled by a series of conflicts: the Greater Poland Uprising (1918–19), the Polish–Ukrainian War, three Silesian Uprisings, the Polish–Lithuanian dispute over Vilnius, and above all the Polish–Soviet War of 1919–21, in which the Polish victory at the Battle of Warsaw (the "Miracle on the Vistula", August 1920) halted the Red Army; the Treaty of Riga (1921) fixed the eastern border. The Treaty of Versailles gave Poland the "Polish Corridor" to the Baltic and made Danzig (Gdańsk) a Free City.

Parliamentary democracy, 1918–1926

The March Constitution of 1921 created a parliamentary republic with a weak presidency. Governments changed frequently amid hyperinflation, land reform disputes and conflict over the large Ukrainian, Belarusian, Jewish and German minorities, who made up about a third of the population. President Gabriel Narutowicz was assassinated in 1922, days after taking office. Władysław Grabski's reforms of 1924 stabilised the currency with the złoty.

The Sanacja regime

In May 1926 Piłsudski seized power in a coup, inaugurating the authoritarian-leaning Sanacja ("Sanation") regime while retaining a façade of parliamentary institutions. After Piłsudski's death in 1935 power passed to a circle of his colonels; the April Constitution of 1935 strengthened the presidency. The Great Depression hit Poland hard, though the port of Gdynia and the Central Industrial Region were major development projects.

Foreign policy

Sandwiched between Germany and the USSR, Poland sought security through alliances with France (1921) and Romania, non-aggression declarations with the Soviet Union (1932) and Germany (1934), and Józef Beck's policy of balancing between Berlin and Moscow. Poland participated in the partition of Czechoslovakia by annexing Trans-Olza in October 1938. Anglo-French guarantees in March 1939 failed to deter Hitler; the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact of 23 August 1939 secretly assigned Poland to be divided. Germany invaded on 1 September 1939 and the USSR on 17 September, ending the Second Republic.
