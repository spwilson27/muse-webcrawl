Novaya Zemlya (Russian: Новая Земля, "New Land") is an archipelago in the Arctic Ocean in northern Russia, separating the Barents Sea from the Kara Sea. It consists of two large islands, Severny (Northern) and Yuzhny (Southern), plus many smaller ones.

History

Known to Russians from around the 11th century, the islands were visited by Hugh Willoughby in 1553 and charted during Willem Barentsz's 1596–97 expedition, whose crew wintered at Het Behouden Huys. The archipelago later hosted hunting, fishing and a small Nenets population.

Nuclear testing

From 1955 the Soviet Union used Novaya Zemlya as a nuclear test site; the resident population was relocated. On 30 October 1961 the site hosted the detonation of the Tsar Bomba, the largest nuclear device ever tested, at about 50 megatons. Testing ended with the USSR's 1990 moratorium.

Geography

Severny Island holds one of Europe's largest ice caps. The climate is polar, wildlife includes polar bears, reindeer and walrus, and the main settlement is Belushya Guba on Yuzhny Island.
