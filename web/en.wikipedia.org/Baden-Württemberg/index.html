Baden-Württemberg is a federal state (Land) in southwestern Germany, bordering France, Switzerland and Austria (across Lake Constance). With around 11.1 million inhabitants it is the third-largest German state by both population and area. Its capital and largest city is Stuttgart.

History

The state was formed on 25 April 1952 by merging the three post-war states of Württemberg-Baden, South Baden and Württemberg-Hohenzollern, following a referendum in 1951. The region's history includes the medieval Duchy of Swabia, the Margraviate of Baden and the Kingdom of Württemberg.

Geography

The Black Forest (Schwarzwald) occupies much of the west, rising to the Feldberg at 1,493 metres. The Rhine forms the western border, the Danube rises in the state, and Lake Constance lies in the southeast. Major cities include Mannheim, Karlsruhe, Freiburg, Heidelberg and Ulm.

Economy

Baden-Württemberg is one of Germany's most prosperous states, a global centre of automobile engineering — Daimler (Mercedes-Benz) and Porsche are headquartered in Stuttgart, and Bosch and SAP are also based in the state. It has one of the lowest unemployment rates in Germany and a strong export-oriented Mittelstand.

Culture

The state is known for Swabian and Alemannic dialects, spa towns such as Baden-Baden, the romantic university city of Heidelberg, and cuisine including Spätzle and Maultaschen.
