United States Bureau of Reclamation

The Bureau of Reclamation, formerly the United States Reclamation Service, is a federal agency under the U.S. Department of the Interior, which oversees water resource management, specifically as it applies to the oversight and operation of numerous water diversion, delivery, and storage projects in the western United States, including hydroelectricity generation.

The agency was established in 1902 by the Reclamation Act (also known as the Newlands Act) to design and construct irrigation projects to help develop the arid West. It has since constructed more than 600 dams and reservoirs, including Hoover Dam on the Colorado River and Grand Coulee Dam on the Columbia River.

Scope of operations

Reclamation is the largest wholesaler of water in the United States, delivering water to more than 31 million people and providing irrigation water for about 10 million acres of farmland. It is also the second-largest producer of hydroelectric power in the country, operating 53 power plants. Its projects span 17 western states.

Shift in mission

Beginning in the late twentieth century, large dam construction largely ceased, and the bureau's mission shifted toward the efficient management of existing facilities, water conservation, environmental restoration, and the settlement of water-rights claims, including those of Native American tribes.
