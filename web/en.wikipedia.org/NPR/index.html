NPR

NPR (National Public Radio)
Founded: February 26, 1970
Headquarters: Washington, D.C.
Type: Nonprofit public radio network
Member stations: 1,000+
Flagship programs: Morning Edition, All Things Considered

NPR, formerly National Public Radio, is an American nonprofit media organization that produces and distributes news and cultural programming to a network of more than 1,000 public radio stations across the United States.

History

NPR was incorporated in 1970 under the Public Broadcasting Act of 1967 and first went on air in April 1971, covering the U.S. Senate hearings on the Vietnam War. Its afternoon newsmagazine All Things Considered launched that May, and Morning Edition followed in 1979; together they remain among the most-listened-to radio programs in the country. A funding crisis in 1983 nearly bankrupted the network and led to restructuring in which federal support flows mainly through member stations.

Programming and funding

Besides news, NPR distributes entertainment and culture programs, and in the podcast era became a dominant producer with shows such as Fresh Air, Planet Money, and Throughline, plus the Tiny Desk concert series. Revenue comes chiefly from member-station fees, corporate sponsorships, grants, and individual contributions; only a small share comes directly from federal appropriations, though stations rely partly on federal funds through the Corporation for Public Broadcasting.

Structure

NPR is a membership organization: stations elect most of its board. It should not be confused with American Public Media or Public Radio Exchange, other distributors serving many of the same stations. See also PBS.
