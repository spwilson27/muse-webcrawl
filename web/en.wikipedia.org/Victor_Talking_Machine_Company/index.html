ProductsPhonographs, gramophone records

The Victor Talking Machine Company was an American recording company and phonograph manufacturer headquartered in Camden, New Jersey. Founded in 1901 by Eldridge R. Johnson, it grew out of the marketing of Emile Berliner's gramophone discs and became the dominant American record company of the early twentieth century. Its trademark, adapted from Francis Barraud's painting His Master's Voice, showed the dog Nipper listening to a phonograph.

History

Victor's Victrola line of internal-horn phonographs, introduced in 1906, became a household fixture, and the company's Red Seal records brought famous classical performers such as Enrico Caruso to a mass audience. In 1929 the Radio Corporation of America (RCA) acquired Victor, forming RCA Victor; the Victor name and trademarks later passed through various owners in the modern music industry.
