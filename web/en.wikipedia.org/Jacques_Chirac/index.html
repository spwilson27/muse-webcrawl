Jacques René Chirac (29 November 1932 – 26 September 2019) was a French politician who served as President of France from 1995 to 2007. A dominant figure of the French right for four decades, he was previously Prime Minister under both Valéry Giscard d'Estaing and — in the first "cohabitation" — under François Mitterrand, and Mayor of Paris for eighteen years.

Presidency

Elected in 1995 on promises to heal France's "social fracture", Chirac ended nuclear testing after a final Pacific series, abolished conscription, and recognised the French state's responsibility for the Vel' d'Hiv deportations of Jews. In 2002 he was re-elected in a landslide against the far-right Jean-Marie Le Pen. His second term is best remembered internationally for France's opposition to the 2003 invasion of Iraq, which sharply strained relations with the United States. Domestically, the 2005 referendum rejection of the EU constitution and riots in the banlieues marked his final years; he also shortened the presidential term from seven to five years.

Later life

After leaving office Chirac founded a foundation for sustainable development and cultural dialogue. In 2011 he was convicted of embezzlement from his time as Mayor of Paris and given a suspended sentence — the first French head of state convicted since the Second World War era. He died in 2019 and received national mourning.
