The Xbox is a home video game console manufactured by Microsoft, the first in the Xbox line. Released on 15 November 2001 in North America, it was the first console offered by an American company since the Atari Jaguar and marked Microsoft's debut in the gaming hardware market. The name derives from "DirectX Box", reflecting the console's PC-derived architecture.

Hardware

Unusually for a console of its era, the Xbox was built around off-the-shelf PC parts: a 733 MHz Intel processor, an Nvidia graphics chip, 64 MB of RAM, an internal hard disk, and an Ethernet port. It launched at US$299 and sold over 24 million units worldwide.

Games and Xbox Live

The system's launch title Halo: Combat Evolved became a defining hit, and its sequel Halo 2 drove adoption of Xbox Live, the broadband online service launched in 2002 that set the template for modern console online play. The Xbox also hosted acclaimed titles such as Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic, Fable, and Ninja Gaiden.

Legacy

The console was discontinued in 2006–2007 following the launch of its successor, the Xbox 360. Its large controller ("the Duke") was widely criticized, but the platform established Microsoft as a lasting competitor to Sony and Nintendo.
