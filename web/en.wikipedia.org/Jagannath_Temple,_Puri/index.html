Jagannath Temple, Puri

LocationPuri, Odisha, India
DeityJagannath (form of Vishnu/Krishna)
ArchitectureKalinga architecture
Built12th century CE (traditionally begun under Anantavarman Chodaganga)
FestivalRatha Yatra

The Jagannath Temple is a Hindu temple dedicated to Jagannath, a form of Vishnu, located in the coastal town of Puri in the state of Odisha, India. The present temple was begun in the 12th century, traditionally under the Eastern Ganga king Anantavarman Chodaganga Deva, on the site of earlier shrines. Puri is one of the original four Char Dham pilgrimage destinations founded, according to tradition, by Adi Shankara, and the temple is one of the holiest sites of Vaishnavism.

Architecture

The temple complex covers more than 400,000 square feet (about 4 hectares) within high fortified walls. Its main tower (shikhara or deul), built in the rekha style of Kalinga architecture, rises about 65 m (214 ft) and is crowned by the great wheel (Nilachakra) and a flag that is changed daily by a priest who climbs the tower — a ritual said to have continued unbroken for centuries. The main entrance, the Singhadwara (Lion Gate), faces the Grand Road (Bada Danda) along which the festival chariots roll. Within the complex stand the sanctum (housing Jagannath, Balabhadra and Subhadra on the Ratnavedi), the Jagamohana hall, the Natamandapa and the Bhogamandapa, plus dozens of smaller shrines.

Rituals and food

The temple follows an elaborate daily round of rituals from before dawn. Its kitchen, the Rosaghara, is often described as the largest kitchen in the world, cooking food for tens of thousands of devotees daily in earthen pots stacked over wood fires; the sanctified food, mahaprasad, is central to the temple's religious life. Entry to the inner temple is restricted to Hindus of Indian origin; non-Hindus may view the complex from the terrace of the nearby Raghunandan Library.

Ratha Yatra

The temple is world-famous for Ratha Yatra, the annual chariot festival in the month of Ashadha (June–July), when Jagannath, Balabhadra and Subhadra are carried in procession on three colossal wooden chariots to the Gundicha Temple, their aunt's temple, where they stay for a week before the return journey (Bahuda Yatra). New chariots are constructed each year from specified trees by hereditary craftsmen. The festival draws well over a million pilgrims to Puri.

History

Despite repeated invasions of Odisha and several attacks on the temple over the centuries — during which the idols were repeatedly hidden, including in the waters of Chilika Lake — worship has continued with remarkable resilience. The temple administration today is headed by the Gajapati Maharaja of Puri, who performs ritual service as the deity's first servant (adya sevaka), most visibly sweeping the chariots during Ratha Yatra in the Chhera Panhara rite.
