MeToo movement

A rally supporting survivors of sexual harassment and assault.

#MeToo is a social movement and awareness campaign against sexual abuse, sexual harassment, and rape culture, in which survivors publicly share their experiences. The phrase "Me too" was first used in this context by activist Tarana Burke in 2006 on Myspace, as part of work with young women of colour who had experienced sexual abuse.

The viral moment of 2017

The hashtag went viral in October 2017, following investigations by The New York Times and The New Yorker into sexual misconduct allegations against film producer Harvey Weinstein. Actor Alyssa Milano encouraged survivors to reply "me too" to a tweet to show the magnitude of the problem; millions responded within days, and the hashtag was used in dozens of countries, with local variants such as #BalanceTonPorc in France, #YoTambién in Spanish-speaking countries, and movements in China and South Korea despite restrictions.

Impact

The movement led to the fall or resignation of numerous prominent figures in entertainment, media, politics, business, sport, and academia. It prompted the founding of the Time's Up legal defence fund in January 2018, changes to workplace policies and non-disclosure agreements in several jurisdictions, and renewed debate about due process, the burden of proof, and the difference between harassment and assault. Weinstein was convicted of sex crimes in New York in 2020 (a conviction overturned on appeal in 2024, with retrial proceedings following) and in California in 2022.

Criticism and debate

Commentators have debated the movement's scope and limits: some credited it with breaking decades of silence; others raised concerns about trial by media, proportionality of consequences, and the under-representation of low-wage workers, whose experiences Burke's original campaign centred. The movement has also been credited with measurable increases in reports to hotlines and in sexual-harassment legislation in multiple US states.
