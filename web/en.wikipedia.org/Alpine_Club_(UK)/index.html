Alpine Club (UK)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Alpine Club is a British mountaineering club based in London. Founded in 1857 by British mountaineers, it is the world's first alpine club and one of the oldest mountaineering organizations. Its members were central figures of the golden age of alpinism, making many first ascents in the Alps, and later in the Himalayas and other great ranges.

History

The club was founded on 22 December 1857. Early members included John Ball, its first president, and climbers such as Edward Whymper, who made the first ascent of the Matterhorn in 1865. Membership was originally restricted to men; a separate Ladies' Alpine Club was founded in 1907, and the two merged in 1975.

Publications and library

The club publishes the Alpine Journal, founded in 1863 and the world's oldest mountaineering journal. It maintains a large library and the Himalayan Index, a record of expeditions and ascents in the Himalaya and Karakoram.
