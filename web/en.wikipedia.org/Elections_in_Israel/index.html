Elections in Israel

Elections in Israel are based on nationwide proportional representation. The electoral system, unique in that the entire country constitutes a single electoral district, requires candidates to be selected by political parties; voters cast ballots for a party list rather than for individual candidates in constituencies. The legislature, the Knesset, has 120 members elected for a term of four years, although early elections are common.

System

Seats are allocated by the D'Hondt method to lists passing the electoral threshold, raised over time to 3.25% in 2014. After an election, the President of Israel consults party leaders and tasks the candidate most likely to form a government with assembling a coalition; no party has ever won an outright majority, so all Israeli governments have been coalitions. Between 1996 and 2001 prime ministers were elected directly in a separate ballot, an experiment that was abandoned after it weakened the large parties.

History

The first Knesset election was held on 25 January 1949, after the War of Independence; the left-wing Mapai party of David Ben-Gurion dominated the first three decades of the state. The 1977 election ("the upheaval") brought Menachem Begin's Likud to power, ending Labour dominance. In recent decades politics has fragmented among right-wing, centrist, religious and Arab parties, and Israel held an unprecedented five elections between 2019 and 2022 amid a prolonged political crisis.

Other elections

Municipal elections are held every five years for mayors and local councils. The president of Israel is elected by the Knesset in a secret ballot for a single seven-year term.
