Propane

Propane is a three-carbon alkane, C3H8.

Propane is a three-carbon alkane with the molecular formula C3H8. It is a gas at standard temperature and pressure but is compressible to a transportable liquid, and is a by-product of natural gas processing and petroleum refining. It is commonly used as a fuel, in mixtures known as liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) together with butane and propylene.

Properties

Propane is colourless and nearly odourless; a distinctive odorant such as ethanethiol is added for safety so leaks can be detected. Its boiling point is −42 °C, so it vaporises readily at low temperatures, and it has a higher heating value of about 50 MJ/kg. Because it is heavier than air, leaked propane can pool in low areas, creating an explosion hazard.

Uses

Propane fuels barbecues, portable stoves, water heaters, home furnaces in areas without natural gas service, hot air balloons, and some vehicle fleets (as autogas). It is also a feedstock and an aerosol propellant, and serves as a refrigerant (R290) in some systems.
