PubMed

Type: Bibliographic database
Operator: U.S. National Library of Medicine (NLM)
Launched: 1996 (public web access 1997)
Content: over 35 million citations (2020s)

PubMed is a free database of references and abstracts on life sciences and biomedical topics, maintained by the United States National Library of Medicine (NLM) at the National Institutes of Health as part of the Entrez system of information retrieval. It primarily provides access to MEDLINE, the NLM's bibliographic database of journal articles in medicine, nursing, dentistry, and related fields.

Content and indexing

PubMed contains tens of millions of citations, with references reaching back to the 19th century. Articles are indexed with Medical Subject Headings (MeSH), a controlled vocabulary that aids precise searching. Each record is assigned a unique identifier, the PubMed ID (PMID). Where available, records link to full text, including the free archive PubMed Central (PMC) and publisher websites.

History

MEDLINE's predecessor, the Index Medicus, dated to 1879. Computerised searching began with MEDLARS in 1964 and online MEDLINE in 1971, initially available mainly to medical libraries. PubMed was released experimentally in 1996 and opened to free public access in June 1997, after which usage grew enormously. A redesigned "new PubMed" became the default interface in 2020.

Searching

Users can search by keyword, author, journal, MeSH term, or PMID, and can apply filters for date, article type, and availability. PubMed supports Boolean operators and automatic term mapping, which translates free text into MeSH vocabulary.
