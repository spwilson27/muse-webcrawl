Robert George Seale (born October 22, 1936) is an American political activist and author who co-founded the Black Panther Party for Self-Defense with Huey P. Newton in Oakland, California, in October 1966. Seale served as the party's chairman and helped shape its Ten-Point Program and community survival programs, including Free Breakfast for Children.

Seale was one of the original "Chicago Eight" defendants charged with conspiracy and inciting to riot in connection with protests at the 1968 Democratic National Convention. During the trial, Judge Julius Hoffman ordered Seale bound and gagged in the courtroom after repeated outbursts demanding his right to represent himself; his case was severed from the others, making them the "Chicago Seven."

Later life

In 1973 Seale ran for mayor of Oakland, finishing second in a large field. He left the Black Panther Party in 1974 and published the memoir Seize the Time and an autobiography, A Lonely Rage. In later years he has lectured widely on the history of the Panthers and on community organizing.
