Muscle

Structure of a skeletal muscle

Muscle is a soft tissue found in most animals. Muscle cells contain protein filaments of actin and myosin that slide past one another to produce a contraction that changes both the length and shape of the cell. Muscles function to produce force and motion.

Types

There are three types of muscle tissue in vertebrates. Skeletal muscle is attached to bones by tendons and produces voluntary movement. Cardiac muscle forms the walls of the heart and contracts involuntarily and rhythmically. Smooth muscle is found in the walls of internal organs such as the intestines and blood vessels and is also involuntary.

Structure and contraction

Skeletal muscle is organised into bundles of fibres, each fibre a long multinucleate cell containing myofibrils divided into repeating contractile units called sarcomeres. Contraction is triggered when calcium ions, released in response to a nerve impulse, allow myosin heads to bind actin and pull the filaments inward — the sliding filament model. The energy for contraction comes from ATP.

Health

Muscles make up roughly 40 percent of human body mass. Regular exercise increases their size and strength (hypertrophy), while disuse, disease, or ageing causes wasting (atrophy and sarcopenia).
