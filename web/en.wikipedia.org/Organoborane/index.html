Organoborane

Organoboranes contain at least one boron–carbon bond and are versatile intermediates in synthesis.

Organoboranes or organoboron compounds are chemical compounds of boron and carbon that are organic derivatives of borane (BH3), for example trialkylboranes. Organoboron chemistry is the chemistry of these compounds, which serve as important reagents in organic synthesis.

Properties

The boron–carbon bond is polarized toward carbon, making alkylboranes useful nucleophilic alkylating agents in some contexts. Most organoboranes react readily with oxygen and must be handled under inert atmosphere; some, such as triethylborane, are pyrophoric and are used as radical initiators.

Synthesis and reactions

The most important route to organoboranes is hydroboration, the addition of a B–H bond across an alkene, discovered by Herbert C. Brown, who received the 1979 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for developing boron compounds as synthetic tools. Oxidation of the resulting organoborane gives alcohols with anti-Markovnikov regiochemistry. Organoboron compounds are also central to the Suzuki–Miyaura coupling, in which boronic acids couple with organic halides under palladium catalysis — a reaction recognised by the 2010 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

Classes

  • Trialkylboranes, e.g. triethylborane
  • Boronic acids and esters
  • Borinic acids and esters
  • Organoboranes with boron–nitrogen or boron–oxygen rings, such as borazine
