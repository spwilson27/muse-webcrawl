2 Live Crew was an American hip hop group from Miami, Florida, led by Luther Campbell, known professionally as Uncle Luke. Formed in 1984, the group became the leading act of the Miami bass sound and achieved national fame — and notoriety — for the sexually explicit lyrics of their 1989 album As Nasty As They Wanna Be, which became the centre of a landmark obscenity and free-speech legal battle in the United States.

Career

The group's debut album, The 2 Live Crew Is What We Are (1986), introduced their bass-heavy party sound, and Move Somethin' (1988) included the hit "Me So Horny" material that followed on the multi-platinum As Nasty As They Wanna Be. The single "Me So Horny" reached the Billboard charts despite minimal radio play. Later albums included the more political Banned in the U.S.A. (1990), whose title track — built around a sample of Bruce Springsteen's "Born in the U.S.A.", used with permission — became their biggest pop hit.

Obscenity cases

In June 1990, a federal judge in Florida ruled As Nasty As They Wanna Be legally obscene — the first album in American history so declared. Group members were arrested after a performance, and a record-store clerk was charged for selling the record; the performers were acquitted, and in 1992 the Eleventh Circuit Court of Appeals overturned the obscenity ruling. In a separate 1994 case, Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music, Inc., the United States Supreme Court ruled that the group's parody of Roy Orbison's "Oh, Pretty Woman" was protected fair use — a foundational decision in copyright law.

Legacy

2 Live Crew's legal battles made them symbols of First Amendment resistance in popular music, and their success helped establish Miami bass and Southern hip hop commercially. Fresh Kid Ice (Christopher Wong Won), a founding member of Chinese and Trinidadian descent, is often cited as one of the first Asian American rappers. He died in 2017; Brother Marquis died in 2024.
