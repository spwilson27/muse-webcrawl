Königswinter

Königswinter is a town in the Rhein-Sieg district of North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany, situated on the right bank of the Rhine opposite Bonn, at the foot of the Siebengebirge hills. It is best known for the Drachenfels ("Dragon's Rock"), crowned by a ruined castle and reached by one of Germany's oldest rack railways.

Geography and landmarks

The town stretches along the Rhine and into the Siebengebirge nature park, whose peaks include the Drachenfels (321 m) and the Petersberg, where the federal guest house hosted state visitors during the Bonn republic era. The Drachenfelsbahn cog railway, opened in 1883, carries visitors to the summit ruins of Burg Drachenfels, built in the 12th century.

History

Königswinter developed around wine-growing and quarrying in the Siebengebirge; basalt and trachyte from its hills were used as far away as Cologne Cathedral. In the 19th century it became a fashionable Rhine resort. The town also includes the village of Ittenbach and the site of Heisterbach Abbey, a former Cistercian monastery.
