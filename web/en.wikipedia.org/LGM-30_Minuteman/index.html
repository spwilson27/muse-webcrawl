The LGM-30 Minuteman is an American land-based intercontinental ballistic missile (ICBM) in service with the Air Force Global Strike Command of the United States Air Force. First deployed in 1962 during the Cold War, it was the first U.S. missile to use solid fuel and to carry multiple independently targetable reentry vehicles (MIRVs). As of the 2020s, the Minuteman III is the only land-based ICBM in the U.S. arsenal, with about 400 missiles deployed in silos at bases in Montana, North Dakota and Wyoming.

Development and variants

Work began in the late 1950s under Edward Hall, whose push for solid-fuel propulsion allowed a smaller, cheaper missile that could be mass-produced and launched within a minute — hence the name "Minuteman," after the militia of the American Revolution. Minuteman I entered service in 1962, Minuteman II from 1965, and Minuteman III from 1970, introducing MIRV capability with up to three warheads, later reduced by treaty to a single warhead on each missile.

Status and replacement

The Minuteman III force has been repeatedly life-extended but is planned for replacement by the LGM-35 Sentinel (formerly the Ground Based Strategic Deterrent) beginning in the 2030s, despite significant cost growth in that programme.
