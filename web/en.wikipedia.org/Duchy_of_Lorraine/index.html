Duchy of Lorraine

The Duchy of Lorraine was a duchy in present-day northeastern France, roughly corresponding to the modern region of Lorraine. It originated in 959 as Upper Lorraine, one of the divisions of Lotharingia, and existed as a state of the Holy Roman Empire until its annexation by France in 1766.

History

Ruled from Nancy by a succession of ducal houses, Lorraine was frequently contested between France and the Empire. France occupied parts of the duchy repeatedly in the 17th century. By the Treaty of Vienna (1738), ending the War of the Polish Succession, Duke Francis III gave up Lorraine in exchange for Tuscany; the duchy was given to Stanisław Leszczyński, former king of Poland, and on his death in 1766 it passed permanently to the French crown.
