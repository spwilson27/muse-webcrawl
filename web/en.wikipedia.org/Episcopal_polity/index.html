Episcopal polity

A bishop, the chief officer in churches with episcopal governance

Episcopal polity is a hierarchical form of church governance in which the chief local authorities are called bishops. The word "episcopal" is derived from the Greek episkopos, meaning "overseer" or "superintendent". This form of governance is used by many churches and denominations, including the Roman Catholic Church, the Eastern Orthodox Church, the Anglican Communion, and many Lutheran, Methodist, and other churches.

Structure

In episcopal polity, bishops exercise authority over a diocese, a defined geographical region, and over the clergy and parishes within it. Above bishops there may be archbishops and, in the Catholic Church, the Pope as universal pontiff. Authority is understood to descend from the top down: bishops ordain priests and deacons, confirm members, and are responsible for doctrine and discipline in their dioceses.

Apostolic succession

Churches with episcopal governance typically teach the doctrine of apostolic succession, holding that their bishops stand in an unbroken line of succession from the apostles through the laying on of hands. This succession is often seen as guaranteeing the validity of sacraments and ordained ministry.

Comparison with other polities

Episcopal polity contrasts with presbyterian polity, in which authority rests with assemblies of elders arranged in graded courts (session, presbytery, synod, general assembly), and with congregational polity, in which each local congregation is autonomous. Many denominations combine elements: for example, United Methodist churches are governed by bishops but also have conferences with lay and clergy representation.

Churches

Churches organised under episcopal polity include the Roman Catholic Church, Eastern Orthodox and Oriental Orthodox churches, the churches of the Anglican Communion, the Old Catholic churches, many Lutheran churches (especially in Scandinavia), the United Methodist Church, and the African Methodist Episcopal Church, among others.
