Ruggero (or Ruggiero) Leoncavallo (23 April 1857 – 9 August 1919) was an Italian opera composer and librettist. Although he produced numerous operas and other songs throughout his career, it is his opera Pagliacci (1892), still a staple of the standard repertoire, that established him as a leading figure of the verismo movement in Italian music.

Life and career

Born in Naples, Leoncavallo studied at the city's San Pietro a Majella Conservatory and at the University of Bologna, where the poet Giosuè Carducci was among his teachers. He worked for years as a café pianist and teacher in Egypt, France and England while trying to establish himself as a composer. Inspired by the success of Pietro Mascagni's Cavalleria rusticana in 1890, he wrote his own one-act-style drama based on a real murder case from his childhood in Calabria, whose proceedings his father, a magistrate, had overseen.

Pagliacci

Pagliacci ("Clowns") premiered at the Teatro Dal Verme in Milan on 21 May 1892, conducted by the young Arturo Toscanini, and was an immediate international success. The opera tells of Canio, leader of a travelling troupe, who murders his unfaithful wife Nedda and her lover on stage during a performance. Its prologue ("Si può?") and Canio's aria "Vesti la giubba" became iconic; Enrico Caruso's 1902 and 1907 recordings of the aria were among the first records to sell a million copies. Pagliacci is today almost invariably performed in a double bill with Cavalleria rusticana, known as "Cav/Pag".

Other works

Leoncavallo's other operas include I Medici (1893), part of an unfinished trilogy on the Italian Renaissance; La bohème (1897), a rival setting to Puccini's; Zazà (1900); and the operetta La reginetta delle rose (1912). He also wrote the popular song "Mattinata" (1904), the first song composed expressly for the Gramophone Company, and recorded by Caruso. Leoncavallo generally wrote his own libretti, an unusual practice for Italian composers of his era.
