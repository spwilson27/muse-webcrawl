Art song

An art song is a vocal music composition, usually written for one singer and piano accompaniment, that sets a poem to music as a deliberate artistic partnership between words and music. The term distinguishes the genre from folk song and popular song: in an art song the accompaniment is fully composed rather than improvised or strummed, and the piano is an equal partner that comments on and illustrates the text.

Traditions

The best-known art song tradition is the German Lied, which flowered in the 19th century with Franz Schubert — who composed more than 600 songs, including the cycles Die schöne Müllerin and Winterreise — and continued through Schumann, Brahms, Wolf and Mahler. The French equivalent, the mélodie, was cultivated by Fauré, Debussy and Poulenc; other national traditions include the Russian romance and a substantial English and American repertoire from composers such as Vaughan Williams, Britten, Barber and Ned Rorem.

Characteristics

Art songs are typically short, strophic or through-composed settings of lyric poetry, performed in recitals by a singer and pianist without staging or costume. Song cycles group several settings into a narrative or thematic whole. The genre's emphasis on intimate expression and the fusion of poetry with music made it a central laboratory for Romantic harmony and text-setting.
