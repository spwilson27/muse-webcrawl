Square (algebra)

OperationExponentiation to the power 2
Notationx²
InverseSquare root
RelatedSquare number, cube, perfect square

In algebra, a square is the result of multiplying a number by itself. The verb "to square" denotes this operation. Squaring is the same as raising to the power 2, and is denoted by a superscript 2; for instance, the square of 3 is written as 3², which equals 9.

Real numbers

For real numbers, the square function maps every number to a non-negative value: negative numbers square to positive results. The squares of integers are called square numbers or perfect squares: 0, 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, and so on. The function y = x² describes a parabola with its vertex at the origin.

Properties

  • The square of a product is the product of the squares: (ab)² = a²b².
  • The square of a sum follows the identity (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b².
  • The sum of the first n odd numbers equals n².
  • No square of a real number is negative; in the complex numbers, i² = −1.

Geometry

The name reflects the geometry: the area of a square with side length x is x². This relationship long predates algebraic notation, appearing in Babylonian and Greek mathematics.

General structures

In abstract algebra, squaring can be defined in any ring or algebra where a product of an element with itself makes sense. In some non-commutative settings, the squaring map interacts with the anticommutator, and in characteristic 2 the Frobenius endomorphism generalises the squaring map of finite fields.
