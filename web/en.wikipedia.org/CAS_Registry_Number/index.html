CAS Registry Number

A CAS Registry Number (also referred to as CAS RN or informally CAS Number) is a unique identification number, assigned by the Chemical Abstracts Service (CAS), to every chemical substance described in the open scientific literature. The CAS Registry contains tens of millions of substances, and the numbers are the de facto standard identifiers in chemistry.

Format

A CAS RN consists of up to ten digits divided by hyphens into three parts: a main sequence of up to seven digits, a two-digit part, and a single check digit. For example, water is 7732-18-5 and aspirin is 50-78-2. The check digit is computed by a weighted sum of the preceding digits, allowing simple validation of transcription errors.

Usage

Because a substance may have many systematic and trade names — and one name may be ambiguous — the CAS RN provides an unambiguous link between a specific molecular structure or composition and information about it in catalogues, safety data sheets and regulations.
