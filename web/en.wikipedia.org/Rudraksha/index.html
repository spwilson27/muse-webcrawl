Rudraksha

Rudraksha (Sanskrit: रुद्राक्ष, "Rudra's [Shiva's] eyes") is the dried stone (endocarp) of the fruit of the tree Elaeocarpus ganitrus and several related Elaeocarpus species, used as prayer beads in Hinduism, especially Shaivism, and also in Buddhism, Jainism and Sikhism. The trees grow principally in the Himalayan foothills, Nepal, India and Indonesia.

Religious significance

According to tradition, the beads formed from the tears of the god Shiva (in his form as Rudra) shed in compassion for humanity. Strung into a mala of 108 beads (plus a binding bead), rudrakshas are used for japa, the repetition of mantras such as Om Namah Shivaya. Texts including the Shiva Purana and the Rudraksha Jabala Upanishad describe the merits of wearing them.

Mukhi classification

Each stone is divided by natural vertical clefts into segments; the number of faces, or mukhi, is said to determine the bead's properties and presiding deity. Five-faced (panchmukhi) beads are by far the most common and are worn by householders. One-faced (ek mukhi) beads are extremely rare and correspondingly valuable, and beads with up to 21 faces are described. Conjoined beads (gauri-shankar) and triple beads (trijuti) also command high prices. The rarity of unusual mukhi counts has made authentication a significant concern in the trade.

Cultivation and trade

Nepal's central hills and the Indonesian island of Java are major sources; Indonesian beads are generally smaller with smoother clefts. The cleaned stones are drilled and strung, sometimes with caps of gold or silver, and are worn as necklaces and bracelets or kept as objects of devotion.
