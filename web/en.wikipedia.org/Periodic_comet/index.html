Periodic comet

Periodic comets (also known as short-period comets) are comets with orbital periods of less than 200 years, or that have been observed during more than a single perihelion passage. They receive a permanent designation consisting of a number followed by "P/" and usually the name of their discoverer.

Families

Jupiter-family comets have periods under 20 years and orbits strongly influenced by Jupiter, with aphelia near the planet's orbit; most originate in the Kuiper belt and scattered disc. Halley-type comets have periods between 20 and 200 years and highly inclined orbits, and are thought to derive from the Oort cloud.

Famous examples

The first recognised periodic comet was 1P/Halley, whose periodicity was predicted by Edmond Halley; it has been observed since 240 BC and returns about every 76 years. Others include 2P/Encke (period 3.3 years, the shortest known), 67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko, visited by the Rosetta spacecraft, and 19P/Borrelly and 81P/Wild, visited by NASA probes.

Numbering

The Minor Planet Center assigns numbers to periodic comets once their orbits are securely established, generally after observation at a second perihelion. As of the mid-2020s more than 400 periodic comets have been numbered.
