The University of Tokyo (Japanese: 東京大学, Tōkyō daigaku, abbreviated Todai or UTokyo) is a public research university in Bunkyō, Tokyo, Japan. Established in 1877 by the Meiji government through the merger of several government schools, it was the first imperial university and is widely regarded as Japan's most prestigious university, consistently ranked at or near the top of Japanese and Asian university rankings.

Yasuda Auditorium on the Hongō campus, symbol of the university.

History

The university was founded in 1877 by merging the Tokyo Kaisei School and the Tokyo Medical School. In 1886 it became the Imperial University, and in 1897 the Tokyo Imperial University, the flagship of Japan's imperial university system. Renamed the University of Tokyo after the Second World War, it absorbed the former First Higher School and Tokyo High School as its College of Arts and Sciences at Komaba. The university was a focus of student protest in the 1968–69 Todai riots, which led to the cancellation of entrance examinations in 1969 and reforms of university governance.

Academics

The university comprises ten faculties — Law, Medicine, Engineering, Letters, Science, Agriculture, Economics, Arts and Sciences, Education, and Pharmaceutical Sciences — and fifteen graduate schools. All undergraduates spend their first two years in the liberal arts programme at Komaba before specialising at Hongō. Alumni and faculty include eight Nobel laureates (including physicists Leo Esaki and Takaaki Kajita), numerous prime ministers of Japan, and founders of major corporations. Entrance to the university is famously competitive, based on the national Common Test and the university's own examinations.

Campuses

The main Hongō campus in Bunkyō ward, built on the former Edo estate of the Maeda clan, is known for the Akamon (Red Gate), Sanshirō Pond and the Yasuda Auditorium. Other campuses include Komaba (liberal arts and mathematical sciences), Kashiwa (frontier sciences, opened 1999), and institutes across Japan such as the Earthquake Research Institute and the Kavli Institute for the Physics and Mathematics of the Universe.
