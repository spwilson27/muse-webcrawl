Asphalt concrete

Asphalt concrete (commonly called asphalt, blacktop, or pavement in North America, and tarmac or bitumen macadam in the United Kingdom and the Republic of Ireland) is a composite material commonly used to surface roads, parking lots, airports, and the core of embankment dams.

A paving machine laying hot-mix asphalt.

Composition

Asphalt concrete consists of mineral aggregate bound together with bitumen (asphalt binder), a sticky, black, highly viscous liquid or semi-solid form of petroleum. Mixtures typically contain about 5 per cent binder by mass. It is laid in layers and compacted while hot, or applied as cold-mix or emulsion formulations for smaller works.

Performance and degradation

Asphalt pavements are flexible and can be opened to traffic quickly, but they are subject to rutting in hot weather, cracking from fatigue and low temperatures, and potholes when water penetrates and weakens the base. Ageing oxidises the binder, making it brittle.

Recycling

Asphalt is among the most recycled materials in the world by weight. Reclaimed asphalt pavement can be milled, reheated, and incorporated into new mixtures at high percentages, reducing demand for virgin aggregate and bitumen.
