The United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child (UNCRC) is an international human rights treaty which sets out the civil, political, economic, social, health and cultural rights of children. The Convention defines a child as any human being under the age of eighteen, unless the age of majority is attained earlier under national legislation. It is the most widely and rapidly ratified international human rights treaty in history, with every United Nations member state except the United States having ratified it.

Content

The Convention has 54 articles. Four are treated as general principles: non-discrimination (Article 2); the best interests of the child as a primary consideration (Article 3); the right to life, survival and development (Article 6); and respect for the views of the child (Article 12). Substantive rights include the right to a name and nationality, freedom of expression, thought and religion, protection from violence and exploitation, the rights of children deprived of a family environment, rights to health, education, and an adequate standard of living, and special protections for refugee and disabled children and those in the juvenile justice system.

Optional protocols and monitoring

Three optional protocols address the involvement of children in armed conflict, the sale of children, child prostitution and child pornography, and a communications procedure allowing children to bring complaints. Implementation is monitored by the UN Committee on the Rights of the Child, to which states parties must submit periodic reports; the Committee issues concluding observations and general comments interpreting the Convention.

History

The Convention builds on the 1924 Geneva Declaration and the UN's 1959 Declaration of the Rights of the Child. Poland proposed a binding convention in 1978, and after a decade of drafting the General Assembly adopted the treaty on 20 November 1989; it entered into force on 2 September 1990. Somalia and South Sudan ratified in 2015, leaving the United States — a signatory since 1995 — as the only UN member state that is not a party.
