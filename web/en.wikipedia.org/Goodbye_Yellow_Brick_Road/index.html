Goodbye Yellow Brick Road

Goodbye Yellow Brick Road is the seventh studio album by English musician Elton John, released on 5 October 1973 as a double album by DJM Records in the UK and MCA Records in the US. It is widely regarded as John's masterpiece and his best-selling studio album, with worldwide sales of more than 20 million copies.

Recording

The album was written by John and Bernie Taupin and recorded in May 1973 at the Château d'Hérouville studio in France, where John had previously recorded Honky Château and Don't Shoot Me I'm Only the Piano Player. An initial attempt to record in Jamaica was abandoned. The sessions were highly productive, with the band — guitarist Davey Johnstone, bassist Dee Murray and drummer Nigel Olsson — cutting many tracks quickly.

Music and reception

The double album ranges across rock, balladry and pop, and includes some of John's most famous songs: the title track, "Candle in the Wind" (a tribute to Marilyn Monroe), "Bennie and the Jets," "Saturday Night's Alright for Fighting," and the opening suite "Funeral for a Friend/Love Lies Bleeding." It topped the charts in the US and UK, and has been inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame; Rolling Stone placed it among the greatest albums of all time.

Legacy

The album's title gave its name to Elton John's farewell concert tour, Farewell Yellow Brick Road (2018–2023), one of the highest-grossing concert tours in history.
