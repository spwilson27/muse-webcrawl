0 (zero) is a number representing an empty quantity. Adding 0 to any number leaves that number unchanged. In mathematical terminology, 0 is the additive identity of the integers, rational numbers, real numbers, and complex numbers, as well as other algebraic structures. Multiplying any number by 0 has the result 0, and consequently, division by zero has no meaning in arithmetic.

As a numerical digit, 0 plays a crucial role in decimal notation: it indicates that the power of ten corresponding to the place containing a 0 does not contribute to the total. For example, 205 is a number where the value in the tens place is zero, and the number is two hundreds plus five ones.

History

The concept of zero as both a placeholder and a number developed over millennia. Babylonian scribes used a placeholder symbol in their sexagesimal system by the middle of the 2nd millennium BC, though it was never used at the end of a number. The ancient Greeks, whose mathematics was based on geometry, had no symbol for zero and debated whether it could be a number at all. In the Americas, the Maya independently used a shell glyph for zero in their Long Count calendar by the 4th century BC.

Zero as a number with its own arithmetic rules was first fully described in India. The Jain text Lokavibhāga (458 AD) uses zero conceptually, and the mathematician Brahmagupta, in his Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta (628 AD), gave rules for arithmetic involving zero and negative numbers. Indian mathematics spread westward through the Islamic world — al-Khwārizmī described the Hindu numeral system in the 9th century — and reached Europe via Fibonacci's Liber Abaci (1202).

In mathematics

Zero is an even number, because it is divisible by 2 with no remainder. It is neither positive nor negative, and neither prime nor composite. 0 is the identity element under addition: a + 0 = a for every a. By convention, 0! (zero factorial) equals 1. The expression 00 is an indeterminate form in calculus, though in many discrete contexts it is defined as 1.

Etymology

The English word zero comes from the Venetian Italian zero, a contraction of zefiro, from the Arabic ṣifr (صفر), meaning "empty" or "nothing", itself a translation of the Sanskrit śūnya (शून्य), "empty" or "void". The word cipher shares the same Arabic root.
