Yenisei River
Length3,487 km (5,539 km with the Selenga–Angara system)

SourceKyzyl, Tuva (confluence of Greater and Lesser Yenisei)

The Yenisei (also spelled Yenisey) is the greatest river system flowing to the Arctic Ocean and the fifth-longest river system in the world. Rising in Mongolia and Tuva, it flows northward across Siberia through Krasnoyarsk Krai to the Kara Sea, draining a basin of about 2.58 million square kilometres.

The Yenisei flowing past Krasnoyarsk, the largest city on its banks.

Course

The river forms at Kyzyl in Tuva from the Greater (Bii-Khem) and Lesser (Kaa-Khem) Yenisei. It passes Krasnoyarsk, where it is crossed by the Trans-Siberian Railway, and receives the Angara — the outflow of Lake Baikal — at Strelka, and the Lower Tunguska further north, before reaching its estuary at the Yenisei Gulf.

Hydroelectricity and transport

The Yenisei hosts two of the world's largest hydroelectric dams, at Krasnoyarsk and Sayano-Shushensk; the 2009 Sayano-Shushensk accident killed 75 people. In summer the river is an important transport artery for Siberia, though it freezes for much of the year and its spring floods, dammed by ice at the mouth, are among the largest in the world.
