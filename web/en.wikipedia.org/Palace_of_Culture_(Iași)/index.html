The Palace of Culture (Romanian: Palatul Culturii) is an iconic edifice located in Iași, Romania. Built between 1906 and 1925 in a flamboyant Neo-Gothic style on the site of the former medieval Princely Court of Moldavia, it served as the Palace of Justice and Administration until 1955, when it was converted to cultural use.

Architecture

Designed by architect Ion D. Berindey, the palace covers some 34,000 square metres and contains 298 rooms. Its central clock tower rises 55 metres, and legend holds that the architect drew inspiration from the Town Hall of Compiègne and other northern French Gothic Revival buildings. The Hall of Honour features a celebrated mosaic floor depicting medieval bestiary motifs, while the Voivodes' Hall is decorated with portraits of Moldavian rulers. Despite its medieval appearance, the building was modern for its time, with electric lighting, central heating, and some of the first elevators in Romania.

Museums

Today the palace houses the Moldova National Museum Complex, comprising four museums: the Ethnographic Museum of Moldavia, the Art Museum, the History Museum of Moldavia, and the "Ștefan Procopiu" Science and Technique Museum, which preserves an important collection of musical automata. A major restoration, completed in 2016, returned the palace to public use, and its square and gardens form the centrepiece of the Palas urban complex.
