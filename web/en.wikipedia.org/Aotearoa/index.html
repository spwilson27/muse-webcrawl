Aotearoa

Aotearoa is the Māori name for New Zealand

Aotearoa (pronounced [aɔˈtɛaɾɔa]) is the Māori-language name for New Zealand. The name is most often translated as "land of the long white cloud," from ao (cloud or world), tea (white), and roa (long). It is now in widespread use alongside, and increasingly in place of, the English name in official and everyday contexts.

Origin

In Māori oral tradition, the name is connected with the arrival of the Polynesian navigator Kupe and the voyaging canoes (waka) that settled the islands; a long white cloud sighted on the horizon is said to have signalled land. Originally, Aotearoa referred specifically to the North Island—whose other traditional names include Te Ika-a-Māui ("the fish of Māui")—while the South Island was Te Waipounamu ("the greenstone waters"). Usage for the whole country expanded during the nineteenth and twentieth centuries.

Modern usage

"Aotearoa" appears in the Māori lyrics of the national anthem "God Defend New Zealand," in the names of government departments and national organisations, and on official documents; the country's name is often rendered bilingually as "Aotearoa New Zealand." It has been the subject of public debate and petitions proposing formal adoption of the name.
