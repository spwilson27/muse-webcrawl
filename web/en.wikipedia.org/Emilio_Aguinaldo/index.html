Emilio Aguinaldo y Famy (22 March 1869 – 6 February 1964) was a Filipino revolutionary, statesman and military leader who is officially recognised as the first and the youngest President of the Philippines. He led Philippine forces first against Spain in the Philippine Revolution and subsequently against the United States in the Philippine–American War, and proclaimed Philippine independence from the balcony of his Kawit home on 12 June 1898 — the date celebrated as the Philippines' Independence Day.

Revolution against Spain

A member of the secret society Katipunan, Aguinaldo rose to command the revolution in Cavite and, after the death of Andrés Bonifacio in controversial circumstances, became the revolution's leader. His forces besieged Spanish troops at Manila, and the Biak-na-Bato truce of 1897 sent him into temporary exile in Hong Kong. He returned in 1898 aboard an American ship as the Spanish–American War broke out, declared independence at Kawit on 12 June, and convened the Malolos Congress, which promulgated the Malolos Constitution and elected him president of the First Philippine Republic in January 1899.

War with the United States

Refusing American annexation, Aguinaldo led guerrilla resistance in the Philippine–American War, eluding capture for two years before being captured at Palanan, Isabela, on 23 March 1901 by forces of General Frederick Funston. He subsequently took an oath of allegiance to the United States and lived largely in retirement.

Later life

Aguinaldo ran unsuccessfully for president against Manuel L. Quezon in 1935 and served in Japanese-sponsored government roles during World War II, for which he was controversially granted amnesty. He died in 1964 and was posthumously given state honours. His shrine at Kawit remains a national symbol, and 12 June is observed annually as Philippine Independence Day.
