Lady Macbeth of the Mtsensk District (novella)

Lady Macbeth of the Mtsensk District (Russian: Леди Макбет Мценского уезда) is a novella by Nikolai Leskov, first published in 1865 in Fyodor Dostoevsky's journal Epoch. Its heroine, Katerina Lvovna Izmailova, is a young merchant's wife in provincial Russia, bored and childless, who takes a lover, Sergei, a worker on the estate, and murders her father-in-law and husband to keep him.

When the crimes are discovered, Katerina and Sergei are sentenced to hard labour in Siberia; Sergei betrays her with another convict, and on the ferry crossing she drowns her rival and herself. Leskov called the story a study of a strong female character driven to crime. It is best known through Dmitri Shostakovich's 1934 opera adaptation of the same name.
