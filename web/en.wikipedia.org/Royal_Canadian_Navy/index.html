Royal Canadian Navy

The Royal Canadian Navy (RCN; French: Marine royale canadienne) is the naval force of Canada. The RCN is one of three environmental commands within the Canadian Armed Forces. As of 2024, the RCN operates 12 frigates, 4 patrol submarines, 4 Arctic and offshore patrol ships, 12 coastal defence vessels, and several support vessels.

History

Founded in 1910 as the Naval Service of Canada, the service received royal sanction in 1911. Small at the start of the First World War, the RCN expanded enormously during the Second World War, when Canada operated the third-largest navy in the world by 1945, principally in the Battle of the Atlantic escorting convoys. After the unification of the Canadian Forces in 1968, the service became Maritime Command; the historic name "Royal Canadian Navy" was restored on 16 August 2011.

Fleet and bases

The fleet is divided between Maritime Forces Atlantic (CFB Halifax, Nova Scotia) and Maritime Forces Pacific (CFB Esquimalt, British Columbia). Flagship classes include the Halifax-class multirole frigates and the Victoria-class submarines. The Canadian Surface Combatant (River-class destroyers), based on the Type 26 design, is being built to replace the frigates.
