DecidedJanuary 9, 1947
Citation159 F.2d 169

United States v. Carroll Towing Co., 159 F.2d 169 (2d Cir. 1947), is a decision by Judge Learned Hand concerning negligence in tort law, famous for the algebraic formula — the Hand formula — for determining whether a legal duty of care has been breached.

Facts

The defendant's tug, the Carroll, was adjusting a line for a flotilla of barges in New York Harbor; in the process the barge Anna C, owned by the Connors Marine Company and laden with flour owned by the United States, broke adrift, collided with a tanker, and sank with its cargo. Connors had no bargee aboard the Anna C during working hours on the day of the accident.

Judgment

Hand apportioned liability and, in analysing whether the barge owner's failure to have a bargee aboard was negligent, stated that duty depends on three variables: the probability (P) that the barge would break away; the gravity of the resulting injury (L); and the burden (B) of adequate precautions. Liability depends upon whether B is less than L multiplied by P: algebraically, B < PL. The formula became a cornerstone of the law-and-economics analysis of negligence.
