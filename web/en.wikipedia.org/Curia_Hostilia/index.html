Curia Hostilia

The Curia Hostilia was the original senate house of the Roman Republic, located on the north side of the Comitium in the Roman Forum. Tradition attributed its construction to Rome's third king, Tullus Hostilius, in the 7th century BC, from whom it took its name.

Curia Hostilia
Location: Roman Forum, Rome
Attributed builder: King Tullus Hostilius
Function: Meeting place of the Roman Senate
Destroyed: Burned 52 BC; replaced by Curia Julia

History

The building served as the Senate's principal meeting place for centuries. It was enlarged by the dictator Sulla around 80 BC to accommodate his expanded Senate of 600 members. In 52 BC, during the riots following the murder of Publius Clodius Pulcher, his supporters cremated his body inside the Curia, and the building burned down.

Successors

Faustus Sulla rebuilt the senate house, but Julius Caesar demolished it as part of his forum reorganisation and began the Curia Julia on a slightly different alignment, completed by Augustus in 29 BC. The Curia Julia survives today and stands on the site of the later senate house; nothing substantial of the Curia Hostilia remains above ground.
