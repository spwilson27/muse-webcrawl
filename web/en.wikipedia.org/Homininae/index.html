Homininae

Scientific classification
Kingdom: Animalia
Phylum: Chordata
Class: Mammalia
Order: Primates
Family: Hominidae
Subfamily: Homininae

Homininae, also called "African hominids" or "African apes", is a subfamily of Hominidae. It includes two tribes, with their extant as well as extinct species: Hominini (hominins, comprising humans and their extinct bipedal relatives) and Gorillini (gorillas). Alternatively, the genus Pan (chimpanzees and bonobos) is sometimes considered a third tribe, Panini. It comprises all hominids that arose after orangutans (subfamily Ponginae) split from the line of great apes.

Evolutionary history

Molecular evidence indicates that the lineage leading to gorillas diverged from the human–chimpanzee lineage roughly 8–9 million years ago, and that the human and chimpanzee lineages split about 6–7 million years ago. Fossil members of Homininae include genera such as Orrorin, Ardipithecus, Australopithecus, Paranthropus, and Homo on the hominin side, and fossil gorilla relatives such as Chororapithecus and Nakalipithecus.

Classification

The subfamily includes the genera Gorilla, Pan, and Homo, together with many extinct genera. The term "hominin" usually refers to members of the tribe Hominini, while "hominid" refers to the wider family Hominidae, the great apes.
