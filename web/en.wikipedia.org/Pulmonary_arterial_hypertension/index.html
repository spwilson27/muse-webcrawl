Pulmonary arterial hypertension

Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) is a progressive disorder characterized by abnormally high blood pressure in the arteries supplying the lungs, caused by remodelling and narrowing of the small pulmonary arteries. It is classified as Group 1 of the pulmonary hypertension classification. Untreated, it leads to right heart failure and death.

Symptoms and diagnosis

Symptoms include breathlessness, fatigue, chest pain, fainting and swelling of the legs. Diagnosis is confirmed by right heart catheterization showing a mean pulmonary arterial pressure above 20 mmHg with normal left-sided filling pressures. PAH may be idiopathic, heritable (often involving BMPR2 mutations), or associated with connective tissue disease, congenital heart disease, liver disease or drugs.

Treatment

Treatments include endothelin receptor antagonists, phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors, prostacyclin analogues, and the activin-signalling inhibitor sotatercept; lung transplantation remains an option for advanced disease.
