The Confederate States Navy (CSN) was the naval branch of the Confederate States armed forces, established by an act of the Confederate Congress on 21 February 1861. Under Secretary of the Navy Stephen Mallory, it was responsible for Confederate naval operations during the American Civil War, opposing the much larger United States Navy, which blockaded Southern ports and controlled major rivers.

Strategy and innovation

Outnumbered and lacking an industrial base, the Confederacy pursued an asymmetric strategy: ironclad warships to break the blockade locally, commerce raiders to attack Union merchant shipping worldwide, and new weapons including naval mines (then called torpedoes), spar-torpedo boats, and the submarine H. L. Hunley, which in 1864 became the first submarine to sink an enemy warship. The ironclad CSS Virginia, built on the hull of the scuttled USS Merrimack, fought the USS Monitor at Hampton Roads in March 1862 in the first battle between ironclads.

Commerce raiding

Confederate cruisers, many built secretly in Britain, devastated Union commerce. CSS Alabama, under Raphael Semmes, captured or sank dozens of merchantmen before being sunk by USS Kearsarge off Cherbourg in 1864; CSS Shenandoah continued raiding whalers in the Pacific until after the war's end. The raiders drove much of the American merchant marine to foreign flags, with lasting consequences addressed by the post-war Alabama Claims arbitration.

Organisation and end

The CSN operated squadrons at ports such as Norfolk, Charleston, Savannah, Mobile, and on the James and Mississippi rivers, but shortages of ships, crews, engines, and armour plate limited its strength, and Union advances captured or forced the destruction of most of its vessels. The navy ceased to exist with the Confederacy's collapse in 1865.
