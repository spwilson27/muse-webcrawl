Ernst Karl Abbe (23 January 1840 – 14 January 1905) was a German physicist, optical scientist, entrepreneur and social reformer. Together with Carl Zeiss and Otto Schott, he laid the foundations of modern optics and transformed microscope manufacture from craft empiricism to applied science.

Work with Zeiss

Abbe joined Carl Zeiss's workshop in Jena in 1866 and became its research director and later part owner. In 1873 he published his theory of image formation in the microscope, deriving the diffraction limit on resolution (d = λ/(2 NA)) and the Abbe sine condition for aberration-free imaging. He designed the Abbe refractometer and the apochromatic objectives made possible by Schott's new optical glasses, and the Abbe number (V-number) quantifying glass dispersion bears his name.

Social reform and the Carl Zeiss Foundation

In 1889 Abbe established the Carl Zeiss Foundation (Carl-Zeiss-Stiftung), transferring his and the Zeiss family's shares to it, and endowed it with a statute that guaranteed workers' rights — including an eight-hour day, pensions, sick pay and a minimum wage — decades ahead of German law. The foundation still owns the Zeiss and Schott companies.
