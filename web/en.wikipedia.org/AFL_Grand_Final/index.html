The AFL Grand Final is the championship match of the Australian Football League, the premier Australian rules football competition. Played at the Melbourne Cricket Ground between the two winners of the preliminary finals, it concludes the AFL finals series and is one of Australia's largest annual sporting events — famously "that one day in September".

Traditions

The Grand Final is preceded by a pre-match entertainment program, the traditional Grand Final parade through Melbourne on the preceding Friday (a public holiday in Victoria), and the presentation of the Norm Smith Medal to the best player afield, an award introduced in 1979. The winning team receives the premiership cup and premiership medallions, and each of its players is declared a premiership player.

History

The competition began as the Victorian Football League in 1897; the first Grand Final was played in 1898. The 1970 decider between Carlton and Collingwood drew 121,696 spectators, still the largest crowd in Australian football history, and is remembered for Carlton's comeback from 44 points down at half-time. Drawn Grand Finals were historically replayed the following week (in 1948, 1977, and 2010); since 2016, draws are resolved by extra time. The league became the AFL in 1990 as it expanded nationally, and interstate clubs have since won many premierships, but the Grand Final remains contracted to the MCG long-term, interrupted only by the COVID-19 pandemic seasons of 2020 (Brisbane) and 2021 (Perth).
