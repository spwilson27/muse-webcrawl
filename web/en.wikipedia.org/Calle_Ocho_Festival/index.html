Calle Ocho Festival

The Calle Ocho Festival (Spanish: Festival de la Calle Ocho) is a one-day street festival held annually in the Little Havana neighbourhood of Miami, Florida. It is the closing event of the Miami Carnaval season and one of the largest Hispanic festivals in the United States, drawing crowds estimated at around a million visitors. The festival takes place along Southwest 8th Street — Calle Ocho — the main street of Little Havana, closing some twenty blocks to traffic.

History

The festival began in 1978, when members of the Kiwanis Club of Little Havana organised a modest celebration intended to showcase Cuban culture and bring together Miami's growing Latin American communities. It grew rapidly into a citywide event celebrating the cultures of all of Latin America and the Caribbean, and the parent Carnaval Miami programme expanded to include concerts, cooking contests, sporting events and the election of a carnival queen.

Music and events

Dozens of stages along the route host live performances of salsa, merengue, reggaeton, bachata and other Latin music, and many of the biggest stars of Latin music have headlined the festival. Food stalls serve dishes from across Latin America. In 1988, festival attendees set a Guinness World Record by forming the world's longest conga line, with 119,986 participants. Other record attempts and traditions have included giant piñatas and massive games of dominoes, a nod to the famous domino games of nearby Máximo Gómez Park.

El Croquetazo and other features

Associated events include a croqueta-eating contest and cooking competitions celebrating Cuban and Latin cuisine. Proceeds support the Kiwanis of Little Havana Foundation's charitable and scholarship programmes.
