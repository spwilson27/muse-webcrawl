Israel, officially the State of Israel, is a country in West Asia, situated on the southeastern shore of the Mediterranean Sea and the northern shore of the Red Sea. It borders Lebanon to the north, Syria to the northeast, Jordan to the east, and Egypt to the southwest, and it adjoins the Palestinian territories of the West Bank and the Gaza Strip. The country has a population of nearly ten million, most of them Jews, with a large Arab minority of about one fifth of the population.

History

The region, historically known as the Land of Israel or Palestine, was the heartland of the ancient kingdoms of Israel and Judah and is central to the Jewish religion. Over the centuries it was ruled by empires including the Assyrian, Babylonian, Persian, Hellenistic, Roman, Byzantine, Arab, Crusader, Mamluk, and Ottoman states, and after the First World War it was administered by Britain under a League of Nations mandate.

The Zionist movement, founded in the late 19th century in response to European antisemitism, promoted Jewish immigration and national self-determination in the land. Following the Holocaust, the United Nations voted in 1947 to partition Mandatory Palestine into Jewish and Arab states. The Jewish leadership accepted; Arab leaders rejected the plan. On 14 May 1948 David Ben-Gurion proclaimed the establishment of the State of Israel. Neighbouring Arab states invaded, and the ensuing 1948 war ended with Israel holding more territory than the partition plan allotted, while hundreds of thousands of Palestinian Arabs fled or were expelled — an event Palestinians call the Nakba ("catastrophe").

Conflicts and peace

Israel fought major wars with its neighbours in 1956, 1967, and 1973. In the Six-Day War of 1967 it captured the West Bank, Gaza Strip, Sinai Peninsula, and Golan Heights; it later returned the Sinai under a 1979 peace treaty with Egypt, and signed a peace treaty with Jordan in 1994. The Oslo Accords of the 1990s created the Palestinian Authority but did not resolve the Israeli–Palestinian conflict, which has continued through uprisings (intifadas), settlement expansion in occupied territory, wars in Gaza, and periodic violence. In 2020 the Abraham Accords normalised relations with the UAE, Bahrain, Morocco, and Sudan. In October 2023, Hamas-led attacks from Gaza killed about 1,200 people in Israel and seized hostages, triggering a devastating Israeli military campaign in Gaza with mass Palestinian casualties and a wider regional confrontation.

Government and society

Israel is a parliamentary democracy; the legislature is the Knesset, and executive power rests with the prime minister. There is no single written constitution; a series of Basic Laws serve a constitutional function. Israel defines itself as a Jewish and democratic state: the 2018 Nation-State Basic Law declared it the nation-state of the Jewish people and made Hebrew the sole official language, while giving Arabic a "special status".

Economy

Israel has a technologically advanced market economy, noted for its high-tech and start-up sector ("Start-up Nation"), pharmaceuticals, agriculture technology, and weapons exports, alongside natural gas production from offshore Mediterranean fields. It is a member of the OECD and has a high standard of living, though income inequality and the cost of housing are persistent concerns.

Geography

Despite its small size, Israel's geography ranges from the coastal plain and Galilee hills to the Negev desert in the south, the Jordan Rift Valley, the Dead Sea — Earth's lowest land elevation — and the port of Eilat on the Red Sea. The climate is Mediterranean on the coast and arid in the south and east.
