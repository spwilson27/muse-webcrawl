The Canadian (train)

The Canadian (French: Le Canadien) is a transcontinental passenger train operated by Via Rail with service between Union Station in Toronto, Ontario, and Pacific Central Station in Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada. The train covers roughly 4,466 kilometres (2,775 mi) and is scheduled to take about four days. Before 1955, the Canadian was a Canadian Pacific Railway (CPR) train between Toronto and Chicago. In 1955, CPR renamed its transcontinental route between Toronto/Montreal and Vancouver the Canadian, with new streamlined stainless-steel equipment.

History

Canadian Pacific introduced the Canadian on 24 April 1955 as its flagship, equipped with Budd Company stainless-steel streamliners including the celebrated Park-series dome-observation cars named for Canadian parks, with mural art by Group of Seven painters. Via Rail took over the service in 1978. In January 1990, federal budget cuts ended the train's original Canadian Pacific southern route through the Rockies at Kicking Horse Pass and Rogers Pass; since then it has run over the more northerly Canadian National route via Edmonton and Jasper.

Service today

The train typically operates two or three times weekly in each direction with sleeper, prestige, dining and dome cars, still using much of the refurbished 1950s Budd equipment — making it one of the last great mid-century streamliners in regular service. Freight-train priority on the single-track CN main line frequently delays it by many hours, and its schedule has been padded accordingly.
