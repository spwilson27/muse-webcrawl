The White House is the official residence and workplace of the president of the United States. Located at 1600 Pennsylvania Avenue NW in Washington, D.C., it has been the residence of every U.S. president since John Adams in 1800, and the term "White House" is often used as a metonym for the president and the executive branch.

History

Designed by Irish-born architect James Hoban, the mansion was built between 1792 and 1800 of painted sandstone. British troops set it on fire in 1814 during the War of 1812, and it was rebuilt under Hoban's supervision. The West Wing, containing the Oval Office, was added under Theodore Roosevelt and expanded later; the East Wing followed in the 1940s. Between 1949 and 1952, under Harry S. Truman, the deteriorating structure was completely dismantled and reconstructed with a new foundation.

The complex

The White House complex includes the Executive Residence, the West Wing (offices of the president, the Cabinet Room and the Situation Room), the East Wing, and the Eisenhower Executive Office Building next door. The grounds cover about 18 acres and include the Rose Garden and Jacqueline Kennedy Garden. It stands in symbolic dialogue with the legislative branch in the Capitol at the other end of Pennsylvania Avenue.
