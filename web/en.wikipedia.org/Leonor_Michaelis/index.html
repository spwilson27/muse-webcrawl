Leonor Michaelis (16 January 1875 – 8 October 1949) was a German biochemist, physical chemist, and physician, known principally for his work with Maud Menten on enzyme kinetics and for his contributions to the theory of pH. The equation they published in 1913 remains a foundation of biochemistry.

Life and career

Michaelis studied medicine at the University of Freiburg and Berlin, receiving his doctorate in 1897. Blocked by the institutional anti-Semitism of German academia from obtaining a professorship, he worked for years in hospital laboratories in Berlin, where he pursued research in his spare time. In 1922 he accepted a chair at Nagoya University in Japan — reputedly the first foreign full professor at a Japanese imperial university — and in 1926 moved to Johns Hopkins University in Baltimore. From 1929 until his retirement he worked at the Rockefeller Institute for Medical Research in New York.

Scientific work

With his collaborator Maud Leonora Menten, Michaelis studied the rate of the invertase-catalysed hydrolysis of sucrose. Their 1913 paper derived what became known as the Michaelis–Menten equation, relating reaction velocity to substrate concentration through the Michaelis constant, Km, and introduced the rigorous use of initial-rate measurements and pH control in enzyme assays.

Michaelis also worked on oxidation–reduction potentials of organic compounds, the theory of buffers and the isoelectric point, membrane potentials, and — late in his career — the chemistry of keratin and hair. The Michaelis–Gutmann body, a structure found in the urinary tract disorder malakoplakia, is named partly after him.
