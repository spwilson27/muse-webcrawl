The Jackson 5 (later known as the Jacksons) is an American pop band composed of members of the Jackson family. Formed in Gary, Indiana, in 1964 by brothers Jackie, Tito, and Jermaine Jackson, with younger brothers Marlon and Michael joining, the group rose to fame with Motown Records at the end of the 1960s.

Career

The group's first four Motown singles — "I Want You Back", "ABC", "The Love You Save", and "I'll Be There" — all reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100 in 1970, an unprecedented run that made them pop sensations and launched Michael Jackson's solo career. Other hits included "Mama's Pearl", "Never Can Say Goodbye", and "Dancing Machine". In 1976 the group (minus Jermaine, with youngest brother Randy) moved to Epic Records and renamed themselves the Jacksons, scoring further hits such as "Enjoy Yourself", "Shake Your Body (Down to the Ground)", and "Can You Feel It".

Legacy

The Jackson 5 were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1997. Their music helped define the Motown sound for a new generation and made them one of the best-selling boy bands of all time.
