Psychologist

A psychologist is a professional who practices psychology and studies mental states, perceptual, cognitive, emotional and social processes and behaviour. Their work often involves the assessment, diagnosis, prevention and treatment of mental disorders, as well as research and teaching.

Types

Clinical psychologists assess and treat mental, emotional and behavioural disorders. Counselling psychologists focus on adjustment and life problems. Other specialities include neuropsychology, health psychology, forensic psychology, industrial–organizational psychology, school psychology and research or experimental psychology. In many jurisdictions the title "psychologist" is legally protected and requires licensure based on a doctoral degree in psychology and supervised experience.

Training

In the United States, clinical psychologists typically complete a PhD or PsyD, while school and industrial–organizational psychologists may qualify with specialist or master's degrees in some jurisdictions. Unlike psychiatrists, psychologists are not medical doctors, though a few US states grant qualified psychologists prescriptive authority after additional training.

Practice

Psychologists work in hospitals, private practice, schools, businesses, government and universities. Their therapeutic approaches include cognitive behavioural therapy, psychodynamic therapy, humanistic therapies and psychological testing.
