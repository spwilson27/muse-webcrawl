Tawhid (Arabic: توحيد, tawḥīd) is the indivisible oneness of God in Islam — the doctrine that God (Allah) is one (al-Ahad) and single (al-Wahid), unique, eternal, and without partner or equal. It is the central and defining doctrine of Islamic theology, expressed in the first part of the shahada: "There is no god but God."

Meaning and Quranic basis

The concept rests on Quranic affirmations such as Sura 112 (Al-Ikhlas): "Say: He is Allah, the One; Allah, the Eternal Refuge. He neither begets nor is born, nor is there to Him any equivalent." Tawhid affirms that God is the sole creator and sustainer of the universe, without offspring, rivals, or intermediaries sharing in divinity. Its denial — associating partners with God (shirk) — is regarded in Islam as the gravest sin.

Categories

Classical and later scholars, especially in the Hanbali and Salafi traditions, commonly analyse tawhid into three categories: tawhid al-rububiyya (oneness of God's lordship and creative acts), tawhid al-uluhiyya (oneness of God in worship — all devotion is owed to Him alone), and tawhid al-asma wa'l-sifat (oneness of God's names and attributes). Sunni theological schools (Ash'ari, Maturidi, Athari) differ in how God's attributes should be understood in relation to His essence, while Shia Islam treats divine unity together with divine justice among the principles of religion. In Sufi metaphysics, especially the doctrine associated with Ibn Arabi, tawhid deepens into wahdat al-wujud, the unity of being.

Significance

Tawhid shapes Islamic worship (direct prayer to God without intercessor saints in the strict sense), art (aniconism and calligraphy), and law and ethics, in which sovereignty and ultimate authority belong to God alone.
