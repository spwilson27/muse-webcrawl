Lucid dream

TypeDream phenomenon
Sleep stageREM sleep
Term coined byFrederik van Eeden (1913)

A lucid dream is a dream in which the dreamer is aware that they are dreaming. During a lucid dream, the dreamer may gain some amount of control over the dream characters, narrative, or environment.

Research

The phenomenon was described by Dutch psychiatrist Frederik van Eeden in 1913 and was scientifically verified beginning in the 1970s and 1980s, notably by Keith Hearne and Stephen LaBerge, who had dreamers signal their awareness with pre-arranged eye movements during REM sleep. Neuroimaging studies associate lucid dreaming with increased activity in frontal regions of the brain.

Induction techniques

Popular techniques include reality testing, the wake back to bed (WBTB) method, and mnemonic induction (MILD). Lucid dreaming has been explored for creativity, motor-skill rehearsal, and treatment of nightmares.
