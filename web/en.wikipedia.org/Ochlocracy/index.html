Ochlocracy

Ochlocracy (Greek: ὀχλοκρατία, okhlokratía; Latin: ochlocratia) or mob rule is the rule of government by the multitude, the mob, or a mass of people, often associated with violence, intimidation, and disregard for law. According to political theorists, ochlocracy is a degenerate form of democracy in which the passions and prejudices of the crowd override deliberation and constitutional restraint.

Etymology and origins

The term originates from Greek okhlos, meaning "mob" or "multitude", and kratos, meaning "rule" or "power". The concept appears in classical political theory, particularly in Polybius's cycle of constitutions (anacyclosis), in which democracy degenerates into ochlocracy when citizens become corrupt and power passes to demagogues who flatter the crowd. Plato and Aristotle also treated rule by an irrational multitude as a perversion of popular government.

Characteristics

Ochlocracy is distinguished from democracy by the absence of lawful procedure: decisions are driven by the immediate will of an assembled or agitated crowd, leaders rule by flattery and intimidation, minorities and opponents may be persecuted without legal protection, and institutions lose their authority to mass sentiment.

Historical examples

Writers have applied the term retrospectively to episodes such as the excesses of the French Revolution's Reign of Terror, lynch mobs, and periods in which street crowds dictated political outcomes. The concept is often used pejoratively rather than as the name of a formal, stable system of government.

Related concepts

Ochlocracy is closely related to demagoguery, majoritarianism without constitutional limits, and mob violence such as rioting and lynching. In Polybius's scheme it is eventually displaced by a monarch or tyrant who restores order, completing the cycle of governments.
