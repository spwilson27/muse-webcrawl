Music of Hungary

The music of Hungary encompasses a distinctive folk tradition, a distinguished classical heritage, and a vibrant popular music scene. Hungarian folk music, with its characteristic pentatonic melodies and dotted rhythms, was famously collected in the early 20th century by Béla Bartók and Zoltán Kodály, who travelled the countryside recording peasant songs on phonograph cylinders and made them the foundation of a national school of composition.

Classical music

Franz Liszt, born in the Kingdom of Hungary, was the pre-eminent pianist-composer of the Romantic era and founded the Academy of Music in Budapest that now bears his name. Other major figures include Ernő Dohnányi, Miklós Rózsa, György Ligeti, György Kurtág and conductor-composers such as Eugene Ormandy and George Szell, who carried Hungarian training abroad. The verbunkos style — recruiting music played by Romani bands — influenced composers from Haydn to Brahms and gave rise to the csárdás dance.

Folk and popular music

The táncház (dance house) movement of the 1970s revived traditional folk dance and music for urban audiences. Hungary's Romani musicians are renowned as performers of both café music and folk repertoire. In popular music, bands such as Omega, Illés and Locomotiv GT achieved fame in the socialist era, and Hungarian performers have been regular participants in the Eurovision Song Contest.
