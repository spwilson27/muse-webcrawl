Thomas James Perkins (January 7, 1932 – June 7, 2016) was an American businessman, venture capitalist and author. He was a co-founder of the venture capital firm Kleiner Perkins (originally Kleiner, Perkins, Caufield & Byers), one of the earliest and most influential venture firms in Silicon Valley, and he played a central role in creating the modern venture capital industry.

Early life and education

Perkins was born in White Plains, New York, and grew up in New Rochelle. He studied electrical engineering and physics at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, graduating in 1953, and later earned an MBA from Harvard Business School. While at MIT he was a member of the Delta Kappa Epsilon fraternity.

Career

After graduation Perkins worked at General Electric and then at Hewlett-Packard, where he became the first general manager of HP's computer divisions and was closely associated with David Packard. At HP he helped build the company's minicomputer business and gained a reputation as a demanding, engineering-driven manager.

In 1972, together with Eugene Kleiner, he founded the venture capital firm Kleiner Perkins in Menlo Park, California, with $8 million in initial capital. The firm was among the pioneers of the "hands-on" venture model, in which partners took active roles in building the companies they funded. Its investments included Genentech, Tandem Computers, Compaq and Sun Microsystems, and Perkins served on the boards of numerous technology companies. He was also a director of Hewlett-Packard during the boardroom scandal of 2006, resigning in protest over the company's pretexting investigation of directors and journalists.

Personal life and sailing

Perkins was an avid ocean sailor. He commissioned the Maltese Falcon, a 289-foot (88 m) square-rigged sailing yacht launched in 2006, which was at the time one of the largest privately owned sailing yachts in the world and notable for its innovative DynaRig of three rotating carbon-fiber masts. He also owned the classic yacht Atlantide and later the sailing yacht Dr. No and the support vessel program. He wrote the novel Sex and the Single Zillionaire (2006), published under his own name by ReganBooks after he had anonymously shopped it as a work by a first-time author.

Perkins was married to Gerd Perkins, who died in 1994, and for a time was married to the novelist Danielle Steel. He died at his home in Tiburon, California, on June 7, 2016, at the age of 84, following a prolonged illness.
