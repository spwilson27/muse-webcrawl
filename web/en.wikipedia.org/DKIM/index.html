DKIM

Illustration: DKIM

DomainKeys Identified Mail (DKIM) is an email authentication method that lets the sender's domain take responsibility for a message by attaching a digital signature, which receiving servers can verify against a public key published in DNS. It is specified in RFC 6376.

Operation

The sending mail server hashes selected headers and the body and signs them with its private key, inserting a DKIM-Signature header. The receiver retrieves the public key from a TXT record under a selector at the signing domain and checks the signature and hashes, detecting tampering in transit.

Use

DKIM complements SPF and DMARC in protecting against spoofing and phishing, and major mailbox providers consider it in spam filtering and sender-reputation decisions. Unlike S/MIME or PGP, DKIM verifies the domain, not the individual author.
