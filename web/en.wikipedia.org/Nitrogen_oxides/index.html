Nitrogen oxides

Nitrogen oxides (NOx) are a group of chemical compounds of nitrogen and oxygen. In environmental and regulatory contexts, NOx refers principally to nitric oxide (NO) and nitrogen dioxide (NO2), gases produced whenever fuel is burned at high temperature — in vehicle engines, power stations, and industrial furnaces — as atmospheric nitrogen and oxygen react.

Environmental effects

NOx emissions contribute to acid rain (through formation of nitric acid), the formation of ground-level ozone and photochemical smog in the presence of sunlight and volatile organic compounds, and the eutrophication of water bodies. Nitrogen dioxide is directly harmful to health, irritating the lungs and aggravating respiratory diseases such as asthma. In the stratosphere, nitrogen oxides from aviation and from nitrous oxide (N2O) decomposition also catalyse ozone destruction.

Control

Emissions are reduced by catalytic converters in petrol vehicles, selective catalytic reduction (SCR) using urea in diesel engines and power plants, low-NOx burners, and exhaust gas recirculation. NOx limits are set by clean-air legislation in the European Union, the United States, and other jurisdictions.
