Daytona 500

The Daytona 500 is a 500-mile (805 km) NASCAR Cup Series motor race held annually at Daytona International Speedway in Daytona Beach, Florida. It is the first race of the Cup Series season and is regarded as the most prestigious and important race in NASCAR, often called "The Great American Race."

History

The first Daytona 500 was held in 1959, the year the speedway opened, and was won by Lee Petty after a photo-finish review that took three days. The race runs 200 laps around the 2.5-mile (4.0 km) tri-oval. Restrictor plates were used from 1988 to 2019 to limit speeds at the superspeedway, producing the tight pack racing for which the event is famous; tapered spacers have since replaced them.

Records and notable moments

Richard Petty holds the record with seven Daytona 500 victories. Famous editions include the 1979 race, the first to be televised live flag-to-flag, which ended with a last-lap crash and a fight between Cale Yarborough and the Allison brothers; Dale Earnhardt's long-awaited win in 1998 after twenty attempts; and the 2001 race in which Earnhardt was killed on the final lap, prompting sweeping safety reforms. The winner receives the Harley J. Earl Trophy.
