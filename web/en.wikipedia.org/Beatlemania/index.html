Beatlemania

Beatlemania was the fanaticism surrounding the English rock band the Beatles from 1963 to 1966. The group's popularity grew to frenzied levels, with concerts marked by crowds of screaming fans, and the phenomenon first erupted in the United Kingdom in late 1963 before sweeping the United States and the rest of the world in 1964, launching the "British Invasion".

Origins

The term "Beatlemania" came into wide use in the British press in the autumn of 1963, following the band's appearance on Sunday Night at the London Palladium and a celebrated Royal Variety Performance. Scenes of screaming, fainting fans accompanied the Beatles' tours, and their records dominated the charts. In February 1964 the band's appearance on The Ed Sullivan Show drew an estimated 73 million American viewers, and Beatlemania became a global cultural event.

Characteristics

Beatlemania was notable for the intensity of fans' emotional displays, the band's distinctive haircuts and fashion influence, and an unprecedented merchandising industry. The screaming at concerts was so loud that the band could barely hear themselves play, one factor in their decision to stop touring in 1966 after a final concert at Candlestick Park, San Francisco.

Legacy

Historians and sociologists regard Beatlemania as a landmark in youth culture and celebrity fandom, reflecting post-war affluence, the rise of the teenager and television's new reach. The phenomenon influenced later fan cultures around pop acts and remains shorthand for intense celebrity devotion.
