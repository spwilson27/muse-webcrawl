Sierra Leonean cuisine

Sierra Leonean cuisine consists of the cooking traditions and practices of Sierra Leone in West Africa. It follows the general pattern of West African cuisine, built around starchy staples — above all rice, the country's staple food — served with rich, spicy stews known locally as "plasas" or sauces, typically thickened with groundnuts (peanuts), palm oil, and leafy greens.

Staples and stews

Rice is eaten at virtually every meal; a common saying holds that a meal without rice is not a meal at all. The most celebrated stew is cassava leaf plasas, made from finely ground cassava leaves cooked with palm oil, fish or meat, and groundnuts, often regarded as the national dish. Other common sauces include groundnut stew (peanut stew), potato leaves, okra soup, binni (black-eyed pea) stew, and pepper soup.

Fufu, made from fermented cassava dough, is served with bitterleaf and other soups. Along the coast, fish — fresh, smoked, or dried — is a major protein source, while inland, beef, goat, and chicken are used. Palm oil gives many dishes their characteristic reddish colour and rich flavour.

Snacks and drinks

Street foods include akara (fried bean cakes), fried plantain, roast corn, and "benni cakes" made from sesame seeds and sugar. Popular drinks include ginger beer (a fiery, home-brewed soft drink), palm wine, and poyo.
