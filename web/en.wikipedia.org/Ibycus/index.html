Ibycus

Ibycus (Greek: Ἴβυκος; fl. 2nd half of the 6th century BC) was an Ancient Greek lyric poet, included among the canonical nine lyric poets prized by the scholars of Hellenistic Alexandria. He was born at Rhegium in Magna Graecia and spent much of his career at the court of the tyrant Polycrates of Samos.

Poetry

Ibycus composed choral lyric, narrative poems on mythological themes (including a long poem to Polycrates), and passionate love poetry. His surviving fragments — preserved through quotation and on papyri from Oxyrhynchus — show a style bridging the monodic tradition of Stesichorus and the elaborate choral odes of Pindar and Bacchylides.

Death and legend

According to a famous legend, Ibycus was murdered by robbers near Corinth and, dying, called upon a flock of passing cranes to avenge him; the cranes later circled the theatre where the murderers sat, prompting one to betray himself. The story — "the cranes of Ibycus" — became proverbial and inspired Schiller's ballad Die Kraniche des Ibykus.
