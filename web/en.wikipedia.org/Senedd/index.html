The Senedd (Welsh: Senedd Cymru; English: Welsh Parliament) is the devolved, unicameral legislature of Wales. It was established in 1999 as the National Assembly for Wales following a referendum in 1997, and was renamed the Senedd in May 2020, also gaining the formal English name "Welsh Parliament". It legislates on devolved matters including health, education, transport, local government and much of the environment, and can vary income tax rates in Wales.

Members and elections

The Senedd has 60 elected members, known as Members of the Senedd (MSs). Elections are normally held every five years. Members are elected under the Additional Member System: 40 representing single-member constituencies and 20 from five electoral regions. The Senedd Cymru (Members and Elections) Act 2024 provides for expansion to 96 members elected by closed-list proportional representation from the 2026 election.

The building

The legislature meets in the Senedd building in Cardiff Bay, designed by Richard Rogers and opened by Queen Elizabeth II on St David's Day, 1 March 2006. The building is noted for its environmentally conscious design and its debating chamber (siambr) arranged in a circular layout beneath a sweeping wooden roof.

Presiding officer

The presiding officer is known as the Llywydd. The First Minister of Wales leads the Welsh Government, which is formed from the Senedd's membership.
