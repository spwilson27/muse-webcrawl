Community ecology
Field: ecology
Subject: assemblages of interacting species in a place and time
Key concepts: competition, predation, mutualism, succession, food webs

Community ecology is the study of the organisation and functioning of communities—assemblages of populations of different species living in the same area—and of the interactions among them, including competition, predation, herbivory, parasitism and mutualism.

Structure and diversity

Community ecologists describe communities by species richness, evenness and diversity indices, and ask what maintains diversity: niche differentiation, disturbance, keystone predators and dispersal among patches all play roles.

Interactions and food webs

Species interactions link populations into food webs. Removal experiments, famously Robert Paine's work on the starfish Pisaster, revealed keystone species whose impact far exceeds their abundance. Trophic cascades describe indirect effects propagating through feeding levels.

Change over time

Communities change through ecological succession after disturbance, from pioneer species toward later assemblages. Modern community ecology also addresses assembly rules, neutral theory, metacommunities and the responses of communities to climate change and biological invasions.
