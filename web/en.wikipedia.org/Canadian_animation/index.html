Canadian animation has been an important part of Canada's film industry since the mid-twentieth century, distinguished by the experimental short films of the National Film Board of Canada (NFB) and by a large commercial sector producing television animation for domestic and international broadcasters.

The National Film Board

Founded in 1939, the NFB became a world centre for auteur animation under Norman McLaren, whose hand-drawn-on-film and pixilation techniques won an Academy Award for Neighbours (1952). Later NFB animators included Ryan Larkin (Walking), Co Hoedeman (The Sand Castle), Cordell Barker (The Cat Came Back) and Chris Landreth, whose Ryan (2004) won the Oscar for Best Animated Short.

Commercial industry

Toronto-based Nelvana, founded in 1971, became one of the world's largest children's animation producers, known for series such as Franklin and Babar and the film Rock & Rule. Studios in Vancouver, Ottawa, Montreal and Halifax produce series for networks and streaming services, supported by Canadian content rules and tax incentives. Canadian animators also contributed early computer-animation milestones, and festivals such as the Ottawa International Animation Festival, founded in 1976, anchor the community.
