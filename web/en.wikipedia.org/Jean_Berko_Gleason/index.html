Jean Berko Gleason

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Jean Berko Gleason (born 1931) is an American psycholinguist and professor emerita of psychology at Boston University. She is known for her research on children's language acquisition, including the creation of the Wug Test, one of the most famous experiments in developmental psycholinguistics.

Career

Gleason earned her bachelor's degree in English and history and her master's and PhD in linguistics and psychology at Harvard University, studying under Roger Brown. She spent most of her career at Boston University, where she directed research on how children acquire language and how parents communicate with children.

The Wug Test

In her classic 1958 study "The child's learning of English morphology", Berko presented preschool and school-age children with nonsense words in pictures — for example, a small creature called a "wug" — and asked them to complete sentences such as "This is a wug. Now there is another one. There are two of them. There are two ___." Children who answered "wugs" demonstrated that they had internalised English plural-formation rules and could apply them to words they had never heard, rather than merely memorising forms. The Wug Test became landmark evidence in debates about the nature of language acquisition and a standard tool in the field.

Other research

Gleason's research also addressed fathers' speech to children, children's acquisition of polite forms and routines (such as "trick or treat" and birthday songs), aphasia, and language in normal aging. She is the editor (with Nan Bernstein Ratner) of the widely used textbook The Development of Language and of Psycholinguistics. She has appeared in popular media, including a segment of 60 Minutes and the PBS series The Human Language Series.
