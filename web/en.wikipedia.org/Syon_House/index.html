Syon House is the west London residence of the Duke of Northumberland. A Grade I listed building, it stands within Syon Park at Isleworth in the London Borough of Hounslow, on the bank of the River Thames opposite Kew Gardens. The house is famous for its interiors by Robert Adam, among the finest achievements of early neoclassical design in Britain.

History

The estate takes its name from Syon Abbey, a wealthy Bridgettine monastery founded by Henry V in 1415 and dissolved under Henry VIII in 1539. The present house was built in the 1550s for Edward Seymour, 1st Duke of Somerset and Lord Protector, and later came to the Percy family through marriage. Catherine Howard was kept prisoner at Syon in 1541–42, and tradition holds that Henry VIII's coffin burst open while resting overnight at Syon on its journey to Windsor, an omen later woven into legend.

Adam interiors and grounds

In the 1760s the 1st Duke and Duchess of Northumberland commissioned Robert Adam to remodel the interior. The resulting suite—the Great Hall, Ante Room, Dining Room, Red Drawing Room, and the spectacular Long Gallery, over 40 metres in length—is celebrated for its coloured marbles, scagliola columns, and classical ornament. Lancelot "Capability" Brown landscaped the park in the 1760s–70s. In 1830s the conservatory, the Great Conservatory designed by Charles Fowler, was built; its dome of glass and iron became one of the first of its kind.

Today

Syon House remains the London home of the Duke of Northumberland, whose main seat is Alnwick Castle. The house and gardens are open to the public seasonally, and the house is a popular film location and wedding venue. The park is a Site of Metropolitan Importance for Nature Conservation, with tidal meadows unique on this stretch of the Thames.
