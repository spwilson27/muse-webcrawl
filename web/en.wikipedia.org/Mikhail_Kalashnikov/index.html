Mikhail Kalashnikov

Mikhail Timofeyevich Kalashnikov
Born: 10 November 1919, Kurya, Russia
Died: 23 December 2013, Izhevsk, Russia
Known for: Designer of the AK-47 assault rifle
Rank: Lieutenant general

Mikhail Timofeyevich Kalashnikov (10 November 1919 – 23 December 2013) was a Russian lieutenant general, inventor, military engineer and small-arms designer. He is best known for designing the AK-47 assault rifle and its descendants, among the most widely produced firearms in history.

Early life and career

Born into a peasant family in Kurya, Altai Krai, Kalashnikov worked as a mechanic before being drafted into the Red Army in 1938. He served as a tank commander and was seriously wounded in the Battle of Bryansk in 1941. While recovering in hospital, he began sketching ideas for a new automatic weapon after hearing soldiers complain about their rifles.

The AK-47

After the war Kalashnikov entered a small-arms design competition. His design was adopted in 1949 as the Avtomat Kalashnikova (AK-47), a gas-operated selective-fire rifle chambered in 7.62×39mm. Simple, rugged and cheap to manufacture, the AK family — including the AKM and AK-74 — was produced in enormous numbers and adopted by armies and armed groups worldwide; estimates of total production exceed 100 million.

Later life

Kalashnikov continued working at the Izhmash (later Kalashnikov Concern) factory in Izhevsk until late in life and rose to the rank of lieutenant general. He received numerous state honours, including Hero of Socialist Labour and Hero of the Russian Federation. He died in Izhevsk in 2013, aged 94. Late in life he expressed regret about the deaths caused by his invention, writing in a 2012 letter to the Russian Orthodox Church that he felt spiritual pain over it.
