CIMMYT (Spanish: Centro Internacional de Mejoramiento de Maíz y Trigo — International Maize and Wheat Improvement Center) is a non-profit research-for-development organisation dedicated to improving the productivity and profitability of maize and wheat farming systems for food security in developing countries. Headquartered at El Batán, near Texcoco in Mexico, it is one of the research centres of the CGIAR consortium.

History

CIMMYT grew out of a cooperative agricultural research programme begun in 1943 between the Mexican government and the Rockefeller Foundation. The programme's wheat work, led from the 1940s by Norman Borlaug, developed semi-dwarf, disease-resistant wheat varieties that transformed Mexican agriculture and were then transferred to India and Pakistan in the 1960s, becoming a central engine of the Green Revolution. Borlaug received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1970 for this work. CIMMYT was formally established as an international centre in 1966.

Work

CIMMYT maintains one of the world's largest maize and wheat germplasm collections in its seed bank, distributes improved varieties and breeding lines to partners in more than 100 countries, and conducts research on sustainable intensification, drought and heat tolerance, disease resistance (including wheat stem rust Ug99), conservation agriculture, and biofortified crops. Varieties derived from CIMMYT breeding are grown on tens of millions of hectares globally.
