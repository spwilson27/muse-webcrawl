Gujeolpan

Gujeolpan (Korean: 구절판) is a traditional Korean dish consisting of eight kinds of delicately prepared ingredients served in an octagonal platter divided into nine sections — the name literally means "nine-section platter". The centre section holds small wheat flour crepes (milssam), while the eight outer sections contain julienned and seasoned ingredients such as beef, egg yolk and egg white strips, shiitake mushrooms, carrots, cucumber, bean sprouts and other vegetables.

Preparation and eating

Diners place a selection of fillings onto a crepe, roll it up, and eat it with a dipping sauce, traditionally a mustard or soy-based sauce. The dish demands time-consuming knife work: every ingredient is cut into fine, uniform strips, and each is cooked separately so its colour and flavour remain distinct.

History

Gujeolpan was originally a dish of the Korean royal court cuisine of the Joseon dynasty and was served on special occasions and banquets. The lacquered wooden or mother-of-pearl inlaid platters used for serving are themselves valued craft objects. In modern Korea the dish is associated with celebrations, holidays and fine traditional restaurants.
