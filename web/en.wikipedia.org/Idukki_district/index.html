Idukki district

Idukki district is one of the fourteen districts of Kerala state, India, in the southern Western Ghats. Formed on 26 January 1972, it is the second-largest district in Kerala by area (about 4,358 km²), but it has the state's lowest population density, as much of its rugged, forested terrain lies between 450 and 1,200 metres above sea level. The district headquarters is at Painavu.

Geography and economy

Idukki is renowned for its spice gardens — cardamom, pepper and tea plantations around towns such as Munnar, Thekkady and Kumily — and for its wildlife sanctuaries, including Periyar National Park and Eravikulam National Park. Anamudi, the highest peak in South India (2,695 m), lies on its border region.

Power generation

The district hosts a large share of Kerala's hydroelectric capacity. The Idukki Dam on the Periyar River, completed in 1973, is one of the tallest arch dams in Asia (about 169 m high), and the associated power station has an installed capacity of 780 MW. Other projects include Mullaperiyar and Mattupetty dams. The Idukki reservoir and its backwaters are major attractions, as are the hill station of Munnar and the Kurinjimala sanctuary, famed for the mass flowering of neelakurinji.
