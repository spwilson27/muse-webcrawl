Shooting sports

Shooting sports are a group of competitive and recreational sports involving proficiency tests of accuracy, precision and speed using various types of ranged weapons — principally firearms (handguns, rifles and shotguns) and air guns, and in some definitions bows and crossbows. Shooting has been an Olympic sport since the first modern Games in 1896, and is governed internationally by the International Shooting Sport Federation (ISSF, founded 1907).

Disciplines

Bullseye and target shooting

In ISSF rifle and pistol events, shooters fire at stationary paper or electronic targets at set distances (10 m air weapons; 25 m and 50 m pistol; 50 m and 300 m rifle), in standing, kneeling and prone positions. Scoring is by the value of the ring struck; finals formats add elimination drama. Related precision disciplines include benchrest shooting, fullbore target rifle, and the running target event.

Shotgun

Clay target shooting includes Olympic trap and skeet, in which competitors break clay discs launched at high speed, and sporting clays, which simulates game shooting across varied stands. Double trap was formerly Olympic.

Dynamic and field disciplines

Practical shooting (IPSC/USPSA) scores accuracy against time on movement courses with handgun, rifle and shotgun; cowboy action shooting, 3-gun, and precision rifle series (PRS) events extend the format. Biathlon combines cross-country skiing with rifle shooting. Field target and metallic silhouette are shot with air and rimfire weapons at knock-down animal-shaped targets.

History

Organised target shooting grew from 19th-century militia and rifle-volunteer movements — Britain's National Rifle Association (1859) and the Wimbledon (later Bisley) meetings, the American NRA (1871), and German and Swiss Schützen traditions. Baron Pierre de Coubertin, himself a pistol shooter, included nine shooting events at Athens 1896. The first ISSF World Championships followed in 1897; women's events entered the Olympics fully from 1984.

Safety and participation

Shooting sports operate under strict range safety rules — muzzle discipline, actions open and unloaded except on the firing point, and hearing and eye protection — and statistically rank among the safer organised sports. Participation is broad worldwide, though firearms licensing law strongly shapes which disciplines flourish in each country.
