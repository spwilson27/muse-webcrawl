Sicilian wine

Sicilian wine is wine made on the island of Sicily in Italy. The region has a long history of viticulture, with viticulture introduced by Phoenician and Greek settlers. Sicily is Italy's largest wine-producing region by vineyard area and is best known internationally for its fortified Marsala wine and increasingly for dry wines from native grape varieties such as Nero d'Avola and the Etna DOC.

History

Wine production in Sicily dates to antiquity; the island supplied wine to Rome and later to markets across the Mediterranean. In the 18th century the English merchant John Woodhouse developed Marsala as an export wine modelled on Port and Madeira. In the late 20th century Sicilian production was dominated by bulk wine, but from the 1990s onward producers shifted toward quality bottled wines, both from indigenous grapes and international varieties.

Grape varieties

Important red varieties include Nero d'Avola, Nerello Mascalese, Frappato, and Perricone. White varieties include Grillo, Catarratto, Inzolia, Carricante, and Grecanico. International varieties such as Syrah, Merlot, and Chardonnay are also widely planted.

Wine regions

The Etna DOC, on the volcanic slopes of Mount Etna, is known for high-altitude reds from Nerello Mascalese and whites from Carricante. Other significant zones include Cerasuolo di Vittoria (Sicily's only DOCG), Noto, Menfi, and the island of Pantelleria, famous for sweet Passito wines made from dried Zibibbo grapes.
