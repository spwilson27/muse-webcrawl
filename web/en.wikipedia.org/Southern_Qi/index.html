Southern Qi

Southern Qi (dark pink) in 497

The Southern Qi (479–502) was the second of the Southern dynasties in China, followed by the Liang dynasty. During its 23-year history, the dynasty was plagued by instability, as after the death of the capable and frugal founders Emperor Gao and Emperor Wu, grandsons of Emperor Wu vied with one another to rule the empire.

History

The dynasty was founded by Xiao Daocheng, a general of the preceding Liu Song dynasty, who in 479 forced Emperor Shun of Liu Song to abdicate and took the throne himself as Emperor Gao. The capital was Jiankang (modern Nanjing). The dynasty's territory roughly comprised the southern half of China, in opposition to the Northern Wei in the north.

After the founder's death, his son Emperor Wu reigned competently until 493. Thereafter the throne passed through a rapid succession of youthful and violent rulers, including Xiao Zhaoye, Xiao Zhaowen, and the notorious marquis of Donghun, Xiao Baojuan, whose extravagance and arbitrary killings provoked rebellion. In 502 the general Xiao Yan overthrew the dynasty and founded the Liang dynasty.

Culture

Despite its political instability, the Southern Qi period saw continued development of the literary and artistic culture of the southern dynasties, and the great tomb sculptures of the dynasty — stone qlins and pillars outside Nanjing — survive as notable examples of early medieval Chinese art.
