Rikken Seiyūkai

The Rikken Seiyūkai (立憲政友会, "Friends of Constitutional Government") was one of the main political parties in the Empire of Japan during the pre-war period. Founded on 15 September 1900 by Itō Hirobumi, it dominated Japanese party politics for much of the period until its dissolution in 1940.

Itō Hirobumi founded the Seiyūkai in 1900 as a pro-government party.

History

Itō, a leading figure of the Meiji oligarchy, created the Seiyūkai as an alliance of bureaucrats, local notables and former members of the Liberal Party, giving the government a reliable base in the House of Representatives. The party first formed a cabinet under Itō in 1900. Its long-serving leader Hara Takashi became prime minister in 1918, heading the first full party cabinet. During the era of "Taishō democracy" the Seiyūkai alternated in power with its rival, the Rikken Minseitō, in the so-called two-party system.

Factional splits in the 1930s, the growing influence of the military, and pressure for national unity weakened the party; in 1940 it dissolved itself into the Imperial Rule Assistance Association. After the war, many of its personnel and networks fed into the conservative parties that merged into the Liberal Democratic Party.

Ideology

The Seiyūkai was broadly conservative and pro-government, favouring cooperation between the bureaucracy and party cabinets, expansion of public works in the provinces, and a strong military budget tempered by fiscal pragmatism.
