Lactobacillus acidophilus

Lactobacillus acidophilus
DomainBacteria
GenusLactobacillus
HabitatHuman and animal gastrointestinal tract, mouth, vagina

Lactobacillus acidophilus (from Latin: "acid-loving milk-bacillus") is a species of gram-positive lactic acid bacteria in the genus Lactobacillus. It ferments sugars into lactic acid, grows optimally around 37 °C, and occurs naturally in the human gut, mouth and vagina.

Characteristics

The bacterium is a homofermentative, microaerophilic rod that tolerates the acidic conditions it creates, growing at pH values below 5. Its genome of about 2 million base pairs was sequenced in 2005.

Uses

L. acidophilus is widely used in the dairy industry, including in acidophilus milk and as an adjunct culture in some yogurts, and is one of the most common probiotic organisms in dietary supplements. Some strains are credited with helping lactose digestion and gut health, though clinical evidence for many marketed claims is limited and strain-specific; health authorities such as the EFSA have rejected most probiotic health claims for lack of substantiation.

Safety

It is generally regarded as safe for healthy people; rare cases of bacteraemia have been reported in severely immunocompromised patients.
