Theatre in the round

Theatre in the round, also known as arena theatre or central staging, is a theatrical staging configuration in which the audience surrounds the stage on all sides. Actors enter and exit through gaps in the seating, and scenery is kept minimal so as not to block sightlines.

Characteristics

The form creates an intimate relationship between performers and audience and demands blocking that plays to all directions. Lighting is typically mounted above the stage. Ancient antecedents include Greek and Roman performance spaces and medieval pageant staging; the modern movement is associated with figures such as Margo Jones, who opened Theatre '47 in Dallas in 1947.

Notable venues

Examples include the Stephen Joseph Theatre in Scarborough, the Royal Exchange in Manchester — the largest theatre in the round in Britain — and the Arena Stage in Washington, D.C.
