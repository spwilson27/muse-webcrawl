Three-surface aircraft

A three-surface aircraft is a fixed-wing airplane with a main wing, a tailplane, and an additional foreplane (canard) surface. The configuration combines features of the conventional layout and the canard layout, giving the designer three lifting surfaces with which to balance the aircraft and distribute lift.

Design

By sharing lift among three surfaces, each surface can be smaller, and the foreplane can be used to improve stall behaviour, reduce trim drag, or ease loading flexibility. Examples include the Piaggio P.180 Avanti, whose forward wing, main wing, and T-tail all provide lift, and experimental and military designs such as the Sukhoi Su-47, which combines canard foreplanes with a conventional tail.

History

Some early pioneers experimented with three surfaces; the Voisin-Farman I of 1907 combined a biplane main wing with surfaces fore and aft. The layout has remained uncommon, appearing mainly in specialized business aircraft and research types.
