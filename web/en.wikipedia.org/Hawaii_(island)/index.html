Hawaii (Hawaiian: Hawaiʻi) is the largest island in the United States, located in the state of Hawaii in the Pacific Ocean. It is the southeasternmost of the Hawaiian Islands, a chain of volcanic islands in the North Pacific Ocean. With an area of 4,028 square miles (10,430 km²), it has 63% of the Hawaiian archipelago's combined landmass; even so, it has only about 13% of the state's population, which makes it the least populous county relative to its size in the United States. The island is coterminous with Hawaii County, whose seat is Hilo. The island is often called the Big Island or Hawaii Island to distinguish it from the state.

Geology and geography

The island was formed by five shield volcanoes: Kohala (extinct), Mauna Kea (dormant), Hualālai (active), Mauna Loa (active), and Kīlauea (active), which erupted in sequence as the Pacific Plate moved over the Hawaii hotspot. Mauna Kea, at 13,803 feet (4,207 m) above sea level, is the highest point in the state; measured from its base on the ocean floor, it is taller than Mount Everest. Mauna Loa is the world's largest subaerial volcano by volume. Kīlauea erupted almost continuously from 1983 to 2018, adding new land to the island's southeast coast, and a major eruption in 2018 destroyed hundreds of homes in the Puna district.

History

Polynesian voyagers settled the island well over a thousand years ago. It was the home island of Kamehameha I, who unified the Hawaiian Islands by 1810 and founded the Kingdom of Hawaii. Captain James Cook was killed at Kealakekua Bay on the island in 1779. In the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, sugar plantations and cattle ranching (including the vast Parker Ranch) shaped its economy; today tourism, coffee growing (Kona coffee), and astronomy are major industries.

Attractions

Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park protects Kīlauea and Mauna Loa. The summit of Mauna Kea hosts some of the world's most important astronomical observatories, taking advantage of the dry, clear, high-altitude air. The island's east side is wet and tropical (Hilo averages over 3,200 mm of rain a year), while the leeward Kona coast is dry and sunny.
