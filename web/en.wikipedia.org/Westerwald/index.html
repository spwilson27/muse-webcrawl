The Westerwald (German pronunciation: [ˈvɛstɐvalt]; literally "Western Forest") is a low mountain range on the right bank of the river Rhine in the German federal states of Rhineland-Palatinate, Hesse and North Rhine-Westphalia. It is part of the Rhenish Massif (Rheinisches Schiefergebirge). Its highest elevation, at 657.3 metres, is the Fuchskaute in the High Westerwald.

The Westerwald is known for its basalt deposits, its deposits of clay which have been mined since historical times for the ceramics industry, and its slate and iron ore. The region is famous for its pottery — the so-called Kannenbäckerland ("jug bakers' land") around Höhr-Grenzhausen and Ransbach-Baumbach is one of the largest clay deposits in Europe and a centre of German stoneware production, producing the distinctive blue-grey salt-glazed pottery decorated with cobalt blue.

Geography

The Westerwald lies roughly between the cities of Frankfurt am Main to the south, Cologne to the northwest, and Siegen to the northeast. It is bounded by the river valleys of the Rhine in the west, the Sieg in the north, the Dill in the east, and the Lahn in the south. The range is divided into three natural regions: the Lower Westerwald (Vorderwesterwald) in the west, the Upper Westerwald, and the High Westerwald (Hoher Westerwald) in the northeast, which contains the highest ground.

Much of the region is covered in forest and used for agriculture. Important towns include Montabaur, Westerburg, Hachenburg, Altenkirchen, Bad Marienberg, Rennerod and Dierdorf. The area is crossed by the A3 motorway (Cologne–Frankfurt) with an ICE high-speed railway line, which stops at Montabaur and Limburg Süd.

History

Traces of Celtic settlement have been found in the Westerwald, and the region was later settled by Germanic tribes. During the Middle Ages the area was divided among numerous small territories, including the counties of Sayn, Wied and Isenburg, and the Electorate of Trier. Basalt quarrying, iron mining and smelting shaped the economy for centuries, and the Westerwald's clay pits supplied potters since the late Middle Ages.

The Westerwald is celebrated in German folk song, most famously in the "Westerwaldlied" ("Oh du schöner Westerwald"), associated with the region's cool winds — the Westerwald is proverbially windy, as expressed in the saying that the wind blows cold there even in summer.
