Stir Crazy (film)

Directed by Sidney Poitier
Starring Gene Wilder, Richard Pryor
Release date December 12, 1980
Country United States

Gene Wilder and Richard Pryor in Stir Crazy

Stir Crazy is a 1980 American comedy film directed by Sidney Poitier, written by Bruce Jay Friedman, and starring Gene Wilder and Richard Pryor as two unemployed friends who, after being framed for a bank robbery, are sentenced to 125 years in prison. It was the second of four films pairing Wilder and Pryor, following Silver Streak (1976).

Plot

Playwright Skip Donahue (Wilder) and actor Harry Monroe (Pryor) leave New York City for Hollywood, stopping in Arizona where they take jobs promoting a bank while dressed in woodpecker costumes. When two criminals steal the costumes and rob the bank, Skip and Harry are wrongly convicted and sent to prison. Inside, Skip discovers an unexpected talent for riding a mechanical bull, and the warden recruits him to compete in the prison rodeo. The pair use the rodeo as cover for an escape attempt, during which the real robbers are exposed.

Reception

The film was a major commercial success, grossing over $100 million at the box office and becoming one of the highest-grossing films of 1980. It cemented the Wilder–Pryor partnership as one of the most popular comedy pairings of the era.

Cast

Gene Wilder as Skip Donahue; Richard Pryor as Harry Monroe; Georg Stanford Brown as Rory Schultebrand; JoBeth Williams as Meredith; Craig T. Nelson as Deputy Ward Wilson; Barry Corbin as Warden Walter Beatty.
