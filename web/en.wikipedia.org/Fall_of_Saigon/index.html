Fall of Saigon

Date30 April 1975
LocationSaigon, South Vietnam (now Ho Chi Minh City)
ResultNorth Vietnamese victory; end of the Vietnam War
A North Vietnamese tank at the Independence Palace marked the war's end.

The Fall of Saigon was the capture of Saigon, the capital of South Vietnam, by North Vietnam on 30 April 1975. The event marked the end of the Vietnam War and the beginning of a transition period that culminated in the formal reunification of Vietnam under communist rule the following year.

Background

After the Paris Peace Accords of 1973 withdrew US combat forces, North Vietnam launched a major offensive in the spring of 1975. South Vietnamese forces collapsed rapidly through the Central Highlands and the northern provinces; President Nguyễn Văn Thiệu resigned on 21 April, and his successor Dương Văn Minh sought a negotiated settlement that Hanoi rejected.

Evacuation and capture

As North Vietnamese forces closed in, the United States conducted Operation Frequent Wind (29–30 April), the largest helicopter evacuation in history, airlifting roughly 7,000 people — remaining American personnel and at-risk South Vietnamese — from the US embassy and other points to ships offshore. On the morning of 30 April, North Vietnamese tanks entered the city; one famously crashed through the gates of the Independence Palace, where Minh announced surrender. Saigon was subsequently renamed Ho Chi Minh City. The fall prompted a large exodus of refugees in the following years. See also Vietnam War and Operation Frequent Wind.
