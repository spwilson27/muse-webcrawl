Drill bit

Drill bits are cutting tools used in a drill to remove material and create holes, almost always of circular cross-section. Bits come in many sizes, shapes, and materials, and create holes in wood, metal, plastic, masonry, and other materials.

Types

  • Twist bits — helical-fluted general-purpose bits for metal, wood, and plastic.
  • Spade (flat) bits — paddle-shaped bits for rough holes in wood.
  • Auger bits — screw-tipped bits that pull themselves into wood.
  • Forstner bits — cut clean, flat-bottomed holes in wood.
  • Masonry bits — carbide-tipped bits used with hammer action in brick, stone, and concrete.
  • Hole saws — cylindrical saws for large-diameter holes.
  • Step bits — conical bits that drill graduated hole sizes in sheet material.
  • Countersink and counterbore bits — shape the top of a hole to accept screw heads.

Materials and coatings

Bits are commonly made from high-speed steel (HSS); cobalt steel and solid carbide bits are used for harder materials. Coatings such as black oxide and titanium nitride reduce friction and extend life. The size of a bit may be given in metric units, fractional inches, or gauge numbers.

Sharpening and care

Dull bits can be resharpened by grinding the cutting edges at the correct point angle (118° is standard for general work). Drilling at appropriate speeds, using cutting lubricant for metal, and clearing chips frequently all prolong bit life.
