Formation (military)

In military science, a formation is the arrangement of troops, vehicles, aircraft, or ships in a coordinated manner, adopted for movement, battle, ceremony, or other purposes. Formations are also, in another sense, large military units composed of subordinate units, such as divisions or brigades—but the tactical arrangement sense is the primary one.

Tactical formations

Infantry formations historically included the phalanx of the ancient Greeks, the Roman legion's lines and wedges, the shield wall, and the musket-era line and column. Modern infantry typically move and fight in formations such as the file, line, wedge, echelon, and diamond, adapted to terrain and the threat. Armoured formations use similar geometries, while aircraft fly in formations such as the finger-four, and naval vessels steam in line-ahead or screen formations.

Purpose

Formations concentrate firepower, maintain command and control, protect flanks, and allow mutual support between elements. The choice of formation balances speed, security, and flexibility; units change formation as the tactical situation evolves.
