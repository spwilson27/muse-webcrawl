West End theatre is mainstream professional theatre staged in the large theatres in and near the West End of London. Along with New York City's Broadway theatre, West End theatre is usually considered to represent the highest level of commercial theatre in the English-speaking world. Seeing a West End show is a common tourist activity in London.

History

London theatre dates to the Restoration: the Theatre Royal, Drury Lane opened in 1663 and remains in use. The theatre district grew through the 19th century with the building of venues along Shaftesbury Avenue and the Strand. In the 20th century the West End became known for musicals as well as straight plays, and long runs became common: Agatha Christie's The Mousetrap has run continuously since 1952, and musicals such as Les Misérables and The Phantom of the Opera ran for decades.

Today

There are around 40 major West End venues, most owned by groups such as Delfont Mackintosh, LW Theatres, and Nimax. Annual attendances before the COVID-19 pandemic exceeded 15 million, with box-office revenues rivalling Broadway. The Society of London Theatre presents the annual Laurence Olivier Awards, the West End's principal honours.
