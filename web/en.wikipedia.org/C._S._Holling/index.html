C. S. Holling

Crawford Stanley "Buzz" Holling (December 6, 1930 – August 16, 2019) was a Canadian ecologist, one of the founders of ecological economics and of the theory of ecological resilience. He is remembered for introducing the concepts of resilience and the adaptive cycle into ecology and resource management.

Career

Holling earned his PhD from the University of Toronto and worked for the Canadian Forest Service, where his studies of spruce budworm outbreaks led to pioneering models of predator–prey dynamics, including the functional response curves (Types I, II and III) that bear his name. He later directed the Institute of Animal Resource Ecology at the University of British Columbia and the International Institute for Applied Systems Analysis in Austria.

Contributions

His 1973 paper "Resilience and Stability of Ecological Systems" contrasted engineering resilience with ecological resilience. With Lance Gunderson he developed panarchy theory, describing how systems change through adaptive cycles of growth, conservation, release and reorganisation. He founded the Resilience Alliance in 1999.
