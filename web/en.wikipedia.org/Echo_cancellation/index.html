Echo cancellation

Echo cancellation is a method in telephony and audio signal processing that removes echo from a voice communication link to improve quality and allow full-duplex conversation. An adaptive filter models the echo path, synthesises a replica of the echo and subtracts it from the return signal.

Origins of echo

Line echo arises at hybrid circuits converting between two-wire and four-wire telephone circuits, where impedance mismatch reflects part of the far-end signal back. Acoustic echo arises when a loudspeaker's output is picked up by a microphone, as in speakerphones and hands-free systems; long network delays make such echoes especially disruptive.

How it works

The canceller builds an adaptive finite-impulse-response model of the echo path, continuously updated using the incoming far-end signal as a reference. The estimated echo is subtracted from the send path, and a residual echo suppressor and comfort-noise generator clean up what remains. Convergence must be fast, but the filter must freeze or adapt carefully during double-talk, when both parties speak at once.

Modern use

Echo cancellation is integral to VoIP software, conference systems, smart speakers and mobile phones, where acoustic echo cancellers handle echo tails of hundreds of milliseconds.
