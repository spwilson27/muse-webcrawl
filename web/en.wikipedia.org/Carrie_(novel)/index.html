Carrie is a 1974 American horror novel by Stephen King. It was his first published novel, released on April 5, 1974, with a first print run of 30,000 copies. Set primarily in the then-future year of 1979, it revolves around the titular Carrie White, a shy teenage girl with telekinetic powers who unleashes her wrath on her bullying classmates and her abusive, religiously fanatical mother. The novel is an epistolary work interweaving narrative with fictional newspaper articles, commission reports and scientific papers.

Plot summary

Carrietta "Carrie" White is a friendless sixteen-year-old in Chamberlain, Maine, tormented by her classmates and dominated at home by her mother Margaret, a Christian fundamentalist who regards everything — including Carrie's first menstruation, which she never explained — as sinful. When Carrie menstruates for the first time in the school showers and panics, her classmates pelt her with tampons while chanting "plug it up". Carrie discovers that stress awakens her latent telekinesis.

One girl, Sue Snell, repents and persuades her boyfriend Tommy Ross to take Carrie to the prom. Another, Chris Hargensen, plots revenge: she has a bucket of pig's blood suspended above the prom stage, rigs the vote so Carrie is crowned prom queen, and drenches her in blood. Believing the whole town is laughing at her, Carrie locks the gymnasium doors telekinetically and unleashes a massacre — electrocuting and burning her tormentors — then destroys much of Chamberlain as she walks home, confronts and kills her mother (who stabs her), and finally dies beside a forgiving Sue in a parking lot as the last fires spread.

Publication and writing

King wrote Carrie while working as a high-school English teacher in Hampden, Maine, living with his family in a trailer. He threw the opening pages in the trash, but his wife Tabitha retrieved them and encouraged him to continue. The $2,500 advance from Doubleday helped modestly; the life-changing payment came when paperback rights sold for $400,000. King has described the book as a story about high school cruelty, conformity and the explosive power of the outsider.

Adaptations and legacy

Brian De Palma's 1976 film adaptation, starring Sissy Spacek and Piper Laurie (both Oscar-nominated), was a major commercial and critical success and is regarded as one of the greatest horror films ever made. It was followed by a sequel, The Rage: Carrie 2 (1999), a 2002 television film, and Kimberly Peirce's 2013 remake starring Chloë Grace Moretz and Julianne Moore. A stage musical premiered in 1988. Carrie launched King's career and remains one of the most frequently challenged books in American libraries, while its prom climax has become one of the most imitated scenes in popular culture.
