Turkish War of Independence
Date19 May 1919 – 29 October 1923
LocationAnatolia, eastern Thrace, northern Mesopotamia
ResultTurkish nationalist victory; Treaty of Lausanne; Republic of Turkey proclaimed
BelligerentsTurkish National Movement vs Greece, France, Armenia, the United Kingdom and the Ottoman government

The Turkish War of Independence (Turkish: Kurtuluş Savaşı, "War of Liberation") was fought between the Turkish National Movement under Mustafa Kemal (later Atatürk) and the proxy forces of the Allied powers occupying the Ottoman Empire after its defeat in the First World War. It led to the overthrow of the Ottoman sultanate, the expulsion of occupying forces, and the founding of the Republic of Turkey.

Course of the war

After the Armistice of Mudros (1918), the Allies occupied Constantinople and partitioned Anatolia. Greek forces landed at Smyrna (İzmir) in May 1919, days before Mustafa Kemal landed at Samsun on 19 May — the conventional start of the war. Congresses at Erzurum and Sivas organised resistance; the Grand National Assembly opened at Ankara in April 1920, rejecting the punitive Treaty of Sèvres.

On the eastern front, Turkish forces defeated the First Republic of Armenia (Treaty of Alexandropol, 1920, later confirmed at Kars). In the south, French forces withdrew after the Ankara Agreement of 1921. Against Greece, the decisive campaigns were the battles of İnönü, the victory on the Sakarya River (1921) — after which Mustafa Kemal received the title Gazi — and the Great Offensive of August 1922, which destroyed the Greek army in Anatolia and retook Smyrna, where a catastrophic fire destroyed the Greek and Armenian quarters.

Outcome

The Armistice of Mudanya (October 1922) ended the fighting; the Treaty of Lausanne (July 1923) recognised the new Turkish state within essentially its modern borders and arranged a compulsory population exchange between Greece and Turkey. The sultanate was abolished in November 1922, and the Republic of Turkey was proclaimed on 29 October 1923 with Ankara as its capital and Mustafa Kemal as its first president.
