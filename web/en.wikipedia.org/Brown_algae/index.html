Brown algae

Brown algae (Phaeophyceae) are a large group of multicellular algae comprising the class Phaeophyceae. They include many seaweeds in colder waters of the Northern Hemisphere, such as kelps, rockweeds, and Sargassum.

Brown algae are coloured by the pigment fucoxanthin, which masks chlorophyll and gives them their olive-brown hue. They range from small filamentous forms like Ectocarpus to giant kelps over 30 metres long that form underwater forests supporting rich ecosystems.

Many species are economically important: kelps are harvested for alginates used as thickeners in food and industry, and some, such as wakame and kombu, are eaten directly, especially in East Asian cuisine.
