Bortezomib

Bortezomib, sold under the brand name Velcade among others, is an anti-cancer medication used to treat multiple myeloma and mantle cell lymphoma. It was the first therapeutic proteasome inhibitor to be approved for use in humans.

Mechanism of action

Bortezomib is a modified dipeptidyl boronic acid that reversibly inhibits the 26S proteasome, a protein complex that degrades unneeded or damaged proteins within cells. By blocking proteasome activity, it disrupts the balance of pro- and anti-apoptotic proteins in cancer cells, leading to cell cycle arrest and programmed cell death (apoptosis). Malignant plasma cells are especially sensitive to this effect.

Medical use and history

The drug was developed by Millennium Pharmaceuticals and was approved by the US Food and Drug Administration in 2003 for multiple myeloma patients whose disease had progressed after prior therapies; its approval was later extended to first-line treatment. It is administered by injection, intravenously or subcutaneously.

Side effects

Common side effects include peripheral neuropathy (numbness or pain in the hands and feet), nausea, diarrhea, fatigue, and low platelet counts. Subcutaneous administration is associated with lower rates of neuropathy than intravenous delivery.
