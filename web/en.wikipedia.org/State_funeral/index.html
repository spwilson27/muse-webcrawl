State funeral

A state funeral is a public funeral ceremony, observing the strict rules of protocol, held to honour people of national significance. State funerals usually include much pomp and ceremony as well as religious overtones and distinctive elements of military tradition. Generally, state funerals are held to involve the general public in a national day of mourning following the death of a head of state, a head of government, or another prominent figure.

Elements

Common elements include a period of official mourning with flags flown at half-mast, a lying in state during which the public may pay respects, a funeral procession often featuring a gun carriage and military escort, and a formal service attended by domestic and foreign dignitaries. The ceremonies mark the formal farewell of the nation to the deceased.

Practices by country

In the United Kingdom, state funerals are reserved principally for monarchs, with a small number of exceptional commoners honoured, most notably Sir Winston Churchill in 1965 and Queen Elizabeth II in 2022. In the United States, state funerals are traditionally held for former presidents; the multi-day ceremonies for John F. Kennedy in 1963 and later for Ronald Reagan, Gerald Ford, George H. W. Bush, and Jimmy Carter followed a template administered by the Military District of Washington. Other countries, including France, India, and South Africa, hold similar ceremonies for leaders such as Charles de Gaulle, Mahatma Gandhi (1948), and Nelson Mandela (2013).
