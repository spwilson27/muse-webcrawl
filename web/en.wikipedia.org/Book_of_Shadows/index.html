Book of Shadows

A Book of Shadows is a book containing religious text and instructions for magical rituals found within the Neopagan religion of Wicca. The concept originated in the 1940s–1950s with Gerald Gardner, the founder of modern Wicca, who claimed that such a book was a traditional personal grimoire kept by witches, copied by hand from teacher to initiate.

Origins

Gardner's original Book of Shadows, developed with his high priestess Doreen Valiente, contained the core rituals of the Gardnerian tradition, drawing on sources including the Key of Solomon, the writings of Aleister Crowley, Masonic ritual and Valiente's own poetry — she rewrote much of it, notably the Charge of the Goddess, removing some of the Crowley material Gardner had borrowed. Traditionally, each initiate copied the book by hand, and some covens required it to be burned or returned when a member died or left.

Contents and use

A Book of Shadows typically contains the coven's ritual texts for casting the circle, drawing down the Moon, the sabbats and initiation rites, together with laws (such as the Wiccan Rede), invocations, spells, herbal and magical lore, and the practitioner's own records. In traditional Wicca the book is oathbound — secret to initiates — while in eclectic and solitary practice the term has broadened: many modern practitioners keep their own Book of Shadows as a combined spellbook and spiritual journal, sometimes preserving a distinction with a "Book of Mirrors" used for reflections.

Public versions

Published versions and reconstructions of Gardner's book appeared from the 1970s onward, and the name entered popular culture through the television series Charmed (1998–2006), whose heroines consult a large ancestral Book of Shadows. Historians of religion regard the book as a mid-20th-century creation rather than a survival from an underground witch cult, though this has not diminished its central ritual importance in Wicca.
