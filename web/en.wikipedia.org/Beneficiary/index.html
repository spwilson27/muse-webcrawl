Beneficiary

FieldLaw (trusts, estates, insurance)
Related conceptsTrustee, settlor, heir, payee

A beneficiary (also, in trust law, cestui que use) is a person or entity who receives money or other benefits from a benefactor. The term is used in several legal contexts: as the person for whose benefit property is held by a trustee under a trust; as the recipient of proceeds under a life insurance policy or retirement account; and as an heir or devisee under a will.

Trusts

In a trust, legal title to the assets is held by the trustee while the beneficiary holds the equitable (beneficial) interest. A valid private trust generally requires ascertainable beneficiaries, who may enforce the trustee's duties in court. Beneficiaries may be primary or contingent (taking only if a prior beneficiary cannot), and their interests may be present or future, vested or discretionary.

Insurance and accounts

Life insurance policies, annuities, pensions and many financial accounts pay a designated beneficiary directly on the holder's death, outside probate. Beneficiary designations typically override instructions in a will for the same asset, so keeping them current is a standard element of estate planning. In international development and welfare contexts, "beneficiary" also denotes a recipient of aid or benefits. See also Trust law and Estate planning.
