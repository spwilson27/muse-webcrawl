The James Bond film series is a series of British spy films based on the fictional character James Bond, a MI6 agent codenamed "007", created by author Ian Fleming. It is one of the longest-running and highest-grossing film franchises in cinema history.

History

Eon Productions, founded by Albert R. "Cubby" Broccoli and Harry Saltzman, released the first film, Dr. No, in 1962, starring Sean Connery. The series established signature elements including exotic locations, gadgetry, title sequences with theme songs, and the "Bond girl" and villain archetypes.

Actors

Six actors have played Bond in the Eon series: Sean Connery, George Lazenby, Roger Moore, Timothy Dalton, Pierce Brosnan, and Daniel Craig, whose tenure from Casino Royale (2006) to No Time to Die (2021) rebooted the character with a more grounded tone.

Impact

The franchise has earned billions at the box office, won Academy Awards including for the songs "Skyfall" and "Writing's on the Wall", and profoundly influenced the spy genre and popular culture.
