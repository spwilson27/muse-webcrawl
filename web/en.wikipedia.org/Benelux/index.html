Benelux

The Benelux Union (Dutch: Benelux Unie; French: Union Benelux) is a politico-economic union and international intergovernmental organisation comprising three neighbouring states in Western Europe: Belgium, the Netherlands, and Luxembourg. The name is a portmanteau formed from joining the first syllables of each country's name, and was first used to name the customs agreement that initiated the union in 1944.

History

The three governments-in-exile signed a monetary agreement in 1943 and a customs convention in London in 1944, which took effect in 1948. In 1958 the Treaty of the Benelux Economic Union created common institutions: a Committee of Ministers, a Council, an Interparliamentary Consultative Council and a Court of Justice. The Benelux served as a model and nucleus for wider European integration: all three countries were founding members of the European Coal and Steel Community and the European Economic Community.

Cooperation today

Since a renewed treaty of 2008, cooperation focuses on the internal market and economic union, sustainable development, and justice and home affairs, including cross-border police cooperation and common visa and readmission arrangements.
