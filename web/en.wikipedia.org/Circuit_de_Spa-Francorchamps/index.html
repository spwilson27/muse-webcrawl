Circuit de Spa-Francorchamps

The Circuit de Spa-Francorchamps, frequently referred to simply as Spa, is a motor-racing circuit located in Francorchamps, Stavelot, Belgium. It is the current venue of the Formula One Belgian Grand Prix and of the Spa 24 Hours and 1000 km Spa endurance races. At 7.004 km (4.352 mi), it is the longest circuit on the Formula One calendar and is widely considered one of the most challenging and beloved tracks in motorsport.

History

The original circuit, laid out in 1920 on public roads between Francorchamps, Malmedy, and Stavelot, was a fast 14.9 km triangle. The first car race was held in 1922 and the first Belgian Grand Prix in 1925. Safety concerns led to a redesigned, shortened permanent circuit opening in 1979, retaining much of the old track's character and elevation change of about 100 metres through the Ardennes forest.

Famous corners

The circuit's signature sequence is Eau Rouge and Raidillon: a left-right sweep plunging downhill then climbing steeply through a blind crest onto the Kemmel Straight, taken flat out in modern Formula One cars. Other famous sections include La Source hairpin, Pouhon, Stavelot, and the Bus Stop chicane. Spa's changeable Ardennes weather — it can rain on one part of the track while the rest is dry — is a defining feature of races there.

Events

Besides Formula One, Spa hosts the FIA World Endurance Championship's 6 Hours of Spa-Francorchamps, the Spa 24 Hours for GT cars, and many national and historic events.
