Telekinesis

Telekinesis (from Greek τῆλε "far off" and κίνησις "motion") is a purported psychic ability allowing a person to influence a physical system without physical interaction. It is also called psychokinesis (PK), a term coined by parapsychologist J. B. Rhine. Examples of claimed psychokinesis include distorting or moving an object and influencing the output of a random number generator.

History

Claims of psychokinetic phenomena appear in spiritualist séances of the 19th century, where mediums were said to levitate tables and move objects. In the 20th century, parapsychologists conducted laboratory experiments, including J. B. Rhine's dice-rolling tests at Duke University and later micro-PK experiments on random number generators at Princeton's PEAR laboratory. In the 1970s, Uri Geller became famous for apparent metal bending, which magicians and scientists attributed to conjuring techniques.

Scientific evaluation

There is no convincing scientific evidence that telekinesis is a real phenomenon, and the topic is generally regarded as pseudoscience. Controlled studies have failed to produce replicable results under conditions excluding fraud and experimenter bias, and claimed effects conflict with established physical laws such as conservation of energy and Newton's laws of motion. Stage magicians have reproduced most celebrated PK feats using ordinary trickery.

In fiction

Telekinesis is a staple of science fiction and fantasy, appearing in works such as Stephen King's Carrie, the Star Wars films (as the Force), and numerous comic books and films featuring characters who move objects with their minds.
