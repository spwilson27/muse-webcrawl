Dionysius Exiguus

Bornc. 470, Scythia Minor
Diedc. 544, Rome
OccupationMonk, computist, translator
Known forAnno Domini era; Easter tables

Dionysius Exiguus (Latin for "Dionysius the Humble"; c. 470 – c. 544) was a Scythian monk and member of the Scythian monks community in Rome. He is best known as the inventor of the Anno Domini (AD) era, used to number the years of the Gregorian and Julian calendars.

Easter tables

In 525, Dionysius prepared new tables for calculating the date of Easter, extending the Alexandrian computus. Rather than counting years from the reign of Diocletian, a persecutor of Christians, he numbered them from the Incarnation of Jesus, founding the AD era — although his calculation placed Christ's birth some years earlier than modern estimates.

Works

Dionysius also translated Greek ecclesiastical canons into Latin, compiled canon law collections that influenced Western practice, and wrote on elementary mathematics and chronology. His era spread through the writings of Bede and became standard in the Christian West.
