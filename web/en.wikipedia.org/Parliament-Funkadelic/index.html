Parliament-Funkadelic

Parliament-Funkadelic Origin: Plainfield, New Jersey / Detroit, Michigan
Years active: 1968–present (collective)
Leader: George Clinton
Genres: Funk, psychedelic funk, P-Funk
Key albums: Maggot Brain (1971), Mothership Connection (1975), One Nation Under a Groove (1978)

Parliament-Funkadelic (abbreviated as P-Funk) is an American music collective of rotating musicians headed by George Clinton, specialising in funk music. Formed in the late 1960s, the P-Funk collective is widely considered one of the most influential ensembles in the history of popular music, credited with pioneering funk as an album-oriented art form and with deeply shaping hip-hop, which sampled its records extensively.

Two bands, one collective

The collective consists primarily of two bands: Parliament, which began as the doo-wop group The Parliaments in the 1950s and scored a hit with "(I Wanna) Testify" (1967), and Funkadelic, formed as the Parliaments' backing band. After losing the rights to the Parliament name in a contract dispute, Clinton recorded his musicians as Funkadelic, producing psychedelic rock-funk albums such as Free Your Mind... and Your Ass Will Follow (1970) and Maggot Brain (1971). When Clinton regained the Parliament name in 1974, he relaunched it as a more commercial R&B and horn-driven funk band, while Funkadelic continued as its guitar-based counterpart — the same pool of musicians recording for two labels.

The Mothership and P-Funk mythology

Parliament's mid-1970s albums built an elaborate science-fiction mythology — the battle of Dr. Funkenstein's clones against Sir Nose D'Voidoffunk, the arrival of the Mothership — staged in concerts with a full-size prop spaceship landing on stage. Landmark records include Mothership Connection (1975), The Clones of Dr. Funkenstein (1976), and Funkentelechy vs. the Placebo Syndrome (1977), featuring the signature bassist Bootsy Collins and keyboardist Bernie Worrell. Funkadelic's One Nation Under a Groove (1978) produced the collective's biggest hit single.

Legacy

Contractual and financial tangles ended the original labels' runs in the early 1980s; Clinton continued as a solo artist and with the P-Funk All-Stars. Parliament-Funkadelic was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1997. In 2019 the collective received the Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award. Its grooves supplied foundational samples for Dr. Dre, Snoop Dogg, De La Soul, and much of G-funk and hip-hop generally.
