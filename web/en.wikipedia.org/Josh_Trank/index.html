Joshua Benjamin Trank (born 19 February 1984) is an American film director and screenwriter. He rose to prominence at age 27 with the found-footage superhero film Chronicle (2012), then directed the critically panned superhero reboot Fantastic Four (2015), whose troubled production became a widely reported Hollywood cautionary tale. He later wrote and directed the Al Capone biopic Capone (2020).

Early life and breakthrough

Trank was born in Los Angeles, the son of a filmmaker. He gained attention with the viral 2007 short Stabbing at Leia's 22nd Birthday and worked as an editor. His feature debut Chronicle, written with Max Landis, told of three teenagers who gain telekinetic powers; made for about $15 million, it grossed over $120 million worldwide and was praised for its grounded take on the superhero genre.

Fantastic Four

Trank was hired by 20th Century Fox to direct Fantastic Four (2015). Reports of conflict with the studio, extensive reshoots and a final act restructured without him preceded the film's release; on the eve of opening Trank publicly disavowed the finished version in a since-deleted tweet. The film failed at the box office and won three Golden Raspberry Awards, including Worst Director. He also departed a planned Star Wars spin-off film in 2015.

Capone and later work

After several years out of the spotlight, Trank returned with Capone (2020), starring Tom Hardy as the ageing, syphilis-stricken gangster in his final year; released on streaming during the COVID-19 pandemic, it received mixed reviews. Trank has since discussed the Fantastic Four production in detail in interviews and pursued independent projects.
