People's Budget

The People's Budget was the name given to the British government budget of 1909, introduced by Chancellor of the Exchequer David Lloyd George in H. H. Asquith's Liberal government. It was a landmark in British social policy, proposing new taxes on the wealthy — including a supertax on high incomes, increased death duties, and taxes on land values — to fund old-age pensions and social welfare programmes, as well as naval rearmament.

Constitutional crisis

The House of Lords rejected the budget in November 1909, breaking a convention of centuries that the Lords did not veto money bills. The ensuing crisis led to two general elections in 1910 and the Parliament Act 1911, which removed the Lords' power to reject budgets and limited their veto over other legislation.

Legacy

The People's Budget is seen as the founding moment of the modern British welfare state and a decisive step in the decline of the House of Lords' political power.
