Jesus Seminar

Founded1985
FounderRobert W. Funk
AffiliationWestar Institute
Key publicationThe Five Gospels (1993)

The Jesus Seminar was a group of about 50 critical biblical scholars and 100 laymen founded in 1985 by Robert Funk under the auspices of the Westar Institute. The seminar sought to determine what the historical Jesus really said and did, applying the methods of the quest for the historical Jesus to the sayings and deeds attributed to Jesus in the Gospels.

Method and findings

Fellows of the seminar debated and voted—using coloured beads—on the authenticity of each saying and act of Jesus, grading results red (Jesus undoubtedly said/did it), pink (probably), gray (probably not), or black (certainly not). Their 1993 publication The Five Gospels—the four canonical Gospels plus the Gospel of Thomas, printed with the words of Jesus colour-coded—concluded that only about 18 percent of the sayings attributed to Jesus were likely authentic, and portrayed him as an aphoristic sage rather than an apocalyptic prophet. A companion volume assessed the deeds of Jesus in 1998.

Criticism

The seminar attracted wide media attention and sharp criticism from other scholars and church figures, who challenged its voting method, its starting assumptions, and the representativeness of its membership. Its work nevertheless became the best-known public face of historical-Jesus scholarship in North America.
