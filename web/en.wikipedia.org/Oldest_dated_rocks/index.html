Oldest dated rocks

The oldest dated rocks formed on Earth are Hadean and Archean in age. The oldest intact rock formations yet dated are the Acasta Gneiss of the Slave Craton in northwestern Canada, dated to about 4.03 billion years, and the disputed Nuvvuagittuq Greenstone Belt of Quebec, for which ages up to about 4.28 billion years have been proposed. Individual mineral grains are older still: detrital zircons from Jack Hills, Western Australia, have been dated to as much as 4.404 billion years old, within about 150 million years of the formation of the Earth itself.

Jack Hills zircons

Zircon crystals eroded from even older source rocks and deposited in younger sedimentary rocks at Jack Hills yield uranium–lead ages up to 4.404 ± 0.008 billion years. Oxygen isotope ratios in these zircons have been interpreted as evidence that liquid water existed at the Earth's surface surprisingly early, and that continental-type crust may have formed within the first few hundred million years of Earth history.

Dating methods

Ages are determined by radiometric dating, principally uranium–lead dating of zircons and samarium–neodymium isochron dating of whole rocks. Older material from beyond Earth is also known: some lunar samples and meteorites date to about 4.5–4.567 billion years, constraining the age of the Solar System.
