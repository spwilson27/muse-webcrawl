Beaver

Beavers (genus Castor) are large, semiaquatic rodents of the Northern Hemisphere. There are two living species: the North American beaver and the Eurasian beaver. Beavers are known for building dams, canals and lodges.

Description

Beavers have stout bodies, webbed hind feet, and a distinctive flat, scaly tail used for balance, swimming and communication. Their continuously growing incisors allow them to fell trees.

Habitat and behaviour

Beaver dams create wetlands that benefit many other species, making beavers a keystone species. They live in family colonies and are mainly nocturnal.

Human relations

Beavers were hunted extensively for fur and castoreum, driving them near extinction in parts of their range before successful reintroduction programmes. See Rodent.
