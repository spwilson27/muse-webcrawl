The Bridge of Sighs (Italian: Ponte dei Sospiri) is a bridge in Venice, Italy. The enclosed bridge, built in 1600 of white limestone, has windows with stone bars and passes over the Rio di Palazzo, connecting the New Prison (Prigioni Nuove) to the interrogation rooms in the Doge's Palace.

History and name

The bridge was designed by Antonio Contino, whose uncle Antonio da Ponte designed the Rialto Bridge, and was built beginning in 1600. Its name, given by the poet Lord Byron in the 19th century, comes from the suggestion that prisoners would sigh at their final view of beautiful Venice through the window before being taken down to their cells. In reality, by the time the bridge was built the days of summary executions were over, and the cells under the palace roof were mostly occupied by small-time criminals; the famous lover Giacomo Casanova was among the prisoners held in the adjacent Piombi prison, from which he escaped in 1756.

In popular culture

The bridge is one of Venice's most famous sights. Local legend holds that lovers will be granted eternal love and bliss if they kiss on a gondola at sunset under the Bridge of Sighs as the bells of St Mark's Campanile toll. Imitation "bridges of sighs" exist elsewhere, including connecting buildings at Oxford and Cambridge universities and in Las Vegas.
