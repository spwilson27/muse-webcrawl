6LoWPAN is an acronym of IPv6 over Low-Power Wireless Personal Area Networks. It is the name of an Internet Engineering Task Force working group and of the adaptation-layer technology it produced, which allows IPv6 packets to be carried efficiently over IEEE 802.15.4-based low-power, low-rate wireless networks, enabling small embedded devices to participate directly in the Internet of Things.

Overview

IPv6 headers are far too large for the tiny frames of IEEE 802.15.4 (127 bytes maximum, including link-layer overhead). The 6LoWPAN adaptation layer therefore provides header compression — reducing an IPv6 header to as little as a few bytes by eliding fields that can be inferred — plus fragmentation and reassembly for packets larger than a single frame, and support for mesh routing at the link or adaptation layer.

Standards history

The foundational specification, RFC 4944, was published in 2007, followed by the improved header compression of RFC 6282 (2011) and neighbor discovery optimizations in RFC 6775. The concept was later extended to other link layers, including Bluetooth Low Energy (RFC 7668), and underpins protocols such as Thread.
