Jean Lesage

Jean Lesage
Born: 10 June 1912, Montreal, Quebec, Canada
Died: 12 December 1980 (aged 68), Quebec City, Quebec
Political party: Liberal Party of Quebec
Office: 19th Premier of Quebec (1960–1966)
Known for: Leader of the Quiet Revolution

Jean Lesage (June 10, 1912 – December 12, 1980) was a Canadian lawyer and politician who served as the 19th premier of Quebec from 1960 to 1966. As leader of the Quebec Liberal Party with the slogan "Maîtres chez nous" ("Masters in our own house"), he presided over the sweeping modernization of Quebec society known as the Quiet Revolution.

Early career

Lesage was educated at Laval University and admitted to the Quebec bar in 1934. He entered federal politics, serving as a Liberal Member of Parliament from 1945 and as a cabinet minister under Prime Ministers Mackenzie King and Louis St. Laurent, holding portfolios including Northern Affairs and National Resources and Finance. After the Liberals' defeat in 1958, he became leader of the Quebec Liberal Party in 1959.

Premier of Quebec

The death of Union Nationale premier Maurice Duplessis in 1959 ended two decades of conservative rule. Lesage led the Liberals to victory in the 1960 provincial election. His government rapidly expanded the role of the provincial state: it nationalized private electricity companies into Hydro-Québec (1962–63), created a modern ministry of education, taking schooling from church control; founded the Société générale de financement, the Quebec Pension Plan, and a ministry of cultural affairs; and reformed health care and labor law. The reforms asserted a confident, modernizing French-Canadian nationalism summarized by the slogan "Maîtres chez nous."

Later life

The Liberals lost the 1966 election to Daniel Johnson's Union Nationale despite winning the popular vote. Lesage remained Liberal leader until 1970, when he resigned following the party's electoral defeat that year. He died in Quebec City in 1980 and is widely regarded as the father of modern Quebec.
