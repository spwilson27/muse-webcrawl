Draconian

Draco, the Athenian lawgiver.

Draconian is an adjective meaning excessively harsh or severe, especially of laws, punishments or measures. The word derives from Draco, the first recorded legislator of Athens, who in about 621 BC produced a written code noted for its severity.

Origin

According to tradition, Draco's laws prescribed death for offences as minor as stealing a cabbage or idleness. When asked why, he is said to have replied that small offences deserved death and he knew of no severer penalty for great ones. The laws were said to have been written not in ink but in blood. Most of Draco's code was replaced by Solon's reforms in 594 BC, though his homicide law survived.

Usage

"Draconian" entered English in the 19th century and is now used broadly of any very strict regime, from emergency legislation to workplace rules and content-moderation policies, conveying that the severity is felt to exceed what is just or necessary.
