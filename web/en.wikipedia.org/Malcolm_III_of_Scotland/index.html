Malcolm III of Scotland

Malcolm III
King of Scots: 1058–1093
Predecessor: Lulach
Successor: Donald III
Consort: Margaret of Wessex
Died: 13 November 1093, Alnwick

Malcolm III (known as Malcolm Canmore, from Gaelic for "great chief") was King of Scots from 1058 to 1093. He was the eldest son of Duncan I, whose killing by Macbeth in 1040 forced Malcolm into exile in England.

Reign

With English support Malcolm defeated Macbeth at Lumphanan in 1057 and Macbeth's stepson Lulach in 1058, taking the throne. His long reign saw growing English and Norman influence. His second wife, Margaret of Wessex — later canonised as Saint Margaret — encouraged religious reform and courtly culture.

Wars and death

Malcolm repeatedly raided northern England and clashed with William the Conqueror and William II. In 1093 he was killed at the Battle of Alnwick while invading Northumbria; his eldest son by Margaret, Edward, died of wounds shortly after. Several of his sons later became kings of Scotland.

In literature

Malcolm appears as a character in Shakespeare's Macbeth, leading the army that overthrows the tyrant.
