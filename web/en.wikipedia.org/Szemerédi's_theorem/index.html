Szemerédi's theorem

In combinatorics, Szemerédi's theorem is a result concerning arithmetic progressions in subsets of the integers. It states that any subset of the natural numbers with positive upper density contains arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions. The theorem was proved by Hungarian mathematician Endre Szemerédi in 1975, settling a conjecture of Paul Erdős and Pál Turán from 1936.

History

Partial results preceded the full theorem: van der Waerden's theorem (1927) guarantees arithmetic progressions in finite colourings of the integers; Klaus Roth proved the case of 3-term progressions in 1953 using Fourier analysis; and Szemerédi himself proved the 4-term case in 1969. The Erdős–Turán conjecture motivated a substantial prize offer, paid in part for the proof.

Proofs

Several distinct proofs are now known. Szemerédi's original proof used a complicated combinatorial argument built around his regularity lemma. Hillel Furstenberg gave a proof in 1977 using ergodic theory, and Timothy Gowers provided a new proof in 2001 using higher-order Fourier analysis, introducing the Gowers norms.

Consequences

A celebrated consequence is the Green–Tao theorem (2004), which shows that the prime numbers contain arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions; its proof uses a relative form of Szemerédi's theorem. The polynomial Szemerédi theorem of Bergelson and Leibman extends the result to polynomial patterns.
