Cookie

A cookie (American English) or biscuit (British English) is a baked snack or dessert that is typically small, flat, and sweet. It usually contains flour, sugar, egg, and some type of oil, fat, or butter. It may include other ingredients such as raisins, oats, chocolate chips, or nuts.

In most English-speaking countries except for the United States, crunchy cookies are called biscuits. Many Canadians also use this term. Chewier biscuits are sometimes called cookies even in the United Kingdom. In Scotland, a cookie is a type of bun.

History

Cookies are thought to have originated in Persia in the 7th century CE, shortly after sugar became common in the region. They spread through Muslim Spain to Europe. The word "cookie" derives from the Dutch koekje, meaning "little cake", and entered American English through Dutch settlers in New Amsterdam.

In the United States, the chocolate chip cookie, created by Ruth Graves Wakefield at the Toll House Inn in Massachusetts in the 1930s, became the most popular variety. Other American staples include the sandwich cookie (such as the Oreo, introduced in 1912), peanut butter cookies, sugar cookies, and oatmeal raisin cookies.

Description

Cookies are broadly categorized by how they are formed: drop cookies, rolled cookies, molded cookies, bar cookies, and refrigerator (slice-and-bake) cookies. Most cookie doughs are similar to cake batter but with less liquid, and baking times are short, typically 8–12 minutes at moderate-to-high temperatures.

Varieties

Popular varieties include chocolate chip, snickerdoodle, shortbread, gingerbread, macaroon, fortune cookie, and biscotti. Cookies are baked commercially in large quantities as packaged snacks, and are a common accompaniment to milk, coffee, or tea.
