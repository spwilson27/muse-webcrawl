Air Force Special Operations Command

Active: 10 February 1983 – present
Country: United States
Branch: United States Air Force
Headquarters: Hurlburt Field, Florida
Nickname: "Air Commandos"

Air Force Special Operations Command (AFSOC), headquartered at Hurlburt Field, Florida, is the special operations component of the United States Air Force and the Air Force component of United States Special Operations Command (USSOCOM). It provides specialised airpower — precision strike, infiltration, exfiltration, resupply of special operations forces, and combat search and rescue support.

History

AFSOC traces its lineage to the activation of Twenty-Third Air Force on 10 February 1983 at Scott Air Force Base, when Military Airlift Command consolidated the Air Force's special operations and combat rescue forces. On 22 May 1990 the organisation was redesignated Air Force Special Operations Command and became a major command.

AFSOC forces have participated in operations in Panama, the Gulf War, Somalia, the Balkans, Afghanistan and Iraq, and in numerous contingency and humanitarian missions worldwide.

Forces and aircraft

The command's units include special operations wings at Hurlburt Field, Cannon Air Force Base in New Mexico, and at bases in the United Kingdom and Japan. Its specialised, often heavily modified aircraft include:

  • AC-130 gunships (close air support and precision strike)
  • MC-130J Commando II (infiltration, exfiltration and refuelling)
  • CV-22 Osprey tiltrotors (long-range infiltration)
  • MQ-9 Reaper (intelligence, surveillance and strike)
  • U-28A Draco and OA-1K Skyraider II light aircraft

Its ground personnel include special tactics operators — combat controllers, pararescuemen, special operations weather technicians and tactical air control party specialists — who deploy with Army, Navy and Marine special operations forces.
