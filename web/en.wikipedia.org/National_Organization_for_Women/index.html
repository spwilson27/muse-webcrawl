The National Organization for Women (NOW) is an American feminist organisation founded in 1966. It is legally a 501(c)(4) social welfare organisation and one of the largest feminist advocacy groups in the United States, with hundreds of local chapters. NOW's stated purpose is "to take action through intersectional grassroots activism to promote feminist ideals, lead societal change, eliminate discrimination, and achieve and protect the equal rights of all women and girls."

Founding

NOW was founded on June 30, 1966, in Washington, D.C., by 49 women and men attending the Third National Conference of Commissions on the Status of Women, among them Betty Friedan — author of The Feminine Mystique (1963) — who wrote the acronym "NOW" on a napkin, and the civil rights lawyer and priest Pauli Murray. Frustrated by the federal government's failure to enforce the prohibition of sex discrimination under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, the founders modelled NOW on civil rights organisations such as the NAACP.

Campaigns

NOW led the campaign for the Equal Rights Amendment, ratified by 35 of the needed 38 states before the 1982 deadline, and organised the Women's Strike for Equality on August 26, 1970. Its core issues have included abortion rights and reproductive freedom, ending violence against women, constitutional equality, combating racism, LGBTQIA+ rights, and economic justice. It lobbied for Title IX (1972), the Pregnancy Discrimination Act (1978), and the Violence Against Women Act (1994).
