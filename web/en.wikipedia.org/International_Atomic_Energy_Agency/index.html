International Atomic Energy Agency

Founded: 29 July 1957

Headquarters: Vienna, Austria

Director General: Rafael Grossi (since 2019)

Members: Over 175 states

The International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) is an intergovernmental organisation promoting peaceful uses of nuclear energy and inhibiting its military use. Inspired by US President Eisenhower's 1953 'Atoms for Peace' speech, it reports to the UN General Assembly and Security Council.

Functions

The IAEA sets nuclear safety standards, provides technical cooperation in medicine, agriculture and energy, and administers safeguards — inspections verifying that declared nuclear material is not diverted to weapons — under the Non-Proliferation Treaty. Its role expanded after the Chernobyl disaster and Fukushima disaster.

Recognition

The agency and its then Director General Mohamed ElBaradei jointly received the Nobel Peace Prize in 2005.
