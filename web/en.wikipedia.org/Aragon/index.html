Aragon (Spanish and Aragonese: Aragón; Catalan: Aragó) is an autonomous community in northeastern Spain, comprising the provinces of Huesca, Zaragoza and Teruel. Its capital and largest city is Zaragoza. The region covers an area of 47,719 km² and is bordered by France to the north, Catalonia to the east, Valencia and Castilla-La Mancha to the south, and Castile and León, La Rioja and Navarre to the west.

Geography

The Pyrenees form the northern border with France, containing some of the range's highest peaks, including Aneto (3,404 m) in the Maladeta massif. The Ebro river flows west to east across the region through the broad Ebro valley, with the Iberian System mountains to the south. The Ordesa y Monte Perdido National Park protects dramatic Pyrenean valleys.

History

The medieval Kingdom of Aragon grew from a small Pyrenean county in the 9th–10th centuries. In 1137 the dynastic union of its queen Petronilla with Count Ramon Berenguer IV of Barcelona created the Crown of Aragon, which expanded across the Mediterranean to Valencia, the Balearic Islands, Sicily, Sardinia and Naples. Through the marriage of Ferdinand II of Aragon to Isabella I of Castile in 1469, Aragon became part of the emerging Spanish monarchy while retaining its own laws (fueros) until the Nueva Planta decrees of the early 18th century.

Culture

Aragon is known for the jota, a traditional dance and music, for Mudéjar architecture (a UNESCO World Heritage listing), and for the Basilica of Our Lady of the Pillar in Zaragoza, a major pilgrimage site. The Aragonese language survives in northern valleys, and Catalan is spoken in the eastern Franja strip.
