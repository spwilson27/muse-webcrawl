Mohism

Mohism was an ancient Chinese philosophy founded by Mozi (c. 470 – c. 391 BCE) and developed by his followers during the Warring States period. Its central doctrine was jian ai, usually translated "universal love" or "impartial care": the view that people should care equally for others regardless of family or social ties, in contrast to the graded love of Confucianism.

Doctrines

The Mohists judged doctrines by their consequences for the welfare of the people, an early form of consequentialist ethics. They condemned offensive warfare, extravagant funerals, and wasteful music and ritual, while promoting frugality, meritocratic government, and belief in ghosts and spirits as enforcers of moral conduct. Later Mohists (the "Later Mohist" school) produced sophisticated work in logic, definitions, optics, and mechanics, preserved in the Mohist Canon.

Organisation and decline

The Mohists were organised as a disciplined, quasi-military community under a Grand Master, and their expertise in defensive siege warfare made them sought after by smaller states resisting aggression. Mohism was a major rival of Confucianism for several centuries but declined sharply after the Qin unification and the Han dynasty's adoption of Confucian orthodoxy; its texts were largely neglected until scholarly revival in the Qing dynasty and the 20th century.
