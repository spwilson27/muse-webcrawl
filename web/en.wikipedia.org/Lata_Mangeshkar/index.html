Lata Mangeshkar

Lata Mangeshkar (born Hema Mangeshkar; 28 September 1929 – 6 February 2022) was an Indian playback singer and occasional music composer. Widely considered one of the greatest and most influential singers in India and one of the most prolific recording artists in history, she recorded songs in over thirty-six Indian languages, primarily Hindi and Marathi, over a career spanning eight decades. She was often called the "Nightingale of India" and the "Queen of Melody".

Career

Born in Indore to the classical musician Deenanath Mangeshkar, she began acting and singing in her father's Marathi musical plays as a child. After his death in 1942 she supported the family, moving to Mumbai and recording her first Hindi film songs in the late 1940s. Her breakthrough came with "Aayega Aanewala" from Mahal (1949). She went on to work with nearly every major music director of the Hindi film industry, including Naushad, S. D. Burman, Shankar–Jaikishan, Madan Mohan, Laxmikant–Pyarelal, and R. D. Burman, and sang for generations of leading actresses.

Honours

Mangeshkar received numerous awards, including the Bharat Ratna (2001), India's highest civilian honour, the Padma Bhushan (1969), the Padma Vibhushan (2001), the Dadasaheb Phalke Award (1989), and several National Film Awards and Filmfare Awards. France conferred on her the Legion of Honour. She also served as a nominated member of the Rajya Sabha, the upper house of India's Parliament, from 1999 to 2005.

Death and legacy

She died in Mumbai in February 2022 of multiple organ failure following COVID-19, prompting two days of national mourning in India. Her voice is regarded as an integral part of Indian cultural life, and she remains one of the most widely recorded voices of the twentieth century.
