John Montagu, 4th Earl of Sandwich

Born: 13 November 1718
Died: 30 April 1792 (aged 73), London
Office: First Lord of the Admiralty (1748–1751; 1763; 1771–1782)
Known for: The food item "sandwich" named after him; patron of Captain James Cook

John Montagu, 4th Earl of Sandwich, PC, FRS (13 November 1718 – 30 April 1792) was a British statesman who succeeded his grandfather Edward Montagu, 3rd Earl of Sandwich, as Earl in 1729. He is today remembered mainly for the claim that the word sandwich was named after him.

Career

Educated at Eton and Trinity College, Cambridge, Sandwich travelled widely in Europe and the Near East before entering politics. He served three times as First Lord of the Admiralty, as Secretary of State for the Northern Department, and as Postmaster General. His longest tenure at the Admiralty, from 1771 to 1782, covered the American Revolutionary War; his administration was criticised for corruption and for spreading the Royal Navy too thinly, though modern historians note the scale of the simultaneous wars Britain faced against America, France, Spain and the Netherlands. He was also a noted patron of music, mounting performances of Handel's works through the Concert of Antient Music.

The sandwich

The popular story, first recorded in a travel book about London, relates that Sandwich, an inveterate gambler, refused to leave the gaming table for meals and ordered his valet to bring him meat tucked between two pieces of bread so that he could eat with one hand and keep his cards in the other; others began to order "the same as Sandwich", and the name stuck. His biographer has argued the tale is plausible in outline, though Sandwich's long hours of work at the Admiralty desk make an alternative desk-lunch origin equally likely.

Legacy

Captain James Cook named the Sandwich Islands (Hawaii) and South Sandwich Islands after him, as did the naming of Montagu Island. He remained a significant figure in eighteenth-century naval administration despite his chequered reputation.
