Henry Parkes

Sir Henry Parkes

Born: 27 May 1815, Canley, Warwickshire, England

Died: 27 April 1896 (aged 80), Annandale, Sydney, Australia

Office: Premier of New South Wales (five terms)

Known for: "Father of Federation"

Parkes in his later years

Sir Henry Parkes (27 May 1815 – 27 April 1896) was a colonial Australian politician and longest non-consecutive Premier of New South Wales, the state of which he is considered the father. He has been called the "Father of Federation" for his work promoting the federation of the Australian colonies, achieved five years after his death.

Early life

Born in Warwickshire to a tenant farmer, Parkes worked as a bone and ivory turner in Birmingham and was involved in Chartist circles. He emigrated to Sydney in 1839, worked as a labourer and later as a customs officer and businessman, and founded the newspaper The Empire in 1850, which became a voice for liberal reform.

Political career

Parkes entered the New South Wales Legislative Assembly in 1854 and served as Premier five times between 1872 and 1891. His governments introduced free, secular public education (the Public Schools Act 1866 and the 1880 Act), railway expansion, and other reforms. A gifted orator despite limited formal education, he dominated colonial politics for decades.

Federation

At the Tenterfield Oration of 24 October 1889, Parkes called for a convention to draft a federal constitution, declaring that "the crimson thread of kinship runs through us all." He presided over the 1891 National Australasian Convention in Sydney. He died in 1896, before federation was proclaimed on 1 January 1901. His portrait appears on the Australian one-dollar coin of 1996 and his name is borne by the Canberra suburb and administrative district of Parkes.
