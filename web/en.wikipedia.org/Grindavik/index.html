Grindavik (Icelandic: Grindavík) is a fishing town on the southern coast of the Reykjanes Peninsula in southwest Iceland, about 50 kilometres from Reykjavík. It is one of the few towns in Iceland with a harbour on the south-facing Atlantic coast and has been an important fishing port, and earlier a trading station, for centuries.

History and economy

Grindavik is mentioned in early Icelandic sources and traded with English and German merchants in the 15th and 16th centuries. Its modern economy rests on fishing and fish processing, and it is home to one of Iceland's major salt-fish producers. The town is also the gateway to the Blue Lagoon, the geothermal spa that lies in a lava field a few kilometres to the north, and to the Svartsengi geothermal power plant.

2023–2024 volcanic crisis

Grindavik sits within the Reykjanes volcanic belt, which reawakened in 2021 after some 800 years of quiet. Following an intense earthquake swarm and ground deformation indicating magma intrusion beneath the town, Grindavik was evacuated on 10–11 November 2023. An eruption opened along the Sundhnúkur crater row in December 2023, and further eruptions in January 2024 sent lava into the town's outskirts, destroying several houses; fissures opened within the town itself and defensive barriers were built to divert lava flows. Repeated eruptions through 2024 kept most residents displaced, and the future of permanent settlement in Grindavik has been a national question in Iceland.

Sport and culture

The town's sports club, Ungmennafélag Grindavíkur (UMFG), fields football and basketball teams; its men's football team won the Icelandic Cup in 1994. Grindavik also hosts the Icelandic Saltfish Museum, reflecting its historic trade.
