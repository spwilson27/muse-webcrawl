Persian calendar

The Persian calendar, also called the Solar Hijri calendar, is the official calendar of Iran and was historically used in Afghanistan. It is a solar calendar whose year begins at the moment of the March equinox, celebrated as Nowruz, the Persian New Year. Its epoch is the Hijra of Muhammad in 622 CE, so its years are counted like those of the Islamic calendar but follow the solar year.

Structure

The first six months have 31 days, the next five have 30 days, and the last month, Esfand, has 29 days in ordinary years and 30 in leap years. Leap years follow a sophisticated cycle — traditionally a 33-year pattern with eight leap years, refined by later astronomers — making the calendar exceptionally accurate: its mean year differs from the tropical year by less than one second.

History

The modern calendar descends from the Jalali calendar, devised in 1079 by a team of astronomers including Omar Khayyam under the Seljuk sultan Malik-Shah I. Iran adopted the Solar Hijri reckoning by law in 1925, with month names drawn from the Zoroastrian tradition.
