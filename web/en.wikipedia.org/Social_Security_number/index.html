In the United States, a Social Security number (SSN) is a nine-digit number issued to U.S. citizens, permanent residents, and temporary (working) residents by the Social Security Administration. Its primary purpose is to track individuals for Social Security purposes and to record earnings for determining benefit eligibility and amounts. Over time it has become a de facto national identification number used for taxation, credit reporting, employment, and many other purposes.

Structure

The number is conventionally written in the form AAA-GG-SSSS. Historically, the first three digits (the area number) reflected the state from which the number was issued, the middle two digits (the group number) followed an administrative sequence, and the final four were a serial number. Since 25 June 2011, SSNs have been randomized, ending the geographic significance of the area number. Certain combinations were never validly issued, and the number 078-05-1120 became infamous after a wallet manufacturer used it as a sample in 1938, leading thousands of people to adopt it as their own.

History

SSNs were created under the Social Security Act of 1935, and the first numbers were issued beginning in November 1936. Initially needed only for workers in covered employment, the number's use expanded steadily: the Internal Revenue Service adopted it as the taxpayer identification number in 1962, and from the late 1980s most American children received numbers at birth through the Enumeration at Birth programme.

Uses and identity theft

Because an SSN is widely used as both an identifier and an authenticator, it is a prime target for identity theft. Breaches exposing large numbers of SSNs have prompted debate about replacing or better protecting the system. The Social Security card itself carries the legend—removed in later versions—that it is "not for identification."

Types of cards

Several versions of the card exist, including unrestricted cards for citizens and unrestricted residents, and restricted cards marked "valid for work only with DHS authorization" for temporary workers.
