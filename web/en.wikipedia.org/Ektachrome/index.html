Ektachrome

Ektachrome is a brand name owned by Kodak for a range of transparency (reversal), still, and motion picture films previously available in many formats and speeds. Ektachrome was first introduced in the early 1940s and became one of the most widely used colour reversal films for still photography, photojournalism, and cinematography.

Ektachrome was Kodak's flagship colour transparency film for much of the 20th century.

History

Kodak introduced Ektachrome sheet film in 1946 and roll films shortly afterward. Unlike Kodachrome, whose processing was so complex that it initially had to be returned to Kodak laboratories, Ektachrome could be processed by independent labs and even by photographers themselves. Successive processing systems included E-1 through E-6; the E-6 process, introduced in 1976, became the industry standard for colour transparency films.

Characteristics

Ektachrome films were known for their relatively neutral colour balance, fine grain, and—depending on the emulsion—either natural or vivid saturation. Variants included Ektachrome 64, 100, 200, and 400, the saturated E100VS, and infrared-sensitive Ektachrome Infrared film. Because a reversal film produces a positive image directly, Ektachrome transparencies were the standard medium for colour reproduction in publishing.

Discontinuation and revival

Declining demand in the digital era led Kodak to discontinue the last Ektachrome films in 2012. Following renewed interest in film photography, Kodak Alaris announced the return of Ektachrome E100 in 2017, and the film went on sale in 2018 in 35 mm and 120 formats, with sheet film following later.
