Nilotic languages

The Nilotic languages are a group of related languages spoken across a wide arc of eastern Africa, from South Sudan and Uganda to Kenya and northern Tanzania. They form a branch of Eastern Sudanic within the proposed Nilo-Saharan family, and are traditionally divided into Western Nilotic (Dinka, Nuer, Luo), Eastern Nilotic (Maasai, Turkana, Bari) and Southern Nilotic (Kalenjin, Datooga).

Features

Nilotic languages are often tonal, show advanced tongue root vowel harmony, and — especially in the east and south — have complex morphology and case marking unusual for African languages. Their speakers include many traditionally cattle-herding peoples, and the languages' spread tracks historic pastoralist expansions around the Nile and the Rift Valley.
