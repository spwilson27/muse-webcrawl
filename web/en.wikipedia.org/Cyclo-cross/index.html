Cyclo-cross

Riders must dismount and carry their bikes over barriers and steep run-ups.

Cyclo-cross (cyclocross, CX or 'cross) is a form of bicycle racing held in the autumn and winter, typically on short laps of 2.5–3.5 km over mixed terrain — grass, mud, sand and pavement — interrupted by obstacles that force riders to dismount, carry their bicycles and remount. Races last about an hour for elite categories, and the sport is governed internationally by the UCI.

History

Cyclo-cross originated in France in the early twentieth century as off-season training for road racers; an early precursor, "steeple-chasing" between towns using any route, gave way to organised events, and the first French national championship was held in 1902. The first World Championships took place in Paris in 1950. The sport's heartland is Belgium — particularly Flanders — and the Netherlands, where the biggest races draw tens of thousands of spectators, with strong traditions also in France, the Czech Republic and the United States.

The bike and the race

A cyclo-cross bike resembles a road bike but with wider tyre clearance, knobbier tyres (limited to 33 mm in UCI racing), cantilever or disc brakes, and lower gearing. Courses feature barriers, sand pits, steep banks and off-camber sections; pits allow riders to swap to a clean spare bike serviced by mechanics. The season centres on the UCI World Cup, the Superprestige and X²O Badkamers Trofee series, and the World Championships held in late January or early February.

Notable riders

Sven Nys dominated the men's sport in the 2000s, while Erik De Vlaeminck won seven world titles in the 1960s–70s. In recent years Mathieu van der Poel and Wout van Aert have headlined a celebrated rivalry; Marianne Vos has won the women's world championship a record eight times, and stars such as Fem van Empel and Puck Pieterse lead the current generation.
