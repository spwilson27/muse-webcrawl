Last glacial period

The last glacial period was the most recent period of widespread glaciation in Earth's history, lasting from approximately 115,000 years ago to about 11,700 years ago, when the Holocene epoch began. It was the latest of the glacial periods that have punctuated the Quaternary Ice Age over the past 2.58 million years, driven by Milankovitch cycles in Earth's orbital parameters.

Overview

At its height, ice sheets several kilometers thick covered Canada and northern Eurasia, and global sea level fell by more than 120 meters. The period encompassed the cold Last Glacial Maximum (about 26,500–19,000 years ago), a stepwise deglaciation, and the Younger Dryas cold snap shortly before the Holocene. Greenland and Antarctic ice cores, together with marine sediment records, document rapid climate oscillations within the glacial period, including Dansgaard–Oeschger events.

Human and biological history

Modern humans dispersed out of Africa, reached Australia and the Americas—crossing the exposed Bering land bridge—and produced Upper Paleolithic art and technology during this period. The close of the glacial period coincided with the extinction of many large mammals and with the beginnings of agriculture in several regions during the ensuing Holocene warmth.
