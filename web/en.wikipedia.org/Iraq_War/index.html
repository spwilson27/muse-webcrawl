The Iraq War was a protracted armed conflict in Iraq from 2003 to 2011. It began with the invasion of Iraq by a United States-led coalition on 20 March 2003 under President George W. Bush, justified principally by claims that Saddam Hussein's regime possessed weapons of mass destruction (WMD) and had links to terrorism. The invasion quickly overthrew the Ba'athist government; Baghdad fell on 9 April 2003, and Bush declared the end of major combat operations on 1 May 2003 under a "Mission Accomplished" banner.

Background

Following the 1991 Gulf War, Iraq remained subject to UN sanctions, weapons inspections and no-fly zones. After the September 11 attacks, the Bush administration argued Iraq posed an imminent threat; the Iraq Survey Group later found no stockpiles of WMD and no active production programme. No UN Security Council resolution explicitly authorised the invasion, and its legality remains widely disputed; millions protested worldwide in February 2003.

Occupation and insurgency

The Coalition Provisional Authority under L. Paul Bremer disbanded the Iraqi army and pursued de-Ba'athification, decisions widely blamed for fuelling a Sunni-led insurgency. The occupation saw escalating violence: battles for Fallujah in 2004, the Abu Ghraib prisoner-abuse scandal, and sectarian civil war in 2006–2007 following the bombing of the al-Askari mosque in Samarra. A "surge" of US troops in 2007, combined with the Sunni Awakening, sharply reduced violence. Saddam Hussein was captured in December 2003 and executed in December 2006 after trial by an Iraqi tribunal.

Withdrawal and aftermath

Under a 2008 status-of-forces agreement, US combat forces withdrew from Iraqi cities in 2009, and the last US troops left on 18 December 2011. Iraq's post-war politics were dominated by Nouri al-Maliki's government; corruption, sectarian tensions and the collapse of security forces in 2014 enabled the rise of the Islamic State, which seized Mosul and much of northern Iraq, prompting renewed foreign intervention.
