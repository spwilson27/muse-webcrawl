Fast-track construction

Fast-track construction overlaps design and construction phases to shorten the overall project schedule.

Fast-track building construction is a construction strategy in which the construction process starts before the design is complete. The purpose is to shorten the time to complete the project. Fast-track allows a facility to be occupied sooner or reduces the time to bring the product to market.

Unlike the design-bid-build process, where the entire design is finished before construction begins, fast-track construction begins with a partial design and construction of portions of the work, such as the foundation or substructure, proceeds while the rest of the design is completed.

Advantages and risks

The primary advantage of fast-track construction is a shorter time between the project start and the project completion — often many months saved compared to traditional sequential delivery. This can be especially valuable where financing costs are high or where market conditions reward early occupancy.

The risks include design changes made after construction has begun, coordination problems between trades, and cost overruns if early work must be demolished or modified to fit later design decisions. Fast-track projects therefore demand experienced teams, strong communication, and contingency allowances.

Related methods

Fast-tracking is closely associated with construction management delivery, design-build, and phased construction approaches in which work packages are issued in sequence as design for each portion is completed.
