Rye bread

Dark rye bread, a staple across northern and eastern Europe.

Rye bread is a type of bread made with various proportions of flour from rye grain. It can be light or dark in color, depending on the type of flour used and the addition of coloring agents, and is typically denser than bread made from wheat flour, with a stronger, slightly sour flavor derived from sourdough fermentation.

Characteristics

Rye flour contains less gluten than wheat flour and more soluble fiber, so rye doughs rely on the starch and pentosans — and usually an acidic sourdough starter — for structure. The result is a close-textured loaf that stays moist for days. Rye breads are higher in fiber than most wheat breads and have a lower glycemic response.

Varieties

Regional varieties include German pumpernickel, steamed for many hours until nearly black; Scandinavian rugbrød, a thin, dense sourdough loaf; Finnish ruisleipä, often baked as a flat round with a hole; Russian borodinsky, flavored with coriander and molasses; and Jewish rye of the American delicatessen, a lighter wheat-and-rye loaf flavored with caraway seeds, classic for pastrami sandwiches.
