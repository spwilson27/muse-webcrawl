Charlotte Brontë

Charlotte Brontë (21 April 1816 – 31 March 1855) was an English novelist and poet, the eldest of the three Brontë sisters who survived into adulthood. She wrote under the pen name Currer Bell.

Life

Born at Thornton and raised at Haworth parsonage in Yorkshire, Charlotte was educated at Roe Head school, where she later taught. She and her sister Emily studied in Brussels in 1842–43, an experience reflected in The Professor and Villette.

Works

Her novel Jane Eyre (1847) was an immediate success, combining Gothic romance with a searching first-person account of a governess's moral and emotional life. She later published Shirley (1849) and Villette (1853).

Death and legacy

Charlotte married Arthur Bell Nicholls in 1854 and died the following year, aged 38, during pregnancy. Jane Eyre remains a central work of Victorian fiction.
