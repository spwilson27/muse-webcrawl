Total conversion

A total conversion is a mod for a video game that replaces almost all of the artistic assets in the original game and sometimes core aspects of gameplay. Total conversions are intended to result in a completely different game from the original, as opposed to a partial conversion that adds new player characters, levels, or weapons while retaining most of the base game's content.

History

Early examples appeared on the FPS scene of the 1990s. Aliens TC (1994), a modification of Doom recreating the world of the film Aliens, is often cited as the first total conversion to attract wide attention. The genre of the base game often limited what could be changed, but some total conversions shifted genre entirely — the Half-Life mod Counter-Strike, while not a full asset replacement at first, evolved into a standalone commercial franchise.

Notable examples

  • Aliens TC for Doom (1994)
  • Counter-Strike, originating as a Half-Life mod (1999)
  • Defense of the Ancients (DotA), a custom map for Warcraft III that created the MOBA genre
  • DayZ, a conversion of ARMA 2
  • Enderal, a total conversion of Skyrim with an entirely new world and story
  • The Third Age and other large conversions of strategy games such as Medieval II: Total War

Commercial conversions

Some total conversions have been released commercially with the original developer's blessing, either as expansion packs or standalone titles. Others have had to be reworked after legal action by rights holders, particularly where they borrowed settings from films or other games franchises.
