Pan-American Highway

The Pan-American Highway is a network of roads stretching from North America to South America, measuring roughly 30,000 km (19,000 mi) in total length. Except for a break of about 106 km (66 mi) across the Darién Gap between Panama and Colombia, the highway links most of the Pacific coastal countries of the Americas.

Route

The northern terminus is at Prudhoe Bay, Alaska, and the route runs through Canada, the United States, Mexico, and Central America before continuing down South America to its southern terminus at Ushuaia, Argentina.

History

The highway was conceived at the Fifth International Conference of American States in 1923 and formalised by convention in 1936. The Inter-American Highway portion through Central America was substantially financed by the United States and completed during the mid-20th century.
