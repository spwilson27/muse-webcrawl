Albireo

Albireo (β Cygni, Beta Cygni) is a double star in the constellation Cygnus, marking the head — the "beak" — of the swan and forming one end of the Northern Cross asterism. Through a small telescope it resolves into two stars of strikingly contrasting colours, a golden-orange giant and a blue-white companion, and is often described as the finest colour-contrast double star in the sky for amateur observers.

Components

The brighter component, Albireo Aa, is an orange bright giant of spectral type K with an apparent magnitude near 3.2; it is itself accompanied by a close companion, Ac, detected spectroscopically and by interferometry. The blue component, Albireo B, is a hot, rapidly rotating B-type main-sequence star of magnitude about 5.1, surrounded by a circumstellar gas disk that makes it a Be star. The wide pair are separated by about 34 arcseconds.

Physical association

Whether the wide components form a true gravitationally bound binary or merely an optical alignment was debated for over a century. Astrometry from the Gaia mission suggests the pair may indeed be co-moving and plausibly bound, with a very long orbital period measured in tens of thousands of years, though the question has not been entirely settled.

Name

"Albireo" is thought to derive, through a chain of translations and copyists' errors, from the Greek name for the constellation, ornis ("bird"), transliterated into Arabic and then garbled in Latin manuscripts. The International Astronomical Union's Working Group on Star Names approved the name Albireo for the primary component in 2016.
