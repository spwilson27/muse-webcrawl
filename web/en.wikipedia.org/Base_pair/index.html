UsageUnit of length for nucleic acids

A base pair (bp) is a fundamental unit of double-stranded nucleic acids consisting of two nucleobases bound to each other by hydrogen bonds. Base pairs form the "rungs" of the DNA double helix and are the basis of the unit used to measure the length of DNA and RNA molecules.

Watson–Crick pairing

In canonical Watson–Crick base pairing, adenine (A) pairs with thymine (T) in DNA — or with uracil (U) in RNA — through two hydrogen bonds, while guanine (G) pairs with cytosine (C) through three hydrogen bonds. Because of this complementarity, the amount of A equals the amount of T and the amount of G equals the amount of C in double-stranded DNA (Chargaff's rules), and each strand can serve as a template for replication.

Non-canonical pairs and wobble

Other geometries, such as Hoogsteen base pairs, occur in some structures, and G–U wobble pairs are common in RNA, contributing to the folding of tRNA and ribosomal RNA.

Genome sizes

Genome sizes are expressed in base pairs, kilobases (kb), megabases (Mb), or gigabases (Gb). The human genome contains roughly 3.2 billion base pairs in its haploid set of chromosomes. The spacing between adjacent base pairs along the helix axis is about 0.34 nanometres, with roughly 10.5 base pairs per turn in B-DNA.
