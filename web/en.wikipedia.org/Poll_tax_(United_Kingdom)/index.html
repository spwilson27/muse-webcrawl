Poll tax (United Kingdom)

“Poll tax” in the United Kingdom commonly refers to the Community Charge, a system of local taxation introduced by Margaret Thatcher's government in Scotland in 1989 and England and Wales in 1990, replacing domestic rates with a flat-rate per-adult charge.

The tax proved deeply unpopular, provoking mass non-payment campaigns and riots in London in March 1990. It contributed to Thatcher's resignation in November 1990 and was abolished by John Major's government, replaced by Council Tax from 1993. Earlier poll taxes include the 1381 tax that helped trigger the Peasants' Revolt.
