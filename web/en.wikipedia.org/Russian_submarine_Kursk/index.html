K-141 Kursk was an Oscar II-class nuclear-powered cruise missile submarine of the Russian Navy. On 12 August 2000, during a naval exercise in the Barents Sea, an explosion in the torpedo room — caused by a leaking dummy torpedo using hydrogen peroxide fuel — triggered a second, far larger explosion. The submarine sank in about 108 metres of water.

Rescue attempts and aftermath

Russian rescue attempts failed, and offers of foreign assistance were initially declined, drawing widespread criticism. Twenty-three sailors survived the explosions in the aft compartments for some hours, as shown by a note written by Captain-lieutenant Dmitri Kolesnikov, but all 118 crew members ultimately died. The wreck was raised in 2001 in an international salvage operation, and the remains of the crew were recovered and buried with honours.
