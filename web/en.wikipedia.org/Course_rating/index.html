Course rating

SportGolf
Administered byUSGA (and national unions under the World Handicap System)
Related measureSlope rating
UnitStrokes, to one decimal place

In golf, the course rating is a numerical measure of the difficulty of a golf course for a scratch golfer (a player with a handicap of zero) under normal course and weather conditions. It is expressed as the expected score, in strokes to one decimal place — for example 72.1 — that a scratch player would average over several rounds on that course from a given set of tees.

Calculation

Course ratings are determined by trained rating teams that measure the effective playing length of the course and evaluate obstacles including topography, fairway width, green targets, recoverability, bunkers, penalty areas, trees, green surface, and psychological factors. Each set of tees on a course receives its own rating for men and women.

Use in handicapping

Together with the slope rating, which measures relative difficulty for a bogey golfer compared with a scratch golfer, the course rating is a cornerstone of the World Handicap System introduced in 2020. A player's score differential for handicap purposes is computed as (adjusted gross score − course rating) × 113 / slope rating. Courses are normally re-rated on a cycle of about ten years, or sooner if substantial changes are made.

History

Formal course rating developed in the United States in the early twentieth century and was standardised by the United States Golf Association; the slope system was added in the 1980s following research led by Dean Knuth.
