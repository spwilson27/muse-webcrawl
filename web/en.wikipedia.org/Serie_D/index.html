Serie D

Serie D is the top level of non-professional football in Italy

The Serie D is the highest level of the Italian non-professional football association league system. It sits below Serie C and above the Eccellenza, and is administered by the Lega Nazionale Dilettanti (National Amateur League). The championship is contested by around 160 clubs divided into nine regional groups (gironi), generally organized along geographical lines.

Format

Each of the nine group winners is promoted to Serie C. A play-off system involving the next best-placed teams determines additional promotion candidates, often used to fill vacancies in the professional leagues. The lowest-placed clubs in each group are relegated to the Eccellenza, directly or through relegation play-outs. The group winners also compete for the honorary title of Campione d'Italia Dilettanti in a post-season tournament for the Scudetto Dilettanti.

History

The league traces its origins to 1948, when it was founded as the top interregional amateur championship. It has undergone several changes of name and structure, being known at various times as the IV Serie, the Campionato Interregionale and the Campionato Nazionale Dilettanti before assuming the name Serie D in 1999. From 2014, following the reunification of the professional lower divisions, Serie D became the fourth tier of Italian football overall.

Notable clubs

Many historically significant clubs have passed through Serie D after financial collapses and refoundations under Italian football's sporting-title rules, using the division as a route back up the pyramid.
