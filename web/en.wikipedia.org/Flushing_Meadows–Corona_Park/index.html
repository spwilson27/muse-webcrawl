Flushing Meadows–Corona Park

Flushing Meadows–Corona Park (often referred to as Flushing Meadow Park, or simply Flushing Meadows) is a public park in the northern part of Queens in New York City. It is bounded by I-678 (Van Wyck Expressway) on the east, Grand Central Parkway on the west, Flushing Bay on the north, and Union Turnpike on the south. At 897 acres (363 ha), it is the largest public park in Queens and the fourth-largest in New York City.

History

The site was originally a wetland of the Flushing River and was used as an ash dump — the "mountains of ash" famously described by F. Scott Fitzgerald in The Great Gatsby as the "valley of ashes". Under Parks Commissioner Robert Moses, the dumps were levelled and the park created as the site of the 1939–40 New York World's Fair, and it again hosted the 1964–65 World's Fair. Structures surviving from the fairs include the Unisphere, the 140-foot stainless-steel globe built for 1964 that has become a symbol of Queens; the New York State Pavilion with its observation towers; and the building now housing the Queens Museum.

Sports and attractions

The park contains the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center, home of the US Open tennis championship, including Arthur Ashe Stadium, the largest tennis stadium in the world; and Citi Field, home of the New York Mets baseball team, opened in 2009 just north of the tennis centre (on the site of the former Shea Stadium). Other attractions include the Queens Zoo, the New York Hall of Science, the Queens Botanical Garden (adjacent), two lakes (Meadow and Willow), and extensive sports fields heavily used by the surrounding communities.
