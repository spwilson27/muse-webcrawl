Canon law

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Canon law (from Greek kanon, a "straight measuring rod" or "rule") is a set of ordinances and regulations made by ecclesiastical authority (church leadership) for the governance of a Christian organisation or church and its members.

By tradition

Canon law exists in several Christian traditions. The Catholic Church maintains the most developed system, codified in the 1983 Code of Canon Law for the Latin Church and the 1990 Code of Canons of the Eastern Churches. The Eastern Orthodox Church bases its canon law on the canons of the ecumenical councils and church fathers. The churches of the Anglican Communion likewise maintain bodies of canon law.

History

Early collections of canons were systematised in the Middle Ages, most famously in Gratian's Decretum (c. 1140), which became a foundational textbook of the discipline. Canon law influenced the development of Western legal systems, including aspects of procedure, marriage law, and the law of corporations.
