Sergei Witte

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Count Sergei Yulyevich Witte (29 June 1849 – 13 March 1915) was a Russian statesman who presided over the extensive industrialisation of the Russian Empire as Minister of Finance from 1892 to 1903. He is regarded as one of the most capable ministers of late imperial Russia and became, in 1905, the first chairman of the Council of Ministers — effectively Russia's first prime minister.

Finance minister

Raised in Tiflis and educated in mathematics, Witte rose through railway administration before entering government. As finance minister he put the rouble on the gold standard (1897), attracted large foreign investment, imposed protective tariffs and a state spirits monopoly, and drove railway expansion, including the Trans-Siberian Railway. Industrial output grew rapidly, though largely on foreign capital and at heavy cost to the peasantry.

Diplomat and prime minister

Witte negotiated the Treaty of Portsmouth (1905) ending the Russo-Japanese War on unexpectedly favourable terms for defeated Russia, winning the title of count. Recalled during the Revolution of 1905, he authored the October Manifesto promising civil liberties and a legislative Duma, and served as the first head of the new Council of Ministers until April 1906, when Nicholas II, who distrusted him, accepted his resignation. His memoirs, published posthumously, remain a major source on the era.
