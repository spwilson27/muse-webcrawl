Zazie in the Metro

Zazie in the Metro (French: Zazie dans le Métro) is a novel by French writer Raymond Queneau, published in 1959. A comic, linguistically inventive work, it was Queneau's first major popular success and was adapted into a film by Louis Malle in 1960.

Plot

Twelve-year-old Zazie arrives in Paris from the provinces, determined above all to ride the Métro. Entrusted to her uncle Gabriel, she finds the Métro closed by a strike and instead embarks on a whirlwind series of adventures through Paris, encountering a colourful cast of characters and persistently outwitting the adults around her.

Style

The novel is famous for its use of colloquial, phonetically rendered French — its opening word, "Doukipudonktan", compresses an entire spoken phrase. Queneau, a co-founder of the Oulipo group, plays with slang, neologisms, and register throughout, making translation a celebrated challenge. Barbara Wright's English translation is regarded as a classic of the art.
