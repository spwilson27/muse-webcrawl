Damdin Sükhbaatar

Damdin Sükhbaatar (2 February 1893 – 20 February 1923) was a Mongolian revolutionary hero and military leader who led the Mongolian People's Revolution of 1921. He co-founded the Mongolian People's Party and commanded the partisan army that, with Red Army support, expelled Chinese occupation forces and the White Russian forces of Roman von Ungern-Sternberg, establishing the modern Mongolian state. Mongolia's capital square, a central province, and a district of Ulaanbaatar bear his name.

Early life

Born into poverty, Sükhbaatar worked as a herder and carter before being conscripted into the army of the newly independent Bogd Khanate in 1912, where his skill earned him the nickname "Sükh" (axe). He later worked at the state printing house and became involved in underground revolutionary circles protesting the Chinese occupation of Mongolia in 1919.

Revolution of 1921

In 1920 Sükhbaatar travelled secretly to Irkutsk to seek Soviet assistance, carrying the revolutionaries' appeal, according to legend, hidden in his horse's hollowed whip handle. As commander of the Mongolian People's Army he captured the border town of Kyakhta/Maimaicheng in March 1921 and, in July 1921, liberated the capital, Urga, from Ungern-Sternberg's forces, while a provisional revolutionary government was established.

Death and legacy

Sükhbaatar died in February 1923, aged 30, officially of illness, though rumours of poisoning persisted. His widow, Sükhbaataryn Yanjmaa, later became acting head of state. He is venerated in Mongolia as a national hero: an equestrian statue of him dominates Sükhbaatar Square in Ulaanbaatar, his portrait appears on Mongolian currency, and the Order of Sukhbaatar was the country's highest decoration.
