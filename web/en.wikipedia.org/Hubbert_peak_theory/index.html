Hubbert peak theory

Hubbert peak theory
Proposed byM. King Hubbert
Year1956
ConceptProduction of a finite resource follows a bell curve

The Hubbert peak theory, proposed by American geologist M. King Hubbert in 1956, says that the production rate of a finite resource — such as oil from a field, region or the world — follows a roughly bell-shaped curve, peaking when about half the ultimately recoverable resource has been extracted. The resulting peak is known as peak oil when applied to petroleum.

Hubbert's prediction

Hubbert successfully predicted that US conventional oil production would peak between 1965 and 1971 — it peaked in 1970 — using logistic curve fitting of discovery and production data (Hubbert linearisation). His forecast of a global peak near the year 2000 proved premature, as unconventional resources and new technology expanded supply.

Debate

Supporters argue the theory's logic is inescapable for any finite stock and point to many regional peaks; critics note that prices, technology and policy continually shift the recoverable total, making single symmetric curves unreliable. The framework is also applied to natural gas, coal, minerals and even whale oil historically.
