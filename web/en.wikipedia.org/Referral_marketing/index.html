Referral marketing

Referral marketing is a method of promoting products or services to new customers through referrals, usually word of mouth. Such referrals often happen spontaneously, but businesses can influence this through appropriate strategies. Referral marketing harnesses the trust that existing customers have within their social networks: a recommendation from a friend or colleague is generally perceived as more credible than conventional advertising.

Mechanisms

Formal referral programmes typically give existing customers a unique link, code or card to share; rewards may go to the referrer only, to the new customer only, or to both ("give-and-get" offers). Well-known growth stories include Dropbox's storage-for-referrals programme, PayPal's cash referral incentives, and ride-hailing and food-delivery apps that credit both parties. Metrics tracked include referral rate, conversion rate of referred leads, and the lifetime value of referred customers, which studies often find to be higher than that of customers acquired through other channels.

Relationship to other concepts

Referral marketing overlaps with word-of-mouth marketing, affiliate marketing and influencer marketing, but is distinguished by its reliance on a company's existing customers rather than paid third-party promoters. It must be distinguished from multi-level marketing, in which participants earn from recruiting further sellers. Programmes must comply with advertising-disclosure and anti-spam regulations: undisclosed paid endorsements and indiscriminate referral spam can breach consumer-protection law in many jurisdictions.
