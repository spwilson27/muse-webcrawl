Freeman John Dyson (15 December 1923 – 28 February 2020) was an English-American theoretical physicist and mathematician known for his work in quantum field theory, astrophysics, random matrices, mathematical formulation of quantum mechanics, condensed matter physics, nuclear physics, and engineering.

Career

Dyson studied at Trinity College, Cambridge, and served as an operations researcher for RAF Bomber Command during the Second World War. After the war he moved to the United States, working at Cornell University with Hans Bethe and Richard Feynman, and later at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton, where he spent most of his career.

Contributions

In 1949 Dyson demonstrated the equivalence of the formulations of quantum electrodynamics developed by Feynman, Schwinger, and Tomonaga, work that helped establish QED as a coherent theory. He originated concepts including the Dyson series, the Dyson sphere, and Dyson's transform. He worked on the nuclear-pulse propulsion concept Project Orion, served on the JASON defence advisory group, and wrote widely read books and essays for general audiences, including The Scientist as Rebel.

Views

Dyson was noted for provocative and sometimes contrarian positions, including scepticism about the precision of climate models while accepting that human activity contributes to warming, and for his writings on religion and science.
