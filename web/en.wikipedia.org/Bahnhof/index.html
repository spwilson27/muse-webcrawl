Bahnhof

Bahnhof (German pronunciation: [ˈbaːnˌhoːf]; plural Bahnhöfe) is the German word for a railway station. It is a compound of Bahn ("way, track, railway") and Hof ("court, yard"). In German-speaking countries, railway stations are classified and signed using the term and its abbreviations, most importantly Hauptbahnhof (abbreviated Hbf), the main or central station of a town or city.

Terminology

German railway usage distinguishes many station types. A Hauptbahnhof is the principal passenger station of a city, such as Berlin Hauptbahnhof or München Hauptbahnhof. Smaller stops may be called Haltepunkt if they have no switches, while S-Bahnhof denotes a station served by S-Bahn suburban trains and Fernbahnhof a long-distance station. Goods stations are Güterbahnhöfe. Official station names combine the place name with the station type, and timetables use standard abbreviations (Hbf, Bf, Hp).

Cultural note

The word also appears in the German idiom nur Bahnhof verstehen — "to understand only 'train station'" — meaning to not understand a word of what is said, roughly equivalent to the English "it's all Greek to me". The expression is often traced to soldiers returning from the First World War who wanted only to reach the station and go home.
