Revolver (Beatles album)

Revolver is the seventh studio album by the English rock band the Beatles. It was released on 5 August 1966, accompanied by the double A-side single "Eleanor Rigby" / "Yellow Submarine". Recorded at EMI Studios in London over three months, the album is regarded as one of the greatest and most innovative albums in popular music history.

Background and recording

After the success of Rubber Soul (1965), the Beatles had an unprecedented three-month break before sessions began on 6 April 1966. The album marked a decisive turn toward studio experimentation: producer George Martin and engineer Geoff Emerick employed tape loops, reversed recordings, varispeed, automatic double tracking (ADT) and close-miking of instruments. George Harrison's interest in Indian music appeared in "Love You To", featuring sitar and tabla, while Paul McCartney's "Eleanor Rigby" used only a string octet. John Lennon's "Tomorrow Never Knows", built on a single chord and tape loops, closed the album and is often cited as a landmark of psychedelic rock.

Artwork and title

The cover was drawn by the German artist and musician Klaus Voormann, an old friend of the band from Hamburg; it won the Grammy Award for Best Album Cover, Graphic Arts. The title is a pun on the revolving motion of a record on a turntable and the handgun.

Legacy

Revolver topped charts in the UK and US and has repeatedly been ranked among the greatest albums of all time; in 2020 Rolling Stone placed it at number 11 on its list of the 500 Greatest Albums of All Time. A remixed and expanded edition, produced by Giles Martin, was released in 2022.
