The Executive Committee Range is a mountain range in Marie Byrd Land, Antarctica, consisting of a chain of five major dormant shield volcanoes extending over about 80 kilometres. It was discovered by the United States Antarctic Service expedition on a flight in December 1940 and named for the Executive Committee of that service.

Geography

The principal peaks, from north to south, are Mount Hampton, Mount Cumming, Mount Hartigan, Mount Sidley and Mount Waesche. Mount Sidley, at 4,285 metres, is the highest dormant volcano in Antarctica and one of the Volcanic Seven Summits. Mount Waesche, at the southern end of the range, rises near the West Antarctic Ice Sheet.

Geology

The volcanoes are composed chiefly of alkaline lavas and were formed over millions of years as the crust moved over a mantle hotspot or zone of melting. Although dormant, Mount Waesche has been suggested to have erupted as recently as about 8,500 years ago, based on ash layers in ice cores.
