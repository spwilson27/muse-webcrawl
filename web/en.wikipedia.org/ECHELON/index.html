ECHELON is the name given in public reports to a signals intelligence (SIGINT) collection and analysis network operated by the five signatory states of the UKUSA Agreement — the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia and New Zealand, collectively known as the "Five Eyes". The system was reportedly created in the late 1960s to monitor the military and diplomatic communications of the Soviet Union and its Eastern Bloc allies.

Capabilities

According to journalists and a 2001 report of the European Parliament, ECHELON stations intercept satellite, microwave, cellular and fibre-optic communications, and computers search the traffic using keyword "dictionaries" for items of intelligence interest. The European Parliament concluded that the system existed but was likely more limited than popular accounts suggested.

Disclosure and controversy

The network was first described publicly in 1988 by journalist Duncan Campbell, drawing on accounts by former intelligence staff. A 1996 book by Nicky Hager, Secret Power, detailed New Zealand's role. ECHELON became a focus of debate over industrial espionage and privacy, prompting the European Parliament's temporary committee investigation of 2000–2001. The revelations of Edward Snowden in 2013 later confirmed the scale of Five Eyes interception programmes more broadly.
