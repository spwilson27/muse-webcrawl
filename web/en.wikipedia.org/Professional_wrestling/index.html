Professional wrestling

Professional wrestling is a form of athletic theatre centred on mock combat, combining athletics, dramatic performance, and scripted storylines. Matches are staged contests in which the outcomes are predetermined by promoters, while the performers — professional wrestlers — execute choreographed throws, holds, and strikes designed to appear violent while minimising genuine injury. The industry traditionally maintained the illusion that competition was real, a practice known as kayfabe.

History

Professional wrestling developed from travelling carnival and music-hall strongman acts in the 19th century. By the early 20th century, wrestlers such as Georg Hackenschmidt drew large crowds. In the United States, regional "territories" flourished in the mid-20th century before Vince McMahon's World Wrestling Federation (now WWE) expanded nationally in the 1980s during the "Rock 'n' Wrestling" era, producing stars such as Hulk Hogan. The 1990s "Monday Night War" between WWF and WCW and the ensuing "Attitude Era" brought record television ratings. In Japan, styles such as puroresu developed under promoters like Antonio Inoki, while in Mexico the colourful masked tradition of lucha libre became a national institution.

Performance and terminology

Wrestlers adopt personas: heroes ("faces" or "babyfaces") and villains ("heels"). Storylines ("angles") build toward matches at major events. Performers sell their opponents' offence — reacting as though genuinely hurt — and protect each other through cooperation even as they compete for crowd favour. Insiders historically used a carny-derived slang; breaking character or revealing the scripted nature of the business was once taboo, though the staged nature of wrestling is now openly acknowledged.

Business

Major promotions include WWE and All Elite Wrestling (AEW) in the United States, New Japan Pro-Wrestling in Japan, and Consejo Mundial de Lucha Libre and Lucha Libre AAA in Mexico. Revenue comes from television rights, live events, pay-per-view and streaming, and merchandise. WrestleMania, WWE's flagship event, is among the most valuable sporting-entertainment properties in the world.

Criticism and safety

Despite choreography, professional wrestling is physically punishing; performers suffer real injuries, and the industry has faced scrutiny over concussions, chronic pain, and the historically independent-contractor status of its performers.
