Operation Anaconda

Operation Anaconda
Date: 1–18 March 2002
Location: Shahikot Valley, Paktia Province, Afghanistan
Part of: War in Afghanistan (2001–2021)

Operation Anaconda or the Battle of Shahikot Valley was a military operation that took place in early March 2002, in which the United States military and CIA paramilitary officers, working with allied Afghan military forces, and other coalition troops attempted to destroy al-Qaeda and Taliban forces in the Shahikot Valley and Arma Mountains southeast of Zormat.

The battle

The operation, the first large-scale battle in the United States' War in Afghanistan since the Battle of Tora Bora in December 2001, pitted roughly 2,000 coalition troops against an estimated several hundred entrenched enemy fighters positioned in caves and ridgelines. The fiercest fighting occurred on 4 March at Takur Ghar, where a Navy SEAL, Neil C. Roberts, fell from a helicopter and was killed, and subsequent rescue attempts cost the lives of additional American servicemen; the engagement is often called the Battle of Roberts Ridge.

Aftermath

Coalition forces eventually cleared the valley, and estimates of enemy losses ranged from several hundred killed. Eight American and seven Afghan troops were killed, and dozens were wounded. The operation drew scrutiny over planning, intelligence on enemy strength, and coordination between conventional and special operations forces.
