Vicksburg campaign

The Vicksburg campaign was a series of manoeuvres and battles in the Western Theater of the American Civil War directed against Vicksburg, Mississippi, a fortress city that dominated the last Confederate-controlled section of the Mississippi River. The campaign consisted of many important naval operations, troop manoeuvres, failed initiatives, and eleven distinct battles from 29 December 1862 to 4 July 1863.

Campaign

Union Major General Ulysses S. Grant commanded the Army of the Tennessee. After early failures — including the repulse at Chickasaw Bayou and abortive canal and bayou expeditions — Grant marched his army down the west bank of the Mississippi, ran transports past Vicksburg's batteries, and crossed at Bruinsburg on 30 April 1863. In a bold manoeuvre cutting loose from his supply line, he defeated Confederate forces at Port Gibson, Raymond, Jackson, Champion Hill and Big Black River Bridge, driving General John C. Pemberton's army into Vicksburg's defences.

Siege and surrender

After two failed assaults (19 and 22 May), Grant settled into a siege. Starving, the garrison surrendered on 4 July 1863 — the day after the Confederate defeat at Gettysburg. The loss of Vicksburg, followed by the fall of Port Hudson, gave the Union complete control of the Mississippi River and split the Confederacy in two. Lincoln called Vicksburg "the key" to victory, and the campaign established Grant's reputation as the Union's foremost general.
