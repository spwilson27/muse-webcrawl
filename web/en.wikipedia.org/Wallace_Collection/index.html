The Wallace Collection is a national museum in London displaying the works of art collected in the 18th and 19th centuries by the first four Marquesses of Hertford and by Sir Richard Wallace, the 4th Marquess's illegitimate son. It is housed in Hertford House in Manchester Square and is especially renowned for 18th-century French art.

History

The collection was assembled across generations: Francis Seymour-Conway, 1st Marquess of Hertford, began collecting; the 3rd and 4th Marquesses, based largely in Paris, amassed outstanding French paintings, furniture, Sèvres porcelain, and armour. Richard Wallace inherited in 1870, moved much of the collection to London during the Franco-Prussian War, and lent it to acclaim at the Bethnal Green Museum. In 1897 his widow, Lady Wallace, bequeathed the collection to the British nation; the museum opened in 1900.

Holdings

The roughly 5,500 works include Old Master paintings by Titian, Rubens, Rembrandt, Velázquez, Poussin, and Hals; the finest collection of Sèvres porcelain in the world; French royal furniture; gold boxes; miniatures; and one of Europe's best collections of arms and armour, both European and Oriental. The terms of the bequest long forbade loans, though temporary exhibition loans have been permitted since 2019.
