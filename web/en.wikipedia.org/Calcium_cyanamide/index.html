Calcium cyanamide

Calcium cyanamide
Formula: CaCN2
Molar mass: 80.10 g/mol
Appearance: white solid (grey/black commercially)
Common name: lime nitrogen, nitrolime

Calcium cyanamide, also known as lime nitrogen or nitrolime, is the inorganic compound with the formula CaCN2. It is the calcium salt of the cyanamide (carbodiimide) anion. The pure compound is a white solid, but commercial grades are usually grey to black because of carbon impurities from its manufacture. It was first synthesised in 1898 by Adolph Frank and Nikodem Caro as part of their search for a way to fix atmospheric nitrogen.

Production

Calcium cyanamide is produced by heating calcium carbide with nitrogen gas at about 1,000 °C in the Frank–Caro process:

CaC2 + N2 → CaCN2 + C

The reaction is carried out in large electric furnaces and was historically one of the first industrial nitrogen-fixation processes, preceding the Haber–Bosch process.

Uses

Its principal use is as a nitrogen fertiliser. In soil it reacts with water and eventually converts through cyanamide and urea to plant-available ammonia, while its lime content reduces soil acidity. It also has some herbicidal and fungicidal side effects, suppressing weeds and certain soil-borne diseases. Industrially, calcium cyanamide is an intermediate in the production of melamine, dicyandiamide, thiourea and other chemicals. The drug cyanamide, used historically in alcohol-aversion therapy, is related to it.

Safety

Calcium cyanamide is an irritant to skin, eyes and mucous membranes, and inhibits aldehyde dehydrogenase, so consuming alcohol after exposure can cause an unpleasant flushing reaction.
