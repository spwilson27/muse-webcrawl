Laxey (Manx: Laksaa, from Old Norse laxa, "salmon river") is a village on the east coast of the Isle of Man, in the parish of Garff, situated in a steep glen at the mouth of the Laxey River, roughly midway between Douglas and Ramsey.

History

Laxey grew in the nineteenth century into a centre of lead and zinc mining. The Great Laxey Mine was among the most productive metal mines in the British Isles, employing over 600 men at its peak. To drain the deep workings, the engineer Robert Casement designed the village's celebrated landmark, the Laxey Wheel — completed in 1854 and named "Lady Isabella" after the wife of the island's governor. At 22.1 metres (72 feet 6 inches) in diameter, it is the largest working waterwheel in the world, and it remains an icon of the island.

The village today

Tourism succeeded mining: Laxey became a popular Victorian resort, and visitors still arrive on the Manx Electric Railway, which opened to the village in 1893–94. From Laxey station, the Snaefell Mountain Railway (opened 1895) climbs to the summit of Snaefell, the island's highest point. Other features include the harbour, the beach and promenade, the restored mines railways and engines, the woollen mills, and Glen Mooar and Laxey Glen gardens.
