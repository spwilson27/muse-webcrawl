Bollandist

The Acta Sanctorum, the Bollandists' monumental collection of saints' lives

The Bollandists are an association of scholars, originally Jesuit, devoted to the critical study of hagiography — the lives of Christian saints. They are named after the Flemish Jesuit Jean Bolland (1596–1665), who in 1643 published the first volumes of their great work, the Acta Sanctorum ("Acts of the Saints"), in Antwerp. The Société des Bollandistes, based in Brussels, continues the work today.

Origins

The project began with Heribert Rosweyde, who planned a critical edition of all known saints' lives. After his death, Bolland took over, expanded the scope and devised the plan of arranging the saints by their feast days through the calendar year. His collaborator Godefroid Henschen helped establish the critical method: printing the earliest texts, comparing manuscripts, and adding scholarly commentary. Daniel Papebroch later extended the rigor of the project — famously questioning the authenticity of documents cherished by some religious orders, which led to heated controversy.

The Acta Sanctorum

The Acta Sanctorum proceeded month by month; by the 18th century the work had reached the beginning of November. The enterprise was disrupted by the suppression of the Jesuits in 1773 and the upheavals of the French Revolution, but it was revived in Belgium in 1837. The Bollandists also publish the journal Analecta Bollandiana (founded 1882) and the reference series Bibliotheca Hagiographica Graeca, Latina, and Orientalis, which catalogue hagiographical texts.

Method and influence

The Bollandists are credited with applying the methods of historical criticism to religious literature long before such methods were standard, distinguishing legend from history and pioneering documentary scholarship. Their work laid foundations for modern critical historiography and liturgical scholarship.
