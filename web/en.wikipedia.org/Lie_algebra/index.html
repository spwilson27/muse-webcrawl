Lie algebra


In mathematics, a Lie algebra is a vector space equipped with a bilinear, alternating bracket operation satisfying the Jacobi identity. Named after Sophus Lie.

In mathematics, a Lie algebra (pronounced "Lee") is a vector space 𝔤 together with an operation called the Lie bracket, [x, y], which is bilinear, alternating, and satisfies the Jacobi identity:

[x,[y,z]] + [y,[z,x]] + [z,[x,y]] = 0.

History and motivation

Lie algebras were introduced by Sophus Lie in the 1870s to study symmetries of differential equations. They arise as the tangent space at the identity element of a Lie group, where the bracket corresponds to the infinitesimal commutator of transformations.

Examples

The cross product on ℝ³ gives a Lie algebra. The space of n×n matrices with bracket [A, B] = AB − BA forms the general linear Lie algebra 𝔤𝔩(n). The semisimple Lie algebras over ℂ are classified by root systems and Dynkin diagrams into the families An, Bn, Cn, Dn and the exceptional types G₂, F₄, E₆, E₇, E₈.
