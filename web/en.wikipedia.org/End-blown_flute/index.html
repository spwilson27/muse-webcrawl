End-blown flute

An end-blown flute is a flute played by directing the player's breath against the sharp rim at the open upper end of the tube. It contrasts with transverse (side-blown) flutes and duct flutes such as the recorder.

Types

Examples include the ney of Middle Eastern and Central Asian music, the Turkish ney and Arabic nay, the Persian ney, the Japanese shakuhachi (with an oblique outer rim), the Andean quena and quenacho, and the Slovak fujara. Some have a notch at the rim (notched flutes); others have a plain rim.

Technique

Tone is produced by an air jet striking the rim; angle, embouchure, and partial covering of the end enable pitch bends, vibrato, and overblowing rich in expressive effect.
