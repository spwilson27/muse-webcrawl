The Mumbai Suburban Railway is a commuter rail system serving the Mumbai Metropolitan Region in Maharashtra, India. Operated jointly by Western Railway and Central Railway zones of Indian Railways, it is often called the lifeline of Mumbai and carries millions of passengers daily over its Western, Central and Harbour lines.

History

The first train in India ran between Bombay (Bori Bunder) and Thane on 16 April 1853. The suburban network grew with the city, and is noted for its extreme crowding at peak hours, when trains routinely carry far more passengers than their nominal capacity.

Lines and operations

The Western line runs from Churchgate to Dahanu Road, the Central line from Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus to Kasara and Khopoli, and the Harbour line serves Navi Mumbai. AC local services have been introduced in recent years.
