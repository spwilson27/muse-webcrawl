Krišjānis Barons (31 October 1835 – 8 March 1923) was a Latvian writer and folklorist who is best known as the collector and editor of Latvian folk songs, the dainas. He is honoured in Latvia as the "Father of the Dainas" (Dainu tēvs) and is one of the central figures of the Latvian National Awakening.

Life

Barons was born in Strutele, in present-day Tukums Municipality, and studied mathematics and astronomy at the University of Dorpat (Tartu). He spent much of his working life in Moscow as a tutor in the household of the landowner Ivan Stankevich, while immersing himself in the collection of Latvian folklore.

The Cabinet of Folk Songs

From 1878, Barons organised the collection of folk songs sent in by contributors across Latvia. His monumental edition Latvju dainas, published in six volumes between 1894 and 1915, contained 217,996 song texts. To organise the enormous manuscript collection he devised a special filing cabinet — the Dainu skapis, the "Cabinet of Folk Songs" — which is now preserved in Riga and was inscribed in UNESCO's Memory of the World register. Barons devised a classification system based on the life cycle and themes of the songs and personally edited the majority of the texts.

Legacy

Barons' portrait appeared on the Latvian 100-lats banknote, and streets and monuments across Latvia bear his name. The dainas collection he assembled remains a foundation of Latvian national identity and scholarship.
