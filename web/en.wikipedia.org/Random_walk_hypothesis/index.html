Random walk hypothesis

The random walk hypothesis is a financial theory holding that stock market prices evolve according to a random walk — that is, price changes are random and unpredictable — so that past movements cannot be used to predict future movements. It is closely related to, and often derived from, the efficient-market hypothesis.

History

The idea traces to Louis Bachelier's 1900 work on speculation and was popularised for general readers by Burton Malkiel's 1973 book A Random Walk Down Wall Street. Eugene Fama's empirical work on market efficiency provided supporting evidence, though critics such as Andrew Lo have proposed weaker forms (the adaptive market hypothesis) and point to momentum and other anomalies.

Testing

Statistical tests include runs tests, variance-ratio tests, and tests of technical trading rules; results are debated, and the hypothesis remains influential but contested in financial economics.
