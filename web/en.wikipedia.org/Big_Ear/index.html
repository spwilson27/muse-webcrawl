Big Ear

The Big Ear was a Kraus-type radio telescope located on the grounds of the Perkins Observatory at Ohio Wesleyan University in Delaware, Ohio, and operated by Ohio State University. Completed in 1963, it conducted the Ohio Sky Survey and, from 1973, one of the longest-running searches for extraterrestrial intelligence (SETI).

Design

The telescope used a large tiltable flat reflector that directed radio waves across a ground plane to a fixed parabolic reflector, with feed horns on a movable carriage. The collecting area was roughly the size of three football fields. It observed at frequencies around 1,420 MHz, near the hydrogen line.

The Wow! signal

On 15 August 1977, the Big Ear detected a strong narrowband radio signal that bore the expected hallmarks of a potential artificial extraterrestrial signal. Volunteer Jerry Ehman circled the printout's intensity sequence "6EQUJ5" and wrote "Wow!" in the margin, giving the Wow! signal its name. Despite repeated searches, the signal was never detected again and its source remains unexplained.

Closure

The telescope was dismantled in 1998 after the land was sold to developers for expansion of a neighbouring golf course and housing. Its SETI programme had been among the longest continuously running searches of its kind.
