Liu Bocheng

Liu Bocheng (4 December 1892 – 7 October 1986) was a Chinese military commander and Marshal of the People's Liberation Army. Nicknamed the "One-Eyed Dragon" after losing an eye in battle in 1916, he is regarded as one of the founders of the PLA.

Career

Liu fought as a warlord-era officer in Sichuan before joining the Communist cause, taking part in the Nanchang Uprising of 1927. He studied in the Soviet Union at the Frunze Military Academy, then served as chief of staff of the Red Army on the Long March. During the Second Sino-Japanese War he commanded the 129th Division of the Eighth Route Army, building the Jin-Ji-Lu-Yu base area. In the renewed civil war he led, with political commissar Deng Xiaoping, the Central Plains field army — the famous "Liu-Deng Army" — including the great Huaihai Campaign of 1948–49 and the crossing of the Yangtze, then took southwest China.

After 1949 he directed military education, serving as president of the PLA Military Academy in Nanjing, was made one of the ten Marshals in 1955, and held senior party and state posts despite periods of political eclipse. Liu also translated Russian military works and is remembered as a theorist of regular warfare.
