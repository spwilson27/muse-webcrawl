The England national football team has represented England in international football since the first international match in 1872. It is controlled by the Football Association (FA), the governing body for football in England, and competes as a member of UEFA. England plays its home matches at Wembley Stadium in London and is one of only eight teams to have won the FIFA World Cup.

History

England played the world's first international, a 0–0 draw with Scotland in Glasgow in 1872, and dominated the early British Home Championship. Having declined to enter the first World Cups, England debuted in 1950 with a famous shock defeat by the United States. The team's greatest triumph came at home in 1966, when Alf Ramsey's side beat West Germany 4–2 after extra time in the final at Wembley, Geoff Hurst scoring a hat-trick. Subsequent decades brought painful near-misses — semi-finals at the 1990 World Cup and Euro 96 — before a revival under Gareth Southgate produced a World Cup semi-final in 2018 and consecutive European Championship finals at Euro 2020 (played in 2021) and Euro 2024, both lost.

Identity

England's crest carries three lions, drawn from the royal arms, and the team's traditional kit is white shirts, navy shorts, and white socks. Its anthem at tournaments is sung by supporters as "Football's Coming Home", from the 1996 song "Three Lions". Rivalries with Scotland, Germany, and Argentina are among the most storied in international football.

Records

Peter Shilton is England's most-capped player with 125 appearances; Wayne Rooney held the scoring record with 53 goals until Harry Kane surpassed it in 2023. England's largest win is 13–0 against Ireland in 1882.
