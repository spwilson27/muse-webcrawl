The Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 is an Act of the Parliament of India that codifies the law relating to marriage among Hindus. It was one of the Hindu Code Bills enacted in the 1950s to reform and consolidate Hindu personal law. It introduced monogamy as a legal requirement, set conditions for a valid marriage, and provided for divorce and judicial separation, which traditional law had largely not recognised.

Applicability

The Act applies to Hindus by religion, as well as to Buddhists, Jains and Sikhs, and to other persons domiciled in India who are not Muslims, Christians, Parsis or Jews, unless it is proved that they would not have been governed by Hindu law or custom.

Conditions for marriage

Section 5 lays down conditions for a valid Hindu marriage: neither party may have a living spouse, both must be capable of giving valid consent and of sound mind, the bridegroom must have completed 21 years and the bride 18 years, and the parties must not be within prohibited degrees of relationship or be sapindas of each other, unless custom permits. Section 7 recognises marriages solemnised according to the customary rites of either party, including the saptapadi (seven steps around the sacred fire) where applicable.

Registration, divorce and remedies

Section 8 provides for registration of marriages. The Act provides remedies including restitution of conjugal rights (Section 9), judicial separation (Section 10) and divorce (Section 13) on grounds such as cruelty, desertion, adultery, conversion, mental disorder and renunciation of the world, as well as divorce by mutual consent (Section 13B, added in 1976).
