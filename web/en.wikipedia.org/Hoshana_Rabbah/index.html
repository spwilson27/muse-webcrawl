Hoshana Rabbah

Willow branches (aravot) used in the Hoshana Rabbah ritual

Hoshana Rabbah (Aramaic: הושענא רבה, "Great Hoshana" or "Great Supplication") is the seventh day of the Jewish harvest festival of Sukkot, the 21st day of the month of Tishrei. The day is marked by a synagogue service in which seven circuits are made around the bimah with the lulav and etrog, while the congregation recites the Hoshanot prayers; afterwards, bundles of willow branches are beaten on the ground.

Customs

The name derives from the refrain "Hoshana" ("Save, we pray") repeated throughout Sukkot but multiplied on the seventh day. During the morning service worshippers circle the Torah scrolls seven times, recalling the processions around the altar in the Temple in Jerusalem, where willow branches were laid beside the altar each day of the festival. At the conclusion, each worshipper takes a bundle of five willow branches and strikes it against the floor five times, a custom described in rabbinic tradition as a prayer for rain and a symbolic shaking off of sin.

Theological significance

According to rabbinic teaching, although judgment is sealed on Yom Kippur, the verdict is not finally delivered until Hoshana Rabbah, giving the day an atmosphere of a last opportunity for repentance. Greeters traditionally wish one another a good "final sealing." In Kabbalistic tradition the night of Hoshana Rabbah is spent in study, and the following day, Shemini Atzeret, begins the prayers for the winter rains.
