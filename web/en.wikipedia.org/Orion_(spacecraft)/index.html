Orion (officially the Orion Multi-Purpose Crew Vehicle) is a partially reusable crewed spacecraft developed by NASA for exploration beyond low Earth orbit. It is launched atop the Space Launch System (SLS) and consists of a crew module built by Lockheed Martin and a European Service Module supplied by the European Space Agency and built by Airbus. Orion is the crew vehicle of the Artemis program, intended to return astronauts to the Moon and eventually support missions to Mars.

Design

The crew module is a conical capsule 5 metres in diameter, similar in shape to the Apollo command module but considerably larger in internal volume (about 690 cubic feet of habitable volume). It carries up to four astronauts for missions of up to 21 days. Its heat shield, 5 metres across, is the largest of its kind and uses an ablative Avcoat material to withstand re-entry speeds of about 40,000 km/h returning from the Moon — roughly 1.4 times the heating of a return from the International Space Station.

The European Service Module provides propulsion, electrical power (from four solar array wings), thermal control, and consumables such as water and oxygen. A launch abort system tower sits atop the capsule during launch to pull the crew module away in an emergency.

History

Orion originated in the Constellation program of the 2000s as the Crew Exploration Vehicle. After Constellation's cancellation in 2010, the vehicle was retained and renamed the Multi-Purpose Crew Vehicle. Its first test flight, Exploration Flight Test-1 (EFT-1), flew uncrewed on a Delta IV Heavy on 5 December 2014, reaching an altitude of about 5,800 km and testing the heat shield at near-lunar return speeds.

The first integrated flight with the SLS, Artemis I, launched uncrewed on 16 November 2022. Orion travelled to a distant retrograde orbit around the Moon and returned to Earth on 11 December 2022, splashing down in the Pacific Ocean after a 25.5-day mission. The follow-on mission, Artemis II, is planned as the first crewed Orion flight, a free-return lunar flyby carrying four astronauts; Artemis III is intended to land astronauts near the lunar south pole.
