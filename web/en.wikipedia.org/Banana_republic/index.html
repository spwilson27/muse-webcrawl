Banana republic

A banana republic is a politically unstable country with an economy dependent upon the export of a single limited-resource commodity, such as bananas or minerals. The term was coined by American author O. Henry in 1904, and came to describe countries — often in Central America — whose politics and economies were dominated by foreign corporations, particularly the United Fruit Company.

Origin of the term

O. Henry (William Sydney Porter) coined the phrase in his 1904 book Cabbages and Kings, set in the fictional Republic of Anchuria, based on his experiences in Honduras. The term originally described servile dictatorships that abetted foreign corporate exploitation.

Characteristics

Typical features include a ruling oligarchy, widespread corruption, a large and poorly paid workforce, dependence on a single export crop, and infrastructure built primarily to serve foreign trade rather than domestic needs.

Modern usage

The term is now applied more broadly to any corrupt or unstable government, and is often used pejoratively in political commentary. Related concepts include the resource curse and neo-colonialism.
