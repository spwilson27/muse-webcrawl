Chukwuemeka Odumegwu Ojukwu

Chukwuemeka "Emeka" Odumegwu Ojukwu (4 November 1933 – 26 November 2011) was a Nigerian military officer and politician who served as the leader of the breakaway Republic of Biafra from 1967 to 1970 during the Nigerian Civil War. An Igbo nationalist, he was military governor of Nigeria's Eastern Region in 1966 and led the region's secession after massacres of Igbo people in northern Nigeria.

Early life and education

Ojukwu was born in Zungeru, northern Nigeria, the son of Sir Louis Odumegwu Ojukwu, a wealthy Igbo businessman and one of the richest men in Nigeria. He was educated at King's College, Lagos, Epsom College in England, and Lincoln College, Oxford, where he earned a degree in history. Against his father's wishes, he joined the Nigerian Army in 1957 and trained at Sandhurst in England.

Military career

Rising quickly through the officer corps, Ojukwu served with the Nigerian contingent of UN peacekeepers in the Congo and commanded the 5th Battalion at Kano. After the military coups of 1966 and the counter-coup that brought Yakubu Gowon to power, Ojukwu, as governor of the Eastern Region, refused to recognize Gowon's authority. Following the failure of the Aburi Accord, he declared the independence of the Republic of Biafra on 30 May 1967.

Nigerian Civil War

The ensuing civil war (1967–1970) saw Biafra besieged and starved; estimates of the dead, mostly from famine, range from one to three million. Ojukwu led the Biafran war effort until January 1970, when, with defeat imminent, he departed for Côte d'Ivoire, leaving his deputy Philip Effiong to surrender to the federal government.

Exile, return, and later life

Ojukwu spent twelve years in exile before receiving a pardon from President Shehu Shagari in 1982. He returned to Nigeria and entered politics, running unsuccessfully for the senate and, later, for the presidency under the All Progressives Grand Alliance (APGA). He died in London in 2011 and was given a state funeral; his remains were buried at his hometown of Nnewi.
