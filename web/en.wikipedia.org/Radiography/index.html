Radiography

Taking a chest radiograph.

Radiography is an imaging technique using X-rays, gamma rays, or similar ionizing radiation and non-ionizing radiation to view the internal form of an object and produce a projectional image called a radiograph. The radiation passes through the body or object and is differentially absorbed according to the density and composition of the material; the emerging beam is recorded on film or, in modern practice, on digital detectors.

Medical radiography

Medical radiography is performed by radiographers (radiologic technologists) and interpreted by radiologists. Common examinations include chest X-rays, skeletal imaging for fractures, mammography, fluoroscopy (real-time imaging), and dental radiography. Computed tomography (CT) extends the principle by reconstructing cross-sectional images from many projections. The technique dates to Wilhelm Röntgen's discovery of X-rays in 1895, for which he received the first Nobel Prize in Physics in 1901.

Industrial radiography

Industrial radiography uses X-rays or gamma sources (such as iridium-192) for non-destructive testing of welds, castings, pipelines and structures, and radiographic scanners are used in cargo and security inspection.
