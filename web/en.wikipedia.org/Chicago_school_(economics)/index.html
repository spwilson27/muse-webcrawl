Chicago school of economics

The Chicago school of economics is a neoclassical school of economic thought associated with the work of faculty at the University of Chicago, known for its strong belief in the efficiency of free markets and opposition to heavy government intervention.

History

The school took shape in the 1940s and 1950s around figures such as Frank Knight and Jacob Viner, and reached its greatest influence under Milton Friedman, George Stigler, and Gary Becker. Its members won numerous Nobel Memorial Prizes in Economic Sciences.

Ideas

Key ideas include monetarism, the efficient-market hypothesis developed by Eugene Fama, rational expectations, human capital theory, and the application of price theory and empirical testing to a wide range of social questions. The school strongly influenced deregulation and privatization policies in the United States, the United Kingdom, and Chile under Augusto Pinochet, where its adherents were known as the "Chicago Boys".
