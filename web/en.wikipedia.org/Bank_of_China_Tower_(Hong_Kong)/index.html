Bank of China Tower (Hong Kong)

The Bank of China Tower is a skyscraper in Central, Hong Kong, housing the headquarters of the Bank of China (Hong Kong). Designed by architect I. M. Pei and completed in 1990, it rises 367 metres including its masts and was the tallest building in Hong Kong and Asia at the time of its completion.

Design

The tower's distinctive triangular, faceted form is inspired by growing bamboo shoots, symbolising livelihood and prosperity. Its structure is carried by a space-truss system of four steel columns at the corners and a central column, allowing column-free interior floors. The design drew discussion among feng shui practitioners because of its sharp angles, said to direct negative energy at neighbouring buildings.

History

The site, formerly occupied by Murray House, was sold by the Hong Kong government on terms reflecting the territory's return to China in 1997. The tower opened in 1990 ahead of the handover and remains one of the most recognisable buildings on the Hong Kong skyline.
