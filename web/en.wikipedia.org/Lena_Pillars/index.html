The Lena Pillars (Russian: Ленские столбы, Lenskiye Stolby) are a natural rock formation along the banks of the Lena River in the Sakha Republic of far-eastern Siberia, Russia. The pillars are dramatic vertical limestone and dolomite formations, in places 150–300 metres high, extending for many kilometres along the river within the Lena Pillars Nature Park.

Geology

The rocks date from the early Cambrian period, roughly 490–540 million years ago, when the region lay beneath a shallow tropical sea. The pillars were sculpted by the extreme continental climate of Yakutia, where annual temperature swings approach 100 °C: freeze–thaw weathering, frost wedging, and erosion by the river fractured the rock along vertical joints, leaving towers, walls, and pinnacles.

The Cambrian limestones are rich in fossils of early marine life, including trilobites and archaeocyathids, and the site is considered of outstanding value for documenting the "Cambrian explosion" of animal life.

Nature park and heritage status

The Lena Pillars Nature Park was established in 1995 and covers two tracts along the Lena and its tributary the Buotama. In 2012 the park was inscribed on UNESCO's World Heritage List under natural criterion (viii). The park protects taiga wildlife including elk, brown bear, sable, and the Siberian musk deer, as well as ancient rock paintings and Turkic runic inscriptions found near the river.

The formation lies about 180 kilometres upriver from Yakutsk and is most commonly reached by river cruise from the city during the brief Siberian summer.
