Game controller

A game controller, or simply controller, is an input device used with video games or entertainment systems to provide input to a game, typically to control an object or character. Controllers connect to consoles, computers, or other devices, wired or wirelessly, and range from simple one-button joysticks to elaborate gamepads, steering wheels, light guns, motion controllers, and musical-instrument replicas.

History

Early controllers included the paddles of Pong and the joysticks of the Atari 2600 (1977). Nintendo's NES controller (1985) popularized the cross-shaped D-pad and the flat gamepad format; the Super NES added shoulder buttons; the Nintendo 64 (1996) mainstreamed the analog stick and rumble feedback; and Sony's DualShock line standardized the twin-stick layout that dominates today. Nintendo's Wii Remote (2006) brought motion control to a mass audience, and later systems added touchpads, adaptive triggers, and HD haptic feedback, as in the PlayStation 5's DualSense.

Types

Common types include gamepads, joysticks and flight sticks, racing wheels, light guns, dance mats, balance boards, VR controllers with positional tracking, and accessibility controllers designed for players with disabilities. On personal computers, the keyboard and mouse serve as the traditional control scheme, though gamepads are widely supported.
