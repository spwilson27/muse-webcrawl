Stephen, King of England

Reign: 22 December 1135 – 25 October 1154
Born: c. 1092/1096, Blois, France
Died: 25 October 1154, Dover, Kent
Spouse: Matilda I, Countess of Boulogne
House: Blois

Stephen (c. 1092/1096 – 25 October 1154), often referred to as Stephen of Blois, was King of England from 1135 until his death in 1154. He was the fourth son of Stephen II, Count of Blois, and Adela of Normandy, a daughter of William the Conqueror, and thus a nephew of King Henry I.

Reign and the Anarchy

On Henry I's death in 1135, Stephen seized the throne despite Henry having made his barons swear loyalty to his daughter, the Empress Matilda. Matilda's invasion of England in 1139 began a prolonged civil war known as the Anarchy. Stephen was captured at the Battle of Lincoln in 1141 but released in exchange for Matilda's half-brother Robert of Gloucester. The war settled into stalemate, with royal authority collapsing in much of the country.

Settlement and death

After the death of Stephen's son Eustace in 1153, Stephen agreed by the Treaty of Winchester to recognise Matilda's son, Henry of Anjou, as his heir. Stephen died the following year, and Henry succeeded him as Henry II, the first Plantagenet king.
