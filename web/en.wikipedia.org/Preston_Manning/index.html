Ernest Preston Manning (born 10 June 1942) is a Canadian politician and founder of the Reform Party of Canada, a populist conservative party that reshaped Canadian politics in the 1990s. He served as leader of the Official Opposition in the House of Commons from 1997 to 2000, and his "unite the right" campaign led to the creation of the Canadian Alliance and ultimately the modern Conservative Party of Canada.

Early life

Manning was born in Edmonton, Alberta, the son of Ernest Manning, the long-serving Social Credit premier of Alberta (1943–1968) and a prominent evangelical radio broadcaster on the "Back to the Bible Hour". Preston Manning studied economics and commerce at the University of Alberta, graduating in 1964. He ran unsuccessfully for Parliament as a Social Credit candidate in the 1965 federal election, then worked as a management consultant, co-authoring the book Political Realignment: A Challenge to Thoughtful Canadians (1967) with his father.

Founding the Reform Party

Frustrated by Western Canadian alienation and the failures of the federal Progressive Conservatives, Manning convened a founding convention in Winnipeg in 1987 — under the slogan "The West Wants In" — to launch the Reform Party of Canada. The party combined fiscal conservatism, populist democratic reforms (referendums, recall, free votes), and opposition to official bilingualism and special status for Quebec. Reform won its first seat in a 1989 by-election (Deborah Grey) and announced its arrival nationally in the 1993 federal election, winning 52 seats, mostly in the West, and helping reduce the governing Progressive Conservatives to two seats.

Leader of the Opposition

In the 1997 election, Reform won 60 seats and Manning became Leader of the Opposition. In 1998–2000 he spearheaded the United Alternative movement to merge Reform with the Progressive Conservatives. The resulting Canadian Reform Conservative Alliance (Canadian Alliance) was founded in 2000, but Stockwell Day defeated Manning in the new party's leadership race. Manning retired from Parliament in 2002.

Later career

After politics, Manning founded the Manning Centre for Building Democracy (2005), a think tank and training organisation for conservative activists, later renamed the Canada Strong and Free Network. He has remained an influential voice in conservative and democratic-reform debates, including during the COVID-19 pandemic, when the Alberta government appointed him to chair a panel reviewing its pandemic response (2023). He was made a Companion of the Order of Canada.
