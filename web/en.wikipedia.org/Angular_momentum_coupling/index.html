Angular momentum coupling

In quantum mechanics, angular momentum coupling is the procedure of constructing eigenstates of a total angular momentum operator from the eigenstates of the individual angular momenta of a system's parts — for example, combining an electron's orbital angular momentum L and its spin S into a total J = L + S, or combining the angular momenta of two particles. Because the components of angular momentum do not all commute, the coupling bookkeeping of quantum numbers and expansion coefficients (Clebsch–Gordan coefficients and their relatives, the 3-j, 6-j, and 9-j symbols) is an essential tool of atomic, nuclear, and particle spectroscopy.

Coupled and uncoupled bases

Two angular momenta j1 and j2 can combine into a total j ranging over |j1 − j2| ≤ j ≤ j1 + j2 in integer steps. The coupled states |j m⟩ are related to the uncoupled product states |j1 m1⟩|j2 m2⟩ by the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients ⟨j1 m1 j2 m2 | j m⟩, which vanish unless m = m1 + m2. Eugene Wigner and Giulio Racah recast the theory in terms of 3-j symbols (symmetric forms of Clebsch–Gordan coefficients), 6-j symbols (for recoupling three angular momenta, as when changing coupling order), and 9-j symbols (for four).

Coupling schemes in atoms

  • LS (Russell–Saunders) coupling — the orbital momenta of all electrons sum to L and their spins to S, which then couple to J. It is a good approximation for light atoms, where electrostatic repulsion between electrons exceeds spin-orbit interaction.
  • jj coupling — each electron's l and s first form its own j; the individual j's then sum to J. Favoured in heavy atoms where spin-orbit coupling is strong.
  • Intermediate coupling — realistic cases between the two limits, treated by mixing configurations.

These schemes generate the term symbols (such as ³P₂) that organize atomic spectra, and analogous couplings structure nuclear shell-model states (including isospin coupling) and the spin wavefunctions of hadrons.

Spin-orbit interaction

The coupling of spin and orbital angular momentum produces fine structure in spectra through the spin-orbit interaction, whose strength grows roughly with the fourth power of nuclear charge; in nuclei the same interaction, with opposite sign and much greater strength, is essential to explaining the magic numbers of the shell model. The Wigner–Eckart theorem, the crowning result of coupling theory, factorizes matrix elements of tensor operators into a geometric (Clebsch–Gordan) part and a physical reduced matrix element, underpinning selection rules throughout spectroscopy.
