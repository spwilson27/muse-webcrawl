La Via Campesina (from Spanish: "the peasants' way") is an international movement bringing together millions of peasants, small and medium-sized farmers, landless people, indigenous people, migrants and agricultural workers from around the world. Founded in 1993, it defends small-scale sustainable agriculture as a way to promote social justice and dignity.

History

The movement was founded in Mons, Belgium, in 1993 by farmers' organisations reacting to the Uruguay Round of GATT negotiations and the effects of trade liberalisation on small producers. It has grown into one of the largest social movements in the world, with member organisations in over 80 countries.

Food sovereignty

La Via Campesina coined and champions the concept of food sovereignty — the right of peoples to define their own food and agriculture systems — which it distinguishes from the narrower concept of food security. It advocates agrarian reform, agroecology, peasant seed systems, and the defence of land and water commons.

Declaration on the Rights of Peasants

The movement was a driving force behind the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Peasants and Other People Working in Rural Areas (UNDROP), adopted by the UN General Assembly in 2018 after years of campaigning.

Organisation

La Via Campesina is organised into regional blocs, holds an international conference every few years, and coordinates campaigns such as the April 17 International Day of Peasants' Struggle.
