Bilinear form

In mathematics, a bilinear form on a vector space V over a field K is a bilinear map V × V → K: that is, a function B : V × V → K that is linear in each argument separately. An important special case is the dot product (inner product) on a real vector space.

Every bilinear form on a finite-dimensional space is represented by a matrix.

Definition

Let V be a vector space over a field K. A bilinear form is a map B : V × V → K satisfying, for all vectors u, v, w in V and all scalars λ in K:

  • B(u + v, w) = B(u, w) + B(v, w)
  • B(u, v + w) = B(u, v) + B(u, w)
  • B(λu, v) = B(u, λv) = λB(u, v)

Matrix representation

Choosing a basis for V, the form is determined by the matrix A with entries B(ei, ej), and B(x, y) = xTAy. The form is called non-degenerate if this matrix is invertible. Under a change of basis the matrix transforms by congruence, A ↦ PTAP.

Symmetry and related forms

A bilinear form is symmetric if B(x, y) = B(y, x) and alternating (or skew-symmetric) if B(x, x) = 0 for all x. Symmetric forms give rise to quadratic forms; alternating forms generalize the symplectic structures central to Hamiltonian mechanics. Over the complex numbers the analogous notion is the sesquilinear form, linear in one argument and conjugate-linear in the other.
