Howard Schultz

Howard Schultz (born July 19, 1953) is an American businessman and author, best known for transforming Starbucks into a global coffeehouse chain. He served as chief executive officer of the Starbucks Coffee Company in several periods, first from 1986 to 2000, then from 2008 to 2017, and again on an interim basis from 2022 to 2023.

Early life

Schultz was born in Brooklyn, New York, and raised in public housing in Canarsie. He attended Northern Michigan University on a football scholarship and worked in sales for Xerox and for the housewares company Hammarplast before encountering Starbucks, then a small Seattle retailer of coffee beans and equipment.

Starbucks

After a trip to Milan inspired him with the culture of Italian espresso bars, Schultz founded his own coffee-bar chain, Il Giornale, in 1985; in 1987 Il Giornale acquired Starbucks and adopted its name. Under Schultz the company expanded across North America and internationally, went public in 1992, and grew into tens of thousands of stores worldwide. He stepped down as CEO in 2000 to become chairman, returned as CEO in 2008 during a difficult period for the company, and retired as executive chairman in 2018. In 2022 he returned as interim CEO before handing over to Laxman Narasimhan in 2023.

Public life and writing

Schultz authored several books, including Pour Your Heart Into It (1997) and Onward (2011). In 2019 he publicly explored an independent candidacy for President of the United States before deciding not to run. He has also been involved in philanthropy and public debate through the Schultz Family Foundation, and was formerly an owner of the Seattle SuperSonics basketball team.
