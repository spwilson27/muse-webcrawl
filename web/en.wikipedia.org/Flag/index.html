Flag

A flag is a piece of fabric (most often rectangular or quadrilateral) with a distinctive design and colours. It is used as a symbol, a signalling device, or for decoration. Flags originally served in military coordination and identification on the battlefield and at sea; they have since become a general tool for conveying identity and messages, used by nations, states, organisations, companies and individuals.

The study of flags is known as vexillology, from the Latin vexillum, a kind of square flag carried by Roman cavalry.

Design

Flags are usually rectangular, most commonly in proportions such as 2:3, 3:5 or 1:2. The parts of a flag include the hoist (the half nearest the pole), the fly (the half farthest from it), the canton (the upper hoist quarter), and the field (background). Common design elements are crosses, stripes, fesses and charges — emblems placed on the field. National flags frequently employ symbolic colours and devices representing a country's history, geography or values. The flags of Nepal (non-rectangular, double-pennon) and Switzerland and Vatican City (square) are notable exceptions to the rectangular norm.

National flags

Every sovereign state has a national flag; the oldest continuously used one is generally considered to be that of Denmark, the Dannebrog, legendarily dating to 1219 and documented from the 14th century. National flags are flown on public buildings and warships, carried in ceremonies, and draped on coffins of honoured dead. Most countries also have distinct civil and state flags and naval ensigns.

Uses and protocol

Beyond national identity, flags serve as maritime signal flags (the International Code of Signals), racing and sports signals, rallying symbols for movements, and markers of office. Many jurisdictions codify flag protocol: rules for hoisting at half-mast in mourning, for precedence among flags flown together, and against allowing a flag to touch the ground. Desecration of flags is a politically charged act that is protected as free expression in some countries and criminalised in others.
