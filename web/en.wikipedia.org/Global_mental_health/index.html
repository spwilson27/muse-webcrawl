Global mental health

Global mental health is the international perspective on different aspects of mental health. It is the field of study, research and practice that places a priority on improving mental health and achieving equity in mental health for all people worldwide. The field addresses the large treatment gap between the burden of mental disorders and the availability of care, particularly in low- and middle-income countries.

Burden of mental disorders

Mental, neurological and substance-use disorders account for a substantial share of the global burden of disease. Depression and anxiety disorders are among the leading causes of disability worldwide. The World Health Organization has estimated that roughly one in eight people in the world lives with a mental disorder, yet in many countries the majority of affected people receive no treatment at all — the so-called treatment gap, which can exceed 75 percent in low-income settings.

Key initiatives

The modern global mental health movement is often dated to the 2007 and 2011 Lancet series on global mental health, which called for scaling up services for mental disorders. The WHO's Mental Health Gap Action Programme (mhGAP), launched in 2008, provides guidance for delivering mental health care in non-specialist settings, an approach known as task shifting or task sharing. The annual Mind Matters reports and the work of organisations such as the Movement for Global Mental Health have kept advocacy prominent. In 2015, mental health was included in the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals under target 3.4.

Criticism

Some scholars and activists criticise the global mental health movement for exporting Western diagnostic categories and biomedical models to cultures in which distress is understood differently, and for under-emphasising social determinants such as poverty, violence and inequality. Supporters respond that untreated mental illness causes immense suffering and that culturally adapted, evidence-based care can be delivered respectfully.
