Jonah Tali Lomu MNZM (12 May 1975 – 18 November 2015) was a New Zealand rugby union player. Widely regarded as one of the greatest and most influential players in the history of the game, he became rugby's first global superstar with his performances at the 1995 Rugby World Cup, where his combination of size, strength and speed on the wing transformed the sport's commercial appeal.

Early life and rise

Lomu was born in Auckland to Tongan parents and raised in Mangere. Educated at Wesley College, he was spotted playing rugby league before switching to union and starring in sevens under coach Gordon Tietjens. In 1994, aged 19 years and 45 days, he became the youngest ever All Black test player when he debuted against France.

1995 Rugby World Cup

At the 1995 World Cup in South Africa, Lomu scored seven tries in five matches, including four in the semi-final against England — one of them an iconic run straight over fullback Mike Catt. Although the All Blacks lost the final to South Africa, Lomu's impact was credited with driving rugby union's move into professionalism, sealed by the SANZAR television deal that followed.

Illness and later career

In 1996 Lomu was diagnosed with nephrotic syndrome, a serious kidney disorder. He missed much of the following seasons but returned to star at the 1999 World Cup, scoring eight tries, and remains the Rugby World Cup's joint record try-scorer with 15. His health forced his retirement from top-level rugby in 2002 after 63 caps and 37 tries. He received a kidney transplant in 2004 and made comeback attempts with North Harbour, Cardiff Blues, Marseille Vitrolles and Counties Manukau before finally retiring in 2007.

Death and legacy

Lomu died unexpectedly in Auckland on 18 November 2015, aged 40, from a heart attack linked to his kidney condition, shortly after returning from the 2015 Rugby World Cup in England. Public memorials were held in Auckland and Wellington. He was inducted into the World Rugby Hall of Fame in 2011, and is remembered as the player who made rugby a global spectacle.
