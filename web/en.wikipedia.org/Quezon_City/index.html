Quezon City (Filipino: Lungsod Quezon) is a highly urbanized city in Metro Manila, Philippines. It is the most populous city in the country and was the national capital from 1948 to 1976. The city was named after Manuel L. Quezon, the second president of the Philippines, who founded it and developed it to replace Manila as the national capital.

History

Quezon City was created in 1939 from land carved out of surrounding towns in Rizal province, including areas of Caloocan, Marikina, Pasig, and San Juan. President Quezon envisioned a planned capital city with wide avenues, parks, and government buildings arranged around an elliptical circle—now the Quezon Memorial Circle. Although the capital reverted to Manila in 1976, many national government offices remain in Quezon City, including the Batasang Pambansa Complex, the seat of the House of Representatives.

Geography and districts

The city is divided into six congressional districts and 142 barangays. It hosts the main campus of the University of the Philippines Diliman, the Ateneo de Manila University, and major broadcasting networks. Landmarks include the Quezon Memorial Shrine, La Mesa Watershed, and the Araneta City commercial centre in Cubao.

Economy

Quezon City is a centre for media, information technology, healthcare, and education in the Philippines. It hosts major television networks, numerous hospitals such as the Philippine Heart Center and St. Luke's Medical Center, and large shopping malls.
