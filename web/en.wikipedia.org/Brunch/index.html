Brunch

TypeMeal
Typical timeLate morning / early afternoon
OriginEngland, 19th century

Brunch is a meal that combines breakfast and lunch, eaten in the late morning to early afternoon, generally between 10:00 a.m. and 2:00 p.m. The word is a portmanteau of breakfast and lunch. Brunch typically includes foods from both meals, such as eggs, bacon, pancakes, pastries, salads, and sandwiches, and is often served with coffee, tea, or alcoholic drinks such as mimosas and Bloody Marys.

History

The term first appeared in print in 1895, in an article by Guy Beringer in Hunter's Weekly titled "Brunch: A Plea", which proposed a late Sunday meal for "Saturday-night carousers" as an alternative to heavy post-church breakfasts. The custom spread at Oxford University and later in the United States, where it became especially popular in New York in the 1930s and as a weekend social ritual after the Second World War.

Foods

Typical brunch dishes include eggs Benedict, omelettes, French toast, waffles, smoked salmon, fruit, and baked goods. Many restaurants offer buffet-style or prix-fixe brunch menus on Saturdays and Sundays.

Cultural significance

Brunch has become a prominent social institution in many cities, associated with leisure, weekends, and holidays such as Easter and Mother's Day, which is one of the busiest brunch days of the year in the United States.
