Yale University is a private Ivy League research university in New Haven, Connecticut. Founded in 1701, it is the third-oldest institution of higher education in the United States and one of the most prestigious universities in the world.

History

Yale traces its beginnings to a 1701 act of the Connecticut Colony's legislature establishing the Collegiate School in Killingworth, later Saybrook. In 1716 the school moved to New Haven, and in 1718 it was renamed Yale College in honour of Elihu Yale, a Welsh merchant and governor of Madras who donated proceeds from the sale of nine bales of goods, 417 books, and a portrait of King George I.

Yale became a university in 1887. Its graduate and professional schools include Yale Law School, the School of Medicine, the School of Management, the Divinity School, and the School of Drama. The residential college system, modelled on Oxford and Cambridge, was established in the 1930s with funding from Edward Harkness.

Campus and collections

The central campus is noted for Collegiate Gothic architecture, including Harkness Tower and Sterling Memorial Library. The Yale University Library system holds more than 15 million volumes. The Beinecke Rare Book and Manuscript Library preserves a Gutenberg Bible and the mysterious Voynich manuscript. The Yale University Art Gallery is the oldest university art museum in the Western Hemisphere.

People

Yale alumni include five U.S. presidents — William Howard Taft, George H. W. Bush, Bill Clinton, George W. Bush, and (as vice president who became associated) — as well as numerous heads of state, Supreme Court justices, and Nobel laureates. Secret societies such as Skull and Bones, founded in 1832, are a famous part of Yale lore. Its undergraduate admissions are among the most selective in the United States.
