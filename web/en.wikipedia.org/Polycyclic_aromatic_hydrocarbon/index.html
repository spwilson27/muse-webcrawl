Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon

A polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) is a hydrocarbon — a chemical compound containing only carbon and hydrogen — composed of multiple aromatic rings. The group is a major subset of the aromatic hydrocarbons. The simplest member is naphthalene, having two aromatic rings, followed by anthracene and phenanthrene with three. Well-known higher PAHs include benzo[a]pyrene, chrysene, fluoranthene and pyrene.

PAHs are uncharged, non-polar molecules found in coal, petroleum and tar deposits, and are produced as by-products of fuel burning, whether fossil fuel or biomass. As pollutants, they are of concern because some compounds have been identified as carcinogenic, mutagenic and teratogenic.

Formation

PAHs form during incomplete combustion of organic matter at high temperatures, such as in engines, incinerators, forest fires, cigarette smoke and charbroiled food. They are also abundant in the interstellar medium: PAH emission features observed by infrared telescopes suggest these molecules are widespread in space and may have contributed carbon compounds to the early Earth.

Health effects

Several PAHs, most notably benzo[a]pyrene, are potent carcinogens. The mechanism involves metabolic activation by cytochrome P450 enzymes to diol epoxides, which bind covalently to DNA and cause mutations. Occupational exposure in coke production, coal gasification and aluminium smelting has been linked to elevated risks of lung, skin and bladder cancer. Regulatory agencies set limits for PAHs in drinking water, food and air emissions.

Environmental behaviour

PAHs are hydrophobic and persist in soils and sediments, where they degrade slowly. Lower-molecular-weight PAHs are more volatile and more soluble, while the larger five- and six-ring compounds bind strongly to particulate matter and bioaccumulate to a lesser extent than many persistent organic pollutants because organisms can metabolise them.

Examples

  • Naphthalene (two rings)
  • Anthracene and phenanthrene (three rings)
  • Pyrene (four rings)
  • Benzo[a]pyrene (five rings)
  • Coronene (seven rings)
