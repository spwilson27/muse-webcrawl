Barton Swing Aqueduct

Barton Swing Aqueduct
Carries: Bridgewater Canal
Crosses: Manchester Ship Canal
Locale: Barton upon Irwell, Greater Manchester
Opened: 1894
Design: Swing bridge / aqueduct

The Barton Swing Aqueduct is a moveable navigable aqueduct in Barton upon Irwell in Greater Manchester, England. It carries the Bridgewater Canal across the Manchester Ship Canal. The aqueduct, which opened in 1894, is the first and only swing aqueduct in the world; when ships need to pass along the ship canal, the water-filled trough is rotated through 90 degrees.

The aqueduct swung open to allow shipping to pass.

Design and operation

The aqueduct replaced Brindley's earlier Barton Aqueduct, a masonry structure demolished to make way for the ship canal. Designed by Edward Leader Williams, the engineer of the Manchester Ship Canal, the swing aqueduct consists of a steel trough 71.6 metres (235 ft) long and 5.5 metres (18 ft) wide, holding about 800 tonnes of water. Gates at each end of the trough, and matching gates on the canal banks, seal in the water while the structure turns on a pivot supported on an island in the middle of the ship canal. Hydraulic machinery originally powered the swing; the aqueduct is operated from a control tower on the island.

Nearby stands the Barton Road Swing Bridge, which swings in coordination with the aqueduct. The structure is a Grade II* listed building.
