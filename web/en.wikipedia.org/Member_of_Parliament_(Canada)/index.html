Member of Parliament (Canada)

A member of Parliament (MP) is a person elected to the House of Commons of Canada in a federal general election or by-election. Members represent single-member electoral districts (ridings) across the country's provinces and territories and sit in the Parliament of Canada in Ottawa, alongside the Senate and the Crown.

Composition

Following redistribution, the House of Commons has 343 seats. Each MP represents one electoral district, apportioned among the provinces roughly by population, with constitutional guarantees of minimum representation and one seat for each of the three territories.

Election and term

MPs are elected by first-past-the-post voting. Under federal law, a Parliament may last up to four years (with a constitutional maximum of five), and general elections are ordinarily held on a fixed date in October of the fourth year, though the Governor General may dissolve Parliament earlier on the advice of the Prime Minister.

Role

Members debate and vote on legislation, serve on committees, hold the government to account during Question Period, and handle constituency casework for residents. Government MPs generally belong to the governing party, whose leader serves as Prime Minister; MPs enjoy parliamentary privilege, including free speech in the House.
