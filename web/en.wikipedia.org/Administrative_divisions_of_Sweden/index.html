The administrative divisions of Sweden consist principally of 21 counties (län) for state administration and 290 municipalities (kommuner) for local self-government. Alongside the counties, 21 regions (regioner, formerly county councils) provide services such as health care and public transport.

Counties

Each county is administered by a County Administrative Board (länsstyrelse) headed by a governor appointed by the national government. The counties date largely from a 1634 reform by Axel Oxenstierna, though boundaries have since changed; the newest counties, such as Västra Götaland and Skåne, were formed by mergers in the 1990s.

Municipalities

Municipalities are responsible for schools, elder care, water, and planning. They range in population from Stockholm, with nearly a million residents, to small rural municipalities of a few thousand. Municipalities may be subdivided into districts for some elections and services; Sweden's historical provinces (landskap) have no administrative role.
