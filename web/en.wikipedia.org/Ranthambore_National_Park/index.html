Ranthambore National Park

LocationSawai Madhopur district, Rajasthan, India
Area1,334 km² (including buffer)
Established1980 (national park); 1973 (tiger reserve)
Governing bodyForest Department, Government of Rajasthan

Ranthambore National Park is a national park in the Sawai Madhopur district of southeastern Rajasthan, India. It is one of the largest national parks in northern India and is famed for its Bengal tigers, which are frequently observed during daylight hours against the backdrop of lakes, ruins and the tenth-century Ranthambore Fort.

History

The area was established as the Sawai Madhopur Game Sanctuary in 1955 and was declared one of the Project Tiger reserves in 1973. In 1980 it became a national park, and the adjacent forests were later added as buffer zones. The park takes its name from the historic Ranthambore Fort, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, which stands on a hill within the reserve.

Flora and fauna

Ranthambore lies at the junction of the Aravalli and Vindhya hill ranges. Its dry deciduous forests, dominated by dhok trees, are interspersed with lakes such as Padam Talao and Raj Bagh. Besides tigers, the park supports leopards, striped hyenas, sambar deer, chital, nilgai, sloth bears, wild boar, and more than 270 species of birds. The tigress Machali, who lived to an exceptional age and was the subject of several documentaries, became the park's most famous resident.

Tourism

The park is open to visitors from October to June and is divided into safari zones. It is one of India's most popular wildlife destinations and is readily accessible by rail to Sawai Madhopur.
