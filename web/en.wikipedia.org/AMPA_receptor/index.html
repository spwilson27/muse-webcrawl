AMPA receptor

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The α-amino-3-hydroxy-5-methyl-4-isoxazolepropionic acid receptor (AMPA receptor, AMPAR), also known as the quisqualate receptor, is an ionotropic transmembrane receptor for glutamate that mediates fast synaptic transmission in the central nervous system.

Structure

AMPA receptors are tetramers assembled from four subunits, GluA1–GluA4 (also called GluR1–GluR4), each encoded by a separate gene. Most native receptors are heterotetramers containing the GluA2 subunit, which — because of RNA editing at the Q/R site — renders the channel impermeable to calcium. Receptors lacking GluA2 are calcium-permeable and inwardly rectifying.

Function

When glutamate binds, the channel opens and permits Na+ (and K+) flux, producing the fast rising phase of the excitatory postsynaptic current. Trafficking of AMPA receptors into and out of the postsynaptic density underlies long-term potentiation and long-term depression, core mechanisms of learning and memory.

Pharmacology

Perampanel, a non-competitive AMPA receptor antagonist, is approved as an antiepileptic drug. Ampakines are positive allosteric modulators studied for cognitive disorders. Classical experimental antagonists include NBQX and CNQX.
