Metro-North Railroad

The Metro-North Commuter Railroad Company, trading as MTA Metro-North Railroad, is a commuter rail service in the New York metropolitan area, run by the Metropolitan Transportation Authority (MTA). It is the second-busiest commuter railroad in the United States, serving the northern suburbs of New York City in New York State and southwestern Connecticut, with lines radiating from Grand Central Terminal in Manhattan.

Lines

East of the Hudson River, Metro-North operates the Hudson, Harlem and New Haven Lines from Grand Central — the New Haven Line, run jointly with the Connecticut Department of Transportation, includes the New Canaan, Danbury and Waterbury branches. West of the Hudson, services from Hoboken and Penn Station are operated under contract by NJ Transit: the Port Jervis Line and part of the Pascack Valley Line. The system covers roughly 385 miles of track and about 120 stations.

History

Metro-North was created in 1983 when the MTA took over Conrail's commuter services inherited from the bankrupt Penn Central and the New York Central and New Haven railroads. It inherited Grand Central Terminal, electrified territory (third rail and, on the New Haven Line, overhead catenary), and a mix of diesel and electric multiple-unit fleets, and has since invested in rolling stock such as the M7 and M8 cars, new stations, and, in 2023, direct service to Penn Station via East Side Access for the Long Island Rail Road freed capacity debates, while Metro-North's own Penn Station Access project is planned to bring New Haven Line trains to Penn Station.
