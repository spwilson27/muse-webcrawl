Isère is a department in the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region of south-eastern France, named after the river Isère, a tributary of the Rhône. Its prefecture is Grenoble, and it is one of the original 83 departments created during the French Revolution in 1790. With terrain ranging from the Rhône plain to high Alpine peaks, it is among the most mountainous departments in France.

Geography

Isère borders Savoie and Haute-Savoie to the north-east, Italy's Piedmont region to the east, Hautes-Alpes, Drôme, Ardèche, Loire, Rhône and Ain. It includes parts of the French Alps — the Belledonne, Écrins, Grandes Rousses, Taillefer and Vercors massifs — and the Chartreuse mountains. The highest point is in the Écrins massif. The river Isère flows through Grenoble, and the Drac and Romanche are other major waterways.

History

The area corresponds largely to the southern part of the historical province of Dauphiné, whose dauphins ruled from Grenoble. It joined the French crown in the 14th century. Grenoble and the department played a notable role in the French Resistance during the Second World War; the Vercors plateau was a famous maquis stronghold.

Economy and tourism

Grenoble is a major centre of research, microelectronics and higher education. Isère hosts major ski resorts including Alpe d'Huez and Les Deux Alpes, and is known for walnuts (the Noix de Grenoble AOP), Chartreuse liqueur made by Carthusian monks near Voiron, and cheese such as Saint-Marcellin.
