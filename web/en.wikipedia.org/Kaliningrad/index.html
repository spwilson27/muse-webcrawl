Kaliningrad (Russian: Калининград) is the largest city and administrative centre of Kaliningrad Oblast, a Russian exclave on the Baltic Sea coast between Poland and Lithuania, with no land connection to the rest of Russia. Founded in 1255 by the Teutonic Knights as Königsberg, the city was for centuries a major German city in East Prussia before passing to the Soviet Union after the Second World War, when its German population was expelled and replaced by settlers from across the USSR.

History

Königsberg grew around the castle built by the Teutonic Order during the Prussian Crusade and became a member of the Hanseatic League. It was the coronation city of the Prussian kings and home to the philosopher Immanuel Kant, who spent nearly his entire life there; his tomb stands beside the reconstructed cathedral on Kant Island. The mathematician Leonhard Euler's analysis of the city's seven bridges founded graph theory. Devastated by Allied bombing in 1944 and by the Soviet assault of April 1945, the city was annexed by the USSR under the Potsdam Agreement and renamed in 1946 for the Soviet leader Mikhail Kalinin.

Geography and economy

The city stands on the Pregolya River near its mouth in the Vistula Lagoon. Its ice-free port and the harbour at Baltiysk are strategically important as the home of Russia's Baltic Fleet. The region is the world's principal source of amber, and the city retains a fishing, shipbuilding, and manufacturing economy, with special economic zone status within Russia.

Sights

Landmarks include the Gothic Königsberg Cathedral, the restored Fishing Village quarter, the Brandenburg Gate and other surviving city gates, the Amber Museum, and Kant Island. The city's stadium hosted matches of the 2018 FIFA World Cup.
