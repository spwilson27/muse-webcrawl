San Siro, officially the Stadio Giuseppe Meazza, is a football stadium in Milan, Italy, and the shared home of the city's two great clubs, AC Milan and Inter Milan. It is one of the largest stadiums in Europe and among the most famous football venues in the world.

History

The stadium was built under AC Milan president Piero Pirelli and opened in 1926 in the San Siro district, from which it takes its common name. Inter moved in as co-tenants in 1947. In 1980 the stadium was renamed in honour of Giuseppe Meazza, the two-time World Cup winner who starred for Inter and also played for Milan. Expansions for the 1990 FIFA World Cup added a third tier and a roof supported by distinctive red girders and eleven cylindrical towers.

Events

San Siro hosted matches at the 1934 and 1990 World Cups, the 1980 European Championship, and four European Cup / Champions League finals, most recently in 2016. It is also a major concert venue. The stadium is scheduled to host the opening ceremony of the 2026 Winter Olympics.

Future

For many years Milan and Inter explored demolishing San Siro and building a new stadium nearby; the project was long blocked by heritage protections on the stadium's second tier, though in the mid-2020s the clubs moved toward purchasing and redeveloping the San Siro site, keeping the old stadium's future uncertain.
