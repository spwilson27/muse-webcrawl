Geometric group theory

Geometric group theory is an area in mathematics devoted to the study of finitely generated groups via exploring the connections between algebraic properties of such groups and topological and geometric properties of spaces on which these groups act — that is, when the groups are considered as geometric symmetries or continuous transformations of some spaces.

Basic ideas

The central tool is the Cayley graph: given a group with a finite generating set, its elements become vertices of a graph with edges labelled by generators. Endowed with the word metric, the graph is a geometric model of the group itself; changing the generating set changes the metric only up to quasi-isometry, so large-scale properties such as growth type, hyperbolicity, and the number of ends are invariants of the group. Max Dehn posed the word, conjugacy, and isomorphism problems in the 1910s, which remain guiding questions.

History

The subject grew out of combinatorial group theory and the geometry of fundamental groups of manifolds. It crystallized as a distinct field in the 1980s and 1990s through Mikhail Gromov's work, especially his 1987 essay on hyperbolic groups, which showed that most groups, in a suitable sense, are negatively curved. Gromov's programme connected the field to Riemannian geometry, low-dimensional topology, and later to the resolution of longstanding conjectures such as the virtually Haken conjecture, proved using Wise's and Agol's work on cube complexes.
