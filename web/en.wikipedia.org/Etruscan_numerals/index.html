Etruscan numerals

Etruscan numerals are the number words and digits used by the ancient Etruscans. The system was partly adapted by the Romans and forms the basis of the Roman numeral system used in Europe for more than a millennium.

Number words

The Etruscan words for the first six numbers are known from a pair of gaming dice inscribed with number words rather than symbols: θu (1), zal (2), ci (3), śa (4), maχ (5), and huθ (6). Higher numbers include semφ (7), cezp (8), nurφ (9), and sar (10). Numbers between ten and twenty were often formed subtractively, as in ciem zaθrum, "three from twenty," for seventeen.

Etruscans generally wrote from right to left, and numeral symbols could accordingly appear in reverse order. The Roman symbols I, V, X, L, and C derive from the Etruscan signs, with L coming from the Etruscan symbol for 50.
