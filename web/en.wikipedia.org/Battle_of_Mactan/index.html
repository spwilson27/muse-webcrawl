Battle of Mactan

The Battle of Mactan was fought on 27 April 1521 on Mactan Island in the Philippines between warriors led by the chieftain Lapulapu and a Spanish landing party led by the Portuguese explorer Ferdinand Magellan, then sailing for Spain. Magellan was killed in the battle, and Lapulapu is remembered in the Philippines as the first national hero for resisting foreign invasion.

Background

Magellan's expedition, seeking a westward route to the Spice Islands, had reached Cebu and formed an alliance with Rajah Humabon, who accepted baptism. When Lapulapu, a chieftain of neighbouring Mactan, refused to submit, Magellan undertook to subdue him with a small force, reportedly confident that European arms would easily prevail.

The battle

Some 49 Spaniards in armour waded ashore through shallow water that kept their boats — and their supporting fire — far from the beach, facing perhaps 1,500 of Lapulapu's warriors. Finding their crossbows and arquebuses of limited effect and their numbers overwhelmed, the landing party was driven back into the sea. Magellan, wounded in the leg by a poisoned arrow and struck down in the shallows, was killed as he covered his men's retreat, as recounted by the expedition's chronicler Antonio Pigafetta.

Aftermath

The survivors of the expedition, under Juan Sebastián Elcano after further losses at Cebu, completed the first circumnavigation of the Earth in 1522 with a single ship, the Victoria. The battle is commemorated by the Lapu-Lapu Monument and the Magellan shrine on Mactan.
