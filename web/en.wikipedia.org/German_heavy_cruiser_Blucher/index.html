German heavy cruiser Blücher

Blücher
ClassAdmiral Hipper-class heavy cruiser
Launched8 June 1937, Deutsche Werke, Kiel
Commissioned20 September 1939
DisplacementAbout 18,200 t full load

FateSunk 9 April 1940, Oslofjord, Norway

Blücher was the second of five Admiral Hipper-class heavy cruisers built for Nazi Germany's Kriegsmarine. Named after the Prussian field marshal Gebhard Leberecht von Blücher, she had barely completed trials when she was sunk during the invasion of Norway in April 1940, in one of the most famous actions of coastal defence in the Second World War.

Battle of Drøbak Sound

As flagship of the naval group tasked with capturing Oslo, Blücher led a force up the Oslofjord on the night of 8–9 April 1940, carrying troops intended to seize the Norwegian capital and its king and government. In the narrow Drøbak Sound, the elderly Norwegian coastal fortress of Oscarsborg opened fire at short range under Colonel Birger Eriksen, hitting Blücher with shells from 28 cm guns and torpedoes from a hidden shore torpedo battery. On fire and listing, the cruiser capsized and sank in the fjord; around 830 men were lost, though estimates vary. The delay allowed the Norwegian king, government and gold reserves to escape Oslo.

Wreck

Blücher still lies in the Oslofjord at a depth of about 64 metres. The wreck is a designated war grave and has long leaked small amounts of oil, prompting monitoring and plans for its preservation or partial oil removal.
