Leon Trotsky (born Lev Davidovich Bronstein; 7 November 1879 – 21 August 1940) was a Russian revolutionary, Soviet politician and Marxist theorist. A principal leader of the October Revolution of 1917, he founded and led the Red Army to victory in the Russian Civil War. After losing the struggle to succeed Lenin against Joseph Stalin, he was exiled in 1929 and assassinated in Mexico in 1940 by a Soviet agent. His political ideas form the basis of Trotskyism, a major current of Marxism distinct from Stalinism.

Early life

Born to a prosperous farming family of Ukrainian-Jewish origin in Yanovka (present-day Bereslavka, Ukraine), Bronstein became a Marxist as a student, was arrested for revolutionary activity in 1898, and exiled to Siberia. He escaped in 1902 using a forged passport bearing the name "Trotsky" — reportedly the name of a jailer — which he kept for life. In London he met Lenin and joined the Russian Social Democratic Labour Party, initially siding with the Mensheviks in the 1903 split before pursuing an independent position.

1905 and permanent revolution

Returning to Russia during the Revolution of 1905, Trotsky chaired the St Petersburg Soviet of Workers' Deputies, was arrested and again exiled to Siberia, escaping en route in 1907. From his 1905 experience he developed the theory of permanent revolution: in backward Russia, the working class could carry through not only the bourgeois-democratic revolution but proceed directly toward socialism, dependent on the spread of revolution to advanced countries.

1917 and the Civil War

Trotsky returned to Petrograd in May 1917, joined the Bolsheviks, and became chairman of the Petrograd Soviet. As head of its Military Revolutionary Committee he organised the October insurrection that brought the Bolsheviks to power. He served briefly as Commissar for Foreign Affairs, negotiating at Brest-Litovsk ("neither war nor peace"), then as Commissar for War built the Red Army from near-nothing into a force of millions, imposing strict discipline and recruiting former Tsarist officers supervised by political commissars. His armoured train became famous as he travelled ceaselessly between fronts during the Civil War (1918–1921).

Struggle with Stalin and exile

Lenin's final writings warned against Stalin, but after Lenin's death in 1924 Trotsky and his Left Opposition lost the power struggle: Trotsky was removed from the War Commissariat in 1925, expelled from the party in 1927, exiled to Alma-Ata in 1928, and deported from the USSR in 1929. From exile in Turkey, France, Norway and finally Mexico, he wrote prolifically — including My Life, the three-volume History of the Russian Revolution, and The Revolution Betrayed — denounced the Moscow Trials that condemned his former comrades, and in 1938 founded the Fourth International. On 20 August 1940 the NKVD agent Ramón Mercader attacked him with an ice axe in his home in Coyoacán, Mexico City; Trotsky died the following day.

Legacy

Trotsky remains one of the most controversial figures of the 20th century: hailed by followers as the uncorrupted heir of October and a brilliant writer and organiser, condemned by critics for his role in the Red Terror, the suppression of the Kronstadt rebellion, and the militarisation of labour. Trotskyist organisations worldwide continue to invoke his theories of permanent revolution and the transitional programme.
