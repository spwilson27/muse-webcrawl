River Ure

Length119 km (74 mi)
SourceUre Head, near the Cumbria border
MouthRiver Ouse at Cuddy Shaw Reach, near Linton-on-Ouse

The River Ure in North Yorkshire, England, is the principal river of Wensleydale, one of the Yorkshire Dales. It is about 119 kilometres (74 miles) long from its source near the Cumbrian border to the point east of Boroughbridge where it changes name to the River Ouse. It is the longest of the dales rivers that form the Ouse system.

Course

The Ure flows east through Hawes and Aysgarth, where it drops over the stepped Aysgarth Falls, then past Middleham, Masham and Ripon. In its lower reaches it is joined by the River Swale near Myton-on-Swale. Formerly the river was also known as the Yore, preserved in names such as Yorebridge.

Features

The valley is famous for Wensleydale cheese, still made at Hawes, and for Middleham Castle and Jervaulx Abbey. The river is fished for salmon, trout and grayling, and parts of its catchment are designated for their limestone pavements and upland habitats within the Yorkshire Dales National Park.
