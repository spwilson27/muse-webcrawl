A battalion is a military unit, typically consisting of 300 to 1,000 soldiers commanded by a lieutenant colonel and subdivided into a number of companies. In some countries, battalions are exclusively infantry, while in others battalions are unit-level organisations. The battalion is often the smallest military unit capable of independent operations and is the principal building block of regiments and brigades.

The word derives from the Italian battaglione ("large battle unit"), and the battalion as a tactical formation emerged in the 16th and 17th centuries as armies organised musketeers and pikemen into manoeuvrable blocks of a few hundred men. In modern armies, a battalion typically comprises a headquarters company and three to five combat companies, with attached support elements such as mortars, anti-tank weapons and medical personnel. Several battalions form a regiment or brigade; battalions group into divisions and corps above that.

Battalions exist across arms of service: in addition to infantry there are tank (armoured), artillery, engineer, signals, logistics and special forces battalions, each structured around their principal equipment and role. Naval and air forces use the term rarely, though some air forces organise squadrons of equivalent size.
