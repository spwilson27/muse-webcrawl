Nenets languages

The Nenets languages are a pair of closely related Samoyedic languages of the Uralic family, spoken by the Nenets people in northern Siberia: Tundra Nenets, with roughly 30,000 speakers, and the much smaller Forest Nenets, with perhaps 1,000–1,500 speakers. The two are generally not mutually intelligible and are treated as separate languages.

Features

Nenets is agglutinative, with a large inventory of consonants and vowels, three numbers (singular, dual, plural), and verbs that mark three different object-agreement patterns depending on definiteness and prominence. Tundra Nenets has a written standard in the Cyrillic script and is used in local media and schooling, though both varieties are considered endangered under pressure from Russian.

Status

Tundra Nenets is the largest Samoyedic language and the best documented, with dictionaries and grammars dating from the 19th-century work of M. A. Castrén onward. Transmission to children remains strong in nomadic reindeer-herding communities but is weakening in settlements.
