Brasília is the federal capital of Brazil and the seat of government of the Federal District. Built in the Brazilian highlands in just over three years under President Juscelino Kubitschek, it was inaugurated in 1960 to replace Rio de Janeiro as capital and to spur development of the country's interior.

Design

Urban planner Lúcio Costa's Pilot Plan arranged the city in the shape of an aeroplane: a Monumental Axis lined with ministries and the National Congress, crossed by a residential axis of superblocks (superquadras) with standardized apartment buildings. Architect Oscar Niemeyer's striking modernist palaces — the Cathedral, the Palácio da Alvorada, and the Itamaraty Palace — made the city a landmark of modern architecture; UNESCO listed it as a World Heritage Site in 1987.

The city today

Brasília is Brazil's fourth-largest city and a major administrative and services centre. Growth has concentrated in satellite cities outside the planned core, and the capital is often debated as both a triumph of modernist planning and a city ill-suited to pedestrians and everyday street life.
