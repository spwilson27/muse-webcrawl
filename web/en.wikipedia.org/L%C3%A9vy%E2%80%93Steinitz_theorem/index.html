Lévy–Steinitz theorem

The Lévy–Steinitz theorem in mathematics, named after Paul Lévy and Ernst Steinitz, concerns the set of sums obtainable by rearranging the terms of a conditionally convergent series in a finite-dimensional real vector space. It states that this set of rearranged limits is either empty or an affine subspace — a translate of a linear subspace.

Background

For real-valued series, Riemann's rearrangement theorem says the possible sums form an interval (possibly a single point, if the series converges absolutely). Lévy proved the multidimensional analogue in 1905, and Steinitz gave a celebrated proof in 1913, using a lemma bounding partial sums that became a cornerstone of functional analysis and discrepancy theory. In infinite-dimensional Banach spaces the conclusion can fail or take weaker forms.
