The Denmark Strait (Danish: Danmarksstrædet; Icelandic: Grænlandssund) is an oceanic strait between Greenland to its northwest and Iceland to its southeast, connecting the Greenland Sea (an extension of the Arctic Ocean) with the Irminger Sea, part of the Atlantic Ocean. The island of Jan Mayen lies northeast of the strait.

Oceanography

The cold East Greenland Current flows south through the strait carrying icebergs and Arctic water, while a branch of the warmer Irminger Current flows north along the Icelandic side. Beneath the surface, the strait contains the Denmark Strait overflow, where dense water cascades over an undersea sill in what is effectively the world's largest waterfall by volume, eventually feeding the North Atlantic Deep Water.

History

In May 1941, during the Second World War, the strait was the site of the Battle of the Denmark Strait, in which the German battleship Bismarck sank the British battlecruiser HMS Hood. During the Cold War the strait formed part of the GIUK gap monitored for Soviet submarine traffic.
