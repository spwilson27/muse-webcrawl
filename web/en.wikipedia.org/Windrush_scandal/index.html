The Windrush scandal was a British political scandal that erupted in 2018, in which long-term residents of the United Kingdom — mostly members of the Windrush generation who had arrived from the Caribbean as children of British subjects — were wrongly detained, denied legal rights, threatened with deportation, and in at least 83 cases wrongly deported, under the Home Office's "hostile environment" policy.

Background and exposure

Many in the generation had never applied for passports and held no documents beyond their arrival decades earlier; crucially, the Home Office had destroyed their landing cards in 2010. Reporting by The Guardian, particularly journalist Amelia Gentleman from late 2017, exposed individual cases of people losing jobs, homes, benefits, and NHS treatment. In April 2018 the scandal dominated a Commonwealth heads of government meeting in London; Prime Minister Theresa May apologised, and Home Secretary Amber Rudd resigned on 29 April 2018 after misleading Parliament over removal targets.

Aftermath

The government established a Windrush Compensation Scheme in 2019, though it was criticised for slowness and low payouts. The independent Windrush Lessons Learned Review by Wendy Williams (2020) found the Home Office had shown "institutional ignorance and thoughtlessness" toward race and recommended wide reforms; claimants and campaigners continued to press for faster, fuller redress, and at least several people are known to have died before receiving compensation.
