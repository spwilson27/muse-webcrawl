The Swingle Singers

The Swingle Singers
Origin: Paris, France
Genres: A cappella, jazz, classical crossover
Years active: 1962–present
Founder: Ward Swingle

The Swingle Singers are a vocal group formed in Paris in 1962 by American musician Ward Swingle. The group is renowned for performing classical music — especially the works of Johann Sebastian Bach — in a jazz-inflected a cappella style, with singers vocalizing instrumental lines using scat syllables, originally accompanied by double bass and drums.

History

Swingle, who had studied piano and worked as an accompanist in Paris, assembled the group from session singers, among them Christiane Legrand, sister of composer Michel Legrand. Their 1963 album Jazz Sébastien Bach was an unexpected international hit and won Grammy Awards, as did subsequent recordings of Bach, Mozart, and other classical composers.

Later years

In 1973 Swingle disbanded the French group and formed a new ensemble in England, known variously as Swingle II and The New Swingle Singers, expanding the repertoire to include contemporary, folk, and pop arrangements. The group, now based in London and performing largely a cappella, continues to tour and record with a rotating cast of eight singers. Their recordings have been widely used in film and television soundtracks.
