Cepheus, King of Aethiopia

In Greek mythology, Cepheus (Ancient Greek: Κηφεύς, Kepheús) was the king of Aethiopia, husband of Cassiopeia, and father of Andromeda. He is best known for his role in the myth of Perseus, in which his daughter was offered as a sacrifice to a sea monster.

Myth

When Cassiopeia boasted that she (or her daughter) was more beautiful than the Nereids, Poseidon sent floods and the sea monster Cetus against Aethiopia. An oracle declared that the land would be spared only if Andromeda were chained to a rock for the monster. Cepheus reluctantly agreed; Perseus arrived, killed Cetus, and freed Andromeda, claiming her in marriage. At the wedding feast, Cepheus's nephew and Andromeda's former suitor Phineus attacked, and Perseus turned the attackers to stone with the head of Medusa.

Legacy

Cepheus was placed among the stars as the constellation Cepheus, near Cassiopeia, Andromeda, and Perseus. Different mythographers give varying accounts of his parentage and kingdom; some traditions also make a Cepheus a king of Tegea in Arcadia and an Argonaut, though these are often treated as separate figures.
