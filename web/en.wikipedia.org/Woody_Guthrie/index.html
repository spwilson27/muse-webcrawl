Woodrow Wilson Guthrie (14 July 1912 – 3 October 1967) was an American singer-songwriter and one of the most significant figures in American folk music. His work focused on themes of American socialism and anti-fascism and inspired generations of musicians.

Early life and Dust Bowl years

Guthrie was born in Okemah, Oklahoma. After the Dust Bowl storms devastated the Great Plains, he joined the migration of "Okies" to California, broadcasting on Los Angeles radio and writing songs about the migrants' plight, later collected as the Dust Bowl Ballads (1940).

New York and legacy

In New York he wrote "This Land Is Your Land" (1940), recorded for Moses Asch's Folkways label, and travelled with the Almanac Singers alongside Pete Seeger. His guitar famously bore the slogan "This machine kills fascists." Guthrie's later years were overshadowed by Huntington's disease, which ended his career and life. He profoundly influenced Bob Dylan and the folk revival, and his son Arlo Guthrie became a noted musician.
