Image sensor format

Relative sizes of common image sensor formats.

In digital photography, the image sensor format is the shape and size of the image sensor. The format of a sensor largely determines the field of view captured by a given lens, the depth of field obtainable, and the noise performance of the camera, since larger photosites generally collect more light. Sensor sizes are commonly described using an archaic "inch" system inherited from vidicon camera tubes, in which a "1-inch" sensor has an actual diagonal of about 16 mm.

Common formats

Formats in widespread use include the small sensors of smartphones (roughly 1/2.3″ to 1″ type), the 1-inch type sensor found in premium compact cameras, Micro Four Thirds (17.3 × 13 mm), APS-C (about 23.6 × 15.7 mm), and 35 mm full frame (36 × 24 mm). Medium format digital sensors, such as the 44 × 33 mm size used in several mirrorless systems, are larger still. The ratio between a lens's focal length and its full-frame equivalent field of view is expressed as the crop factor.

Consequences of format

Smaller sensors allow smaller, lighter cameras and lenses and give greater depth of field at the same aperture and framing, while larger sensors provide shallower depth of field and typically better low-light performance and dynamic range. These trade-offs shape the design of everything from camera phones to cinema cameras.
