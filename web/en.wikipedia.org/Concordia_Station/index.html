Concordia Research Station, which opened in 2005, is a French-Italian research facility in Antarctica, located at Dome C on the Antarctic Plateau, 1,100 km inland from the French station at Dumont d'Urville. It is one of only a handful of inland Antarctic stations operating year-round.

Environment

Concordia sits at an altitude of 3,233 metres on about 3 km of ice. Temperatures average around −50 °C in winter and rarely rise above −25 °C in summer; a record low near −84.7 °C has been reported. The air is extremely dry, precipitation is minimal, and the station experiences about three months of continuous night each winter.

Research

The site hosted the EPICA ice-core project, which drilled to bedrock and recovered ice about 800,000 years old, providing a record of past climate. Other work includes astronomy (the site offers exceptionally stable, clear skies), glaciology, atmospheric science, seismology and human physiology. The European Space Agency studies Concordia crews as an analogue for long-duration spaceflight, because winter-over crews of about 13 people are isolated for nine months with no possibility of evacuation.

Facilities and logistics

The station consists principally of two large cylindrical buildings joined by a walkway, mounted on adjustable platforms to compensate for snow accumulation. Summer population can reach about 80. Supplies arrive by traverse convoy from Dumont d'Urville or by ski-equipped aircraft via a prepared skiway.
