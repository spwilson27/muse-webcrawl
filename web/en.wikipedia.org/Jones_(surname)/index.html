Jones (surname)

Jones is especially common in Wales.

Jones is a surname of Welsh origin meaning "son of Ioan" — Ioan being the Welsh form of John. It is one of the most common surnames in the English-speaking world: the most common surname in Wales, and among the most frequent in England, the United States, Canada, Australia and New Zealand.

Origin and distribution

The name arose under the Welsh patronymic system, in which a son was identified by his father's given name; when fixed hereditary surnames were adopted in Wales under English administration, "ap Ioan" was anglicised as Jones. Because John was an extremely popular given name, the surname emerged independently in many families. In modern Wales roughly one in twenty people bears the name. Welsh emigration carried it widely through the coalfields and industrial towns of Britain and to the Americas.

Notable bearers

Notable people named Jones include the architect Inigo Jones, the mathematician William Jones (who proposed the symbol π), the folk hero and railway engineer Casey Jones, the actor James Earl Jones, musicians Tom Jones, Quincy Jones and Grace Jones, and the fictional archaeologist Indiana Jones. The name also appears in idioms such as "keeping up with the Joneses" and in the Dow Jones Industrial Average, named for journalist Charles Dow and statistician Edward Jones.
