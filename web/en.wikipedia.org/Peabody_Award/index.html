The George Foster Peabody Awards (or simply Peabody Awards) are annual American awards honoring excellence in storytelling across television, radio, and online media. First awarded in 1941 for radio programs of the previous year, they are presented by the Henry W. Grady College of Journalism and Mass Communication at the University of Georgia and are among the oldest and most prestigious honors in electronic media.

History

The awards were conceived in 1938 by Lambdin Kay of Atlanta radio station WSB, who sought a radio equivalent of the Pulitzer Prize. They are named for banker and philanthropist George Foster Peabody, who provided the funds to endow them. Television was added in 1948, and internet and streaming content became eligible in later decades as the program expanded beyond broadcasting.

Selection

Unlike most awards, Peabodys have no set categories or fixed number of winners; a board of jurors selects roughly thirty winners each year by unanimous vote from more than a thousand entries, recognizing "stories that matter." Honorees have included Edward R. Murrow, The Daily Show, Sesame Street, and landmark news documentaries. Institutional and career achievement awards are also given periodically.
