DREAM Act

The DREAM Act (Development, Relief, and Education for Alien Minors Act) is a United States legislative proposal that would grant temporary conditional residency, with a path to permanent residency, to certain immigrants who arrived in the United States as children.

History

First introduced in 2001 by Senators Dick Durbin and Orrin Hatch, versions of the bill have been reintroduced repeatedly but have never passed both houses of Congress; a 2010 version passed the House but failed in the Senate.

Provisions

Eligible individuals — often called Dreamers — would generally need to have arrived before a set age, lived continuously in the US, graduated from high school, and completed college, military service, or employment during a conditional period.

Related policy

In 2012 the Obama administration created DACA, a deferred-action program providing similar protections by executive action, though not a path to citizenship.
