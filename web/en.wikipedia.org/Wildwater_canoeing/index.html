Wildwater canoeing

[Image: Wildwater canoeing]

Wildwater canoeing is a competitive discipline of canoeing in which athletes race specialised canoes or kayaks down a whitewater river course as fast as possible. Races are typically held over courses of several kilometres, with classic races lasting 10 to 30 minutes and sprint races about one minute.

Boats and classes

Wildwater boats are long, narrow and unstable compared with recreational craft, with rounded hulls designed for speed in rough water. Competition classes include men's and women's kayak singles (K1), canoe singles (C1), and canoe doubles (C2). Athletes use wing paddles (in kayaks) or single-bladed paddles (in canoes, kneeling).

Competition

The discipline is governed by the International Canoe Federation (ICF), which organises World Championships and a World Cup series. Wildwater racing was the original form of competitive whitewater paddling; a short, obstacle-based variant developed into canoe slalom, which became an Olympic sport, while wildwater itself is not on the Olympic programme.
