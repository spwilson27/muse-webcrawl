Primary Chronicle

A page from a manuscript copy of the Primary Chronicle

The Primary Chronicle, also known as the Tale of Bygone Years, is a history of Kievan Rus' from about 850 to 1110, written in Old East Slavic in Kiev in the 1110s. The original compilation is believed to have been made by the monk Nestor of the Kiev Caves Monastery around 1113, though modern scholarship recognizes multiple authors and redactions. It is the fundamental source for the early history of the East Slavs.

Content

The chronicle opens with the biblical Table of Nations and the legendary settlement of the Slavs along the Danube and Dnieper. It recounts the founding of Kiev by Kyi and his brothers, the "calling of the Varangians" in 862—when the Slavic and Finnic tribes invited the Rus' prince Rurik to rule over them—and the reigns of his successors Oleg, Igor, Olga, and Sviatoslav. Its most famous narrative is the Christianization of Rus' under Vladimir the Great in 988, including the story of his envoys examining Judaism, Islam, and Latin and Byzantine Christianity before choosing the Orthodox faith. The text blends annals, treaties with Byzantium, saints' lives, speeches, and folk legend.

Manuscripts

The original text is lost. The two principal surviving witnesses are the Laurentian Codex, copied in 1377 by the monk Laurentius for Prince Dmitry of Suzdal, and the Hypatian Codex, copied around 1425. Both transmit the chronicle in different redactions, and their comparison, pioneered by the philologist Aleksey Shakhmatov, underlies modern reconstructions of the lost earlier compilations that preceded Nestor's work.

Significance

For centuries the chronicle was accepted as literal history, and its accounts—especially the Varangian invitation and the baptism of Rus'—became foundational narratives for Russian, Ukrainian, and Belarusian national identities. Modern historians treat it critically, distinguishing contemporary records from legend and later interpolation, but it remains the single most important written source for the first centuries of East Slavic history, language, and culture.
