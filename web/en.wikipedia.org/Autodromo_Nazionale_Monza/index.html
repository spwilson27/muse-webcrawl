Autodromo Nazionale Monza

Location: Monza, Lombardy, Italy
Opened: 1922
Length: 5.793 km (Grand Prix circuit)
Major events: Italian Grand Prix

The Autodromo Nazionale Monza is a motor racing circuit near the city of Monza, north of Milan, in Italy. Built in 1922, it is one of the world's oldest purpose-built racing circuits and is best known as the home of the Formula One Italian Grand Prix, which it has hosted almost every year since 1950.

History

The original circuit combined a road course with a steeply banked high-speed oval. The banked sections, scene of many fatal accidents in the early decades, were progressively taken out of use; the oval last held Formula One cars in 1961. The road course was repeatedly modified with chicanes, including the Variante del Rettifilo and the Variante della Roggia, to reduce speeds.

Characteristics

Monza is famous for its long straights and fast corners such as Curva Grande, the Lesmo curves, and the Parabolica (now Curva Alboreto), producing some of the highest average speeds in Formula One. Its passionate Italian fans, the tifosi, create a distinctive atmosphere, especially when Ferrari wins.

Notable events

The circuit hosted the fastest race in Formula One history (the 2003 Italian Grand Prix by average speed) and the closest finish, in 1971. Beyond Formula One, Monza hosts endurance racing, touring cars, and the Monza Rally Show.
