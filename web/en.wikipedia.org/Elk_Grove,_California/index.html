Elk Grove, California

Elk Grove is a city in Sacramento County, California, United States, located just south of the state capital of Sacramento. It is part of the Sacramento metropolitan area. The population was 176,124 at the 2020 census, making Elk Grove the second-largest city in Sacramento County.

History

Elk Grove was founded in 1850 as a stage stop and hotel on the Monterey Trail by James Hall, and named for the elk herds once common in the region. It remained a small agricultural community for more than a century, centered on the historic Old Town district and served by the railroad.

Beginning in the 1980s and accelerating in the 1990s, Elk Grove experienced rapid suburban growth as housing developments spread south from Sacramento. In 2000, residents voted to incorporate as a city. During the mid-2000s it was at times the fastest-growing city in the United States, before the housing downturn slowed growth.

Geography and economy

The city covers about 42 square miles in the Sacramento Valley. Major employers include the Elk Grove Unified School District — one of the largest school districts in California — along with healthcare, retail, and logistics operations along the Highway 99 corridor. Elk Grove is also home to Cosumnes River Preserve, a major protected wetland along the Pacific Flyway.
