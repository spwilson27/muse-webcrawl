San Cristóbal de las Casas is a city and municipality in the central highlands of the state of Chiapas in southeastern Mexico. Founded in 1528 by Diego de Mazariegos, it was the colonial capital of Chiapas and is named in part for Bartolomé de las Casas, the first bishop of Chiapas and defender of indigenous rights.

History

The city grew as a Spanish colonial administrative centre in a region inhabited mainly by Tzotzil and Tzeltal Maya peoples. In 1994 it briefly came to world attention when the Zapatista Army of National Liberation (EZLN) occupied it at the start of its uprising.

Geography and climate

Set in a valley at about 2,200 metres elevation, surrounded by pine forests, the city has a mild subtropical highland climate with cool nights.

Culture

Known for its well-preserved colonial architecture, churches such as Santo Domingo, colourful markets and indigenous textiles, San Cristóbal is a designated Pueblo Mágico and a popular destination for travellers and language students.
