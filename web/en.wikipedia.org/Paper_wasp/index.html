FamilyVespidae
SubfamilyPolistinae (and others)
GeneraPolistes, Mischocyttarus, Ropalidia, etc.
NestOpen comb of grey papery material

Paper wasps are a group of vespid wasps that gather plant fibres, chew them and mix them with saliva to build water-resistant nests of grey or brown papery material. The name is most often applied to the subfamily Polistinae, whose colonies are founded by a single queen or small group of foundresses and whose nests typically consist of a single open comb suspended from a stalk, without an enclosing envelope.

Behaviour

Colonies are generally small, with a few dozen workers. Adults feed on nectar and other sugary fluids and hunt caterpillars and other insects to feed their larvae. Paper wasps are far less aggressive than yellowjackets and usually sting only when their nest is disturbed, though their sting is painful and they can sting repeatedly.

Social structure

In many Polistes species, several foundresses cooperate to start a nest and then form a dominance hierarchy; the dominant female becomes the principal egg-layer while subordinates forage and may inherit the nest if the queen dies. Paper wasps can recognise individual faces of nestmates, a rare ability among insects.
