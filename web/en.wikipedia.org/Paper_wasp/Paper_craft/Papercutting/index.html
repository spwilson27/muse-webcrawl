Paper craft

Paper craft is the collection of art forms employing paper or card as the primary artistic medium for the creation of two- or three-dimensional objects. Paper has been used for art since its invention in China in the 2nd century CE (traditionally credited to Cai Lun, 105 CE). Paper crafts are practised worldwide, both as folk traditions and as contemporary art and hobby activities.

Types of paper craft

Major forms include origami (paper folding), kirigami and paper cutting (such as Chinese jianzhi, Japanese monkiri, and Mexican papel picado), quilling (paper filigree), papier-mâché, paper modelling (scale models from printed sheets), decoupage, cardmaking, scrapbooking, bookbinding, and paper marbling. Papercutting is among the oldest forms: the oldest surviving paper cut-out is a symmetrical circle from the 6th century, found in Xinjiang, China.

Materials and techniques

Tools range from simple scissors, craft knives, and glue to die-cutting machines and laser cutters. Archival-quality papers are used where longevity matters; recycled and handmade papers are popular for texture. Paper craft is widely used in education, festivals and celebration decoration (banners, lanterns, piñatas), and increasingly in commercial design, packaging prototypes, and stop-motion animation.
