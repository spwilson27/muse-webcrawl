Giuseppe Verdi

Born9 or 10 October 1813, Le Roncole, Italy
Died27 January 1901, Milan, Italy
OccupationComposer
Notable worksRigoletto, La traviata, Aida, Otello, Falstaff

Giuseppe Fortunino Francesco Verdi (9 or 10 October 1813 – 27 January 1901) was an Italian composer best known for his operas. He was born near Busseto to a provincial, religiously observant family and developed a musical education with the help of a local patron. Despite an early setback — rejection by the Milan Conservatory — he became the dominant figure in Italian opera after the era of Donizetti, and his works remain central to the standard repertoire.

Early career

After the failure of his second opera and the deaths of his wife and children, Verdi achieved a breakthrough with Nabucco (1842), whose chorus "Va, pensiero" became an emblem of Italian national aspiration. A string of operas followed in what he called his "galley years".

Middle period

The trio of Rigoletto (1851), Il trovatore (1853) and La traviata (1853) established his international fame. Later works of this period include Un ballo in maschera, La forza del destino, Don Carlos and Aida (1871), commissioned for Cairo.

Later years

After the Requiem (1874), written for the writer Alessandro Manzoni, Verdi retired to his estate at Sant'Agata. Persuaded by the librettist Arrigo Boito, he returned to opera with two Shakespearean masterpieces, Otello (1887) and Falstaff (1893). He died in Milan in 1901; hundreds of thousands lined the streets for the procession of his remains.

Legacy

Verdi's name became a symbol of the Risorgimento — the slogan "Viva VERDI" was read as an acronym for Vittorio Emanuele Re D'Italia — and his operas are among the most frequently performed worldwide.
