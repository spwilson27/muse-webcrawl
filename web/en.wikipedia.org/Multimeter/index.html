Multimeter


Type: Electronic measuring instrument
Measures: Voltage, current, resistance, and more
Also called: VOM (volt-ohm-milliammeter)

A multimeter is a measuring instrument that can measure multiple electrical properties, typically voltage, electrical current, and resistance. Multimeters are indispensable diagnostic tools for electricians, electronics technicians, and engineers.

Types

Analog multimeters use a moving-pointer galvanometer and a mirrored scale to reduce parallax error. Digital multimeters (DMMs) display readings numerically and are now dominant; they commonly add capacitance, frequency, temperature, diode and continuity tests. Bench multimeters offer higher precision, while clamp meters measure current without breaking the circuit.

History

The first widely produced multimeter is generally credited to Donald Macadie of the British Post Office, whose "Avometer" (measuring amps, volts, and ohms) appeared in the 1920s and was manufactured by the Automatic Coil Winder and Electrical Equipment Company. Pocket watch-style meters preceded it in the United States.

Use and safety

To measure voltage, the probes connect in parallel; for current, in series; resistance is measured on unpowered components. Instrument safety categories (CAT I–IV) rate suitability for different parts of an electrical installation, and using an under-rated meter on mains circuits can be dangerous.
