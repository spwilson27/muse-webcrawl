Hearsay

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hearsay, in a legal forum, is an out-of-court statement that is offered by a witness to prove the truth of the matter asserted. In most courts adhering to common-law rules of evidence, hearsay is generally inadmissible — the hearsay rule — unless an exception or exemption applies. The rationale is that the original speaker was not under oath, and the opposing party cannot cross-examine them about perception, memory, narration, and sincerity.

Definition

Under the US Federal Rules of Evidence, hearsay (Rule 802) is a statement — an oral or written assertion, or conduct intended as an assertion — made by a declarant other than while testifying at the current trial or hearing, offered to prove the truth of the matter asserted. A statement offered for another purpose (for example, to show its effect on the listener, or as legally operative words) is not hearsay. Statements by an opposing party, and certain prior statements by testifying witnesses, are defined as non-hearsay ("exemptions").

Exceptions

Numerous exceptions recognise circumstances carrying guarantees of trustworthiness: present sense impressions and excited utterances; statements of then-existing mental, emotional, or physical condition; statements for medical diagnosis or treatment; recorded recollections; business, public, and vital records; ancient documents; and dying declarations. A residual exception allows other statements with equivalent circumstantial guarantees of trustworthiness. In US criminal cases, testimonial hearsay is further limited by the Confrontation Clause of the Sixth Amendment, as interpreted in Crawford v. Washington (2004).

Other jurisdictions

England and Wales, the rule's birthplace, has substantially relaxed it: civil cases admit hearsay under the Civil Evidence Act 1995, and criminal cases under the Criminal Justice Act 2003 admit it through defined gateways, with weight left to the fact-finder. Civil-law systems generally have no strict hearsay rule, evaluating evidence freely.
