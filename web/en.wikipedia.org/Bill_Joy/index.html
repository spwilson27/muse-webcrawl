Bill Joy

William Nelson Joy (born November 8, 1954), commonly known as Bill Joy, is an American computer engineer and venture capitalist. He co-founded Sun Microsystems in 1982 along with Scott McNealy, Vinod Khosla, and Andy Bechtolsheim, and served as chief scientist and CTO at the company until 2003.

Early life and education

Joy was born in Farmington Hills, Michigan. He received a Bachelor of Science in electrical engineering from the University of Michigan in 1975 and a Master of Science in electrical engineering and computer science from the University of California, Berkeley in 1979.

Career at Berkeley

While at Berkeley, Joy worked on Berkeley Unix (BSD), developing the vi text editor, the C shell (csh), and TCP/IP networking code. His work on BSD was foundational; the TCP/IP stack he refined was adopted widely and influenced later operating systems. He also authored the termcap capability database.

Sun Microsystems

At Sun, Joy was a key architect of NFS, SPARC, Java-related strategy, Solaris and network computing ideas. He was a proponent of open systems and networked computing, encapsulated in Sun's slogan "The Network Is the Computer."

Later work and views

Joy gained wider public attention for his 2000 Wired essay "Why the Future Doesn't Need Us," warning about risks from genetics, nanotechnology and robotics. He later became a partner at Kleiner Perkins, focusing on green technology investments, and co-founded HighBAR.
