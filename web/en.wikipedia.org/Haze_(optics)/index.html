Haze (optics)

In optics, haze is a measure of the scattering of light by a transparent or translucent material. It is defined as the percentage of transmitted light that is scattered so that its direction deviates more than a specified angle (commonly 2.5 degrees) from the direction of the incident beam. A material with high haze appears milky or cloudy while still transmitting much of the incident light.

Measurement

Haze is measured with a hazemeter or spectrophotometer following standards such as ASTM D1003 and ISO 13468/14782. The instrument compares total light transmittance with the diffuse portion scattered beyond the acceptance angle, using an integrating sphere. Total luminous transmittance and haze are reported separately because a film can transmit most light yet still look frosted.

Applications

Haze values matter for packaging films, glazing, display screens and lighting diffusers: low haze is wanted for clear windows and lenses, while controlled high haze is desirable in privacy glass, greenhouse films and light-diffusing covers. Haze should not be confused with turbidity (used for liquids) or with atmospheric haze, the reduction of visibility caused by aerosols in air.
