James McGill (October 6, 1744 – December 19, 1813) was a Scottish-Canadian businessman and philanthropist in Montreal. A leading figure in the North American fur trade, he accumulated a substantial fortune in trade and land, served in the Legislative Assembly of Lower Canada, and left the endowment and estate in his will that founded the Royal Institution for the Advancement of Learning — the origin of McGill University, which bears his name.

Early life and the fur trade

McGill was born in Glasgow and educated at the University of Glasgow before emigrating to North America in the 1760s. Based in Montreal after the British conquest of New France, he became a partner in fur-trading ventures reaching into the Great Lakes and the northwest interior. He was among the merchants who organised the early partnerships that developed into the North West Company, Montreal's great rival to the Hudson's Bay Company. Like many merchants of his time and place, he was also a slave owner, holding enslaved Indigenous and Black people in his household.

Business and public life

McGill diversified into land speculation, shipping and finance; he was involved in founding the Bank of Montreal and in promoting the construction of the Lachine Canal. In politics he represented Montreal in the Legislative Assembly of Lower Canada from 1792, and served as an honorary lieutenant-colonel of militia during the War of 1812. His country estate, Burnside, stood on the slopes of Mount Royal above the city.

Legacy

In his will McGill left his Burnside estate and £10,000 to the Royal Institution for the Advancement of Learning, on condition that a college bearing his name be established on the property within ten years. Legal disputes with his stepchildren's family delayed matters, but McGill College received its royal charter in 1821 and began teaching in 1829; it grew into McGill University, one of Canada's leading research universities. McGill's remains were reinterred on the university campus in front of the Roddick Gates on Sherbrooke Street, where a statue of him stands.
