Claremont Colleges

The seven Claremont Colleges share a contiguous campus in Claremont, California.

The Claremont Colleges (officially the Claremont University Consortium) are a consortium of seven private institutions of higher education located in Claremont, California, about 30 miles east of Los Angeles. The consortium comprises five undergraduate colleges — Pomona College, Scripps College, Claremont McKenna College, Harvey Mudd College and Pitzer College — and two graduate institutions, Claremont Graduate University and Keck Graduate Institute. Together they enrol around 8,500 students on adjacent campuses covering about 540 acres.

History and model

The consortium was founded in 1925 under the leadership of Pomona College president James A. Blaisdell, who proposed a "group plan" inspired by the collegiate universities of Oxford and Cambridge: a cluster of small, autonomous colleges sharing central facilities, allowing each to retain the intimacy of a small liberal arts college while pooling resources like those of a large university. Claremont Graduate University and Scripps College were the first new members in 1925–26; the others joined through the century, most recently Keck Graduate Institute in 1997.

Cooperation

Students at the undergraduate colleges may cross-register for courses at the other colleges, use shared libraries (centred on the Honnold/Mudd Library), dining halls, and facilities, and participate in joint athletic teams: Pomona and Pitzer compete together as the Pomona-Pitzer Sagehens, while Claremont McKenna, Harvey Mudd and Scripps compete as the Claremont-Mudd-Scripps Stags and Athenas. The consortium operates shared services through Claremont University Consortium, founded as the coordinating body in 1929.

Member institutions

Pomona College (founded 1887), the founding member, is a liberal arts college; Scripps College (1926) is a women's college; Claremont McKenna College (1946) emphasises economics, government and public affairs; Harvey Mudd College (1955) focuses on science, engineering and mathematics; Pitzer College (1963) emphasises social sciences and social justice; Claremont Graduate University (1925) offers graduate degrees only; and Keck Graduate Institute (1997) focuses on the applied life sciences.
