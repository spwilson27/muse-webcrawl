Sustainable energy

Sustainable energy is energy production and use that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs. Sustainable energy systems combine renewable energy sources, energy efficiency, and technologies that minimise environmental harm, air pollution, and greenhouse gas emissions.

Definition

The concept derives from the Brundtland Commission's 1987 definition of sustainable development. Energy is considered sustainable when it can be supplied indefinitely, causes little damage to ecosystems, and is affordable and accessible. Unlike fossil fuels, renewable flows such as sunlight and wind are replenished on human timescales.

Renewable sources

Major sustainable sources include solar energy, wind power, hydropower, geothermal energy and sustainably managed biomass. In the 2020s solar photovoltaics and wind became the cheapest sources of new electricity in most markets, driving rapid growth in installed capacity.

Energy efficiency and conservation

Using less energy to deliver the same service — through insulation, efficient appliances, LED lighting and better industrial processes — is often described as the "first fuel", because avoided demand is usually the cheapest and cleanest option.

Energy transition

The global energy transition involves electrifying transport, heating and industry, expanding grids and storage, and phasing down coal, oil and gas. International agreements such as the Paris Agreement frame national targets, while debates continue over the role of nuclear power and carbon capture in a sustainable mix.
