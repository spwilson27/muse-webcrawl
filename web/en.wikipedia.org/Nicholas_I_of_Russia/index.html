Nicholas I of Russia

Reign: 1825–1855
Born: 6 July 1796, Gatchina
Died: 2 March 1855, St Petersburg
House: Romanov

Nicholas I (Russian: Николай I) was Emperor of Russia, King of Congress Poland, and Grand Duke of Finland from 1825 until his death in 1855. Remembered as one of the most reactionary of Russian monarchs, he championed the doctrine of "Orthodoxy, Autocracy, and Nationality" and acted as the "gendarme of Europe," suppressing revolutions abroad.

Reign

His accession was challenged by the Decembrist revolt of liberal officers in December 1825, which he crushed and which shaped his lifelong hostility to reform. He expanded censorship and the secret police (the Third Section), codified Russian law under Speransky, and crushed the Polish November Uprising of 1830–31. In 1849 he intervened to help Austria suppress the Hungarian revolution.

Crimean War and death

Russia's expansion toward the Ottoman Empire led to the Crimean War (1853–1856) against Britain, France, and the Ottomans. Nicholas died in March 1855, during the siege of Sevastopol, leaving his son Alexander II a war that exposed Russia's backwardness and spurred the Great Reforms.
