C*-algebra

In mathematics, a C*-algebra (read "C-star algebra") is a Banach algebra of operators on a Hilbert space, closed under the operation of taking adjoints and satisfying the C*-identity ‖a*a‖ = ‖a‖². Abstractly, it is a Banach *-algebra satisfying that identity.

Theory

The Gelfand–Naimark theorem (1943) shows every abstract C*-algebra can be represented as an algebra of operators, and every commutative C*-algebra is isomorphic to the algebra of continuous functions vanishing at infinity on some locally compact Hausdorff space — linking the theory to topology.

Applications

C*-algebras were developed in the study of quantum mechanics, where observables form such an algebra, and they remain central to operator algebras, noncommutative geometry and mathematical physics. Von Neumann algebras are a distinguished subclass.
