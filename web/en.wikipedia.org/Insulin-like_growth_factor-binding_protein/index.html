Insulin-like growth factor-binding protein

The insulin-like growth factor-binding proteins (IGFBPs) are a family of six proteins (IGFBP-1 to IGFBP-6) that bind the insulin-like growth factors IGF-1 and IGF-2 with high affinity and regulate their transport, half-life and access to receptors. Unlike most hormone carriers, the IGFBPs do not merely buffer their ligands: they can inhibit or potentiate IGF action, prolong IGF half-life from minutes to hours, deliver IGFs to specific tissues, and exert IGF-independent effects of their own, including influences on cell growth, migration and apoptosis. A wider group of lower-affinity "IGFBP-related proteins" (IGFBP-rPs) shares the conserved cysteine-rich N-terminal domain.

Structure

The six IGFBPs share a three-domain organisation: a highly conserved, cysteine-rich N-terminal domain and a conserved C-terminal domain that together form the high-affinity IGF-binding site, joined by a variable linker region that is the main site of post-translational modification — phosphorylation, glycosylation and proteolysis. Regulated cleavage of IGFBPs by specific proteases (such as PAPP-A acting on IGFBP-4 and -5, and various matrix metalloproteinases) lowers their affinity and releases IGF to its receptor; this is a key local control mechanism, for example in the ovarian follicle, bone and the placenta.

Individual members

  • IGFBP-1 — produced mainly by the liver and decidua; acutely regulated by insulin (which suppresses it) and fasting; modulates short-term IGF availability and glucose homeostasis.
  • IGFBP-2 — the predominant IGFBP in cerebrospinal fluid; elevated in several cancers and in ageing; implicated in metabolic regulation.
  • IGFBP-3 — the most abundant in adult serum; carries most circulating IGF in the ternary complex with ALS under growth-hormone control; also has IGF-independent pro-apoptotic, anti-tumour actions involving a putative cell-surface receptor and nuclear signalling.
  • IGFBP-4 — generally inhibitory to IGF action; cleaved by PAPP-A.
  • IGFBP-5 — binds extracellular matrix, which lowers its affinity and concentrates IGF near cells; important in bone and muscle.
  • IGFBP-6 — binds IGF-2 preferentially; expressed in the nervous system and involved in neuronal development.

Clinical significance

Circulating IGFBP-3 (with IGF-1) is measured as a marker of growth-hormone status in the evaluation of short stature and acromegaly. Altered IGFBP expression is reported in diabetes, chronic kidney disease, cancer and critical illness, and the IGFBP axis is an active target in oncology and metabolic research.
