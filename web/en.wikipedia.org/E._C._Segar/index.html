Elzie Crisler Segar (December 8, 1894 – October 13, 1938), usually credited as E. C. Segar, was an American cartoonist best known as the creator of Popeye the Sailor Man, who first appeared in his comic strip Thimble Theatre in 1929 and became one of the most enduring characters of 20th-century popular culture.

Early life and career

Born in Chester, Illinois, where his father worked as a house painter, Segar decided on cartooning after taking a correspondence course and moved through newspaper jobs in Chicago — including drawing Charlie Chaplin's Comic Capers — before joining King Features Syndicate in New York. There he launched Thimble Theatre on 19 December 1919, a humorous strip starring Olive Oyl and her family, and later added the adventure serial Sappo as a topper strip.

Popeye

On 17 January 1929 Segar introduced a rough, squinting sailor named Popeye as a minor walk-on character; readers responded so strongly that he became the strip's star. Popeye's spinach-fuelled strength dramatically boosted real-world spinach sales, and the cast Segar built around him — J. Wellington Wimpy, Bluto, Swee'Pea, and the Sea Hag — populated a distinctive comic world mixing slapstick, adventure, and eccentric dialect. In 1933 the Fleischer Studios began the hugely successful Popeye animated cartoons.

Death and legacy

Segar died of leukemia and liver disease on 13 October 1938, aged 43, at his home in Santa Monica. Thimble Theatre was continued by other hands, notably Bud Sagendorf. Segar's hometown of Chester honors him with a Popeye trail of statues, and he was inducted into the Will Eisner Comic Book Hall of Fame in 2001.
