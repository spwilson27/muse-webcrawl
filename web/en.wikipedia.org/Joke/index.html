Joke

A joke is a display of humour in which words are used within a specific and well-defined narrative structure to make people laugh and is usually not meant to be interpreted literally. It usually takes the form of a story, often with dialogue, and ends in a punch line, whereby the humorous element of the story is revealed; this can be done using a pun or other type of word play, irony or sarcasm, a logical incompatibility, hyperbole, or other means.

Structure

A typical joke consists of a setup, which establishes the situation and expectations, and a punch line that violates those expectations in a surprising yet retrospectively sensible way. Jokes may be one-liners, anecdotes, question-and-answer exchanges ("knock-knock" jokes, lightbulb jokes), or longer shaggy-dog stories that subvert the form itself.

Functions and study

Jokes serve social functions: bonding, easing tension, expressing taboo thoughts, and mocking authority. Theories of humour that attempt to explain joking include superiority theory, relief theory, and incongruity theory. Joke-telling is central to stand-up comedy, and jokes circulate widely as folklore, in joke books, and online. Practical jokes differ in that the humour is enacted on an unsuspecting person rather than narrated.
