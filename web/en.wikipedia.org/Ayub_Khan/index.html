Muhammad Ayub Khan (14 May 1907 – 19 April 1974) was a Pakistani army officer and statesman who served as the second president of Pakistan from 1958 to 1969. The first native commander-in-chief of the Pakistan Army, he seized power in the 1958 coup d'état — the first military takeover in Pakistan's history — and ruled as an authoritarian moderniser for eleven years.

Early life and military career

Born in Rehana village in the Hazara district of the North-West Frontier Province, Ayub Khan was educated at Aligarh Muslim University and the Royal Military College, Sandhurst. Commissioned into the British Indian Army, he served in the Burma campaign of the Second World War. After partition he rose rapidly, becoming commander-in-chief in 1951 and defence minister in 1954 while still in uniform.

Presidency

In October 1958 President Iskander Mirza abrogated the constitution and appointed Ayub chief martial law administrator; within weeks Ayub deposed Mirza himself. His government promulgated the 1962 constitution, introduced the "Basic Democracies" system, pursued land reform, family planning and rapid industrialisation, and presided over the "Decade of Development" with high growth rates. Pakistan aligned with the United States through SEATO and CENTO, and fought the 1965 war with India, after which Ayub's standing declined.

Fall and legacy

Mass protests in 1968–69, sparked by economic inequality and political repression, forced his resignation in March 1969; he handed power to General Yahya Khan. Ayub died in 1974. His legacy remains contested: credited with economic modernisation and stability, he is also criticised for inaugurating military rule in Pakistan and for centralising power.
