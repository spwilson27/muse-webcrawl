Data Matrix

A Data Matrix code; the solid L-shaped border is the finder pattern

A Data Matrix is a two-dimensional barcode consisting of black and white cells arranged in a square or rectangular pattern, bordered on two sides by a solid "L finder pattern" and on the other two sides by an alternating "timing pattern". Invented by International Data Matrix, Inc. in 1994, it can encode up to about 2,335 alphanumeric characters and includes Reed–Solomon error correction (ECC 200) that allows codes to remain readable when up to roughly 30 per cent damaged.

Applications

Because a Data Matrix can store useful data in a very small area, it is widely used for direct part marking in electronics, automotive and aerospace manufacturing, where components are laser-etched with serial numbers. It is also standard in healthcare and pharmaceutical labelling (including GS1 DataMatrix), on postal items, and in the US Department of Defense's Item Unique Identification programme.

Structure

Symbols range from 10×10 to 144×144 modules; larger symbols are divided into data regions separated by alignment patterns. The ECC 200 version, standardised as ISO/IEC 16022, is the only version in current use. Data is encoded in a compact set of modes (ASCII, C40, Text, Base 256) and protected by Reed–Solomon codes interleaved across the symbol.

Comparison with QR codes

Data Matrix codes are typically smaller than QR codes for the same data and are preferred for industrial marking, while QR codes dominate consumer applications such as advertising and payments.
