Varangian Guard

The Varangian Guard (Greek: Τάγμα τῶν Βαράγγων, Tágma tōn Varángōn) was an elite unit of the Byzantine Army from the 10th to the 13th century. It served as the personal bodyguard of the Byzantine emperors and was composed mainly of Norsemen and later also Anglo-Saxons. The Varangians were noted for their loyalty, their long service, and their distinctive battle axes.

Origins

In 988, Emperor Basil II requested military assistance from Vladimir I of Kiev against a revolt; Vladimir sent 6,000 Varangian (Norse) warriors, who formed the nucleus of the Guard. Pleased with their reliability — unlike frequently rebellious Greek troops — Basil made them his personal guard, richly paid and quartered in Constantinople.

Service and reputation

Varangians fought in campaigns across the empire, from Italy and Sicily to Armenia and the Danube frontier. Their loyalty to the person of the emperor, rather than to factions, made them invaluable; they famously defended Constantinople during the Fourth Crusade in 1204. Recruits came first from Scandinavia and Kievan Rus', and after 1066 many Anglo-Saxons displaced by the Norman Conquest joined the ranks.

Legacy

Several guardsmen later became famous at home, most notably Harald Hardrada, who served in the Guard before becoming king of Norway. Runestones in Sweden commemorate men who travelled to "Greece" (Byzantium), and Byzantine sources record the corps until the early 15th century, though its importance faded after 1204.
