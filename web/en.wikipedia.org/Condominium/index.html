Condominium

A condominium (often shortened to condo) is a form of property ownership in which individual units within a larger property are owned separately, while common areas — hallways, grounds, amenities — are owned collectively by the unit owners through an owners' association.

Structure

Condominium ownership combines fee-simple ownership of the interior of a unit with an undivided interest in the common elements. Owners pay periodic assessments (condo or HOA fees) for maintenance, insurance and reserves, and are governed by a declaration, bylaws and rules enforced by an elected board. The legal form developed in the United States in the 1960s — the first modern condominium is often cited as Graystone Manor in Utah (1960) — drawing on earlier concepts such as Puerto Rico's Horizontal Property Act of 1958.

Condominiums may be residential apartment buildings, townhouses, detached houses in planned communities, or commercial and mixed-use properties. Related forms include cooperatives, in which residents own shares in a corporation, and strata title in Australia and British Columbia.
