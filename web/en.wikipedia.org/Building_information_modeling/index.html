Building information modeling

Building information modeling (BIM) is a process involving the generation and management of digital representations of the physical and functional characteristics of buildings and other physical assets. BIM models are files that can be extracted, exchanged or networked to support decision-making throughout a building's life cycle, from design and construction to operation and demolition.

Overview

Unlike simple 3D CAD drawings, a BIM model contains intelligent objects — walls, beams, pipes, equipment — that carry geometric and non-geometric data, know how they relate to one another, and update consistently when the design changes. Additional "dimensions" of the model are commonly recognised: 4D adds time (construction scheduling), 5D adds cost, and further dimensions address sustainability and facility management.

Uses

BIM supports coordinated design across architecture, structural engineering and building services, with automated clash detection between disciplines. It is used for quantity take-off, construction simulation, energy analysis and, after handover, as the basis of a digital twin for asset management. Interoperability between software is handled largely through the open Industry Foundation Classes (IFC) standard.

Adoption and standards

Several governments mandate BIM for public projects: the United Kingdom required collaborative BIM (Level 2) on centrally procured public sector projects from 2016, and ISO 19650 now provides an international framework for information management over the life cycle of a built asset. Common BIM software includes Autodesk Revit, Graphisoft ArchiCAD and Bentley Systems products.
