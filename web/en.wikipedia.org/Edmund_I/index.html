Edmund I (c. 920/921 – 26 May 946), sometimes known as Edmund the Elder or Edmund the Magnificent, was King of the English from 939 until his death. The son of Edward the Elder and half-brother of Æthelstan, Edmund succeeded to a kingdom whose control of the north was immediately challenged by the Viking kings of York.

Reign

On Edmund's accession, Olaf Guthfrithson, the Norse king of Dublin, seized York and invaded the English Midlands, forcing Edmund to cede the north-eastern territories. After Olaf's death in 941, Edmund progressively recovered the Five Boroughs of the Danelaw by 942, a success celebrated in verse in the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle, and in 944 he expelled the Viking rulers from York and re-established control of Northumbria. In 945 he conquered Strathclyde and granted it to Malcolm I of Scotland in return for an alliance. In 943 Edmund also stood as sponsor at the baptism of Olaf Sihtricson, reflecting the mix of warfare and diplomacy of the period.

Legislation and church

Edmund issued law codes addressing blood feuds, theft and the conduct of the clergy, and promoted the monastic revival then beginning under Dunstan, whom he recalled from exile and made Abbot of Glastonbury.

Death

Edmund was killed at Pucklechurch in Gloucestershire on 26 May 946, stabbed by an exiled outlaw named Leofa while intervening in a brawl at a royal feast, according to the chroniclers. He was buried at Glastonbury Abbey and succeeded by his brother Eadred; his sons Eadwig and Edgar would later reign in turn.
