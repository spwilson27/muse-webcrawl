Northern Sámi (Northern Sámi: Davvisámegiella) is the most widely spoken of the Sámi languages, with roughly 20,000 to 25,000 speakers in northern Norway, Sweden and Finland. It belongs to the Western Sámi group of the Uralic language family.

Orthography

Northern Sámi is written in a Latin-based alphabet containing the letters á, č, đ, ŋ, š, ŧ and ž. A standardised orthography was adopted in 1979 and revised in 1985, replacing several earlier competing systems in the three countries.

Status

The language has official recognition in several municipalities in Norway, Sweden and Finland, and is used in education, media and the Sámi parliaments. Like the other Sámi languages it is classified as endangered, though revitalisation programmes are under way.

Grammar

Northern Sámi is an agglutinative language with consonant gradation, several noun cases and a dual grammatical number alongside singular and plural.
