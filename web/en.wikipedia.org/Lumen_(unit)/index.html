Lumen (unit)

The lumen (symbol: lm) is the SI derived unit of luminous flux, a measure of the total quantity of visible light emitted by a source per unit of time. One lumen is defined as the luminous flux of light produced by a source that emits one candela of luminous intensity over a solid angle of one steradian: 1 lm = 1 cd·sr.

Definition and context

Unlike the watt, which measures radiant flux in absolute physical terms, the lumen is weighted by the luminosity function, a standard model of the human eye's sensitivity to different wavelengths — peaking at about 555 nm (green). A source emitting one watt of monochromatic green light at 555 nm produces 683 lumens; all other wavelengths produce fewer lumens per watt. Illuminance — lumens falling per unit area — is measured in lux (lm/m²).

Typical values

A traditional 100 W incandescent bulb emits about 1,300–1,400 lumens; modern LED lamps achieve the same output from around 12–15 W, with luminous efficacies exceeding 100 lm/W. A candle emits roughly 13 lumens in total, and a bright video projector several thousand lumens. Since 2011, light bulbs in the European Union and United States are labelled primarily in lumens rather than watts.
