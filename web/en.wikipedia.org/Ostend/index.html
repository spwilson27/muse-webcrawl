Ostend

Ostend (Dutch: Oostende) is a coastal city and municipality in the province of West Flanders, Belgium, on the North Sea coast. It is Belgium's largest coastal resort and a major ferry and fishing port, with a population of roughly 70,000.

History

Ostend began as a fishing village at the eastern end ("oost-einde") of a former island. It received city rights around 1265. The city is famous for the Siege of Ostend (1601–1604), one of the longest sieges in history, during the Eighty Years' War, after which the ruined town was rebuilt. In the 18th century the Ostend Company briefly pursued overseas trade to India and China before being suppressed under diplomatic pressure.

The royal resort

In the 19th century Kings Leopold I and Leopold II made Ostend their summer residence, earning it the nickname "Queen of the Belgian sea-side resorts". Grand hotels, a casino, a hippodrome and the Royal Galleries (Koninklijke Gaanderijen) date from this period. The painter James Ensor, Ostend's most famous son, lived and worked in the city; his house is now a museum. The singer Marvin Gaye also lived briefly in Ostend in the early 1980s, where he recorded "Sexual Healing".

Transport and economy

Ostend has ferry and rail links, an international airport, and a working fishing port. Tourism, offshore wind energy support and maritime services dominate the local economy.
