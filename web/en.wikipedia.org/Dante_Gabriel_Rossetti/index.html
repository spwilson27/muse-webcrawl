Gabriel Charles Dante Rossetti (1828–1882), generally known as Dante Gabriel Rossetti, was an English poet, illustrator, painter, and translator, and a founding member of the Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood in 1848. His art and poetry, steeped in medievalism and sensual romanticism, profoundly influenced European symbolism and the Aesthetic movement.

Early life

Rossetti was born in London to Italian émigré scholar Gabriele Rossetti. His siblings included the poet Christina Rossetti and the critic William Michael Rossetti. He studied at the Royal Academy Schools and under Ford Madox Brown, and with William Holman Hunt and John Everett Millais founded the Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood, whose journal The Germ published his early poems.

Art and poetry

Early Pre-Raphaelite works such as The Girlhood of Mary Virgin (1849) and Ecce Ancilla Domini (1850) gave way to watercolours of medieval subjects and, later, to large oil portraits of statuesque women — among them Beata Beatrix (1864–1870), Proserpine (1874), and Astarte Syriaca. His models included Elizabeth Siddal, whom he married in 1860, Fanny Cornforth, and Jane Morris. His poetry collection Poems (1870) included "The Blessed Damozel" and the sonnet sequence The House of Life.

Later life

After Siddal's death from a laudanum overdose in 1862, Rossetti famously buried his manuscript poems with her — and controversially had them exhumed in 1869 for publication. Attacked for the "fleshly school of poetry", he suffered a breakdown in 1872 and became dependent on chloral hydrate. He died at Birchington-on-Sea in 1882.
