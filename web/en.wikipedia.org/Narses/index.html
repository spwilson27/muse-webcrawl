Narses

Narses
Born: c. 478–483, Armenia
Died: c. 573, Rome
Allegiance: Byzantine Empire
Service: Chamberlain; general under Justinian I
Battles: Taginae (552), Mons Lactarius (552/553), Casilinum (554)

Narses (c. 478/483 – c. 573) was, with Belisarius, one of the two great generals of the Byzantine emperor Justinian I. An Armenian eunuch who rose through the imperial household to become grand chamberlain, he completed the Byzantine reconquest of Italy from the Ostrogoths.

Career

Narses first appears as a trusted court official who helped suppress the Nika riots of 532 in Constantinople. In 538 Justinian sent him to Italy with reinforcements, but friction with Belisarius hampered the campaign, and he was recalled. When the Gothic war revived under King Totila in the 540s, the aging Narses received full command with generous resources.

Conquest of Italy

In 552 Narses marched around the head of the Adriatic and destroyed Totila's army at the Battle of Taginae, where Totila was mortally wounded. He then defeated the last Gothic king, Teia, at Mons Lactarius near Mount Vesuvius, and in 554 crushed a Frankish and Alemannic invasion at Casilinum. Narses reorganized Italy's administration under the Pragmatic Sanction and governed from Ravenna and Rome until his recall in 567/568, shortly before the Lombard invasion. Legend later claimed that in revenge he invited the Lombards into Italy — a story historians consider apocryphal. See also Belisarius and Gothic War.
