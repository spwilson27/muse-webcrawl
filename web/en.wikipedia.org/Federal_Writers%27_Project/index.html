Federal Writers' Project

The Federal Writers' Project (FWP) was a United States federal government project created in 1935 under the Works Progress Administration (WPA) to provide employment for unemployed writers, editors, historians and researchers during the Great Depression. It was part of Federal Project Number One, the arts programmes of the New Deal.

Active1935–1943

Projects

The FWP's best-known output is the American Guide Series: guidebooks to every state, plus many cities and regions, describing geography, history, folklore and local life. Teams across the country included future literary figures such as Ralph Ellison, Zora Neale Hurston, John Steinbeck and Studs Terkel.

Slave narratives and legacy

Project field workers also conducted thousands of interviews, including more than 2,300 first-person accounts from formerly enslaved people — the Born in Slavery collection now held by the Library of Congress — and extensive folklore and life-history material. The project wound down by 1943 as war mobilisation ended mass unemployment.
