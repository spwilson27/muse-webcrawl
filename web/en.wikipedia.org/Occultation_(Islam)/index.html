Occultation (Islam)

In Islam, the occultation (Arabic: ghayba) is the eschatological belief that a messianic figure — in Twelver Shia Islam, Muhammad al-Mahdi, the twelfth Imam — is not dead but has been concealed by God from humanity, and will return at the end of time to restore justice. Related beliefs about hidden or occulted imams also occur in Ismaili and other Shia traditions.

Twelver doctrine

Twelvers hold that the twelfth Imam, born in 869, went into the Minor Occultation in 874, during which he communicated through four successive deputies. In 941 began the Major Occultation, which continues: the Imam remains hidden, without official deputies, until his reappearance (zuhur) alongside Isa (Jesus) to fill the earth with justice. During the occultation, religious authority is exercised by scholars, a basis for doctrines such as the Iranian velayat-e faqih.

Other traditions

Ismaili traditions differ: some hold that their imams entered concealment (dawr al-satr) at various points, while Nizari Ismailis affirm a living, present Imam. Sunni eschatology expects a Mahdi who has not yet been born or identified rather than one in occultation.
