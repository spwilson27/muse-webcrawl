Heathenry, also termed Heathenism, Germanic paganism or Germanic neopaganism, is a modern Pagan new religious movement. Scholars of religious studies classify it as a form of new religious movement. Its practitioners model their religion on the beliefs and practices of the Germanic peoples of Iron Age and early medieval Europe, centred on deities such as Odin, Thor and Freyja known from Old Norse and other Germanic sources.

Beliefs and practice

Heathenry is polytheistic and typically emphasises reciprocity with gods, ancestors and land spirits (landvættir), a worldview often summarised by the concept of wyrd (fate). Its characteristic rites are the blót — an offering ceremony, historically involving sacrifice and now usually of food or drink — and the sumbel, a ritualised round of toasts and oaths. Communities are organised locally into groups variously called kindreds, hearths or fellowships; there is no central authority or single dogma, and Iceland's Ásatrúarfélagið (founded 1972 by Sveinbjörn Beinteinsson) became one of the first officially recognised Heathen organisations, later building the Ásatrú temple Hof Ásatrúarfélagsins in Reykjavík.

History

The movement draws on 19th- and early-20th-century Germanic revivalism and occultism, some strands of which were entangled with völkisch nationalism — a legacy the modern movement continues to contest. It took its contemporary form in the early 1970s with near-simultaneous foundings in Iceland, Britain and the United States.

Internal divisions

A persistent debate divides Heathens over ancestry and inclusion: "folkish" Heathens hold that the religion is tied to Germanic ancestry, while "universalist" or inclusivist Heathens welcome anyone. Many organisations have formally repudiated racism and discrimination, especially after some far-right groups adopted Norse symbolism.
