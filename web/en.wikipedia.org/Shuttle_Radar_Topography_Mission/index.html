Shuttle Radar Topography Mission

The Shuttle Radar Topography Mission (SRTM) is an international research effort that obtained digital elevation models on a near-global scale from 56°S to 60°N. It was flown aboard the Space Shuttle Endeavour during the STS-99 mission in February 2000.

SRTM used specially modified radar systems that flew on Endeavour for 11 days, employing single-pass radar interferometry from two antennas, one in the shuttle's payload bay and one at the end of a 60-metre mast extending from the bay.

The resulting elevation data, at resolutions of 1 arc-second (about 30 metres) and 3 arc-seconds (about 90 metres), became one of the most widely used geospatial datasets in the world, freely distributed by the United States Geological Survey.
