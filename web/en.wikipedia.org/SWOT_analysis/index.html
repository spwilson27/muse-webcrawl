SWOT analysis

SWOT analysis is a strategic planning and strategic management technique used to help a person or organisation identify its Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities and Threats related to business competition or project planning. Strengths and weaknesses are internal factors, while opportunities and threats arise from the external environment. The technique is usually credited to Albert Humphrey, who led a research project at the Stanford Research Institute in the 1960s and 1970s using data from Fortune 500 companies.

The framework

Results are typically presented in a 2×2 matrix. Decision makers list internal capabilities and deficiencies, then scan the external environment for favourable conditions to exploit and dangers to defend against. The analysis is intended as a starting point for strategy formulation: matching strengths to opportunities, remedying weaknesses, and building defences against threats. Extensions include the TOWS matrix, which systematically pairs internal and external factors to generate strategic options.

Applications

SWOT analysis is used in corporate strategy, marketing plans, product development, nonprofit planning, community and regional development, and personal career planning. It is frequently taught in business schools as an introductory strategic tool because of its simplicity and flexibility.

Criticism

Scholars have criticised SWOT for producing long, unprioritised lists without evidence, for confusing internal and external factors, and for not by itself generating strategy. Empirical studies (notably Hill and Westbrook, 1997) found that companies often completed the exercise without using the outputs in subsequent planning. Used rigorously — with evidence, weighting, and linkage to decisions — it remains a widely used first-pass framework.
