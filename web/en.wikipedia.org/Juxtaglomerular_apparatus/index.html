Juxtaglomerular apparatus

The juxtaglomerular apparatus (also known as the juxtaglomerular complex) is a structure in the kidney that regulates the function of each nephron and plays a central role in controlling blood pressure and the filtration rate of the glomerulus. It lies where the thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle (via the distal tubule) passes between the afferent and efferent arterioles of its own glomerulus.

Components

  • Juxtaglomerular (granular) cells — modified smooth-muscle cells of the afferent arteriole that synthesise, store and secrete the enzyme renin.
  • Macula densa — a plaque of specialised cells in the wall of the distal tubule that senses sodium chloride concentration in the tubular fluid.
  • Extraglomerular mesangial (lacis) cells — cells between the arterioles and macula densa, thought to relay signals between them.

Function

When blood pressure or sodium delivery falls, the macula densa signals the granular cells to release renin, initiating the renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system, which raises blood pressure and promotes sodium and water retention. The apparatus also mediates tubuloglomerular feedback, adjusting afferent arteriolar tone to stabilise glomerular filtration.
