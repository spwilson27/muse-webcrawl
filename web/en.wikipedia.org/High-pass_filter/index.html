High-pass filter

A high-pass filter (HPF) is an electronic filter that passes signals with a frequency higher than a certain cutoff frequency and attenuates signals with frequencies lower than the cutoff frequency. The amount of attenuation for each frequency depends on the filter design. A high-pass filter is usually modelled as a linear time-invariant system. It is sometimes called a low-cut filter or bass-cut filter in the context of audio engineering.

Description

The simplest high-pass filter is an RC circuit in which the output is taken across the resistor; its cutoff frequency is fc = 1/(2πRC), above which the output approaches the input and below which the response falls at 20 dB per decade (first-order roll-off). Higher-order filters, built from multiple stages or with inductors and active components, roll off more steeply. Discrete-time digital implementations include the simple one-pole filter y[i] = α·(y[i−1] + x[i] − x[i−1]).

Applications

High-pass filters are used to remove DC offsets and low-frequency rumble in audio, to block DC while passing AC signals, in loudspeaker crossovers to protect tweeters, in image processing for edge enhancement and sharpening, and throughout telecommunications and instrumentation.
