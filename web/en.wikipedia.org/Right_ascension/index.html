Right ascension

Right ascension (abbreviated RA; symbol α) is the angular distance of a particular point measured eastward along the celestial equator from the Sun at the March equinox to the hour circle of the point in question above the Earth. Paired with declination, it specifies the location of a point on the celestial sphere in the equatorial coordinate system.

Units

Right ascension is measured in hours, minutes and seconds of time, reflecting Earth's rotation: the full circle of 360° corresponds to 24 hours, so one hour of right ascension equals 15 degrees. The unit originated in the practice of timing stars as they crossed the meridian. Right ascension increases eastward, in the direction in which stars appear to move across the sky during the night.

History

The concept of right ascension is ancient — possibly dating back to Hipparchus, who used equatorial coordinates in the 2nd century BC — and was formalised through the work of later astronomers including Tycho Brahe and John Flamsteed. Because the equinox precesses slowly, catalogued coordinates must be quoted for a standard epoch, such as J2000.0.
