Hartree–Fock method

In computational physics and chemistry, the Hartree–Fock (HF) method is a method of approximation for the determination of the wave function and the energy of a quantum many-body system in a stationary state. The Hartree–Fock method often assumes that the exact N-body wave function of the system can be approximated by a single Slater determinant (in the case where the particles are fermions) or by a single permanent (in the case of bosons) of N spin-orbitals. By invoking the variational method, one can derive a set of coupled equations known as the Hartree–Fock equations, whose solution gives an approximate wave function and energy.

History

The method is named after Douglas Hartree and Vladimir Fock. Hartree proposed a self-consistent field procedure in 1928 in which each electron moves in the average field of the others; Fock (and independently John C. Slater) showed in 1930 that an antisymmetrized determinant ansatz introduces exchange interactions automatically, leading to the full Hartree–Fock equations. Clemens C. J. Roothaan's 1951 reformulation, expanding the orbitals in a finite basis set, made the method practical for molecules and underlies most modern quantum chemistry codes.

Algorithm

The Hartree–Fock equations are solved iteratively in a self-consistent field (SCF) procedure: starting from a guessed set of orbitals, one constructs Coulomb and exchange operators, solves the (generalized) eigenvalue problem to obtain new orbitals, and repeats until the energy and density converge. The method is exact for a one-electron system and captures the Pauli exchange exactly, but it neglects electron correlation — the tendency of electrons to avoid one another beyond the average-field effect — which accounts for chemical accuracy errors of the order of 1% of total energy but often much more of binding energies.

Limitations and post-Hartree–Fock methods

Because correlation is missing, Hartree–Fock can fail qualitatively for bond dissociation and systems with near-degenerate configurations. A family of post-Hartree–Fock methods improves on it systematically, including configuration interaction, Møller–Plesset perturbation theory (MP2 and higher), coupled cluster theory, and multi-configurational self-consistent field approaches. Hartree–Fock theory is also the starting point for deriving the Kohn–Sham equations of density functional theory with exact exchange.
