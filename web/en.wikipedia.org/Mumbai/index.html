Mumbai (formerly called Bombay) is the capital city of the Indian state of Maharashtra. It is the most populous city in India and one of the largest urban agglomerations in the world. Mumbai lies on the Konkan coast of western India and has a deep natural harbour. It is the financial, commercial, and entertainment capital of India, home to the Bombay Stock Exchange, the Reserve Bank of India, and the Bollywood film industry.

History

The area was originally inhabited by Koli fishing communities and centred on seven islands. It passed through the hands of indigenous empires, the Portuguese (from 1534), and the English East India Company, to whom the islands were leased in 1668 after coming to the British Crown as part of Catherine of Braganza's dowry. Land reclamation joined the islands into a single landmass, and the city grew into a major port and cotton-trading centre during the 19th century.

Geography and climate

Mumbai sits on Salsette Island at the mouth of the Ulhas River. It has a tropical climate with a pronounced monsoon season from June to September, during which heavy rainfall frequently disrupts transport.

Economy

Mumbai contributes a large share of India's GDP, income tax, and foreign trade. Major industries include finance, textiles, information technology, healthcare, and film and television production centred in Bollywood.

Transport

The city's suburban railway network carries millions of passengers daily and is among the busiest in the world. Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport is one of India's busiest airports. The Mumbai Metro and the Bandra–Worli Sea Link are prominent modern additions.
