First-person (video games)

In video games, first-person refers to a graphical perspective rendered from the viewpoint of the player character, so the player sees the world through the character's eyes. It is best known from first-person shooters (FPS) but is also used in adventure, role-playing, simulation and horror games.

History

Early examples include Maze War (1973) and Spasim (1974). The perspective was popularised in the 1990s by Wolfenstein 3D (1992) and Doom (1993), and refined by titles such as Half-Life, Halo and immersive sims like System Shock.

Characteristics

First-person presentation typically shows only the character's hands or weapon, emphasises immersion and aiming precision, and is well suited to virtual reality. Drawbacks include motion sickness in some players and difficulty judging the character's own body position for platforming.
