PRIDE Fighting Championships

Founded: 1997 (as KRS-PRIDE)

Defunct: 2007

Headquarters: Tokyo, Japan

Parent: Dream Stage Entertainment

Sport: Mixed martial arts

Pride Fighting Championships (also known as Pride FC or simply Pride) was a Japanese mixed martial arts promotion company founded in 1997 and operated by Dream Stage Entertainment. At its peak in the early-to-mid-2000s it was the most popular and commercially successful MMA promotion in the world, staging events before crowds of tens of thousands in the Tokyo Dome and Saitama Super Arena and producing many of the era's defining fighters and fights.

History

Pride began on 11 October 1997 at the Tokyo Dome with an event headlined by Rickson Gracie versus Nobuhiko Takada, created to showcase the Japanese pro wrestler Takada. From 2000, under the Pride FC banner, the promotion built a roster including Fedor Emelianenko, Wanderlei Silva, Antônio Rodrigo Nogueira, Mirko Cro Cop, Quinton "Rampage" Jackson, Dan Henderson, Mauricio "Shogun" Rua, Kazushi Sakuraba and Takanori Gomi. Its hallmark events included Grand Prix tournaments in the heavyweight, middleweight (205 lb) and lightweight classes, the New Year's Eve Shockwave shows, and the Bushido series for lighter fighters.

Rules and presentation

Pride fought in a ring rather than a cage, under rules more permissive than the Unified Rules used in the United States: soccer kicks and stomps to a downed opponent's head and knees to the head of a grounded fighter were legal, while elbows to the head were banned. Bouts consisted of a ten-minute first round followed by two five-minute rounds, judged as a whole rather than round-by-round, with yellow cards penalising inactivity by docking pay. Production values were theatrical — elaborate entrances, pyrotechnics and the announcing of Lenne Hardt — contributing to an enduring nostalgic reputation.

Decline and sale

In 2006, Japanese broadcaster Fuji Television cancelled its contract with Pride amid reports in the magazine Shūkan Gendai linking Dream Stage Entertainment executives to organised crime (yakuza). Loss of television revenue crippled the promotion. In March 2007, Zuffa, owner of the Ultimate Fighting Championship, purchased Pride's assets. Hopes of continuing the brand and staging a "super bowl" of MMA faded; the last Pride event was held in April 2007 and most contracted fighters migrated to the UFC. Former Pride staff later founded the Dream promotion, and in 2015 Pride co-founder Nobuyuki Sakakibara launched Rizin Fighting Federation as a spiritual successor.
