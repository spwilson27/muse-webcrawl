Caucasus campaign

Date29 October 1914 – 30 October 1918
LocationEastern Anatolia and the Caucasus
BelligerentsRussian Empire (to 1917) vs Ottoman Empire, with German support
Key commandersEnver Pasha; Illarion Vorontsov-Dashkov; Nikolai Yudenich
ResultFront dissolved with the collapse of both empires, 1917–18

The Caucasus campaign comprised armed conflicts between the Russian Empire and the Ottoman Empire, later including Armenia, Azerbaijan, Georgia, and German forces, during the First World War. Fighting took place in the mountainous borderlands between the two empires, from the Black Sea to Lake Van and into north-western Persia.


Operations in the Caucasus front, 1914–1918.

Background

Russia and the Ottoman Empire had fought a series of wars over the Caucasus and eastern Anatolia through the 19th century. When war broke out in 1914, Russia sought to pin down Ottoman forces and protect the Baku oilfields, while Ottoman war minister Enver Pasha dreamed of recovering lost provinces and uniting Turkic peoples of the Caucasus and Central Asia.

Course of the war

Sarikamish and the first offensives

After Russian forces advanced in the Köprüköy offensive of November 1914, Enver Pasha personally led a winter offensive against Sarikamish (December 1914 – January 1915). The Ottoman Third Army was catastrophically defeated, losing most of its strength to Russian resistance, cold, and disease. In 1915 Russian forces advanced toward Lake Van and relieved the Armenian defenders of the city of Van.

Russian offensives of 1916

Under General Nikolai Yudenich, Russia mounted its most successful operations of the campaign: the Erzurum offensive (January–February 1916) captured the fortress city of Erzurum, and in April 1916 Russian forces took the port of Trabzon, supplied by the Black Sea Fleet. A further offensive captured Erzincan in July. Ottoman forces under Mustafa Kemal stabilised the front around Bitlis and Muş later that year.

Collapse, 1917–1918

The Russian Revolutions of 1917 destroyed the Russian Caucasus Army's cohesion; the Erzincan armistice of December 1917 ended formal fighting. In 1918 Ottoman armies advanced into the power vacuum, retaking lost territory and capturing Baku in September 1918 with the Army of Islam, weeks before the Armistice of Mudros ended the war.

Consequences

The campaign took place amid the Armenian genocide: Ottoman authorities deported and massacred the Armenian population of eastern Anatolia, in a catastrophe closely connected to the war on this front. Hundreds of thousands of soldiers and civilians on both sides died, many from disease and exposure. The post-war settlement briefly produced independent Armenian, Georgian, and Azerbaijani republics before Sovietisation of the region.
