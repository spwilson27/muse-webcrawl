John Singleton Copley

Born: 3 July 1738, Boston, Massachusetts Bay, British America
Died: 9 September 1815 (aged 77), London, England
Known for: Portrait painting
Notable works: Paul Revere (1768); Watson and the Shark (1778); The Death of Major Peirson (1783)

John Singleton Copley (July 3, 1738 – September 9, 1815) was an Anglo-American painter, active in both colonial America and England. He is considered the finest portraitist of colonial America and a pioneer of large-scale history painting in Britain.

American period

Largely self-taught in Boston, influenced by his stepfather, the engraver Peter Pelham, Copley by the 1760s was painting strikingly realistic portraits of New England merchants and families, such as his famous portrait of Paul Revere (c. 1768–1770) holding a teapot, and of John Hancock and Samuel Adams. His painting A Boy with a Flying Squirrel (1765), exhibited in London, won praise from Joshua Reynolds.

London career

Uneasy as political tensions grew, Copley left for Europe in 1774 and settled in London in 1775; his family, whose sympathies were Loyalist, joined him. He turned to contemporary history painting: Watson and the Shark (1778), depicting Brook Watson's rescue from a shark attack in Havana harbour, and The Death of Major Peirson, 6 January 1781 (1783) were major successes. He became a member of the Royal Academy in 1779.

Later years

Copley's later career was hampered by disputes over payment and changing tastes, and by a long legal battle over his commission for The Siege of Gibraltar. He died in London in 1815. Boston's Copley Square and the Copley Medal of the Royal Society (a different family connection) keep the name prominent; his works anchor American collections at the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston.
