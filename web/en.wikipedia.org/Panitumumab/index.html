Panitumumab

Panitumumab, sold under the brand name Vectibix, is a fully human monoclonal antibody used in the treatment of metastatic colorectal cancer. It was developed by Abgenix (later acquired by Amgen) and approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration in 2006.

Mechanism of action

Panitumumab is an IgG2 antibody that binds with high affinity to the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), blocking its natural ligands from activating the receptor and thereby inhibiting tumour cell growth, proliferation and survival signalling. Because it is fully human rather than chimeric, it carries a lower risk of infusion reactions than the partly mouse-derived antibody cetuximab.

Clinical use

Panitumumab is indicated for metastatic colorectal cancer expressing EGFR, in tumours with wild-type RAS genes; patients whose tumours carry KRAS or NRAS mutations do not benefit, so mutational testing is required before treatment. It may be given alone or in combination with chemotherapy regimens such as FOLFOX or FOLFIRI.

Side effects

Characteristic adverse effects include an acne-like (acneiform) skin rash — which correlates with treatment response — dry skin and nail changes, diarrhoea, and low magnesium levels. The drug carries a boxed warning for dermatologic toxicity.
