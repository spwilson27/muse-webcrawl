Restoration (England)

The Restoration was the return of the Stuart monarchy to England, Scotland, and Ireland in May 1660, when Charles II was restored to the throne after the Interregnum that followed the execution of Charles I in 1649 and the rule of Oliver Cromwell's Commonwealth and Protectorate. The term is also applied to the period of Charles's reign (1660–1685) and its distinctive culture.

Background

After Cromwell's death in 1658 and the political chaos under his son Richard, General George Monck marched on London and arranged for a freely elected Convention Parliament, which invited Charles to return from exile under the Declaration of Breda, promising amnesty and religious toleration.

The Restoration settlement

The restored regime executed or punished the surviving regicides, re-established the Church of England — though the Cavalier Parliament imposed the restrictive Clarendon Code on dissenters — and revived a court culture of conspicuous pleasure. The era saw the reopening of theatres, Restoration comedy, the founding of the Royal Society, the Great Plague of 1665, and the Great Fire of London in 1666.
