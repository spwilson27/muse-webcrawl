Civil Code of Quebec

Civil Code of Quebec
Citation: CCQ-1991
Enacted: 1991
In force: January 1, 1994
Predecessor: Civil Code of Lower Canada (1866)
Jurisdiction: Quebec, Canada

The Civil Code of Quebec (French: Code civil du Québec, CCQ) is the principal civil code governing private law in the Canadian province of Quebec. Adopted in 1991 and in force since January 1, 1994, it replaced the Civil Code of Lower Canada of 1866. It is the cornerstone of Quebec's civil law tradition, which coexists within Canada's bijural federal legal system alongside the common law of the other provinces.

Structure

The Code contains a preliminary provision and ten books covering persons, family, successions (inheritance), property, obligations, prior claims and hypothecs (security interests), evidence, prescription (limitation periods), publication of rights, and private international law. It comprises over 3,000 articles and is published in both French and English, both versions being equally authoritative.

History

Quebec's civil law descends from the French Coutume de Paris introduced in New France, which was preserved after the British conquest by the Quebec Act of 1774. The Civil Code of Lower Canada (1866) was modeled on the French Civil Code of 1804. A revision office was created in 1955, and after decades of work the new code was adopted by the National Assembly of Quebec in 1991.

Significance

The Code opens with the declaration that it "governs persons, relations between persons and property" and states that it is "the foundation of all other laws" in civil matters in Quebec. Its preliminary provision emphasizes good faith, which permeates Quebec civil law.
