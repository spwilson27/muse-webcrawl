Zebra crossing

A zebra crossing (or crosswalk in North American usage, though the term there covers other designs too) is a type of pedestrian crossing marked by longitudinal white stripes on the road surface, resembling the coat of a zebra. In the United Kingdom, where the term originates, drivers are legally required to give way to pedestrians on the crossing.

History

The zebra design was introduced in the UK after trials beginning in 1949 and was standardized under the Transport Minister Ernest Marples in the early 1950s. The Belisha beacon — a flashing amber globe named after minister Leslie Hore-Belisha — marks crossings in the UK and several Commonwealth countries.

International use

Zebra-style markings are now used worldwide, though their legal meaning varies: in some countries pedestrians have absolute priority, while elsewhere the stripes are advisory. A zebra crossing on Abbey Road in London became famous through the Beatles' 1969 album cover.
