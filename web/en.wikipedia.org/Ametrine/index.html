Ametrine, also known as trystine or by its trade name as bolivianite, is a naturally occurring variety of quartz. It is a mixture of amethyst and citrine with zones of purple and yellow or orange. Almost all commercially available ametrine is mined in Bolivia.

Structure

Ametrine is composed of silicon dioxide (SiO2) and carries the same crystal structure as quartz. The bicolor appearance results from differing oxidation states of iron impurities within the crystal, combined with a temperature gradient during its formation, so that some zones develop the purple color of amethyst and others the yellow of citrine.

Sources

The Anahí mine in eastern Bolivia, near the border with Brazil, is the world's principal source. Legend holds that the deposit was introduced to Europe through a Spanish conquistador who received the land as a dowry upon marrying a princess named Anahí of the Ayoreo tribe.

Synthetic material

Much ametrine sold in the gem trade is synthetic, produced by hydrothermal growth in Russia, or is natural material that has been treated by irradiation and heating to produce the two-color effect.
