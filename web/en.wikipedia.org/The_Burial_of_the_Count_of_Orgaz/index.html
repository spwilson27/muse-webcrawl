The Burial of the Count of Orgaz


The Burial of the Count of Orgaz (1586–1588) by El Greco.

The Burial of the Count of Orgaz (Spanish: El Entierro del Conde de Orgaz) is a painting by El Greco, a Greek painter of the Spanish Renaissance. Widely regarded as his masterpiece, it was painted between 1586 and 1588 for the parish church of Santo Tomé in Toledo, Spain, where it remains. The monumental canvas, over 4.8 metres tall, depicts a popular local legend concerning the burial in 1323 of Don Gonzalo Ruiz de Toledo, Count of Orgaz, a pious nobleman.

Subject

According to the legend, at the Count's burial the saints Stephen and Augustine of Hippo miraculously descended from heaven to lay the body in its tomb with their own hands, in reward for the count's charity and devotion. The painting is divided into two zones in the Mannerist fashion: the earthly lower register, rendered in a sober, portrait-like naturalism, and the swirling, elongated celestial realm above, where Christ, the Virgin Mary, and John the Baptist preside over the reception of the count's soul, depicted as an infant-like spirit carried upward by an angel.

Composition

The lower register contains a row of contemporary Toledan gentlemen in black mourning dress; their grave, portraiture-like faces are believed to depict actual members of the local nobility and clergy, including possibly a self-portrait of El Greco himself. The artist's young son, Jorge Manuel, appears at the far left holding a torch. The upper register, by contrast, abandons naturalistic space: elongated figures twist among clouds in cold, flickering light characteristic of El Greco's mature style.

Reception

The work is regarded as one of the greatest paintings of the Spanish Renaissance and a defining example of Mannerism. It has drawn sustained commentary from art historians and writers, including an extended analysis in relation to the theory of "involution" in art history.
