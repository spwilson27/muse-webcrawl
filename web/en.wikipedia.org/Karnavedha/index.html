Karnavedha

Karnavedha
Type: Hindu samskara (rite of passage)
Meaning: Ear-piercing ceremony
Timing: Infancy or early childhood (varies by tradition)

Karnavedha (Sanskrit: कर्णवेध, "ear-piercing") is one of the sixteen samskaras (sacraments) in Hinduism, marking the ritual piercing of a child's ears. It is performed for both boys and girls and is regarded as both a rite of passage and, in traditional Ayurvedic thought, a practice beneficial to health.

Ritual

The ceremony is traditionally performed on an astrologically auspicious day, in the first months or years of life, though timing varies by region and family custom. The ears are pierced by a goldsmith or physician with gold, silver or copper needles, accompanied by Vedic mantras and a feast.

Significance

Texts such as the Sushruta Samhita associate ear-piercing with protection against certain ailments, and the rite also serves to adorn the child and mark identity within the community. See also Samskara.
