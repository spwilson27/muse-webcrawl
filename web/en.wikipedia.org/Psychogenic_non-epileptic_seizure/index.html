Psychogenic non-epileptic seizure

Other namesPNES, functional seizures, dissociative seizures
SpecialtyNeurology, psychiatry
SymptomsSeizure-like events without abnormal cortical electrical discharges

Psychogenic non-epileptic seizures (PNES), also called functional or dissociative seizures, are episodes that resemble epileptic seizures but are not caused by abnormal electrical discharges in the brain. They are considered a form of functional neurological disorder and are often associated with psychological stress, trauma, or other psychiatric conditions.

Signs and symptoms

Events may involve convulsions, unresponsiveness, side-to-side head movements, pelvic thrusting, or prolonged duration with fluctuating course. Features such as closed eyes during the event, asynchronous limb movements, and preserved awareness can suggest PNES, but overlap with epilepsy is substantial and no single feature is diagnostic.

Diagnosis

The gold standard for diagnosis is capturing a typical event during video-electroencephalography (EEG) monitoring and confirming the absence of epileptiform activity. Misdiagnosis is common: many patients are treated with antiseizure medication for years before PNES is recognised, and some patients have both epilepsy and PNES.

Treatment

Treatment centres on clear communication of the diagnosis and psychological therapy, particularly cognitive behavioural therapy tailored to functional seizures. Antiseizure medicines are generally not helpful unless epilepsy coexists, and addressing comorbid anxiety, depression, or post-traumatic stress is important.
