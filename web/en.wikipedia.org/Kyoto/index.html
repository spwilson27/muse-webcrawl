Kyoto (Japanese: 京都), officially Kyoto City, is the capital city of Kyoto Prefecture in Japan. Located in the Kansai region on the island of Honshu, Kyoto forms part of the Keihanshin metropolitan area along with Osaka and Kobe. It was Japan's capital and the emperor's residence from 794 until 1868.

History

In 794, Emperor Kanmu moved the capital to Heian-kyō, modelled on the Chinese Tang-dynasty capital Chang'an. Kyoto remained the imperial capital for over a thousand years, though real political power often lay elsewhere, notably during the Kamakura and Edo shogunates. The city was largely spared large-scale destruction in the Second World War and retains an exceptional concentration of pre-war buildings, temples and gardens.

Culture and sights

Kyoto has roughly 1,600 Buddhist temples and 400 Shinto shrines. Seventeen sites, including Kiyomizu-dera, Kinkaku-ji (the Golden Pavilion), Ginkaku-ji, Ryōan-ji and Nijō Castle, are inscribed as the UNESCO World Heritage "Historic Monuments of Ancient Kyoto". The geisha district of Gion, the bamboo grove of Arashiyama and the thousands of vermilion torii gates at Fushimi Inari-taisha are among Japan's most famous sights.

Modern Kyoto is also an industrial and university city — the headquarters of Nintendo are located there — and it lent its name to the 1997 Kyoto Protocol on climate change.
