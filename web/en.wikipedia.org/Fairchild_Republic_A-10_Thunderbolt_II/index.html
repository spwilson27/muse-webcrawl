The Fairchild Republic A-10 Thunderbolt II is a single-seat, twin-turbofan, straight-wing, subsonic attack aircraft developed by Fairchild Republic for the United States Air Force (USAF). In service since 1977, it is named after the Republic P-47 Thunderbolt, but is commonly referred to as the "Warthog" or simply "Hog". The A-10 was designed to provide close air support (CAS) to friendly ground troops by attacking armored vehicles, tanks, and other ground forces; it is the only production-built aircraft designed solely for CAS to have served with the USAF.

Design

The aircraft is built around the GAU-8 Avenger, a powerful 30 mm rotary cannon that fires depleted uranium armor-piercing rounds. The airframe was designed for durability, with measures including a titanium armored "bathtub" protecting the pilot, redundant flight control systems, and foam-filled fuel tanks. Its large straight wing and externally mounted engines give good low-speed maneuverability and survivability, and allow operations from short, austere airstrips near the front lines.

Operational history

The A-10 first saw combat in the 1991 Gulf War, where it destroyed large numbers of Iraqi tanks and other vehicles. It has since flown in the Balkans, Afghanistan and Iraq, and in operations against the Islamic State. The upgraded A-10C variant, with modern avionics and precision-guided weapons capability, entered service in 2007. Despite repeated proposals to retire the type, the A-10 remains in the USAF inventory, and its planned service life has been extended into the 2030s and beyond.
