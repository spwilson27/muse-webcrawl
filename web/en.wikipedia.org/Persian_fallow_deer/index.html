Persian fallow deer

The Persian fallow deer (Dama mesopotamica) is a rare deer species native to the Middle East. It is larger than the European fallow deer, with males standing up to about 110 cm at the shoulder and bearing broad, palmate antlers, though with less-developed palmation than the European species.

Taxonomy

The Persian fallow deer is often classified as a subspecies of the fallow deer (Dama dama mesopotamica), but many authorities treat it as a full species, Dama mesopotamica. It was described by Victor Brooke in 1875 from specimens from Persia.

Distribution and habitat

Historically the species ranged from the Levant through Mesopotamia to Persia. Hunting and habitat loss reduced it drastically, and by the mid-20th century it was feared extinct. Small populations were rediscovered in southwestern Iran, in Khuzestan province along the Dez and Karkheh rivers.

Conservation

The species is listed as endangered. Captive breeding and reintroduction programmes in Iran and in Israel (notably at the Hai-Bar Carmel reserve and near Mount Carmel, and in the Jerusalem Biblical Zoo) have established new populations. Wild populations remain small and vulnerable to poaching and drought.
