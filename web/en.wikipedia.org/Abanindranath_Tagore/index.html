Abanindranath Tagore

Abanindranath Tagore (7 August 1871 – 5 December 1951) was an Indian artist and writer, a leading figure of the Bengal School of Art and the nephew of the poet Rabindranath Tagore. He was the principal artist behind the Swadeshi-inspired revival of Indian artistic traditions in the early 20th century, reacting against the academic oil painting promoted under British colonial rule.

Life

Tagore was born in Jorasanko, Calcutta, into the eminent Tagore family. He studied at Sanskrit College and later learned pastel, watercolour, and life drawing, initially imitating European naturalism before turning decisively to Indian sources. In 1905, during the partition of Bengal, his celebrated painting Bharat Mata depicted India as a four-armed goddess and became an icon of the nationalist movement.

Art

Tagore founded the Bengal School, which drew on Mughal and Rajput miniatures, Ajanta mural painting, and Japanese wash techniques learned through his association with Okakura Kakuzō and the visiting Japanese artists Yokoyama Taikan and Hishida Shunsō. His works include the illustrations to Omar Khayyam, the Arabian Nights series, and many wash paintings of moods and landscapes. He taught at the Government School of Art in Calcutta, where his students included Nandalal Bose and Kshitindranath Majumdar.

Writing

He was also a notable author of children's literature in Bengali, writing works such as Shakuntala, Khirer Putul, and Rajkahini, admired for their lyrical prose.
