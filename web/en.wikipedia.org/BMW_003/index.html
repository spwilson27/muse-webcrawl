BMW 003

The BMW 003 (designated RLM 109-003) was an early axial-flow turbojet engine produced by BMW during the Second World War. Together with the Junkers Jumo 004, it was one of only two German jet engines to reach production before the war ended.

Development

Development began before the war under Hermann Östrich, drawing on a contract awarded in 1939. The engine used a seven-stage axial compressor, an annular combustion chamber and a single-stage turbine, producing around 7.8 kN (1,760 lbf) of thrust. Early prototypes suffered from compressor and turbine-blade failures, and the programme was repeatedly delayed by shortages of heat-resistant alloys.

Service use

The BMW 003 entered limited service powering the Heinkel He 162 Volksjäger single-jet fighter, and was fitted experimentally to variants of the Arado Ar 234 and as auxiliary boost engines on other aircraft including early flights of the Messerschmitt Me 262 prototype. After the war, captured and derivative designs influenced early French and Soviet jet engines, including the Soviet RD-20 used in the MiG-9.
