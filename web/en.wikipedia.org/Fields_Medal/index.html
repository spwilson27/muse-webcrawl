Fields Medal

The Fields Medal is a prize awarded to two, three or four mathematicians under 40 years of age at the International Congress of the International Mathematical Union (IMU), a meeting that takes place every four years. It is often described as the highest honour a mathematician can receive, and is frequently called the "Nobel Prize of Mathematics".

History

The medal was first awarded in 1936 to Lars Ahlfors and Jesse Douglas, and it has been awarded every four years since 1950. It is named after the Canadian mathematician John Charles Fields, who proposed the prize, designed the medal and funded the monetary award. The obverse of the medal bears the profile of Archimedes and a Latin inscription.

Conditions

The medal recognises both existing work and the promise of future achievement. By an informal rule adopted in 1966, recipients must be under 40 on 1 January of the award year, reflecting Fields's wish that the award encourage further work. The monetary prize is modest — 15,000 Canadian dollars — compared with the Nobel Prizes.

Notable laureates

Laureates include Jean-Pierre Serre (the youngest recipient, at 27), Alexander Grothendieck, Stephen Smale, Terence Tao, Grigori Perelman — who declined the medal in 2006 — and Maryam Mirzakhani, the first woman to receive it, in 2014. As of 2022, 64 medals have been awarded.

Comparison with other prizes

Unlike the annual Nobel Prizes, the Fields Medal is quadrennial and age-limited. The Abel Prize, established in 2002, is awarded annually for lifetime achievement and is in some respects closer to a Nobel analogue.
