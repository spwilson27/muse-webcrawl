The Sarawak Museum, in Kuching, Sarawak, Malaysia, is the oldest museum in Borneo. Founded in 1891 at the encouragement of the naturalist Alfred Russel Wallace and established by Charles Brooke, the second Rajah of Sarawak, it is one of the best-known museums in Southeast Asia for ethnology and zoology, housing extensive collections relating to the Indigenous peoples and natural history of Borneo.

The old Sarawak Museum building, opened in 1891.

Collections and buildings

The museum's old building, designed in a Queen Anne style reminiscent of a Normandy town hall, displays ethnographic collections of the Dayak and other peoples of Sarawak, including a reconstructed Iban longhouse, traditional crafts, and zoological specimens. The museum campus grew to include an art gallery, an Islamic heritage museum, and aquarium facilities. In 2022 the new Borneo Cultures Museum opened nearby as Malaysia's second-largest museum, taking over as the flagship exhibition space of the Sarawak Museum Department, while conservation work on the historic building continues.

Journal

The museum publishes the Sarawak Museum Journal, founded in 1911, one of the oldest scholarly journals in the region, covering the archaeology, anthropology, and natural history of Borneo. Museum staff have played key roles in major research, including the excavations at Niah Cave that revealed evidence of some of the earliest human settlement in Southeast Asia.
