Stroke play

Stroke play, also known as medal play, is a scoring system in the sport of golf. In the regular form of stroke play, also known as total stroke play, every competitor counts each stroke taken to complete a hole in a round or series of rounds. The competitor with the fewest strokes at the conclusion of the round or rounds is the winner.

In stroke play the winner is the player who has taken the fewest number of strokes over the course of the round, or rounds. Stroke play is the most common format at virtually all levels of play, but most especially in professional golf.

Scoring

Unlike in Stableford, in stroke play a player must complete the hole; there is simply a cap on the number of strokes that can be recorded — in practice, if a player cannot improve their position in competition as a result of their score on a hole, they may pick their ball up and move to the next hole. Penalty strokes are counted toward the player's score. The player with the lowest total number of strokes wins.

To keep track of scores, each player in a group is responsible for marking the card of another player in the group, in accordance with the Rules of Golf. In the event that a player who is to hole out is unable to count the score correctly, the player is disqualified.

History

Stroke play was first recorded in Edinburgh on 15 April 1759, when it was used to settle a wager. Medal play became the standard competition format for the Open Championship and most other major tournaments during the nineteenth century, displacing match play as the primary championship format.

Comparing formats

If a player or team has a terrible hole, their chances of winning a stroke play competition can be severely compromised, since all strokes are counted. This is particularly applicable in professional stroke play events, where a hole score of seven can ruin a player's round, and also their chances of making the cut.

Stroke play is the format used in all four of golf's major championships: the Masters Tournament, the PGA Championship, the U.S. Open, and the Open Championship. It is also the format used for Olympic golf competition.
