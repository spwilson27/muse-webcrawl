1883 eruption of Krakatoa

The 1883 eruption of Krakatoa (Indonesian: Letusan Krakatau 1883) in the Sunda Strait between Java and Sumatra was one of the deadliest and most destructive volcanic events in recorded history. The cataclysmic climax on 27 August 1883 destroyed more than two-thirds of the island of Krakatoa, generated tsunamis that killed more than 36,000 people, and produced a sound heard nearly 5,000 kilometres away — often described as the loudest sound in recorded history.

Sequence of events

After months of increasing activity beginning in May 1883, the eruption culminated on the morning of Monday, 27 August, with four enormous explosions. The third, at about 10:02 a.m. local time, blew the northern two-thirds of the island apart and triggered the collapse of the caldera, which generated the largest tsunamis. Waves reaching 30 metres or more struck the coasts of Java and Sumatra, destroying roughly 165 villages and towns.

Global effects

The eruption, rated VEI-6, ejected some 20 cubic kilometres of rock and ash high into the stratosphere. Fine aerosols circled the globe, lowering average global temperatures by roughly 1.2 °C in the following year and producing vivid red sunsets for years — phenomena sometimes linked to the background of Edvard Munch's painting The Scream. Barometers around the world recorded the pressure wave, which circled the Earth multiple times.

Anak Krakatau

In 1927 a new island, Anak Krakatau ("Child of Krakatoa"), emerged from the caldera and grew into an active volcano. In December 2018 a flank collapse of Anak Krakatau triggered a tsunami that killed more than 400 people on the Sunda Strait coasts.
