Colonne Vendôme

The Colonne Vendôme is a monumental column in the Place Vendôme in Paris, France. Erected by Napoleon I between 1806 and 1810 to commemorate the Battle of Austerlitz, it was modelled on Trajan's Column in Rome and clad in a spiral bas-relief made — according to tradition — from bronze of cannon captured from the defeated Austrian and Russian armies.

History

The column stands about 44 metres high and is topped by a statue of Napoleon. The statue was replaced several times with regime changes: Napoleon as Caesar gave way during the Restoration, returned as the "Petit Caporal" under Louis-Philippe, and was restored in Roman dress under Napoleon III.

During the Paris Commune of 1871, the column was pulled down as a symbol of imperial militarism — an act for which the painter Gustave Courbet, who had proposed its removal, was held financially responsible. It was rebuilt in 1873–1875 and still stands today.
