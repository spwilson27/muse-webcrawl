Gothic War (535–554)

Date535 – 554 AD
LocationItaly and Dalmatia
ResultByzantine victory; Italy annexed to the Byzantine Empire
BelligerentsByzantine Empire vs. Ostrogothic Kingdom (with Frankish interventions)
CommandersBelisarius, Narses; Vitiges, Totila, Teia

The Gothic War between the Byzantine (Eastern Roman) Empire and the Ostrogothic Kingdom of Italy was fought from 535 until 554. It was the second of the Emperor Justinian I's wars of reconquest of the former Western Roman Empire, following the Vandalic War in North Africa. The war devastated Italy, ending the Ostrogothic kingdom but leaving the peninsula depopulated and economically ruined; within fifteen years of its end, much of Italy fell to the Lombards.

Background

Since 493 Italy had been ruled by the Ostrogoths under Theodoric the Great. After Theodoric's death in 526, dynastic disputes weakened the kingdom. The murder of the regent Amalasuntha in 535 gave Justinian a pretext for intervention, and he aimed to restore direct imperial rule over Italy.

First phase: Belisarius, 535–540

General Belisarius landed in Sicily in 535 and took it easily, then crossed to the mainland, capturing Naples and Rome in 536. The Goths deposed King Theodahad and raised Vitiges, who besieged Rome for over a year (537–538) but failed to retake it. Belisarius advanced north, and in 540 entered Ravenna — according to Procopius, by feigning acceptance of the Goths' offer to make him emperor of the West — capturing Vitiges and apparently ending the war.

Second phase: Totila's revival, 541–552

Justinian's harsh taxation and divided commands provoked a Gothic revival under the charismatic king Totila, who won victories at Faventia and Mugello (542), retook Naples (543) and Rome itself in 546 and again in 550, and raided Sicily and the Balkans. Belisarius's second Italian command (544–548) was under-resourced and inconclusive.

Third phase: Narses, 551–554

Justinian finally sent the eunuch general Narses with a large army in 551. Narses destroyed Totila's army at the Battle of Taginae (552), where Totila was mortally wounded, and crushed the last Gothic king, Teia, at the Battle of Mons Lactarius later that year. A Frankish invasion was defeated at the Volturnus in 554, and the Pragmatic Sanction of 554 reorganised Italy as an imperial province.

Consequences

The historian Procopius, an eyewitness, described a peninsula ravaged by war, plague and famine; Rome changed hands repeatedly and its population collapsed. The reconquest also overstrained Byzantine resources, contributing to difficulties on the Persian and Balkan frontiers. In 568 the Lombards invaded, taking most of the interior and leaving the empire with coastal enclaves such as Ravenna, Rome and Naples.
