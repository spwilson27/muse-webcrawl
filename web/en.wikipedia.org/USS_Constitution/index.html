USS Constitution

USS Constitution is a wooden-hulled, three-masted heavy frigate of the United States Navy. She is the world's oldest ship still afloat and was launched in 1797, one of six original frigates authorized by the Naval Act of 1794. Designed by Joshua Humphreys, the frigates were larger, more heavily armed, and more strongly built than standard frigates of the period.

Constitution saw action in the Quasi-War with France, the First Barbary War, and most famously the War of 1812, where she defeated five British warships including HMS Guerriere and HMS Java. During the battle with Guerriere, British shot was seen to rebound from her thick live-oak hull, earning her the nickname "Old Ironsides."

She served as a training ship and a receiving ship in later years and was preserved as a museum ship after public campaigns, including inspiration from Oliver Wendell Holmes Sr.'s 1830 poem "Old Ironsides." Today she is berthed at the Charlestown Navy Yard in Boston, Massachusetts, part of Boston National Historical Park, and remains a commissioned US Navy vessel crewed by active-duty sailors. She makes periodic turn-around cruises in Boston Harbor and underwent major restorations, including a 2015–2017 dry-docking.
