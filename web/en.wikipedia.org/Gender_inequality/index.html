Gender inequality

Gender inequality is the social phenomenon in which people are not treated equally on the basis of gender. The disparity may arise from distinctions regarding biology, psychology, or cultural norms and laws. Gender inequality is experienced differently across the world and affects access to education, employment, income, political representation, healthcare and personal safety.

Areas of inequality

Education

Globally, girls' school enrolment has risen dramatically since the late 20th century, and women now outnumber men in tertiary education in most developed countries; however, in parts of sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia girls still leave school earlier because of poverty, early marriage, and distance or safety concerns.

Employment and income

Women participate in paid work at lower rates than men almost everywhere and are over-represented in informal and unpaid care work. The gender pay gap — the difference in median earnings — persists in nearly all economies, reflecting occupational segregation, part-time work, career interruptions for childcare, and direct discrimination. Women also remain under-represented in senior management and boardrooms (the "glass ceiling").

Politics and health

Women hold roughly a quarter of seats in national parliaments worldwide, though quotas and electoral systems have raised representation in some countries. Health inequalities include maternal mortality, unequal access to reproductive healthcare, and in some regions sex-selective practices. Men face their own disadvantages in areas such as life expectancy, workplace deaths, military conscription and suicide rates.

Measurement

International indices include the UNDP's Gender Inequality Index, the World Economic Forum's Global Gender Gap Report, and the OECD's Social Institutions and Gender Index, which combine indicators on health, education, economic participation and political empowerment.
