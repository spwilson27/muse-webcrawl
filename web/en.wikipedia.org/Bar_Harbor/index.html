Bar Harbor, Maine

Country: United States
State: Maine
County: Hancock
Island: Mount Desert Island

Bar Harbor is a resort town on Mount Desert Island in Hancock County, Maine, United States. As of the 2020 census its population was about 5,500. The town is a popular tourist destination in the Down East region of Maine and a gateway to Acadia National Park.

History

The area was originally inhabited by the Wabanaki people, who called the island Pemetic, "the sloping land". Samuel de Champlain visited in 1604. The town was first settled by Europeans in 1763 and incorporated in 1796 as Eden; it was renamed Bar Harbor in 1918, after the sand and gravel bar, visible at low tide, at the entrance to its harbour. In the late 19th and early 20th centuries Bar Harbor became a fashionable summer resort for wealthy families, including the Rockefellers, Morgans, Fords, Vanderbilts, and Astors, who built large summer "cottages". The Great Fire of 1947 destroyed many of these estates and much of the surrounding forest.

Tourism and economy

Tourism is the town's principal industry. Visitors come for Acadia National Park, whale watching, kayaking, sailing from the town's marina and pier, and the shops and restaurants along Cottage Street and Main Street. The town is also a port of call for cruise ships. The Jackson Laboratory, a biomedical research institution founded in 1929, is a major local employer.
