Juan Valdez

Created: 1958–1959
Creator: Doyle Dane Bernbach, for the National Federation of Coffee Growers of Colombia
Companion: Conchita (his mule)
Purpose: Advertising character for 100% Colombian coffee

Juan Valdez is a fictional character who has appeared in advertisements for the National Federation of Coffee Growers of Colombia since 1959, representing the typically Colombian cafetero (coffee farmer). He typically appears together with his mule Conchita, carrying sacks of harvested coffee beans. He has become an icon of Colombian coffee and of Colombia itself, and the Juan Valdez name now fronts a chain of coffee shops.

History

The character was created by the advertising agency Doyle Dane Bernbach as part of a campaign to differentiate Colombian coffee — grown by hundreds of thousands of small farmers — from coffees blended from other origins. The idea came from the Federation's general manager, Arturo Gómez Jaramillo. Juan Valdez first appeared in print and television advertising in 1959–1960, picking beans by hand on steep Andean slopes to dramatize the labour behind "100% Colombian coffee" and its slogan, "the richest coffee in the world."

Portrayals

The role was first played by Cuban actor José F. Duval, followed by Colombian actor Carlos Sánchez, who portrayed Valdez from 1969 until his retirement in 2006 and death in 2018. After a nationwide search among real coffee growers, Carlos Castañeda, a cafetero from Andes, Antioquia, took over the role in 2006; he died in 2024. The character's image, in poncho and aguadeño hat with a carriel bag, appears on the Federation's logo and on packaging worldwide.

Brand and legacy

In 2002 the Federation launched the Juan Valdez Café chain, competing internationally with Starbucks and returning retail value to Colombian growers, who hold shares through the Federation. Juan Valdez is consistently ranked among the most recognizable advertising icons in the Americas, and in 2005 he was voted ahead of Ronald McDonald and the Energizer Bunny in an Advertising Week poll in New York.
