Acute respiratory distress syndrome

Symptoms: Severe shortness of breath, rapid breathing, low blood oxygen
Causes: Sepsis, pneumonia, trauma, aspiration, pancreatitis
Treatment: Mechanical ventilation, treating the underlying cause
Mortality: roughly 35–50% depending on severity

Acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) is a life-threatening form of respiratory failure characterised by widespread inflammation in the lungs, increased permeability of the alveolar–capillary barrier, and flooding of the alveoli with protein-rich fluid. The result is stiff, heavy lungs and severe hypoxemia that responds poorly to supplemental oxygen alone.

Definition

The Berlin definition (2012) requires: onset within one week of a known clinical insult; bilateral opacities on chest imaging not explained by effusion or collapse; respiratory failure not fully explained by cardiac failure or fluid overload; and impaired oxygenation, graded mild, moderate, or severe by the PaO₂/FiO₂ ratio measured on at least 5 cm H₂O of positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP).

Causes and course

Common causes include sepsis (the most frequent), severe pneumonia — prominently including COVID-19 — aspiration of gastric contents, major trauma, massive transfusion, and acute pancreatitis. The syndrome evolves through exudative, proliferative, and fibrotic phases; some survivors recover near-normal lung function, while others have lasting fibrosis and impaired quality of life.

Treatment

No drug therapy has been shown to cure ARDS; care is supportive. Key evidence-based measures are mechanical ventilation with low tidal volumes (6 mL/kg predicted body weight) and limited plateau pressures, which reduces ventilator-induced lung injury; prone positioning for moderate-to-severe cases; conservative fluid management; and treatment of the underlying cause. In refractory hypoxemia, extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) may be used. Mortality remains substantial — approximately 35% for mild, 40% for moderate, and near 46% for severe ARDS.
