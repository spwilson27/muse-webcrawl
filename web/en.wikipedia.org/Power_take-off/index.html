Power take-off

A power take-off or power takeoff (PTO) is one of several methods for taking power from a power source, such as a running engine, and transmitting it to an application such as an attached implement or separate machine. Most commonly, it is a splined drive shaft installed on a tractor or truck, allowing implements with a matching connector to be powered directly by the engine.

Agricultural use

On agricultural tractors, the rear PTO shaft is a standard feature used to power implements such as mowers, balers, hay rakes, augers, and sprayers. Standardized speeds are 540 revolutions per minute and 1000 rpm, defined by standards such as ISO 500 and ASAE S203, with shaft diameters and spline counts specified so implements can be exchanged between tractor brands. Older tractors often had transmission-driven PTOs that stopped when the clutch was disengaged; "live" and fully independent PTOs, engaged by their own clutch or hydraulic control, allow the implement to keep running regardless of the tractor's motion.

Vehicle and aviation use

PTOs are also fitted to trucks to drive hydraulic pumps for dump bodies, cranes, winches, snowploughs, and cement mixers, and to aircraft engines and auxiliary power units to drive generators and hydraulic systems. In marine applications, similar arrangements power winches and pumps.

Safety

The exposed rotating PTO shaft is one of the most dangerous parts of farm machinery: loose clothing or limbs caught by the shaft can cause severe injury or death in a fraction of a second. Modern standards require shielding of the shaft and master shields on the tractor, and safety campaigns stress disengaging the PTO and shutting off the engine before dismounting or servicing equipment.
