Music of Mali

The music of Mali is internationally celebrated and deeply rooted in the traditions of the Mandé peoples and the griots — hereditary praise singers and historians who preserve genealogies and epics. Mali is often described as the musical heartland of West Africa.

Traditional instruments

Signature instruments include the kora (a 21-string lute-harp), the ngoni lute, the balafon xylophone, the djembe and dunun drums, and the kamale ngoni. Tuareg guitar bands from the north, such as Tinariwen, developed the "desert blues" style.

Modern era

Malian artists who achieved global fame include Ali Farka Touré, Toumani Diabaté, Salif Keïta, Oumou Sangaré, Rokia Traoré and Amadou & Mariam. The Festival au Désert near Timbuktu drew international audiences before insecurity in northern Mali disrupted it after 2012, when armed groups briefly banned music in areas under their control.
