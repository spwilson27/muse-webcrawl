Angiosperm Phylogeny Group

The Angiosperm Phylogeny Group (APG) is an informal international collaboration of systematic botanists that produces the APG system of flowering plant classification. Founded in the 1990s, it synthesises molecular phylogenetic evidence—chiefly from DNA sequence data—into a consensus classification of the angiosperms, revised as new analyses appear.

The APG systems

The first APG paper (1998) broke with morphology-based systems such as Cronquist's by grouping plants strictly by common descent (monophyly), recognising 40 orders. APG II (2003) offered optional bracketed families for disputed circumscriptions. APG III (2009) expanded to 59 orders and 413 families and was adopted by major herbaria and databases, including Kew's. The current version, APG IV (2016), recognises 64 orders and 416 families.

Principles and impact

The APG deliberately works at the ranks of order and family, requires groups to be monophyletic, and presents its classification as a working consensus rather than a final authority; individual botanists and floras may depart from it. Its broad structure—early-diverging ANA-grade lineages, magnoliids, monocots, and the great eudicot radiation of rosids and asterids—is now the standard framework of botanical teaching, floras, and databases such as Plants of the World Online.
