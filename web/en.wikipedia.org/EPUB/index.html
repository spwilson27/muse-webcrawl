Reading an EPUB e-book

EPUB (short for electronic publication) is a free and open e-book file format developed by the International Digital Publishing Forum (IDPF) and now maintained within the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C). Files have the extension .epub and are ZIP archives containing XHTML content documents, CSS stylesheets, images and metadata. EPUB became an official IDPF standard in 2007, superseding the Open eBook (OEB) format.

Features

EPUB supports reflowable text that adapts to screen size and reader preferences — the dominant style for novels and text-heavy works — as well as fixed-layout publications for children's books, comics and textbooks. EPUB 3 added HTML5 content, MathML, SVG, multimedia overlays for read-aloud, and improved accessibility metadata aligned with WCAG. Optional digital rights management can be applied by vendors, though the format itself does not mandate DRM.

Adoption

EPUB is supported by most e-readers and reading apps — Kobo, Apple Books, Google Play Books, Adobe Digital Editions and many others — making it the closest thing to a universal e-book standard. Amazon's Kindle ecosystem long used proprietary formats (MOBI, AZW3/KF8), but Kindle devices and apps accept EPUB through Send to Kindle, converting it in the process. Publishers produce EPUB alongside print and PDF editions as their standard digital workflow.
