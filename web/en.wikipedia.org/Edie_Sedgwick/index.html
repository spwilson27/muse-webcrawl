Edie Sedgwick

Edith Minturn Sedgwick Post (April 20, 1943 – November 16, 1971), known as Edie Sedgwick, was an American actress, model, and socialite. She is best known for being one of Andy Warhol's superstars, starring in several of his underground films in the mid-1960s, and for her role as an icon of the era's youth culture.

Early life

Sedgwick was born in Santa Barbara, California, into the prominent Sedgwick family of New England. Despite the family's wealth, her childhood was troubled; she struggled with eating disorders and spent time in psychiatric hospitals, including Silver Hill and Bloomingdale. Two of her brothers died within eighteen months of each other in 1964–65.

The Factory years

In 1965 Sedgwick moved into New York's artistic circles and met Andy Warhol at a dinner party. She soon became a fixture at the Factory, Warhol's studio, and starred in films including Vinyl, Kitchen, Beauty No. 2, and Outer and Inner Space, in which she was famously filmed in split screen. With her cropped silver hair, heavy eyeliner, black tights, and chandelier earrings, she was dubbed the "It Girl" and a "Youthquaker" by Vogue, and her style was widely copied.

By 1966 the relationship with Warhol had deteriorated. Sedgwick drifted toward the circle of Bob Dylan — the Dylan songs "Just Like a Woman" and "Leopard-Skin Pill-Box Hat" have often been linked to her — and attempted a mainstream modelling and acting career, hampered by worsening drug addiction.

Later years and death

Sedgwick's final film, the semi-autobiographical Ciao! Manhattan, was completed shortly before her death. On November 16, 1971, she died at age 28 from an overdose of barbiturates, officially recorded as "probable acute barbiturate intoxication." She is buried in Oak Hill Cemetery in Ballard, California.

Her life has been dramatised in the 2006 biographical film Factory Girl. Sedgwick remains a lasting symbol of 1960s glamour and self-destruction.
