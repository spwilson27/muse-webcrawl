Coloane

Coloane (Chinese: 路環; Cantonese Yale: Louhwàahn; Portuguese: Coloane) is an island in Macau, China. Together with the neighbouring island of Taipa, it forms one of the two main islands of the Macao Special Administrative Region. The two islands have been joined by land reclamation into the Cotai strip, so that Coloane is now effectively connected to Taipa by reclaimed land rather than separated by water.

Coloane is the southernmost and least densely populated part of Macau. Covering about 7.6 square kilometres, it is characterised by green hills, small villages, beaches and a slower pace of life compared with the densely built-up Macau Peninsula, and it is often described as the "green lung" of the territory.

Geography

The highest point of Macau, Coloane Alto, rises to about 172.4 metres on the island. The coastline includes the popular beaches of Hac Sa ("black sand") and Cheoc Van. The village of Coloane, on the northwest shore, retains a Portuguese colonial character with pastel-coloured houses, narrow lanes and the small Chapel of St. Francis Xavier, built in 1928, facing a square paved in the Portuguese style.

History

Coloane was historically a base for pirates and salt production before coming under Portuguese administration as part of Macau. The island remained largely rural through the Portuguese period. In 1910, Portuguese forces defeated a pirate fleet near the island in an engagement remembered locally. Development accelerated in the late twentieth century with the construction of shipping facilities, a deep-water port, power station and housing, while much of the island's woodland was preserved as country park.

Attractions

Visitors come to Coloane for the A-Ma Cultural Village and the giant statue of the goddess A-Ma on Coloane Alto, the Macau Giant Panda Pavilion, hiking trails across the hills, and the village's bakeries, including the original branch of Lord Stow's Bakery, famous for its Portuguese egg tarts.
