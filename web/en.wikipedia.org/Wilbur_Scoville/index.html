Wilbur Scoville

Wilbur Lincoln Scoville (January 22, 1865 – March 10, 1942) was an American pharmacist best known for his creation of the "Scoville Organoleptic Test", now standardized as the Scoville scale.

Life and career

Scoville was born in Bridgeport, Connecticut. He worked for the pharmaceutical company Parke-Davis in Detroit, where in 1912 he devised his test to measure the pungency — the "heat" — of chili peppers.

The Scoville scale

In the original test, an extract of the pepper was diluted in sugar water until a panel of tasters could no longer detect heat; the degree of dilution gives the measure in Scoville Heat Units (SHU). Pure capsaicin measures 16 million SHU, while a bell pepper measures zero. Modern measurements usually use high-performance liquid chromatography instead of human tasters.

Other work

Scoville wrote The Art of Compounding, a pharmacy textbook first published in 1895 that went through many editions, and served as a professor at the Massachusetts College of Pharmacy.
