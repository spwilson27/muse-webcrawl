Thomas Perez

Thomas Edward Perez
Born: 7 October 1961, Buffalo, New York, U.S.
Political party: Democratic
Offices: U.S. Secretary of Labor (2013–2017); Chair of the Democratic National Committee (2017–2021)

Thomas Edward Perez (born 7 October 1961) is an American civil rights lawyer and Democratic Party politician who served as the 26th United States Secretary of Labor from 2013 to 2017 and as chair of the Democratic National Committee (DNC) from 2017 to 2021. He later served as a senior advisor to President Joe Biden and director of the White House Office of Intergovernmental Affairs.

Early life and education

Perez was born in Buffalo, New York, to immigrants from the Dominican Republic. His father, a physician, died when Perez was twelve. Perez worked his way through Brown University in part by working on a garbage truck, graduating in 1983. He earned a Master of Public Policy and a Juris Doctor from Harvard University in 1987.

Career

Perez spent much of his early career as a civil rights attorney at the U.S. Department of Justice, prosecuting police misconduct and hate crimes, and worked as an aide to Senator Edward M. Kennedy. He served on the Montgomery County Council in Maryland from 2002 to 2006, and then as Maryland's Secretary of Labor, Licensing and Regulation under Governor Martin O'Malley.

In 2009 President Barack Obama appointed Perez Assistant Attorney General for Civil Rights. In that role he revitalised the Civil Rights Division, enforcing voting rights, fair lending, and disability rights laws, and challenging voter identification laws in several states. In 2013 Obama nominated him as Secretary of Labor; he was confirmed by the Senate 54–46. As Labor Secretary he oversaw rules expanding overtime pay eligibility, strengthening workplace safety standards, and the fiduciary rule for retirement advisers.

In February 2017 Perez was elected chair of the Democratic National Committee, defeating Representative Keith Ellison; Ellison became deputy chair. Perez led the party through the 2018 midterms and the 2020 presidential election, stepping down in January 2021. In June 2023 he joined the Biden White House as a senior advisor and director of intergovernmental affairs.
