MINURSO

MINURSO
Full name: United Nations Mission for the Referendum in Western Sahara
Established: 29 April 1991 (UNSCR 690)
Headquarters: Laayoune, Western Sahara
Mandate: Monitor ceasefire; organise referendum

MINURSO is the United Nations peacekeeping mission in Western Sahara, established by Security Council Resolution 690 of 29 April 1991 under the Settlement Plan agreed by Morocco and the Polisario Front. Its name derives from the French Mission des Nations unies pour l'organisation d'un référendum au Sahara occidental.

Mandate

The mission was charged with monitoring the ceasefire that ended the Western Sahara War, identifying and registering voters, and organising a referendum in which the Sahrawi people would choose between independence and integration with Morocco.

Deadlock

Voter identification was completed in provisional form by 2000, but disputes over appeals and the political process prevented the referendum from ever being held. MINURSO's mandate has since been renewed annually, focusing on ceasefire monitoring; it is unusual among modern UN missions for lacking a human-rights monitoring mandate. Following the breakdown of the ceasefire in November 2020, the mission continued to observe and report on developments along the Berm.
