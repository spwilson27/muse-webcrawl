Morocco, officially the Kingdom of Morocco, is a country in the Maghreb region of North Africa, overlooking the Mediterranean Sea to the north and the Atlantic Ocean to the west, with land borders with Algeria to the east and the disputed territory of Western Sahara to the south.

History

Morocco's history runs from ancient Berber (Amazigh) kingdoms and Roman Mauretania through the Arab conquests and a succession of Islamic dynasties — Idrisids, Almoravids, Almohads, Marinids, Saadians and the Alaouites, who still reign — to the French and Spanish protectorates of 1912–1956 and independence under Mohammed V.

Geography and economy

The country spans coastal plains, the Rif and Atlas mountain ranges, and the fringes of the Sahara. Agriculture, phosphates (Morocco holds most of the world's reserves), tourism, automotive and aeronautics manufacturing, and renewable energy — including the Noor solar complex — anchor the economy. Morocco administers most of Western Sahara, whose status remains the subject of the Western Sahara conflict.

Culture

Moroccan culture blends Arab, Amazigh, African and European influences, visible in its medinas (Fes el Bali and Marrakech are UNESCO sites), its cuisine of tagines and couscous, and its music from Andalusi ensembles to chaabi and gnawa.
