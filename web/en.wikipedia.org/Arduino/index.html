Arduino is an Italian open-source hardware and software company, project, and user community that designs and manufactures single-board microcontrollers and microcontroller kits for building digital devices. Its hardware products are licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution Share-Alike licence, while software is licensed under the GNU Lesser General Public License or the GNU General Public License, permitting the manufacture of Arduino boards and software distribution by anyone. Arduino boards are available commercially from the official website or through authorised distributors.

History

The project began in 2005 at the Interaction Design Institute Ivrea (IDII) in Ivrea, Italy, aiming to provide a low-cost and easy way for novices and professionals to create devices that interact with their environment using sensors and actuators. It built on Hernando Barragán's Wiring platform and the Processing language created by Casey Reas and Ben Fry. Named after a bar in Ivrea frequented by the founders (itself named for Arduin of Ivrea), the platform's first boards used Atmel AVR microcontrollers. A trademark dispute between the project's founders between 2014 and 2016 (Arduino LLC versus Arduino SRL) was settled with a reunification under Arduino AG in 2016–2017. In 2025, Qualcomm announced its acquisition of Arduino, with the platform continuing to operate as an independent brand.

Hardware and software

Arduino boards expose analog and digital input/output pins that interface with expansion boards ("shields"), breadboards, sensors, LEDs, motors, and displays. Widely used boards include the Uno (ATmega328P), Nano, Mega, and the ARM-based and IoT families such as the MKR and Nano 33 series, Portenta, and the Uno R4. The Arduino Integrated Development Environment (IDE), a cross-platform application written in Java and later rewritten, compiles C and C++ "sketches" built around setup() and loop() functions and uploads them over USB via a bootloader. Official and community libraries, together with inexpensive clones and derivatives (such as ESP8266/ESP32 boards programmed through the Arduino core), made the platform a standard tool in education, prototyping, art installations, and the maker movement.

Impact

Arduino is credited with democratising embedded electronics: its combination of cheap hardware, gentle learning curve, and open designs underpinned the growth of DIY electronics, interactive art, and STEM teaching worldwide, and influenced later ecosystems including MicroPython boards and the BBC micro:bit.
