El Alto

El Alto spreads across the Altiplano above La Paz

El Alto (Spanish for "The Heights") is a city in Bolivia, located on the Altiplano highlands west of La Paz at an elevation of about 4,150 metres (13,615 feet), making it one of the highest major cities in the world. With a population of close to one million, it is Bolivia's second-largest city.

History

The dry, cold plateau above the canyon of La Paz saw little settlement until the arrival of the railway and the construction of an airfield in the early 20th century. Growth accelerated after the Bolivian National Revolution of 1952, as rural migrants settled on the rim above La Paz. El Alto was formally established as a separate municipality in 1985. Its population is predominantly Aymara, and the city became a centre of indigenous activism; its residents played a key role in the Bolivian gas conflict of 2003.

Geography and transport

El Alto hosts El Alto International Airport, one of the world's highest international airports, and is connected to La Paz by cable car lines (Mi Teleférico) opened in 2014. The city's colourful "cholet" architecture — lavish mansions built by Aymara merchants, designed by architect Freddy Mamani — has become internationally recognised. The vast 16 de Julio market, one of the largest in South America, is held twice a week.
