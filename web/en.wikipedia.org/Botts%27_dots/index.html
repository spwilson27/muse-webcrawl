Botts' dots

Type: Raised pavement markers
Named for: Elbert Dysart Botts
Introduced: 1950s, California
Purpose: Lane delineation

Botts' dots are round, non-reflective raised pavement markers used to mark lanes on highways and streets. Named after Elbert Dysart Botts, the Caltrans engineer who oversaw the research that led to their development, they are most associated with California and other warm-climate states in the United States.

History

Botts developed the markers in the 1950s at the California Division of Highways materials laboratory. Raised markers provided lane guidance that remained visible when painted lines were wet, and gave drivers a tactile rumble and audible thump when crossed. They were widely installed from the 1960s onward.

Use and decline

Botts' dots are typically made of ceramic or plastic and glued to the pavement with epoxy. Because snowploughs would destroy them, they are rare in snowy regions. In recent years California has moved away from them in favour of reflective markers and wider painted stripes, citing cost and the needs of driver-assistance systems.
