Kennet and Avon Canal

The Kennet and Avon Canal is a waterway in southern England, linking the River Thames at Reading with the Bristol Channel at Bristol via the River Kennet and the River Avon. Its total length is about 87 miles (140 km), of which 57 miles are artificial canal.

History

Surveyed by John Rennie, the canal was built between 1794 and 1810. Its most famous feature is the Caen Hill Locks at Devizes, a flight of 29 locks raising the canal 237 feet (72 m). Other notable structures include the Dundas and Avoncliff aqueducts and the Bruce Tunnel. The canal declined with the coming of the Great Western Railway and fell into disrepair, but was restored by volunteers and reopened by Queen Elizabeth II in 1990.

Today

The canal is now a popular leisure waterway managed by the Canal & River Trust, and much of it is designated a Site of Special Scientific Interest for its aquatic wildlife.
