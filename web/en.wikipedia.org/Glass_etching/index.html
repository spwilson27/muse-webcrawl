Glass etching

Glass etching comprises techniques for creating decorative art or lettering on the surface of glass by corroding, abrading or otherwise roughening the surface so that it becomes translucent and frosted in the treated areas.

Techniques

Acid etching uses hydrofluoric acid or etching creams containing fluoride compounds to dissolve the glass surface; areas to remain clear are masked with a resist such as wax or vinyl stencils. Sandblasting (abrasive blasting) propels fine grit, traditionally sand and now usually silicon carbide or aluminium oxide, against the glass through a stencil, allowing deeper carving and shading. Mould etching produces frosted patterns during manufacture. Laser engraving is a modern computer-controlled alternative.

Applications

Etched glass is used for doors, windows, mirrors, drinking vessels, awards and signage, providing decoration and privacy while still transmitting light. Acid-etched and sandblasted glass became especially popular for pub and shop windows in Victorian Britain.
