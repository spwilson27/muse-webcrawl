Delhi is a city and union territory in northern India, containing New Delhi, the national capital. Situated on the Yamuna River, it is among the world's largest metropolitan areas and has been continuously inhabited since at least the sixth century BCE.

History

Tradition links the site to Indraprastha of the Mahabharata epic. Delhi rose to prominence under the Tomara and Chauhan dynasties, then became the capital of the Delhi Sultanate from 1206 and later of the Mughal Empire; the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan built Shahjahanabad (Old Delhi), including the Red Fort and Jama Masjid, in the seventeenth century. After 1857 the British made it their capital in 1911, constructing New Delhi to Edwin Lutyens' design. It became the capital of independent India in 1947 and expanded enormously after Partition.

Geography and life

Delhi has a humid subtropical climate with very hot summers, a monsoon season and cool, foggy winters, and it suffers severe air pollution. Landmarks include Humayun's Tomb, Qutub Minar, the Lotus Temple and India Gate. The Delhi Metro, opened in 2002, is one of the world's largest rapid transit systems.
