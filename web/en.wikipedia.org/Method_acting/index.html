Method acting

Method acting, known informally as the Method, is a range of training and rehearsal techniques that seek to encourage sincere and emotionally expressive performances by fully inhabiting the role of a character. It developed from the "system" of the Russian theatre practitioner Konstantin Stanislavski.

Origins

Stanislavski's system emphasised emotional memory, the "magic if," and the actor's identification with the character's objectives. In the United States his ideas were taken up by the Group Theatre in the 1930s and diverged into the interpretations of Lee Strasberg (who stressed affective memory), Stella Adler (who emphasised imagination and given circumstances after working with Stanislavski in Paris), and Sanford Meisner (who developed repetition exercises and living truthfully under imaginary circumstances).

Practice

Method actors may remain in character offstage, undertake research and physical transformations, and draw on personal experience to evoke emotion. The approach produced celebrated screen performances by actors such as Marlon Brando, James Dean, Robert De Niro and Daniel Day-Lewis.

Criticism

The Method has been criticised for self-indulgence, for encouraging behaviour that disrupts productions, and for psychological risks to performers asked to relive traumatic experiences; many actors and teachers dispute Strasberg's version while still using other parts of Stanislavski's work.
