Pentecost

Pentecost (Shavuot in Jewish tradition) is a Christian feast commemorating the descent of the Holy Spirit upon the Apostles and other followers of Jesus, as described in the Acts of the Apostles (Acts 2). It is celebrated on the seventh Sunday (the fiftieth day, counting inclusively) after Easter, and is often regarded as the "birthday of the Church".

Biblical account

According to Acts 2, the disciples were gathered in Jerusalem for the Jewish festival of Weeks when a sound like rushing wind filled the house, tongues of fire rested on each of them, and they began to speak in other languages. Peter preached to the crowd, and about three thousand people were baptised that day. The event is understood by Christians as the fulfilment of Christ's promise of the Spirit and the reversal of the confusion of tongues at Babel.

Liturgical observance

Pentecost ranks among the great feasts of the liturgical year. In Western churches the liturgical colour is red, symbolising the fire of the Spirit; customs include the release of doves, processions, and in some places the scattering of rose petals. In Britain the following Monday (Whit Monday) was traditionally a holiday. Eastern Orthodox churches celebrate Pentecost together with the feast of the Holy Trinity and hold a service of kneeling prayers. The season after Pentecost, sometimes called Ordinary Time, occupies much of the second half of the church year.

Pentecostalism

The modern Pentecostal movement, beginning in the early 20th century with events such as the Azusa Street Revival of 1906, takes its name from the feast, emphasising baptism in the Holy Spirit evidenced by speaking in tongues. Pentecostal and charismatic Christianity has become one of the fastest-growing segments of world Christianity.
