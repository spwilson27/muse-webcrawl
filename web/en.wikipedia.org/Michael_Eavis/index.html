Michael Eavis CBE (born 17 October 1935) is an English dairy farmer and the founder of the Glastonbury Festival, which takes place at Worthy Farm, Pilton, Somerset, near the village where he was born. He is one of the best-known figures in the British music industry, recognizable by his beard, shorts, and commitment to charitable causes.

Early life

Eavis was brought up in Pilton and educated at Wells Cathedral School. After considering a career in the Merchant Navy and working briefly as a trainee midshipman, he returned to take over the 150-acre Worthy Farm on his father's death in 1958, building it into a dairy operation with a herd of pedigree cows.

Glastonbury Festival

Inspired by an open-air blues festival at the Bath and West Showground, Eavis staged the first Pilton Pop, Blues & Folk Festival in September 1970, charging £1 admission (including free milk from the farm) to an audience of about 1,500; the headliners were Tyrannosaurus Rex. The event grew irregularly through the 1970s and 1980s into the Glastonbury Festival of Contemporary Performing Arts, now attended by over 200,000 people. The festival supports Oxfam, Greenpeace, and WaterAid, donating millions of pounds over its history. Since the 1990s Eavis has increasingly shared management with his daughter Emily Eavis.

Politics and recognition

A Methodist and a longtime Labour Party supporter, Eavis stood as a Labour candidate in Wells in the 1997 general election and later controversially endorsed Jeremy Corbyn. He was appointed CBE in 2007 for services to music. In 2024 he received a knighthood in the New Year Honours for services to music and charity.
