Fengtian clique

The Fengtian clique was one of the major military-political factions of warlord-era China, based in Manchuria (the Northeast) and named after Fengtian province (modern Liaoning). It was led by the former bandit-turned-general Zhang Zuolin, the "Old Marshal", and later by his son Zhang Xueliang.

Rise

Zhang Zuolin consolidated control over the three northeastern provinces with Japanese tolerance and support, building a modern army and administration headquartered in Mukden (Shenyang). The clique fought the Zhili clique in the First Zhili–Fengtian War (1922), which it lost, and the Second Zhili–Fengtian War (1924), after which it exercised great influence in the Beijing government.

Decline

During the Kuomintang's Northern Expedition, Zhang Zuolin was forced to withdraw toward Manchuria and was assassinated by Japanese officers in the Huanggutun incident of June 1928, when his train was bombed. His son Zhang Xueliang succeeded him and in December 1928 declared the Northeast Flag Replacement, nominally pledging allegiance to the Nationalist government in Nanjing, which ended the clique's independent rule and formally reunified China.
