The Ministry of Foreign Affairs of the People's Republic of China is the first-ranked executive department under the State Council of the People's Republic of China, responsible for the country's foreign policy and international relations.

Role

The ministry formulates and implements foreign policy, manages China's diplomatic missions abroad, represents China at the United Nations and in intergovernmental conferences, handles treaty and legal matters, and oversees consular protection of Chinese citizens overseas. Its spokespersons hold regular press briefings widely covered by international media.

History

The ministry was founded in 1949 with Premier Zhou Enlai concurrently serving as its first foreign minister. Veteran diplomats such as Chen Yi and Qian Qichen later led the ministry. Although a State Council body, foreign policy decision-making rests with the Communist Party's Central Foreign Affairs Commission, and the ministry carries out its directives alongside the Party's International Department.
