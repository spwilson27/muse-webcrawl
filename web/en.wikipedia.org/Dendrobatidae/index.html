Dendrobatidae, commonly known as poison dart frogs or poison arrow frogs, is a family of small, often brightly coloured frogs native to the tropical forests of Central and South America. The family contains more than 200 species in around a dozen genera, including Dendrobates, Oophaga, Ranitomeya and Phyllobates.

Description

Most dendrobatids are small, from about 1.5 to 6 cm in length, and many display striking aposematic coloration — brilliant yellows, reds, blues and greens — advertising their toxicity to predators. Their skin contains alkaloid toxins obtained from their diet of mites, ants and other small arthropods; captive-bred frogs raised on different prey are not toxic. The golden poison frog, Phyllobates terribilis, is among the most toxic of all animals, and the toxins of some species were traditionally used by Indigenous peoples to poison blowgun darts.

Behaviour and reproduction

Many species show elaborate parental care: parents transport tadpoles on their backs to small pools of water in bromeliads or tree holes, and in some species mothers return to feed the tadpoles with unfertilised eggs. Several species are popular in the pet trade, where captive breeding is common; some wild populations are threatened by habitat loss and the chytrid fungus.
