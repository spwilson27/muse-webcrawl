Foreign internal defense

Foreign internal defense (FID) is the participation by civilian and military agencies of a government in any of the action programmes taken by another government or other designated organization to free and protect its society from subversion, lawlessness, insurgency, terrorism, and other threats to its security. In United States doctrine, FID is one of the core activities of special operations forces.

Doctrine

Under U.S. joint doctrine (Joint Publication 3-22), FID involves supporting a host nation's internal defense and development strategy. The emphasis is on working "by, with, and through" host-nation forces so that the partner government develops the capacity to secure itself, rather than having foreign forces conduct combat operations directly.

Activities

FID activities include training, advising, and assisting partner security forces; providing equipment and logistics support; intelligence sharing; and civil-military operations. In the United States, such support is coordinated through security cooperation programmes and is typically led in-country by the U.S. embassy country team, with military elements working under the authority of the geographic combatant commander.

History

The concept was formalized during the Cold War era of counterinsurgency, notably during the Vietnam War, and FID missions expanded after 2001 as part of counterterrorism partnerships around the world. U.S. Army Special Forces ("Green Berets") regard FID as their signature mission.
