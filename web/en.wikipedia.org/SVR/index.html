The Foreign Intelligence Service of the Russian Federation (Russian: Служба внешней разведки, Sluzhba vneshney razvedki, abbreviated SVR) is Russia's primary civilian foreign intelligence agency. It is the successor to the First Chief Directorate of the Soviet KGB.

History

When the Soviet Union dissolved in 1991, the KGB's foreign intelligence arm was separated from the domestic security organs and reorganised as the SVR under the direct authority of the President of Russia. Its headquarters are at Yasenevo in southwestern Moscow.

Role

The SVR collects political, economic, scientific and military intelligence abroad, conducts counterintelligence outside Russia, and is frequently discussed alongside the military intelligence agency GRU and the domestic FSB. Its directors have included Yevgeny Primakov and, since 2016, Sergey Naryshkin.
