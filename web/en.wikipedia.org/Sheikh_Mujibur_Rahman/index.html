Sheikh Mujibur Rahman


Sheikh Mujibur Rahman, founding leader of Bangladesh.
Born: 17 March 1920, Tungipara, Bengal Presidency, British India
Died: 15 August 1975 (aged 55), Dhaka, Bangladesh
Political party: Awami League
Offices: President of Bangladesh (1971–72, 1975); Prime Minister of Bangladesh (1972–75)

Sheikh Mujibur Rahman (17 March 1920 – 15 August 1975), popularly known as Bangabandhu ("Friend of Bengal"), was a Bangladeshi revolutionary, statesman and politician who was the founding leader of Bangladesh. He served as the first President of Bangladesh and later as its Prime Minister from 1972 until his assassination in 1975. He is credited as the driving force behind the Bengali nationalist movement and the Bangladesh Liberation War of 1971.

Early life and education

Mujib was born in Tungipara, a village in the Faridpur district of the Bengal Presidency, into a Bengali Muslim family of sheikhs. He studied law and political science at the University of Dhaka and became involved in politics as a student activist in the movement for the creation of Pakistan. He was a co-founder of the Awami League in 1949 and played a prominent role in the Bengali Language Movement of 1952.

Political career in Pakistan

As leader of the Awami League, Mujib articulated the Six Points in 1966, a programme demanding extensive autonomy for East Pakistan that became the charter of Bengali self-rule. He was repeatedly imprisoned by the Pakistani authorities, including during the Agartala Conspiracy Case of 1968. In the 1970 general election, the Awami League won an absolute majority in the National Assembly, but the military regime of Yahya Khan refused to transfer power.

Liberation War and independence

On 7 March 1971 Mujib delivered a famous speech at the Ramna Race Course in Dhaka calling for a struggle for independence. Following the military crackdown of Operation Searchlight on the night of 25–26 March 1971, he proclaimed the independence of Bangladesh; he was arrested and flown to West Pakistan, where he was held during the war. After nine months of war, Bangladesh became independent in December 1971, and Mujib returned to Dhaka on 10 January 1972 to a hero's welcome.

Leadership of Bangladesh

Mujib became Prime Minister in January 1972 and oversaw the adoption of a constitution based on nationalism, socialism, democracy and secularism. His government faced enormous challenges: a war-ravaged economy, famine in 1974, and rising disorder. In January 1975 he introduced the one-party BAKSAL system and became President. On 15 August 1975, Mujib and most of his family were assassinated by a group of army officers in a military coup.

Legacy

Mujib is regarded as the "Father of the Nation" in Bangladesh, and his 7 March speech was recognised by UNESCO as part of the Memory of the World Register. His daughter Sheikh Hasina has served multiple terms as Prime Minister of Bangladesh. His unfinished memoir, The Unfinished Memoirs, was published posthumously in 2012.
