Gdańsk Agreement

Lech Wałęsa signing the Gdańsk Agreement, 31 August 1980.

The Gdańsk Agreement (or Gdańsk Accords) was an accord signed on 31 August 1980 between the government of the Polish People's Republic and the Inter-Enterprise Strike Committee at the Gdańsk Shipyard, represented by Lech Wałęsa. It ended a wave of strikes that had begun on 14 August 1980 after the dismissal of crane operator Anna Walentynowicz.

Terms

The agreement granted workers the right to form independent, self-governing trade unions — an unprecedented concession in a Soviet-bloc state — together with the right to strike, freedom of expression and publication, the release of political prisoners, broadcast of Sunday Mass on state radio, and economic reforms. It was based on the strikers' famous 21 demands, posted on the shipyard gates.

Consequences

Within weeks the independent union Solidarity (Solidarność) was founded and grew to some ten million members. The agreement opened a sixteen-month period of relative liberalisation that ended with the imposition of martial law in December 1981, but it is widely regarded as a decisive step toward the peaceful end of communist rule in Poland in 1989.
