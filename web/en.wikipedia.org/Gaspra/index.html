951 Gaspra is an S-type asteroid in the inner main belt and a member of the Flora family. It was discovered in 1916 by Russian astronomer Grigory Neujmin and named after the Black Sea resort of Gaspra in Crimea.

Galileo flyby

On 29 October 1991 NASA's Galileo spacecraft passed within about 1,600 km of Gaspra on its way to Jupiter, making it the first asteroid to be visited by a spacecraft. Images showed an irregular, cratered body roughly 18 km long.

Surface and composition

Gaspra's spectra indicate an olivine-rich silicate composition. Its surface has craters, grooves and smooth-looking facets consistent with a rubble-and-rock body reshaped by impacts. Its rotation period is about 7.04 hours.

Significance

The encounter demonstrated navigation and imaging techniques later used at Ida and other small bodies, and provided early close-range evidence about asteroid geology and space weathering.
