Scalar field

A scalar field is a function that assigns a single number — a scalar — to every point in space (or spacetime). Unlike a vector field, a scalar field has no direction. Familiar examples include temperature distributions, air pressure in a room, and the height of terrain above sea level.

In mathematics

Scalar fields on open subsets of Euclidean space are studied in multivariable calculus through gradients, line integrals and the Laplacian; the gradient of a scalar field is a vector field pointing in the direction of fastest increase.

In physics

Scalar fields are fundamental in physics. In quantum field theory, spin-0 particles are described by scalar fields; the Higgs field is the only fundamental scalar field in the Standard Model, its excitation being the Higgs boson discovered in 2012. In cosmology, hypothetical scalar fields such as the inflaton are invoked to drive cosmic inflation, and quintessence models use scalar fields to explain dark energy. Pseudoscalar fields change sign under parity inversion; the pion fields of nuclear physics are examples.
