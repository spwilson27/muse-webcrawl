Airbrush

An airbrush is a small, air-operated tool that atomizes and sprays a variety of media, most often paint or ink, but also dye and foundation. Spray painting developed from the airbrush and is considered to employ a type of airbrush.

History

The first airbrush-type device is often credited to Francis Edgar Stanley in 1876, and a workable paint distributor was patented by Abner Peeler in 1879. The modern airbrush was developed in the 1890s by Charles Burdick, an artist who founded the Aerograph company, and by Thayer and Chandler. Airbrushes became widely used for photo retouching, illustration, and commercial art in the twentieth century.

Types

Airbrushes are classified by trigger mechanism: single-action brushes control only airflow, with paint flow preset; double-action brushes let the user control air and paint independently with one trigger. Feed types include gravity feed (paint cup on top), siphon feed (bottle below), and side feed. Paint is mixed with air either internally or externally to produce a fine, controllable spray that can create smooth gradients impossible with a conventional brush.

Uses

Airbrushes are used in illustration, automotive and motorcycle custom painting, scale model finishing, nail art, makeup and body painting, cake decoration, and tanning. The term "airbrushing" also became a metaphor for the retouching and idealization of photographs.
