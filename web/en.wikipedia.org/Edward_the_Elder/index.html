Edward the Elder (c. 874 – 17 July 924) was King of the Anglo-Saxons from 899 until his death. The son of Alfred the Great, he continued his father's work of defending Wessex against the Vikings and, with his sister Æthelflæd, Lady of the Mercians, conquered the southern Danelaw, laying the foundations for the unified English kingdom achieved by his son Æthelstan. The epithet "the Elder" distinguishes him from the later King Edward the Martyr.

Reign

Edward's accession was challenged by his cousin Æthelwold, who allied with the Danes of Northumbria and East Anglia; the revolt ended with Æthelwold's death at the Battle of the Holme in 902. Edward then built a network of fortified burhs across the Midlands, often in tandem with Æthelflæd's parallel line of fortresses in Mercia. Between 909 and 918 he and his sister subdued Danish East Anglia and the Five Boroughs, and after Æthelflæd's death in 918 Edward took direct control of Mercia. By the end of his reign he was recognised as overlord by the kings of Scotland and Strathclyde and the Welsh rulers.

Death and succession

Edward died at Farndon in Cheshire on 17 July 924, shortly after suppressing a revolt at Chester, and was buried in the New Minster at Winchester. He was succeeded briefly by his son Ælfweard in Wessex and more durably by Æthelstan in Mercia and then the whole kingdom; his youngest son by Eadgifu of Kent was Edmund I. Edward married three times and had a large family whose marriages tied Wessex to the ruling houses of continental Europe.
