Acropolis of Athens

Acropolis of Athens
Location: Athens, Greece
Elevation: 156 m above sea level
Principal buildings: Parthenon, Erechtheion, Propylaea, Temple of Athena Nike
UNESCO World Heritage Site (1987)

The Acropolis of Athens (Ancient Greek: Ἀκρόπολις) is an ancient citadel located on a rocky outcrop above the city of Athens, Greece, containing the remains of several ancient buildings of great architectural and historical significance, the most famous being the Parthenon. The word acropolis comes from the Greek words ἄκρον ("highest point, extremity") and πόλις ("city").

History

The site was inhabited from the Neolithic period and served as a fortified centre in Mycenaean times. After the Persians destroyed the earlier temples in 480 BC, Pericles launched an ambitious building programme in the fifth century BC, employing the architects Ictinus, Callicrates and Mnesicles and the sculptor Phidias. The Parthenon (447–432 BC), the Propylaea, the Erechtheion and the Temple of Athena Nike were constructed in this period. The monuments survived remarkably intact until the Parthenon, then used as an Ottoman gunpowder magazine, was catastrophically damaged by a Venetian bombardment in 1687.

Archaeology and restoration

Excavations and restorations have continued since Greek independence in the 19th century. A major conservation programme begun in 1975 has repaired damage from earlier restorations and pollution, and the Acropolis Museum, opened in 2009 at the foot of the rock, displays finds from the site. The Acropolis was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1987 and is Greece's most visited archaeological site.
