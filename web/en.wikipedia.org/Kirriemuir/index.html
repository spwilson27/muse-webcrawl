Kirriemuir


Kirriemuir is a burgh in Angus, Scotland.

Kirriemuir, sometimes called Kirrie or the Wee Red Toon, is a burgh in Angus, Scotland. It lies some 5 miles (8 km) north of the town of Forfar and is known as the birthplace of the writer J. M. Barrie, creator of Peter Pan, and of AC/DC frontman Bon Scott.

History

The town has a long history, with evidence of settlement in the surrounding area reaching back to Pictish times; several carved Pictish stones, known as the Kirriemuir Sculptured Stones, were found in the area. Kirriemuir developed as a market town and a centre for linen weaving, and in the 19th century it became notable for jute production, with mills producing canvas and other coarse fabrics.

The town's nickname, the "Wee Red Toon", derives from the red sandstone of which many of its older buildings are constructed.

People and culture

J. M. Barrie was born in Kirriemuir in 1860; his birthplace is preserved as a museum, and a statue of Peter Pan stands in the town square. The Camera Obscura on Kirrie Hill, given to the town by Barrie, offers panoramic views over the Vale of Strathmore to the Angus Glens. Bon Scott, lead singer of AC/DC, was born in nearby Forfar and raised in Kirriemuir before emigrating to Australia; an annual festival, Bonfest, celebrates his memory.

The town is regarded as a gateway to the Angus Glens, including Glen Clova and Glen Prosen, popular with walkers.
