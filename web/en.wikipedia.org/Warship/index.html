A warship or combatant ship is a ship that is built and primarily intended for naval warfare. Usually they belong to the armed forces of a state. As well as being armed, warships are designed to withstand damage and are usually faster and more manoeuvrable than merchant ships. Unlike a merchant ship, which carries cargo, a warship typically carries only weapons, ammunition and supplies for its crew. Warships usually belong to a navy, but they may also be operated by individuals, cooperatives or corporations, as was common in the era of privateering.

History

In ancient times, oared galleys such as the Greek trireme and Roman warships dominated naval combat, fighting by ramming and boarding. In the medieval period, northern European cogs and Mediterranean galleys shared the seas. The age of sail produced the ship of the line — heavily armed sailing warships that fought in line-of-battle formations — along with frigates used for scouting and commerce raiding. The 19th century brought steam propulsion, shell guns, and armour, culminating in ironclads such as HMS Warrior (1860).

The launch of HMS Dreadnought in 1906 revolutionized battleship design and set off a naval arms race. During the two world wars, new types — aircraft carriers, submarines, destroyers, and landing craft — eclipsed the battleship. The Second World War demonstrated the dominance of carrier aviation, and post-war developments brought guided missiles, nuclear propulsion, and nuclear-powered ballistic-missile submarines.

Modern types

Today's principal warship types include aircraft carriers, floating airbases that are the largest warships afloat; submarines, including attack and ballistic-missile boats; destroyers and cruisers, large multi-mission surface combatants carrying guided missiles; frigates and corvettes, smaller escort and patrol warships; amphibious warfare ships for landing troops; mine warfare vessels; and patrol vessels for coastal duties. Auxiliary ships — supply ships, hospital ships, and tenders — support the fleet but are not classed as warships in the strict sense.

Legal status

Under the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea, a warship is a ship belonging to the armed forces of a state, bearing external marks distinguishing its nationality, under the command of a duly commissioned officer, and manned by a crew under regular armed forces discipline. Warships enjoy sovereign immunity on the high seas.
