Ensign (flag)

An ensign is a flag bearing a national symbol, flown to indicate nationality, typically on ships and other vessels, aircraft and military formations. Maritime ensigns are usually flown from the stern, the most senior position of honour on a ship. Many countries distinguish civil, state and naval (war) ensigns for merchant vessels, government vessels and warships respectively.

British ensigns

The best-known family of ensigns descends from the British system formalised in 1864. The Red Ensign, a red flag with the Union Jack in the canton, is flown by British merchant ships; the Blue Ensign is used by government and naval auxiliary vessels and by ships commanded by Royal Naval Reserve officers; and the White Ensign, bearing a red cross on white with the Union Jack in the canton, is reserved to the Royal Navy. Defaced versions — charged with a colonial or organisational badge in the fly — served as the flags of numerous British colonies and remain the basis of flags such as those of Australia and New Zealand.

Other usages

Some countries fly a naval ensign entirely different from the national flag, Japan's rising-sun naval ensign being a famous example. Air force ensigns, such as the Royal Air Force Ensign, typically place a roundel on a coloured field. In heraldic usage, "ensign" can also mean to charge a flag or shield with a badge, or an armorial achievement itself.
