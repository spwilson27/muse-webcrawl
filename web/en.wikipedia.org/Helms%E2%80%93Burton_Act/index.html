Helms–Burton Act

Cuban Liberty and Democratic Solidarity (LIBERTAD) Act of 1996
Enacted12 March 1996
Signed byPresident Bill Clinton

PurposeStrengthen the US embargo against Cuba

The Helms–Burton Act, formally the Cuban Liberty and Democratic Solidarity (LIBERTAD) Act of 1996, is a United States federal law that strengthened and codified the US embargo against Cuba. Named after its sponsors, Senator Jesse Helms and Representative Dan Burton, it was signed into law by President Bill Clinton on 12 March 1996, in the aftermath of the shootdown of two Brothers to the Rescue aircraft by Cuban forces.

Provisions

The Act has four titles. Title I codified the embargo regulations into statute, so that they cannot be lifted by the president alone while specified conditions remain unmet. Title II sets out conditions for assistance to a transition government in Cuba. Title III created a right for US nationals whose property was confiscated by the Cuban government after 1959 to sue foreign companies "trafficking" in that property in US courts. Title IV provides for the exclusion from the United States of executives of companies that traffic in confiscated property.

Title III suspension and activation

Every president from Clinton onward suspended Title III's right of action in six-month increments, citing foreign-policy concerns, since the provision provoked strong opposition from US allies such as Canada, Mexico and the European Union, which enacted blocking legislation to protect their companies. In 2019 the Trump administration allowed the suspension to lapse, activating Title III for the first time on 2 May 2019; lawsuits were subsequently filed against cruise lines, hotels and other companies using expropriated properties in Cuba.

International reaction

The Act has been widely criticised abroad as an extraterritorial application of US law, and the UN General Assembly's annual resolutions against the embargo regularly cite it. The European Union challenged the law at the World Trade Organization before reaching a political understanding with Washington, and maintains a blocking statute forbidding EU companies from complying with its provisions.
