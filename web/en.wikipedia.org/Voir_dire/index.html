Voir dire

Voir dire (from Old French, "to speak the truth") is a legal phrase referring to the preliminary examination of prospective jurors, witnesses or evidence. Most commonly it denotes the process by which judges and lawyers question members of a jury pool (venire) to identify bias and select an impartial jury.

Jury selection

During jury voir dire, potential jurors may be excused for cause if they cannot be impartial, and each side may exercise a limited number of peremptory challenges to dismiss jurors without stating a reason — subject to constitutional limits, such as the prohibition on excluding jurors because of race established in Batson v. Kentucky (1986). The extent of questioning by attorneys varies by jurisdiction; in some courts the judge conducts most of the examination.

Other uses

The term is also used for a "trial within a trial" in which a judge determines the admissibility of evidence or the competence of a witness outside the jury's presence — for example, whether a confession was voluntary.
