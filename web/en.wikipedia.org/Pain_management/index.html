Pain management
SpecialtyPain medicine
ApproachesPharmacological, interventional, physical, psychological

Pain management is a branch of medicine that applies science and healthcare practices to relieve pain. It encompasses the treatment of acute pain after injury or surgery, chronic pain conditions, and pain from advanced disease such as cancer, drawing on an interdisciplinary team that may include physicians, pharmacists, psychologists, and physical therapists.

Medications

The WHO analgesic ladder, developed for cancer pain, organizes treatment by intensity: non-opioid analgesics such as paracetamol (acetaminophen) and NSAIDs first, then weak opioids, then strong opioids such as morphine and oxycodone. Adjuvant drugs — including certain antidepressants and anticonvulsants — are mainstays for neuropathic pain. The widespread use of opioids, especially in the United States, contributed to an opioid epidemic of addiction and overdose deaths, prompting guidelines that emphasize cautious prescribing and multimodal, opioid-sparing strategies.

Interventional and non-drug approaches

Interventional techniques include epidural steroid injections, nerve blocks, radiofrequency ablation, and implanted spinal cord stimulators. Physical therapy, exercise, acupuncture, and transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (TENS) help many chronic pain patients, while cognitive behavioural therapy and other psychological approaches address the emotional and behavioural dimensions of persistent pain and improve function even when pain itself cannot be eliminated.
