Mattress

A mattress is a large, usually rectangular pad for supporting a lying person. It is designed to be used as a bed, or on a bed frame as part of a bed. Mattresses may consist of a quilted or similar fastened case, usually of heavy cloth, containing materials such as hair, straw, cotton, foam rubber, or a framework of metal springs. Mattresses may also be filled with air or water.

History

Early mattresses consisted of piles of leaves, grass, or straw covered with animal skins; archaeological evidence from South Africa suggests bedding layers tens of thousands of years old. The word mattress derives from the Arabic matrah, a place where something is thrown down, transmitted through Crusader contact with the Levant. Coiled steel springs were patented for mattress use in the nineteenth century by firms such as Simmons and Sealy, and foam and memory foam (developed from NASA research) arrived in the twentieth century.

Types and construction

Common types include innerspring, memory foam, latex, hybrid, air, and waterbed mattresses. Sizes range from crib and twin to king and California king. Comfort is commonly described by firmness, and mattresses are paired with foundations or box springs.

Care and health

Mattresses accumulate dust mites and allergens; protectors, rotation, and periodic replacement (often recommended every 7–10 years) are standard advice.
