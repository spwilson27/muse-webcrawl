Barrow

A barrow is a type of tumulus — a mound of earth or stone raised over a grave or graves. Barrows were constructed across many parts of the world from the Neolithic period onward, and in Europe the term is used especially for the earthen burial mounds of prehistoric Britain and continental Europe, also known in some traditions as kurgans on the Eurasian steppe.

Types

British archaeology classifies barrows by shape and construction. Long barrows, dating mainly to the early Neolithic (roughly 4000–3000 BC), are elongated trapezoidal mounds, often covering timber or stone burial chambers and communal deposits of human remains; famous examples include West Kennet Long Barrow in Wiltshire. Round barrows — bowl, bell, disc, saucer, and pond barrows among other forms — are characteristic of the Early Bronze Age (roughly 2200–1100 BC) and usually cover individual burials, frequently cremated remains placed in urns together with grave goods such as pottery, bronze weapons, and ornaments.

Distribution and significance

Barrows cluster on the chalk downlands of southern England — the landscapes around Stonehenge and Avebury contain some of the densest cemeteries of round barrows in Europe — and comparable mounds occur from Scandinavia and the Danube basin to Japan, where the great keyhole-shaped kofun are monumental examples. Barrows often remained landmarks for millennia: Anglo-Saxon communities reused them for burials, and many survive as scheduled ancient monuments in the modern landscape, their contents (where legally excavated) providing key evidence for prehistoric ritual and social hierarchy.

Excavation history

Antiquarians of the seventeenth to nineteenth centuries — including John Aubrey, William Stukeley, and the Wiltshire excavators William Cunnington and Sir Richard Colt Hoare — opened hundreds of barrows, laying early foundations for archaeological method even as their digging destroyed much context. Modern excavation and non-invasive survey have refined understanding of barrow construction and use.
