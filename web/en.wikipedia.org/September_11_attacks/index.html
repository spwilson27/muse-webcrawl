September 11 attacks

The World Trade Center in New York City, the principal target of the attacks.

The September 11 attacks, commonly known as 9/11, were four coordinated Islamist suicide terrorist attacks carried out by al-Qaeda against the United States on Tuesday, September 11, 2001. Nineteen terrorists hijacked four commercial airliners scheduled to travel from the East Coast to California. Two aircraft were flown into the Twin Towers of the World Trade Center in New York City, one into the Pentagon in Arlington County, Virginia, and the fourth, United Airlines Flight 93, crashed in a field near Shanksville, Pennsylvania, after passengers resisted the hijackers.

The attacks

American Airlines Flight 11 struck the North Tower at 8:46 a.m., and United Airlines Flight 175 struck the South Tower at 9:03 a.m. Both towers collapsed within two hours from fire-induced structural failure. American Airlines Flight 77 struck the Pentagon at 9:37 a.m. United Flight 93, believed to be headed for the U.S. Capitol or the White House, crashed at 10:03 a.m. The attacks killed 2,977 victims, including 343 firefighters and 72 law-enforcement officers, and remain the deadliest terrorist attack in history.

Background

Al-Qaeda, led by Osama bin Laden, had declared war on the United States in fatwas issued in 1996 and 1998, citing U.S. support for Israel, the presence of U.S. troops in Saudi Arabia, and sanctions against Iraq. The plot was principally organised by Khalid Sheikh Mohammed. Al-Qaeda had previously bombed the World Trade Center in 1993, U.S. embassies in East Africa in 1998, and the USS Cole in 2000.

Aftermath

The United States responded by launching the war on terror, invading Afghanistan in October 2001 to depose the Taliban regime that harboured al-Qaeda, and enacting the Patriot Act. Bin Laden eluded capture for nearly a decade before being killed by U.S. special forces in Abbottabad, Pakistan, in May 2011. The Department of Homeland Security and the Transportation Security Administration were created, aviation security was transformed worldwide, and the 9/11 Commission investigated intelligence failures preceding the attacks.

Memorials

The National September 11 Memorial & Museum opened on the World Trade Center site in 2011–2014, featuring two reflecting pools in the footprints of the Twin Towers. Memorials also stand at the Pentagon and at the Flight 93 National Memorial in Pennsylvania. The anniversary is observed in the U.S. as Patriot Day.
