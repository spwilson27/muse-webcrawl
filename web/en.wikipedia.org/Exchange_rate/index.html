Exchange rate

In finance, an exchange rate is the rate at which one currency will be exchanged for another currency. It is also regarded as the value of one country's currency in relation to another currency. Exchange rates are determined in the foreign exchange market, and can be quoted as the domestic currency price of a unit of foreign currency or the reverse.

Regimes

Under a floating exchange rate regime, a currency's value is set by supply and demand on the market. Under a fixed exchange rate (or peg), the government or central bank ties the currency's value to another currency, a basket of currencies, or a commodity such as gold, and intervenes to maintain it. Many countries use managed floats between these extremes.

Factors

Exchange rates are influenced by interest rates, inflation, trade balances, political stability, and market speculation. A currency that appreciates makes a country's exports more expensive and imports cheaper; depreciation has the opposite effect. Changes in rates affect international trade, investment, and the cost of foreign debt.

Quotation

Rates are quoted in pairs such as EUR/USD. Retail travellers typically buy foreign currency at rates less favourable than interbank rates, the difference being the dealer's margin.
