Restriction of Hazardous Substances Directive (RoHS)

The Restriction of Hazardous Substances Directive 2002/95/EC, commonly shortened to RoHS, is a European Union directive restricting the use of specific hazardous materials found in electrical and electronic products. All applicable products placed on the EU market since 1 July 2006 must pass RoHS compliance. It is closely linked with the Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment Directive (WEEE).

Restricted substances

The original directive restricted six substances: lead, mercury, cadmium, hexavalent chromium, polybrominated biphenyls (PBB) and polybrominated diphenyl ethers (PBDE), each generally limited to 0.1% by weight (0.01% for cadmium) in homogeneous materials. The recast directive, RoHS 2 (2011/65/EU), added CE marking requirements, and Delegated Directive (EU) 2015/863 added four phthalates (DEHP, BBP, DBP and DIBP), bringing the list to ten substances.

Impact

RoHS drove the worldwide electronics industry toward lead-free solder and away from cadmium plating and certain flame retardants. Several other jurisdictions, including China, California, South Korea and the United Kingdom (after Brexit), have enacted similar rules. Some applications — such as certain medical devices, military equipment and lead in specific alloys — are covered by exemptions reviewed periodically.
