Slag

Slag is a by-product of a number of pyrometallurgical processes, including the smelting of ores and the refining of metals. It consists mainly of a molten mixture of silicates, metal oxides and other materials that floats on the surface of molten metal in a furnace, from which it can be separated and removed. Historically, slag was treated as waste and deposited in large heaps, but modern metallurgy treats it as a valuable secondary raw material used in cement, road construction and agriculture.

Formation and composition

In a blast furnace, iron ore, coke and a flux such as limestone are charged together. The flux combines with impurities in the ore — chiefly silica and alumina — to form a liquid slag of calcium and magnesium silicates that floats on the denser molten iron and is tapped off separately. In steelmaking, slags also capture phosphorus and sulfur removed from the melt. Slag composition is controlled carefully, because its basicity, viscosity and melting behavior govern furnace operation and metal quality.

Uses

Ground granulated blast-furnace slag is widely used as a supplementary cementitious material, improving the durability and reducing the carbon footprint of concrete. Air-cooled slag is crushed for use as aggregate in asphalt and road base, as railway ballast and in mineral wool insulation. Steel slag can be used as a soil amendment supplying calcium and silicon, and some slags are processed to recover residual metals. Slag heaps from historical smelting are also studied by archaeologists as evidence of ancient metallurgy, and in some regions slag has been reprocessed for its vanadium, titanium or rare-earth content.

Environmental considerations

While slag reuse is generally beneficial, stockpiled or landfilled slag can leach heavy metals and alkaline compounds into water, and weathering of sulfide-bearing slags may contribute to drainage pollution. Regulatory practice therefore favors beneficial reuse under controlled conditions, and modern smelters typically design slag output for specific downstream markets rather than disposal.
