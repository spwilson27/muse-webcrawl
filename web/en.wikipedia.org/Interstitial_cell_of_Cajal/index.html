Interstitial cell of Cajal

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Interstitial cells of Cajal (ICC) are specialised cells found within the muscle layers of the gastrointestinal tract. They act as the pacemaker cells of the gut, generating the rhythmic electrical slow waves that organise peristalsis, and they mediate inputs from the enteric nervous system to smooth muscle. They are named after the Spanish histologist Santiago Ramón y Cajal, who first described them in the late nineteenth century using silver staining techniques.

Structure and distribution

ICCs form networks between and within the circular and longitudinal muscle layers. Distinct subtypes are recognised by location, including ICC of the myenteric plexus region (ICC-MY), which generate slow waves, and intramuscular ICC (ICC-IM), which are closely associated with nerve varicosities and participate in neurotransmission. ICCs express the receptor tyrosine kinase KIT (CD117), which is essential for their development and maintenance, and signalling through KIT helps identify them in tissue sections.

Function

Pacemaker ICCs generate spontaneous electrical oscillations — slow waves — driven substantially by calcium-activated chloride channels (ANO1/TMEM16A). The slow waves spread to smooth muscle cells through gap junctions, setting the maximum frequency of contractions in each region of the gut (about three per minute in the stomach and around twelve per minute in the duodenum). ICCs thereby determine the rhythm and propagation of gastrointestinal motility, though actual contraction also depends on neural and hormonal inputs reaching threshold.

Clinical significance

Loss or dysfunction of ICCs has been reported in motility disorders including gastroparesis, chronic intestinal pseudo-obstruction and some forms of constipation. Gastrointestinal stromal tumours (GISTs), the most common mesenchymal tumours of the gut, are thought to arise from ICCs or their precursors; most GISTs carry activating mutations in KIT or PDGFRA and typically respond to the tyrosine-kinase inhibitor imatinib.
