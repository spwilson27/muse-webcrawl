Deferred prosecution agreement

A deferred prosecution agreement (DPA) is a voluntary agreement between a prosecutor and an organisation — usually a company — accused of criminal wrongdoing. The organisation agrees to fulfil specified requirements, such as paying a financial penalty, compensating victims, cooperating with investigations and reforming its compliance procedures; in return, prosecution is deferred and, if the terms are met, the charges are dropped at the end of the term.

Use in the United States and United Kingdom

DPAs have been used by the US Department of Justice since the 1990s, especially in Foreign Corrupt Practices Act cases, and are typically accompanied by a detailed statement of facts and sometimes an independent compliance monitor. The United Kingdom introduced DPAs in 2014 under the Crime and Courts Act 2013, with judicial approval required; early cases involved Standard Bank and Rolls-Royce, the latter agreeing to pay over £497 million.

Debate

Supporters argue DPAs secure penalties and reforms without the collateral damage of convicting a company, which can destroy it and harm innocent employees. Critics contend they let large corporations and executives avoid convictions that individuals could not escape, and that fines are absorbed as a cost of business.
