Maritime lien

A vessel under arrest in port following enforcement of a lien (image placeholder).

A maritime lien is a privileged claim against a vessel (or other maritime property) that arises by operation of law in favour of a person who has provided services or suffered loss in connection with the ship, such as crew members owed wages, salvors, repairers, suppliers of necessaries, or victims of a collision.

Characteristics

Unlike ordinary liens, a maritime lien attaches to the vessel itself from the moment the claim arises, requires no registration or possession, follows the ship into the hands of new owners, and is enforced by an admiralty action in rem, typically by arresting the vessel. Claims rank by priority categories — seamen's wages and salvage usually rank very high — and liens can be extinguished by payment, lapse of time (laches), or judicial sale of the vessel, which transfers the claims to the sale proceeds.

International law

The scope and priority of maritime liens are partly harmonised by international conventions of 1926, 1967 and 1993, though states differ on which claims qualify, notably for necessaries supplied to a ship.
