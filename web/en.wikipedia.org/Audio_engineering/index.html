Audio engineering

Audio engineering, also known as sound engineering, is a branch of engineering concerned with the recording, manipulation, mixing, reproduction and reinforcement of sound. An audio engineer works with the technical aspects of music, film, radio, television, video games and live events, operating equipment such as mixing consoles, microphones, digital audio workstations and loudspeakers.

Areas of practice

The discipline encompasses several specialisations. Recording engineers capture performances in studios; mix engineers balance and process recorded tracks; mastering engineers prepare final mixes for distribution. Live sound engineers manage front-of-house and monitor mixes at concerts. Broadcast engineers handle audio for radio and television, while engineers in film and games work on dialogue, sound effects, Foley and interactive audio.

Science and technology

Audio engineering draws on acoustics, psychoacoustics, electronics and signal processing. Practitioners study how sound propagates in spaces, how microphones transduce acoustic energy, and how digital systems sample, quantise and process audio signals. Professional bodies such as the Audio Engineering Society (AES), founded in 1948, publish standards and research for the field.

Education

Audio engineering is taught at universities, music schools and specialist institutes, combining electrical engineering and physics with practical training in production. Historically, many engineers entered through studio apprenticeships, assisting senior engineers before progressing to independent work.
