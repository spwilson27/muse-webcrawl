Nan Madol is an archaeological site adjacent to the eastern shore of the island of Pohnpei in the Federated States of Micronesia. It was the capital of the Saudeleur dynasty until about 1628 — a city built on a series of small artificial islands linked by a network of canals, often called the "Venice of the Pacific".

Construction

The complex covers about 200 acres and consists of some 92 artificial islets built of stacked basalt and coral boulders over the tidal flats. Its walls — at Nan Douwas rising up to 8 metres — were assembled from naturally columnar basalt logs, some weighing many tonnes, transported from quarries elsewhere on Pohnpei by raft. The site served as the ceremonial and political centre of the Saudeleur rulers, housing priests, nobles, and the royal tombs.

Decline and heritage

After the overthrow of the Saudeleurs by Isokelekel, the city was gradually abandoned. In 2016 UNESCO listed Nan Madol as a World Heritage Site, simultaneously placing it on the List of World Heritage in Danger because of siltation and vegetation threatening the ruins.
