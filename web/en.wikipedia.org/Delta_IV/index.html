Delta IV

Delta IV was a group of five expendable launch systems in the Delta rocket family, developed by Boeing and operated by United Launch Alliance (ULA). It was introduced in 2002 as part of the United States Air Force's Evolved Expendable Launch Vehicle (EELV) program, and flew its final mission in April 2024.

Design

All Delta IV variants were built around the Common Booster Core (CBC), powered by a single RS-68 engine burning liquid hydrogen and liquid oxygen. The Medium configurations added GEM-60 solid rocket motors and used either a four- or five-metre payload fairing; the upper stage was the Delta Cryogenic Second Stage, powered by an RL10 engine. The Delta IV Heavy combined three CBCs and could lift about 28,800 kg to low Earth orbit, making it for a time the most capable operational US launcher.

Notable missions

Delta IV launches carried GPS and military communications satellites, the NRO reconnaissance payloads, and NASA missions including the first flight test of the Orion spacecraft (Exploration Flight Test-1, December 2014) and the Parker Solar Probe in 2018. Launches took place from Cape Canaveral Space Force Station in Florida and Vandenberg Space Force Base in California.

Retirement

ULA retired the Delta IV in favour of the Vulcan Centaur. The final Delta IV Heavy flight, carrying the NROL-70 mission, launched on 9 April 2024, ending the Delta family's six-decade service.
