Trajan's Column

Trajan's Column (Italian: Colonna Traiana; Latin: Columna Traiani) is a Roman triumphal column in Rome, Italy, that commemorates Roman emperor Trajan's victory in the Dacian Wars. Completed in AD 113, it stands in Trajan's Forum, north of the Roman Forum, and is famous for its spiral bas relief depicting the wars in narrative detail.

LocationTrajan's Forum, Rome
CompletedAD 113
Heightc. 30 m (38 m including pedestal)
MaterialLuna (Carrara) marble
DesignerAttributed to Apollodorus of Damascus

Description

The freestanding column is composed of stacked marble drums hollowed out to contain a spiral staircase of 185 steps lit by narrow slits. Around the shaft winds a continuous sculpted frieze about 190 metres long, showing roughly 2,500 figures in scenes of the two Dacian campaigns of 101–102 and 105–106: marches, bridge-building, sieges, speeches, and battles. A dedicatory inscription on the pedestal records that the Senate and people of Rome erected it, noting that the column's height marked the depth of earth removed for the forum.

History

Trajan's ashes were placed in a chamber in the base after his death in 117. Originally a gilded statue of the emperor crowned the column; in 1587 Pope Sixtus V replaced it with the bronze statue of Saint Peter that still stands there. The relief was once painted, and its details were likely more legible to ancient viewers than today.

Legacy

The column is a principal visual source for the Roman army and Dacian warfare, and its spiral narrative influenced later monuments including the Column of Marcus Aurelius and Napoleon's Vendôme Column in Paris.
