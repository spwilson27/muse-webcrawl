Service Employees International Union

SEIU members, identifiable by the union's purple colours, at a demonstration.

The Service Employees International Union (SEIU) is a labour union representing almost two million workers in the United States, Canada and Puerto Rico. Its members work mainly in three sectors: health care, including hospital, nursing-home and home-care workers; public services, including local and state government employees; and property services, including janitors, security officers and food-service workers.

History

SEIU was founded in Chicago in 1921 as the Building Service Employees Union, organising janitors, elevator operators and window cleaners who were largely immigrants. It grew slowly for decades before expanding rapidly from the 1960s into public-sector and hospital workers. Under presidents George Hardy, John Sweeney and Andy Stern, the union pursued aggressive organising campaigns, including the Justice for Janitors movement that began in the 1980s and won contracts for low-wage building cleaners in major cities. In 2005 SEIU was a leading member of the Change to Win federation that split from the AFL–CIO over strategy; it later rejoined the AFL–CIO in the 2020s.

Activities

SEIU is known for corporate campaigns that pressure employers through public, political and consumer action as well as strikes, and for organising workers traditionally considered hard to unionise, such as home-care providers and adjunct faculty. Its Fight for $15 campaign, launched with fast-food workers in 2012, helped popularise demands for a fifteen-dollar minimum wage and influenced wage legislation in several states and cities. The union is also a major participant in electoral politics, endorsing candidates and campaigning on health-care funding, immigration and labour law reform.

Organisation

The union is composed of local unions grouped into divisions by industry, coordinated by an international executive board. Its headquarters is in Washington, D.C., and its official colour is purple, giving rise to the nickname "the purple army" for its mobilised membership.
