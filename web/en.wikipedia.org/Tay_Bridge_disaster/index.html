Tay Bridge disaster

The Tay Bridge disaster occurred on the evening of 28 December 1879, when the central spans of the Tay Rail Bridge in Scotland collapsed during a violent storm as a train was crossing, killing everyone aboard — an estimated 75 people.

Tay Bridge disaster
Date: 28 December 1879
Location: Firth of Tay, Dundee, Scotland
Deaths: approx. 75

Causes and aftermath

The bridge, designed by Sir Thomas Bouch and opened in 1878, was the longest in the world at the time. The Court of Inquiry found the collapse resulted from design, construction and maintenance failures: the cast-iron piers and their cross-bracing could not withstand the wind loading. Bouch's reputation was ruined and he died within a year. The disaster prompted stricter design standards, including explicit wind-pressure requirements used for the Forth Bridge. William McGonagall commemorated the event in his famously poor poem "The Tay Bridge Disaster". A replacement bridge opened in 1887.
