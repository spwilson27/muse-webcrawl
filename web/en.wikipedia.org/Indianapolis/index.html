Indianapolis

Indianapolis, colloquially known as Indy, is the capital and most populous city of the U.S. state of Indiana and the county seat of Marion County. Located in central Indiana along the White River, it had a population of about 880,000 at the 2020 census, with a metropolitan area of over two million. Founded in 1821 as a planned state capital, Indianapolis occupies a site near the geographic centre of the state.

History

The site of Indianapolis was selected in 1820 as Indiana's new capital, replacing Corydon, because of its central location. Surveyor Alexander Ralston, who had helped lay out Washington, D.C., designed a mile-square plat centred on a circle — now Monument Circle. The city grew slowly until the arrival of railroads in the 1840s, after which it became a major rail and manufacturing hub, earning the nickname "Crossroads of America". In 1970, city and county governments were consolidated under the "Unigov" system.

Sport

Indianapolis is famous worldwide as the home of the Indianapolis 500, the world's largest single-day sporting event, held since 1911 at the Indianapolis Motor Speedway in the enclave town of Speedway. The city also hosts the NFL's Indianapolis Colts at Lucas Oil Stadium and the NBA's Indiana Pacers at Gainbridge Fieldhouse, and it has hosted Super Bowl XLVI, the NCAA Final Four many times, and the 2024 U.S. Olympic swimming trials.

Economy

The economy rests on manufacturing, logistics and distribution, life sciences — pharmaceutical giant Eli Lilly and Company is headquartered there — insurance, and convention tourism. The Indiana Convention Center and a compact, walkable downtown with an extensive skywalk and cultural trail system make it a frequent host of national events.
