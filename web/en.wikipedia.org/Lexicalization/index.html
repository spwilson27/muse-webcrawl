Lexicalization

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In linguistics, lexicalization is the process of adding words, set phrases, or word patterns to a language's lexicon. The term is used in several related senses in different subfields of linguistics.

Senses of the term

Word formation and idiomatization

In morphology and historical linguistics, lexicalization refers to the process by which a complex expression — a phrase or a derived/compound form built by productive rules — becomes a single lexical unit whose meaning is no longer fully predictable from its parts. For example, English forget-me-not began as a phrase but now functions as a single noun naming a flower; similarly blackbird denotes a specific species rather than any bird that is black. In this sense lexicalization is closely related to idiomatization and to the loss of internal transparency over time.

Semantic typology

In the typological work of Leonard Talmy, lexicalization describes the systematic ways in which languages encode semantic components (such as motion, path, manner, figure, and ground) in their lexical items. Languages differ in their characteristic lexicalization patterns: "satellite-framed" languages like English typically encode manner in the verb and path in a particle ("run out"), whereas "verb-framed" languages like Spanish typically encode path in the verb and manner in an adjunct ("salir corriendo", literally "exit running").

Grammaticalization counterpart

Lexicalization is sometimes presented as the counterpart of grammaticalization: where grammaticalization turns lexical items into grammatical markers, lexicalization can turn grammatical material or multi-word constructions into contentful lexical items, as when the suffix-like element in -ism becomes the independent noun ism. Some scholars, such as Brinton and Traugott, treat lexicalization as a distinct change process involving fusion and loss of compositionality.

Examples

  • holiday from "holy day" — the compound's meaning narrowed to any day of rest or celebration.
  • nevertheless, notwithstanding — phrases fused into single words.
  • handkerchief — historically a compound whose structure is no longer transparent to most speakers.
