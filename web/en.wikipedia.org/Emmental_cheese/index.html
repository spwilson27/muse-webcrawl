Emmental, Emmentaler, or Emmenthal is a yellow, medium-hard cheese that originated in Emmen, Switzerland. It is classified as a Swiss-type cheese known for its distinctive holes, or "eyes", formed by carbon dioxide released by bacteria during ripening.

Production

Emmental is made from unpasteurised cow's milk in large wheels weighing around 75–90 kilograms. Three types of bacteria are important: Streptococcus thermophilus, Lactobacillus helveticus, and Propionibacterium freudenreichii. The propionibacteria consume lactic acid and release carbon dioxide gas, which forms bubbles that become the characteristic eyes. Wheels are aged in warm cellars to develop holes, then in cooler storage to mature.

Flavour and use

Emmental has a savoury but mild, slightly nutty and buttery taste, and melts well. It is a classic ingredient in fondue (with Gruyère), croque-monsieur, and gratins. Outside Switzerland, similar "Swiss cheese" is produced in France, Germany, Finland and the United States; the name "Emmentaler" is protected within the EU, and authentic Swiss Emmentaler AOP carries a protected designation of origin.

History

Cheese-making in the Emme valley is documented from the 13th century, and Emmental was first mentioned by name in 1542. Its large wheel format was suited to transport down the Emme river for export.
