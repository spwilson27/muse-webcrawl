Asian monsoon

The Asian monsoon is a large-scale monsoon system affecting the Indian subcontinent, Southeast Asia, and East Asia. It comprises two main components: the South Asian (Indian) monsoon, driven by the seasonal heating of the landmass and the Tibetan Plateau, and the East Asian monsoon, which brings moist air from the Pacific and South China Sea.

Mechanism

During summer, intense heating over Asia creates low pressure that draws moist oceanic air inland, while the Intertropical Convergence Zone shifts northward. The onset of the southwest monsoon over India typically occurs around 1 June over Kerala. In winter the pattern reverses, producing the dry northeast monsoon, which nevertheless brings rain to southeastern India and Sri Lanka.

Effects

The monsoon supplies the majority of annual rainfall for agriculture in India, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Thailand, Vietnam, southern China, Korea, and Japan. Its variability — influenced by El Niño–Southern Oscillation and the Indian Ocean Dipole — has major consequences for harvests, flooding, and water resources for well over a billion people.

Sub-systems

The East Asian monsoon advances in stages marked by the baiu/meiyu rainy season in China and Japan, while the South Asian system is organised into active and break spells governed by intraseasonal oscillations.
