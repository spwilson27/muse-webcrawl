Trinity House


Trinity House, Tower Hill, London

The Corporation of Trinity House of Deptford Strond, commonly known as Trinity House, is the official authority for lighthouses in England, Wales, the Channel Islands, and Gibraltar. It is also responsible for other aids to navigation such as lightvessels, buoys, and maritime radio and satellite communication systems.

History

Trinity House received a royal charter from Henry VIII in 1514, growing out of a guild of mariners based at Deptford. Over the following centuries it acquired responsibility for licensing pilots and erecting lighthouses, including many built by celebrated engineers of the lighthouse Stevensons and James Walker. Its headquarters, on Tower Hill in London, was designed by Samuel Wyatt and completed in 1796.

Modern role

Today Trinity House maintains around 65 lighthouses and thousands of buoys and beacons, funded by light dues levied on commercial shipping. It is governed by a board including Elder Brethren, historically drawn from senior naval and merchant officers; the corporation also acts as a deep-sea pilotage authority and a maritime charity supporting retired seafarers.
