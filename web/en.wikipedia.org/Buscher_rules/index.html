Buscher rules

Buscher rules
Concept in string theory
Named after: Theo Buscher
Relates to: T-duality, supergravity backgrounds

In string theory, the Buscher rules are a set of transformation rules that relate a supergravity background possessing an isometry to its T-dual background. They were derived by Theo Buscher in 1987–88 and provide the explicit supergravity realization of T-duality.

Content

Given a background with metric, antisymmetric B-field and dilaton that is independent of one coordinate, the rules give the dual fields: the component of the metric along the isometry is inverted, mixed metric and B-field components are exchanged, and the dilaton is shifted by a term involving the logarithm of the metric component. In compact form, a circle of radius R maps to one of radius α′/R.

Significance

The Buscher rules underlie the understanding of T-duality as a symmetry of string theory, mapping type IIA string theory on a circle to type IIB and vice versa. Generalizations handle non-abelian and Poisson–Lie T-duality, and the rules are used throughout the study of string compactifications and dual geometries.
