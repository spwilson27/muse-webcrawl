Kofi Annan

Kofi Atta Annan (8 April 1938 – 18 August 2018) was a Ghanaian diplomat who served as the seventh Secretary-General of the United Nations from 1997 to 2006. He and the UN were jointly awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in 2001.

Born8 April 1938, Kumasi, Gold Coast (Ghana)
Died18 August 2018, Bern, Switzerland
OfficeSecretary-General of the United Nations, 1997–2006

Secretary-General

Annan oversaw UN reform, launched the Global Compact with business, and championed the Millennium Development Goals. His tenure included the controversies of the Iraq War, which he called illegal, and the Oil-for-Food scandal. After leaving office he founded the Kofi Annan Foundation and served as UN–Arab League envoy for Syria in 2012.
