Divination

Divination (from Latin divinare, "to foresee, to foretell, to predict, to prophesy") is the attempt to gain insight into a question or situation by way of an occultic, standardized process or ritual. Diviners ascertain their interpretations of how a querent should proceed by reading signs, events, or omens, or through an alleged contact with a supernatural agency.

History

Divination is attested in virtually every ancient culture. In China, oracle bones inscribed with questions were heated and cracked under the Shang dynasty, producing some of the earliest Chinese writing. Mesopotamian priests practised extispicy, reading the livers of sacrificed animals, and compiled elaborate omen compendia. In ancient Greece, oracles such as that of Apollo at Delphi delivered pronouncements sought by cities and individuals; in Rome, augurs interpreted the flight of birds and haruspices examined entrails, and divination was integrated into state decision-making.

Methods

  • Astrology — interpreting the positions of celestial bodies.
  • Cleromancy — casting lots, dice, bones or yarrow stalks, as in the I Ching.
  • Augury — observing birds and other natural phenomena.
  • Haruspicy and extispicy — examining animal entrails.
  • Oneiromancy — interpreting dreams.
  • Cartomancy — reading cards, including tarot.
  • Palmistry, scrying, dowsing and geomancy, among many others catalogued since antiquity.

Attitudes and analysis

Major religious traditions have varied in their stance: biblical law prohibited several forms of divination, yet lots were also cast with approval in the Hebrew Bible, and lot-casting appears in early Christian practice. Islamic jurisprudence generally condemns divination while permitting the istikhara prayer for guidance. Anthropologists interpret divination as a social institution that can randomize decisions, legitimate authority, and allocate responsibility. Psychologists and sceptics attribute its apparent successes to cold reading, confirmation bias and the Barnum effect; scientific tests of astrology and other methods have found no predictive power beyond chance.

Modern practice

Divination remains widely practised: astrology columns and apps reach mass audiences, tarot has enjoyed renewed popularity, and Ifá divination of the Yoruba people is inscribed on UNESCO's list of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
