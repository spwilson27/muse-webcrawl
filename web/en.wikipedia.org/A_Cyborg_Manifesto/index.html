A Cyborg Manifesto

A Cyborg Manifesto, full title "A Cyborg Manifesto: Science, Technology, and Socialist-Feminism in the Late Twentieth Century", is an essay by the American scholar Donna Haraway, first published in Socialist Review in 1985 and republished in her book Simians, Cyborgs, and Women (1991). It is a foundational text of postmodern and cyborg feminism.

Argument

Haraway proposes the figure of the cyborg – a hybrid of machine and organism – as an ironic political myth for feminism. She argues that late twentieth-century technologies have eroded three boundaries: between human and animal, between organism and machine, and between the physical and non-physical. Because the cyborg has no origin story in an imagined state of natural innocence, cyborg politics can reject essentialist identities and instead build coalitions based on affinity rather than shared identity.

The essay criticizes strands of radical feminism that, in Haraway's view, idealized an organic female essence, and it analyzes the "informatics of domination", the new global system of power organized through communications technology, microelectronics, and the feminized labor of the integrated circuit.

Influence

The manifesto became a touchstone for science and technology studies, posthumanism, and cyberfeminism, and its cyborg figure has been widely taken up, debated, and critiqued in scholarship and art.
