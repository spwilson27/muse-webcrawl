Cotangent space

In differential geometry, the cotangent space at a point of a smooth manifold is the dual vector space of the tangent space at that point. Its elements are called cotangent vectors or covectors, and can be thought of as linear functionals acting on tangent vectors. The disjoint union of the cotangent spaces over all points of the manifold forms the cotangent bundle.

Definition

Let M be a smooth manifold and p a point of M. If TpM denotes the tangent space at p, the cotangent space T*pM is its dual space, consisting of all linear maps from TpM to the real numbers. In coordinates, if (x1, …, xn) are local coordinates around p, the differentials dx1, …, dxn form a basis of the cotangent space, dual to the tangent basis of partial derivatives.

Differentials of functions

For a smooth function f on M, its differential df at p is a cotangent vector: it maps a tangent vector (a derivation) to the directional derivative of f. Cotangent vectors thus encode the linear part of a function's behaviour at a point, and their level sets picture them as stacks of parallel hyperplanes.

Applications

Cotangent bundles carry a natural symplectic structure, making them the standard setting for Hamiltonian mechanics, where position coordinates and conjugate momenta together fill the cotangent bundle of configuration space. Pullbacks of covectors under smooth maps, differential forms, and the exterior derivative all build on the cotangent space.
