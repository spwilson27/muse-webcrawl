Michael Caine

Sir Michael Caine (born Maurice Joseph Micklewhite; 14 March 1933) is a retired English actor. Known for his distinctive Cockney accent, he has appeared in more than 130 films over a career spanning seven decades and is regarded as one of the most iconic British film stars.

Career

Caine rose to prominence in the 1960s with Zulu (1964), The Ipcress File (1965), Alfie (1966), The Italian Job (1969) and Battle of Britain (1969). Later acclaimed roles include Get Carter (1971), Sleuth (1972), The Man Who Would Be King (1975), Educating Rita (1983), and The Cider House Rules (1999). He won two Academy Awards for Best Supporting Actor, for Hannah and Her Sisters (1986) and The Cider House Rules. A younger generation discovered him as Alfred Pennyworth in Christopher Nolan's Dark Knight trilogy and in films such as Inception and Interstellar. He announced his retirement from acting in 2023, following The Great Escaper.

Personal life and honours

Born in Bermondsey, London, Caine was knighted in 2000. He has been married to Shakira Baksh since 1973. His catchphrases — "Not a lot of people know that" and "You were only supposed to blow the bloody doors off!" — have entered British popular culture. He is also the author of several memoirs, beginning with What's It All About? (1992).
