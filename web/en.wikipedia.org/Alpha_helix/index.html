Alpha helix

The alpha helix (α-helix) is a common secondary structure in proteins: a right-handed helix in which each backbone N–H hydrogen bonds to the C=O four residues earlier, with 3.6 residues per turn and a rise of 5.4 Å per turn.

Alpha helix
Residues per turn: 3.6
H-bond: i to i+4
Proposed: Linus Pauling, 1951

Properties

Side chains of the amino acids project outward. Alanine favours helix formation; proline breaks helices. Pauling, Corey and Branson proposed the structure in 1951, confirmed by myoglobin crystallography.

Occurrence

Abundant in globular proteins, keratins, and transmembrane helices crossing lipid bilayers (~20 hydrophobic residues). Contrasts with the extended beta sheet.
