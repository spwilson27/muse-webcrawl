Jahiliyyah
Arabicجاهلية
Literal meaning"Ignorance"
Refers toPre-Islamic Arabia, before c. 610 CE

Jahiliyyah (Arabic: جاهلية, jāhiliyya, "ignorance") is an Islamic term referring to the religious and social condition of Arabia before the revelation of Islam to the Prophet Muhammad in the early 7th century CE. Derived from the root j-h-l ("to be ignorant or foolish"), it denotes in the Quran the state of those who lived without divine guidance.

Historical Arabia

Pre-Islamic Arabia was religiously and politically fragmented. Most Arabs practised polytheism, venerating deities such as al-Lat, al-Uzza, and Manat alongside a high god, Allah; the Kaaba in Mecca housed numerous idols and served as a pilgrimage sanctuary. Jewish tribes were established in oases such as Yathrib (Medina) and Khaybar, Christians were present especially in the south (Najran) and in the buffer kingdoms aligned with Byzantium and Persia, and indigenous monotheists known as hanifs are mentioned in Islamic tradition. Society was organised by tribe and clan, governed by custom (sunna), blood-feud, and a code of honour; the era is also celebrated in Arabic literary tradition for its poetry, including the suspended odes (Mu'allaqat).

Use of the term

In the Quran, jahiliyyah appears in four verses, characterising the judgement, display, and zealotry of the pre-Islamic age. Later Muslim scholars used the term broadly for the whole pre-Islamic period. In the 20th century, thinkers such as Abul A'la Maududi and Sayyid Qutb extended the concept: Qutb's Milestones (1964) notoriously described contemporary societies, including nominally Muslim ones, as living in a new jahiliyyah — an idea influential in Islamist thought and widely debated. Western scholarship, meanwhile, cautions that the traditional picture of pre-Islamic Arabia derives largely from later Islamic sources and that Arabia was more connected to the late antique world than the term "ignorance" might suggest.
