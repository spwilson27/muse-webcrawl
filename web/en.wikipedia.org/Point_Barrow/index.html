Point Barrow

Point Barrow (Iñupiaq: Nuvuk) is a headland on the Arctic coast in the U.S. state of Alaska, at approximately 71°23′N 156°47′W. It is the northernmost point of the United States and of all territories under its jurisdiction. Distances to it are often used as a reference on maps and globes; Point Barrow is 1,122 nautical miles (2,078 km) from the geographic North Pole. To its south is the city of Utqiaġvik (formerly Barrow).

Geography

The point forms a narrow gravel spit separating the Chukchi Sea to the west from the Beaufort Sea (Elson Lagoon) to the east. The Arctic Ocean coast in this region is typically ice-bound for much of the year, and the waters around Point Barrow are a major seasonal passage for migrating bowhead whales, hunted by the local Iñupiat in a subsistence tradition of great antiquity.

History

The Iñupiaq name Nuvuk simply means "the point of land". A major prehistoric settlement and burial site, the Birnirk culture site, lies nearby, evidence of centuries of occupation. The point was named by the English explorer Frederick William Beechey in 1826 for Sir John Barrow of the British Admiralty. Point Barrow became a base for Arctic exploration, whaling, and research; it was the site of a rescue station for shipwrecked whalers and later of military and scientific installations, including a Distant Early Warning Line radar site. In 1935, humorist Will Rogers and aviator Wiley Post died in a plane crash shortly after takeoff near Point Barrow.
