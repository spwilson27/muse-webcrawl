Panda diplomacy

Panda diplomacy is the practice of the People's Republic of China sending giant pandas to other countries as a tool of diplomacy and soft power. China has given or loaned the animals to nations it wishes to court or reward, and pandas have become barometers of bilateral relations as well as ambassadors for conservation.

History

The practice dates to the Tang dynasty, when Empress Wu Zetian reportedly sent pandas to Japan. In the modern era, China began presenting pandas to friendly states in the 1950s, sending animals to the Soviet Union and North Korea. The most famous gift followed President Richard Nixon's 1972 visit to China: Ling-Ling and Hsing-Hsing were given to the United States and installed at the National Zoo in Washington, D.C., where they drew enormous crowds.

From gifts to loans

In 1984 China ended outright gifts and moved to long-term loans, typically ten years, for which host zoos pay an annual fee (often around US$1 million per pair) directed to panda conservation in China. Cubs born abroad remain Chinese property and are usually repatriated after a few years. Loan announcements often coincide with trade agreements or state visits, leading analysts to track panda transfers as signals of diplomatic favour.
