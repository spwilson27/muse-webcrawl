St. Gallen (German: Sankt Gallen) is a city in northeastern Switzerland and the capital of the canton of St. Gallen. It grew around the Abbey of St. Gall, founded in the 8th century, whose library and cathedral are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

The town traces its origins to the hermitage of the Irish monk Saint Gall around 612 and the abbey founded by Otmar in 719. In the Middle Ages it became a free imperial city known for linen and later embroidery textiles.

Economy and education

St. Gallen is an economic centre for eastern Switzerland and home to the University of St. Gallen, noted for management and economics. Its old town features distinctive painted oriel windows (Erker).
