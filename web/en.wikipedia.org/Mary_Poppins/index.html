Mary Poppins

Mary Poppins is a series of eight children's books written by Australian-British writer P. L. Travers and published between 1934 and 1988. The books centre on the magical English nanny Mary Poppins, who is blown by the east wind to Number 17 Cherry Tree Lane, London, to care for the Banks children.

The books

The first book, Mary Poppins (1934), introduces the nanny and her charges Jane, Michael and the twins John and Barbara. Mary Poppins is stern, vain and mysterious, yet takes the children on magical adventures involving figures such as the Bird Woman, Mr. Twigley and the stars of a circus in the sky. Each book ends with her departure when the wind changes.

Adaptations

The books were adapted into the 1964 Walt Disney film Mary Poppins, starring Julie Andrews and Dick Van Dyke, which won five Academy Awards. A sequel film, Mary Poppins Returns, was released in 2018. A stage musical premiered in London's West End in 2004 and on Broadway in 2006. The making of the 1964 film was dramatized in the 2013 film Saving Mr. Banks.
