Mesothelioma

Mesothelioma is a type of cancer that develops from the thin layer of tissue that covers many of the internal organs (known as the mesothelium). The most common area affected is the lining of the lungs and chest wall (pleural mesothelioma). Less commonly the lining of the abdomen, and rarely the sac surrounding the heart or the testis, may be affected.

Causes

Working with asbestos is the primary risk factor, accounting for the great majority of cases; the interval between exposure and disease is typically 25 to 70 years. The link was established epidemiologically in the 1960s, notably by J. Christopher Wagner's studies of South African miners. Genetic factors, including mutations of the BAP1 tumor suppressor gene, also influence susceptibility.

Signs, diagnosis and treatment

Signs and symptoms may include shortness of breath due to fluid around the lung, chest wall pain, cough, and unexplained weight loss. Diagnosis often follows imaging and requires biopsy. Treatment may include surgery, chemotherapy (pemetrexed with cisplatin is the standard first-line regimen), radiation therapy, and increasingly immunotherapy. The prognosis is generally poor, with median survival around one year, though outcomes vary by cell type — epithelioid tumors respond better than sarcomatoid ones.
