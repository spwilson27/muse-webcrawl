Albite is a plagioclase feldspar mineral, the pure sodium endmember of the plagioclase solid solution series, with the chemical formula NaAlSi3O8. Its name derives from the Latin albus, "white", referring to its colour.

Properties and occurrence

Albite crystallizes in the triclinic system, commonly forming white to colourless tabular crystals with characteristic fine polysynthetic twinning ("albite twinning"). It occurs in granitic and pegmatitic rocks, in some metamorphic rocks, and as a low-temperature alteration product.

Uses

Like other feldspars, albite is used in the manufacture of glass and ceramics and as a mild abrasive. Transparent varieties, including moonstone-like peristerite, have gem uses.
