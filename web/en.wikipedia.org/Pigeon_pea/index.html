Pigeon pea

Scientific name
Cajanus cajan
Family
Fabaceae (legume)
Origin
Indian subcontinent
Uses
Dal, fodder, soil improvement

The pigeon pea (Cajanus cajan) is a perennial legume of the family Fabaceae, widely cultivated in the tropics and subtropics for its edible seeds. It was domesticated in India at least 3,500 years ago and spread to Africa and the Americas.

Cultivation

Pigeon pea is a drought-tolerant shrub growing 1–4 m tall, valued by smallholder farmers because its deep roots break up soil, its root nodules fix atmospheric nitrogen, and fallen leaves add organic matter. Major producers include India, Myanmar, and several East African countries.

Uses

In India the split, hulled seeds are cooked as toor dal, a staple protein source; in the Caribbean they feature in dishes such as rice and peas and moro de guandules. The plant also serves as fodder, green manure, and firewood, and the seeds are sometimes used as pigeon feed, giving the crop its English name.
