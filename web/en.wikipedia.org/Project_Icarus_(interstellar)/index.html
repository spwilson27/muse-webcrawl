Project Icarus (interstellar)

Project Icarus aims to design a credible interstellar probe.

Project Icarus is an international engineering study, launched in 2009 by the British Interplanetary Society and the Tau Zero Foundation's successor organization Icarus Interstellar, to design a credible, mainly fusion-propelled uncrewed spacecraft capable of reaching a nearby star within a human lifetime. It is the successor to the BIS's Project Daedalus study of the 1970s.

Terms of reference

The design must use current or near-future technology, reach a target star within about 100 years (preferably much sooner), and accelerate to a substantial fraction of the speed of light. Unlike Daedalus, Icarus must be able to decelerate at its destination to study the target system in depth. Candidate targets include Alpha Centauri and other stars within roughly 15 light-years.

Design work

Volunteer teams have studied fusion propulsion options (including inertial confinement using deuterium/helium-3 and alternative schemes), fuel acquisition from gas giants, interstellar dust shielding, and autonomous repair. The project has published design papers through the British Interplanetary Society and helped spur broader renewed interest in interstellar flight studies.
