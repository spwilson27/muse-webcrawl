Ernest Shackleton

Sir Ernest Henry Shackleton (15 February 1874 – 5 January 1922) was an Anglo-Irish Antarctic explorer who led three British expeditions to the Antarctic. He was one of the principal figures of the Heroic Age of Antarctic Exploration.

Early life

Shackleton was born in Kilkea, County Kildare, Ireland, and educated at Dulwich College. He joined the merchant navy and served with Robert Falcon Scott on the Discovery Expedition of 1901–1904.

Expeditions

He led the Nimrod Expedition (1907–1909), which reached a new Farthest South record, and the Imperial Trans-Antarctic Expedition (1914–1917) aboard Endurance. When Endurance was crushed by ice in the Weddell Sea, Shackleton led his crew to safety, famously sailing the lifeboat James Caird 800 nautical miles to South Georgia — an achievement celebrated as one of the greatest feats of seamanship and leadership.

Death and legacy

Shackleton died of a heart attack aboard Quest in South Georgia in 1922 and was buried there. He is remembered for his leadership, endurance, and concern for his men.
