In military terminology, a squad is the smallest military unit that is led by a non-commissioned officer and is a subdivision of a platoon. Squads typically consist of eight to fourteen soldiers, depending on the country and service, and may be further divided into fireteams.

Composition

In the United States Army, an infantry rifle squad has nine soldiers: a squad leader (staff sergeant) and two fireteams of four, each led by a team leader and including an automatic rifleman, a grenadier and a rifleman. The US Marine Corps uses 13-man rifle squads with three fireteams. In British and Commonwealth usage the equivalent unit is called a section, typically of eight to ten soldiers commanded by a corporal.

Role

The squad is the basic manoeuvre element of infantry tactics: it can assault, provide a base of fire, patrol and hold ground. Because it is the level at which soldiers know each other personally and are directly led by an experienced NCO, historians often describe the bonds of the squad as central to combat motivation and cohesion.

Other uses

"Squad" is also used for small specialist teams in civilian contexts, such as police squads, sports squads (the pool of players from which a team is selected) and, by extension, development teams in software organisations.
