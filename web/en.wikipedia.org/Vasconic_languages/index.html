Vasconic languages

The Vasconic languages (from Latin vasco, "Basque") are a putative family of languages that includes Basque and the extinct Aquitanian language, spoken in the western Pyrenees and surrounding parts of present-day southwestern France and northern Spain before and during the Roman period.

Evidence

Aquitanian is known almost entirely from personal and divine names in Latin inscriptions of the 1st–3rd centuries AD. Elements of these names closely match Basque words — for example Aquitanian Andere (Basque andere, "woman/lady") and Nescato (Basque neskato, "girl") — leading most specialists to regard Aquitanian as an ancestor or close relative of Basque, and Basque as the sole survivor of the family.

Substrate hypothesis

Linguist Theo Vennemann proposed a much wider "Vasconic substrate" across Europe, arguing that Basque-like languages were spoken over much of the continent before the arrival of Indo-European languages and left traces in place names and vocabulary. This stronger hypothesis has been rejected by most mainstream linguists.
