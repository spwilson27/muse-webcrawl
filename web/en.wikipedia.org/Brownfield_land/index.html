Brownfield land

A former industrial site classified as brownfield land. (Image placeholder)

Brownfield is land that has been previously used for industrial or commercial purposes and may be contaminated by low concentrations of hazardous waste or pollution, which complicates its redevelopment. In the United States, the term applies to land whose expansion, redevelopment or reuse may be complicated by the presence or potential presence of a hazardous substance, pollutant or contaminant, as defined in environmental law and administered by the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA).

Redevelopment

Brownfield redevelopment aims to return idle or underused land to productive use — housing, parks, retail or new industry — while cleaning up contamination. Redevelopment typically involves environmental site assessment, remediation (such as soil removal, capping or groundwater treatment), and sometimes institutional controls limiting future uses. Reusing brownfields, often in established urban areas with existing infrastructure, can reduce pressure to develop undeveloped "greenfield" land on city edges.

Policy

In the US, the Small Business Liability Relief and Brownfields Revitalization Act of 2002 provided funding and liability protections to encourage cleanup and reuse. The EPA estimates hundreds of thousands of brownfield sites nationwide. The United Kingdom's planning policy has similarly encouraged building on "previously developed land" to protect countryside and regenerate urban areas. Obstacles to redevelopment include cleanup costs, liability fears, financing difficulties and uncertainty about contamination levels.
