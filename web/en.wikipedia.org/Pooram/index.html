Pooram

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Caparisoned elephants at a Kerala pooram

A pooram (Malayalam: പൂരം) is an annual Hindu temple festival held mainly in central Kerala, India, after the summer harvest. Poorams are celebrated when the moon is in conjunction with the Pooram asterism (Delta and Epsilon Leonis) in the Malayalam month of Medam (mid-April to mid-May). They are famous for processions of richly caparisoned elephants, traditional percussion ensembles and fireworks.

Features

The hallmark of a major pooram is the parade of elephants wearing golden headgear (nettipattam), decorative bells and ornaments, each ridden by mahouts holding swaying white tufts (venchamaram) and peacock-feather fans (aalavattam). Large parasols are exchanged atop the elephants in the competitive kudamattam ceremony. Music is supplied by the powerful percussion ensembles panchavadyam and pandi melam, building in pace and intensity over several hours. Night festivities culminate in elaborate fireworks displays.

Major poorams

The most famous is the Thrissur Pooram, instituted in the late 18th century by the ruler Sakthan Thampuran, in which two groups of temples — Paramekkavu and Thiruvambady — compete in displays before the Vadakkunnathan Temple. Other celebrated poorams include those at Arattupuzha (one of the oldest), Uthralikavu, Nenmara–Vallangi and Chinakkathoor.

Concerns

Festival organisers face recurring debate over the welfare of captive elephants, crowd safety, and the risks of mass fireworks — sharpened after fatal accidents at temple fireworks events such as the 2016 Puttingal temple fire. Regulations on elephant parades and firework storage have tightened, and the balance between tradition and safety remains contested.
