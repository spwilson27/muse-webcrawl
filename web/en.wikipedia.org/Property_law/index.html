Property law

Property law is the area of law that governs the various forms of ownership in real property (land) and personal property (movable possessions). Property refers to legally protected claims to resources, and property rights give individuals and entities the rights to possess, use, dispose of and exclude others from things.

Classification

Common law traditionally divides property into real property — land and interests in it such as freeholds, leaseholds, easements and mortgages — and personal property, split into chattels (tangible goods) and choses in action (intangible rights). Intellectual property (copyright, patents, trademarks) is a distinct modern category. Civil law systems similarly distinguish immovable and movable property.

Key doctrines

Core concepts include title and its transfer by sale, gift, inheritance or adverse possession; concurrent ownership (joint tenancy and tenancy in common); landlord and tenant law; nuisance and trespass; and the registration of land. Property theory draws on Locke's labour theory of property, utilitarian justifications and legal positivism, and property regimes are frequently debated in economics and political philosophy.

History

English land law developed after the Norman Conquest around feudal tenure, reshaped by statutes such as Quia Emptores (1290) and, in modern times, comprehensive registration reforms such as the English Law of Property Act 1925. Comparable codifications exist in civil law jurisdictions.
