Mortimer Jerome Adler (December 28, 1902 – June 28, 2001) was an American philosopher, educator, encyclopedist, and popular author. As a philosopher he worked within the Aristotelian and Thomistic traditions. He taught at Columbia University and the University of Chicago, served as chairman of the board of editors of Encyclopædia Britannica, and co-founded the Aspen Institute and the Great Books Foundation.

Life and work

Adler left school at 14 and educated himself while working as a copy boy, later gaining a PhD in psychology from Columbia. With Robert Maynard Hutchins at the University of Chicago he promoted a curriculum built on the Great Books. His bestseller How to Read a Book (1940, revised with Charles Van Doren 1972) taught analytical and syntopical reading.

Great Books and the Syntopicon

Adler edited Great Books of the Western World (1952, 2nd ed. 1990), published by Encyclopædia Britannica, and its two-volume index of 102 "Great Ideas," the Syntopicon, a project he directed over many years. He also edited The Paideia Proposal (1982), advocating a single, rigorous, non-specialized curriculum for all schoolchildren. A lifelong religious seeker influenced by Thomism, Adler converted to Roman Catholicism in 1999.

Selected works

Dialectic (1927); How to Read a Book (1940); How to Think About God (1980); Ten Philosophical Mistakes (1985); Intellect: Mind Over Matter (1990); The Difference of Man and the Difference It Makes (1967).
