English literature

English literature is literature written in the English language from the English-speaking world. It begins with Old English works such as the epic poem Beowulf (c. 700–1000 AD) and encompasses medieval writing, the drama of Shakespeare, the rise of the novel, Romantic and Victorian literature, modernism, and contemporary writing from Britain, Ireland, America, and the wider Anglophone world.

Old and Middle English

Old English literature includes heroic poetry, religious verse, and chronicles. After the Norman Conquest, Middle English literature flourished with Geoffrey Chaucer's The Canterbury Tales and the Arthurian romance Sir Gawain and the Green Knight.

Early modern to modern

The English Renaissance produced William Shakespeare, Christopher Marlowe, and John Milton. The eighteenth century saw the rise of the novel with Defoe, Richardson, and Fielding; the Romantic poets Wordsworth, Coleridge, Keats, and Shelley followed, then the great Victorian novelists Dickens, the Brontës, Eliot, and Hardy. The twentieth century brought modernists such as T. S. Eliot, Virginia Woolf, and James Joyce, and English literature became a global literature with major writers from the Commonwealth, the United States, and postcolonial nations.
