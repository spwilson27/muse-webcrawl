Zhoushan (Chinese: 舟山; pinyin: Zhōushān) is a prefecture-level city in northeastern Zhejiang Province, China. It consists of the Zhoushan Archipelago, a group of islands lying just off the coast of the mainland at the southern entrance to Hangzhou Bay. Zhoushan is the only prefecture-level city in China administered entirely on an archipelago, and its seat is Dinghai District on Zhoushan Island, the largest island of the group.

Geography

The Zhoushan Archipelago comprises roughly 1,390 islands and numerous reefs scattered across the East China Sea. The principal islands include Zhoushan Island, Daishan, Jintang, Liuheng and Zhujiajian. The city administers two districts (Dinghai and Putuo) and two counties (Daishan and Shengsi). The landscape is hilly, with Mount Huaiguang on the main island rising about 503 metres above sea level.

History

The islands were settled from Neolithic times and were long known for their fishing fleets. During the Tang and Song dynasties the port grew as a trading point with Japan and Korea. In the nineteenth century Zhoushan was occupied by British forces during the First Opium War, and Britain briefly considered it as an alternative entrepôt to Hong Kong. The city became home to major naval installations and, in 2011, the Zhoushan Archipelago New Area was established as a state-level new area focused on marine industries.

Economy

Fishing has historically been the backbone of the local economy; the Zhoushan fishing ground is one of the largest in China. In recent decades shipbuilding, ship repair, petrochemicals, seafood processing and tourism have become dominant. Zhoushan is connected to the mainland near Ningbo by a series of long sea-crossing bridges, and its port forms part of the Ningbo–Zhoushan Port, the world's busiest by cargo tonnage.

Transport

The Zhoushan Putuoshan Airport serves the city, and ferries link the islands with Shanghai and Ningbo. The Zhoushan Cross-sea Bridges, completed in 2009, connect the archipelago to the mainland highway network.
