Capgras delusion

The Capgras delusion is a psychiatric disorder in which a person holds a delusion that a friend, spouse, parent, another close family member, or pet has been replaced by an identical impostor. It is named after the French psychiatrist Joseph Capgras (1873–1950), who first described it in 1923.

Causes

Capgras delusion is classified as a delusional misidentification syndrome. It is most often associated with schizophrenia, but also occurs with brain injury and neurodegenerative diseases such as Lewy body dementia. A leading cognitive explanation proposes a disconnection between the face-recognition system and the autonomic emotional response: the person recognises the face but feels none of the expected familiarity, and rationalises that the person must be an impostor.

Treatment

There is no single established treatment; management generally addresses the underlying condition, often with antipsychotic medication and supportive therapy.
