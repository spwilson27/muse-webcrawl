Politics of Svalbard

TerritorySvalbard (Kingdom of Norway)
GovernorSysselmester, appointed by Norway
Main settlementLongyearbyen
Legal basisSvalbard Treaty (1920); Svalbard Act (1925)

The politics of Svalbard are conducted within Norway's sovereignty over the Arctic archipelago, as qualified by the Svalbard Treaty of 1920. The treaty, signed in Paris on 9 February 1920, recognised Norwegian sovereignty while granting nationals and companies of signatory states equal rights of residence, fishing, hunting and economic activity, and prohibited the use of the islands for warlike purposes. Norway formally took possession under the Svalbard Act of 1925.

Administration

Svalbard is not a county and is not part of the Schengen Area; it is administered by the Governor of Svalbard (Sysselmesteren), appointed by the Norwegian government, who combines the roles of chief of police, environmental authority and registrar. The main settlement, Longyearbyen, has an elected local council (Lokalstyre, established 2002) responsible for municipal services. Because the treaty limits taxation to what is needed for the islands' administration, Svalbard has unusually low taxes and no general system of social welfare; residents must be able to support and house themselves.

International aspects

Russia maintains the mining settlement of Barentsburg through the state company Trust Arktikugol, and other states conduct Arctic research at Ny-Ålesund. Disputes with other treaty parties concern the extent of Norway's rights over the continental shelf and the fisheries protection zone around the archipelago, and whether treaty equal-access rights apply there. Svalbard residents do not vote in Norwegian parliamentary elections for a constituency of their own; Norwegian citizens resident on the mainland electoral register retain voting rights there.
