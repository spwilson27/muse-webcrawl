White flight

White flight is a term for the large-scale migration of people of various European ancestries, especially white Americans, from racially mixed urban areas to more racially homogeneous suburbs or exurbs. In the United States it accelerated after the Second World War as Black Americans moved to northern and western cities during the Great Migration.

Causes

Scholars link white flight to discriminatory housing practices, blockbusting, redlining, highway construction, suburban housing subsidies, concerns about schools and crime, and racial prejudice. Government policy often made suburban homes available to white buyers while restricting access for minorities.

Effects

White flight contributed to metropolitan segregation, urban disinvestment and unequal tax bases. Later patterns include minority suburbanisation and, in some cities, gentrification and displacement that complicate the original model.
