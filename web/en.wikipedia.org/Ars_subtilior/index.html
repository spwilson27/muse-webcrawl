Ars subtilior

The ars subtilior ("subtler art") is a musical style of the late 14th and early 15th centuries, centred on courts in southern France, Aragon and Cyprus. It is characterised by extreme rhythmic and melodic complexity, notated in elaborate mensuration systems.

Characteristics

Composers such as Solage, Baude Cordier and Johannes Ciconia wrote music using simultaneous conflicting metres, syncopations and proportional tempo changes that were not equalled in notated music for centuries. Many pieces survive in the Chantilly Codex; some are written in shaped notation, such as pieces in the form of a heart or a harp.

Context

The style flourished amid the cultural rivalry of courts during the Western Schism. Its complexity appears largely decorative and intellectual rather than practical, and it faded as musical fashion turned toward simpler textures in the early 15th century.
