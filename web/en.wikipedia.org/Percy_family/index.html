The Percy family is an English noble family. They were among the most powerful noble families in Northern England for much of the Middle Ages, having descended from William de Percy, 1st Baron Percy (died c. 1096/99), who came to England from Normandy with William the Conqueror in 1066 and was rewarded with substantial estates, including Topcliffe in Yorkshire.

History

The Percys rose to prominence as wardens of the Scottish Marches, defending the Anglo-Scottish border. Henry Percy, 1st Earl of Northumberland (1341–1408), was created Earl of Northumberland in 1377. His son, Henry "Hotspur" Percy (1364–1403), famed for his martial daring, was killed rebelling against Henry IV at the Battle of Shrewsbury. The family suffered forfeitures during the Wars of the Roses and again after the Pilgrimage of Grace, and Henry Percy, 6th Earl, died without heirs in 1537.

The earldom was revived in 1557 for Thomas Percy. The direct male line of the medieval earls failed in 1670, but the Percy inheritance passed through Elizabeth Percy to Charles Seymour, 6th Duke of Somerset. In 1749/50, their descendant Algernon Seymour, 7th Duke of Somerset, was created Earl of Northumberland with special remainder to his son-in-law Sir Hugh Smithson, who took the name Percy and in 1766 was created Duke of Northumberland. The present duke, Ralph Percy, 12th Duke of Northumberland, descends from this line.

Seats

The family's principal seat is Alnwick Castle in Northumberland; its London residence is Syon House in Brentford. Other historic properties include Warkworth Castle, Bamburgh Castle, Petworth House and Northumberland House.
