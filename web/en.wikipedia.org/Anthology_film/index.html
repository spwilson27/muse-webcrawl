Anthology film

Poster of Dead of Night (1945), a classic horror anthology film

An anthology film (also known as an omnibus film, package film or portmanteau film) is a single film consisting of several shorter films, each complete in itself and distinguished by its own story, and often directed by a different filmmaker. The segments may be held together by a common theme, a shared setting or characters, or a framing narrative ("frame story") that links them.

Characteristics

Unlike a film with subplots, an anthology film presents discrete narratives that could stand alone as short films. Sometimes a single director handles all segments; in other cases the format allows production companies to showcase multiple directors, writers and actors in one release. The format has been especially durable in horror cinema, where it descends from the Ealing Studios classic Dead of Night (1945) and the Amicus productions of the 1960s and 1970s.

Notable examples

  • Intolerance (1916) — D. W. Griffith's parallel historical narratives
  • Dead of Night (1945) — Ealing horror anthology with a celebrated framing story
  • Boccaccio '70 (1962) — segments by Fellini, Visconti, De Sica and Monicelli
  • Creepshow (1982) — George A. Romero and Stephen King
  • New York Stories (1989) — Scorsese, Coppola and Woody Allen
  • Pulp Fiction (1994) — interwoven crime stories by Quentin Tarantino
  • Paris, je t'aime (2006) — eighteen shorts set in Paris arrondissements
  • The Ballad of Buster Scruggs (2018) — the Coen brothers' Western anthology
