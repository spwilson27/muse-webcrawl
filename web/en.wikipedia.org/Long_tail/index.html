Long tail

The long tail is a business and statistics concept describing distributions in which a high-frequency or high-amplitude “head” is followed by a gradual decline — the tail — that never fully reaches zero. In retail and media, it refers to the large number of niche products that individually sell in small quantities but collectively can rival the sales of bestsellers.

Popularisation

The term was popularised by Chris Anderson, first in a 2004 Wired magazine article and then in his 2006 book The Long Tail: Why the Future of Business Is Selling Less of More. Anderson argued that the low distribution and inventory costs of online businesses such as Amazon and Netflix allow them to profitably stock vastly more niche titles than physical stores.

Statistics

Long-tail distributions relate to power laws and the Pareto principle. Critics, including Anita Elberse, have argued that hit products remain dominant and that the tail is often thin, sparking ongoing debate in marketing research.
