Indra Jatra

Indra Jatra (Yenyā Punhi)
TypeReligious festival
LocationKathmandu, Nepal
DurationEight days
ObservedDuring Bhadra (September) in the Nepali calendar

Indra Jatra, known in Nepal Bhasa as Yenyā Punhi, is the biggest religious street festival in Kathmandu, Nepal. Lasting eight days, it honours Indra, the Hindu god of rain and king of heaven, and is marked by masked dances, chariot processions, and displays of sacred images. The festival also inaugurates the autumn festival season in the Kathmandu Valley.

Legend

According to legend, Indra descended to earth in disguise to gather parijat flowers for his mother Dagini's ritual. Caught stealing the flowers, he was bound and held captive by the people of Kathmandu until Dagini revealed his identity; in return for his release she promised timely rains and dew for the winter crops, and to take to heaven the souls of those who had died that year.

Observances

The festival opens with the erection of the Yosin, a ceremonial pine pole, at Kathmandu Durbar Square. A central feature is the chariot procession of the living goddess Kumari, accompanied by the deities Ganesh and Bhairav, through the old city—the Kumari Jatra instituted in the 18th century by King Jaya Prakash Malla. On the first day, masked Lakhey dancers perform, displays of Bhairava images are unveiled, and devotees make offerings at shrines along the processional route.

Significance

Indra Jatra blends Hindu and Buddhist elements characteristic of Newar religion. The head of state traditionally receives tika from the Kumari during the festival, a ceremony that underscores its civic as well as religious importance.
