Prosecutorial misconduct

In jurisprudence, prosecutorial misconduct or prosecutorial error is "an illegal act or failing to act, on the part of a prosecutor, especially an attempt to sway the jury to wrongly convict a defendant or to impose a harsher than appropriate punishment." It includes a prosecutor's failure to honor the obligation to disclose exculpatory evidence (Brady v. Maryland), making improper arguments, or bringing charges in bad faith.

Types

Common forms include withholding exculpatory or impeachment evidence, knowingly presenting false testimony, discriminatory jury selection, commenting on a defendant's silence, and inflammatory remarks to the jury. Courts distinguish misconduct from mere error, and reversals typically require that the misconduct was prejudicial or not harmless.

Consequences and accountability

Consequences can include dismissal of charges, reversal of convictions, and retrials, but personal discipline of prosecutors is rare, and prosecutors generally enjoy absolute immunity from civil damages suits for their prosecutorial functions in the United States.
