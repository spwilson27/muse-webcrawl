Horatio Walpole, 4th Earl of Orford (24 September 1717 – 2 March 1797), better known as Horace Walpole, was an English writer, art historian, man of letters, antiquarian, and Whig politician. He is now best remembered for Strawberry Hill, the Gothic Revival villa he built in Twickenham, and for his novel The Castle of Otranto (1764), generally regarded as the first Gothic novel.

Early life

Walpole was the youngest son of Prime Minister Robert Walpole, 1st Earl of Orford. He was educated at Eton College and King's College, Cambridge. At Eton he formed a close circle of friends, the "Quadruple Alliance", which included the poet Thomas Gray, with whom he later undertook a Grand Tour of France and Italy.

Strawberry Hill

From 1749 onwards Walpole remodelled a modest house at Twickenham into Strawberry Hill, a pioneering example of Gothic Revival architecture. The house, with its battlements, pointed arches, and theatrical interiors, became one of the most celebrated buildings of the eighteenth century and helped popularise the Gothic taste in England.

Literary works

Walpole's novel The Castle of Otranto, published in 1764, founded the Gothic literary genre, combining medieval romance with elements of supernatural horror. He also wrote the historical work Historic Doubts on the Life and Reign of King Richard the Third (1768) and a vast correspondence, spanning nearly fifty years, which is valued as a social and political chronicle of Georgian England. He is credited with coining the word "serendipity" in a letter of 1754.

Political career

Walpole sat in Parliament from 1741 to 1768, representing Callington, Castle Rising, and King's Lynn in succession. Though a Whig, he never held high office and is remembered more for his letters and antiquarian pursuits than for his politics. In 1791 he succeeded his nephew as 4th Earl of Orford.
