The North York Moors is an upland area in North Yorkshire, England, containing one of the largest expanses of heather moorland in the United Kingdom. The area was designated as a National Park in 1952 and covers 1,436 square kilometres (554 sq mi). It is bounded by the Cleveland Hills and Howardian Hills, the Vale of Pickering, and the North Sea coast between Whitby and Scarborough.

Landscape and geology

The moors are formed mainly of Jurassic sandstones and shales, dissected by deep dales such as Rosedale and Farndale. Heather moorland, much of it managed by rotational burning for red grouse shooting, dominates the high plateau, while the dales carry pasture, woodland and villages of local stone. Farndale is famous for its spring display of wild daffodils.

History and attractions

The area is rich in prehistoric remains — Bronze Age barrows, Iron Age hillforts and the Roman road known as Wade's Causeway — as well as medieval abbeys at Rievaulx, Byland and Whitby. The preserved North Yorkshire Moors Railway runs steam trains between Pickering and Whitby. RAF Fylingdales, a ballistic missile early-warning station, stands prominently on the moor near the coast.
