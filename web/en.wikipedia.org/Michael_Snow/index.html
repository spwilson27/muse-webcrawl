Michael Snow

Born10 December 1928, Toronto, Canada
Died5 January 2023 (aged 94), Toronto
FieldsFilm, painting, sculpture, photography, music
Notable worksWavelength (1967), La Région Centrale (1971), Flight Stop (1979)

Michael Snow (1928–2023) was a Canadian artist, filmmaker, sculptor, photographer and musician, regarded as one of the most influential experimental filmmakers of all time and a central figure in North American avant-garde art.

Career

Born in Toronto, Snow studied at the Ontario College of Art and worked first as an animator and jazz musician. Living in New York through much of the 1960s, he developed the Walking Woman works — a silhouetted female figure explored across paintings, sculptures, photographs and public installations. His film Wavelength (1967), a 45-minute slow zoom across a loft toward a photograph on the far wall, became a founding work of structural film and is consistently ranked among the greatest experimental films ever made. He followed it with Back and Forth (1969) and La Région Centrale (1971), a three-hour film shot in the Quebec wilderness by a robotically programmed camera apparatus, producing landscape images no human cameraman could frame.

Public art and later work

Snow's public sculptures are landmarks of Toronto: Flight Stop (1979), sixty Canada geese suspended in the Eaton Centre atrium, and The Audience (1989), comic sports fans at the Rogers Centre. He explored photography, holographic and book works (including the celebrated artist's book Cover to Cover, 1975), piano improvisation and sound installations, returning to gallery work such as video installations Corpus Callosum (2002) late in life.

Recognition

Snow represented Canada at the Venice Biennale (1970), received a major retrospective at the Art Gallery of Ontario and the Power Plant in 1994, and was made a Companion of the Order of Canada. Critics credit his rigorous, idea-driven work with changing how artists think about time, framing and perception in moving images.
