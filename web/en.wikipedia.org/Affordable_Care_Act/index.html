Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act
Enacted: 111th United States Congress
Signed: 23 March 2010
Nicknames: ACA, Obamacare

The Affordable Care Act (ACA), formally known as the Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act (PPACA) and colloquially as Obamacare, is a landmark United States federal statute enacted by the 111th United States Congress and signed into law by President Barack Obama on 23 March 2010. It represents the most significant overhaul of the US healthcare system since the passage of Medicare and Medicaid in 1965.

Provisions

The Act aimed to decrease the cost of health insurance and increase the number of insured Americans. Key provisions included an individual mandate (whose penalty was later reduced to zero in 2019), the expansion of Medicaid eligibility, the creation of health insurance marketplaces (exchanges) where individuals could buy subsidised coverage, requirements that insurers accept all applicants without regard to pre-existing conditions, allowing young adults to stay on parents' plans until age 26, and preventive services covered without cost-sharing.

Legal challenges and effects

The law survived major Supreme Court challenges, including National Federation of Independent Business v. Sebelius (2012), which upheld the individual mandate as a tax but made Medicaid expansion optional for states, and California v. Texas (2021). Following implementation, the uninsured rate in the United States fell from roughly 16% in 2010 to under 9% within several years, with over 20 million additional people gaining coverage.
