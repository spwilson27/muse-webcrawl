Bilateralism

Bilateralism is the conduct of political, economic, or cultural relations between two sovereign states. It is one of the main forms of international relations, alongside unilateralism (a state acting alone) and multilateralism (relations among three or more states, often through international organisations).

Characteristics

Bilateral relations are conducted through resident embassies, state visits, bilateral treaties, and trade and investment agreements. Bilateral deals allow states to tailor agreements to their specific interests and can be negotiated faster than multilateral ones, but they may disadvantage smaller states facing a larger partner, and a patchwork of bilateral trade agreements can complicate the global trading system — a phenomenon economist Jagdish Bhagwati criticised as the "spaghetti bowl" effect.

Examples

Bilateralism is prominent in trade policy, where free trade agreements between pairs of countries proliferated after slow progress in multilateral WTO rounds, and in security policy, where alliances such as the US–Japan and US–South Korea treaties are bilateral. States often combine bilateral and multilateral approaches in a strategy sometimes called "minilateralism" when small groups of states cooperate.
