Mordant

A mordant or dye fixative is a substance used to set (fix) dyes on fabrics. It does this by forming a coordination complex with the dye, which then attaches to the fabric. Mordants include metal salts such as alum (potassium aluminium sulfate), iron sulfate, and chromium compounds, as well as substances like tannic acid.

UseDyeing textiles, staining specimens
ExamplesAlum, iron sulfate, chrome alum, tannic acid
MechanismMetal–dye coordination complex

Use in dyeing

Fibres may be pre-treated with a mordant before dyeing, or the mordant may be applied during or after dyeing. The choice of mordant affects the final colour: iron tends to "sadden" or darken shades, while alum brightens them. Traditional natural dyers use mordants to obtain fast colours from dyes such as madder, weld and cochineal.

Other uses

In microscopy, mordants are used in staining techniques such as the Gram stain, where iodine acts as a mordant fixing crystal violet in bacterial cell walls. Mordants are also used in etching, where the same French-derived word describes a biting agent.
