INS Vikrant is an aircraft carrier constructed by the Cochin Shipyard Limited (CSL) in Kochi, Kerala for the Indian Navy. It is the first aircraft carrier to be built in India and the first of the Vikrant-class vessels. The name Vikrant means "courageous" in Sanskrit; the ship inherited the name and crest of the earlier INS Vikrant (R11), a British-built Majestic-class carrier that served India from 1961 to 1997 and played a key role in the Indo-Pakistani War of 1971.

Design and construction

The carrier was designed by the Indian Navy's Warship Design Bureau, with construction beginning at Cochin in 2009. At roughly 262 metres long and displacing about 45,000 tonnes, it is the largest warship built in India. It uses a STOBAR (Short Take-Off But Arrested Recovery) configuration with a 14-degree ski-jump and arrestor wires, and is powered by four General Electric LM2500+ gas turbines driving two shafts, giving a top speed of about 28 knots. The ship has a crew of around 1,600 including air group personnel.

Trials and commissioning

Vikrant began sea trials in August 2021, undergoing extensive trials of propulsion, aviation facilities and weapons. It was commissioned into the Indian Navy on 2 September 2022 by Prime Minister Narendra Modi at Kochi. Its air group initially centred on MiG-29K fighters and Kamov and MH-60R helicopters, with the Dassault Rafale M subsequently selected as the navy's carrier fighter.
