Salade niçoise

CourseSalad, starter or main
Place of originNice, France
Main ingredientsTomatoes, eggs, olives, anchovies or tuna, olive oil

Salade niçoise is a composed salad originating in the French city of Nice. Traditionally it combines raw vegetables — tomatoes, peppers, onions and radishes — with hard-boiled eggs, Niçoise olives and anchovies, dressed with olive oil; canned tuna is common in many versions.

The composition debate

Purists in Nice insist that a true salade niçoise contains no cooked vegetables: potatoes and green beans, frequently added elsewhere, are considered inauthentic, as is lettuce in some traditional accounts. The salad may be served with the components arranged separately on a platter or tossed together.

History

Early versions were simple workers' fare of tomato, anchovy and olive oil. The salad was popularised internationally in the twentieth century by chefs and writers including Auguste Escoffier, who controversially added potatoes and green beans.
