Parton (particle physics)

In particle physics, the parton model is a model of hadrons, such as protons and neutrons, proposed by Richard Feynman. It pictures hadrons as composed of point-like constituents, called partons, which were later identified with quarks and gluons in quantum chromodynamics.

History

Deep inelastic scattering experiments at SLAC in the late 1960s showed that high-energy electrons scatter from protons as if from free point-like particles. Feynman's parton model explained this behaviour; combined with Bjorken scaling, it provided strong evidence for the quark model.

Parton distribution functions

Within the model, partons carry fractions of the hadron's momentum. The probabilities of finding a parton of a given type with a given momentum fraction are described by parton distribution functions (PDFs), which evolve with energy scale according to the DGLAP equations and are essential inputs for predictions at hadron colliders.

Relation to QCD

Quantum chromodynamics refined the parton picture: partons are quarks, antiquarks, and gluons, and their interactions explain the small deviations from exact scaling observed in scattering experiments.
