Londinium

Londinium around AD 120

Londinium, also known as Roman London, was the capital of Roman Britain during most of the period of Roman rule. It was founded by the Romans around AD 47–50 as a commercial port on the Thames, at the site that later became the City of London. Most historians and archaeologists believe it developed from a trading settlement established shortly after the Claudian invasion of AD 43.

History

Londinium grew rapidly into Britain's largest city. In AD 60 or 61 it was destroyed during the revolt of Boudica, whose forces burned the town to the ground, leaving a distinctive red layer of burnt debris found by archaeologists. Rebuilt in planned Roman style, the city acquired a forum and basilica (the largest north of the Alps), an amphitheatre, temples including a Mithraeum, bathhouses, and a governor's palace. Around AD 200 the London Wall was built, enclosing about 330 acres.

By the 3rd century Londinium was the seat of the imperial administration of Britain. Following the end of Roman rule in the early 5th century, the city was largely abandoned, and settlement shifted west to the Anglo-Saxon town of Lundenwic; the walled city was reoccupied later in the 9th century under Alfred the Great.

Archaeology

Extensive excavations, including those before the construction of Bloomberg's London headquarters (which uncovered the writing tablets known as the Bloomberg tablets), have revealed the street grid, waterfront quays, houses, and thousands of artefacts. Finds are displayed at the Museum of London.
