ITU-R

The ITU Radiocommunication Sector (ITU-R) is one of the three sectors (divisions or units) of the International Telecommunication Union (ITU), a specialised agency of the United Nations. It is responsible for managing the international radio-frequency spectrum and satellite orbit resources, and for developing technical standards — ITU-R Recommendations — for radiocommunication systems.

Role

ITU-R coordinates the allocation of frequency bands through the Radio Regulations, an international treaty updated at World Radiocommunication Conferences (WRCs) held roughly every four years. Its work covers terrestrial and space services including broadcasting, mobile communications, satellite systems, radionavigation and radio astronomy. The sector also maintains the Master International Frequency Register, recording frequency assignments notified by administrations.

Structure

The sector is led by the Radiocommunication Bureau, headed by an elected Director, and its technical work is performed by study groups composed of experts from member states and industry. Well-known outputs include the BT.709 and BT.2020 television standards and the international coordination procedures that prevent harmful interference between radio services of different countries.

History

ITU-R replaced the International Radio Consultative Committee (CCIR), founded in 1927, when the ITU restructured in 1992–1993. Its predecessor's work on early radio regulation dates back to the first International Radiotelegraph Conference in Berlin in 1906.
