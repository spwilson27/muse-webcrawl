Heinrich von Ofterdingen is an unfinished novel by the German Romantic poet Novalis (Friedrich von Hardenberg), published posthumously in 1802 after the author's early death. It is one of the defining works of German early Romanticism.

Plot and themes

The novel follows the young medieval Minnesinger Heinrich, modelled loosely on the legendary singer of the Wartburg song contest. In the first part, "Expectation" (Die Erwartung), Heinrich dreams of a mysterious blue flower and sets out from Eisenach on a journey to Augsburg, meeting merchants, a hermit, crusaders and the poet Klingsohr, whose daughter Mathilde he loves. The projected second part, "Fulfilment" (Die Erfüllung), was to dissolve the narrative into a fairy-tale world in which poetry reconciles all opposites; Novalis completed only its opening before his death in 1801.

Influence

The blue flower of Heinrich's dream became the central symbol of Romantic longing (Sehnsucht) in German literature and beyond. The novel, conceived as a Romantic answer to Goethe's Wilhelm Meister's Apprenticeship, influenced later German writers and the wider Bildungsroman tradition.
