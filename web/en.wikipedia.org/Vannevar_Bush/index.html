Vannevar Bush

Vannevar Bush
Born: 11 March 1890, Everett, Massachusetts, U.S.
Died: 28 June 1974 (aged 84)
Known for: Differential analyser, Memex, OSRD
Fields: Electrical engineering, science administration

Vannevar Bush (11 March 1890 – 28 June 1974) was an American engineer, inventor and science administrator who headed the U.S. Office of Scientific Research and Development (OSRD) during the Second World War, coordinating the work of some 6,000 scientists in the war effort, including the early stages of the Manhattan Project.

Career

Bush taught at Tufts and then at MIT, where he became dean of engineering. In the early 1930s he built the differential analyser, an analogue computer for solving differential equations. He co-founded the company that became Raytheon. As president of the Carnegie Institution of Washington and head of the National Defense Research Committee, then OSRD, he became the central figure in American wartime science.

As We May Think

His 1945 essay "As We May Think", published in The Atlantic, proposed the Memex, a personal device for storing and retrieving information with associative trails — a vision widely cited as anticipating hypertext and the modern personal computer. His report Science, The Endless Frontier (1945) laid the groundwork for the National Science Foundation and the postwar system of federal funding for basic research.
