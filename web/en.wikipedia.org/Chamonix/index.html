Chamonix-Mont-Blanc, commonly known as Chamonix, is a commune in the Haute-Savoie department of southeastern France. Situated at the foot of Mont Blanc, the highest summit in the Alps, it is one of the oldest ski resorts in France and is widely regarded as the birthplace of alpinism. Chamonix hosted the first Winter Olympic Games in 1924.

History

The valley was first recorded in 1091, when it was granted to the Benedictine priory of Saint-Michel-de-la-Cluse. For centuries it remained a remote farming valley under the rule of the House of Savoy. The visit of English travelers William Windham and Richard Pococke in 1741, and their published accounts of the glaciers, began Chamonix's transformation into a destination for adventurous tourists. The first ascent of Mont Blanc in 1786 by Jacques Balmat and Michel-Gabriel Paccard cemented the valley's fame.

Geography

The commune occupies a deep valley carved by the river Arve, hemmed in by the Mont Blanc massif to the south and the Aiguilles Rouges to the north. Famous peaks above the town include the Aiguille du Midi, reached by one of the highest cable cars in the world, and the Aiguille du Dru. The Mer de Glace, France's largest glacier, descends toward the valley and is served by the Montenvers rack railway.

Tourism and sport

Chamonix is a center for mountaineering, skiing, paragliding, and trail running; it hosts the Ultra-Trail du Mont-Blanc, one of the world's most prestigious ultramarathons. The valley's ski areas include Brévent-Flégère, Les Grands Montets above Argentière, and the Vallée Blanche off-piste descent from the Aiguille du Midi. The Mont Blanc Tunnel links the town to Courmayeur in Italy.
