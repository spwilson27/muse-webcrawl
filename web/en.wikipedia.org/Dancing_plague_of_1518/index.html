Dancing plague of 1518

The dancing plague of 1518, or dance epidemic of 1518, was a case of dancing mania that occurred in Strasbourg, Alsace (modern-day France, then part of the Holy Roman Empire), in July 1518. Somewhere between 50 and 400 people reportedly took to dancing for weeks, with some dancers collapsing and even dying of heart attacks, strokes, or exhaustion. The cause of the episode remains debated.

Events

The outbreak began when a woman, variously recorded as Frau Troffea, began dancing fervently in the street. Within a week dozens had joined her, and by August the number had grown into the hundreds. Believing the affliction to be a natural disease caused by "hot blood" — or a curse of Saint Vitus — local authorities paradoxically encouraged more dancing, clearing guildhalls and even a stage for the victims, accompanied by musicians. The dancers were eventually taken to a shrine of Saint Vitus in the hills above Saverne, where the episode subsided.

Explanations

Proposed causes include ergot poisoning (from a fungus growing on rye, producing convulsive symptoms), mass psychogenic illness triggered by extreme stress, famine, and disease in the region, and religious mania associated with the cult of Saint Vitus, the patron saint of dancers. Similar dancing manias were reported elsewhere in medieval Europe.
