Chinese orchestra

A Chinese orchestra is a large ensemble of traditional Chinese instruments modeled on the Western symphony orchestra, developed in the 20th century. Orchestras of this kind are common in China, Taiwan, Singapore, and Malaysia, and typically comprise four sections: bowed strings, plucked strings, winds, and percussion, often supplemented by cellos and double basses borrowed from the Western orchestra.

Sections and instruments

The bowed-string section is dominated by the erhu family (gaohu, erhu, zhonghu), whose volume is balanced by plucked instruments including the pipa, liuqin, yangqin, ruan, and guzheng. The wind section features the dizi (transverse flute), sheng (mouth organ), and suona (double-reed shawm), while percussion draws on Chinese drums, gongs, and cymbals as well as concert percussion.

History

The modern Chinese orchestra emerged from experiments in the 1920s and 1930s, notably in Shanghai, and was formalized after 1949 under state-supported ensembles that standardized instrumentation and commissioned new repertoire. Composers created works blending folk melodies with Western harmony and forms, and the format spread through schools and conservatories across the Chinese-speaking world.

Notable orchestras

Leading ensembles include the China Broadcasting Chinese Orchestra, the Shanghai Chinese Orchestra, the Hong Kong Chinese Orchestra, the Singapore Chinese Orchestra, and the National Chinese Orchestra of Taiwan.
