Russia in the European energy sector

Russia in the European energy sector refers to the long-standing role of Russian oil, natural gas, coal and nuclear fuel supplies in the energy mix of European countries, and to the political and economic disputes surrounding that dependence. Before 2022 Russia was the European Union's largest single energy supplier, providing roughly 40% of its imported natural gas, about a quarter of its crude oil imports, and around half of its hard coal imports.

Development

Soviet gas exports to Western Europe began in the late 1960s, with pipelines to Austria, Germany and Italy built under long-term contracts linking Siberian fields to European markets. The network expanded after 1991 through Ukraine and Belarus, the Yamal–Europe pipeline, and the offshore Nord Stream pipeline (2011) directly to Germany; Nord Stream 2 was completed in 2021 but never certified.

Disputes and dependence

Gas price and transit disputes between Russia and Ukraine in 2006 and 2009 briefly cut supplies to parts of Europe and prompted EU efforts to diversify routes and suppliers. Critics long argued that Europe's reliance gave Moscow geopolitical leverage; defenders noted that trade also made Russia dependent on European revenue.

After 2022

Following Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine, the EU banned most Russian oil and coal imports, Russian pipeline gas flows collapsed — hastened by the September 2022 sabotage of the Nord Stream pipelines — and Europe replaced the gas with liquefied natural gas imports, demand reduction and accelerated renewables deployment. Russian pipeline gas fell to a small fraction of EU imports by 2023–24, ending five decades of energy interdependence.
