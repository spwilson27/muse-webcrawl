Orange Juice were a Scottish post-punk band founded in Bearsden, Glasgow, in 1979 as the Nu-Sonics and renamed in 1979. Fronted by singer and guitarist Edwyn Collins, they became the flagship act of the independent Postcard Records — the self-styled "Sound of Young Scotland" — and pioneered a bright, jangling guitar-pop style that mixed post-punk with disco, soul, and the Velvet Underground, hugely influential on later indie pop.

Career

The band's early Postcard singles, including "Falling and Laughing" (1980), "Blue Boy", and "Simply Thrilled Honey", were critical successes. Signing to Polydor, they released the albums You Can't Hide Your Love Forever (1982), Rip It Up (1982), Texas Fever (1984), and The Orange Juice (1984). The title track of Rip It Up, notable for its use of the Roland TB-303 bass synthesizer, reached No. 8 in the UK Singles Chart in 1983 — their only UK top-40 hit.

Legacy

After the band split in 1985, Collins pursued a solo career, achieving worldwide success with "A Girl Like You" (1994). Orange Juice's Postcard recordings were reissued to acclaim, and bands such as Belle and Sebastian and Franz Ferdinand cite them as a formative influence.
