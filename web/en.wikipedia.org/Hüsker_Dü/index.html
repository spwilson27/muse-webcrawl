Hüsker Dü was an American punk rock band formed in Saint Paul, Minnesota, in 1979. The band's core members were guitarist/vocalist Bob Mould, drummer/vocalist Grant Hart, and bassist Greg Norton. They initially gained notice as a hardcore punk band, then transcended their genre to become one of the key acts of the 1980s American alternative rock scene; their melodic, song-based approach helped shape the sound later popularised by alternative and indie rock. The band's name derives from "Husker Du?", a Scandinavian memory board game, meaning "do you remember?"

History

After early releases including the live album Land Speed Record (1982) and the EP Metal Circus (1983), Hüsker Dü signed to SST Records and released a rapid sequence of acclaimed albums: Zen Arcade (1984), an ambitious double concept album recorded largely in single takes; New Day Rising (1985); and Flip Your Wig (1985). The distinct songwriting voices of Mould and Hart — Mould's tormented, buzzing guitar songs against Hart's more melodic pop sensibility — gave the band unusual range.

In 1986 Hüsker Dü became one of the first American independent bands to sign to a major label, releasing Candy Apple Grey (1986) and the double album Warehouse: Songs and Stories (1987) on Warner Bros. Internal tensions, drug problems and the suicide of their manager David Savoy Jr. led to the band's acrimonious breakup in January 1988.

Legacy

Hüsker Dü influenced the Pixies, Nirvana (Kurt Cobain cited them as a favourite), Green Day and many others. Zen Arcade regularly appears on critics' lists of the greatest albums of the 1980s. Mould went on to form Sugar and pursue a solo career; Hart released solo records and played with Nova Mob before his death in 2017; Norton worked as a chef and later returned to music.
