Ethnic conflict

An ethnic conflict is a conflict between two or more ethnically distinct groups, often over territory, political power, resources, cultural rights or self-determination. Ethnic conflicts range from discrimination and communal riots to civil wars and genocide, and they became a central concern of international politics after the Cold War.

Causes

Scholars debate underlying causes. Primordialist accounts stress deep-seated identities and ancient hatreds; instrumentalists argue elites mobilise ethnicity for political gain; constructivists emphasise how identities are formed and hardened by institutions, colonial boundaries, competition for resources and security dilemmas when state authority collapses. Fear of domination, demographic shifts and contested histories commonly interact.

Examples

Notable ethnic conflicts include the Yugoslav Wars, the Rwandan genocide, the Sri Lankan Civil War, the Troubles in Northern Ireland, the Nagorno-Karabakh conflict, and conflicts in Sudan, Myanmar and the former Soviet space. Many are protracted because compromise is portrayed as existential surrender.

Resolution

Approaches include power-sharing and consociationalism, federalism and autonomy, truth commissions, partition (controversially), integration policies and international peacekeeping. Durable settlements typically combine security guarantees with political inclusion and economic reconstruction.
