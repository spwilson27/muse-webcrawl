Theodosius II (Latin: Theodosius Iunior; 10 April 401 – 28 July 450) was Roman emperor in the East from 402 until his death in 450. Proclaimed Augustus as an infant, he became sole Eastern emperor on his father Arcadius's death in 408, aged seven, and reigned for 42 years — the longest reign of any Roman emperor. His government, guided first by the praetorian prefect Anthemius and then by his elder sister Pulcheria, presided over the building of the great Theodosian Walls of Constantinople and the promulgation of the Theodosian Code.

Reign

Theodosius's government completed the massive triple land walls that would defend Constantinople for a thousand years, and in 425 reorganized the capital's university. In 438 the Codex Theodosianus, a compilation of imperial laws since Constantine, was published. Theologically, the reign was consumed by the Nestorian controversy: the emperor convoked the Council of Ephesus (431), which condemned Nestorius, and a second council in 449 — the so-called "Robber Council" — whose decisions his successor reversed at Chalcedon. In foreign policy the East paid heavy tribute to Attila the Hun after Hunnic invasions of the Balkans in the 440s, while relations with the Western court of Valentinian III, who married Theodosius's daughter Licinia Eudoxia, bound the two halves of the empire.

Character and death

Sources depict Theodosius as mild, scholarly, and devoted to calligraphy — he was nicknamed "the Calligrapher" — but largely content to let ministers and his sister govern. He died on 28 July 450 from injuries sustained in a riding accident, and was succeeded by Marcian, who married Pulcheria. He is venerated as a saint in the Eastern Orthodox Church.
