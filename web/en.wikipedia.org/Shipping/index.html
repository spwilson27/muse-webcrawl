Shipping

Shipping is the transport of cargo and passengers by water.

Shipping, in its original and most specific sense, is the carriage of goods and passengers by sea, although in modern usage the term has broadened to cover freight transport generally, including by land and air. Maritime shipping is the backbone of world trade: the great majority of international trade by volume — roughly four-fifths — is carried by sea, in bulk carriers, oil and gas tankers, container ships, and specialised vessels.

The industry

The shipping industry comprises shipowners, charterers, shipbrokers, classification societies, insurers and port operators, and is governed internationally in large part through the International Maritime Organization (IMO), which sets safety and environmental standards. Flag states, ship registries (including large "open registries" such as Panama and Liberia), and crewing arrangements reflect the industry's strongly globalised character. Freight rates in the tramp, tanker and container markets are notoriously cyclical, driven by the balance between world trade and fleet capacity.

Environmental issues

Ships move cargo efficiently per ton-kilometre, but the industry as a whole produces significant greenhouse gas emissions, sulphur oxides, and other pollutants, along with problems such as ballast-water invasive species and oil spills. IMO regulations have progressively tightened sulphur limits (notably the IMO 2020 cap) and adopted targets and measures aimed at decarbonising shipping through efficiency standards and alternative fuels such as methanol and ammonia.
