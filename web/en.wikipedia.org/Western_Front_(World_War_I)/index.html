Western Front (World War I)

Western Front
Dates: 4 August 1914 – 11 November 1918
Location: Belgium, north-eastern France, Alsace-Lorraine
Theatre: First World War
Result: Allied victory; armistice of 11 November 1918

The Western Front was the main theatre of war during the First World War. Following the outbreak of war in July 1914, the German Army opened the Western Front by invading Luxembourg and Belgium, then gained control of important industrial regions in France. The front stretched more than 700 km (430 mi) from the North Sea in Belgium through northern France to the Swiss border.

Trench warfare

After the First Battle of the Marne in September 1914 and the "Race to the Sea", the armies dug a continuous system of trenches facing each other across a devastated no man's land. The line changed very little for more than three years despite enormous offensives: Verdun and the Somme in 1916, Arras and Passchendaele in 1917, each costing hundreds of thousands of casualties. New weapons — machine guns, poison gas, tanks, aircraft and heavy artillery — shaped the fighting, while defence held the tactical advantage.

1918 and the end of the war

In spring 1918 Germany launched the Spring Offensive using troops freed by Russia's exit from the war, making large gains but exhausting its reserves. The Allied Hundred Days Offensive, beginning at Amiens on 8 August 1918, drove the German army back in a series of coordinated attacks with tanks, artillery and aircraft, supported by growing numbers of American troops. Germany signed the Armistice of Compiègne on 11 November 1918.
