Cerebral hemisphere

The vertebrate cerebrum is formed of two cerebral hemispheres, left and right, separated by the longitudinal fissure and connected by the corpus callosum and other commissures. Each hemisphere contains an outer layer of grey matter, the cerebral cortex, overlying white matter and deep nuclei.

Structure

Each hemisphere is divided into four major lobes — frontal, parietal, temporal and occipital — named for the skull bones above them. The hemispheres control the opposite sides of the body: the left hemisphere governs the right side and vice versa.

Lateralisation

Although structurally similar, the hemispheres show functional specialisation, or lateralisation: in most people, language functions are concentrated in the left hemisphere, while spatial attention is weighted toward the right. Research on split-brain patients, whose corpus callosum was severed, revealed how the hemispheres can operate semi-independently.
