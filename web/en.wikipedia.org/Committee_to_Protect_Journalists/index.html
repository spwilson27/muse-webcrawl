The Committee to Protect Journalists (CPJ) is an independent, nonprofit organisation based in New York City that promotes press freedom worldwide. Founded in 1981 by a group of American foreign correspondents, CPJ documents attacks on the press, campaigns for the release of imprisoned journalists, and advocates for legal and policy reforms protecting newsgathering.

Work

CPJ maintains databases of journalists killed, imprisoned, exiled, or missing in connection with their work, publishing an annual prison census and an annual report on attacks on the press. Its Global Impunity Index ranks countries where journalists are murdered and their killers go free. CPJ also provides emergency assistance, safety advice, and legal support referrals to journalists at risk, and leads advocacy missions to governments.

History

CPJ was founded after the 1981 detention of Paraguayan journalist involvement spurred American correspondents to organise systematic responses to abuses against their colleagues abroad; among its founders was Michael Massing. Honouring journalist and founder-member activities, early campaigns focused on Latin America before expanding globally. CPJ presents the annual International Press Freedom Awards, honouring journalists who have faced arrest, violence, or threats for their reporting, and the Gwen Ifill Press Freedom Award.

Funding and stance

CPJ is funded by foundations, corporations, and individuals and does not accept government funding, in keeping with its nonpartisan watchdog role. It regularly criticises press-freedom violations by governments of every political alignment, including the United States.
