TRPM8

Gene: TRPM8 (human)
Type: Transient receptor potential cation channel
Activated by: Cold (< ~28 °C), menthol, icilin
Function: Principal sensor of environmental cold

TRPM8, also known as the cold and menthol receptor 1 (CMR1), is a protein that in humans is encoded by the TRPM8 gene. It is a member of the transient receptor potential (TRP) family of ion channels and is the principal molecular sensor of environmental cold in the somatosensory system.

Function

TRPM8 is expressed in sensory neurons whose endings innervate the skin, teeth and eyes. The channel opens when temperature falls below roughly 28 °C, allowing cations — mainly Ca2+ and Na+ — to enter the cell and signal coolness or cold. It is also activated chemically by cooling compounds such as menthol and icilin, which explains the cold sensation produced by mint.

Discovery and research

The receptor was identified independently in 2002 by the groups of David Julius and Ardem Patapounian. Knockout mice lacking TRPM8 show greatly reduced sensitivity to innocuous cold. TRPM8 is also expressed in some tumours, including prostate cancer, and is studied as a target for treatments of cold allodynia, chronic pain and migraine.
