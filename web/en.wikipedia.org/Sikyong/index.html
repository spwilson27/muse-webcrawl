Sikyong

The Sikyong (Tibetan: སྲིད་སྐྱོང་, Wylie: srid skyong) is the political leader of the Central Tibetan Administration (CTA), the Tibetan government-in-exile based in Dharamshala, India. The office was created in 2012 when the title Kalon Tripa (chairman of the cabinet) was renamed Sikyong—literally "political leader"—after the 14th Dalai Lama devolved his temporal and political authority to the elected leadership in 2011.

History

From 2001, the Kalon Tripa was directly elected by the Tibetan diaspora, a step in the Dalai Lama's long-stated goal of democratizing the exile polity. Lobsang Sangay, a Harvard-trained legal scholar, won the 2011 election and became the last Kalon Tripa and, upon the 2012 renaming of the office, the first Sikyong. He was re-elected in 2016. In the 2021 general election, Penpa Tsering, a former speaker of the Tibetan Parliament-in-Exile, was elected Sikyong.

Role

The Sikyong is the head of the Kashag (cabinet) and effectively the head of government of the CTA, overseeing departments responsible for education, health, finance, home affairs, security, religion and culture, and information and international relations for roughly 130,000 Tibetans in exile. The office-holder serves a five-year term and may serve at most two terms. Elections are held across the global Tibetan diaspora.
