Kildare (Irish: Cill Dara, meaning "church of the oak") is a town in County Kildare, Ireland. One of Ireland's oldest towns, it grew around the monastery founded by Saint Brigid in the 5th century and lies about 50 km west of Dublin, on the edge of the Curragh plain.

History

According to tradition, St Brigid established a double monastery of monks and nuns at Kildare around 470 AD, beneath a great oak tree that gave the place its name. The settlement became one of the great monastic towns of early medieval Ireland, seat of a bishopric and renowned for learning and for the illuminated manuscript known as the Book of Kildare, now lost. A perpetual sacred fire tended by nuns was recorded as burning there into the medieval period, and a modern flame is maintained in Brigid's honour.

Landmarks

St Brigid's Cathedral, built by the Normans in the 13th century on the monastic site, stands beside one of Ireland's tallest accessible round towers. Nearby are the ruins of a small early church and St Brigid's Well. Just outside the town, the Irish National Stud — founded in 1900 by Lord Wavertree — and the Japanese Gardens, created in 1906–1910, draw many visitors, reflecting the county's deep association with horse breeding and racing on the Curragh.

The town today

Kildare is a market and commuter town on the Dublin–Cork railway line and the M7 motorway. The Kildare Village outlet shopping centre, opened in 2006, is a major retail attraction. The town gives its name to the surrounding county, though the county town is Naas.
