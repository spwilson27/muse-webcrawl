Mausoleum of the First Qin Emperor

The Mausoleum of the First Qin Emperor (Chinese: 秦始皇帝陵) is located in Lintong District, Xi'an, Shaanxi, China. It is the mausoleum of Qin Shi Huang, the first emperor of China, founder of the Qin dynasty (r. 221–210 BCE). Construction began in 246 BCE when Qin Shi Huang was just 13 years old, and the project is estimated to have involved 700,000 workers at its peak. It is famous as the site of the Terracotta Army.

The layout of the mausoleum is modelled on the Qin capital Xianyang, divided into inner and outer cities. The circumference of the inner city is 2.5 km (1.55 miles) and the outer is 6.3 km (3.9 miles). The tomb is located in the southwest of the inner city and faces east. The main tomb chamber housing the coffin and burial artifacts has not yet been excavated.

Historical accounts

The historian Sima Qian, writing about a century after the emperor's death, described the tomb in the Records of the Grand Historian: palaces and scenic towers for a hundred officials were constructed, the tomb was filled with rare artifacts, crossbows were rigged to shoot intruders, rivers of mercury simulated the Yangtze and Yellow Rivers, and the ceiling was inlaid with pearls depicting the heavenly constellations. Modern soil surveys have found anomalously high mercury concentrations at the mound, lending some support to the account.

Terracotta Army

In 1974, farmers digging a well discovered fragments of terracotta figures northeast of the mound. Excavations revealed pits containing an estimated 8,000 life-size soldiers, horses, and chariots arranged in battle formation to guard the emperor in the afterlife. The mausoleum complex was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1987.

Excavation status

The central burial chamber itself remains unexcavated, in part to preserve its contents from damage and in part because of the reported mercury hazards. Geophysical surveys suggest a large stepped-pyramid structure within the mound.
