Camp John Hay

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Camp John Hay is a former United States military installation in Baguio, Philippines, established in 1903 as a rest and recreation area for American soldiers and Department of War personnel. Named after U.S. Secretary of State John Hay, the 690-hectare hill station lies about 1,500 metres above sea level amid pine forests, long serving as a cool-climate retreat from the tropical lowlands.

History

The reservation was set aside by executive order of President Theodore Roosevelt in 1903. During the Second World War, Camp John Hay was the first place in the Philippines bombed by Japanese forces on December 8, 1941, and served as an internment camp for Allied civilians and later as headquarters of General Tomoyuki Yamashita, who formally surrendered there in September 1945. After the war it operated as John Hay Air Base, a major American recreation facility with a golf course — the only one in the Philippines designed in part with Jack Nicklaus involvement in its later redesign — together with cottages, a commissary, and broadcasting facilities of the Voice of America relay station.

Turnover and development

Following the expiry of the U.S.–Philippine Military Bases Agreement, the camp was turned over to the Philippine government in 1991 and placed under the Bases Conversion and Development Authority. Much of the reservation has since been developed as a tourism and commercial zone with hotels, a convention centre, restaurants, and residential leases, while preserving large areas of pine forest as a watershed. The historic Bell House and amphitheatre remain landmarks of Baguio.
