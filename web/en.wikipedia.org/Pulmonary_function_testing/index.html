Pulmonary function testing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Pulmonary function testing
Abbreviation: PFT
Specialty: Pulmonology
Components: spirometry, lung volumes, diffusing capacity (DLCO)
Used for: diagnosing and monitoring asthma, COPD, interstitial lung disease

Pulmonary function testing (PFT) is a complete evaluation of the respiratory system including patient history, physical examinations, and tests of pulmonary function. The primary purpose of pulmonary function testing is to identify the severity of pulmonary impairment and to distinguish patterns of disease — principally obstructive, restrictive and mixed defects.

Spirometry

The central test is spirometry, in which the patient breathes forcefully into a spirometer. Key measures include forced vital capacity (FVC), the volume forcibly exhaled after full inspiration; forced expiratory volume in one second (FEV1); and their ratio. A reduced FEV1/FVC ratio indicates airflow obstruction, as in asthma and COPD, while a reduced FVC with a preserved ratio suggests restriction. Improvement after a bronchodilator supports a diagnosis of asthma.

Other measurements

Lung volumes, including total lung capacity and residual volume, are measured by body plethysmography or gas dilution techniques and confirm true restriction. The diffusing capacity for carbon monoxide (DLCO) assesses gas transfer across the alveolar–capillary membrane and is reduced in emphysema and interstitial lung diseases but relatively preserved in asthma.

Interpretation

Results are compared with predicted values from reference equations derived from healthy populations, adjusted for age, sex, height and ancestry. Guidelines from the American Thoracic Society and European Respiratory Society standardise technique, acceptability and interpretation.
