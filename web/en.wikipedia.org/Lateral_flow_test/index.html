Lateral flow test

A lateral flow test (LFT), also known as a lateral flow immunoassay or rapid test, is a simple paper-based device intended to detect the presence of a target substance in a liquid sample without specialised equipment. The best-known example is the home pregnancy test. Lateral flow tests are used in medicine, veterinary diagnostics, food safety, and environmental testing, and became globally prominent during the COVID-19 pandemic as rapid antigen tests.

Principles

A sample is applied to a sample pad at one end of a nitrocellulose strip. Capillary action draws the liquid along the strip, rehydrating dried reagents — typically antibodies conjugated to coloured particles such as colloidal gold or latex. If the target analyte is present, it binds the labelled antibodies, and the complex is captured at a test line of immobilised antibodies, producing a visible coloured line. A control line further along captures excess reagent and confirms the test has worked.

Formats and performance

In a sandwich assay, the signal increases with the amount of analyte; in a competitive assay, used for small molecules, the appearance of a line indicates absence of the target. Results are usually read within 5 to 30 minutes. Lateral flow tests are inexpensive, portable, and stable, but are generally less sensitive than laboratory methods such as PCR or ELISA, particularly early in an infection when antigen concentrations are low.

Applications

Beyond pregnancy tests and COVID-19 antigen tests, lateral flow devices detect infectious diseases such as malaria, HIV, dengue, and influenza, drugs of abuse, cardiac markers, allergens, and contaminants in food and water. During the COVID-19 pandemic, governments distributed billions of rapid antigen tests for home and workplace screening.
