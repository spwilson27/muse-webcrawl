Mie scattering

Mie scattering is the elastic scattering of electromagnetic radiation by a sphere whose diameter is comparable to or larger than the wavelength of the incident light. The theory, published by German physicist Gustav Mie in 1908, gives an exact analytical solution of Maxwell's equations for the scattering of a plane electromagnetic wave by a homogeneous sphere.

Theory

Mie's solution expands the incident, internal and scattered fields in vector spherical harmonics. The scattered field is described by two amplitude functions built from the Mie coefficients an and bn, which depend on the size parameter x = πd/λ (particle diameter d, wavelength λ) and the relative refractive index of the sphere. The series converges after roughly x + 4x1/3 + 2 terms.

Characteristics

Unlike Rayleigh scattering — the limiting case for particles much smaller than the wavelength — Mie scattering is only weakly dependent on wavelength and is strongly forward-peaked: most light is scattered in the direction of propagation, with a complex pattern of lobes and a glory near the backward direction. Because it is nearly wavelength-independent, Mie scattering by water droplets makes clouds and fog appear white.

Applications

The theory underpins laser particle-size measurement, atmospheric optics (haze, cloud droplets, aerosols), radar meteorology, biomedical optics, and the design of pigments and coatings. Modern implementations such as Wiscombe's MIEV0 code compute efficiencies for scattering, extinction and absorption, the asymmetry parameter and radiation pressure.
