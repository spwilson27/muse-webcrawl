The Deutsche Oper Berlin is an opera house and opera company located in the Charlottenburg district of Berlin, Germany. It is the second opera house in the city, alongside the Staatsoper Unter den Linden and the Komische Oper, and with roughly 1,865 seats it is Berlin's largest opera venue. The company is particularly noted for its Wagner and late-Romantic repertoire and for performances of works by composers such as Richard Strauss.

History

The original theatre, the Deutsches Opernhaus, opened on 3 November 1912 with Beethoven's Fidelio, conducted by Ignatz Waghalter. Built by the then-independent city of Charlottenburg partly in reaction against the perceived conservatism of the imperial court opera, it was renamed the Städtische Oper (Municipal Opera) in 1925. During the Nazi period the house came under Joseph Goebbels' propaganda ministry and was renamed Deutsches Opernhaus again; on 23 November 1943 the building was destroyed in an air raid.

After the Second World War the company performed at the Theater des Westens. With Berlin divided, the ensemble became West Berlin's principal opera company and took the name Deutsche Oper Berlin. A new building by architect Fritz Bornemann, on the original Bismarckstraße site, opened on 24 September 1961 — shortly after the construction of the Berlin Wall — with Mozart's Don Giovanni.

Notable events

The Deutsche Oper is linked with a pivotal episode in West German history: on 2 June 1967, the student Benno Ohnesorg was shot by a police officer during demonstrations against the Shah of Iran, who was attending a performance of Mozart's The Magic Flute at the opera house that evening. Ohnesorg's death became a catalyst for the West German student movement and the formation of the group that called itself the Bewegung 2. Juni (June 2nd Movement).

Leadership

Post-war general music directors and chief conductors have included Ferenc Fricsay, Lorin Maazel, Jesús López Cobos, Giuseppe Sinopoli, Christian Thielemann, and Donald Runnicles (from 2009). Dietmar Schwarz served as Intendant (general director) from 2012. Sir Donald Runnicles' tenure and the house's chorus — repeatedly named "Chorus of the Year" in critics' polls — have been hallmarks of the modern company.
