United States Cyber Command (USCYBERCOM) is one of the eleven unified combatant commands of the United States Department of Defense. Headquartered at Fort George G. Meade, Maryland, it is responsible for military cyberspace operations: defending Department of Defense information networks, supporting combatant commanders with cyber capabilities, and defending the nation against significant cyberattacks when directed.

History

Secretary of Defense Robert Gates directed the creation of Cyber Command in June 2009. It achieved initial operational capability on 21 May 2010 under General Keith B. Alexander, who served concurrently as director of the National Security Agency (NSA) — a "dual-hat" arrangement that has continued since. Initially a sub-unified command under U.S. Strategic Command, USCYBERCOM built its forces around service components from the Army, Navy, Air Force, and Marine Corps.

The Cyber Mission Force — 133 teams of military and civilian personnel — reached full operational capability in May 2018. In August 2017, President Donald Trump announced the elevation of Cyber Command to a full unified combatant command, effective 4 May 2018, reflecting the growing importance of cyberspace as a warfighting domain alongside land, sea, air, and space.

Mission

USCYBERCOM's mission encompasses three main lines of effort:

  • Defend the Department of Defense Information Network (DODIN) — operating and securing DoD networks
  • Support combatant commanders — providing offensive and defensive cyber capabilities integrated into military plans
  • Defend the nation — when directed, conducting operations to counter significant cyberattacks against U.S. critical infrastructure, including under the "defend forward" strategy adopted in 2018

The command works closely with the NSA, the Department of Homeland Security's Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA), and the FBI on national cyber defense, and publicly attributes malicious cyber activity through joint advisories.

Organization

Service cyber components include Army Cyber Command (ARCYBER), Fleet Cyber Command/Tenth Fleet, Air Forces Cyber (Sixteenth Air Force), and Marine Corps Forces Cyberspace Command. The Cyber Mission Force consists of Cyber National Mission Teams, Cyber Combat Mission Teams, and Cyber Protection Teams.

Commanders

Commanders have included Keith Alexander (2010–2014), Michael S. Rogers (2014–2018), Paul M. Nakasone (2018–2024), and Timothy D. Haugh (from 2024), each dual-hatted as NSA director.
