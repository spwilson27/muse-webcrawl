The Battles of Lexington and Concord were the first military engagements of the American Revolutionary War, fought on April 19, 1775, within the towns of Lexington and Concord and the surrounding area in the Province of Massachusetts Bay. They marked the outbreak of armed conflict between the Kingdom of Great Britain and its thirteen colonies in America.

Background

General Thomas Gage, the British military governor of Massachusetts, ordered troops from Boston to seize colonial military stores at Concord. Forewarned by riders including Paul Revere and William Dawes, colonial militia assembled to resist.

The battles

At dawn in Lexington, about 77 militiamen under Captain John Parker faced the British advance guard; a shot — the famous "shot heard round the world" — was fired, and the ensuing volley killed eight militiamen. The British marched on to Concord and destroyed some stores, but militiamen from surrounding towns engaged them at the North Bridge. On the march back to Boston, thousands of militiamen ambushed the column from behind walls and trees, inflicting heavy casualties before the British were rescued by a relief force and reached safety.

Aftermath

By the next day, a colonial army of some 15,000 men besieged Boston. The Revolutionary War had begun in earnest; within two months the armies fought again at Bunker Hill. April 19 is commemorated in Massachusetts as Patriots' Day.
