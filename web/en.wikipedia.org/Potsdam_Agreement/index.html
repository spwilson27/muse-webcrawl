Potsdam Agreement

The Potsdam Agreement (German: Potsdamer Abkommen) was the agreement among the leaders of the three major Allied powers—the Soviet Union, the United Kingdom, and the United States—at the Potsdam Conference on 1 August 1945, following the end of World War II in Europe.

Terms

The agreement addressed the post-war order in Europe, the treatment of Germany, and reparations. Germany and Austria were to be divided into four occupation zones, controlled by the USSR, the UK, the US, and France, with Berlin likewise divided into four sectors. The agreement stipulated the demilitarization, denazification, democratization, and decentralization of Germany (the "four Ds"), along with decartelization of its economy.

The agreement established the Oder–Neisse line as the provisional western border of Poland, transferring former German territories east of the line to Poland and the Soviet Union. It also sanctioned the "orderly transfer" of German populations from Poland, Czechoslovakia, and Hungary, which led to the expulsion of millions of ethnic Germans. A Council of Foreign Ministers was created to prepare peace treaties, and an Allied Control Council would govern occupied Germany as a whole. On reparations, each occupying power would extract reparations from its own zone, with the USSR receiving additional transfers from the western zones.

Signatories

The participants were Joseph Stalin for the Soviet Union, Harry S. Truman for the United States, and Winston Churchill for the United Kingdom—though Churchill was replaced midway by Clement Attlee after the Conservative Party lost the 1945 general election. France was not represented at the conference but reserved its rights as an occupying power.

Legacy

The Potsdam Agreement shaped the division of Europe that hardened into the Cold War. Disagreements over reparations, the Polish border, and the administration of Germany contributed to the breakdown of Allied cooperation. The provisional status of the Oder–Neisse line was a source of tension until it was recognized by West Germany in 1970 and finally confirmed in 1990 by the Two Plus Four Agreement preceding German reunification.
