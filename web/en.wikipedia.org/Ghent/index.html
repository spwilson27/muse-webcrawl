Ghent (Dutch: Gent; French: Gand) is a city and municipality in the Flemish Region of Belgium. It is the capital and largest city of the province of East Flanders, and the third largest in the country, after Brussels and Antwerp. The city originally started as a settlement at the confluence of the Rivers Scheldt and Leie, and in the Late Middle Ages became one of the largest and richest cities of northern Europe, with some 50,000 inhabitants in 1300.

The municipality comprises the city of Ghent proper and the surrounding suburbs and villages. The metropolitan area covers the city and a number of surrounding municipalities and has a population of around 700,000, making it one of the largest metropolitan areas in Belgium.

History

Archaeological evidence shows human presence in the region of the confluence of the Scheldt and Leie going back as far as the Stone Age and the Iron Age. Most historians believe that the older name for Ghent, 'Ganda', is derived from the Celtic word ganda, meaning "confluence". In the Middle Ages Ghent became the largest city in Europe after Paris, thanks to its flourishing cloth industry and its trade connections. The counts of Flanders had a castle in the city, the Gravensteen, which still stands today.

In 1539 the city rebelled against the rule of Charles V, Holy Roman Emperor, and the Ghent Calvinist Republic was established in 1577 before the city was recaptured by Spanish forces. Ghent declined in the 17th and 18th centuries but revived with the mechanisation of the textile industry in the 19th century; the first mechanical weaving workshop on the European mainland was established there by Lieven Bauwens.

Architecture and sights

Much of the city's medieval architecture remains intact and remarkably well preserved. The skyline is dominated by the three towers of Saint Bavo's Cathedral (home to the Ghent Altarpiece by Jan van Eyck), the Belfry of Ghent — a UNESCO World Heritage Site — and Saint Nicholas' Church. Other landmarks include the Gravensteen castle, the Graslei and Korenlei quays along the Leie, and the medieval port area.

Culture and education

Ghent is home to Ghent University, founded in 1817 and one of the largest Dutch-speaking universities in Belgium, as well as several museums, an opera house, and the Flanders Expo complex. The city hosts the Gentse Feesten, a large cultural festival held every July. Ghent has pursued ambitious sustainability policies and is known for its car-free city centre, the largest low-traffic pedestrian zone in Belgium.

Economy

The Port of Ghent, located in the north of the city, is the third largest port of Belgium. Major employers include steel producer ArcelorMittal, Volvo Cars' Ghent plant, and numerous research institutes and biotech firms associated with the university.
