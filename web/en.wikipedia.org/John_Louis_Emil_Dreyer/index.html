John Louis Emil Dreyer

Born13 February 1852, Copenhagen, Denmark
Died14 September 1926, Oxford, England
FieldsAstronomy, history of astronomy

John Louis Emil Dreyer (13 February 1852 – 14 September 1926) was a Danish astronomer who compiled the New General Catalogue of Nebulae and Clusters of Stars (NGC), still the standard catalogue whose "NGC" numbers designate thousands of galaxies, nebulae and star clusters.

Career in Ireland

Dreyer studied in Copenhagen and in 1874 moved to Ireland as assistant to Lord Rosse at Birr Castle, observing with the great 72-inch "Leviathan of Parsonstown" telescope. In 1878 he became assistant at Dunsink Observatory, Dublin, and from 1882 to 1916 he was director of Armagh Observatory. His observations there included stellar spectroscopy and work on the Birr nebulae observations.

The New General Catalogue

Commissioned by the Royal Astronomical Society to update John Herschel's General Catalogue (1864), Dreyer published the New General Catalogue in 1888, listing 7,840 objects, followed by two Index Catalogues (1895 and 1908) adding over 5,300 more. Incorporating observations from William and John Herschel and many others — with errors he carefully checked against the Birr records — the NGC remains in daily use by astronomers alongside the Messier catalogue.

History of astronomy

Dreyer was also a leading historian of astronomy. He edited the collected works of Tycho Brahe (15 volumes) and wrote his biography (1890), and authored A History of Astronomy from Thales to Kepler (1906, originally History of the Planetary Systems from Thales to Kepler), a standard work for decades. He won the Gold Medal of the Royal Astronomical Society in 1916 and served as its president (1923–25). The lunar crater Dreyer is named for him. He retired to Oxford, where he died in 1926.
