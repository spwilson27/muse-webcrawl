Full titleThe Omnivore's Dilemma: A Natural History of Four Meals

The Omnivore's Dilemma: A Natural History of Four Meals is a 2006 non-fiction book by American author Michael Pollan. It investigates how modern Americans decide what to eat, given that, as omnivores, they can eat almost anything — the "omnivore's dilemma" identified by psychologist Paul Rozin.

Contents

Pollan traces four meals through four food chains: the industrial (a fast-food meal based on corn), the industrial organic, the local sustainable (a meal from a self-sufficient farm in Virginia), and the personal (a meal hunted, gathered, and grown by the author himself). The book examines the dominance of corn in the industrial food system and critiques factory farming and industrial agriculture.

Reception

The book was a bestseller, was named one of the ten best books of 2006 by The New York Times, and influenced wider public debate about food systems. A young readers' edition appeared in 2009.
