Victorian gold rush

The Victorian gold rush was a period in the history of Victoria, Australia, roughly between 1851 and the late 1860s, during which the newly separated colony became one of the richest gold-producing regions in the world. Gold was discovered at Clunes, Ballarat, Bendigo, Castlemaine and many other fields, drawing hundreds of thousands of prospectors from Britain, China, North America and continental Europe.

Impact

Victoria's population rose from about 77,000 in 1851 to over 500,000 within a decade, and Melbourne grew into a boom city — "Marvellous Melbourne". The rush funded railways, public buildings and institutions, and accelerated demands for democratic reform. Resentment over the expensive miner's licence culminated in the Eureka Rebellion at Ballarat in December 1854, a defining event in Australian democratic history. Deep-lead and quartz mining later gave way to company mining, and towns such as Ballarat and Bendigo retain rich gold-rush architecture.
