West Side Story

Broadway premiere: 26 September 1957

West Side Story is an American musical with music by Leonard Bernstein, lyrics by Stephen Sondheim, a book by Arthur Laurents and direction and choreography by Jerome Robbins. Inspired by Shakespeare's Romeo and Juliet, it transposes the feuding families to rival street gangs — the white Jets and the Puerto Rican Sharks — on the Upper West Side of New York City in the 1950s.

Plot and songs

Tony, a former Jet, falls in love with Maria, the sister of the Sharks' leader Bernardo. Their romance is destroyed by gang violence, ending with Tony's death. Famous numbers include "Maria", "Tonight", "America", "Somewhere" and "Gee, Officer Krupke".

Adaptations

The 1961 film adaptation, directed by Robert Wise and Robbins, won ten Academy Awards including Best Picture, with Rita Moreno as Anita. A new film directed by Steven Spielberg, with a screenplay by Tony Kushner, was released in 2021 to critical acclaim.

Legacy

The musical is widely considered a landmark of American theatre for its integration of music, dance and drama and its treatment of immigration, prejudice and urban violence.
