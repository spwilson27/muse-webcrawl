Steven Paul Jobs (24 February 1955 – 5 October 2011) was an American businessman, inventor and investor, best known as the co-founder (with Steve Wozniak and Ronald Wayne) of Apple Inc. As Apple's chief executive and chairman, he oversaw the development of the Apple II, the Macintosh, the iPod, iPhone and iPad, products that transformed the personal computing, music, mobile phone and tablet industries.

Career

Jobs and Wozniak founded Apple in 1976 in Los Altos, California. The Apple II became one of the first mass-produced personal computers; the Macintosh (1984), inspired by the graphical user interface Jobs saw at Xerox PARC, introduced the mouse-driven GUI to a mass market. Forced out of Apple in 1985 after a boardroom struggle, Jobs founded NeXT, whose computer workstations and software would later form the basis of macOS, and bought the graphics group that became Pixar, producing hit animated films beginning with Toy Story (1995).

Return to Apple

Apple bought NeXT in 1997 and Jobs returned as interim CEO, reviving the company with the iMac, then the iPod (2001), iTunes Store (2003), iPhone (2007) and iPad (2010). Under his leadership Apple became the world's most valuable publicly traded company.

Death and legacy

Jobs was diagnosed with a pancreatic neuroendocrine tumour in 2003 and resigned as CEO in August 2011, dying that October. He is remembered as a visionary product designer and marketer, and for his intense, demanding management style and his philosophy of integrating technology with the liberal arts.
