Internuclear ophthalmoplegia

Internuclear ophthalmoplegia
SpecialtyOphthalmology, Neurology
SymptomsImpaired adduction of one eye, nystagmus of the abducting eye, diplopia
CausesLesion of the medial longitudinal fasciculus; multiple sclerosis, brainstem stroke

Internuclear ophthalmoplegia (INO) is a disorder of conjugate lateral gaze in which the affected eye shows impairment of adduction (movement toward the nose) during horizontal eye movements. When the patient attempts to look to the opposite side, the eye on the side of the lesion fails to adduct fully, while the other eye abducts with nystagmus. Convergence is usually preserved, distinguishing INO from a medial rectus palsy.

Cause

INO results from a lesion of the medial longitudinal fasciculus (MLF), the tract connecting the abducens nucleus of one side with the oculomotor nucleus of the other, which coordinates conjugate horizontal gaze. In younger patients bilateral INO is most commonly due to multiple sclerosis; in older patients a unilateral INO is more often caused by a brainstem infarction. Other causes include tumours, infection and trauma.

Related syndromes

The combination of an INO with an ipsilateral horizontal gaze palsy is known as the "one-and-a-half syndrome". Wall-eyed bilateral internuclear ophthalmoplegia (WEBINO) describes bilateral INO with exotropia in primary gaze.

Symptoms and treatment

Patients may complain of horizontal double vision, blurring when looking to the side, or no symptoms at all. Treatment is directed at the underlying cause; prisms or strabismus surgery may help persistent diplopia.
