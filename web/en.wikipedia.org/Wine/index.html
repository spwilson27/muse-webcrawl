Wine

Wine is an alcoholic drink made from fermented fruit, most commonly grapes. Yeast converts the sugars in the juice into ethanol and carbon dioxide, releasing heat in the process. Differences in grape variety, climate and soil (terroir), and winemaking technique produce the enormous diversity of wines: still, sparkling, fortified, red, white, and rosé.

History

The earliest evidence of winemaking comes from Georgia around 6000 BC, with early production also in Iran, Armenia and the Balkans. Wine spread through the Mediterranean with Phoenician, Greek and Roman trade; the Romans planted vineyards across Europe, including in regions — France, Spain, Germany and Italy — that still dominate production. In the modern era, "New World" regions such as California, Australia, Chile, Argentina and South Africa became major producers.

Production

Grapes are harvested, crushed, and fermented in stainless steel, concrete or oak vessels. Red wines ferment with their skins, extracting colour and tannins; white wines are usually pressed before fermentation. Many wines are aged before bottling. Major grape varieties include Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Pinot noir, Chardonnay, Sauvignon blanc and Riesling.

Culture and consumption

Wine plays central roles in cuisine, religion, and celebration in many cultures. It is also an economic commodity and a collectible; fine wines are traded at auction and stored as investments. Health effects of moderate consumption remain debated, and alcohol is a known carcinogen, so public-health guidance increasingly emphasises moderation or abstinence.
