Solder

Solder is a fusible metal alloy used to create a permanent bond between metal workpieces, a process called soldering. Solder melts at a lower temperature than the parts being joined, wets their surfaces, and solidifies to form a mechanical and (in electronics) electrical connection.

Types

Soft solders melt below about 450 °C. Traditional electronics solder is a tin–lead alloy such as 63/37 Sn-Pb (melting point 183 °C), but health and environmental rules — notably the EU RoHS directive — drove adoption of lead-free solders such as SAC305 (tin–silver–copper). Plumbing solders are also now lead-free in most jurisdictions.

Forms and use

Solder is supplied as wire (often with a flux core), bar, paste, and preforms. Flux removes oxides and promotes wetting. Hard solders and brazing alloys, melting at higher temperatures, are used for jewellery, refrigeration, and structural joints.
