Placentia

Placentia may refer to several places and historical subjects. Most prominently, Placentia is a town in the Canadian province of Newfoundland and Labrador, located on the Avalon Peninsula at the eastern end of Placentia Bay. Founded by French colonists as Plaisance in the early 1660s, it served as the capital of the French colony of Newfoundland until it was ceded to Britain under the Treaty of Utrecht in 1713. The town's strategic harbour made it an important fishing and defensive settlement; the ruins of Fort Louis and Castle Hill recall its colonial past.

The name also recalls Placentia, the Latin name of Piacenza, a city in the Emilia-Romagna region of northern Italy, founded as a Roman colony in 218 BC. It is also the name of a neighbourhood and former municipality in Orange County, California: the city of Placentia, California, incorporated in 1926, whose name derives from the Latin word for "pleasant".

Other uses

  • Placentia Bay, a large bay on the southeast coast of Newfoundland.
  • The Battle of Placentia (1692), a French naval engagement during the Nine Years' War.
  • The Palace of Placentia, an English royal palace at Greenwich, London, built in the fifteenth century and the birthplace of Henry VIII and Elizabeth I.
