Gunship

A helicopter gunship of the type developed during the Vietnam War era.

A gunship is a military aircraft armed with heavy aircraft cannons, machine guns, rockets, and missiles intended for attacking ground targets. The term is most commonly applied to fixed-wing aircraft such as the Lockheed AC-130 and to heavily armed attack helicopters such as the AH-64 Apache and the Mil Mi-24.

History

The modern gunship concept emerged during the Vietnam War, when the United States Air Force converted C-47 transport aircraft into the AC-47 "Spooky," mounting side-firing miniguns that could orbit a target and deliver sustained fire. Its success led to larger conversions, including the AC-119 and the AC-130 Spectre/Spooky, which carried combinations of 20 mm cannons, 40 mm Bofors guns, and a 105 mm howitzer.

At the same time, helicopters were increasingly fitted with door guns, rocket pods, and turreted weapons. Dedicated attack helicopters such as the Bell AH-1 Cobra, introduced in 1967, became the archetypal helicopter gunship, a role later filled by the AH-64 Apache.

Characteristics

Fixed-wing gunships typically fire from the left side of the aircraft while flying a pylon turn around the target, allowing continuous fire on a fixed point. They are valued for close air support of ground troops, convoy escort, and perimeter defense, particularly at night. Their large size and relatively slow speed make them vulnerable to modern air defenses, so they are generally employed where friendly forces have air superiority.

Examples

  • Lockheed AC-130 – United States fixed-wing gunship
  • Bell AH-1 Cobra – first dedicated attack helicopter
  • Boeing AH-64 Apache – modern attack helicopter
  • Mil Mi-24 – Soviet/Russian large attack helicopter
