Datagram

A datagram is a basic transfer unit associated with a packet-switched network. Datagrams are typically structured in header and payload sections, and provide a connectionless communication service across the network: each datagram is routed independently, with no prior setup of a path, and delivery, ordering and duplication are not guaranteed by the datagram service itself.

Origin and use

The term derives from "data" and "telegram" and was adopted in the 1970s in the work of Louis Pouzin's CYCLADES network and adopted by Vint Cerf and others for the Internet protocols. In the Internet Protocol (IP), the unit of transfer is formally called a packet but is often termed an IP datagram. The User Datagram Protocol (UDP) explicitly offers a datagram service to applications, in contrast to TCP's reliable byte-stream.

Properties

Because each datagram carries complete addressing information and is forwarded hop by hop, datagrams of one message may travel different routes and arrive out of order or not at all. Reliability, when needed, must be added by higher layers or the application — an approach central to the end-to-end principle of Internet design.
