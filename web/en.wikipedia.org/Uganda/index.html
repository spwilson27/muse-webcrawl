Uganda

Uganda, officially the Republic of Uganda, is a landlocked country in East Africa, bordered by Kenya to the east, South Sudan to the north, the Democratic Republic of the Congo to the west, Rwanda to the southwest, and Tanzania to the south. The southern part of the country includes a substantial part of Lake Victoria, shared with Kenya and Tanzania. The capital and largest city is Kampala. Uganda has a population of roughly 46 million, and its official languages are English and Swahili, with Luganda widely spoken in the central region.

History

The area was home to Bantu-speaking kingdoms including Buganda, Bunyoro, and Ankole. Britain established a protectorate over Buganda in 1894, extending it over the region that took its name from the Swahili rendering of Buganda. Uganda became independent on 9 October 1962, with Milton Obote as prime minister; he was overthrown by Idi Amin in 1971, whose brutal eight-year rule killed hundreds of thousands before he was ousted by Tanzanian forces and Ugandan exiles in 1979. After a period of instability and civil war, Yoweri Museveni's National Resistance Movement took power in 1986; Museveni remains president, with constitutional term and age limits having been removed.

Geography and economy

Though landlocked and astride the equator, Uganda's elevation gives it a moderate climate. The country is rich in natural resources — fertile land, regular rainfall, deposits of copper and gold, and more recently discovered oil reserves near Lake Albert. Agriculture employs most of the workforce, with coffee the chief export. Uganda is known for its biodiversity, including mountain gorillas in Bwindi Impenetrable National Park, and Winston Churchill popularized its description as the "Pearl of Africa."
