The Arab–Israeli conflict is a long-running political and military conflict between Israel and various Arab states and Palestinian and other Arab movements, rooted in competing national claims to the land between the Jordan River and the Mediterranean Sea. It grew out of the rise of Zionism and Arab nationalism in the late 19th and early 20th centuries under Ottoman and then British rule.

History

After the 1947 United Nations Partition Plan and the end of the British Mandate, the 1948 Arab–Israeli War followed Israel's declaration of independence, resulting in Israeli victory, the displacement of hundreds of thousands of Palestinians (the Nakba), and armistice lines that left the West Bank under Jordan and Gaza under Egypt. Subsequent major wars included the Suez Crisis of 1956, the Six-Day War of 1967—in which Israel captured the West Bank, Gaza Strip, East Jerusalem, the Golan Heights, and Sinai—and the Yom Kippur War of 1973.

Peace efforts and later developments

Egypt and Israel signed a peace treaty in 1979, and Jordan followed in 1994. The Oslo Accords of the 1990s established the Palestinian Authority but did not resolve final-status issues such as borders, Jerusalem, refugees, and settlements. The conflict has continued through intifadas, wars in Gaza, and regional involvement by actors such as Hezbollah and Iran. In 2020 the Abraham Accords normalised relations between Israel and the United Arab Emirates, Bahrain, and later Morocco and Sudan.
