Dinorwig Power Station

CountryWales, United Kingdom
LocationLlanberis, Gwynedd
TypePumped-storage hydroelectricity
Capacity1,728 MW

Dinorwig Power Station is a pumped-storage hydroelectric scheme near Llanberis in Snowdonia, north Wales. With an installed capacity of 1,728 megawatts, it can deliver maximum power in about 12 seconds and was the largest scheme of its kind in Europe when commissioned.

Construction

Built between 1974 and 1984 inside the disused Dinorwig slate quarry, the station's machine hall is one of the largest artificial caverns in the world. Water is held in the Marchlyn Mawr reservoir high above and released down to Llyn Peris below.

Operation

The station pumps water uphill when electricity demand and prices are low, and generates during peaks, providing rapid-response reserve capacity and frequency control for the National Grid. It is nicknamed "Electric Mountain", and a visitor centre of that name operated nearby.
