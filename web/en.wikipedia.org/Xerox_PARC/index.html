Xerox PARC

PARC (formerly Xerox PARC, the Palo Alto Research Center) is a research and development company in Palo Alto, California, founded in 1970 as a division of Xerox Corporation. It is famed for inventing or pioneering many foundations of modern computing — the graphical user interface, the computer mouse as popularised with windows and icons, Ethernet, laser printing, and object-oriented programming — yet is equally famous as a case study in a parent company failing to commercialise its own inventions.

The Alto and the modern interface

Under physicist George Pake and computer-science laboratory head Bob Taylor, PARC assembled an extraordinary team. In 1973 they built the Alto, arguably the first personal computer designed around a bitmapped display, mouse and graphical user interface, networked via Robert Metcalfe and David Boggs's new Ethernet. Alan Kay's Learning Research Group developed Smalltalk, the pioneering object-oriented language, embodying Kay's Dynabook vision. Other inventions included the laser printer (Gary Starkweather), Bravo, the first WYSIWYG text editor (Charles Simonyi), and contributions to TCP/IP networking through the PARC Universal Packet system.

Diffusion of the technology

Xerox launched the expensive Star workstation in 1981 but failed to exploit the personal-computing market. Steve Jobs's visits to PARC in 1979 — in exchange for Xerox's right to buy pre-IPO Apple shares — inspired the Apple Lisa and Macintosh. Former PARC researchers founded influential companies: Adobe (Warnock and Geschke), 3Com (Metcalfe) and others. Xerox spun PARC off as a subsidiary in 2002; in 2023 Xerox donated PARC to SRI International.
