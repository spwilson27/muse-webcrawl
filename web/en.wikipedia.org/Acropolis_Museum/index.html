Acropolis Museum

Location: Dionysiou Areopagitou Street, Athens, Greece
Opened: 20 June 2009
Architect: Bernard Tschumi with Michael Photiadis
Collection size: over 4,250 objects

The Acropolis Museum is an archaeological museum in Athens, Greece, housing the finds of the Acropolis of Athens and its slopes. It opened to the public on 20 June 2009, replacing a much smaller museum on the Acropolis rock itself. The building stands about 300 metres southeast of the Parthenon, on the Makrygianni archaeological site.

Building

Designed by Swiss-American architect Bernard Tschumi with Greek architect Michael Photiadis, the museum is a structure of glass, steel, and concrete organised on three levels. Glass floors on the ground level reveal the excavated remains of an ancient Athenian neighbourhood beneath the building, which remain visible to visitors. The top floor — the Parthenon Gallery — is rotated 23 degrees to match the orientation of the Parthenon itself and is wrapped in glass, giving a direct line of sight to the temple.

Collections

The galleries are arranged chronologically, from finds of the slopes of the Acropolis, through Archaic sculpture (including the Moschophoros and the korai), to the Classical masterpieces: the frieze, metopes, and pedimental sculpture of the Parthenon, the Erechtheion's Caryatids (displayed at ground level since 1979 to protect them from pollution), and works from the Propylaea, the Temple of Athena Nike, and the Roman and early Christian periods.

Parthenon marbles debate

The museum was built in part to answer the British Museum's argument that Athens had no suitable place to display the Parthenon sculptures removed by Lord Elgin in the early 19th century. In the Parthenon Gallery, original blocks are displayed alongside plaster casts of those held in London, making Greece's case for reunification visible in the arrangement itself.
