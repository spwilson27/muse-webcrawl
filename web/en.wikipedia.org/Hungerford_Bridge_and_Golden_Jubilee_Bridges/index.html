Hungerford Bridge and Golden Jubilee Bridges

The Hungerford Bridge crosses the River Thames in London, between Waterloo Bridge and Westminster Bridge. It is a steel truss railway bridge carrying trains into Charing Cross station, flanked on either side by pedestrian crossings known as the Golden Jubilee Bridges.

History

The crossing takes its name from Hungerford Market, which stood near the north bank. The first Hungerford Bridge, opened in 1845, was a suspension footbridge designed by Isambard Kingdom Brunel; its chains were later reused for the Clifton Suspension Bridge in Bristol. The present railway bridge, designed by Sir John Hawkshaw, opened in 1864 to serve the South Eastern Railway's new Charing Cross terminus, with a footway along its eastern side.

Golden Jubilee Bridges

The flanking pedestrian bridges were built as part of a competition-winning design by architects Lifschutz Davidson, with engineering by WSP, and opened in 2002; they were named in honour of the Golden Jubilee of Elizabeth II in that year. Supported by distinctive inclined masts and fan-like arrays of steel stays tied to the railway bridge piers, the footbridges replaced the narrow and widely disliked former walkway and won several design awards, including the Royal Fine Art Commission's Building of the Year in 2003.

Location

The bridge connects Charing Cross on the north bank with the South Bank cultural quarter near the Royal Festival Hall and the London Eye.
