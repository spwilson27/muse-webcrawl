Moscow is the capital and largest city of Russia, situated on the Moskva River in European Russia. With about 13 million residents within the city limits and some 20 million in its metropolitan area, it is among the largest cities in Europe and the political, economic, cultural, and scientific centre of Russia. It is home to the Kremlin, the fortified seat of the Russian government, and to landmarks such as Red Square and Saint Basil's Cathedral.

History

Moscow is first mentioned in chronicles in 1147, when Prince Yuri Dolgorukiy invited his ally to "come to me, brother, in Moscow". From a minor frontier town it rose under Daniel I and his successors as the centre of the Grand Duchy of Moscow, gradually absorbing rival Russian principalities and throwing off Mongol suzerainty (conventionally by 1480, the stand on the Ugra River). Ivan III and Ivan the Terrible made it capital of a centralised tsardom; the city survived the Time of Troubles and Polish occupation (1610–1612) but lost the capital to Peter the Great's new St Petersburg in 1712.

Moscow burned during Napoleon's occupation in 1812, was rebuilt, and became capital again in March 1918 when the Bolshevik government moved from Petrograd. The Nazi advance on Moscow was halted in the Battle of Moscow (1941–42). The city hosted the 1980 Summer Olympics, witnessed the crises of the Soviet collapse in 1991 and 1993, and expanded dramatically in 2012 with the annexation of "New Moscow" territory to the southwest.

Geography and climate

The city stands in the central East European Plain, on the bends of the Moskva River, whose course through the city totals about 80 km. Moscow has a humid continental climate: long, cold winters (January averages around −7 °C) and mild, sometimes hot summers (July averages around 19 °C).

Economy and transport

Moscow concentrates a large share of Russia's finance, industry, media, and technology; it is among the cities with the highest numbers of billionaires. The Moscow Metro, opened in 1935 and famed for its ornate Stalin-era stations, is one of the world's busiest; ring roads and several circle railway lines structure the city's radial growth. Four international airports (Sheremetyevo, Domodedovo, Vnukovo, Zhukovsky) serve the city.

Culture and landmarks

Major sights include the Kremlin and Red Square (a UNESCO World Heritage Site), Saint Basil's Cathedral (1555–61), the Bolshoi Theatre, the Tretyakov Gallery of Russian art, the Pushkin Museum, the rebuilt Cathedral of Christ the Saviour, the 540-metre Ostankino Tower, and the skyscrapers of the Moscow-City financial district. Moscow State University, founded in 1755, crowns the Sparrow Hills in one of the Stalinist "Seven Sisters" high-rises.
