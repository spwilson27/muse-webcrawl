The Río Negro massacres were a series of mass killings of Maya Achi villagers by the Guatemalan military and its civil patrols between 1980 and 1982, centered on the village of Río Negro in Baja Verapaz. More than 440 people were killed as the community resisted forced relocation for the Chixoy hydroelectric dam.

Background

Río Negro stood on the banks of the Chixoy River, in the basin to be flooded by the Chixoy dam, a project of Guatemala's national electricity institute financed by the World Bank and Inter-American Development Bank. Villagers who refused inadequate resettlement terms were branded guerrilla sympathizers at the height of the Guatemalan Civil War's counterinsurgency campaigns.

The massacres

In February 1980 soldiers killed villagers at a neighboring settlement after a confrontation; in March 1982, 70 women and 107 children from Río Negro were taken to the hill of Pacoxom and murdered, and in September 1982 a further massacre killed 92 people. Survivors fled to the mountains, enduring years of hunger and pursuit before resettling at Pacux. The village site was submerged by the reservoir.

Accountability

Guatemala's truth commission classified the killings as acts of genocide. Several civil patrollers were convicted in Guatemalan courts beginning in 1999, and in 2012 the Inter-American Court of Human Rights held the Guatemalan state responsible in the Río Negro Massacres v. Guatemala judgment, ordering reparations that survivors' organizations long pressed the dam's international financiers to support.
