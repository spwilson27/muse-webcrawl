Monument Avenue

Monument Avenue is a tree-lined grand avenue in Richmond, Virginia, long famous for its large statues of Confederate generals and other figures of the Confederacy placed along a broad grassy median. Extending westward from downtown Richmond into Henrico County, the avenue and its historic district are listed on the National Register of Historic Places and designated a National Historic Landmark District.

Monument Avenue's grassy median once held a succession of Confederate statues.

The monuments

The first monument, to Robert E. Lee, was unveiled in 1890 to a crowd of some 100,000. Statues of J.E.B. Stuart, Stonewall Jackson, Jefferson Davis and Matthew Fontaine Maury followed between 1907 and 1929. In 1996 a statue of Richmond-born African-American tennis champion Arthur Ashe was added, a controversial but ultimately symbolic broadening of the avenue's commemorations. On the Fourth of July, an annual reading of the Declaration of Independence tradition and Easter parade events have long been associated with the street.

Removal of the Confederate statues

After years of debate, the protests of 2020 following the murder of George Floyd made Monument Avenue a focal point: protesters toppled the Jefferson Davis statue and covered the monuments with graffiti. Mayor Levar Stoney ordered the city-owned statues removed, and the state-owned Lee monument — the avenue's largest — was removed in September 2021 after litigation reached the Virginia Supreme Court. The empty pedestals were later dismantled, leaving the Arthur Ashe monument and the avenue's historic homes and median landscape.
