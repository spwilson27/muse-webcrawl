Der Blaue Reiter

Der Blaue Reiter (The Blue Rider) was a group of artists and a German movement in modern art from 1911 to 1914, a fundamental part of Expressionism, along with Die Brücke ("The Bridge"), founded earlier in 1905. The group was formed by Russian and German artists including Wassily Kandinsky, Franz Marc, August Macke, Alexej von Jawlensky, Marianne von Werefkin, Gabriele Münter, and Paul Klee.

The cover of the Der Blaue Reiter almanac (1912), designed by Kandinsky

Origins and name

Kandinsky and Marc founded the group in Munich after breaking with the Neue Künstlervereinigung München over the rejection of Kandinsky's work. The name came from a shared love of horses and the colour blue: Marc favoured horses and the spirituality of blue, Kandinsky riders; Kandinsky had also painted a work called Der Blaue Reiter in 1903.

Exhibitions and almanac

The group held two exhibitions (1911–12 and 1912) that travelled in Germany, showing members alongside French and Russian avant-gardists. In May 1912 they published the Der Blaue Reiter Almanac, edited by Kandinsky and Marc, a landmark anthology of art, music, and theory — including Kandinsky's writings on the spiritual in art — illustrated with everything from Bavarian folk painting to children's drawings and works by Matisse and Picasso.

Dissolution and legacy

The First World War ended the group: Marc and Macke were killed in action, and the Russian members returned home. Its legacy was immense — it helped establish abstract art in Europe, and its members reunited in spirit in the 1920s as part of the Bauhaus circle (Die Blaue Vier with Lyonel Feininger).
