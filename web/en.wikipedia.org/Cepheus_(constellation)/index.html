Cepheus (constellation)

Cepheus
Genitive: Cephei
Brightest star: Alderamin
Notable: Delta Cephei, Garnet Star

Cepheus is a northern constellation named after Cepheus, king of Aethiopia in Greek mythology, husband of Cassiopeia and father of Andromeda. Listed by Ptolemy, it lies near the north celestial pole and is circumpolar from much of the northern hemisphere.

Stars

Its brightest star is Alderamin (Alpha Cephei), which will become a pole star around the year 7500 due to precession. Delta Cephei is the prototype of the Cepheid variable stars used to measure cosmic distances. Mu Cephei, Herschel's Garnet Star, is a huge red supergiant.

Deep-sky objects

Cepheus contains the Iris Nebula, the Elephant's Trunk Nebula and the Fireworks Galaxy. See Cassiopeia and Cepheid variable.
