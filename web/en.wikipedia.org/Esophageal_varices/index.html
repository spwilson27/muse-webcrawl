Esophageal varices

Esophageal varices are extremely dilated sub-mucosal veins in the lower third of the esophagus. They are most often a consequence of portal hypertension, commonly due to cirrhosis. Bleeding from esophageal varices is a life-threatening emergency.

Cause

Raised pressure in the portal venous system diverts blood through portosystemic collaterals, including the left gastric vein into esophageal veins. The thin-walled varices enlarge and may rupture.

Diagnosis and treatment

Varices are diagnosed by upper endoscopy. Acute bleeding is managed with vasoactive drugs, endoscopic band ligation, and antibiotics; prevention uses non-selective beta blockers or banding, and refractory cases may need a transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS).
