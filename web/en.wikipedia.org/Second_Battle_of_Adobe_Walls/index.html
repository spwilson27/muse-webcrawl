Second Battle of Adobe Walls

Date27 June 1874
LocationAdobe Walls, Texas Panhandle
ResultAttack repelled; trading post abandoned weeks later
BelligerentsBuffalo hunters and settlers vs. Comanche, Kiowa, Cheyenne, and Arapaho warriors

The Second Battle of Adobe Walls took place on 27 June 1874, when a force of several hundred Native American warriors attacked a small buffalo hunters' trading post called Adobe Walls in the Texas Panhandle. The attack was led in part by the Comanche leader Quanah Parker, inspired by the medicine man Isa-tai, who had promised that his medicine would protect the warriors from the hunters' bullets.

The Adobe Walls post, a handful of sod and picket buildings on the open plains.

The battle

Only 28 men and one woman were at the post when the attack began before dawn. Three of the defenders, including the saloon keeper, were caught sleeping in a wagon outside and killed in the opening moments. The rest took shelter in the stockaded buildings and, armed with long-range buffalo rifles such as the .50-calibre Sharps, held off repeated charges throughout the day. The battle became famous for the long shot fired by scout Billy Dixon, who reportedly knocked a mounted warrior from his horse at a distance later surveyed at about 1,538 yards (roughly nine-tenths of a mile).

Aftermath

After several days the attackers withdrew. Though the post's defenders won the fight, the hunters abandoned Adobe Walls in August 1874 because of continuing danger. The battle helped trigger the Red River War (1874–1875), the U.S. Army campaign that finally forced the Southern Plains tribes onto reservations. The first Battle of Adobe Walls had been fought a decade earlier, in 1864, under Kit Carson.
