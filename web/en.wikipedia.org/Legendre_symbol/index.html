Legendre symbol

In number theory, the Legendre symbol is a multiplicative function with values in {−1, 0, 1} that is a quadratic character modulo an odd prime number p: its value (a/p) is 1 if a is a quadratic residue modulo p (and not divisible by p), −1 if a is a quadratic non-residue, and 0 if p divides a. It was introduced by Adrien-Marie Legendre in 1797 or 1798 in the course of his work on the law of quadratic reciprocity.

Properties

Euler's criterion states that (a/p) ≡ a(p−1)/2 (mod p). The symbol is completely multiplicative in its top argument, periodic modulo p, and satisfies the law of quadratic reciprocity: for distinct odd primes p and q, (p/q)(q/p) = (−1)((p−1)(q−1)/4). Supplementary laws give the values of (−1/p) and (2/p).

Generalizations

The Legendre symbol generalizes to the Jacobi symbol for odd composite denominators, and further to the Kronecker symbol. It is a special case of a Dirichlet character modulo p.
