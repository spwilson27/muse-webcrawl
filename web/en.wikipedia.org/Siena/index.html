Siena

Region: Tuscany, Italy
Province: Siena
Known for: Piazza del Campo, Palio

Siena is a city in Tuscany, central Italy, capital of the province of Siena, famous for its remarkably preserved medieval centre, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and for the Palio, a horse race run twice yearly in the shell-shaped Piazza del Campo.

History

Legend ascribes Siena's founding to Senius, son of Remus; historically it was an Etruscan and then Roman settlement. As a free commune in the 12th–14th centuries it rivalled Florence in banking and trade, building Siena Cathedral and the Palazzo Pubblico with Ambrogio Lorenzetti's frescoes of good and bad government. The Black Death of 1348 devastated the city, and Siena eventually fell to Florence in 1559, becoming part of the Grand Duchy of Tuscany.

Culture

The city's seventeen contrade (districts) compete in the Palio. Siena is the birthplace of St. Catherine of Siena and preserves a major school of medieval painting, the Sienese School.
