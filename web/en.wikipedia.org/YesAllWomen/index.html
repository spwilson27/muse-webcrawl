YesAllWomen

#YesAllWomen is a Twitter hashtag and social media campaign in which users share examples or stories of misogyny and sexual harassment. First used in an online conversation in May 2014, the campaign became viral within days, with over a million tweets by the end of that month, and is credited with raising mainstream awareness of everyday sexism.

Origins

After the Isla Vista killings of 23 May 2014, in which Elliot Rodger murdered six people and left a manifesto of grievances against women, online debate revived the defensive phrase "not all men" (as in, not all men are like that). A Twitter user, anonymously known by the handle @gildedspine, proposed #YesAllWomen in response: the point was not that all men are perpetrators, but that all women have stories of harassment, fear or discrimination. She later asked to remain anonymous because of the harassment she herself received.

Spread and themes

Tweets described catcalling, groping, workplace discrimination, fear of walking alone at night, and the precautions women take daily (keys held between fingers, fake phone calls, avoiding eye contact). The hashtag trended worldwide and was widely covered by international media. It also drew criticism and parody hashtags such as #YesAllPeople, and debate about whether hashtag activism ("slacktivism") produces lasting change.

Legacy

#YesAllWomen is regarded as a precursor of the later #MeToo movement (2017), forming part of a wave of fourth-wave feminist digital activism in which personal testimony on social media became a form of protest.
