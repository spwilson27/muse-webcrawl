The McDonnell Douglas MD-11 is an American wide-body trijet airliner developed from the DC-10. It features a stretched fuselage, increased wingspan with winglets, a two-crew glass cockpit, and more powerful, fuel-efficient engines. Launched in 1986 and first flown in 1990, the MD-11 was produced in passenger (MD-11), combi (MD-11C), freighter (MD-11F) and extended-range (MD-11ER) variants.

Development and service

The MD-11 initially fell short of promised range and fuel-burn figures, which hurt sales against the Airbus A340 and Boeing 777; after McDonnell Douglas merged with Boeing in 1997, production continued only briefly, ending in 2001 after 200 aircraft. Passenger airlines — including Finnair, KLM, Swissair, Delta, American and Varig — progressively retired the type, KLM operating the last scheduled passenger flight in October 2014. The MD-11 found a durable second career as a freighter: FedEx Express, UPS Airlines and Lufthansa Cargo operated large fleets, prized for the type's payload and nose-loading cargo variants; the last cargo operators retired the type in the mid-2020s.

Design

The MD-11 retains the DC-10's tail-mounted third engine but has a smaller horizontal stabilizer with a fuel trim tank, contributing to demanding landing characteristics; its high approach speeds and sensitivity in the flare were factors in several hard-landing and hull-loss accidents, including FedEx Express Flight 80 at Narita in 2009. Swissair Flight 111, an MD-11 that crashed off Nova Scotia in 1998 after an in-flight fire fed by flammable insulation and wiring for the entertainment system, led to sweeping changes in aircraft materials certification.
