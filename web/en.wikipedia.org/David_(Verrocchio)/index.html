David (Verrocchio)

The David is a bronze statue of the biblical hero David by the Italian Renaissance artist Andrea del Verrocchio, created around 1473–1475. It depicts David as a slender, confident youth standing over the severed head of Goliath. The work is one of the most celebrated sculptures of the Florentine Renaissance and is now housed in the Museo Nazionale del Bargello in Florence.

Description

The statue shows David not as the mature king of Michelangelo's later version, but as a slight adolescent, dressed in a short leather tunic and skirt. He stands in a relaxed contrapposto, one hand on his hip, holding a short sword in the other, with the head of the giant Goliath between his feet. The figure's slight smile and poised bearing convey quiet triumph rather than violent exertion.

History

The sculpture was probably commissioned by the Medici family, Florence's dominant patrons of the arts. In 1476 the Florentine Signoria purchased the statue from Lorenzo and Giuliano de' Medici and placed it in the Palazzo della Signoria. Tradition holds that the model for the youthful David may have been the young Leonardo da Vinci, who was an apprentice in Verrocchio's workshop at the time, though this cannot be confirmed.

Significance

Verrocchio's David revived the classical tradition of the freestanding nude or lightly clad bronze figure and directly influenced later treatments of the subject, including Donatello's earlier bronze David and Michelangelo's marble colossus of 1501–1504. Together with Verrocchio's equestrian monument to Bartolomeo Colleoni in Venice, it established his reputation as the leading Florentine sculptor of his generation.
