Hyperglycemia

Hyperglycemia is a condition in which an excessive amount of glucose circulates in the blood plasma. This is generally a blood sugar level higher than 11.1 mmol/L (200 mg/dL), but symptoms may not start to become noticeable until even higher values such as 15–20 mmol/L (270–360 mg/dL). A subject with a consistent fasting blood glucose range between ~5.6 and ~7 mmol/L (100–126 mg/dL) is considered slightly hyperglycemic (prediabetes), while above 7 mmol/L (126 mg/dL) is generally held to indicate diabetes.

Causes

The most common cause is diabetes mellitus, in which the body either produces too little insulin or cannot use it effectively. Other causes include stress hyperglycemia during acute illness or trauma, pancreatic disease, endocrine disorders such as Cushing's syndrome and acromegaly, and medications including corticosteroids and some antipsychotics.

Symptoms

Short-term symptoms include increased thirst (polydipsia), frequent urination (polyuria), increased hunger (polyphagia), blurred vision, fatigue, headache and fruity-smelling breath. Sustained severe hyperglycemia can progress to diabetic ketoacidosis or hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state, both medical emergencies. Chronic hyperglycemia damages blood vessels and nerves, causing retinopathy, nephropathy, neuropathy and cardiovascular disease.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the cause and severity: insulin or oral hypoglycemic drugs, intravenous fluids for hyperglycemic crises, dietary management and monitoring of blood glucose and HbA1c.
