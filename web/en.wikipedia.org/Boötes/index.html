Boötes is a constellation in the northern sky, one of the 48 listed by the 2nd-century astronomer Ptolemy and among the 88 modern constellations. Its name derives from the Greek for "herdsman" or "ploughman", and it is often pictured driving the neighbouring hunting dogs, Canes Venatici, or following Ursa Major around the pole.

Stars

The constellation contains Arcturus, an orange giant that is the brightest star in the northern celestial hemisphere and the fourth-brightest in the night sky overall. Other named stars include Muphrid, Izar (a notable double star), Rho Boötis, and Nekkar. The kite- or diamond-shaped figure of Boötes is easy to find by following the arc of the Big Dipper's handle: "arc to Arcturus".

Deep-sky features

Boötes contains the Boötes Void, an enormous, roughly spherical region of space some 330 million light-years across containing very few galaxies, discovered in 1981. The constellation also hosts the globular cluster NGC 5466 and several galaxies, and the Quadrantid meteor shower radiates from its northern part each January.
