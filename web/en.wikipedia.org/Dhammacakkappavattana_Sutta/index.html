The Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta (Pali; Sanskrit: Dharmacakrapravartana Sūtra), the "Setting in Motion of the Wheel of the Dhamma Sutta", is a Buddhist text regarded as the record of the first teaching given by Gautama Buddha after his enlightenment. It was delivered in the Deer Park at Isipatana (Sarnath) to the five ascetics who had formerly practised austerities with him.

Contents

The Middle Way

The Buddha opens by rejecting two extremes to be avoided by one who has gone forth: indulgence in sensual pleasure, which is "low, vulgar, the way of ordinary people", and devotion to self-mortification, which is "painful, unworthy, unbeneficial". The path between them, which he has discovered, is the Noble Eightfold Path: right view, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness and right concentration.

The Four Noble Truths

The discourse then sets out the Four Noble Truths: there is suffering (dukkha); suffering arises from craving (samudāya); suffering can cease (nirodha); and the way to its cessation is the Eightfold Path (magga). Each truth is presented in three phases — knowledge of the truth, the task it entails, and the accomplishment of that task — making the "three turnings and twelve aspects" of the wheel of Dhamma.

Significance

On hearing the sermon, the ascetic Kondañña attained the "dustless, stainless Dhamma eye", becoming the first stream-enterer and, on ordination, the first monk of the Buddha's sangha. The day of the sermon is commemorated as Asalha Puja in Theravada countries. Parallels to the sutta survive in the Chinese Āgamas and in Sanskrit and Tibetan sources, though some modern scholars argue that the Four Noble Truths framework crystallised in the tradition over time.
