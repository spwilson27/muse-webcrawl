Differential topology

In mathematics, differential topology is the field dealing with the topological properties and smooth properties of smooth manifolds. In this sense differential topology is distinct from the closely related field of differential geometry, which concerns the geometric properties of smooth manifolds, including notions of size, distance, and shape.

Overview

The central objects are smooth manifolds and smooth maps between them. Because smooth structures are much more rigid than general topological structures, differential topology can draw on analytic tools such as derivatives, tangent spaces, and vector fields. Landmark results include the hairy ball theorem, the Poincaré–Hopf theorem, Sard's theorem on critical values, and Whitney's embedding theorem.

History

John Milnor's 1956 discovery of exotic spheres — smooth structures on the 7-sphere inequivalent to the standard one — revealed that the differentiable and topological categories diverge in surprising ways and opened modern differential topology. Later work by Milnor, Smale (the h-cobordism theorem), Thom (cobordism theory), and Donaldson transformed the field and connected it deeply with algebraic topology and gauge theory.
