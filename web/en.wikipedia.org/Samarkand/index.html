Samarkand

Samarkand (Uzbek: Samarqand) is a city in southeastern Uzbekistan and among the oldest continuously inhabited cities in Central Asia. A legendary stop on the Silk Road between China and the Mediterranean, it was the capital of the Sogdian region in antiquity, conquered by Alexander the Great in 329 BC, and in the fourteenth century became the capital of the empire of Timur (Tamerlane), whose grand mosques and madrasas made it one of the most celebrated cities of the Islamic world.

History

Samarkand was founded between the eighth and seventh centuries BC and prospered as Maracanda, capital of Sogdiana. Alexander the Great conquered it in 329 BC, reportedly declaring, "Everything I have heard about Maracanda is true, except that it is more beautiful than I had imagined." Arab armies took the city in the early eighth century AD; it flourished under the Samanids and successive Turkic dynasties, and its paper-making — learned, tradition says, from Chinese prisoners — spread the technology westward. Genghis Khan destroyed the city in 1220.

Timur made Samarkand his imperial capital after 1370, filling it with artisans and monumental buildings. His descendant Ulugh Beg turned it into a centre of astronomy and scholarship. The city later passed to the Shaybanids, then declined as trade routes shifted, before falling to the Russian Empire in 1868. It was the first capital of the Uzbek Soviet Socialist Republic (1924–1930).

Landmarks

The city's glory is the Registan, a public square framed by three madrasas (Ulugh Beg, Sher-Dor, and Tilya-Kori) with turquoise domes and tiled façades. Other monuments include the vast Bibi-Khanym Mosque, the Gur-e Amir mausoleum where Timur is buried, the Shah-i-Zinda necropolis of tiled tombs, and the remains of Ulugh Beg's observatory with its giant sextant. The ancient site of Afrasiyab preserves the older city destroyed by the Mongols.

Modern city

Modern Samarkand is a major centre of tourism, education, and industry in Uzbekistan, with a population mixing Uzbek and Tajik speakers. Its historic centre is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, inscribed in 2001.
