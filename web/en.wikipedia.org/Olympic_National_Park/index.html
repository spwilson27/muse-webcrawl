Olympic National Park

LocationOlympic Peninsula, Washington, United States
Area922,650 acres (3,733 km²)
Established29 June 1938
Administered byNational Park Service

Olympic National Park is a United States national park occupying most of Washington's Olympic Peninsula. It protects three distinctly different ecosystems in one park: the glacier-capped Olympic Mountains, centred on Mount Olympus (7,980 ft / 2,432 m); some of the finest remaining temperate rainforest in the contiguous United States, in the Hoh, Queets and Quinault valleys; and about 73 miles of wild, roadless Pacific coastline.

History

President Theodore Roosevelt created Mount Olympus National Monument in 1909, largely to protect the Roosevelt elk that bear his name. President Franklin D. Roosevelt signed the bill establishing Olympic National Park in 1938. UNESCO designated it an International Biosphere Reserve in 1976 and a World Heritage Site in 1981. In 1988 Congress designated about 95 percent of the park as wilderness, later named the Daniel J. Evans Wilderness.

Nature

The park's west side receives up to 14 feet (4.3 m) of rain a year, sustaining rainforests of Sitka spruce, western hemlock and bigleaf maple draped in mosses. Wildlife includes Roosevelt elk, black-tailed deer, black bears, mountain goats (an introduced species since removed in a major relocation programme), marbled murrelets and several endemic species such as the Olympic marmot. Rivers support salmon runs, and the Elwha River restoration — following the removal of two dams in 2011–2014, then the largest dam removal in history — has reconnected historic spawning habitat.

Visiting

Popular areas include Hurricane Ridge, with mountain views from a road-accessible summit; Lake Crescent; the hot springs of the Sol Duc valley; and coastal beaches at Rialto, Ruby and Kalaloch. There is no road across the park's interior, which remains largely trail-accessed wilderness.
