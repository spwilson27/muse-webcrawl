Domestic violence

Domestic violence is violence or other abuse that occurs in a domestic setting, such as in a marriage or cohabitation. It encompasses physical, sexual, emotional, economic, and psychological abuse, stalking, and coercive control by one person against another in an intimate relationship. Closely related is intimate partner violence, committed by a current or former partner or spouse.

Prevalence

The World Health Organization estimates that about one in three women worldwide has experienced physical or sexual violence, mostly by an intimate partner. Men are also victims, though at lower reported rates, and abuse occurs in same-sex relationships as well. Under-reporting is widespread because of stigma, fear, and economic dependence.

Patterns and effects

Researchers such as Lenore Walker described a cycle of abuse — tension building, acute incident, reconciliation, and calm — while later frameworks emphasise ongoing coercive control rather than discrete incidents. Health consequences include injury, chronic pain, depression, post-traumatic stress, and elevated risk of homicide; children exposed to domestic violence face developmental and psychological harms.

Responses

The modern shelter movement began in the 1970s with refuges such as Chiswick Women's Aid in London, followed by hotlines, protection orders, and specialised policing and prosecution. International law frames domestic violence as a human-rights violation, notably in the 1993 UN Declaration on the Elimination of Violence against Women and the Council of Europe's Istanbul Convention (2011). Prevention programmes focus on education, economic independence, and intervention with perpetrators.
