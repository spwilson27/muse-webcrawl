San Juan (Spanish: [saŋ ˈxwan], "Saint John") is the capital and most populous city of Puerto Rico, an unincorporated territory of the United States. Founded by Spanish colonists in 1521, it is the second-oldest European-established capital city in the Americas after Santo Domingo. The municipality had 342,259 residents at the 2020 census, while its metropolitan area holds roughly two million people, about two-thirds of the island's population.

History

Juan Ponce de León founded the original settlement of Caparra in 1508; in 1521 the colony moved to the site of present-day Old San Juan, whose natural harbor made it a key port of the Spanish Empire. The massive fortresses of Castillo San Felipe del Morro and Castillo San Cristóbal, built over the following centuries, defended the city against English and Dutch attacks. Puerto Rico was ceded to the United States after the Spanish–American War of 1898. Old San Juan's fortifications and La Fortaleza are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Geography and districts

The city lies on the island's northern coast. Old San Juan occupies a small islet at the entrance to San Juan Bay, connected by bridges to districts such as Santurce, Condado, Miramar, Hato Rey — the financial district known as the "Golden Mile" — and Río Piedras, home to the University of Puerto Rico's main campus.

Economy and culture

San Juan is Puerto Rico's principal seaport, financial center and tourism hub; the Port of San Juan is among the busiest cruise ports in the Caribbean. Landmarks include the Puerto Rico Capitol, the Cathedral of San Juan Bautista (containing Ponce de León's tomb), and the cobblestoned blue-brick streets of the old city.
