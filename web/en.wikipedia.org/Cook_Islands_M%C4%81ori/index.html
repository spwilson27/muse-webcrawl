Cook Islands Māori

Cook Islands Māori is an Eastern Polynesian language that is the official language of the Cook Islands. It is closely related to New Zealand Māori and Tahitian, though each is a distinct language. The Rarotongan dialect has become the standard form of the language, which is also called te reo Māori Kūki 'Āirani.

Dialects

Cook Islands Māori comprises several island dialects, including those of Rarotonga, Aitutaki, Mangaia, Atiu, Mauke, and Mitiaro, along with the more divergent varieties spoken on Penrhyn, Manihiki, and Rakahanga. Pukapukan, spoken in the northern group, is a distinct Samoic language rather than a dialect of Cook Islands Māori.

Status

Although it is an official language and is taught in schools, Cook Islands Māori is considered endangered: English dominates in government, media, and commerce, and many younger Cook Islanders — including the large diaspora in New Zealand — are more fluent in English. Revitalisation efforts include language nests, broadcasting, and documentation projects. See also Cook Islands and Polynesian languages.
