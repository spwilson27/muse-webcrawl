Peer-to-peer

Peer-to-peer (P2P) computing or networking is a distributed application architecture that partitions tasks or workloads between peers. Peers are equally privileged, equipotent participants in the network, and are said to form a peer-to-peer network of nodes. Peers make a portion of their resources — such as processing power, disk storage, or network bandwidth — directly available to other network participants, without the need for central coordination by servers or stable hosts.

History

While P2P systems had previously been used in many application domains, the architecture was popularized by the file sharing system Napster, originally released in 1999. The concept has inspired new structures and philosophies in many areas of human interaction. In such social contexts, peer-to-peer as a meme refers generally to egalitarian social networking that has emerged throughout society, enabled by Internet technologies in general.

Architecture

A peer-to-peer network is designed around the notion of equal peer nodes simultaneously functioning as both clients and servers to the other nodes on the network. This differs from the traditional client–server model where communication is usually to and from a central server. A typical example of a file transfer that uses the client–server model is the File Transfer Protocol (FTP) service in which the client and server programs are distinct.

Applications

P2P networks are used for sharing content files containing audio, video, data, or anything in digital format. They also underlie many distributed systems including blockchains and cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin, content delivery, and scientific computing projects.
