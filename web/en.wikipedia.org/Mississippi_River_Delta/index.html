Mississippi River Delta

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Mississippi River Delta is the area of land created by sediment deposited as the Mississippi River flows into the Gulf of Mexico in southeastern Louisiana. Over roughly the last 5,000 years, successive delta lobes built about 40,000 km² of coastal wetlands — one of the largest river deltas and wetland systems in North America.

The modern active delta, the Balize or "bird's foot" delta, extends past the Head of Passes where the river divides into several passes, including Southwest Pass and South Pass. It should not be confused with the Mississippi Delta, the inland floodplain region in northwestern Mississippi.

Formation

The Mississippi drains about 40 percent of the continental United States. As the river's current slows on reaching the Gulf, it drops its sediment load. Periodically the river has abandoned its channel and cut a shorter course to the sea, building a new delta lobe while the old one subsides; this cycle produced the overlapping lobes of the deltaic plain, including the Teche, St. Bernard, Lafourche, Plaquemines and Balize deltas.

Land loss

Since the 1930s Louisiana has lost roughly 5,000 km² of coastal land. Levees built for flood control and navigation prevent the river from depositing sediment across its floodplain, while canals, subsidence and sea-level rise allow salt water to intrude and erode marshes. The Atchafalaya River, a distributary capturing an increasing share of the river's flow, is building new land in Atchafalaya Bay — the only part of the coast currently gaining land naturally. Large-scale restoration plans propose sediment diversions to reconnect the river with its wetlands.

Ecology and economy

The delta's marshes, bayous and estuaries support major fisheries (shrimp, oysters, menhaden and blue crab), millions of migratory waterfowl on the Mississippi Flyway, and the Port of South Louisiana, among the largest bulk-cargo ports in the world. The communities of Plaquemines Parish and the city of New Orleans lie within the deltaic plain.
