Saint Christopher-Nevis-Anguilla

Saint Christopher-Nevis-Anguilla (or Saint Kitts-Nevis-Anguilla) was a British colony and later an associated state in the Caribbean, comprising the islands of Saint Christopher (Saint Kitts), Nevis, and Anguilla in the Leeward Islands. The three islands were administered together from the nineteenth century until Anguilla's secession in the late 1960s and 1970s; Saint Kitts and Nevis became independent as the Federation of Saint Kitts and Nevis in 1983, while Anguilla remains a British Overseas Territory.

History

Saint Kitts and Nevis were among the earliest English and French colonies in the Caribbean, settled in the 1620s, and Anguilla was colonised from Saint Kitts around 1650. In 1882 the three islands were formally joined as a single presidency within the federal colony of the Leeward Islands, an arrangement the Anguillans persistently resented, since they felt neglected by the administration in Basseterre and, before that, Antigua.

Anguilla's secession

When Saint Christopher-Nevis-Anguilla became a state in association with Britain in 1967, Anguillans, fearing domination by Saint Kitts, expelled the Kittitian police and declared independence unilaterally. After two referendums and a brief British intervention in 1969 ("Operation Sheepskin"), Anguilla was formally separated in 1971 and placed under direct British administration; it became a separate British dependent territory in 1980.

Independence

The remaining state of Saint Christopher and Nevis proceeded to full independence on 19 September 1983 as the Federation of Saint Kitts and Nevis, within the Commonwealth and with the British monarch as head of state, represented by a Governor-General. Nevis retained a right to secede by referendum under the constitution; a 1998 referendum on Nevisian independence fell short of the required two-thirds majority.
