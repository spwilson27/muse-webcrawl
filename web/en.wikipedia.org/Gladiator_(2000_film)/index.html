Gladiator is a 2000 epic historical drama film directed by Ridley Scott, starring Russell Crowe as Maximus Decimus Meridius, a betrayed Roman general who is sold into slavery and rises through the gladiatorial arena to avenge the murders of his family and his emperor. The film revitalized the sword-and-sandal epic genre, grossed about $460 million worldwide, and won five Academy Awards including Best Picture and Best Actor.

Plot

In AD 180, General Maximus leads Rome's armies to victory in Germania. The dying emperor Marcus Aurelius (Richard Harris) asks Maximus to succeed him and restore the Republic, but the emperor's son Commodus (Joaquin Phoenix) murders his father, seizes the throne, and orders Maximus and his family killed. Maximus escapes but finds his wife and son murdered; captured by slavers, he is trained as a gladiator by Proximo (Oliver Reed) in North Africa. Fighting his way to Rome's Colosseum, "the Spaniard" becomes a crowd favourite and reveals his identity to Commodus, vowing vengeance. After surviving staged combats and conspiracies, Maximus kills Commodus in a duel in the arena but dies of his wounds, reuniting in death with his family and ordering the restoration of the Republic as his dying act.

Production

The screenplay, originated by David Franzoni and revised by John Logan and William Nicholson, drew inspiration from Daniel P. Mannix's book Those About to Die and the 19th-century painting Pollice Verso by Jean-Léon Gérôme. Filming took place in England, Morocco and Malta, where a partial replica of the Colosseum was built; digital effects extended the arena and crowds. Oliver Reed died during production, and his remaining scenes were completed with body doubles and CGI. Hans Zimmer and Lisa Gerrard's score, including the elegiac "Now We Are Free," became one of the best-selling soundtracks of its era.

Reception and legacy

Critics praised the film's scale, battle sequences and Crowe's performance, though historians noted many inaccuracies — the historical Commodus reigned for over a decade and was not killed by a gladiator-general. Gladiator won the Academy Award for Best Picture and its success sparked a revival of ancient-world epics, including Troy (2004) and the series Rome. A sequel, Gladiator II, directed by Scott and starring Paul Mescal, was released in 2024.
