Grappling

Grappling is a category of martial arts techniques and combat sports focused on gripping, clinching, throwing, takedowns, joint locks, chokes and ground fighting, rather than striking. Grappling arts emphasise leverage, body positioning and control, aiming to bring an opponent to the ground and achieve a dominant position or a submission hold.

Techniques

Grappling techniques are commonly divided into clinch fighting, takedowns and throws, submission holds (joint locks and chokeholds), sweeps and reversals, escapes, and pinning or controlling techniques. Standing grappling seeks to control an opponent's posture and balance, while ground fighting works through positional hierarchies such as guard, mount and side control familiar from Brazilian jiu-jitsu.

Styles

Prominent grappling disciplines include the Olympic sports of freestyle and Greco-Roman wrestling and judo; Brazilian jiu-jitsu and its no-gi variant; Russian sambo; Japanese jujutsu; catch wrestling; and submission wrestling (submission grappling), in which competitors from different styles meet under rules centred on submissions. Grappling is also an essential component of mixed martial arts, where it complements striking and allows fights to be finished on the ground.

Competition

Modern grappling competition ranges from the Olympic wrestling and judo tournaments to events such as the ADCC Submission Fighting World Championship, the IBJJF World Jiu-Jitsu Championship and the FILA/UWW grappling world championships. Rule sets differ widely in whether points, pins or only submissions decide the outcome.
