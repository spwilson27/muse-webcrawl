Sanssouci is a historical building in Potsdam, near Berlin, built by Prussian king Frederick the Great as his summer palace between 1745 and 1747. Its name, from the French sans souci ("without worries"), reflects Frederick's wish for a private retreat where he could pursue philosophy, music, and conversation away from the Berlin court. The single-storey palace above terraced vineyards is the masterpiece of Frederician Rococo architecture.

Architecture and gardens

Designed by Georg Wenzeslaus von Knobelsdorff from Frederick's own sketches, the palace has an intimate scale: a single main floor of ten rooms, decorated with stucco, gilding, and paintings. The vineyard terraces below, laid out in 1744, form one of the most famous garden prospects in Europe. The wider Sanssouci Park later grew to include the New Palace, Charlottenhof, the Orangery, and numerous follies.

History

Frederick hosted figures of the Enlightenment at Sanssouci, most famously Voltaire, who lived there from 1750 to 1753. The king composed flute music and wrote philosophy in its rooms. He asked to be buried on the terrace beside his beloved greyhounds; the wish was only fulfilled in 1991, when his remains were reinterred there. After 1945 the palace became a museum; since 1990 it has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the Palaces and Parks of Potsdam and Berlin.
