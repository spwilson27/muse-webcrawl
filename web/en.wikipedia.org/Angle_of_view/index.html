Angle of view

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In photography, angle of view (AOV) describes the angular extent of a given scene that is imaged by a camera. It is used interchangeably with the more general term field of view. The angle of view depends on the focal length of the lens and the size of the image sensor or film format.

Calculation

For a rectilinear lens, the angle of view can be calculated from the chosen dimension d of the sensor and the effective focal length f: AOV = 2 arctan(d / 2f). Because a rectangular sensor has width, height and diagonal dimensions, a lens has three angles of view: horizontal, vertical and diagonal. A 50 mm lens on a full-frame camera gives a diagonal angle of view of about 46°, close to that of the human eye, which is why it is called a "normal" lens.

Types of lenses

Lenses with longer focal lengths have narrow angles of view and magnify distant subjects; these are telephoto lenses. Short focal lengths give wide angles of view: wide-angle lenses cover roughly 60°–100°, while fisheye lenses may reach 180° or more, at the cost of strong distortion. Zoom lenses allow the angle of view to be varied continuously.

Uses

Choosing an angle of view is a fundamental creative decision: wide angles exaggerate perspective and depth, while narrow angles compress space. The same concept applies in cinematography, video games (where it is usually called field of view) and virtual reality.
