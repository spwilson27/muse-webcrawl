International Maritime Organization

International Maritime Organization (IMO)
Founded: Convention adopted 1948; in force 1958
Headquarters: London, United Kingdom
Type: UN specialised agency
Membership: 175 member states

The International Maritime Organization (IMO) is a specialised agency of the United Nations responsible for measures to improve the safety and security of international shipping and to prevent and control pollution from ships.

History

The IMO Convention was adopted at a conference in Geneva in 1948, though it entered into force only in 1958 and the organisation first met in 1959. It was originally called the Inter-Governmental Maritime Consultative Organization (IMCO) before adopting its present name in 1982.

Conventions

The IMO's framework includes the SOLAS convention on the safety of life at sea (first adopted after the Titanic disaster and kept current under IMO), the MARPOL convention for the prevention of pollution from ships, the STCW convention on training and certification of seafarers, and the collision regulations (COLREGs). Collectively its treaties cover the vast majority of world merchant tonnage.

Structure

The Assembly of all member states meets every two years, a Council acts as governing body, and technical committees—including the Maritime Safety Committee and Marine Environment Protection Committee—develop the regulations, which are enforced mainly by flag and port states.
