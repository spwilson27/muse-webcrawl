Border barrier

Synonyms: Border wall, border fence

Purposes: Immigration control, smuggling prevention, security

Examples: US–Mexico barrier, Israeli West Bank barrier, India–Bangladesh fence

A border barrier is a wall, fence, or other physical structure built along or near an international border to restrict the movement of people and goods. Barriers range from vehicle bollards and chain-link fencing to concrete walls several metres high, and are typically supplemented by patrol roads, lighting, sensors, and surveillance towers. Their stated purposes include preventing unauthorised immigration and smuggling, and defending against attack or infiltration.

History

Walled frontiers are ancient — the Great Wall of China and Hadrian's Wall are famous early examples — but the modern border barrier proliferated after the Cold War. At the fall of the Berlin Wall in 1989 there were roughly fifteen border walls worldwide; by the 2020s there were more than seventy, built on every inhabited continent.

Notable barriers

The United States has constructed barriers along about a third of its border with Mexico since the 1990s, expanded under several administrations. Israel's West Bank barrier, begun in 2002, combines electronic fencing with concrete wall sections in urban areas and remains politically contentious. India has fenced much of its borders with Pakistan and Bangladesh; the Korean Demilitarized Zone is guarded by fences and minefields on both sides; and after the 2015 European migrant crisis several EU states, including Hungary, built border fences. Ceuta and Melilla are ringed by triple fences separating Spain from Morocco.

Debate

Supporters argue barriers channel crossings to ports of entry and deter smuggling and attack. Critics question their effectiveness against tunnels, ladders, boats, and visa overstays, and cite high construction and maintenance costs, harm to border communities and wildlife migration, and the diplomatic symbolism of division.
