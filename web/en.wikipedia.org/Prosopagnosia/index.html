Prosopagnosia

Prosopagnosia
Specialty: Neurology
Also called: face blindness
Associated region: fusiform gyrus

Prosopagnosia, also known as face blindness, is a cognitive disorder of face perception in which the ability to recognize familiar faces, including one's own face in severe cases, is impaired, while other aspects of visual processing and intellectual functioning remain intact. The term was coined by Joachim Bodamer in 1947.

Causes

Acquired prosopagnosia results from damage to the occipital and temporal lobes, particularly the fusiform face area of the right fusiform gyrus, through stroke, injury, or disease. Developmental (congenital) prosopagnosia occurs without brain injury, appears to run in families, and is estimated to affect around 2–2.5% of the population.

Management

No cure exists; people with prosopagnosia rely on compensatory strategies such as recognizing voices, hairstyles, gait, and context. Research uses rehabilitation training and studies of face-processing stages to develop assistive approaches.
