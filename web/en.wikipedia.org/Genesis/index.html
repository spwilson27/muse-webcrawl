Genesis

Genesis may refer to several different subjects. Most commonly, it denotes the Book of Genesis, the first book of the Hebrew Bible and of the Christian Old Testament, which recounts the creation of the world, the early history of humanity, and the origins of the Israelites through the patriarchs Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and Joseph.

Religion and mythology

The Book of Genesis (from Greek génesis, "origin"; Hebrew Bereshit, "in the beginning") contains the creation narratives of Genesis 1–2, the stories of Adam and Eve, Cain and Abel, Noah and the Flood, and the Tower of Babel, followed by the patriarchal history. "Genesis" is also used generically for creation myths and origin accounts in other traditions.

Music

Genesis is also the name of an English rock band formed at Charterhouse School in 1967 by Peter Gabriel, Tony Banks, Mike Rutherford, Anthony Phillips, and Chris Stewart. With Gabriel as theatrical frontman the band became leading figures of progressive rock with albums such as Selling England by the Pound (1973) and The Lamb Lies Down on Broadway (1974). After Gabriel's departure in 1975, drummer Phil Collins became lead singer and the band moved toward pop, achieving worldwide commercial success in the 1980s with albums including Duke, Abacab, Genesis, and the multi-platinum Invisible Touch (1986), whose title track reached number one in the United States. The classic trio of Banks, Collins, and Rutherford reunited for tours in 2007 and again for a farewell tour in 2021–2022.

Other uses

Other notable uses include the Sega Genesis video game console (known as the Mega Drive outside North America), NASA's Genesis spacecraft, which returned samples of the solar wind in 2004, and numerous books, films, and companies named for the idea of an origin or beginning.
