Viceroyalty of New Spain

The Viceroyalty of New Spain (Spanish: Virreinato de Nueva España) was the largest and most important territorial division of the Spanish Empire in the Americas, established in 1535 and lasting until 1821. Ruled by a viceroy from its capital, Mexico City, at its greatest extent it encompassed present-day Mexico and Central America, the US Southwest and Florida, Venezuela, the Caribbean islands, and even the Philippines across the Pacific.

Establishment and government

Following Hernán Cortés's conquest of the Aztec Empire (1519–1521), the Crown created the viceroyalty in 1535 with Antonio de Mendoza as its first viceroy. The viceroy governed as the king's alter ego, presiding over the audiencia (high court) and supervising a hierarchy of provinces, captaincies general, and royal treasury officials. The Council of the Indies in Spain legislated for the colonies, while the trade of the empire was channelled through the monopoly port system and the annual Manila galleon linking Acapulco with the Philippines.

Economy and society

Silver was the engine of the colonial economy: the mines of Zacatecas, Guanajuato, and Taxco produced a large share of the world's silver, much of it exported to Spain and to China. Agriculture, ranching, and textile workshops rested on indigenous labour, at first through the encomienda and repartimiento systems, within a stratified society ordered by the casta hierarchy of peninsulares, criollos, mestizos, indigenous people, and enslaved Africans. The Catholic Church, through its orders and missions, was central to colonial life, founding towns, schools, and universities — the Royal and Pontifical University of Mexico dates to 1551.

Decline and independence

The Bourbon Reforms of the 18th century tightened imperial control and raised revenue, while also alienating American-born Spaniards. After Napoleon occupied Spain in 1808, juntas and insurgencies spread; the independence war begun by Miguel Hidalgo in 1810 ended in 1821 with the Plan of Iguala and the creation of the Mexican Empire, dissolving the viceroyalty after nearly three centuries.
