Dunkerque was the lead ship of the Dunkerque class of battleships built for the French Navy in the 1930s. Designed in response to the German Deutschland-class "pocket battleships", she carried her main armament of eight 330 mm guns in two quadruple turrets mounted entirely forward, an arrangement unique among battleships.

Design

The Dunkerque class was a fast, lightly armoured capital ship, sometimes classed as a battlecruiser, intended to outgun commerce raiders and older battleships while reaching speeds of about 29.5 knots. Dunkerque's sister ship was Strasbourg.

Service

At the outbreak of the Second World War Dunkerque operated with the Force de Raid from Brest. After the French armistice she was at Mers-el-Kébir in Algeria when the British attacked on 3 July 1940 to prevent the fleet falling into German hands; Dunkerque was damaged and run aground, then further damaged by torpedo bombers on 6 July. She was later refloated and returned to Toulon, where her crew scuttled her on 27 November 1942 to prevent her seizure by Germany. The wreck was scrapped after the war.
