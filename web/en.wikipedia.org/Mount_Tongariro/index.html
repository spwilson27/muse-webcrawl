Mount Tongariro

Elevation1,978 m (6,490 ft)
LocationTongariro National Park, New Zealand
TypeCompound stratovolcano
Last eruption2012 (Te Maari)

Mount Tongariro is a compound andesitic volcano in the Taupō Volcanic Zone of New Zealand's North Island, forming the northern massif of Tongariro National Park. Rather than a single cone, it is a sprawling complex of craters, lava flows and explosion pits built over the last 275,000 years; its highest point reaches 1,978 metres. The younger cone of Ngauruhoe, on its southern flank, is its most active vent.

Features

The massif contains the brilliant Emerald Lakes and Blue Lake in explosion craters, the steaming Ketetahi hot springs, and Red Crater, whose dike-fed vent erupted in 1926. The Tongariro Alpine Crossing, widely rated among the world's best one-day walks, traverses the volcano from Mangatepopo to Ketetahi, passing between Tongariro and Ngauruhoe.

2012 eruptions

After more than a century of quiet at the Te Maari craters, Tongariro erupted on 6 August 2012, scattering ash and blocks and closing the Crossing briefly; a second, smaller eruption followed on 21 November 2012. No one was injured.

History and significance

In 1887 the paramount chief Horonuku Te Heuheu Tukino IV gifted the mountain summits to the Crown, creating the nucleus of Tongariro National Park — New Zealand's first national park and now a dual World Heritage Site for both its volcanic landscape and its Māori cultural associations.
