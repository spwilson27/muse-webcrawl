The Jungle Book

The Jungle Book is a collection of stories by the English author Rudyard Kipling. It was first published in book form in 1894, with a sequel, The Second Jungle Book, following in 1895. Most of the characters are animals such as Shere Khan the tiger and Baloo the bear, though a principal character is the boy or "man-cub" Mowgli, who is raised in the jungle by wolves.

The Mowgli stories

The best-known tales follow Mowgli, found as an infant by Father Wolf and Mother Wolf of the Seeonee wolf pack. At the Council Rock, the black panther Bagheera buys his life with a bull, and the bear Baloo teaches him the Law of the Jungle and the Master Words of the birds and the Snake People. Mowgli's enemies include the lame tiger Shere Khan, the monkey-folk (Bandar-log) who abduct him, and the white cobra of the ruined city; in "Letting in the Jungle" and other tales he gradually returns to human society, killing Shere Khan with a herd of buffalo. Other stories in the collection include "Rikki-Tikki-Tavi", about a brave mongoose, "Toomai of the Elephants", and "The White Seal".

Themes and legacy

The stories are fables using animals in an anthropomorphic manner to give moral lessons through the "Law of the Jungle" ("Now this is the Law of the Jungle — as old and as true as the sky"). Kipling drew on his birth and early childhood in India and on Indian folklore. The books inspired Scouting founder Robert Baden-Powell's use of jungle lore in the Cub Scouts (whose leaders are still named Akela, Bagheera and Baloo), and remain classics of children's literature, adapted in Disney's animated film of 1967, its 2016 live-action remake, and many other versions.
