Montessori education

The Montessori method of education is a type of educational method that involves children's natural interests and activities rather than formal teaching methods. It was developed by Italian physician Maria Montessori. The method emphasises independence, freedom within limits, and respect for a child's natural psychological, physical, and social development.

History

Maria Montessori opened her first classroom, the Casa dei Bambini, in a Rome housing project in 1907. Her observations of how children learned through hands-on activity led to the publication of The Montessori Method in 1909, and the approach spread internationally in the following decades.

Educational elements

Montessori classrooms are "prepared environments" with child-sized furniture and specially designed didactic materials. Children choose their own work for long, uninterrupted blocks of time, typically in mixed-age groups spanning three years. Teachers act primarily as guides, introducing materials and observing progress rather than delivering whole-class lessons.

Planes of development

Montessori described four planes of development: infancy (0–6), childhood (6–12), adolescence (12–18) and maturity (18–24), each with characteristic sensitivities that education should serve.
