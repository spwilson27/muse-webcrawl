Taxila

The Dharmarajika stupa at Taxila, an ancient centre of learning in Gandhara.

Taxila (Pali: Takkasilā) is a city in the Punjab province of Pakistan, and the site of one of the most important archaeological complexes of ancient South Asia. Situated in the valley between the Indus and Jhelum rivers, Taxila was a major city of the Gandhara region and a renowned centre of learning in the ancient world.

History

Taxila's settlements date back to the Neolithic period, and by the 6th century BC the city was part of the Achaemenid Empire's Gandhara satrapy. It passed successively under the rule of Alexander the Great's successors, the Mauryan Empire — during which it served as a provincial capital — the Indo-Greeks, Indo-Scythians, and the Kushan Empire, when the new city of Sirkap and later Sirsukh were built. Ancient tradition held Taxila's university to be among the oldest in the world; students came from across Asia to study law, medicine, military science, and the Vedas, and teachers associated with the city include the strategist Chanakya. The Chinese pilgrims Faxian and Xuanzang both visited and described its Buddhist monasteries. Taxila declined after its destruction by the Hephthalites (White Huns) in the 5th century AD.

Archaeology

The ruins were systematically excavated in the early 20th century under Sir John Marshall. The main sites include the successive city mounds of Bhir Mound, Sirkap, and Sirsukh, and religious complexes such as the Dharmarajika stupa, Jaulian monastery, and Jandial temple. Taxila was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1980.
