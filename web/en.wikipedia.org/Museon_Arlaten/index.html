The Museon Arlaten ("Museum of Arles" in Provençal) is an ethnographic museum dedicated to the culture and traditions of Provence, located in the centre of Arles, France. It was founded by the poet Frédéric Mistral, leader of the Félibrige movement for the revival of Provençal language and culture, who used the money from his 1904 Nobel Prize in Literature in part to expand and endow the museum.

History

Mistral first proposed the museum in 1896, and it opened in 1899 in the Hôtel Laval-Castellane, a Renaissance mansion built over part of the ancient Roman forum of Arles — the archaeological remains beneath the building are incorporated into the displays. Mistral conceived the museum as a "poem in objects," preserving the costumes, furniture, tools, religious artefacts, and everyday items of traditional Provençal life that he feared were disappearing with industrialisation.

Collections and renovation

The collections comprise tens of thousands of objects, including celebrated displays of 18th- and 19th-century Arlésienne costume and jewellery, dioramas of Provençal interiors and seasonal festivals, and works connected with the Camargue's bull-herding culture. The museum closed in 2009 for a major renovation led by architect Michel Bertreux with scenography involving designer Christian Lacroix, a native of Arles, and reopened in 2021 with reorganised galleries presenting Provençal identity from antiquity to the present.
