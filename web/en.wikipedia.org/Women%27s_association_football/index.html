Women's association football

Women's association football, more commonly known simply as women's football or women's soccer, is the team sport of association football played by women. It is played at the professional level in multiple countries and by national teams that compete in international competitions, above all the FIFA Women's World Cup.

History

Women played football in organised matches from the 1890s, and during the First World War factory teams such as Dick, Kerr Ladies of Preston drew crowds of tens of thousands — one Boxing Day 1920 match attracted about 53,000 spectators. In 1921, however, England's Football Association banned women from playing on affiliated grounds, declaring the game "quite unsuitable for females"; similar restrictions applied in several countries and stunted the sport for half a century. The FA ban was lifted in 1971, and women's football rebuilt steadily. FIFA staged the first Women's World Cup in China in 1991, and women's football became an Olympic sport at Atlanta in 1996.

Modern game

The United States, Germany, France, England, Spain, and Japan are among the leading nations; the United States women's national team won four of the first nine World Cups. Professional leagues such as the American NWSL, England's Women's Super League, and Spain's Liga F have grown rapidly in attendance, broadcasting, and sponsorship, and the UEFA Women's Champions League has become a major club competition. The 2023 World Cup in Australia and New Zealand set records for attendance and audience.
