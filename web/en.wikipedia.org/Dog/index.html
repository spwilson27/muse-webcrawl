The dog is a domesticated descendant of the wolf, characterized by an upturning tail. Also called the domestic dog, it was selectively bred from an extinct population of wolves during the Late Pleistocene, and was the first species to be domesticated by humans — hunter-gatherers kept dogs more than 15,000 years ago, well before the development of agriculture.

Biology and senses

Dogs vary enormously in size, coat, and form across hundreds of recognized breeds, from the tiny Chihuahua to the Great Dane, yet all belong to a single species. Dogs have acute senses: a highly developed sense of smell — with up to 300 million olfactory receptors — hearing extending into ultrasonic frequencies, and dichromatic vision. Their intelligence and social cognition, shaped by co-evolution with humans, allow them to read human gestures such as pointing.

Roles with humans

Long called "man's best friend", dogs work as herders, hunters, guards, sled pullers, service and assistance animals, and detection animals, and are among the most popular companion animals worldwide. The global dog population is estimated at around 900 million, though a large share are free-ranging village and feral dogs rather than pets.
