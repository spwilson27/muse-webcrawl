Nijmegen

Nijmegen (Dutch pronunciation: [ˈnɛiˌmeːɣə(n)]) is a city in the Netherlands, in the province of Gelderland, on the Waal river near the German border. With a population of around 180,000, it is often regarded as the oldest city in the Netherlands, tracing its origins to a Roman settlement founded around the 1st century BCE; it received city rights around 1230.

History

The Romans established a military camp, Oppidum Batavorum, at the site, and nearby Ulpia Noviomagus became an important town of Germania Inferior. The city later hosted a palace of Charlemagne at the Valkhof. In 1678–1679 the Treaties of Nijmegen ended a series of European wars. During the Second World War, Nijmegen was heavily damaged by an accidental American bombing in February 1944 and again during Operation Market Garden, when it was liberated in September 1944 after fierce fighting for the Waal bridge.

Education and events

Nijmegen is home to Radboud University (founded 1923) and the Radboud University Medical Center. The city is internationally known for the International Four Days Marches Nijmegen, the world's largest multi-day walking event, held each July and drawing tens of thousands of participants.
