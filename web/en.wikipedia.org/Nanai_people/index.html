Nanai people

The Nanai people (Nanai: Нанай, Nanai; Russian: нанайцы, nanaitsy) are a Tungusic people of East Asia who have traditionally lived along the Heilongjiang (Amur), Songhua and Wusuli rivers in Manchuria (China) and the Russian Far East. In China they are known as the Hezhe (赫哲族) and are one of the 56 officially recognised ethnic groups. The population is roughly 17,000 in total, split between Russia (around 12,000) and China (around 5,000).

Culture

Traditionally the Nanai were fishers and hunters; fish skin was used to make distinctive waterproof garments, earning them the historical nickname "Fish-skin Tartars". Their religion was shamanistic, with belief in a spirit world mediated by shamans. The Nanai language belongs to the Tungusic family and is seriously endangered, with fluent speakers now few and mostly elderly.

Notable people

The most famous Nanai is Dersu Uzala, the hunter and guide who accompanied the Russian explorer Vladimir Arsenyev in the Sikhote-Alin mountains in the early 1900s and became the subject of Arsenyev's book and Akira Kurosawa's 1975 film. The Nanai writer and politician Eremey Aypin and singer Kola Beldy are also well known in Russia.
