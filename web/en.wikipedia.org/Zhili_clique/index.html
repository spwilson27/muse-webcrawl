The Zhili clique (Chinese: 直系) was one of several mutually hostile warlord factions that split from the Beiyang Army during China's Warlord Era. It took its name from Zhili Province (now largely Hebei), the region around Beijing where its power was centred.

History

The clique formed around Feng Guozhang after the death of Yuan Shikai in 1916, in opposition to Duan Qirui's Anhui clique. After Feng's death in 1919, leadership passed to Cao Kun and the brilliant general Wu Peifu. The Zhili clique defeated the Anhui clique in the Zhili–Anhui War of 1920 and, allied temporarily with Zhang Zuolin's Fengtian clique, took control of the Beijing government.

Decline

Zhili won the First Zhili–Fengtian War in 1922, and Cao Kun became President of the Republic in 1923 after openly bribing the National Assembly. But in the Second Zhili–Fengtian War of 1924 the clique was betrayed by its own general Feng Yuxiang in the Beijing Coup, and Wu Peifu's army was crushed. The clique fought on against both Fengtian and the Nationalists' Northern Expedition before its remnants were absorbed or destroyed by 1928.
