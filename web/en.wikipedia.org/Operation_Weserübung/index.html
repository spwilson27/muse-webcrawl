Operation Weserübung

Operation Weserübung was the German invasion of Denmark and Norway on 9 April 1940, opening the Norwegian campaign of the Second World War. The name means "Operation Weser Exercise", after the River Weser.

Objectives

Germany sought to secure the supply of Swedish iron ore shipped through Narvik, pre-empt a possible Allied occupation of Norway, and gain naval and air bases extending its reach into the North Atlantic. Denmark was occupied within hours; Norway, aided by Britain and France, resisted for two months.

Outcome

Both countries remained under German occupation until 1945. The Kriegsmarine suffered heavy losses, including the heavy cruiser Blücher, sunk by the Oscarsborg Fortress in the Oslofjord. King Haakon VII and the Norwegian government escaped to Britain, and the Norwegian merchant fleet served the Allied cause.
