Alfonso VI of León and Castile

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Reign: 1065–1109 (León); 1072–1109 (Castile and Galicia)

Born: c. 1040/1041

Died: 1 July 1109, Toledo

House: Jiménez dynasty

Father: Ferdinand I of León

Alfonso VI (c. 1040/1041 – 1 July 1109), nicknamed the Brave (el Bravo), was king of León from 1065 and of Castile and Galicia from 1072 until his death. He was the second son of Ferdinand I, who divided his realms among his sons; after wars against his brothers Sancho II of Castile and García II of Galicia, Alfonso reunited the inheritance and became the dominant Christian ruler of Iberia.

Reign

Alfonso styled himself "emperor of all Spain" (imperator totius Hispaniae), reflecting his overlordship over both Christian kingdoms and the Muslim taifa states, from which he extracted tribute payments called parias. His most famous achievement was the conquest of Toledo in 1085, the former Visigothic capital and the first major city of al-Andalus to fall to a Christian king during the Reconquista.

Almoravid wars

The fall of Toledo alarmed the taifa rulers, who called in the Almoravids of Morocco. Alfonso was heavily defeated at the Battle of Sagrajas (1086) and again at Uclés (1108), where his only son, Sancho Alfónsez, was killed. Despite these setbacks, Toledo remained in Christian hands. Alfonso also sheltered the exiled Rodrigo Díaz de Vivar, El Cid, with whom he had a long and ambivalent relationship, and he encouraged Cluniac monastic influence and the adoption of the Roman rite in his kingdoms.

Succession

Alfonso died in Toledo in 1109 and was succeeded by his daughter Urraca, the first reigning queen of León-Castile. His reign is remembered as a turning point in medieval Iberian history, marking the definitive shift of power toward the Christian north.
