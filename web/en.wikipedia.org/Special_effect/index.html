Special effect

Special effect
Abbreviations: SFX, SPFX
Categories: Practical (physical/mechanical/optical) and visual effects (VFX)
Used in: Film, television, theatre, simulation

A special effect (often abbreviated SFX or SPFX) is an illusion or visual trick used in film, television, theatre, video games and the simulator industry to simulate imagined events in a story or virtual world. Special effects are conventionally divided into practical effects — created physically on set — and visual effects (VFX), created or manipulated in post-production, increasingly with computers (CGI).

Practical effects

Practical effects include mechanical effects (rain machines, breakaway props, animatronics and creature suits), pyrotechnics, scale miniatures, prosthetic make-up and atmospheric effects such as fog. They were the sole means of screen illusion for most of cinema history, from Georges Méliès's trick photography around 1900 to the model work of films such as 2001: A Space Odyssey (1968) and the original Star Wars trilogy.

Optical and visual effects

Optical effects — matte paintings, rear projection, multiple exposures and optical printing — extended what could be photographed. The digital revolution, marked by films such as Jurassic Park (1993) and The Matrix (1999), shifted much of this work to computer-generated imagery, motion capture and digital compositing, blurring the line between special and visual effects.

Live and other uses

Stage productions use trapdoors, flying rigs, projections and pyrotechnics, while themed attractions and simulators combine physical effects with media. Safety in practical effects is heavily regulated, following accidents that spurred modern stunt and explosives protocols.
