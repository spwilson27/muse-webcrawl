Fage

Fage International S.A. (Greek: Φάγε, pronounced "fa-yeh") is a Luxembourg-based dairy company and one of the best-known producers of Greek strained yoghurt. Founded in Athens in 1926, the company pioneered the commercial production of strained yoghurt and popularised it internationally under brands such as Total.

History

The company began as a small dairy shop in Athens opened by Athanassios Filippou. In 1954 the family business was formalised, and in 1964 it opened Greece's first industrial yoghurt plant in Galatsi, Athens. Fage began exporting strained yoghurt in the 1980s, first to the United Kingdom and Italy, and by the 1990s its Total yoghurt had become a major brand in European supermarkets. Expansion into the United States followed, with a large production plant in Johnstown, New York, opened in 2008. The company's headquarters later moved to Luxembourg.

Products and "Greek yoghurt" disputes

Fage produces strained yoghurt in several fat levels (Total 0%, 2% and 5%), as well as other dairy products. Because Greek yoghurt is strained to remove whey, it is thicker and higher in protein than ordinary yoghurt. Fage has been involved in legal disputes over the term "Greek yoghurt", including a 2013 UK court ruling that only yoghurt made in Greece could be sold under that name in Britain — a case Fage won against a US rival whose product was made in America.
