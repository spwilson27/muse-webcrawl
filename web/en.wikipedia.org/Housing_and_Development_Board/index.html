The Housing and Development Board (HDB) is the statutory board of the Singapore government responsible for public housing. Established on 1 February 1960 to tackle a severe housing shortage, it has built more than a million flats, and over 80 percent of Singapore's residents live in HDB housing.

History

HDB replaced the Singapore Improvement Trust, which had failed to keep pace with demand, leaving many people in overcrowded slums and squatter settlements. Within a decade, HDB built low-cost flats at scale, resettling families into planned estates. The Home Ownership for the People Scheme, launched in 1964, encouraged citizens to buy their flats, using savings from the Central Provident Fund.

New towns and design

HDB develops self-contained new towns with schools, markets, clinics, and transport links organized around neighbourhood and precinct planning. Later developments, such as the Pinnacle@Duxton and sky gardens in Punggol, pushed denser, greener designs. Ethnic integration quotas, introduced in 1989, cap the proportions of ethnic groups in each block to prevent enclaves.

Flats and eligibility

New flats are sold under the Build-To-Order system with 99-year leases, with sizes from two-room Flexi units to five-room and executive flats. Resale is permitted subject to a minimum occupation period. As leases age, schemes such as the Selective En bloc Redevelopment Scheme and the announced Voluntary Early Redevelopment Scheme address lease decay.
