Port Phillip (sometimes called Port Phillip Bay) is a horsehead-shaped enclosed bay on the central coast of the state of Victoria, Australia. It covers about 1,930 square kilometres and is extremely shallow for its size, with an average depth of about 8 metres. The city of Melbourne stands at its northern end, at the mouth of the Yarra River, and Geelong lies on its western arm, Corio Bay.

Geography

The bay is connected to Bass Strait by a narrow entrance, the Rip, between the Mornington and Bellarine peninsulas, notorious for strong tidal currents. Rivers flowing into the bay include the Yarra, Maribyrnong, and Werribee. Port Phillip formed around 10,000 years ago when rising seas flooded the lower Yarra valley.

History and ecology

Europeans first entered the bay in 1802; a short-lived convict settlement was established at Sorrento in 1803, and permanent settlement followed with the founding of Melbourne in 1835. The bay supports diverse marine life, including bottlenose dolphins, Australian fur seals, and a resident pod of burrunan dolphins recognized as a distinct species.
