Ásatrúarfélagið (Icelandic for the Ásatrú Association) is an Icelandic religious organisation devoted to the revival of the Old Norse pagan religion, known as Ásatrú or Icelandic Norse paganism. Founded in 1972 by the farmer and poet Sveinbjörn Beinteinsson, it was the first modern neopagan organisation of its kind, and it was officially recognised by the Icelandic state as a religious organisation in 1973.

History

The association was founded at a meeting in Reykjavík in the summer of 1972. Sveinbjörn Beinteinsson, a goði (chieftain-priest) from Borgarfjörður, became its first allsherjargoði (high chieftain). After petitioning the Icelandic government, Ásatrúarfélagið received official recognition in 1973, giving it the right to perform legally binding ceremonies such as marriages and to receive a share of the church tax.

Growth was slow for decades, but membership rose sharply in the twenty-first century, making it one of the largest non-Christian religious organisations in Iceland. In 2015 the association began construction of the Hof Ásatrúarfélagsins, a temple on the Öskjuhlíð hill in Reykjavík — the first pagan temple built in Iceland in roughly a thousand years.

Beliefs and practices

Ásatrúarfélagið has no fixed dogma or scripture-based doctrine; its practice centres on the Old Norse gods, seasonal blót feasts, and rites of passage such as naming ceremonies, weddings, and funerals. The organisation emphasises tolerance, respect for nature, and personal responsibility, and it has publicly distanced itself from racist appropriations of Norse symbolism.

High chieftains

Sveinbjörn Beinteinsson led the association until his death in 1993. He was succeeded by Jón Jóhannesson, then Jónína Kristín Berg (1994–2002), and from 2003 by the composer Hilmar Örn Hilmarsson.
