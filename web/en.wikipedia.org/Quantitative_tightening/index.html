Quantitative tightening

TypeMonetary policy
OppositeQuantitative easing
MechanismShrinking the central bank balance sheet

Quantitative tightening (QT) is a contractionary monetary policy tool used by central banks to reduce the money supply and the size of their balance sheets, normally by allowing bonds they hold to mature without reinvestment, or by actively selling them. It is the reverse of quantitative easing (QE).

Mechanism

Under QT, a central bank lets its holdings of government bonds and mortgage-backed securities run off as they mature, or sells them into the market. This withdraws reserves from the banking system, tends to push long-term interest rates upward, and tightens financial conditions.

History

The US Federal Reserve ran a QT programme from 2017 to 2019 after its post-2008 QE purchases, and began a much larger runoff in June 2022 as inflation surged. The Bank of England and the European Central Bank also adopted balance-sheet reduction after the 2021–22 inflation shock.
