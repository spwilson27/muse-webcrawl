Pedro Álvares Cabral

Pedro Álvares Cabral (c. 1467/1468 – c. 1520) was a Portuguese nobleman, military commander, navigator and explorer regarded as the European discoverer of Brazil. In 1500, commanding a fleet of thirteen ships bound for India, Cabral made landfall on the South American coast and claimed the land for Portugal.

Early life

Cabral was born into a noble family in Belmonte, in central Portugal, and was educated at the court of King Afonso V. He was appointed to lead the second Portuguese expedition to India, following Vasco da Gama's pioneering voyage of 1497–1499.

The voyage of 1500

Cabral's fleet departed Lisbon on 9 March 1500. After passing the Cape Verde islands, the fleet swung wide into the Atlantic—whether deliberately or blown off course remains debated—and on 22 April 1500 sighted land, which Cabral named the Island of the True Cross (Ilha de Vera Cruz). He claimed it for King Manuel I of Portugal; the territory would become Brazil. Cabral then continued to India, reaching Calicut, where conflict with local rulers and Arab merchants led to violence, before returning to Lisbon in 1501 with valuable cargoes of spices.

Later life and legacy

Cabral retired from royal service and died around 1520 in Santarém. His tomb was identified in 1839. In Brazil he is commemorated as the country's discoverer, and his landing near modern Porto Seguro is a foundational event in Brazilian history.
