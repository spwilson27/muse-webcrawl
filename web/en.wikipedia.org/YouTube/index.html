YouTube is an American online video-sharing platform owned by Google, headquartered in San Bruno, California. Founded in 2005 by three former PayPal employees, it allows users to upload, view, rate, share and comment on videos, and is the world's most visited video site and one of the most visited websites overall.

History

The first video, "Me at the zoo", was uploaded by co-founder Jawed Karim on 23 April 2005. Growth was explosive; Google acquired the company in November 2006 for US$1.65 billion in stock. Creator monetisation via the Partner Program (from 2007), HD and live streaming, and mobile apps followed. Features now include Shorts (vertical short video, launched globally 2021), podcasts, and the Content ID copyright-management system.

Impact

YouTube created a professional creator economy and reshaped music distribution (with Vevo-era premieres and record-breaking music videos), education, politics and news. Its recommendation algorithm and advertising model have also drawn sustained criticism and regulation debates over misinformation, children's content (a US$170 million COPPA settlement in 2019), and extremist material. YouTube's advertising revenue exceeded US$30 billion annually in the early 2020s.

Leadership

Long-time CEO Susan Wojcicki led the company from 2014 to 2023, when Neal Mohan succeeded her.
