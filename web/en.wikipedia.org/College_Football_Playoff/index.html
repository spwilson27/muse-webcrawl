College Football Playoff

The College Football Playoff (CFP) is an annual postseason tournament that determines the national champion of NCAA Division I Football Bowl Subdivision (FBS) college football in the United States. It began with the 2014 season, replacing the Bowl Championship Series (BCS). From 2014 through 2023, four teams were selected by a committee to compete in two semifinal bowl games, with the winners advancing to the College Football Playoff National Championship.

Expansion to twelve teams

Beginning with the 2024 season, the playoff expanded to 12 teams. Under the format, the highest-ranked conference champions receive first-round byes, automatic bids go to the top conference champions, and the remaining places are filled by at-large teams chosen by the selection committee. First-round games are played on campus sites, while quarterfinals and semifinals rotate among the New Year's Six bowl games — the Rose, Sugar, Orange, Cotton, Peach, and Fiesta Bowls.

Selection committee

The CFP selection committee, a rotating panel of athletic administrators and former players and coaches, publishes weekly rankings over the final weeks of the season and sets the final bracket. Its decisions have frequently been controversial, most notably when undefeated conference champions have been left out or seeded below teams with losses.

History and champions

Ohio State won the first playoff in January 2015. Alabama and Clemson dominated the four-team era, meeting in three championship games, and Georgia won back-to-back titles for the 2021 and 2022 seasons. Michigan won the final four-team playoff for the 2023 season. The playoff's television rights, held by ESPN, are among the most valuable in American sports, and further format changes for 2026 and beyond have been the subject of continuing negotiation among conferences.
