Power supply

Function: Converts electrical energy to the form a load requires

Types: Linear, switched-mode (SMPS), programmable, UPS

Common outputs: Regulated DC voltages

A power supply is an electrical device that supplies electric power to an electrical load. Typically it converts power from a source — mains electricity, a battery, or another generator — into the voltage, current and frequency characteristics the load requires. Most electronic equipment needs regulated low-voltage DC, so most power supplies rectify AC input and regulate the output.

Types

Linear power supplies use a transformer and a series regulator; they are simple and quiet but heavy and inefficient. Switched-mode power supplies (SMPS) switch the input at high frequency through a small transformer, achieving high efficiency and small size, and are used in computers (to the ATX standard), chargers and consumer electronics. A programmable power supply allows remote setting of voltage and current and is a staple of laboratories and test equipment; an uninterruptible power supply (UPS) adds a battery to bridge mains failures.

Key specifications

Supplies are rated by output voltage and current, power capacity, efficiency, ripple and noise, load and line regulation, and protection features such as over-current, over-voltage and short-circuit protection. Certification marks attest to electrical safety and electromagnetic compatibility.
