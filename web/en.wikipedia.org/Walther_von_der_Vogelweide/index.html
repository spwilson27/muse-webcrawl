Walther von der Vogelweide (c. 1170 – c. 1230) was a Minnesinger — a medieval German lyric poet and composer — and is widely regarded as the greatest German lyric poet before Goethe. His name means "Walther of the bird meadow," presumably a place name, and he appears as a celebrated figure in the Codex Manesse, which portrays him in his famous pensive pose.

Life

Almost nothing is known of Walther's birthplace or family; he likely learned his art in Austria, at the Babenberg court in Vienna under the patronage of Duke Frederick I. After Frederick's death in 1198 he became a wandering singer, serving a succession of patrons including Philip of Swabia, Otto IV, and the young Frederick II, who in 1220 granted him a small fief near Würzburg — the only documentary record of his life, besides an entry in a bishop's travel accounts noting a payment for a fur coat in 1203.

Works

Around 200 of Walther's songs and didactic poems (Sprüche) survive. His love lyrics (Minnesang) include the famous "Under der linden" ("Under the linden tree"), remarkable for its female speaker. His political Sprüche engage with the struggle between Empire and Papacy, criticizing Rome in poems such as "Ich hân mîn lêhen" and the so-called "Elegy." The melodies to most of his songs are lost, though a few survive in later sources.
