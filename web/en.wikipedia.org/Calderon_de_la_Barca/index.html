Pedro Calderón de la Barca (17 January 1600 – 25 May 1681) was a Spanish dramatist, poet, and writer of the Spanish Golden Age. He is widely regarded, alongside Lope de Vega, as one of the two greatest playwrights of Spain's Golden Age of theatre, and his work is generally considered the culmination of the Spanish Baroque theatre.

Life

Calderón was born in Madrid to a family of hidalgo status; his father served as a secretary in the Treasury. He was educated at the Jesuit college in Madrid and studied at the universities of Alcalá and Salamanca. Abandoning a planned ecclesiastical career, he turned to the theatre in the 1620s, when Madrid was the theatrical capital of Europe. Calderón also served as a soldier in Italy and Flanders and later became a court dramatist under Philip IV, producing plays for the royal theatre at the Buen Retiro palace. In 1651 he was ordained a priest, yet continued to write secular drama for the court. He died in Madrid in 1681.

Works

Calderón wrote some 120 comedias (three-act plays) and about 80 autos sacramentales, allegorical one-act plays on religious themes performed during Corpus Christi. His most celebrated work is La vida es sueño (Life Is a Dream, 1635), a philosophical allegory about the illusory nature of earthly life, centred on the Polish prince Segismundo. Other major plays include El alcalde de Zalamea (The Mayor of Zalamea), a drama of honour and justice; El príncipe constante (The Constant Prince); and La dama duende (The Phantom Lady), a brilliant cape-and-sword comedy.

Style and legacy

Calderón's drama is marked by intellectual rigour, elaborate verse, philosophical depth, and a highly structured, almost architectural sense of plot. Where Lope de Vega embodied the vitality of the earlier Golden Age, Calderón represented its reflective, Baroque maturity. His works fell out of fashion in the eighteenth century but were revived by German Romantics such as August Wilhelm Schlegel, and he remains a central figure in the Spanish canon.
