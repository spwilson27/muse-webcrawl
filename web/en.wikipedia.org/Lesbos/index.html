Lesbos

Lesbos or Lesvos is a Greek island located in the northeastern Aegean Sea, often called Mytilene after its capital. It is the third largest Greek island, with an area of about 1,633 km² and a population of roughly 83,000.

History

Lesbos was an important Aeolian Greek city-state in antiquity, famous as the home of the poets Sappho and Alcaeus (7th–6th centuries BC) and later of Theophrastus, Aristotle's successor. In the Middle Ages it passed between Byzantine, Genoese — under the Gattilusio family — and Ottoman rule, which lasted until 1912, when it became part of Greece.

Geography and economy

The island is known for olive oil (some eleven million olive trees), ouzo from Plomari, sardines from the Gulf of Kalloni, and the Petrified Forest of Lesbos, a UNESCO-recognised geopark of fossilised trees up to 20 million years old.

Culture

The island's association with Sappho gave rise to the word "lesbian". In 2015–16 Lesbos was a principal entry point for refugees arriving in Europe, and its residents were widely praised for their humanitarian response.
