The Westman Islands (Icelandic: Vestmannaeyjar) are an archipelago of volcanic islands off the south coast of Iceland. The group comprises about 15 islands, of which only Heimaey is inhabited, with a population of around 4,500 centered on the town of Vestmannaeyjar, one of Iceland's most important fishing ports.

Name and early history

The islands are named for Irish slaves (Vestmenn, "Westmen"), who, according to Icelandic tradition, were killed there after rebelling against their Norse master Hjörleifr Hróðmarsson, one of the first settlers. In 1627 the islands were raided by Barbary pirates in the "Turkish Abductions," in which 242 islanders were captured and carried off to slavery in Algiers.

Volcanism

The archipelago sits on a young volcanic system. In 1963 the new island of Surtsey rose from the sea during an eruption lasting until 1967; it is now a protected natural laboratory for studying colonization by plants and animals. In January 1973 the Eldfell volcano erupted on Heimaey, burying part of the town under lava and tephra and forcing the evacuation of the entire population; seawater pumped onto the lava front helped save the harbor. The eruption created the mountain Eldfell and enlarged the island, and the event is commemorated at the Eldheimar museum.

Wildlife

The islands hold Iceland's largest colony of Atlantic puffins, and each summer millions of puffins breed in burrows on the cliffs. The annual Þjóðhátíð festival, held since 1874, is one of Iceland's largest outdoor festivals.
