The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) is a space telescope designed to conduct infrared astronomy. Its high-resolution and high-sensitivity instruments allow it to view objects too old, distant, or faint for the Hubble Space Telescope. It is named after James E. Webb, administrator of NASA from 1961 to 1968.

Design

Webb's primary mirror consists of 18 hexagonal segments of gold-plated beryllium, which unfold after launch to form a 6.5-metre aperture. A five-layer sunshield the size of a tennis court shields the optics from the Sun, Earth and Moon, allowing the telescope to cool below 50 K (−223 °C). The Mid-Infrared Instrument (MIRI) is cooled further, to below 7 K, by a helium cryocooler.

Launch and deployment

Webb launched on 25 December 2021 from Kourou, French Guiana, aboard an Ariane 5 rocket. Over the following two weeks it performed a complex sequence of deployments — sunshield tensioning and mirror unfolding — before travelling to its halo orbit around the Sun–Earth L2 Lagrange point, about 1.5 million km from Earth, which it reached on 24 January 2022. The first full-colour images and spectra were released on 12 July 2022.

Instruments

The Integrated Science Instrument Module contains four instruments: NIRCam (near-infrared camera), NIRSpec (near-infrared spectrograph), MIRI (mid-infrared instrument), and FGS/NIRISS (fine guidance sensor and near-infrared imager and slitless spectrograph).

Science goals

Webb's four main science goals are to search for light from the first stars and galaxies formed after the Big Bang; to study the formation and evolution of galaxies; to understand star and planet formation; and to study planetary systems and the origins of life, including characterising exoplanet atmospheres.
