The Ganges (in India: Ganga; in Bangladesh: Padma) is a trans-boundary river of Asia which flows through India and Bangladesh. Rising in the western Himalayas in the Indian state of Uttarakhand, it flows south and east through the Gangetic plain of northern India, receiving the Yamuna at Prayagraj, then enters Bangladesh, where it joins the Brahmaputra and Meghna before emptying into the Bay of Bengal through the world's largest river delta. The 2,525 km river supports one of the most densely populated regions on Earth, with more than 400 million people in its basin.

Course

The river's traditional source is the Gangotri Glacier at Gomukh, from which the Bhagirathi stream emerges; at Devprayag the Bhagirathi meets the Alaknanda, and from there the combined stream is called the Ganges. The river descends to the plains at Haridwar and flows past Kanpur, Prayagraj (where it meets the Yamuna and the mythical Sarasvati in the Triveni Sangam), Varanasi and Patna, then through West Bengal past Kolkata (via the Hooghly distributary) before crossing into Bangladesh, where as the Padma it joins the Jamuna (Brahmaputra) and flows on to the sea as the Meghna.

Religious significance

The Ganges is sacred in Hinduism, personified as the goddess Ganga, and is believed to purify sins and aid liberation (moksha). Pilgrims bathe in its waters at holy cities including Haridwar, Prayagraj and Varanasi; the Kumbh Mela festival at Prayagraj is among the largest religious gatherings in the world. Its water (Gangajal) is carried home for rituals, and ashes of the dead are commonly immersed in the river.

Pollution and ecology

The Ganges is severely polluted by untreated sewage, industrial effluents (notably from tanneries at Kanpur) and agricultural runoff, despite major cleanup programmes such as the Ganga Action Plan (1986) and Namami Gange (2014); it was named the national river of India in 2008, and its dolphin, the Ganges river dolphin, is India's national aquatic animal. Other fauna include the gharial and the Ganges shark. In 2017 the Uttarakhand High Court controversially declared the Ganges and Yamuna legal persons, a ruling later stayed.
