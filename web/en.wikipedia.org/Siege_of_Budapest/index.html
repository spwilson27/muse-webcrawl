Siege of Budapest

Date29 December 1944 – 13 February 1945
LocationBudapest, Hungary
ResultSoviet victory
BelligerentsSoviet Union, Romania vs. Nazi Germany, Hungary

The Siege of Budapest or the Battle of Budapest was the 50-day-long encirclement by Soviet forces of the Hungarian capital of Budapest, near the end of World War II. Part of the broader Budapest Offensive, the siege began on 29 December 1944, when the Red Army and Romanian Army encircled the city, and ended on 13 February 1945 with the surrender of the German and Hungarian defenders.

Background

Having suffered nearly 200,000 deaths by the end of 1944, and having lost a significant part of its territory to the Allies, Germany still had some reserves. Hungary was vital to Germany for its oil supplies, and Adolf Hitler declared Budapest a fortress city (Festung) to be held to the last man. Roughly 79,000 German and Hungarian troops were trapped in the city along with close to a million civilians.

The siege

Soviet forces of the 2nd and 3rd Ukrainian Fronts, under Rodion Malinovsky and Fyodor Tolbukhin, fought street by street across Pest and then Buda. German relief attempts — Operations Konrad I, II, and III — failed to break the encirclement. On 18 January 1945 the defenders abandoned Pest, destroying the bridges over the Danube. Fighting in Buda continued until the remaining Axis troops attempted a breakout on 11 February, in which only a few hundred men reached German lines.

Aftermath

The siege devastated Budapest: most of its buildings were damaged or destroyed, tens of thousands of civilians died, and the city's Jewish population suffered massacres by the Arrow Cross even as Soviet troops fought to take the city. The fall of Budapest opened the road to Vienna for the Red Army.
