Svetambara

Svetambara monks wear simple white garments.

Svetambara (also spelled Shvetambara) is one of the two main branches of Jainism, the other being Digambara. The word Svetambara means "white-clad," referring to the plain white garments worn by its ascetics, in contrast to the "sky-clad" (nude) Digambara monks.

History

Tradition holds that the Jain community split around the 3rd century BCE to the early centuries CE, following a famine and migration led by Bhadrabahu to the south. The Svetambara tradition is associated with the councils that compiled the Jain canon (Agamas) at Pataliputra and later at Valabhi in Gujarat in the 5th–6th century CE.

Beliefs and practices

Svetambaras accept the Agamas as authoritative scripture, believe that women can attain liberation (moksha) — regarding the 19th Tirthankara, Mallinatha, as female — and hold that Mahavira was married. Idols of the Tirthankaras in Svetambara temples are depicted wearing garments and ornamentation, with eyes that appear to gaze outward.

Sub-traditions

Within Svetambara there are divisions between the image-worshipping Murtipujakas and the aniconic Sthanakvasis and Terapanthis, who rejected temple worship in later reform movements.
