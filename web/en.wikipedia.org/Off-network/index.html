Off-network

For other uses, see Off network (disambiguation).

Off-network is a term used in broadcasting to describe programming that was first run on a television network and is later sold into syndication. When a series completes its initial network run (or is cancelled), its episodes may be licensed to local stations, cable channels, or streaming services, where the reruns are described as "off-network" programming, in contrast to first-run syndication, which is produced directly for the syndication market.

Economics

Off-network syndication has historically been one of the most lucrative stages in a television series' life cycle. Producers traditionally aimed for about 100 episodes — roughly four to five seasons — to make a series attractive for daily "stripping" in syndication schedules. Successful off-network series such as Seinfeld, Friends, The Simpsons, and Law & Order generated billions of dollars in licence fees and advertising revenue for their studios and participants.

Practice

Deals may be struck on a cash basis, a barter basis (in which the syndicator retains some advertising time), or a combination. Cable networks and, later, streaming platforms became major buyers of off-network rights; the high-profile streaming licensing of series such as The Office and Friends is a direct descendant of the traditional off-network syndication model.
