The Battle of France, also known as the Western Campaign or the Fall of France, was the German invasion of France and the Low Countries during the Second World War. Beginning on 10 May 1940, German forces defeated Allied forces in a six-week campaign using combined-arms Blitzkrieg tactics, conquering France, Belgium, Luxembourg, and the Netherlands and bringing land operations in western Europe largely to an end until the Allied landings in Normandy in 1944.

Background

After the "Phoney War" winter of 1939–40, Allied strategy expected a German attack through Belgium, to be met by an advance to the Dyle Line under the Dyle Plan. The German plan, Fall Gelb, as revised under Erich von Manstein's concept, instead placed the main armoured thrust through the Ardennes forest — a sector the French considered impassable to tanks — with a secondary feint into Belgium and the Netherlands drawing the best Allied forces northward.

Battle

German airborne and armoured forces overran the Netherlands (which surrendered on 15 May after the Rotterdam Blitz) and Belgium, while Army Group A's panzer divisions crossed the Ardennes and forced the Meuse at Sedan on 13–15 May. Driving to the Channel coast, reached at Abbeville on 20 May, they encircled the Allied armies in the north. The British Expeditionary Force and many French troops were evacuated from Dunkirk between 26 May and 4 June, abandoning their heavy equipment. In the second phase, Fall Rot, German forces broke the Weygand Line, entered Paris on 14 June, and pushed south; Italy declared war on 10 June and attacked in the Alps.

Aftermath

France signed an armistice on 22 June 1940 at Compiègne — in the same railway carriage used in 1918. Germany occupied the north and west; a collaborationist government under Marshal Philippe Pétain, seated at Vichy, ruled the unoccupied south until November 1942. General Charles de Gaulle, broadcasting from London on 18 June, called for continued resistance, founding the Free French movement. The swiftness of France's defeat shocked the world and left Britain to fight on alone.
