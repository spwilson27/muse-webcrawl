Korean Demilitarized Zone

Established27 July 1953
Length~250 km (160 mi)
Width4 km (2 km each side of the MDL)
BordersNorth Korea / South Korea

The Korean Demilitarized Zone (DMZ; Korean: 한반도 비무장 지대) is a strip of land running across the Korean Peninsula that serves as a buffer zone between North Korea and South Korea. Created by the Korean Armistice Agreement of 1953, the DMZ follows the Military Demarcation Line (MDL) for about 250 kilometres and extends 2 kilometres on either side of it. Despite its name, it is one of the most heavily fortified borders in the world, guarded by large forces on both sides and laced with landmines, tank traps, and fencing.

Geography and nature

The DMZ runs from the Han River estuary on the Yellow Sea to the East Sea (Sea of Japan) north of Goseong. Largely untouched by human activity for seven decades, it has inadvertently become a wildlife refuge: its forests, wetlands, and river valleys shelter species such as the red-crowned crane, white-naped crane, musk deer, and possibly the Amur leopard, and it is frequently proposed as a future trans-boundary peace park.

Joint Security Area

Near the western end of the DMZ lies the Joint Security Area (JSA) at Panmunjom, where negotiations take place in a row of blue conference buildings straddling the MDL, guarded face-to-face by soldiers of both sides. The "propaganda villages" of Daeseong-dong (South) and Kijŏng-dong (North) face each other across the zone, as do rival flagpoles — the southern one at Daeseong-dong was for a time the world's tallest.

Incidents and tunnels

The DMZ has seen numerous armed clashes, defections, and infiltration attempts since 1953, including the 1976 axe murder incident. Beginning in 1974, South Korea discovered four "tunnels of aggression" dug beneath the zone by North Korea; more are suspected. Parts of the southern edge of the DMZ, including the Third Tunnel and Dora Observatory, are popular tourist destinations.
