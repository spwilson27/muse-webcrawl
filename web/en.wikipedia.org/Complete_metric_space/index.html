Complete metric space

In mathematical analysis, a complete metric space is a metric space in which every Cauchy sequence converges to a point in the space. Intuitively, a complete space has no missing points or holes.

Examples

The real numbers are complete under the usual metric, while the rational numbers are not: a sequence of rationals converging to the square root of 2 has no limit within the rationals. Every finite-dimensional Euclidean space is complete.

Completion

Every metric space has a completion, a complete space into which it embeds densely. The real numbers can be constructed as the completion of the rationals. Completeness is central to the Baire category theorem and the Banach fixed-point theorem.
