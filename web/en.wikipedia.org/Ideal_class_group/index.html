Ideal class group

In algebraic number theory, the ideal class group (or class group) of an algebraic number field is a group measuring the failure of unique factorisation in its ring of integers. It is the quotient of the group of fractional ideals by the subgroup of principal fractional ideals.

Definition

For a number field K with ring of integers OK, nonzero fractional ideals form a free abelian group under multiplication. The principal ideals form a subgroup; the quotient is the class group Cl(K). Its order, the class number hK, equals 1 exactly when OK is a principal ideal domain, hence a unique factorisation domain.

Properties

The class group is always finite — a landmark theorem. The Minkowski bound gives an effective way to compute it for a given field. Quadratic fields with class number 1 relate to the Stark–Heegner theorem and Gauss's class number problem.

History

The theory grew from Kummer's work on Fermat's Last Theorem, where ideal numbers were introduced to restore factorisation in cyclotomic fields, and was formalised by Dedekind using ideals.
