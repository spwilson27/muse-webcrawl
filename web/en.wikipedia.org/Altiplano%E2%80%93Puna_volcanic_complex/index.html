Altiplano–Puna volcanic complex

The Altiplano–Puna volcanic complex is a vast province of large silicic calderas and ignimbrite deposits in the central Andes, spanning parts of Bolivia, Argentina, and Chile. It formed during a major flare-up of volcanism between about 11 and 1 million years ago, associated with subduction of the Nazca Plate beneath South America.

Geology

The complex overlies one of the thickest crustal sections on Earth and a large partially molten body in the mid-crust known as the Altiplano–Puna magma body. Its eruptions produced some of the largest known ignimbrite sheets, each representing hundreds to thousands of cubic kilometres of magma, from caldera systems such as La Pacana, Pastos Grandes, and Cerro Guacha.

Significance

Because of its scale and preservation, the complex is a natural laboratory for studying how enormous silicic magma systems assemble in the crust and erupt as super-eruptions.
