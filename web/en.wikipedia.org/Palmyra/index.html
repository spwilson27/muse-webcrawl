Palmyra (Arabic: تدمر‎, Tadmor) is an ancient city in present-day Homs Governorate, Syria, built around an oasis in the Syrian Desert northeast of Damascus. A caravan crossroads linking the Roman world with Persia, India, and China, it grew rich on trade and reached its zenith in the 3rd century CE under Queen Zenobia, whose Palmyrene Empire briefly broke away from Rome before Aurelian reconquered the city in 273 CE.

History

Mentioned as Tadmor in records of the early 2nd millennium BCE, Palmyra came under Roman influence in the 1st century BCE and was visited by Hadrian in 129 CE. Its bilingual Greek–Palmyrene Aramaic culture fused Roman and Parthian art and architecture. Notable monuments include the Temple of Bel (dedicated 32 CE), the Great Colonnade, the Tetrapylon, and elaborate tower tombs in the Valley of the Tombs.

Modern era and damage

Palmyra was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1980. During the Syrian civil war, the Islamic State occupied the city twice (2015 and 2016–17) and destroyed major monuments, including the Temple of Bel, the Temple of Baalshamin, and the Arch of Triumph, attracting worldwide condemnation. The site's chief archaeologist, Khaled al-Asaad, was murdered by IS in August 2015.
