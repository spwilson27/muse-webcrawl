Achromatic lens

An achromatic lens or achromat is a lens designed to limit the effects of chromatic and spherical aberration. Achromatic lenses are corrected to bring two wavelengths (typically red and blue) into focus on the same plane. One wavelength will be in focus, others will be out of focus to a lesser degree than in a simple lens.

Design

The most common type of achromat is the achromatic doublet, which consists of two individual lenses made from glasses with different dispersion properties, typically crown glass and flint glass. The elements are usually cemented together with Canada balsam or a modern adhesive. The doublet corrects chromatic aberration for two colours and, by suitable choice of curvatures, can also reduce spherical aberration.

History

The achromatic doublet was developed in the 18th century. Chester Moore Hall designed an early achromat around 1733, while John Dollond patented and commercialised the design in 1758. Achromatic refracting telescopes greatly improved image quality, and achromats remain widely used in binoculars, microscopes, camera lenses, and small telescopes. Lenses corrected at three wavelengths are called apochromats.
