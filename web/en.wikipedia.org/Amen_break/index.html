Amen break

The Amen break is a drum break that has been widely sampled in popular music. It comes from the 1969 track "Amen, Brother" by the American soul group the Winstons, performed by drummer Gregory "G.C." Coleman. The six-second, four-bar segment became a foundational building block of hip hop, jungle, drum and bass, and breakbeat hardcore.

History

"Amen, Brother" was released as the B-side of the Winstons' 1969 single "Color Him Father", which won a Grammy Award. The break itself attracted little attention until the 1980s, when it appeared on the Ultimate Breaks and Beats compilation series and was adopted by hip hop producers, appearing in tracks such as "I Desire" by Salt-N-Pepa and "Straight Outta Compton" by N.W.A. In the early 1990s, British producers sped the break up and chopped it into individual hits, creating the signature rhythms of jungle and drum and bass. It has since been used in thousands of recordings across pop, rock, and advertising.

Copyright and recognition

Neither Coleman nor Winstons bandleader Richard Lewis Spencer received royalties for the break's use; Spencer was unaware of its ubiquity for years, and Coleman died in 2006. A 2015 crowdfunding campaign by British DJ Martyn Webster raised over £24,000 for Spencer in recognition of the break's cultural value. The break's legal status as an unlicensed but ubiquitous sample is often cited in discussions of copyright and sampling.
