The Tokyo Anime Award (東京アニメアワード) is a Japanese animation award established in 2002, originally presented as part of the Tokyo International Anime Fair. It honours outstanding anime works and creators of the preceding year, with its top prize — the Grand Prize, later known as Anime of the Year — regarded as one of the Japanese animation industry's principal honours. When the Tokyo International Anime Fair merged into AnimeJapan in 2014, the awards continued as the centrepiece of the separate Tokyo Anime Award Festival (TAAF).

Categories

The awards recognise theatrical films and television series, with the Animation of the Year Grand Prize divided between the two formats. Individual awards have included Best Director, Best Screenplay (original script), Best Animator, Best Character and Mechanical Design, Best Art Direction, Best Music and Best Voice Actor. A fan-voted Anime Fan Award, decided by public ballot from a list of eligible titles, was introduced in the festival era, alongside merit awards honouring lifetime contributions to the art form.

Notable winners

Early Grand Prize winners reflected the breadth of the medium: Spirited Away won at the first ceremony, and subsequent honorees included works by Studio Ghibli and directors such as Satoshi Kon, whose Millennium Actress shared the first Grand Prize. In the festival era, winners of Anime of the Year have included Attack on Titan, Love Live!, Shirobako, Your Name, In This Corner of the World, Kemono Friends, Zombie Land Saga, Demon Slayer: Kimetsu no Yaiba, Violet Evergarden: The Movie, Jujutsu Kaisen and Spy × Family.

History

The award was created in 2002 by the organisers of the Tokyo International Anime Fair, an industry event supported by the Tokyo Metropolitan Government. The fair's 2011 edition was cancelled following the Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami, and in 2014 the fair merged with the Anime Contents Expo to form AnimeJapan; the award programme was preserved and reconstituted as the Tokyo Anime Award Festival, held annually in Ikebukuro, Tokyo, with screenings, competitions for independent and student animation, and the awards ceremony.
