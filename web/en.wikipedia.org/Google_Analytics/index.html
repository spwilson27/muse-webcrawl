Google Analytics


The Google Analytics dashboard

Google Analytics is a web analytics service offered by Google that tracks and reports website traffic and also mobile app traffic and events, currently as a platform inside the Google Marketing Platform brand. Google launched the service in November 2005 after acquiring the Urchin Software Corporation. As of 2019, Google Analytics is the most widely used web analytics service on the Internet, installed on a majority of the most-visited websites.

Features

Google Analytics provides an SDK that allows gathering usage data from iOS and Android apps, known as Google Analytics for Mobile Apps. For websites, a JavaScript tracking snippet placed on each page collects data about visitors: pages viewed, session duration, traffic sources (search engines, referring sites, campaigns), geographic and technical characteristics, and conversions and e-commerce transactions. Reports are organised around real-time monitoring, audience demographics and interests, acquisition channels, behaviour flow, and goal conversions, and can be integrated with Google Ads and Search Console.

History

Google Analytics is built on the technology of Urchin Software Corp., whose Urchin On Demand service Google acquired in April 2005; the free Google-branded version launched that November to such demand that sign-ups were suspended within days. Google also acquired Measure Map (2006) and incorporated its visualisations. Major versions include the asynchronous tracking code (2009), Universal Analytics (announced 2012, with cross-device user measurement), and Google Analytics 4 (GA4, launched 2020), which replaced Universal Analytics with an event-based model spanning web and apps and greater reliance on machine learning; Universal Analytics stopped processing new data on 1 July 2023. A paid enterprise tier, Google Analytics 360, offers higher limits, unsampled reports, and support.

Privacy and legal issues

Because Google Analytics sets cookies and transfers usage data to Google servers, it has been a focus of privacy debate and regulation. German and other European data-protection authorities have contested its legality at various times; in 2022, the Austrian, French, and Italian regulators found that use of Google Analytics violated the GDPR's rules on transfers of personal data to the United States (which lacks equivalent protections) in light of the Schrems II judgment, prompting many sites to seek alternatives or additional safeguards. The service is blocked by many privacy tools, ad blockers, and by browsers restricting third-party cookies, which can bias measured traffic. Google responded with features such as IP anonymisation, consent mode, and cookieless measurement options.
