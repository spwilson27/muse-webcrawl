Valkyrie

A valkyrie bearing a slain hero to Valhalla.

In Norse mythology, a valkyrie (Old Norse: valkyrja, "chooser of the slain") is one of a host of female figures who guide souls of the dead to the afterlife. According to the sources, valkyries choose those who may die in battle and those who may live; the chosen slain warriors are taken to Valhalla, ruled over by the god Odin, where they become einherjar and prepare to aid Odin during Ragnarök. In Valhalla the valkyries also serve the warriors mead.

Sources

Valkyries are attested throughout Old Norse literature: in the Poetic Edda, the Prose Edda of Snorri Sturluson, sagas, skaldic poetry, and runic inscriptions. Named valkyries include Brynhildr, Sigrún, Gunnr, Róta, and Skuld — the youngest of the Norns. They are frequently associated with ravens, swans, and horses, and in heroic lays they appear as lovers and protectors of heroes.

Legacy

The figure of the valkyrie has remained popular, notably through Wagner's opera cycle Der Ring des Nibelungen, whose "Ride of the Valkyries" is among the most famous passages in classical music. The name was also given to Operation Valkyrie, the 1944 German plot against Hitler, and to the North American XB-70 Valkyrie bomber.
