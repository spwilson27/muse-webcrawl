Icosidodecahedron

In geometry, an icosidodecahedron is a polyhedron with twenty triangular faces and twelve pentagonal faces. It has 30 identical vertices, with two triangles and two pentagons meeting at each, and 60 identical edges. It is one of the Archimedean solids and is quasiregular, being the rectification of both the regular icosahedron and the regular dodecahedron — the two solids whose names it combines.

Properties

The icosidodecahedron has icosahedral symmetry (Ih) and the vertex configuration 3.5.3.5. Its pentagonal faces are arranged in the same pattern as the faces of a dodecahedron, and its triangular faces like those of an icosahedron; it can be formed by truncating either solid at the midpoints of its edges. Ten great circles around the solid correspond to the planes of a pentagonal structure, a feature Buckminster Fuller exploited in the geometry of his "vector equilibrium" and tensegrity designs.

Related polyhedra

The dual polyhedron is the rhombic triacontahedron. The icosidodecahedron appears in the small rhombicosidodecahedron family of expansions, in star polyhedra, and as the shape approximated by some virus capsids and geodesic structures.
