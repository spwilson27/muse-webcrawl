Advanced Power Management

Advanced Power Management (APM) is a technical specification developed by Intel and Microsoft and released in 1992 which governs power management in personal computers. It allows the BIOS of a computer to control power-saving functions such as suspending or shutting down components when idle.

APM
Developers: Intel, Microsoft
Released: 1992
Successor: ACPI

Design

Under APM, the BIOS is in charge of power management decisions, with the operating system communicating through an APM driver. Power states range from full-on through standby and suspend to off. Because the operating system had limited visibility into the BIOS's actions, APM could cause conflicts and instability.

Replacement by ACPI

APM was superseded by the Advanced Configuration and Power Interface (ACPI), which places power management under operating-system control. ACPI support became standard in the late 1990s, and APM was removed from later versions of Windows and Linux.
