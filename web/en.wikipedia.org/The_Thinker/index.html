The Thinker (French: Le Penseur), originally titled The Poet (Le Poète), is a bronze sculpture by Auguste Rodin, usually placed on a stone pedestal. It depicts a gigantic nude male figure of heroic build, seated on a rock, leaning forward with his right elbow on his left thigh, his chin resting on the back of his right hand. The work shows the figure in deep contemplation, often used as an image representing philosophy.

Origins

Rodin conceived the figure in 1880–1882 as the centrepiece of The Gates of Hell, his monumental commission based on Dante's Inferno. There the figure — representing Dante himself, or Adam, or Rodin as creator — surveys the damned below. The figure's pose, with its twisted torso and intense musculature, owes debts to Michelangelo's figures in the Sistine Chapel and Medici Chapel.

Casts

Rodin made the figure in several sizes. The first bronze cast dates to 1884; the enlarged monumental version was first exhibited in Copenhagen in 1888 and in Paris in 1904, where it was placed before the Panthéon in 1906 (later moved to the Rodin Museum). Some 28 full-sized casts exist, in museums worldwide, including the Metropolitan Museum of Art, the National Gallery of Art in Washington, and a cast placed over Rodin's own grave at Meudon.

Interpretation

Unlike serene images of contemplation, Rodin's Thinker strains: every muscle is engaged, suggesting that thought is a physical, creative labour. The sculpture has become one of the most widely recognised works of modern art.
