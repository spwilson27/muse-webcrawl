The New York State Department of Environmental Conservation (NYSDEC) is a department of the New York state government responsible for the conservation, protection, and enhancement of the state's natural resources and environment. The department manages nearly five million acres of land, including the Adirondack and Catskill Forest Preserves, regulates hunting and fishing, protects air and water quality, enforces environmental laws, and responds to oil and chemical spills.

History

The DEC was created in 1970, the year of the first Earth Day, by consolidating several older agencies and commissions dating back to the 19th-century Fisheries Commission and the state's forest and water supply bodies. Its headquarters are in Albany, with nine regional offices across the state.

Divisions and responsibilities

The department's work spans fish and wildlife management, lands and forests, water resources, air resources, mineral resources, environmental quality and remediation, and climate policy. Its uniformed Environmental Conservation Officers and Forest Rangers enforce environmental law and perform search-and-rescue operations in wild lands. The DEC also operates campgrounds, boat launches, and environmental education centers, issues permits for activities affecting wetlands and waterways, oversees the cleanup of contaminated sites such as Superfund and brownfield locations, and plays a key role in implementing New York's climate law, the Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act of 2019.

The department manages state land within the Adirondack Park — about 2.6 million acres — and the Catskill Park, lands protected as "forever wild" by Article XIV of the New York State Constitution.
