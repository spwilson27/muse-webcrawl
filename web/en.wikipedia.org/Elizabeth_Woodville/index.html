Elizabeth Woodville

Elizabeth Woodville (c. 1437 – 8 June 1492) was Queen of England as the wife of King Edward IV, from her marriage in 1464 until Edward's death in 1483. She was the mother of Edward V and of Elizabeth of York, wife of Henry VII.

Marriage and queenship

A widow of a Lancastrian knight, Elizabeth secretly married Edward IV, a Yorkist, in 1464. The marriage and the rapid advancement of her many Woodville relatives provoked resentment, notably from the Earl of Warwick. Twice during the Wars of the Roses she took sanctuary in Westminster Abbey.

Later life

After Edward IV died, her young son Edward V was proclaimed king but was displaced by his uncle, Richard III; Edward and his brother Richard — the "Princes in the Tower" — disappeared. Elizabeth later supported Henry Tudor, whose marriage to her daughter Elizabeth of York united the houses of York and Lancaster. She spent her last years in retirement at Bermondsey Abbey.
