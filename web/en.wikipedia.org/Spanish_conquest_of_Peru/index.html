Spanish conquest of Peru

Date1532–1572
LocationInca Empire (modern Peru)
ResultSpanish victory; Viceroyalty of Peru established
Key figuresFrancisco Pizarro, Atahualpa

The Spanish conquest of Peru was one of the most important campaigns in the Spanish colonisation of the Americas. Led by Francisco Pizarro, a small force of conquistadors, aided by disease and Inca civil war, overthrew the Inca Empire between 1532 and 1572.

Background

The Inca Empire had recently been weakened by an epidemic of European disease, likely smallpox, and by a war of succession between the brothers Huáscar and Atahualpa following the death of the emperor Huayna Capac.

Cajamarca and aftermath

In November 1532 Pizarro captured Atahualpa at the Battle of Cajamarca. The emperor filled a room with gold and silver as ransom but was nevertheless executed in 1533. Spanish forces occupied Cuzco, installed puppet rulers, and faced a major rebellion under Manco Inca in 1536. The last Inca stronghold at Vilcabamba fell in 1572.
