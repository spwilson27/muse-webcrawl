Multiple independently targetable reentry vehicle

MIRV warheads of a Peacekeeper missile during a test

Abbreviation: MIRV
First deployed: 1970 (Minuteman III)

A multiple independently targetable reentry vehicle (MIRV) is an exoatmospheric ballistic missile payload containing several warheads, each capable of striking a separate target. By contrast, a unitary warhead missile carries one warhead, and a multiple reentry vehicle (MRV) releases several warheads that cannot be aimed independently.

Operation

After the main rocket stages burn out, a manoeuvrable "bus" carrying the warheads makes small course corrections with its own thrusters, releasing each warhead on a trajectory toward a different target, sometimes along with decoys and penetration aids to confuse missile defences. MIRVed missiles therefore let a single launcher threaten many aimpoints, and they complicate defence: an interceptor force must engage many warheads for each incoming missile.

History and arms control

The United States first deployed MIRVs on the Minuteman III ICBM in 1970 and on the Poseidon submarine-launched missile in 1971, initially partly to defeat Soviet anti-ballistic missile defences. The Soviet Union followed with MIRVed ICBMs such as the SS-18. MIRVs were controversial in arms control because large numbers of accurate warheads on vulnerable silo-based missiles were seen as destabilising — a possible incentive to strike first. The START II treaty (1993) would have banned MIRVed ICBMs entirely but never entered into force; Russia and the United States still field MIRVed ICBMs and SLBMs, and China, India, Pakistan, France, and the United Kingdom deploy or are developing MIRV-capable systems.
