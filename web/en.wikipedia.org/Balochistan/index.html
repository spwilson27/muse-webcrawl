Balochistan

Balochistan (Balochi: بلۏچستان; Urdu: بلوچستان) is a province of Pakistan. Located in the southwestern region of the country, it is the largest province of Pakistan by land area but the least populated. Its provincial capital and largest city is Quetta. The name means "the land of the Baloch" in Persian.

Geography

Balochistan is located at the eastern edge of the Iranian plateau and borders Iran to the west and Afghanistan to the north. To the south it has a coastline of about 770 kilometres along the Arabian Sea. The terrain is largely arid mountains and desert, including the Sulaiman Mountains in the northeast and the Makran range along the coast. The port city of Gwadar, developed with Chinese investment as part of the China–Pakistan Economic Corridor, lies on the southwestern coast.

History

The region was part of various Persian, Macedonian, Arab, Mongol and Mughal spheres before coming under the influence of the Khanate of Kalat. British influence grew during the nineteenth century, and after the partition of India in 1947 the province acceded to Pakistan. Balochistan became a full province of Pakistan on 1 July 1970. The province has experienced several periods of insurgency by Baloch nationalist groups seeking greater autonomy or independence.

Demographics

The population is predominantly Baloch, with significant Pashtun communities in the north and smaller numbers of Brahui, Sindhi, Punjabi and Hazara people. Islam is the predominant religion. Much of the province is rural and sparsely settled, and literacy rates are among the lowest in Pakistan.

Economy

The economy rests on agriculture, livestock, mining and natural gas. The Sui gas field, discovered in 1952, was for decades a major source of Pakistan's natural gas. The province is rich in minerals including coal, chromite, copper and gold; the Reko Diq copper-gold deposit is among the largest undeveloped deposits of its kind in the world.
