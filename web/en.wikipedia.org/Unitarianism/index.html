Unitarianism

Unitarianism
Type: Christian theological tradition
Origins: 16th-century Poland and Transylvania
Key figures: Fausto Sozzini, Joseph Priestley, William Ellery Channing

Unitarianism is a Christian theological tradition holding that God is a single entity, in contrast to the doctrine of the Trinity. Unitarians regard Jesus as a teacher and moral exemplar rather than as God incarnate, and historically have emphasised reason, free inquiry, and ethical living over creed and ritual.

History

The movement's roots lie in the Radical Reformation: the Polish Brethren in Poland-Lithuania and the Unitarian Church of Transylvania, whose Edict of Torda (1568) was an early decree of religious tolerance. In Britain, figures such as Joseph Priestley and Theophilus Lindsey organised Unitarian congregations in the 18th century. In the United States, Unitarianism became influential in New England; William Ellery Channing's 1819 sermon "Unitarian Christianity" and the founding of the American Unitarian Association in 1825 consolidated the denomination, from which emerged Transcendentalists such as Ralph Waldo Emerson. In 1961 American Unitarians merged with the Universalists to form the Unitarian Universalist Association.
