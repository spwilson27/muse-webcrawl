The MIT Media Lab is a research laboratory at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, founded in 1985 by Nicholas Negroponte and former MIT president Jerome Wiesner. Growing out of MIT's Architecture Machine Group, the Lab is known for an "antidisciplinary" research culture that mixes engineering, computer science, design, art and social science, and for a consortium funding model in which corporate sponsors gain access to research across the whole laboratory rather than funding individual projects.

Research at the Lab has produced or incubated technologies and projects including wearable computing, tangible user interfaces, electronic ink (E Ink), the LEGO Mindstorms robotics kits (with the LEGO Group), the Scratch programming language for children, the One Laptop per Child initiative, and affective computing. Its graduate programme, Media Arts and Sciences, awards master's and doctoral degrees.

History

Negroponte's Architecture Machine Group had explored human–computer interaction since the late 1960s. The Media Lab opened in the Wiesner Building (E15), designed by I. M. Pei, in 1985. Negroponte directed the Lab for its first 15 years and popularised its philosophy in his 1995 bestseller Being Digital. He was succeeded by Walter Bender, Frank Moss (2006–2011), Joi Ito (2011–2019), and Dava Newman (2021– ). A major expansion, the E14 building designed by Fumihiko Maki, opened in 2010.

In 2019 the Lab and MIT were engulfed in controversy over donations from convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein, which had been solicited and accepted under Ito's directorship despite Epstein's disqualified-donor status; Ito resigned in September 2019, and MIT commissioned an external review (the Goodwin Procter report, 2020) and adopted new donor-vetting policies.

Research

The Lab is organised into research groups led by faculty, who change over time; well-known groups have included Affective Computing (Rosalind Picard), Tangible Media (Hiroshi Ishii), Lifelong Kindergarten (Mitchel Resnick, creator of Scratch), Responsive Environments (Joe Paradiso), Fluid Interfaces (Pattie Maes), the Center for Bits and Atoms (Neil Gershenfeld), Civic Media, and the Biomechatronics group (Hugh Herr). Signature spinoffs and projects include E Ink Corporation, the OLPC foundation, Affectiva, Formlabs (founded by Lab alumni), and the open-source hardware culture surrounding fab labs.

Funding and structure

Unlike conventional university labs, the Media Lab is funded overwhelmingly by a consortium of corporate members — historically over 90% of its budget — whose members receive royalty-free licences to Lab intellectual property. Faculty are appointed across MIT departments, and research is showcased at regular sponsor events noted for polished live demonstrations ("demo or die" was a longtime Lab motto).
