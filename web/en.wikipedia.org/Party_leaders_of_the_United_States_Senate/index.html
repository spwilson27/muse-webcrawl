Party leaders of the United States Senate

The party leaders of the United States Senate are the majority leader and minority leader, elected by the party conferences that control the majority and minority in the Senate. The majority leader, who is also the leader of the largest party in the Senate, serves as the chief representative and parliamentarian of that party on the Senate floor and is the chamber's de facto chief legislator.

The Senate chamber, where the party leaders manage floor business.

Roles

The majority leader schedules legislation, negotiates the terms under which bills and amendments are debated, and speaks first for the majority party. By Senate precedent, the presiding officer recognizes the majority leader preferentially, giving the office powerful control over the floor agenda. The minority leader leads the opposition party, coordinates its legislative strategy, and serves as its chief spokesperson. Each leader is assisted by a whip — the majority whip and minority whip — who count votes and maintain party discipline, and by a conference chair and policy committee chair.

History

The offices developed gradually. Democrats first designated a floor leader in 1920, and Republicans followed in 1925; before that, party leadership on the floor rested with committee chairs and the caucus chair. Influential leaders have included Lyndon B. Johnson, whose mastery of Senate procedure as majority leader in the 1950s became legendary, Mike Mansfield, the longest-serving majority leader, and Robert Byrd. In recent decades the leaders have included Mitch McConnell, Harry Reid, Chuck Schumer, and John Thune.
