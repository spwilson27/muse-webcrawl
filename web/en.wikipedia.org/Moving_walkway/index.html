Moving walkway

A moving walkway (also called a travelator, moving sidewalk, or moving pavement) is a slow-moving conveyor mechanism that transports people across a horizontal or slightly inclined plane over a short to medium distance. Moving walkways are common in airports, railway and metro stations, exhibition centres, supermarkets and other large buildings where pedestrians must cover long distances.

Design and use

Most travelators use a continuous belt of metal plates or rubber over rollers, running at about 0.5 m/s (roughly half walking speed) and up to about 1 m/s; passengers may stand or walk, effectively doubling their speed while walking. Inclined versions (up to about 12°) connect levels in shops and stations and can carry shopping carts with special grooves. Safety features include comb plates at the ends, handrails moving at the same speed, and emergency stop buttons.

History

An early moving walkway was exhibited at the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago, and a famous high-speed trottoir roulant operated at the 1900 Paris Exposition. Airport installations proliferated from the 1950s. Experimental accelerated walkways, which speed passengers up in a middle section, have been installed in a few stations (e.g., Montparnasse, Paris, and Toronto Pearson Airport's high-speed walkway, later removed).
