Beer–Lambert law

The Beer–Lambert law (also called Beer's law or the Beer–Lambert–Bouguer law) relates the absorption of light to the properties of the material through which the light travels. It states that absorbance is directly proportional to the concentration of the absorbing substance and to the path length of the sample.

Formulation

The law is written A = εlc, where A is absorbance, ε is the molar absorptivity (a constant for a given substance and wavelength), l is the path length, and c is the concentration. It is the basis of quantitative spectrophotometry in chemistry.

History and limits

The law combines the observations of Pierre Bouguer (1729), Johann Heinrich Lambert (1760), and August Beer (1852). It holds best for dilute solutions and monochromatic light; at high concentrations, chemical interactions and instrument effects cause deviations.
