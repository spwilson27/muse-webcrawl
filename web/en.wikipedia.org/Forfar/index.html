Forfar is a town and former royal burgh in Angus, Scotland, and the historic county town of Angus, which was formerly known as Forfarshire. It lies in the valley of Strathmore, northeast of Dundee, and has a population of roughly 14,000.

History

The area around Forfar has been occupied since Neolithic times, and a Roman camp was established nearby. The town became a royal residence in the medieval period—King Malcolm III (Canmore) is associated with a castle at Forfar—and it received royal burgh status. In 1651 the town was occupied by the forces of Oliver Cromwell. Forfar was also the scene of witch trials in the 1660s, in which several local women were accused and executed.

Economy and culture

Forfar was historically a centre for the manufacture of linen and jute, and for shoemaking. The town gives its name to the Forfar bridie, a meat pastry said to have been created in the town in the nineteenth century. Forfar Loch, on the town's western edge, is the centrepiece of a country park; legend associates the loch's island with a castle of Malcolm Canmore.

Sport

The town's football club, Forfar Athletic, plays at Station Park and has competed in the Scottish league system since the 1920s.
