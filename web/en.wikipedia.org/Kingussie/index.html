Kingussie (Scottish Gaelic: Ceann a' Ghiùthsaich, "head of the pine forest") is a small town in the Badenoch and Strathspey district of the Scottish Highlands, within the Highland council area and the Cairngorms National Park. It lies on the River Spey and on the A9 road and Highland Main Line railway between Perth and Inverness, and has long served as the historic capital of the district of Badenoch.

History

The town grew up around routes through the central Highlands; the nearby ruins of Ruthven Barracks, built by the British government after the Jacobite rising of 1715 and burned by retreating Jacobites in 1746, stand on a mound above the village on the site of earlier castles of the Comyns and Gordons. The arrival of the railway in 1863 made Kingussie a popular Victorian holiday resort for shooting, fishing, and walking, and its high street retains much 19th-century character.

Shinty

Kingussie is famous for its shinty club, Kingussie Camanachd, founded in 1893 and regarded as the most successful club in the history of the sport. The team famously went unbeaten for long stretches in the 1980s and 1990s, winning a world-record run of league titles and many Camanachd Cups, and its rivalry with Newtonmore, three miles away, is the sport's great derby.

Attractions

Visitors are drawn to the Highland Folk Museum at nearby Newtonmore (founded by Isabel Frances Grant), walks along the Spey and to Ruthven Barracks, and the surrounding Cairngorms scenery. The town's railway station serves the Highland Main Line, and the golf club, founded in 1891, lies at the foot of the hill of Creag Bheag.
