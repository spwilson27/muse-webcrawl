Michael Braungart

Born: 1958, Schwäbisch Gmünd, Germany
Occupation: Chemist, environmental designer
Known for: Cradle to Cradle design

Michael Braungart (born 1958) is a German chemist and environmental thinker best known as co-author, with architect William McDonough, of Cradle to Cradle: Remaking the Way We Make Things (2002), which proposed designing products so that every material circulates as a "nutrient" in biological or technical cycles, eliminating the concept of waste.

Career

Braungart studied chemistry and process engineering and was active in Greenpeace's chemistry section in the 1980s. In 1987 he founded the Environmental Protection Encouragement Agency (EPEA) in Hamburg, which assesses the health and recyclability of materials and operates the Cradle to Cradle Certified products program with McDonough's firm MBDC. He has held professorships at Leuphana University Lüneburg and Erasmus University Rotterdam.

Ideas

Braungart argues that eco-efficiency — being "less bad" — is insufficient, and advocates eco-effectiveness: products designed from the outset for disassembly, safe chemistry, and perpetual reuse, modeled on natural systems such as a cherry tree that produces abundance without waste. His follow-up book The Upcycle (2013) extended the argument. Critics note challenges in scaling certification and in defining safe material flows, but the framework has influenced corporate design at firms including Ford, Nike, and Herman Miller.
