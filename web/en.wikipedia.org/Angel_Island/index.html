Angel Island

Angel Island is the largest natural island in San Francisco Bay, California, with an area of about 3.1 km². Its highest point is Mount Livermore (236 m). Almost the entire island is Angel Island State Park, accessible by ferry from Tiburon and San Francisco.

History

The island was a hunting and fishing ground of the Coast Miwok people. In 1775 the Spanish ship San Carlos anchored in Ayala Cove. During the 19th century the island served as a cattle ranch and a U.S. Army post (Camp Reynolds, later Fort McDowell), used in the Civil War and both World Wars as a transit and discharge centre for troops.

Immigration Station

From 1910 to 1940 the Angel Island Immigration Station processed about one million immigrants, mostly from Asia — often called the "Ellis Island of the West." Under exclusion laws, many Chinese immigrants were detained for weeks or years in barracks whose walls preserve carved poems expressing their sorrow and anger. The station is now a National Historic Landmark and museum.

A Nike missile site operated on the island during the Cold War. Today the island is popular for hiking, cycling, and panoramic views of the bay.
