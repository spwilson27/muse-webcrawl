Cao Kun

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cao Kun (simplified Chinese: 曹锟; traditional Chinese: 曹錕; pinyin: Cáo Kūn; 12 December 1862 – 15 May 1938) was a Chinese warlord and politician, who served as the President of the Republic of China from 1923 to 1924, as well as the military leader of the Zhili clique in the Beiyang government.

Rise to power

Born in Tianjin to a poor family, Cao rose through the ranks of the Beiyang Army. After the death of Feng Guozhang he became leader of the Zhili clique and, with Wu Peifu as his principal commander, defeated the Anhui clique in 1920 and the Fengtian clique in the First Zhili–Fengtian War of 1922.

Presidency

Cao openly bought the presidency in 1923, bribing members of the National Assembly — some checks were issued at 5,000 yuan per vote — a scandal that discredited his regime. A constitution was promulgated under him in 1923. In October 1924, during the Second Zhili–Fengtian War, his ally Feng Yuxiang betrayed him in the Beijing Coup, imprisoning Cao and ending his presidency.

Later life

Released in 1926, Cao retired to Tianjin, where he devoted himself to painting and calligraphy, and died in 1938.
