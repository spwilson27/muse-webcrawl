Szemerédi's theorem

Szemerédi's theorem
Field: Combinatorics, ergodic theory
Proved: 1975, by Endre Szemerédi
Conjecture of: Erdős and Turán (1936)

Szemerédi's theorem is a result in arithmetic combinatorics stating that any subset of the integers of positive upper density contains arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions. It was proved by Endre Szemerédi in 1975, settling a conjecture of Paul Erdős and Pál Turán.

Statement

For any percentage δ > 0 of the integers 1, …, N, and any integer k, there exists a threshold N₀ such that for all N > N₀, every subset of {1, …, N} of size at least δN contains an arithmetic progression of length k.

Proofs and consequences

Szemerédi's original proof was combinatorial and highly complex; his regularity lemma, developed in its course, became a major tool in graph theory. Hillel Furstenberg gave an alternative proof using ergodic theory in 1977, and Timothy Gowers later provided Fourier-analytic proofs with quantitative bounds. A famous corollary is the Green–Tao theorem (2008), which uses Szemerédi-type arguments to show the primes contain arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions.
