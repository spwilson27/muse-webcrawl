Aeroplan is the frequent-flyer program of Air Canada.

Aeroplan is the frequent-flyer program owned by Air Canada, Canada's flag carrier. The program was created in 1984 and allows members to earn points for travel on Air Canada and its Star Alliance partner airlines, as well as through co-branded credit cards, hotels, car rentals and retail partners. Points can be redeemed for flights, upgrades, merchandise, gift cards and other rewards.

History

Aeroplan was established by Air Canada in 1984 as one of the early airline loyalty programs. In 2002 it became a wholly owned subsidiary of Air Canada, and in 2005 its parent company, ACE Aviation Holdings, took the program public as the Aeroplan Income Fund, one of the world's first publicly traded loyalty programs. The operating company was later renamed Aimia Inc. and expanded into loyalty programs in other countries.

In 2017 Air Canada announced it would launch a new in-house loyalty program in 2020 and end its exclusive relationship with Aimia, sending Aimia's share price sharply lower. However, in 2018 a consortium including Air Canada agreed to purchase the Aeroplan program from Aimia for C$450 million plus the assumption of points liabilities; the transaction closed in January 2019. The relaunched Aeroplan program debuted in November 2020 with dynamic and fixed flight reward charts, points pooling for families and expanded partners.

Program structure

Members earn points based on distance and fare class for flights, or on spending with program partners. Elite status within the program is branded Aeroplan Elite Status (25K, 35K, 50K, 75K and Super Elite tiers), aligned with Air Canada's overall status tiers, and confers benefits such as lounge access, priority services and upgrade credits. Aeroplan points do not expire while accounts remain active under program rules, which have changed over time.
