Unequal treaties

The signing of unequal treaties in 19th-century China and Japan.

Unequal treaties (Chinese: 不平等條約; Japanese: 不平等条約) is a term used to describe a series of treaties signed during the 19th and early 20th centuries between Asian states — principally the Qing dynasty of China, the Tokugawa shogunate of Japan, and the Joseon dynasty of Korea — and Western powers, on terms that were disadvantageous to the Asian signatories. Such treaties were often concluded after military defeat or under threat of force, and typically granted the Western powers privileges such as extraterritoriality, fixed low tariffs, the opening of treaty ports, and territorial concessions.

China

The first treaty widely described as unequal was the Treaty of Nanking (1842), which ended the First Opium War. It ceded Hong Kong Island to Britain, opened five treaty ports, and imposed an indemnity. Further treaties followed, including the Treaties of Tientsin (1858), the Convention of Peking (1860), the Treaty of Shimonoseki (1895, with Japan), and the Boxer Protocol (1901). The last vestiges of the system in China were relinquished by the United States and Britain in 1943.

Japan

Japan was subjected to unequal treaties beginning with the Harris Treaty of 1858, which granted extraterritoriality and deprived Japan of tariff autonomy. Unlike China, Japan successfully renegotiated the treaties: extraterritoriality ended in 1899 and full tariff autonomy was restored by 1911, following the modernization programme of the Meiji Restoration.

Korea and other states

Korea signed unequal treaties with Japan, the United States, and European powers beginning with the Japan–Korea Treaty of 1876. Siam and the Ottoman Empire were also subject to capitulatory regimes, in which foreign subjects were exempted from local jurisdiction.

Legacy

In China, the unequal treaties are a central element of the national narrative of the "century of humiliation". In modern international law, treaties procured by the threat or use of force are considered void under the Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties.
