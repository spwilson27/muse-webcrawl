Bacterial vaginosis

Bacterial vaginosis
Specialty: Gynaecology
Symptoms: Often none; thin grey-white discharge, fishy odour, burning
Cause: Imbalance of vaginal bacteria (loss of lactobacilli, overgrowth of anaerobes)
Treatment: Antibiotics such as metronidazole or clindamycin

Bacterial vaginosis (BV) is a condition of the vagina caused by an imbalance of its normal bacterial flora. Common symptoms include increased vaginal discharge, often thin and grey-white with a fishy odour, and burning during urination; about half of those affected have no symptoms. BV is the most common vaginal condition in women of reproductive age.

Cause and diagnosis

BV develops when the normally dominant hydrogen-peroxide-producing lactobacilli decrease and anaerobic bacteria such as Gardnerella vaginalis, Prevotella, and Mobiluncus species proliferate, raising vaginal pH. Risk factors include douching, new or multiple sexual partners, and antibiotic use. Diagnosis is commonly made using the Amsel criteria or by Gram stain scoring (Nugent score), which shows the characteristic loss of lactobacilli and the presence of "clue cells."

Complications and treatment

Having BV increases the risk of infection by HIV and other sexually transmitted infections, and in pregnancy it is associated with preterm birth. Treatment is with antibiotics such as oral or vaginal metronidazole or clindamycin, though recurrence within a year is common. Treatment of male partners has not traditionally been recommended, though recent evidence has prompted renewed study.
