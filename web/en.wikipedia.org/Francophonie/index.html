Francophonie

The Francophonie: an international community of French-speaking peoples and states

The term Francophonie (or la Francophonie) refers both to the community of peoples and countries that use French, in whole or in part, as their language, and to the international organisation representing them, the Organisation internationale de la Francophonie (OIF). The word was first coined by the French geographer Onésime Reclus around 1880.

The organisation

Founded in 1970 as the Agency for Cultural and Technical Cooperation, the OIF today brings together more than 80 member states and governments, representing over a billion people, including some 320 million French speakers. Its summits, held every two years, address politics, economics, culture, education, and the defence of democracy and human rights. The OIF supports French-language education, media such as TV5Monde, and the Jeux de la Francophonie sporting and cultural games.

The French-speaking world

French is an official language in 29 countries, from France, Canada (Quebec), Belgium, and Switzerland to Senegal, Ivory Coast, and the Democratic Republic of the Congo — the most populous Francophone country. Demographic growth in Africa means a rising majority of the world's French speakers are African. The Francophonie is frequently compared to the Commonwealth of Nations, though it emphasises linguistic and cultural bonds over shared constitutional history.
