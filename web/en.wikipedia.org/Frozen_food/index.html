Frozen food

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Frozen food is food preserved by freezing, which slows decomposition by turning residual moisture into ice, inhibiting the growth of most bacteria. Freezing is one of the oldest and most widely used methods of food preservation, and the modern frozen food industry rests on fast-freezing techniques developed in the early twentieth century.

History

Indigenous peoples in cold climates froze fish and game naturally, and commercial cold storage spread in the nineteenth century with mechanical refrigeration. In the 1920s, American naturalist and inventor Clarence Birdseye, inspired by Inuit ice fishing in Labrador, developed a quick-freezing process that cooled food rapidly between metal plates, producing small ice crystals that preserved texture. His company, acquired by Postum and later General Foods, launched retail frozen vegetables in 1930 under the Birds Eye name. The industry expanded after the Second World War with home freezers, frozen concentrates, and complete frozen dinners (the "TV dinner", popularised by Swanson in 1954).

Technology

Modern methods include blast freezing, cryogenic freezing with liquid nitrogen, and individual quick freezing (IQF) of items such as peas and berries. Vegetables are usually blanched before freezing to inactivate enzymes; meat and prepared meals are frozen raw or cooked. Frozen food must be kept in an unbroken "cold chain" from factory to consumer, generally at −18 °C or colder.

Quality and safety

Freezing preserves most nutrients well; frozen vegetables picked and frozen at peak ripeness can exceed out-of-season fresh produce in vitamin content. Freezing does not sterilise food — many microorganisms survive and resume growth on thawing — so frozen products still require proper cooking and handling. Texture changes from ice crystal formation and freezer burn are the main quality issues.

Market

Frozen pizza, ice cream, vegetables, and ready meals are among the largest categories in a global market worth hundreds of billions of dollars annually.
