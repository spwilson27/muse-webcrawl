John Arthur Stevens, Baron Stevens of Kirkwhelpington (born 21 October 1942) is a former British senior police officer who served as Commissioner of Police of the Metropolis (head of London's Metropolitan Police) from 2000 to 2005, and led the Stevens Inquiries into collusion in Northern Ireland.

Police career

Stevens joined the Metropolitan Police in 1963 and rose through detective ranks, studying at the University of Leicester and Southampton. After senior posts in Hampshire and Cambridgeshire, he became Chief Constable of Northumbria Police in 1991, then Deputy Commissioner of the Met in 1998 under Sir Paul Condon. He became Commissioner in January 2000, succeeding Condon, pursuing neighbourhood policing and management reforms, and leading the force through the aftermath of the 2001 September 11 attacks era of counter-terrorism. He was succeeded by Sir Ian Blair in 2005.

Stevens Inquiries

From 1989 Stevens headed a series of investigations (Stevens 1–3) into allegations of collusion between loyalist paramilitaries and members of the security forces in Northern Ireland, including the 1989 murder of Belfast solicitor Pat Finucane. His team's offices were attacked by arson on the eve of the first report; the inquiries led to arrests and, in his 2003 report, he confirmed that collusion had occurred and that Finucane's murder could have been prevented. He also later led investigations including the Metropolitan Police's Operation Paget inquiry (2003–2006) into the deaths of Diana, Princess of Wales, and Dodi Fayed, which concluded the 1997 Paris crash was a tragic accident, and advised on security for the 2012 London Olympics after chairing border security reviews for the Conservative Party.

Later life

Stevens was knighted in 2000 and created a life peer in 2005, taking the title Baron Stevens of Kirkwhelpington, of Kirkwhelpington in Northumberland. He has chaired companies in the security sector and served as Chancellor of Northumbria University (2005–2015). His autobiography, Not for the Faint-Hearted, appeared in 2005.
