Trauma-informed care

Trauma-informed services emphasize safety, trust and collaboration.

Trauma-informed care (TIC) is a framework in health, mental health, education and social services that assumes an individual is more likely than not to have a history of trauma, and that organizes services to recognize the presence of trauma symptoms, acknowledge the role trauma plays in people's lives, and avoid re-traumatization. It shifts the central question from "What is wrong with you?" to "What happened to you?"

Principles

The U.S. Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration (SAMHSA) describes a trauma-informed approach through four assumptions — realize the widespread impact of trauma, recognize its signs, respond by integrating knowledge into policies and practice, and resist re-traumatization — and six key principles: safety; trustworthiness and transparency; peer support; collaboration and mutuality; empowerment, voice and choice; and attention to cultural, historical and gender issues.

Applications

Trauma-informed approaches have been adopted in schools, where they inform discipline policies and staff training; in healthcare, where they shape screening for adverse childhood experiences and patient interactions; and in child welfare, homelessness services, corrections and veterans' services. The Sanctuary Model, developed by Sandra Bloom, is one influential organizational approach.

Evidence and criticism

Evaluations suggest trauma-informed organizational change can improve staff attitudes, reduce seclusion and restraint, and improve client engagement, though rigorous evidence for improved clinical outcomes remains limited and implementation varies widely. Critics note that the term is sometimes applied loosely as a label without substantive changes in practice, and that focusing on adversity screening without adequate services risks harm.
