Child abuse

Child protection services work to identify and prevent abuse and neglect.

Child abuse or child maltreatment is physical, sexual, emotional and/or psychological maltreatment or neglect of a child or children, especially by a parent or a caregiver. Child abuse may include any act or failure to act by a parent or caregiver that results in actual or potential harm to a child and can occur in a child's home, or in organizations, schools, or communities that the child interacts with.

Types

The main forms of child maltreatment are generally grouped into four categories: physical abuse, sexual abuse, emotional or psychological abuse, and neglect. Physical abuse involves deliberate use of force causing injury or pain. Child sexual abuse involves engaging a child in sexual activities the child does not comprehend or cannot consent to. Emotional abuse includes persistent rejection, humiliation, terrorizing and isolation. Neglect is the failure to provide for a child's basic physical, emotional, medical or educational needs, and is the most commonly reported form of maltreatment in many jurisdictions.

Effects

Abuse and neglect in childhood are associated with long-term consequences for physical and mental health, including increased risk of depression, anxiety, post-traumatic stress disorder, substance misuse, chronic disease and difficulties in relationships. Research on adverse childhood experiences (ACEs) has shown a graded relationship between the number of categories of childhood adversity experienced and later health and social problems. Early intervention and supportive relationships can mitigate many of these effects.

Prevention and response

Most countries have laws mandating reporting of suspected child abuse by professionals such as teachers and healthcare workers, and child protective services agencies investigate reports and may provide family support or remove children from unsafe homes. Prevention strategies include home-visiting programmes for new parents, parenting education, public awareness campaigns and economic supports for families. The United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child obliges signatory states to protect children from all forms of violence, abuse and neglect.
