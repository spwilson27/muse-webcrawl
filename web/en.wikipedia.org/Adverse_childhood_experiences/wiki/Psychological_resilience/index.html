Psychological resilience

Resilience involves adapting well in the face of adversity and stress.

Psychological resilience is the ability to cope mentally and emotionally with a crisis, or to return to a pre-crisis state quickly. It describes the process of adapting well in the face of adversity, trauma, tragedy, threats, or significant sources of stress — such as family and relationship problems, serious health problems, or workplace and financial stressors. Resilience is not a fixed trait; it involves behaviours, thoughts and actions that can be learned and developed.

Factors

Research, including Ann Masten's studies of children at risk, identifies recurring protective factors: close relationships with competent, caring adults; good cognitive and self-regulation skills; positive views of oneself; motivation to be effective; and, at the community level, effective schools and neighbourhood resources. Masten famously described resilience as "ordinary magic" arising from ordinary adaptive systems rather than rare qualities. Risk and protective factors operate at individual, family and community levels, and their balance shifts across development.

Development and childhood adversity

In the context of adverse childhood experiences, resilience helps explain why many people exposed to adversity do not develop lasting disorders. Supportive relationships are considered the most important buffer: a stable bond with at least one committed adult can sustain children's development despite hardship. Resilience can also be undermined when stress is chronic and overwhelming, and it may fluctuate over the life course rather than remaining constant.

Building resilience

Interventions aim to strengthen connections, foster wellness, find purpose and embrace healthy thoughts, following guidance popularized by the American Psychological Association. In children, programmes that support caregivers, improve self-regulation and ensure stable environments show the strongest evidence. Some researchers caution that emphasizing individual resilience should not displace efforts to reduce the adversities themselves.
