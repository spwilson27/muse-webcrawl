Childhood trauma

Supportive adult relationships are a key buffer against the effects of childhood trauma.

Childhood trauma refers to adverse experiences in childhood that overwhelm a child's ability to cope, including abuse, neglect, household dysfunction, violence, serious accidents, disasters, medical trauma and the loss of a caregiver. Such experiences, particularly when repeated or prolonged and when they occur without the buffer of supportive adult relationships, can affect development across the lifespan.

Adverse childhood experiences

The landmark Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACE) Study, conducted by the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention and Kaiser Permanente in the 1990s, surveyed adults about ten categories of childhood adversity, including abuse, neglect and household challenges such as parental substance misuse, mental illness, incarceration and divorce. The study found a strong, graded relationship between the number of ACEs and adult risk of chronic disease, mental illness, substance misuse and early death.

Effects on development

Traumatic stress in childhood can alter the development of stress-response systems, including the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis, and is associated with changes in brain regions involved in threat detection, emotion regulation and executive function. Outcomes vary widely: many children show resilience, especially with stable, caring relationships, while others develop post-traumatic stress symptoms, attachment difficulties, learning problems or physical health conditions.

Treatment and prevention

Evidence-based treatments for traumatized children include trauma-focused cognitive behavioural therapy (TF-CBT), child-parent psychotherapy and, for some children, eye movement desensitization and reprocessing (EMDR). Prevention efforts focus on strengthening families, reducing poverty and violence, screening for adversity in healthcare settings, and building trauma-informed systems in schools and services so that responses to children's behaviour account for the effects of trauma.
