Epilepsy is a group of non-communicable neurological disorders characterized by recurrent, unprovoked seizures. A seizure is a brief episode of abnormal excessive or synchronous neuronal activity in the brain, which can produce convulsions, loss of awareness, unusual sensations, or subtle changes in behaviour. A person is generally diagnosed with epilepsy after two unprovoked seizures, or one unprovoked seizure with a high risk of recurrence.

Epilepsy affects about 50 million people worldwide, making it one of the most common neurological diseases globally. It occurs in all ages, with peaks in young children and older adults.

Classification

Seizures are broadly classified by the International League Against Epilepsy as:

  • Focal seizures — beginning in one area of the brain; awareness may be retained (focal aware) or impaired.
  • Generalized seizures — involving both hemispheres from the outset, including tonic-clonic ("grand mal"), absence ("petit mal"), myoclonic, atonic, and tonic seizures.
  • Unknown onset seizures, where the beginning cannot be determined.

Epilepsies themselves are classified by cause as genetic, structural, metabolic, immune, infectious, or unknown.

Causes

In many people no cause is found. Known causes include genetic mutations, head trauma, stroke, brain tumours, infections of the brain such as meningitis or neurocysticercosis, developmental disorders, and neurodegenerative disease. Provoked seizures — caused by fever in children, alcohol withdrawal, or metabolic disturbance — do not by themselves constitute epilepsy.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis rests on a detailed clinical history, ideally including an eyewitness account. An electroencephalogram (EEG) can record epileptiform discharges, and MRI may identify a structural cause. Blood tests and lumbar puncture may be used to exclude provoked seizures.

Treatment

Daily antiseizure medications control seizures in about 70% of people. Common agents include levetiracetam, lamotrigine, valproate, and carbamazepine. For drug-resistant focal epilepsy, surgery to remove the seizure focus may be considered; other options include vagus nerve stimulation, responsive neurostimulation, and ketogenic diets, particularly in children. People with epilepsy may face restrictions on driving and certain occupations, and the condition has historically carried substantial social stigma.
