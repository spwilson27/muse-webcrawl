Castles in Scotland

Castles in Scotland number in the thousands historically; more than 2,000 sites are recorded, of which many survive as ruins, restored residences, or visitor attractions. Scottish castles range from medieval royal fortresses such as Edinburgh Castle and Stirling Castle to the tower houses built by clan chiefs and lairds from the 15th to the 17th century.

History

Stone castles were introduced under Norman influence in the 12th century. Motte-and-bailey castles and great keeps gave way to curtain-walled enclosures, and later to fortified tower houses suited to the conditions of raiding and feuding. Gunpowder artillery in the 16th century, and political union, reduced the military role of castles, and many evolved into country houses.

Notable examples

Famous Scottish castles include Eilean Donan, Dunnottar, Urquhart on Loch Ness, Balmoral (the royal family's Highland residence), Glamis, and the island fortress of Blackness. Many are cared for by Historic Environment Scotland or the National Trust for Scotland.
