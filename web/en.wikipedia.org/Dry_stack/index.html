Dry stack

Dry stack is a term used in several fields for the stacking of materials without mortar, water or other binding medium.

Stone construction

In masonry, dry stacking (or dry stone construction) is the building of walls and structures from stones fitted together without mortar, stability coming from careful selection, weight and friction. Dry stone walls are a traditional feature of the countryside in Britain, Ireland and many other regions, used for field boundaries, terracing and dwellings since prehistoric times. Techniques such as through-stones and battered (inward-sloping) wall faces give the structure strength, and the craft is recognised by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage.

Boat storage

In the marina industry, a dry stack is a facility in which boats are stored out of the water on racks inside a large covered building, stacked several levels high. Forklifts launch and retrieve boats on demand, a service sometimes called "dry stack marina" storage; keeping boats dry reduces hull fouling and corrosion and frees harbour space compared with wet slips.

Other uses

The phrase is also applied to tailings and waste rock stacked without slurry ("dry stack tailings") in mining, and to fuel-cell and battery cell assemblies stacked in a dry state.
