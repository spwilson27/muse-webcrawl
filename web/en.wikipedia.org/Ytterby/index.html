Ytterby

The Ytterby mine on Resarö island

Ytterby
LocationResarö, Vaxholm Municipality, Stockholm County, Sweden
Known forDiscovery site of seven chemical elements

Ytterby is a village on the island of Resarö in the Stockholm archipelago, Sweden. Its feldspar and quartz mine is famous in chemistry as the richest single source of new elements in history: seven elements were discovered from its minerals, and four of them — yttrium, ytterbium, terbium and erbium — are named after the village itself.

The mine

The mine was worked for quartz and feldspar used by ironworks and porcelain factories. In 1787 the amateur geologist Carl Axel Arrhenius found an unusually heavy black mineral there, later named gadolinite. Analysis of the mineral by Johan Gadolin in 1794 revealed a new "earth", yttria, from which yttrium and several lanthanides were eventually isolated.

Elements discovered

Elements first identified from Ytterby ores are yttrium (Y), terbium (Tb), erbium (Er), ytterbium (Yb), scandium (Sc), thulium (Tm) and gadolinium (Gd, named after Gadolin). No other locality has given its name to so many elements: the names yttrium, ytterbium, terbium and erbium are all derived from "Ytterby".

Legacy

The mine is now disused and marked with a commemorative plaque. Streets in the village are named after elements, and Ytterby is frequently cited as the most important single locality in the history of the periodic table.
