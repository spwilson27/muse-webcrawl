Waverley (novel)

AuthorWalter Scott
Published1814
GenreHistorical novel

Waverley; or, 'Tis Sixty Years Since is a historical novel by Sir Walter Scott, published anonymously in 1814. It is frequently regarded as one of the first historical novels in Western literature and launched Scott's career as a novelist.

Plot

The hero, Edward Waverley, is a young English soldier sent to Scotland in 1745. Drawn into Highland society and the Jacobite rising of that year, he is torn between loyalty to the Hanoverian government he serves and sympathy for the Jacobite cause and its figures, including the clan chieftain Fergus Mac-Ivor and his sister Flora. After the rising's failure, Waverley is pardoned and reconciles his divided loyalties.

Significance

Waverley was an immediate success and established the template for the historical novel — combining fictional characters with real events and social history — influencing writers across Europe. Scott published a long series of successors, the "Waverley novels", including Ivanhoe and The Heart of Mid-Lothian.
