1923 in Japan

Events in the year 1923 in Japan. The year corresponded to Taishō 12, the twelfth year of the reign of Emperor Taishō, with Crown Prince Hirohito serving as regent.

Events

  • September 1 – The Great Kantō earthquake, with an estimated magnitude of 7.9, devastates Tokyo and Yokohama, killing well over 100,000 people in the shaking, fires, and ensuing firestorm. Martial law is declared, and in the following days thousands of Koreans and Japanese socialists are murdered by vigilantes, police, and soldiers in the Kantō Massacre.
  • September – Prime Minister Katō Tomosaburō dies in office (August 24); Admiral Yamamoto Gonnohyōe forms a government and oversees the emergency response.
  • December 27 – The Toranomon Incident: the communist student Nanba Daisuke attempts to assassinate the Prince Regent Hirohito, prompting the resignation of the Yamamoto cabinet.

Births and deaths

Notable events of the year unfolded against the backdrop of Taishō democracy, as party governments alternated with military and bureaucratic cabinets. The earthquake prompted large-scale reconstruction of Tokyo under Home Minister Gotō Shinpei and spurred the introduction of modern urban planning, while also accelerating militarist and anti-foreign sentiment in some quarters.
