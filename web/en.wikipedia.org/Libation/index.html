Libation

A libation is a ritual pouring of a liquid — most commonly wine, oil, milk or water — as an offering to a deity, a spirit, or in memory of the dead. It was a central feature of religion in the ancient world and persists in various forms in many cultures.

Ancient practice

In ancient Greece, libations (spondai) accompanied prayers, oaths, treaties and sacrifices, poured from a shallow phiale bowl. Roman religion likewise featured libations at domestic and public rites. In ancient Egypt, water libations symbolised life and purification, and libation tables and vessels are common grave goods.

Other traditions

Libations appear in Mesopotamian, Vedic and Chinese ancestral rites, in Andean offerings to Pachamama, and in West African and diaspora traditions honouring ancestors. The pouring of drink for absent or deceased friends in modern popular culture echoes the ancient practice.
