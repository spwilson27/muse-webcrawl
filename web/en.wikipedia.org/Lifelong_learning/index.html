Lifelong learning

Lifelong learning is the ongoing, voluntary and self-motivated pursuit of knowledge for personal or professional reasons. It encompasses learning throughout the lifespan, from early childhood education through adult education and into retirement, and includes formal education, non-formal training, and informal learning through daily experience, work, and leisure.

Concept

The concept gained prominence in the 1970s through the work of UNESCO, the OECD, and the Council of Europe, and was reinforced by reports such as the 1996 Delors Report, Learning: The Treasure Within, which identified four pillars of education: learning to know, learning to do, learning to live together, and learning to be. The European Union designated 1996 the European Year of Lifelong Learning.

Motivations

Adults pursue lifelong learning to adapt to changing labour markets, technological change, and longer working lives; to change careers or progress within them; and for personal fulfilment, health, and civic participation. Employers increasingly emphasise reskilling and upskilling, while governments promote adult learning to maintain competitiveness and social inclusion.

Forms

Lifelong learning occurs in community colleges, universities of the third age, workplaces, libraries, museums, and online through massive open online courses (MOOCs) and other digital platforms. Recognition of prior learning schemes allow experience gained outside formal education to count toward qualifications. Critics note inequalities in access, as participation is highest among those already well educated.
