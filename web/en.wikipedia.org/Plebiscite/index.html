Plebiscite

Citizens voting in a plebiscite.

A plebiscite is a direct vote by the electorate on a particular proposal or question, typically one of major national importance such as a change of sovereignty, a constitution, or the form of government. The term derives from the Latin plebiscitum, a decree of the plebeian assembly in ancient Rome. Plebiscites overlap closely with referendums, and usage of the two terms varies by country: in some traditions a plebiscite is an advisory vote initiated by the government, while a referendum is constitutionally required or legally binding, though the distinction is not applied consistently.

History

The modern plebiscite gained prominence during the French Revolution, when votes were held on annexations and constitutions. Napoleon Bonaparte used plebiscites (1800, 1802, 1804) to legitimise his rule, as did Napoleon III, whose plebiscites of 1851–52 and 1870 confirmed the Second Empire. After the First World War, the principle of national self-determination led to a series of territorial plebiscites under the Treaty of Versailles and the League of Nations — in Schleswig (1920), Upper Silesia (1921), and the Saar Basin (1935) — deciding which state disputed districts would join.

Uses and criticism

Plebiscites have been used to decide independence (for example in Norway in 1905 and in numerous decolonising territories), constitutional change, and the status of territories such as Kashmir (proposed but never held), Crimea (2014, not recognised internationally), and Puerto Rico. Because the government usually frames the question and controls timing, plebiscites can serve as tools of legitimation for authoritarian leaders — a practice sometimes called "plebiscitary democracy". Critics also note that binary votes may oversimplify complex questions and expose minority rights to majority decision.

In Australia, the term is used for advisory national votes not affecting the Constitution, such as the conscription plebiscites of 1916 and 1917 and the 2017 postal survey on same-sex marriage; constitutional changes require a referendum under section 128.
