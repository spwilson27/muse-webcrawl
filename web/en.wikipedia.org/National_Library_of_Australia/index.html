National Library of Australia

Established: 1960 (National Library Act); origins 1901

Location: Parkes, Canberra, Australia

Collection size: Over 10 million items

Services: Trove; legal deposit; Pandora web archive

The National Library of Australia (NLA) is the largest reference library in Australia, responsible under the National Library Act 1960 for maintaining and developing a national collection of library material, including a comprehensive collection of material relating to Australia and the Australian people. It is located in Parkes, Canberra, on the shore of Lake Burley Griffin, in a building opened in 1968.

History and collections

The library grew from the Commonwealth Parliamentary Library established in 1901. The National Library Act of 1960 separated it from the parliamentary library, and its present building opened in 1968. Under legal deposit provisions, copies of every publication issued in Australia must be deposited with the library. Its holdings include the papers of many notable Australians, extensive newspaper and map collections, oral histories, photographs, and the journals of Captain James Cook, including the journal of HMS Endeavour.

Digital services

The NLA operates Trove, a free online discovery service (launched in 2009) that aggregates digitised newspapers, books, images, maps and archived websites from libraries and cultural institutions across Australia; its digitised newspaper corpus is among the world's largest. The library also curates Pandora, Australia's national web archive, and the National edeposit (NED) service for born-digital publications, jointly with state and territory libraries.
