Porch

A porch (from Old French porche, from Latin porticus) is a room or gallery located in front of the entrance of a building. A porch is placed in front of the facade of a building, commands the entrance, and in most cases is covered by a roof and supported by columns or enclosed by walls. Porches serve both practical purposes — sheltering arrivals from weather — and social ones, as transitional spaces between the public street and the private interior.

Types

Porches vary widely by region and period. The veranda is a long, roofed gallery extending along one or more sides of a house, common in warmer climates. The portico is a classical, columned porch, often grand in scale. A stoop is a small raised platform with steps at a building entrance, characteristic of urban rowhouses. Screened porches, enclosed with mesh to exclude insects, and sleeping porches, designed for outdoor sleeping in the era before air conditioning, are other common forms. In church architecture, a porch is a small enclosed chamber at the principal entrance.

History

The porch descends from ancient porticoes and loggias. In North America, the broad front porch became a defining feature of houses from the eighteenth through the early twentieth centuries, functioning as an outdoor living room and a place for informal socialising with neighbours. Its decline after the Second World War is often attributed to air conditioning, television, the automobile, and the shift of domestic leisure to back gardens and patios.
