GeForce is a brand of graphics processing units (GPUs) designed by Nvidia and marketed for the consumer PC gaming market. The brand was introduced in 1999 with the GeForce 256, which Nvidia promoted as "the world's first GPU", notable for integrating transform and lighting engines on a single chip.

History

Successive generations — including the GeForce 2, 3, and 4 series, the FX series, and the unified-shader 8 series (2006) — made GeForce the dominant discrete graphics brand alongside ATI/AMD's Radeon. The GeForce 10 series (Pascal, 2016) was followed in 2018 by the RTX 20 series (Turing), which introduced real-time ray tracing hardware and AI-based upscaling (DLSS), and by the RTX 30 (Ampere), RTX 40 (Ada Lovelace), and RTX 50 (Blackwell) series.

Product tiers

GeForce cards are sold in tiers historically labelled GT, GTX, and RTX, with suffixes such as Ti and Super denoting higher-performance variants. Nvidia also offers GeForce Now, a cloud gaming service streaming games rendered on GeForce hardware.
