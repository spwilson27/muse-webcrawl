Taputapuātea marae

Taputapuātea marae is a large marae complex at Ōpoa on the island of Raiatea in French Polynesia. It was the religious and political centre of eastern Polynesia for centuries and is dedicated to the god ʻOro. In 2017 the marae and its surrounding landscape were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.

History

According to tradition, the sacred site — originally called Vaiʻotaha — dates to around the 10th century, and the great marae was greatly expanded as the cult of ʻOro spread from Raiatea across the Society Islands and beyond, carried by voyaging priests and chiefs to the Cook Islands, the Tuamotus, the Marquesas, Hawaii, and New Zealand. Chiefs from across the Polynesian triangle gathered at Taputapuātea, and alliance and learning networks (including navigators' schools) radiated from it.

The complex

The complex comprises several marae built of coral stone and basalt, with paved courts, large upright stones, and the great ahu platform of Marae Taputapuātea itself. The site overlooks Te Avarua (Te Ava Moʻa) pass, the sacred opening in Raiatea's reef through which voyaging canoes departed and arrived.

Legacy

Taputapuātea is regarded throughout Polynesia as a spiritual homeland (a "piko," or navel), and traditional voyaging canoes on revival journeys across the Pacific commonly begin or end pilgrimages there. Its UNESCO inscription recognized it as an exceptional testimony to Polynesian civilization and its seafaring culture.
