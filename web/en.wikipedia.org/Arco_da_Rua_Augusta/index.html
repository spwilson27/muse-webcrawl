The Arco da Rua Augusta (Rua Augusta Arch) is a stone, triumphal arch-like structure in Lisbon, Portugal, built to commemorate the city's reconstruction after the 1755 earthquake. It stands at the northern side of the Praça do Comércio (Commerce Square), opening onto the pedestrian Rua Augusta in the Baixa district.

History and description

The arch was first projected in the Pombaline rebuilding of Lisbon after the earthquake, and an initial version was built in 1755–1763; it was demolished in 1775. Construction of the present arch began again in 1843 on a design associated with architect Veríssimo José da Costa, and it was completed only in 1875.

The crowning group by sculptor Célestin-Anatole Calmels shows Glory rewarding Valour and Genius. Below are statues by Vítor Bastos of historical figures including the Marquis of Pombal, Vasco da Gama, Viriatus and Nuno Álvares Pereira. The Latin inscription reads Virtvtibvs Maiorvm ("By the virtues of the elders"), with the added line that the monument was raised so that it might stand as a lesson. Since 2013 visitors have been able to ride a lift and climb to a viewpoint at the top of the arch.
