The University of Victoria (UVic) is a public research university located in the municipalities of Saanich and Oak Bay, within Greater Victoria on the southern tip of Vancouver Island, British Columbia, Canada. It traces its origins to Victoria College, founded in 1903, and gained full university status in 1963.

History

Victoria College operated for six decades as an affiliated college of McGill University and later the University of British Columbia, famously housed for a time in Craigdarroch Castle. After the University Act of 1963, UVic became an independent university and developed its Gordon Head campus, a ring-road design set in 403 acres of woodland gardens noted for native Garry oak ecosystems.

Academics and research

UVic comprises faculties including Humanities, Science, Social Sciences, Engineering and Computer Science, Business (the Gustavson School), Law, Education, Fine Arts, and Human and Social Development. It runs one of Canada's largest co-operative education programs. The university hosts Ocean Networks Canada, which operates cabled ocean observatories, and is a leader in climate, oceans, and Indigenous language revitalization research.

Athletics and campus life

The UVic Vikes compete in U Sports, with notable strength in rowing, cross country, and field hockey. The campus is known for its gardens, resident black-tailed deer, and the CARSA athletics facility. The university consistently ranks among Canada's top comprehensive universities.
