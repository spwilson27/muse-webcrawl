H2 receptor antagonist

H2 receptor antagonists, often shortened to H2 blockers, are a class of drugs that block the action of histamine at the histamine H2 receptors of the parietal cells in the stomach, thereby reducing gastric acid production. They are used in the treatment of peptic ulcer disease, gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) and dyspepsia.

History

The first H2 blocker, cimetidine, was developed by James Black and colleagues at Smith, Kline & French in the 1960s and 1970s through a landmark programme of rational drug design, reaching the market in 1976. It was followed by ranitidine, famotidine and nizatidine, which offered improved potency and fewer interactions.

Clinical use

H2 blockers are less potent acid suppressants than proton pump inhibitors but remain useful for mild reflux, nocturnal acid symptoms and over-the-counter treatment of heartburn. In 2019–2020 ranitidine products were withdrawn in many markets after the discovery of contamination with the probable carcinogen NDMA.
