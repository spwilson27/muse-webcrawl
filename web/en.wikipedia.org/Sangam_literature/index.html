Sangam literature
LanguageOld Tamil
Periodc. 300 BCE – 300 CE (debated)
WorksEttuthokai, Pattuppattu, Tolkappiyam

Sangam literature is the ancient Tamil literature of South India, composed at poets' assemblies (sangams) traditionally held in Madurai. It is among the oldest secular literatures in India and a principal source for early Tamil society, kingship, trade, love, and war.

Corpus

The surviving corpus includes the Tolkappiyam, a work on grammar and poetics, the Eight Anthologies (Ettuthokai) and Ten Idylls (Pattuppattu) — together 18 major works comprising over 2,300 poems, plus the 18 minor works of later date. Poems are classed as akam (interior: love) and puram (exterior: war, public life), mapped to landscapes called tinai.

Themes and Society

Verses praise Chera, Chola, and Pandya kings, describe ports trading with Rome and Southeast Asia, pearl fisheries, and everyday life of farmers, bards, and warriors. Women poets such as Avvaiyar are prominent in the tradition.

Rediscovery

Many texts were nearly lost and were recovered in the 19th century by scholars such as U. V. Swaminatha Iyer, who collected palm-leaf manuscripts from monasteries and published critical editions.
