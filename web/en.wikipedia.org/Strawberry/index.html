Strawberry

The garden strawberry (or simply strawberry; Fragaria × ananassa) is a widely grown hybrid species of the genus Fragaria, collectively known for their fruit, which is known worldwide for its characteristic aroma, bright red color, juicy texture, and sweetness. It is consumed in large quantities, either fresh or in such prepared foods as jam, juice, pies, ice cream, milkshakes, and chocolates. Artificial strawberry flavorings and aromas are also widely used in products such as candy, soap, lip gloss, and perfume.

The garden strawberry was first bred in Brittany, France, in the 1750s via a cross of Fragaria virginiana from eastern North America and Fragaria chiloensis, which was brought from Chile by the French explorer Amédée-François Frézier in 1714. Cultivars of Fragaria × ananassa have replaced the woodland strawberry (Fragaria vesca) in commercial production, which was the first strawberry species cultivated in the early 17th century.

Botany

From a botanical point of view, the strawberry is not a berry but an aggregate-accessory fruit, meaning that the fleshy part is derived not from the plant's ovaries but from the receptacle that holds the ovaries. The apparent "seeds" (achenes) on the outside of the fruit are actually the true fruits of the plant. The garden strawberry is an octoploid, having eight sets of chromosomes. The genome was sequenced in 2019.

Production

In 2022, world production of strawberries was 9.6 million tonnes, led by China with 36% of the total, followed by the United States and Mexico. Strawberries are propagated by runners and grown in a wide variety of climates; major pests include slugs, powdery mildew, and the fungus Botrytis cinerea, which causes grey mould.

Nutrition

Raw strawberries are 91% water, 8% carbohydrates, and 1% protein, and are a rich source of vitamin C and manganese.
