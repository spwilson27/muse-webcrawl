Kitzbühel

Kitzbühel's medieval old town beneath the Hahnenkamm.

Kitzbühel is a medieval town and ski resort in the Austrian state of Tyrol, in the Kitzbühel Alps between the mountains Hahnenkamm (1,712 m) and Kitzbüheler Horn (1,996 m). With a population of around 8,300, it is one of Austria's most famous winter sports destinations and a byword for luxury alpine tourism.

History

Settled in prehistory and mined for copper and silver in the Middle Ages, Kitzbühel received town rights in 1271 and passed between Bavaria and the Habsburgs before definitively becoming Austrian in 1816. Tourism began in the late 19th century; Franz Reisch pioneered skiing in the area in 1893, and the town hosted some of the first ski races in Austria.

Skiing and the Hahnenkamm

Kitzbühel is best known for the annual Hahnenkamm races, held since 1931 and a highlight of the FIS Alpine Ski World Cup. The downhill course, the Streif, is considered the most demanding on the World Cup circuit, with gradients of up to 85% on the Mausefalle section. In summer the area offers hiking, golf and the Kitzbühel Alps trail network. The town's historic centre, with its painted facades and the church of St Catherine, preserves much of its medieval character.
