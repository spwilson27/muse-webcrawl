Weight

Extensive?Yes

In science and engineering, the weight of an object is a quantity associated with the gravitational force acting on it. In Newtonian physics, weight is commonly defined as the magnitude of the force of gravity on the object, W = mg, where m is the mass and g is the local acceleration due to gravity (about 9.81 m/s² on Earth's surface).

Definitions

Several definitions coexist. The operational definition used in commerce treats weight as the quantity measured by weighing, effectively the force the object exerts on its support. ISO defines weight as the product of mass and the acceleration due to gravity. Because g varies with location, an object's weight differs slightly between the equator and the poles, and dramatically on other planets, while its mass stays constant.

Units

The SI unit of weight, as a force, is the newton. In everyday and commercial usage, "weight" usually means mass and is given in kilograms or pounds; the pound is legally defined as a unit of mass in the United States for trade purposes.

Weightlessness

Objects in free fall experience weightlessness in the operational sense: astronauts in orbit are continuously falling toward Earth, so support forces vanish even though gravity still acts on them.
