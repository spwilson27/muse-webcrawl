Inaccessible Pinnacle

The Inaccessible Pinnacle, often called the In Pinn, is a fin-shaped rock on Sgurr Dearg, a mountain in the Cuillin range on the Isle of Skye, Scotland. At 986 metres (3,235 ft), its summit is the highest point of Sgurr Dearg and one of the 282 Munros. It is famous as the only Munro whose summit requires technical rock climbing, making it the notorious final obstacle for many Munro-baggers.

Description

The Pinnacle is a narrow blade of basalt about 50 metres long that rests on top of Sgurr Dearg, rising above the parent summit. The easiest route, the East Ridge, is graded Moderate in British climbing terms, and the West Ridge is shorter but harder. Descent is usually made by abseil from a fixed anchor. Because the mountain is not itself of Munro height without the Pinnacle, the summit of the Munro is the top of the rock itself.

History

The Pinnacle was first climbed in 1880 by the brothers Charles and Lawrence Pilkington. For decades it was rarely attempted, but with the growth of Munro-bagging — completing all 282 Munros — it became a rite of passage, and local guides regularly lead clients up it in summer. In poor weather the exposed Cuillin ridge makes the climb considerably more serious.
