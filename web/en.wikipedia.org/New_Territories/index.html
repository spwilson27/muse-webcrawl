New Territories

The New Territories are the northern part of Hong Kong, making up about 86.2% of the territory's land area and containing around half of its population. Together with Hong Kong Island and the Kowloon Peninsula, they form the three main regions of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region.

History

The area north of Boundary Street, together with more than 200 offshore islands, was leased by Qing China to Britain for 99 years under the Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory in 1898. The expiry of that lease in 1997 set the date for the handover of the whole of Hong Kong to China.

Geography

The New Territories include districts such as Sha Tin, Tsuen Wan, Tuen Mun, Yuen Long, Fanling-Sheung Shui, Tai Po and Sai Kung, developed from the 1970s into large new towns, alongside rural villages of the indigenous clans, country parks and Hong Kong's highest peak, Tai Mo Shan.
