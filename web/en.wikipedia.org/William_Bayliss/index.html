William Bayliss

Sir William Maddock Bayliss (2 May 1860 – 27 August 1924) was an English physiologist, celebrated for the discovery of the hormone secretin with Ernest Starling.

Life and career

Bayliss was born in Wolverhampton and studied at University College London, where he later became professor of physiology. He worked initially on electrical changes in glands and on the nervous control of blood vessels.

Discovery of secretin

In 1902 Bayliss and Starling showed that acid in the duodenum causes the release of a chemical messenger from the intestinal lining that travels in the blood and stimulates the pancreas to secrete digestive juice. They named it secretin — the first substance to be recognised as a hormone — and Starling coined the word "hormone" itself in 1905. This demonstrated chemical, rather than purely nervous, coordination of the body.

Other work

Bayliss studied enzymes, colloids and the physical chemistry of living systems; his textbook Principles of General Physiology (1915) was highly influential. During the First World War he researched wound shock and promoted intravenous gum-saline infusions. He was knighted in 1922.
