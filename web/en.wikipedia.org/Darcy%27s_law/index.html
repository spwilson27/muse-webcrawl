Darcy's law

Illustration: Darcy's law

Darcy's law is an equation describing the flow of a fluid through a porous medium, formulated by Henry Darcy from experiments on water flowing through sand beds published in 1856. It states that the discharge rate is proportional to the hydraulic gradient.

Equation

In its common form, Q = −KA(dh/dl), where Q is discharge, K is hydraulic conductivity, A is cross-sectional area, and dh/dl is the hydraulic head gradient. The specific discharge is proportional to the gradient through the permeability of the medium and the fluid's properties.

Applications and limits

The law underpins groundwater hydrology, petroleum reservoir engineering, and soil science. It holds for slow, laminar (creeping) flow at low Reynolds numbers; at higher velocities inertial effects require corrections such as the Forchheimer equation.
