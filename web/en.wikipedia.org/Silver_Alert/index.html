Silver Alert

Silver Alert is a public notification system in the United States used to broadcast information about missing persons, especially senior citizens with Alzheimer's disease, dementia, or other mental disabilities. Modelled on the AMBER Alert for abducted children, Silver Alerts spread word through highway signs, media, and phone networks.

Criteria

States set their own thresholds, commonly requiring that the missing person be over a certain age (often 60 or 65), suffer from a cognitive impairment, and be believed to be in danger, frequently while driving or on foot away from home. Some states extend similar alerts under names such as Golden Alert or Missing Endangered Persons Alert.

Criticism

Critics warn of "alert fatigue" when notifications are over-broad, and some programmes have been used for missing persons who do not meet the cognitive-impairment standard. Supporters credit the alerts with recovering many wandering seniors alive.
