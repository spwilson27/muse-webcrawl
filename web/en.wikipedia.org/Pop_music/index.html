Pop music

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A stadium pop concert

Pop music is a genre of popular music that originated in its modern form during the mid-1950s in the United States and the United Kingdom. The terms "popular music" and "pop music" are often used interchangeably, although the former more accurately describes all music that is targeted at a mass audience. Pop is characterised by its emphasis on catchy melodies and hooks, verse–chorus structures, short-to-medium length songs, and appeal to a general audience rather than a subculture.

Characteristics

Musicologists identify recurring features: songs typically last between two and a half and three and a half minutes, built on a repeated chorus or hook; rhythms are danceable; production emphasises studio craft, vocal prominence and immediate accessibility. Pop readily absorbs elements of rock, hip hop, R&B, electronic dance music, country and Latin styles, and is shaped by technologies from multitrack recording to Auto-Tune and streaming algorithms.

History

Pop emerged from rock and roll and Tin Pan Alley songcraft in the 1950s, flourished through the British Invasion and Motown in the 1960s, and expanded with disco, synth-pop and the music-video era of MTV in the 1980s, when stars such as Michael Jackson and Madonna defined the modern pop celebrity. The 1990s and 2000s brought teen pop, boy bands and globalised production teams; the streaming era further globalised the genre, with Korean K-pop acts and Latin pop reaching worldwide audiences.

Industry and influence

Pop is the central commercial engine of the recording industry, organised around singles, charts such as the Billboard Hot 100, and professional songwriting camps. Critics debate its formulaic tendencies, but its best work is widely credited with shaping fashion, youth culture and the sound of mainstream music worldwide.
