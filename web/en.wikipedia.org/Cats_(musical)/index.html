Cats is a sung-through musical with music by Andrew Lloyd Webber, based on the 1939 poetry collection Old Possum's Book of Practical Cats by T. S. Eliot. It tells the story of a tribe of cats called the Jellicles and the night they make the "Jellicle choice" by deciding which cat will ascend to the Heaviside Layer and come back to a new life.


A production of Cats

Productions

The original London production, directed by Trevor Nunn and choreographed by Gillian Lynne, opened in 1981 and ran for 21 years and almost 9,000 performances. The Broadway production opened in 1982, won seven Tony Awards including Best Musical, and ran for 18 years and 7,485 performances — at the time a record. Cats has been translated into more than 20 languages and performed worldwide, pioneering the era of the global "megamusical".

Music

The score is largely a setting of Eliot's poems. Its best-known number, "Memory", sung by the faded glamour cat Grizabella, uses lyrics adapted by Trevor Nunn from Eliot's poem "Rhapsody on a Windy Night" and became a standard recorded by many artists. Other numbers introduce characters such as Rum Tum Tugger, Mr. Mistoffelees, Macavity, and Old Deuteronomy.

Reception and adaptations

Critics were divided by the show's minimal plot, but audiences made it one of the most commercially successful musicals in history. A direct-to-video film was released in 1998, and a feature film adaptation directed by Tom Hooper appeared in 2019 to poor reviews. A Broadway revival ran in 2016–2017.
