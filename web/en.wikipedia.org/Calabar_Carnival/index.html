Calabar Carnival

Location: Calabar, Cross River State, Nigeria

Frequency: Annual

Period: December (festival month)

First held: 2004

The Calabar Carnival is an annual festival held in Calabar, Cross River State, Nigeria, every December. Created in 2004 under Governor Donald Duke as part of a strategy to make Cross River State a destination for tourism and the "tourism hub of Nigeria", it has grown into what is promoted as "Africa's Biggest Street Party", drawing visitors and participants from across Nigeria and abroad.

Events

The carnival is the climax of a month-long programme of festivities in Calabar. Its centrepieces are the competing carnival bands — large costumed troupes such as Seagull, Passion 4, Masta Blasta, Bayside and Freedom — whose members parade through the streets in elaborate costumes before judges, with themes interpreting historical and cultural subjects. Other events include the children's carnival, a cultural carnival celebrating Nigerian ethnic traditions, street parties, concerts, the Ekpe festival of Efik culture, boat regattas and the crowning of the Carnival Queen.

Significance

The carnival is a major driver of the Cross River State economy during the festive season, filling hotels and supporting vendors and performers. It has been credited with inspiring a wave of similar carnivals in other Nigerian states and with reshaping perceptions of Calabar as a leisure destination alongside attractions such as the Tinapa resort and the nearby Obudu Mountain Resort.
