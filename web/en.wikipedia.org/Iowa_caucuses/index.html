Iowa caucuses

The Iowa caucuses are biennial electoral events for members of the Democratic and Republican parties in the U.S. state of Iowa. Unlike primary elections in most other U.S. states, where registered voters go to polling places to cast ballots, Iowans instead gather at local caucus meetings to discuss and vote on candidates. The caucuses are also used to select delegates to the county conventions and party platforms.

History

Iowa has held caucuses since the 19th century, but they gained national importance after reforms following the chaotic 1968 Democratic National Convention. In 1972 Iowa held the first nominating contest of the presidential cycle, and in 1976 Jimmy Carter's strong showing propelled his rise to the presidency, cementing Iowa's first-in-the-nation status.

Process

Democratic caucuses historically required voters to publicly align in preference groups, with candidates failing to reach a 15% viability threshold in a precinct being eliminated and their supporters realigning. After severe reporting delays marred the 2020 Democratic caucuses, the Democratic National Committee demoted Iowa in 2024; Iowa Democrats instead conducted a mail-in preference vote, while Republicans continued to caucus first in January 2024.

Significance and criticism

Winning Iowa generates media momentum and fundraising, though caucus winners often fail to win their party's nomination. Critics argue that Iowa's largely rural, predominantly white electorate is unrepresentative, that caucuses depress turnout and exclude voters unable to attend lengthy evening meetings, and that the state wields disproportionate influence over national policy, notably on ethanol.
