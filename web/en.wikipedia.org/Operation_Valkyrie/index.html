Operation Valkyrie (German: Unternehmen Walküre) was an emergency continuity-of-government plan of the German Wehrmacht during the Second World War. Officially designed to keep order in the event of a breakdown of civil authority—such as an uprising by the millions of foreign labourers in Germany—it was secretly modified by officers plotting against Adolf Hitler so that, on his assassination, the Replacement Army (Ersatzheer) could be used to seize control of the government, disarm the SS, and arrest the Nazi leadership.

The plan

The original plan was drawn up under General Friedrich Olbricht. In 1943 Olbricht, Henning von Tresckow, and Claus von Stauffenberg rewrote it: upon activation, Replacement Army units in Berlin and the military districts would occupy ministries, radio stations, and SS installations, believing—per the conspirators' orders—that the SS had murdered the Führer and was attempting a coup. Stauffenberg, chief of staff to the Replacement Army's commander, was ideally placed both to plant the assassin's bomb and to trigger Valkyrie.

20 July 1944

On 20 July 1944 Stauffenberg placed a briefcase bomb in the conference room at the Wolf's Lair in East Prussia. The explosion killed four men but Hitler survived, shielded by a heavy table support and the hut's open windows. In Berlin, delays and hesitation followed: Valkyrie orders were issued only in the late afternoon, and the commander of the Replacement Army, Friedrich Fromm, refused to act without confirmation of Hitler's death. Communications from the Wolf's Lair soon made clear Hitler was alive; Major Otto Ernst Remer's Guard Battalion, ordered to arrest Joseph Goebbels, instead secured the government quarter for the regime after Remer spoke with Hitler by telephone.

Collapse and reprisals

By midnight the coup had collapsed. Fromm, seeking to cover his own knowledge of the plot, had Stauffenberg, Olbricht, and two others shot in the courtyard of the Bendlerblock that night. In the ensuing purge, thousands were arrested; some 200 were executed, many after show trials before Roland Freisler's People's Court, including Field Marshal Erwin von Witzleben and, later, Admiral Wilhelm Canaris. Rommel, implicated by testimony, was forced to commit suicide.

Legacy

The Bendlerblock is now the Memorial to the German Resistance, and 20 July is commemorated annually in Germany. The 2008 film Valkyrie, starring Tom Cruise as Stauffenberg, popularised the story internationally.
