The Christian Social Union in Bavaria (German: Christlich-Soziale Union in Bayern, CSU) is a Christian democratic and conservative political party in Germany. Having a regionalist identity, the CSU operates only in the state of Bavaria, while its larger counterpart, the Christian Democratic Union (CDU), operates in the other fifteen states of Germany. The two parties, collectively known as the Union, form a common parliamentary group in the Bundestag.

History

The CSU was founded in the aftermath of the Second World War, with early meetings held in Munich and Würzburg in 1945. Franz Josef Strauß, who joined the party in its early years, served as its chairman from 1961 until his death in 1988 and became the defining figure of the party. The CSU has governed Bavaria continuously since 1957, an unusually long tenure in post-war European politics, and held an absolute majority in the Bavarian Landtag for most of that period.

Relationship with the CDU

The CSU and CDU are described as sister parties. Under a long-standing agreement, the CDU does not contest elections in Bavaria and the CSU does not operate outside it. The parties cooperate in federal elections as the Union, though they have occasionally clashed over policy, notably during the European migrant crisis of 2015, when CSU leader Horst Seehofer publicly criticised Chancellor Angela Merkel's refugee policy.

Ideology

The party combines Christian democratic and conservative positions with a distinctly Bavarian identity. It traditionally emphasises law and order, support for families, a social market economy, and scepticism toward centralisation of power in Berlin and Brussels. It has generally taken more conservative positions on social issues than the CDU.

Electoral performance

In Bavarian state elections the CSU routinely wins pluralities, though its absolute majority was lost in 2008 and again after the 2018 election, after which it formed coalitions with the Free Voters. At the federal level, CSU candidates contest the Bavarian constituencies for the Bundestag.
