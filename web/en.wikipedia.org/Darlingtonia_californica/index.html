Darlingtonia californica

Family: Sarraceniaceae
Genus: Darlingtonia (monotypic)
Common name: Cobra lily, cobra plant

Darlingtonia californica, also called the cobra lily or cobra plant, is a species of carnivorous plant in the family Sarraceniaceae, and the sole member of the genus Darlingtonia. It is native to Northern California and Oregon, growing in bogs and seeps with cold running water.

Description

The plant's tubular leaves rise up to a metre tall and terminate in an inflated hood with a forked, tongue-like appendage, resembling a rearing cobra with a forked tongue. Insects attracted to nectar enter the hood; translucent patches in the hood act as false exits, confusing the prey until it tires and falls into the fluid-filled tube, where it drowns and decomposes. Unusually for a pitcher plant, Darlingtonia does not trap rainwater but pumps water into its pitchers through its roots.

Habitat and cultivation

The species requires cool roots and cold, oxygenated water, typically growing in mountain seeps fed by cold streams. This makes it notoriously difficult to cultivate in warm climates. It was discovered on Mount Shasta in 1841 by the botanist William D. Brackenridge.
