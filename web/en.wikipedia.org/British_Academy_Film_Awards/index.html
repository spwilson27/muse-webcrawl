The British Academy Film Awards, more commonly known as the BAFTA Awards, are annual awards presented by the British Academy of Film and Television Arts honouring the best national and foreign films of the year. They are regarded as the British counterpart of the Academy Awards.

History

The first awards were presented in 1949, honouring films of 1947 and 1948. The ceremony is traditionally held in February, before the Oscars, and since 2023 has taken place at the Royal Festival Hall in London.

Trophy

Winners receive a distinctive theatrical mask trophy designed by artist Mitzi Cunliffe, introduced in 1955.
