Bajío

The Bajío is a cultural and geographical region in west-central Mexico, encompassing parts of the states of Guanajuato, Querétaro, Aguascalientes, Jalisco, and Michoacán. Centered on a fertile plateau between roughly 1,700 and 2,000 metres above sea level, it is one of Mexico's most productive agricultural zones.

Geography

The Bajío is a broad lowland plain drained by the Lerma River system. Its fertile volcanic soils and moderate climate made it the breadbasket of colonial New Spain.

History

The region was a heartland of the Mexican War of Independence, which began in 1810 with the Grito de Dolores in the town of Dolores Hidalgo, Guanajuato. In the 20th and 21st centuries the Bajío became a major manufacturing hub, particularly for the automotive industry.

Cities

Major cities include León, Guanajuato, Querétaro, Irapuato, Celaya, and Aguascalientes.
