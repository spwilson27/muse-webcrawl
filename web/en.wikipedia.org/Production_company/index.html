Production company

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Sound stages on a production company lot

A production company, production house, production studio, or production team is a studio that creates works in the fields of performing arts, new media art, film, television, radio, comics, interactive arts, video games, websites, music, and video. These groups consist of technical staff to produce the media, and are often incorporated as a distinct legal entity that organises financing, hires cast and crew, and manages the production process from development through distribution.

Operation

A production company may be directly responsible for fundraising for the production or may accomplish this through a parent company, partner, or private investor. It handles budgeting, scheduling, scripting, the supply of talent and resources, the organisation of staff, the production itself, post-production, distribution, and marketing. Production companies are often owned or under contract with a media conglomerate, film studio, record label or television network, in which case they may operate semi-autonomously, or they may be entirely independent — ranging from major studios such as Warner Bros. or Walt Disney Studios to small independent firms founded by individual filmmakers or showrunners under "overall deals" with networks and streamers.

Types

Film and television production companies dominate the term's usage, but production companies also exist in theatre, music recording, video games, radio and digital media. In television, the production company creates the programme and sells or licenses it to a broadcaster or streaming service, which usually commissions and finances it.
