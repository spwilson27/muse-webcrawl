Siege of Jadotville

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The siege of Jadotville took place in September 1961 during the Congo Crisis, when a company of Irish troops serving with the United Nations Operation in the Congo (ONUC) was attacked at Jadotville (now Likasi) in the breakaway province of Katanga by forces loyal to the Katangese leader Moise Tshombe, including European mercenaries.

The battle

"A" Company, 35th Battalion, commanded by Commandant Pat Quinlan, comprised about 155 Irish soldiers, most of them young and none with combat experience. From 13 September 1961, they were attacked by a much larger Katangese force — estimated at up to 3,000–5,000 men supported by mortars, artillery and even a Fouga Magister jet. The Irish defended a hastily dug position around a villa for five days, inflicting heavy casualties (often estimated at several hundred killed and over a thousand wounded) while suffering no fatalities themselves, though several were wounded.

Surrender and captivity

By 17 September, out of ammunition, water and food, and with relief attempts — including by Gurkha and Swedish UN troops at the Lufira Bridge — having failed, Quinlan surrendered on terms. The company was held prisoner for about a month before being released in a prisoner exchange.

Recognition

For decades the action was neglected in Ireland, the surrender overshadowing the defence in official memory. Quinlan's reputation and that of his men were reassessed in the 2010s: the unit received a Unit Citation in 2016 and surviving veterans were awarded medals in 2017. The siege was dramatised in the 2016 film The Siege of Jadotville.
