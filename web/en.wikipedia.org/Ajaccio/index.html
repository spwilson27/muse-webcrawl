Ajaccio

Country: France

Region: Corsica

Department: Corse-du-Sud (prefecture)

Population: approx. 73,000 (city proper)

Ajaccio (French: [aʒaksjo]; Corsican: Aiacciu) is the capital and largest city of Corsica and the prefecture of the department of Corse-du-Sud. It lies on the island's west coast, on the Gulf of Ajaccio, and is best known as the birthplace of Napoleon Bonaparte, who was born there in 1769.

History

The modern city was founded by the Republic of Genoa in 1492, though settlement in the area is much older. Corsica passed to France in 1768 under the Treaty of Versailles, a year before Napoleon's birth in the Maison Bonaparte, now a museum. Ajaccio was briefly occupied during the Second World War and was liberated in September 1943, becoming the first French city freed from Axis occupation.

Geography and climate

Ajaccio occupies a sheltered position at the head of a deep bay, backed by hills and the mountains of Corsica's interior. It has a Mediterranean climate with hot, dry summers and mild, wetter winters. The Sanguinaires Islands lie at the entrance to the gulf.

Economy and transport

Tourism, port activity, and administration dominate the economy. Ferries connect Ajaccio with Marseille, Toulon, and Nice, and Ajaccio Napoleon Bonaparte Airport serves the city. The local football club is AC Ajaccio.
