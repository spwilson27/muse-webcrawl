University press

A university press is an academic publishing house specializing in scholarly monographs and other works, usually connected to a large research university. Most university presses are non-profit and are an integral part of a research university's mission to disseminate scholarship.

History

The oldest university presses are Oxford University Press (printing since 1478; rights to print granted 1586) and Cambridge University Press (founded 1534), both of which evolved into the two largest university presses in the world. In the United States, Cornell University Press (1869) was the first, followed by presses at Johns Hopkins (1878), Chicago (1890), Columbia (1893), and California (1893). The Association of University Presses, founded in 1937, now represents more than 150 member presses worldwide.

Publishing model

University presses publish primarily for scholarly audiences. Their books typically undergo rigorous peer review, and print runs may be only a few hundred copies. Presses publish monographs, scholarly journals, reference works, and increasingly regional and trade titles that subsidize specialized scholarship. Titles likely to sell well — reference works, textbooks, and regional books — help offset losses on specialized monographs.

Challenges

Since the late 20th century, university presses have faced declining library sales of monographs, university budget cuts, and the transition to digital and open-access publishing. Several presses have closed or merged, while others have experimented with open-access models for scholarly books.
