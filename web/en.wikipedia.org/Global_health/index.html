Global health

Global health is the area of study, research and practice concerned with improving health and achieving health equity for people worldwide. It emphasises transnational health issues, determinants and solutions, and draws on public health, medicine, epidemiology, economics and the social sciences. Its priorities are often measured using metrics such as the global burden of disease and disability-adjusted life years.

Scope and actors

Global health addresses communicable diseases such as HIV/AIDS, tuberculosis, malaria and neglected tropical diseases, as well as the rising burden of non-communicable diseases, maternal and child health, nutrition, and health-system strengthening. Key actors include the World Health Organization, UNICEF, the Global Fund, Gavi, national aid agencies, philanthropic foundations and non-governmental organisations.

Challenges

Major challenges include pandemics and health security — highlighted by the COVID-19 pandemic — antimicrobial resistance, health inequities between and within countries, shortages of health workers in low-income settings, and the health effects of climate change. The Sustainable Development Goals, particularly SDG 3, frame much current global health policy.
