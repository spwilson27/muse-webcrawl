Cartier (French: [kaʁtje]), formally Cartier International SNC, is a French luxury jewellery and watch manufacturer founded in Paris in 1847. Long described as "the jeweller of kings and the king of jewellers" — a phrase attributed to Edward VII of the United Kingdom — Cartier is part of the Swiss Richemont group and operates hundreds of boutiques worldwide.

History

Louis-François Cartier took over his master's workshop on the Rue Montorgueil in Paris in 1847. His son Alfred expanded the business, and Alfred's three sons — Louis, Pierre, and Jacques — established Cartier as an international house in the early twentieth century, opening branches in London (1902) and New York (1909) and designing for royalty across Europe and India.

Iconic designs

Cartier created some of the most recognisable objects in modern jewellery and watchmaking: the Santos wristwatch (1904), made for aviator Alberto Santos-Dumont and one of the first purpose-built men's wristwatches; the Tank watch (1917); the Trinity ring (1924); the Love bracelet (1969), fastened with a screwdriver; and the Panthère motif, which became the house's emblem. The firm also popularised the use of platinum in fine jewellery and the "Tutti Frutti" style of carved coloured stones.

Present day

Cartier remains a flagship brand of Richemont, selling high jewellery, watches such as the Ballon Bleu and Santos lines, leather goods, fragrances, and accessories, and it ranks among the largest luxury jewellery brands in the world by revenue.
