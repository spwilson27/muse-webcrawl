The House of Savoy (Italian: Casa Savoia) is a European royal family founded in the 11th century by Humbert I in the historical region of Savoy. Through gradual territorial expansion, the family came to rule the Kingdom of Sardinia–Piedmont and, from 1861, the unified Kingdom of Italy, making it one of the longest-reigning dynasties in Europe. The Italian monarchy ended in 1946, when a referendum abolished it following the Second World War and the reign of the last king, Umberto II.

History

From their Alpine base, the counts of Savoy controlled the strategic passes between France and Italy and expanded into Piedmont and, by purchase and conquest, toward the Ligurian coast. Amadeus VIII received the ducal title in 1416. After the family's fortunes were battered in the Italian Wars, Emmanuel Philibert ("Ironhead") restored the state after the Battle of St Quentin (1557) and moved the capital to Turin. Victor Amadeus II acquired Sicily in 1713 and exchanged it for Sardinia in 1720, taking the royal crown his descendants would keep.

Kings of Italy

In the 19th century the Savoyard king Charles Albert granted the Statuto Albertino constitution (1848), and under Victor Emmanuel II and his minister Cavour the kingdom of Sardinia-Piedmont led the unification of Italy (Risorgimento), proclaimed in 1861. Victor Emmanuel III reigned through both world wars; his association with Fascism and flight from Rome in 1943 badly damaged the dynasty. Umberto II, the "May King", reigned for just over a month before the institutional referendum of 2 June 1946 made Italy a republic. Male members of the house were exiled by the constitution until the ban was lifted in 2002.

Branches and succession dispute

Since the death of Umberto II in 1983, the headship of the house has been disputed between the senior line represented by Vittorio Emanuele, Prince of Naples (died 2024), and his son Emanuele Filiberto, and the Aosta branch represented by Prince Amedeo (died 2021) and his son Aimone. The family retains the dynastic orders of Saints Maurice and Lazarus and the Supreme Order of the Most Holy Annunciation, and its residences in Turin and Piedmont are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
