Folsom, California

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Folsom is a city in Sacramento County, California, United States, about 25 miles (40 km) northeast of Sacramento on the American River. Its population was about 80,000 at the 2020 census. Folsom is internationally known for Folsom State Prison, immortalised in Johnny Cash's 1955 song "Folsom Prison Blues," and for Folsom Lake, a major recreation reservoir.

History

The area was home to the Nisenan people and was settled during the California Gold Rush; the town grew as a rail terminus when the Sacramento Valley Railroad — California's first railroad — reached it in 1856. The town was named for Joseph Libbey Folsom, a US Army officer and land developer who died before its founding. The historic Folsom Powerhouse (1895) delivered hydroelectric power to Sacramento, a milestone in long-distance AC transmission. Folsom Dam was completed in 1955, creating Folsom Lake.

Economy and culture

Modern Folsom is a prosperous suburb with major technology employers, notably an Intel campus, and extensive cycling infrastructure including the Lake Natoma loop and connections to the American River Parkway. Johnny Cash performed two concerts at Folsom Prison in January 1968, recorded as the album At Folsom Prison; a trail and bridge named for him overlook the prison grounds and the site of his famous photograph.
