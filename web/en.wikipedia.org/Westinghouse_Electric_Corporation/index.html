The Westinghouse Electric Corporation was an American manufacturing company founded in 1886 by George Westinghouse in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. It became one of the world's largest electrical manufacturers, a pioneer of alternating current (AC) power distribution, and a major producer of steam turbines, nuclear power plants, home appliances, and broadcasting equipment.

Early history and the war of the currents

Westinghouse had earlier invented the railway air brake. His electric company championed alternating current against Thomas Edison's direct current in the so-called "war of the currents." Employing patents from Nikola Tesla for the AC induction motor and polyphase system, Westinghouse won the contract to light the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago and built the first hydroelectric generators at Niagara Falls (Adams Power Plant, 1895), demonstrating the superiority of AC for long-distance transmission.

Twentieth century

The company diversified into radio (founding station KDKA in Pittsburgh in 1920, the first commercially licensed radio station in the United States), television, appliances marketed under the slogan "You Can Be Sure... If It's Westinghouse," and nuclear energy — it built the reactor for the first US nuclear submarine, USS Nautilus, and the first commercial pressurized water reactor at Shippingport (1957). Civil War–era corporate crises included near-bankruptcy in the Panic of 1907.

Decline and breakup

After financial losses in the 1970s–90s, Westinghouse bought the CBS broadcasting network in 1995, then sold off its industrial businesses (including its power generation unit to Siemens in 1997–98) and renamed itself CBS Corporation in 1997; the renaming took full effect by 1999. The Westinghouse name survives through brand licensing and in the nuclear business, Westinghouse Electric Company.
