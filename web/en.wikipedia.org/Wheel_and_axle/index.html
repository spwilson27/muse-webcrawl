Wheel and axle

The wheel and axle is a simple machine consisting of a wheel fixed to a smaller axle so that the two rotate together. A force applied at the rim of the wheel produces a larger force at the axle, or a force applied to the axle can move a load at the rim through a greater distance.

Mechanical advantage

The ideal mechanical advantage is the radius of the wheel divided by the radius of the axle. If the wheel radius is much larger than the axle radius, a small effort can lift or pull a heavy load, although the effort must move farther. Friction reduces the actual advantage.

Uses

Examples include doorknobs, steering wheels, winches, capstans, water wheels and vehicle wheels. In transport the wheel and axle also reduce resistance by rolling rather than sliding, making them one of the foundational technologies of machinery.
