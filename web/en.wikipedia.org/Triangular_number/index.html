Triangular number

[Image: The first six triangular numbers arranged as triangles of dots]

A triangular number or triangle number counts objects arranged in an equilateral triangle. The nth triangular number is the number of dots in the triangular arrangement with n dots on each side, and is equal to the sum of the n natural numbers from 1 to n. The sequence of triangular numbers begins 0, 1, 3, 6, 10, 15, 21, 28, 36, 45, ...

Formula

The nth triangular number is given by the formula Tn = n(n + 1)/2, equivalently the binomial coefficient C(n + 1, 2) — the number of handshakes that occur if each of n + 1 people shakes hands with each other exactly once. Carl Friedrich Gauss is said to have rediscovered this formula as a schoolboy by pairing the terms of 1 + 2 + ... + 100.

Properties

The sum of two consecutive triangular numbers is a square number: Tn−1 + Tn = n2. Every even perfect number is triangular. A number is triangular if and only if 8T + 1 is a perfect square. Gauss proved in his diary in 1796 (the "Eureka" theorem) that every positive integer is the sum of at most three triangular numbers. Triangular numbers generalise to polygonal numbers and to higher-dimensional simplex (tetrahedral) numbers.
