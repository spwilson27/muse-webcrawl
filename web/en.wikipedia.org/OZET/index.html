OZET (Russian: ОЗЕТ, from Общество землеустройства еврейских трудящихся, "Society for Settling Working Jews on the Land") was a Soviet public organisation created in 1925 to promote the settlement of Jewish workers in agriculture, particularly in designated territories in Ukraine, Belarus, Crimea and, later, the Far East.

Activities

Working alongside the government agency KOMZET, OZET recruited settlers, raised funds — including from foreign Jewish organisations — and publicised agricultural colonisation as a path to "productivising" the Jewish population, many of whom had been traders and artisans dislocated by war and revolution. Its supporters abroad included the American Jewish Joint Distribution Committee's Agro-Joint programme.

Birobidzhan and decline

From 1928 OZET supported settlement in the Birobidzhan region on the Amur River, which in 1934 became the Jewish Autonomous Oblast. By the late 1930s the policy had lost favour; OZET was dissolved in 1938 amid the Great Purge, and many of its officials were arrested.
