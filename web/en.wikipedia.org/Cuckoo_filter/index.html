Cuckoo filter


Diagram of cuckoo filter insertion
TypeProbabilistic data structure
Introduced2014, by Fan, Andersen, Kaminsky and Mitzenmacher
PurposeApproximate set membership
OperationsInsert, lookup, delete

A cuckoo filter is a space-efficient probabilistic data structure used to test whether an element is a member of a set, in the same family as the Bloom filter. Introduced in 2014, it stores short hash "fingerprints" of items in a cuckoo hash table and, unlike a standard Bloom filter, supports deletion of items and typically achieves lower false-positive rates at small fingerprint sizes, with better locality of reference.

How it works

For each item, the filter computes a short fingerprint and two candidate buckets derived from the item's hash and the fingerprint itself (partial-key cuckoo hashing). The fingerprint is placed in one of the two buckets; if both are full, an existing fingerprint is "kicked out" and relocated to its own alternate bucket, echoing the cuckoo's habit of displacing eggs. Lookup checks only the two candidate buckets, and deletion removes a matching fingerprint from either.

Comparison with Bloom filters

Cuckoo filters offer deletion, bounded lookup cost (two bucket reads), and better space efficiency when target false-positive rates are below about 3%. They are used in databases, networks and storage systems as an alternative to Bloom filters where deletions are required or lookup speed matters.
