Oblast

An oblast (/ˈɒblæst/; also UK: /ˈɒblɑːst/) is a type of administrative division in Bulgaria and several post-Soviet states, including Belarus, Russia and Ukraine. The term is a loanword in English, though it is often translated as "area", "zone", "province", or "region".

Etymology

The word derives from the Slavic ob- ("over, around") and vlast ("power, authority"), literally the territory over which power is exercised. Oblasts existed in the Russian Empire from administrative reforms of the 18th century and were greatly multiplied in the Soviet Union.

By country

  • Russia — 48 of the federal subjects are oblasts, the most numerous type, each governed by a governor and a regional legislature.
  • Ukraine — 24 oblasts form the primary administrative division, most named after their administrative centres.
  • Belarus — six oblasts (voblasts), each named after its capital city.
  • Bulgaria — the 28 provinces are officially called oblasti.
  • Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan — also use oblasts or derived terms as first-level divisions.
