Sumer Is Icumen In

"Sumer Is Icumen In", also called the Summer Canon and the Cuckoo Song, is an English medieval musical composition written in the Wessex dialect of Middle English around the mid-13th century. The manuscript resides in the British Library (Harley 978). It is a canon, or round, for four voices over two lower voices (the pes), and is the oldest surviving piece of six-part polyphony in England.

The manuscript

The manuscript is believed to come from Reading Abbey, and the piece may have been composed by a monk there, possibly W. de Wycombe. The song is written in mensural notation on a six-line stave with red notation for the pes. Instructions in Latin explain how the canon is to be performed.

Lyrics

The Middle English text celebrates the arrival of summer: "Sumer is icumen in, lhude sing cuccu!" ("Summer has arrived, loudly sing, cuckoo!"). It describes growing seeds, blooming meadows, and lively farm animals. The manuscript also gives an alternative Latin text, Perspice Christicola, suitable for religious performance — a contrafactum sharing the same music.

Legacy

The piece is one of the most famous works of medieval English music and has been widely recorded and referenced in popular culture. Ease of performance as a round has made it a staple of school singing, though its exact rhythmic interpretation remains debated by scholars.
