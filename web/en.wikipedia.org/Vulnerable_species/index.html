Vulnerable species

A vulnerable species is a species which has been categorised by the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) that is threatened with extinction unless the circumstances that are threatening its survival and reproduction improve. A species that has been heavily exploited, is declining in population, or has a limited range may be listed as vulnerable.

Vulnerability is mainly caused by habitat loss or destruction of the species' home. Vulnerable habitats or species are monitored and can become increasingly threatened. Some species listed as "vulnerable" may be common in captivity, an example being the military macaw.

Criteria

The IUCN uses several criteria for listing a species as vulnerable. A taxon is vulnerable when it is not critically endangered or endangered but is facing a high risk of extinction in the wild in the medium-term future, as defined by criteria including a population reduction of at least 50% over the last 10 years or three generations, an extent of occurrence of less than 20,000 km², or a population estimated at fewer than 10,000 mature individuals with a continuing decline.

Conservation

There are currently many thousands of species classified as vulnerable on the IUCN Red List. Conservation measures for vulnerable species include habitat protection, captive breeding, legal protection from hunting and trade, and monitoring of population trends.
