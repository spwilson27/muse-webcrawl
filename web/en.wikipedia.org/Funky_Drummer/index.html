Funky Drummer

"Funky Drummer" is a funk song recorded by James Brown and his band in 1969 and released as a two-part single in 1970. Despite modest chart success on release — it reached number 20 on the R&B chart and number 51 on the pop chart — the record became famous for its drum break performed by Clyde Stubblefield, one of the most sampled passages in popular music.

The break

Midway through the track, Brown calls out to his drummer — "Give the drummer some" — and Stubblefield plays an improvised solo break of roughly twenty seconds: a tight, syncopated groove combining snare, hi-hat, kick drum, and ghost notes. Brown famously counts the band back in with "One, two, three, four!"

Sampling legacy

The break was widely adopted by hip hop producers in the 1980s and appears in tracks by Public Enemy ("Fight the Power", "Bring the Noise"), Run-DMC, LL Cool J, N.W.A, and Eric B. & Rakim, among hundreds of others across hip hop, pop, and electronic music. Stubblefield received no royalties for the sample use, since he was not a credited songwriter; he later became a widely cited example in debates over sampling and musicians' rights.
