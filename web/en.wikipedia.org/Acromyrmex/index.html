Acromyrmex

Scientific classification
Kingdom:Animalia
Phylum:Arthropoda
Class:Insecta
Order:Hymenoptera
Family:Formicidae

Genus:Acromyrmex Mayr, 1865

Acromyrmex is a genus of New World ants of the subfamily Myrmicinae. This genus is known as the leafcutter ants, along with the related genus Atta, and comprises some 30-plus species found from the southern United States to northern Argentina. Like Atta, they cultivate fungus gardens on which the colony feeds, but Acromyrmex colonies are generally smaller and their nests less conspicuous.

Biology

Workers cut fragments of leaves and other vegetation and carry them back to the nest, where the material is processed and used as substrate for the symbiotic fungus (usually Leucoagaricus gongylophorus), which the ants eat. The ants cannot digest fresh leaf material directly; the fungus breaks it down and also detoxifies plant defensive compounds. Colonies have a polymorphic worker caste — minims, minors, mediae, and large majors (soldiers) with powerful mandibles. A newly mated queen carries a pellet of fungus in her infrabuccal pocket to seed the garden of a new colony.

Ecology and pest status

Species such as Acromyrmex octospinosus and Acromyrmex heyeri can be significant agricultural and garden pests, defoliating crops and ornamentals. At the same time, leafcutter ants are major herbivores and soil-turners in Neotropical ecosystems and a model system for the study of symbiosis, caste evolution, and social behaviour. Their gardens also harbour additional symbionts, including antibiotic-producing actinobacteria (e.g. Pseudonocardia) that help suppress fungal parasites of the garden.
