Tumor marker

FieldOncology, pathology
SpecimenBlood, urine, tissue
UsesMonitoring, prognosis, screening support

A tumor marker is a biomarker found in blood, urine, or body tissues that can be elevated by the presence of one or more types of cancer. Tumor markers are substances produced by cancer cells themselves or by the body in response to cancer, and measuring them can help in detecting and managing some malignancies.

Classification

Markers include oncofetal antigens such as alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) and carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), hormones such as human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG), enzymes such as prostate-specific antigen (PSA), glycoproteins such as CA-125 and CA 19-9, and genetic alterations including mutations of BRCA1/2 and HER2 amplification.

Uses

Tumor markers are most valuable for monitoring response to treatment and detecting recurrence after therapy. Some aid in prognosis or in guiding the choice of targeted therapy. Because most markers lack the sensitivity and specificity needed for population screening, they are rarely sufficient to diagnose cancer on their own and are interpreted alongside imaging and biopsy findings.

Limitations

Elevated levels can occur in benign conditions — for example, CA-125 rises in endometriosis and PSA in benign prostatic hyperplasia — while some cancers never elevate a given marker. Serial measurements over time are generally more informative than a single value.
