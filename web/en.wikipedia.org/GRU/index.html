GRU

The GRU (Russian: ГРУ), formally the Main Directorate of the General Staff of the Armed Forces of the Russian Federation (and earlier the Main Intelligence Directorate), is Russia's military intelligence agency. It is the largest of Russia's intelligence services by personnel and operates under the General Staff, with a remit covering military espionage, signals and geospatial intelligence, and special forces (Spetsnaz) operations.

History

The GRU was founded in 1918 as the Registration Directorate of the Red Army's Field Staff, under Leon Trotsky's orders, and survived the Soviet period intact — unlike the civilian security organs, it was never broken up, though it was renamed and reorganised several times (including as the 2nd Directorate of the General Staff). Its wartime networks, such as the "Red Orchestra" and Richard Sorge's ring in Japan, became famous. After 1991 the GRU continued within the Russian Armed Forces; in 2010 it was formally renamed the Main Directorate (GU), though the GRU name remains in universal use.

Activities and notoriety

The GRU controls the Spetsnaz special-purpose regiments and brigades and is widely reported to conduct cyber operations — Western governments attribute major intrusions to its units 26165 ("Fancy Bear" / APT28) and 74455 (linked to the Sandworm group and the NotPetya attack). Western officials and courts have attributed to GRU officers the 2018 poisoning of Sergei and Yulia Skripal in Salisbury, England, interference operations around foreign elections, and sabotage plots in Europe. GRU officers played prominent roles in the annexation of Crimea, the war in Donbas, and the full-scale invasion of Ukraine from 2022, during which its chief since 2016, Admiral Igor Kostyukov, remained in post. The agency's headquarters is at the "Aquarium" complex on Grizodubovoy Street in Moscow.
