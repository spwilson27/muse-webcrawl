The paloma (Spanish for "dove") is a tequila-based cocktail from Mexico, traditionally made with tequila blanco or reposado, grapefruit soda (such as Jarritos Toronja, Fresca, or Squirt), a squeeze of lime juice, and a pinch or rim of salt, served over ice in a highball glass. Variants made with fresh grapefruit juice topped with soda water are common in craft cocktail bars. It is widely said to be Mexico's most popular tequila cocktail, more commonly drunk there than the margarita.

History

The drink's origins are uncertain. Its creation is sometimes credited to Don Javier Delgado Corona, owner of the La Capilla bar in the town of Tequila, Jalisco, who is also credited with the related batanga (tequila, cola, lime and salt). Grapefruit sodas became widely available in Mexico in the mid-20th century, and the simple tequila-and-soda serve spread from there; the name "paloma" may reference the traditional folk song "La Paloma".

Variations

Variations include the paloma brava (a large-format version for sharing), versions with mezcal in place of tequila, spiced versions with chilli-salt rims (such as Tajín), and the "dove" made with grapefruit liqueur. Canned ready-to-drink palomas are widely sold. The drink appears on the International Bartenders Association list of contemporary classics in a grapefruit-juice-based form.
