Archon

Archon (Greek: ἄρχων, "ruler") was a chief magistrate in many Greek city-states. In Athens, the archons became the central officials of the state after the decline of the monarchy, and the years of Athenian history were dated by the name of the eponymous archon.

Athenian archons

By the classical period Athens had nine archons chosen annually: the eponymous archon (chief magistrate and namesake of the year), the archon basileus (responsible for religious matters), the polemarch (originally the military commander), and six thesmothetai (judicial officials). After the reforms of Solon and Cleisthenes, and especially after 487 BC when they began to be selected by lot, their political power declined in favour of the generals (strategoi) and the popular assembly.

Other uses

The word also appears in Gnosticism, where the archons are powerful beings who rule the material world, and in the Byzantine Empire as a title for certain officials and rulers.
