Maternal mortality
Definition (WHO)Death of a woman while pregnant or within 42 days of termination of pregnancy, from causes related to or aggravated by the pregnancy
MeasureMaternal mortality ratio (MMR): deaths per 100,000 live births

Maternal mortality is the death of a woman during pregnancy or childbirth, or shortly after the end of pregnancy, from pregnancy-related causes. The World Health Organization defines a maternal death as "the death of a woman while pregnant or within 42 days of termination of pregnancy, irrespective of the duration and site of the pregnancy, from any cause related to or aggravated by the pregnancy or its management but not from accidental or incidental causes."

Causes

The major complications that account for most maternal deaths worldwide are severe bleeding (postpartum hemorrhage), infections (usually after childbirth), high blood pressure during pregnancy (pre-eclampsia and eclampsia), complications from delivery, and unsafe abortion. Other deaths result from diseases such as malaria and HIV/AIDS interacting with pregnancy. The "three delays" model — delays in deciding to seek care, in reaching a facility, and in receiving adequate treatment — explains why most of these deaths are considered preventable.

Global trends

Global maternal mortality fell substantially between 1990 and 2015 — by roughly 44 percent — under the Millennium Development Goals, though progress fell short of the target of a three-quarters reduction. Sustainable Development Goal 3.1 calls for reducing the global maternal mortality ratio to less than 70 per 100,000 live births by 2030. Rates remain highest in sub-Saharan Africa, where ratios in some countries exceed 500 per 100,000, and recent UN estimates suggest progress has stalled or reversed in many regions, including a widely noted rise in the United States. Proven interventions include skilled birth attendance, emergency obstetric care, access to contraception, safe abortion services, and treatment of anemia and hypertension.
