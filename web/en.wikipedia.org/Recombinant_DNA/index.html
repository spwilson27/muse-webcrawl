Recombinant DNA

Recombinant DNA (rDNA) molecules are DNA sequences created in the laboratory by combining genetic material from multiple sources, producing sequences that would not otherwise be found in the genome. Recombinant DNA technology is a central tool of modern molecular biology and biotechnology.

Method

A fragment of DNA containing a gene of interest is cut with restriction enzymes and joined, using DNA ligase, to a vector such as a plasmid. The recombinant plasmid is introduced into a host organism, commonly the bacterium Escherichia coli, which replicates the DNA and may express the encoded protein.

Applications

The technology underlies the production of recombinant proteins such as human insulin and growth hormone, genetically modified crops, gene therapy research, and the manufacture of vaccines and diagnostic tests. The first recombinant DNA experiments were carried out in the early 1970s by Paul Berg, Herbert Boyer, and Stanley Cohen; safety concerns led to the 1975 Asilomar conference and the first guidelines for the work.
