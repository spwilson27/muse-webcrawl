An integrated care system (ICS) is a partnership of organisations that provide health and care services within a geographic area of England. The 42 ICSs were placed on a statutory footing by the Health and Care Act 2022, taking effect on 1 July 2022, replacing clinical commissioning groups.

Structure

Each ICS comprises an integrated care board (ICB), an NHS body responsible for planning and funding most NHS services in the area, and an integrated care partnership (ICP), a joint committee of the ICB and local authorities charged with producing an integrated care strategy. ICSs bring together NHS trusts, primary care, local government, and voluntary-sector organisations.

Purpose

The stated aims are to improve population health outcomes, tackle inequalities, enhance productivity and value for money, and support broader social and economic development. ICSs are intended to coordinate care for people with complex needs, integrate health and social care, and shift emphasis toward prevention and community services.

Development

ICSs evolved from the sustainability and transformation partnerships created in 2016 following the NHS Five Year Forward View. Their introduction was a major structural change to the English NHS, and their effectiveness has been debated, particularly regarding accountability, funding pressures, and variation between systems.
