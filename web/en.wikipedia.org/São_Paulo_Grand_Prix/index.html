São Paulo Grand Prix

The São Paulo Grand Prix (Portuguese: Grande Prêmio de São Paulo) is a Formula One motor race held at the Autódromo José Carlos Pace, commonly known as Interlagos, in São Paulo, Brazil. First held under that name in 2021, it is the successor to the Brazilian Grand Prix, which was staged at Interlagos (and, for a period, at Jacarepaguá in Rio de Janeiro) from 1972 onwards.

History

The Brazilian Grand Prix joined the Formula One world championship in 1973, won by home hero Emerson Fittipaldi. Interlagos, built on undulating land between two reservoirs, became famous for its anti-clockwise layout, the sweeping Senna 'S' curves, and changeable weather. Ayrton Senna's emotional victories in 1991 and 1993 cemented the race in Brazilian sporting folklore. Title deciders at Interlagos include the 2007, 2008, and 2012 finales.

After the 2020 race was cancelled due to the COVID-19 pandemic, a new contract rebranded the event as the São Paulo Grand Prix from 2021. The race has featured the sprint format in several editions, including the memorable 2021 weekend when Lewis Hamilton charged from the back of the sprint grid to win the Grand Prix.

The circuit

Interlagos is one of the few anti-clockwise circuits on the Formula One calendar. Its 4.309 km lap combines the fast, banked final sector and long uphill start-finish straight with a twisty infield. Its altitude of roughly 800 metres slightly reduces engine power and downforce, and sudden rain showers frequently disrupt races.
