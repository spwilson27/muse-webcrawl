Mariachi

Mariachi is an ensemble of Mexican folk music, as well as the musicians who perform it. Originating in western Mexico — particularly the state of Jalisco — in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, mariachi developed from rural string ensembles into a professional urban form during the twentieth century. In 2011 UNESCO inscribed mariachi on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Instrumentation and costume

A modern mariachi ensemble typically includes violins, trumpets, a classical guitar, a high-pitched five-string vihuela, a deep-bodied rhythm guitar called the guitarrón, and sometimes a harp. Singing — solo and choral, including falsetto — is central to many genres performed. Musicians wear the ornate embroidered charro suit with short jacket, fitted trousers decorated with silver buttons (botonadura), bow tie and wide-brimmed sombrero.

Repertoire and history

The repertoire includes sones, rancheras, corridos, boleros, huapangos and polkas, covering themes of love, betrayal, death, heroes and the countryside. Mariachi moved to Mexico City in the early 1900s, where groups such as Mariachi Vargas de Tecalitlán (founded 1897) standardised the modern format and, through radio, film and recordings during the Golden Age of Mexican cinema, made mariachi a symbol of Mexican national identity. Singers such as Pedro Infante, Jorge Negrete and Vicente Fernández popularised its songs.

Mariachi today

Mariachi remains essential at weddings, quinceañeras, funerals, serenades and festivals in Mexico and the US Southwest, and school mariachi programmes flourish in both countries. The annual International Mariachi Festival in Guadalajara gathers hundreds of ensembles.
