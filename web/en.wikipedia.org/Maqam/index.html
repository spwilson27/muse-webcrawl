Maqam

In Arab music, a maqam (plural maqamat) is a melodic mode: a set of notes with specific intervals, characteristic melodic phrases, and conventions governing improvisation and composition. The maqam system underlies traditional music across the Arab world and influenced Turkish makam and Persian dastgah systems.

Structure

Each maqam is built from tetrachords called ajnas. Scales commonly use neutral intervals — three-quarter tones — that cannot be played on an equally tempered piano, giving maqamat such as Hijaz, Rast, Bayati and Saba their distinctive sound. Performance often centres on taqsim, unmetered improvisation that explores a maqam and modulates between related ones.

Related meanings

The word also denotes a shrine or holy tomb in Islamic tradition, and in Sufi terminology the spiritual "stations" along a mystic path.
