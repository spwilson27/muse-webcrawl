American Chemical Society

The American Chemical Society (ACS) is a scientific society based in the United States that supports scientific inquiry in the field of chemistry. Founded in 1876 at New York University, the ACS is a non-profit organisation chartered by the US Congress. With more than 155,000 members in over 140 countries, it is one of the world's largest scientific societies.

Activities

The ACS publishes numerous scientific journals, including the Journal of the American Chemical Society (JACS), Chemical Reviews, ACS Nano, and the trade magazine Chemical & Engineering News. Its Chemical Abstracts Service (CAS) division maintains the CAS Registry, the largest database of chemical substances and their registry numbers. The society holds two national meetings each year, organises local sections and technical divisions, and sets educational guidelines for undergraduate and graduate chemistry programs.

Awards and outreach

The ACS administers major awards including the Priestley Medal, its highest honour, and the ACS Award in Pure Chemistry. It designates National Historic Chemical Landmarks, runs National Chemistry Week outreach, and coordinates the United States National Chemistry Olympiad team.
