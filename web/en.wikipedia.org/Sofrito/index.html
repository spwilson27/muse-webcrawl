Sofrito

Sofrito is a base of aromatic ingredients cut into small pieces and slowly cooked in oil, used at the beginning of many dishes across Mediterranean, Latin American, Spanish, Italian, and Portuguese cuisines. The term comes from the Spanish verb sofreír, "to lightly fry".

Regional forms

In Spanish (especially Catalan) cooking, sofregit consists of tomato, onion, garlic, and olive oil cooked down slowly. The Italian soffritto typically uses finely chopped onion, carrot, and celery, sometimes with pancetta. In Puerto Rico, sofrito is a puréed mixture of peppers, ajíes dulces, onion, garlic, culantro, and cilantro, usually made in batches and frozen.

Use

Sofrito seasons rice dishes such as arroz con pollo, beans, stews, and soups, and forms the flavour foundation of Cuban, Dominican, and Puerto Rican cooking, where Cuban cooks add it to black beans and picadillo.
