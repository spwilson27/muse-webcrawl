Papal conclave

Cardinal electors gather in the Sistine Chapel, where conclaves have been held since 1492.

ElectorsCardinals under the age of 80 (maximum 120 electors)
Required majorityTwo-thirds
LocationSistine Chapel, Apostolic Palace, Vatican City
Governing lawUniversi Dominici Gregis (1996, as amended)

A papal conclave is a gathering of the College of Cardinals convened to elect a bishop of Rome, also known as the pope. Catholics consider the pope to be the apostolic successor of Saint Peter and the earthly head of the Catholic Church. The word conclave comes from the Latin cum clave ("with a key"), recalling the practice of locking the electors in seclusion until they reached a decision.

History

Early popes were chosen by clergy and laity of Rome, but in 1059 Nicholas II reserved election to the cardinal bishops, and in 1179 the Third Lateran Council extended the vote to all cardinals equally, requiring a two-thirds majority. The conclave system itself was imposed after the nearly three-year vacancy of 1268–1271 at Viterbo, when exasperated officials locked the cardinals in the episcopal palace and removed its roof; Gregory X formalised the practice in the constitution Ubi periculum (1274). Rules were progressively refined, most recently by John Paul II's Universi Dominici Gregis (1996) and amendments by Benedict XVI.

Procedure

On the death or resignation of a pope, cardinals under 80 years of age assemble in Rome, attend the Mass Pro Eligendo Papa, and process into the Sistine Chapel, where they swear an oath of secrecy and are sealed off from the outside world (extra omnes — "everyone out"). Electors are housed in the Domus Sanctae Marthae. Scrutineers, revisers and infirmarii conduct the ballots, and any communication with outsiders is forbidden on pain of excommunication.

Balloting and smoke signals

Up to four ballots are held per day (one on the first afternoon, then two each morning and afternoon). Ballots are burned after each round: black smoke (fumata nera) signals no election, while white smoke (fumata bianca) and the ringing of St Peter's bells announce a successful election. The dean then asks the chosen cardinal, Acceptasne electionem de te canonice factam in Summum Pontificem? ("Do you accept your canonical election as Supreme Pontiff?"), and the new pope chooses his regnal name before the announcement Habemus Papam from the balcony of St Peter's Basilica.
