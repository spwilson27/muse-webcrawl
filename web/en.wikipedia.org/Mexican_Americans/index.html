Mexican Americans

Mexican Americans (Spanish: mexicoamericanos) are Americans of full or partial Mexican descent. They are the largest subgroup of Hispanic and Latino Americans, numbering roughly 37 million people and about 11 percent of the total population of the United States, concentrated especially in California, Texas, Arizona, Illinois and other states.

History

Mexican settlement in what is now the United States long predates the country; after the Mexican–American War and the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo of 1848, about 100,000 Mexicans became U.S. citizens in the ceded territories. Large-scale immigration followed during the Mexican Revolution, the bracero program of 1942–1964, and after the Immigration and Nationality Act of 1965. The Chicano movement of the 1960s and 1970s asserted Mexican American cultural identity and civil rights.

Culture

Mexican American culture blends Mexican and U.S. elements in language (including Spanglish and Chicano English), cuisine such as Tex-Mex, music from mariachi to Tejano, and celebrations including Cinco de Mayo and Día de los Muertos.
