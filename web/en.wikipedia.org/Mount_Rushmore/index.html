Mount Rushmore

Mount Rushmore National Memorial is a colossal sculpture carved into the granite face of Mount Rushmore in the Black Hills of South Dakota. Sculptor Gutzon Borglum and his son Lincoln supervised the project from 1927 to 1941, carving 60-foot (18 m) heads of U.S. presidents George Washington, Thomas Jefferson, Theodore Roosevelt and Abraham Lincoln.

History and controversy

Some 400 workers removed 450,000 tons of rock with dynamite and honeycomb drilling, without a single fatality. The memorial draws about two million visitors annually. The Black Hills are sacred to the Lakota Sioux, from whom the land was taken despite the 1868 Treaty of Fort Laramie; the memorial is thus controversial, and the nearby Crazy Horse Memorial, still under construction, was begun partly in response. The site was originally known to the Lakota as the Six Grandfathers.
