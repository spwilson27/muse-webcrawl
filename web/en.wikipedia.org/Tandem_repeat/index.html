Tandem repeat

In genetics, a tandem repeat is a sequence of two or more DNA base pairs that is repeated such that the repetitions lie adjacent to each other on the chromosome. Tandem repeats make up a large fraction of eukaryotic genomes and include satellite DNA, minisatellites, and microsatellites, distinguished by the length of the repeated unit.

Classes

Satellite DNA consists of very long arrays of units typically 5–171 bp repeated thousands of times, concentrated at centromeres and heterochromatin. Minisatellites (VNTRs) have units of roughly 10–60 bp repeated tens to hundreds of times. Microsatellites or short tandem repeats (STRs) have units of 1–6 bp, such as the dinucleotide repeat (CA)n. Copy number at these loci is highly variable because of replication slippage and unequal crossing over.

Applications

Variable tandem repeats underpin DNA profiling: minisatellite fingerprints were used in early forensic genetics, and STR typing is now the standard method for identification and paternity testing. Repeat arrays are also used as genetic markers in linkage analysis and population genetics.

Disease

Expansion of trinucleotide repeats beyond a threshold causes a class of disorders including Huntington's disease (CAG in HTT), fragile X syndrome (CGG in FMR1), myotonic dystrophy (CTG), and many spinocerebellar ataxias. Repeat length often increases between generations — genetic anticipation — worsening onset and severity.
