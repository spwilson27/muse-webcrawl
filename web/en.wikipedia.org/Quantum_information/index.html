Quantum information

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Quantum information is information held in the state of a quantum system. It is the basic entity of study in quantum information science, an interdisciplinary field that involves quantum mechanics, computer science, information theory, philosophy, and cryptography. The fundamental unit of quantum information is the qubit, the quantum analogue of the classical bit.

Properties

Unlike classical information, quantum information can exist in superpositions of basis states, can be entangled between distant systems, and cannot be perfectly copied. Quantum states are also disturbed by measurement, a fact exploited in quantum key distribution. The amount of classical information accessible from a quantum system is bounded by the Holevo theorem.

Applications

Quantum information processing underlies quantum computers, which can solve certain problems — such as integer factorization with Shor's algorithm — exponentially faster than known classical methods. Other applications include quantum teleportation, superdense coding, quantum sensing and metrology, and quantum simulation of physical systems, an idea going back to Richard Feynman's 1981 proposal.
