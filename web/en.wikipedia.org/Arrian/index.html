Lucius Flavius Arrianus (Greek: Ἀρριανός; c. 86 – c. 160 AD), known in English as Arrian, was a Greek historian, public servant, military commander and philosopher of the Roman period. His Anabasis of Alexander is considered one of the most reliable surviving sources on the campaigns of Alexander the Great.

Life

Arrian was born in Nicomedia, the capital of the Roman province of Bithynia. He studied under the Stoic philosopher Epictetus and recorded his teachings in the Discourses and the Enchiridion. He pursued a successful senatorial career under Hadrian, becoming suffect consul around 129–130 AD and governor of Cappadocia, where he repelled an invasion by the Alani. Later he settled in Athens, holding the office of archon.

Works

Anabasis of Alexander

The Anabasis Alexandri, in seven books, narrates Alexander's conquests from his accession in 336 BC to his death in 323 BC. Arrian relied chiefly on the accounts of Ptolemy I Soter and Aristobulus, judging them the most trustworthy. His account is prized for its careful, campaign-by-campaign treatment.

Other works

His Indica describes India and the voyage of Nearchus from the Indus to the Persian Gulf. The Periplus of the Euxine Sea is a survey of the Black Sea coasts, and the Cynegeticus treats hunting. Several works, including a history of events after Alexander, are lost.
