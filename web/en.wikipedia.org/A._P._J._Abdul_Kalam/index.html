A. P. J. Abdul Kalam

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam (15 October 1931 – 27 July 2015) was an Indian aerospace scientist and statesman who served as the 11th President of India from 2002 to 2007. Known as the "Missile Man of India" for his work on ballistic missile and launch vehicle technology, he was one of the country's most beloved public figures.

Early life and career

Born in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, Kalam studied physics and aerospace engineering. He joined the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) and later the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO), where he was project director of the SLV-III, which in 1980 placed the Rohini satellite in orbit — India's first satellite launch vehicle.

Missile programmes

From 1983 Kalam directed the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme, which produced the Agni and Prithvi missiles. He also played a central role in India's 1998 Pokhran-II nuclear tests. His scientific leadership earned him the Padma Bhushan, Padma Vibhushan, and in 1997 the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian honour.

Presidency and later life

Elected with support from both the ruling and opposition parties, Kalam used the presidency to advocate science education, youth development, and his "Vision 2020" plan for a developed India. After leaving office he taught and wrote widely; his autobiography Wings of Fire is among India's best-known books. He died in 2015 while delivering a lecture at IIM Shillong.
