AP site

An AP site (apurinic/apyrimidinic site), also known as an abasic site, is a location in DNA (also in RNA but much less likely) that has neither a purine nor a pyrimidine base, usually because of DNA damage. If left unrepaired, AP sites can lead to mutation during semiconservative replication, because a random nucleotide may be inserted opposite the lesion.

Formation

AP sites form spontaneously through depurination and depyrimidination — hydrolysis of the N-glycosidic bond that links a base to the sugar-phosphate backbone. Thousands of purines are estimated to be lost from the DNA of a typical mammalian cell each day. AP sites are also intermediates in base excision repair, created deliberately by DNA glycosylases that remove damaged bases.

Repair

AP endonucleases (such as APE1 in humans) cleave the phosphodiester backbone at the abasic site, after which DNA polymerase and ligase restore the correct nucleotide. Cells also contain enzymes that can recognize and process modified AP sites produced by oxidation or alkylation.
