European Defence Fund

Established2017 (precursor programmes); Regulation (EU) 2021/697 for 2021–2027
Budget~€8 billion (2021–2027)
Administered byEuropean Commission (DG DEFIS)
PurposeCo-funding collaborative defence research and capability development in the EU

The European Defence Fund (EDF) is a programme of the European Union that co-finances collaborative defence research and the development of defence products and technologies by consortia of companies and research bodies from different member states. It is a key instrument of the EU's efforts to strengthen its defence industrial base and reduce fragmentation and duplication among national defence industries.

Origins

The Fund was announced under Commission President Jean-Claude Juncker in 2016 and launched in 2017, initially through precursor programmes: the Preparatory Action on Defence Research and the European Defence Industrial Development Programme (EDIDP). Its full legal basis, Regulation (EU) 2021/697, set a budget of just under €8 billion for the 2021–2027 multiannual financial framework — split between research (around €2.7 billion) and capability development (around €5.3 billion).

How it works

Projects must involve entities from at least three member states or associated countries (Norway is associated to the Fund). The Commission, through its Directorate-General for Defence Industry and Space (DG DEFIS), issues annual calls in categories ranging from cyber and space to naval combat, sensors, and disruptive technologies; grants cover up to 100% of eligible research costs and a share of development costs, with bonuses for involvement of small and medium-sized enterprises and for projects linked to the Permanent Structured Cooperation (PESCO).

Debates

The Fund marked a significant extension of EU-level involvement in defence, ending the long-standing taboo on using the common budget for military purposes. Critics have raised questions about ethics oversight, export implications, and the participation of entities controlled from third countries, which the regulation restricts. After Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine, the EDF was complemented by emergency instruments such as EDIRPA and the Act in Support of Ammunition Production (ASAP).
