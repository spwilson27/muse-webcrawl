Prince Hermann Ludwig Heinrich von Pückler-Muskau (30 October 1785 – 4 February 1871) was a German nobleman, celebrated landscape gardener, travel writer, and dandy. He created two of Europe's most admired landscape parks, at Muskau on the Neisse river and at Branitz near Cottbus, and popularized the English landscape style in Germany.

Muskau Park

Inheriting the Muskau estate in 1811, Pückler devoted decades and much of his fortune to transforming some 830 hectares into a sweeping landscape park in the English manner, with lakes, meadows, woodland, and carefully staged vistas. The debts incurred drove his eventual sale of Muskau in 1845; the park, now divided between Germany and Poland, became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2004.

Travels and writings

Pückler travelled widely and published witty, bestselling accounts, including Letters of a Dead Man (1830–31), reporting on England, Wales, and Ireland, and later volumes on the Near East and North Africa. His Andeutungen über Landschaftsgärtnerei ("Hints on Landscape Gardening", 1834) remains a classic of garden theory.

Branitz

After selling Muskau he created a second, smaller masterpiece at Branitz, where two earthen pyramids — one in a lake, one on land — dominate the park; he is buried in the land pyramid. His name survives in German culinary tradition in Fürst-Pückler-Eis, the layered Neapolitan-style ice cream.
