The Arado Ar 234 Blitz ("Lightning") was a jet-powered reconnaissance and bomber aircraft produced by the German Arado company during the Second World War. It was the world's first operational jet-powered bomber, and its reconnaissance version flew some of the last Luftwaffe missions over Britain in 1945.

Design and development

The design, led by Walter Blume, originated in a 1940 requirement for a fast reconnaissance aircraft powered by the new turbojet engines. Early prototypes took off from a jettisonable trolley and landed on skids to save weight, but production aircraft received conventional tricycle landing gear. The aircraft was powered by two Junkers Jumo 004 engines; some later variants used four smaller BMW 003 engines. The single pilot sat in a pressurised, extensively glazed nose and aimed bombs with a periscopic sight. Walter Riemensperger and others contributed to the design, and rocket-assisted take-off units were commonly fitted.

Operational service

Ar 234s began reconnaissance operations in August 1944, photographing the Normandy beachhead and, later, targets in England and Italy at altitudes and speeds that made interception nearly impossible. The bomber version entered service late in 1944 with Kampfgeschwader 76, attacking targets in Belgium and the Netherlands, including the Ludendorff Bridge at Remagen in March 1945. Fuel shortages and Allied air superiority limited its impact.

Survivors

The only surviving complete Ar 234 is displayed at the Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum's Udvar-Hazy Center in Virginia, restored in the markings of an aircraft of KG 76 captured in Norway in 1945.
