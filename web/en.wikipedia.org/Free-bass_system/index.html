Free-bass system

A free-bass system is a system of single-note bass buttons on an accordion, designed to give the left hand a melodic range comparable to the right-hand keyboard. Unlike the standard Stradella bass system, which provides preset major, minor, and seventh chords in a compact arrangement, a free-bass system provides individual pitches over several octaves, allowing the left hand to play independent melodies, counterpoint, and complex harmony.

Development

Experiments with free-bass accordions date to the nineteenth century, but the system was developed seriously in the Soviet Union, Denmark, and elsewhere in the mid-twentieth century for the performance of classical music on the bayan and the classical accordion. Danish accordionist Mogens Ellegaard worked with manufacturers to produce free-bass instruments suitable for original compositions, and composers began writing substantial works for the instrument.

Layouts and converters

Free-bass button layouts mirror chromatic button accordion arrangements (C-system or B-system) and may span four or more octaves. Many professional instruments are "converter" accordions, in which a lever switches the left-hand manual between Stradella bass and free bass. The free-bass system makes much of the keyboard repertoire — including Bach transcriptions and contemporary works — playable on the accordion, and it is standard in conservatory accordion training.
