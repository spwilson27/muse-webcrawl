Consommé

Consommé is a type of clear soup made from richly flavoured stock or broth that has been clarified. In classical French cuisine it is prized for combining an intense, concentrated flavour with perfect transparency. The name derives from the French verb consommer, "to complete" or "to concentrate".

Clarification

Traditional clarification uses a "raft": a mixture of finely ground meat, egg whites, mirepoix vegetables, tomato, and sometimes eggshell is stirred into cold stock and slowly brought to a bare simmer. As the proteins coagulate, they rise and trap suspended particles and impurities in a floating crust. The liquid is then carefully ladled out through a hole in the raft and strained, yielding a sparkling, amber-clear soup. Modern alternatives include gelatin filtration — freezing the stock and letting it thaw through cloth, as described by Harold McGee and popularized by chefs such as Heston Blumenthal — and centrifuge or agar clarification.

Varieties and service

Beef consommé is the classic form; chicken, veal, game, and fish versions are also made. A "double consommé" is reduced or fortified with extra meat for still greater intensity. Consommés are served hot or jellied (en gelée) and take the names of their garnishes — julienned vegetables, quenelles, royale (egg custard cubes), profiteroles, or pasta — in an elaborate classical nomenclature codified by Escoffier. A small glass of Madeira or sherry is a traditional enrichment.
