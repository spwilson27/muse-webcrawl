Pizzo (extortion)

The pizzo is protection money paid to the Mafia, typically by businesses in Sicily and southern Italy, in exchange for being left alone by the criminal organization. The term derives from the Sicilian pizzu, meaning "beak"; the phrase "to wet the beak" (fari vagnari u pizzu) describes taking a cut or commission.

Practice

Paying the pizzo is one of the oldest and most pervasive methods by which organized crime taxes economic activity in its territory. Merchants who refuse may face vandalism, arson, theft, or violence. Estimates by Italian business associations have suggested that a large majority of businesses in cities such as Palermo historically paid the pizzo, and that the exaction across Italy amounts to billions of euros annually, affecting the price of goods throughout the country.

Anti-pizzo movements

In 2004, a group of young graduates in Palermo founded Addiopizzo ("Goodbye, pizzo"), a grassroots movement that encourages consumers to patronize only businesses that refuse to pay, and supports shopkeepers who denounce extortion. Its slogan is "An entire people who pay the pizzo is a people without dignity." The movement helped shift public opinion and encouraged more victims to report demands to the police. Similar efforts include the testimony and activism of business owners such as Libero Grassi, a Palermo textile manufacturer who publicly refused to pay and was murdered by the Mafia in 1991, and whose stand became a symbol of resistance.
