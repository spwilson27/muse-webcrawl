Zionism

Zionism is an ethnic or ethno-cultural nationalist movement that emerged in 19th-century Europe to support the establishment of a homeland for the Jewish people in Palestine, a region roughly corresponding to the Land of Israel in Jewish tradition. Following the establishment of the State of Israel in 1948, Zionism has continued primarily in support of Israel and the protection of the Jewish national home there.

The movement arose in response to centuries of persecution of Jews in Europe, culminating in pogroms in the Russian Empire and the antisemitism revealed by the Dreyfus affair in France. In 1896 the Viennese journalist Theodor Herzl published Der Judenstaat (The Jewish State), arguing that Jews could be secure only in a state of their own; the following year he convened the First Zionist Congress in Basel, Switzerland, which founded the World Zionist Organization and adopted the goal of a publicly and legally assured home in Palestine. Earlier currents included the religious proto-Zionism of rabbis such as Yehuda Alkalai and Zvi Hirsch Kalischer, and the Hibbat Zion ("Lovers of Zion") movement that sponsored the First Aliyah of Jewish immigration to Ottoman Palestine in 1882.

Development

Zionism developed diverse strands: political Zionism, the socialist Labour Zionism that dominated the Yishuv (the Jewish community in Palestine) through the kibbutz movement, Revisionist Zionism founded by Ze'ev Jabotinsky, and religious Zionism. Britain's Balfour Declaration of 1917 endorsed a "national home for the Jewish people" in Palestine, a commitment incorporated into the League of Nations Mandate. After the Holocaust, international support grew, and the United Nations voted in 1947 to partition Palestine into Jewish and Arab states. On 14 May 1948 David Ben-Gurion proclaimed the State of Israel. The ideology remains the subject of substantial political debate, with supporters framing it as a national liberation movement and critics, including Palestinian movements, opposing it on grounds of its impact on the Palestinian population.
