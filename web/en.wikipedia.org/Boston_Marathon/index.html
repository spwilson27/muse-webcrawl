Boston Marathon

The Boston Marathon is an annual marathon race held in the Boston area of Massachusetts, United States, traditionally on Patriots' Day, the third Monday in April. Established in 1897, it is the world's oldest annual marathon and one of the six World Marathon Majors.

The course

The point-to-point course runs 26 miles 385 yards (42.195 km) from Hopkinton through Ashland, Framingham, Natick, Wellesley, Newton, and Brookline to the finish line on Boylston Street in Boston's Back Bay. It is famous for the Newton hills, culminating in Heartbreak Hill near mile 21, and for the Wellesley College "scream tunnel." The course is net downhill and point-to-point, so times set there are not eligible for world records.

History

Inspired by the marathon at the 1896 Athens Olympics, the first Boston Marathon in 1897 had just 15 entrants. Women were officially admitted in 1972, after Bobbi Gibb ran unofficially in 1966 and Kathrine Switzer entered under the name "K. V. Switzer" in 1967. The race is organized by the Boston Athletic Association. On April 15, 2013, two bombs exploded near the finish line, killing three spectators and injuring hundreds; the event returned the following year with a record field and the motto "Boston Strong."
