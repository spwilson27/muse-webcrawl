Easter egg (media)

An Easter egg is a message, image, or feature hidden within a work of media such as a video game, film, software program, or book. The term was coined around 1979 to describe a secret message left by programmer Warren Robinett in the Atari video game Adventure, placed there because Atari did not credit its game designers at the time.

In software and games

Easter eggs in software range from hidden credits screens and jokes to entire secret levels or mini-games. The practice spread through early personal computer software — Microsoft products of the 1990s famously contained elaborate hidden features — and later became more restricted as companies grew concerned about undisclosed code.

In film and other media

Filmmakers and authors also plant Easter eggs: background references to other works, cameo appearances, or clues rewarding attentive fans. Studios such as Pixar are known for cross-references between their films.
