Ian McKay

Born: 7 May 1953, Wortley, South Yorkshire
Died: 12 June 1982 (aged 29), Mount Longdon, Falkland Islands
Allegiance: United Kingdom
Unit: 3rd Battalion, Parachute Regiment
Awards: Victoria Cross

Ian John McKay VC (7 May 1953 – 12 June 1982) was a British soldier of the Parachute Regiment who was posthumously awarded the Victoria Cross, the highest award for gallantry in the British honours system, for his actions during the Battle of Mount Longdon in the Falklands War.

Early life and career

McKay was born in Wortley, near Barnsley, and worked as a Post Office clerk and played semi-professional football before enlisting. By 1982 he was a sergeant and platoon commander in 4 Platoon, B Company, 3 PARA, which sailed south with the task force after Argentina invaded the Falkland Islands in April 1982.

Mount Longdon

On the night of 11–12 June 1982, 3 PARA attacked Argentine positions on Mount Longdon. When 4 Platoon's commander was wounded and the advance pinned down by a machine-gun position, McKay led a small group forward and then, realising the assault had to continue, charged the bunker alone, killing its occupants with small arms and grenades before being killed himself. The position was silenced and the platoon renewed its attack. His citation, gazetted in October 1982, described his "outstanding valour and leadership". McKay is buried at Aldershot Military Cemetery.
