Lesene

A lesene, also called a pilaster strip, is a narrow, low-relief vertical strip projecting from a wall, typically without a base or capital. Lesenes are a characteristic feature of early medieval architecture, especially Lombard, Carolingian, Ottonian and Romanesque buildings.

Characteristics

Unlike a classical pilaster, a lesene has no capital or entablature of its own. Lesenes divide wall surfaces into bays and are often connected at the top by arched corbel tables or Lombard bands, creating a rhythmic pattern of shallow arches across a façade or apse.

Use

Lesenes appear widely in northern Italian Lombard churches, in German pre-Romanesque and Romanesque architecture, and as far afield as Catalonia and Scandinavia. Though they suggest structural support, their role is primarily decorative and organisational, articulating the wall to reflect the internal bay system.
