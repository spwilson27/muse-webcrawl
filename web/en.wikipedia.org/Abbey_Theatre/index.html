Abbey Theatre

Location26 Abbey Street Lower, Dublin, Ireland
Opened27 December 1904
FoundersW. B. Yeats, Lady Gregory, Annie Horniman
TypeNational theatre

The Abbey Theatre (Irish: Amharclann na Mainistreach), also known as the National Theatre of Ireland, is a theatre in Dublin, Ireland. Founded in 1904 as part of the Irish literary revival, it was the first state-subsidised theatre in the English-speaking world and became closely identified with the cultural movement that preceded Irish independence.

History

The theatre grew out of the Irish Literary Theatre founded by W. B. Yeats, Lady Gregory and Edward Martyn. With a subsidy from the English patron Annie Horniman, the company took over a former morgue and music hall on Abbey Street, opening on 27 December 1904 with plays by Yeats and Lady Gregory.

Early productions included works by J. M. Synge; the first performances of The Playboy of the Western World in 1907 provoked riots in the audience. Sean O'Casey's Dublin trilogy of the 1920s, including Juno and the Paycock, was also premiered at the Abbey, though his The Plough and the Stars likewise caused disturbances.

The modern theatre

The original building was destroyed by fire in 1951, and the present theatre, designed by Michael Scott, opened on the same site in 1966. The Abbey continues to stage Irish classics and new writing, and operates the smaller Peacock Theatre in its basement.
