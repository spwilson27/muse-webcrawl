Triton (mythology)


Triton with his conch-shell trumpet

Triton is a Greek god of the sea, the son and herald of Poseidon and Amphitrite. He is usually depicted as a merman, with the upper body of a human and the tail of a fish, carrying a twisted conch shell on which he blows like a trumpet to calm or raise the waves.

Mythology

In Hesiod's Theogony, Triton lives with his parents in a golden palace beneath the sea. Virgil's Aeneid recounts how Triton drowned the Trojan Misenus for challenging him in trumpet-playing. In the tale of Jason and the Argonauts, Triton (or a local Libyan Triton) helped the stranded Argo escape the shallows of Lake Tritonis.

Tritons and legacy

Later art and literature multiplied Tritons into a whole class of sea creatures, companions of Oceanus and Poseidon, often shown with horses' forelegs or blowing shells. The name was later given to the largest moon of Neptune, discovered in 1846, and to a genus of sea snails whose shells resemble his trumpet.
