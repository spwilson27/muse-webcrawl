Conversations on the Plurality of Worlds

Conversations on the Plurality of Worlds (French: Entretiens sur la pluralité des mondes) is a popular science book by the French author Bernard Le Bovier de Fontenelle, published in 1686. It was one of the first works to present the new astronomy of Copernicus and Descartes to a general readership, and it became one of the most widely read scientific books of the Enlightenment.

Content

The book is presented as a series of evening conversations between a philosopher and a marquise in the gardens of her country estate. Over six evenings (a sixth was added in the 1687 edition), the philosopher explains the heliocentric system, the Cartesian theory of vortices, and the possibility that the Moon and the planets are inhabited. The dialogue form, witty and deliberately light in tone, made potentially dangerous ideas approachable: rather than refuting the plurality of worlds outright, the Church placed the book on the Index of Prohibited Books in 1687.

Reception and influence

The Entretiens went through many editions and translations, including several English versions in the decades after publication, among them one by Aphra Behn. It helped establish the vernacular "conversation" as a vehicle for science writing and shaped the speculative tradition of imagining inhabited other worlds that runs through later science fiction. Fontenelle went on to become perpetual secretary of the French Academy of Sciences, in which role he further developed the genre of popular scientific exposition.
