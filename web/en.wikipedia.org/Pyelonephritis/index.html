Pyelonephritis

SpecialtyNephrology; infectious disease
SymptomsFever, flank pain, nausea, painful urination
Usual causeBacterial infection, most often Escherichia coli
TreatmentAntibiotics; hospitalisation if severe

Pyelonephritis is inflammation of the kidney due to a bacterial infection of the renal pelvis and kidney tissue. It is a form of upper urinary tract infection and is generally more serious than a bladder infection (cystitis), with the potential to cause permanent kidney damage or sepsis.

Signs and symptoms

Typical features include high fever, pain in the flank or back (often with tenderness at the costovertebral angle), nausea and vomiting, and lower urinary symptoms such as frequent, painful urination. The condition usually develops when bacteria, most commonly Escherichia coli from the bowel, ascend from the lower urinary tract; less often they reach the kidney via the bloodstream.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis rests on the clinical picture together with urinalysis and urine culture; blood tests and imaging are used in complicated cases. Treatment is with antibiotics, initially often given empirically and adjusted to culture results; severe cases, pregnant patients, and those unable to take oral medication may require hospital admission. Risk factors include female sex, pregnancy, urinary obstruction such as kidney stones or an enlarged prostate, diabetes, and vesicoureteral reflux in children.
