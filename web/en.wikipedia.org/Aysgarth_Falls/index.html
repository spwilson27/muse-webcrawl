Aysgarth Falls

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Aysgarth Falls are a triple flight of waterfalls on the River Ure in Wensleydale, in the Yorkshire Dales National Park in North Yorkshire, England, near the village of Aysgarth. The falls are arranged in three broad steps — the Upper, Middle and Lower Force — where the river descends over limestone ledges through wooded scenery.

RiverRiver Ure
Number of drops3 (Upper, Middle, Lower Force)

Description

The waterfalls stretch over nearly a mile of the river's course. The Middle Force can be viewed from a bridge that has stood on the site for centuries, and the surrounding woods are rich in wildflowers in spring. After heavy rain the falls are especially dramatic, with the whole width of the Ure pouring over the limestone shelves.

History and culture

The falls have attracted visitors since the Romantic era; the poet William Wordsworth and the painter J. M. W. Turner both visited and recorded them. In the 1991 film Robin Hood: Prince of Thieves, the fight scene between Robin Hood and Little John was filmed at Aysgarth Falls. The falls lie close to the Yorkshire Dales National Park visitor centre at Aysgarth and are a popular stop for walkers touring Wensleydale.

Access

Paths link the three falls, and the surrounding Freeholders' Wood is managed as a nature reserve noted for its wild garlic, bluebells and dormice. The village of Aysgarth, with its famous Edwardian rock garden created by Heather Speight, lies a short walk away.
