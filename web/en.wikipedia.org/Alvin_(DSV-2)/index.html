Alvin (DSV-2)

Alvin (DSV-2) is a deep-ocean submersible operated by the Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution. Commissioned in 1964, it is one of the world's longest-serving and most famous research submersibles, capable of carrying three people — two scientists and a pilot — to the ocean floor.

OperatorWoods Hole Oceanographic Institution
Launched1964
Crew3
Depth rating6,500 m (after 2022 upgrade)

Notable missions

Alvin helped recover a lost hydrogen bomb from the Mediterranean Sea in 1966, took part in the first exploration of the wreck of the RMS Titanic in 1986, and in 1977 carried scientists to the Galápagos Rift, where they discovered hydrothermal vent ecosystems sustained by chemosynthesis — life without sunlight. Repeated overhauls have replaced nearly every component, and a major upgrade certified in 2022 extended its range to 6,500 metres, opening about 99 percent of the seafloor to direct observation.
