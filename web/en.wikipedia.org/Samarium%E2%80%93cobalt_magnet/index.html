Samarium–cobalt magnet

TypeRare-earth permanent magnet

A samarium–cobalt (SmCo) magnet is a type of rare-earth permanent magnet made from an alloy of samarium and cobalt. Developed in the late 1960s and early 1970s, samarium–cobalt magnets were the first rare-earth magnets to be commercialised and remain prized for their exceptional resistance to demagnetisation, excellent temperature stability and good corrosion resistance.

Types and properties

Two families are produced: SmCo5, with a maximum energy product around 16–22 MGOe, and Sm2Co17, often alloyed with iron, copper and zirconium, reaching about 24–32 MGOe. Although neodymium magnets (NdFeB) are stronger at room temperature, samarium–cobalt grades retain their strength at much higher temperatures, with Curie points of roughly 720 °C (SmCo5) to 920 °C (Sm2Co17) and reversible temperature coefficients far lower than those of neodymium grades. The material is hard and brittle, so magnets are usually ground to shape with diamond tools after sintering.

Manufacture

Production involves alloying, milling to a fine powder, pressing in a magnetic field to align the particles, sintering and heat treatment, followed by machining and magnetisation. The reliance on cobalt and samarium makes SmCo magnets considerably more expensive than ferrite or neodymium alternatives, so they are used where performance justifies the cost.

Applications

Samarium–cobalt magnets are used in high-performance motors and generators for aerospace and motorsport, travelling-wave tubes and satellite systems, sensors, magnetic bearings, medical devices and precision instruments — anywhere that high temperature, stability or corrosion resistance rules out neodymium magnets.
