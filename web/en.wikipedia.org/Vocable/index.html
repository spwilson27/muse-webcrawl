Vocable

A vocable is a word, or a unit of language with a distinctive sound, especially a word considered as a sound unit apart from its meaning. In linguistics the term is used for the physical form of a word — its sequence of sounds or letters — in contrast to its meaning or lexical identity. For example, the homophones "flour" and "flower" may be treated as a single vocable but two different lexemes.

Usage

The term is also used in the study of oral and musical traditions for non-lexical syllables — sounds sung or chanted that carry rhythm or melody rather than semantic meaning, such as the vocables used in some Native American songs, in scat singing, or in folk-song refrains like "fa la la". Ethnomusicologists record such vocables when transcribing songs whose syllables have no translation.
