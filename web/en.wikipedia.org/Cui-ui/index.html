Scientific nameChasmistes cujus
FamilyCatostomidae (suckers)

StatusEndangered (U.S. ESA, listed 1967)

The cui-ui (Chasmistes cujus) is a large freshwater sucker fish endemic to Pyramid Lake and the lower Truckee River in northwestern Nevada, United States. One of the lake suckers of the genus Chasmistes, it can grow to about 60 cm and live for more than 40 years, feeding on zooplankton strained from the water. It is a culturally central food fish of the Pyramid Lake Paiute Tribe, whose name for themselves, Cuiyui Ticutta, means "cui-ui eaters".

Decline and recovery

Once abundant, the cui-ui collapsed in the 20th century after Derby Dam (1905) diverted Truckee River water to the Newlands Project, lowering Pyramid Lake by some 24 metres and blocking spawning runs up the river; a delta of silt at the river mouth further impeded migration. It was among the first species protected under U.S. endangered species law, listed as endangered in 1967. A fishway and lift at Numaga Dam, artificial spawning channels, hatchery support and negotiated water purchases have since restored spawning runs, and the population has partially recovered, though it remains endangered.
