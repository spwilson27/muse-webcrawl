Dream of the Red Chamber

Dream of the Red Chamber (simplified Chinese: 红楼梦; traditional Chinese: 紅樓夢; pinyin: Hónglóumèng), also called The Story of the Stone, is a novel by the Chinese author Cao Xueqin, written in the mid-18th century during the Qing dynasty. It is considered one of the Four Great Classical Novels of Chinese literature and is generally regarded as the pinnacle of the Chinese novel.

Summary

The novel chronicles the rise and decline of the wealthy, aristocratic Jia clan through the eyes of Jia Baoyu, the family's heir, who prefers the company of the women of the household to his official studies. Central to the story is the love triangle between Baoyu, his sickly, melancholy cousin Lin Daiyu, and his other cousin Xue Baochai, whom he is ultimately tricked into marrying after Daiyu's death. Around them, dozens of characters — servants, relatives, and officials — populate a vast, minutely observed portrait of 18th-century Chinese family life.

Text and authorship

Cao Xueqin appears to have written the first 80 chapters; the last 40 chapters of the standard 120-chapter edition were published by Gao E in 1791–92 and are usually attributed to Gao or to another hand. The enormous scholarly field devoted to the novel is known as "Redology" (红学).

The first English translation of note was David Hawkes's The Story of the Stone (1973–86, completed by John Minford), which remains highly regarded.
