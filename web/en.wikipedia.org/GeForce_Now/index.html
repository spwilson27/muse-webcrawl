PlatformsWindows, macOS, Android, iOS (browser), Android TV, browsers

GeForce Now (stylised as GeForce NOW) is the brand used by Nvidia for its cloud gaming service. The service allows subscribers to stream games from Nvidia's servers to their own devices; unlike some rival services, it primarily lets users play PC games they already own through storefronts such as Steam, the Epic Games Store and Ubisoft Connect, rather than selling a separate game library.

History

Nvidia's cloud gaming efforts began with the GRID beta for its Shield devices in 2013–2015, relaunched as GeForce Now for Shield in 2015. A version for PC and Mac entered beta in 2017, and the service exited beta and launched publicly on 4 February 2020. Following launch, several publishers temporarily withdrew their catalogues over licensing questions, prompting Nvidia to move to an opt-in policy for developers; the library has since grown to well over a thousand supported titles.

Service tiers and features

GeForce Now offers a free tier with standard rigs and one-hour sessions, and paid tiers (Priority and the flagship tier variously called Ultimate/RTX 3080 tier) offering more powerful virtual GPUs, longer sessions and higher resolutions and frame rates — up to 4K and 120 frames per second on supported plans — as well as ray tracing and DLSS. Games run on Nvidia server hardware and are streamed over the internet to the client, which passes the player's input back to the server.
