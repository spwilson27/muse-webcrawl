Tuscan wine

Vineyards in Chianti, the best-known wine district of Tuscany, Italy.

Tuscan wine is Italian wine produced in the region of Tuscany, in central Italy. Tuscany is one of the most famous and prolific wine regions in Europe, best known for red wines based on the Sangiovese grape, which accounts for the great majority of the region's vineyard plantings. Among its celebrated wines are Chianti and Chianti Classico, Brunello di Montalcino, Vino Nobile di Montepulciano, Carmignano, and the coastal wines of Bolgheri, as well as white wines such as Vernaccia di San Gimignano and the sweet Vin Santo.

History

Viticulture in Tuscany dates to the Etruscans, and wine has been made there continuously through Roman, medieval and Renaissance times. In 1716 Cosimo III de' Medici issued an edict delimiting the Chianti zone — one of the earliest official wine appellations in the world. In the nineteenth and twentieth centuries Chianti became a mass-market export wine (familiar from the straw-covered fiasco bottle), while reforms from the 1970s onwards raised quality sharply.

Key wines and the "Super Tuscans"

The classic Sangiovese denominations — Chianti Classico DOCG, Brunello di Montalcino DOCG and Vino Nobile di Montepulciano DOCG — are among Italy's most prestigious reds. From the 1970s, a group of producers around Bolgheri and elsewhere rebelled against the restrictive DOC rules and made acclaimed wines from international varieties (Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot) or Sangiovese outside the rules, sold at first as humble vino da tavola. These "Super Tuscans" — including Sassicaia, Tignanello, Ornellaia and Solaia — became some of Italy's most expensive and sought-after wines, and the rules were eventually reformed (with the creation of the Toscana IGT category) partly in response to them.

Grapes and styles

Besides Sangiovese, Tuscany grows Colorino, Canaiolo, Ciliegiolo, Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot and Syrah for reds, and Trebbiano, Vernaccia, Vermentino and Malvasia for whites. The region also produces Vin Santo, a traditional dessert wine made from dried grapes. Tuscany has dozens of DOC and DOCG appellations and consistently ranks among Italy's leading wine-exporting regions.
