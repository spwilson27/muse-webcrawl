Nahalal is a moshav in northern Israel, located in the Jezreel Valley. Founded in 1921, it was the first moshav ovdim ("workers' cooperative settlement") established in Mandatory Palestine, and it is famed for its distinctive circular town plan.

History and design

Nahalal's settlers, largely Second Aliyah pioneers from Eastern Europe who had worked at Degania and other early settlements, wanted a cooperative village that preserved the private family farm — in contrast to the communal kibbutz. Architect Richard Kauffmann designed the village as a near-perfect circle: public buildings at the center, family farmsteads arranged in a ring with equal plots, and agricultural land radiating outward in wedges. The layout embodied ideals of equality and became a model for dozens of later moshavim.

Notable residents

Nahalal was home to military leader and politician Moshe Dayan, who was raised there and is buried in its cemetery, overlooking the valley; astronaut Ilan Ramon is also buried there. Other notable figures include Hannah Senesh, who lived briefly at Nahalal while attending its agricultural school for girls, founded in the 1930s. Today Nahalal has roughly a thousand residents, many still farming. See also Moshav and Jezreel Valley.
