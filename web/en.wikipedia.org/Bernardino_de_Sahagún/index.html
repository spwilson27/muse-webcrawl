Bernardino de Sahagún

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Bernardino de Sahagún (c. 1499 – 5 February 1590) was a Franciscan friar, missionary priest and pioneering ethnographer who participated in the Catholic evangelization of colonial New Spain (now Mexico). Born Bernardino de Rivera in Sahagún, Spain, he journeyed to New Spain in 1529 and spent more than 60 years documenting Aztec (Mexica) beliefs, culture and history from indigenous sources.

The Florentine Codex

Sahagún's masterwork is the Historia general de las cosas de Nueva España ("General History of the Things of New Spain"), known as the Florentine Codex. Working with Nahua scribes, artists and elders at the Colegio de Santa Cruz de Tlatelolco and elsewhere, he compiled a bilingual Nahuatl–Spanish encyclopedia of Aztec religion, calendrics, medicine, flora, fauna and the Spanish conquest — the latter including an invaluable indigenous account of the fall of Tenochtitlan. The twelve-book manuscript, illustrated with nearly 2,500 images, is now held by the Laurentian Library in Florence.

Legacy

Because he aimed to understand indigenous religion in order to eradicate it, Sahagún's project was viewed with suspicion by the Spanish authorities, and his manuscripts were confiscated for a time. Today he is regarded as a founder of modern ethnography and anthropological method, and his work is one of the richest surviving sources on pre-conquest Aztec civilization.
