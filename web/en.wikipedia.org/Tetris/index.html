Tetris

Tetris is a puzzle video game created in 1984 by Soviet software engineer Alexey Pajitnov. Players complete lines by rotating and moving falling pieces called tetrominoes — geometric shapes composed of four square blocks — into a rectangular well. Completed lines disappear; the game ends when the pieces stack to the top.

DesignerAlexey Pajitnov
Release1984
GenrePuzzle
OriginSoviet Union

History

Pajitnov developed Tetris on an Electronika 60 computer at the Soviet Academy of Sciences, taking the name from tetra (four) and tennis. The game spread informally through Moscow and then the West, triggering a long dispute over licensing rights. Nintendo's 1989 Game Boy version bundled with the handheld became a phenomenon and made Tetris one of the best-selling video games of all time, with hundreds of millions of copies across platforms.

Cultural impact

The falling-block design spawned a genre, competitive play culminating in the Classic Tetris World Championship, and psychological research into the "Tetris effect", in which players see falling pieces in their peripheral vision and dreams. The Korobeiniki folk tune is inseparably associated with the Game Boy version.
