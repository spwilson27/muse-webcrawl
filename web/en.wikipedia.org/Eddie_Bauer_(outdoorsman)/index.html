Eddie Bauer (October 19, 1899 – April 18, 1986) was an American outdoorsman, inventor, author, and businessman. In 1920 he founded the Eddie Bauer sporting-goods company in Seattle, Washington. A lifelong hunter and fisherman, Bauer built the business around gear he tested personally in the Pacific Northwest and Alaska.

Inventions

After nearly dying of hypothermia on a winter fishing trip, Bauer developed the first quilted goose-down jacket in the United States, patented in 1940 as the "Skyliner". During the Second World War his company supplied down-filled flight suits and sleeping bags to the US military, and Bauer persuaded the government to let the gear bear his label — an early example of a brand name on military equipment. He held numerous patents for outdoor equipment, including shuttlecocks for badminton.

Legacy

Bauer sold the company in 1968; it later passed through several owners and became a mainstream outdoor-clothing retailer. He was inducted posthumously into the Washington State Hall of Fame, and his name remains associated with down insulation and Pacific Northwest outdoor culture.
