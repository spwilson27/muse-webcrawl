Ramanagara district

Ramanagara district is a district in the Indian state of Karnataka. It was carved out of the former Bangalore Rural district in 2007. The district headquarters is the city of Ramanagara. The district comprises the taluks of Ramanagara, Channapatna, Kanakapura, Magadi and Harohalli, and lies to the southwest of Bengaluru, forming part of its extended metropolitan region.

Geography

The district is noted for its rugged terrain of granite hills and rocky outcrops, including the Ramadevarabetta and Savandurga hills, which are popular for trekking and rock climbing. The Arkavathi and Kanva rivers flow through the district. Ramadevarabetta is also recognised as a vulture sanctuary protecting endangered long-billed vultures.

Economy

Ramanagara is famous for sericulture and its silk market, one of the largest cocoon markets in Asia. Channapatna is renowned for its traditional wooden toys and lacquerware, which carry a Geographical Indication tag. Agriculture (ragi, coconut, mulberry) remains important, while industrial areas near Bidadi — home to a major Toyota Kirloskar plant — link the district to Bengaluru's economy.

Tourism and culture

The rocky hills around Ramanagara served as the setting for the fictional town in the classic 1975 film Sholay, drawing film tourism to "Ramgarh". Other attractions include the Kanva reservoir, Mekedatu and Sangama on the Kaveri river near Kanakapura, and the Janapada Loka folk arts museum on the Bengaluru–Mysuru highway.

Demographics

According to the 2011 census, the district had a population of about 1.08 million, predominantly Kannada-speaking, with agriculture the main occupation.
