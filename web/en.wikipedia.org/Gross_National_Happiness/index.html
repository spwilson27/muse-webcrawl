Gross National Happiness

Gross National Happiness (GNH) is a philosophy that guides the government of Bhutan. It includes an index to measure the collective happiness and well-being of a population, coined by the fourth King of Bhutan, Jigme Singye Wangchuck, in 1972.

Origins

The King declared that "Gross National Happiness is more important than Gross National Product", proposing that development should take a holistic approach balancing material and non-material values. The concept was later enshrined in Bhutan's 2008 constitution.

Index

The GNH Index covers nine domains: psychological well-being, health, education, time use, cultural diversity and resilience, good governance, community vitality, ecological diversity and resilience, and living standards, measured by periodic national surveys.

Influence

GNH helped inspire wider international interest in well-being economics and alternative measures to GDP, including happiness indices in other countries and UN resolutions on happiness.
