National Day

A national day is a day on which celebrations mark the nationhood of a nation or state. It may be the date of independence, of becoming a republic, of unification, of the signing of a constitution, or a significant day for a patron saint or ruler.

Significance

National days are typically public holidays marked by parades, fireworks, speeches, flag-raising ceremonies, and cultural events. For many countries the day commemorates independence from colonial rule: the United States celebrates 4 July, India 15 August, and many Latin American nations their September or May independence dates.

Variations

Some countries have more than one national day, such as India (Independence Day and Republic Day), while a few, notably the United Kingdom, have no single official national day, though patron saints' days are observed in its constituent countries. Others mark the monarch's birthday, as in the Netherlands' King's Day.

List examples

France: 14 July (Bastille Day). Japan: 11 February (National Foundation Day). China: 1 October (founding of the People's Republic, 1949). Canada: 1 July (Confederation, 1867). Australia: 26 January.
