DIY culture

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

DIY culture — "do it yourself" culture — comprises activities and scenes in which people create, modify, or repair things, and produce media, outside professional and commercial channels. Rooted in self-reliance and often in a deliberate anti-consumerist ethic, it spans home improvement, crafts, independent publishing, and music.

History

The phrase "do it yourself" became common in the early 20th century in connection with home improvement. DIY took on a cultural and political meaning in the 1970s punk movement, which championed self-released records, gigs organised outside the music industry, and photocopied fanzines (zines). Similar ethics animated indie rock, hardcore punk, riot grrrl, and later the craft, maker, and open-source movements. The Internet expanded DIY culture through tutorial media, print-on-demand, crowdfunding, and marketplaces for handmade goods such as Etsy.

Characteristics

DIY culture values skill-sharing, low-cost improvisation, and independence from gatekeepers; its products often embrace an unpolished aesthetic as a mark of authenticity. Scholars connect it with earlier Arts and Crafts ideals and with contemporary practices of repair cafés, hackerspaces, and community workshops.
