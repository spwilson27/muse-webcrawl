Imaginary unit

The imaginary unit, denoted i, is a solution to the quadratic equation x² = −1. No real number has a negative square, so i extends the real numbers to the complex numbers, in which every polynomial equation has a root (the fundamental theorem of algebra).

Definition and powers

By definition i² = −1. Its powers cycle with period 4: i, −1, −i, 1, …. A complex number has the form a + bi with real a and b, plotted as a point in the complex plane, where i corresponds to a quarter-turn rotation.

History

Square roots of negative numbers appeared in the work of Cardano in the 16th century and were developed by Bombelli; the term "imaginary" was used dismissively by Descartes, but the numbers became indispensable in mathematics, physics, and engineering.

Notation

Electrical engineers often write j instead of i to avoid confusion with current. The other square root of −1 is −i.
