Alpujarras

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Alpujarras (Spanish: La Alpujarra) is a mountainous region of Andalusia in southern Spain, on the southern slopes of the Sierra Nevada in the provinces of Granada and Almería. It is known for its whitewashed villages, terraced hillsides, and irrigation channels of Moorish origin.

History

After the fall of Granada in 1492, the region became a refuge for Muslims who resisted Christian rule. The Morisco revolt of 1568–1571, led by Aben Humeya, ended with the expulsion of the Moriscos and repopulation by Christian settlers from elsewhere in Spain.

Geography and culture

Villages such as Pampaneira, Bubión, and Capileira in the Poqueira gorge feature flat-roofed Berber-style houses. The area is noted for its cured ham from Trevélez, its weaving, and Gerald Brenan's memoir South from Granada, set in the village of Yegen.
