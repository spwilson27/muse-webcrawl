Asfotase alfa

Trade name: Strensiq

Type: Recombinant fusion protein (enzyme replacement therapy)

Target enzyme: Tissue-nonspecific alkaline phosphatase (TNSALP)

Routes of administration: Subcutaneous injection

Approved: FDA and EMA, 2015

Manufacturer: Alexion Pharmaceuticals (AstraZeneca Rare Disease)

Asfotase alfa, sold under the brand name Strensiq, is a medication for the treatment of people with hypophosphatasia. It is a recombinant fusion protein providing enzyme replacement therapy with tissue-nonspecific alkaline phosphatase (TNSALP), the enzyme whose deficiency causes the disease.

Medical uses

In the United States, asfotase alfa is indicated for the treatment of patients with perinatal/infantile- and juvenile-onset hypophosphatasia (HPP). In the European Union it is indicated for long-term enzyme replacement therapy in patients with paediatric-onset hypophosphatasia to treat the bone manifestations of the disease. Hypophosphatasia is a rare inherited disorder in which defective mineralisation causes soft, weak bones (rickets-like disease), fractures, respiratory failure in infants, and premature loss of teeth.

Mechanism of action

Asfotase alfa is a fusion protein comprising the catalytic domain of human TNSALP, the Fc region of human IgG1, and a deca-aspartate peptide that targets the enzyme to bone tissue. By supplying functional alkaline phosphatase, it restores hydrolysis of inorganic pyrophosphate, a natural inhibitor of mineralisation, allowing normal bone mineral deposition.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include injection site reactions, hypersensitivity reactions, lipodystrophy at injection sites, and ectopic calcifications of the eye and kidney; patients are monitored accordingly.

History

Asfotase alfa was developed by Enobia Pharma and acquired by Alexion Pharmaceuticals in 2012. It was approved in Japan in July 2015, in the European Union in September 2015, and by the US Food and Drug Administration in October 2015 — the first approved treatment for hypophosphatasia. It received orphan drug and breakthrough therapy designations; its high cost placed it among the most expensive drugs in the world.
