Hebridean Terrane

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Hebridean Terrane is one of the terranes that form part of the Caledonian orogenic belt in northwest Scotland. The terrane lies west of the Moine Thrust and includes the Outer Hebrides and the far northwest mainland coast of Scotland. Its basement consists largely of the Lewisian complex — some of the oldest rocks in Europe, dating back nearly three billion years to the Archaean — overlain unconformably by the Torridonian sandstones and Cambrian–Ordovician sediments along the Foreland.

Extent and boundaries

The terrane's eastern boundary is the Moine Thrust Belt, along which metamorphic rocks of the Northern Highlands Terrane were thrust northwestward over it during the Caledonian orogeny. To the west, the terrane is bounded offshore by the Minch Fault and related structures. The Outer Hebrides are composed almost entirely of Lewisian gneiss cut by later intrusions and faults.

Geology

The Lewisian complex records a long history of igneous intrusion, high-grade metamorphism and deformation during the Archaean and Palaeoproterozoic. The unmetamorphosed red sandstones of the Torridonian Supergroup, roughly a billion years old, form the dramatic mountains of Torridon and Applecross. Along the narrow coastal strip, Cambrian quartzites and the distinctive fucoid beds and Durness Group limestones record the early Palaeozoic passive margin of the ancient continent Laurentia.
