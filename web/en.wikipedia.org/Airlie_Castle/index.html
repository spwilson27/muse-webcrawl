Airlie Castle

Location: Angus, Scotland

Built: 15th century (tower); later additions

Occupants: Ogilvy family, Earls of Airlie

Airlie Castle is a castle and country house near the village of Airlie in Angus, Scotland. It stands at the junction of the Melgund Water and the River Isla, on the site of an earlier fortified house, and is the seat of the Ogilvy family, whose head is the Earl of Airlie.

History

The Ogilvys received the barony of Airlie in the 15th century, and Walter Ogilvy built a tower house around 1432. The castle was destroyed around 1640 during the Civil War era, when the Earl of Airlie was a Royalist and the house was attacked by the forces of the Earl of Argyll — an event remembered in the ballad "The Bonnie House o' Airlie." The castle was rebuilt afterwards and extended in later centuries into a mansion house incorporating the surviving 15th-century tower.

The grounds include notable walled gardens. Airlie Castle remains a private family home of the Earls of Airlie and is not generally open to the public, though the gardens have occasionally opened under Scotland's Gardens Scheme.
