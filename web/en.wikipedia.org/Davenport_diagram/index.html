Davenport diagram

A Davenport diagram plotting blood pH against bicarbonate concentration

A Davenport diagram is a graphical tool used in acid–base physiology to describe the acid–base status of blood. It plots the plasma bicarbonate concentration ([HCO3−]) against blood pH for a given partial pressure of carbon dioxide (PCO2). The diagram was developed from the work of Horace W. Davenport, who described the underlying relationships in his textbook The ABC of Acid-Base Chemistry.

Construction

Curved lines on the diagram represent constant values of PCO2 (isobars), derived from the Henderson–Hasselbalch equation. A straight buffer line shows how pH and bicarbonate change when a strong acid or base is added to blood at constant PCO2. By locating a patient's measured pH and bicarbonate on the diagram, clinicians can classify disturbances such as respiratory acidosis, metabolic acidosis, and their compensations.

Clinical use

The Davenport diagram provides a visual alternative to algebraic approaches and to the later Siggaard-Andersen nomogram. It illustrates why respiratory changes move a point along a buffer line, whereas metabolic changes shift the buffer line itself.
