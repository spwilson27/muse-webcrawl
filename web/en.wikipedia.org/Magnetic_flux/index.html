Magnetic flux

Symbol: ΦB
SI unit: weber (Wb)
Definition: ΦB = ∫ B · dA

In physics, specifically electromagnetism, the magnetic flux through a surface is the surface integral of the normal component of the magnetic field B over that surface. It is usually denoted Φ or ΦB and measured in the SI unit of webers (Wb).

Definition

For a flat surface of area A in a uniform field, the flux is ΦB = BA cos θ, where θ is the angle between the field lines and the normal to the surface. Flux is greatest when the field is perpendicular to the surface and zero when parallel to it. Unlike the field lines of a magnet, which form closed loops, flux through any closed surface is always zero — a statement of Gauss's law for magnetism, reflecting that isolated magnetic poles do not exist.

Faraday's law

A changing magnetic flux through a circuit induces an electromotive force in it, as described by Faraday's law of induction. This principle underlies electric generators, transformers, and induction motors. The flux is quantised in superconductors in units of the magnetic flux quantum.
