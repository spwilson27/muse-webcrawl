Internet Assigned Numbers Authority

AbbreviationIANA
Founded1988
OperatorPTI, an affiliate of ICANN
PurposeGlobal coordination of DNS root, IP addressing, and protocol parameters

The Internet Assigned Numbers Authority (IANA) is responsible for the global coordination of the Domain Name System root zone, Internet Protocol addressing, and other Internet protocol resources. Its functions include allocating IP address blocks to the five regional Internet registries, maintaining the DNS root zone, and administering protocol parameter registries used by Internet standards.

History

IANA's functions were originally performed under U.S. government contracts, largely by Jon Postel at the University of Southern California's Information Sciences Institute. In 1998 the newly formed ICANN took over the IANA functions contract. Since 2016, following the IANA stewardship transition, the functions have been performed by Public Technical Identifiers (PTI), an affiliate of ICANN, without direct U.S. government oversight.

Responsibilities

IANA maintains registries of IP addresses, autonomous system numbers, top-level domains, port numbers, and protocol parameters. It also manages the .int top-level domain and the time zone database.
