Reformed Christianity

Reformed Christianity, also called Calvinism, is a major branch of Protestantism that began in the 16th-century Reformation under Huldrych Zwingli in Zurich and John Calvin in Geneva. Reformed churches emphasise the sovereignty of God, salvation by grace through faith, the authority of Scripture, and covenant theology.

Distinctive doctrines are often summarised by the five points of Calvinism (TULIP): total depravity, unconditional election, limited atonement, irresistible grace and perseverance of the saints. Church government is typically presbyterian or congregational, worship historically simple and centred on preaching.

Reformed traditions include Presbyterian, Dutch Reformed, Swiss Reformed, Huguenot and many Congregational and Baptist churches, with confessions such as the Westminster Confession and Heidelberg Catechism. See also John Calvin and Reformation.
