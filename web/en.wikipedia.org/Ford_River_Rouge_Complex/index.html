Ford River Rouge Complex

Location: Dearborn, Michigan, United States

Construction started: 1917

Architect: Albert Kahn

Owner: Ford Motor Company

The Ford River Rouge Complex (commonly The Rouge) is a Ford Motor Company automobile factory complex located in Dearborn, Michigan, along the River Rouge. Construction began in 1917, and when it was completed in 1928, it was the largest integrated factory in the world, roughly 1.5 miles (2.4 km) wide by 1 mile (1.6 km) long, including 93 buildings with 16 million square feet of factory floor space.

Vertical integration

The Rouge epitomised Henry Ford's vision of vertical integration: raw materials — iron ore, coal, limestone, and rubber — arrived by ship and rail at one end, and finished automobiles rolled out the other. The complex contained its own steel mills, foundries, glass plant, cement plant, power house, and a fleet of ore boats and railroad locomotives. At its peak in the 1930s it employed around 100,000 workers.

History

Initially the complex built Eagle boats for the Navy during the First World War, then Fordson tractors, and from 1927 the Model A. During the Second World War it produced aircraft engines, armoured vehicles, and military trucks. The Rouge was also a landmark in labour history: the 1937 "Battle of the Overpass", in which Ford security staff beat union organisers, became a famous episode in the United Auto Workers' organising campaign, and the plant was unionised in 1941.

Modern era

In the late 1990s and early 2000s Ford redeveloped part of the site, building the Dearborn Truck Plant with one of the world's largest living roofs, designed by William McDonough + Partners as part of an environmental revitalisation of the site. The Rouge now produces the Ford F-150, including the electric F-150 Lightning, and public factory tours run from The Henry Ford museum complex.
