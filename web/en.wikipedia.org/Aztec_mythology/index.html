Aztec mythology

[Image placeholder: Aztec gods as depicted in a colonial codex]

Aztec mythology is the body of myths and religious beliefs of the Mexica people of central Mexico, developed from earlier Mesoamerican traditions including those of the Toltecs and the peoples of Teotihuacan. It is known from pre-conquest pictorial codices and from post-conquest sources such as the Florentine Codex compiled under Bernardino de Sahagún.

Cosmology

Aztec cosmology divided the universe into thirteen heavens and nine underworlds, with the earth suspended between them. Time moved in cycles: according to the Legend of the Suns, four previous worlds had each been destroyed by catastrophe, and the present world — the Fifth Sun — would end by earthquake. The 52-year calendar round governed ritual life, and the New Fire ceremony at its close renewed the world.

Major deities

The Mexica pantheon included hundreds of gods. Huitzilopochtli, the tribal god of the Mexica and god of war and the sun, was worshipped at the Templo Mayor alongside Tlaloc, the ancient rain god. Quetzalcoatl, the feathered serpent, was god of wind, learning and creation; Tezcatlipoca, the "Smoking Mirror", was a capricious god of night and sorcery, Quetzalcoatl's rival. Other major figures include Coatlicue, mother of Huitzilopochtli; Xipe Totec, god of renewal; and Mictlantecuhtli, lord of the dead.

Sacrifice

Human sacrifice was central to Aztec religion: the sun was believed to require nourishment in the form of blood (chalchihuatl, "precious water") to keep moving across the sky and to forestall the end of the world. Victims, often war captives, were sacrificed at the Templo Mayor by heart extraction; other rites involved flaying, drowning (for Tlaloc, often children) and gladiatorial combat. The scale of the sacrifices remains debated among scholars.

Afterlife

The destination of the dead depended on the manner of death rather than moral conduct: warriors and sacrificial victims accompanied the sun; those who died by water went to Tlalocan, a paradise of the rain god; most others undertook a four-year journey through the nine levels of Mictlan, the underworld.
