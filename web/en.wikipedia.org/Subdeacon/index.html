Subdeacon

A subdeacon is a minor cleric in some branches of Christianity. The subdiaconate is a minor order or ministry below the deacon. A subdeacon assists the deacon and, in the Eastern Orthodox Church and Eastern Catholic Churches, has a specific set of duties at the divine liturgy: he tends to the altar vessels, dresses the bishop, holds the dikerion and trikerion (episcopal candlesticks), presents the Book of the Gospels, and attends at the Great Entrance. Subdeacons are vested in a sticharion with an orarion worn crossed over it.

Western practice

In the Latin Church, the subdiaconate was long counted among the major ("sacred") orders, with duties at solemn Mass — reading the Epistle, presenting the chalice and paten, and pouring the water into the chalice — and obligations of clerical celibacy and the Liturgy of the Hours. Pope Paul VI's motu proprio Ministeria quaedam (1972) suppressed the subdiaconate in the ordinary form of the Roman Rite, entrusting its functions to instituted lectors and acolytes; it continues in some traditionalist communities and in the Eastern Churches, where men may be ordained subdeacon while married.

History

The order is attested from the third century, appearing in the letters of Cyprian of Carthage and in Pope Cornelius's list of Roman clergy (251 AD), where subdeacons already ranked below deacons and above acolytes. In Anglican usage the role survives as a lay liturgical function at solemn services in some parishes.
