Antigua and Barbuda is a sovereign island country in the Caribbean lying between the Atlantic Ocean and the Caribbean Sea. It consists of two major islands, Antigua and Barbuda, together with smaller islands including the uninhabited Redonda. The capital and largest port is St. John's on Antigua.

History

The islands were inhabited by Arawak and later Carib peoples before European contact. Antigua was colonised by English settlers from 1632 and developed a sugar-plantation economy worked by enslaved Africans; Barbuda was leased to the Codrington family. Slavery was abolished in 1834. After a period as part of the Leeward Islands colony and the short-lived West Indies Federation, Antigua became an associated state in 1967 and achieved full independence on 1 November 1981, remaining a Commonwealth realm.

Geography and economy

Antigua is a low-lying limestone and volcanic island famous for its many beaches, while Barbuda is a flat coral island whose population was evacuated after Hurricane Irma devastated it in September 2017. Tourism dominates the economy, supplemented by financial services and agriculture.
