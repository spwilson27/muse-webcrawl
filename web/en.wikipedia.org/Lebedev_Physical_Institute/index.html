Lebedev Physical Institute

The Lebedev Physical Institute of the Russian Academy of Sciences (FIAN, from Fizicheskii Institut Akademii Nauk) is a research institute in Moscow, Russia, and one of the country's leading physics institutes. Founded in 1934 under the directorship of Sergey Vavilov, it traces its origins to a physics laboratory established in St Petersburg in the 18th century and is named after the physicist Pyotr Lebedev.

Research at the institute has included the discovery of Cherenkov radiation, foundational work on lasers and masers, cosmic rays, plasma physics, astrophysics and quantum electronics. Seven Nobel laureates have been associated with FIAN, among them Pavel Cherenkov, Ilya Frank, Igor Tamm, Nikolay Basov, Alexander Prokhorov, Vitaly Ginzburg and Andrei Sakharov, who worked there on the Soviet hydrogen bomb project and later on fundamental physics.

The institute comprises numerous specialised laboratories and observatories, including the Pushchino Radio Astronomy Observatory, and publishes leading journals in physics.
