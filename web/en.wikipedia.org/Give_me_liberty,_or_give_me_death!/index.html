"Give me liberty, or give me death!" is a quotation attributed to American politician and orator Patrick Henry, from a speech he made to the Second Virginia Convention on 23 March 1775, at St. John's Church in Richmond, Virginia. Henry is credited with having argued in favour of mobilising Virginia's militia against the British, and his speech is said to have swung the convention toward committing troops to the American Revolutionary War.

The speech and its text

No contemporary transcript of the speech survives. The famous text was reconstructed decades later by William Wirt in his 1817 biography of Henry, drawing on the recollections of witnesses. The speech, as reconstructed, builds through a review of failed petitions to the Crown to its peroration: "I know not what course others may take; but as for me, give me liberty or give me death!" Scholars note that the wording may owe something to Wirt's literary craft, though the speech's galvanising effect on the convention is well attested: the resolutions to arm the militia passed.

Legacy

The phrase became one of the most famous slogans of the American Revolution and a durable expression of resistance to tyranny. Variants have been adopted by independence and protest movements around the world, and the words appear in numerous later political speeches, songs and works of art. St. John's Church, where the convention met, is preserved as a historic site in Richmond.
