Native American Church

The Native American Church (NAC), also known as Peyotism and the Peyote Religion, is a syncretic Native American religion that combines traditional Indigenous beliefs with elements of Christianity. Its central sacrament is the peyote cactus (Lophophora williamsii), which contains the psychoactive alkaloid mescaline and is consumed in all-night prayer ceremonies.

History

Peyote use in ritual contexts is ancient in Mexico and the American Southwest, but the modern church took shape in the late 19th century among peoples of the southern Plains, including the Comanche, Kiowa and Apache. Leaders such as the Comanche chief Quanah Parker helped spread the peyote rite northward as Indigenous communities sought spiritual resilience amid forced relocation and cultural suppression. The Native American Church of Oklahoma was formally incorporated in 1918 to protect the practice under religious freedom guarantees.

Ceremony

A typical meeting is held in a tipi from sundown to sunrise, led by a "roadman" and attended by a fireman and cedarman. Participants sit in a circle around a crescent-shaped earthen altar and a sacred fire, singing peyote songs accompanied by a water drum and gourd rattle, praying, and eating or drinking peyote at intervals. Ceremonies may be held for healing, thanksgiving, birthdays or other occasions, and often conclude with a ritual breakfast.

Beliefs

Practitioners follow the "Peyote Road" — a moral code emphasising brotherly love, honesty, care for family, abstinence from alcohol and self-reliance. Many members also participate in Christian worship; others follow traditional tribal religions alongside the church.

Legal status

After decades of state-level restrictions, the American Indian Religious Freedom Act Amendments of 1994 expressly protected the sacramental use of peyote by members of federally recognised tribes nationwide. The church is among the largest Indigenous religious organisations in the United States, with chapters across many states and into Canada and Mexico.
