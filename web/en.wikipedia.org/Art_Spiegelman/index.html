Art Spiegelman (born Itzhak Avraham ben Zeev Spiegelman; February 15, 1948) is an American cartoonist, editor, and comics advocate best known for his graphic novel Maus. His work as co-editor on the comics magazines Arcade and Raw has been influential. From 1992 to 2001 he spent a decade as contributing artist for The New Yorker, where his iconic covers frequently made powerful political statements.

Maus

Maus (serialized 1980–1991) recounts Spiegelman's father's experiences as a Polish Jew and Holocaust survivor, interweaving them with the author's own troubled relationship with his father. It depicts Jews as mice, Germans as cats, and Poles as pigs. In 1992 Maus became the first graphic novel to win a Pulitzer Prize (in the Letters category, receiving a special award), and it is widely credited with establishing the literary legitimacy of the graphic novel. In 2022, a Tennessee school board's decision to remove Maus from its curriculum drew national attention and propelled the book back onto bestseller lists.

Career

Born in Stockholm to Polish-Jewish Holocaust survivors, Spiegelman emigrated with his family to the United States as a small child and grew up in Queens, New York. He began his career in the underground comix movement of the early 1970s, contributed to Wacky Packages and Garbage Pail Kids at Topps, and with his wife Françoise Mouly founded the avant-garde comics anthology Raw (1980–1991), which introduced many European and American alternative cartoonists. His later works include In the Shadow of No Towers (2004), a response to the September 11 attacks, and Breakdowns (2008), a reissue and memoir built around his early experimental strips.
