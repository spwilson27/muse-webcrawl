Colonization of the Moon

The colonization of the Moon is the proposed establishment of permanent human settlements on the Moon. Long a staple of science fiction, lunar settlement became a serious engineering topic after the Apollo landings of 1969–1972 and has returned to prominence with programs such as NASA's Artemis and China's lunar exploration plans.

Rationale

Proposed benefits include scientific research, a proving ground for Mars missions, tourism, and the use of local resources (in-situ resource utilization), such as water ice in permanently shadowed polar craters, oxygen from regolith, and potentially helium-3 as a fusion fuel.

Challenges

Settlers would face intense radiation, micrometeorite impacts, abrasive lunar dust, extreme temperature swings, and the health effects of one-sixth gravity. Habitats are often proposed to be buried under regolith or built inside lava tubes for shielding. Supply costs from Earth remain the largest economic obstacle.

Current plans

The Artemis program aims to return astronauts to the lunar surface and build a sustainable presence near the south pole, supported by the Lunar Gateway station. The International Lunar Research Station, led by China and Russia, is a parallel initiative. Legal questions are governed by the Outer Space Treaty, which bars national appropriation of the Moon.
