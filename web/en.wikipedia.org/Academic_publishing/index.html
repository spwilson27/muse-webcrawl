Academic publishing

Academic publishing is the subfield of publishing that distributes academic research and scholarship. Most academic work is published in peer-reviewed journals, academic books, theses, conference proceedings and, increasingly, open repositories and preprint servers. The system functions to disseminate findings, register priority, certify quality through peer review, and preserve the scholarly record, while also underpinning careers through the imperative summarised as "publish or perish".

History and structure

The first scientific journals, the Journal des sçavans and the Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society, both appeared in 1665. Scholarly publishing expanded enormously after the Second World War, and commercial publishers such as Elsevier, Springer Nature, Wiley, Taylor & Francis and SAGE came to dominate journal publishing, alongside university presses and learned societies. Peer review — evaluation of manuscripts by independent experts — became the standard quality-control mechanism in the twentieth century, though its forms (single-blind, double-blind, open) and effectiveness remain debated.

Business models and open access

Traditionally readers or libraries paid subscriptions, giving rise to the "serials crisis" as prices outpaced library budgets. The open access movement seeks free public access to research: "gold" open access funded by article processing charges, "green" self-archiving in repositories, and funder mandates such as Plan S (2018). Criticisms of the system include high publisher profit margins, the unpaid labour of authors and reviewers, predatory journals that charge fees without genuine review, and biases in citation and prestige metrics such as the impact factor.
