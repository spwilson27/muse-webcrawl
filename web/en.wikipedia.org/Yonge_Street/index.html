Yonge Street (pronounced "young") is a major arterial road in Ontario, Canada, running north from the shore of Lake Ontario in Toronto to the city of Barrie on Lake Simcoe, a distance of about 86 kilometres. It was formerly a major section of provincial Highway 11 and was long (and incorrectly) promoted as "the longest street in the world", a claim once recognised by Guinness World Records before the designation was corrected.

History

The road was laid out in 1794–1796 by John Graves Simcoe, lieutenant governor of Upper Canada, who named it for his friend Sir George Yonge, the British secretary of war. Built partly by settlers as a condition of their land grants and partly by soldiers, it followed an Indigenous portage route between Lake Ontario and the upper Great Lakes and served a military purpose during the War of 1812.

The street today

In Toronto, Yonge Street divides the city's east from west — street addresses count east and west from it — and its southern end at Queens Quay is often cited as the city's main street. Major landmarks along it include Yonge–Dundas Square, the Eaton Centre, and the Hockey Hall of Fame. The Yonge subway line (Line 1) runs beneath it through much of the city.
