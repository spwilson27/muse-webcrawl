The European storm petrel, British storm petrel, or just storm petrel (Hydrobates pelagicus) is a small seabird of the northern storm petrel family Hydrobatidae. It is the only member of its genus found in the Atlantic, breeding on islands off the coasts of western Europe — principally in Iceland, the Faroe Islands, Ireland, and western Britain — and wintering at sea as far south as South Africa.

Description

At 14–18 cm in length with a wingspan of 36–39 cm and a weight of about 23–29 g, the European storm petrel is one of the smallest seabirds in the world. It has dark, sooty-brown plumage with a conspicuous white rump extending onto the flanks, a white band on the underwing, and a fluttering, bat-like flight, often pattering its feet on the sea surface. The name "petrel" is traditionally derived from St. Peter, for the birds' apparent walking on water. Sailors called them "Mother Carey's chickens" and considered them harbingers of storms.

Behaviour and breeding

Entirely pelagic outside the breeding season, the species feeds on surface plankton, small fish, squid, and offal, locating food by smell. It breeds colonially on predator-free islands, nesting in burrows and rock crevices; a single white egg is incubated by both parents for about 40 days, and the chick is fed at night on regurgitated oily food. Adults return to colonies only after dark to avoid gulls and skuas. Colonies such as those on Skokholm (Wales), Mousa (Shetland) and Nólsoy (Faroes) host many thousands of pairs; the world population has been estimated in the hundreds of thousands of pairs, the great majority in the northeast Atlantic. Storm petrels are long-lived for their size, with individuals recorded beyond 30 years.
