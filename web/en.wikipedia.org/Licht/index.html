Licht ("Light"), subtitled Die sieben Tage der Woche ("The Seven Days of the Week"), is a cycle of seven operas by the German composer Karlheinz Stockhausen (1928–2007), composed over 26 years between 1977 and 2003. Together the operas run to roughly 29 hours of music, making Licht one of the most ambitious works in the history of Western music.

The cycle

Each opera is named for a day of the week and focuses on combinations of the cycle's three central characters: Michael (the archangel, associated with humanity and the cosmos), Eve (the mother figure, associated with birth and nature) and Lucifer (associated with intellect and rebellion). The operas are Donnerstag (Thursday, 1981), Samstag (Saturday, 1984), Montag (Monday, 1988), Dienstag (Tuesday, 1993), Freitag (Friday, 1996), Mittwoch (Wednesday, 2012 premiere) and Sonntag (Sunday, 2011 premiere).

Musical structure

The entire cycle is generated from a "super-formula" (Superformel): three interwoven melodies representing Michael, Eve and Lucifer, which are expanded to form the structure of each opera and of individual scenes. Many components can also be performed independently as concert pieces. The cycle is notorious for spectacular theatrical demands: Mittwoch includes the Helikopter-Streichquartett (Helicopter String Quartet), in which the four players perform in separate helicopters flying above the hall, their sound and image transmitted to the audience.

Performance history

Individual operas were premiered at La Scala, the Leipzig Opera and other houses during Stockhausen's lifetime, but the complete cycle was not staged in his lifetime. A landmark production of all seven operas was mounted in Amsterdam in 2019 by De Nationale Opera in collaboration with the Royal Conservatoire of The Hague and the Stockhausen Foundation.
