Boston Post Road

Route: New York City – Boston
Established: Late 17th century
Now follows: Mainly U.S. Route 1
Purpose: Colonial mail and travel

The Boston Post Road was a system of mail delivery routes between New York City and Boston, Massachusetts, that evolved into one of the first major highways in the United States. The route largely followed earlier fur-trading and Native American trails, including the Pequot Trail.

History

The first organised post along the route began in 1673 under Governor Francis Lovelace of New York. Three branches — the Upper, Middle and Lower Post Roads — developed over time, the upper road passing through Springfield and the lower road along the Connecticut and Rhode Island shoreline.

Milestones and travel

Milestones placed by figures such as Benjamin Franklin and Paul Dudley still survive along the route. During the American Revolutionary War the road was a vital communications artery. Today much of the route is followed by U.S. Route 1 and local roads bearing the name Boston Post Road in Connecticut and elsewhere.
