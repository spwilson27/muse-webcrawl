Alcaeus of Mytilene

Alcaeus of Mytilene (c. 625 – c. 580 BC) was an ancient Greek lyric poet from Mytilene on the island of Lesbos. He is credited with inventing the Alcaic stanza, a verse form later perfected in Latin by Horace. Alongside his contemporary Sappho, Alcaeus was canonised by Alexandrian scholars among the Nine Lyric Poets.

Life

Alcaeus belonged to the aristocratic faction of Mytilene during a period of violent struggle between the city's leading families and a series of tyrants, including Melanchrus, Myrsilus and Pittacus. He fought — and by his own famous admission, fled, abandoning his shield — in the war against Athens for Sigeum. After the ascendancy of Pittacus, Alcaeus was exiled and spent time in Egypt, though he was later pardoned and returned to Lesbos.

Poetry

Alcaeus wrote in the Aeolic dialect across many genres: hymns to the gods, political or factional songs (stasiotika), drinking songs (skolia), and love poems — including poems addressed to Sappho. His most famous image, the "ship of state" allegory, was imitated by Horace. His work survives only in fragments quoted by later authors and on papyri.

Legacy

Horace adapted the Alcaic stanza to Latin and described himself as Alcaeus's heir. Fragments of Alcaeus remain central to the study of archaic Greek lyric and the social world of seventh- and sixth-century Lesbos.
