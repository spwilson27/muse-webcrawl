Lúcia de Jesus Rosa dos Santos (28 March 1907 – 13 February 2005), known as Sister Lúcia, was a Portuguese Catholic nun and one of the three children who reported witnessing apparitions of the Virgin Mary at Fátima in 1917. Unlike her cousins Francisco and Jacinta Marto, who died young in the influenza pandemic and were later canonised, Lúcia lived to the age of 97 and became the principal witness and chronicler of the Fátima events.

The Fátima apparitions

Between May and October 1917, Lúcia, aged ten, and her younger cousins reported six apparitions of a lady identified as Our Lady of the Rosary at Cova da Iria near Fátima. The events culminated in the "Miracle of the Sun" on 13 October 1917, witnessed by a large crowd. Lúcia said the Virgin entrusted the children with three secrets, which she recorded in memoirs written in the 1930s and 1940s; the third secret was kept sealed by the Vatican until its release in 2000.

Religious life

After the apparitions, Lúcia was educated by the Sisters of St Dorothy at Vilar, near Porto, taking the name Maria das Dores. In 1948 she transferred to the Discalced Carmelite convent of Santa Teresa in Coimbra, where she lived in enclosure for the rest of her life as Sister Maria Lúcia of Jesus and the Immaculate Heart. She received several popes as visitors and corresponded about the interpretation of the Fátima message, including the consecration of Russia.

Death and beatification cause

Sister Lúcia died on 13 February 2005. Her cause for beatification was opened in 2008 after the customary waiting period was waived by Pope Benedict XVI; she was declared Venerable by Pope Francis in 2023. Her memoirs remain the primary source for the Fátima apparitions and the associated devotions of the Five First Saturdays.
