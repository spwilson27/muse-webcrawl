Natural parks in Japan

Natural parks in Japan are protected areas designated under the Natural Parks Act of 1957 (successor to the National Parks Act of 1931). The system comprises three tiers — national parks, quasi-national parks, and prefectural natural parks — which together cover roughly 14% of Japan's land area.

National parks

Japan has 34 national parks, designated by the Ministry of the Environment as outstanding natural landscapes representative of the country. The first parks, Setonaikai, Unzen, and Kirishima, were established in 1934. Famous examples include Fuji-Hakone-Izu (containing Mount Fuji), Shiretoko in Hokkaidō — a UNESCO World Heritage Site — Akan Mashu, Nikko, Ise-Shima, and Yakushima. Unlike the North American model, Japanese national parks include extensive private and inhabited land, and are managed through zoning that regulates development rather than excluding residents.

Quasi-national and prefectural parks

Quasi-national parks (57 areas) are designated by the national government but managed by prefectures, protecting landscapes of slightly lesser, though still nationally significant, quality. Prefectural natural parks (more than 300) are designated and managed by prefectural governments.

Administration

The Ministry of the Environment oversees the system, employing rangers known as "active rangers" for site management. The parks protect volcanoes, coastlines, coral reefs, forests, and wetlands, including several Ramsar sites, and are central to domestic tourism.
