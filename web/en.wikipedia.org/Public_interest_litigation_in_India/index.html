Public interest litigation in India

Public interest litigation (PIL) in India is litigation introduced in a court of law for the protection of the public interest, in which any public-spirited person may seek remedy on behalf of the poor, marginalised or disadvantaged, even without being personally affected. It is a distinctive feature of Indian jurisprudence developed by the Supreme Court and High Courts from the late 1970s onward under Articles 32 and 226 of the Constitution.

Development

Judges such as P. N. Bhagwati and V. R. Krishna Iyer relaxed the traditional rule of locus standi, holding that anyone acting bona fide could approach the court for the enforcement of fundamental rights of those unable to do so themselves. Landmark cases addressed prisoners' rights (Hussainara Khatoon), bonded labour, environmental protection (M. C. Mehta cases) and workplace sexual harassment (Vishaka).

Criticism

PIL has been praised for expanding access to justice, but criticised for judicial overreach, frivolous "publicity interest" petitions, and courts administering policy. The Supreme Court has issued guidelines to filter petitions and imposed costs for abuse of the process.
