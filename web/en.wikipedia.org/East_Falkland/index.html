East Falkland

East Falkland (Spanish: Isla Soledad) is the largest island of the Falkland Islands in the South Atlantic Ocean, with an area of about 6,605 square kilometres — just over half the total land area of the archipelago. It is separated from West Falkland by the Falkland Sound.

Geography

The island is mountainous in the north, where the Wickham Heights include Mount Usborne, the highest point in the Falklands at 705 metres. The southern half, known as Lafonia, is lower and flatter. The capital and largest settlement, Stanley, lies in the northeast, and Mount Pleasant Airport, the islands' international airport and a Royal Air Force station, is in the south.

History

East Falkland hosts the islands' oldest settlement sites, including Port Louis, founded by the French in 1764. During the Falklands War of 1982, Argentine forces occupied the island and major battles were fought around Goose Green, San Carlos, and the mountains west of Stanley. Most of the island's population and sheep farming are concentrated here.
