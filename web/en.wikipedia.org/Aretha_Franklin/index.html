Aretha Louise Franklin (March 25, 1942 – August 16, 2018) was an American singer, songwriter and pianist. Referred to as the "Queen of Soul", Rolling Stone twice named her the greatest singer of all time. With global sales of more than 75 million records, Franklin is among the world's best-selling music artists.

Early life

Franklin was born in Memphis, Tennessee, the daughter of Barbara Siggers and the Baptist preacher and civil rights activist C. L. Franklin, and was raised in Detroit, Michigan. She began her career as a child, singing gospel at New Bethel Baptist Church in Detroit, where her father was minister, and recorded her first album, Songs of Faith, at age 14.

Career

After signing with Columbia Records in 1960, Franklin achieved modest success; her breakthrough came after signing with Atlantic Records in 1966. Produced by Jerry Wexler and recorded with the Muscle Shoals rhythm section, hits such as "I Never Loved a Man (The Way I Love You)", "Respect", "(You Make Me Feel Like) A Natural Woman", "Chain of Fools", "Think" and "I Say a Little Prayer" made her a defining voice of the civil rights and women's movements. Her 1967 recording of Otis Redding's "Respect" became her signature song and an anthem of empowerment.

Franklin recorded 112 charted singles on the US Billboard charts — the most of any female artist — including 77 Hot 100 entries and 20 number-one R&B singles. Albums such as I Never Loved a Man the Way I Love You, Lady Soul, Young, Gifted and Black and the live gospel album Amazing Grace (1972), the best-selling live gospel album of all time, cemented her reputation. In the 1980s she returned to prominence with Who's Zoomin' Who? and a duet with George Michael, "I Knew You Were Waiting (For Me)".

Honours

Franklin won 18 Grammy Awards. She was the first woman inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame (1987), and also entered the Gospel Music Hall of Fame and the National Women's Hall of Fame. She received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2005 and, posthumously, a Pulitzer Prize special citation in 2019. She performed at the inaugurations of three US presidents: Jimmy Carter, Bill Clinton and Barack Obama.

Death

Franklin died of pancreatic neuroendocrine cancer at her home in Detroit on August 16, 2018, aged 76. Her funeral at Greater Grace Temple was attended by numerous dignitaries and performers, and she was widely mourned as one of the greatest vocalists in American music history.
