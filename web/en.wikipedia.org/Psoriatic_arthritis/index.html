Psoriatic arthritis

Psoriatic arthritis (PsA) is a chronic inflammatory arthritis associated with the skin disease psoriasis. It affects roughly 20–30% of people with psoriasis, usually appearing years after the skin disease, though in some cases joint symptoms come first. It is classified among the seronegative spondyloarthropathies and is typically rheumatoid-factor negative.

Signs and patterns

Five patterns are classically described: asymmetric oligoarthritis, symmetric polyarthritis resembling rheumatoid arthritis, distal interphalangeal-predominant arthritis, arthritis mutilans (a rare destructive form), and axial spondylitis-like disease. Characteristic features include dactylitis ("sausage digits"), enthesitis (inflammation where tendons attach to bone), and nail dystrophy such as pitting and onycholysis. The CASPAR criteria are widely used for classification.

Treatment

Management is guided by severity and domain involvement. NSAIDs and physiotherapy help mild disease; conventional DMARDs such as methotrexate treat skin and peripheral joints, while biologic therapies targeting TNF, interleukin-17, or interleukin-23, and targeted synthetic drugs such as JAK inhibitors, are used for more severe or refractory cases.
