The Hubble Space Telescope (often referred to as HST or Hubble) is a space telescope that was launched into low Earth orbit in 1990 and remains in operation. It was not the first space telescope, but it is one of the largest and most versatile, renowned both as a vital research tool and as a public-relations boon for astronomy. The Hubble telescope is named after astronomer Edwin Hubble and is one of NASA's Great Observatories. The Space Telescope Science Institute (STScI) selects Hubble's targets and processes the resulting data, while the Goddard Space Flight Center controls the spacecraft.

History

Opting for a large reflecting telescope in space was first proposed by Lyman Spitzer in 1946. After decades of advocacy, NASA and the European Space Agency developed the telescope during the 1970s and 1980s. Hubble was built by Lockheed, with its 2.4-metre primary mirror polished by Perkin-Elmer. It was launched aboard the Space Shuttle Discovery on 24 April 1990 and deployed the following day.

Flawed mirror and repair

Within weeks of launch, images revealed a serious problem: the primary mirror had been ground to the wrong shape, suffering from spherical aberration of about 2.2 micrometres at its edge. Although the images were still better than those from ground-based telescopes, the flaw prevented the sharpest observations. In December 1993, the first servicing mission (STS-61, aboard Endeavour) installed the COSTAR corrective optics package, restoring Hubble's vision to its intended quality. Four further servicing missions followed, the last in 2009 (STS-125, Atlantis), which installed the Wide Field Camera 3 and the Cosmic Origins Spectrograph and replaced gyroscopes and batteries.

Discoveries

Hubble has helped resolve long-standing problems in astronomy and produced breakthroughs including: precise determination of the rate of expansion of the universe and evidence for its acceleration; the Hubble Deep Field and Ultra-Deep Field images, revealing thousands of distant galaxies; evidence that supermassive black holes are common in galaxy centres; measurements of the atmospheres of exoplanets; and identification of the first organic molecule in an exoplanet atmosphere. Its images, including the "Pillars of Creation" in the Eagle Nebula, have become iconic.

Instruments

Current instruments include the Advanced Camera for Surveys (ACS), Wide Field Camera 3 (WFC3), Cosmic Origins Spectrograph (COS), Space Telescope Imaging Spectrograph (STIS), and Fine Guidance Sensors. Hubble observes primarily in visible and ultraviolet light, with some near-infrared capability.

Future

Hubble continues to operate alongside the James Webb Space Telescope, launched in 2021, which observes mainly in the infrared. Without further servicing, Hubble's orbit will decay and it is expected to re-enter the atmosphere sometime between the 2030s and 2040s.
