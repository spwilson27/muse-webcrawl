Peripheral membrane protein

Peripheral membrane proteins associate reversibly with the surface of the lipid bilayer or with integral membrane proteins.

Peripheral membrane proteins, or extrinsic membrane proteins, are membrane proteins that adhere only temporarily to the biological membrane with which they are associated. These proteins attach either to integral membrane proteins or penetrate the peripheral regions of the lipid bilayer. Unlike integral membrane proteins, which span the membrane, peripheral proteins interact with membranes through non-covalent forces such as electrostatic interactions and hydrogen bonding, or through covalently attached lipid anchors.

Binding mechanisms

Peripheral membrane proteins associate with membranes by several distinct mechanisms:

  • Electrostatic interactions with anionic membrane lipids such as phosphatidylserine
  • Amphipathic helices that lie parallel to the membrane surface, inserting hydrophobic residues shallowly into one leaflet
  • Lipid anchors, including prenyl, myristoyl, and palmitoyl groups, or GPI anchors
  • Protein–protein interactions with integral membrane proteins
  • Membrane-targeting domains such as PH, C1, C2, PX, and FYVE domains that recognize specific lipids

Reversibility and regulation

The reversible attachment of peripheral proteins allows cells to control their localization in response to signals. Many signaling proteins, including protein kinase C, phospholipase C, and small GTPases of the Ras family, cycle between the cytosol and membrane surfaces. Changes in local pH, calcium concentration, lipid composition, or the covalent addition and removal of lipid anchors regulate this recruitment. Because their binding relies on relatively weak interactions, peripheral proteins can typically be released from membranes by treatment with high salt, alkaline buffers, or chelating agents, without dissolving the bilayer.

Examples

Examples include cytochrome c, which associates with the inner mitochondrial membrane; lactadherin; annexins; spectrin and other cytoskeletal proteins at the plasma membrane; and numerous enzymes of signal transduction pathways.
