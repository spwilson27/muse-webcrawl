The Living Wage Foundation is a United Kingdom organisation, launched in 2011 by Citizens UK, that campaigns for employers to pay a voluntary hourly wage rate—the "real Living Wage"—calculated according to the actual cost of living rather than the statutory minimum. It accredits employers who commit to paying the rate to their directly employed staff and regular contract workers.

History

The Foundation grew out of the Living Wage Campaign begun by Citizens UK community organisers in East London in 2001. Early victories included campaigns at London hospitals and banks in Canary Wharf. In 2005 the Greater London Authority began calculating an official London living wage, and in 2011 the Living Wage Foundation was established to own the accreditation mark and oversee the rate across the UK. In 2016 the UK government rebranded its increased minimum wage for workers aged 25 and over (later 21+) as the "National Living Wage"; the Foundation distinguishes its higher, independently calculated figure as the "real Living Wage."

The rates

Two rates are announced each autumn: one for London, reflecting higher housing and transport costs, and one for the rest of the UK. The calculations draw on the Minimum Income Standard research produced by the Centre for Research in Social Policy at Loughborough University, overseen by the Resolution Foundation since 2016. Rates in the mid-2020s stood at roughly £13–14 per hour across the UK and about £15 in London; accredited employers are expected to implement each new rate within six months of its announcement.

Accreditation and reach

By the mid-2020s more than 14,000 employers had been accredited, including around half of the FTSE 100, major employers such as IKEA, Aviva, Everton Football Club, and thousands of small businesses, charities, and local authorities. The Foundation also runs related initiatives including Living Hours accreditation, which addresses insecure scheduling and guaranteed minimum hours, and has licensed sister living-wage movements in other countries.
