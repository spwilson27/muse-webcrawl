In numerical analysis, a B-spline (short for basis spline) is a piecewise polynomial function defined over intervals delimited by a knot vector, with minimal support for a given degree and smoothness. The B-splines of a given degree form a basis for all spline functions on that knot vector, so any spline curve can be written as a weighted sum of B-spline basis functions with control points as weights.

Definition and properties

Basis functions are computed recursively by the Cox–de Boor formula: degree-zero functions are indicator functions of knot intervals, and higher-degree functions are convex combinations of lower-degree ones. A degree-k B-spline is non-zero on at most k+1 knot intervals, giving local control — moving one control point affects only a small part of the curve. B-splines of degree 3 (cubic) are the most widely used, balancing smoothness with computational simplicity; their basis functions are bell-shaped, non-negative and sum to one.

Applications

B-spline curves and surfaces are a standard representation in computer-aided design, computer graphics and animation, where they model smooth shapes stably and efficiently. Their rational extension, NURBS, can represent conic sections exactly. B-splines are also used in statistics for regression smoothing and in finite-element analysis.
