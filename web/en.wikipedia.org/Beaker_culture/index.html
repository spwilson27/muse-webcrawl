Beaker culture

Periodc. 2800–1800 BCE (Late Neolithic / Early Bronze Age)
RegionEurope, from Iberia to Central Europe and the British Isles
Named forDistinctive bell-shaped drinking vessels ("beakers")
Followed byEarly Bronze Age cultures (e.g. Wessex, Únětice successor horizons)

The Beaker culture (also Bell Beaker culture or Beaker folk) was an archaeological culture of western and central Europe in the late Neolithic and early Bronze Age, roughly 2800–1800 BCE. It is named after its characteristic inverted bell-shaped pottery drinking vessels, decorated with horizontal bands of impressed ornament, which are found in burials across an enormous area from Portugal and Morocco to Poland and Hungary, and prominently in Britain and Ireland.

A Bell Beaker, the type fossil of the culture.

Characteristics

The "Beaker package" characteristically includes the beakers themselves, copper daggers, archers' stone wrist-guards, barbed-and-tanged flint arrowheads, V-perforated buttons and early metalwork in copper and gold. Burials were typically single inhumations under round barrows, the body crouched, with men and women often laid on opposite sides according to regional custom — a marked change from the collective tombs of earlier Neolithic societies. Beakers are widely interpreted as drinking vessels, possibly associated with alcohol consumption and feasting; residues identified in some vessels suggest beer or mead.

Origins and spread

The earliest beakers of the "Maritime" style appear in the Tagus estuary region of Portugal around 2800 BCE. How the phenomenon then spread — by migration, by exchange of prestige goods and ideas, or both — was debated for a century. Ancient DNA studies in the 2010s transformed the question: they showed substantial population movement associated with the Beaker horizon in many regions. In Britain, the arrival of Beaker-using people after about 2450 BCE brought a replacement of roughly 90 per cent of the island's genetic ancestry within a few centuries, associated with the spread of steppe-related ancestry from continental Europe; in Iberia, by contrast, the spread involved much less population turnover initially.

Significance

The Beaker horizon coincides with the spread of early metallurgy, new burial individualism and possibly of Indo-European languages into western Europe. In Britain it is associated with the later phases of Stonehenge and rich burials such as the Amesbury Archer. Regional successor cultures of the Bronze Age developed from Beaker roots across Europe.
