Peterborough is a cathedral city in the City of Peterborough unitary authority in Cambridgeshire, England. It lies on the River Nene, about 75 miles (121 km) north of London. Historically part of Northamptonshire (the Soke of Peterborough), it became a new town in 1967 and expanded rapidly thereafter.

Peterborough Cathedral, one of the finest Norman cathedrals in England

The settlement grew around a Benedictine abbey founded in the seventh century. Peterborough Cathedral, built between 1118 and 1238, is celebrated for its unique English Gothic west front and its painted wooden nave ceiling. Catherine of Aragon, the first wife of Henry VIII, is buried in the cathedral. The arrival of the railway in the nineteenth century turned Peterborough into an important junction and industrial centre, long known for brick-making — the surrounding clay pits supplied much of London's building brick — and for diesel engine manufacturing by Perkins Engines.

History

The area has been inhabited since at least the Bronze Age; the Flag Fen archaeological site preserves a Bronze Age causeway and ritual landscape. The Anglo-Saxon settlement was called Medeshamstede. The abbey was destroyed by Viking raiders in 870 and rebuilt in the tenth century. After the Norman Conquest a great new church was begun, the present cathedral. The city suffered in the Dissolution of the Monasteries, though the abbey church survived as a cathedral.

Modern city

Designated a new town in 1967, Peterborough grew with overspill housing townships such as Bretton, Orton and Hampton, alongside the Queensgate shopping centre. It is a major rail hub on the East Coast Main Line, with fast services to London King's Cross in under an hour. The local football club, Peterborough United, plays at London Road. Nearby attractions include Burghley House, Nene Park and the Nene Valley Railway.
