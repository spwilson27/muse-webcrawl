Florence Cathedral

Florence Cathedral, formally the Cathedral of Saint Mary of the Flower (Italian: Cattedrale di Santa Maria del Fiore), is the cathedral of Florence, Italy. It was begun in 1296 in the Gothic style to a design of Arnolfo di Cambio and was structurally completed by 1436, with the dome engineered by Filippo Brunelleschi. The exterior of the basilica is faced with polychrome marble panels in various shades of green and pink, bordered by white, and has an elaborate 19th-century Gothic Revival façade by Emilio De Fabris.

History

The cathedral was built on the site of the earlier church of Santa Reparata. The ambitious design, begun under Arnolfo di Cambio, grew under successive architects — Giotto, who designed the campanile, Andrea Pisano and Francesco Talenti — into one of the largest churches in Christendom. Yet by the early 15th century the great crossing still stood open to the sky: no one knew how to build a dome of the required span, some 45 metres, without it collapsing.

Brunelleschi's dome

In 1418 the city held a competition for the dome, won by Filippo Brunelleschi. His solution — a double-shelled dome of brick laid in a self-supporting herringbone pattern, built without wooden centring — remains the largest masonry dome ever constructed. It was completed in 1436, and the cathedral was consecrated by Pope Eugene IV on 25 March of that year. The lantern was finished after Brunelleschi's death to his design.

Exterior and interior

The present marble façade dates only from 1871–1887, built to Emilio De Fabris's design after the original unfinished façade was demolished in the 16th century. Inside, the vast, austere space holds works including Paolo Uccello's equestrian monument to Sir John Hawkwood, Andrea del Castagno's monument to Niccolò da Tolentino, and Giorgio Vasari and Federico Zuccari's fresco of the Last Judgment inside the dome. Below the cathedral, excavations reveal the remains of Santa Reparata.
