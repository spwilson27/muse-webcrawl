Copernicus Programme

OperatorEuropean Commission / ESA
Established1998 (as GMES)
PurposeEarth observation
Satellite familySentinel

Copernicus is the Earth observation component of the European Union Space Programme, managed by the European Commission in partnership with the European Space Agency (ESA) and other partners. It offers information services based on satellite Earth observation and in situ (non-space) data, covering areas such as land, marine, atmosphere, climate change, emergency management and security.

History

The programme began in 1998 under the name GMES (Global Monitoring for Environment and Security) and was renamed Copernicus in 2012, after the astronomer Nicolaus Copernicus. Its dedicated satellite missions, the Sentinel family, began launching in 2014 with Sentinel-1A.

Sentinel missions

Each Sentinel mission is designed around a specific set of observations: Sentinel-1 provides radar imaging in all weather; Sentinel-2 provides high-resolution optical land imagery; Sentinel-3 measures ocean and land temperature and colour; Sentinel-4 and Sentinel-5 monitor atmospheric composition; and Sentinel-6 measures global sea level.

Data policy

Copernicus data and information are provided on a full, free and open basis, supporting applications in agriculture, disaster response, urban planning and environmental monitoring worldwide.
