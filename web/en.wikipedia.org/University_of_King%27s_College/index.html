University of King's College

Type: Public liberal arts university
Established: 1789
Location: Halifax, Nova Scotia, Canada
Students: ~1,200

The University of King's College is a public liberal arts university in Halifax, Nova Scotia, Canada. Founded in 1789 by Loyalists in Windsor, Nova Scotia — making it Canada's oldest chartered university — it moved to Halifax in 1923 after a fire destroyed its main building, and there entered into association with Dalhousie University, with which it shares a campus.

Academics

King's is best known for its Foundation Year Programme (FYP), a first-year interdisciplinary great-books course taken by most of its students, and for its School of Journalism, which offers undergraduate and graduate journalism degrees. Students take King's humanities programmes alongside Dalhousie science and social science courses, receiving jointly conferred degrees.

Traditions

A small residential college with an Oxbridge flavour, King's maintains college traditions including formal meals in Prince Hall, a quad designed on the Oxford model, and the student newspaper The Watch.
