Vitamin B12 deficiency

Vitamin B12 deficiency, also known as cobalamin deficiency, is the medical condition in which the blood and tissue have a lower than normal level of vitamin B12. Symptoms can vary from none to severe, and may include fatigue, weakness, megaloblastic anemia, glossitis, cognitive changes such as memory problems and confusion, and neurological damage — numbness and tingling in the hands and feet, gait disturbance, and in advanced cases spinal cord degeneration (subacute combined degeneration).

Causes

Absorption of vitamin B12 from food requires stomach acid to release it from protein, and intrinsic factor secreted by gastric parietal cells to bind it for uptake in the ileum. Causes of deficiency therefore include pernicious anemia (autoimmune loss of intrinsic factor), gastrectomy or gastric bypass, Crohn's disease and ileal resection, pancreatic insufficiency, use of proton pump inhibitors or metformin, and nitrous oxide exposure. Inadequate intake occurs in strict vegan diets without supplementation, since B12 is found naturally only in animal products and fortified foods. Because the liver stores several years' supply, deficiency develops slowly.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is based on blood B12 levels, with methylmalonic acid and homocysteine as more sensitive markers, together with blood counts showing macrocytosis. Treatment is replacement with oral high-dose cyanocobalamin or intramuscular hydroxocobalamin; neurological symptoms may be only partially reversible if treatment is delayed, making early recognition important.
