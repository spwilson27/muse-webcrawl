Johannes Erwin Eugen Rommel (15 November 1891 – 14 October 1944) was a German field marshal during the Second World War. He earned fame commanding the 7th Panzer Division in the 1940 invasion of France — the "Ghost Division" — and, above all, the Afrika Korps in North Africa, where his daring armoured manoeuvres won him the nickname the Desert Fox and the respect of his enemies. He later commanded the defences of Normandy against the Allied invasion of 1944.

Career

Rommel, a decorated infantry officer of the First World War (Pour le Mérite for his actions at Caporetto), wrote the influential tactics book Infantry Attacks (1937) and commanded Hitler's headquarters guard before receiving the 7th Panzer Division. In February 1941 he was sent to Libya, where he drove British forces back across Cyrenaica, besieged Tobruk, and in June 1942 captured it, advancing into Egypt before being halted at El Alamein. Defeated by Montgomery's Eighth Army in the Second Battle of El Alamein (October–November 1942) and pressed from the west after the Allied landings in Morocco and Algeria, he was recalled to Germany in March 1943.

As commander of Army Group B, Rommel fortified the Atlantic Wall and argued for defeating the invasion on the beaches. He was severely wounded when his car was strafed on 17 July 1944, three days before the attempt on Hitler's life.

Death and reputation

Implicated by association with the 20 July plot — though the extent of his involvement remains debated — Rommel was given the choice between a people's court trial, which would endanger his family, and suicide with full military honours. He took poison on 14 October 1944 and was buried with a state funeral, the public being told he had died of his wounds.

Rommel's posthumous reputation is complex. He was used by Nazi propaganda as a model soldier, and after the war was central to the "Rommel myth" — the image of a brilliant, apolitical, chivalrous commander. Historians continue to debate how far this image reflects reality, while acknowledging his tactical gifts, his relative distance from Nazi crimes compared with other commands, and his popularity with his troops.
