Weekly Torah portion

The weekly Torah portion (Hebrew: פָּרָשַׁת הַשָּׁבוּעַ, Parashat ha-Shavua, popularly just parashah or parsha) is a section of the Torah read during Jewish public worship. The Torah is divided into 54 portions, and one is read each week of the year in synagogue, chiefly on Shabbat, so that the entire scroll is completed over the course of a single year, ending and beginning again on Simchat Torah.

Structure

Each parashah is named by one of its first distinctive words — for example Bereshit ("In the beginning"), Noach, Lech-Lecha. The weekly reading is divided into seven aliyot, portions for which congregants are called up to the Torah. A related passage from the Prophets, the haftarah, follows. Shorter sections are also read on Monday and Thursday mornings and on Shabbat afternoon.

Origins

The annual cycle originated in Babylonia, while a triennial cycle was once used in the Land of Israel. The annual cycle became universal in the Middle Ages. On festivals, special readings replace the regular portion. The study of the weekly portion with the commentary of Rashi is a traditional practice, sometimes completed "twice in the original and once in translation" (shnayim mikra ve-echad targum).
