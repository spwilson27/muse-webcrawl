2020 Beirut explosion

Date4 August 2020
Time18:08 EEST
LocationPort of Beirut, Lebanon
CauseFire detonating stored ammonium nitrate
Deaths218
InjuriesMore than 7,000
DamageUS$15 billion; 300,000 homeless

On 4 August 2020, a large amount of ammonium nitrate stored at the Port of Beirut, the capital city of Lebanon, exploded, causing at least 218 deaths, 7,000 injuries, and US$15 billion in property damage, as well as leaving an estimated 300,000 people homeless.

Cause

Around 2,750 tonnes of ammonium nitrate — equivalent to about 1.1 kilotons of TNT — had been stored in a warehouse at the port for six years after being confiscated from the abandoned ship MV Rhosus. A fire in the warehouse preceded the detonation. The blast was among the largest non-nuclear explosions in history and was felt hundreds of kilometres away.

Aftermath

The explosion devastated the port and surrounding neighbourhoods, including much of Beirut's waterfront, and deepened Lebanon's economic and political crisis. Mass protests followed, and the government of Prime Minister Hassan Diab resigned within days. Investigations into official negligence continued for years amid political disputes.
