Real gas

Compressibility factors show how real gases deviate from ideal behaviour

Real gases are non-ideal gases whose molecules occupy a discernible volume and interact with one another. The behaviour of a real gas departs from the ideal gas law, which treats molecules as point particles with no interactions, most strongly at high pressures and low temperatures, where a real gas may condense into a liquid.

Models

The best-known correction is the van der Waals equation (1873), which adds parameters for molecular volume and intermolecular attraction. More accurate engineering models include the Redlich–Kwong, Soave–Redlich–Kwong and Peng–Robinson equations, and the virial equation of state, which expresses the compressibility factor as a power series in density or pressure with temperature-dependent coefficients fitted to experiment. The compressibility factor Z = PV/(nRT) measures deviation from ideality: Z = 1 for an ideal gas, while for real gases Z varies with pressure and temperature.

Thermodynamic treatment

Real-gas behaviour is treated in engineering thermodynamics with generalised charts based on the principle of corresponding states, which holds that gases at the same reduced temperature and pressure (relative to their critical points) have approximately the same compressibility. Such methods are essential in the design of compressors, pipelines, refrigeration systems and chemical plant operating far from ideal conditions.
