Fire point

Fire point
DefinitionLowest temperature at which a liquid's vapours sustain combustion after ignition
Compared toFlash point (lower), autoignition temperature (higher, no ignition source)
Test methodsCleveland open cup (ASTM D92), Pensky–Martens closed cup
Used forFuel and lubricant safety classification

The fire point of a fuel is the lowest temperature at which the vapour of that fuel will continue to burn for at least five seconds after ignition by an open flame. At lower temperatures, a liquid's vapours may ignite momentarily — a flash — but the vapour produced is not sufficient to sustain a continuous flame.

[Image placeholder: A Cleveland open cup apparatus used to measure flash and fire points]

The main difference between the fire point and the flash point is that at the flash point, the substance will ignite momentarily but vapour might not be produced at a rate sufficient to sustain the fire. The fire point is typically a few degrees (often 5–10 °C) above the flash point for a given liquid. Both values depend on the test method and apparatus used.

Measurement

Fire points are measured with open-cup or closed-cup testers. In the Cleveland open cup method (ASTM D92), the sample is heated in an open brass cup while a test flame is passed across the surface at regular temperature intervals; the flash point is recorded when a flash appears, and heating continues until the vapours ignite and burn continuously for at least five seconds — the fire point. Closed-cup methods such as the Pensky–Martens tester generally give lower flash-point values and are standard for regulatory classification of flammable liquids.

Significance

Flash and fire points are used to classify fuels, solvents, and lubricating oils for storage, transport, and handling regulations. A liquid with a low fire point presents a sustained-fire hazard once ignited. The autoignition temperature — the temperature at which a substance ignites spontaneously without an external flame — is a distinct, and always higher, property; fire points should not be confused with it.

Example values

  • Gasoline: flash point about −43 °C; fire point only slightly higher
  • Ethanol: flash point about 13 °C
  • Kerosene: flash point 38–72 °C
  • Diesel fuel: flash point above 52 °C
  • Motor oil: flash point around 220–260 °C, fire point somewhat higher
