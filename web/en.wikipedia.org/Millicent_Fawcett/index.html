Millicent Fawcett

Dame Millicent Garrett Fawcett (11 June 1847 – 5 August 1929) was an English political activist and writer, best known as a leader of the British women's suffrage movement. She campaigned for women's right to vote through peaceful, constitutional means.

Career

Fawcett led the National Union of Women's Suffrage Societies (NUWSS), the largest suffrage organisation in Britain, from 1897 to 1919, distinguishing its non-militant "suffragist" approach from the "suffragette" militancy of the Pankhursts' WSPU. She also co-founded Newnham College, Cambridge, one of the first women's colleges at the university.

Legacy

Women over 30 gained the vote in 1918 and equal voting rights in 1928, a year before her death. In 2018, a statue of Fawcett was unveiled in Parliament Square, London — the first monument to a woman in the square.
