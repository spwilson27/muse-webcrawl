SOX9

SOX9 (SRY-box transcription factor 9) is a transcription factor encoded by the SOX9 gene on human chromosome 17q24. A member of the SOX family of HMG-box proteins, SOX9 is a master regulator of chondrogenesis (cartilage formation) and plays a decisive role in male sex determination, where it drives the differentiation of Sertoli cells and testis development downstream of SRY.

Function in sex determination

In XY embryos, the SRY protein, together with SF1, activates an enhancer of SOX9 in the bipotential gonad. SOX9 upregulation commits supporting cells to the Sertoli fate; SOX9 then maintains its own expression through feed-forward loops involving FGF9 and prostaglandin D2, while suppressing the ovarian pathway (WNT4/RSPO1/β-catenin). Sertoli cells secrete anti-Müllerian hormone and organise testis cords, driving male development. Ectopic SOX9 expression can cause XX male development, while loss of one functional copy can cause XY sex reversal.

Function in cartilage and other tissues

SOX9, with SOX5 and SOX6, activates cartilage matrix genes such as COL2A1 (type II collagen) and aggrecan in chondrocytes and is essential for the formation of the cartilaginous skeleton. It also functions in neural crest cells, the pancreas, intestine, hair follicles, and the developing nervous system, and marks several adult stem-cell populations.

Clinical significance

Heterozygous loss-of-function mutations in or around SOX9 cause campomelic dysplasia, a severe skeletal malformation syndrome with bowed long bones, often accompanied by XY sex reversal and frequently lethal respiratory complications. Regulatory mutations farther upstream cause isolated Pierre Robin sequence. Duplications of the gene region can produce XX male sex development.
