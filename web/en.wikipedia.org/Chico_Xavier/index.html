Francisco Cândido Xavier (April 2, 1910 – June 30, 2002), known as Chico Xavier, was a Brazilian philanthropist and spiritist medium. During a career that spanned over six decades, he claimed to have psychographed (channel-written) over 490 books, which he attributed to various spirits, most famously his spirit guide Emmanuel and the spirit author André Luiz. His books sold tens of millions of copies in Brazil, and he donated all proceeds to charity, becoming one of the most beloved public figures in Brazilian history. In 2012 he was voted "the greatest Brazilian of all time" in a popular television poll.

Biography

Xavier was born into a poor family in Pedro Leopoldo, Minas Gerais, and had little formal education. He reported experiencing spirit manifestations from early childhood. In 1931–32 he published his first book, Parnaso de Além-Túmulo ("Parnassus from Beyond the Tomb"), a collection of poems attributed to deceased Brazilian and Portuguese poets, which caused a sensation — and a lawsuit by the family of one poet, which he won: the court declined to rule on whether the dead could hold copyright. In 1944 he published Nosso Lar ("Our Home"), attributed to André Luiz, describing a spirit colony above Rio de Janeiro; it became one of the best-selling books in Brazilian history and was adapted into a successful 2010 film.

Work and charity

From 1959 Xavier lived in Uberaba, where crowds of thousands sought his psychographed letters from deceased relatives. He worked as a modest public employee, refused personal payment for his mediumship, and channelled book royalties to the Brazilian Spiritist Federation and charitable works, including soup kitchens and aid for the poor. Psychographed letters attributed to him were controversially admitted in a few Brazilian court cases in the 1970s, contributing to acquittals.

Death and legacy

Xavier died on June 30, 2002, the day Brazil won its fifth FIFA World Cup — a coincidence often remarked on in Brazil, since he had said he wished to die on a day of national joy so as not to sadden the country. His hometown reputation and philanthropy made him an enduring icon of Brazilian Spiritism, the world's largest spiritist movement.
