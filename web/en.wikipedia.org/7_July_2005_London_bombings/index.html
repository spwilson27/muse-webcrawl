7 July 2005 London bombings

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The 7 July 2005 London bombings, often referred to as 7/7, were a series of coordinated Islamist suicide attacks on London's public transport system during the morning rush hour. Four attackers detonated bombs on three London Underground trains and one double-decker bus, killing 52 people and injuring more than 770. It remains the deadliest terrorist attack in the United Kingdom since the Lockerbie bombing of 1988.

The attacks

At 08:49, three bombs exploded within 50 seconds of one another on Underground trains: on the Circle line near Aldgate, on the Circle line at Edgware Road, and on the Piccadilly line between King's Cross St Pancras and Russell Square. Nearly an hour later, at 09:47, a fourth bomb exploded on the number 30 bus in Tavistock Square. The attacks took place the day after London had won its bid to host the 2012 Olympic Games, and while the G8 summit was meeting in Scotland.

The bombers

The four suicide bombers were British citizens: Mohammad Sidique Khan (30), a teaching assistant from Leeds; Shehzad Tanweer (22); Germaine Lindsay (19), a Jamaican-born convert to Islam; and Hasib Hussain (18), who detonated the bus bomb. Their homemade devices were made from hydrogen peroxide-based explosives. Two weeks later, on 21 July 2005, a second group attempted copycat attacks, but their devices failed to explode and all four would-be bombers were arrested and convicted.

Response and legacy

The bombings prompted major reviews of transport security and counter-terrorism policing, and contributed to debate over the UK's involvement in the Iraq War. Inquests concluded in 2011 with a verdict of unlawful killing; the coroner made recommendations on emergency response and intelligence sharing. A memorial of 52 stainless steel pillars was unveiled in Hyde Park on the fourth anniversary, and annual commemorations are held each 7 July.
