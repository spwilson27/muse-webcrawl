Tempio Malatestiano

The Tempio Malatestiano (Malatesta Temple) is the cathedral of Rimini, Italy. Originally a Gothic church dedicated to Saint Francis, it was transformed in the mid-15th century by the Renaissance architect Leon Battista Alberti for Sigismondo Pandolfo Malatesta, the ruler of Rimini, who intended it as a mausoleum for himself and his mistress (later wife) Isotta degli Atti.

Architecture

Alberti encased the medieval church in a classical marble shell inspired by Roman triumphal arches. The facade, with its three great arches, remained unfinished: the planned dome and upper portion were never built after Malatesta's fall from power. Inside, sculptors including Agostino di Duccio and Matteo de' Pasti created celebrated reliefs, and Piero della Francesca painted a fresco of Sigismondo kneeling before Saint Sigismund in 1451.

History

Work began around 1450 but halted after Sigismondo's death in 1468 and the loss of his territories. The building was heavily damaged by bombing in the Second World War and subsequently restored. It became Rimini's cathedral in 1809.
