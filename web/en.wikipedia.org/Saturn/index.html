Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun and the second-largest in the Solar System, after Jupiter. A gas giant composed mostly of hydrogen and helium, it is famous for its extensive ring system, made chiefly of ice particles with smaller amounts of rock and dust. Its average density is lower than water's. Saturn has the most confirmed moons of any planet — 274 as of 2025 — including Titan, larger than the planet Mercury, and Enceladus, whose icy geysers suggest a subsurface ocean.

Physical characteristics

Saturn's rapid rotation makes it the most oblate planet, visibly flattened at the poles. Its interior is thought to contain a rocky core surrounded by metallic hydrogen. Winds in the upper atmosphere reach 1,800 km/h, and a persistent hexagonal storm pattern surrounds the north pole. Intense radiation of internal heat — more than twice what it receives from the Sun — powers its weather.

Rings

The rings span up to 280,000 km in diameter yet are only about ten metres thick in places. They are divided into named bands (D, C, B, A, F, G, E), with gaps such as the Cassini Division. Galileo first observed them in 1610 but mistook them for "handles"; Christiaan Huygens recognised them as a ring in 1655. The rings are thought to be relatively young — perhaps only a few hundred million years old — possibly the debris of a shattered icy moon.

Exploration

Saturn has been visited by Pioneer 11 (1979), Voyager 1 and 2 (1980–81), and the Cassini–Huygens mission, which orbited from 2004 to 2017 and landed the Huygens probe on Titan in 2005. Cassini ended with a deliberate plunge into Saturn's atmosphere in September 2017.
