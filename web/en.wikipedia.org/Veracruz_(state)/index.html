Veracruz, formally the Free and Sovereign State of Veracruz de Ignacio de la Llave, is one of the 31 states of Mexico. It stretches along the Gulf of Mexico coast in eastern Mexico, with its capital at Xalapa and its largest city the historic port of Veracruz. It is one of Mexico's most populous and economically important states.

Geography

Veracruz runs in a long strip from the Huasteca region in the north to the Isthmus of Tehuantepec in the south. The coastal plain rises to the Sierra Madre Oriental in the west, culminating in Pico de Orizaba, Mexico's highest mountain. Major rivers include the Pánuco, Papaloapan, and Coatzacoalcos. The climate ranges from tropical coast to temperate and alpine highlands.

History

The state was home to the Olmec, Totonac, and Huastec civilisations; the ruined city of El Tajín is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Hernán Cortés founded Veracruz in 1519, the first Spanish municipality on the American mainland, and the port became the gateway between New Spain and Europe. Veracruz state was formally established in 1824.

Economy and culture

Petrochemicals, oil, agriculture (coffee, sugarcane, citrus, vanilla), and port trade anchor the economy. The state is famed for its son jarocho music, the harp-and-jarana sound popularised by "La Bamba", its carnival, and a cuisine blending Spanish, African, and Caribbean influences.
