Chief of Naval Operations

The chief of naval operations (CNO) is the highest-ranking officer of the United States Navy and a member of the Joint Chiefs of Staff. The CNO is nominated by the President, confirmed by the Senate, and serves a four-year term.

Duties

The CNO is the principal naval adviser to the President, the Secretary of Defense, and the Secretary of the Navy on the conduct of naval warfare. The office administers the Navy through the Office of the Chief of Naval Operations at the Pentagon, overseeing the organization, training, and equipping of naval forces, though operational command of forces rests with the combatant commanders.

History

The position was created in 1915 to provide unified professional leadership for the Navy. Notable holders include Ernest King, the fleet commander of the Second World War, Arleigh Burke, and Elmo Zumwalt, who issued the famous "Z-grams" reforming personnel policy.
