Brazilian Academy of Letters

The Brazilian Academy of Letters (Portuguese: Academia Brasileira de Letras, ABL) is a Brazilian literary non-profit society established in Rio de Janeiro on 20 July 1897. Modelled on the Académie française, it consists of 40 members, known as the "Immortals", chosen by election to lifetime seats.

History

The Academy was founded by a group of writers including Machado de Assis, its first president, and Lúcio de Mendonça. Its headquarters, the Petit Trianon palace, was donated by the French government in 1923.

Role

The ABL is devoted to the cultivation of the Portuguese language and Brazilian literature. It publishes dictionaries and the Vocabulário Ortográfico da Língua Portuguesa, the official orthographic vocabulary of Brazilian Portuguese.

Notable members

Past members include Machado de Assis, Jorge Amado, João Cabral de Melo Neto, Rachel de Queiroz (the first woman admitted, in 1977), and Gilberto Gil.
