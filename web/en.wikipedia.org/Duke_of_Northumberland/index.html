Duke of Northumberland

Alnwick Castle, seat of the Duke of Northumberland (placeholder image)

Duke of Northumberland is a noble title in the peerage of Great Britain that has been created twice. It is held by the Percy family, one of the most powerful families in northern England since the Middle Ages. The family seat is Alnwick Castle in Northumberland.

History

The title was first created in 1551 for John Dudley, Earl of Warwick, a leading figure in the reign of Edward VI; it was forfeited after his execution in 1553 following the attempt to place Lady Jane Grey on the throne. The second creation came in 1766, when Hugh Percy, 2nd Earl of Northumberland — heir of the ancient Percy earls — was created Duke of Northumberland. His descendants have held the dukedom continuously since.

Estates and role

The dukes of Northumberland have been among England's largest landowners, with estates including Alnwick Castle, Syon House in London, and extensive land in Northumberland and elsewhere. Alnwick Castle gained renewed fame as a filming location for the Harry Potter films. The current holder of the title is Ralph Percy, 12th Duke of Northumberland, who succeeded in 1995.
