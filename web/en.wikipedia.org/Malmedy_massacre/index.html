Malmedy massacre

The Malmedy massacre was a German war crime committed by soldiers of the Waffen-SS on 17 December 1944 at the Baugnez crossroads near the city of Malmedy, Belgium, during the Battle of the Bulge (Ardennes Offensive). Soldiers of Kampfgruppe Peiper, part of the 1st SS Panzer Division Leibstandarte SS Adolf Hitler, summarily killed 84 American prisoners of war. The massacre is used as an example of the SS's brutality on both fronts.

The massacre

On the second day of the German offensive, Kampfgruppe Peiper, a spearhead under SS-Obersturmbannführer Joachim Peiper, overran a convoy of Battery B, 285th Field Artillery Observation Battalion, at the Baugnez crossroads. The disarmed Americans were assembled in a field. In the early afternoon the SS opened fire on the group with machine guns and pistols; survivors who were wounded were finished off with shots to the head, while a few escaped by feigning death and fleeing once the Germans moved on. Some 43 men survived. News of the killings spread rapidly through American lines and hardened resistance during the battle.

Other killings

Kampfgruppe Peiper murdered other prisoners and Belgian civilians during its advance, including at Honsfeld, Büllingen, Ligneuville and Stavelot; the unit's total victims along its route numbered several hundred prisoners of war and civilians.

Trial and controversy

In 1946 a US military tribunal at Dachau tried 74 members of the unit. Forty-three were sentenced to death, including Peiper, though the verdicts became controversial amid allegations of irregularities in interrogation. US Senate hearings, in which Senator Joseph McCarthy played a prominent role, reviewed the case; no death sentence was carried out, and the last prisoner, Peiper, was released in December 1956. Peiper was murdered in France in 1976, in circumstances widely attributed to former Resistance members.
