Contingent Reserve Arrangement

The CRA provides liquidity support among the BRICS countries.

The BRICS Contingent Reserve Arrangement (CRA) is a framework for providing liquidity through currency swaps in response to actual or potential short-term balance of payments pressures. It was established by treaty signed on 15 July 2014 at the sixth BRICS summit in Fortaleza, Brazil, by the central banks of Brazil, Russia, India, China and South Africa, and entered into force in 2015.

Structure

The CRA has initial committed resources of US$100 billion: China contributes $41 billion, Brazil, Russia and India $18 billion each, and South Africa $5 billion. A member's maximum access differs by country, being a multiple of its contribution (0.5 for China, 1 for Brazil, Russia and India, and 2 for South Africa). Seventy percent of a member's maximum access is linked to the existence of an IMF-supported programme; the remaining 30 percent can be drawn on a de-linked basis.

Purpose

The arrangement is regarded as part of a BRICS "financial safety net" alongside the New Development Bank, reducing members' exclusive reliance on the International Monetary Fund and the dollar-based system in crises. The CRA has not yet been drawn upon.
