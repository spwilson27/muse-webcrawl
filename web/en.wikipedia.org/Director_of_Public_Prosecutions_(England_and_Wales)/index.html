Director of Public Prosecutions (England and Wales)

The Director of Public Prosecutions (DPP) is the head of the Crown Prosecution Service (CPS) and the third-highest ranking prosecuting authority in England and Wales, after the Attorney General and the Solicitor General. The DPP is the principal public prosecutor for England and Wales, responsible for the conduct of criminal prosecutions instituted by the police and other investigators, and for advising on the most serious, complex and sensitive cases.

History

The office was created by the Prosecution of Offences Act 1879, following long debate about the absence of a public prosecution system; before then, prosecutions were largely brought privately by victims. For most of its history the DPP handled only a limited class of important cases, while police forces conducted the bulk of prosecutions. Following the Philips Royal Commission on Criminal Procedure (1981), the Prosecution of Offences Act 1985 created the Crown Prosecution Service in 1986, with the DPP as its head, separating the investigation of crime (police) from decisions to prosecute (CPS).

Role and powers

The DPP issues the Code for Crown Prosecutors, which sets out the two-stage test applied to every case: whether there is sufficient evidence for a realistic prospect of conviction, and whether prosecution is in the public interest. Certain offences require the personal consent of the DPP (or the Attorney General) before proceedings can be brought. The DPP operates independently of government in individual casework, under the superintendence of the Attorney General, to whom the DPP is accountable to Parliament. Holders of the office have included Sir Thomas Hetherington, Dame Barbara Mills, Sir David Calvert-Smith, Sir Ken Macdonald, Keir Starmer (2008–2013), Alison Saunders (2013–2018), Max Hill (2018–2023) and Stephen Parkinson, who took office in November 2023.
