Ascent of Mount Carmel

Ascent of Mount Carmel (Spanish: Subida del Monte Carmelo) is a spiritual treatise by the sixteenth-century Spanish mystic and poet Saint John of the Cross, written between about 1578 and 1585. Together with its sequel, the Dark Night, it gives a systematic account of the soul's journey toward union with God.

Contents

The work is a commentary on John's poem "In a dark night", explaining stanza by stanza how the soul must pass through the "dark night" of purgation. The first book treats the night of the senses — the detachment from disordered appetites — while the second and third books treat the night of the spirit, addressing the purification of the intellect by faith, the memory by hope, and the will by love.

The drawing of Mount Carmel

John illustrated his teaching with a famous sketch of Mount Carmel, on whose summit he wrote that only the honour and glory of God dwell, and along whose proper path appear the repeated words nada — "nothing" — signifying that the soul must desire nothing outside God in order to reach union.

Influence

The Ascent is considered one of the masterpieces of Christian mystical literature and a key text of Carmelite spirituality. It remained unfinished, breaking off before completing the commentary on the poem, which is taken up in the Dark Night.
