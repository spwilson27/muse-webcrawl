Cabeus (crater)

Cabeus
Coordinates: 84.9° S, 35.5° W
Diameter: 98 km
Depth: unknown
Eponym: Niccolò Cabeo
Cabeus lies near the lunar south pole; parts of its floor are in permanent shadow.

Cabeus is a lunar impact crater located about 100 km (62 mi) from the south pole of the Moon, at 84.9° S, 35.5° W. It has a diameter of about 98 km (61 mi). The crater is named after the 17th-century Italian Jesuit philosopher and scientist Niccolò Cabeo. Because of its polar location, Cabeus is viewed at an oblique angle from Earth, and portions of its interior floor lie in permanent shadow.

Exploration and the LCROSS impact

Cabeus attracted scientific attention because permanently shadowed regions at the lunar poles can cold-trap volatile compounds, including water ice. In October 2009, NASA's LCROSS mission deliberately crashed the Centaur upper stage of its launch vehicle into Cabeus, followed minutes later by the shepherding spacecraft, which observed the impact plume before also crashing. Analysis of the plume, announced in November 2009, found a significant amount of water — roughly 155 kg in the observed ejecta — along with carbon monoxide, carbon dioxide, ammonia and other volatiles. The result, published in Science in 2010, estimated water at about 5.6 per cent of the regolith mass in the sampled material, a landmark confirmation that usable water ice exists at the lunar poles.

Characteristics

The crater's rim is worn and eroded by subsequent impacts, and the satellite crater Cabeus A lies across its northeastern rim. Radar and neutron-spectrometer data from orbiters such as Lunar Prospector and the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter had previously indicated enhanced hydrogen in the region, consistent with buried ice. Cabeus remains a reference site in discussions of in-situ resource utilisation for future crewed lunar bases near the south pole.
