Howard Scott Warshaw

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Howard Scott Warshaw (born July 30, 1957) is an American psychotherapist and former video game designer who worked at Atari in the early 1980s. He designed the bestselling Yars’ Revenge and the critically panned but historically famous E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial, both for the Atari 2600.

Born: July 30, 1957, Colorado, United States
Occupation: Video game designer, psychotherapist, author, filmmaker
Known for: Yars’ Revenge, Raiders of the Lost Ark, E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial

Atari career

Warshaw joined Atari in 1981. His first game, Yars’ Revenge (1982) — an adaptation of the arcade game Star Castle reworked into an original title named after CEO Ray Kassar (“Yar” is “Ray” reversed) — became the best-selling original game for the Atari 2600. He followed it with the ambitious adventure game Raiders of the Lost Ark.

E.T. and the video game crash

In July 1982 Warshaw was assigned the game of Steven Spielberg’s film E.T. and given only about five weeks to complete it so it could be manufactured for Christmas. The resulting game was a commercial disappointment and became a symbol of the video game crash of 1983; urban legend held that unsold cartridges were buried in Alamogordo, New Mexico, a story confirmed when the landfill was excavated in 2014. Warshaw later wrote about his career in the book Once Upon Atari and retrained as a psychotherapist in Silicon Valley.
