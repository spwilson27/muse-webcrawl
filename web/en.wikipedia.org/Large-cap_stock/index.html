Large-cap stock

A large-cap stock (large capitalisation) is a share in a publicly traded company with a market capitalisation of roughly US$10 billion or more, though thresholds vary by index provider and over time. Large-cap companies are usually established, widely followed firms such as those in the S&P 500.

Characteristics

Large caps tend to be less volatile than mid-cap and small-cap stocks, pay dividends more often, and offer greater liquidity, but may grow more slowly. They form the core holdings of many mutual funds and exchange-traded funds.
