Vilfredo Pareto

Vilfredo Federico Damaso Pareto (15 July 1848 – 19 August 1923) was an Italian engineer, economist, sociologist, and philosopher. Born in Paris to an exiled Genoese family, he succeeded Léon Walras in the chair of political economy at the University of Lausanne in 1893. He is known for the concepts of Pareto efficiency and the Pareto distribution, and for observing that roughly 80% of effects flow from 20% of causes — the basis of the popular "80/20 rule".

Economics

In his Manual of Political Economy (1906), Pareto placed consumer theory on an ordinal footing, showing that equilibrium could be described using indifference curves without measurable utility — a foundation of modern microeconomics. A state in which no one can be made better off without making someone worse off is now called Pareto optimal. His empirical study of income in several countries produced the Pareto distribution, a power law whose ubiquity later influenced statistics, network science, and the study of inequality.

Sociology

Pareto's Treatise on General Sociology (1916) argued that human action is mostly non-logical, driven by persistent sentiments he called "residues", and rationalised afterwards by variable "derivations". He developed the theory of the circulation of elites, distinguishing governing classes dominated by "foxes" (cunning, adaptable) from those dominated by "lions" (forceful, conservative), and concluded cynically that "history is a graveyard of aristocracies". His work also influenced fascist thinkers, a legacy that continues to shadow his reputation.
