Sacred Band of Thebes

Activec. 378–338 BC
CountryThebes, Boeotia
TypeElite heavy infantry (hoplites)
Size300 men — 150 pairs
EngagementsTegyra, Leuctra, Chaeronea

The Sacred Band of Thebes (Ancient Greek: Hieros Lokhos) was an elite unit of the Theban army composed of 150 pairs of male lovers, 300 men in total. Created around 378 BC, it was the spearhead of the Theban forces during the period of Theban hegemony in Greece, and was annihilated by Philip II of Macedon at the Battle of Chaeronea in 338 BC.

Formation

According to Plutarch, the Band was formed by the commander Gorgidas, who deliberately composed it of pairs of lovers, reasoning that men would fight all the harder beside those they loved and would be ashamed to show cowardice before them. The city maintained the soldiers at public expense and trained them in wrestling and military exercises; they were stationed on the Cadmea, the citadel of Thebes. The idea had precedents in Greek thought — Plato's Symposium imagines an army of lovers as nearly invincible.

Victories

Under the general Pelopidas the Band won its first famous victory at Tegyra (375 BC), defeating a larger Spartan force, and at the Battle of Leuctra (371 BC) it formed the striking force of Epaminondas's deepened left wing, which crushed the Spartan right and killed King Cleombrotus — ending Spartan military supremacy. The Band remained undefeated for some forty years.

Destruction at Chaeronea

At Chaeronea in 338 BC, facing Philip II and his son Alexander, the Sacred Band held its position and was wiped out where it stood, refusing to retreat. Plutarch reports that Philip, viewing the corpses lying in pairs, wept and cursed anyone who supposed the men had done anything dishonourable. A mass grave beneath the Lion of Chaeronea monument, excavated in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, contained 254 skeletons arranged in rows and is generally identified as the Band's burial.
