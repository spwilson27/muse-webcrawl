The Pakistan Armed Forces are the military forces of the Islamic Republic of Pakistan. They consist of three formally uniformed services — the Pakistan Army, Pakistan Navy, and Pakistan Air Force — supported by paramilitary organizations. With roughly 660,000 active personnel, Pakistan has one of the largest standing militaries in the world, and it is the only Muslim-majority country with nuclear weapons.

History

The armed forces were created at the partition of British India in 1947, when Pakistan received a share of the former British Indian Army's personnel and assets. Pakistan has fought three major wars with India (1947–48, 1965, and 1971) as well as the Kargil conflict of 1999. The 1971 war ended with the secession of East Pakistan as Bangladesh. In 1998 Pakistan conducted nuclear weapons tests in response to India's, formalizing its nuclear deterrent.

Role in society

The military has played a dominant role in Pakistani politics, ruling the country directly under martial law for more than three decades in total — under Ayub Khan, Yahya Khan, Zia-ul-Haq, and Pervez Musharraf — and exercising substantial influence behind the scenes at other times. The Inter-Services Intelligence (ISI) is the premier intelligence agency. Internally, the armed forces have conducted long counter-insurgency campaigns against militant groups in the northwest, notably Operations Zarb-e-Azb and Radd-ul-Fasaad, and they contribute troops to United Nations peacekeeping missions, among the largest contingents worldwide.
