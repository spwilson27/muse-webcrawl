Orans

A female figure in the orans posture, from early Christian catacomb art.

Orans, a loanword from Medieval Latin ōrāns (praying), is a posture or bodily attitude of prayer, usually standing, with the elbows close to the sides of the body and with the hands outstretched sideways, palms up. It was a common posture in early Christianity and can be seen in art of the period, notably in the Roman catacombs.

History

The orans posture was known in the ancient world before Christianity and appears in Greek and Roman religious art. Early Christians adopted it as the characteristic attitude of prayer; figures of the Virgin Mary in Byzantine art, such as the famous mosaic in Saint Sophia Cathedral in Kyiv, are often shown as an orans (often called Oranta).

Liturgical use

In modern practice, the orans posture is used by the celebrant during certain prayers of the Roman Catholic Mass and other liturgies, and it is also common among charismatic and Pentecostal worshippers and in private devotion.
