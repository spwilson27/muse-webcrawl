Dragon 2 is a class of partially reusable spacecraft developed by American aerospace company SpaceX. It comes in two variants: Crew Dragon, a human-rated capsule capable of carrying up to seven astronauts, and Cargo Dragon, used for resupply of the International Space Station (ISS).

The spacecraft launches atop a Falcon 9 rocket from Kennedy Space Center and splashes down under parachutes in the ocean. On 30 May 2020, Crew Dragon Demo-2 carried NASA astronauts Douglas Hurley and Robert Behnken to the ISS — the first crewed orbital launch from US soil since the Space Shuttle retired in 2011, and the first ever by a commercial company.

Design

Dragon 2 is a gumdrop-shaped capsule about 8.1 m tall and 3.7 m in diameter, with Draco thrusters for manoeuvring and eight SuperDraco engines forming a launch-escape system. Unlike the original Dragon, it docks autonomously to the ISS rather than being berthed by the station's robotic arm. The capsule is designed for reuse, and individual Crew Dragons have flown multiple missions.

Operations

Beyond NASA crew-rotation missions, Crew Dragons have flown private missions including Inspiration4 (2021), the first all-civilian orbital flight, and Axiom Space missions to the ISS. In 2024 the Polaris Dawn mission flew a Crew Dragon to the highest Earth orbit since Apollo and performed the first commercial spacewalk.
