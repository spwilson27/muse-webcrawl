Atlas V is an expendable launch system in the Atlas rocket family, developed by Lockheed Martin and operated by United Launch Alliance (ULA). It was first launched in August 2002 and has carried many of NASA's and the U.S. military's most important payloads.

Design

The first stage is powered by a single Russian-built RD-180 engine burning kerosene and liquid oxygen; up to five Aerojet Rocketdyne solid rocket boosters can augment thrust. The Centaur upper stage uses one or two RL10 engines burning liquid hydrogen and oxygen. Configurations are denoted by a three-digit code (e.g., 401, 551) describing fairing size, number of boosters, and number of Centaur engines.

Notable missions

Atlas V launched the Mars Science Laboratory (Curiosity), Perseverance, New Horizons to Pluto, Juno to Jupiter, OSIRIS-REx, and Boeing's Starliner crewed spacecraft. It maintained a perfect mission success record for many years and is scheduled to be replaced by ULA's Vulcan Centaur; remaining Atlas Vs are reserved largely for Starliner and Amazon's Kuiper constellation launches.
