Methyl salicylate (oil of wintergreen or wintergreen oil) is an organic compound with the formula C6H4(OH)(CO2CH3). It is the methyl ester of salicylic acid and a colourless, viscous liquid with a sweet, fruity odour reminiscent of root beer. It is produced by many species of plants, particularly wintergreens such as Gaultheria procumbens, and also occurs in sweet birch bark.

Production and uses

Methyl salicylate is synthesised commercially by the esterification of salicylic acid with methanol. It is used as a flavouring agent in confectionery and chewing gum at low concentrations, as a fragrance, and widely in over-the-counter topical analgesic rubs and liniments for muscle and joint pain, where it acts as a rubefacient and counterirritant. It is also used as a transfer agent in fluorescence microscopy and as a clearing agent in histology because its refractive index closely matches that of tissue.

Safety

Concentrated methyl salicylate is toxic: a teaspoon contains the salicylate equivalent of many aspirin tablets, and ingestions — particularly in children — have caused fatal salicylate poisoning. It was also investigated historically as a simulant for the chemical weapon sulfur mustard because of similar physical properties.
