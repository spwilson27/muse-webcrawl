Mission San Carlos Borromeo de Carmelo, also known as the Carmel Mission, is a Spanish Catholic mission located in Carmel-by-the-Sea, California. It was the second of the twenty-one California missions, founded on June 3, 1770, by Saint Junípero Serra, and it served as the headquarters of the Alta California mission system during Serra's lifetime.

The mission was initially established at the Presidio of Monterey and moved in 1771 to its present site near the Carmel River, where better soil and water were available. The present stone church, completed in 1797, is noted for its distinctive Moorish-style bell tower and catenary arches.

History

Junípero Serra died at the mission in 1784 and is buried beneath the sanctuary floor, alongside his companions Juan Crespí and Fermín Lasuén. After secularization in the 1830s the mission fell into ruin; the roof collapsed in 1851. Restoration began in the 1880s and continued under Harry Downie from the 1930s onward. Pope John XXIII granted the church the status of a minor basilica in 1961.

Today

The mission is an active parish church, a museum, and a U.S. National Historic Landmark, regarded as one of the most beautiful of the California missions. Its grounds include several museum exhibits, the Serra Memorial Cenotaph by sculptor Jo Mora, and the burial site of the oldest Native American woman of the mission era, who died in 1873 at over 100 years of age.
