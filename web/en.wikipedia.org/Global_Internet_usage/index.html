Global Internet usage

Global Internet usage refers to the worldwide adoption and use of the Internet. By the mid-2020s more than five billion people — roughly two-thirds of the world's population — were online, most of them accessing the Internet through mobile devices and broadband connections.

Growth

Internet use grew from around 16 million users in 1995, when less than half of one percent of the world's population was online, to a majority of humanity by the late 2010s. Growth has been fastest in Asia, which accounts for the largest share of users, while penetration rates are highest in Northern Europe and North America. China, India and the United States have the largest national online populations.

The digital divide

Usage remains uneven: rural areas, lower-income countries and older populations show lower adoption, a disparity termed the digital divide. Barriers include infrastructure, affordability, digital literacy and language. International bodies such as the International Telecommunication Union publish annual statistics on connectivity and promote universal access goals.
