Drum Corps International

Drum Corps International (DCI) is a nonprofit governing body for junior drum and bugle corps in North America — marching music ensembles of brass, percussion and colour guard performers aged up to about 21–22. Founded in 1972, DCI organises a summer tour of competitions across the United States culminating in the DCI World Championships each August.

Founded: 1972
Headquarters: Indianapolis, Indiana
Sport: Marching arts / drum corps

History

DCI was formed by a group of leading corps that broke away from veterans' organisations such as the VFW and American Legion in order to standardise rules and judging and to share revenues. The first championship was held in Whitewater, Wisconsin, in 1972. The finals have been held at Lucas Oil Stadium in Indianapolis since 2009 under a long-term agreement.

Competition

Corps are divided into World Class and Open Class. Each corps presents a field show of roughly 11–13 minutes, judged on musical and visual achievement and general effect. Historically dominant corps include the Blue Devils, The Cavaliers, The Cadets, Santa Clara Vanguard, Carolina Crown and the Bluecoats. Membership is by audition, and members rehearse and tour full-time through the summer, a regime often described as "marching music's major league".

Instrumentation

Modern DCI corps use B♭ brass (trumpets, mellophones, baritones/euphoniums and tubas), a battery of marching snare, tenor and bass drums, a front ensemble ("pit") of mallet percussion and electronics, and a colour guard performing with flags, rifles and sabres. Rule changes over the decades have admitted multi-valve brass, grounded percussion, amplification and electronic instruments.
