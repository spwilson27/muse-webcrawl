Astro Boy

Astro Boy (Tetsuwan Atomu, "Mighty Atom")
Created by: Osamu Tezuka
Manga: 1952–1968
Anime series: 1963, 1980, 2003
Film: 2009 (CGI feature)

Astro Boy, known in Japan as Tetsuwan Atomu ("Mighty Atom"), is a Japanese manga series written and illustrated by Osamu Tezuka. It was serialized in Shonen magazine from 1952 to 1968 and follows Astro Boy, a powerful android created by the head of the Ministry of Science, Doctor Tenma, to replace his son Tobio, who had died in a car accident.

Tezuka's work became hugely influential in establishing the visual style of manga and anime. The 1963 anime adaptation was the first popular animated Japanese television series and helped introduce anime to audiences in the United States, where it aired in syndication.

Plot

After creating the robot in his son's image, Dr. Tenma grows disillusioned and sells him to a circus. The kindly Professor Ochanomizu rescues Astro and gives him a robot family. Possessing super strength, flight via rockets in his legs, machine guns in his hips, and a kind heart, Astro fights crime, evil robots, and alien invaders while seeking acceptance in a society divided over robot rights.

Legacy

Astro Boy is one of the most successful manga and anime franchises in history, with the manga selling an estimated 100 million copies. The character became a cultural icon in Japan, where he was made an official ambassador for overseas safety and a citizen of the city of Sagamihara. Later adaptations include the 1980 and 2003 anime series and a 2009 computer-animated film produced by Imagi Animation Studios.
