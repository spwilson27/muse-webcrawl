Systematic desensitization

Systematic desensitization, also known as graduated exposure therapy, is a type of behaviour therapy developed by South African psychiatrist Joseph Wolpe in the 1950s. It is used to overcome phobias and other anxiety disorders.

Procedure

The client first learns relaxation techniques such as deep muscle relaxation and breathing. Therapist and client then build a hierarchy of feared situations, from least to most anxiety-provoking, and the client confronts each step — imagined or in vivo — while maintaining relaxation.

Principle

The method rests on reciprocal inhibition: a person cannot be both deeply relaxed and anxious at the same time, so pairing relaxation with the feared stimulus gradually weakens the conditioned fear response.

Applications

It is commonly used for specific phobias such as fear of flying, heights, animals, and medical procedures, and forms a precursor to modern exposure therapy within cognitive behavioural therapy.
