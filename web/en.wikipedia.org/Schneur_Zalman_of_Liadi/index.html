The Alter Rebbe, founder of Chabad

Schneur Zalman of Liadi (1745–1812), known as the Alter Rebbe ("Old Rabbi"), was an Orthodox rabbi, mystic and philosopher who founded the Chabad movement of Hasidic Judaism. He is the author of the Tanya, the foundational work of Chabad philosophy, and of a widely used code of Jewish law, the Shulchan Aruch HaRav.

Life

Born in Liozna (in present-day Belarus), Schneur Zalman became a leading disciple of Dov Ber of Mezeritch, the successor of the Baal Shem Tov. Settling in Liadi, he developed an intellectual, contemplative approach to Hasidism — the name Chabad being an acronym of the Hebrew words for wisdom (Chochmah), understanding (Binah) and knowledge (Da'at). His approach provoked controversy with Lithuanian opponents of Hasidism (Mitnagdim), and accusations by rivals led to his arrest and imprisonment in St Petersburg in 1798 and again in 1800; the date of his release, 19 Kislev, is celebrated by Chabad as a holiday. Fleeing Napoleon's invasion in 1812, he died during the journey and was buried in Hadiach.

Works and legacy

His Tanya (first published 1796–97) systematises the doctrine of the two souls and the role of the intermediate person, the Beinoni. He also composed liturgical works and arranged the Chabad prayer rite. His son Dov Ber Schneuri succeeded him, and the movement later centred on Lubavitch under subsequent rebbes, becoming one of the largest Hasidic movements worldwide.
