Hallgrímskirkja

Hallgrímskirkja (Icelandic pronunciation: [ˈhatl̥ˌkrimsˌcʰɪr̥ca], "church of Hallgrímur") is a Lutheran (Church of Iceland) parish church in Reykjavík, Iceland. At 74.5 metres (244 ft) tall, it is the largest church in Iceland and among the tallest structures in the country. Originally it had been intended to be less tall, but the leaders of the Church of Iceland wanted a large spire so as to outshine the Landakotskirkja, the cathedral of the Catholic Church in Iceland.

Architecture

The church was commissioned in 1937 and designed by state architect Guðjón Samúelsson, whose design was said to resemble the trap rocks, mountains and glaciers of Iceland's landscape — its sweeping stepped wings recall columns of basalt formed by cooling lava. Construction took 41 years, from 1945 to 1986, though the landmark tower was completed long before the whole church was finished. The crypt beneath the choir was consecrated in 1948, the steeple and wings in 1974, and the nave in 1986.

The church is named after the Icelandic poet and clergyman Hallgrímur Pétursson (1614–1674), author of the Passion Hymns. In front of the church stands a statue of the explorer Leif Erikson, a gift from the United States in 1930 commemorating the 1000th anniversary of Iceland's parliament, the Althing. The church's large pipe organ, built by Johannes Klais of Bonn, has 5,275 pipes and was completed in 1992. An observation deck in the tower offers panoramic views over Reykjavík.
