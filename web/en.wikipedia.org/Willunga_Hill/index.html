Willunga Hill

Willunga Hill is a locality and hill on the Fleurieu Peninsula in South Australia, situated inland from the town of Willunga and overlooking the McLaren Vale wine region and the Gulf St Vincent. The hill lies along Main South Road and Willunga Hill Road, in an area of farmland, vineyards, and almond orchards.

Tour Down Under

Willunga Hill is best known nationally and internationally as the traditional decisive climb of the Tour Down Under, Australia's premier professional cycling race and the opening event of the UCI WorldTour. Since 2012 the hill has hosted a summit finish on the race's penultimate stage, and its steep ascent — roughly 3 kilometres at an average gradient of about 7–8% — regularly draws large crowds. Australian rider Richie Porte became strongly associated with the climb, winning the Willunga Hill stage six consecutive times (2014–2019), leading to the ascent being informally nicknamed "Porte Hill".

Local area

The hill and its surrounds form part of the agricultural hinterland of the historic township of Willunga, settled in the 1830s and once a centre of the slate quarrying industry. The locality is within the City of Onkaparinga local government area.
