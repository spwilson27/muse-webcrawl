Unintended consequences
Field: Social sciences, policy analysis
Key thinker: Robert K. Merton (1936)
Related: Perverse incentive, Cobra effect

Unintended consequences are outcomes of a purposeful action that are not intended or foreseen by the actor. The sociologist Robert K. Merton systematised the concept in his 1936 paper "The Unanticipated Consequences of Purposive Social Action", identifying sources such as ignorance, error, and the "imperious immediacy of interest" that leads actors to disregard longer-term effects.

Categories

Merton and later writers distinguish unexpected benefits (serendipity), unexpected drawbacks that add to the intended effect, and perverse results in which an intervention worsens the very problem it targets — the "cobra effect", named for a bounty on dead cobras in colonial India that allegedly incentivised cobra breeding. Unintended consequences are a central concern in economics, public policy, environmental management, and technology ethics.
