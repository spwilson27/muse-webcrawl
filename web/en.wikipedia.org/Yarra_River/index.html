The Yarra River (Kulin languages: Berrern, Birr-arung) is a perennial river in east-central Victoria, Australia. It flows some 242 kilometres westwards from its source in the Yarra Ranges through the Yarra Valley and the city of Melbourne before emptying into Hobsons Bay at the head of Port Phillip Bay.

History

The river was a major food source and meeting place for the Wurundjeri people of the Kulin nation, who called it Birr-arung, meaning "river of mists and shadows". The city of Melbourne was founded on its north bank in 1835. The river's brown colour, caused by clay soils in its upper catchment, earned the city the local joke that the Yarra is "the river that flows upside down". Yarra Falls, a series of rapids near present-day Queen Street, marked the limit of navigation and separated fresh from salt water; it was blasted away in the 1880s.

Course and use

The upper catchment, closed to public access, supplies much of Melbourne's drinking water through reservoirs including the Upper Yarra Reservoir. The lower reaches pass through central Melbourne, lined by parklands, the Royal Botanic Gardens, and sporting precincts including Melbourne Park and the Melbourne Cricket Ground. Rowing on the Yarra is a well-established tradition, and the river hosts the Moomba festival each March.
