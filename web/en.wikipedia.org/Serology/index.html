Serology

FieldImmunology, medical laboratory science
SpecimenBlood serum
Key assaysELISA, agglutination, complement fixation, immunofluorescence, neutralisation
ApplicationsDiagnosis, blood typing, epidemiology, forensics

Serology is the scientific study of serum and other body fluids, and in practice usually refers to the diagnostic identification of antibodies (or antigens) in the serum. Such antibodies are typically formed in response to an infection, to foreign proteins in other contexts (for example after a mismatched blood transfusion), or to one's own proteins in autoimmunity. Serological tests are a cornerstone of laboratory diagnosis and of seroprevalence surveys in epidemiology.

Methods

Classic serological methods include agglutination tests (the Widal test for typhoid; blood grouping), precipitation and complement-fixation reactions (historically the Wassermann test for syphilis), and fluorescent antibody techniques. Modern laboratories rely chiefly on the enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) and its variants, chemiluminescent immunoassays, western blotting, and virus neutralisation assays, which measure functional antibodies. Serology distinguishes IgM antibodies (early infection) from IgG antibodies (later or past infection), and rising titres in paired samples can confirm recent disease.

Applications

Serology is used to diagnose infections such as HIV, hepatitis, syphilis, dengue and COVID-19; to type blood for transfusion; to screen donated blood; in forensic serology to identify blood and other fluids; and in population studies to estimate how widely a pathogen has spread (for example, SARS-CoV-2 antibody surveys during the COVID-19 pandemic). Its main limitations are the "window period" before antibodies appear, cross-reactivity between related antigens, and the fact that antibodies indicate exposure rather than necessarily current infection.
