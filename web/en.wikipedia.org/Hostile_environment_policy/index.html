Hostile environment policy

The hostile environment policy is a set of British immigration measures announced in 2012 by Home Secretary Theresa May, whose stated aim was to create "a really hostile environment for illegal migrants" in Britain. The policy required landlords, employers, banks, the NHS, and other services to check immigration status, making everyday life difficult for people unable to prove a right to remain.

Legislation

The Immigration Acts of 2014 and 2016 extended status checks to private tenancies, bank accounts, and driving licences, and stiffened penalties for employing or housing people without leave to remain. Data-sharing between public services and immigration enforcement expanded correspondingly.

Windrush scandal

The policy's gravest failure surfaced in 2018, when it emerged that many long-resident members of the Windrush generation — Commonwealth citizens who had arrived legally before 1973 — had been wrongly denied healthcare, jobs, and housing, detained, and in some cases deported, because they lacked documents the state itself had failed to provide or had destroyed. The scandal forced the resignation of Home Secretary Amber Rudd, a compensation scheme, and the Williams "Lessons Learned" review, which found institutional ignorance and thoughtlessness toward race. The Home Office subsequently rebranded the approach "compliant environment."
