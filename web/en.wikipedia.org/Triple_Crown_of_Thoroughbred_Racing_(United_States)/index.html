Triple Crown of Thoroughbred Racing (United States)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Triple Crown of Thoroughbred Racing, often shortened to the Triple Crown, is a series of horse races for three-year-old Thoroughbreds, consisting of the Kentucky Derby, Preakness Stakes, and Belmont Stakes. The three races were inaugurated in different years: the Belmont in 1867, the Preakness in 1873, and the Kentucky Derby in 1875. The series is marketed by Triple Crown Productions, LLC.

History

The term "Triple Crown" was popularized in connection with the American races by sportswriter Charles Hatton after Gallant Fox won the three races in 1930; it was later adopted officially. Only thirteen horses have won the American Triple Crown. Sir Barton was the first in 1919, and Justify was the most recent in 2018, following American Pharoah's 2015 triumph that ended a 37-year drought dating back to Affirmed in 1978. Other winners include War Admiral (1937), Whirlaway (1941), Count Fleet (1943), Assault (1946), Citation (1948), Secretariat (1973), and Seattle Slew (1977).

The races

The Kentucky Derby is run on the first Saturday in May at Churchill Downs in Louisville, Kentucky, over 1¼ miles. The Preakness follows two weeks later at Pimlico Race Course in Baltimore, Maryland, over 1 3/16 miles. The Belmont Stakes, the longest and oldest of the three, is held five weeks after the Derby at Belmont Park in Elmont, New York, over 1½ miles — a distance that earned it the nickname "Test of the Champion."

Near misses

Many horses have won two of the three races and fallen short in the third, including I'll Have Another (2012), who was scratched from the Belmont with an injury, and California Chrome (2014). Fillies may compete in the series, and three — Regret, Genuine Risk, and Winning Colors — have won the Kentucky Derby.
