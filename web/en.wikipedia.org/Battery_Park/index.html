Battery Park

Battery Park, officially The Battery, is a 25-acre (10 ha) public park located at the southern tip of Manhattan in New York City, facing New York Harbor. The park takes its name from the artillery batteries that were positioned there in the early years of the city to protect the settlement behind them.

History

The area was the site of Fort Amsterdam under Dutch rule and later British Fort George. Castle Clinton, built in 1808–1811 as the West Battery for the War of 1812, later served as the Castle Garden theatre and, from 1855 to 1890, as the city's immigrant landing depot, processing some eight million immigrants before Ellis Island opened. The park was enlarged with landfill, including material excavated for the World Trade Center in the 1970s, which also created neighbouring Battery Park City.

Features

Ferries to the Statue of Liberty and Ellis Island depart from the park. It contains numerous memorials, including the East Coast Memorial to American servicemen who died in the Atlantic in the Second World War, The Sphere (the Fritz Koenig sculpture recovered from the World Trade Center site), and the Hope Garden. The Battery Conservancy, founded in 1994 by Warrie Price, has restored the park's gardens and waterfront promenade.
