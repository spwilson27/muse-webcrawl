Treaty of Versailles

Treaty of Versailles
Signed: 28 June 1919
Location: Palace of Versailles, France
Effective: 10 January 1920

The Treaty of Versailles was the peace treaty between Germany and the Allied Powers that officially ended the state of war of the First World War. It was signed on 28 June 1919 in the Hall of Mirrors at the Palace of Versailles, exactly five years after the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand.

Terms

Germany accepted responsibility for the war in the "war guilt" clause (Article 231), agreed to pay reparations, lost territory including Alsace-Lorraine to France and land to a reborn Poland, surrendered its colonies, and saw its army limited to 100,000 men with the Rhineland demilitarized. The treaty also created the League of Nations.

Reception

In Germany the treaty was widely denounced as a Diktat. The US Senate refused to ratify it, and the United States made a separate peace with Germany in 1921. Historians continue to debate the treaty's role in the origins of the Second World War.
