Fourteen Points

Fourteen Points
Author: Woodrow Wilson
Delivered: 8 January 1918
Purpose: US war aims and peace principles

The Fourteen Points were a statement of principles for peace, outlined by US President Woodrow Wilson in an address to Congress on 8 January 1918, to be used in negotiations to end the First World War.

The points

The points called for open diplomacy, freedom of the seas, removal of economic barriers, reduction of armaments, impartial adjustment of colonial claims, evacuation of occupied territories, restoration of Belgium, return of Alsace-Lorraine to France, readjustment of Italy's frontiers, autonomous development for the peoples of Austria-Hungary and the Ottoman Empire, an independent Poland with access to the sea, and — in the fourteenth point — a general association of nations, which became the League of Nations.

Legacy

Germany agreed to the Armistice of November 1918 partly on the basis of the Fourteen Points, but the Treaty of Versailles departed substantially from them, contributing to German resentment in the interwar years.
