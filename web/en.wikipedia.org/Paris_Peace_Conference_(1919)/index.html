Paris Peace Conference (1919)

The Paris Peace Conference was an international meeting of the victorious Allied powers held in Paris in 1919, after the end of the First World War, to set the peace terms for the defeated Central Powers.

Proceedings

The conference opened on 18 January 1919 and was dominated by the "Big Four": Georges Clemenceau of France, David Lloyd George of the United Kingdom, Woodrow Wilson of the United States, and Vittorio Emanuele Orlando of Italy. The defeated nations were not permitted to negotiate. Wilson promoted his Fourteen Points, including the creation of a League of Nations, while France sought security guarantees and reparations from Germany.

Treaties

The conference produced five treaties, most notably the Treaty of Versailles with Germany, signed on 28 June 1919 in the Hall of Mirrors at the Palace of Versailles. Other treaties settled terms with Austria (Saint-Germain), Bulgaria (Neuilly), Hungary (Trianon), and the Ottoman Empire (Sèvres). The settlement redrew the map of Europe and the Middle East, created the League of Nations, and imposed reparations and territorial losses on Germany — terms whose consequences shaped the politics of the following decades.
