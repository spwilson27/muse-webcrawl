League of Nations

League of Nations
Founded: 10 January 1920
Dissolved: 18 April 1946
Headquarters: Geneva, Switzerland
Successor: United Nations

The League of Nations was the first worldwide intergovernmental organisation whose principal mission was to maintain world peace. Founded on 10 January 1920 by the Paris Peace Conference that ended the First World War, it was headquartered in Geneva.

Structure

The League worked through an Assembly of all member states, a smaller Council dominated by the great powers, and a permanent Secretariat. Attached bodies included the Permanent Court of International Justice and the International Labour Organization. At its peak in the mid-1930s it had 58 members — though the United States, despite President Wilson's advocacy, never joined.

Record and dissolution

The League settled some minor disputes in the 1920s but failed to prevent Japanese aggression in Manchuria, the Italian invasion of Ethiopia, or the slide into the Second World War. In 1946 it dissolved itself and transferred its assets to the new United Nations.
