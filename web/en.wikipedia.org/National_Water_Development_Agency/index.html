National Water Development Agency

The National Water Development Agency (NWDA) is an autonomous agency of the Government of India under the Ministry of Jal Shakti. It was set up on 17 July 1982 as a society under the Societies Registration Act, 1860, to study and promote the optimum utilisation of India's water resources through inter-basin transfer of water.

Functions

The NWDA's principal functions include:

  • Carrying out detailed surveys and investigations of possible reservoir sites and interconnecting channels for the peninsular rivers development component of the National Perspective Plan.
  • Preparing feasibility reports and detailed project reports (DPRs) for inter-state river-linking proposals.
  • Undertaking studies for the Himalayan rivers development component, added to its mandate in 1990–91.
  • Providing technical support to state governments on water-transfer proposals and intra-state links.

National Perspective Plan

The National Perspective Plan (NPP), formulated in 1980, envisages inter-basin water transfer in two components: the Himalayan rivers component and the peninsular rivers component. Together they identify about 30 major link proposals intended to transfer water from surplus basins to water-deficit regions, augmenting irrigation potential and drinking-water supply and moderating floods and droughts.

Notable projects

The Ken–Betwa link project in Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh became the first inter-linking of rivers project under the NPP to reach the implementation stage, with a cooperation agreement among the Union government and the two states signed in 2021. Other priority links studied by the NWDA include the Par–Tapi–Narmada, Damanganga–Pinjal, Parbati–Kalisindh–Chambal and Godavari–Krishna links.

Organisation

The agency is headed by a Director General and has its headquarters in New Delhi, with field offices and investigation circles across the country.
