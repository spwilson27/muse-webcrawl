Quirinal Palace

Quirinal Palace (Palazzo del Quirinale)
Location: Quirinal Hill, Rome, Italy
Built: from 1583
Current use: Official residence of the President of Italy

The Quirinal Palace (Italian: Palazzo del Quirinale) is a historic building in Rome, Italy, standing on the Quirinal Hill, the highest of the seven hills of Rome. It has been the residence in turn of thirty popes, four kings of Italy and the presidents of the Italian Republic; since 1946 it has been the official residence of the President of Italy.

History

The palace was begun in 1583 under Pope Gregory XIII, who intended it as a summer residence removed from the humidity and smells of the Vatican. Successive popes enlarged it with the work of architects including Domenico Fontana, Flaminio Ponzio, Carlo Maderno and Gian Lorenzo Bernini. It became the principal papal residence, and four conclaves were held there.

After the capture of Rome in 1870, the palace was confiscated by the Kingdom of Italy and became the royal residence of the House of Savoy. Victor Emmanuel III abdicated in the palace in 1946; following the referendum that abolished the monarchy, it passed to the new Republic.

The palace today

At roughly 110,500 square metres, the Quirinal is among the largest presidential palaces in the world — some twenty times the size of the White House. Its gardens date to the seventeenth century, and its interiors preserve frescoes, tapestries and furnishings from its papal and royal periods. Parts of the palace and gardens are open to visitors by reservation.
