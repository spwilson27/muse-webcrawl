Functionalism (international relations)

Functionalism is a theory of international relations that arose during the interwar period, principally from the strong concern about the obsolescence of the state as a form of social organisation. Its leading proponent was David Mitrany, whose A Working Peace System (1943) proposed building peace through international agencies managing technical, non-political functions.

Principles

Mitrany's maxim 'form follows function' held that needs should determine each agency's scope; successful cooperation in welfare and technical areas would spill over, overlay political divisions with a web of shared activities, and gradually transfer loyalties.

Neofunctionalism

Ernst Haas adapted functionalism to regional integration, adding political actors, interest groups and supranational entrepreneurship — a more political theory applied to the European Communities.
