European Economic Community

The European Economic Community (EEC), sometimes called the Common Market, was a regional organisation created by the Treaty of Rome (1957) to bring about economic integration among its member states. It became the European Community and later the central pillar of the European Union.

Development

The six founding members — France, West Germany, Italy and Benelux — built a customs union (completed 1968) and common policies, notably the Common Agricultural Policy. The United Kingdom, Ireland and Denmark joined in 1973, followed by Mediterranean enlargements in the 1980s.

Institutions and legacy

The Single European Act (1986) set the goal of a single market by 1992. The Maastricht Treaty (1993) absorbed the EEC as the European Community pillar of the EU; the Lisbon Treaty later folded it fully into the Union.
