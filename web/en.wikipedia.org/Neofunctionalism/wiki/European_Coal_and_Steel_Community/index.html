European Coal and Steel Community

The European Coal and Steel Community (ECSC) was a European organisation created by the Treaty of Paris (1951) that united France, West Germany, Italy, Belgium, the Netherlands and Luxembourg in a common market for coal and steel, administered by supranational institutions. It was the first step toward European integration and a model for neofunctionalist theory.

ECSC
Established23 July 1952 (Treaty of Paris, 1951)
MembersFrance, West Germany, Italy, Benelux
Expired23 July 2002

History

Proposed in Robert Schuman's declaration of 9 May 1950 and designed by Jean Monnet, the ECSC pooled war-making industries to make war between France and Germany 'materially impossible'. Its High Authority, Common Assembly, Council and Court of Justice prefigured EU institutions.

Legacy

The ECSC merged its executives with the EEC in 1967 and expired in 2002, its assets passing to the European Community.
