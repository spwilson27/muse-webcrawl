Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards

The Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards (FMVSS) are regulations promulgated by the U.S. National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) that prescribe design, construction, performance and durability requirements for motor vehicles and motor vehicle equipment sold in the United States. They are codified at Title 49 of the Code of Federal Regulations, Part 571.

History

The standards were authorised by the National Traffic and Motor Vehicle Safety Act of 1966, enacted after growing public concern over road deaths, spurred in part by Ralph Nader's Unsafe at Any Speed. The first standards took effect for the 1968 model year and covered items such as seat belts, energy-absorbing steering columns and side marker lamps.

Organisation

The FMVSS are grouped in series: the 100-series covers crash avoidance (lighting, brakes, tyres, controls), the 200-series covers crashworthiness (seat belts, head restraints, flammability), and the 300-series covers post-crash standards (fuel system integrity, school bus safety). Manufacturers self-certify compliance, subject to NHTSA testing and recall authority.
