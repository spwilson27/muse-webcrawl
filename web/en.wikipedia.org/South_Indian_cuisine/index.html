South Indian cuisine

South Indian cuisine comprises the cuisines of the five southern states of India — Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka, Kerala, and Tamil Nadu — together with the union territories of Puducherry and Lakshadweep. It is characterised by rice as a staple, the use of lentils, coconut, tamarind, curry leaves, mustard seeds, and dried red chillies, and a profusion of fermented rice-and-dal batters.

Common dishes

The best-known dishes internationally are the dosa (a crisp fermented crepe, often filled with spiced potato as masala dosa), idli (steamed rice cakes), vada (savoury fried doughnuts of urad dal), and uttapam (a thick pancake with toppings), typically served with sambar (a lentil and vegetable stew) and coconut chutney. Meals are traditionally served on a banana leaf.

Regional varieties

Tamil cuisine includes Chettinad cooking with freshly ground spices and pongal; Kerala cuisine features coconut milk, seafood, puttu, and the sadya feast; Andhra and Telangana cuisines are famed for fiery chillies, pickles (avakaya), and Hyderabadi biryani; Karnataka contributes bisi bele bath, neer dosa, and the Udupi tradition of temple vegetarian cooking that inspired the ubiquitous "Udupi hotels". Filter coffee, frothed by pouring between vessels, is the classic drink.
