In abstract algebra, a discrete valuation ring (DVR) is a principal ideal domain (PID) with exactly one non-zero maximal ideal. Equivalently, it is a local ring whose maximal ideal is principal and which is Noetherian and integrally closed of dimension one. Every DVR carries a discrete valuation: a surjective function v from the non-zero elements of its field of fractions to the integers, satisfying v(xy) = v(x) + v(y) and v(x + y) ≥ min(v(x), v(y)).

Examples

The localisation of the integers at a prime p — the ring of rational numbers whose denominator is not divisible by p — is a DVR, with uniformiser p. The ring of p-adic integers ℤp is a DVR. In algebraic geometry, the local ring of a smooth point on an algebraic curve is a DVR; the valuation measures the order of vanishing of a function at the point. Formal power series rings k[[t]] over a field are DVRs with uniformiser t.

Structure

A DVR has a unique (up to multiplication by a unit) irreducible element π, called a uniformiser or uniformising parameter. Every non-zero element can be written uniquely as uπn with u a unit and n = v(x) ≥ 0. The ideals of the ring are exactly the powers of the maximal ideal (π), forming a single descending chain. Complete DVRs are classified in part by their residue field: a complete DVR with residue field of characteristic zero is isomorphic to a power series ring, while in mixed characteristic the theory leads to Witt vectors.

Role in number theory

Dedekind domains, such as rings of algebraic integers, localise at every non-zero prime ideal to a DVR. This reduces many global questions in algebraic number theory — factorisation of ideals, ramification, and decomposition of primes — to the well-understood local behaviour in discrete valuation rings.
