The Secret History is the first novel by the American author Donna Tartt, published by Alfred A. Knopf in September 1992. Set in New England, the novel tells the story of a closely knit group of six classics students at an elite college who, under the influence of their charismatic professor, discover a way of thinking and living far removed from ordinary life — and murder one of their own.

Plot

Narrator Richard Papen joins Julian Morrow's Greek class at Hampden College. The group attempts a Dionysian bacchanal during which Henry Winter and others kill a local farmer; their friend Bunny Corcoran blackmails them and is murdered by the group, an inverted detective structure revealing the killers from the start.

Themes and reception

The novel explores beauty, Dionysian frenzy, elitism and moral decay. A bestseller on publication, it became a foundational text of the "dark academia" aesthetic and remains highly influential.

Background

Tartt began the novel at Bennington College, on which Hampden is partly modelled.
