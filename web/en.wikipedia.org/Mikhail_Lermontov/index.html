Mikhail Lermontov

Born15 October 1814, Moscow, Russian Empire
Died27 July 1841 (aged 26), Pyatigorsk, Russian Empire
OccupationPoet, novelist, playwright, officer
Notable worksA Hero of Our Time, "Borodino", "The Demon"

Mikhail Yuryevich Lermontov (15 October 1814 – 27 July 1841) was a Russian Romantic writer, poet, and painter, sometimes called "the poet of the Caucasus." After Alexander Pushkin's death in 1837, Lermontov became the foremost poet in Russia; his novel A Hero of Our Time (1840) is a founding work of Russian psychological prose. He was killed in a duel at the age of 26, mirroring Pushkin's fate.

Life

Raised by his grandmother at Tarkhany estate, Lermontov entered the cavalry school in St Petersburg and joined a hussar regiment. His poem "Death of the Poet" (1837), blaming the court for Pushkin's death, led to exile in the Caucasus, where he served in campaigns against the mountain peoples and found the landscape that pervades his work. A duel in 1840 brought a second exile; the next year, in Pyatigorsk, a quarrel with retired officer Nikolai Martynov ended in the fatal duel.

Works

Lermontov's verse — including "Borodino," "The Sail," "The Demon," and "Mtsyri" — combines Byronic rebellion with precision of form. A Hero of Our Time, built from linked novellas narrated from shifting viewpoints, introduced the "superfluous man" Pechorin, one of Russian literature's defining anti-heroes. Lermontov was also an accomplished watercolorist of Caucasian scenes.

Legacy

Alongside Pushkin, Lermontov is credited with creating the modern Russian literary language, and his influence runs through Russian poetry, prose, and music down to the present day.
