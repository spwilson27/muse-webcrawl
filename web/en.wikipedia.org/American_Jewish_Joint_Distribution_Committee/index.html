American Jewish Joint Distribution Committee

The American Jewish Joint Distribution Committee, commonly called the Joint or the JDC, is a Jewish relief organisation based in New York City. Founded in 1914 to aid Jews suffering in Palestine and Eastern Europe during the First World War, it is the largest international Jewish humanitarian organisation, active in more than 70 countries.

History

The JDC was created by the merger of three relief committees: the American Jewish Relief Committee, the Central Relief Committee, and the People's Relief Committee. It raised funds from American Jews, principally with the backing of banker Felix Warburg. Between the wars it supported Jewish communities in Eastern Europe and helped establish Agro-Joint, which settled Soviet Jews in agricultural colonies. During and after the Second World War, the JDC provided food, medicine, and emigration assistance to Holocaust survivors in displaced persons camps, and later helped resettle Jews from the Soviet Union, Ethiopia, and the Balkans.

Work today

Today the JDC provides welfare services to elderly Jews in the former Soviet Union and Eastern Europe, supports Jewish community renewal, runs the JDC-Israel institute, and delivers non-sectarian disaster relief worldwide through its International Development Program, including responses to earthquakes, tsunamis, and refugee crises.
