Thriller (genre)

Thriller is a genre of fiction, film, television and drama with numerous overlapping subgenres, characterised by suspense, tension, excitement and anticipation. Thriller narratives make readers or viewers uncertain, anxious and "on the edge of their seats" as the plot builds toward a climax, often using red herrings, cliffhangers and twists, and commonly placing a protagonist against an antagonist whose threat must be overcome.

Characteristics

Thrillers emphasise mood and pacing as much as plot. The protagonist is typically an ordinary person — or a professional such as a detective, spy or soldier — thrust into danger, while the antagonist is a powerful, concealed or psychologically disturbed adversary. Subgenres include the psychological thriller, crime thriller, spy thriller, techno-thriller, legal thriller, medical thriller and conspiracy thriller; thrillers frequently blend with horror, mystery and action, differing from horror in aiming primarily at suspense rather than shock or fear.

History and examples

The genre descends from epics such as the Odyssey and from Gothic and adventure fiction; early landmarks include John Buchan's The Thirty-Nine Steps (1915) and the spy novels of Eric Ambler and Graham Greene. Alfred Hitchcock defined the thriller film with works such as The 39 Steps, Rear Window, Vertigo and North by Northwest. Modern masters of the thriller novel include Patricia Highsmith, Frederick Forsyth, John le Carré, Gillian Flynn and Lee Child, and the genre remains among the most commercially successful in publishing and cinema.
