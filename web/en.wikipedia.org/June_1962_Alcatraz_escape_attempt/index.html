June 1962 Alcatraz escape attempt

Alcatraz Federal Penitentiary, from which Morris and the Anglin brothers escaped in 1962.

The June 1962 Alcatraz escape attempt was an escape from Alcatraz Federal Penitentiary in San Francisco, California, carried out by inmates Frank Morris and brothers John and Clarence Anglin on the night of June 11, 1962. The three men were never found, and their ultimate fate remains unknown.

The plan

Over many months the men widened the vent holes in their cell walls using spoons and a makeshift drill, concealing the work with painted cardboard. They fashioned dummy heads from plaster, paint, and real human hair, which they left in their beds to fool guards during the night count. In a utility corridor behind their cells they built a raft and life vests from more than fifty stolen raincoats, and assembled a concertina-based pump to inflate the raft.

The escape

On the night of June 11 the men climbed through a vent to the cellhouse roof, descended to the ground, and launched their raft from the island's northeastern shore. A fourth conspirator, Allen West, failed to get out of his cell in time and was left behind. The escape was not discovered until the following morning.

Investigation and aftermath

The FBI investigated for 17 years before closing the case in 1979, concluding the men most likely drowned in the cold, swift currents of San Francisco Bay. Debris including a paddle and a wallet wrapped in plastic was recovered. The U.S. Marshals Service maintains an open case, and the escapees' wanted posters remained active. The escape was dramatized in the 1979 film Escape from Alcatraz starring Clint Eastwood. Theories and alleged evidence of survival have continued to surface for decades.
