Maracanã Stadium (Portuguese: Estádio do Maracanã), officially Estádio Jornalista Mário Filho, is an association football stadium in the Maracanã neighborhood of Rio de Janeiro, Brazil. Owned by the state of Rio de Janeiro, it is operated jointly by the rival clubs Flamengo and Fluminense. When it opened in 1950 to host the FIFA World Cup, it was the largest stadium in the world, with an official capacity near 200,000 including standing room.

History

The stadium's most famous match was the climax of the 1950 World Cup, when Uruguay defeated Brazil 2–1 before an estimated crowd of nearly 200,000 — a shock known in Brazil as the Maracanaço. The Maracanã went on to host Pelé's thousandth career goal in 1969, the 2007 Pan American Games, the 2013 Confederations Cup final, and the final of the 2014 FIFA World Cup, in which Germany beat Argentina 1–0. Its capacity was progressively reduced by conversion to all-seater configuration and major reconstructions for the 2014 World Cup and the 2016 Summer Olympics, for which it hosted the opening and closing ceremonies.

Concerts and other events

Beyond football, the stadium has hosted huge concerts — Frank Sinatra, Paul McCartney, the Rolling Stones, and Madonna among them — and a 1980 mass celebrated by Pope John Paul II. The record concert attendance of about 184,000 was set by Tina Turner and Paul McCartney in the late 1980s and early 1990s.
