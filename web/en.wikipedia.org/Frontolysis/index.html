Frontolysis

In meteorology, frontolysis is the dissipation or weakening of an atmospheric front. It is the opposite of frontogenesis, the tightening of temperature gradients that creates or strengthens a front.

Process

A front weakens when the horizontal temperature gradient across it decreases. This can happen when the wind field deforms the temperature pattern so as to spread isotherms apart, when the contrasting air masses are modified toward similar properties (for example by surface heating or by passage over water), or when the parent low-pressure system fills and its circulation decays. As frontolysis proceeds, associated cloud and precipitation diminish and the wind shift across the boundary becomes less marked.

Significance

Forecasters track frontolysis on surface analysis charts to predict the decay of rain bands and the end of frontal weather. Fronts often undergo alternating frontogenesis and frontolysis as they interact with upper-level troughs and jet streaks, so a front that weakens in one region may redevelop downstream.
