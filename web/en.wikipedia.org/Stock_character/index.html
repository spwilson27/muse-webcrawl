Stock character

A stock character, also known as a character archetype, is a type of character in a narrative (e.g. a novel, play, television show, or film) whom audiences recognize from frequent recurrences in a particular literary tradition. Stock characters rely on cultural types or characteristics — such as the femme fatale, the absent-minded professor, or the damsel in distress — that are instantly recognizable to members of a given culture.

History

Stock characters date to ancient Greek and Roman theatre. The plays of Plautus and Terence employed recurring types such as the braggart soldier (miles gloriosus) and the clever slave. The Italian commedia dell'arte systematized masked stock figures including Pantalone, Il Dottore, Arlecchino, and the innamorati, whose improvisations shaped European comedy for centuries.

Examples

Common stock characters include the hero, the villain, the mentor, the sidekick, the love interest, the femme fatale, the mad scientist, the hardboiled detective, and the girl next door. In genre fiction they provide economical storytelling: a few cues establish a character the audience already half-knows, though critics note the risk of stereotype and cliché.

Related concepts

Stock characters differ from archetypes in being culturally specific rather than universal. See also archetype, commedia dell'arte, and character.
