St Helens, Merseyside

St Helens
County: Merseyside (historically Lancashire)
Country: England
Population: about 100,000
Known for: Glassmaking, rugby league

St Helens is a town in Merseyside, England, historically part of Lancashire. It grew rapidly during the Industrial Revolution as a centre for coal mining and glass manufacturing.

Industry

The town became a major centre of the British glass industry, home to Pilkington, founded in 1826 and developer of the float glass process. Coal, chemicals and copper smelting also shaped its growth, aided by the Sankey Canal and early railways.

Sport

St Helens is famous for its rugby league club, St Helens R.F.C. ("Saints"), one of the most successful clubs in British rugby league history.
