Failure mode and effects analysis

Failure mode and effects analysis (FMEA) is a systematic, step-by-step approach for identifying all possible failures in a design, a manufacturing or assembly process, or a product or service. "Failure modes" are the ways in which something might fail; "effects analysis" studies the consequences of those failures.

Method

A cross-functional team lists potential failure modes for each component or process step, their causes and their effects. Each mode is scored for severity, occurrence (likelihood) and detection (ability to catch the failure before it reaches the customer), typically on 1–10 scales. The product of the three scores gives a risk priority number (RPN), used to rank corrective actions. Design FMEA and Process FMEA are the common variants.

History and use

FMEA procedures were formalized by the U.S. military in the 1940s (MIL-P-1629), adopted by NASA for the Apollo program, and taken up by the automotive industry in the 1970s — notably after the Ford Pinto affair — becoming a standard quality tool in automotive, aerospace, medical device and other safety-critical industries.
