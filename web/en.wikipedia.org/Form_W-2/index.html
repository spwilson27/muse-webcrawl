Form W-2

Form W-2 reports an employee's annual wages and withheld taxes.

Form W-2, officially the Wage and Tax Statement, is a United States federal tax form issued by employers to each employee and to the Social Security Administration. It reports the employee's annual wages and tips, federal, state and local income tax withheld, and Social Security and Medicare wages and withholding, so that the employee can complete an accurate income tax return on Form 1040.

Contents

Key numbered boxes include Box 1 (wages, tips and other compensation subject to federal income tax), Box 2 (federal income tax withheld), Boxes 3–6 (Social Security wages and tax, Medicare wages and tax), and Box 12, which carries letter codes for items such as 401(k) elective deferrals (code D) and health savings account contributions (code W). Box 10 reports dependent care benefits, and Boxes 16–20 report state and local wage and tax information.

Filing and distribution

Employers must furnish copies to employees by January 31 following the tax year and file Copy A with the Social Security Administration, which shares the data with the IRS. Employees receive several copies for federal, state and personal records. Employers with many forms file summaries on Form W-3. An employer that discovers an error files a correction on Form W-2c.

History

The form dates to the introduction of income tax withholding under the Current Tax Payment Act of 1943, when employers first became responsible for deducting tax from wages at source.
