New Laws

The New Laws (Leyes Nuevas), formally the Laws and Ordinances Newly Made for the Government of the Indies and Good Treatment and Preservation of the Indians, were issued on 20 November 1542 by King Charles I of Spain to reform the encomienda system and protect the indigenous peoples of the Americas.

Provisions

Influenced by the campaigning of Bartolomé de las Casas, the laws forbade the enslavement of Native Americans, prohibited new encomiendas, and ordered that existing encomiendas revert to the Crown on the death of the holder.

Resistance

Enforcement provoked fierce resistance among settlers; in Peru it triggered a revolt led by Gonzalo Pizarro in which the viceroy was killed. The Crown retreated on key clauses in 1545, but the New Laws remained an important landmark in the history of indigenous rights and Spanish colonial administration.
