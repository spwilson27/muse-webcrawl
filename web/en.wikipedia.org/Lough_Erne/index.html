Lough Erne

Lough Erne (from Irish Loch Éirne) refers to two connected lakes in County Fermanagh, Northern Ireland — Upper Lough Erne and Lower Lough Erne — formed by the River Erne. Together they make up the second-largest lake system in Northern Ireland and the fourth largest in Ireland, studded with more than 150 islands. The town of Enniskillen sits on an island between the two lakes.

Geography

Lower Lough Erne, the broader northern lake, covers about 110 km² and contains many islands, including Devenish Island with its well-preserved monastic site and round tower, and Boa Island with its carved stone figures. The Upper Lough is a maze of channels and smaller islands to the south. The Erne flows northwest from the Lower Lough to enter the Atlantic near Ballyshannon. The Shannon–Erne Waterway links the system to the River Shannon, creating a navigable route across Ireland.

History

The lakeland is rich in early Christian and medieval remains. During the Second World War, RAF Castle Archdale on Lower Lough Erne hosted flying boats that patrolled the Atlantic; a corridor of Irish airspace (the "Donegal corridor") allowed them shorter routes to the ocean.
