Cholesterol is a waxy, fat-like substance found in every cell of the body. It is a sterol — a type of lipid molecule — biosynthesised by all animal cells and an essential structural component of animal cell membranes, where it modulates membrane fluidity and permeability. Cholesterol also serves as a precursor for the biosynthesis of steroid hormones, bile acids, and vitamin D.

Physiology

Cholesterol is synthesised mainly in the liver through the mevalonate pathway, whose rate-limiting enzyme, HMG-CoA reductase, is the target of statin drugs. The body obtains cholesterol both from synthesis and from the diet. Because cholesterol is insoluble in blood, it is transported within lipoproteins — chiefly low-density lipoprotein (LDL), which carries it from the liver to tissues, and high-density lipoprotein (HDL), which returns it to the liver.

Function

Within membranes, cholesterol fills gaps between phospholipid tails, stiffening the membrane while keeping it flexible across temperatures. It is also concentrated in lipid rafts involved in cell signalling. As a precursor molecule it is converted into bile acids and salts for fat digestion, steroid hormones such as cortisol, testosterone and oestrogen, and vitamin D.

Clinical significance

High levels of LDL cholesterol in the blood (hypercholesterolaemia) are strongly associated with atherosclerosis — the build-up of cholesterol-rich plaques in arteries — and with increased risk of coronary heart disease and stroke. Total blood cholesterol is typically considered desirable below 200 mg/dL (5.2 mmol/L). Management includes diet, exercise, statins, ezetimibe, and PCSK9 inhibitors. Rare genetic disorders such as familial hypercholesterolaemia cause severely elevated LDL from birth.

History

Cholesterol was first identified in solid form in gallstones in 1784 by François Poulletier de la Salle; its name derives from the Greek cholē (bile) and stereos (solid). Its structure was largely worked out by the early 20th century, earning Nobel Prizes for Adolf Windaus and Heinrich Wieland.
