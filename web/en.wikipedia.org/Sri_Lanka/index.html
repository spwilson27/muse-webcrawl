Sri Lanka, historically known as Ceylon, is an island country in South Asia, lying in the Indian Ocean southwest of the Bay of Bengal and separated from India by the Palk Strait and the Gulf of Mannar. It is home to about 22 million people of Sinhalese, Tamil, Moor, Burgher and other communities.

History

Sri Lanka has documented history spanning more than two millennia. According to chronicles such as the Mahāvaṃsa, Indo-Aryan settlers arrived in the first millennium BC; Buddhism was introduced in the third century BC and became central to the Sinhalese kingdoms of Anuradhapura and later Polonnaruwa, whose irrigation works and stupas remain monumental. Tamil kingdoms and traders linked the north and east to South India. From 1505 the Portuguese, then the Dutch, controlled the coasts; the British took the maritime provinces in 1796 and the Kandyan kingdom in 1815, unifying the island as British Ceylon. Independence came peacefully in 1948; the country became the republic of Sri Lanka in 1972.

Tensions between the Sinhalese majority and Tamil minority escalated into a civil war (1983–2009) against the separatist LTTE ("Tamil Tigers"), which ended with the government's military victory amid grave civilian suffering. In 2004 the Boxing Day tsunami killed more than 30,000 people in Sri Lanka. In 2022 an economic crisis — the worst since independence — triggered mass protests (the Aragalaya) that forced President Gotabaya Rajapaksa from office.

Geography and economy

The island rises from coastal plains to a central highland massif, where tea estates cover the hills around Nuwara Eliya and the sacred city of Kandy. Sri Lanka is famed for Ceylon tea, cinnamon and other spices, gems, garments and tourism; its ancient cities, the rock fortress of Sigiriya among them, are UNESCO World Heritage Sites. The island's rich literary tradition includes the Sanskrit epic heritage shared with India — the Ramayana places its antagonist Ravana's kingdom, Lanka, on the island.
