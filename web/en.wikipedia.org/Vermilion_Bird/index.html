Vermilion Bird

The Vermilion Bird (Chinese: 朱雀; pinyin: Zhūquè) is one of the Four Symbols of the Chinese constellations, representing the south and the season of summer. It is one of the four cardinal guardian creatures of Chinese cosmology, alongside the Azure Dragon of the East, the White Tiger of the West, and the Black Tortoise of the North.

Astronomy

In Chinese astronomy, the Vermilion Bird comprises the seven lunar mansions of the southern palace of the sky: Well, Ghosts, Willow, Star, Extended Net, Wings, and Chariot. Together the twenty-eight mansions divide the ecliptic for tracking the Moon and planets.

Mythology and symbolism

The Vermilion Bird is depicted as an elegant, flame-red bird resembling a pheasant or peacock with five-coloured plumage, and is associated with the element of fire. Although sometimes confused with the Fenghuang, the two are distinct creatures. It appears in tomb murals from the Han dynasty onward, guarding the southern wall, and figures in feng shui and in the layout of palaces and capitals, which traditionally faced south with the Vermilion Bird avenue before them. The creature's name was also used in titles, gates (such as the Vermilion Bird Gate of ancient Chang'an), and later in popular culture throughout East Asia, including in Korean, Japanese (Suzaku), and Vietnamese traditions.
