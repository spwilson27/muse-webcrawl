Maria II of Portugal

Maria II
Reign: 28 May 1826 – 23 June 1828; 26 May 1834 – 15 November 1853
Born: 4 April 1819, Rio de Janeiro, Brazil
Died: 15 November 1853 (aged 34), Lisbon
Spouses: Auguste de Beauharnais; Ferdinand of Saxe-Coburg and Gotha
House: Braganza

Maria II (Maria da Glória Joana Carlota Leopoldina; 4 April 1819 – 15 November 1853), also known as "the Educator," was Queen of Portugal from 1826 to 1828 and again from 1834 until her death. She was the first Brazilian-born monarch of a European country.

First reign and the Liberal Wars

Maria inherited the Portuguese crown when her father, Pedro IV (Pedro I of Brazil), abdicated in her favour in 1826, intending that she marry her uncle Miguel, who would rule as regent. Miguel instead proclaimed himself king in 1828, sparking the Liberal Wars. Pedro returned to Europe, defeated Miguel, and restored his daughter in 1834, dying shortly afterwards.

Second reign

Declared of age at fifteen, Maria married Auguste de Beauharnais, who died within months, and then Ferdinand of Saxe-Coburg and Gotha, a consort of ability and taste who became king consort as Ferdinand II. Her reign saw repeated political turmoil — the Belenzada, the Maria da Fonte revolt and the Patuleia civil war — alongside genuine reforms: a public health law prompted by cholera, expansion of roads and telegraphs, and the founding of schools. Her government put down the last great absolutist resistance and stabilised constitutional monarchy.

Death

Maria, whose health was worn by eleven pregnancies, died in childbirth in 1853 giving birth to a stillborn son. She was succeeded by her eldest son, Pedro V.
