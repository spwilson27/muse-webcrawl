William Jackson Hooker

Sir William Jackson Hooker (6 July 1785 – 12 August 1865) was an English botanist and botanical illustrator who became the first director of the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew, transforming them into a world centre for plant science. His son, Joseph Dalton Hooker, succeeded him at Kew.

Career

Born in Norwich, Hooker inherited an income that let him devote himself to botany, travelling to Iceland in 1809 and publishing on its flora. In 1820 he became Regius Professor of Botany at the University of Glasgow, where he built an influential teaching collection and published works including British Jungermanniae, Flora Scotica, and the multi-volume Icones Plantarum, illustrating thousands of new species. When the gardens at Kew were transferred to the state in 1841, Hooker was appointed their first director; over 24 years he vastly expanded the grounds, opened the great Palm House (with architect Decimus Burton and iron founder Richard Turner), founded the Museum of Economic Botany, and organized a global network of collectors.

Knighted in 1836, Hooker died at Kew in 1865. The standard author abbreviation Hook. is used for plant names he described.
