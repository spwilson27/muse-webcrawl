Georgia Totto O'Keeffe (15 November 1887 – 6 March 1986) was an American modernist painter, celebrated for her dramatically enlarged paintings of flowers, her views of New York skyscrapers, and her stark landscapes and bones of the New Mexico desert. She has been called the "Mother of American modernism".

Early life and career

O'Keeffe was born on a farm near Sun Prairie, Wisconsin. She studied at the School of the Art Institute of Chicago and the Art Students League of New York. In 1916 the photographer and gallery owner Alfred Stieglitz exhibited her abstract charcoal drawings in New York, beginning a professional and personal relationship: they married in 1924. Stieglitz championed her work and organised annual exhibitions that made her one of the best-known American artists of the 1920s.

New Mexico

From 1929 O'Keeffe spent part of nearly every year in New Mexico, drawn to the desert light, the adobe architecture and the bones bleached by the sun, and she moved there permanently in 1949 after Stieglitz's death. Her homes at Ghost Ranch and in Abiquiú became the centres of her late work. In 2014 her painting Jimson Weed/White Flower No. 1 sold for $44.4 million, then a record for a work by a woman artist.

Legacy

The Georgia O'Keeffe Museum opened in Santa Fe in 1997. She received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1977 and the National Medal of Arts in 1985. She painted until failing eyesight forced her to stop in the early 1970s, and died at 98 in Santa Fe.
