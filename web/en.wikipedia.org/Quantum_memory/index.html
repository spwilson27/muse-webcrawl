Quantum memory

In quantum computing, quantum memory is the quantum-mechanical version of ordinary computer memory: a system that can store the quantum state of photons or other carriers of quantum information and retrieve it later on demand, preserving superposition and entanglement. Quantum memories are essential components of quantum repeaters and long-distance quantum communication.

Approaches

Common physical platforms include ensembles of atoms in atomic vapours (using electromagnetically induced transparency or Raman protocols), rare-earth-doped crystals, and single atoms in optical cavities. Performance is measured by storage efficiency, storage time, fidelity and bandwidth; solid-state memories have demonstrated storage times from milliseconds to, in specially engineered systems, much longer.

Applications

Quantum memories synchronize probabilistic operations in quantum networks, enable quantum repeaters that extend entanglement over long distances, and enhance single-photon sources and quantum sensors.
