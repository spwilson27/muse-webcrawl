Metafiction

Metafiction is a form of fiction that emphasises its own narrative structure in a way that deliberately draws attention to its status as an artefact. A metafictional work reminds the reader that what they are reading is a constructed story, often through devices such as a narrator who addresses the reader directly, characters who become aware that they are fictional, stories within stories, or commentary on the process of writing itself.

History

Although the term was coined by the American critic William H. Gass in 1970, the practice is much older. Miguel de Cervantes' Don Quixote (1605/1615) plays with manuscripts, false authors and characters who have read the first part of their own story. Laurence Sterne's Tristram Shandy (1759–1767) foregrounds the act of narration with digressions, blank pages and typographic jokes. Modernist and postmodernist writers expanded the repertoire: Jorge Luis Borges wrote stories about imaginary books; John Barth, Italo Calvino, Vladimir Nabokov and Kurt Vonnegut all made self-reference a central technique. Calvino's If on a winter's night a traveler begins by addressing "you, the reader" as you start the very book you are holding.

Techniques

Common techniques include breaking the fourth wall, unreliable or self-conscious narrators, footnotes that contradict the main text, alternate endings, characters reading the book they appear in, and explicit discussion of fiction's artificiality. The literary scholar Patricia Waugh described metafiction as writing which "self-consciously and systematically draws attention to its status as an artefact in order to pose questions about the relationship between fiction and reality".

Significance

Metafiction is closely associated with postmodern literature and its scepticism toward grand narratives and stable meaning. Critics have praised it for honesty about the nature of storytelling, while others have charged it with narcissism and exhaustion of the novel form. Related self-referential forms occur in other media, including metacinema, metatheatre (as in Pirandello's Six Characters in Search of an Author) and self-aware video games.
