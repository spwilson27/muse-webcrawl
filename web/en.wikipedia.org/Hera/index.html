Hera (Greek: Ἥρα) is the Greek goddess of marriage, women, family, and childbirth and the queen of the gods of Mount Olympus. Daughter of the Titans Cronus and Rhea, she is the wife and sister of Zeus, and in mythology is characterised both by her majesty as queen of heaven and by her jealous persecution of Zeus's many lovers and their children.

Mythology

Swallowed by Cronus at birth along with her siblings, Hera was freed when Zeus forced their father to disgorge them. As Zeus's queen she features throughout Greek myth: she persecuted Heracles, Zeus's son by Alcmene, from before his birth; she championed the Greeks in the Trojan War, enraged that Paris had judged Aphrodite fairer than her; and she pursued Io, Dionysus, and Leto, among many others. Myths also show her power in her own right — she periodically renews her virginity by bathing in the spring of Canathus, and by some accounts bore Hephaestus alone in retaliation for Zeus producing Athena from his own head.

Cult

Hera's principal sanctuaries included the Heraion of Argos, the great temple on Samos, and her precinct at Olympia, where the Heraean Games — footraces for women — were held in her honour. Her Roman counterpart is Juno.
