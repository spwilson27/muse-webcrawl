Castor (star)

Castor (Alpha Geminorum)
Constellation: Gemini
Apparent magnitude: 1.93 (combined)
Distance: about 51 light-years
System: sextuple star

Castor is the second-brightest object in the zodiac constellation of Gemini, after Pollux. It bears the Bayer designation Alpha Geminorum. Although it appears as a single star to the naked eye, Castor is a sextuple star system composed of three binary pairs gravitationally bound together.

The system

The two brightest components, Castor A and Castor B, are A-type main-sequence stars each accompanied by a faint red dwarf companion, and orbit each other with a period of roughly 460 years. A third, much wider pair, Castor C (also known as YY Geminorum), is an eclipsing binary of two red dwarfs that orbits the inner system over tens of thousands of years.

Observation and name

The star is named for Castor, one of the Dioscuri twins of Greek mythology, whose brother is represented by the neighbouring star Pollux. Castor's multiple nature was progressively revealed from the 18th century onwards, making it a classic object in the study of multiple star systems.
