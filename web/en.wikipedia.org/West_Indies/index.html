West Indies

West Indies
Region: Caribbean, North Atlantic
Subregions: Greater Antilles, Lesser Antilles, Lucayan Archipelago
First European contact: 1492 (Christopher Columbus)

The West Indies is a region of the North Atlantic Ocean and the Caribbean that includes the island countries and surrounding waters of the Antilles and the Lucayan Archipelago. The term originated with European explorers, who, seeking a route to India, believed they had reached the Indies when they landed in the Americas in 1492.

Geography

The West Indies is conventionally divided into three groups: the Greater Antilles (including Cuba, Hispaniola, Jamaica and Puerto Rico), the Lesser Antilles (the Leeward and Windward island arcs), and the Lucayan Archipelago (the Bahamas and the Turks and Caicos Islands).

History

Before European contact the islands were inhabited by Indigenous peoples including the Taíno, Kalina (Caribs) and Ciboney. Spanish colonisation was followed by French, British, Dutch and Danish settlement, plantation economies based on sugar and the labour of enslaved Africans, and eventually independence movements across the twentieth century. The short-lived West Indies Federation (1958–1962) attempted political union of the British Caribbean colonies.

Culture

The region is known for its music, including calypso, reggae, soca and dancehall; for its creole languages; and for the West Indies cricket team, which unites players from across the Caribbean nations and dominated world cricket in the 1970s and 1980s.
