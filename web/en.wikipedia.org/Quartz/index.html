Quartz is a hard, crystalline mineral composed of silica (silicon dioxide, SiO2). It is the second most abundant mineral in Earth's continental crust, after feldspar. Quartz exists in two forms: normal α-quartz and high-temperature β-quartz, which transform at 573 °C. Its atoms are linked in a continuous framework of SiO4 tetrahedra, giving the mineral its hardness of 7 on the Mohs scale, its lack of cleavage, and its conchoidal fracture.

Rock crystal, the colourless variety of quartz

Varieties

Quartz occurs in many coloured varieties, several of which are used as gemstones. Macrocrystalline varieties include rock crystal (clear), amethyst (purple), citrine (yellow), rose quartz (pink), smoky quartz (brown to black), and milky quartz (white). Microcrystalline varieties, collectively called chalcedony, include agate, onyx, carnelian, and jasper. Impurities and irradiation produce most of the colours; amethyst's purple, for example, comes from trace iron and natural radiation.

Occurrence

Quartz is a defining constituent of granite and other felsic igneous rocks, and is common in sedimentary rocks such as sandstone and shale, and in metamorphic rocks including quartzite. Because it is resistant to chemical weathering, it accumulates in soils and beach sands. Large crystals form in pegmatites and hydrothermal veins; notable deposits occur in Brazil, Madagascar, the Alps, and Arkansas in the United States.

Properties and uses

Quartz is piezoelectric: it generates an electric charge under mechanical stress and vibrates at a precise resonant frequency when a voltage is applied. This property makes quartz crystals invaluable as oscillators in clocks and watches, radios, and electronic circuits, a use pioneered after the First World War. Because natural crystals of electronic quality are scarce, most industrial quartz is grown synthetically by the hydrothermal process.

Quartz sand is the principal raw material of glass and is used in foundry casting, abrasives, ceramics, and construction. Silica is also the feedstock for silicon metal used in semiconductors and solar cells, though high-purity quartz for crucibles is a specialized product.

Health note

Inhalation of fine respirable crystalline silica dust, generated when cutting or grinding quartz-bearing stone, can cause the lung disease silicosis, which is regulated in workplaces worldwide.
