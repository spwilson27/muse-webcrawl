Lin-Manuel Miranda (born January 16, 1980) is an American songwriter, actor, singer, filmmaker, and playwright. He created the Broadway musicals In the Heights (2008) and Hamilton (2015), and the soundtracks for the animated films Moana (2016) and Encanto (2021). His awards include a Pulitzer Prize for Drama, three Tony Awards, three Grammy Awards, an Emmy Award, and a MacArthur "Genius" Fellowship.

Early life

Miranda was born in New York City to parents of Puerto Rican descent and raised in the Inwood neighbourhood of Manhattan. He attended Hunter College High School and Wesleyan University, where he began writing what became In the Heights.

Career

In the Heights, set in the Dominican-American neighbourhood of Washington Heights, premiered on Broadway in 2008 and won four Tony Awards, including Best Musical. Miranda then read Ron Chernow's biography of Alexander Hamilton on vacation and spent years developing Hamilton, which opened on Broadway in August 2015 to extraordinary acclaim. Blending hip-hop, R&B and traditional show tunes with colour-conscious casting, Hamilton won 11 Tony Awards and the 2016 Pulitzer Prize for Drama.

For Disney, Miranda wrote songs for Moana, including "How Far I'll Go", and for Encanto, including "We Don't Talk About Bruno", which became a chart-topping hit in 2022. He directed the film Tick, Tick... Boom! (2021), played Jack in Mary Poppins Returns (2018), and has acted in television series including His Dark Materials and House. He is also a founder of the freestyle rap group Freestyle Love Supreme and an advocate for Puerto Rico, raising funds after Hurricane Maria.
