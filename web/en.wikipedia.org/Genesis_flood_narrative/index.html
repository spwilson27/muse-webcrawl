Genesis flood narrative

The Genesis flood narrative (chapters 6–9 of the Book of Genesis) is the Hebrew version of a widespread ancient flood myth. It tells how God, seeing the wickedness and corruption of humankind, decides to return the earth to its pre-creation state of watery chaos and remake it through the microcosm of Noah's Ark.

Summary

God instructs Noah, "a righteous man, blameless in his generation", to build an ark of gopher wood and to bring aboard his family and pairs of every animal (in one strand of the text, seven pairs of clean animals). Rain falls for forty days and nights and the waters of the heavens and the great deep cover the earth, rising above the mountains; every living thing on land dies. After the waters recede — the whole episode lasting about a year — the ark comes to rest on the mountains of Ararat. Noah sends out a raven and then a dove to test whether the ground is dry. God then makes a covenant with Noah and all living creatures, promising never again to destroy the earth by flood, and sets the rainbow as the sign of the covenant.

Sources and parallels

Scholars have long noted that the narrative appears to weave together two sources, the Yahwist (J) and the Priestly source (P), which differ in details such as the number of animals and the duration of the flood. The story closely parallels earlier Mesopotamian flood myths, especially the account of Utnapishtim in the Epic of Gilgamesh (tablet XI) and the Sumerian flood story, suggesting a shared ancient Near Eastern tradition.

Interpretation

Jewish and Christian traditions read the narrative as a story of judgment and grace. Flood geology and literalist readings have been rejected by mainstream geology and biology; most scholars treat the narrative as mythic or theological history rather than an account of a literal global event.
