Post-industrial society

A post-industrial society is a stage of society's development when the service sector generates more wealth than the manufacturing sector of the economy. The term was popularized by sociologist Daniel Bell in his 1973 book The Coming of Post-Industrial Society, building on earlier work by Alain Touraine.

Characteristics

According to Bell, a post-industrial society is marked by a shift from manufacturing to services, the centrality of theoretical knowledge as a source of innovation, the growing importance of information technology, and the rise of professional and technical occupations. Value depends increasingly on knowledge and information rather than on raw materials and physical labour, a transition later described with terms such as "information society" and "knowledge economy".

Criticism

Critics note that manufacturing has often been relocated rather than eliminated, that service work includes much low-wage employment, and that the concept can understate continuing industrial production's importance to material life and global inequality.
