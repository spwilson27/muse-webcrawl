Nankai Trough

The Nankai Trough (南海トラフ, Nankai Torafu) is a submarine trough located south of the Nankaidō region of Japan's island of Honshū, extending approximately 900 km (560 mi) off the Pacific coast. It marks the subduction zone where the Philippine Sea Plate slides beneath the Eurasian (Amurian) Plate, and it is the source of the great Nankai megathrust earthquakes.

Earthquakes

The trough is divided into segments (from west to east: Nankai, Tōnankai and Tōkai) that have ruptured in large earthquakes at intervals of roughly 100–200 years, including the 1707 Hōei earthquake (estimated magnitude 8.4–8.6, rupturing the whole trough), the 1854 Ansei Tōkai and Nankai earthquakes, the 1944 Tōnankai and 1946 Nankaidō earthquakes. The still-unruptured Tōkai segment has been the focus of Japan's earthquake-prediction programme since the 1970s. Japanese government estimates give a high probability (around 70–80%) of a magnitude 8–9 Nankai Trough earthquake within 30 years, with worst-case scenarios projecting enormous casualties and damage across central and western Japan, and large tsunamis.

Research

The trough is a major site for scientific ocean drilling by the vessel Chikyū under the Integrated Ocean Drilling Program's NanTroSEIZE project, which aims to drill into the seismogenic zone, and for seafloor observatory networks such as DONET that monitor crustal deformation and seismicity.
