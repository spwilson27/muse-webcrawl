Glossary of golf

Golf has an extensive specialized vocabulary.

The following is a glossary of golf terms, including equipment, scoring, course features, and common expressions.

Albatross
A score of three strokes under par on a single hole; also called a double eagle.
Birdie
A score of one stroke under par on a hole.
Bogey
A score of one stroke over par on a hole. Two over is a double bogey, three over a triple bogey.
Bunker
A hazard filled with sand, typically near the fairway or green.
Caddie
A person who carries a player's clubs and offers advice during a round.
Chip
A short, low shot played from near the green.
Divot
The piece of turf displaced by a shot, or the resulting scar in the ground.
Dogleg
A hole whose fairway bends sharply left or right.
Drive
The first shot from the tee on a longer hole, usually played with a driver.
Eagle
A score of two strokes under par on a hole.
Fairway
The closely mown area between the tee and the green.
Fore!
A warning shout given when a ball is struck toward other people.
Green
The area of very short grass surrounding the hole, where putting takes place.
Handicap
A numerical measure of a golfer's ability used to level competition.
Hazard
An obstacle such as a bunker or penalty area (water).
Hole in one
A ball holed with a single stroke from the tee; also called an ace.
Links
A seaside course built on sandy coastal land, in the original Scottish style.
Mulligan
An informal, unofficial second attempt at a shot, taken without penalty, usually only on the first tee among friends.
Par
The standard number of strokes an expert golfer is expected to need for a hole or round.
Putt
A shot played on the green with a putter, rolling the ball along the ground.
Rough
The longer, thicker grass bordering the fairway.
Slice / Hook
Shots that curve sharply right or left (for a right-handed player) respectively.
Stroke play
Competition decided by total number of strokes; contrasted with match play.
Tee
The area from which the first shot of a hole is played; also the small peg supporting the ball.
