Meazza, Italy's greatest pre-war footballer, gives his name to Milan's San Siro stadium.

Giuseppe Meazza (23 August 1910 – 21 August 1979) was an Italian footballer widely regarded as one of the greatest players of the pre-war era and Italy's finest of his generation. Nicknamed il Balilla after a popular children's opera character, he spent most of his career with Inter Milan (then called Ambrosiana-Inter) and captained Italy to victory at the 1938 World Cup, having also won the 1934 tournament. Milan's San Siro stadium is officially named the Stadio Giuseppe Meazza in his honour.

Club career

Rejected by AC Milan as a youth for his slight build, Meazza joined Inter and debuted at 17 in 1927, scoring twice. Playing first as a centre-forward and later as an inside-forward, he was Serie A's top scorer three times and led Inter to league titles in 1930, 1938 and 1940 and the Coppa Italia in 1939. Celebrated for his dribbling, feints and the flamboyant "gol alla Meazza" — rounding the goalkeeper after inviting him to commit — he later played for AC Milan, Juventus, Varese and Atalanta before returning to Inter for a final season in 1946–47. His Serie A goals total, around 242, kept him among the league's all-time top scorers for decades.

International career

Meazza earned 53 caps for Italy between 1930 and 1939, scoring 33 goals — for many years the national record. He played in the 1934 World Cup won on home soil, and by 1938 in France he was captain of Vittorio Pozzo's side, which retained the trophy, beating Hungary 4–2 in the final. He and Giovanni Ferrari are among the few players to have won two World Cups in that era.

Style and legacy

Small, quick and technically gifted, Meazza combined creativity with a showman's flair, famously scoring after dribbling from midfield and, in one celebrated anecdote, with his bootlaces undone. After retiring he coached, including a spell with Inter and work with Italian youth teams. He died in 1979, two days before his 69th birthday. In 1980 the city of Milan renamed San Siro the Stadio Giuseppe Meazza.
