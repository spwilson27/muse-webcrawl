Tales of Mother Goose

Histoires ou contes du temps passé, avec des moralitez (English: Histories or Tales from Past Times, with Morals), better known as Tales of Mother Goose (Les Contes de ma mère l'Oye), is a collection of literary fairy tales written by Charles Perrault and published in Paris in 1697. It is one of the most famous collections of fairy tales ever published and established many tales in the forms in which they are still retold today.

Contents

The collection contains eight prose tales:

  • "Sleeping Beauty" (La Belle au bois dormant)
  • "Little Red Riding Hood" (Le Petit Chaperon rouge)
  • "Bluebeard" (La Barbe bleue)
  • "Puss in Boots" (Le Maître chat ou le Chat botté)
  • "Cinderella" (Cendrillon)
  • "Riquet with the Tuft" (Riquet à la houppe)
  • "Little Thumb" (Le Petit Poucet)
  • "Diamonds and Toads" (Les Fées)

Most tales close with one or two rhymed morals. Three verse tales from Perrault's earlier works were added in later editions.

Publication and attribution

The book appeared in 1697 under the name of Perrault's youngest son, Pierre Perrault Darmancour, though scholars generally credit Charles Perrault as the author. The subtitle "Tales of Mother Goose" referred to the archetypal storytelling peasant woman, and the figure of Mother Goose later became associated in English with nursery rhymes as a whole.

Influence

Perrault's tales drew on oral folklore but were written for the sophisticated audience of the French court salons. The collection popularised the literary fairy tale as a genre, influenced the Brothers Grimm, and its versions of Cinderella, Sleeping Beauty, and Puss in Boots remain the basis for countless adaptations in theatre, ballet, and film.
