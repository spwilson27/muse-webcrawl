Music of Ghana

The music of Ghana includes traditional drumming and dance traditions of its many ethnic groups — among them the Akan, Ewe and Ga — and modern popular forms, above all highlife, which spread across West Africa, and the more recent hiplife fusion of highlife with hip hop.

Traditional music

Ghanaian traditional music is dominated by percussion ensembles: the Akan fontomfrom and atumpan "talking drums", the Ewe master-drum ensembles of dances such as agbadza, and the seprewa harp-lute and mbira-like instruments of older court traditions. The gyil xylophone is central to the music of the northwest. Drumming, singing and dance are inseparable and serve social, religious and ceremonial functions.

Highlife and popular music

Highlife arose in the early 20th century in coastal towns, blending local rhythms with brass bands, palm-wine guitar styles and imported dance music. Pioneers such as E. T. Mensah and later stars including Osibisa, Nana Ampadu and George Darko made it Ghana's defining popular genre. Since the 1990s, hiplife — pioneered by Reggie Rockstone — gospel music and Afrobeats-influenced sounds have dominated youth culture, while contemporary Ghanaian artists achieve international reach.
