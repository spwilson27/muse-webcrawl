Hamas

Hamas
Founded: 1987
Ideology: Palestinian nationalism, Islamism
Headquarters: Gaza City / Doha

Hamas, officially the Islamic Resistance Movement, is a Palestinian Sunni Islamist political and militant organisation founded in 1987 during the First Intifada. Its name is an Arabic acronym for Harakat al-Muqawama al-Islamiyya ("Islamic Resistance Movement") and also echoes the Arabic word for "zeal".

History

Hamas grew out of the Palestinian branch of the Muslim Brotherhood, whose Gaza networks were built by Sheikh Ahmed Yassin. Its 1988 charter called for the establishment of an Islamic state in the whole of mandate Palestine; a 2017 policy document accepted a Palestinian state on the 1967 lines as a formula of national consensus while still rejecting recognition of Israel.

Hamas won the 2006 Palestinian legislative elections and, after a violent split with Fatah in 2007, took sole control of the Gaza Strip, which it governed until the war that followed its October 2023 attack on Israel. The organisation has an armed wing, the Izz ad-Din al-Qassam Brigades, and is designated a terrorist organisation by Israel, the United States, the European Union, the United Kingdom, and other states.

Structure

Leadership is divided between an internal leadership in Gaza and an external political bureau, long based in Damascus and later Doha. Hamas also runs an extensive social-services network of schools, clinics, and charities known as dawah.
