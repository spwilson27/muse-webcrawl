Line integral

In mathematics, a line integral is an integral where the function to be integrated is evaluated along a curve. The terms path integral, curve integral and curvilinear integral are also used.

The function may be a scalar field or a vector field. The value of a scalar line integral is the integral of the field along the curve weighted by arc length, independent of parametrisation. A vector line integral such as the work done by a force field depends on direction; for a gradient field it is path independent, a consequence of the fundamental theorem for line integrals.

Vector calculus

For a curve C parametrised by r(t), a ≤ t ≤ b, the scalar integral is ∫C f ds = ∫ab f(r(t)) |r′(t)| dt. The vector integral ∫C F · dr sums the tangential component of F along the path.

Applications

Line integrals are used in physics for work, circulation and flux, and in complex analysis, where contour integrals generalise them to the complex plane.
