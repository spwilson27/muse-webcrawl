Districts of the Democratic Republic of the Congo

The provinces of the Democratic Republic of the Congo were formerly divided into districts, which in turn were divided into territories. Under the country's earlier administrative organization, there were several dozen districts grouped within the former eleven provinces.

History

The district structure dates to the colonial Congo Free State and Belgian Congo, when districts served as intermediate units of administration between the provinces and the territories. After independence in 1960, the Mobutu-era state retained districts within its provinces (later called regions). Examples included districts such as Haut-Uélé and Bas-Uélé in Orientale Province, Sankuru and Kabinda in Kasaï-Oriental, and Nord-Ubangi and Sud-Ubangi in Équateur.

Reform

The 2006 constitution provided for a new division of the country into 26 provinces. When this découpage was finally implemented in 2015, most former districts were elevated into provinces in their own right, and the district ceased to be a standard tier of Congolese administration; territories remain as the subdivision below the province, and the city of Kinshasa has a distinct status with its own communes.
