Zombie (computing)

In computing, a zombie is a computer connected to the Internet that has been compromised by a hacker, computer virus or trojan horse program and can be used to perform malicious tasks under remote direction. Most owners of "zombie" computers are unaware that their system is being used in this way. Because the owner tends to be unaware, these computers are metaphorically compared to zombies. A coordinated set of zombie machines is called a botnet.

Botnets and attacks

Zombie computers are used to send email spam — historically, the majority of the world's spam — to host phishing and malware sites, to steal credentials and banking data, and to launch denial-of-service attacks, in which thousands of machines flood a target with traffic simultaneously. Well-known botnets have included Storm, Conficker, Zeus and Mirai, the latter built largely from compromised Internet-of-things devices.

Detection and prevention

Zombies are typically infected through drive-by downloads, malicious attachments or unpatched vulnerabilities. Detection relies on antivirus software, network traffic monitoring and takedowns of command-and-control servers by security researchers and law enforcement; prevention involves patching, firewalls and user caution with unsolicited attachments and downloads.

Related meanings

The term also describes a zombie process in Unix-like operating systems — a process that has finished executing but whose exit status has not yet been collected by its parent process.
