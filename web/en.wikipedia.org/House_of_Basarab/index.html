House of Basarab

The House of Basarab (also known as the Basarabi dynasty) was a ruling dynasty of Wallachia, a historical and geographical region of Romania. It was founded by Basarab I, who became the first independent ruler of Wallachia in the early 14th century after breaking away from the Kingdom of Hungary.

The Basarab dynasty ruled Wallachia from the 14th to the 17th century.

The dynasty takes its name from Basarab I (died 1352), who consolidated the voivodeship south of the Carpathians and defeated Charles I of Hungary at the Battle of Posada in 1330, securing Wallachian independence. The name Basarab is believed to be of Cuman origin, possibly meaning "father ruler" or "conquering father".

History

The Basarabs ruled Wallachia, with interruptions and rival branches, for roughly three centuries. Over time the house split into two competing branches: the Dănești, descended from Dan I, and the Drăculești, descended from Mircea the Elder. Their rivalry produced long periods of instability and frequent changes of ruler, as each branch contested the throne, often with Ottoman or Hungarian backing.

Famous members of the dynasty include Mircea the Elder, who resisted Ottoman expansion; Vlad II Dracul and his son Vlad III, known as Vlad the Impaler; Radu the Great; and Neagoe Basarab, a patron of culture and author of the Teachings addressed to his son Theodosius. Later rulers such as Matei Basarab in the 17th century were also of Basarab lineage.

Legacy

The region of Bessarabia in neighbouring Moldavia derives its name from the Basarab dynasty, reflecting the family's early influence north of the Danube. Many Wallachian monasteries, churches, and charters are associated with Basarab princes.
