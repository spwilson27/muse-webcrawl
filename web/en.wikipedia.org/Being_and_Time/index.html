Being and Time (German: Sein und Zeit) is the 1927 masterpiece of German philosopher Martin Heidegger and one of the most influential works of 20th-century philosophy. In it Heidegger seeks to recover the question of the meaning of Being (Sein), which he argued Western philosophy had forgotten since Plato.

Contents

Heidegger approaches Being through an analysis of Dasein—the being of human existence. He describes Dasein as "being-in-the-world", characterised by thrownness, projection, care (Sorge), being-toward-death, and temporality as the horizon of Being. Everyday existence tends toward inauthenticity, absorbed in "the They" (das Man), while authenticity involves owning one's finitude and possibilities.

Influence

The book profoundly shaped existentialism, hermeneutics, deconstruction and theology; Sartre's Being and Nothingness responds directly to it. Only the first two divisions of the projected work were published, and Heidegger's later philosophy took a different path in the so-called "turn" (Kehre).
