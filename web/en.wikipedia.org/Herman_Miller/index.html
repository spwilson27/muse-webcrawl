Herman Miller

This article is about the furniture company. For the American screenwriter, see Herman Miller (writer). For the furniture company formerly known as MillerKnoll's predecessor, see below.

Herman Miller, Inc. was an American company that produced office furniture, equipment, and home furnishings. Founded in 1905 in Zeeland, Michigan, it became one of the most influential manufacturers of modernist furniture in the world. In 2021, Herman Miller acquired Knoll, Inc. and the combined company was renamed MillerKnoll, Inc., with Herman Miller continuing as one of its principal brands.

History

The company began in 1905 as the Michigan Star Furniture Company. In 1923, clerk-turned-president D. J. De Pree and his father-in-law, Herman Miller, purchased a majority of the stock, and De Pree renamed the company after Miller. Initially a maker of traditional bedroom suites, the company nearly failed during the Great Depression.

In 1931 De Pree hired designer Gilbert Rohde, who steered the firm toward modernism. Rohde was succeeded in 1947 by George Nelson as design director; Nelson brought in Charles and Ray Eames, Isamu Noguchi, and Alexander Girard, and the company produced icons of mid-century modern design, including the Eames Lounge Chair and Ottoman (1956), the Noguchi table, and the marshmallow sofa. In 1964 it introduced the Action Office system, designed by Robert Propst, which gave rise to the office cubicle. The Aeron chair (1994), designed by Don Chadwick and Bill Stumpf, became one of the best-selling office chairs in history and is held in the Museum of Modern Art's permanent collection.

MillerKnoll

Herman Miller went public in 1970. In April 2021 it announced the acquisition of rival design firm Knoll for $1.8 billion; the transaction closed in July 2021 and the combined company adopted the name MillerKnoll. Its brands include Herman Miller, Knoll, Hay, Design Within Reach, Muuto, Geiger, and Maharam.

Notable products and designers

  • Eames Lounge Chair and Ottoman (Charles and Ray Eames, 1956)
  • Aeron chair (Chadwick and Stumpf, 1994)
  • Action Office (Robert Propst, 1964)
  • Marshmallow sofa (George Nelson Associates, 1956)
  • Noguchi table (Isamu Noguchi, 1947)
  • Equa, Ergon, Embody, and Cosm seating lines

Herman Miller is also noted for its environmental sustainability programmes, its "Living Office" research, and an employee-owned corporate culture influenced by De Pree's leadership writings and those of his son, Max De Pree.
