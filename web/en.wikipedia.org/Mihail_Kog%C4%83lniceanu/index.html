Mihail Kogălniceanu (September 6, 1817 – July 1, 1891) was a Moldavian, later Romanian, statesman, historian, writer, journalist, and publisher. A leading figure of the Romanian national revival, he served as Prime Minister of Romania under Prince Alexandru Ioan Cuza and was the principal architect of the sweeping reforms of the 1860s, including the secularization of monastery estates and the land reform of 1864.

Early life and career

Kogălniceanu was born in Iași into a boyar family and educated in France and at the University of Berlin, where he studied history and came under the influence of German scholarly methods. Returning to Moldavia, he founded the literary journal Dacia Literară (1840), a landmark of Romanian cultural nationalism, and published the Histoire de la Valachie, de la Moldavie et des Valaques transdanubiens (1837), the first modern history of the Romanians. He took part in the Moldavian revolutionary movement of 1848 and was a prominent advocate of the union of Moldavia and Wallachia, achieved in 1859.

Prime minister and reforms

As Cuza's prime minister from 1863 to 1865, Kogălniceanu pushed through the secularization of the estates of monasteries dedicated to foreign holy places, a new civil code, compulsory primary education, and the Rural Law of 1864, which abolished serfdom-like obligations and distributed land to peasant households. He later served as foreign minister and was Romania's envoy in Paris, where he died in 1891. The Black Sea port town of Mihail Kogălniceanu and Constanța's international airport are named in his honour.
