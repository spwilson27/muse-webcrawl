Opéra Bastille

LocationPlace de la Bastille, Paris, France
ArchitectCarlos Ott
OpenedJuly 13, 1989
Capacity2,723 (Grand Salle)
CompanyParis Opera

The Opéra Bastille is a modern opera house in the 12th arrondissement of Paris, France, and the principal venue of the Paris Opera, sharing productions with the older Palais Garnier. It stands on the Place de la Bastille, on the site of the former Gare de la Bastille railway station.

History

The project was launched by President François Mitterrand as one of his Grands Projets, intended to create a "modern and popular" opera house with advanced stage technology and large workshops on site. Uruguayan-Canadian architect Carlos Ott won the 1983 design competition. The building was inaugurated on July 13, 1989, for the bicentennial of the storming of the Bastille, with a gala concert conducted by Georges Prêtre; regular opera performances began in 1990.

Building

The building's curved glass-and-granite facade echoes the outline of the Colonne de Juillet nearby. Inside, the Grand Salle seats 2,723 in a fan-shaped, contemporary auditorium finished in dark materials and Breton granite. The complex includes a smaller amphitheatre and the Studio Bastille, together with set-building and costume workshops, rehearsal rooms, and a movable stage system designed to allow rapid changes between productions.

Reception

The Opéra Bastille has been praised for its stage machinery and sightlines but criticized for its exterior appearance and for early acoustic and construction problems, including falling facade tiles that required protective netting. Together with the Palais Garnier, it hosts the Paris Opera's full season of opera and ballet.
