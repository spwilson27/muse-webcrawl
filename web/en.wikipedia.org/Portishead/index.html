Portishead are an English band formed in Bristol in 1991. The band's name is taken from the nearby town of Portishead in Somerset. The group comprises Beth Gibbons (vocals), Geoff Barrow (production, drums) and Adrian Utley (guitar). Portishead are considered one of the pioneers of the trip hop genre, alongside Massive Attack and Tricky.

History

Geoff Barrow, who had worked as a tape operator at Coach House Studios in Bristol and assisted on Massive Attack's Blue Lines, met singer Beth Gibbons in 1991. After making the short film To Kill a Dead Man and its soundtrack, the band signed to Go! Discs.

Dummy (1994)

Their debut album Dummy was released in 1994 to critical acclaim, featuring the singles "Numb", "Sour Times" and "Glory Box". It won the Mercury Prize in 1995 and brought trip hop to a wide international audience. Its sound combined samples from 1960s and 1970s records, scratching, and Gibbons's fragile, melancholic vocals.

Later work

The self-titled second album Portishead (1997) was darker and more abrasive, and the live album Roseland NYC Live followed in 1998. After a long hiatus, the band returned with Third in 2008, which incorporated krautrock and electronic influences and was widely praised.
