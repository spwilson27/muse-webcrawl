K-factor (marketing)

In marketing, the K-factor is a measure of the virality of a product, service or piece of content, borrowed from the epidemiological concept of the basic reproduction number. It describes the rate at which existing users bring in new users through invitations and referrals.

Definition

The K-factor is calculated as K = i × c, where i is the average number of invitations sent by each user and c is the average conversion rate of those invitations. If K is greater than 1, growth is exponential and self-sustaining; if K is less than 1, each generation of users is smaller than the last and growth eventually decays unless supplemented by other acquisition channels.

Related metrics

Viral growth also depends on the viral cycle time — how quickly a new user sends invitations — so a modest K-factor with a short cycle can outgrow a higher K-factor with a long one. Companies optimise K through referral incentives, shareable content and frictionless onboarding, and combine it with paid and organic acquisition in growth models.
