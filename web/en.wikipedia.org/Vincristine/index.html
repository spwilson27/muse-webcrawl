Vincristine

Vincristine
Trade namesOncovin, Vincasar, Marqibo
ClassVinca alkaloid; antimitotic
SourceMadagascar periwinkle (Catharanthus roseus)
RoutesIntravenous only

Vincristine, also known as leurocristine and marketed under the brand name Oncovin among others, is a chemotherapy medication used to treat a number of types of cancer, including acute lymphocytic leukemia, acute myeloid leukemia, Hodgkin disease, neuroblastoma, and non-Hodgkin lymphoma. It is given intravenously.

Mechanism

Like its relative vinblastine, vincristine is a vinca alkaloid that binds tubulin dimers and prevents microtubule polymerisation, halting cell division at metaphase. Its dose-limiting toxicity is neurotoxicity — peripheral neuropathy, jaw pain, constipation and ileus — rather than the myelosuppression more typical of vinblastine. Accidental intrathecal administration causes ascending paralysis and death, so the drug is dispensed in minibags rather than syringes as a safety measure.

Uses

Vincristine is a backbone of paediatric oncology protocols (for example, in acute lymphoblastic leukaemia and Wilms tumour) and of regimens such as CHOP for lymphoma. A liposomal formulation (Marqibo) is approved for certain relapsed leukaemias. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.
