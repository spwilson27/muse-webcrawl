Bicycle commuting

Bicycle commuting (or cycle commuting) is the use of a bicycle to travel from home to a place of work or study. It is a form of utility cycling and active travel, particularly common in countries with dedicated cycling infrastructure, such as the Netherlands and Denmark, where large shares of urban journeys are made by bike.

Benefits

Studies associate regular bicycle commuting with improved cardiovascular fitness, lower risk of heart disease, diabetes and some cancers, and reduced all-cause mortality. For employers and cities, it lowers transport costs, congestion and air pollution; for individuals it is typically cheaper than driving or public transport, though journeys are limited by distance, weather and terrain — constraints partly eased by electric bicycles.

Infrastructure and policy

High rates of cycle commuting are supported by protected cycle lanes, secure bicycle parking, showers and changing facilities at workplaces, bike-share schemes, and financial incentives such as cycle-to-work purchase schemes and mileage allowances. Safety — real and perceived — is the main barrier cited by would-be commuters.

Statistics

In the Netherlands roughly a quarter of all trips are by bicycle; Copenhagen has reported bicycle shares approaching half of commuter journeys to work and education. In most English-speaking countries, commuting shares are far lower, commonly in the low single digits nationally, with higher rates in some university and city-centre areas.
