Triveni Sangam

Triveni Sangam
LocationPrayagraj, Uttar Pradesh, India
RiversGanges, Yamuna, Sarasvati (mythical)
SignificanceSite of the Kumbh Mela

In Hindu tradition, a Triveni Sangam is the confluence of three rivers (tri meaning three and veni meaning streams). The most famous is at Prayagraj (formerly Allahabad), where the Ganges and Yamuna rivers meet the mythical, subterranean Sarasvati. It is the holiest of sangams and the site of the Kumbh Mela, the largest religious gathering in the world.

Religious significance

Bathing at the sangam, especially during the Kumbh Mela and Magh Mela festivals, is believed to wash away sins and liberate devotees from the cycle of rebirth. The visibly different colours of the Ganges (greenish) and Yamuna (clearer) waters can be seen merging at the point of confluence.

Other triveni sangams

Other three-river confluences in India bear the name, including those at Bhagamandala in Karnataka (Kaveri, Kannike and Sujyoti) and at Tirumakudal Narasipura in Karnataka.
