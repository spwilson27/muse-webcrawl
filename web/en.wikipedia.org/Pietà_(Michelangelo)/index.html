Pietà (Michelangelo)

The Pietà is a marble sculpture by Michelangelo Buonarroti, completed in 1498–1499 when the artist was in his early twenties. It depicts the Virgin Mary cradling the body of Jesus after the Crucifixion, and is housed in St. Peter's Basilica, Vatican City. It is the only work Michelangelo ever signed — his name is carved across the sash on Mary's chest.

Description

Carved from a single block of Carrara marble for the French cardinal Jean de Bilhères, the sculpture is renowned for its pyramidal composition and serene, youthful Mary, whose monumental drapery supports Christ's body. Michelangelo explained her youthfulness as a sign of her purity. In 1972 the statue was attacked by Laszlo Toth, who struck it with a hammer; after restoration it was placed behind protective glass.
