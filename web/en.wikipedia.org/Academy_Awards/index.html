The Academy Awards, commonly known as the Oscars, are awards for artistic and technical merit in film, presented annually by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences (AMPAS) in the United States. They are regarded as the most prestigious film awards in the world.

History

The first Academy Awards ceremony was held on 16 May 1929 at the Hollywood Roosevelt Hotel, honouring films of 1927–28; Wings won the top production prize. The gold-plated statuette — a knight holding a crusader's sword standing on a reel of film — was designed by Cedric Gibbons and sculpted by George Stanley; its nickname "Oscar" became official in 1939.

Categories and records

Awards are given in more than twenty competitive categories, including Best Picture, Best Director, the four acting categories, and craft awards. Ben-Hur (1959), Titanic (1997) and The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King (2003) share the record of 11 wins. Walt Disney remains the most decorated individual, with 22 competitive Oscars. Voting is conducted by the Academy's roughly ten thousand members across branches of the industry.
