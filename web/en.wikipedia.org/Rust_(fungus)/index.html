Rusts are plant diseases caused by fungi of the order Pucciniales (formerly Uredinales). They are obligate parasites that infect leaves, stems and sometimes fruits, producing characteristic pustules of powdery spores in rusty shades of orange, yellow, brown or black. The order contains several thousand species affecting many economically important plants.

Life cycle

Many rusts have complex life cycles with up to five spore stages, and some are heteroecious, requiring two unrelated host species — for example, wheat stem rust (Puccinia graminis) alternates between wheat and barberry.

Agricultural impact

Rusts are among the most damaging diseases of cereals and coffee. Wheat stem rust, soybean rust and coffee leaf rust (Hemileia vastatrix) have caused major epidemics; the emergence of the highly virulent stem-rust race Ug99 in 1999 threatened global wheat production and spurred international breeding efforts for resistant varieties. Management combines resistant cultivars, fungicides and, historically, eradication of alternate hosts.
