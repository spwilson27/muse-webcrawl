Édouard Manet (23 January 1832 – 30 April 1883) was a French modernist painter. He was one of the first 19th-century artists to paint modern life, as well as a pivotal figure in the transition from Realism to Impressionism.

Early works

Born into an upper-class household, Manet trained under Thomas Couture. His early masterpieces The Luncheon on the Grass (Le Déjeuner sur l'herbe) and Olympia, both exhibited in 1863–65, created controversy with their frank depictions and bold technique, and are considered watershed paintings that mark the start of modern art.

Later career

Manet's later works, including A Bar at the Folies-Bergère (1882), show the influence of the younger Impressionists, though he never exhibited with them. He died in Paris in 1883 and is now recognised as a foundational figure of modern painting.
