Pasha

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A pasha of high rank, with the horsetails (tugh) that marked his grade

Pasha (Ottoman Turkish: پاشا; Turkish: paşa) was a high rank in the Ottoman political and military system, typically granted to governors, generals and dignitaries. It stood above the rank of bey and agha, and was in origin a title of honour conferred by the sultan personally.

Origins

The etymology of the word is debated; it is commonly connected with the Persian pādshāh ("master king") or with Turkish baş ("head, chief"). In the early Ottoman state the title was borne by members of the ruling family and leading commanders, and over time it became the standard honorific for the empire's senior civil and military officials.

Grades

Pashas were graded by the number of horsetail standards (tughs) they were entitled to display: the grand vizier and the highest commanders rated three tughs, provincial governor-generals (beylerbeyi) two, and lesser pashas one. The governor of Egypt and certain other great officers enjoyed special precedence. In the nineteenth century the title was increasingly systematised within the Tanzimat bureaucracy and the reformed army, where it corresponded roughly to general officer ranks; senior naval officers could hold the corresponding rank of kapudan pasha if commanding the fleet.

In Egypt and after the empire

Under the Muhammad Ali dynasty in Egypt, pasha became the highest title of the Egyptian nobility, borne by princes, ministers and great landowners, and its use continued under the monarchy. The Turkish Republic abolished Ottoman titles in 1934, though "paşa" survived informally as a respectful form of address for generals. In English the word acquired a figurative sense of a lordly or imperious person, and it appears in many surnames and place-names across the former Ottoman world.

Famous pashas

Well-known holders of the title include Ibrahim Pasha of Egypt, the Ottoman admiral Hayreddin Barbarossa (as Kapudan Pasha), the reforming grand vizier Midhat Pasha, the Albanian ruler Ali Pasha of Ioannina, and Mustafa Kemal, who was widely known as Gazi Mustafa Kemal Pasha before the Turkish surname law.
