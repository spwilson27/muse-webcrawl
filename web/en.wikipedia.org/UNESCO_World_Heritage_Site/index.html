A World Heritage Site is a landmark or area with legal protection by an international convention administered by UNESCO. Sites are designated as having cultural, historical, scientific or other significance of "outstanding universal value" to humanity, and are listed under the World Heritage Convention adopted in 1972.

Selection

Countries nominate sites from their tentative lists; the World Heritage Committee, advised by ICOMOS (cultural) and IUCN (natural), inscribes those meeting at least one of ten criteria. Inscription brings prestige, tourism and access to conservation funding, but also obligations to protect the site. Sites whose value is threatened may be placed on the List of World Heritage in Danger, and three have been delisted entirely (in Oman, Germany and the United Kingdom).

Distribution

More than 1,200 sites are listed across some 170 countries. Italy and China have the most (around 59–60 each), followed by Germany, France and Spain. Famous examples include the Great Wall of China, the Pyramids of Giza, the Forbidden City, Machu Picchu, the Grand Canyon and the Great Barrier Reef. In the Netherlands, listed sites include the canals of Amsterdam, the Kinderdijk windmills and the Rietveld Schröder House near Rotterdam's wider region.
