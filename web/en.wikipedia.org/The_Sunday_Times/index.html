The Sunday Times

The Sunday Times is a British Sunday national newspaper, the largest-selling quality newspaper in the United Kingdom. Founded in 1821 as the New Observer and renamed the following year, it has been published by Times Newspapers, now part of News UK, since the 1960s and is the sister paper of The Times, with a separate editorial staff.

History

Under editor Harold Evans in the 1960s and 1970s the paper's Insight team pioneered investigative journalism, campaigning for compensation for thalidomide children and exposing the spy Kim Philby. The paper moved to Wapping in 1986 during the dispute that transformed British newspaper production. Rupert Murdoch's News International acquired the paper in 1981.

Features

The Sunday Times is known for its magazine supplements, its annual Rich List of the wealthiest people in Britain, published since 1989, and for sports and culture coverage. It adopted a digital subscription model alongside its sister paper in the 2010s.
