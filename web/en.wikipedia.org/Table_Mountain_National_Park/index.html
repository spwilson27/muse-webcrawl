Table Mountain National Park

Location: Cape Peninsula, Western Cape, South Africa
Established: 29 May 1998
Area: ~221 km²
Managed by: SANParks
Highest point: Maclear's Beacon, 1,086 m

Table Mountain National Park, previously known as the Cape Peninsula National Park, is a national park in South Africa proclaimed on 29 May 1998. Managed by South African National Parks (SANParks), it protects the mountain chain running the length of the Cape Peninsula, from Signal Hill in the north to Cape Point in the south, together with the surrounding marine protected area.

Sections

The park has three main sections: the Table Mountain section, including the famous flat-topped mountain (1,085 m) overlooking Cape Town, Kirstenbosch's surrounding slopes and the Back Table; the Silvermine–Tokai section in the central peninsula; and the Cape Point section, covering the Cape of Good Hope area. Boulders Beach near Simon's Town, famous for its colony of African penguins, is also part of the park.

Biodiversity

The park lies within the Cape Floristic Region and is dominated by fynbos vegetation, with an exceptional number of endemic plant species. It forms part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site (the Cape Floral Region Protected Areas), and Table Mountain was named one of the New7Wonders of Nature in 2011.
