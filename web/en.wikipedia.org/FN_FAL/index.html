FN FAL

The FN FAL (French: Fusil Automatique Léger, "Light Automatic Rifle") is a battle rifle designed by Dieudonné Saive and manufactured by the Belgian firm FN Herstal. Chambered for the 7.62×51mm NATO cartridge, it was one of the most widely used rifles of the 20th century, adopted by more than 90 countries — a ubiquity that earned it the nickname "the right arm of the Free World".

Design

The FAL is gas-operated with a tilting bolt and a short-stroke piston, and is normally fed from a 20-round detachable box magazine. Variants include the metric-pattern and inch-pattern (Commonwealth L1A1) families, fixed-stock and folding-stock paratrooper models, and heavy-barrel light-machine-gun versions. Most military versions are select-fire or semi-automatic only.

Service

The FAL served as a standard rifle across NATO and allied nations from the 1950s and appeared in numerous Cold War conflicts, including the Falklands War, where both sides used it. Although largely replaced by smaller-calibre assault rifles, it remains in service in several countries.
