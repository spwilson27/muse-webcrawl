Charmat method

The Charmat method, also known as the tank method or Metodo Italiano, is a process for producing sparkling wine in which secondary fermentation takes place in large pressurized stainless steel tanks rather than in individual bottles.

Italian winemaker Federico Martinotti developed the method in 1895; Frenchman Eugène Charmat patented improvements in 1907, giving it its common name. Base wine, sugar and yeast are sealed in a tank; carbon dioxide produced by fermentation dissolves in the wine. The wine is then filtered and bottled under pressure.

The method preserves fresh, fruity aromas and is cheaper and faster than the traditional method used for Champagne. It is standard for Prosecco, many Asti wines, Lambrusco, and other lightly sparkling wines meant to be drunk young.
