National Gallery

Established: 1824

Location: Trafalgar Square, London, United Kingdom

Collection: Over 2,300 paintings, mid-13th century to 1900

Admission: Free

The National Gallery is an art museum in Trafalgar Square in the City of Westminster, in Central London, England. Founded in 1824, it houses a collection of more than 2,300 paintings dating from the mid-13th century to 1900, covering the major European traditions of Western painting. The collection belongs to the British public, and entry to the main collection is free of charge, as it has been since the gallery's founding.

History

The gallery began when the British government purchased 38 paintings from the heirs of the banker John Julius Angerstein in 1824. Unlike many continental national collections formed from royal holdings, the National Gallery was built entirely by purchase and donation for public benefit. It moved to its current building, designed by William Wilkins, in 1838. Major later additions include the Sainsbury Wing (opened 1991), designed by Robert Venturi and Denise Scott Brown, which houses the early Renaissance collection.

Collection

Highlights of the collection include Van Eyck's Arnolfini Portrait, Velázquez's Rokeby Venus, Turner's The Fighting Temeraire, Constable's The Hay Wain, Van Gogh's Sunflowers, works by Leonardo da Vinci (including the Virgin of the Rocks and the Burlington House Cartoon), Michelangelo, Raphael, Titian, Rembrandt, Vermeer, Monet and Cézanne. Paintings after 1900 are largely held by its sister institution, Tate.
