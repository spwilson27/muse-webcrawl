Gayatri Chakravorty Spivak (born 24 February 1942) is an Indian scholar, literary theorist and feminist critic. She is University Professor at Columbia University, the highest rank awarded to only a small number of faculty. Best known for her essay "Can the Subaltern Speak?" (1988), she is considered one of the founders of postcolonial theory.

Life and education

Spivak was born Gayatri Chakravorty in Calcutta, India. She graduated from the University of Calcutta in 1959 with first-class honours in English, and went on to study at Cornell University in the United States, where she completed her MA and her PhD (1967) with a dissertation on W. B. Yeats supervised by Paul de Man.

Work

Spivak rose to prominence with her 1976 translation of Jacques Derrida's Of Grammatology, whose substantial translator's preface introduced deconstruction to a wide Anglophone audience. Her essay "Can the Subaltern Speak?" examines how the voices of the oppressed — the "subaltern", a term drawn from Antonio Gramsci — are systematically silenced, and critiques both Western intellectuals who claim to speak for them and colonial representations such as the British abolition of sati, which she famously glossed as "white men saving brown women from brown men".

Her major books include In Other Worlds (1987), The Post-Colonial Critic (1990), Outside in the Teaching Machine (1993), A Critique of Postcolonial Reason (1999) and Death of a Discipline (2003). She introduced and developed the concept of "strategic essentialism" and has written extensively on feminism, Marxism, deconstruction and globalisation. She is also a celebrated translator of the Bengali writer Mahasweta Devi.

Activism and honours

Spivak has long been involved in rural education, founding schools for rural children in West Bengal. She received the Kyoto Prize in Arts and Philosophy in 2012 and the Padma Bhushan, India's third-highest civilian award, in 2013.
