First Nagorno-Karabakh War
Date20 February 1988 – 12 May 1994
LocationNagorno-Karabakh and surrounding districts, South Caucasus
ResultArmenian victory; Bishkek Protocol ceasefire
BelligerentsNagorno-Karabakh Republic and Armenia; Azerbaijan

The First Nagorno-Karabakh War was an ethnic and territorial conflict that took place from February 1988 to May 1994, in the enclave of Nagorno-Karabakh in southwestern Azerbaijan, between the majority ethnic Armenians of Nagorno-Karabakh backed by Armenia, and the Republic of Azerbaijan with support at various points from Turkey and Afghan mujahideen volunteers. As the war progressed, Armenia and Azerbaijan became entangled in a protracted, undeclared war in the mountainous heights of Karabakh as Azerbaijan attempted to curb the secessionist movement in Nagorno-Karabakh.

Background

Nagorno-Karabakh, an autonomous oblast within the Azerbaijan Soviet Socialist Republic, had a population that was roughly three-quarters ethnic Armenian. In February 1988 the regional soviet voted to transfer the oblast to the Armenian SSR, a demand rejected by Moscow and Baku. The Sumgait pogrom of February 1988 and subsequent violence in Baku and Kirovabad drove tens of thousands of refugees in both directions and set the stage for open warfare as the Soviet Union collapsed.

Course of the war

Full-scale fighting erupted in early 1992 after both republics declared independence. Armenian forces captured Shusha in May 1992 and opened the Lachin corridor linking Nagorno-Karabakh to Armenia. During 1993 Armenian and Karabakh forces seized seven surrounding Azerbaijani districts, including Kelbajar, Agdam, Fizuli, Jabrayil, Qubadli and Zangilan, creating a buffer zone and a large wave of Azerbaijani internally displaced persons.

Ceasefire and aftermath

A Russian-brokered ceasefire, the Bishkek Protocol, was signed in May 1994. By the end of the war, Armenian forces controlled Nagorno-Karabakh and the seven adjacent districts. Estimates of the death toll range from 20,000 to 30,000, and more than a million people were displaced. The unresolved status of the region led to the Second Nagorno-Karabakh War in 2020.
