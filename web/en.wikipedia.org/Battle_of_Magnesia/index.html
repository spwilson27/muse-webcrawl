Battle of Magnesia

The Battle of Magnesia was fought in December 190 BC (or January 189 BC by some reckonings) near Magnesia ad Sipylum in Lydia, western Anatolia, during the Roman–Seleucid War. The army of the Roman Republic, commanded nominally by the consul Lucius Cornelius Scipio (with his brother Scipio Africanus as adviser) and decisively aided by Eumenes II of Pergamon, defeated the much larger army of the Seleucid king Antiochus III the Great. The defeat ended Seleucid power in Asia Minor and established Rome as the dominant power in the eastern Mediterranean.

Course of the battle

Antiochus's cosmopolitan army included a Macedonian-style phalanx in the centre, cataphracts, Galatian infantry, scythed chariots and Arab camel archers. On the Seleucid left, Eumenes II's skirmishers panicked the scythed chariots back into their own cavalry, disordering that wing, which the Romans routed. Antiochus, commanding the right, broke the Roman left and pursued to the Roman camp, which the camp guard repulsed. In the centre, the phalanx formed a defensive square but, assailed by missiles and abandoned by its cavalry, was destroyed when its elephants panicked. By the Treaty of Apamea (188 BC) Antiochus surrendered all territory west of the Taurus Mountains, gave up his elephants and navy, and paid an indemnity of 15,000 talents.
