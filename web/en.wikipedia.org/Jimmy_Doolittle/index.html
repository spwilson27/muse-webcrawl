James Harold Doolittle (14 December 1896 – 27 September 1993) was an American military general and aviation pioneer who received the Medal of Honor for his leadership of the Doolittle Raid, the first American air raid on Tokyo in April 1942. He later commanded the Twelfth, Fifteenth and Eighth Air Forces in the Second World War.

Early aviation career

Born in Alameda, California, and raised partly in Alaska, Doolittle became an Army flying cadet in 1917. In 1922 he made the first cross-country flight of the United States in under a day. He earned a doctorate in aeronautical engineering from MIT — an early Sc.D. in the field — and became a leading test pilot, performing the first fully "blind" flight using instruments in 1929. As a racing pilot he won the Schneider Trophy (1925), the Bendix Trophy (1931) and the Thompson Trophy (1932), setting several speed records.

The Doolittle Raid

On 18 April 1942, Doolittle led sixteen B-25 Mitchell bombers launched from the aircraft carrier USS Hornet against targets in Tokyo and other Japanese cities. The raid caused modest physical damage but was a major psychological victory for the United States after Pearl Harbor, and prompted Japan to retain fighter forces at home and accelerate its Midway operation. Doolittle's crews mostly bailed out over China; he expected to be court-martialled for losing all the aircraft, but instead received the Medal of Honor and promotion to brigadier general.

Later war service

Doolittle commanded the Twelfth Air Force in North Africa, the Fifteenth Air Force in the Mediterranean, and from January 1944 the Eighth Air Force in England, where he released fighters to hunt the Luftwaffe and helped achieve air superiority over Europe. He finished the war as a lieutenant general.

Postwar life

After the war Doolittle worked in the oil and aerospace industries, served on government commissions on aviation and space, and remained an elder statesman of air power. In 1985 he was promoted to full general on the retired list. He received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1989 and died in 1993, aged 96.
