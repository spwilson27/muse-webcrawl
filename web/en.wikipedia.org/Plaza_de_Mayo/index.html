Plaza de Mayo

The Plaza de Mayo (Spanish pronunciation: [ˈplasa ðe ˈmaʝo]; "May Square") is a city square and main foundational site of Buenos Aires, Argentina. It was formed in 1884 after the demolition of the Recova building, unifying the city's Plaza Mayor and Plaza de Armas, known at the time as Plaza de la Victoria and Plaza 25 de Mayo, respectively. The Plaza de Mayo is flanked by some of the most important buildings in the country and has been the scene of the most momentous events in Argentine history.

Surroundings

The square is overlooked by the Casa Rosada, the seat of the national government, at its eastern end; the Buenos Aires Metropolitan Cathedral; the May Pyramid at its centre, erected in 1811 to commemorate the first anniversary of the May Revolution; the Buenos Aires Cabildo; and the headquarters of the Banco de la Nación Argentina.

Political life

The plaza is Argentina's traditional site of political demonstration. On 17 October 1945, a mass demonstration there to demand the release of Juan Domingo Perón marked the birth of Peronism. The square was bombed by naval aircraft during the attempted coup of 16 June 1955. Since 1977, the Mothers of the Plaza de Mayo have marched around the May Pyramid every Thursday, demanding information about their children disappeared during the Dirty War; painted white headscarves on the pavement around the pyramid commemorate their rounds. The square remains the focal point of celebrations, protests, and national commemorations, including the mass gatherings after Argentina's 2022 World Cup victory.
