Palazzo Montecitorio

LocationPiazza del Parlamento, Rome, Italy
ArchitectsGian Lorenzo Bernini; Carlo Fontana; Ernesto Basile (extension)
Construction1653 – 1697; extended 1902 – 1918
Current useSeat of the Italian Chamber of Deputies

The Palazzo Montecitorio is a palace in Rome, Italy, and the seat of the Italian Chamber of Deputies, the lower house of the Italian Parliament. The name derives from the Mons Citatorius, a small hill — partly artificial, built up from debris — on which the palace stands, where Roman assemblies were once cited (summoned).

History

The palace was commissioned by Pope Innocent X as a residence for the Ludovisi family and designed by Gian Lorenzo Bernini; construction began in 1653 and, interrupted by financial difficulties, was completed under Carlo Fontana in 1697. It later housed the papal courts (the Curia Innocenziana). After Italian unification, it was chosen as the seat of the Chamber of Deputies and was first used by parliament in 1871.

Architecture

Bernini's gently curving Baroque facade follows the irregular lines of the hill. In the early 20th century the Sicilian architect Ernesto Basile added a major Art Nouveau (Stile Liberty) extension at the rear, including the celebrated semicircular debating chamber (the "Transatlantico" corridor) with oak panelling and stained glass. In the square in front stands the Obelisk of Montecitorio, an ancient Egyptian obelisk brought to Rome by Augustus in 10 BC to serve as the gnomon of a giant sundial.
