Crystal lattice

In crystallography, a crystal lattice is a three-dimensional arrangement of points that describes the periodic order of atoms, ions, or molecules in a crystalline solid. Each lattice point represents an identical environment within the structure; the actual crystal is produced by attaching a basis — a group of atoms — to every lattice point.

Unit cell

The unit cell is the smallest parallelepiped that, when repeated by translation in three dimensions, generates the entire lattice. It is described by three edge lengths (a, b, c) and three angles (α, β, γ). A primitive cell contains exactly one lattice point, while conventional cells may contain more.

Bravais lattices

In three dimensions there are 14 distinct Bravais lattices, grouped into seven crystal systems: triclinic, monoclinic, orthorhombic, tetragonal, cubic, trigonal, and hexagonal. They include primitive, body-centred, face-centred, and base-centred arrangements.

Reciprocal lattice

The reciprocal lattice is a mathematical construction used in the analysis of diffraction patterns and in solid-state physics, where it defines the Brillouin zone and relates directly to the crystal's X-ray diffraction pattern.
