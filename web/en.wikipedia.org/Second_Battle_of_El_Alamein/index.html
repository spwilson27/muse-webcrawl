The Second Battle of El Alamein (23 October – 11 November 1942) was a battle of the Second World War that took place near the Egyptian railway halt of El Alamein during the Western Desert campaign. Following the First Battle of El Alamein, which had stalled the Axis advance in July, the British Eighth Army, commanded by Lieutenant-General Bernard Montgomery, launched a methodical offensive that decisively defeated the Panzer Army Africa of Field Marshal Erwin Rommel. The victory was the turning point of the North African campaign and, coming alongside the Battle of Stalingrad and the Allied landings of Operation Torch, of the war's momentum against Germany.

Background

After the Axis capture of Tobruk in June 1942, Rommel advanced deep into Egypt toward the Nile and the Suez Canal. The front stabilised at El Alamein, the only defensible line between the Mediterranean and the impassable Qattara Depression. Montgomery, appointed in August, rebuilt the Eighth Army's strength and morale, refusing to attack until he had overwhelming superiority in men, tanks, guns and air power — aided by Ultra intelligence and the sinking of Axis supply ships, which starved Rommel of fuel.

The battle

The battle opened on the night of 23 October with Operation Lightfoot: a barrage from nearly 900 guns and infantry assaults to clear lanes through the vast minefields (the "Devil's gardens"). Progress was slower than planned, and the battle developed into a grinding attritional struggle — Montgomery's "crumbling" operations — around Kidney Ridge and Tel el Aqqaqir. On 2 November, Operation Supercharge, a set-piece breakthrough near the coast by New Zealand, British and Australian formations, finally broke the Axis front. Denied permission to retreat in time by Hitler's stand-fast order, Rommel began a retreat on 4 November that became a rout; about 30,000 Axis troops were captured, though much of the motorised force escaped westward across Libya. Churchill famously wrote: "Before Alamein we never had a victory. After Alamein we never had a defeat."
