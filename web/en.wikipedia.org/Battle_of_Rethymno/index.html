The Battle of Rethymno was one of the engagements of the Battle of Crete during the Second World War, fought between 20 and 29 May 1941 around the town of Rethymno on the northern coast of Crete.

Background

After the German invasion of Greece in April 1941, Allied forces withdrew to Crete. Germany planned an airborne invasion, Operation Mercury, with parachute and glider landings at Maleme, Chania, Rethymno and Heraklion. The Rethymno sector was defended mainly by Australian infantry battalions supported by Greek troops, guarding an airstrip east of the town.

The battle

On 20 May, German paratroopers of the 2nd Parachute Rifle Regiment dropped around the airstrip and nearby hills. The landings suffered heavily, and the defenders initially held the key high ground and prevented the Germans from using the airstrip. Cut off and short of ammunition and food after days of fighting, and with the wider battle for Crete lost, the Australian force was ordered to withdraw; most of its survivors surrendered on 29 May, though some escaped into the hills and were aided by Cretan civilians.

Aftermath

Crete fell to Germany, but the heavy losses suffered by German airborne forces at Rethymno and elsewhere led Hitler to forbid further large-scale parachute operations for the rest of the war.
