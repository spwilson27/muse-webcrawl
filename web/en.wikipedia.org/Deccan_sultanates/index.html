The Deccan sultanates were five late medieval Indian kingdoms — Ahmadnagar, Berar, Bidar, Bijapur, and Golkonda — located on the Deccan Plateau between the Krishna River and the Vindhya Range. They arose from the disintegration of the Bahmani Sultanate around 1490, and are occasionally collectively called by that name's legacy, though they were frequently at war with one another.

The five kingdoms

The Nizam Shahi sultanate of Ahmadnagar (1490–1636) was founded by Malik Ahmad Nizam Shah I, son of a Bahmani minister. The Imad Shahi sultanate of Berar (1490–1574) was the first to declare independence and the first to be absorbed, annexed by Ahmadnagar. The Barid Shahi sultanate of Bidar (1489–1619) began with the Barid-i-Mumalik ministers ruling in the Bahmani capital's name. The Adil Shahi sultanate of Bijapur (1490–1686), founded by Yusuf Adil Shah, became the largest and most powerful of the five. The Qutb Shahi sultanate of Golkonda (1518–1687), founded by Quli Qutb Shah, grew rich on diamond mines and the trade of the Coromandel Coast.

Wars and the Vijayanagara alliance

Though rivals, the sultanates set aside their quarrels to unite against the Hindu empire of Vijayanagara. Their confederacy crushed Vijayanagara at the Battle of Talikota in 1565, one of the decisive battles of Indian history; the victors then resumed fighting among themselves. Ahmadnagar produced celebrated figures such as Malik Ambar, the Ethiopian-born minister who resisted Mughal expansion with guerrilla tactics in the early seventeenth century, and Chand Bibi, who defended Ahmadnagar against Akbar's armies.

Culture

The sultanates fostered a distinctive Deccani culture blending Persian, Turkish, Marathi, Telugu, and Kannada influences. They patronised Dakhini Urdu literature, miniature painting, and architecture — including the Gol Gumbaz mausoleum at Bijapur, one of the largest domes of the pre-modern world — and maintained extensive trade with Persia and Arabia.

Decline

The Mughal Empire absorbed the sultanates one by one: Berar fell to Ahmadnagar in 1574, Ahmadnagar itself was annexed by Shah Jahan in 1636, Bidar was absorbed by Bijapur in 1619, and Bijapur and Golkonda finally fell to Aurangzeb in 1686 and 1687 respectively.
