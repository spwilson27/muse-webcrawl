Black Church (Brașov)

The Black Church (Romanian: Biserica Neagră; German: Schwarze Kirche) is a Gothic Lutheran church in Brașov, Transylvania, Romania. Built by the German (Transylvanian Saxon) community beginning in the late fourteenth century, it is the largest Gothic church in Romania and one of the largest in southeastern Europe.

History

Construction began around 1383 on the site of an earlier church and continued for nearly a century. Originally a Roman Catholic church dedicated to the Virgin Mary, it became Lutheran during the Reformation under the influence of Johannes Honterus. In 1689 a great fire blackened its walls, giving the church its present name; the interior was subsequently remodelled in a Baroque style.

Features

The church is about 89 metres long and its bell tower, 65 metres high, houses some of the largest bells in Romania. Inside are a renowned Buchholz organ with around 4,000 pipes, a valuable collection of Anatolian carpets donated by Saxon merchants, and a medieval baptismal font. The church remains the parish church of Brașov's Lutheran congregation and a major concert venue.
