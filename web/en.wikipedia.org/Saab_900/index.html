Saab 900


Image placeholder: Saab 900

The Saab 900 is a mid-sized automobile produced by the Swedish manufacturer Saab from 1978 to 1998 in two generations. The first generation (1978–1993, including the "classic" 900) was based on the Saab 99 chassis but with a longer front end; the second generation (1994–1998, "NG900") was built on General Motors' Opel Vectra platform. In total about 908,000 first-generation cars were built.

First generation (1978–1993)

Introduced in May 1978 as a larger successor to the 99, the classic 900 was offered as a two- or four-door sedan, three- or five-door hatchback, and from 1986 a convertible designed with American Sunroof Company; the convertible became a defining model of the range. Engines were longitudinally mounted, tilted at 45 degrees, and drove the front wheels; turbocharged versions (from 1980) made Saab a pioneer of production turbocharging, and the 900 Turbo 16 with its distinctive three-spoke wheels became an icon of the brand. Features unusual for the class included a wraparound windscreen, aircraft-inspired ergonomics reflecting Saab's aviation heritage, and the ignition switch mounted on the centre console between the front seats.

Second generation (1994–1998)

The NG900, launched for 1994 after GM's purchase of a stake in Saab, shared its platform with the Opel Vectra and offered Saab's Sensonic automated manual gearbox and the Night Panel feature, which dimmed instrument lighting. It was replaced in 1998 by the Saab 9-3, initially a heavily revised version of the same car.
