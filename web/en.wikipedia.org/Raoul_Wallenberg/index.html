Raoul Wallenberg

Raoul Gustaf Wallenberg (4 August 1912 – disappeared 17 January 1945) was a Swedish architect, businessman, diplomat and humanitarian. He saved tens of thousands of Jews in German-occupied Hungary during the Holocaust by issuing protective passports and sheltering people in buildings designated as Swedish territory.

Rescue work in Budapest

Working with the American War Refugee Board, Sweden appointed Wallenberg a special envoy to Budapest in July 1944, during the mass deportation of Hungarian Jews to Auschwitz. He issued thousands of "protective passports" (Schutz-Pässe) identifying their bearers as Swedish subjects awaiting repatriation, rented buildings across the city as "Swedish houses" flying the Swedish flag to shelter Jews, and personally intervened at deportation trains and death marches, distributing food and papers and bluffing German and Arrow Cross officials. Estimates of the number he helped save range from tens of thousands up to 100,000, including those protected in the Budapest ghetto he helped establish.

Disappearance

After Soviet forces took Budapest, Wallenberg was detained by SMERSH on suspicion of espionage on 17 January 1945 and taken to Moscow. The Soviet Union long denied holding him, then claimed in 1957 that he had died of a heart attack in Lubyanka Prison in July 1947. His fate was never fully established; Sweden formally declared him dead only in 2016, and archives released since have not resolved the case.

Legacy

Wallenberg is recognised as one of the Righteous Among the Nations by Yad Vashem and has been made an honorary citizen of the United States (only the second person so honoured, after Winston Churchill), Canada, Hungary, Australia and Israel. Monuments to him stand in dozens of cities worldwide.
