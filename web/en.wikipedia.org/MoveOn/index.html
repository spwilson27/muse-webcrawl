MoveOn

MoveOn is an American progressive public-interest and political action organisation founded in 1998, notable as a pioneer of online political organising. It operates through affiliated entities including MoveOn.org Civic Action and MoveOn.org Political Action, and claims several million members mobilised principally by email and online petitions.

History

MoveOn began as a one-line online petition by Silicon Valley entrepreneurs Wes Boyd and Joan Blades during the impeachment of President Bill Clinton, urging Congress to "censure President Clinton and move on to pressing issues facing the nation"; it drew hundreds of thousands of signatures within weeks. The organisation grew rapidly during the 2000s, opposing the Iraq War, running televised advertising (including the famous "Child's Pay" and the controversial 2007 "General Betray Us" newspaper ad), endorsing Barack Obama in the 2008 primaries and fundraising for Democratic candidates through small online donations.

Methods and influence

MoveOn developed a template — rapid-response petitions, member votes on priorities, distributed local events and small-dollar bundling — that reshaped progressive campaigning and was imitated internationally by groups such as GetUp! in Australia and Avaaz globally. It has campaigned on healthcare, climate policy, immigration, gun control and abortion rights, and mobilised protests during the Trump administrations. Critics, including some on the left and right, have disputed its influence and tactics, but its role in demonstrating that an email list could function as a mass-membership political movement is widely acknowledged.
