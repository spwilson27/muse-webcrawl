Bram Stoker

Born: 8 November 1847, Dublin, Ireland
Died: 20 April 1912, London, England
Occupation: Novelist, theatre manager
Notable work: Dracula (1897)
Bram Stoker, c. 1906

Abraham "Bram" Stoker (8 November 1847 – 20 April 1912) was an Irish author best known for his 1897 Gothic horror novel Dracula, one of the most famous works of English literature and the defining text of vampire fiction. During his lifetime he was better known as the personal assistant and business manager of the actor Henry Irving, and as manager of the Lyceum Theatre in London.

Life

Stoker was born in Clontarf, Dublin, and studied at Trinity College Dublin, where he excelled at athletics. He worked as a civil servant at Dublin Castle and as a theatre critic before meeting Irving, whose company he managed for 27 years. He married Florence Balcombe in 1878.

Dracula

Dracula is an epistolary novel told through letters, diary entries, and newspaper articles. It recounts Count Dracula's attempt to move from Transylvania to England and the efforts of Professor Abraham Van Helsing and a small group to defeat him. Stoker drew on Eastern European folklore and history, including the figure of Vlad the Impaler. The novel established many conventions of the vampire genre in literature and film.

Other works

Stoker wrote numerous other novels, including The Snake's Pass (1890), The Jewel of Seven Stars (1903), and The Lair of the White Worm (1911), as well as short stories such as "Dracula's Guest".
