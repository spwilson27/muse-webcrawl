The Grey Cup (French: Coupe Grey) is both the championship game of the Canadian Football League and the trophy awarded to the winning team — the premier prize in Canadian football and one of the country's largest annual sporting and television events.

History

The cup was donated in 1909 by Albert Grey, 4th Earl Grey, then Governor General of Canada, originally intended as a trophy for amateur rugby football in the Dominion. The University of Toronto Varsity Blues won the first game, defeating the Parkdale Canoe Club. Through the early decades the game mixed university, amateur and increasingly professional teams; during the 1950s the sport professionalised, and the CFL, founded in 1958, assumed sole control of the trophy.

Remarkably, from 1945 to 1953 teams from outside Canada's core leagues — notably American service teams during the war years and, later, in the 1990s the Baltimore Stallions, who in 1995 became the only US-based team to win the Cup during the CFL's American expansion — contested the championship.

The game

The Grey Cup is played in late November, rotating among CFL cities, between the champions of the league's East and West divisions, decided by single-elimination playoffs. The week-long Grey Cup Festival surrounding the game — parties, parades, concerts — is a fixture of Canadian cultural life. Famous editions include the 1950 "Mud Bowl", the 1962 "Fog Bowl", suspended and finished the next day because of fog at Toronto's Exhibition Stadium, and the 1977 "Ice Bowl" in Montreal.

Trophy

The trophy, a silver chalice on an expanding base engraved with champions' names, has survived being stolen, held for ransom, dropped and fire-damaged. The Toronto Argonauts have won the most Grey Cups, followed by the Edmonton Elks and Winnipeg Blue Bombers.
