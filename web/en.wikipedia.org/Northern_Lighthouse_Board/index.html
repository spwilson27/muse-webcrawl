The Northern Lighthouse Board (NLB) is the general lighthouse authority for Scotland and the Isle of Man. It is responsible for marine navigation aids — lighthouses, buoys, beacons and a differential GPS service — around some of the most dangerous coasts in northern Europe. Its working motto is In salutem omnium ("for the safety of all").

History

The board was established in 1786 as the Commissioners of Northern Lighthouses, initially with four lights (Kinnead Head, North Ronaldsay, Scalpay and Mull of Kintyre). It became famous through its engineers, the Stevenson family, who over four generations built most of Scotland's great rock lighthouses, including the Bell Rock (1810), Skerryvore and Muckle Flugga. The writer Robert Louis Stevenson was himself from this family and trained briefly as an engineer with the board.

Modern operations

Automation was completed in 1998, when the Fair Isle South lighthouse became the last to lose its keepers; lights are now monitored from the Edinburgh headquarters. The board operates the tenders NLV Pharos and NLV Pole Star and works with Trinity House and the Commissioners of Irish Lights, funded by light dues levied on shipping.
