The Arctic Archipelago, also known as the Canadian Arctic Archipelago, is a group of 36,563 islands lying north of the Canadian mainland in the Arctic Ocean. It is one of the largest archipelagos in the world by area, covering about 1.42 million square kilometres, and contains three of the world's ten largest islands: Baffin Island, Victoria Island and Ellesmere Island.

Geography

The archipelago extends about 2,400 km from the Beaufort Sea in the west to Greenland in the east. Its islands are divided by a network of channels that together form the Northwest Passage, the sea route between the Atlantic and Pacific oceans. The northern Queen Elizabeth Islands are mountainous, bearing ice caps and glaciers, while the southern islands are lower and flatter. Most of the archipelago lies within the territory of Nunavut, with the western portion in the Northwest Territories.

Climate and ecology

Climate ranges from polar desert in the north — receiving less than 250 mm of precipitation a year — to tundra in the south. Permafrost underlies the whole region. Wildlife includes polar bears, muskoxen, caribou, narwhals, beluga whales and large seabird colonies; vegetation is sparse, consisting of mosses, lichens and low shrubs in sheltered places.

Population and sovereignty

The population, fewer than 20,000 people, is overwhelmingly Inuit and lives in small communities such as Iqaluit (on Baffin Island), Cambridge Bay and Resolute. Canada bases its sovereignty claim over the archipelago partly on the 1880 British transfer of its Arctic territories and on Inuit use and occupancy; the legal status of the Northwest Passage waters — Canadian internal waters or an international strait — remains disputed, notably with the United States.
