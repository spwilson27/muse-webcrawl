Egyptian revolution of 2011

Protesters gathered in Tahrir Square, Cairo, in February 2011

The Egyptian revolution of 2011, also known as the 25 January Revolution, was a series of demonstrations, marches, and acts of civil disobedience that took place in Egypt beginning on 25 January 2011 and continuing until 11 February 2011. The demonstrations and riots were in response to police brutality, corruption, high unemployment, food price inflation, and the long presidency of Hosni Mubarak.

Background

Egypt had been under a state of emergency since 1967, and Hosni Mubarak had ruled since 1981. The 2010 parliamentary elections were widely considered fraudulent. Inspired by the Tunisian revolution, which forced President Zine El Abidine Ben Ali to flee, Egyptian activists called for a "day of revolt" on 25 January 2011, Police Day. Social media, including the "We Are All Khaled Said" Facebook page commemorating a young man beaten to death by police, helped mobilise protesters.

Course of events

Hundreds of thousands gathered in Cairo's Tahrir Square. On 28 January, the "Friday of Anger," the government shut down internet and mobile networks, and the army was deployed. Mubarak dismissed his government and appointed Omar Suleiman vice president, but protesters demanded his resignation. On 2 February, pro-Mubarak supporters attacked demonstrators in Tahrir Square. After 18 days of protests, on 11 February 2011, Vice President Suleiman announced that Mubarak had resigned and transferred power to the Supreme Council of the Armed Forces.

Aftermath

More than 800 people were killed during the uprising. Parliamentary elections in late 2011 and early 2012 were won by Islamist parties, and Mohamed Morsi of the Muslim Brotherhood was elected president in June 2012. Morsi was removed by the military in July 2013 following further mass protests, and Abdel Fattah el-Sisi became president in 2014.
