Sheela na gig

Sheela na gigs are figurative carvings of naked women displaying an exaggerated vulva, found on churches, castles, tower houses and other medieval buildings, chiefly in Ireland and Britain, with related figures in Romanesque art across France and Spain. They are most commonly dated to the 11th–13th centuries (with later examples in the Irish tower-house tradition), and usually take the form of a small stone figure set into a wall, often over a door or window.

Description

A sheela na gig typically shows a female figure, often emaciated or with ribs visible, a large head, and hands pulling apart or pointing to an exaggerated vulva. Many stare directly outward. The name is Irish and of uncertain origin — explanations include Síle na gCíoch ("Sheila of the breasts") and Síle na giob ("Sheila on her hunkers") — and appears in print only in the 19th century; there is no known medieval name for the figures.

Origins and theories

Scholars have proposed many functions. The figures may be apotropaic — intended to ward off evil, like the exhibitionist figures and monsters on Romanesque churches. They may be warnings against lust, part of medieval church imagery of sin and death, an interpretation supported by their Romanesque context alongside carvings of sinners and beasts. Others have argued for a fertility or pre-Christian goddess origin, later adopted into church decoration; still others connect them with childbirth charms or with folk traditions of "healing" stones. Modern cataloguing projects (notably in Ireland) have recorded well over a hundred examples in Ireland and dozens in Britain, many removed or defaced in the 19th century as offensive; others survive in museums.

Distribution

Well-known examples include the sheela at Kilpeck Church in Herefordshire, England, and figures at the Rock of Cashel and numerous Irish tower houses. Comparable exhibitionist carvings — male and female — occur on Romanesque churches along the pilgrimage roads of France and northern Spain, suggesting the motif travelled with masons and patrons.
