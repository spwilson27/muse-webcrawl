Petrozavodsk (Russian: Петрозаводск; Karelian: Petroskoi; Finnish: Petroskoi) is the capital city of the Republic of Karelia in northwestern Russia, stretching along the western shore of Lake Onega. It is the largest city in Karelia and its administrative, industrial and cultural centre.

The Onega embankment, with sculptures gifted by sister cities

The city was founded in 1703 when Peter the Great ordered the construction of an ironworks and cannon foundry — the Petrovsky works, from which the name derives ("Peter's factory") — to supply the Baltic Fleet during the Great Northern War. Granted town status by Catherine the Great in 1777, it became the seat of the Olonets Governorate. During the Second World War the city was occupied by Finnish forces (1941–1944), who renamed it Äänislinna.

Modern city

Today Petrozavodsk is known for its embankment on Lake Onega, decorated with avant-garde sculptures from twin cities, and as the gateway to Kizhi Island, famous for its wooden churches and open-air museum, reached by hydrofoil across the lake. It is home to Petrozavodsk State University, a railway junction on the line between St Petersburg and Murmansk, and industries including machinery and shipbuilding at the Onega shipyard.
