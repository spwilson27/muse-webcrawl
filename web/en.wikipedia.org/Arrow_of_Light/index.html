Arrow of Light

The Arrow of Light is the highest rank attainable in the Cub Scout programme of the Boy Scouts of America (now Scouting America). It is earned by Webelos Scouts — typically children in the fifth grade — who complete requirements in outdoor skills, citizenship, fitness, first aid and preparation for joining a Scouts BSA troop, and it is the only Cub Scout award that may be worn on the Scouts BSA uniform.

The award and its symbol

The badge depicts a golden arrow below a rising sun with seven rays, symbolising the seven virtues of the Scout Law's spirit — wisdom, courage, self-control, justice, faith, hope and love. Requirements culminate in a bridging ceremony in which the Cub Scout crosses over into a troop, often over a symbolic bridge, and receives the award from their leaders and parents. Many troops and packs present a handcrafted arrow or plaque commemorating the achievement.

History

The Arrow of Light was introduced in 1941 as part of the Webelos programme, taking its name and symbolism from Rudyard Kipling's The Jungle Book and from Akela, the wolf leader used as a figure in Cub Scouting. The award's requirements have been revised several times as the Cub Scout programme has been restructured, most recently with the updated Cub Scout adventures introduced in 2024.
