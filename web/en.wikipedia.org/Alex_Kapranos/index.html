Alex Kapranos

Born: 20 March 1972, Almondsbury, England
Occupation: Singer, guitarist, songwriter, author
Known for: Lead singer of Franz Ferdinand

Alexander Paul Kapranos Huntley (born 20 March 1972) is a British musician, singer, songwriter, and author. He is best known as the lead singer and guitarist of the Glasgow-based indie rock band Franz Ferdinand, which he co-founded in 2002 and which won the Mercury Prize in 2004 for its debut album.

Early life

Kapranos was born in Gloucestershire to a Greek father and an English mother, and grew up partly in Greece before the family settled in Scotland. He studied English literature at the University of Glasgow and worked in a variety of jobs — chef, barman, driver — while playing in Glasgow bands including The Karelia, whose 1997 album Divorce at High Noon he released under the name Alex Huntley.

Franz Ferdinand

Formed with Nick McCarthy, Bob Hardy, and Paul Thomson, Franz Ferdinand rose quickly with the single "Take Me Out" (2004); their self-titled debut won the Mercury Prize and helped define the 2000s post-punk revival. The band has released further albums including You Could Have It So Much Better (2005), Tonight (2009), Right Thoughts, Right Words, Right Action (2013), Always Ascending (2018), and The Human Fear (2025), and collaborated with Sparks as the supergroup FFS.

Other work

Kapranos has worked as a record producer for acts including The Cribs, written about food in his book Sound Bites (2006), drawn from his column for The Guardian, and presented radio and television programmes on music and food.
