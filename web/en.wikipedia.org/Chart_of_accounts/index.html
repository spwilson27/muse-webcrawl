Chart of accounts

A chart of accounts (COA) is a list of the accounts used by an organization to define each class of items for which money or its equivalent is spent or received. It is used by the organization to organize its finances and segregate expenditures, revenue, assets and liabilities in order to give interested parties a better understanding of the financial health of the entity.

The accounts are typically arranged in the order in which they appear in the financial statements, beginning with the balance sheet accounts and continuing with the income statement accounts. A common numbering convention assigns ranges of codes to each major category:

RangeCategory
1000–1999Assets
2000–2999Liabilities
3000–3999Equity
4000–4999Revenue
5000–5999Cost of goods sold
6000–6999Expenses

Purpose

The chart of accounts provides the structure for a company's general ledger. Each account is assigned a unique identifier, often numeric, so that transactions can be posted consistently and reports can aggregate balances by category. A well-designed chart balances detail with simplicity: enough accounts to give management useful information, but not so many that bookkeeping becomes unwieldy.

Standardization

Some countries prescribe standardized national charts of accounts. France's Plan Comptable Général and Germany's standard charts (SKR) are prominent examples. Government-wide standards also exist; the United States federal government uses the U.S. Government Standard General Ledger (USSGL). Small businesses commonly adopt the default chart provided by their accounting software and modify it to their needs.
