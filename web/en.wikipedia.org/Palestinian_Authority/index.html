The Palestinian Authority, officially the Palestinian National Authority (PA or PNA), is the Fatah-controlled government body that exercises partial civil control over the Palestinian enclaves of the Israeli-occupied West Bank as a consequence of the 1993–1995 Oslo Accords. It was established in 1994 as an interim self-governing body pending final-status negotiations, and its administrative capital is Ramallah. Since the 2007 Fatah–Hamas conflict, the PA's effective control has been limited to the West Bank, while Hamas governs the Gaza Strip.

History

The PA was created by the Gaza–Jericho Agreement of May 1994 following the Oslo I Accord (1993) between Israel and the Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO). Yasser Arafat, chairman of the PLO, returned from exile and became the PA's first president; in the 1996 elections he was confirmed in office and Fatah won a majority in the new Palestinian Legislative Council. The Oslo II Accord (1995) divided the West Bank into Area A (full PA civil and security control), Area B (PA civil control, joint security), and Area C (full Israeli control, comprising about 60 percent of the territory).

The eruption of the Second Intifada (2000–2005), Arafat's death in 2004, and the succession of Mahmoud Abbas reshaped the PA. Hamas's victory in the 2006 legislative elections led to international sanctions against the Hamas-led government, and armed conflict in 2007 left Hamas in control of Gaza and the PA confined to the West Bank. No national elections have been held since 2006.

Government and politics

The PA has a semi-presidential system: a directly elected president and a prime minister appointed by the president, with the Legislative Council as legislature. In 2013 President Abbas issued a decree renaming the PA the "State of Palestine" on official documents, following the UN General Assembly's 2012 vote granting Palestine non-member observer state status. The PA's ministries run education, health, and civil administration for Palestinians in Areas A and B, while Israel retains control of borders, airspace, the population registry, and Area C.

Economy and finances

The PA budget depends heavily on foreign aid and on clearance revenues — taxes and customs duties collected by Israel on the PA's behalf under the 1994 Paris Protocol, which Israel has periodically withheld. The PA employs a large public sector, including substantial security forces that coordinate with Israel, a source of both international support and domestic criticism. Recurrent fiscal crises, high unemployment, and restrictions on movement and access constrain the West Bank economy.

International recognition

As of the mid-2020s, more than 140 UN member states recognise the State of Palestine; the PA represents Palestine in international forums through the PLO and, since 2012, as a UN non-member observer state. Palestine has joined bodies including UNESCO and the International Criminal Court.
