Old San Juan

Old San Juan (Spanish: Viejo San Juan) is the oldest settlement within Puerto Rico and the historic colonial core of the city of San Juan. Located on a narrow island at the entrance to San Juan Bay, it was founded by Spanish colonists in 1521 and is bounded by massive city walls and defensive forts built between the sixteenth and eighteenth centuries. Old San Juan is a U.S. National Historic Landmark District and, with La Fortaleza, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1983.

History

The settlement was moved from nearby Caparra to its defensible islet site in 1521. To protect the harbour — a crucial port of call for Spain's treasure fleets — the Crown raised the fortresses of San Felipe del Morro and San Cristóbal and ringed the city with walls. The forts repelled attacks by Francis Drake in 1595 and the Dutch in 1625, though the Earl of Cumberland briefly took the city in 1598. Puerto Rico passed to the United States in 1898 after the Spanish–American War.

Character

Seven square blocks of narrow streets paved with blue-tinted adoquín cobblestones are lined by more than 400 restored colonial buildings in pastel colours. Landmarks include San Juan Cathedral, which holds the tomb of Juan Ponce de León, La Fortaleza — the oldest executive mansion in continuous use in the Americas — and the San Juan Gate. The district is now a centre of tourism, festivals, galleries, cafés, and government offices.
