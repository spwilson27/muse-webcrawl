Turntablism

A turntablist uses two turntables and a DJ mixer as musical instruments, manipulating records by hand.

Turntablism is the art of manipulating sounds and creating new music, sound effects, mixes and other creative sounds and beats, typically by using two or more turntables and a DJ mixer equipped with a crossfader. The turntablist treats the record player as a musical instrument: scratching vinyl records, cutting between copies of the same record, beat juggling, and using the mixer's controls to shape rhythm and texture in real time.

History

Turntablism emerged from hip hop culture in the Bronx, New York, in the 1970s. DJ Kool Herc pioneered breakbeat DJing, using two copies of the same record to extend the drum breaks dancers favoured. Grandmaster Flash developed precise cueing and cutting techniques, popularised in "The Adventures of Grandmaster Flash on the Wheels of Steel" (1981). Grand Wizard Theodore is credited with inventing the scratch in 1975, and Afrika Bambaataa helped spread the techniques. In the 1980s and 1990s the art became more virtuosic and competitive: DJs such as the Invisibl Skratch Piklz, DJ Qbert, Mix Master Mike, DJ Craze and the X-Ecutioners developed beat juggling and complex scratch vocabularies, and the DMC World DJ Championships became its premier showcase. The word "turntablist" was popularised in the mid-1990s (often credited to DJ Babu) to distinguish these performers from DJs who simply play records.

Techniques

Core techniques include the baby scratch, scribble, chirp, flare and crab scratches; cutting and back-cueing; beat juggling, in which two copies of a record are recombined live into a new rhythmic pattern; needle dropping; and body tricks performed for showmanship. Digital vinyl systems and controllers have since blurred the line between turntablism and digital DJing, but the direct-drive turntable (classically the Technics SL-1200) remains the emblematic instrument.
