Space exploration

Space exploration is the use of astronomy and space technology to explore outer space. While the exploration of space is currently carried out mainly by astronomers with telescopes, its physical exploration is conducted both by uncrewed robotic space probes and human spaceflight. Space exploration, like its classical form astronomy, is one of the main sources for space science.

History

The first steps towards spaceflight were made with the development of large liquid-fuelled rockets in the first half of the 20th century, notably by Konstantin Tsiolkovsky, Robert Goddard and Hermann Oberth. After World War II, the United States and the Soviet Union used captured German V-2 technology in their missile and space programmes, and competition between them drove the Space Race. On 4 October 1957 the USSR launched Sputnik 1, the first artificial satellite. On 12 April 1961, Soviet cosmonaut Yuri Gagarin became the first human in space aboard Vostok 1. The United States responded with the Apollo program, and on 20 July 1969 Apollo 11 astronauts Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin became the first humans to land on the Moon.

Key achievements

  • First artificial satellite: Sputnik 1 (1957).
  • First human in space: Yuri Gagarin (1961).
  • First Moon landing: Apollo 11 (1969); six crewed landings in total through 1972.
  • First space station: Salyut 1 (1971); later Skylab, Mir, and the continuously occupied International Space Station (since 2000).
  • Interplanetary probes have visited all the planets of the Solar System; Voyager 1 and Voyager 2 have entered interstellar space.
  • Landers and rovers have explored the Moon, Mars, Venus, Titan, asteroids, and a comet.

Targets

From the beginnings of spaceflight, exploration has targeted the Sun, the Moon, the planets (especially Mars and Venus), asteroids and comets. Robotic missions include flybys, orbiters, landers, rovers, and sample-return missions, such as Japan's Hayabusa2 and NASA's OSIRIS-REx asteroid sample returns. The Hubble Space Telescope and the James Webb Space Telescope have transformed observational astronomy from orbit.

Future

Current plans include NASA's Artemis program to return humans to the Moon, Chinese crewed lunar ambitions, extensive commercial activity in Earth orbit, and long-term aspirations for human missions to Mars. Space exploration is increasingly international and commercial, with private companies providing launch services and spacecraft.
