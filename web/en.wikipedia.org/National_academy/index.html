National academy

A national academy is an organizational body, usually operating with state financial support and approval, that co-ordinates scholarly research activities and standards for academic disciplines, and serves as a national learned society. Most countries have national academies, many of which advise governments on scientific, scholarly, and cultural matters.

History

The national academy model emerged in early modern Europe. The Accademia dei Lincei in Rome (1603), the Royal Society of London (1660), and the French Academy of Sciences (1666) were among the earliest institutions to gather scholars under national patronage. The model spread across Europe and eventually worldwide during the 18th and 19th centuries.

Functions

National academies typically elect distinguished scholars as members or fellows, publish scholarly work, award prizes, maintain libraries and collections, and represent their country's scholarship internationally, including through bodies such as the International Science Council. In the United States, the National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine provide independent advice to the federal government; in the United Kingdom, the Royal Society and the British Academy fulfil corresponding roles for science and the humanities respectively.
