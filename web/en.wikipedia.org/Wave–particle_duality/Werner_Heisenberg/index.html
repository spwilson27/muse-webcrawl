Werner Heisenberg

Born: 5 December 1901, Würzburg, German Empire
Died: 1 February 1976, Munich, West Germany
Known for: Uncertainty principle, matrix mechanics
Awards: Nobel Prize in Physics (1932)

Werner Heisenberg, one of the principal founders of quantum mechanics.

Werner Karl Heisenberg (5 December 1901 – 1 February 1976) was a German theoretical physicist, one of the main pioneers of the theory of quantum mechanics. He published his Umdeutung paper in 1925, a major reinterpretation of old quantum theory, in which he created the basis for matrix mechanics.

In 1927, he published his uncertainty principle, which states that the more precisely the position of some particle is determined, the less precisely its momentum can be predicted from initial conditions, and vice versa. Heisenberg was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics for 1932 "for the creation of quantum mechanics".

Early life and education

Heisenberg was born in Würzburg, Germany, to Kaspar Ernst August Heisenberg, a secondary school teacher of classical languages who became a professor of Middle and Modern Greek studies at the University of Munich, and his wife Annie Wecklein. He studied physics at the University of Munich under Arnold Sommerfeld, receiving his doctorate in 1923 with a dissertation on turbulence in fluid streams.

Quantum mechanics

Working in Göttingen with Max Born and Pascual Jordan, and during stays in Copenhagen with Niels Bohr, Heisenberg developed matrix mechanics, the first complete formulation of quantum mechanics. His uncertainty principle became a cornerstone of the Copenhagen interpretation and of the broader debate — including his discussions with Bohr and Einstein — over the meaning of wave-particle duality and the limits of measurement.

Later career

During World War II Heisenberg was a principal scientist in the German nuclear weapons program, a role that remained controversial. After the war he helped rebuild German science, directed the Max Planck Institute for Physics, and worked on unified field theory and the theory of elementary particles. He died in Munich in 1976.
