The Charles Brownlow Trophy, better known as the Brownlow Medal (and informally as "Charlie"), is an award presented to the player judged the best and fairest in the Australian Football League (AFL) during the regular season. It is widely regarded as the most prestigious individual award in Australian rules football.

Voting

After each home-and-away match, the field umpires confer and award three, two and one votes to the players they regard as the best, second-best and third-best on the ground. A player suspended for a reportable offence during the season is ineligible for the award, even if he polls the most votes. The votes are counted on Brownlow Medal night, held on the Monday before the AFL Grand Final, in one of Australian sport's major televised events.

History

The medal is named after Charles Brownlow, a long-serving Geelong player and administrator who died in 1924, the year the award was first presented to Edward "Carji" Greeves of Geelong. Haydn Bunton Sr., Dick Reynolds, Bill Hutchison and Ian Stewart each won the medal three times in the VFL era; in the AFL era, multiple winners include Gary Ablett Jr., Chris Judd and Nat Fyfe (twice each).
