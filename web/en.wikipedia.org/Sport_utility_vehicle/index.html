A sport utility vehicle (SUV) is a car classification that combines elements of road-going passenger cars with features from off-road vehicles, such as raised ground clearance and four-wheel drive. There is no commonly agreed-upon definition of an SUV, and usage of the term varies between countries. Thus, it is "a loose term that traditionally covers a broad range of vehicles with four-wheel drive."

Some definitions claim that an SUV must be built on a light truck chassis; however, broader definitions consider any vehicle with off-road design features to be an SUV. A crossover SUV is often defined as an SUV built with a unibody construction, as in passenger cars, whereas traditional SUVs were built on a body-on-frame basis.

History

Precursors of SUVs date to the military and low-volume models of the late 1930s, such as the Willys Jeep and the Land Rover, and later the Jeep Cherokee and Range Rover. The term "sport utility vehicle" came into wide popular use in the late 1980s. From the mid-2010s, SUVs became the largest and fastest-growing market segment worldwide, and by the early 2020s they accounted for close to half of global car sales.

Types

SUVs are commonly marketed by size — mini, subcompact, compact, mid-size, and full-size — and include luxury, off-road-oriented, and sporty variants, as well as electric models. Three-row SUVs offer seating for up to eight passengers.

Criticism

SUVs have been criticized for their larger mass and size, which pose greater risks to pedestrians and occupants of smaller vehicles; for higher fuel consumption and emissions than comparably sized cars; and for contributing to road wear. Their popularity has nonetheless continued to grow, driven by consumer preferences for high seating positions, space, and perceived safety.
