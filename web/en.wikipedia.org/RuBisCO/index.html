RuBisCO

Ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase/oxygenase, commonly known by the abbreviations RuBisCo, RuBPCase, or RBC, is an enzyme involved in the light-independent (or "dark") reactions of photosynthesis, where it catalyses the first major step of carbon fixation. It is generally considered the most abundant protein on Earth.

Function

RuBisCO adds a molecule of carbon dioxide to ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate (RuBP), producing two molecules of 3-phosphoglycerate in the Calvin cycle. However, RuBisCO also reacts with oxygen — a competing process called photorespiration that reduces photosynthetic efficiency by 20–30% in many crops. Plants such as maize and sugarcane have evolved C4 photosynthesis, and others CAM photosynthesis, largely to concentrate CO₂ around RuBisCO.

Structure and significance

In plants, the enzyme is a large complex of eight large subunits (encoded in the chloroplast genome) and eight small subunits (nuclear-encoded), requiring chaperones and the activase enzyme to function. Because RuBisCO fixes some 120–250 billion tonnes of carbon annually, improving its speed and specificity is a major goal of crop bioengineering research.
