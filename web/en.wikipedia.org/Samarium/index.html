Samarium

Samarium, a lanthanide metal used in powerful magnets.

Samarium is a chemical element with symbol Sm and atomic number 62. It is a moderately hard silvery metal of the lanthanide series that slowly oxidises in air. It was isolated in 1879 by French chemist Paul-Émile Lecoq de Boisbaudran and named after the mineral samarskite, itself named for Russian mining official Vasili Samarsky-Bykhovets — making samarium the first element named after a person.

Properties and isotopes

Samarium has several naturally occurring isotopes; 147Sm is a very long-lived alpha emitter (half-life about 1.06×1011 years) used in samarium–neodymium radiometric dating of rocks. The element's compounds are typically in the +3 oxidation state, though the +2 state is unusually accessible.

Applications

Samarium–cobalt magnets are the element's best-known application: they retain magnetism at high temperatures and resist corrosion, and are used in precision motors, headphones and aerospace equipment. Samarium is also used in cancer treatment (samarium-153 lexidronam for bone pain), in neutron-absorbing control rods, in infrared-absorbing glass and as a catalyst in organic chemistry (samarium(II) iodide).

Occurrence

Like other lanthanides, samarium is obtained from minerals such as monazite and bastnäsite, mainly mined in China, the United States and Australia.
