Andvari

Andvari (Old Norse, "the careful one") is a dwarf in Norse mythology who lives beneath a waterfall in the form of a fish and possesses a great hoard of gold, including the magic ring Andvaranaut, which generates more gold.

Myth

In the Völsunga saga and the Poetic Edda poem Reginsmál, the god Loki catches Andvari in a net borrowed from the sea goddess Rán, in order to obtain gold to pay a ransom (weregild) for the slain otter Ótr, whose father Hreidmar demands the corpse be covered in gold. Andvari surrenders all his treasure but tries to keep Andvaranaut; when Loki takes it too, the dwarf curses the gold, prophesying that it will bring death to its possessors.

The curse's consequences

The curse drives the ensuing tragedy: Fafnir kills his father Hreidmar for the hoard and becomes a dragon; Regin plots against Fafnir; Sigurd slays the dragon and is himself eventually murdered. The cursed gold cycle is a central thread of the Völsung legend and influenced Wagner's Der Ring des Nibelungen (as Alberich's ring) and Tolkien's work.
