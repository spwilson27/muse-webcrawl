Philodemus

Philodemus of Gadara (c. 110 – c. 40/35 BC) was an Epicurean philosopher and poet from Gadara in Syria (in modern Jordan). He studied under Zeno of Sidon at Athens before moving to Italy, where he became associated with the circle of Lucius Calpurnius Piso Caesoninus. His writings were preserved in the library of the Villa of the Papyri at Herculaneum, carbonized in the eruption of Mount Vesuvius in AD 79.

Life

Philodemus was a pupil of Zeno of Sidon, head of the Epicurean school at Athens. Around 75 BC he settled in Italy, where his patron was Piso, father-in-law of Julius Caesar. Cicero, in his speech In Pisonem, grudgingly praised Philodemus's learning and poetry. He probably influenced Virgil and Horace; Horace's Ars Poetica is thought to draw on Philodemus's treatise On Poems.

Works

His surviving treatises, recovered from the Villa of the Papyri and progressively deciphered since the 18th century, include On Music, On Poems, On Rhetoric, On Anger, On Death, On Frank Criticism, On the Gods, and On Piety. He also wrote elegant epigrams, some thirty of which survive in the Greek Anthology. Modern imaging techniques, including multispectral imaging and virtual unwrapping, continue to recover new text from the Herculaneum scrolls.
