The Texas State Guard (TXSG) is the state defense force of Texas, one of three components of the Texas Military Forces alongside the Texas Army National Guard and Texas Air National Guard. As a state defense force, it is a state military organization authorized under federal and state law, but it is not part of the federal armed forces and cannot be federalized; it serves under the governor of Texas through the state's adjutant general.

Mission and organization

The State Guard's mission is to provide mission-ready military forces to assist state and local authorities during emergencies in Texas, and to augment the Texas National Guard when it is deployed or unavailable. The TXSG is organized into brigades and battalions across the state, including Army and maritime components, plus medical, engineering, and civil-affairs elements. Its members are volunteers who generally serve without pay unless called to state active duty.

Operations

State Guardsmen have been activated for hurricane response — notably after Hurricanes Harvey and Ike — flooding, wildfire support, border-support logistics, and the COVID-19 pandemic, performing tasks such as shelter operations, search and rescue support, traffic control, and food distribution. During Hurricane Harvey in 2017, effectively the entire Texas Military Forces, including the State Guard, were mobilized.

History

Texas militia traditions trace to the Republic of Texas. The modern State Guard descends from home-defense forces organized during the world wars, when the Texas Defense Guard of 1941–47 guarded installations while the National Guard was in federal service. The force was permanently authorized under Texas law in the postwar period and has operated continuously as the state's all-volunteer military reserve.
