Cilician pirates

The Cilician pirates were a powerful confederation of pirates who dominated the eastern Mediterranean Sea from the 2nd century BC until their suppression by Pompey the Great in 67 BC. Operating from strongholds along the rocky coast of Cilicia in southern Anatolia, they raided shipping, sacked coastal cities, and traded in slaves on a large scale.

Rise

Piracy flourished as the Hellenistic kingdoms weakened and the Seleucid Empire declined. The Cilicians operated hundreds of ships, maintained fortified harbours such as Coracesium (modern Alanya), and cooperated at times with Rome's enemies, including Mithridates VI of Pontus and the rebel Spartacus's opponents in the slave trade. Their raids are said to have plundered hundreds of cities, and they famously captured the young Julius Caesar in 75 BC, holding him for ransom before he returned to have them executed.

Suppression

By the 60s BC the pirates threatened Rome's grain supply. In 67 BC the lex Gabinia gave Pompey extraordinary command over the whole Mediterranean. In a campaign of about three months, Pompey swept the sea clear, then reduced the Cilician strongholds and resettled surrendered pirates in inland towns, ending them as a major power.
