The Inter-State River Water Disputes Act, 1956 is an Act of the Parliament of India enacted under Article 262 of the Constitution, which empowers Parliament to provide for the adjudication of disputes relating to the waters of inter-state rivers and river valleys, and to exclude the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court and other courts in such disputes.

Most major peninsular rivers cross state boundaries, making allocation a recurring federal issue.

Mechanism

When a state government requests it and the central government is of the opinion that a water dispute cannot be settled by negotiation, the Centre constitutes a Water Disputes Tribunal for adjudication. The tribunal investigates and issues a decision, which, once published by the central government, is final and binding on the parties and has the force of a decree of the Supreme Court. Before referring a dispute, the 2002 amendment inserted a requirement that the Centre first attempt settlement through a Dispute Resolution Committee and set time limits — one year for the Centre's decision to refer, and three years (extendable by two) for the tribunal to report.

Major tribunals and disputes

  • Godavari and Krishna (I) tribunals (constituted 1969) — awards in the 1970s; a second Krishna tribunal (2004) reported in 2010 and was revisited after the creation of Telangana.
  • Narmada Water Disputes Tribunal (1969) — award 1979, underpinning the Sardar Sarovar project.
  • Ravi and Beas Tribunal (1986) — linked to the Sutlej–Yamuna Link canal dispute between Punjab and Haryana.
  • Cauvery Water Disputes Tribunal (1990) — final award 2007; the Supreme Court modified allocations in 2018 and a Cauvery Water Management Authority was created.
  • Mahadayi (Mandovi) and Mahanadi tribunals in the 2010s.

Criticism and reform

The process has been criticised for delays in constituting tribunals, in issuing awards, and in implementing them, and for politicisation of water sharing between states. The Sarkaria and Punchhi Commissions on Centre–State relations recommended reforms. An amendment bill passed by the Lok Sabha in 2019 proposed replacing ad hoc tribunals with a single permanent Inter-State River Water Disputes Tribunal with benches, together with a Dispute Resolution Committee — a proposal that remained pending in the Rajya Sabha for years.
