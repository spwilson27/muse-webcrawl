Bofors scandal

The Bofors scandal was a major political scandal that occurred in India and Sweden in the late 1980s. It concerned alleged kickbacks paid by the Swedish arms manufacturer Bofors AB to secure a 1986 contract, worth about US$1.4 billion, to supply the Indian Army with 410 FH-77B 155 mm field howitzers.

The allegations

In April 1987, Swedish Radio reported that Bofors had paid bribes to Indian politicians and defence officials through middlemen. The story was pursued vigorously by Indian journalists, notably Chitra Subramaniam and N. Ram of The Hindu, whose reporting, based on documents obtained from a whistle-blower, identified payments routed through coded Swiss bank accounts and named intermediaries including the businessman Ottavio Quattrocchi, who was close to the family of Prime Minister Rajiv Gandhi.

Political fallout

The scandal badly damaged the government of Rajiv Gandhi, whose image as "Mr. Clean" had been central to his 1984 election campaign. Defence minister V. P. Singh resigned and later led the opposition; the Congress party lost the 1989 general election. "Bofors" became a byword for corruption in Indian public life and featured in election campaigns for decades.

Investigations

A Joint Parliamentary Committee, and later investigations by the Central Bureau of Investigation, ran for many years. Charges against Rajiv Gandhi were dropped after his death, and in 2004 the Delhi High Court quashed charges against other accused, citing failure to prove they had received money; further proceedings against Quattrocchi were closed after his death in 2013. No convictions were ever secured, and the howitzers themselves later performed well in the 1999 Kargil War.
