Stanley Cup

The Stanley Cup, the oldest professional sports trophy in North America.

The Stanley Cup (French: La Coupe Stanley) is the championship trophy awarded annually to the National Hockey League (NHL) playoff champion. It is the oldest existing trophy to be awarded to a professional sports franchise in North America, and the International Ice Hockey Federation considers it one of the "most important championships available to the sport".

History

The cup was donated in 1893 by Lord Stanley of Preston, then Governor General of Canada, as a challenge cup for Canada's top amateur ice hockey team. Professional teams began competing for it in 1906, and it became the exclusive championship trophy of the NHL in 1947. The trophy's bowl is made of silver, and the names of winning players and staff are engraved on its bands; unlike most sports trophies, a new one is not made each year.

Traditions

The Stanley Cup is surrounded by famous traditions: players are said to refrain from touching it until they have won it, each member of the winning team traditionally spends a day with the cup, and the winning team drinks champagne from its bowl. The Montreal Canadiens have won it a record 24 times.
