The Sandinista National Liberation Front (Spanish: Frente Sandinista de Liberación Nacional, FSLN) is a political party in Nicaragua. Founded in 1961 as a guerrilla movement, it overthrew the US-backed Somoza dictatorship in 1979 and governed Nicaragua through the 1980s; it returned to power in 2007 under Daniel Ortega and has governed since.

Origins

The FSLN was founded by Carlos Fonseca, Tomás Borge and Silvio Mayorga, among others, and named for Augusto César Sandino, who led resistance to the US occupation of Nicaragua in the 1920s–30s. After years of guerrilla struggle against the regime of Anastasio Somoza Debayle, a broad insurrection culminated in the Sandinista victory of 19 July 1979.

In government, 1979–1990

The Sandinista government introduced a literacy crusade, land reform and expanded health care, while nationalising Somoza-linked assets. The United States under President Reagan, viewing the FSLN as a Soviet-Cuban proxy, funded and armed the Contra rebels, producing a devastating civil war throughout the 1980s and the Iran–Contra affair in Washington. In 1984 the FSLN won Nicaragua's first free elections of the era; in 1990, to widespread surprise, it lost power peacefully to Violeta Chamorro's UNO coalition.

Return to power

After 16 years in opposition, Daniel Ortega won the 2006 presidential election and has been re-elected repeatedly. His later governments have been marked by economic growth and reduced poverty in their early years, but also by growing authoritarianism, the violent suppression of the 2018 protests, and disputed elections condemned by many international observers.
