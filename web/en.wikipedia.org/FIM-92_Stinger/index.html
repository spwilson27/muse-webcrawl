FIM-92 Stinger

The FIM-92 Stinger is an American man-portable air-defense system (MANPADS) that operates as an infrared homing surface-to-air missile. It can be adapted to fire from a wide variety of ground vehicles, helicopters, and other aircraft. Developed in the United States, it entered service in 1981.

History

The Stinger was developed by General Dynamics as a successor to the FIM-43 Redeye. It gained fame during the Soviet–Afghan War, when Stingers supplied to the Afghan mujahideen were credited with shooting down numerous Soviet aircraft and helicopters.

Design

The missile uses a passive infrared seeker and an identification friend-or-foe (IFF) system. Later variants, such as the Stinger-RMP, added reprogrammable microprocessors to counter enemy countermeasures.
