Coroners and Justice Act 2009

The Coroners and Justice Act 2009 (c. 25) is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom. It is a wide-ranging statute that reformed the coroner system in England and Wales and made changes across the criminal justice system, including to the partial defences to murder, the law on assisting suicide, witness anonymity, sentencing, and the retention of DNA profiles. It received royal assent on 12 November 2009.

Coroners

The Act created a national structure for coroners in England and Wales, including the office of Chief Coroner, and reformed the system of death investigation, inquests and treasure trove procedures. Many of the coronial provisions were implemented only after delay, with the office of Chief Coroner first filled in 2012.

Criminal law provisions

The Act abolished the partial defence of provocation to murder and replaced it with a new partial defence of "loss of control," and clarified that sexual infidelity alone may not be relied on as a qualifying trigger. It reformed the partial defence of diminished responsibility. It also addressed the offence of encouraging or assisting suicide, following debate about prosecutions of relatives who accompanied terminally ill people to the Dignitas clinic in Switzerland.

Other provisions

Other parts of the Act dealt with sentencing guidelines and the Sentencing Council, criminal memoirs exploitation, bail in murder cases, anonymity of witnesses, legal aid, and — controversially — the storage of DNA samples and fingerprints of people not convicted of an offence, a regime later replaced by the Protection of Freedoms Act 2012 following a ruling of the European Court of Human Rights.
