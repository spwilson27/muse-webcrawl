The Hatha Yoga Pradīpikā (Sanskrit: हठयोगप्रदीपिका, haṭhayogapradīpikā, "Light on Hatha Yoga") is a classic fifteenth-century Sanskrit manual on haṭha yoga, written by Svātmārāma, who connects the teaching's lineage to Matsyendranātha of the Nātha tradition. It is among the most influential surviving texts on haṭha yoga, and is one of the three classic texts of the tradition alongside the Gheranda Samhita and the Shiva Samhita.

Contents

The text is arranged in four chapters (upadeśas) containing roughly 389 verses in total. The first chapter discusses the proper environment for yoga practice, the ethical duties of the yogi, diet (including the concept of mitāhāra, moderate eating), and a set of āsanas (postures). The second chapter covers prāṇāyāma (breath control) and the ṣaṭkarmas, the six purification techniques. The third chapter treats mudrās (hand and body seals) and bandhas (locks) intended to awaken kuṇḍalinī. The fourth chapter deals with samādhi, the goal of the practice.

Āsanas

The Pradīpikā describes fifteen āsanas, the first textual list of its kind, most of them seated meditation postures such as Svastikāsana, Gomukhāsana, Vīrāsana, and the lotus-like Padmāsana. It gives particular emphasis to Siddhāsana, which it praises as the most important of all postures, and it mentions a total of 84 āsanas by tradition, describing only the most significant in detail.

Influence

The Hatha Yoga Pradīpikā was widely copied and commented upon; a notable commentary, the Jyotsnā, was written by Brahmānanda in the nineteenth century. The text has been translated into English numerous times, including an 1893 translation by Pancham Sinh, and it strongly influenced the development of modern postural yoga in the twentieth century.
