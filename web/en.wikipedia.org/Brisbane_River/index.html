The Brisbane River (Turrbal: Maiwar) is the longest river in south-east Queensland, Australia, flowing 344 km through the city of Brisbane before emptying into Moreton Bay. It is dammed by the Wivenhoe Dam, forming Lake Wivenhoe, a major water supply for the region.

History

Explorer John Oxley named the river in 1823 after Sir Thomas Brisbane, Governor of New South Wales. The river has flooded catastrophically several times, notably in 1893, 1974, 2011 and 2022, inundating large parts of the city.

Crossings and transport

The river is crossed by numerous bridges including the Story Bridge (1940) and the Gateway Bridge. CityCat ferries provide public transport along its lower reaches.
