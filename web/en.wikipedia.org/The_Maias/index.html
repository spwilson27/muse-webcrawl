The Maias

The Maias (Portuguese: Os Maias) is an 1888 realist novel by Portuguese author José Maria de Eça de Queirós. It is widely regarded as the greatest Portuguese novel of the 19th century.

Plot

The novel traces three generations of the Maia family. Carlos da Maia, a wealthy young physician in Lisbon, falls in love with Maria Eduarda — who is revealed to be his long-lost sister, separated in childhood after their mother Maria Monforte eloped. The revelation destroys the family: their grandfather Afonso da Maia dies of shock, and Carlos leaves Portugal.

Themes

Beyond the tragic incest plot, the novel is a sweeping satire of Lisbon high society, politics, journalism and romantic decadence in 1870s Portugal. It has been adapted for film (2014, João Botelho) and television. See also The Spanish Tragedy.
