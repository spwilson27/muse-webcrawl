The Schuylkill River at Fairmount Park

Fairmount Park is the largest municipal park in Philadelphia, and the historic name for a group of parks located throughout the city. Situated on both banks of the Schuylkill River and on the Wissahickon Creek, it covers 2,052 acres (830 ha) and is one of the largest urban park systems in the United States. The park system was named a National Historic Landmark in recognition of its design and of the many surviving historic houses, bridges, and structures within it.

History

The park originated with Lemon Hill, the estate of Robert Morris acquired by the city in 1844, and with the city's purchase of land around the Fairmount Water Works on the Schuylkill to protect the water supply from industrial pollution. The Fairmount Park Commission was established in 1867 to administer the growing park. In 1876, Fairmount Park hosted the Centennial Exposition, the first official World's Fair in the United States, celebrating the 100th anniversary of the Declaration of Independence.

Features

The park contains the Philadelphia Museum of Art, the Philadelphia Zoo (the oldest zoo in the United States, chartered in 1859 and opened in 1874), the Please Touch Museum, the Shofuso Japanese House and Garden, Boathouse Row along the Schuylkill, and the Horticulture Center. The Fairmount Park Horticulture Center was built on the site of the 1876 exposition's Horticultural Hall. Many historic mansions are preserved in the park, including Mount Pleasant, Strawberry Mansion, Lemon Hill, and Woodford.
