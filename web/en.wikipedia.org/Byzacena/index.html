Byzacena (also spelled Byzacium) was a Late Roman and early Byzantine province in what is now the Sahel region of Tunisia, on the eastern coast of North Africa. It was formed when the emperor Diocletian divided the large province of Africa Proconsularis, separating the southern coastal territory into a province of its own, administered under the Diocese of Africa.

Geography and cities

The province's capital was Hadrumetum (modern Sousse), a wealthy port founded by Phoenicians. Other important cities included Thysdrus (El Jem), famous for its enormous amphitheatre, Sufetula (Sbeitla), Leptis Minor, Tacape (Gabès) on the edge of the desert, and Hadrumetum's rival ports along the Gulf of Hammamet. Inland areas produced olive oil on a large scale — a major source of the province's prosperity and tax revenue.

History

Byzacena was overrun by the Vandals in the 5th century and formed part of their North African kingdom until the Byzantine reconquest under Belisarius in 533–534, after which it was incorporated into the Praetorian Prefecture of Africa and later the Exarchate of Africa. The province was a stronghold of Donatist and later Catholic Christianity, and suffered in the Berber revolts of the 6th century. Arab forces conquered the region in the late 7th century, and the Battle of Sufetula (647) marked a decisive step toward the fall of Byzantine Africa.
