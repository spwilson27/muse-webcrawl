Information retrieval

FieldComputer science, information science
Core taskFinding material relevant to an information need
Key modelsBoolean, vector space, probabilistic (BM25), neural
EvaluationPrecision, recall, MAP, nDCG

Information retrieval (IR) is the science and practice of obtaining information resources relevant to an information need from a collection of those resources. Searches can be based on full-text or other content-based indexing. Information retrieval is the science of searching for information in a document, searching for documents themselves, and also searching for the metadata and databases of texts, images, or sounds.

Overview

An IR system represents documents and queries in a comparable form, matches them, and ranks results by estimated relevance. Classic approaches include the Boolean model, the vector space model with tf–idf term weighting, and probabilistic models such as BM25, which remains a strong baseline. Modern systems add learning-to-rank methods and neural representations, including dense embeddings produced by transformer models, and combine retrieval with language models in retrieval-augmented generation.

Evaluation

Systems are evaluated with test collections of documents, queries, and relevance judgments — a methodology pioneered by the Cranfield experiments and institutionalised in the TREC conferences. Standard measures include precision and recall, mean average precision, and normalised discounted cumulative gain (nDCG), which reward systems that place relevant documents at the top of the ranking.

Applications

Web search engines are the best-known application, but IR techniques also power library catalogues, legal and medical document search, enterprise search, recommender systems, question answering, and digital forensics. The field overlaps with information science, natural language processing, databases, and human–computer interaction.
