Swing vote

Image placeholder: voters on election day.

A swing vote is a vote that is seen to hold the balance of power in an election, especially one cast by a swing voter — a voter who has no strong allegiance to a political party and who can be persuaded to vote for either side. Swing voters are prized targets of political campaigns, particularly in swing states and marginal constituencies.

Characteristics

Swing voters are often described as political independents or moderates, though research suggests they include both genuinely undecided citizens and low-information or "paying sporadic attention" voters whose preferences shift between elections. Classic archetypes used by pollsters include "soccer moms" and "NASCAR dads" in the United States.

Influence

Because elections are frequently decided by a small share of persuadable voters in competitive areas, campaign resources are disproportionately directed at swing voters, while parties simultaneously work to turn out their committed "base". Critics argue this distorts policy toward the preferences of a small, atypical slice of the electorate.
