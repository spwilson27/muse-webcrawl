Lewis H. Morgan

Lewis Henry Morgan (21 November 1818 – 17 December 1881) was an American anthropologist and social theorist, considered one of the founders of modern anthropology. His studies of the Iroquois and of kinship systems worldwide influenced both anthropology and later social theory.

Work

Morgan worked closely with the Seneca people and the Ely Parker (Donehogawa), publishing League of the Ho-dé-no-sau-nee or Iroquois in 1851. His Systems of Consanguinity and Affinity of the Human Family (1871) compared kinship terminologies around the world.

Ancient Society

In Ancient Society (1877) Morgan proposed that societies evolve through stages he called savagery, barbarism and civilization, driven by technological change. Though his unilineal evolutionism is no longer accepted, the work influenced Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels's The Origin of the Family, Private Property and the State (1884).
