Chronic condition

A chronic condition (also known as a chronic disease or chronic illness) is a health condition or disease that is persistent or otherwise long-lasting in its effects, or that develops over time. The term is often applied when the course of the disease lasts for more than three months. Common chronic diseases include diabetes, cardiovascular diseases, arthritis, asthma, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, chronic kidney disease, Alzheimer's disease and other dementias, depression and some cancers.

Characteristics

Chronic conditions often have complex, multiple causes, long latency periods, and a prolonged course of illness that may lead to disability or functional impairment. Many are non-communicable diseases (NCDs) associated with risk factors such as tobacco use, unhealthy diet, physical inactivity, harmful use of alcohol, air pollution and high blood pressure, though infectious diseases such as HIV/AIDS and hepatitis C are also chronic.

Burden

According to the World Health Organization, noncommunicable diseases kill about 41 million people each year, equivalent to roughly 74% of all deaths globally. Management typically emphasises long-term self-care, monitoring and lifestyle change rather than cure, placing a heavy burden on health systems as populations age; multimorbidity — several chronic conditions at once — becomes common among older adults.
