Warsaw (Polish: Warszawa) is the capital and largest city of Poland, situated on the Vistula River in east-central Poland. Its population is officially estimated at around 1.86 million residents within the city limits, with a metropolitan area of roughly 3.1 million, making it one of the most populous cities in the European Union. Warsaw is an alpha global city, a major centre of culture, politics and the economy, and the seat of the Polish president, parliament (Sejm and Senate) and government.

History

Warsaw originated as a small fishing village on the Vistula. It became the seat of the Dukes of Masovia and, after the incorporation of Masovia into the Kingdom of Poland in 1526, grew in importance. In 1596 King Sigismund III Vasa moved the royal court from Kraków to Warsaw, making it the effective capital of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth. The city flourished in the 17th and 18th centuries, especially under the last king, Stanisław II Augustus, a patron of the arts.

After the partitions of Poland, Warsaw passed between Prussian and Russian rule and was the capital of the Napoleonic Duchy of Warsaw and later of Congress Poland within the Russian Empire. It became the capital of the reborn Polish state in 1918.

Second World War and rebuilding

Warsaw suffered catastrophic destruction in the Second World War. Following the invasion of 1939, the city's Jewish population was confined in the Warsaw Ghetto, whose 1943 uprising was crushed. In August 1944 the Polish Home Army rose in the Warsaw Uprising; after 63 days of fighting, the Germans expelled the surviving population and systematically demolished the city — about 85 per cent of Warsaw was destroyed. After the war, the historic Old Town was meticulously rebuilt from paintings, drawings and surviving fragments, an effort recognised by UNESCO, which inscribed the Old Town as a World Heritage Site in 1980 as an outstanding example of near-total reconstruction.

Economy and culture

Today Warsaw is Poland's economic powerhouse, home to the Warsaw Stock Exchange, the National Bank of Poland, and the headquarters of many international companies. Landmarks include the Royal Castle, Łazienki Park with its Palace on the Isle, Wilanów Palace, and the socialist-realist Palace of Culture and Science (1955), for decades the city's tallest building. The city is associated with the composer Frédéric Chopin, born nearby, and with Marie Skłodowska-Curie, born in Warsaw in 1867.
