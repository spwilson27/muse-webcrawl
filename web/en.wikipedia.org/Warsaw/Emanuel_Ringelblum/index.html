Emanuel Ringelblum

Emanuel Ringelblum (21 November 1900 – 10 March 1944) was a Polish-Jewish historian, politician, educator and social worker, known for his Notes from the Warsaw Ghetto and for founding the clandestine Oyneg Shabbos archive in the Warsaw Ghetto, one of the most important documentary collections of the Holocaust.

Early life and career

Ringelblum was born in Buczacz in Austrian Galicia (now Ukraine) and studied history at the University of Warsaw, receiving his doctorate in 1927. He taught in Jewish secondary schools, worked with the YIVO Institute and the American Jewish Joint Distribution Committee, and wrote historical studies of Jewish life in Poland, including a history of the Jews of Warsaw. Active in the Left Poale Zion party, he was a leading figure in Jewish social welfare work.

The Oyneg Shabbos archive

Confined in the Warsaw Ghetto, Ringelblum organised a secret group, code-named Oyneg Shabbos (“Joy of the Sabbath”), whose dozens of contributors — rabbis, journalists, economists, teachers and ordinary residents — collected diaries, essays, official decrees, ration cards, posters, tram tickets, candy wrappers and studies of ghetto life. The archive’s purpose was to document Jewish experience under Nazi occupation from the victims’ own perspective. Its materials were buried in milk cans and metal boxes in three caches in 1942 and 1943.

Death and recovery of the archive

Ringelblum escaped to the “Aryan” side of Warsaw but returned to the ghetto; he later hid with his family in a bunker in the Żoliborz area. Betrayed in March 1944, he was arrested, tortured and executed in the ruins of the ghetto together with his wife and son. Two of the three archive caches were recovered after the war, in 1946 and 1950, yielding some 6,000 documents; the third was never found. The Ringelblum Archive is preserved at the Jewish Historical Institute in Warsaw and was inscribed on UNESCO’s Memory of the World Register in 1999.
