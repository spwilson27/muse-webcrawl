Combined Operations Headquarters

Combined Operations Headquarters was a department of the British War Office set up during the Second World War to harass the Germans by means of raids in occupied Europe carried out by the Commandos and to develop the techniques and equipment for amphibious warfare. It was established in June 1940, initially under Admiral of the Fleet Sir Roger Keyes.

Leadership and operations

In October 1941 Lord Louis Mountbatten became Chief of Combined Operations, and under him the organisation grew greatly in influence. Combined Operations planned and supported raids including the Lofoten Islands raid, the Bruneval raid, the St Nazaire Raid and the disastrous Dieppe Raid of August 1942. The department's insignia — an eagle over a submachine gun over an anchor — symbolised the three services working together.

Legacy

Experience gained in landing craft design, beach organisation, floating "Mulberry" harbours and the PLUTO undersea pipelines fed directly into the planning of the Normandy landings (Operation Overlord). Mountbatten was succeeded in 1943 by Major-General Robert Laycock. Combined Operations Headquarters was wound up after the war, but its joint-service amphibious doctrine shaped postwar British forces.
