The Kurdistan Region (Kurdish: هەرێمی کوردستان, Herêmî Kurdistan) is an autonomous federal region in northern Iraq, governed by the Kurdistan Regional Government (KRG). It comprises the governorates of Erbil, Sulaymaniyah, Duhok and Halabja, with Erbil as its capital, and borders Turkey to the north, Iran to the east and Syria to the west.

History

Kurdish self-rule was first recognised in the 1970 Autonomy Accord with Baghdad, which was never fully implemented. After the 1991 Gulf War, a Western-enforced no-fly zone protected the region following the Kurdish uprising against Saddam Hussein, and in 1992 the Kurds elected their own parliament and government — though the two dominant parties, the Kurdistan Democratic Party (KDP) and the Patriotic Union of Kurdistan (PUK), fought a civil war in the mid-1990s before reconciling.

Iraq's 2005 constitution formally recognised the Kurdistan Region as a federal entity, with its own parliament, presidency, and security forces, the Peshmerga. The region experienced an economic boom driven by oil production and foreign investment, and it bore the brunt of the war against the Islamic State from 2014, sheltering nearly two million displaced people and refugees while Peshmerga forces held a thousand-kilometre front line.

The 2017 referendum and aftermath

On 25 September 2017 the region held a referendum in which over 92% of voters backed independence. Baghdad, backed by regional powers, rejected the vote; Iraqi forces retook the disputed city of Kirkuk and its oilfields in October 2017, and the KRG froze the referendum's results. Disputes over oil exports, budget shares and disputed territories continue to define relations between Erbil and Baghdad.

Geography and economy

The region ranges from the plains around Erbil to the Zagros mountains along the Iranian and Turkish borders, home to ski resorts and the region's highest peak, Cheekha Dar (3,611 m). Its economy rests on oil and gas, agriculture, trade and tourism; the region issues its own investment law and has been marketed as Iraq's secure gateway, though salary arrears in the dominant public sector and dependence on oil revenue are persistent weaknesses.
