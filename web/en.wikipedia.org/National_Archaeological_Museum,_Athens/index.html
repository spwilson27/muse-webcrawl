National Archaeological Museum, Athens

Location: Exarcheia, Athens, Greece
Established: 1829 (current building opened 1889)
Collection: over 11,000 exhibits

The National Archaeological Museum (Greek: Εθνικό Αρχαιολογικό Μουσείο) in Athens houses some of the most important artifacts from a variety of archaeological locations around Greece from prehistory to late antiquity. It is considered one of the greatest museums in the world, holding the richest collection of Greek antiquities anywhere.

Collections

Highlights include the gold funerary "Mask of Agamemnon" and other treasures from Mycenae unearthed by Heinrich Schliemann; the Antikythera mechanism, the ancient geared astronomical calculator recovered from a shipwreck in 1901, together with the Antikythera Ephebe bronze; the Artemision Bronze — a monumental statue of Zeus (or Poseidon) recovered from the sea; the frescoes of Akrotiri on Thera (Santorini); the marble statue of the Jockey of Artemision; Cycladic figurines; and an extensive Egyptian collection. The museum's holdings span the Neolithic era through Roman Greece.

The building

The imposing neoclassical building in the Exarcheia district was designed by Ludwig Lange and Ernst Ziller; construction began in 1866 and the museum opened to the public in 1889. During the Second World War the treasures were buried and dispersed for protection. Modern renovations, completed in stages around the 2004 Athens Olympics, renewed the displays.
