Tajik language

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Tajik (Tajik: забони тоҷикӣ, zaboni tojikī), also called Tajiki Persian (форсӣ тоҷикӣ, forsii tojikī), is the variety of Persian spoken in Tajikistan and Uzbekistan by Tajiks. It is closely related to neighbouring Dari of Afghanistan and the less closely related Iranian Persian, forming a mutually intelligible continuum of the Persian language. Several scholars consider Tajik as a dialectal variety of Persian.

Tajik is the official language of Tajikistan. In Afghanistan, where the Tajik population forms a large minority, this language is spoken under the name of Dari — one of the country's two official languages. In Uzbekistan, Tajik is a recognized minority language spoken in the cities of Samarkand and Bukhara.

Writing system

Tajik was written in the Perso-Arabic script until 1928, when the Soviet authorities introduced a Latin-based alphabet. This was replaced in 1940 by a modified Cyrillic alphabet, which remains in use today. Tajik Cyrillic includes additional letters for sounds not found in Russian, such as ҳ, ҷ, ӣ, ӯ, қ and ғ.

History

The language descends from Classical Persian, the literary language of the Samanid court in Bukhara and Samarkand, where poets such as Rudaki and Ferdowsi composed works that remain foundational to Persian literature. Soviet nationality policy in the 1920s established Tajik as a distinct standardized language, and the Tajik Soviet Socialist Republic was created in 1929.

Vocabulary

Tajik retains many archaic features of Persian and has absorbed numerous Russian loanwords during the Soviet and post-Soviet periods, particularly in technical and administrative terminology, alongside Uzbek borrowings in everyday speech.
