Film

Film
MediumMoving images, usually with sound
OriginsLate 19th century (Lumière brothers, Edison)
Also calledMotion picture, movie

A film, also known as a movie or motion picture, is a work of visual art that simulates experiences and communicates ideas, stories, perceptions, and emotions through moving images, generally accompanied by sound. Films are produced by recording real people and objects, by animation, or by computer-generated imagery, and are shown in cinemas, on television, and via streaming.

History

The technology emerged in the 1890s from the work of inventors including Thomas Edison and the Lumière brothers, whose Cinématographe gave public screenings in Paris in 1895. Early films were short, silent, and black-and-white; the 1910s–20s saw feature-length narrative cinema flourish. Synchronised sound arrived with The Jazz Singer (1927), colour became standard by the 1950s, and the late 20th century brought the blockbuster era, then digital production and streaming distribution in the 21st.

Production

Filmmaking proceeds through development, pre-production, principal photography, post-production (editing, sound, visual effects), and distribution. Key creative roles include the director, screenwriter, cinematographer, editor, and producer. Industries are centred in places such as Hollywood (United States) and Mumbai (India, "Bollywood"), with significant national cinemas worldwide.

Study and appreciation

Film is the subject of an academic discipline, film studies, and of popular appraisal by critics — see film criticism. Major festivals such as Cannes, Venice, and Berlin, and awards such as the Academy Awards (Oscars), shape its culture and canon.
