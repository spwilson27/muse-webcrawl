Eurodisco

Eurodisco (also spelled Euro disco) is the variety of European forms of electronic dance music that evolved from disco in the 1970s and incorporated elements of pop, new wave, and rock. It typically features a "four-on-the-floor" beat, synthesizer lines, and lyrics often sung in English regardless of the performers' native language.

History

The genre emerged in the mid-1970s in Germany, France, and Italy. Munich-based producer Giorgio Moroder pioneered the electronic Eurodisco sound with Donna Summer's records, including "Love to Love You Baby" (1975) and the fully electronic "I Feel Love" (1977), produced with Pete Bellotte. Other influential acts included the French group Cerrone, Germany's Silver Convention and Boney M., and the Swedish group ABBA, whose sound epitomized mainstream European disco-pop.

Legacy

In the 1980s Eurodisco evolved into Italo disco and Eurobeat and fed into Hi-NRG and synth-pop. Its emphasis on drum machines, sequencers, and synthesizers laid groundwork for house, techno, and modern electronic dance music, and the term "Eurodisco" is also applied retrospectively to later European dance-pop styles.
