Industrial ecology


Material and energy flows through an industrial system, modelled on natural ecosystems.

Industrial ecology (IE) is the study of material and energy flows through industrial systems. It seeks to quantify the flows of resources, products and wastes in industry and to redesign industrial processes so that the waste of one process becomes the input of another, in analogy with natural ecosystems in which, in a mature system, virtually nothing is wasted. The field draws on systems ecology, engineering, economics and the environmental sciences, and provides much of the scientific basis for the concept of the circular economy.

Overview

Industrial ecology examines industry not as a set of isolated firms but as a network of processes connected by flows of materials, energy, water and by-products. Its practitioners apply tools such as material flow analysis, substance flow analysis, life-cycle assessment and input–output analysis to trace these flows from extraction through manufacture, use and final disposal. The goal is to reduce the total environmental burden of industrial activity by closing material loops, substituting benign inputs for hazardous ones, dematerialising products and extending product lifetimes.

History

The intellectual roots of industrial ecology reach back to the 1960s and 1970s, when systems thinkers began describing industrial activity in metabolic terms. The term was popularised by a 1989 Scientific American article by Robert Frosch and Nicholas Gallopoulos, "Strategies for Manufacturing", which proposed that industrial systems should be modelled on biological ecosystems. The field was institutionalised during the 1990s through the work of researchers such as Robert Ayres, Thomas Graedel and Braden Allenby, and through the founding of the Journal of Industrial Ecology in 1997 and the International Society for Industrial Ecology in 2000.

Key concepts

Industrial symbiosis

Industrial symbiosis describes exchanges of materials, energy, water and by-products between co-located firms. The classic example is the eco-industrial park at Kalundborg, Denmark, where a power station, refinery, pharmaceutical plant, plasterboard manufacturer and other firms exchange steam, gypsum, fly ash, water and other streams.

Industrial metabolism

Industrial metabolism, a term associated with Robert Ayres, treats the whole industrial system as an organism that ingests raw materials and energy and excretes products and wastes, allowing mass-balance accounting of economies at national and global scales.

Life-cycle assessment

Life-cycle assessment (LCA) evaluates the environmental impacts of a product or service across its entire life, from "cradle to grave" or, in closed-loop formulations, "cradle to cradle". LCA is standardised in the ISO 14040 series.
