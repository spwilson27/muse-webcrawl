The Franco-Dutch War, also known as the Dutch War (French: Guerre de Hollande), was a conflict between the Dutch Republic and France from 1672 to 1678, into which several other European states were drawn. France, supported by England and several German prince-bishoprics, launched a massive invasion of the Republic in 1672 — the Dutch Rampjaar ("Disaster Year") — which came close to destroying the Dutch state, but the Dutch held out behind their water lines, and from 1673 a broad coalition formed against France.

Background

Louis XIV of France sought glory, territory in the Spanish Netherlands, and revenge on the Dutch Republic, whose Triple Alliance of 1668 had forced him to halt his advance in the War of Devolution. He also resented Dutch commercial dominance and the flood of anti-French pamphlets printed in the Republic. By the secret Treaty of Dover (1670), Louis secured the alliance of Charles II of England, and also gained the support of Münster and Cologne.

The invasion of 1672

In April 1672 France declared war, and some 120,000 French troops — one of the largest armies Europe had yet seen — invaded, bypassing the fortress of Maastricht and crossing the Rhine at Lobith on 12 June. Utrecht and much of the eastern Republic fell within weeks; the Dutch government of Johan de Witt collapsed amid panic, De Witt and his brother Cornelis were murdered by a mob in The Hague, and William III of Orange was appointed stadtholder. The Dutch saved Amsterdam by opening the sluices and flooding the Dutch Waterline. At sea, Admiral Michiel de Ruyter fought the combined Anglo-French fleets to a standstill.

Coalition and conclusion

In 1673 French forces evacuated most of the Republic to face a growing coalition of Spain, the Holy Roman Emperor, Brandenburg, and Denmark. England made peace at the Treaty of Westminster (1674). The war continued in Flanders, the Rhineland, Sicily, and overseas until the Treaties of Nijmegen (1678–79) ended it: France gained Franche-Comté and several Flemish towns from Spain, while the Dutch Republic survived intact and retained its independence — a strategic defeat for Louis's ambitions despite French battlefield successes.
