Kodiak Island

Location: Gulf of Alaska
Area: 3,670 sq mi (9,310 km²)
Largest settlement: Kodiak
Administration: Kodiak Island Borough, Alaska

Kodiak Island is a large island on the south coast of Alaska, separated from the Alaska Peninsula by the Shelikof Strait. It is the largest island in the Kodiak Archipelago and the second-largest island in the United States, after the island of Hawaii.

Geography and wildlife

The island covers about 3,670 square miles (9,310 km²). Its mountainous, forested terrain and abundant salmon streams support the Kodiak bear, a large subspecies of brown bear that rivals the polar bear in size. Much of the island lies within the Kodiak National Wildlife Refuge, established in 1941.

History and economy

The Alutiiq (Sugpiaq) people have inhabited the island for millennia. Russian colonists under Grigory Shelikhov founded Three Saints Bay in 1784, the first permanent Russian settlement in Alaska. The city of Kodiak, the island's main community, is a major commercial fishing port, and the island hosts a U.S. Coast Guard base and the Pacific Spaceport Complex – Alaska.
