Customer retention

Customer retention refers to the ability of a company or product to retain its customers over a specified period. High customer retention means customers tend to return to purchase or continue using a company's goods or services. It is the opposite of customer attrition (churn), and is closely related to customer loyalty and satisfaction.

Measurement

The customer retention rate is the percentage of customers a business keeps over a given period, often computed as ((customers at end − new customers) / customers at start) × 100. The complementary churn rate measures how many customers are lost. Retention strongly affects profitability: classic studies found that acquiring a new customer can cost several times more than retaining an existing one, and small increases in retention can produce disproportionate increases in profit.

Strategies

Common retention strategies include loyalty programmes, personalised communication, excellent customer service, subscriptions, onboarding support, and proactive engagement with customers at risk of leaving. Customer relationship management (CRM) systems are widely used to track interactions and identify churn risks.
