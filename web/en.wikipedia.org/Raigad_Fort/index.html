Raigad Fort

Raigad Fort, capital of the Maratha Empire under Shivaji.

Raigad is a hill fort in the Raigad district of Maharashtra, India, situated in the Sahyadri mountain range about 820 metres (2,700 ft) above sea level. Formerly known as Rairi, it was seized by the Maratha ruler Shivaji in 1656 from the More family of Jawali. Shivaji greatly expanded and fortified it, and in 1674 he was crowned Chhatrapati (king) there, making Raigad the capital of the Maratha Empire.

Features

The fort is reached by a climb of some 1,700–1,800 steps (a ropeway now also serves visitors). Within the walls stand the ruins of the royal palace, the queen's quarters, the main marketplace street in stone, watch towers such as the Maha Darwaza bastions, water reservoirs, and the samadhi (memorial tomb) of Shivaji near the Jagadishwar temple. A famous episode of 1680 recounts that Shivaji's dog Waghya was cremated alongside him; a statue of the dog stands at the site.

Later history

After Shivaji's death in 1680 the fort passed to Sambhaji, was captured by the Mughals in 1689, returned to Maratha hands, and was finally taken and partly dismantled by the British in 1818. It remains a major pilgrimage and tourist site in Maharashtra; in 2025 it was inscribed as part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing of the Maratha Military Landscapes of India.
