Deepwater Horizon oil spill

The Deepwater Horizon oil spill was an environmental disaster that began on 20 April 2010 in the Gulf of Mexico, off the coast of the United States. It is considered the largest marine oil spill in the history of the petroleum industry.

Explosion and spill

An explosion and fire on the Deepwater Horizon, a semi-submersible drilling rig operated by Transocean and leased to BP, killed eleven workers and caused the rig to sink two days later. The Macondo well, located about 1,500 metres below the surface, discharged oil for 87 days before it was capped on 15 July 2010. The US government estimated a total discharge of about 134 million gallons (roughly 3.2 million barrels) of oil, after accounting for oil collected.

Environmental and economic impact

Oil washed ashore along the coasts of Louisiana, Mississippi, Alabama and Florida, damaging wetlands, fisheries and tourism. Extensive use was made of the dispersant Corexit. Long-term studies have documented effects on marine life including dolphins, sea turtles and deep-sea corals.

Legal consequences

BP and its partners faced extensive litigation. In 2012 BP pleaded guilty to criminal charges and agreed to pay $4.5 billion in fines and penalties. In 2015 a settlement of civil claims by the US government and five Gulf states totalled $20.8 billion, the largest environmental damage settlement in US history. The disaster also prompted new offshore drilling regulations and the reorganisation of the federal regulator.
