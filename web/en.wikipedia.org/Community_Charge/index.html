Community Charge

The Community Charge, popularly known as the poll tax, was a system of local taxation introduced by the government of Margaret Thatcher in the United Kingdom. It replaced domestic rates — a property tax — with a flat-rate charge payable by every adult resident, set by the local authority. It was introduced in Scotland in 1989 and in England and Wales in 1990.

Opposition

The charge was widely perceived as unfair because a low-paid worker paid the same amount as a wealthy resident of a large house. A mass campaign of non-payment, organised by groups such as the All Britain Anti-Poll Tax Federation, saw millions refuse to pay. On 31 March 1990 a large demonstration in London ended in the Poll Tax Riots in and around Trafalgar Square. Non-payment rates reached 30 per cent or more in some areas.

Abolition

The unpopularity of the tax contributed to Thatcher's resignation in November 1990. Her successor, John Major, announced its replacement, and the Community Charge was abolished in 1993 in favour of the Council Tax, a property-based tax with a personal element.
