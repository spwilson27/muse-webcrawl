Far from the Madding Crowd (1874) is Thomas Hardy's fourth novel and his first major literary success. It first appeared as a monthly serial in Cornhill Magazine, where its authorship was widely mistaken for that of George Eliot. The novel is set in Hardy's fictional Wessex, a rural landscape modelled on south-west England, and its title is taken from Thomas Gray's "Elegy Written in a Country Churchyard" (1751).

Plot

The beautiful and independent Bathsheba Everdene inherits a farm in the village of Weatherbury and resolves to run it herself. She is pursued by three very different suitors: the steadfast shepherd Gabriel Oak, who loses his own flock in a disaster and comes to work for her; the prosperous, reserved farmer William Boldwood, whose obsession she carelessly awakens by sending a thoughtless valentine; and the dashing, irresponsible Sergeant Francis Troy, whom she impulsively marries. Troy's former love, Fanny Robin, dies in childbirth bearing his child, and the marriage collapses; Troy is presumed drowned, then returns at a Christmas party and is shot dead by Boldwood. Boldwood is imprisoned, and after a period of mourning Bathsheba finally marries Gabriel Oak, whose quiet devotion has outlasted every rival.

Themes and adaptations

The novel explores love, pride, social class, and the rhythms of rural labour against a nature that is magnificent but indifferent. Its early success allowed Hardy to give up architecture and write full-time. It has been adapted many times, notably in John Schlesinger's 1967 film with Julie Christie and Terence Stamp, and Thomas Vinterberg's 2015 film starring Carey Mulligan and Matthias Schoenaerts.
