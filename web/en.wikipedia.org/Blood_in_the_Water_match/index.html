Blood in the Water match

The "Blood in the Water" match (Hungarian: melbourne-i vérfürdő, "Melbourne bloodbath") was a water polo match between Hungary and the USSR at the 1956 Melbourne Summer Olympics, played on 6 December 1956. The match, which Hungary won 4–0, was held against the backdrop of the Hungarian Revolution of 1956, which Soviet forces had violently suppressed weeks earlier.

Background

By the time of the match, many of the Hungarian athletes had heard the revolt had been crushed, and tensions between the teams were extreme. The semi-final round match was exceptionally rough from the start, with kicks and punches exchanged as the game progressed.

The match

Hungary adopted a simple strategy of man-to-man marking and quick passes, and led 4–0 late in the game. With about a minute remaining, Soviet player Valentin Prokopov punched Hungary's Ervin Zádor above the eye, opening a cut that bled into the pool. Photographs of Zádor emerging from the water with blood streaming down his face were published around the world and gave the match its name. The game was halted and police were called as spectators surged toward the Soviet team; Hungary was declared the winner and went on to defeat Yugoslavia in the final for the gold medal.

Legacy

Several Hungarian players defected after the Games rather than return to Soviet-occupied Hungary. The match is the subject of the 2006 documentary film Freedom's Fury, produced by Quentin Tarantino and Lucy Liu.
