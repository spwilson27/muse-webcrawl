Alcyone and Ceyx

Alcyone and Ceyx
SourceGreek mythology; Ovid, Metamorphoses Book 11

In Greek mythology, Alcyone and Ceyx were a devoted married couple — she the daughter of Aeolus, king of the winds, and he the king of Trachis. Their story, most famously told in Book 11 of Ovid's Metamorphoses, ends in their transformation into kingfishers.

The myth

Ceyx sailed to consult an oracle despite Alcyone's forebodings, and was drowned in a storm raised by the gods — punishment, in some versions, for the couple's having called each other Zeus and Hera. Alcyone, informed of his death in a dream by Morpheus, found his body washed ashore and threw herself into the sea. Out of compassion the gods transformed them both into halcyon birds (kingfishers).

Halcyon days

The myth explains the "halcyon days": a period of calm weather around the winter solstice during which, it was believed, Alcyone brooded her eggs in a floating nest and Aeolus stilled the winds. The phrase now means a time of peace and happiness.
