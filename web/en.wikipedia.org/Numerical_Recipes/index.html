Numerical Recipes

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Cover of Numerical Recipes: The Art of Scientific Computing

Numerical Recipes: The Art of Scientific Computing is a series of books on algorithms and numerical analysis by William H. Press, Saul A. Teukolsky, William T. Vetterling and Brian P. Flannery. First published in 1986, the books combine expository text with complete, ready-to-use source code implementing each algorithm.

Contents

The series covers solution of linear equations, interpolation and extrapolation, integration of functions, evaluation of special functions, random numbers, sorting, root finding, minimisation, eigensystems, Fourier methods, ordinary and partial differential equations, data modelling and statistics, and many other topics central to scientific computing.

Editions and languages

Editions have appeared for FORTRAN, Pascal, BASIC, C, C++, Fortran 90 and Fortran 2003. The third edition, Numerical Recipes: The Art of Scientific Computing (2007), was substantially reorganised and rewritten in C++. The books' distinctive style — informal, opinionated, and practical — made them among the most widely used references in scientific computing.

Reception

While praised for accessibility, the series has been criticised by some numerical analysts for favouring simple methods over state-of-the-art algorithms, and for its restrictive software licence, which limits copying of the code.
