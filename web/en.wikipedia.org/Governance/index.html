Governance

Governance is the overall process of making and implementing decisions within an organization or society. It encompasses the systems, rules, procedures, and relationships by which authority is exercised and accountability is ensured, whether in a government, a corporation, a non-profit organization, or a network of actors.

Concept

The term derives from the Latin gubernare, "to steer". Unlike "government", which refers to a specific institution, "governance" describes processes of coordination and decision-making that may involve public, private, and civil-society actors. Key elements commonly identified include accountability, transparency, participation, the rule of law, responsiveness, and effectiveness.

Forms

Public governance concerns how states and public institutions make and deliver policy; scholars distinguish hierarchical government from governance through markets and networks. Corporate governance is the system by which companies are directed and controlled, involving boards of directors, shareholders, and disclosure rules. Global governance refers to the institutions and norms — such as the United Nations, the World Trade Organization, and international treaties — that manage issues across national borders.

Good governance

Development institutions such as the World Bank and the United Nations Development Programme promote "good governance" as a condition for sustainable development, measuring it through indicators of voice and accountability, political stability, government effectiveness, regulatory quality, rule of law, and control of corruption.
