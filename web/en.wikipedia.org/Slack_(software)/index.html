Slack is a cloud-based team communication platform developed by Slack Technologies, a subsidiary of Salesforce. Launched in August 2013, Slack organises workplace communication into channels — dedicated spaces for teams, projects, or topics — alongside direct messaging, voice and video "huddles", file sharing, and integrations with other software services.

History

Slack grew out of an internal tool built by Stewart Butterfield's company Tiny Speck while developing the online game Glitch. After the game shut down in 2012, the communication tool was developed into Slack, whose name is an acronym for "Searchable Log of All Conversation and Knowledge". The company went public via a direct listing in 2019 and was acquired by Salesforce in 2021 in a deal valued at about $27.7 billion.

Features

Slack offers public and private channels, threaded replies, emoji reactions, search across message history, and a large directory of third-party app integrations. Paid plans add unlimited message history, group calls, and enterprise security features. In recent years the platform has added canvas documents, lists, automation via Workflow Builder, and AI-assisted summaries and search.

Reception

Slack was credited with popularising channel-based messaging in the workplace and reducing internal email, though critics have raised concerns about information overload, constant availability, and the blurring of work-life boundaries.
