Reign in Blood

Reign in Blood is the third studio album by American thrash metal band Slayer, released on October 7, 1986, by Def Jam Recordings. Produced by Rick Rubin, the album marked a significant departure from the band's earlier, longer compositions: its ten songs run under thirty minutes in total and are played at breakneck tempos. It is widely regarded as one of the most influential extreme metal albums ever recorded.

Recording and release

Working with Rubin, Slayer adopted a stripped-down, dry production that emphasised speed and precision. The album opens with "Angel of Death", whose lyrics about Nazi physician Josef Mengele drew controversy and accusations — rejected by the band — of Nazi sympathies. Other tracks, including "Piece by Piece", "Necrophobic", "Altar of Sacrifice", and the closing "Raining Blood", helped define the sonic template of thrash and influenced the later development of death metal and black metal.

Def Jam's distributor, Columbia Records, initially refused to release the album because of its graphic cover art and lyrical content; it was eventually distributed by Geffen Records, though it did not appear in Geffen's official release schedule. Despite minimal radio or television promotion, Reign in Blood became Slayer's first album to enter the Billboard 200.

Legacy

The album is routinely placed at or near the top of lists of the greatest metal albums of all time and is often cited as the high-water mark of thrash metal alongside works by Metallica, Megadeth, and Anthrax. It has been certified gold in the United States.
