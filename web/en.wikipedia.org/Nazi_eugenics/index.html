Nazi eugenics

Nazi eugenics were the racially based social policies of Nazi Germany that placed the supposed biological improvement of the Aryan master race at the centre of Nazi ideology. These policies led to compulsory sterilisation, forced "euthanasia" killings, and ultimately contributed to the Holocaust.

Programmes

The 1933 Law for the Prevention of Hereditarily Diseased Offspring authorised the forced sterilisation of about 400,000 people deemed to have hereditary conditions. From 1939, the Aktion T4 programme murdered tens of thousands of institutionalised people with disabilities; related child "euthanasia" programmes killed thousands of children. The Lebensborn programme encouraged the reproduction of those deemed racially valuable.

After the war

The crimes of Nazi racial hygiene were addressed at the Nuremberg trials, including the Doctors' Trial, which produced the Nuremberg Code on human experimentation.
