The Wasatch Range is a mountain range in the western United States that runs about 160 miles (260 km) from the Utah–Idaho border south through central Utah. It forms the eastern edge of the Great Basin and the western rim of the Rocky Mountains. The name derives from a Ute word meaning "mountain pass" or "low place in a high mountain".

Geography

The range's highest summit is Mount Nebo (3,636 m), near Nephi; other prominent peaks include Mount Timpanogos, Lone Peak, Twin Peaks and Mount Olympus, which tower over the Salt Lake Valley. The western front of the range rises abruptly from valley floors at about 1,300 m, a relief produced by movement on the Wasatch Fault, a major normal fault running along the range's base. Geologists consider a large earthquake on the fault a significant hazard for the urban corridor at its foot.

The Wasatch Front

Some 85 per cent of Utah's population lives in the Wasatch Front metropolitan corridor along the western base of the range, including Salt Lake City, Ogden and Provo. The mountains provide the region's water supply through snowpack-fed streams, and several canyons — Big and Little Cottonwood, Provo, Ogden and Logan canyons — cut into the range.

Recreation

The range is famous for its ski resorts, which market the light, dry powder produced by lake-effect snow from the Great Salt Lake as "the Greatest Snow on Earth". Resorts include Alta, Snowbird, Brighton, Solitude, Park City Mountain and Deer Valley (on the range's eastern slopes), Snowbasin and Powder Mountain. Ski jumping, bobsleigh and other venues in and near the range hosted events of the 2002 Winter Olympics in Salt Lake City. In summer the canyons offer hiking, climbing and mountain biking close to the metropolitan area.
