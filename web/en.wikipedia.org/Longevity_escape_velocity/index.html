Longevity escape velocity

Longevity escape velocity (LEV), also known as actuarial escape velocity, is a hypothetical future point at which medical progress extends remaining life expectancy by more than one year per year that passes, so that people who keep up with therapies could in principle avoid death from age-related causes indefinitely.

Concept

The term was popularised by biogerontologist Aubrey de Grey, who compared it to escape velocity in physics: once life expectancy gains outrun the passage of time, ageing effectively recedes. De Grey and other life-extension advocates argue that incremental “bridge” therapies — first-generation rejuvenation treatments, then more comprehensive ones — could carry patients to LEV.

Criticism

Critics regard the concept as speculative, noting that historical life-expectancy gains have slowed in many countries and that no therapy has yet been shown to substantially extend maximum human lifespan. The idea remains a motivating frame in the longevity research and SENS communities rather than a demonstrated milestone.
