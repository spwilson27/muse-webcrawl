Wolfgang Köhler

Born21 January 1887, Reval (Tallinn), Russian Empire
Died11 June 1967, Enfield, New Hampshire, US
Known forGestalt psychology, insight learning, the Köhler effect

Wolfgang Köhler (21 January 1887 – 11 June 1967) was a German psychologist and phenomenologist who, together with Max Wertheimer and Kurt Koffka, founded Gestalt psychology. A critic of behaviourism and structuralism, he argued that psychological phenomena must be understood as organised wholes.

Tenerife experiments

During the First World War Köhler directed an anthropoid research station on Tenerife in the Canary Islands. His studies of chimpanzees, published as The Mentality of Apes (1925), described how the chimpanzee Sultan solved problems by sudden insight — for example, joining two sticks to reach a banana — rather than by trial and error.

Later life

Köhler became director of the Psychological Institute at the University of Berlin in 1922. After publicly criticising the Nazi regime, he emigrated to the United States in 1935 and taught at Swarthmore College. He served as president of the American Psychological Association in 1959.
