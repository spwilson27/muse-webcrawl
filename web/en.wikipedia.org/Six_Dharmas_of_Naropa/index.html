The Six Dharmas of Naropa (Tibetan: na ro'i chos drug), sometimes called the Six Yogas of Naropa, are a set of advanced Tibetan Buddhist tantric practices compiled from the teachings transmitted by the Indian mahasiddha Tilopa to his disciple Naropa, and brought to Tibet by Marpa the Translator in the 11th century. They form a core practice system of the Kagyu schools.

The six practices

The six dharmas are: (1) tummo, the yoga of inner heat, in which the practitioner generates warmth through visualisation and breath retention; (2) the illusory body, recognising all phenomena as dreamlike; (3) dream yoga, maintaining awareness within dreams; (4) clear light (ösel), the luminosity of mind experienced in deep sleep and at death; (5) the bardo, guidance through the intermediate state after death; and (6) phowa, the transference of consciousness at the time of death.

Practice and transmission

The practices are traditionally taught only after empowerments and preliminary practices (ngöndro), under the guidance of a qualified lama, and are often undertaken during long retreats, including the traditional three-year retreat. Milarepa, Marpa's most famous disciple, was said to have relied chiefly on tummo, wearing only a cotton cloth in the Himalayan winter—hence his epithet "the cotton-clad one".

Influence

Related sets of six yogas exist in the Kalachakra tradition (the Six Yogas of Kalachakra), and a parallel set is attributed to Naropa's sister Niguma. Tummo meditation has attracted scientific interest for practitioners' demonstrated ability to raise peripheral body temperature.
