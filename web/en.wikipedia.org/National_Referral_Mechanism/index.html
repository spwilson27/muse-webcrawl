National Referral Mechanism

The National Referral Mechanism (NRM) is the United Kingdom's framework for identifying and supporting victims of modern slavery and human trafficking. It was introduced in 2009 to meet the UK's obligations under the Council of Europe Convention on Action against Trafficking in Human Beings.

Process

Only designated "first responders" — such as police forces, local authorities, the Home Office, and certain charities including the Salvation Army — can refer a potential victim into the NRM. The Single Competent Authority within the Home Office then makes two decisions: a "reasonable grounds" decision on whether there are grounds to believe the person is a victim, and later a "conclusive grounds" decision on the balance of probabilities.

Support

Adults who receive a positive reasonable grounds decision are entitled to a recovery period with support, including accommodation, subsistence and counselling, delivered in England and Wales through the Modern Slavery Victim Care Contract; arrangements differ in Scotland and Northern Ireland. Referrals have risen steeply since the system's creation, exceeding 17,000 a year in the early 2020s; the most commonly reported exploitation types are labour, criminal and sexual exploitation. The Illegal Migration Act 2023 introduced disqualifications for some people arriving irregularly, prompting legal and political controversy.
