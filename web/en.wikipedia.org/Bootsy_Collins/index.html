William Earl "Bootsy" Collins (born 26 October 1951) is an American bass guitarist, singer, and songwriter. One of the most influential bassists in funk music, he rose to fame with James Brown in the early 1970s before joining George Clinton's Parliament-Funkadelic collective, where his flamboyant stage persona, star-shaped basses, and rubbery, effects-laden playing helped define the P-Funk sound.

Career

Collins formed his first band, The Pacemakers, in Cincinnati in 1968. In 1970 the group was recruited as James Brown's backing band, the J.B.'s, and Collins played on Brown classics including "Get Up (I Feel Like Being a) Sex Machine", "Super Bad", and "Soul Power". After leaving Brown, Collins joined Clinton's Funkadelic in 1972 and appeared on Parliament albums such as Mothership Connection (1975), contributing songs like "Tear the Roof Off the Sucker (Give Up the Funk)".

In 1976 Collins launched Bootsy's Rubber Band, whose albums Stretchin' Out in Bootsy's Rubber Band (1976) and Ahh... The Name Is Bootsy, Baby! (1977) reached number one on the R&B charts; the single "Bootzilla" (1978) topped the R&B chart. His solo and collaborative work continued through the 1980s and beyond, including the album What's Bootsy Doin'? (1988) and work with artists from Deee-Lite to Snoop Dogg and Bruno Mars (on the Silk Sonic project).

Style and legacy

Collins pioneered the use of envelope filters and other effects on electric bass and popularised a syncopated, melodic approach centred on "the One" — the first beat of the bar emphasised by James Brown. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1997 as a member of Parliament-Funkadelic. His bass lines have been widely sampled in hip hop, and his influence extends to players such as Flea of the Red Hot Chili Peppers.
