Janata Party

The Janata Party (People's Party) was an Indian political party founded in January 1977 by merging the principal opposition parties that had resisted Indira Gandhi's Emergency (1975–77). In the March 1977 general election it won a sweeping victory, becoming the first non-Congress party to govern India.

Formation and government

The party united the Congress (O), Bharatiya Jana Sangh, Bharatiya Lok Dal, Socialist Party and defectors from Congress led by Jagjivan Ram, under the moral leadership of Jayaprakash Narayan. Morarji Desai became prime minister, with Charan Singh and Jagjivan Ram as deputies. The government restored civil liberties and press freedom, set up the Shah Commission on Emergency excesses, and pursued decentralised, Gandhian-flavoured economic policies.

Collapse and legacy

Riven by leadership rivalry — notably between Desai and Charan Singh — and ideological tensions over Jana Sangh members' dual loyalty to the RSS, the government fell in 1979 after barely 28 months. Charan Singh briefly headed a caretaker government, and Congress returned to power in 1980. The party fragmented; its most important successor, formed in 1980 by former Jana Sangh members, is the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP), while other offshoots fed into the later Janata Dal. A rump Janata Party continued under Subramanian Swamy and merged with the BJP in 2013.
