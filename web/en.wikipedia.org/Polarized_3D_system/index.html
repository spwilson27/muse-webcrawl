Polarized 3D system

A polarized 3D system uses polarization glasses to create the illusion of three-dimensional images by restricting the light that reaches each eye. To present stereoscopic images, two images are projected superimposed onto the same screen through different polarizing filters. The viewer wears low-cost eyeglasses containing a pair of different polarizing filters, so each eye receives only one of the two images, producing depth perception.

Linear and circular polarization

Early systems used linear polarization, requiring two projectors and a silver screen to preserve polarization; viewers had to keep their heads level to avoid crosstalk. Modern cinema systems such as RealD use circular polarization, which tolerates head tilt, and can use a single projector with a rapidly switching polarizer. IMAX 3D uses large-format projectors with linear or, in digital venues, other polarization schemes.

Advantages and disadvantages

Polarized systems offer full colour, cheap passive glasses and no electronics in the eyewear. Disadvantages include light loss at the filters, the need for a polarization-preserving silver screen, and possible ghosting if alignment is poor.
