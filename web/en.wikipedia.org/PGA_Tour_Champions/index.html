PGA Tour Champions (formerly the Senior PGA Tour and the Champions Tour) is a men's professional senior golf tour administered by the PGA Tour. It is open to golfers aged 50 and older, many of whom previously starred on the main PGA Tour.

History

The tour grew out of the success of the 1978 Legends of Golf tournament and formally began in 1980 with two events. It expanded rapidly through the 1980s as celebrated players such as Arnold Palmer, Lee Trevino, Gary Player and later Hale Irwin, Bernhard Langer and Fred Couples joined after turning fifty.

Format

Most events are played over 54 holes (three rounds) with no cut, and competitors are generally permitted to use golf carts. The season-long points race culminates in the Charles Schwab Cup, awarded after a series of playoff events. The tour holds five senior major championships, including the Senior PGA Championship and the U.S. Senior Open.
