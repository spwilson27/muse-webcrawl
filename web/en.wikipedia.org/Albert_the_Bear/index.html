Albert the Bear

Albert the Bear (German: Albrecht der Bär; c. 1100 – 18 November 1170) was the first Margrave of Brandenburg from 1157 until his death and was briefly Duke of Saxony between 1138 and 1142. He was a member of the House of Ascania and played a central role in the German eastward expansion (Ostsiedlung).

Life

Albert was the son of Otto, Count of Ballenstedt. In 1123 he inherited his father's counties, and in 1134 Emperor Lothair III granted him the Saxon Northern March after its margrave died without an heir. In 1138 he was made Duke of Saxony but lost the duchy to Henry the Lion in 1142.

Founding of Brandenburg

Albert campaigned against the Slavic Wends east of the Elbe, took part in the Wendish Crusade of 1147, and in 1150 inherited Brandenburg an der Havel from the Christian Slav prince Pribislav-Henry. On 11 June 1157 he definitively reconquered the town and proclaimed himself Margrave of Brandenburg, a date regarded as the founding of the margraviate. He invited settlers from the Low Countries, the Rhineland, and Flanders to repopulate the land, drained marshes, and founded towns and monasteries.

Legacy

Albert is regarded as the founder of Brandenburg and, by tradition, of Berlin — the bear in Berlin's coat of arms is often linked to his nickname, though the association may be folk etymology. He was buried at Ballenstedt.
