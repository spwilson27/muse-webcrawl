NCAA Division II (D-II) is an intermediate-level division of competition in the National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA) in the United States. It offers an alternative to both the larger, better-funded Division I and the scholarship-free Division III.

History

The NCAA split into University and College Divisions in 1957; the College Division's smaller schools were further divided in 1973, creating Divisions II and III. Division II schools tend to be smaller public and private institutions, many with enrollments under 10,000 students, and they emphasize regional competition to limit travel costs and missed class time.

Scholarships

Division II operates on a "partial scholarship" model: most sports use equivalency scholarships, in which a fixed pool of aid (for example, 36 full-scholarship equivalents in football) is divided among many athletes. This distinguishes D-II from Division I's head-count sports and Division III's ban on athletic scholarships.

Championships and identity

The division sponsors national championships in 25 sports, including its football championship and the Elite Eight for basketball. Its stated philosophy stresses a balance between athletics, academics, and community involvement, summarized in the "Life in the Balance" platform and the "Make It Yours" brand. The Division II National Championships Festival periodically stages multiple championship finals at a single site. See also NCAA Division I and NCAA Division III.
