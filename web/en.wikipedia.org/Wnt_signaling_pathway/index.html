Wnt signaling pathway

The Wnt signaling pathways are a group of signal transduction pathways which begin with proteins that pass signals into a cell through cell surface receptors. The name Wnt is a portmanteau created from the names Wingless (in Drosophila) and Int-1 (in vertebrates). Wnt signaling pathways use either nearby cell-cell communication (paracrine) or same-cell communication (autocrine), and they are highly evolutionarily conserved across animals.

Pathways

Three Wnt signaling pathways have been characterized: the canonical Wnt pathway, the noncanonical planar cell polarity pathway, and the noncanonical Wnt/calcium pathway. In the canonical pathway, Wnt binding to Frizzled receptors and LRP5/6 co-receptors inhibits the destruction complex that normally degrades β-catenin; β-catenin then accumulates, enters the nucleus, and activates transcription of target genes through TCF/LEF factors.

Functions

Wnt signaling controls embryonic development, including body axis patterning, cell fate specification, cell proliferation and cell migration. In adults it regulates stem cell maintenance, including in the intestine and hair follicles. Deregulation of the pathway is implicated in cancer—most famously, mutations in the APC tumor suppressor gene that activate canonical Wnt signaling in most colorectal cancers—as well as in degenerative diseases and developmental disorders.
