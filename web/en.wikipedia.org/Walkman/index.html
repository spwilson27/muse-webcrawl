Walkman is a brand of portable audio players manufactured and marketed by Sony since 1979. The original Walkman, the TPS-L2, was a portable cassette player that allowed users to listen to music through headphones while on the move, revolutionizing personal music listening and becoming an icon of 1980s popular culture.

History

The Walkman was developed after Sony co-founder Masaru Ibuka asked for a portable stereo cassette player for listening to opera on long flights. Engineer Nobutoshi Kihara adapted the company's Pressman dictation recorder, removing the recording function and adding stereo playback and dual headphone jacks. The TPS-L2 went on sale in Japan on July 1, 1979, at a price of about ¥33,000.

Initially met with skepticism — it could not record, and critics doubted consumers would buy a playback-only device — the Walkman became a worldwide phenomenon. Sony sold tens of millions of cassette Walkman units, and the brand expanded to include CD players (later renamed Discman, then absorbed into Walkman), MiniDisc players, and digital media players.

Cultural impact

The Walkman changed listening habits by making music a private, portable experience, and influenced the design of later personal electronics. The word "Walkman" entered dictionaries in several languages as a generic term for personal stereo cassette players. Sony retired the cassette Walkman in 2010, while continuing the name on digital audio players noted for high-resolution audio support.

Successors

The Walkman line eventually faced competition from Apple's iPod and smartphones, but Sony continued producing Walkman-branded digital players, including the anniversary NW-WM1Z and retro-styled models released for the brand's 40th anniversary in 2019.
