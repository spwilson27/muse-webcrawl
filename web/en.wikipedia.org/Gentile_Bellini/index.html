Gentile Bellini

Gentile Bellini (c. 1429 – 23 February 1507) was an Italian painter of the Venetian school, the son of Jacopo Bellini and elder brother of Giovanni Bellini. He is best known for his large narrative canvases of Venetian ceremonial life and for his portrait of the Ottoman sultan Mehmed II, painted in Constantinople.

Career in Venice

Trained in his father's workshop, Gentile became the leading official painter of the Venetian Republic. He worked on the great cycle of paintings in the Doge's Palace (mostly destroyed by fire in 1577) and on narrative cycles for the Venetian scuole, the city's lay confraternities. For the Scuola Grande di San Giovanni Evangelista he painted, with collaborators, the cycle of the Miracles of the True Cross, including the Procession in Piazza San Marco (1496) and the Miracle of the Cross at the Bridge of San Lorenzo (1500), now in the Gallerie dell'Accademia. These works are prized as detailed visual records of fifteenth-century Venice.

Constantinople

In 1479, at the request of Sultan Mehmed II, the Venetian Senate sent Gentile to Constantinople as painter and cultural ambassador. He remained until 1481, painting the sultan's portrait (now in the National Gallery, London) and producing drawings and medals — an extraordinary episode of artistic exchange between Renaissance Italy and the Ottoman court.
