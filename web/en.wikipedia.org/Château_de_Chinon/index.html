Château de Chinon

Location: Chinon, Indre-et-Loire, France

Type: Medieval fortress

The Château de Chinon is a large medieval fortress above the town of Chinon and the river Vienne in the Loire Valley. Its long ridge holds three formerly separate castles: Fort Saint-Georges, the Château du Milieu, and the Fort du Coudray.

History

The site was strategically important to the counts of Anjou and kings of England; Henry II used it as a major residence and treasury, and it passed to the French crown under Philip II. In 1429 Joan of Arc met the Dauphin Charles at Chinon, an encounter central to her mission and his path to coronation.

Today

After centuries of ruin, the fortress has been excavated and partly restored, including a modern visitor centre. It is a protected historic monument overlooking one of France's best-known wine towns.
