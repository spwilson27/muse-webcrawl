Urban rail transit in China encompasses the metro, light rail, monorail, tram and suburban railway systems of the People's Republic of China. China operates the largest urban rail network in the world: since the turn of the 21st century, dozens of cities have built metro systems, and Chinese networks account for well over half of the world's total metro route length.

History

The Beijing Subway, opened in 1969, was China's first metro, built initially with military and civil-defence purposes in mind. The Tianjin Metro followed in the 1980s, and the Shanghai Metro opened its first line in 1993. Construction accelerated dramatically after 2000 as the central government approved metro plans for a succession of major cities — Guangzhou, Shenzhen, Nanjing, Chengdu, Wuhan, Xi'an and many others — financed largely by municipal governments and land development.

Scale

By the early 2020s more than 50 Chinese cities operated metro systems, with total urban rail route length exceeding 10,000 kilometres. The Shanghai Metro (over 800 km) and Beijing Subway (over 750 km) are the world's two largest networks by length, and several Chinese systems carry more than three billion passengers a year. China also builds much of its own rolling stock, led by the state manufacturer CRRC.

Regulation and expansion

Metro construction requires approval from the National Development and Reform Commission, which sets thresholds for city population, economic output and fiscal revenue; requirements were tightened in 2018 after concerns about local government debt. Despite this, construction continues, and Chinese firms increasingly export urban rail technology and construction services abroad.
