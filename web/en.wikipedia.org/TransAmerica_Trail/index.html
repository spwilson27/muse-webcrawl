The TransAmerica Trail (TAT) is a cross-continental route across the United States composed primarily of unpaved roads—dirt roads, gravel roads, forest roads, farm tracks, and trails—together with paved county and rural roads. It was created by Sam Correro, who scouted and mapped the route over many years, publishing roll charts and later GPS tracks for riders.

The trail is most popular with dual-sport and adventure motorcyclists, but it is also driven by 4x4 vehicles and ridden by some cyclists. It is a route, not a formally maintained or signposted trail, and riders navigate using purchased maps, roll charts, or GPS files.

Route

The original route begins in southeastern Tennessee/northern Georgia area (later versions start near Andrews, North Carolina) and heads west through Mississippi, Arkansas—crossing the Ozarks—then Oklahoma's panhandle, northern New Mexico, Colorado over high alpine passes such as Engineer Pass, Utah's canyon country and the Great Basin, Nevada, and eastern Oregon, ending at Port Orford on the Pacific coast. Optional spurs and later extensions reach the Atlantic coast. The route crosses the Great Plains, the Rocky Mountains, and the deserts of the Great Basin.

Travel

Most riders take three to five weeks to complete the trail. The high Colorado passes are generally only passable after snowmelt in early summer, so most attempts run from June to September. Riders must plan for long distances between fuel stops, especially in Nevada and Utah, and for terrain ranging from mud in the east to sand, rocks, and washboard gravel in the west.
