Music of Japan

Traditional genresGagaku, shōmyō, biwa music, nō, kabuki music, min'yō
InstrumentsShamisen, koto, shakuhachi, taiko, biwa
Modern genresJ-pop, J-rock, enka, city pop, anime music, Vocaloid

The music of Japan includes a wide array of distinct styles, both traditional and modern. The word for "music" in Japanese is 音楽 (ongaku), combining the kanji 音 (on, "sound") with 楽 (gaku, "enjoyment"). Japan is one of the largest music markets in the world, and its domestic music has drawn on influences from China and Korea in ancient times and from the West since the Meiji period.

Traditional and folk music

The oldest surviving form of Japanese music is gagaku, the ceremonial music of the imperial court, imported from China and Korea in the 5th to 8th centuries and preserved at the Imperial Household. Buddhist chanting (shōmyō) and the narrative music of the biwa also developed during this period. In the Edo period, the shamisen, koto, and shakuhachi supported the theatrical music of kabuki and bunraku puppet theatre, while min'yō folk songs accompanied rural work and festivals.

Popular music

After the Meiji Restoration, Western classical music and military band music were introduced. In the postwar era, enka ballads and kayōkyoku pop dominated, followed by the rise of J-pop in the 1990s, built around idol groups and production agencies. Japanese rock, electronic music, hip hop, and the "visual kei" movement developed substantial followings. Music from anime and video games, and songs produced with Vocaloid voice-synthesis software, have become globally influential.

Instruments

Traditional instruments include the three-stringed shamisen, the thirteen-stringed koto zither, the bamboo shakuhachi flute, the short-necked biwa lute, and taiko drums of many sizes used in festival and theatre ensembles.
