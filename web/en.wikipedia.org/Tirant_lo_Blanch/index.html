Tirant lo Blanch (modern spelling Tirant lo Blanc) is a chivalric romance written in Valencian by the Valencian knight Joanot Martorell, begun around 1460 and published posthumously in Valencia in 1490. It is considered one of the masterpieces of Catalan literature and one of the finest novels of the European 15th century.

Plot

Tirant, a knight from Brittany, wins fame at tournaments in England, then sails to Rhodes, which he relieves from a Turkish siege. He goes on to Constantinople, where he becomes captain-general of the Byzantine Empire's armies against the Ottomans and falls in love with the princess Carmesina. After further adventures in North Africa, Tirant dies of pneumonia just before his wedding and imperial succession; Carmesina dies of grief shortly after. Unlike most romances, its hero dies not in battle but of illness, and its characters are psychologically drawn, fallible and often comic.

Reputation

In Don Quixote, Cervantes has the priest spare Tirant lo Blanch from the burning of Quixote's books, calling it — for its style and its believable knights who "eat and drink, and sleep, and die in their beds" — a treasure of delight and the best book of its kind in the world. The Peruvian novelist Mario Vargas Llosa was among its modern champions. The novel's mixture of realism, eroticism and military detail distinguishes it from the fantasy romances of the period.
