Rodion Malinovsky

Rodion Yakovlevich Malinovsky
Born: 23 November 1898, Odessa, Russian Empire
Died: 31 March 1967, Moscow, Soviet Union
Rank: Marshal of the Soviet Union
Office: Minister of Defence of the USSR (1957–1967)

Rodion Yakovlevich Malinovsky (23 November 1898 – 31 March 1967) was a Soviet military commander and Marshal of the Soviet Union. During World War II, he commanded the 2nd Ukrainian Front and led operations that liberated Ukraine, Romania, Hungary, Austria, and Czechoslovakia. After the war he served as Minister of Defence of the Soviet Union from 1957 until his death.

Early career

Malinovsky enlisted in the Imperial Russian Army in 1914 and served as a machine gunner in World War I. He was sent to France with the Russian Expeditionary Force in 1916 and later served in the French Foreign Legion. After the Russian Revolution he returned home and joined the Red Army, fighting in the Russian Civil War in Siberia.

World War II

At the start of Operation Barbarossa, Malinovsky commanded the 48th Rifle Corps in Bessarabia. He commanded the Southern Front, survived the disasters of 1942, and played a key role in the aftermath of Stalingrad. In 1944, his 2nd Ukrainian Front conducted the Jassy–Kishinev Offensive, knocking Romania out of the Axis, and went on to capture Budapest and Vienna. In mid-1945 he was transferred to the Far East, where his Transbaikal Front spearheaded the Soviet invasion of Manchuria against Japan's Kwantung Army.

Postwar

Malinovsky commanded Soviet forces in the Far East, served as ground forces commander-in-chief, and in October 1957 succeeded Georgy Zhukov as Minister of Defence. He held the post under Nikita Khrushchev and Leonid Brezhnev, overseeing the buildup of Soviet strategic rocket forces. He died in office in 1967 and is buried in the Kremlin Wall Necropolis.
