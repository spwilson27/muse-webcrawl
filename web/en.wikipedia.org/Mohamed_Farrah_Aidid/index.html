Mohamed Farrah Hassan Aidid (Somali: Maxamed Faarax Caydiid; 1934–1996) was a Somali general and faction leader who became one of the most powerful figures in Somalia following the collapse of the Siad Barre regime in 1991. His Somali National Alliance fought rival factions for control of Mogadishu and clashed with United Nations and United States forces in 1993.

Early career

Born in Beledweyne, Aidid was educated in Rome and Moscow and rose through the Somali National Army, serving in the Ogaden War against Ethiopia. Under President Siad Barre he was detained for several years on suspicion of plotting a coup, later rehabilitated and posted as ambassador to India. He defected to the rebel United Somali Congress (USC) and became its military leader in the campaign that drove Barre from Mogadishu in January 1991.

Civil war and international intervention

When the USC split, Aidid's Habr Gidr-based faction fought the rival wing of Ali Mahdi Mohamed for the capital in a devastating civil war that, with drought, produced a famine killing hundreds of thousands. The UN and US-led interventions of 1992–93 (UNITAF and UNOSOM II) initially delivered relief, but relations with Aidid collapsed; after his forces killed 24 Pakistani peacekeepers in June 1993, a manhunt followed. On 3–4 October 1993 his militia fought the Battle of Mogadishu against US special forces, downing two Black Hawk helicopters and killing 18 American soldiers — an event that precipitated the US withdrawal from Somalia.

Final years

In 1995 Aidid declared himself president of Somalia, though he was never internationally recognised. He died in Mogadishu in August 1996, reportedly of wounds received in fighting with rival militias, and was succeeded as faction leader by his son Hussein Farrah Aidid, a former US Marine.
