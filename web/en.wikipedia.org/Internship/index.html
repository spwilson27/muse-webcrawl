Internship

An internship is a period of work experience offered by an organization for a limited period of time. Once confined to medical graduates, internship is now used widely in business, non-profit and government settings. Internships are placements most commonly taken by students and recent graduates seeking relevant skills and experience in a particular field.

Types

Internships may be paid, paid in part (such as a stipend), or unpaid, and may be part-time or full-time, lasting from a few weeks to a year. In medicine, "internship" refers to the first year of residency training for physicians. Many employers use internships as an extended recruitment process, offering full-time positions to successful interns.

Benefits

For interns, the benefits include gaining practical experience, building a professional network, earning academic credit, and testing a career field. For employers, internships provide additional labour at low cost, a pipeline of evaluated candidates, and fresh perspectives.

Criticism and regulation

Unpaid internships have been criticized for exploiting young workers and for favouring candidates wealthy enough to work without pay. Legal frameworks vary: in the United States, unpaid internships in the for-profit sector are governed by guidance under the Fair Labor Standards Act, including a "primary beneficiary" test; in several European countries, unpaid internships outside education are restricted or must meet minimum-wage rules. Critics also note that unpaid requirements can entrench inequality in access to professions.
