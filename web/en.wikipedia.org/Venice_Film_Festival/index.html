The Venice Film Festival (Italian: Mostra Internazionale d'Arte Cinematografica della Biennale di Venezia) is the world's oldest film festival, founded in 1932 as part of the Venice Biennale. Held annually in late August or early September on the Lido island in the Venetian Lagoon, it is one of the "Big Five" international film festivals and a major launchpad for the autumn awards season.

History

The first festival took place in August 1932 on the terrace of the Hotel Excelsior on the Lido. Through the 1930s it became an annual event, though awards in that era reflected Fascist-era politics. The festival was suspended during part of the Second World War and refounded after 1946, moving into the Palazzo del Cinema. Political turmoil in 1968–1969 led to a decade of non-competitive editions before the festival was relaunched in 1979–80.

Awards

The festival's highest prize is the Golden Lion for best film, introduced (in its present name) in 1949; notable winners include Rashomon (1951), Brokeback Mountain (2005), The Shape of Water (2017), Joker (2019), and Poor Things (2023). Other awards include the Silver Lion for best direction, the Volpi Cups for best actor and actress, and the Golden Lion for Lifetime Achievement. The independent Orizzonti (Horizons) section showcases innovative and debut works.
