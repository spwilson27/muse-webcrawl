Variance

In probability theory and statistics, variance is the expected value of the squared deviation from the mean of a random variable. It measures how far a set of numbers is spread out from their average value. The variance of a random variable X is written Var(X), σ², or simply σ² when unambiguous. Its square root is the standard deviation.

Definition

For a random variable with mean μ, the variance is Var(X) = E[(X − μ)²] = E[X²] − (E[X])². For a sample x₁…xₙ with mean x̄, the population variance divides the sum of squared deviations by n, while the (unbiased) sample variance divides by n − 1 (Bessel's correction).

Properties

Variance is always non-negative; a small variance indicates that data points lie close to the mean. Adding a constant leaves variance unchanged, while scaling by a constant a multiplies variance by a². For independent variables, the variance of a sum equals the sum of the variances. Variance is the second central moment and is central to the law of large numbers, the central limit theorem, and analysis of variance (ANOVA).
