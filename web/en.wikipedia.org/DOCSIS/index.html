Data Over Cable Service Interface Specification (DOCSIS) is an international telecommunications standard that permits the addition of high-bandwidth data transfer to an existing cable television (CATV) system. It is used by many cable television operators to provide cable Internet access over their existing hybrid fiber-coaxial (HFC) infrastructure.

History

DOCSIS was developed by CableLabs and contributing companies including Broadcom, Cisco, Harmonic, Intel, Motorola, Terayon, and Texas Instruments. The first version, DOCSIS 1.0, was released in March 1997. DOCSIS 1.1 followed in April 1999, adding quality of service (QoS) mechanisms needed for services such as voice over IP. DOCSIS 2.0 (2002) increased upstream capacity. DOCSIS 3.0 (2006) introduced channel bonding, significantly increasing both downstream and upstream speeds, along with support for IPv6. DOCSIS 3.1 (2013) moved to OFDM and OFDMA modulation with LDPC error correction, enabling multi-gigabit downstream rates. DOCSIS 4.0 (2017, finalized in subsequent years) targets symmetric multi-gigabit service through full-duplex operation and extended spectrum.

Architecture

A DOCSIS architecture includes two primary components: a cable modem (CM) located at the customer premises, and a cable modem termination system (CMTS) located at the cable operator's headend or hub. The CMTS allocates bandwidth, schedules upstream transmissions, and enforces service tiers. Modern deployments increasingly use distributed access architectures, moving physical-layer functions to remote nodes in the field.

Throughput

Channel widths vary by region: 6 MHz channels in North America and 8 MHz channels in Europe (EuroDOCSIS). Throughput scales with the number of bonded channels and modulation order. DOCSIS 3.1 supports downstream rates up to about 10 Gbit/s and upstream rates of 1–2 Gbit/s; DOCSIS 4.0 extends downstream to 10 Gbit/s with up to 6 Gbit/s upstream in extended-spectrum deployments.

Security

DOCSIS includes security features at the MAC layer under the Baseline Privacy Interface specifications (BPI/BPI+), which encrypt traffic between the modem and CMTS and authenticate modems using X.509 certificates, discouraging theft of service and eavesdropping on the shared medium.
