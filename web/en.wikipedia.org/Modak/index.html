Modak

Modak (Marathi: मोदक)

Course: Dessert

Place of origin: India

Region: Maharashtra

Main ingredients: Rice flour or wheat flour, coconut, jaggery

Modak (Marathi: मोदक; Japanese: kangidan) is an Indian sweet dish popular in many Indian states and cultures. According to Hindu belief, it is considered one of the favourite dishes of the god Ganesha and is therefore used in prayers. The sweet filling inside the modak consists of freshly grated coconut and jaggery, while the outer soft shell is made from rice flour or wheat flour mixed with khowa or maida flour.

Varieties

There are two main types of modak:

  • Ukdiche modak — steamed modak made with rice flour dough and a coconut-jaggery filling, traditionally shaped by hand with pleats or formed in a mould. They are the classic offering during the festival of Ganesh Chaturthi in Maharashtra.
  • Talniche modak — fried modak, often made with wheat flour, with a longer shelf life.

Modern variations include fillings of chocolate, dry fruits, mawa (khoya), and flavoured versions such as kesar (saffron) modak sold by sweet shops during the Ganesh festival season.

Religious significance

During Ganesh Chaturthi, an offering (naivedya) of 21 modaks is traditionally made to Ganesha. Legend holds that modak gives Ganesha great satisfaction; the Sanskrit name modaka is associated with joy and bliss. In Japan, a version of the sweet, called kangidan (歓喜団, "bliss bun"), is offered to the god Kangiten, the Japanese form of Ganesha.
