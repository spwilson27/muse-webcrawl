Cheminformatics

Cheminformatics (also chemoinformatics) is the use of computer and information techniques to store, search, and analyse collections of chemical data, especially molecular structures and their properties. It is a core technology of modern drug discovery and materials science.

Representations

Molecules are encoded in machine-readable forms: connection tables and file formats such as MOL and SDF; linear notations including SMILES and InChI; and numerical descriptors and fingerprints (bit strings encoding structural features) that enable similarity searching — the principle that structurally similar molecules tend to have similar properties. Large public databases such as PubChem and ChEMBL hold tens of millions of compounds.

Methods and applications

Key techniques include substructure and similarity search, quantitative structure–activity relationship (QSAR) modelling, virtual screening of compound libraries against a target protein, and prediction of absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion, and toxicity (ADMET). Machine learning and deep learning on molecular graphs have become major growth areas, including generative models that propose new candidate molecules. Related fields include computational chemistry, bioinformatics, and pharmacoinformatics.
