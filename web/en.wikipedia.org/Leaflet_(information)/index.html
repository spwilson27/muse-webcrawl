Leaflet (information)

A leaflet, also called a flyer or flier, is a form of paper advertisement intended for wide distribution, typically given out or posted in a public place, handed out to individuals or sent through the mail. Leaflets are usually printed on a single sheet of paper, sometimes folded, and are a cheap and effective means of mass communication used for advertising, political campaigning, and public information.

Uses

Leaflets are used to advertise events, products, businesses, and services, and are a staple of political campaigning, religious outreach, and public health communication. During wartime, leaflets have been dropped from aircraft over enemy territory as psychological warfare. They are commonly distributed by hand in the street (leafleting), inserted into newspapers, displayed in racks, or delivered door to door.

Formats

Common sizes include A4, A5, and DL (one-third of A4). Leaflets may be single-sided or double-sided, monochrome or full colour, and folded as a half-fold, tri-fold (gate), or z-fold. Although related to pamphlets and brochures, a leaflet is distinguished by being a single sheet, whereas a pamphlet may be a small booklet and a brochure is a more elaborate folded or bound document.
