International Klein Blue

International Klein Blue (IKB) is a deep blue hue first mixed by the French artist Yves Klein in the late 1950s. The colour, a rich ultramarine, became the signature of Klein's monochrome paintings and performances.

Development

Klein worked with the Paris paint supplier Edouard Adam to suspend pure ultramarine pigment in a synthetic resin binder (Rhodopas M60A), which preserved the pigment's intensity and matte velvety texture instead of dulling it as linseed oil did. Klein registered the formula with the French patent office in 1960.

Use in art

Klein applied IKB to canvases, sponges, sculptures and even models' bodies in his Anthropométries performances. The colour became so identified with him that his 1957 Milan exhibition of identical blue monochromes is a landmark of postwar art.
