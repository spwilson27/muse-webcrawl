Zeno Vendler (24 December 1921 – 13 January 2004) was a Hungarian-American philosopher and linguist, best known for his classification of verbs by their temporal structure — the four Vendler classes — which became fundamental to the study of lexical aspect in linguistics.

Life and career

Vendler studied in Budapest and, after the Second World War, in the Netherlands and at Harvard University, where he received his doctorate. He taught at several American universities, including Cornell, and later at the University of Calgary in Canada, where he helped build up the philosophy department. His works include Linguistics in Philosophy (1967) and Res Cogitans (1972).

Vendler classes

In his influential 1957 paper "Verbs and Times", Vendler divided verbs into four classes based on how they unfold in time: states (know, love), activities (run, push a cart), accomplishments (build a house, run a mile), and achievements (win a race, reach the top). The distinctions rest on tests such as compatibility with the progressive, with "for an hour" versus "in an hour", and with adverbs like "almost". Later refined by scholars such as David Dowty, the scheme remains a starting point for theories of aspect (aktionsart).
