Kampfgruppe

In German military terminology, a Kampfgruppe (literally "battle group" or "combat group") is an ad hoc combined-arms formation, usually of temporary nature, created to carry out a specific mission. The concept is comparable to the American task force and the British battlegroup.

Use in the Second World War

During the Second World War, the Wehrmacht and Waffen-SS routinely organised Kampfgruppen by grouping whatever battalions, companies and support units were at hand under a single commander, often the senior officer of the largest component, whose name the group then bore — for example Kampfgruppe Peiper, commanded by Joachim Peiper during the Battle of the Bulge. Sizes ranged from a company to a force larger than a corps, though most were battalion- to regiment-sized mixtures of infantry, armour, artillery, engineers and anti-tank units. The flexibility of the system reflected German doctrinal emphasis on mission-type orders (Auftragstaktik) and helped units continue fighting effectively even after heavy losses disrupted their parent formations.

Postwar usage

The Bundeswehr retained the term for battlegroups, and NATO's modern "battlegroups" — such as the EU Battlegroups — are a direct conceptual descendant. In wargaming and popular history, "Kampfgruppe" remains strongly associated with the improvised German formations of 1944–45.
