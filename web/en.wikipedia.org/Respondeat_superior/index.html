Respondeat superior

Respondeat superior (Latin: "let the master answer") is a doctrine in common law under which an employer is legally responsible (vicariously liable) for the torts of its employees, when those acts occur within the course and scope of employment.

Doctrine

Liability under respondeat superior does not depend on the employer's own fault: an employer may be liable even if it exercised due care in hiring and supervision. The rationale is that the employer benefits from the employee's work, can spread the cost of injuries through insurance and prices, and is best placed to reduce the risk of harm. Whether an act falls within the scope of employment is judged by tests such as whether it was authorised, incidental to authorised work, or motivated at least in part by serving the employer. Deliberate wrongdoing and "frolics" outside the job may fall outside the doctrine, though courts often hold employers liable even for intentional torts closely connected to the work.

Distinctions

The doctrine generally applies to employees, not independent contractors, though exceptions exist (for example, non-delegable duties). It is distinct from direct negligence claims against the employer, such as negligent hiring or retention.
