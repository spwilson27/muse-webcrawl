Fremont Street

Fremont Street is a historic street in downtown Las Vegas, Nevada, named for the explorer and politician John C. Frémont, who passed through the Las Vegas Valley in 1844. Laid out in 1905 when Las Vegas was founded as a railroad town, it became the city's first paved street (1925), received Nevada's first traffic light, and housed many of its earliest casinos. Long known as "Glitter Gulch" for its dense concentration of neon signs, Fremont Street was the center of Las Vegas gambling before the Strip eclipsed it in the later 20th century.

Landmarks

Historic casinos along or near Fremont Street include the Golden Nugget (1946), Binion's Horseshoe (1951), the Golden Gate (1906, the city's oldest), the Pioneer Club — whose 1951 sign introduced Vegas Vic, the waving neon cowboy — the Fremont Hotel and Casino (1956), and the Four Queens (1966). The street also saw the first high-rise in Las Vegas and held Nevada's first gaming license issued to the Northern Club in 1931.

Fremont Street Experience

In 1995, a five-block section of Fremont Street was closed to traffic and covered by the Fremont Street Experience, a barrel-vault canopy 27 m (90 ft) high and about 460 m (1,500 ft) long, fitted over time with ever-larger LED displays — the current Viva Vision screen is among the largest video displays in the world. The pedestrian mall, with free concerts, light shows, and the SlotZilla zip line, revitalized downtown tourism and now anchors the Fremont East entertainment district.
