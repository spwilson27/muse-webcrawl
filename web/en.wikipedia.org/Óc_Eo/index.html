Óc Eo

Óc Eo is an archaeological site in Thoại Sơn District, An Giang Province, in southern Vietnam, in the Mekong Delta. It was a busy port of the kingdom of Funan between the 1st and 7th centuries AD, and is widely regarded as one of the most important early trading centres of Southeast Asia.

History

Óc Eo flourished as an entrepôt connecting maritime trade routes between India, China, and the Roman world. Merchant vessels from as far away as Persia and the Mediterranean are believed to have called at its harbour. The city declined as Funan weakened and maritime routes shifted, and by roughly the 7th century it had been largely abandoned.

Excavations

The site was identified and excavated beginning in 1942 by the French archaeologist Louis Malleret of the École française d'Extrême-Orient. Malleret's excavations uncovered an extensive system of canals, foundations of buildings, workshops, jewellery, and religious objects, and he named the associated material culture the "Óc Eo culture."

Artefacts

Finds from Óc Eo include gold medallions in the style of the Roman emperors Antoninus Pius and Marcus Aurelius, Indian seals and intaglios bearing Brahmi inscriptions, Buddhist and Hindu statuary, mirrors of Han-dynasty China, and large quantities of locally made pottery and beads. The canal network around the site extended for tens of kilometres and may have linked Óc Eo to other Funanese centres such as Angkor Borei.
