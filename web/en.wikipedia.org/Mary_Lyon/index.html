Mary Mason Lyon (28 February 1797 – 5 March 1849) was an American pioneer in women's education. She founded Mount Holyoke Female Seminary in South Hadley, Massachusetts, in 1837 — the first institution of higher education for women in the United States chartered to offer a curriculum equivalent to that of men's colleges — and served as its first principal. She also co-founded Wheaton Female Seminary (now Wheaton College) in Norton, Massachusetts.

Early life

Lyon was born in Buckland, Massachusetts, to a farming family. Her father died when she was five, and she began teaching in district schools at about age seventeen while pursuing her own studies at academies including Sanderson Academy in Ashfield and Byfield Seminary, where she studied with the Reverend Joseph Emerson. She also studied chemistry with Amos Eaton. She taught and helped administer Ipswich Female Seminary under Zilpah Grant in the late 1820s and early 1830s.

Founding Mount Holyoke

Determined to create a permanent, well-endowed school offering women rigorous study — including science and mathematics — at a cost ordinary families could afford, Lyon spent years raising funds door-to-door, carrying her famous green velvet donation bag. Mount Holyoke Female Seminary opened in November 1837 with about eighty students. Its model combined a demanding collegiate curriculum, domestic work by students to keep costs low (a practice that persisted into the 20th century), and a missionary emphasis that sent graduates to teach across the United States and abroad.

Death and legacy

Lyon died of erysipelas in 1849, having led the seminary for twelve years. Mount Holyoke became Mount Holyoke College, the oldest member of the Seven Sisters colleges, and Lyon's model influenced women's higher education nationwide. She is buried on the college grounds, and Lyon's name appears on buildings at both Mount Holyoke and Wheaton.
