The Rite of Spring

ComposerIgor Stravinsky
ChoreographerVaslav Nijinsky
PremiereMay 29, 1913, Théâtre des Champs-Élysées, Paris
CompanyBallets Russes

The Rite of Spring (French: Le Sacre du printemps) is a ballet and orchestral concert work by the Russian composer Igor Stravinsky. It was written for the 1913 Paris season of Sergei Diaghilev's Ballets Russes company and choreographed by Vaslav Nijinsky, with stage designs and costumes by Nicholas Roerich.

Music and scenario

Subtitled "Pictures of Pagan Russia in Two Parts", the work depicts rituals celebrating the advent of spring, culminating in the selection of a young girl who dances herself to death as a sacrificial victim. The score is renowned for its innovative rhythm, metre, dissonance, and orchestration, including the famous opening bassoon solo.

Premiere and legacy

The premiere on May 29, 1913 provoked one of the most famous audience disturbances — often called a riot — in classical music history. The work is now regarded as one of the most influential compositions of the 20th century, and the score has been widely performed as a concert piece.
