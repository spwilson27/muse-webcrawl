Charles Edward Ives (October 20, 1874 – May 19, 1954) was an American modernist composer, one of the first American composers of international renown. His music was largely ignored during his early career, and many of his works went unperformed for years; later he was recognised as an "American original". He was among the first composers to engage systematically in experimental practices such as polytonality, polyrhythm, tone clusters, aleatory elements and quarter tones.

Life

Ives was born in Danbury, Connecticut. His father George, a Civil War bandmaster, encouraged youthful experiments such as hearing two bands playing different music simultaneously — an experience reflected throughout his work. He studied at Yale with Horatio Parker, then made a successful career in insurance, co-founding the agency Ives & Myrick, and composed in his spare time. Ill health forced his retirement from business in 1930 and he composed little thereafter.

Music

Major works include the Concord Sonata for piano (with movements named for Emerson, Hawthorne, the Alcotts and Thoreau), the orchestral The Unanswered Question (1908), Central Park in the Dark, Three Places in New England, the monumental Fourth Symphony, and more than 100 songs quoting American hymn tunes, marches and Stephen Foster melodies. His Third Symphony won the Pulitzer Prize in 1947, decades after its composition.

Legacy

Ives's collage-like techniques anticipated later modernism, and advocates such as Henry Cowell, Elliott Carter and Leonard Bernstein helped bring his music to a wide audience in the mid-20th century.
