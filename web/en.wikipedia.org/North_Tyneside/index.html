North Tyneside

North Tyneside is a metropolitan borough in the county of Tyne and Wear, England. It lies on the north bank of the River Tyne and the North Sea coast east of Newcastle upon Tyne, taking in the towns of North Shields, Wallsend, Tynemouth, and Whitley Bay.

Tynemouth Priory and the mouth of the Tyne

History

The borough was created in 1974 from parts of Northumberland. Its history is defined by the Tyne: Wallsend (Segedunum) marked the eastern end of Hadrian's Wall; North Shields and Wallsend became major centres of shipbuilding — the Swan Hunter yard at Wallsend built famous liners including the Mauretania and the carrier Ark Royal — and of coal export and fishing. The collapse of shipbuilding in the late 20th century brought decline, followed by regeneration of the coast and the growth of office parks such as Cobalt, one of the UK's largest.

Governance

North Tyneside Council is led by a directly elected mayor, a system adopted in 2002. The borough forms part of the North East Combined Authority.
