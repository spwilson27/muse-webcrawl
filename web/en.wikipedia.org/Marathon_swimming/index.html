Marathon swimming

Marathon swimming is a class of open water swimming defined by long distances (at least 10 kilometers) and traditional rules based on the regulations of the first swim of the English Channel in 1875. Unlike most swimming competitions, marathon swimmers generally do not use wetsuits in officially ratified swims and are escorted by support boats for feeding and navigation.

Marathon swimming
Typical distance: 10 km and above
Olympic event: 10 km marathon swim (since 2008)
Governing body: World Aquatics (formerly FINA)
Famous swims: English Channel, Catalina Channel, Manhattan Island

History

Captain Matthew Webb became the first person to swim the English Channel in 1875, completing the crossing in 21 hours 45 minutes. Channel swimming grew into a tradition codified by the Channel Swimming Association (founded 1927). The Oceans Seven challenge, modeled on mountaineering's Seven Summits, links seven major channel crossings including the North Channel, Cook Strait, and Tsugaru Strait.

Competition

A 10 km marathon swim was added to the Olympic program at Beijing in 2008, swum in open water as a mass-start race lasting about two hours. World Aquatics also stages ultra-marathon grand prix races exceeding 15 km, such as the 57 km Santa Fe–Coronda swim in Argentina and Canada's 32 km Traversée internationale du lac St-Jean.

Rules and techniques

In traditional channel rules, swimmers may not touch the escort boat or wear thermal protection beyond a standard swimsuit, cap, and goggles; feeds of carbohydrate drinks are passed on poles or lines every 30 minutes or so. Swimmers contend with cold water, tides, jellyfish, and seasickness, and rely heavily on stroke efficiency and drafting behind the pilot boat.
