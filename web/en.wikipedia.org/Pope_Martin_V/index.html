Pope Martin V

Pope Martin V (Latin: Martinus V; Italian: Martino V; January/February 1369 – 20 February 1431), born Oddone Colonna, was head of the Catholic Church and ruler of the Papal States from 11 November 1417 to his death in February 1431. His election by the Council of Constance effectively ended the Western Schism, which had divided the Church among rival claimants at Rome, Avignon and Pisa for nearly forty years.

Election

A member of the powerful Colonna family, Oddone Colonna studied law at Perugia and was made a cardinal in 1405. At the Council of Constance — convened to settle the schism, reform the Church and suppress heresy — the Roman pope Gregory XII resigned, the Pisan claimant John XXIII was deposed, and the Avignon claimant Benedict XIII was deposed (though he refused to accept it). On St Martin's Day, 11 November 1417, a conclave enlarged by deputies of the council's "nations" elected Colonna, who took the name Martin V.

Pontificate

Martin restored the papacy to Rome in 1420, finding the city depopulated and in ruins, and set about rebuilding its churches, aqueducts and administration, laying foundations for the Renaissance papacy. He negotiated concordats with the European nations (1418) and reasserted papal authority against the conciliarist claim that councils outrank popes, while reluctantly complying with the decree Frequens by summoning the Council of Pavia-Siena (1423) and designating Basel for the next council, which opened shortly after his death.

Martin condemned the Hussites and proclaimed crusades against them, which repeatedly failed in Bohemia. He also worked to restore the Papal States' finances and territories, employing condottieri such as Braccio da Montone. He died of a stroke on 20 February 1431 and was buried in the Archbasilica of Saint John Lateran.
