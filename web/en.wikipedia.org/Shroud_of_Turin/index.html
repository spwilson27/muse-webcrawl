Shroud of Turin

The Shroud of Turin, also known as the Holy Shroud, is a length of linen cloth bearing the negative image of a man. Some believe it to be the burial shroud of Jesus of Nazareth, in which he was wrapped after crucifixion, though the Catholic Church has never formally endorsed or rejected the claim.

Description

The shroud measures approximately 4.41 metres by 1.13 metres. It bears the faint front and back images of a naked man with his hands folded across his groin, together with markings consistent with wounds. It is kept in the Cathedral of Turin, Italy.

History

The shroud first appears in documented history in the 1350s at Lirey, France, in the possession of the knight Geoffroi de Charny. It passed through the House of Savoy and was moved to Turin in 1578, where it has remained, apart from being kept in hiding during the Second World War.

Scientific examination

In 1978 the Shroud of Turin Research Project conducted extensive tests. In 1988, radiocarbon dating performed independently by three laboratories dated samples of the cloth to between 1260 and 1390, indicating a medieval origin. The results remain disputed by some researchers, who argue the sampled corner may have been a medieval repair.
