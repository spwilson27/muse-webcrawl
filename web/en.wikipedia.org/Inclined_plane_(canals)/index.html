Inclined plane (canals)

A canal inclined plane is a system for transporting boats between two levels of water on a canal, as an alternative to a flight of locks. Boats are carried up or down a steep slope on a cradle or in a water-filled caisson running on rails.

Operation

Inclined planes use far less water than locks, which waste a lockful at each passage, and can lift boats over much greater height differences in a single movement. They require machinery to haul the cradle and a structure strong enough to bear the load.

Examples

Famous examples include the Foxton inclined plane in England (opened 1900, later dismantled), the Ronquières inclined plane in Belgium, the Saint-Louis-Arzviller plane in France, and the planes of the Morris Canal in New Jersey.

Decline

Most canal inclined planes were superseded by locks or closed with their canals, though a few modern examples remain in operation for freight or leisure traffic.
