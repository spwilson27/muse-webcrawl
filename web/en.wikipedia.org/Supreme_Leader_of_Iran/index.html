The Supreme Leader of Iran (Persian: رهبر معظم ایران, rahbar-e mo'azzam-e Irân), also referred to as the Supreme Leader of the Islamic Revolution, also simply the Leader (rahbar) or the Supreme Leader, is the head of state and the highest political and religious authority of the Islamic Republic of Iran (above the president). The armed forces, judiciary, state radio and television, and other key government organisations such as the Guardian Council and the Expediency Discernment Council are subject to the Supreme Leader.

According to the constitution, the Supreme Leader delineates the general policies of the Islamic Republic (article 110), supervising the legislature, the judiciary, and the executive branches (article 57). The current lifetime office holder, Ali Khamenei, has issued decrees and made the final decisions on the economy, the environment, foreign policy, education, national planning, and other aspects of governance in Iran. Khamenei also makes the final decisions on the amount of transparency in elections, and has fired and reinstated presidential cabinet appointees.

History

The office was established by the Constitution of Iran in 1979, based on Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini's concept of the Guardianship of the Islamic Jurist (velayat-e faqih), which holds that in the absence of the Mahdi, a just and capable Islamic jurist should assume guardianship over the affairs of Muslims. Khomeini served as the first Supreme Leader from 1979 until his death in 1989. On 4 June 1989, the Assembly of Experts elected Ali Khamenei, then the president of Iran, as his successor.

Selection

The Supreme Leader is elected by the Assembly of Experts, a body of 88 Islamic scholars who are themselves elected by the public every eight years; candidates for the Assembly, however, are vetted by the Guardian Council, half of whose members are appointed by the Supreme Leader himself. The Assembly is also constitutionally charged with supervising the Leader and may dismiss him if he is found incapable of fulfilling his duties, although this power has never been exercised.

Powers and duties

Per article 110 of the constitution, the duties and powers of the Supreme Leader include delineating the general policies of the Islamic Republic after consultation with the Expediency Discernment Council; supervision over the proper execution of those policies; issuing decrees for national referenda; assuming supreme command of the armed forces; declaring war and peace; appointing, dismissing, and accepting the resignations of the head of the judiciary, the head of state radio and television, the chief of the general staff, and the commanders of the armed forces; and signing the decree formalising the election of the president.

Office

The Supreme Leader's office operates from the Beit-e Rahbari compound in central Tehran. The Leader oversees extensive institutions and foundations, including the bonyads, and his office plays a major role in the country's political, military, and economic life.
