Recreational vehicle

A recreational vehicle, often abbreviated as RV, is a motor vehicle or trailer equipped with living space and amenities found in a home, used primarily for recreational camping and travel. The term is most common in North America; similar vehicles are called motorhomes or campervans elsewhere.

Types

Motorized RVs are classed as Class A (bus-like), Class B (van conversions), and Class C (built on a truck cab chassis). Towable types include travel trailers, fifth-wheel trailers, and pop-up campers. Amenities typically include a kitchen, bathroom, sleeping quarters, and fresh-water and waste tanks, allowing self-contained stays at campgrounds and RV parks.

History

Commercial RV production began in the 1910s and 1920s, and the industry expanded after the Second World War with companies such as Airstream and Winnebago. RV travel is a significant leisure industry, and some people live in RVs full-time.
