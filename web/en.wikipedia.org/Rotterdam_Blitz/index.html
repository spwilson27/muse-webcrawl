The Rotterdam Blitz was the aerial bombardment of Rotterdam by the German Luftwaffe on 14 May 1940, during the German invasion of the Netherlands in the Second World War. Although negotiations for the city's surrender were under way, about 100 tonnes of high-explosive bombs were dropped on the city centre, destroying almost the entire historic district.

Background

German forces invaded the Netherlands on 10 May 1940. Dutch marines and army units held Rotterdam, where German airborne troops who had landed on bridges and at Waalhaven airfield were pinned down. The German command issued an ultimatum demanding the city's surrender and threatened to destroy Rotterdam; while Dutch envoys negotiated, the attack by Heinkel He 111 bombers of Kampfgeschwader 54 went ahead — a recall order using red flares was seen by only part of the formation.

The bombing

On the afternoon of 14 May, bombs ignited fires that merged into a firestorm. Roughly 24,000 homes, 70 schools, 21 churches, thousands of shops and the port district were destroyed; about 900 people were killed and 85,000 left homeless. Fearing similar attacks on Utrecht and Amsterdam, the Dutch commander-in-chief Henri Winkelman signed a general capitulation the following day (Zeeland excepted).

Legacy

The ruined centre was cleared and Rotterdam rebuilt after the war as a showcase of modernist architecture and planning. The bombing is commemorated by Ossip Zadkine's sculpture The Destroyed City (De verwoeste stad, 1953), depicting a figure with a hole where its heart should be.
