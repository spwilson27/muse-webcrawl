Donkey Kong (video game)

Donkey Kong
Developer: Nintendo R&D1 / Ikegami Tsushinki
Publisher: Nintendo
Released: 1981 (arcade)
Genre: Platform
Designer: Shigeru Miyamoto

Donkey Kong is a 1981 arcade platform game developed and published by Nintendo. The player controls Jumpman—later renamed Mario—in his debut role, climbing construction-site girders to rescue Pauline from the giant ape Donkey Kong, who hurls barrels and other obstacles. It was one of the first games with jumping as a core mechanic and is credited with popularizing the platform genre.

Development and reception

Designed by Shigeru Miyamoto, the game was created as a replacement for unsold Radar Scope cabinets in North America and borrowed its story structure from Popeye and King Kong. It became a massive commercial success, establishing Nintendo in the American video game market. A lawsuit by Universal City Studios claiming infringement of King Kong ended in Nintendo's favor.

Legacy

Donkey Kong launched two of Nintendo's most enduring franchises and became a fixture of competitive gaming culture, notably through high-score rivalries documented decades later. It has been ported to numerous home systems and is routinely listed among the greatest video games of all time.
