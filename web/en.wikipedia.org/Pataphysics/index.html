'Pataphysics (French: 'pataphysique) is a concept and literary-philosophical movement defined by the French writer Alfred Jarry (1873–1907) as "the science of imaginary solutions, which symbolically attributes the properties of objects, described by their virtuality, to their lineaments". Jarry defined 'pataphysics as a science placed beyond metaphysics, just as metaphysics is beyond physics: it studies the laws governing exceptions and explains the universe supplementary to this one.

Origins

The term appears in Jarry's works from the 1890s, including the play cycles around Ubu Roi (1896), and is developed most fully in his novel Gestes et opinions du docteur Faustroll, pataphysicien (Exploits and Opinions of Dr. Faustroll, Pataphysician), written in 1897–98 and published posthumously in 1911. The leading apostrophe in 'pataphysics was deliberate — Jarry wrote that it served "to avoid a simple pun" (patte à physique, "physics paw").

Concepts

Central 'pataphysical ideas include the clinamen (the atomic swerve borrowed from Lucretius, recast as the smallest deviation that produces everything), the syzgy, and Jarry's notion that 'pataphysics examines "the particular, in spite of the common opinion that the only function of science is the consideration of the general". Its reasoning embraces parody of scientific method, absurd logic and the equivalence of opposites.

The Collège de 'Pataphysique

In 1948 the Collège de 'Pataphysique was founded in Paris as a mock-learned society devoted to the study and promotion of 'pataphysics. Its members and associates have included Marcel Duchamp, Raymond Queneau, Boris Vian, Eugène Ionesco, Jean Dubuffet, Man Ray and Umberto Eco. The Collège publishes journals such as the Cahiers du Collège de 'Pataphysique and maintains an elaborate parody hierarchy and its own calendar, beginning with Jarry's birthday.

Influence

'Pataphysics influenced the Dada and Surrealist movements, the Oulipo group, and conceptual art; sister institutes were founded abroad, including the London Institute of 'Pataphysics. It is frequently invoked as an ancestor of postmodern irony in literature and art.
