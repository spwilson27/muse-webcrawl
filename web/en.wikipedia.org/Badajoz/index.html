Badajoz

Badajoz is a city in western Spain, capital of the province of Badajoz in the autonomous community of Extremadura. It stands on the left bank of the Guadiana River, close to the border with Portugal, and is the most populous city in Extremadura, with a population of roughly 150,000.

History

Badajoz was founded as Batalyos under Muslim rule around 875 by Ibn Marwan. After the Christian reconquest it became a frontier fortress of great strategic importance in the repeated wars between Spain and Portugal. During the Peninsular War it endured several sieges, most famously the storming of the city by Wellington's Anglo-Portuguese army in April 1812 after a costly assault on the breaches. In the Spanish Civil War, the Battle of Badajoz (1936) was followed by the massacre of thousands of prisoners by Nationalist forces in the city's bullring.

Landmarks

The old town retains the Alcazaba, one of the largest Moorish citadels in Spain, the cathedral of San Juan Bautista, the Giralda de Badajoz tower, and the gardens along the Guadiana. The city is known for its Carnival, one of Spain's major carnival celebrations.
