Monterey Canyon

Monterey Canyon is a great submarine canyon in Monterey Bay, off the coast of central California. Comparable in scale to the Grand Canyon, it begins at Moss Landing, near the middle of the bay, and extends about 153 kilometres (95 miles) out to sea, reaching depths of more than 3,600 metres (11,800 ft) below sea level.

Monterey Canyon
Location: Monterey Bay, California
Length: c. 153 km
Maximum depth: over 3,600 m

Formation

The canyon's origins remain debated. It may have begun as the offshore course of an ancient river — possibly draining California's Central Valley — carved during periods of lower sea level, and has been maintained and reshaped by turbidity currents: underwater avalanches of sediment that periodically sweep down its axis. Today it continues to be moulded by sediment flows, internal tides, and dense shelf-water cascading.

Research

The canyon is a natural laboratory studied intensively by the Monterey Bay Aquarium Research Institute (MBARI), whose remotely operated vehicles have documented its deep-sea ecosystems: cold seeps, whale falls, deep-water corals, and unusual communities around chemosynthetic clams. A long-term observatory has captured turbidity currents in action, showing flows powerful enough to carry instruments kilometres down-canyon.

Ecology

Upwelling of cold, nutrient-rich water along the canyon helps make Monterey Bay one of the most biologically productive marine regions on the United States' west coast, supporting krill, squid, whales, and dense kelp forests at its head.
