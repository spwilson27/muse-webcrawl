Vertex operator algebra

In mathematics, a vertex operator algebra (VOA) is an algebraic structure that plays an important role in two-dimensional conformal field theory and in string theory. Vertex operator algebras were first introduced by Richard Borcherds in 1986, formalising the notion of a vertex algebra developed by Igor Frenkel; the axioms encode the operator product expansions of fields in a chiral conformal field theory.

Definition

A vertex operator algebra consists of a graded vector space V, a vacuum vector, a conformal (Virasoro) element, and a state–field correspondence Y assigning to each state a formal series of operators (vertex operators) in a formal variable. These data satisfy axioms including the Jacobi identity, translation and Virasoro relations, and grading restrictions. The grading is governed by the Virasoro algebra, whose central charge is a key invariant of the algebra.

Examples and applications

Important examples include VOAs built from lattices, affine Lie algebras at positive integer level, and the Virasoro algebra itself. The most famous example is the moonshine module V♮, constructed by Frenkel, Lepowsky and Meurman, whose automorphism group is the Monster group; Borcherds used vertex operator algebras to prove the monstrous moonshine conjectures, work for which he received the Fields Medal in 1998.
