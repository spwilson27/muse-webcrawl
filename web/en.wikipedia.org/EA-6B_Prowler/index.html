Grumman EA-6B Prowler

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Grumman EA-6B Prowler is a twin-engine, four-seat, mid-wing electronic-warfare aircraft derived from the A-6 Intruder airframe. The EA-6B had the ability to carry an additional anti-radiation missile, the AGM-88 HARM, along with pods housing jamming equipment. It was operated by the United States Navy and Marine Corps, and was the primary tactical jamming aircraft of the United States military until replaced by the EA-18G Growler.

Development

The EA-6B was developed as an advanced electronic countermeasures version of the two-seat EA-6A, itself derived from the A-6 Intruder attack aircraft. The fuselage was stretched to accommodate four crew, and a distinctive pod containing receivers was mounted on top of the vertical stabilizer. The aircraft carried AN/ALQ-99 jamming pods under its wings.

Operational history

Prowlers entered service in 1971 and flew in the Vietnam War, the Gulf War, operations over the Balkans, Iraq, and Afghanistan, and counter-ISIS operations. Its mission set expanded from radar jamming to suppression of enemy air defenses (SEAD) with HARM missiles and communications intelligence. The Navy retired the type in 2015 and the Marine Corps in March 2019, ending nearly five decades of service.

Specifications

The EA-6B was powered by two Pratt & Whitney J52 turbojets, reached a maximum speed of about 920 km/h (566 mph), and had a range of roughly 3,250 km (2,020 mi) unrefueled. Length was 17.7 m with a wingspan of 16.15 m.
