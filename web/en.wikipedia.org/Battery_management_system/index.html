Battery management system

TypeElectronic control system
MonitorsVoltage, current, temperature, state of charge
Used inElectric vehicles, laptops, grid storage, spacecraft

A battery management system (BMS) is any electronic system that manages a rechargeable battery (cell or battery pack), such as by protecting the battery from operating outside its safe operating area, monitoring its state, calculating secondary data, reporting that data, controlling its environment, authenticating it, and balancing it. A battery pack built together with a battery management system with an external communication data bus is known as a smart battery pack, which must be charged by a smart charger.

Functions

The primary functions of a BMS are protection and monitoring. It measures cell voltages, pack current, and temperatures, and can disconnect the battery through contactors or switches if values exceed safe limits, preventing overcharge, over-discharge, over-current, and overheating — failures that in lithium-ion packs can lead to thermal runaway. The BMS estimates the state of charge (SoC) and state of health (SoH) of the pack using coulomb counting and voltage models, and computes permissible charge and discharge power.

Cell balancing

Because individual cells in a series string differ slightly in capacity and self-discharge, their states of charge drift apart over time. Balancing circuits equalize them: passive balancing bleeds charge from the fullest cells through resistors, while active balancing transfers charge between cells. Balancing maximizes the usable capacity of the pack and extends its service life.

Applications

Battery management systems are essential in electric vehicles, where packs contain thousands of cells; in consumer electronics; in uninterruptible power supplies and grid-scale energy storage; and in aerospace applications. Automotive BMS units communicate with vehicle controllers over CAN bus and are subject to functional-safety standards.
