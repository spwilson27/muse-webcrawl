Digital nomad

Digital nomads are people who travel freely while working remotely using technology and the internet. Such people generally have minimal material possessions and work remotely in temporary housing, hotels, cafes, public libraries, co-working spaces, or recreational vehicles, using Wi-Fi, smartphones or mobile hotspots to access the Internet.

The term was popularised by the 1997 book Digital Nomad by Tsugio Makimoto and David Manners. The lifestyle grew rapidly in the 2010s with the spread of remote work, and expanded further after the COVID-19 pandemic normalised telecommuting.

Lifestyle

Digital nomads often work in technology, marketing, design, writing, and consulting. Popular destinations have included Bali, Chiang Mai, Lisbon, Mexico City, and Medellín, chosen for low cost of living, climate, and internet infrastructure. Many nomads practice "geoarbitrage" — earning income from high-wage countries while living where costs are lower.

Legal issues

Nomads typically travel on tourist visas, which in many jurisdictions do not formally permit work. In response, a growing number of countries — among them Estonia, Barbados, Costa Rica, Portugal, and Thailand — have introduced dedicated digital nomad visas allowing stays of six months to several years. Tax residency, health insurance, and visa runs remain common complications.

Criticism

The lifestyle has been criticised for contributing to rising rents and gentrification in host cities, and for a perceived lack of integration with local communities. Researchers also note loneliness and burnout among long-term nomads.
