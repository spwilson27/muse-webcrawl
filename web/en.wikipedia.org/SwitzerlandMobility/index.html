SwitzerlandMobility (German: SchweizMobil, French: SuisseMobile) is the Swiss national network for non-motorised transport, especially for leisure and tourism. It coordinates a uniformly signposted system of routes for hiking, cycling, mountain biking, skating, and canoeing across Switzerland, presented through a coordinated system of maps, guidebooks, and a website and app.

Network

The network comprises national, regional, and local routes marked with distinctive red route markers. Its backbone includes the national long-distance hiking trails such as the Alpine Passes Trail and the Jura Crest Trail, national cycling routes such as the Rhine Route and the Rhone Route, and mountain bike routes such as the Alpine Bike. SwitzerlandMobility also designates barrier-free routes accessible to wheelchairs and has a winter branch, SwitzerlandMobility Winter, covering signposted winter hiking paths, snowshoe trails, cross-country ski trails, and sledging runs.

Organisation

The SwitzerlandMobility Foundation was established in 2006 by federal offices, cantons, and tourism and transport organisations ahead of the network's official launch in April 2008. The foundation coordinates route quality and marketing, while cantons and municipalities maintain the physical signage. The project grew out of earlier initiatives such as Cycling in Switzerland (founded 1995) and is regarded as a model for coordinating slow-travel infrastructure nationally.
