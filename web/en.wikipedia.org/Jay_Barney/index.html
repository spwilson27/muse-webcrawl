Jay Barney

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Jay B. Barney (born October 8, 1954) is an American professor of strategic management and entrepreneurship. He is best known for his contributions to the resource-based view (RBV) of the firm, a framework in strategic management theory which holds that firms achieve sustained competitive advantage by acquiring and controlling valuable, rare, inimitable, and non-substitutable (VRIN) resources and capabilities.

Career

Barney received his bachelor's degree from Brigham Young University and his PhD in administrative sciences from Yale University in 1982. He has taught at the University of California, Los Angeles (Anderson School of Management), Texas A&M University, Ohio State University (Fisher College of Business), and the University of Utah (David Eccles School of Business), where he holds the Presidential Chair of Strategic Management and Lassonde Chair of Social Entrepreneurship.

Resource-based view

Barney's 1991 paper, "Firm Resources and Sustained Competitive Advantage", published in the Journal of Management, is one of the most cited works in management research. It argues that resources that are valuable, rare, imperfectly imitable, and without strategically equivalent substitutes can be sources of sustained competitive advantage. His related framework, VRIO (valuable, rare, costly to imitate, and organised to capture value), elaborated in his textbook Strategic Management and Competitive Advantage (co-authored with William Hesterly), is widely used in management education and practice.

Other work

Barney has also written on organisational culture, trust, and entrepreneurship, and has served as editor of the Academy of Management Review. He is a fellow of the Academy of Management and the Strategic Management Society.
