Hundred Years' War
Date1337–1453 (116 years)
BelligerentsKingdom of England vs Kingdom of France and allies
ResultFrench victory; England retains only Calais

The Hundred Years' War (French: Guerre de Cent Ans) was a series of armed conflicts fought between the kingdoms of England and France during the Late Middle Ages from 1337 to 1453. The war originated over rival claims to the French throne after the extinction of the direct Capetian line.

Course

Early English victories at Crécy (1346), Poitiers (1356), and Agincourt (1415) gave England control of large parts of France. The tide turned with the campaigns inspired by Joan of Arc, who relieved Orléans in 1429, and French forces gradually reconquered the lost territories.

End and consequences

The war effectively ended in 1453 after the French victory at Castillon; England kept only the Pale of Calais on the continent. The long conflict transformed warfare — longbows, then gunpowder artillery — and hardened French and English national identities.
