John Lydon

Born: John Joseph Lydon, 31 January 1956, London, England
Also known as: Johnny Rotten
Occupation: Singer, songwriter, author, television presenter
Years active: 1975–present
Associated acts: Sex Pistols; Public Image Ltd (PiL)

John Joseph Lydon (born 31 January 1956), also known by his stage name Johnny Rotten, is a British singer, songwriter and television presenter. He is best known as the lead singer of the late-1970s punk band the Sex Pistols, which lasted from 1975 until 1978, and which had several re-formations over the years, and as the lead singer of the post-punk band Public Image Ltd (PiL), which he founded and fronted from 1978 until 1993, and again since 2009.

Early life

Lydon was born in Finsbury Park, London, to working-class Irish immigrant parents. As a child he contracted meningitis, spending months in hospital in a coma; the illness left him with a distinctive stare and affected his memory and schooling. He was expelled from school and attended further-education colleges, falling in with the circle around Malcolm McLaren and Vivienne Westwood's King's Road shop, SEX.

Sex Pistols

In 1975 Lydon auditioned for the Sex Pistols — famously miming to Alice Cooper's "I'm Eighteen" — and was hired as frontman under the name Johnny Rotten, reportedly after his rotten teeth. With songs including "Anarchy in the U.K.", "God Save the Queen" and "Pretty Vacant", the band became the focal point of British punk; their only studio album, Never Mind the Bollocks, Here's the Sex Pistols (1977), is a landmark of popular music. The band imploded on an American tour in January 1978, Lydon announcing from the stage, "Ever get the feeling you've been cheated?"

Public Image Ltd and later work

Later in 1978 Lydon founded Public Image Ltd with guitarist Keith Levene and bassist Jah Wobble. PiL's early albums — First Issue, Metal Box (1979) and The Flowers of Romance (1981) — pioneered post-punk with dub, disco and avant-garde influences. He later scored a hit with "This Is Not a Love Song" (1983) and an international success with "Rise" (1986). Lydon has also worked as a television presenter, appeared in reality television, written two volumes of autobiography (Rotten: No Irish, No Blacks, No Dogs and Anger Is an Energy), and re-formed both PiL and, for tours, the Sex Pistols.
