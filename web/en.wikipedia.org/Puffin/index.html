Puffin

Puffins
FamilyAlcidae (auks)
GenusFratercula

Puffins are any of three species of small alcids (auks) in the bird genus Fratercula. They are pelagic seabirds that feed primarily by diving in the water, and breed in large colonies on coastal cliffs or offshore islands, nesting in crevices among rocks or in burrows in the soil.

Species

The three species are the Atlantic puffin (Fratercula arctica) of the North Atlantic, the horned puffin (F. corniculata) and the tufted puffin (F. cirrhata) of the North Pacific. All have black and white plumage and a large, brightly coloured beak during the breeding season.

Behaviour

Puffins fly with rapid wingbeats of up to 400 beats per minute and can reach speeds of over 80 km/h. They use their wings to "fly" underwater in pursuit of fish such as sand eels, carrying multiple small fish crosswise in the beak.
