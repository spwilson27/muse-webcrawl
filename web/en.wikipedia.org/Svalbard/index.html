Svalbard

Svalbard is a Norwegian archipelago in the Arctic Ocean, roughly midway between mainland Norway and the North Pole, spanning latitudes 74° to 81° north. Its largest island is Spitsbergen, and its administrative centre and largest settlement is Longyearbyen, one of the world's northernmost communities, with a population of around 2,400; the total population of the archipelago is about 2,900.

Status and history

The 1920 Svalbard Treaty, signed in Paris after the First World War, granted Norway sovereignty over the archipelago while preserving equal rights for nationals and companies of all signatory states to engage in fishing, hunting and mining, and stipulating that Svalbard remain demilitarised. Whalers had used the islands from the early 17th century, and coal mining began in the early 20th century, with American, British, Dutch, Soviet/Russian and Norwegian companies establishing settlements such as Barentsburg — still operated by the Russian state company Trust Arktikugol — and the now-abandoned Pyramiden.

Geography and climate

About 60% of Svalbard's land area is covered by glaciers. The climate is polar but significantly moderated by the North Atlantic Current; nevertheless, sea ice, polar night from late October to mid-February, and the midnight sun in summer define the seasons. Svalbard is warming faster than most of the Arctic. Wildlife includes polar bears (which outnumber permanent residents), Svalbard reindeer, Arctic foxes, walruses, seals and large seabird colonies.

Economy and research

Coal mining has declined sharply and the last Norwegian mine, Gruve 7 near Longyearbyen, was scheduled for closure in the 2020s. The economy now centres on research and education (the University Centre in Svalbard, UNIS), tourism, and satellite services — the SvalSat ground station benefits from the polar location. The Svalbard Global Seed Vault, opened in 2008 inside a mountain near Longyearbyen, stores duplicate seed samples from the world's crop collections as a backup against loss of genetic diversity.
