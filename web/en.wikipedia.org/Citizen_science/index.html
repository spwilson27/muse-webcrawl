Citizen science

Volunteers collecting observations for a citizen science project.

Citizen science (also called community science, crowd science or volunteer monitoring) is scientific research conducted, in whole or in part, by amateur or non-professional scientists. Members of the public contribute observations, classifications, measurements or analysis, often in collaboration with professional researchers, allowing studies at scales of time, space and sample size that would otherwise be impractical.

Examples

One of the oldest continuing projects is the Audubon Society's Christmas Bird Count, begun in 1900. The Cornell Lab of Ornithology's eBird platform gathers hundreds of millions of bird observations from birdwatchers worldwide. On the Zooniverse platform, volunteers classify galaxies (Galaxy Zoo), transcribe historical documents and monitor wildlife camera traps; the game Foldit enlisted players to solve protein-folding puzzles. SETI@home and similar distributed-computing projects donate idle computer processing power.

Methods and issues

Projects range from contributory designs, in which participants simply collect data, to co-created projects in which the public helps define the questions. Common concerns include data quality and validation, observer bias, participant retention, ethics and the proper crediting of volunteers. Smartphone apps and cheap sensors have greatly expanded participation since the 2000s, and the term "citizen science" entered the Oxford English Dictionary in 2014.
