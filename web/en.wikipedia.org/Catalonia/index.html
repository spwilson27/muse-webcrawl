Catalonia (Catalan: Catalunya; Spanish: Cataluña) is an autonomous community of Spain, designated as a nationality by its Statute of Autonomy. It comprises the provinces of Barcelona, Girona, Lleida, and Tarragona in the northeast of the Iberian Peninsula, bordering France and Andorra and the Mediterranean Sea.

With about 7.9 million inhabitants and the second-largest regional economy in Spain, Catalonia is a major industrial, commercial, cultural, and tourist centre. Its language, Catalan, is co-official and widely used in administration and education.

History

The region was Romanised early (Tarraco was a provincial capital) and later formed part of the Visigothic kingdom. Frankish counts established the Spanish March; the County of Barcelona became hereditary under Wilfred the Hairy in the late 9th century. Through the 1137 dynastic union with Aragon, Catalonia became a maritime power of the Crown of Aragon in the medieval Mediterranean.

The Reapers' War (1640–1652) and the War of the Spanish Succession ended with the fall of Barcelona in 1714 and the abolition of Catalan institutions. The 19th century brought industrialisation and the Renaixença cultural revival. Autonomy was restored in 1932, suppressed under Franco, and re-established in 1979. A contested independence referendum on 1 October 2017 and a unilateral declaration of independence led Spain to impose direct rule temporarily and prosecute Catalan leaders; pardons followed in 2021.

Geography and economy

Catalonia ranges from the Pyrenees and pre-coastal ranges to the Ebro delta and the Costa Brava and Costa Daurada coasts. Its economy includes chemicals, automotive manufacturing, food processing, publishing, finance, and tourism — Barcelona is one of Europe's most visited cities, famed for Gaudí's Modernista landmarks and FC Barcelona.

Culture

Catalan traditions include building human towers (castells, UNESCO-listed), the sardana dance, giants and big-heads in festivals, and the Diada national day on 11 September, commemorating the 1714 fall of Barcelona.
