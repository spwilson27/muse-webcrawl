Chania


Chania (Greek: Χανιά), a city on the northwest coast of Crete, Greece.

Chania (Greek: Χανιά, pronounced [xaˈɲa]) is a city on the northwest coast of the Greek island of Crete, and the capital of the Chania regional unit. It lies about 70 kilometres west of Rethymno and 145 kilometres west of Heraklion. With roughly 55,000 inhabitants in the city proper and about 110,000 in its wider urban area, it is the island's second-largest city and a major tourist destination, famed for its Venetian harbour.

History

The site was inhabited in Minoan times, when the settlement was known as Kydonia and appears in Linear B tablets. Kydonia remained an important city-state through the classical and Hellenistic periods. After Byzantine and Arab rule, Venice took Chania in 1252 and fortified it; the Venetians built the harbour, arsenals and the city walls that still define the old town. The Ottomans captured Chania in 1645 after a two-month siege, and it became the seat of the Pasha of Crete. Chania was the capital of the autonomous Cretan State (1898–1913) before Crete united with Greece.

Old town and harbour

The old town surrounds the 14th-century Venetian harbour, whose landmarks include the Firkas fortress, the Great Arsenal, the mosque of the Janissaries (1645, the oldest Ottoman building in Crete) and the 16th-century lighthouse, rebuilt in Egyptian style in the 1830s. Behind the harbour lie the quarters of Topanas, the Jewish quarter Evraiki, Kasteli hill and the covered municipal market, opened in 1913.

Modern city

Today Chania is served by an international airport on the Akrotiri peninsula and the port of Souda, one of the Mediterranean's finest natural harbours, which also hosts a NATO naval base. The Technical University of Crete and the Mediterranean Agronomic Institute are based in the city. Chania was heavily fought over during the Battle of Crete in May 1941.
