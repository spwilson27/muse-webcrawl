Electrospray ionization

Electrospray ionization (ESI) is a soft ionization technique used in mass spectrometry to produce ions from large molecules, including proteins and other biomolecules, without fragmenting them significantly.

Mechanism

A solution of the analyte is pumped through a fine capillary held at high voltage, forming a fine spray of charged droplets. As solvent evaporates, the droplets shrink until electrostatic repulsion releases gas-phase ions, which are guided into the mass analyser. Large molecules typically acquire many charges, bringing their mass-to-charge ratios into a measurable range.

History

The technique was developed in the 1980s, notably by John Fenn, who shared the 2002 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for work on soft ionization methods for biological macromolecules.

Applications

ESI is widely coupled with liquid chromatography (LC–MS) for proteomics, drug analysis and clinical testing.
