Romanos IV Diogenes (Greek: Ῥωμανὸς Διογένης; c. 1030 – 1072) was a Byzantine emperor from 1068 to 1071. A capable general from a distinguished Cappadocian military family, he was chosen as husband by the widowed empress Eudokia Makrembolitissa and crowned co-emperor in order to defend the empire against the Seljuk Turks.

Campaigns against the Seljuks

Romanos led expeditions into eastern Anatolia in 1068, 1069 and 1070 with mixed results. In 1071 he assembled a large army — estimates range from 40,000 to 70,000 men — and marched to recover the fortress of Manzikert in Armenia, which had fallen to the Seljuk sultan Alp Arslan.

Battle of Manzikert

On 19 August 1071 the Byzantine army was decisively defeated at the Battle of Manzikert. Betrayal by Andronikos Doukas, who withdrew the reserve, contributed to the rout, and Romanos was captured — one of the very few Byzantine emperors taken prisoner in battle. Alp Arslan treated him honourably and released him in exchange for a ransom and territorial concessions.

Downfall and death

During his captivity the Doukas family in Constantinople declared Romanos deposed and proclaimed Michael VII emperor. Romanos resisted briefly in Cilicia but surrendered on a promise of personal safety; he was nevertheless blinded — so brutally that he died of infected wounds on 4 August 1072, exiled on the island of Proti. The Manzikert disaster and the ensuing civil wars opened Anatolia to Turkish settlement, a turning point in Byzantine history.
