The Sansad Bhavan (House of the People) is the seat of the Parliament of India, located in New Delhi. Designed by British architects Edwin Lutyens and Herbert Baker, the circular building was constructed between 1921 and 1927 as the Council House of British India. It housed the Lok Sabha (House of the People) and Rajya Sabha (Council of States) from independence until 2023.

Architecture

The building is circular in plan, about 170 metres in diameter, and ringed by a colonnade of 144 sandstone columns. Its design draws on Indian precedents — the colonnaded form is often compared to the Chausath Yogini Temple at Morena — combined with classical detailing. Inside lie the two legislative chambers, the Central Hall (used for joint sittings and for the Constituent Assembly's work on the Constitution), a library, and committee rooms, set around open courtyards.

History

The foundation stone was laid by the Duke of Connaught in 1921 and the building was opened by the Viceroy, Lord Irwin, in 1927. After independence it became the home of India's Parliament; the Constituent Assembly met in its Central Hall, where Jawaharlal Nehru delivered his "Tryst with Destiny" speech on the night of 14–15 August 1947. In September 2023 parliamentary business moved to a new Parliament House built opposite as part of the Central Vista redevelopment, and the Sansad Bhavan was redesignated Samvidhan Sadan (Constitution House), retained for committee meetings, offices, and events.
