IPv6 deployment

The deployment of IPv6, the latest version of the Internet Protocol (IP), has been ongoing since the mid-2000s. IPv6 was developed by the Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF) to deal with the long-anticipated problem of IPv4 address exhaustion, which was formally reached when IANA allocated its last blocks of IPv4 addresses to the regional registries in February 2011.

Adoption

Adoption has grown steadily, driven by large access networks, mobile carriers and content providers. World IPv6 Day (8 June 2011) and World IPv6 Launch (6 June 2012) saw major operators enable the protocol permanently. By the mid-2020s, global measurements by Google indicated that roughly 40–45% of users reached its services over IPv6, with countries such as India, France, Germany and the United States showing particularly high adoption rates.

Transition mechanisms

Because IPv6 is not backward compatible with IPv4, networks typically run both protocols simultaneously ("dual stack") or use translation and tunnelling techniques such as NAT64/DNS64, 464XLAT, DS-Lite and 6rd. Operating systems have supported IPv6 by default since the mid-2000s, with preferential selection rules defined in standards such as RFC 6724 and the "Happy Eyeballs" algorithm.

Obstacles

Deployment has been slowed by the cost of upgrading equipment and software, the widespread use of network address translation (NAT) prolonging IPv4's life, and a lack of immediate end-user demand. Nevertheless, the exhaustion of IPv4 space and growth of mobile and Internet-of-things networks continue to drive migration.
