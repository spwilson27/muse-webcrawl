The Triple Crown of Hiking informally refers to the three major long-distance hiking trails in the United States: the Appalachian Trail (AT), the Pacific Crest Trail (PCT), and the Continental Divide Trail (CDT). Hikers who complete all three trails are said to have achieved the triple crown; together the trails total some 7,900 miles (12,700 km) and pass through 22 states.

The trails

The Appalachian Trail runs about 2,200 miles from Springer Mountain, Georgia, to Mount Katahdin, Maine. The Pacific Crest Trail runs about 2,650 miles from Mexico to Canada through California, Oregon, and Washington. The Continental Divide Trail, the longest and most remote, runs about 3,100 miles along the Rocky Mountains from Mexico to Canada through New Mexico, Colorado, Wyoming, Idaho, and Montana.

Recognition

The American Long Distance Hiking Association – West (ALDHA-West) presents the Triple Crown Award at its annual Gathering, based on an honours system. Eric Ryback walked all three trails as a teenager in the late 1960s and early 1970s. Completing all three trails within a single calendar year — the "Calendar-Year Triple Crown" — is an even rarer feat, first accomplished by Brian Robinson ("Flyin' Brian") in 2001. The number of recognised Triple Crowners has grown steadily with the popularity of thru-hiking but remains under a thousand.
