Case fatality rate

In epidemiology, a case fatality rate (CFR) — sometimes called a case fatality ratio — is the proportion of people who have been diagnosed with a certain disease and die from it within a given period. It is usually expressed as a percentage and is a measure of disease severity among diagnosed cases.

Calculation and caveats

CFR = deaths attributed to the disease ÷ confirmed cases of the disease. Because it depends on how completely cases are ascertained, the CFR typically overestimates the probability of death per infection when mild or asymptomatic infections go untested; the infection fatality rate (IFR), which uses all infections as the denominator, is lower. During ongoing outbreaks, naive CFR estimates are also biased by the delay between diagnosis and death.

Examples

CFRs vary enormously: rabies is almost invariably fatal once symptoms appear; seasonal influenza has a CFR well under 0.1 percent; the 2002–2004 SARS outbreak had a CFR of about 9.6 percent; and COVID-19 CFRs varied widely by country, age structure, and testing regime, fuelling debate during the pandemic.
