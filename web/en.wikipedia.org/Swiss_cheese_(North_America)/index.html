Swiss cheese (North America)

Image placeholder: North American Swiss cheese with its characteristic "eyes".

Swiss cheese is any variety of cheese that resembles Emmental cheese, a yellow, medium-hard cheese that originated in Switzerland. The term is generic in North America; in the United States and Canada several varieties of cheese are produced under the name "Swiss cheese", some of them processed. Swiss cheese is known for its distinctive holes, called "eyes", formed by carbon dioxide released by the bacterium Propionibacterium freudenreichii during fermentation.

Production

North American Swiss is made from pasteurised cow's milk using cultures similar to those of Emmental, but it is typically aged for a shorter time and has a milder flavour. Major production centres include Ohio and Wisconsin. "Baby Swiss" is made with whole milk and aged briefly, giving smaller holes and a buttery taste; Lacy Swiss has a delicate, lace-like texture.

Uses

Swiss cheese melts well and is a standard ingredient in Reuben sandwiches, croque-monsieur, fondue blends and burgers. It differs from authentic Swiss Emmentaler AOP, which is protected by law in Europe and produced in the Emme valley with raw milk and longer ageing. Compare Swiss chocolate.
