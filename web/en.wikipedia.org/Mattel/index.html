Mattel

Mattel, Inc. is an American multinational toy manufacturing and entertainment company founded in 1945 and headquartered in El Segundo, California. The company was founded by Harold "Matt" Matson, Elliot Handler, and Ruth Handler — its name is a portmanteau of "Matt" and "Elliot". Mattel produces products including Barbie and Ken dolls, Hot Wheels, Fisher-Price toys, American Girl dolls, Thomas & Friends, Masters of the Universe, and board games such as Uno.

In the early 1980s, Mattel produced video game systems under its own brand under the name Intellivision and, in partnership with Nintendo, the HyperScan. The company has a presence in 40 countries and territories and sells products in more than 150 countries.

History

Mattel was founded in 1945 in a garage workshop in Los Angeles, initially selling picture frames and, from the scraps, dollhouse furniture. The company turned to toys with the success of a toy ukulele, the "Uke-A-Doodle", and became the first company to sign a year-round advertising deal with ABC for The Mickey Mouse Club, breaking the tradition of advertising toys only at Christmas.

Barbie and major brands

Ruth Handler introduced the Barbie doll in 1959, inspired by the German Bild Lilli doll; Barbie became Mattel's flagship and one of the best-known toys in the world. Hot Wheels die-cast cars followed in 1968. The company acquired Fisher-Price in 1993 and Tyco Toys in 1997, and purchased American Girl in 1998. In 2023, the Barbie film, produced by Mattel Films with Warner Bros., grossed over $1.4 billion worldwide.

Mattel Films

Mattel has expanded into entertainment through Mattel Films and Mattel Television, developing films and series based on properties including Barbie, Hot Wheels, Masters of the Universe, and Polly Pocket.
