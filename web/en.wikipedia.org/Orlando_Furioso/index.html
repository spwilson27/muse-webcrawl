Orlando Furioso ("The Frenzy of Orlando") is an Italian epic poem by Ludovico Ariosto. First published in 1516 in 40 cantos and expanded to 46 cantos in the definitive 1532 edition, it is one of the most influential works of the Italian Renaissance and a continuation of Matteo Maria Boiardo's unfinished Orlando Innamorato.

Plot

Set against the war between Charlemagne's Christians and the Saracen armies invading Europe, the intricately interlaced narrative (entrelacement) follows many knights and heroines. The paladin Orlando (Roland) is driven mad — furioso — by unrequited love for the pagan princess Angelica, who instead marries the foot-soldier Medoro. Orlando's wits, lost on Earth, are recovered from the Moon by Astolfo in one of literature's most famous episodes. Parallel strands include the love of Ruggiero and the warrior-maiden Bradamante, the legendary ancestors of Ariosto's Este patrons, and the exploits of Astolfo, who rides a hippogriff.

Style and influence

Written in ottava rima, the poem combines chivalric adventure with irony, humour and contemporary reflection. It shaped European literature profoundly: Spenser's The Faerie Queene imitates its interlaced structure, and its episodes inspired operas by Handel (Orlando, Alcina, Ariodante), Vivaldi, Haydn and many others, as well as plays, paintings and Tasso's Gerusalemme liberata.
