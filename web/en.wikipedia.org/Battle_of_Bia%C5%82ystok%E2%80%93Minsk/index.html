Battle of Białystok–Minsk
Date22 June – 9 July 1941
LocationBiałystok and Minsk regions, Byelorussian SSR
ResultGerman victory; double encirclement of the Soviet Western Front
BelligerentsNazi Germany vs. Soviet Union
CommandersFedor von Bock; Heinz Guderian; Hermann Hoth vs. Dmitry Pavlov

The Battle of Białystok–Minsk was a German strategic operation conducted by Army Group Centre during Operation Barbarossa in the opening weeks of the German-Soviet War. Between 22 June and 9 July 1941, the Panzer groups of Heinz Guderian and Hermann Hoth executed a double encirclement, trapping the bulk of the Soviet Western Front in pockets around Białystok and Minsk.

Course of the battle

Two panzer groups broke through the frontier defenses and converged east of Minsk on 27 June, cutting off three Soviet armies. Further closures around Białystok and Volkovysk trapped additional forces. Soviet counterattacks, including the fighting around Slonim, failed to break the encirclement, and organized resistance in the pockets was eliminated by early July.

Outcome

Germany claimed some 340,000–400,000 Soviet prisoners, along with thousands of tanks and guns destroyed or captured. The Western Front effectively ceased to exist; its commander, Dmitry Pavlov, was arrested and executed. The disaster opened the road toward Smolensk, though the delay imposed on the German timetable remained a subject of historical debate.
