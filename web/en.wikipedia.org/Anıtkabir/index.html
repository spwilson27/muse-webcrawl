Anıtkabir (literally, "memorial tomb") is the mausoleum complex of Mustafa Kemal Atatürk, founder and first President of the Republic of Turkey, located in Ankara. Designed by architects Emin Onat and Ahmet Orhan Arda—winners of a 1941 competition—the complex was built between 1944 and 1953, and Atatürk's remains were transferred there on 10 November 1953, the fifteenth anniversary of his death.

The complex

Anıtkabir combines elements inspired by ancient Anatolian civilisations (Hittite, Phrygian, Greek, Roman, Seljuk, and Ottoman motifs) in a monumental, rationalist style. Its parts include the Road of Lions, flanked by twelve pairs of Hittite-style lion statues; the Ceremonial Plaza; and the Hall of Honour, whose 42-metre-long colonnaded block houses Atatürk's symbolic sarcophagus—a 40-tonne monolith of red marble above the actual tomb chamber. Ten themed towers line the plaza, and reliefs depict scenes from the Turkish War of Independence.

The complex also contains the tomb of İsmet İnönü, Atatürk's successor as president, and the Atatürk and War of Independence Museum, which displays Atatürk's personal effects, the restored wagon and funeral carriage, and panoramic battle paintings. Anıtkabir is the site of official commemorations, especially on 10 November (Atatürk's death anniversary), 29 October (Republic Day), and 23 April and 30 August holidays, and visiting foreign heads of state customarily lay a wreath there.
