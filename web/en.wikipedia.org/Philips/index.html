Koninklijke Philips N.V., commonly shortened to Philips, is a Dutch multinational health technology company headquartered in Amsterdam. Founded in Eindhoven in 1891 by Gerard Philips and his father Frederik, it began as a manufacturer of light bulbs and grew into one of the world's largest electronics conglomerates before refocusing on healthcare technology in the twenty-first century.

History

Philips expanded from lamps into vacuum tubes and radios in the 1920s, and after the Second World War into a vast range of consumer electronics, appliances, and semiconductors. Its research laboratory, Philips NatLab, produced many notable inventions. The company co-developed the compact cassette (1963) and the Compact Disc with Sony (1982), and launched the Video 2000 and LaserDisc formats. Its electric shavers, sold under the Philishave and Norelco brands, became global products.

Refocus on health technology

From the 2000s, Philips divested its semiconductor business (which became NXP), its lighting division (spun off as Signify), its television and consumer electronics lines, and its domestic appliances business. The modern company concentrates on diagnostic imaging (MRI, CT, ultrasound), image-guided therapy, patient monitoring, and personal health products such as Sonicare toothbrushes and Avent baby-care goods.
