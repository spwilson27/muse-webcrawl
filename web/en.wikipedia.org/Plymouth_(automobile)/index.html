Plymouth was a brand of automobiles produced by Chrysler Corporation and DaimlerChrysler from 1928 to 2001. The brand was created by Walter Chrysler to compete in the low-priced field with Ford and Chevrolet, and it was named after Plymouth Rock and the Mayflower, with a ship used as its emblem.

[Image placeholder: Plymouth_(automobile).png — a Plymouth car]

History

The first Plymouth, the Model Q, debuted in 1928. Plymouth quickly became one of the top-selling American car brands, holding third place in US sales for much of the 1930s–1950s. Notable models included the Valiant compact (1960), the Barracuda fastback, the Road Runner and Duster muscle cars, and the Voyager minivan (1984), one of the first modern minivans.

Decline and discontinuation

From the 1970s onward Plymouth increasingly sold badge-engineered versions of Dodge and Chrysler models, losing its distinct identity. Sales declined, and after the DaimlerChrysler merger the brand was discontinued; the last Plymouth, a Neon, was built on June 28, 2001.
