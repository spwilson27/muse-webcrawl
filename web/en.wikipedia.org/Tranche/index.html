Tranche

In finance, a tranche (from the French for "slice") is a portion of a structured financial instrument or loan issue that is distinguished by risk, maturity or payment priority. A single offering may be divided into multiple tranches that share the same underlying assets but are repaid in a defined order.

Structured finance

In securitisations such as mortgage-backed securities and collateralised debt obligations (CDOs), cash flows from the asset pool are directed first to senior tranches, which carry the lowest risk and lowest yield, while junior or "equity" tranches absorb losses first in exchange for higher returns. Credit rating agencies rate tranches individually, so a single pool can yield AAA-rated senior slices alongside speculative junior slices.

Other uses

Loans and fund commitments are also disbursed in tranches — portions released at different times or when milestones are met — and governments issue bonds in tranches.
