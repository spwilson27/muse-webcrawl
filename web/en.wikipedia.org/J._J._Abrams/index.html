Jeffrey Jacob Abrams (born 27 June 1966) is an American filmmaker. He is best known for his work in the genres of action, drama, and science fiction. Abrams created or co-created the television series Felicity (1998–2002), Alias (2001–2006), Lost (2004–2010), and Fringe (2008–2013), and directed the films Mission: Impossible III (2006), Star Trek (2009), Super 8 (2011), Star Trek Into Darkness (2013), Star Wars: The Force Awakens (2015), and Star Wars: The Rise of Skywalker (2019).

Early life

Abrams was born in New York City and raised in Los Angeles, the son of television producers Gerald W. Abrams and Carol Ann Kelvin. He attended Palisades High School and Sarah Lawrence College. Fascinated by filmmaking from childhood, he made Super 8 films as a teenager; at 16 he composed music for the low-budget horror film Nightbeast (1982). While at college he co-wrote a film treatment that became his first produced screenplay, Taking Care of Business (1990).

Career

Early screenwriting

During the 1990s Abrams wrote or co-wrote the dramas Regarding Henry (1991) and Forever Young (1992), the comedy Gone Fishin' (1997), and the blockbuster Armageddon (1998). He founded his production company, Bad Robot, in 1999.

Television

Abrams achieved major success in television. Felicity, co-created with Matt Reeves, was followed by the spy drama Alias, starring Jennifer Garner. In 2004 Lost, co-created with Jeffrey Lieber and Damon Lindelof, became a global phenomenon; Abrams directed the pilot, one of the most expensive in television history at the time, and won an Emmy Award for his direction. He later co-created Fringe and produced numerous other series through Bad Robot, including Person of Interest and Westworld.

Film directing

Abrams made his feature directorial debut with Mission: Impossible III (2006). His reboot Star Trek (2009) revived the franchise for a new generation, and the original sci-fi film Super 8 (2011) paid homage to early Steven Spielberg. In 2015 he directed Star Wars: The Force Awakens, which grossed over $2 billion worldwide and relaunched the Star Wars saga; he returned to direct the concluding chapter, The Rise of Skywalker (2019). As a producer he has overseen the Cloverfield films, the Mission: Impossible sequels, and other Bad Robot projects.

Style and public image

Abrams's storytelling is associated with the "mystery box" concept — narratives built around concealed information that drives audience curiosity — which he described in a 2007 TED talk. His visual style frequently features lens flares, a trait often parodied. In 2019 Bad Robot signed a major overall deal with WarnerMedia.

Personal life

Abrams is married to Katie McGrath, a public relations executive; they have three children. He is known for charitable work in education and the arts and for political activism supporting Democratic candidates.
