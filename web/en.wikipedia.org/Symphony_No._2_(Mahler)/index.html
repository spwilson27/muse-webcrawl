Symphony No. 2 (Mahler)

Symphony No. 2
Composer: Gustav Mahler
Nickname: "Resurrection"
Composed: 1888–1894
Premiere: 13 December 1895, Berlin
Movements: 5; with soprano, alto, chorus, organ

The Symphony No. 2 in C minor by Gustav Mahler, known as the Resurrection Symphony, was written between 1888 and 1894 and first performed complete in Berlin in 1895 under the composer's direction. One of the most popular and successful works of Mahler's lifetime, it traces a journey from a funeral rite through reflection to a vision of resurrection and eternal life.

Structure

The symphony has five movements. A monumental opening Allegro maestoso functions as a funeral rite (Totenfeier); a gentle Andante evokes memories of the deceased's life; a scherzo derived from Mahler's song "Des Antonius von Padua Fischpredigt" depicts life's meaningless bustle. The alto song "Urlicht" ("Primeval Light") leads without pause into the vast finale, which sets verses from Klopstock's ode "Die Auferstehung" ("The Resurrection") together with Mahler's own words for soprano, alto, mixed chorus, organ, and offstage brass.

Reception

Although early critics were divided by its scale — the work typically lasts 80 to 90 minutes — the Second Symphony established Mahler's reputation as a symphonist and remains among the most frequently performed of his works.
