Hayreddin Barbarossa

Hayreddin Barbarossa (c. 1466/1478 – 4 July 1546), born Khizr Reis, was an Ottoman corsair and admiral who secured Ottoman dominance over the Mediterranean in the 16th century. Nicknamed Barbarossa ("Redbeard" in Italian) — a name he inherited from his elder brother Oruç Reis — he became governor of Algiers, then Kapudan Pasha (grand admiral) of the Ottoman fleet under Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent.

Early life and the corsair years

Khizr was born on Lesbos, then Ottoman territory, to a former Janissary of Albanian or Turkish origin and a Greek mother. With his brothers Oruç and Ilyas he took up privateering in the Aegean and western Mediterranean. The brothers established themselves in North Africa, capturing Jijel and then Algiers in 1516. After Oruç was killed by Spanish forces at Tlemcen in 1518, Khizr offered Algiers to the Ottoman sultan in exchange for protection and was appointed beylerbey.

Rise in Ottoman service

Barbarossa raided Christian shipping and coasts throughout the 1520s, rescued thousands of Moriscos fleeing Spain, and captured the Peñón of Algiers in 1529. Recalled to Constantinople in 1532, he was made Kapudan Pasha in 1534 and rebuilt the imperial fleet. He conquered Tunis in 1534 (lost to Charles V's expedition the following year), sacked Italian ports, and in 1538 won his most famous victory at the Battle of Preveza, defeating a Holy League fleet under Andrea Doria and giving the Ottomans naval supremacy for a generation.

Later campaigns

In 1543 Barbarossa sailed to Marseille to assist France, then allied with the Ottomans against the Habsburgs, wintering at Toulon before capturing Elba, raiding the Italian coast and taking Lipari. He retired in 1545 to his palace at Beşiktaş in Constantinople, where he died in 1546. His türbe (tomb) there, designed by Sinan, remains a shrine for the Turkish Navy, and his memoirs, the Gazavat-ı Hayreddin Paşa, were dictated to Seyyid Muradi.

Legacy

Barbarossa is remembered as one of history's great naval commanders; submarines and districts in Turkey bear his name, and Turkish naval vessels still salute his tomb when passing through the Bosphorus.
