Beauty and the Beast (1991 film)

Beauty and the Beast is a 1991 American animated musical romantic fantasy film produced by Walt Disney Feature Animation and released by Walt Disney Pictures. The 30th Disney animated feature film, it is based on the French fairy tale by Jeanne-Marie Leprince de Beaumont and was directed by Gary Trousdale and Kirk Wise, with songs by Alan Menken and Howard Ashman.

Plot

A selfish prince is cursed by an enchantress to live as a Beast until he learns to love and be loved in return. Years later, Belle, a bookish young woman from a nearby village, offers herself as a prisoner in exchange for her father Maurice, whom the Beast imprisoned for trespassing. Aided by the castle's enchanted servants—Lumière the candelabra, Cogsworth the clock, and Mrs. Potts the teapot—Belle gradually softens the Beast's temper. When she leaves to save her father from the villainous hunter Gaston, the Beast lets her go; Gaston leads a mob to kill the Beast, but Belle returns in time to confess her love as the last petal of the enchanted rose falls, breaking the spell.

Production and music

The film was a landmark of the Disney Renaissance, structured like a Broadway musical. Its score won the Academy Award for Best Original Score, and the title song "Beauty and the Beast" won Best Original Song; "Be Our Guest" and "Belle" were also nominated. Ashman, who died of AIDS-related complications before the film's release, is honored in a dedication in the credits.

Reception and legacy

The film grossed over $424 million worldwide and was the first animated film nominated for the Academy Award for Best Picture. It was adapted into a Broadway musical (1994) and a live-action remake (2017), was selected for the U.S. National Film Registry in 2002, and is widely regarded as one of the greatest animated films ever made.
