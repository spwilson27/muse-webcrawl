Postfix (software)

Postfix

Developer: Wietse Venema

Initial release: 1998

Written in: C

Type: Mail transfer agent

Licence: IBM Public License / Eclipse Public License

Postfix is a free and open-source mail transfer agent (MTA) that routes and delivers electronic mail. It was written by Wietse Venema at IBM's Thomas J. Watson Research Center and first released in 1998 as an alternative to the widely used Sendmail program, with a design emphasis on security, speed and ease of administration.

Unlike monolithic mail servers, Postfix is built from a collection of small, cooperating daemon programs, each running with the least privilege necessary. Message reception, queue management, delivery and address rewriting are handled by separate components coordinated by a master daemon.

History

Venema began the project in the late 1990s, motivated by the poor security record of Sendmail. Early versions circulated under the names VMailer and IBM Secure Mailer before the software was renamed Postfix. It became the default MTA on several operating systems, including macOS Server and many Linux distributions, and remains one of the most widely deployed mail servers on the Internet.

Features

Postfix supports SMTP and LMTP delivery, IPv6, TLS encryption, SASL authentication, virtual domains, content filtering through the Milter protocol, and extensive access controls for fighting spam. Its configuration files are deliberately human-readable, in contrast to Sendmail's notoriously complex syntax.

Architecture

Incoming mail is accepted by the smtpd daemon, cleaned up by the cleanup daemon, and placed in queues managed by the queue manager (qmgr). Local delivery is performed by the local daemon, remote delivery by the smtp client daemon, and utilities such as postqueue and postsuper administer the queues.
