Whole grain

A whole grain is a grain of any cereal and pseudocereal that contains the endosperm, germ, and bran, in contrast to refined grains, which retain only the endosperm.

Definition

As part of a general healthy diet, consumption of whole grains is associated with lower risk of several diseases. Whole grains are a source of carbohydrates, multiple nutrients and dietary fiber.

Anatomy of a grain

The bran is the outer protective skin, rich in fiber and B vitamins. The germ is the embryo, containing oils, vitamin E and minerals. The endosperm, the largest portion, is mostly starch and provides energy for the germinating seed.

Examples

Common whole grains include whole wheat, oats, brown rice, barley, rye, millet, quinoa, buckwheat, sorghum and maize (corn) when consumed whole, as in popcorn or wholemeal corn flour.

Health effects

Epidemiological studies link regular whole grain intake with reduced incidence of type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease and colorectal cancer, generally attributed to fiber, micronutrients and phytochemicals removed during refining.
