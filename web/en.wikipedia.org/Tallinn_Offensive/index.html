Tallinn Offensive

The Tallinn Offensive (Russian: Таллинская наступательная операция) was a strategic offensive operation of the Soviet Red Army's Leningrad Front against the German Army Detachment Narwa on the Eastern Front of the Second World War, fought from 17 to 26 September 1944. It formed part of the wider Baltic Offensive and resulted in the Soviet capture of mainland Estonia and its capital, Tallinn.

Background

Following the Soviet breakthrough at Narva and the fighting around the Tannenberg Line in the summer of 1944, German forces in Estonia prepared to withdraw. Hitler authorised the evacuation of Estonia (Operation Aster) on 16 September, and German and Estonian units began embarking from Tallinn and other ports toward Germany and Latvia.

The offensive

The Leningrad Front under Marshal Leonid Govorov attacked with the 2nd Shock Army striking from the Tartu area toward Rakvere and the 8th Army advancing along the coast from Narva. The Estonian Rifle Corps participated in the advance. Tallinn was taken on 22 September 1944 with relatively light fighting in the city itself, as most German forces had already evacuated by sea. Soviet forces reached the western coast within days, though German units held the Moonsund Islands until late November.

Aftermath

The offensive completed the Soviet reoccupation of mainland Estonia, which was reincorporated into the Soviet Union as the Estonian SSR. The evacuation by sea carried tens of thousands of soldiers and Estonian refugees westward; several transport ships were sunk with heavy loss of life. The date of Tallinn's capture remains contested in Estonian memory between liberation and renewed occupation.
