Lucilia sericata

Lucilia sericata
Common name: common green bottle fly
Family: Calliphoridae (blow flies)
Order: Diptera

The common green bottle fly (Lucilia sericata) is a blow fly found in most areas of the world, most numerous in the temperate Northern Hemisphere. The adult is a striking metallic blue-green or golden-green fly, 10–14 mm long, feeding on nectar, pollen and carrion odours, and an important pollinator as well as scavenger.

Life cycle and importance

Females lay eggs in carrion, faeces, wounds and neglected meat; the larvae (maggots) feed on dead tissue. Because the developmental rate of the larvae depends on temperature, L. sericata is a key species in forensic entomology for estimating the minimum post-mortem interval of a corpse. Conversely, in sheep farming the species is a serious pest: "blow fly strike" (myiasis) occurs when eggs laid in soiled wool hatch and larvae invade living skin.

Medical use

Sterile larvae of L. sericata are used in maggot therapy (biosurgery): applied to chronic wounds such as diabetic and venous ulcers, they debride necrotic tissue, disinfect the wound and promote healing. The species' secretions contain antimicrobial compounds and enzymes studied for wound care.
