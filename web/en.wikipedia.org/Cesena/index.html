Cesena

Cesena is a city and comune in the Emilia-Romagna region of northern Italy, south of Ravenna and west of Rimini, on the Savio River. It lies at the foot of the Apennines and has a population of around 97,000.

History

Originally an Umbrian and then Etruscan town, Cesena was known in Roman times as Caesena and lay on the Via Emilia. In the Middle Ages it was contested between the Papal States, the Holy Roman Empire and local lords. Under the Malatesta family in the 14th and 15th centuries the city flourished; Domenico Malatesta Novello founded the Biblioteca Malatestiana in 1452, the first public library in Europe, whose original reading room survives intact and is inscribed in UNESCO's Memory of the World register.

Main sights

Landmarks include the Rocca Malatestiana fortress, Cesena Cathedral, the elegant Piazza del Popolo with its Masini fountain, and the Abbey of Santa Maria del Monte above the city. Cesena is also one of the birthplaces of the piadina flatbread.
