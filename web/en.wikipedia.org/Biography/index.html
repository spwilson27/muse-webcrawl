Biography

GenreNonfiction / literary form
SubjectThe life of an individual person
Related formsAutobiography, memoir, hagiography
Earliest examplesAncient Egyptian tomb inscriptions; Greek and Roman lives

A biography, or simply bio, is a detailed description of a person's life. It involves more than just basic facts like education, work, relationships, and death; it portrays a person's experience of these life events. Unlike a profile or curriculum vitae (résumé), a biography presents a subject's life story, highlighting various aspects of their life, including intimate details of experience, and may include an analysis of the subject's personality.


Biographies range from ancient royal inscriptions to modern multi-volume scholarly works.

Biographical works are usually nonfiction, but fiction can also be used to portray a person's life. One in-depth form of biographical coverage is called legacy writing. Together in all these forms, biography constitutes a genre in literature in the form of biography and autobiography and forms a subgenre in the form of history.

History

Initially, biographical writings were regarded merely as a subsection of history, dealing with one particular individual of historical importance. The independent genre of biography as distinct from general history writing began to emerge in the 18th century and reached its contemporary form at the turn of the 20th century.

Ancient biography

Some of the earliest biographical writing survives in ancient Egyptian tomb inscriptions and Mesopotamian royal annals. In classical antiquity, the Greek writer Xenophon composed a life of Cyrus the Great, and Plutarch's Parallel Lives paired eminent Greeks and Romans for moral comparison. Suetonius's Lives of the Twelve Caesars set a model for anecdotal imperial biography, while Tacitus wrote a laudatory life of his father-in-law Agricola. In China, Sima Qian's Records of the Grand Historian (c. 91 BC) included extensive biographies that shaped the dynastic-history tradition for two millennia.

Medieval and Renaissance biography

During the Middle Ages, biography was dominated by hagiography — lives of saints written to inspire devotion, such as the Golden Legend. Secular biography revived in the Renaissance with works such as Giorgio Vasari's Lives of the Artists (1550), which chronicled painters, sculptors, and architects of the Italian Renaissance.

Modern biography

James Boswell's Life of Samuel Johnson (1791) is widely considered a landmark of the modern genre, combining exhaustive research with vivid scene-setting. The 19th century saw multi-volume "life and letters" works, while Lytton Strachey's Eminent Victorians (1918) revolutionised the form with brevity and psychological scepticism. In the 20th and 21st centuries, biography expanded to include oral history, documentary film, and digital formats.

Types of biography

  • Autobiography — written by the subject, sometimes with a collaborator or ghostwriter.
  • Memoir — focuses on selected episodes or themes rather than a whole life.
  • Hagiography — a life of a saint or revered figure, written from reverence.
  • Collective biography (prosopography) — studies a group through the pattern of its members' lives.
  • Biographical fiction and film — dramatised treatments, including the biopic.

Biographical research

Biographers draw on letters, diaries, interviews, archives, and the testimony of contemporaries. Authorised biographies are written with the subject's or estate's permission and access to private papers, whereas unauthorised biographies proceed without it. Modern scholarly biography is expected to cite sources, weigh conflicting evidence, and situate the subject in historical context.

Awards

Several major literary prizes recognise biography, including the Pulitzer Prize for Biography, the Whitbread/Costa Biography Award, and the National Book Award for nonfiction. Biographical dictionaries — from the Dictionary of National Biography to the modern Oxford Dictionary of National Biography — collect short lives of notable figures of a nation or field.
