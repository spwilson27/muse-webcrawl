Environmental justice

Environmental justice is the fair treatment and meaningful involvement of all people, regardless of race, color, national origin, or income, with respect to the development, implementation, and enforcement of environmental laws, regulations, and policies. The movement emerged in the United States in the 1980s, driven by evidence that hazardous waste facilities, polluting industries, and other environmental burdens were disproportionately sited in low-income communities and communities of color.

History

A pivotal moment came in 1982, when residents of Warren County, North Carolina — a predominantly African American community — protested the dumping of soil contaminated with polychlorinated biphenyls (PCBs) in a local landfill. Although the protests did not stop the landfill, they drew national attention and are widely regarded as the birth of the environmental justice movement. A 1987 report by the United Church of Christ, Toxic Wastes and Race in the United States, found that race was the strongest predictor of where hazardous waste facilities were located.

In 1994, President Bill Clinton signed Executive Order 12898, directing federal agencies to identify and address disproportionately high adverse human health or environmental effects of their programs on minority and low-income populations.

Concepts

Environmental justice encompasses distributive justice (fair sharing of environmental benefits and burdens), procedural justice (meaningful participation in decision-making), and recognition justice (acknowledging the histories and identities of affected communities). Related concepts include environmental racism and the idea of "sacrifice zones" — areas permanently impaired by heavy pollution.

Global scope

While the term originated in the United States, analogous movements exist worldwide, addressing issues such as the export of electronic waste, mining impacts on Indigenous peoples, and unequal exposure to the effects of climate change. The principle that those least responsible for climate change often suffer its worst effects is sometimes called climate justice.
