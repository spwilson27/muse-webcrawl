Olympia (1938 film)

Directed byLeni Riefenstahl
Released1938
Subject1936 Summer Olympics, Berlin
PartsFest der Völker (Festival of the Nations); Fest der Schönheit (Festival of Beauty)

Olympia is a 1938 German documentary film written, directed, and produced by Leni Riefenstahl, documenting the 1936 Summer Olympic Games in Berlin. Released in two parts — Festival of the Nations and Festival of Beauty — it is widely regarded as a landmark in the history of sports filmmaking and cinematography, while remaining controversial for its origins as a prestige production of Nazi Germany.

Production and technique

Riefenstahl and her large camera crews pioneered or popularized numerous techniques now standard in sports coverage: slow motion, underwater cameras, tracking shots alongside runners, cameras mounted on rails and towers, telephoto lenses, and dramatic low angles. A famous sequence follows the marathon; another, in the diving section, dissolves athletes into near-abstract silhouettes against the sky. The film also prominently features African-American athlete Jesse Owens, whose victories contradicted Nazi racial ideology.

Reception and legacy

Olympia won awards at the Venice Film Festival and was praised internationally for its artistry. It has frequently appeared on lists of the greatest documentaries ever made, though discussion of it is inseparable from its propaganda context alongside Riefenstahl's Triumph of the Will. The International Olympic Commission later recognized the film's importance to Olympic imagery.
