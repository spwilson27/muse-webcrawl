Super Bowl halftime show

The Super Bowl halftime show is a performance during the halftime break of the Super Bowl, the championship game of the National Football League (NFL). What began as marching-band entertainment has become one of the most-watched musical events in the world, featuring many of the biggest names in popular music.

History

Early Super Bowl halftimes featured college marching bands and drill teams. During the 1990s, the shows evolved into pop spectacles; Michael Jackson's 1993 performance at Super Bowl XXVII is often credited with transforming the halftime into a headline pop event. Subsequent headliners have included Prince, Madonna, Beyoncé, Bruno Mars, Lady Gaga, Shakira and Jennifer Lopez, Dr. Dre and guests, Rihanna, Usher, and Kendrick Lamar.

Notable incidents

The 2004 show at Super Bowl XXXVIII became infamous for the "wardrobe malfunction" during Janet Jackson and Justin Timberlake's performance, which prompted fines, litigation, and stricter broadcast-delay policies. Prince's 2007 rain-soaked performance is frequently ranked the greatest halftime show ever.

Audience and production

Halftime performances run roughly 12–15 minutes, with performers traditionally not paid an appearance fee by the NFL, which covers production costs. The exposure routinely drives large increases in the headliner's music sales and streaming.
