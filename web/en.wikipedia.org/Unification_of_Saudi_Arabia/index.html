The unification of Saudi Arabia was a military and political campaign, led by Abdulaziz Al Saud (Ibn Saud) and the House of Saud, that resulted in the creation of the modern Kingdom of Saudi Arabia. The campaign began in January 1902 when Ibn Saud, exiled in Kuwait, recaptured Riyadh, the ancestral home of his family. Over the following three decades he conquered most of the Arabian Peninsula, defeaching the rival Rashidi dynasty of Ha'il and the Hashemite rulers of the Hejaz.

Background

Two earlier Saudi states had risen and fallen: the Emirate of Diriyah (the first Saudi state, 1744–1818), destroyed by Ottoman-Egyptian forces, and the Emirate of Nejd (the second Saudi state, 1824–1891), which collapsed amid internal conflict and Rashidi expansion, driving the Saud family into exile in Kuwait.

Campaigns

After retaking Riyadh in 1902, Ibn Saud conquered al-Hasa from the Ottomans in 1913, gaining a Gulf coastline. During the First World War he concluded the Treaty of Darin (1915) with Britain, making his territories a British protectorate. He completed the conquest of Jabal Shammar and the Rashidis in 1921. His forces, including the Ikhwan tribal levies, then turned on the Hejaz: Taif and Mecca fell in 1924, and Medina and Jeddah in 1925, ending Hashemite rule and four centuries of Ottoman suzerainty over the holy cities. In 1927 the Treaty of Jeddah recognised Ibn Saud's sovereignty, and his dominions were styled the Kingdom of Hejaz and Nejd.

Proclamation of the Kingdom

After suppressing the Ikhwan Revolt (1927–1930), which challenged his authority and his management of relations with Britain, Ibn Saud unified his realms. On 23 September 1932 a royal decree proclaimed the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, uniting the Hejaz and Nejd. The date is now celebrated as Saudi National Day. Abdulaziz reigned until his death in 1953.
