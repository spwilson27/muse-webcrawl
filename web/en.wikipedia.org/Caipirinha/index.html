Caipirinha

TypeCocktail
Primary alcoholCachaça
ServedOn the rocks
IngredientsCachaça, lime, sugar, ice

The caipirinha is Brazil's national cocktail, made with cachaça, sugar and lime. It is prepared by muddling lime wedges with sugar to release the juice and oils, then adding cachaça and ice. The International Bartenders Association includes it among its official cocktails in the "contemporary classics" category.

History

The drink's origins are debated. One account traces it to 19th-century rural São Paulo state, where mixtures of cachaça, lime, garlic and honey were used against colds; another popular version links it to 1918, when a medicinal recipe was adapted into a refreshment during the Spanish flu era, and sailors and farmers in the interior popularized the name, a diminutive referring to country folk (caipiras).

Variations

Substituting vodka produces the caipiroska, white rum gives the caipirissima, and sake the caipisake; other fruits—passion fruit, strawberry, kiwi—are often muddled in place of or with the lime. By Brazilian law and international recognition, a true caipirinha is made only with cachaça, lime, sugar and ice.
