Castillo San Cristóbal

Castillo San Cristóbal
Location: San Juan, Puerto Rico
Built: 1634–1783
Part of: San Juan National Historic Site
Status: UNESCO World Heritage Site (1983)

Castillo San Cristóbal is a Spanish fort in San Juan, Puerto Rico. Built by Spain over a period of about 150 years to protect the city against land-based attacks, it was the largest fortification constructed by the Spanish in the New World, covering some 27 acres.

History

Construction began in 1634 and the fort was substantially completed by 1783. It formed the landward defence of San Juan together with the seaward fortress of Castillo San Felipe del Morro. In 1898, during the Spanish–American War, the fort's guns fired on the United States fleet, delivering one of the first shots of the conflict in Puerto Rico. It remained a military installation through the Second World War.

Present day

The fort is administered by the U.S. National Park Service as part of the San Juan National Historic Site, and was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1983. Notable features include the Garita del Diablo ("Devil's Sentry Box"), one of the oldest sentry boxes, and a network of tunnels and dry moats built in defence in depth.
