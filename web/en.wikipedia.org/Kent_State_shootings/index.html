The Kent State shootings (also known as the May 4 massacre or the Kent State massacre) were the killings of four and wounding of nine unarmed college students by the Ohio National Guard on May 4, 1970, at Kent State University in Kent, Ohio, during a mass protest against the bombing of Cambodia by United States military forces.

Background

The shootings took place against the backdrop of the Vietnam War and President Richard Nixon's announcement on April 30, 1970, that the war would be expanded into Cambodia. Protests erupted on campuses across the United States. At Kent State, demonstrations over several days included the burning of the campus ROTC building, prompting the governor to dispatch the National Guard.

The shootings

On Monday, May 4, about 2,000 people gathered on the university Commons despite an attempt to ban the rally. Guardsmen ordered the crowd to disperse and fired tear gas. Shortly after noon, 29 of the 77 guardsmen present fired their weapons, discharging around 67 rounds over 13 seconds. Four students were killed: Allison Krause, Jeffrey Miller, Sandra Scheuer, and William Knox Schroeder. The photograph of Mary Ann Vecchio kneeling over Jeffrey Miller's body, taken by John Filo, became an iconic image of the era.

Aftermath

The shootings triggered a nationwide student strike that forced hundreds of universities to close. A presidential commission later concluded that the gunfire was "unnecessary, unwarranted, and inexcusable." No guardsman was convicted of a crime. The event remains a landmark in American public memory and inspired songs such as Crosby, Stills, Nash & Young's "Ohio."
