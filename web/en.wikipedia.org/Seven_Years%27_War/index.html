Seven Years' War

Date17 May 1756 – 15 February 1763
LocationEurope, North America, South Asia, Africa, Philippines
ResultAnglo-Prussian victory; Treaty of Paris (1763)

The Seven Years' War (1756–1763) was a global conflict between Great Britain and Prussia on one side and France, Austria, Russia, Spain, and their allies on the other. Fought on five continents, it has been called the first world war, and it redrew the colonial map of the world.

Background

The war grew out of the unresolved rivalry between Britain and France in North America (where fighting began in 1754 as the French and Indian War) and the "Diplomatic Revolution" that aligned France with its old enemy Austria against Prussia and Britain.

Course

Prussia under Frederick the Great survived against a coalition in Europe, while Britain's victories — at Plassey in India (1757), Quebec (1759), and Quiberon Bay (1759) — destroyed French colonial power. Spain's late entry on France's side cost it Florida.

Aftermath

The Treaty of Paris (1763) ceded Canada and French India to Britain, Louisiana to Spain, and left Britain the dominant colonial power. The huge war debt contributed to the taxation disputes that led to the American Revolution.
