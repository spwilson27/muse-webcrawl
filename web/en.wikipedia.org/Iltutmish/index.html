Iltutmish

Reign: 1211–1236
Predecessor: Aram Shah
Successor: Ruknuddin Firuz (briefly), then Razia Sultana
Died: 30 April 1236, Delhi

Shams ud-Din Iltutmish (died 30 April 1236) was the third ruler of the Delhi Sultanate and the first to rule from Delhi itself. A former Turkic slave (mamluk) of Qutb ud-Din Aibak, founder of the Mamluk dynasty, Iltutmish is regarded as the true consolidator of Muslim rule in northern India.

Reign

Iltutmish defeated rival claimants and regional rulers, brought Bengal and Bihar under Delhi's control, and dealt successfully with the threat of the Mongols under Genghis Khan by refusing asylum to the fleeing Khwarazmian prince Jalal ad-Din in 1221, thereby sparing India invasion. In 1229 he received formal investiture from the Abbasid Caliph in Baghdad, legitimising his rule.

Administration and legacy

He organised the iqta system of land assignments, issued a standard silver coinage (the tanka), completed the Qutb Minar in Delhi, and shifted the capital firmly to Delhi. Breaking with custom, he nominated his daughter Razia as his heir; she ruled as Razia Sultana after a short interval. His tomb in the Qutb complex in Delhi is among the earliest surviving royal tombs of Islamic India.
