Otaku

Otaku (Japanese: おたく or オタク) is a Japanese term for people with consuming interests, particularly in anime, manga, video games and computers. The word derives from a formal second-person pronoun meaning "your house" and came to be applied in the 1980s to obsessive fans, partly through its use as an awkward form of address among hobbyists themselves.

In modern Japanese slang, an otaku is any person devoted to a hobby with deep, detailed knowledge — there are train otaku, military otaku, idol otaku and many other varieties. Outside Japan, the word is most often used specifically for fans of Japanese popular culture, especially anime and manga, and often carries a more neutral or even positive connotation than in its country of origin.

History and perception

The term was popularised in its subcultural sense by the essayist Akio Nakamori in a 1983 column in the magazine Manga Burikko, where he applied it mockingly to obsessive fans at events such as Comiket. Public perception in Japan darkened sharply after 1989, when the serial murderer Tsutomu Miyazaki was dubbed the "Otaku Murderer" by the media, which dwelt on his large collection of anime and horror videos; the association fuelled a moral panic about the subculture in the 1990s.

From the 2000s onward, the image of otaku culture softened. The 2004 hit film and novel Densha Otoko ("Train Man") portrayed an otaku protagonist sympathetically, the Tokyo district of Akihabara became a celebrated centre of otaku commerce, and the Japanese government and businesses increasingly treated otaku culture as an exportable asset — the related concept of moe and the global anime market made the label more acceptable, and many fans adopted it as a badge of identity.

Subculture and economy

Otaku culture is closely associated with dōjinshi (self-published works), cosplay, figurine collecting, maid cafés, idol fandom, and large conventions such as Comiket in Tokyo, the world's largest dōjinshi fair. Surveys by Japanese research institutes in the 2010s estimated that roughly a fifth of Japanese teenagers and a substantial share of adults identified with some form of otaku interest, and the market for otaku-oriented goods and services was valued at hundreds of billions of yen.

In the West

In English, "otaku" generally denotes a devoted fan of anime and manga, roughly equivalent to "anime geek". Some Western fans use it proudly, while others prefer "anime fan" because of the term's lingering negative associations in Japan. Related Western slang such as "weeaboo" (later shortened to "weeb") describes non-Japanese people perceived as excessively obsessed with Japanese culture.
