Sidewise Award

The Sidewise Award for Alternate History was established in 1995 to recognize excellence in alternate history fiction. The award takes its name from Murray Leinster's 1934 short story "Sidewise in Time", in which a strange storm causes portions of Earth to swap places with their analogs from other timelines.

The award is generally presented annually in two categories: Best Long Form (works longer than 60,000 words, including novels and complete series) and Best Short Form (works shorter than 60,000 words, including short stories, novelettes, and poems). A Special Achievement Award is occasionally granted for earlier works or lifetime contributions to the genre.

History

The award was founded by Evelyn C. Leeper, Robert J. Schmunk, and Steven H Silver, who also served as its first judges. Winners have included works by Stephen Baxter, Ken MacLeod, Harry Turtledove, Ian R. MacLeod, Lois McMaster Bujold, and Eric Flint.
