Free church

A free church is a Christian denomination or congregation that is intrinsically separate from government, as distinct from a state church, the established church of a nation. Free churches govern themselves without state control, are financed by the voluntary contributions of their members rather than by taxation, and typically emphasize the voluntary, personal nature of religious faith and church membership.

History

The free church tradition has roots in the Radical Reformation — Anabaptist and congregationalist movements that rejected state-enforced religion — and in the dissenting traditions of England, where Baptists, Congregationalists, Methodists, and Quakers stood outside the established Church of England and were often called "Free Churches" or Nonconformists. In Scotland, the Disruption of 1843 produced the Free Church of Scotland, whose members left the Church of Scotland over the issue of state interference in church affairs.

Free churches flourished in the United States, where disestablishment was constitutional, and in Scandinavia, where free church movements (such as the Mission Covenant Church of Sweden) arose in reaction against the Lutheran state churches in the 19th century. In the United Kingdom, the Free Churches Group today represents denominations including Methodists, Baptists, the United Reformed Church, and the Salvation Army in ecumenical and public affairs.

Characteristics

Free churches commonly affirm the separation of church and state, believer's baptism or voluntary membership, congregational or synodal self-government, and freedom of conscience in interpretation of scripture. They contrast with "folk churches" or national churches in which membership follows citizenship or infant baptism within a state-recognized institution.
