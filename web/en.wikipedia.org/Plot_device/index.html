Plot device

Objects and techniques used to move a story's plot forward.

A plot device or plot mechanism is any technique in a narrative used to move the plot forward. Plot devices include objects that characters seek or use, events that change a character's situation, narrative structures such as flashbacks, and conventions such as the cliffhanger. A well-written plot device is integrated into the story's internal logic; devices that resolve the plot too conveniently, or appear without preparation, are often criticised as contrived.

Common devices

Among the most familiar is the MacGuffin, an object whose importance motivates the characters while its precise nature is secondary — the stolen plans, the priceless jewel, the briefcase. The deus ex machina, from classical theatre, introduces an unexpected power or event that resolves a seemingly unsolvable problem and is generally considered a mark of weak plotting. The red herring misleads the audience with a false clue; foreshadowing plants hints of future events; Chekhov's gun is the principle that elements introduced in a story should pay off. Other devices include the plot twist, the cliffhanger, the in medias res opening, the quest, the love triangle, mistaken identity, amnesia, and the ticking clock, which imposes a deadline to raise tension.

Assessment

Writers and critics distinguish between devices as neutral tools and their clumsy use: the same twist may be praised as satisfying when planted fairly and condemned when arbitrary. Aristotle already criticised the deus ex machina in the Poetics, arguing that resolution should arise from the plot itself. Modern screenwriting manuals catalog devices extensively and treat their recognition as part of a storyteller's craft.
