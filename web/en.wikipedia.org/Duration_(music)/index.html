Duration (music)

In music, duration is the length of time a pitch, note or sound lasts. Along with pitch, timbre and loudness, it is considered one of the basic elements of a musical sound. The patterning of durations creates rhythm and metre.

Notation

In Western staff notation, durations are indicated by note values — whole notes, half notes, quarter notes, eighth notes and their subdivisions — whose lengths are relative to the beat specified by the tempo and time signature. Dots extend a note by half its value, ties join durations, and tuplets divide beats into unusual groupings such as triplets.

Rhythm and perception

Perceived duration differs from notated duration: articulation, silence between notes and tempo all affect how long a sound seems to last. In many non-Western traditions, including West African drumming and Indian classical music, complex cyclical durational patterns form the organising framework of performance.

Twentieth-century developments

Serialist composers such as Olivier Messiaen and the Darmstadt school treated duration as a parameter that could be organised by series, just like pitch. In electroacoustic music and sound design, duration can be stretched or compressed independently of pitch through time-stretching algorithms.
