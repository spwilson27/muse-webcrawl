Chicago Water Tower

The Chicago Water Tower, built in 1869 to house a standpipe for the city's pumping station, is one of the few buildings to survive the Great Chicago Fire of 1871 and became a symbol of the city's recovery.

Designed by William W. Boyington in a castellated Gothic style of yellow limestone, it stands on Michigan Avenue and now houses a gallery. The adjacent Pumping Station still operates.
