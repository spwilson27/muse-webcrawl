Educational psychology

Educational psychology is the branch of psychology concerned with the scientific study of human learning. It examines how people learn and retain knowledge, particularly in educational settings, and applies the findings to instructional design, classroom management, assessment, and support for learners with special needs.

Applying learning science in the classroom

Perspectives on learning

The field draws on several major theories. Behaviourism (Thorndike, Skinner) explains learning through conditioning and reinforcement. Cognitivism focuses on mental processes such as memory and problem solving; Jean Piaget described stages of cognitive development, while Lev Vygotsky emphasised social interaction and the zone of proximal development. Constructivist and humanistic approaches stress active construction of meaning and learner motivation. Later frameworks include information-processing theory, self-regulated learning, and Albert Bandura's social cognitive theory.

Key topics

Educational psychologists study individual differences in intelligence and development, motivation — including intrinsic and extrinsic motivation and Carol Dweck's work on growth mindsets — memory and forgetting, effective study strategies such as retrieval practice and spaced repetition, and the design and interpretation of educational assessment from classroom quizzes to standardised testing.

History

William James's Talks to Teachers (1899) and Edward Thorndike's experimental studies in the early 20th century are conventionally taken to found the discipline. John Dewey, B. F. Skinner, and Benjamin Bloom — whose taxonomy of educational objectives remains widely used — shaped its development during the 20th century.

Practice

Practising educational psychologists work in schools and local authorities assessing learning difficulties, advising teachers, and designing interventions, as well as in universities conducting research and training teachers.
