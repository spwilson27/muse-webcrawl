Wild swimming

[Image: Wild swimming]

Wild swimming is the practice of swimming outdoors in natural bodies of water such as rivers, lakes, lochs, ponds and the sea, rather than in purpose-built swimming pools. The term became popular in the United Kingdom in the 2000s, popularised by writers such as Roger Deakin, whose 1999 book Waterlog chronicled a swimming journey through Britain.

History

Outdoor swimming was the norm before the spread of indoor pools in the 20th century, and bathing clubs, lidos and sea-bathing resorts flourished in the 19th and early 20th centuries. The modern wild swimming movement combines recreation, nature appreciation and advocacy for access to inland waterways and clean water.

Safety and advocacy

Wild swimming carries risks including cold water shock, currents, and water quality issues; safety guidance recommends acclimatisation, swimming with others, and checking conditions. Organisations such as the Outdoor Swimming Society (founded 2006) promote the activity, and campaigners in England have pressed for a "right to swim" similar to Scotland's statutory access rights.
