Bruno S. Frey (born 4 May 1941 in Basel) is a Swiss economist and a pioneer of the application of economics to fields beyond the market, including politics, art and happiness. He was a professor of economics at the University of Zurich for many years and later a permanent visiting professor at the University of Basel.

Work

Frey is known for research on the economics of happiness — notably Happiness: A Revolution in Economics (2008) — arguing that life satisfaction can be measured and studied systematically and that income is far from its only determinant. With psychologists and collaborators, he developed the theory of motivation crowding: external interventions such as monetary rewards or commands can undermine ("crowd out") intrinsic motivation, a finding influential in behavioural economics and management. He has also written extensively on political economy and direct democracy, the economics of art and culture, prizes and awards as incentives, and — with co-authors — proposals for dealing with terrorism and for a functional, overlapping system of competing political jurisdictions in Europe (FOCJ).

Controversy and later career

In 2011 Frey faced criticism for publishing similar work in multiple journals and for briefly holding affiliations at institutions including the University of Warwick; an investigation at Zurich found no academic misconduct regarding the duplicate publications. He continues to publish on happiness, awards and economics through Zurich-based research centres he helped found.
