Mariel boatlift

The Mariel boatlift (Spanish: éxodo del Mariel) was a mass emigration of Cubans who left Cuba's port of Mariel for the United States between 15 April and 31 October 1980. Approximately 125,000 Cubans, along with some 25,000 Haitians, reached Florida during the episode.

Background

The exodus followed a crisis at the Peruvian embassy in Havana, where thousands of Cubans sought asylum after a bus crashed through the embassy gates in April 1980. In response, Fidel Castro announced that anyone wishing to leave could do so through the port of Mariel. Cuban exiles in Florida organised a flotilla of private boats to ferry relatives across the Straits of Florida.

The exodus

Hundreds of boats carried refugees to Key West, overwhelming US reception facilities. President Jimmy Carter initially welcomed the Cubans, but the administration grew critical as it emerged that Cuba had included in the outflow people released from prisons and mental health institutions — though these formed only a small minority of the "Marielitos", most of whom were working people seeking to leave. The United States imposed fines on boat operators and the boatlift was formally ended by mutual agreement in October 1980.

Impact

The Marielitos transformed Miami and other receiving communities, expanding the Cuban-American population. The episode had political repercussions in the 1980 US elections, prompted changes in American refugee policy, and left a lasting cultural mark — it forms the backdrop to the 1983 film Scarface and the story of the writer Reinaldo Arenas, who left via Mariel.
