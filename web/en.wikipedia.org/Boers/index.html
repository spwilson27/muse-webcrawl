Boers

Boers (from Dutch boer, "farmer") are the descendants of the Dutch-speaking settlers of the eastern Cape frontier in Southern Africa during the 17th and 18th centuries. From the Dutch word for farmer, the term later came to denote the Afrikaans-speaking settlers who trekked inland and founded independent republics.

Dissatisfaction with British rule — including the abolition of slavery and anglicisation policies — led to the Great Trek of the 1830s and 1840s, in which Voortrekkers migrated into the interior and established the South African Republic (Transvaal) and the Orange Free State.

Boer Wars

The Boers fought two wars against the British Empire: the First Boer War (1880–1881), which restored Transvaal independence, and the Second Boer War (1899–1902), after which the republics were annexed and eventually incorporated into the Union of South Africa in 1910. Boer commandos pioneered guerrilla tactics that were answered by British blockhouses, scorched-earth policy, and concentration camps, in which many Boer civilians died.

Today the term "Boer" is often used interchangeably with, or as a subset of, Afrikaner identity.
