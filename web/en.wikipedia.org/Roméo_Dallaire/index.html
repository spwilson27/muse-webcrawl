Roméo Antonius Dallaire OC CMM GOQ MSC CD (born 25 June 1946) is a Canadian humanitarian, author, retired senator, and former lieutenant-general of the Canadian Armed Forces. He is best known as the Force Commander of the United Nations Assistance Mission for Rwanda (UNAMIR) during the 1994 Rwandan genocide, in which roughly 800,000 people were killed while the international community declined to reinforce his small, underequipped mission.

Early life and military career

Dallaire was born in Denekamp, the Netherlands, to a Canadian soldier and a Dutch nurse, and grew up in Quebec. He joined the Canadian Army in 1964 and was commissioned into the Royal Canadian Artillery, rising through staff and command appointments over three decades, including command of the 5th Canadian Mechanized Brigade Group.

Rwanda

In October 1993 Dallaire took command of UNAMIR, a peacekeeping mission sent to oversee the Arusha Accords ending the Rwandan Civil War. In January 1994 an informant revealed plans for mass killings and arms caches; Dallaire's famous "genocide fax" to UN headquarters requested authority to act, but was refused. When the genocide began in April 1994, most of UNAMIR's troops were withdrawn by their governments, leaving Dallaire with a few hundred soldiers who nonetheless protected tens of thousands of Rwandans at sites such as the Amahoro Stadium and the Hôtel des Mille Collines.

Aftermath and advocacy

Dallaire returned to Canada suffering from post-traumatic stress disorder, about which he has spoken openly. He retired from the army in 2000 and published the acclaimed memoir Shake Hands with the Devil: The Failure of Humanity in Rwanda (2003), which won the Governor General's Award. He served as a Liberal senator from 2005 to 2014, founded the Roméo Dallaire Child Soldiers Initiative to end the recruitment of child soldiers, and has been a prominent advocate for veterans' mental health and the responsibility to protect.
