Prototype

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Prototype
DefinitionEarly sample or model built to test a concept or process
EtymologyGreek prōtotypon, "primitive form"
Used inEngineering, software, design, manufacturing

A prototype is an early sample, model, or release of a product built to test a concept or process. Designers, engineers, and inventors use prototypes to evaluate a new design, verify that it works, gather user feedback, and communicate an idea to stakeholders before committing to full production. The term comes from the Greek prōtotypon, meaning the first or primitive form of something.

Types

Prototypes vary in fidelity. Proof-of-concept or proof-of-principle prototypes test whether a single idea is feasible. Form study prototypes explore size and appearance without function. Working prototypes implement most functions of the final product, while pre-production prototypes closely approximate the finished article and its manufacturing process. In software, prototypes range from paper mock-ups and wireframes to interactive throwaway models and evolving "beta" releases.

Prototyping methods

Traditional prototyping involved machining, casting, or hand-building models. Rapid prototyping techniques — including CNC machining, 3D printing, and laser cutting — greatly reduced the time and cost of producing physical iterations, enabling designers to test many versions. In software engineering, prototyping is used to clarify requirements, and can be throwaway (discarded after evaluation) or evolutionary (refined into the final system).
