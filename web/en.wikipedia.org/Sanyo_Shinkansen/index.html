The San'yō Shinkansen (Japanese: 山陽新幹線) is a line of the Japanese Shinkansen high-speed rail network, connecting Shin-Ōsaka in Osaka with Hakata Station in Fukuoka, the two largest cities in western Japan. Operated by the West Japan Railway Company (JR West), it was constructed between 1967 and 1975 as a continuation of the Tōkaidō Shinkansen, which connects Tokyo to Shin-Ōsaka. Despite being the second Shinkansen line built, it is the third busiest, behind the Tōkaidō and Tōhoku lines.

History

Construction of the line began in 1967. The first section, from Shin-Ōsaka to Okayama, opened on 15 March 1972, and the remainder of the line to Hakata was completed on 10 March 1975. The line passes through the cities of Kobe, Himeji, Okayama, Hiroshima, and Kitakyushu, running largely parallel to the older San'yō Main Line along the northern shore of the Seto Inland Sea. Roughly half of the route is in tunnel, including the Shin-Kanmon Tunnel beneath the Kanmon Straits, which links Honshu with Kyushu.

Services

The fastest services on the line are the Nozomi and Mizuho trains, which complete the 553.7 km journey in about 2 hours 21 minutes at speeds of up to 300 km/h. Hikari and Sakura services stop at more stations, while the all-stations Kodama trains serve every station along the route. Through services operate onto the Tōkaidō Shinkansen to Tokyo and, via Hakata, onto the Kyushu Shinkansen to Kagoshima.

Rolling stock

Trains operating on the line have included the original 0 series, the 500 series introduced in 1997 — the first Japanese trains to operate in regular service at 300 km/h — and the N700 and N700A series with tilting bodies. JR West also operates the 700 series on Hikari Rail Star services and refurbished 500 series sets on Kodama duties.
