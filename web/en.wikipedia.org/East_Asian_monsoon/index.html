East Asian monsoon

The East Asian monsoon is a monsoonal flow that affects large parts of East and Southeast Asia, including China, Korea, Japan, and Taiwan. It is driven by temperature differences between the Asian landmass and the Pacific Ocean and is divided into a warm, humid summer monsoon and a cold, dry winter monsoon.

Summer monsoon

During summer, the continent heats faster than the ocean, drawing moist air inland from the Pacific and South China Sea. This brings the seasonal rainy periods known as meiyu in China, baiu in Japan, and jangma in Korea, associated with a stationary front that slowly migrates northward. The rains are vital for rice agriculture but can cause severe flooding.

Winter monsoon

In winter, high pressure over Siberia drives cold, dry air southeastward across the region. When this air crosses the Sea of Japan it picks up moisture, producing heavy snowfall on Japan's western coasts.

Variability

The strength and timing of the East Asian monsoon vary from year to year and are influenced by El Niño–Southern Oscillation and the position of the subtropical high over the western Pacific. Paleoclimate records from cave deposits in China have made the monsoon's past behaviour an important archive of climate change.
