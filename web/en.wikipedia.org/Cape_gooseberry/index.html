Cape gooseberry

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Cape gooseberry (Physalis peruviana), also known as the goldenberry, Inca berry, or ground cherry, is a plant in the nightshade family Solanaceae native to the Andean highlands of South America. It bears a small, sweet-tart orange fruit enclosed in a papery husk (calyx) resembling a miniature Chinese lantern.

Cultivation and history

The plant was cultivated by the Incas and taken to the Cape of Good Hope — from which the "Cape" in its common name derives — by the early 19th century, and thence to Australia, New Zealand, India, and elsewhere. It is now grown commercially in Colombia, South Africa, Kenya, and other countries with mild climates.

Uses

The fruit is eaten fresh, used in salads, desserts, jams, and chutneys, and is rich in vitamin C and other nutrients. The rest of the plant, like many nightshades, contains potentially toxic alkaloids and is not eaten.
