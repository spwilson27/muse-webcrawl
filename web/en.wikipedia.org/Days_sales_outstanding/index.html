Days sales outstanding

In accountancy, days sales outstanding (also called DSO and days receivables) is a calculation used by a company to estimate the size of their outstanding accounts receivable. It measures how long, on average, it takes a company to collect payment for sales made on credit.

Calculation

Days sales outstanding is calculated as the accounts receivable for a given period divided by the total credit sales for the same period, multiplied by the number of days in the period:

DSO = (Accounts receivable ÷ Total credit sales) × Number of days

For example, a company with $100,000 of accounts receivable and $1,000,000 of annual credit sales would have a DSO of about 36.5 days.

Interpretation

A low DSO generally indicates that a company is efficient at collecting payments from its customers and has good liquidity, while a high or rising DSO may signal that customers are paying slowly, that credit terms are too generous, or that the company may struggle to convert sales into cash. DSO is one component of the cash conversion cycle, together with days inventory outstanding (DIO) and days payable outstanding (DPO):

Cash conversion cycle = DIO + DSO − DPO

Best possible DSO

Analysts sometimes compare actual DSO with a "best possible" DSO, calculated using only current (not yet due) receivables. The gap between the two indicates the proportion of sales that are overdue.
