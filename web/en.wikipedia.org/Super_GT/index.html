Super GT

Super GT is a grand touring car racing series that began in 1993, based in Japan. Originally titled the All Japan Grand Touring Car Championship (JGTC), it was renamed Super GT in 2005 when the series began racing internationally. It is the top level of sports car racing in Japan and is sanctioned by the Japan Automobile Federation and run by the GTA (GT Association).

Classes

Super GT features two classes racing simultaneously. GT500, the premier class, consists of purpose-built silhouette prototypes campaigned by Toyota, Honda and Nissan with engines producing around 550 horsepower or more; since 2020 the class has shared its basic technical framework with the German DTM's Class One regulations. GT300 is a mixed class of FIA GT3 cars and Japanese-built "Mother Chassis" and JAF-GT cars, with a wider variety of privateer entries.

Format

Races are typically 250–500 km long endurance events with mandatory pit stops and driver changes, and success ballast ("success weight") is applied to leading cars to equalise performance. A season of about eight rounds visits circuits including Fuji Speedway, Suzuka, Okayama, Motegi and Sportsland Sugo.

History

The JGTC began in 1993 and grew in popularity through the 1990s with fierce competition between Japanese manufacturers. Renamed Super GT in 2005, the series expanded its profile in Asia with races in Malaysia and Thailand. The 2020 unification of GT500 and DTM Class One regulations allowed cars from both series to compete together in joint "Dream Race" events.
