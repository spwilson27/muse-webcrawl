Shaka era


An epigraph using the Śaka era

The Shaka era (also Śālivāhana Śaka) is a historical Hindu calendar era (year numbering), the epoch (its year zero) of which corresponds to 78 CE. It is widely used in Indian epigraphy and remains in use in the Indian national calendar, as well as in Bali and elsewhere in Southeast Asia.

Origin

The era is traditionally associated with King Śālivāhana, the legendary Satavahana ruler said to have defeated the Shakas (Scythians); modern scholars connect its establishment with the Western Satraps, possibly the Saka king Chashtana or the accession of Kanishka-era polities in the 1st–2nd centuries CE. Its use is attested in inscriptions from the Western Satraps and the Gupta period onward.

Usage

The Shaka era is the basis of the Indian national calendar, adopted by the Government of India in 1957, in which the year begins on Chaitra 1 (usually 22 March, or 21 March in leap years). Shaka dates appear on Indian coins, gazettes, and in All India Radio broadcasts. The era also underlies the Balinese Hindu calendar in Indonesia, and historically was used across Cambodia, Thailand, and other Indianised kingdoms of Southeast Asia.

Conversion

To convert a Shaka year to a Gregorian year, add 78 for dates after the start of the Shaka year (mid-March), or 79 for dates earlier in the Gregorian year. For example, Indian independence on 15 August 1947 fell in Shaka year 1869.
