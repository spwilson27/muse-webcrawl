Usain St Leo Bolt (born 21 August 1986) is a retired Jamaican sprinter widely considered the greatest sprinter of all time. He is the world record holder in the 100 metres, 200 metres and 4×100 metres relay, and an eight-time Olympic gold medallist.

Early life

Bolt was born in Sherwood Content, a small town in Trelawny Parish, Jamaica, and grew up playing cricket and football. His speed was noticed by his school coaches, and by the age of 15 he had won the 200 m at the 2002 World Junior Championships in Kingston, becoming the youngest world junior gold medallist at the time.

Career

Bolt turned professional in 2004 but was hampered by injuries in his first seasons. His breakthrough came in 2008, when he set a 100 m world record of 9.72 s in New York and then won the 100 m, 200 m and 4×100 m relay at the Beijing Olympics, the first two in world record times.

At the 2009 World Championships in Berlin he lowered his own records to 9.58 s in the 100 m and 19.19 s in the 200 m — marks that still stand. He repeated his triple gold at the London 2012 and Rio 2016 Games, achieving the "triple-triple". He retired after the 2017 World Championships in London.

Legacy

Nicknamed "Lightning Bolt", he is the only sprinter to win the Olympic 100 m and 200 m titles at three consecutive Games. His dominance revitalised global interest in sprinting, and he was named IAAF World Athlete of the Year six times and Laureus World Sportsman of the Year four times.
