Odds ratio

An odds ratio (OR) is a statistic that quantifies the strength of the association between two events, A and B. The odds ratio is defined as the ratio of the odds of event A occurring in the presence of B to the odds of A occurring in the absence of B — or equivalently, by symmetry, the ratio of the odds of B in the presence of A to the odds of B in the absence of A. An odds ratio of 1 indicates that the events are independent; a value greater than 1 indicates a positive association, and a value less than 1 a negative association.

The odds ratio is computed from a 2×2 contingency table as ad/bc.

Definition

Given a 2×2 table with cell counts a (both events), b (A only), c (B only), and d (neither), the odds ratio is:

BNot B
Aab
Not Acd

OR = (a/c) / (b/d) = ad / bc

Use in medical research

The odds ratio is the standard effect measure in case-control studies, where the incidence of disease cannot be estimated directly, and it is the natural output of logistic regression. When the outcome is rare, the odds ratio approximates the relative risk; when the outcome is common, the odds ratio exaggerates the association relative to the risk ratio, a frequent source of misinterpretation. The logarithm of the odds ratio has an approximately normal sampling distribution, which makes it convenient for computing confidence intervals and meta-analysis.

Example

If 100 smokers include 30 with a disease and 70 without, while 100 non-smokers include 10 with the disease and 90 without, the odds ratio is (30×90)/(70×10) ≈ 3.86, meaning the odds of disease are nearly four times higher in smokers.
