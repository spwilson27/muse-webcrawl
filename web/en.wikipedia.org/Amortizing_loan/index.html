Amortizing loan

An amortizing loan is a loan where the principal of the loan is paid down (amortized) over the life of the loan according to an amortization schedule, typically through a series of equal periodic payments. Each payment consists of a portion applied to interest and a portion applied to reducing the principal balance, so that by the end of the loan term the entire principal has been repaid.

Amortizing loans contrast with bullet loans and other structures in which the principal is repaid in a single lump sum at maturity, and with interest-only loans, where payments during the term cover interest only.

Amortization schedule

An amortization schedule is a table detailing each periodic payment on an amortizing loan. Early in the schedule, a large share of each payment goes toward interest; as the balance falls, the interest portion shrinks and the principal portion grows, even though the total payment amount stays constant. For a fixed-rate loan with principal P, periodic interest rate r, and n payments, the payment amount is given by the standard annuity formula.

Common examples of amortizing loans include residential mortgages, auto loans, student loans, and personal loans. A loan that is partially amortized may still require a balloon payment at the end of its term.
