Effective theory

In science, an effective theory is a purposefully limited scientific theory applicable under specific circumstances, which for the most part reproduces — approximately, but to a useful precision — an underlying, better theory. In physics, an effective field theory (EFT) is a type of approximation to an underlying physical theory, such as quantum field theory or statistical mechanics, that includes the appropriate degrees of freedom to describe physical phenomena occurring at a chosen length scale, while ignoring substructure and degrees of freedom at shorter distances (or, equivalently, at higher energies).

Core idea

Intuitively, one disregards the physics at shorter length scales in the underlying theory than the one at which one would like to make predictions, subject to the rule that the degrees of freedom describing difference scales do not mix strongly. Under certain circumstances, the omitted high-energy physics affects low-energy observables only through a finite set of local interaction terms — the operators of the effective theory — whose coefficients (low-energy constants) encode the influence of the unknown short-distance physics.

Renormalisation group and power counting

The systematic basis of effective theories is the renormalisation group: integrating out heavy degrees of freedom generates an infinite tower of operators suppressed by powers of the ratio of the energy to a heavy scale Λ. Truncating this tower at a given order in the power counting yields predictions with a quantifiable theoretical error — the hallmark of a modern EFT.

Examples

  • Fermi theory of beta decay: the historical prototype, in which the heavy W boson is replaced by a pointlike four-fermion interaction.
  • Chiral perturbation theory: the EFT of low-energy QCD in terms of pions and nucleons.
  • Heavy quark effective theory and soft-collinear effective theory: EFTs for systems containing heavy quarks and energetic light particles.
  • Standard Model effective field theory (SMEFT): parametrises possible new physics above the electroweak scale.
  • Euler–Heisenberg Lagrangian: the low-energy EFT of photons in QED.
  • Effective theories in gravity: general relativity itself treated as the leading term of an EFT.

Philosophy

Effective theories have reshaped the philosophy of physics: rather than a single monolithic "theory of everything", physical understanding is organised into a tower of theories, each valid at its own scale and connected to the next by renormalisation-group flow. Kenneth G. Wilson's work on the renormalisation group was central to this perspective.
