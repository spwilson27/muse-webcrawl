Lok Sabha

Lok Sabha
House of the People — lower house of India's Parliament
Seats: 543 elected (maximum 550)
Term: 5 years
Leader: Prime Minister of India

The Lok Sabha, constitutionally the House of the People, is the lower house of India's bicameral Parliament, alongside the Rajya Sabha (Council of States). Members of the Lok Sabha are directly elected by the adult population of India from single-member constituencies using first-past-the-post, for a maximum term of five years.

Composition and powers

The Constitution, as amended by the 104th Amendment, provides for 543 elected members (the former two nominated Anglo-Indian seats were abolished in 2020). The Lok Sabha has primacy in money bills, which can be introduced only there, and the Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to it: a government must command its confidence to remain in office. The Speaker presides over its sittings.

History

The first Lok Sabha was constituted in 1952 after India's first general elections. Sessions are held in the Parliament House (Sansad Bhavan) in New Delhi; the house moved to the new Parliament building inaugurated in 2023.
