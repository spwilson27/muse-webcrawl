Maurice Herzog

Maurice André Raymond Herzog (15 January 1919 – 13 December 2012) was a French mountaineer and politician. Together with Louis Lachenal, he reached the summit of Annapurna on 3 June 1950 — the first ascent of any peak over 8,000 metres — a landmark in mountaineering history. The descent cost him most of his fingers and toes to frostbite. His account, Annapurna, became the best-selling climbing book ever written.

Annapurna 1950

Herzog led the French Himalayan expedition that, without a precise map and in a single season, reconnoitred and climbed Annapurna I (8,091 m) via its north face. He and Lachenal summited without supplementary oxygen; the harrowing descent in a storm and avalanche became legendary.

Political career

Herzog served as France's secretary of state for youth and sports under Charles de Gaulle, was mayor of Chamonix, and sat on the International Olympic Committee. Later accounts by teammates and historians questioned parts of his official narrative and the expedition's treatment of Lachenal's account.
