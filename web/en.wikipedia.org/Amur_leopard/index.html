Amur leopard

The Amur leopard (Panthera pardus orientalis) is a leopard subspecies native to the Primorye region of southeastern Russia and northern China. It is one of the rarest big cats in the world and is listed as Critically Endangered on the IUCN Red List. Adapted to the cold temperate forests of the Russian Far East, it has a thick coat that grows long in winter and legs relatively longer than other leopards, suiting it to walking in deep snow.

Distribution and population

Historically the Amur leopard ranged across the Korean Peninsula, northeast China and the Russian Far East. Habitat loss, poaching and depletion of prey reduced it to a tiny remnant population. Camera-trap surveys in Russia's Land of the Leopard National Park and adjacent areas of China have documented a slow recovery, with estimates rising from roughly 30 individuals around 2000 to more than 100 in recent counts.

Characteristics

Males weigh about 32–48 kg and females 25–43 kg. The winter coat is pale and dense, up to 7 cm long, while the summer coat is shorter and more vividly spotted. Amur leopards are solitary, nocturnal hunters of roe deer, sika deer, hares and badgers.

Conservation

Conservation measures include the creation of Land of the Leopard National Park in 2012, anti-poaching patrols, cross-border cooperation with China, and captive breeding programmes in zoos that maintain a genetic reserve. There are longstanding proposals to reintroduce the leopard to parts of its former range.
