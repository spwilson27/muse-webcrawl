Hoist (device)

A hoist is a device used for lifting or lowering a load by means of a drum or lift-wheel around which rope or chain wraps. It may be manually operated, electrically or pneumatically driven, and may use chain, fibre or wire rope as its lifting medium. The most familiar form is an elevator, the car of which is raised and lowered by a hoist mechanism. Most hoists couple to their loads using a lifting hook. Today, there are a few governing bodies for the North American overhead hoist industry which include the Hoist Manufacturers Institute, the American Society of Mechanical Engineers, and the Occupational Safety and Health Administration.

Types

The term "hoist" is used for many kinds of equipment that lift and lower loads. Types include:

  • Chain hoist (chain block) – a manual hoist in which an endless hand chain drives, through gearing, a second chain attached to the load.
  • Lever hoist (come-along) – operated by a ratchet lever, portable and widely used in rigging and rescue work.
  • Wire rope hoist – uses wire rope wound on a grooved drum, common on electric overhead travelling cranes.
  • Electric hoist – powered by an electric motor, controlled by a pendant station or radio remote control.
  • Pneumatic (air) hoist – driven by compressed air, often used where electric sparks must be avoided.
  • Construction hoist (buck hoist) – a temporary rack-and-pinion elevator used on construction sites to move personnel and materials.
  • Mine hoist – raises and lowers conveyances in a mine shaft.

Safety

Because a falling load can cause serious injury, hoists are subject to safety standards and periodic inspection. Features such as brakes, upper and lower limit switches, and overload clutches are commonly fitted. Operators are trained never to stand under a suspended load and to inspect chains and ropes for wear, corrosion and distortion before use.

History

Simple hoisting devices are ancient: the Roman architect Vitruvius described compound pulley machines powered by men and animals. In the Middle Ages hoists were used to move goods into lofts in warehouses, which often extended over the street so that carts could be loaded directly; the gabled "hoist beams" of Amsterdam canal houses are a surviving example. The invention of the steam engine and later the electric motor transformed hoisting in industry and mining.
