Jean Bolland

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Jean Bolland (Latin: Johannes Bollandus; 13 August 1596 – 12 September 1665) was a Jesuit priest, theologian, and hagiographer from the Spanish Netherlands. He founded the Acta Sanctorum, the great critical collection of saints' lives, and the society of scholars who continued his work are known as the Bollandists after him.

Life

Bolland was born at Julémont, in the province of Liège. He entered the Society of Jesus in 1612 and studied at Louvain, then taught in Jesuit colleges in Flanders. His superiors assigned him to carry out the project conceived by Heribert Rosweyde (1569–1629), who had planned a critical edition of all saints' lives in manuscript across European libraries but died before beginning publication.

Acta Sanctorum

Working in Antwerp with assistants including Godefridus Henschenius (Godfrey Henschen), Bolland organised the material by feast day, arranged the research trips to libraries, and published the first two volumes of the Acta Sanctorum — covering the saints of January — in 1643. The volumes for February followed in 1658. The work applied rigorous historical criticism to hagiographical legends, printing the earliest texts and commentaries, and provoked both acclaim and controversy, notably with the Carmelites over claims about their order's antiquity.

Legacy

Bolland died in Antwerp in 1665. The project he founded continued for centuries under the Bollandist Society, eventually filling dozens of folio volumes and completing the calendar of saints in the 20th century; the society still exists in Brussels and remains a centre of hagiographical research.
