Euclid of Megara

Bornc. 435 BC, Megara
Diedc. 365 BC
EraAncient Greek philosophy
SchoolMegarian school
Main interestsLogic, dialectic, ethics

Euclid of Megara (Greek: Εὐκλείδης ὁ Μεγαρεύς; c. 435 – c. 365 BC) was a Greek Socratic philosopher who founded the Megarian school of philosophy. He was a pupil and friend of Socrates, and was present at Socrates' death, as recorded in Plato's Phaedo. Euclid must not be confused with the later and more famous Euclid of Alexandria, the mathematician.

Life

Euclid was born in Megara, a city-state between Athens and Corinth. According to tradition, when Athens forbade Megarians to enter the city on pain of death, Euclid would sneak into Athens disguised in women's clothing to attend Socrates' lectures. After Socrates' execution in 399 BC, Euclid sheltered Plato and other Socratic philosophers at Megara.

Philosophy

Euclid combined the Eleatic doctrine of Parmenides — that being is one and unchanging — with Socratic ethics. He held that the Good is one, though called by many names (wisdom, god, reason), and that its opposite, evil, does not exist in itself. The Megarians became famous for dialectic and logical puzzles; Euclid's followers, including Eubulides of Miletus, developed paradoxes such as the liar paradox. The school also emphasized refutation in argument and denied the reality of motion and plurality, in the tradition of Zeno of Elea.

Three dialogues attributed to Euclid were known in antiquity, though none survive: Lamprias, Aeschines, and Phoenix.
