Second lieutenant

Second lieutenant is a junior commissioned officer military rank in many armed forces. It is typically the lowest rank of commissioned officer, held by officers in training or in their first appointment, often commanding a platoon of around thirty soldiers. The rank corresponds to NATO code OF-1 (junior).

By country

In the United States Army, Marine Corps, Air Force, and Space Force, second lieutenant is the entry-level commissioned rank (pay grade O-1), denoted by a single gold bar — hence the nickname "butter bar". In the British Army and Royal Marines, second lieutenants are junior officers below lieutenant; the Royal Air Force equivalent is pilot officer and the Royal Navy's is midshipman or sub-lieutenant. Many Commonwealth and European armies use the rank similarly.

Role

Second lieutenants usually lead a platoon, assisted by an experienced senior non-commissioned officer. The appointment is often a probationary period of one to two years before promotion to first lieutenant or lieutenant.

History

The title derives from the French lieu-tenant, "place-holder". In earlier centuries the corresponding junior officer was often called an ensign or cornet, titles that the rank of second lieutenant replaced during the nineteenth and twentieth centuries.
