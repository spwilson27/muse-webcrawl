5.1 surround sound

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

5.1 surround sound ("five-point one") is the common name for six-channel surround sound audio systems. 5.1 is the most commonly used layout in home theatres and cinemas. It uses five full-bandwidth channels and one low-frequency effects channel (the "point one"). Dolby Digital, DTS, SDDS, and THX are all common 5.1 systems. 5.1 is also the standard surround sound audio component of digital broadcast and music.

All 5.1 systems use the same speaker channels and configuration, having a front left and right, a center channel, two surround channels (left and right) and the low-frequency effects channel designed for a subwoofer.

History

5.1 dates back to 1976, when Dolby Laboratories introduced a version of their analog Dolby Stereo format for films that split the soundtrack into six channels. The concept was explored earlier in the 1950s with multi-channel cinema formats such as CinemaScope and Cinerama, and by film soundtracks such as Fantasia. The modern 5.1 configuration was standardized when the digital film sound formats emerged in the early 1990s: Batman Returns (1992) was the first film released with Dolby Digital, whose 5.1 layout became the de facto standard for cinema and then for home theatre DVD releases.

Channels

The five main channels are:

  • Center — reproduces dialogue and on-screen action; placed above or below the screen.
  • Front left and front right — the main stereo pair for music and off-center effects.
  • Surround left and surround right — placed to the sides or rear of the listener for ambient and directional effects.

The sixth channel is the LFE (Low Frequency Effects) channel, reproduced by one or more subwoofers. Because it carries only a limited low-frequency range, it is counted as ".1" rather than a full channel.

Channel order

Channel order varies between formats. Files in WAV format for 5.1 typically follow the Microsoft/Dolby order: front left, front right, center, LFE, surround left, surround right. Other container formats, such as AAC, use the order center, front left, front right, surround left, surround right, LFE.

Speaker placement

For a film-based presentation, standard recommendations (such as those from Dolby and the ITU) place the three front speakers in an arc in front of the listener, with the surround speakers roughly 90–110 degrees from the listening position, slightly above ear level. For music listening, the ITU-R BS.775 recommendation places all five main speakers on a circle around the listener, with the surround speakers at about 100–120 degrees.

Applications

Music

5.1 has been used for music releases on Super Audio CD and DVD-Audio, as well as on concert films and Blu-ray music releases.

Television and film

Digital television broadcasting (such as ATSC and DVB) adopted Dolby Digital, making 5.1 the default sound format for HDTV programming and for DVDs.

Games

Video game consoles from the sixth generation onwards have supported real-time 5.1 output, using encoders such as Dolby Digital Live to transport the mixed audio to a receiver.
