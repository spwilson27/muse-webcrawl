Mola (art form)

Mola (plural: molas)

Origin: Guna (Kuna) people, Panama and Colombia

Technique: Reverse appliqué

Use: Blouse panels; also bags, wall hangings

Molas are handmade textiles forming part of the traditional women's clothing of the Guna (also spelled Kuna or Cuna) people of Panama and Colombia. A mola is a colourful panel made using a reverse appliqué technique: several layers of different-coloured cloth are sewn together, and the design is formed by cutting away parts of each layer and stitching the edges to reveal the colours beneath, supplemented in some pieces with appliqué and embroidery.

History

The Guna originally decorated their bodies with painted designs; after Spanish colonisation and contact with missionaries, they transferred these geometric and figurative designs to cloth. Mola making in its modern form dates from the 19th and early 20th centuries. Traditionally, two mola panels form the front and back of a woman's blouse (mola means "clothing" in the Guna language), worn with a patterned wrap skirt, headscarf, and beaded leg ornaments (wini).

Designs

Traditional molas feature abstract geometric patterns, often inspired by body painting. More recent designs depict birds, animals, plants, sea creatures, religious and political imagery, and motifs from popular culture. Quality is judged by the number of layers, fineness of stitching, evenness of cut-outs, and complexity of the design.

Cultural significance

Molas are a marker of Guna identity and an important source of income, sold widely to tourists and collectors. The art form is associated with the Guna Yala comarca (autonomous region) of Panama, whose flag features a design derived from mola patterns.
