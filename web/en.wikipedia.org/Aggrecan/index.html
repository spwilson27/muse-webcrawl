Aggrecan (chondroitin sulfate proteoglycan 1, or cartilage-specific proteoglycan core protein) is a large proteoglycan that is a major structural component of cartilage. It gives cartilage its ability to resist compressive loads and is essential for normal skeletal development.

Structure

The aggrecan core protein (~250 kDa) has three globular domains (G1, G2, G3). Between G2 and G3 extends a long region bearing around 100 chondroitin sulfate chains and numerous keratan sulfate chains, whose dense negative charge attracts water. The G1 domain binds hyaluronic acid, stabilized by link protein; more than a hundred aggrecan monomers can attach to a single hyaluronan filament, forming enormous aggregates of up to ~2.5 MDa that are trapped within the collagen network of cartilage.

Function and pathology

The swollen, hydrated aggrecan aggregates provide cartilage with its load-bearing resilience. In osteoarthritis and rheumatoid arthritis, enzymes called aggrecanases (ADAMTS-4 and ADAMTS-5) and matrix metalloproteinases cleave aggrecan, contributing to cartilage breakdown. Mutations in the human ACAN gene cause forms of short stature and skeletal dysplasias, including spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia type Kimberley and familial osteochondritis dissecans.
