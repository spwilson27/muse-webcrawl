Cheapside is a street in the City of London, the historic and modern financial centre of London. It runs east from St Paul's Cathedral towards Poultry and the Bank junction, and gives its name to a City ward. In the Middle Ages it was the city's principal market street.

History

The name derives from Old English ceapan, "to buy or bargain": "Cheap" designated the market district, as in Eastcheap and Cheapside's side streets named for their trades — Bread Street, Milk Street, Wood Street and Honey Lane. Goldsmiths and jewellers concentrated here for centuries, and processions, tournaments and the Eleanor Cross marked its ceremonial role. The Great Fire of 1666 destroyed the medieval street; it was rebuilt, and Wren's church of St Mary-le-Bow (with its famous "Bow bells") still stands on its south side. Geoffrey Chaucer and John Milton were born nearby, and Shakespeare lodged close by.

Modern Cheapside

Today the street is lined with offices, shops and the One New Change shopping centre opposite St Paul's. The claim that "born within the sound of Bow bells" defines a true Cockney originates with St Mary-le-Bow. The word "cheap" acquired its modern sense of "low-priced, shoddy" from the street's market associations.
