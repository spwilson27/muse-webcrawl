Prophecy

Prophecy is a message that is claimed by a person (generally a prophet) to have been communicated to them by a deity. Such messages typically involve inspiration, interpretation, or revelation of divine will concerning the prophet's social world and events to come. Prophecy has been a common element in many cultures and belief systems throughout history.

In religion

In the Abrahamic religions, prophets such as Moses, Isaiah, and Muhammad are understood as recipients of divine revelation. Prophetic texts in the Hebrew Bible include oracles of judgment and hope, while Christian interpretation often reads them as foretelling the life of Jesus. Prophecy differs from divination in that the prophet is regarded as a passive recipient of a divine message rather than a practitioner of techniques for extracting omens.

Interpretation and criticism

Scholars study prophecy as literature, history, and social phenomenon. Phenomena such as self-fulfilling prophecy, postdiction (vaticinium ex eventu), and the vagueness of many oracular pronouncements are discussed in critical analyses of prophetic claims.
