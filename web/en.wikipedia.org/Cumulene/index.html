Cumulene

A cumulene is a hydrocarbon composed of three or more cumulative double bonds — consecutive double bonds sharing common carbon atoms, as in butatriene, H2C=C=C=CH2. Cumulenes are the carbon-chain analogues of allenes, which have two cumulative double bonds.

Cumulene
Type: Hydrocarbon
Feature: ≥3 cumulative double bonds
Simplest example: Butatriene

Structure and bonding

The central carbons of a cumulene are sp-hybridised, giving the carbon chain a rigid, linear geometry. Whether the terminal substituents lie in the same plane depends on the parity of the number of double bonds: cumulenes with an even number of double bonds have perpendicular end planes (like allene), while those with an odd number have coplanar ends, allowing cis–trans isomerism in longer examples.

Synthesis and occurrence

Short cumulenes are reactive and difficult to isolate, but longer, substituted cumulenes — including cyclic and metal-complexed forms — have been prepared. Cumulene chains are of interest as molecular wires in nanoelectronics. cumulene-type species have also been detected in interstellar molecular clouds.
