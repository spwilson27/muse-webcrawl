The montane guinea pig (Cavia tschudii) is a species of cavy found in the high Andes of South America. It is the most likely wild ancestor of the domestic guinea pig, Cavia porcellus.

Description and distribution

The montane guinea pig is a medium-sized cavy with grizzled brownish-grey fur, weighing around 640 g and measuring about 22 cm in length. It inhabits moist, rocky habitats in the Andes of Peru, Bolivia, northwestern Argentina, and northern Chile, at elevations from about 2,000 to 3,800 metres (6,600 to 12,500 ft), living in dense ground vegetation near swamps and streams.

Behaviour

The species lives in colonies in burrows with multiple entrances, grazing on grasses and herbs. It is preyed upon by various mammals and raptors. Females give birth to litters of one to four pups after a gestation of about two months.

Relation to the domestic guinea pig

Genetic studies have shown that the domestic guinea pig descends from this species or a close relative; the two readily interbreed and produce fertile offspring. Guinea pigs were domesticated in the Andes several thousand years ago as a food source.
