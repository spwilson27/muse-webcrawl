Capital: Tbilisi

Georgia (Georgian: Sakartvelo) is a country in the Caucasus region at the intersection of Eastern Europe and Western Asia. It is bounded by the Black Sea to the west, Russia to the north, Turkey and Armenia to the south, and Azerbaijan to the southeast. Its capital and largest city is Tbilisi.

History

The ancient kingdoms of Colchis and Iberia occupied the region, which adopted Christianity in the fourth century. A unified Georgian kingdom flourished in the eleventh and twelfth centuries under David IV and Queen Tamar. After centuries of Persian and Ottoman pressure, Georgia was absorbed into the Russian Empire in the nineteenth century, briefly independent (1918–1921), then a Soviet republic until independence in 1991. The Russo-Georgian War of 2008 left the regions of Abkhazia and South Ossetia under Russian-backed separatist control.

Culture

Georgia has its own language and a unique alphabet, a celebrated polyphonic singing tradition, and an ancient winemaking culture using clay qvevri vessels that stretches back some 8,000 years.
