Caterina Sforza (1463 – 28 May 1509) was an Italian noblewoman, Countess of Forlì and Lady of Imola, first with her husband Girolamo Riario and, after his death, as regent for her son Ottaviano. A member of the powerful Sforza family, she was one of the most famous women of the Italian Renaissance, remembered for her fierce defence of her territories against Cesare Borgia.


Caterina Sforza, Countess of Forlì

Early life and marriages

Caterina was the illegitimate daughter of Galeazzo Maria Sforza, Duke of Milan, and was raised at the Milanese court, receiving a humanist education. At the age of ten she was married to Girolamo Riario, a nephew of Pope Sixtus IV, and moved to Rome. When Sixtus died in 1484, the pregnant Caterina seized the Castel Sant'Angelo and held it until favourable terms were secured, earning a reputation for extraordinary resolve.

Rule of Forlì and Imola

After Girolamo was assassinated in 1488, Caterina ruled Forlì and Imola as regent. She suppressed conspiracies, rebuilt fortifications, reformed taxation, and defended her states in person. In 1499–1500 she resisted the forces of Cesare Borgia, holding out in the fortress of Forlì until it fell in January 1500. Captured and taken to Rome, she was eventually released and retired to Florence.

Legacy

Caterina was also a patron of the arts and an experimenter in alchemy and medicine; her manuscript of remedies and cosmetics, the Experimenti, circulated widely. Her descendants included Giovanni dalle Bande Nere and, through him, the later Medici grand dukes of Tuscany. Her life inspired numerous literary treatments, including in Machiavelli's works.
