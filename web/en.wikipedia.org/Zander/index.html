The zander (Sander lucioperca), also known as the pikeperch, is a species of ray-finned fish from the genus Sander within the family Percidae. It is a predatory freshwater and brackish-water fish found across Eurasia, from western Europe to Siberia and in the basins of the Caspian, Black and Baltic seas.

Description

The zander is the largest member of the perch family in Eurasia. It has an elongated, streamlined body with two dorsal fins, the first spiny and the second soft-rayed. Its colouring is greenish-grey with dark vertical bars along the flanks. It commonly reaches 40–70 cm in length, and exceptionally large specimens exceed 100 cm and 15 kg. Like its close relative the walleye of North America, it has a reflective tapetum lucidum that aids vision in low light and turbid water.

Distribution and habitat

Zander inhabit large, turbid rivers, eutrophic lakes and brackish coastal waters. They have been widely introduced outside their native range, including into British waters, where their spread through canal systems has been controversial among anglers because of their predation on smaller fish.

Relationship with humans

The zander is a prized food fish in much of Europe, valued for its firm, white, delicately flavoured flesh, and is also a popular target for sport anglers. It is farmed in aquaculture in several countries.
