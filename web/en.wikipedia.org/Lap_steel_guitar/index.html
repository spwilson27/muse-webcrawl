Lap steel guitar

The lap steel guitar, also known as a Hawaiian guitar, is a type of steel guitar without pedals that is typically played with the instrument in a horizontal position across the performer's lap or supported by a stand. The player holds a metal slide or tone bar in one hand while plucking the strings with the other, often using fingerpicks.

History

The style originated in Hawaii in the late 19th century, popularised by Joseph Kekuku, and spread to the mainland United States in the early 20th century. It became prominent in Hawaiian music, country music, western swing, blues and later rock.

Design

Lap steels are usually solid-body electric instruments with raised strings, a high nut, and six or more strings in open tunings such as C6, E7 or open G. Unlike the pedal steel guitar, pitch changes depend entirely on the position of the bar and on string pulls behind the bar.
