Queen Charlotte Sound (British Columbia)


Queen Charlotte Sound, between Vancouver Island and the mainland coast

Queen Charlotte Sound is a sound of the Pacific Ocean in British Columbia, Canada, lying between Vancouver Island in the south and the mainland coast in the north. It is the southernmost of the major sounds of the British Columbia coast, connecting Queen Charlotte Strait southeastward to Hecate Strait northwestward, and it forms part of the Inside Passage shipping route between British Columbia and Alaska.

Geography

The sound is a deep basin, in places more than 500 m deep, bounded by the Goose Island Group and other offshore islands. A trough, the Moresby Trough, runs through it toward Hecate Strait. Because it is open to the Pacific without the sheltering island screen found further south, Queen Charlotte Sound is notorious among mariners for heavy seas generated by winter storms and the meeting of long ocean swell with strong tidal currents.

Name and history

The sound was named by fur trader James Strange in 1786 for Queen Charlotte, consort of King George III. The Queen Charlotte Islands (now Haida Gwaii) to the northwest share the name. The waters are the traditional territories of the Heiltsuk, Wuikinuxv, Kitasoo/Xai'xais and other coastal First Nations, and the area is important salmon, herring and groundfish habitat.
