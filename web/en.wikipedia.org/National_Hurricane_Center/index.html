The National Hurricane Center (NHC) is the division of the United States National Weather Service responsible for tracking and predicting tropical weather systems in the Atlantic and eastern Pacific basins. It is co-located with the Miami National Weather Service office on the campus of Florida International University.

Responsibilities

The NHC issues advisories, forecasts and warnings on tropical cyclones every six hours, including the widely reproduced "cone of uncertainty" graphic showing the probable track of a storm's center. Its Tropical Analysis and Forecast Branch supports marine forecasting, and the center coordinates with emergency managers and the media during landfalling storms. The NHC is designated by the World Meteorological Organization as the Regional Specialized Meteorological Center for the Atlantic and eastern Pacific.

History

Hurricane forecasting in the United States dates to a warning service established in 1935; the Miami Hurricane Warning Office gradually took on basin-wide duties, and the National Hurricane Center was formally established in 1965. Noted directors and specialists include Neil Frank, Max Mayfield and Bob Sheets. In 1995 the center moved to its hurricane-resistant building at Florida International University.
