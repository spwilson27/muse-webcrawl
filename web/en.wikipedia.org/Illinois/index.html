Illinois

Illinois (/ˌɪlɪˈnɔɪ/ IL-in-OY) is a state in the Midwestern United States. It borders Lake Michigan to its northeast, Wisconsin to the north, Iowa and Missouri to the west, Kentucky to the south, and Indiana to the east. Its capital is Springfield, and its largest city and economic center is Chicago, the third-most populous city in the United States.

History

Illinois was inhabited by Native American peoples for thousands of years; the Mississippian city of Cahokia, near present-day Collinsville, was one of the largest pre-Columbian settlements north of Mexico. French explorers arrived in the 17th century, and the region passed to Britain in 1763 and to the United States after the American Revolutionary War. Illinois became the 21st state on December 3, 1818. Abraham Lincoln, the 16th U.S. president, spent most of his adult life in Springfield, earning Illinois the nickname "Land of Lincoln."

Geography

Most of Illinois consists of flat prairie, part of the Central Lowland. The state is drained by the Mississippi, Ohio, Wabash, and Illinois rivers. The northeast corner of the state lies on the southwestern shore of Lake Michigan. Southern Illinois, including the Shawnee National Forest, is more hilly and forested.

Economy

Illinois has a diverse economy anchored by Chicago, a global center of finance, commerce, transportation, and industry. The state is a major agricultural producer, especially of corn and soybeans, and a leading transportation hub: O'Hare International Airport is among the world's busiest, and Chicago is the nation's principal rail freight center.

Demographics

With nearly 13 million residents, Illinois is the sixth-most populous U.S. state. Roughly two-thirds of the population lives in the northeastern part of the state, in the Chicago metropolitan area.
