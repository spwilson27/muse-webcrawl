Bait ball

A bait ball, or baitball, is a tightly packed, roughly spherical formation assumed by schooling fish when they are threatened by predators. Schooling species such as sardines, anchovies and herring bunch together in this way as a defensive response, each fish seeking the centre of the group and constantly changing position within it.

Formation and defence

The formation exploits the "safety in numbers" effect: a predator faced with thousands of nearly identical, rapidly shifting targets has difficulty singling out one fish, and an individual fish reduces its chance of being the one caught. Bait balls are usually a last resort, formed when a school is cut off from escape routes or when predators have herded the fish against the surface.

Predator responses

Many predators cooperate to create and exploit bait balls. Dolphins may herd fish from below while seabirds attack from above; sailfish and other billfish slash through the ball with their bills to injure prey, and Bryde's whales may lunge through the centre of a bait ball, engulfing large numbers of fish at once. Spectacular feeding events built around bait balls, such as the sardine run off South Africa, attract dense assemblages of sharks, dolphins, whales and birds, and are a favourite subject of natural history filmmaking.
