Diversity Immigrant Visa

The Diversity Immigrant Visa program, commonly known as the green card lottery, is a United States government lottery program for receiving a United States Permanent Resident Card. Established by the Immigration Act of 1990, it makes up to 55,000 immigrant visas available annually (later effectively reduced to about 50,000) to natives of countries with historically low rates of immigration to the United States.

Operation

Entries are submitted electronically during a registration period each autumn. Selectees are chosen at random by computer, but selection only entitles the applicant to apply: they must still meet admissibility requirements, demonstrate a high-school education or qualifying work experience, and complete consular processing or adjustment of status before the fiscal year ends. No more than seven percent of the visas may go to natives of any single country, and natives of countries that sent more than 50,000 immigrants in the previous five years are ineligible. Millions of entries are received each year, and fraudulent agents charging would-be entrants have been a persistent problem.

Debate

The program has been repeatedly debated in Congress, with proposals both to expand it and to abolish it and reallocate its visas to employment-based categories; its existence was also questioned in debates over merit-based immigration.
