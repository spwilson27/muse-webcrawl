Benin City

Benin City is the capital of Edo State in southern Nigeria and the historic centre of the Kingdom of Benin, one of the oldest and most highly developed states in precolonial West Africa. Founded by the Edo people — according to tradition consolidated under Oba Ewuare the Great in the 15th century — the city was renowned for its vast earthworks (the Benin Moat, among the largest human-made structures in the world), its broad planned streets, and the brass and ivory art of its royal guilds. Today it is a major Nigerian city and centre of the rubber and oil-palm industries. It should not be confused with the neighbouring Republic of Benin.

History

Edo tradition traces the city to the Ogiso ("sky king") dynasty and the subsequent line of Obas beginning with Eweka I in the 13th–14th centuries. Under Ewuare the Great (c. 1440–1473) the city was rebuilt, fortified with its great moat and walls, and expanded into an empire. Portuguese visitors from 1485 described a large, well-ordered city; Dutch accounts in the 17th century compared its main streets favourably with Amsterdam's. The city's brasscasters of Igun Street produced the plaques and heads now known worldwide as the Benin Bronzes.

The Benin Expedition and the bronzes

In 1897 a British punitive expedition sacked and burned much of the city, exiled Oba Ovonramwen, and carried off thousands of royal artworks, which were dispersed to museums in Europe and America. The monarchy was restored in 1914. Since the late 20th century Nigeria and the Oba's court have pressed for restitution; from 2021–2023 major museums in Germany, the United States, and Britain (including the Horniman Museum and Jesus College, Cambridge) formally returned bronzes, and a new museum — the Museum of West African Art (MOWAA) — has been developed in the city to receive them.

Modern city

Modern Benin City is an educational and commercial hub, home to the University of Benin (founded 1970), and remains the seat of the Oba of Benin, whose palace anchors its ceremonial life. Its ring road layout overlays the historic moat, fragments of which survive as a scheduled monument.
