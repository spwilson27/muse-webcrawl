Regulation

Regulation
FieldLaw, public administration, economics
FormsRules, licensing, standards, prohibitions
InstrumentsRegulatory agencies, inspections, penalties

Regulation is the management of complex systems according to a set of rules and trends. In government, regulation refers to rules made and maintained by an authority — typically a regulatory agency acting under legislation — that govern economic and social activity. Systems of regulation are found in fields as diverse as banking, environmental protection, public health, transport, and consumer safety.

Forms of regulation

Economic regulation historically controlled prices and market entry in industries such as railroads, utilities, and telecommunications. Social regulation addresses health, safety, and the environment, covering workplace safety, pollution, food and drug safety, and product standards. Regulation may take the form of licensing requirements, direct prohibitions, disclosure rules, liability rules, or performance standards enforced by penalties.

Rationale

Economists justify regulation as a response to market failures: monopolies, externalities such as pollution, information asymmetries between sellers and buyers, and the under-provision of public goods. Regulation is also used to pursue distributional and rights-based goals, such as anti-discrimination rules or universal service obligations.

Criticism and deregulation

Critics argue that regulation imposes compliance costs, can be captured by the industries it oversees (regulatory capture), and may stifle innovation. Waves of deregulation in the late twentieth century reduced state control over airlines, telecommunications, and finance in many countries, while other fields, such as data protection, have seen expanded regulation. Regulatory impact analysis and cost-benefit review are now standard tools in many governments.
