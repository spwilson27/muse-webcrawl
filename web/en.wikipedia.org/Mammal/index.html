A mammal (from Latin mamma, "breast") is a vertebrate animal of the class Mammalia, characterised by the presence of mammary glands that produce milk to feed the young, a neocortex, fur or hair, and three middle ear bones. Mammals are endothermic ("warm-blooded") and most give birth to live young; the egg-laying monotremes are the exception.

Diversity

The largest orders are rodents, bats and shrew-like eulipotyphlans, together accounting for the majority of species. Mammals range in size from the 2-cm bumblebee bat to the blue whale, the largest animal known to have existed. Humans are primates within the order Primates.

Evolution

Mammals evolved from cynodont therapsids during the Late Triassic. Small and mostly nocturnal through the Mesozoic era, they radiated dramatically after the extinction of the non-avian dinosaurs 66 million years ago.

Characteristics

Defining features include hair at some stage of life, a four-chambered heart, a diaphragm, differentiated teeth (heterodonty), and a lower jaw composed of a single bone (the dentary). Most mammals have a high metabolic rate and complex social behaviours.
