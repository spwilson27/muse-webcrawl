Arthur Miller

Born: 17 October 1915, New York City

Died: 10 February 2005, Roxbury, Connecticut

Occupation: Playwright, essayist

Notable works: Death of a Salesman, The Crucible, All My Sons, A View from the Bridge

Arthur Asher Miller (17 October 1915 – 10 February 2005) was an American playwright, essayist and prominent figure of twentieth-century American theatre. His plays, which often examine morality, responsibility and the American Dream, include All My Sons (1947), Death of a Salesman (1949), The Crucible (1953) and A View from the Bridge (1955).

Life and career

Born in Harlem to a Jewish immigrant family whose clothing business collapsed in the Great Depression, Miller studied at the University of Michigan and worked in radio before his Broadway breakthrough with All My Sons. Death of a Salesman, the tragedy of the salesman Willy Loman, won the Pulitzer Prize for Drama and the Tony Award for Best Play and is regarded as one of the greatest American plays.

McCarthyism

The Crucible, a dramatisation of the Salem witch trials, was an allegory for the anti-communist investigations of Senator Joseph McCarthy. In 1956 Miller was called before the House Un-American Activities Committee; he refused to name others and was convicted of contempt of Congress, a conviction later overturned on appeal.

Personal life

Miller was married three times: to Mary Slattery, to the actress Marilyn Monroe (1956–1961), for whom he wrote the screenplay of The Misfits (1961), and to the photographer Inge Morath. He continued writing into old age, publishing the memoir Timebends (1987) and new plays such as The Ride Down Mt. Morgan and Broken Glass.
