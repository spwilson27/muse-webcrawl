The First World War, also known as the Great War, was a global conflict between two coalitions: the Allies and the Central Powers. Fighting took place mainly in Europe and the Middle East, as well as in parts of Africa and the Asia-Pacific, and in and on the Atlantic and Pacific oceans. In Europe the war was characterised by trench warfare on the Western Front and wide manoeuvre in the east; it saw the introduction of tanks, aircraft, poison gas and unrestricted submarine warfare. Some 60 million European soldiers were mobilised, and more than 8 million were killed.

Outbreak

The war began after the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria by the Bosnian Serb student Gavrilo Princip in Sarajevo on 28 June 1914. The July Crisis escalated through alliance commitments and mobilisation timetables: Austria-Hungary declared war on Serbia on 28 July, Germany declared war on Russia (1 August) and France (3 August) and invaded Belgium, prompting Britain to declare war on Germany on 4 August 1914.

Course of the war

The German Schlieffen offensive was halted at the First Battle of the Marne in September 1914, and the Western Front settled into four years of attritional trench warfare — Verdun and the Somme in 1916 cost roughly two million casualties between them. Italy joined the Allies in 1915; the Gallipoli campaign and the Mesopotamian and Palestine campaigns engaged the Ottoman Empire. The Russian Revolution of 1917 took Russia out of the war (Treaty of Brest-Litovsk, March 1918), but the entry of the United States in April 1917, following the resumption of unrestricted submarine warfare and the Zimmermann Telegram, tipped the balance. Germany's Spring Offensive of 1918 failed, the Allied Hundred Days Offensive broke the German army, and Bulgaria, the Ottomans and Austria-Hungary sued for peace in turn. Germany signed the Armistice of 11 November 1918.

Aftermath

The peace settlement, above all the Treaty of Versailles (1919), redrew the map of Europe and the Middle East, dissolved the German, Austro-Hungarian, Ottoman and Russian empires, created the League of Nations, and imposed reparations and the "war guilt" clause on Germany — grievances that fed the instability leading to the Second World War twenty years later. The war transformed societies: mass female employment, the expansion of state power, and the influenza pandemic of 1918–19, which killed more people than the fighting itself.
