Quetzalcoatl

Quetzalcoatl (Nahuatl: Quetzalcōātl, "feathered serpent") is a deity in Aztec culture and literature. Among the Aztecs, he was related to gods of the wind, the planet Venus, the sun, merchants, arts, crafts, knowledge, and learning. He was also the patron god of the Aztec priesthood. He was one of several important gods in the Aztec pantheon, along with the gods Tezcatlipoca, Tlaloc, and Huitzilopochtli. The two other gods represented by the planet Venus are Tlaloc (ally and the god of rain) and Xolotl (psychopomp and its twin).

Feathered serpent deity

Worship of a feathered serpent deity is widespread across Mesoamerica. At Teotihuacan, the Temple of the Feathered Serpent (c. 200 CE) is adorned with serpent heads. The Maya equivalent was Kukulkan (Yucatec) or Qʼuqʼumatz (Kʼicheʼ). In Aztec myth Quetzalcoatl descended to the underworld, Mictlan, to retrieve the bones of earlier generations from which humankind was created, and he was associated with Ehecatl, the wind aspect that sweeps the road for the rain god.

Ce Acatl Topiltzin

Traditions also link Quetzalcoatl with a legendary Toltec priest-king, Ce Acatl Topiltzin, said to have ruled at Tula and to have departed eastward, in some accounts promising to return. The later claim that the Aztec emperor Moctezuma II identified Hernán Cortés with the returning god is now regarded by most historians as a post-conquest invention.
