Cacık (Turkish pronunciation: [dʒaˈdʒɯk]) is a Turkish dish of seasoned, diluted yogurt with cucumbers, similar to the Greek tzatziki but generally thinner in consistency. It is served cold as a side dish, meze or accompaniment to grilled meats and rice dishes, especially in summer.

Preparation

Cacık is made by combining strained or regular yogurt with finely chopped or grated cucumber, crushed garlic, salt and chopped herbs such as dill, mint or parsley. Water is often added to thin the mixture to a soup-like consistency, and it is finished with a drizzle of olive oil and sometimes a sprinkling of dried mint, sumac or red pepper flakes. In some regional versions, other greens such as lettuce or purslane replace or supplement the cucumber.

History and variants

Yogurt-and-cucumber dishes are found across the Balkans, the Middle East and South Asia, reflecting the shared culinary heritage of the Ottoman world. The Greek tzatziki uses thicker strained yogurt and is usually served as a dip, while Bulgarian tarator is a cold soup with walnuts. A related Turkish preparation, haydari, is much thicker and served as a meze.

Serving

Cacık is commonly served with kebabs, köfte, pilaf and bean dishes, and is valued as a cooling counterpoint to spicy or fatty food. It is also eaten on its own as a light cold soup.
