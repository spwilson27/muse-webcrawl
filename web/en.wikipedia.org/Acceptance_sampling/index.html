Acceptance sampling

Acceptance sampling
Field: Quality control / statistics
Purpose: Accept or reject lots
Standards: ANSI/ASQ Z1.4, ISO 2859

Acceptance sampling uses statistical sampling to determine whether to accept or reject a production lot of material. It has been a common quality control technique used in industry and particularly the military for contracts and procurement.

Method

A sampling plan specifies the sample size and the acceptance number: a random sample is drawn from the lot, inspected, and the lot is accepted if the number of defective items found does not exceed the threshold. Plans may be single, double, or multiple, and may sample by attributes or by variables.

Risks

Acceptance sampling involves two statistical risks: the producer's risk of rejecting a good lot and the consumer's risk of accepting a bad one. Operating characteristic (OC) curves describe a plan's performance, and the acceptable quality limit (AQL) is a key parameter.

History

The technique was developed during the Second World War by statisticians including Harold Dodge and Harry Romig at Bell Laboratories, and was codified in military standards such as MIL-STD-105.
