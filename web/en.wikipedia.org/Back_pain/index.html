Back pain

Back pain (Latin: dorsalgia) is pain felt in the back. It is among the most common reasons for visiting a doctor and for missed work worldwide, and most adults experience an episode of low back pain at some point in their lives.

Causes

Most back pain is mechanical or non-specific, arising from muscles, ligaments, joints or intervertebral discs without a serious underlying cause. Specific causes include disc herniation, spinal stenosis, fractures, infection, inflammatory disease such as ankylosing spondylitis, and, rarely, tumours. "Red flag" features such as unexplained weight loss, fever, bladder or bowel disturbance, or saddle numbness require prompt evaluation.

Classification

Pain is classified by duration as acute (under six weeks), subacute (six to twelve weeks) or chronic (over twelve weeks), and by location as neck pain, middle back pain and low back pain, with or without radiation into the leg (sciatica).

Management

For non-specific pain, guidelines advise reassurance, staying active, exercise therapy and simple analgesia; routine imaging is discouraged in the absence of red flags. Most acute episodes improve substantially within a few weeks. See also Vertebral column.
