Fonio

KingdomPlantae
FamilyPoaceae
GenusDigitaria
SpeciesD. exilis, D. iburua

Fonio is the term for two cultivated grasses in the genus Digitaria that are important crops in parts of West Africa: white fonio (Digitaria exilis) and black fonio (Digitaria iburua). They are notable both for their small grain size (about 1.5 mm) and for their rapid growth, maturing in as little as six to eight weeks. Fonio is considered one of the oldest cultivated grains in Africa and has been grown in the Fouta Djallon highlands of Guinea for thousands of years.

Cooked fonio, a staple grain of West Africa.

Cultivation and use

Fonio thrives on poor, shallow, and sandy soils where other cereals fail, and it is a staple for several million people in countries including Guinea, Mali, Burkina Faso, Senegal, and Nigeria. The grain is very small, which makes threshing and dehusking laborious—traditionally done by hand with mortar and pestle. Fonio is cooked into porridges and couscous-like dishes, brewed into beer, and ground into flour. It is prized for its nutty flavour and is considered a prestige food served at celebrations.

Nutrition

Fonio is rich in the amino acids methionine and cysteine, which are deficient in other major cereals, and it has a low glycemic index, making it suitable for people with diabetes. It is naturally gluten-free, a property that has driven recent interest in fonio as an export product and health food in Europe and North America.

Genomics

The genome of white fonio was sequenced in 2020, revealing it to be an allotetraploid. Researchers hope genomic resources will help improve this "orphan crop," which has received little formal breeding investment despite its importance to food security.
