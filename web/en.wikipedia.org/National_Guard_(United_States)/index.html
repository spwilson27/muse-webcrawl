National Guard (United States)

Founded: 13 December 1636 (as colonial militia regiments)

Components: Army National Guard; Air National Guard

Size: Approximately 430,000 personnel

Motto: "Always Ready, Always There!"

The National Guard is a state-based military force that becomes part of the reserve components of the United States Army and the United States Air Force when activated for federal missions. It consists of the Army National Guard and the Air National Guard, with units in all 50 states, the District of Columbia, and the territories of Puerto Rico, Guam and the U.S. Virgin Islands. National Guard units are under dual control: they normally operate under the authority of their state governor while also being organised, trained and equipped as federal reserve forces.

History

The National Guard traces its lineage to the militia regiments organised in the Massachusetts Bay Colony on 13 December 1636, regarded as its official birthdate. The Militia Act of 1903 (the Dick Act) organised state militias as the National Guard and set federal standards for their training and equipment. Guardsmen served in both world wars, Korea, Iraq and Afghanistan, as well as in domestic roles.

Activation and roles

Guard units may be activated by their governor for state emergencies such as natural disasters, civil unrest and public-health crises (State Active Duty or Title 32 status), or federalised by the president under Title 10 for overseas deployments and national defence. The National Guard Bureau, headed by a four-star general who is a member of the Joint Chiefs of Staff, administers federal support to the state guards.
