Tashilhunpo Monastery

Tashilhunpo Monastery in Shigatse, Tibet, seat of the Panchen Lama.

Tashilhunpo Monastery (Tibetan: བཀྲ་ཤིས་ལྷུན་པོ་), founded in 1447 by the 1st Dalai Lama, Gendun Drup, is a historically and culturally important monastery in Shigatse, the second-largest city in Tibet. It is the traditional seat of successive Panchen Lamas, the second-highest-ranking tulku lineage in the Gelug tradition of Tibetan Buddhism.

History

The monastery was founded in 1447 by Gendun Drup, the disciple of Tsongkhapa who was retroactively recognized as the first Dalai Lama. In 1600, the 4th Panchen Lama, Lobsang Chokyi Gyaltsen, became its abbot, and from that time it served as the seat of the Panchen Lamas. At its height the monastery housed several thousand monks.

Tashilhunpo suffered damage during the Cultural Revolution, though many of its major buildings survived or were restored. The monastery is famed for its Maitreya Chapel, built in 1914 under the 9th Panchen Lama, which contains a 26-metre-high statue of Maitreya, the future Buddha, among the largest gilded copper statues in the world.

Architecture and features

The complex covers some 150,000 square metres at the foot of Drolmari hill. Its principal buildings include the Maitreya Chapel, the Assembly Hall (Tsokchen), the chapels housing the stupas of the Panchen Lamas, and the Kelsang Temple courtyard, where monks engage in debate.
