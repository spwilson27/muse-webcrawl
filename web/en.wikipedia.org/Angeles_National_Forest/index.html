The Angeles National Forest (ANF) is a United States National Forest located in the San Gabriel Mountains and Sierra Pelona Mountains of Los Angeles County, California, immediately north of the Los Angeles metropolitan area. It covers about 655,387 acres and includes the San Gabriel Mountains National Monument within its boundaries.

Geography and ecology

The forest ranges from about 1,200 to 10,064 feet in elevation at Mount San Antonio (Mount Baldy). Much of it is covered with dense chaparral and oak woodland, with conifer forests at higher elevations. It provides habitat for bighorn sheep, black bears, mountain lions and the endangered mountain yellow-legged frog.

Recreation and hazards

With millions of visitors a year, the forest offers hiking, camping, skiing at Mount Baldy and Mount Waterman, and the Angeles Crest Highway scenic drive. Its proximity to a vast urban population also makes it one of the most fire-prone national forests; major fires, including the Station Fire of 2009 and the Bobcat Fire of 2020, have burned large areas.
