Fragile States Index

The Fragile States Index ranks countries by their vulnerability to conflict and instability.

The Fragile States Index (formerly the Failed States Index) is an annual ranking of countries by their susceptibility to conflict, instability or collapse, published by the American think tank The Fund for Peace and, from 2005 to 2018, in the magazine Foreign Policy. It assesses 179 countries and is widely cited by governments, aid agencies and media as an early-warning tool for state fragility.

Methodology

Each country is scored on twelve indicators, each rated 0–10 (for a maximum total of 120, with higher scores indicating greater fragility), grouped into four categories:

Cohesion: security apparatus, factionalised elites, group grievance. Economic: economic decline, uneven economic development, human flight and brain drain. Political: state legitimacy, public services, human rights and rule of law. Social and cross-cutting: demographic pressures, refugees and internally displaced persons, external intervention.

Scores are produced with the Fund for Peace's CAST (Conflict Assessment System Tool) framework, combining quantitative data, content analysis of news and reports, and expert review.

Rankings and criticism

Countries such as Yemen, Somalia, Syria, South Sudan and Sudan have occupied the most fragile positions in recent editions, while Nordic countries (Finland, Norway, Denmark) and Switzerland typically rank as most sustainable. Critics object that "fragility" rankings can be subjective, that the single composite score hides divergent indicator trends, and that the earlier "failed states" label was stigmatising — prompting the 2014 renaming.
