Team sport

A team sport is a sport in which two or more players work together as a team toward a shared objective, usually outscoring or outperforming an opposing team. Success depends not only on individual skill but on coordination, strategy, and role specialisation. Team sports include some of the world's most popular spectator activities, notably association football (soccer), basketball, cricket, rugby, ice hockey, volleyball, baseball, and American football.

Characteristics

Teams typically have a fixed or variable number of players on the field at once, defined positions or roles, and rules governing substitution. Training emphasises drills, set plays, communication, and collective tactics. Many team sports are governed by leagues and tournaments at school, amateur, collegiate, and professional levels, and several are Olympic sports.

Distinctions

The boundary with individual sports is not always sharp. Some sports such as rowing, cycling, and relay racing involve athletes competing in teams but with effort measured in essentially individual ways; doubles tennis and team competitions in golf or gymnastics are hybrids. Esports titles are frequently team-based as well.

Social role

Team sports are widely credited with teaching cooperation, leadership, and resilience, and they form a major part of physical education and community life around the world. They also underpin large professional entertainment industries.
