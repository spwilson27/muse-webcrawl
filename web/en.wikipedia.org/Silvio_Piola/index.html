Silvio Piola

Silvio Piola
Born: 29 September 1913, Robbio, Italy
Died: 4 October 1996 (aged 83), Gattinara, Italy
Position: Striker (centre-forward)
Clubs: Pro Vercelli, Lazio, Juventus, Novara
Italy caps: 34 · Goals: 30
Honour: 1938 FIFA World Cup winner

Silvio Piola (29 September 1913 – 4 October 1996) was an Italian footballer who played as a striker. He is the all-time top scorer in Serie A history, with 274 goals, and he won the 1938 FIFA World Cup with Italy. A prolific and physically strong centre-forward, Piola was famed for his spectacular overhead (bicycle) kicks — Italians still call such a shot a rovesciata alla Piola.

Club career

Piola began his career with Pro Vercelli, making his Serie A debut in 1930, and moved to Lazio in 1934, where he was twice Serie A top scorer (1936–37 and 1942–43). After a brief spell with Torino during the wartime championship, he played for Juventus (1945–1947) and finished his career with Novara, whom he helped win promotion to Serie A, retiring in 1954 at the age of 40. His record of 274 Serie A goals has never been surpassed; he also scored six goals in a single match for Pro Vercelli in 1933, a Serie A record shared only with Omar Sívori.

International career

Piola scored 30 goals in 34 appearances for Italy between 1935 and 1952. At the 1938 World Cup in France he scored five goals, including two in the final against Hungary, which Italy won 4–2 to retain the title. His international career, spanning 17 years, was interrupted by the Second World War, which cost him the chance of further tournaments.

Legacy

Stadiums in Vercelli and Novara bear his name, and he remains the benchmark for Italian centre-forwards: his Serie A scoring record has stood for decades despite the careers of later stars such as Francesco Totti, who finished second on the list.
