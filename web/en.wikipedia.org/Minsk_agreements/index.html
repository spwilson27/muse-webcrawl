The Minsk agreements were a series of international agreements which sought to end the Donbas war fought between armed Russian separatist groups and Armed Forces of Ukraine. The first agreement, known as the Minsk Protocol (Minsk I), was drafted by the Trilateral Contact Group on Ukraine, consisting of Ukraine, Russia, and the Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe (OSCE), with mediation by the leaders of France and Germany in the so-called Normandy Format. After extensive talks in Minsk, Belarus, it was signed on 5 September 2014 by representatives of Ukraine, the Russian Federation, the OSCE, and the leaders of the self-proclaimed Donetsk People's Republic (DPR) and Luhansk People's Republic (LPR).

Minsk I (2014)

The Protocol provided for an immediate ceasefire, release of hostages, withdrawal of illegal armed groups and military equipment from Ukraine, OSCE monitoring of the Ukrainian–Russian border, decentralisation of power in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts, and local elections. The ceasefire collapsed within weeks, and by January 2015 fighting had intensified around Donetsk International Airport and the rail hub of Debaltseve.

Minsk II (2015)

A second agreement, the "Package of Measures for the Implementation of the Minsk Agreements" (Minsk II), was negotiated by the leaders of Ukraine, Russia, France and Germany after a marathon summit in Minsk and signed on 12 February 2015. Its thirteen points included an immediate and comprehensive ceasefire, withdrawal of heavy weapons to create a security zone, OSCE verification, dialogue on local elections, amnesty, exchange of prisoners, restoration of Ukraine's control over its state border, withdrawal of foreign armed formations, and constitutional reform in Ukraine providing for decentralisation and a special status for parts of Donbas. UN Security Council Resolution 2202 endorsed the package.

Implementation disputes and failure

The agreements were never fully implemented. Russia and Ukraine disagreed over the sequence of steps: Ukraine insisted that security and border control precede elections, while Russia demanded political settlement first. Ambiguities about Russia's own status — party to the conflict or mediator — were never resolved. Ceasefires were violated thousands of times, and the "Steinmeier formula" (2019) failed to break the deadlock. In February 2022 Russia recognised the DPR and LPR as independent states, declaring the agreements no longer existed, and launched a full-scale invasion of Ukraine on 24 February 2022.
