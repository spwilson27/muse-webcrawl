Fort William Historical Park

Fort William Historical Park is a Canadian historical site located in Thunder Bay, Ontario. It is a reconstruction of the Fort William fur trade post, which was the inland headquarters of the North West Company from 1803 to 1821 and the centre of its vast fur-trading empire. The reconstructed fort, opened in 1973 on the banks of the Kaministiquia River, contains dozens of reconstructed buildings on its grounds.

History

The original Fort William was established after the North West Company moved its headquarters from Grand Portage, which had become United States territory. Named for William III, the post hosted the annual summer rendezvous at which Montreal-based agents met the wintering partners who traded with Indigenous peoples in the interior. After the North West Company merged with the Hudson's Bay Company in 1821, the post declined in importance.

The park today

The living-history museum portrays life at the post in 1816, with costumed interpreters representing company partners, clerks, voyageurs, artisans and Ojibwa community members. It is one of Ontario's major tourist attractions and hosts educational programmes and events.
