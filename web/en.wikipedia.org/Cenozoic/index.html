Cenozoic

The Cenozoic Era (also Cainozoic; meaning "new life") is the current and most recent of the three geological eras of the Phanerozoic Eon. It follows the Mesozoic Era and extends from 66 million years ago to the present day. The Cenozoic is also known as the Age of Mammals because the terrestrial animals that dominated both hemispheres were mammals — the eutherians (placentals) in the northern hemisphere and the marsupials in the southern hemisphere.

Subdivisions

The Cenozoic is divided into three periods: the Paleogene (66–23 million years ago), the Neogene (23–2.58 million years ago), and the Quaternary (2.58 million years ago to the present). These are further divided into epochs: Paleocene, Eocene and Oligocene; Miocene and Pliocene; and Pleistocene and Holocene (with the proposed Anthropocene as a successor).

Geology and climate

During the Cenozoic the continents assumed their modern positions: India collided with Asia raising the Himalayas, the Atlantic widened, and Australia separated from Antarctica. Global climate cooled from the greenhouse conditions of the early Eocene climatic optimum through the formation of the Antarctic ice sheet about 34 million years ago to the ice age cycles of the Pleistocene.

Life

After the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event eliminated the non-avian dinosaurs, mammals and birds diversified rapidly. Grasslands spread in the Miocene, favouring grazing animals; hominins appeared in Africa in the late Neogene, and anatomically modern humans evolved in the Quaternary.
