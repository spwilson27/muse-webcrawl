Dry Tortugas

The Dry Tortugas are a small group of islands in the Gulf of Mexico at the end of the Florida Keys, about 67 miles (108 km) west of Key West. Together with the surrounding waters they form Dry Tortugas National Park, one of the most remote U.S. national parks, accessible only by boat or seaplane.

Geography and history

Juan Ponce de León named the islands Las Tortugas ("the turtles") in 1513 for the sea turtles found there; "dry" was added because the islands lack fresh surface water. The main island, Garden Key, is dominated by Fort Jefferson, a massive unfinished coastal fortress built from 1846, which served as a Union prison during the American Civil War—its most famous prisoner was Dr. Samuel Mudd, convicted in connection with the Lincoln assassination.

Nature

The park protects coral reefs, shipwrecks, and important seabird colonies; nearby Bush Key hosts nesting sooty terns and brown noddies, and the waters shelter sea turtles, sharks, and rich reef fish communities popular with snorkelers and divers. The area was first protected as a bird reserve in 1908, became Fort Jefferson National Monument in 1935, and was redesignated Dry Tortugas National Park in 1992.
