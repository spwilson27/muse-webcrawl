Transparency

Transparency may refer to several distinct concepts in science, technology, politics and the arts. This article gives an overview of the principal meanings.

Optics and materials

In optics, transparency is the physical property of allowing light to pass through a material without appreciable scattering. A transparent material, such as glass or clear water, permits objects behind it to be seen clearly; the opposite property is opacity, and the intermediate state is translucency, in which light is transmitted but diffused. Transparency is determined by the absorption and scattering of light at the wavelengths concerned: many materials transparent to visible light absorb ultraviolet or infrared radiation.

Politics and business

In governance, transparency denotes openness in government and business: the disclosure of information, decisions, rules and financial accounts so that stakeholders, citizens and markets can monitor conduct and hold institutions accountable. Instruments of transparency include freedom of information laws, open data, published budgets, and disclosure requirements for corporations and public officials. Non-governmental organisations such as Transparency International (founded 1993) promote the concept worldwide and publish the annual Corruption Perceptions Index.

Other uses

In computer graphics and imaging, transparency refers to the alpha channel of an image, which records the degree to which each pixel is see-through. In radiology and photography, a transparency is a positive photographic image on a transparent base, such as a slide. In economics, market transparency describes the extent to which information about prices and trading is available to participants.
