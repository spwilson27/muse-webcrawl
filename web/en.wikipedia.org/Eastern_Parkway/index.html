Eastern Parkway

Eastern Parkway is a major boulevard in the borough of Brooklyn, New York City. Designed by Frederick Law Olmsted and Calvert Vaux, it is widely regarded as the world's first parkway, built between 1870 and 1874 as a tree-lined approach to Prospect Park.

Design

Olmsted coined the word "parkway" for the design: a broad central roadway flanked by service roads, with wide pedestrian malls shaded by rows of trees separating local from through traffic. The model was later adapted for parkways across the United States.

Route and neighbourhoods

The parkway runs from Grand Army Plaza at Prospect Park eastward through Crown Heights and Brownsville to Bushwick Avenue. Along it stand the Brooklyn Museum, the Brooklyn Botanic Garden, the central branch of the Brooklyn Public Library nearby, and 770 Eastern Parkway, headquarters of the Chabad-Lubavitch movement.

Events

Eastern Parkway hosts the annual West Indian Day Parade on Labor Day, one of New York's largest cultural festivals, drawing up to two million spectators.
