Keep

A keep is a type of fortified tower built within castles during the Middle Ages by European nobility. It was the strongest and most secure part of the castle, serving as a last refuge if the outer defences fell, and often contained the lord's residence and great hall.

Early keeps, such as the White Tower at the Tower of London (begun c. 1078), were massive square stone towers with thick walls, few windows and an entrance above ground level reached by external stairs. The French term donjon and English dungeon derive from the same root denoting the lord's tower.

Development

Stone keeps replaced timber motte-and-bailey towers from the 11th century. By the 13th and 14th centuries keeps became more elaborate residences, before artillery and changing warfare made them obsolete as fortifications, though many survived as symbols of lordship.
