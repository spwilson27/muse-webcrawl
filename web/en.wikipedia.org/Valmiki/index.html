Valmiki

Valmiki (Sanskrit: वाल्मीकि, Vālmīki) is traditionally regarded as the author of the Ramayana, one of the two Sanskrit epics of ancient India. He is revered as the Ādi Kavi ("First Poet") and is credited with inventing the shloka, the verse metre in which the epic is composed.

Legend

According to tradition, Valmiki was originally a highway robber named Ratnakara who was transformed into a sage after an encounter with the sage Narada. Instructed to chant the name of Rama, he meditated so long that anthills (Sanskrit: valmīka) grew over his body, giving him his name.

The invention of the shloka is attributed to Valmiki's spontaneous curse, uttered in verse after witnessing a hunter kill one of a pair of mating krauncha birds. The god Brahma is said to have then commissioned him to compose the life of Rama.

Role in the Ramayana

Within the epic itself, Valmiki shelters Sita at his hermitage after her exile and teaches her sons, Lava and Kusha, to recite the Ramayana. The first and last books of the received text are considered later additions by many scholars, though tradition attributes the whole work to him.

Legacy

Valmiki's birthday, Valmiki Jayanti, is celebrated on the full moon day of the Hindu month of Ashwin. He is especially venerated by the Valmiki community in India and by the Balmiki sect.
