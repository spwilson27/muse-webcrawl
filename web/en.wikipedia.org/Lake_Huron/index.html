Lake Huron is one of the five Great Lakes of North America. Hydrologically it is part of Lake Michigan–Huron, the largest lake in the world by surface area. Lake Huron's drainage basin lies in Ontario, Canada, and Michigan, United States. It is the second-largest of the Great Lakes by surface area and the third-largest freshwater lake on Earth.

Geography

Lake Huron is separated from Lake Michigan by the Straits of Mackinac, and from Lake Superior by the St. Marys River. Its major features include Georgian Bay, a large bay in the northeast, and the North Channel. Manitoulin Island, in the northern part of the lake, is the largest lake island in the world. The lake has the longest shoreline of any of the Great Lakes, about 6,157 km counting its 30,000 islands.

Water flows from Lake Huron southward through the St. Clair River, Lake St. Clair, and the Detroit River into Lake Erie.

History

The lake takes its name from the Huron (Wendat) people. French explorers called it La Mer Douce, "the freshwater sea". Étienne Brûlé probably reached it around 1615, and Samuel de Champlain followed. During the Great Lakes Storm of 1913, Lake Huron saw the worst losses: eight ships sank with all hands, and more than 230 sailors died across the lakes.

Ecology

The lake supports fisheries for lake trout, walleye, and whitefish, and has been affected by invasive species including zebra mussels, quagga mussels, and the round goby. Because of their shared basin, water levels in Huron and Michigan rise and fall together, reaching record highs in 2020 after record lows in 2013.
