Batch processing

Batch processing is a method of running computer programs ("jobs") in batches, without manual intervention. Inputs are collected in advance and processed together, rather than interactively one transaction at a time.

History and use

Batch processing dates to the era of punched cards and mainframes, when jobs were queued and run sequentially. Modern batch systems schedule large data-processing tasks — such as payroll, billing and end-of-day banking reconciliation — often overnight when computing resources are less contended. Scheduler tools such as cron and dedicated workload-automation platforms manage batch workloads; the term contrasts with real-time and interactive processing.
