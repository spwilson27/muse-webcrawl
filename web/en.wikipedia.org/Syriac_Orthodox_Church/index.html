Syriac Orthodox Church

The Syriac Orthodox Church (Classical Syriac: ܥܺܕܬܳܐ ܣܽܘܪܝܳܝܬܳܐ ܬܪܺܝܨܰܬ݂ ܫܽܘܒܚܳܐ), officially the Syriac Orthodox Patriarchate of Antioch and All the East, is an Oriental Orthodox church centered in Syria and the Middle East, with a worldwide diaspora and a large following in Kerala, India. It traces its origins to the ancient Church of Antioch and is headed by the Patriarch of Antioch, resident in Damascus.

History

The church's distinct identity emerged from the Christological controversies following the Council of Chalcedon (451). In the 6th century, Bishop Jacob Baradaeus reorganised the non-Chalcedonian (miaphysite) church in Syria and Mesopotamia, earning its members the nickname "Jacobites". The church flourished under early Islamic rule, producing a golden age of Syriac literature and scholarship, but suffered severe losses during the Mongol invasions, the Ottoman-era persecutions, and especially the Sayfo genocide of 1915 in southeastern Anatolia, which killed a large fraction of its faithful.

Theology and liturgy

The church is miaphysite in Christology, in communion with the other Oriental Orthodox churches. Its worship uses the West Syriac Rite, celebrated in Classical Syriac and local languages, with the Divine Liturgy attributed to St James the Just as its central service. The Holy Qurobo is marked by elaborate chant, incense, and ancient anaphoras, of which dozens survive.

Today

Since 2014 the patriarch has been Ignatius Aphrem II. Warfare in Syria and Iraq has driven heavy emigration, and today large communities exist in Sweden, Germany, the Netherlands, the United States, and Australia, alongside the historic communities of Syria, Lebanon, Turkey's Tur Abdin region, and the Malankara Jacobite Syrian Church in India.
