Zhuang Zedong (Chinese: 庄则栋; 25 August 1940 – 10 February 2013) was a Chinese table tennis player and politician. He won three consecutive men's singles titles at the World Table Tennis Championships (1961, 1963 and 1965) and became one of the most celebrated athletes in the People's Republic of China. He is best remembered internationally for his chance encounter with American player Glenn Cowan at the 1971 World Championships in Nagoya, an episode that helped spark "ping-pong diplomacy" between China and the United States.

Playing career

Zhuang rose to prominence in the late 1950s with an attacking penhold style and a notably fast two-winged attack, unusual for penhold players of his era. At the 1961 World Championships in Beijing he defeated compatriot Li Furong in the final to take his first world title, repeating the feat in Prague in 1963 and Ljubljana in 1965. He also won numerous team and doubles titles with the Chinese national team.

Ping-pong diplomacy

At the 1971 World Championships, Zhuang greeted American player Glenn Cowan on the Chinese team bus and presented him with a silk portrait of the Huangshan mountains. Photographs of the exchange were published worldwide, and days later the American team received an invitation to visit China — the first such visit by Americans since 1949. The episode paved the way for Richard Nixon's 1972 visit to China.

Political career and later life

Zhuang later served as head of the State Physical Culture and Sports Commission (1974–1976), effectively China's sports minister. Closely associated with Jiang Qing during the Cultural Revolution, he was purged after 1976 and spent several years in detention and exile in Shanxi. He later worked as a youth table tennis coach in Beijing and died of colorectal cancer in 2013.
