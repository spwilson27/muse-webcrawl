The United States Border Patrol (USBP) is a federal law enforcement agency of the United States Department of Homeland Security, within U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP). It is responsible for securing the borders of the United States between ports of entry — primarily the land borders with Mexico and Canada — against unlawful entry of people and contraband.

History

The Border Patrol was established on 28 May 1924 by the Labor Appropriation Act of 1924, initially within the Department of Labor, with 450 agents tasked with enforcing the Immigration Act of 1924 and alcohol prohibition along the borders. Early agents were recruited from among Texas Rangers, local sheriffs, and members of the mounted customs inspector service that had patrolled the southern border since 1904.

The agency was transferred to the Department of Justice in 1940 as part of the Immigration and Naturalization Service. Following the September 11 attacks, the Border Patrol became part of the new Department of Homeland Security in 2003, when CBP was created. Its mission expanded explicitly to include preventing terrorists and weapons of mass destruction from entering the United States.

Organization and operations

The Border Patrol is organized into sectors — 20 along the southwest and northern borders and in coastal areas — subdivided into stations. The largest sector by staffing is the Rio Grande Valley sector in South Texas. Agents patrol using vehicles, horses, all-terrain vehicles, boats, and aircraft, supported by ground sensors, cameras, and drones.

Under its national strategy, the agency emphasizes a risk-based, intelligence-driven approach combining personnel, technology, and infrastructure such as border fencing. Its authority to conduct patrols and checkpoints extends within a "reasonable distance" of the border, defined by regulation as 100 air miles, though its primary operations occur much closer to the boundary.

Specialized units include BORTAC (the Border Patrol Tactical Unit), BORSTAR (search, trauma, and rescue teams), horse patrols, and K-9 units.

Staffing

The Border Patrol grew rapidly in the 2000s, roughly doubling in size between 2004 and 2011, and has employed around 19,000–21,000 agents in recent years, the vast majority assigned to the southwest border. Agents are trained at the Border Patrol Academy in Artesia, New Mexico, one of the Federal Law Enforcement Training Centers.
