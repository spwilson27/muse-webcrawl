JAMA: The Journal of the American Medical Association is a peer-reviewed medical journal published weekly by the American Medical Association. Founded in 1883, it is one of the most widely circulated and influential general medical journals in the world, read by physicians, researchers, and health policymakers.

History

The journal began publication in 1883 under the editorship of Nathan Smith Davis, a founder of the AMA. For most of its history it was titled The Journal of the American Medical Association; in 1960 the title was shortened to the acronym JAMA. Long-serving editors have included Morris Fishbein, who shaped the journal for decades, and George D. Lundberg. The journal has published landmark studies in fields from infectious disease to clinical trials methodology.

Contents and network

JAMA publishes original research, clinical reviews, editorials, opinion pieces ("Viewpoints"), medical news, and patient information pages. Since the 1990s the AMA has expanded the JAMA brand into the JAMA Network, a family of specialty journals including JAMA Internal Medicine, JAMA Pediatrics, JAMA Psychiatry, JAMA Cardiology, JAMA Oncology, JAMA Neurology, JAMA Surgery, JAMA Dermatology, JAMA Ophthalmology, and the open-access JAMA Network Open (launched 2018).

Influence

The journal has an impact factor among the highest of general medical journals and its articles frequently shape clinical guidelines and public debate. JAMA enforces strict disclosure and peer-review standards and is a member of the International Committee of Medical Journal Editors.
