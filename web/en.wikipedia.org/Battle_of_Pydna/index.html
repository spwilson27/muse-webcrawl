Battle of Pydna

The Battle of Pydna took place on 22 June 168 BC during the Third Macedonian War between the Roman Republic, commanded by Lucius Aemilius Paullus, and King Perseus of Macedon. The decisive Roman victory ended the Antigonid dynasty, destroyed Macedonian independence, and established Roman supremacy in the Greek world.

Background

Perseus had renewed Macedonian influence in Greece, alarming Rome. After two indecisive campaigning seasons, the veteran consul Aemilius Paullus took command in 168 BC and manoeuvred Perseus out of his strong position on the River Elpeus, forcing battle on the plain near the coastal town of Pydna in southern Macedonia.

The battle

The Macedonian phalanx, some 21,000 men with long sarissa pikes, initially drove the Romans back and even broke the first line. But as the phalanx advanced over uneven ground its formation opened gaps; Paullus ordered his maniples to infiltrate the phalanx in small groups, where the Roman short sword was lethal against pikemen who could not manoeuvre. The Macedonian cavalry fled almost unused, and the phalanx was slaughtered — some 20,000–25,000 Macedonians died, against roughly a hundred Roman dead.

Aftermath

Perseus was captured and paraded in Paullus's triumph in Rome. Macedonia was divided into four client republics and later annexed as a province. Pydna is conventionally taken to demonstrate the superiority of the flexible Roman legion over the Hellenistic phalanx.
