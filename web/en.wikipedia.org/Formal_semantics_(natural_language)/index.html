Formal semantics (natural language)

Formal semantics is the study of grammatical meaning in natural languages using formal tools from logic and theoretical computer science. It is an interdisciplinary field, sometimes regarded as a subfield of both linguistics and philosophy of language. It provides accounts of what linguistic expressions mean and how those meanings are composed from the meanings of their parts, following the principle of compositionality.

History

The modern field grew out of the work of logicians such as Gottlob Frege, Alfred Tarski and Rudolf Carnap, and was brought into linguistics largely through Richard Montague's work in the 1970s, which treated fragments of English with the same model-theoretic techniques used for formal languages ("Montague grammar"). Barbara Partee and others helped establish formal semantics within mainstream generative linguistics.

Topics

Major topics include quantification and generalized quantifiers, reference and anaphora, tense and aspect, modality, conditionals, presupposition, implicature, plurality, event semantics, and the semantics of questions. Approaches include truth-conditional semantics, possible-worlds semantics, dynamic semantics, and type-theoretic frameworks such as those based on the lambda calculus.
