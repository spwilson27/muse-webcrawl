Percival Baxter

Percival Proctor Baxter (November 22, 1876 – June 12, 1969) was an American politician and philanthropist from Maine. A Republican, he served as the 53rd governor of Maine from 1921 to 1925. He is best remembered for his donation of the land that became Baxter State Park.

Early life and career

Born in Portland, Maine, to a wealthy family — his father James Phinney Baxter was a historian and mayor of Portland — Baxter attended Bowdoin College and Harvard Law School. He served in the Maine House of Representatives and Senate before becoming governor, succeeding to the office when Governor Frederic Hale Parkhurst died shortly after inauguration.

Baxter State Park

Beginning in 1930, Baxter purchased over 200,000 acres around Mount Katahdin, Maine's highest mountain, and deeded the land to the state in a series of gifts through 1962, stipulating that it remain "forever wild". Baxter State Park is his enduring legacy.

Other philanthropy

Baxter also founded the Governor Baxter School for the Deaf and championed animal welfare causes.
