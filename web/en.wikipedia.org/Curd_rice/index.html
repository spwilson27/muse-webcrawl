Curd rice

Curd rice, also called yogurt rice, is a dish originating from India. It is most popular in the South Indian states of Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka, Telangana, and Andhra Pradesh, and also in Maharashtra. The word "curd" in Indian English refers to unsweetened plain yogurt.

Preparation

In its simplest form, curd rice is prepared by mixing steamed white rice with plain curd and salt. More elaborate preparations temper spices in oil — mustard seeds, cumin seeds, dried red chillies, curry leaves, urad dal, and asafoetida — and fold them into the mixture. Common garnishes include pomegranate seeds, grated carrot, cucumber, raw mango, grapes, and cashews. The rice is often cooked until soft and slightly mashed so that it absorbs the curd and keeps well in hot weather.

Cultural role

Curd rice is traditionally eaten at the end of a South Indian meal and is believed to cool the stomach after spicy food and aid digestion. It is a standard offering (prasadam) in Hindu temples — in Tamil it is called thayir sadam — and is commonly packed for travel and picnics because it stays fresh longer in warm climates. It is frequently served with pickles, papad, or fried chillies.
