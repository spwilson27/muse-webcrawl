Short track motor racing

Short track motor racing is racing on oval circuits less than one mile in length.

Short track racing is a form of automobile racing on oval tracks measuring less than 1 mile (1.6 km) in length, commonly a quarter-mile, three-eighths-mile, half-mile or five-eighths-mile. Because of the small circuit length, speeds are lower than on superspeedways, the racing is usually close and physical, and spectators can see the entire track from the grandstands. Short tracks — paved with asphalt or concrete, or surfaced with dirt or clay — number in the hundreds across the United States and form the grassroots base of American stock car racing.

Classes and culture

Typical short track programmes feature classes such as late models, modifieds, street stocks, sprint cars, midgets and legends cars, usually run as weekly local series with heat races and feature events. Many top NASCAR drivers began their careers at short tracks, and tracks like Martinsville Speedway (a half-mile) and Bristol Motor Speedway anchor the NASCAR national calendar, while historic local tracks such as Bowman Gray Stadium are famous for their intense, bumping-and-banging racing. Dirt short track racing, sanctioned by bodies such as the World of Outlaws, is a major branch of the sport in its own right.
