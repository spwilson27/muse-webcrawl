Mobutu Sese Seko Kuku Ngbendu wa za Banga (born Joseph-Désiré Mobutu; 14 October 1930 – 7 September 1997) was a Congolese military officer and politician who was the second and last President of the Democratic Republic of the Congo from 1965 to 1971 and President of Zaire from 1971 to 1997. His 32-year rule was characterised by a personality cult, African nationalism under the doctrine of "authenticity" (authenticité), widespread corruption and kleptocracy, and — for much of the Cold War — staunch anti-communism that earned him Western support.

Rise to power

Mobutu served in the colonial Force Publique and later as a journalist before becoming army chief of staff at independence in 1960. During the Congo Crisis he helped depose Prime Minister Patrice Lumumba, who was murdered in 1961. On 25 November 1965 Mobutu seized full power in a bloodless coup, declaring the Second Republic.

Rule

In 1971 he renamed the country Zaire, ordered citizens to adopt African names (renaming himself Mobutu Sese Seko), and required men to wear the abacost suit instead of Western dress. His party, the MPR, was the sole legal party and Mobutu its unchallengeable head. The 1973 "Zairianisation" campaign expropriated foreign-owned businesses and precipitated economic decline. Mobutu amassed a personal fortune estimated in the billions of dollars while national infrastructure crumbled; the term "kleptocracy" is frequently illustrated by his regime. He hosted the "Rumble in the Jungle" boxing match between Muhammad Ali and George Foreman in Kinshasa in 1974.

Fall and death

With the Cold War over, Western backing waned. In 1996–97, rebels led by Laurent-Désiré Kabila, backed by Rwanda and Uganda, advanced across Zaire in the First Congo War. Mobutu fled Kinshasa in May 1997 and died of prostate cancer in exile in Rabat, Morocco, on 7 September 1997.
