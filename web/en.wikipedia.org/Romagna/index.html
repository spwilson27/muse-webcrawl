Romagna (Romagnol: Rumâgna) is an Italian historical region that approximately corresponds to the south-eastern portion of present-day Emilia-Romagna in northern Italy. Together with Emilia, it forms the administrative region of Emilia-Romagna, though the two areas retain distinct histories, dialects and identities.

Geography

Romagna extends from the Adriatic coast westward to the Tuscan-Emilian Apennines, and from the River Reno and the Po delta southward toward the Republic of San Marino and the Marche. Its principal cities are Ravenna, Forlì, Cesena, Rimini, Faenza and Imola. The Adriatic coast, known as the Riviera Romagnola, is one of Italy's most developed seaside resort areas.

History

The name derives from Romània, the land of the Romans, referring to the territory that remained under Byzantine (Eastern Roman) rule after the Lombard invasions of Italy. Ravenna was the seat of the Exarchate of Ravenna and before that, from 402 AD, a capital of the Western Roman Empire. In the Middle Ages and Renaissance the region was divided among competing signorie, including the Malatesta of Rimini, the Ordelaffi of Forlì and the Manfredi of Faenza, before passing largely to the Papal States. Romagna joined the Kingdom of Italy in 1860.

Culture

Romagna is known for its cuisine, including piadina flatbread, cappelletti pasta and Sangiovese wine. The Romagnol language, distinct from Italian and from Emilian, is still spoken especially in rural areas. The region was the birthplace of the poet Giovanni Pascoli and the film director Federico Fellini, who was born in Rimini.
