Tribe of Benjamin

According to the Torah, the Tribe of Benjamin (Hebrew: בִּנְיָמִן, Binyamin) was one of the Twelve Tribes of Israel. The tribe was descended from Benjamin, the youngest son of the patriarch Jacob and his wife Rachel, born in Canaan in a place between Bethel and Ephrath. The Tribe of Benjamin, located to the north and east of the Tribe of Judah (its "border town" was Bethlehem) and south of the Tribe of Ephraim, had strategic significance as it stood on the main north–south trade route and was the connecting link between Ephraim, the main northern tribe, and Judah, the main southern tribe. Jerusalem lay within Benjaminite territory according to some biblical boundary lists.

Biblical narrative

After the death of Moses, Joshua led the Israelites into the Promised Land and divided the territory among the twelve tribes, with Benjamin receiving a small but central allotment that included Jericho and Bethel. During the period of the Judges, the tribe was nearly destroyed in the civil war described in Judges 19–21 (the Levite's concubine incident), in which the other tribes fought against Benjamin at Gibeah. The tribe recovered and later produced Saul, the first king of Israel. The apostle Paul described himself as being of the tribe of Benjamin.

After the monarchy

Following the division of the kingdom after Solomon's reign, Benjamin joined with Judah to form the southern Kingdom of Judah, and the tribe largely shared Judah's fate, including the Babylonian exile and return.
