Ghana, officially the Republic of Ghana, is a country in West Africa. It lies adjacent to the Gulf of Guinea and the Atlantic Ocean to the south, sharing borders with Ivory Coast in the west, Burkina Faso in the north, and Togo in the east. Ghana covers an area of 238,533 square kilometres (92,098 sq mi) and spans savanna, forest, and coastal biomes. With over 33 million inhabitants, it is the second-most populous country in West Africa after Nigeria. The capital and largest city is Accra; other major cities include Kumasi, Tamale, Sekondi-Takoradi, and Sunyani.

History

The territory of modern Ghana was home to powerful precolonial states, most notably the Ashanti Empire (Asanteman), founded in the late 17th century under Osei Tutu and the priest Okomfo Anokye, with its capital at Kumasi. Europeans arrived in 1471, when the Portuguese built the castle of Elmina (1482) to trade in gold — giving the coast its colonial name, the Gold Coast — and later in enslaved people. Britain gradually established control during the 19th century, defeating the Ashanti in a series of wars, and formally proclaimed the Gold Coast a crown colony in 1874.

After the Second World War, the independence movement led by Kwame Nkrumah and his Convention People's Party pressed for self-government. On 6 March 1957 the Gold Coast became independent as Ghana — the first sub-Saharan African colony to achieve independence — taking its name from the medieval Ghana Empire. In 1960 Ghana became a republic with Nkrumah as president. His government was overthrown in 1966, beginning two decades of alternating military and civilian rule, until the Fourth Republic was established in 1993. Since then Ghana has been regarded as one of Africa's most stable democracies, with multiple peaceful transfers of power between the two main parties, the NDC and the NPP.

Geography

Ghana lies just north of the Equator, giving it a warm tropical climate. The country includes Lake Volta, one of the largest artificial lakes in the world, created by the Akosombo Dam on the Volta River in 1965. The south is forested and humid; the north is drier savanna. Wildlife areas include Kakum National Park, famous for its canopy walkway, and Mole National Park, known for elephants.

Economy

Ghana is a major producer of gold — among the largest in Africa — and cocoa, of which it is the world's second-largest exporter after Ivory Coast. Oil production from the offshore Jubilee field began in 2010. Accra has grown as a regional hub for technology and services; the country has also promoted tourism and diaspora engagement, notably with the 2019 "Year of Return" initiative.

Demographics and culture

Major ethnic groups include the Akan (about 47 percent of the population), Mole-Dagbon, Ewe, and Ga-Dangme peoples. English is the official language, while Akan languages such as Twi are widely spoken; altogether more than 80 languages are in use. Roughly 71 percent of Ghanaians are Christian, about 20 percent Muslim, and the remainder follow traditional religions or others. Ghanaian culture is renowned for kente cloth, highlife and hiplife music, and a strong football tradition — the national team, the Black Stars, has qualified for several FIFA World Cups.
