Robert E. Lee Monument (Richmond, Virginia)

The Robert E. Lee Monument was a monumental bronze equestrian statue of Confederate general Robert E. Lee on Monument Avenue in Richmond, Virginia, erected in 1890. It was the largest and best-known of Richmond's Confederate monuments and the first installed on Monument Avenue.

History

The statue, by French sculptor Antonin Mercié, depicted Lee on his horse Traveller atop a tall granite pedestal. Unveiled on 29 May 1890 before a huge crowd, it became a symbol both of the Lost Cause and, to critics, of white supremacy. After the murder of George Floyd in 2020, the monument's pedestal was covered in graffiti and the site became a focal point of protests in the former Confederate capital.

Removal

Virginia's governor ordered the statue removed in June 2020; after litigation, the Virginia Supreme Court upheld the removal, and the statue was taken down on 8 September 2021. The graffiti-covered pedestal was removed in December 2021, and the statue was later transferred to the Black History Museum and Cultural Center of Virginia.
