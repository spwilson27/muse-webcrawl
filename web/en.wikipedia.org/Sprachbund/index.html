Sprachbund

A sprachbund (German: [ˈʃpʁaːxbʊnt], lit. "language federation"), also known as a linguistic area, area of linguistic convergence, or diffusion area, is a group of languages that share areal features resulting from proximity and language contact, rather than from common origin or genetic relationships.

Examples

The classic example is the Balkan sprachbund, in which Greek, Albanian, Romanian, Bulgarian, Macedonian and other languages share features such as a postposed definite article and loss of the infinitive, although they belong to different branches of Indo-European. Other well-studied areas include the South Asian, Mesoamerican, and Standard Average European linguistic areas.

History of the concept

The notion was developed by linguists of the Prague Circle; Nikolai Trubetzkoy proposed the term and distinguished such unions from language families in the 1920s.
