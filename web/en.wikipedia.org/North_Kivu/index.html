Country: Democratic Republic of the Congo
Capital: Goma
Area: 59,483 km²
Population: approx. 8 million (est.)
Languages: French (official), Swahili, Nande, Hunde, others

North Kivu (French: Nord-Kivu) is a province in the far east of the Democratic Republic of the Congo, bordering Lake Kivu to the south and Uganda and Rwanda to the east. Its capital and largest city is Goma, situated on the northern shore of Lake Kivu. The province is known for its dramatic mountain scenery, its volcanoes, and, since the 1990s, for protracted armed conflict.

Geography

North Kivu includes part of Virunga National Park, Africa's oldest national park and a UNESCO World Heritage Site, home to endangered mountain gorillas. Mount Nyiragongo, an active stratovolcano whose lava lake is among the world's largest, looms over Goma; its eruptions in 1977, 2002, and 2021 destroyed parts of the city and displaced hundreds of thousands.

Conflict

The province was deeply affected by the First and Second Congo Wars and their aftermath. Armed groups, including the Democratic Forces for the Liberation of Rwanda (FDLR) and the March 23 Movement (M23), have fought the Congolese army and each other over territory, ethnic grievances, and control of mineral wealth such as coltan, gold, and cassiterite. Millions have been displaced, and a major Ebola outbreak struck the province in 2018–2020.

Economy

Agriculture is the mainstay of the population, supplemented by artisanal and industrial mining and cross-border trade with Rwanda and Uganda through Goma and the northern city of Butembo.
