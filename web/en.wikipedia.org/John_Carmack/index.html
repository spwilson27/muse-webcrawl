John D. Carmack II (born August 21, 1970) is an American computer programmer and video game developer. He co-founded the video game company id Software and was the lead programmer of its games Commander Keen, Wolfenstein 3D, Doom, Quake, and their sequels. Carmack made innovations in 3D computer graphics, such as his Carmack's Reverse algorithm for shadow volumes.

Carmack's engines pioneered techniques including adaptive tile refresh for side-scrolling platform games, ray casting (in Hovertank 3D and Wolfenstein 3D), binary space partitioning, surface caching, and binary space partitioning–based visibility in Doom and Quake. In 1999 he appeared as number 10 in Time's list of the 50 most influential people in technology.

Later career

In 2000 Carmack founded Armadillo Aerospace, a rocket company that competed for the Ansari X Prize and won NASA Lunar Lander Challenge prizes, before winding down in 2013. That year he joined Oculus VR as chief technology officer, helping drive the modern virtual-reality revival; he stepped back in 2019 to focus on artificial general intelligence, and in 2022 founded Keen Technologies to pursue AGI research.

Carmack is known for his intense work ethic, his public .plan file updates, and his advocacy of open-source software — id's game engines were released under the GPL. He received the BAFTA Fellowship in 2016.
