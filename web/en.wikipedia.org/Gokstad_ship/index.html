The Gokstad ship is a 9th-century Viking ship found in a burial mound at Gokstad farm near Sandefjord in Vestfold, Norway. Built around 890 AD of oak, it is one of the best-preserved Viking ships and the larger of the two great vessels excavated from Norwegian ship burials, the other being the Oseberg ship.

The ship

The vessel is 23.8 metres long and 5.1 metres broad, built clinker-fashion with 16 strakes, a single square sail of about 110 square metres, and 32 pairs of oars — the gunwale has 32 oar holes, each covered by a shutter. Sixty-four round shields were hung along the gunwales for the burial. Three small boats were buried with it.

The burial

The ship was interred in blue clay, which preserved the timber, as the grave ship of a chieftain who died around 900 AD. The burial chamber behind the mast contained a skeleton — a powerfully built man aged about 40–50, possibly Olaf Geirstad-Alf, a petty king of Vestfold. Grave goods had been partly plundered in antiquity, but the finds included beds, a sledge, tents, and the skeletons of horses, dogs and a peacock.

Legacy

Excavated in 1880 under Nicolay Nicolaysen, the ship was long displayed at the Viking Ship House on Bygdøy, Oslo. A full-scale replica, the Viking, crossed the Atlantic in 1893 to the World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago, proving the seaworthiness of the design.
