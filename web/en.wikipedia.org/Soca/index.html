Soca


Soca is the music of Trinidad and Tobago Carnival

Soca is a genre of music that originated in Trinidad and Tobago in the early 1970s. It is a fusion of calypso with Indian rhythms, thus combining the musical traditions of the Afro-Caribbean and South Asian cultures of that part of the Caribbean. The name is generally credited to its founder, Lord Shorty (Garfield Blackman), who called his fusion "solka" (soul of calypso), later spelled "soca".

History

As a calypsonian, Lord Shorty experimented throughout the 1960s with blending calypso with East Indian instrumentation and rhythms, recording "Indrani" in 1973 and releasing the album Endless Vibrations in 1974. His 1974 track "Endless Vibrations" and the album of the same name promoted the new sound. By the late 1970s soca had become the dominant music of Trinidad Carnival, with artists such as Calypso Rose—whose "Tempo" and other hits carried the genre—helping it spread across the Caribbean and its diaspora.

Styles

Soca encompasses many substyles. Groovy soca is slower and melodic, power soca is fast and built for carnival "jump and wave" dancing, and later forms include ragga soca, chutney soca (another Indo-Caribbean fusion), bouyon soca from Dominica, and jab jab music from Grenada. Annual Soca Monarch competitions in Trinidad award titles for both groovy and power categories.

Cultural role

Soca is the soundtrack of carnival in Trinidad and Tobago and of Caribbean carnivals worldwide, including Toronto Caribbean Carnival, Notting Hill Carnival in London, and Brooklyn's Labor Day Parade. Major performers include Machel Montano, Bunji Garlin, Fay-Ann Lyons, Destra Garcia, and Alison Hinds. The genre has influenced and blended with dancehall, Afrobeat, and electronic dance music.
