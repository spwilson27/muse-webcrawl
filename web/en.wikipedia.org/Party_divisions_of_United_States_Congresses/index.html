Party divisions of United States Congresses

The party divisions of United States Congresses record the political party affiliations in the United States Congress — comprising the Senate, with 100 members, and the House of Representatives, with 435 members — for each two-year Congress from the 1st Congress of 1789–1791 to the present. The tables summarize which party held a majority in each chamber, how the seats were divided, and who served as president during each Congress.

Party control of the Senate and House over time.

Overview

For most of American history, two major parties have dominated Congress. In the early republic the contest was between Federalists and Democratic-Republicans; after the collapse of the Federalists and the later Whig Party, the durable rivalry between the Democratic Party and the Republican Party emerged in the 1850s and has continued ever since. Periods of unified control, in which one party holds the presidency and both chambers, alternate with periods of divided government.

Historical patterns

The Democratic Party dominated Congress for much of the mid-20th century, holding the House almost continuously from 1931 to 1995, with brief Republican interruptions. The Republican Revolution of 1994 ended that era, and since then control of both chambers has changed hands repeatedly. Mid-term changes, vacancies, deaths, resignations, and party switches can alter divisions within a Congress from the figures recorded at its opening.
