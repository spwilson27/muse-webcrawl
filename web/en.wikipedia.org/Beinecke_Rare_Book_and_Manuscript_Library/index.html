Beinecke Rare Book and Manuscript Library

The Beinecke Rare Book and Manuscript Library (/ˈbaɪnɪki/) is the rare book library and literary archive of the Yale Library in New Haven, Connecticut. It is one of the largest buildings in the world dedicated to rare books and manuscripts and is the main repository for a number of major collections held by Yale, including the papers of many prominent writers. Established by a gift from the Beinecke family — Edwin J. Beinecke, Frederick W. Beinecke, and Walter Beinecke — it opened in 1963. Its celebrated modernist building, designed by Gordon Bunshaft of Skidmore, Owings & Merrill, is clad in translucent Vermont marble panels that protect the collections from direct sunlight while diffusing a warm glow into the interior.

Architecture

The building's windowless cube is faced with thin panels of veined white marble set in a granite grid, glowing amber in sunlight. Inside, a central glass tower six storeys high displays some 180,000 volumes in a climate-controlled stack, visible from a public exhibition mezzanine. Below ground are reading rooms, offices, and additional stacks. A sunken courtyard by Isamu Noguchi features abstract marble sculptures symbolising time, sun, and chance.

Collections

The Beinecke holds one of Yale's two copies of the Gutenberg Bible (the first major book printed with movable type in the West), the mysterious Voynich manuscript, and the Vinland Map (now considered a forgery). Its literary archives include the papers of James Joyce, Ezra Pound, Gertrude Stein, Langston Hughes, Thornton Wilder, and many others, as well as extensive collections of early Americana, British literature, and Western Americana.

Access

Although the collections are non-circulating, the library's reading room is open to accredited researchers, and exhibitions in the public spaces are free and open to visitors. The building underwent a major renovation in 2015–16 that added teaching spaces and upgraded its systems.
