San Miguel de Allende is a city and municipality in the far eastern part of Guanajuato, Mexico. A part of the Bajío region, it lies about 274 kilometres (170 mi) northwest of Mexico City and 97 kilometres (60 mi) from the state capital of Guanajuato. The city was founded in 1542 by the Franciscan friar Juan de San Miguel as San Miguel el Grande, a mission and military outpost protecting the silver road to Zacatecas.

It was renamed in honour of Ignacio Allende, a hero of the Mexican War of Independence who was born in the city in 1769. Together with the Sanctuary of Jesús Nazareno de Atotonilco nearby, San Miguel was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2008 for its well-preserved Baroque and Neoclassical colonial architecture.

Landmarks

The city's emblem is the parish church of San Miguel Arcángel, La Parroquia, whose pink limestone neo-Gothic towers — built by indigenous stonemason Zeferino Gutiérrez in the late 19th century — rise over the main square, El Jardín. Other notable buildings include the Casa del Mayorazgo de la Canal, the Instituto Allende art school, and numerous colonial mansions around the historic centre. The city was named a Pueblo Mágico in 2002, a designation later superseded by the World Heritage listing.

Culture and population

San Miguel is noted for its art schools, festivals — including the September celebrations of its patron saint with the traditional Alborada fireworks — and a large community of foreign retirees and artists, principally from the United States and Canada, drawn since the mid-20th century by the Instituto Allende and the Escuela de Bellas Artes. The expatriate population has significantly influenced the local real-estate market and cultural life.
