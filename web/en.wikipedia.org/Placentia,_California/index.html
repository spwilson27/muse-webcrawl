Placentia is a city in northern Orange County, California, United States. Its name derives from the Latin word for "pleasant," and the city's motto is "A Pleasant Place to Live." Historically an agricultural community known for citrus, walnuts, avocados, and Valencia oranges, Placentia is now a largely suburban city within the Los Angeles metropolitan area.

History

The area formed part of the Rancho San Juan Cajón de Santa Ana land grant in the Mexican era. In 1865 Daniel Kraemer became the first English-speaking pioneer to purchase land in the district, followed by William McFadden in 1868. By 1910 the community covered about 500 acres of farmland. The arrival of the Atchison, Topeka and Santa Fe Railway and the packing of Valencia oranges — Placentia was a major citrus packing centre — spurred growth, and the city incorporated in 1926.

Geography and demographics

Placentia is bordered by Anaheim to the south, Fullerton to the west, Brea and Yorba Linda to the north and east. According to the 2020 United States census, the city had a population of roughly 51,000, with a diverse demographic mix and a median household income above the national average.

Education and landmarks

The city is served by the Placentia-Yorba Linda Unified School District and is home to El Dorado High School. Notable sites include the A.S. Bradford House, a historic Queen Anne-style home, and Kraemer Memorial Park.
