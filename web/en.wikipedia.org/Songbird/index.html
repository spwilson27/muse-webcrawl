SuborderPasseri (oscines)

A songbird is a bird belonging to the suborder Passeri (the oscines) of the perching birds (Passeriformes). With roughly 5,000 species — about half of all bird species — songbirds are among the most successful groups of terrestrial vertebrates, found on every continent except Antarctica. They are distinguished by a highly developed vocal organ, the syrinx, which in many species produces elaborate and learned song.

Song and learning

Songbirds are one of the few animal groups that learn their vocalisations, alongside parrots and hummingbirds. Young birds pass through a babbling phase and crystallise their adult song by imitating tutors, often forming local dialects. Song serves to defend territories and attract mates; in many temperate species only males sing, though research increasingly shows female song is widespread, especially in the tropics. Two groups of muscles and independent control of the two sides of the syrinx allow some species to sing two notes at once or rapidly varying phrases.

Evolution and families

Molecular studies indicate songbirds originated in Australasia and spread worldwide. Major families include the lyrebirds and scrub-birds (basal lineages), and the great radiation of corvids, thrushes, warblers, finches, sparrows, starlings, and the New World blackbirds and warblers. Familiar songbirds include the nightingale, the European robin, the common blackbird, and the zebra finch — a model organism for the neuroscience of vocal learning.
