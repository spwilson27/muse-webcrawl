Pu'er tea

Pu'er or pu-erh tea is a variety of fermented tea traditionally produced in Yunnan province, China, from large-leaf varieties of Camellia sinensis var. assamica. It is named after the city of Pu'er, a historic trading post for tea. Unlike most teas, pu'er is commonly pressed into cakes, bricks or discs and is often aged, with its flavour changing over years of storage.

Types

Two main types are produced. Raw (sheng) pu'er is sun-dried and compressed, then gradually oxidises and ferments through microbial activity during storage. Ripe (shou) pu'er, developed in the 1970s at Kunming Tea Factory, undergoes an accelerated wet-piling fermentation (wo dui) that mimics aged flavour in a matter of months.

Production and trade

After picking, leaves are pan-fired to halt most enzymatic activity, rolled, sun-dried into maocha, and then compressed or piled. The mid-2000s saw a speculative bubble in pu'er prices followed by a sharp crash in 2007. Genuine pu'er tea production is restricted by geographical indication to Yunnan.

Preparation

Pu'er is typically brewed gongfu-style with short repeated infusions, rinsing the leaves first. Aged raw pu'er is prized for camphor, wood and earthy notes.
