Corporal punishment

Corporal punishment is a punishment intended to cause physical pain to a person. Historically applied to adults by courts and to children by parents and schools, it includes spanking, caning, flogging and similar methods.

Judicial corporal punishment

Judicial corporal punishment remains lawful in some countries, notably Singapore, Malaysia and Brunei, where caning is imposed for offences such as vandalism and drug trafficking. It has been abolished in most Western nations.

Children

As of the 2020s more than 60 countries have banned all corporal punishment of children, beginning with Sweden in 1979. Domestic corporal punishment by parents remains legal in the United States and many other jurisdictions, though school corporal punishment is banned in most developed countries.

Arguments

Opponents cite evidence linking it to harm and aggression; defenders argue for parental rights and deterrence. The UN Convention on the Rights of the Child is interpreted as requiring prohibition.
