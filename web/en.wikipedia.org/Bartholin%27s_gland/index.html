Bartholin's gland

The Bartholin's glands, also called the greater vestibular glands, are two pea-sized glands located slightly posterior to and to the left and right of the opening of the vagina in women. They secrete mucus to lubricate the vagina.

Anatomy and clinical aspects

The glands were first described in the 17th century by the Danish anatomist Caspar Bartholin the Younger. They are homologous to the bulbourethral glands (Cowper's glands) in males. If the duct becomes blocked, a Bartholin's cyst can form, which may become infected and develop into an abscess requiring drainage or marsupialisation.
