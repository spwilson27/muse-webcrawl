Empress Elisabeth of Austria (born Duchess Elisabeth Amalie Eugenie in Bavaria, 24 December 1837 – 10 September 1898), nicknamed Sisi or Sissi, was Empress of Austria and Queen of Hungary as the wife of Emperor Franz Joseph I. Born into the Bavarian House of Wittelsbach, she married Franz Joseph at sixteen in 1854 and became one of the most famous — and most mythologised — royal consorts of nineteenth-century Europe.

Life

Elisabeth was raised in the comparatively informal ducal branch of the Bavarian royal family at Possenhofen. Her marriage brought her into conflict with the rigid Habsburg court and her formidable mother-in-law, Archduchess Sophie. She became closely associated with Hungary, learned Hungarian, and played a part in the Austro-Hungarian Compromise of 1867, after which she and Franz Joseph were crowned in Budapest; she was presented with Gödöllő Palace, her favourite residence.

Obsessed with maintaining her celebrated beauty and slender figure, Elisabeth practised demanding exercise regimes and elaborate hair care, and increasingly withdrew from court life, travelling restlessly through Europe. Her life was marked by tragedy: two of her children died young, and her only son, Crown Prince Rudolf, died in the Mayerling incident of 1889, after which she wore mourning and withdrew further.

Assassination and legacy

On 10 September 1898, while walking incognito in Geneva, Elisabeth was stabbed with a sharpened file by the Italian anarchist Luigi Lucheni and died. Her beauty, melancholy and violent death created a lasting cult: statues and museums across Austria and Hungary, and the popular Sissi film trilogy of the 1950s starring Romy Schneider, reshaped her image for modern audiences.
