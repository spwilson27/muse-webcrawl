The Bic Cristal (styled as Bic Cristal) is an inexpensive disposable ballpoint pen mass-produced and sold by Société Bic of Clichy, Hauts-de-Seine, France. It was introduced in December 1950 and is the best-selling pen in the world, with the 100 billionth sold in September 2006.

Design

The Cristal has a transparent polystyrene hexagonal barrel, modelled on a traditional pencil to prevent rolling, which shows the ink level. A small hole in the barrel equalizes air pressure inside and outside the pen. The ballpoint is made of tungsten carbide, and the cap, originally the source of the pen's colour coding, includes a hole introduced in 1991 to reduce the risk of suffocation if swallowed.

History

Marcel Bich bought a factory in Clichy in 1944 and, after the war, purchased the patent for the ballpoint pen from László Bíró. Using Swiss watchmaking equipment to precision-form the tiny ball bearings, Bic launched the Cristal in 1950, dropping the final "h" of Bich's name for the brand.

Cultural status

The pen's design is included in the permanent collection of the Museum of Modern Art in New York. It has become a ubiquitous everyday object and a common subject for artists, including works built from thousands of pens, and "bic" has become a generic term for a ballpoint pen in several languages.
