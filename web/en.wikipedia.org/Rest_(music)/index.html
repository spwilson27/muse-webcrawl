Rest (music)

A rest is a symbol in musical notation indicating a measured silence, and by extension the interval of silence itself. Rests have rhythmic values corresponding to notes: a quarter rest occupies the same duration as a quarter note, although no pitch is sounded.

Types

RestCorresponding note value
Whole restWhole note
Half restHalf note
Quarter restQuarter note
Eighth restEighth note
Sixteenth restSixteenth note

A whole rest is a rectangle hanging from a staff line; a half rest is a similar rectangle sitting on a line. In a complete bar of many time signatures, a whole-bar rest may be used even when its nominal duration differs from the bar's total. Dots extend rests just as they extend notes.

Use

Rests articulate phrases, separate motifs, create syncopation, and allow performers to breathe or change equipment. A silence can carry dramatic force: the sudden general pause, in which all parts rest, is sometimes marked G.P. A fermata over a rest can suspend measured time. In multivoice notation, rests also distinguish the rhythms of individual voices even when another voice continues.

Measured and unmeasured silence

Not every absence of sound is written as a rest. Caesuras, breath marks, and fermatas may indicate pauses not governed strictly by the beat. John Cage's 4′33″ made ambient sound and the framing of silence its central material, although the work is structured in movements rather than simply notated as ordinary rests.
