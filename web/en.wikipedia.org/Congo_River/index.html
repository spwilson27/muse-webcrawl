Congo River

The Congo River, formerly also known as the Zaire River, is the second-longest river in Africa, shorter only than the Nile. Flowing for about 4,700 km (2,900 mi) through the Congo rainforest, it has the second-largest discharge of any river in the world — after the Amazon — averaging about 41,000 cubic metres per second, and its basin covers some 4 million square kilometres.

Course

The river rises in the highlands of northeastern Zambia as the Chambeshi, arcs north and west through the Democratic Republic of the Congo, receiving great tributaries such as the Ubangi and the Kasai, and is joined near Mbandaka by the Ruki before passing Kinshasa and Brazzaville — the two closest capital cities in the world, facing each other across the river. Below the Malebo Pool the river plunges through the Livingstone Falls, a series of rapids that block navigation to the Atlantic.

Significance

The Congo is the deepest recorded river in the world, with depths exceeding 220 m, and its basin contains the world's second-largest rainforest and exceptional biodiversity, including the bonobo. The river and its tributaries form the principal transport network of the region, and its hydroelectric potential at the Inga site is among the largest on Earth.
