The Perito Moreno Glacier (Spanish: Glaciar Perito Moreno) is a glacier located in Los Glaciares National Park in southwest Santa Cruz Province, Argentina. It is one of the most important tourist attractions in Argentine Patagonia. The 250 km² ice formation, 30 km long, is one of 48 glaciers fed by the Southern Patagonian Ice Field in the Andes, shared with Chile.

Formation and behaviour

The glacier's terminus is about 5 km wide, with an average height of 74 m above Lago Argentino and a total ice depth of about 170 m. Unlike most glaciers worldwide, Perito Moreno long maintained an approximate balance between accumulation and loss, though measurements in recent decades have shown increasing thinning. Periodically the advancing glacier dams the Brazo Rico arm of the lake; the resulting pressure eventually ruptures the ice dam in a spectacular event, sometimes forming an ice arch that collapses — a major tourist spectacle.

Name

The glacier is named after the explorer Francisco Moreno, called "Perito" (expert/specialist) for his role in the border survey between Argentina and Chile in the late nineteenth century.
