Thermal expansion

Thermal expansion is the tendency of matter to change shape, area, and volume in response to a change in temperature. Heating generally increases the average spacing between atoms, so materials expand; a few, such as water between 0 and 4 °C, contract.

Coefficients

The linear coefficient α gives the fractional change in length per degree: ΔL = αL₀ΔT. Aluminium has α ≈ 23 × 10⁻⁶/K, steel about 12, and Invar only about 1.2, making Invar valuable in precision instruments. The volumetric coefficient is roughly three times the linear one for isotropic solids.

Applications

Engineers accommodate expansion with bridge expansion joints, gaps in railway track, and expansion loops in pipelines, while exploiting it in bimetallic strips, thermostats, and liquid-in-glass thermometers.
