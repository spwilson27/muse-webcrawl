House of Representatives (Japan)

House of Representatives
Japanese: 衆議院 (Shūgiin)
Type: Lower house of the National Diet
Seats: 465
Term: 4 years (subject to dissolution)
Meeting place: National Diet Building, Tokyo

The House of Representatives (衆議院, Shūgiin) is the lower house of the National Diet of Japan. The House of Councillors is the upper house. The House of Representatives has 465 members, elected for a four-year term, though the chamber is frequently dissolved early by the prime minister.

Powers

Under the Constitution of Japan, the House of Representatives is more powerful than the House of Councillors. Its decisions override the upper house on the passage of the budget, the ratification of treaties, and the designation of the prime minister. Ordinary legislation rejected by the Councillors can be enacted if repassed by a two-thirds majority of the Representatives. Only members of the House of Representatives can pass a motion of no confidence in the cabinet, forcing its resignation or a dissolution and general election.

Electoral system

Members are elected by parallel voting: 289 members from single-seat constituencies by first-past-the-post, and 176 from eleven regional proportional representation blocks by the D'Hondt method, with voters casting two ballots. Citizens aged 18 and over may vote; candidates must be at least 25.

History

The original House of Representatives was established under the Meiji Constitution of 1889 and first met in 1890 as the lower house of the Imperial Diet, with a franchise initially limited to men paying a threshold of direct taxes. Universal male suffrage came in 1925, and full adult suffrage after the Second World War. The modern house derives from the 1947 constitution.
