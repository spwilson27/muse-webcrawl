Marshal of the Soviet Union

Marshal of the Soviet Union (Russian: Маршал Советского Союза, Marshal Sovetskogo Soyuza) was the highest military rank in the Soviet Union. It was created in 1935 and abolished in 1991, and was held by 41 men over its history.

History

The rank was introduced on 22 September 1935 as part of the restoration of personal military ranks in the Red Army. The first five marshals were Kliment Voroshilov, Mikhail Tukhachevsky, Alexander Yegorov, Semyon Budyonny and Vasily Blyukher; three of the five were executed or died in custody during the Great Purge of 1937–38.

During the Second World War the rank was conferred on the leading Soviet commanders, including Georgy Zhukov, Aleksandr Vasilevsky, Ivan Konev, Konstantin Rokossovsky and Rodion Malinovsky. Joseph Stalin, who took the rank in 1943, was subsequently given the still higher (and unique) title of Generalissimus of the Soviet Union.

Insignia and status

Marshals wore distinctive shoulder boards with a large star and the state emblem, and from 1940 received the Marshal's Star, a diamond-studded five-pointed star worn at the neck, as a badge of the rank. The rank ranked above Army General and below only the largely nominal Generalissimus; holders typically commanded fronts during the war and later served as Ministers of Defence, chiefs of the General Staff or commanders of military districts.

End of the rank

The last holder was Dmitry Yazov, appointed in 1990 and implicated in the August 1991 coup attempt. The rank was abolished with the dissolution of the USSR; the Russian Federation later created the separate rank of Marshal of the Russian Federation, held to date only by Igor Sergeyev.
