Mount Weather

The Mount Weather Emergency Operations Center is a United States government facility in the Blue Ridge Mountains of Virginia, near Bluemont, operated by the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA). Built during the Cold War as a continuity-of-government site, it is designed to shelter senior civilian and military leadership and to serve as a relocation centre in a national catastrophe; it also hosts FEMA training and operations functions.

History

The site began as a Weather Bureau station in the late nineteenth century — hence the name — and federal mining engineers tunnelled into the mountain in the 1950s under the Bureau of Mines before the facility passed to civil-defence use. Its above-ground complex grew into a small self-contained town with offices, dormitories, a hospital, dining and recreation facilities, water reservoirs and its own power plant, while the underground area is protected by massive blast doors.

Activations and publicity

The centre's existence became widely public after a 1974 plane crash nearby and investigative reporting drew attention to it. During the 11 September 2001 attacks, senior officials including the Vice President's party and congressional leadership were moved to secure sites, and Mount Weather featured in reporting on the shadow-government arrangements of the period. The facility also appeared in the television series The 100 as a fictional underground refuge.
