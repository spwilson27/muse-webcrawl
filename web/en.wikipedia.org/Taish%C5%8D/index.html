Taishō era
Period: 30 July 1912 – 25 December 1926
Emperor: Taishō (Yoshihito)
Preceded by: Meiji era
Followed by: Shōwa era

The Taishō era (大正時代, Taishō jidai) is the period of Japanese history corresponding to the reign of Emperor Taishō, from 30 July 1912 to 25 December 1926. It is remembered above all for "Taishō democracy", a flowering of liberal politics, party government and cultural modernity.

Politics

During the era, political power shifted from oligarchic cliques toward party cabinets: Hara Takashi became the first commoner prime minister in 1918, and universal male suffrage was enacted in 1925. The same year, however, the Peace Preservation Law signalled growing pressure on dissent.

Society and events

Japan fought on the Allied side in the First World War and emerged with an expanded economy, though the Rice Riots of 1918 and the devastating Great Kantō earthquake of 1923 (which killed over 100,000 people) shook society. Taishō culture blended Western fashions — the "modern girl" (moga) and "modern boy" (mobo) — with Japanese traditions in the booming cities of Tokyo and Osaka.
