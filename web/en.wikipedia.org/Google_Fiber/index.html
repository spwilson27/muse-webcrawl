Google Fiber, sometimes styled GFiber, is a fiber broadband Internet and IPTV service operated by Alphabet Inc. It provides fiber-to-the-premises service in a growing number of metropolitan areas in the United States, offering gigabit-speed Internet access.

History

Google announced the project in February 2010, inviting cities to apply for an experimental ultra-high-speed network. More than 1,100 communities applied. Kansas City, Kansas and Kansas City, Missouri were selected as the first locations, with service launching in 2012. The service later expanded to cities including Austin, Provo, Atlanta, Charlotte, Nashville, Salt Lake City, and others.

Impact and restructuring

Google Fiber was credited with pressuring incumbent providers to lower prices and raise speeds in markets it entered. In 2016, parent company Alphabet scaled back expansion plans, paused rollouts in several candidate cities, and restructured the Access unit, though deployment continued in existing markets and later resumed expansion, including multi-gigabit tiers.
