Show tune

A show tune is a song originally written as part of the score of a musical theatre production, including Broadway musicals and off-Broadway productions, or a film musical. Show tunes are usually written to advance the plot, develop characters, or provide comic or emotional emphasis within the show, though some become popular standards independent of their original productions.

Characteristics

Show tunes vary widely in style, but they commonly feature lyrics closely tied to character and situation, with melodies designed to be memorable and singable. Unlike most popular songs, a show tune's lyrics may reference specific plot points, which sometimes limits their standalone appeal; successful crossover show tunes tend to have more general lyrics about love, ambition, or self-expression.

Notable examples

Many show tunes have entered the Great American Songbook, including songs by George and Ira Gershwin, Cole Porter, Irving Berlin, Rodgers and Hammerstein, and Stephen Sondheim. Examples include "My Funny Valentine" from Babes in Arms, "Some Enchanted Evening" from South Pacific, "Memory" from Cats, and "Defying Gravity" from Wicked.

Recording and performance

Original cast recordings preserve show tunes and help popularize productions. Show tunes are staples of cabaret and concert performance, and competitions such as theatre auditions commonly require performers to prepare them. The term is also applied to songs written in the style of musical theatre songs.
