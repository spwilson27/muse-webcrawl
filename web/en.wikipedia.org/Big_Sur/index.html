Big Sur

Big Sur is a rugged and mountainous section of the Central Coast of the US state of California, lying between the Monterey Bay area and San Simeon, where the Santa Lucia Mountains rise abruptly from the Pacific Ocean. It is frequently praised for its dramatic views, redwood forests, hiking, beaches, and other recreational opportunities. The name derives from the Spanish el país grande del sur, "the big country of the south".

Geography

The region is roughly 70 miles (110 km) long and served almost entirely by State Route 1, a National Scenic Byway famous for landmarks such as the Bixby Creek Bridge (completed 1932). Notable places include Pfeiffer Big Sur State Park, Julia Pfeiffer Burns State Park with McWay Falls, Point Sur Lighthouse, and the Ventana Wilderness. Cone Peak rises to 5,155 feet (1,571 m), one of the highest points so close to the ocean in the contiguous United States.

History and culture

The area was home to the Esselen, Salinan and Rumsen peoples before Spanish colonisation. Because of its isolation, Big Sur long attracted artists, writers and seekers: Robinson Jeffers, Henry Miller — who wrote Big Sur and the Oranges of Hieronymus Bosch — and Jack Kerouac, whose novel Big Sur is set there, all lived in or wrote about the region. The Esalen Institute, founded in 1962, became a centre of the human potential movement. The Henry Miller Memorial Library preserves this literary heritage.

Tourism

Big Sur draws millions of visitors annually despite having very few residents and limited services. Landslides periodically close Highway 1, and wildfires are a recurring hazard in the dry chaparral and forest.
