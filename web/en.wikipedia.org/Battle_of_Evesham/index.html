Battle of Evesham

Battle of Evesham
Date: 4 August 1265
Location: Evesham, Worcestershire, England
Result: Royalist victory
Part of the Second Barons' War

The Battle of Evesham was fought on 4 August 1265 between the army of Simon de Montfort, Earl of Leicester, and the royalist forces of Prince Edward, the future Edward I of England. One of the principal engagements of the Second Barons' War, it ended in Montfort's death and the destruction of his army, restoring the authority of King Henry III.

Background

Montfort had captured the king at the Battle of Lewes in 1264 and governed England through a revolutionary parliament, but his regime unravelled as barons defected. Prince Edward escaped custody in May 1265, joined the Marcher lords, defeated Montfort's son Simon at Kenilworth in a night attack on 31 July, and then moved to trap Montfort himself at Evesham, blocking the crossings of the River Avon.

The battle

Montfort, heavily outnumbered, mistook Edward's banners — some of them captured standards flown deceitfully — for reinforcements and realised too late he was surrounded. A thunderstorm broke as the armies met on Green Hill north of the town. Montfort fought and died in the press with his son Henry and other leading supporters, in what chroniclers called not a battle but the "murder of Evesham". His body was mutilated by the victors.

Aftermath

The battle ended Montfort's revolution and restored royal power, though resistance continued at Kenilworth and the Isle of Ely until the Dictum of Kenilworth in 1266. Despite his defeat, Montfort's parliaments left a lasting mark on English constitutional history.
