Kanha National Park

LocationMandla and Balaghat districts, Madhya Pradesh, India
Nearest cityMandla
Area940 km² (core); 1,009 km² including buffer
Established1933 (sanctuary); 1955 (national park); 1973 (tiger reserve)
Governing bodyMadhya Pradesh Forest Department

Kanha National Park, also known as Kanha–Kisli National Park, is one of the largest national parks of India, lying in the state of Madhya Pradesh. Its sal and bamboo forests, grassy meadows and ravines provided the setting for Rudyard Kipling's The Jungle Book. The park is famed for its Bengal tigers and for its successful conservation of the hard-ground barasingha (swamp deer).

History

The area was made a sanctuary in 1933 and a national park in 1955. In 1973 it became one of the first nine tiger reserves under Project Tiger. Kanha's grasslands (maidans) occupy the sites of villages relocated from the park, and the meadows of the Kanha and Kisli ranges are prime wildlife-viewing areas.

Wildlife

Kanha supports around a hundred tigers, as well as leopards, dholes (Indian wild dogs), sloth bears, gaur, sambar, chital, barking deer and langurs. Its signature success is the recovery of the hard-ground barasingha, the state animal of Madhya Pradesh, from about 66 animals in the 1960s to several hundred today through habitat protection and predator-proof enclosures. More than 300 bird species have been recorded.

Tourism

The main entry gates are Khatia (for the Kisli and Kanha zones) and Mukki. Safaris run in the morning and afternoon in the cooler months; the park closes during the monsoon.
