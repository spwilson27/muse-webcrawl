Capital gain

A capital gain is the increase in the value of a capital asset (such as a security, real estate, or other investment) that gives it a higher worth than the purchase price. The gain is not realized until the asset is sold. A capital gain may be short-term (one year or less) or long-term (more than one year) and must be claimed on income taxes in most jurisdictions.

Capital gains are generally taxed at a lower rate than ordinary income in many countries, including the United States, where long-term gains benefit from preferential rates of 0%, 15%, or 20% depending on taxable income. A capital loss arises when an asset is sold for less than its cost basis; losses can generally offset gains and, within limits, ordinary income.

Calculation

The gain is the difference between the amount realized on sale and the taxpayer's adjusted basis (usually the purchase price plus certain costs). For securities, rules such as the wash sale rule in the United States disallow losses on sales where a substantially identical security is repurchased within 30 days.
