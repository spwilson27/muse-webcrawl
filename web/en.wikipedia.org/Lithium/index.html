Lithium

Lithium
Symbol: Li
Atomic number: 3
Atomic mass: 6.94
Group: Alkali metals
Electron configuration: [He] 2s1
Melting point: 180.5 °C
Density: 0.534 g/cm3

Lithium is a chemical element with the symbol Li and atomic number 3. It is a soft, silvery-white alkali metal and the least dense metal and least dense solid element. Like all alkali metals, lithium is highly reactive and must be stored under oil or an inert atmosphere. It is one of the few elements synthesised in the Big Bang, though in small quantities.

Properties

Lithium burns with a crimson flame. It reacts with water, though less violently than sodium, liberating hydrogen and forming lithium hydroxide. It has the highest specific heat capacity of any solid element and a very low electrochemical potential, properties that make it ideal for batteries.

Occurrence and production

Lithium does not occur free in nature. It is extracted from pegmatite minerals such as spodumene (notably in Western Australia) and from continental brines beneath salt flats in the "Lithium Triangle" of Chile, Argentina and Bolivia. World production has grown rapidly with demand for electric vehicles.

Applications

The dominant use of lithium is in rechargeable lithium-ion batteries for portable electronics, electric vehicles and grid storage. Lithium compounds are also used in ceramics and glass, lubricating greases (lithium stearate), air treatment, and as a flux in metallurgy.

Medicine

Lithium salts, particularly lithium carbonate, have been used since 1949 as a mood stabiliser in the treatment of bipolar disorder. Their therapeutic mechanism remains incompletely understood, and because the therapeutic index is narrow, blood levels must be monitored to avoid toxicity.
