West Kennet Long Barrow

West Kennet Long Barrow
Location: Wiltshire, England
Type: Chambered long barrow
Built: approx. 3650 BC (Neolithic)
Length: about 104 m
Part of: Avebury World Heritage Site

The West Kennet Long Barrow, also known as South Long Barrow, is a chambered long barrow near the village of Avebury in Wiltshire, England. Built in the early Neolithic period, around 3650 BC, it is one of the largest and best preserved long barrows in Britain and forms part of the Stonehenge, Avebury and Associated Sites UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Description

The earthen mound is about 104 metres long and up to 3.3 metres high, flanked by ditches from which the mound material was quarried. At its eastern end, a facade of sarsen stones frames a forecourt and a passage leading into a gallery with five burial chambers lined with sarsen uprights and dry-stone walling.

Excavations

The barrow was partially excavated in 1859 by John Thurnam and more fully in 1955–56 by Stuart Piggott and Richard Atkinson. The chambers contained the disarticulated remains of at least 46 individuals, along with pottery, flint tools and animal bones. After roughly a millennium of use the chambers were filled in and the entrance blocked with large sarsen stones.

Access

The barrow is in the care of English Heritage and open to the public; visitors may enter the passage and chambers, which lie a short walk from the road between Avebury and Marlborough, close to Silbury Hill.
