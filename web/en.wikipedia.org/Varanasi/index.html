Varanasi (also Benares, officially Vārāṇasī) is a city on the banks of the river Ganges in northern India. One of the world's oldest continuously inhabited cities, it is the holiest of the seven sacred cities (Sapta Puri) in Hinduism and a major pilgrimage destination.

The ghats along the Ganges at sunrise.

Religion

Hindus believe dying and being cremated in Varanasi, on the banks of the Ganges, grants moksha (liberation from rebirth). The city is sacred to Shiva, worshipped at the Kashi Vishwanath Temple. Nearby Sarnath is where the Buddha delivered his first sermon; the city also has deep Jain and Sikh associations.

The ghats

Some 88 ghats (stepped embankments) line the riverfront, including Dashashwamedh Ghat, site of the evening Ganga Aarti ceremony, and Manikarnika Ghat, the principal cremation ground.

Culture and economy

Varanasi is renowned for Banaras silk saris, brassware, classical Hindustani music (the Banaras gharana), and Banaras Hindu University, founded in 1916. Mark Twain famously called it "older than history, older than tradition, older even than legend".
