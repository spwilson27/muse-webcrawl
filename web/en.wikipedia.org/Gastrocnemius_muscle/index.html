Gastrocnemius muscle

Gastrocnemius
OriginMedial and lateral condyles of the femur
InsertionCalcaneus via the Achilles tendon
ArterySural arteries
NerveTibial nerve (S1–S2)
ActionsPlantar flexion of the foot; flexion of the knee

The gastrocnemius muscle is a large, two-headed muscle forming the bulk of the calf at the back of the lower leg. Its name derives from the Greek gaster (belly) and kneme (leg), referring to its bulging shape. Together with the soleus and plantaris it forms the triceps surae.

Structure

The gastrocnemius arises by two heads from the medial and lateral condyles of the femur. The two bellies descend and merge, joining the tendon of the soleus to form the calcaneal (Achilles) tendon, which inserts on the back of the calcaneus (heel bone). Because it crosses both the knee and ankle joints, the muscle both flexes the knee and plantar-flexes the foot — pointing the toes downward, as in walking, running and jumping.

Function

Rich in fast-twitch fibres, the gastrocnemius provides the explosive push-off power used in sprinting and jumping, while the deeper, slow-twitch soleus handles postural work. The muscle is most powerful when the knee is extended.

Clinical significance

The gastrocnemius is a common site of strains during sport; a tear of its medial head is often called "tennis leg". It is also used in reconstructive surgery as a flap to cover defects around the knee, and gastrocnemius recession is sometimes performed to treat chronic plantar fasciitis and Achilles tendinopathy.
