Soviet–Japanese Neutrality Pact

The Soviet–Japanese Neutrality Pact (日ソ中立条約), also known as the Japanese–Soviet Non-aggression Pact, was a neutrality pact between the Soviet Union and the Empire of Japan signed in Moscow on 13 April 1941, two years after the brief Soviet–Japanese border conflicts. Under the pact both parties pledged to maintain peaceful relations and to respect each other's territorial integrity, and to remain neutral if the other became involved in war with a third party.

Background

Relations between the USSR and Japan had been strained by clashes along the Manchurian border, culminating in the Soviet victory at Khalkhin Gol in 1939. Both governments had reasons to stabilise their relations: Japan wished to secure its northern flank while it expanded southward, and the Soviet Union wanted to avoid a two-front war as tensions with Nazi Germany grew. The pact was negotiated by Japanese foreign minister Yōsuke Matsuoka and signed by Matsuoka and Vyacheslav Molotov in the presence of Joseph Stalin.

Operation

The pact held for most of World War II. Japan did not attack the Soviet Union even after Germany invaded in June 1941, instead striking south against the Western colonies and the United States; intelligence from the spy Richard Sorge confirming Japan's intentions helped Moscow transfer Siberian divisions west for the defence of Moscow. The Soviet Union, for its part, remained at peace with Japan while fighting Germany.

Termination

The pact was due to run for five years, with renewal unless denounced a year in advance. On 5 April 1945 the USSR denounced the pact, declaring it would not be renewed. At the Yalta Conference the Soviet Union had secretly agreed to enter the war against Japan within three months of Germany's defeat. On 9 August 1945, still formally within the pact's term, Soviet forces invaded Japanese-held Manchuria in Operation August Storm. The Soviet declaration of war, delivered hours before the invasion began, was regarded by Japan as a violation of the pact, while Moscow argued that Japan's alliance with Germany and its war conduct had voided it.
