Bob Knight

Robert Montgomery Knight (October 25, 1940 – November 1, 2023) was an American men's college basketball coach. Nicknamed "the General", he won 902 NCAA Division I men's basketball games, a record at the time of his retirement and currently third all-time behind Mike Krzyzewski and Jim Boeheim. He is best known for his long tenure as head coach of the Indiana Hoosiers from 1971 to 2000, and also coached at Army (1965–1971) and Texas Tech (2001–2008).

Career

Knight's Indiana teams won three NCAA championships, in 1976, 1981, and 1987. The 1975–76 Hoosiers finished 32–0 and remain the last undefeated national champion in Division I men's basketball. Knight also coached the United States men's national basketball team to a gold medal at the 1984 Summer Olympics in Los Angeles.

He played college basketball at Ohio State, where he was a reserve on the 1960 national championship team that also featured future stars John Havlicek and Jerry Lucas.

Coaching style and controversies

Knight was known for his motion offense, demanding man-to-man defense, and emphasis on academics and discipline. His fiery temper produced numerous controversies, including a widely publicized 1985 incident in which he threw a chair across the court during a game against Purdue. His tenure at Indiana ended in 2000 after a series of incidents, including an allegation that he grabbed a student by the arm.

Legacy

Knight was inducted into the Basketball Hall of Fame in 1991. Despite his accomplishments, his polarizing personality and confrontations with players, officials, and the media made him one of the most debated figures in American sports.
