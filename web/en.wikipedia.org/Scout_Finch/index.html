Scout Finch

Created by: Harper Lee

Full name: Jean Louise Finch

Family: Atticus Finch (father), Jem Finch (brother)

Jean Louise "Scout" Finch is the narrator and protagonist of Harper Lee's novel To Kill a Mockingbird (1960) and a character in its sequel, Go Set a Watchman (2015). The story is told from the adult Scout's perspective, looking back on her childhood in the fictional town of Maycomb, Alabama, during the Great Depression.

Character

Scout is the young daughter of lawyer Atticus Finch and the younger sister of Jem. Tomboyish, curious and outspoken, she begins the novel aged six and grows through her experiences: her fascination with the reclusive Boo Radley, her schooling, and her father's defence of Tom Robinson, a Black man falsely accused of rape. Through these events she learns about empathy, courage and racial injustice.

Portrayal

In the 1962 film adaptation, Scout was played by Mary Badham, whose performance earned an Academy Award nomination. The character is widely regarded as one of the most memorable narrators in American literature.

Themes

Scout's coming-of-age arc embodies the novel's themes of innocence, moral education and the loss of prejudice through understanding; her father's lesson — that one must climb into another person's skin and walk around in it — frames her development.
