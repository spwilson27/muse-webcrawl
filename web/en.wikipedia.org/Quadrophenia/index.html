Quadrophenia

Quadrophenia is the sixth studio album by the English rock band the Who, released as a double album on 26 October 1973 by Track Records. It is the group's second rock opera, following Tommy (1969). Set in London and Brighton in 1965, the story follows a young mod named Jimmy and his search for self-worth and importance. Quadrophenia is the only Who album entirely composed by guitarist Pete Townshend.

The album's title is a play on the outdated popular term "schizophrenia" and the four distinct personalities of Jimmy, each represented by a musical theme and associated with one member of the band: "Helpless Dancer" (Roger Daltrey), "Is It Me?" (John Entwistle), "Bell Boy" (Keith Moon) and "Love Reign O'er Me" (Townshend). Townshend also used the then-new technology of quadraphonic sound as a reference in the title.

Music and production

The group recorded the album at Ramport Studios in London with extensive use of synthesisers, sound effects recorded on location (including waves and a train), and multi-tracked instrumentation. Songs include "The Real Me", "5:15", "Love, Reign O'er Me" and "Drowned". The album peaked at number two on both the UK Albums Chart and the US Billboard 200, and has been acclaimed as one of the Who's greatest works.

Adaptations

A film adaptation, directed by Franc Roddam and starring Phil Daniels as Jimmy, was released in 1979 and became a cult classic that revived interest in mod culture. The Who have performed the album in full on several tours, including in 1996–97 and 2012–13, and a classical orchestral version, Classic Quadrophenia, was released in 2015.
