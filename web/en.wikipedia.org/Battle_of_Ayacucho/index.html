Battle of Ayacucho

The Battle of Ayacucho was the decisive military confrontation of the Peruvian War of Independence and of the Spanish American wars of independence as a whole. It was fought on 9 December 1824 on the high plain of Quinua near Ayacucho, Peru, between the United Liberation Army commanded by Antonio José de Sucre and the royalist army of the Viceroy of Peru, José de la Serna.

Background

After Simón Bolívar's victories in northern South America and the patriot victory at Junín in August 1824, command of the liberation army in Peru passed to Sucre. The royalists, though numerically superior, were divided and demoralised. The two armies met on the Pampa de Quinua at an altitude of about 3,400 metres.

The battle

Sucre deployed some 5,800 men against roughly 9,000 royalists. The royalist attack down from the Condorcunca heights was repulsed, and a patriot counterattack, spearheaded by the cavalry of José María Córdoba, broke the royalist line. Viceroy de la Serna was wounded and captured. By midday the royalist army had collapsed, losing around 1,800 killed and 700 wounded, against some 300 patriot dead.

Capitulation and significance

The royalist commander José de Canterac signed the Capitulation of Ayacucho, under which the remaining royalist forces in Peru surrendered. The battle ended Spanish rule in South America; only the garrison of Callao held out until 1826. Ayacucho is commemorated as the seal of South American independence.
