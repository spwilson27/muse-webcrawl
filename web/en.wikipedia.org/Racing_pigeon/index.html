Racing pigeon
Variety of domestic pigeon bred for homing ability and speed
Races range from 100 to over 1,000 km

A racing pigeon is a variety of domestic pigeon (Columba livia domestica) developed from the rock dove, selectively bred for its ability to find its way home over prodigious distances and at increasing speed. The sport of flying these specially bred birds is pigeon racing; the principal racing breed is the Racing Homer.

History

Homing pigeons carried messages for millennia — in ancient Egypt and Rome, during the Siege of Paris (1870–71), and in both World Wars, when individual birds such as Cher Ami won medals. Organized racing began in Belgium in the early 19th century and spread widely in Europe and beyond.

The sport

Birds are trained from their home loft, then transported to a release point and liberated together. Each bird returns to its own loft; an electronic or rubber-ring timing system records arrival, and velocity is computed over the measured distance. Races range from short sprints of about 100 km to marathon events over 1,000 km. Valuable bloodlines command high prices at auction, and the sport remains especially popular in Belgium, the Netherlands, Britain, China and South Africa.

Homing ability

Pigeons navigate using the sun, Earth's magnetic field (magnetoreception, probably involving the inner ear and beak), landmarks and possibly smell and infrasound.
