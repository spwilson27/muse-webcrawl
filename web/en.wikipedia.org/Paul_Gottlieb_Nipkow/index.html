Paul Gottlieb Nipkow

Paul Julius Gottlieb Nipkow (22 August 1860 – 24 August 1940) was a German inventor best known for the Nipkow disk, a rotating scanning disk that became the basis of mechanical television.

The Nipkow disk

In 1884, as a student in Berlin, Nipkow filed a patent for an "electric telescope" in which an image was scanned line by line through holes arranged in a spiral on a rapidly spinning disk, converting variations in brightness into electrical signals. No practical system was built from his patent — it lapsed for want of funds — but the principle underlay John Logie Baird's mechanical television demonstrations of the 1920s and the German television service of the 1930s, which named its first station, Fernsehsender Paul Nipkow, after him.

Later life

Nipkow worked as an engineer in railway signalling in Berlin. He died in Berlin in 1940, two days after his 80th birthday.
