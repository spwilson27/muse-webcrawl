Propraetor

A propraetor (Latin: pro praetore) was a Roman official who exercised the powers of a praetor outside the city of Rome, typically as governor of a province, after having served as praetor or with his imperium prorogued. The office arose as Rome's overseas possessions expanded and there were too few annually elected magistrates to govern them.

History

The first propraetors were appointed in the third century BC to administer Sicily and Sardinia. Under the Republic, provincial commands were assigned to ex-praetors and ex-consuls (propraetors and proconsuls) by lot or senatorial decree. In the Imperial period, the emperor's own provincial governors, the legati Augusti pro praetore, held propraetorian rank while governing imperial provinces with legions.

Powers

Within his province a propraetor held imperium, commanding troops, administering justice, and supervising taxation, though abuses could lead to prosecution for extortion (repetundae) on return to Rome.
