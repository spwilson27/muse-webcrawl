American entry into World War I

The American entry into World War I came on April 6, 1917, when the United States declared war on the German Empire, joining the Allied Powers nearly three years after the war began. President Woodrow Wilson had maintained neutrality, winning re-election in 1916 partly on the slogan "He kept us out of war."

Causes

Germany's resumption of unrestricted submarine warfare in February 1917, sinking of American merchant ships, and the publication of the Zimmermann Telegram — in which Germany proposed a military alliance with Mexico against the United States — drove Wilson to ask Congress for a declaration of war.

Mobilization and impact

The United States raised an army of millions through conscription under the Selective Service Act and sent the American Expeditionary Forces under General John J. Pershing to France. By the armistice of November 11, 1918, more than two million American soldiers had reached Europe, providing the manpower that helped break the stalemate on the Western Front.
