Oil field

An oil field is an area of land or seabed underlain by one or more petroleum reservoirs from which crude oil (and often natural gas) can be economically extracted. A field may contain a single reservoir or several stacked reservoirs in separate geological strata. Oil fields are classified as onshore or offshore and range from small accumulations to "supergiant" fields such as Ghawar in Saudi Arabia, the largest conventional oil field in the world.

Formation and discovery

Petroleum forms when organic-rich source rock is buried and heated, expelling oil that migrates upward until trapped in porous reservoir rock beneath an impermeable cap rock. Geologists locate prospective traps using seismic surveys and confirm them with exploration wells. Once discovered, a field is appraised, developed with production wells, and produced over a life cycle that may span decades.

Production and recovery

Primary recovery relies on natural reservoir pressure; secondary recovery uses water or gas injection, and tertiary (enhanced) recovery employs steam, chemicals, or carbon dioxide. Recovery factors typically range from about 20 to 50 percent of the oil in place. Surface facilities separate oil, gas, and water before transport by pipeline or tanker.
