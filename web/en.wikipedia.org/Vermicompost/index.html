Vermicompost

Vermicompost (vermi-compost) is the product of the decomposition process using various species of worms, usually red wigglers (Eisenia fetida), white worms, and other earthworms, to create a mixture of decomposing vegetable or food waste, bedding materials, and vermicast. Vermicast (also called worm castings, worm humus, worm manure, or worm faeces) is the end-product of the breakdown of organic matter by earthworms. The process of producing vermicompost is called vermicomposting, and rearing the worms is vermiculture.

Properties and uses

Vermicompost contains water-soluble nutrients and is an excellent, nutrient-rich organic fertilizer and soil conditioner. Castings contain plant-available forms of nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, calcium and magnesium, along with beneficial microorganisms, and are widely used in organic farming and home gardening, both mixed into soil and brewed into "worm tea".

Systems

Vermicomposting ranges from small household bins fed with kitchen scraps, through stacked-tray and continuous-flow reactors, to large-scale windrow operations processing manure and municipal green waste. Systems require moisture, neutral pH, moderate temperatures, and avoidance of meat, dairy and oily wastes that attract pests.
