Timothy Pickering

Born17 July 1745, Salem, Massachusetts
Died29 January 1829, Salem, Massachusetts
OfficesU.S. Secretary of State (1795–1800), Secretary of War, Postmaster General

Timothy Pickering (July 17, 1745 – January 29, 1829) was a politician from Massachusetts who served in a variety of roles, as a soldier in the American Revolutionary War, as the third United States Secretary of State under Presidents George Washington and John Adams, and in both houses of Congress.

Career

Pickering served as Adjutant General and Quartermaster General of the Continental Army. In Washington's cabinet he was successively Postmaster General, Secretary of War and, from 1795, Secretary of State. Adams dismissed him in 1800 after disputes over policy toward France during the Quasi-War.

Later life

As a Federalist senator and congressman, Pickering opposed the War of 1812 and flirted with New England secession, which destroyed his political standing after the Hartford Convention. He retired to farming and agricultural experimentation in Massachusetts.
