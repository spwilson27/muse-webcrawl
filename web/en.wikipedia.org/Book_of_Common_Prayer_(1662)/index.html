Book of Common Prayer (1662)

Published1662
LanguageEarly Modern English
DenominationChurch of England / Anglican Communion

The 1662 Book of Common Prayer is the classic and largely unchanged liturgical book of the Church of England, authorised by the Act of Uniformity 1662 following the Restoration of Charles II. It remains, in law, the official prayer book of the Church of England and is the historical benchmark for prayer books throughout the Anglican Communion.

History

The first prayer book, largely compiled by Thomas Cranmer, appeared in 1549, with more reformed editions in 1552 and 1559. After the Commonwealth period, during which the book was suppressed, the Savoy Conference of 1661 considered Puritan objections; the resulting 1662 revision made over 600 changes but stayed close to the 1559 text. Clergy who refused to conform were ejected in the "Great Ejection" of 1662.

Contents

The book contains the orders for Morning and Evening Prayer, the Litany, Holy Communion, baptism, confirmation, matrimony, visitation of the sick, and burial, together with the Psalter and the Ordinal for making bishops, priests, and deacons. Its language — much of it Cranmer's prose, drawing on the King James Bible — profoundly shaped English religious and literary culture.

Legacy

Although modern services in England now chiefly use Common Worship, the 1662 book remains in daily use in cathedrals and parishes, and continues in print from the university presses and the Prayer Book Society's advocacy.
