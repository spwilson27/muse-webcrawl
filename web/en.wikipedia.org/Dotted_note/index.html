Dotted note

Dotted notes of successively shorter values.

In Western musical notation, a dotted note is a note with a small dot written after it. The dot adds one half of the note's value to its duration. For example, a dotted half note lasts as long as a half note plus a quarter note — three quarter-note beats in total. A second dot (a double-dotted note) adds a further quarter of the original value.

Notation and duration

The dot is placed in the space immediately to the right of the notehead. If the note's value is 1, a single dot extends it to 1½, and a second dot to 1¾. Dotted notes are fundamental to dotted rhythms, such as the characteristic patterns of the French overture style, marches, and much dance music, where a dotted note is typically paired with a short note of half its added value.

History

Augmentation dots have been used since at least the 10th century in various forms; their modern usage crystallised with the mensural notation of the late Middle Ages and Renaissance. In early notation a dot could also serve as a punctus divisionis, separating rhythmic groups, which must be distinguished from the augmentation dot by context.

Dotted rests

The same convention applies to rests: a dotted rest is lengthened by half its value in silence.
