Aimé Césaire

Aimé Césaire (26 June 1913 – 17 April 2008) was a Martinican poet, playwright and politician, and one of the founders of the négritude movement in Francophone literature. His long poem Cahier d'un retour au pays natal (1939) is a landmark of surrealist and anti-colonial writing.

Life and politics

Born in Basse-Pointe, Martinique, Césaire studied in Paris, where with Léopold Sédar Senghor and Léon-Gontran Damas he developed the concept of négritude. He was elected mayor of Fort-de-France and deputy for Martinique in the French National Assembly, serving for decades, and co-founded the Martinican Progressive Party.

Works

His prose work Discourse on Colonialism (1950) is a fierce critique of European colonialism. His plays include A Tempest (1969), a postcolonial reworking of Shakespeare. He died in Fort-de-France in 2008 and was honoured with a plaque in the Panthéon.
