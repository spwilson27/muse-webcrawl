Peripherally inserted central catheter
Other namesPICC line, PIC line
SpecialtyInterventional radiology, nursing
UsesLong-term IV therapy, chemotherapy, TPN

A PICC line inserted in the upper arm, with its tip in the superior vena cava.

A peripherally inserted central catheter (PICC), sometimes called a PICC line, is a form of intravenous access that can be used for a prolonged period of time, such as for long chemotherapy regimens, extended antibiotic therapy, or total parenteral nutrition. It is a thin, flexible catheter inserted into a peripheral vein — typically the basilic, cephalic or brachial vein of the upper arm — and advanced until the tip terminates in a central vein, usually the superior vena cava.

Insertion and care

PICCs are commonly placed at the bedside by specially trained nurses using ultrasound guidance and sterile technique, with tip position confirmed by chest X-ray or electrocardiographic guidance. Compared with centrally inserted catheters, PICCs avoid the risks of insertion into the neck or chest vessels, such as pneumothorax, but they carry risks of catheter-related bloodstream infection, upper-extremity deep vein thrombosis, occlusion and malposition.

Uses

PICCs may remain in place for weeks to months and can be used for administering medications, fluids, blood products and irritant or vesicant drugs that would damage peripheral veins, as well as — in suitably rated devices — power injection of contrast for CT imaging.
