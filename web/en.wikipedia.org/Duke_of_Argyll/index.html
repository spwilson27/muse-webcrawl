Duke of Argyll is a title in the Peerage of Scotland, created in 1701 for Archibald Campbell, 10th Earl of Argyll. The dukes are the chiefs of Clan Campbell, historically one of the most powerful families in the Scottish Highlands, and their seat is Inveraray Castle in Argyll.

History

The Campbells rose from the earldom of Argyll, created in 1457, to dominate the western Highlands. The 9th Earl was executed after Monmouth's rebellion in 1685, but his son was restored and raised to the dukedom. The 2nd Duke, John Campbell, was a leading Unionist and field marshal, commanding government forces at Sheriffmuir in 1715. A second, United Kingdom dukedom was conferred in 1892 on the 8th Duke, making the holder one of five peers holding two dukedoms.

Notable dukes

The 8th Duke, George Campbell, was a Liberal politician and father-in-law of Princess Louise, Queen Victoria's daughter. The present holder descends in the male line from the original creation, and the subsidiary titles include Marquess of Kintyre and Lorne, Earl of Argyll and Baron Sundridge.
