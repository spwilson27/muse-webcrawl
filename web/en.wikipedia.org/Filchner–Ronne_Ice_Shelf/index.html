Filchner–Ronne Ice Shelf

The Filchner–Ronne Ice Shelf, also known as the Ronne–Filchner Ice Shelf, is an Antarctic ice shelf bordering the Weddell Sea. It is divided into the eastern Ronne Ice Shelf and the western Filchner Ice Shelf by Berkner Island. With an area of roughly 430,000 km², it is the second-largest ice shelf in Antarctica after the Ross Ice Shelf, and by volume likely the largest single body of floating ice in the world.

Discovery

The Filchner portion was discovered by the German Antarctic Expedition of 1911–1912 under Wilhelm Filchner. The Ronne portion was sighted and photographed from the air in 1947 by the Ronne Antarctic Research Expedition led by Finn Ronne, who named it for his wife, Edith Ronne. The Filchner–Ronne Ice Shelf Programme (FRISP) has conducted glaciological research on the shelf since the 1980s.

Glaciology

The shelf is fed by several large ice streams, including the Rutford, Institute, Möller, Foundation, and Evans ice streams. Ice thickness exceeds 600 metres near the grounding line in places. Beneath the shelf, water circulates in a sub-ice cavity whose oceanography matters for global thermohaline circulation, because cold, dense shelf water formed there contributes to Antarctic Bottom Water.

Climate significance

Modelling studies examine whether warming ocean water could intrude into the Filchner cavity and increase basal melting, with consequences for sea-level rise. In July 2017 the giant iceberg A-68 calved from the separate Larsen C Ice Shelf to the north, an event frequently discussed alongside the future of Weddell Sea ice shelves.
