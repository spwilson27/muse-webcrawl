Unimodular matrix

In mathematics, a unimodular matrix M is a square integer matrix with determinant exactly +1 or −1. Equivalently, it is an integer matrix that is invertible over the integers: there exists another integer matrix N with MN = I. Unimodular matrices form the general linear group GL(n, Z) and preserve the integer lattice Zn under multiplication.

Properties and examples

Examples include permutation matrices, identity and diagonal matrices with ±1 entries, and upper or lower triangular integer matrices with all diagonal entries equal to ±1. The product of two unimodular matrices is unimodular, and the inverse of a unimodular matrix is again an integer matrix — a direct consequence of the adjugate formula, since the inverse involves division by the determinant, which is ±1.

Totally unimodular matrices

A totally unimodular matrix is one whose every square submatrix has determinant 0, +1 or −1. Such matrices are central to combinatorial optimization: by the Hoffman–Kruskal theorem, if the constraint matrix of a linear program is totally unimodular and the right-hand side is integral, the program has integer optimal vertices. Network flow matrices and node–edge incidence matrices of bipartite graphs are totally unimodular, which explains why many flow and matching problems are solvable by linear programming.

Applications

Unimodular transformations appear in lattice theory and the Hermite and Smith normal forms, in toric geometry, in loop transformations for compilers, and wherever a change of basis must map the integer lattice bijectively onto itself.
