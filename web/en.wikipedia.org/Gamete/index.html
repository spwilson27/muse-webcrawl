Gamete

A gamete is a haploid cell that fuses with another haploid cell during fertilisation in organisms that reproduce sexually. In animals, the female gamete is the ovum (egg cell) and the male gamete is the sperm. Gametes carry half the genetic information of an individual, one copy of each chromosome type.

Types

In species with isogamy, gametes are similar in size and shape; in anisogamy they differ, with small motile male gametes (sperm) and large non-motile female gametes (eggs) in oogamy, the pattern found in animals and many algae. Gametes are produced by a type of cell division called meiosis in organs called gonads in animals, or in specialised structures in plants.

Plants

In flowering plants the male gametes are contained in pollen grains and the female gamete (egg cell) lies within the embryo sac of the ovule. Double fertilisation, characteristic of angiosperms, produces both the zygote and the endosperm that nourishes the seed.

Formation

The production of gametes — gametogenesis — involves meiosis, which halves the chromosome number and generates genetic diversity through crossing over and independent assortment. Errors in meiosis can produce gametes with abnormal chromosome numbers, leading to conditions such as Down syndrome.
