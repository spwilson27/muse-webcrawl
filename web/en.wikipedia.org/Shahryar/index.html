Shahryar

Shahryār (Persian: شهریار, from Middle Persian šahr-dār, "holder of the realm") is a Persian name and royal title. In literature, Shahryar is best known as the fictional king in the frame story of One Thousand and One Nights.

In the Arabian Nights

In the frame tale, King Shahryar discovers his wife's infidelity and, in rage, has her executed and resolves to marry a new bride each day and have her killed the next morning. The vizier's daughter Scheherazade volunteers to marry him and saves herself by telling a story each night that ends on a cliffhanger, so the king postpones her execution to hear the ending. After a thousand and one nights of tales, Shahryar abandons his vow and makes her his queen. The king's brother, Shah Zaman, rules in Samarkand in the frame story.

Other uses

Shahryar is also a given name in Iran and South Asia; notable bearers include the Urdu poet Akhtar Shirani's pen-name contemporaries and the Mughal-era figures, and "Shahryar" appears as the name of Sassanid and legendary Iranian kings in Persian epic tradition.
