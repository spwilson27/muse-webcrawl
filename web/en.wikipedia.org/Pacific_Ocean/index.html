Pacific Ocean

The Pacific Ocean is the largest and deepest of Earth's five oceanic divisions. It extends from the Arctic Ocean in the north to the Southern Ocean (or, depending on the definition, to Antarctica) in the south, and is bounded by the continents of Asia and Oceania in the west and the Americas in the east.

At 165,250,000 square kilometres (63,800,000 square miles) in area — as defined with a southern Antarctic border — this largest division of the World Ocean and the hydrosphere covers about 46% of Earth's water surface and about 32% of the planet's total surface area, larger than all of Earth's land area combined (148,000,000 square kilometres). The centres of both the Water Hemisphere and the Western Hemisphere, as well as the oceanic pole of inaccessibility, are in the Pacific Ocean.

Etymology

The early 16th-century Spanish explorer Vasco Núñez de Balboa sighted the Pacific in 1513 after crossing the Isthmus of Panama and called it Mar del Sur ('Ocean of the South'). The ocean's current name was coined by Portuguese explorer Ferdinand Magellan during the Spanish circumnavigation of the world in 1521, as he encountered favourable winds on reaching it. He called it Mar Pacífico, which in both Portuguese and Spanish means 'peaceful sea'.

Geography

The Pacific Ocean separates Asia and Oceania from the Americas. It may be further subdivided by the equator into northern (North Pacific) and southern (South Pacific) portions. It extends from the Antarctic region in the south to the Arctic in the north. The Pacific Ocean lies between Asia and Oceania in the west and the Americas in the east.

The ocean contains some 25,000 islands, more than all other oceans combined, most of which are found south of the equator. Its irregular margins include many marginal seas such as the Philippine Sea, the Coral Sea, the South China Sea, the Tasman Sea, the Bering Sea, and the Sea of Okhotsk.

Geology

The Pacific basin is enclosed by the Ring of Fire, a belt of volcanoes and oceanic trenches partly ringing the basin, associated with intense seismic activity. The average depth of the Pacific is 4,000 metres. Challenger Deep in the Mariana Trench, located in the western North Pacific, is the deepest point in the world, reaching a depth of 10,928 metres (35,853 feet).

Climate and currents

Surface currents in the Pacific are driven by the subtropical gyres of each hemisphere. The El Niño–Southern Oscillation, centred in the tropical Pacific, is a major driver of interannual climate variability worldwide. Tropical cyclones form in the western and eastern Pacific, known as typhoons and hurricanes respectively.

History

The ocean was first settled by Austronesian-speaking peoples, who spread from Taiwan across Maritime Southeast Asia and into Remote Oceania in one of the great prehistoric migrations. European contact began in the 16th century, and the Pacific became an arena of trade, colonisation, and, in the 20th century, the Pacific War theatre of the Second World War.
