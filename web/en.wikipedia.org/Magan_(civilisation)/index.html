Magan (civilization)

Magan (also Makkan) was an ancient region referred to in Sumerian cuneiform texts of the third millennium BCE as a source of copper and diorite for Mesopotamia. It is generally identified with the area of the modern Sultanate of Oman and the United Arab Emirates, including the Oman Peninsula, though some scholars have proposed locations as far afield as the Indus region or Iran.

Trade

Sumerian texts describe ships of Magan, Dilmun, and Meluhha bringing goods to Akkad and Ur. Magan's copper, mined in the mountains of Oman (for example at Maysar and Lasail), and its dark diorite and gabbro stone were prized for statues such as those of Gudea of Lagash. Trade routes ran by sea through Dilmun (Bahrain) into the Gulf.

Archaeology

The Bronze Age Umm an-Nar culture of the Oman Peninsula, with its distinctive circular tombs and fortified oasis settlements such as Bat and Hili, is associated with Magan. Copper production continued on a large scale into the Iron Age, and the region later appears in later Mesopotamian texts under changing trade patterns.
