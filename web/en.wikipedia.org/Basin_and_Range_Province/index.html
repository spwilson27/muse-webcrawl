Basin and Range Province

LocationWestern United States and northwestern Mexico
AreaAbout 440,000 km² (170,000 sq mi)
Highest pointBoundary Peak, Nevada (4,007 m)
GeologyExtensional tectonics; horst and graben

The Basin and Range Province is a vast physiographic region covering much of the inland Western United States and northwestern Mexico. It is defined by unique basin and range topography, characterized by abrupt changes in elevation, alternating between narrow faulted mountain chains and flat arid valleys or basins. The region covers most of Nevada, the western half of Utah, southeastern California, and parts of Arizona, Oregon, Idaho, and New Mexico, extending into the Mexican states of Sonora, Chihuahua, and others.

Geography

The province consists of hundreds of elongated mountain ranges, generally trending north–south, separated by broad sediment-filled basins. Many of the basins are internally drained, producing playas and salt flats; the lowest point, Badwater Basin in Death Valley, lies 86 metres below sea level, the lowest point in North America. Major subregions include the Great Basin, the Mojave Desert, the Sonoran Desert's northern fringe, and the Rio Grande rift in New Mexico.

Geology

The topography is the product of crustal extension that began in the Miocene epoch, roughly 17 million years ago. As the crust pulled apart, blocks of rock were tilted and uplifted along normal faults, forming upthrown blocks (horsts) that became mountain ranges, while down-dropped blocks (grabens) became the intervening basins. The crust in the province has been stretched by up to 100% in places, making it unusually thin, with correspondingly high heat flow, widespread volcanic activity in its geologic past, and ongoing seismicity along active faults.

Climate and ecology

Most of the province is arid, lying in the rain shadow of the Sierra Nevada and Cascade Range. Vegetation ranges from sagebrush steppe in the northern Great Basin to creosote bush and cactus deserts in the south. Isolated high ranges — "sky islands" — harbour relict conifer forests and endemic species separated by seas of desert.
