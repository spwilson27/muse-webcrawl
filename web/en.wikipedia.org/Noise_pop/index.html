Noise pop

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Noise pop is a subgenre of alternative and indie rock that developed in the mid-1980s, combining the abrasive feedback and distortion of noise rock with the melodic structures of conventional pop songwriting. Where noise rock often abandons melody, noise pop submerges catchy choruses and harmonies beneath layers of guitar noise, producing a characteristically woozy, shimmering sound.

Characteristics

Typical noise pop features heavily processed electric guitars using effects such as chorus, reverb and tremolo, soft or breathy vocals buried in the mix, and standard verse–chorus structures. The genre is closely related to shoegaze and dream pop, and the three labels are often applied to the same bands.

History

The style emerged from the work of the Velvet Underground, the Byrds and 1960s jangle pop, filtered through punk. The Jesus and Mary Chain's debut album Psychocandy (1985) is widely regarded as the foundational noise pop record, marrying Beach Boys-style melodies to squalls of feedback. My Bloody Valentine, the early work of Yo La Tengo, Dinosaur Jr. and British bands such as The Pastels also shaped the genre. In the 2000s and 2010s acts including Japandroids, Wavves, Best Coast, Sleigh Bells and Alvvays revived the sound. See also indie rock and lo-fi.
