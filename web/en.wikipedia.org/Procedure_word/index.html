Procedure word

Radiotelephony procedure words standardise voice communications.

A procedure word (abbreviated proword) is standardized shorthand used in radiotelephony procedure. Prowords are spoken in place of longer phrases to save time, reduce ambiguity, and ensure messages are understood even under poor channel conditions. They form part of voice procedure in military, aviation, maritime, and amateur radio communications.

Examples

Commonly used prowords include ROGER (message received and understood), WILCO (received, understood, and will comply), OVER (transmission finished, reply expected), OUT (transmission finished, no reply expected), SAY AGAIN (repeat your transmission), CORRECTION (an error was made; the correct version follows), and BREAK (separation between parts of a message). The internationally recognized distress prowords are MAYDAY for distress and PAN-PAN for urgency.

Standardization

Procedure words are standardized in publications such as NATO's ACP 125 and ICAO radiotelephony manuals. Their meanings are fixed and must not be improvised, so that operators from different services and countries can interoperate. Prowords are distinct from the NATO phonetic alphabet, which is used to spell out words and call signs letter by letter.
