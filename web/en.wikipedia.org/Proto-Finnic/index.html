Proto-Finnic is the reconstructed protolanguage of the Finnic languages. It is not attested in any written sources but has been reconstructed through the comparative method from its daughter languages, including Finnish and Estonian. Scholars conventionally distinguish three stages: Early, Middle, and Late Proto-Finnic.

History

Proto-Finnic developed from Proto-Finno-Saamic. Late Proto-Finnic, spoken around the shores of the Gulf of Finland, remained fairly uniform until roughly the first millennium CE, after which dialectal differences deepened into the separate Finnic languages, partly under the influence of neighbouring Baltic, Germanic, and Slavic languages.

Phonology

Reconstructed features include a system of vowel harmony, contrastive consonant and vowel length, and the consonant gradation that survives in modern Finnish and Estonian. Proto-Finnic borrowed numerous loanwords from Baltic and Germanic languages, which provide evidence for dating its stages.
