Rinus Michels

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Marinus Jacobus Hendricus "Rinus" Michels (9 February 1928 – 3 March 2005) was a Dutch football player and coach. He is regarded as the inventor of Total Football, the tactical system in which players interchange positions fluidly, and was named "coach of the century" by FIFA in 1999. He led Ajax to the 1971 European Cup, Barcelona to a Spanish league title, and the Netherlands to victory at UEFA Euro 1988.

Playing career

Michels spent his entire playing career as a forward for Ajax (1946–1958), scoring 122 goals in 264 matches and winning two Dutch championships. He earned five caps for the Netherlands national team, all in 1950–51.

Coaching career

After coaching amateur sides, Michels became Ajax head coach in 1965. Building around Johan Cruyff, he developed Total Football: a high defensive line, pressing, and positional interchange. Ajax won four league titles and the 1971 European Cup before Michels moved to Barcelona, where he won La Liga in 1974. He managed the Netherlands at the 1974 World Cup, reaching the final, and returned to win Euro 1988 with a side featuring Ruud Gullit and Marco van Basten. His methods profoundly influenced later coaches, above all Cruyff himself.
