General number field sieve

In number theory, the general number field sieve (GNFS) is the most efficient classical algorithm known for factorising integers larger than 100 digits. It is a generalisation of the special number field sieve: while the latter can only factorise integers of a special form, the general number field sieve can factorise any integer (except prime powers).

Overview

The algorithm works by selecting a polynomial whose root modulo the integer to be factored is known, then searching for pairs of integers whose values are smooth — that is, composed entirely of small prime factors — both as ordinary integers and as algebraic integers in the corresponding number field. Linear algebra over these relations yields a congruence of squares, from which a non-trivial factor can be extracted using the greatest common divisor.

Steps

The main stages are polynomial selection, sieving to collect smooth relations, filtering and matrix solving (typically with the block Lanczos or block Wiedemann algorithm), and a final square-root step in the number field. The sieving and linear algebra stages dominate the running time and are highly parallelisable.

Complexity and significance

The heuristic running time grows as exp(((64/9)1/3 + o(1)) (ln n)1/3 (ln ln n)2/3), placing it in the sub-exponential class. Because the security of RSA depends on the difficulty of factoring, the GNFS determines practical key-size recommendations: distributed GNFS computations factored the 768-bit RSA-768 in 2009 and the 829-bit RSA-250 in 2020.
