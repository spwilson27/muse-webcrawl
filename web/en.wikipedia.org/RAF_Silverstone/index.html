Royal Air Force Silverstone or RAF Silverstone was a Royal Air Force station built during the Second World War near the village of Silverstone, on the border of Northamptonshire and Buckinghamshire, England.

History

Construction began in 1942 and the airfield opened in 1943 as the home of No. 17 Operational Training Unit (OTU), flying Vickers Wellington bombers. The station trained bomber crews for operations over Europe, using satellite landing grounds nearby. Flying accidents were frequent among trainee crews, and the station's war memorial commemorates those lost.

Layout

RAF Silverstone was built to the standard wartime pattern with three concrete runways in a triangular arrangement, perimeter track, hangars, and dispersed accommodation sites in the surrounding countryside.

Postwar use

The RAF departed after the war, and in 1948 the Royal Automobile Club leased the airfield and converted its runways and perimeter roads into the Silverstone Circuit. The circuit hosted the first World Championship Formula One Grand Prix in 1950 and remains the home of the British Grand Prix.
