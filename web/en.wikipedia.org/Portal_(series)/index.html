Portal (series)

Portal is a series of first-person puzzle-platform video games developed by Valve. The series consists of Portal (2007) and Portal 2 (2011), together with spin-offs and expansions including Portal: Still Alive (2008), the virtual-reality-adjacent tech demos Aperture Hand Lab and Aperture Desk Job, and the community-sanctioned mods elevated to official releases, such as Portal: Revolution (2024). Both main games are set in the Aperture Science laboratories, part of the same fictional universe as Valve's Half-Life series.

Gameplay and story

The player controls a test subject — Chell in the main games — armed with the Aperture Science Handheld Portal Device, which creates two linked portals on flat surfaces through which the player, objects, and energy can pass, conserving momentum. Puzzles combine portal placement with weighted cubes, laser beams, excursion funnels, and propulsion gels. The games are overseen by GLaDOS, a sardonic artificial intelligence voiced by Ellen McLain, whose darkly comic narration and the closing songs by Jonathan Coulton became widely quoted in popular culture. Portal 2 added a two-player cooperative campaign featuring the robots Atlas and P-Body, and the character of Cave Johnson, founder of Aperture, voiced by J. K. Simmons.

Reception

The series is among the most acclaimed in video game history. The original Portal, developed by a small team partly recruited from the DigiPen student project Narbacular Drop, was praised for its originality and writing; Portal 2 sold millions of copies, won numerous Game of the Year awards, and is frequently ranked among the greatest games ever made.
