Amatriciana sauce

Amatriciana sauce (Italian: sugo all'amatriciana) is a traditional Italian pasta sauce based on guanciale (cured pork cheek), pecorino romano cheese, tomato and black pepper. It originates from the town of Amatrice in the province of Rieti, Lazio, and is counted — along with carbonara, cacio e pepe and gricia — among the classic pasta sauces of Rome, where it is commonly served with bucatini.

History and ingredients

The sauce descends from pasta alla gricia, the older white (tomato-less) preparation of guanciale, pecorino and pepper cooked by shepherds of the Amatrice area; tomatoes, introduced from the Americas, were added by the late 18th or early 19th century as the dish spread to Rome with migrating Amatriciani. The officially recognised Amatrice recipe specifies guanciale, peeled tomatoes, pecorino romano (or the local pecorino di Amatrice), black pepper, and white wine or vinegar; onion is not part of the Amatrice version though it appears in some Roman variants. The dish was given traditional-specialty recognition, and Amatrice itself was devastated by the 2016 Central Italy earthquake, after which solidarity amatriciana fundraisers were held worldwide.
