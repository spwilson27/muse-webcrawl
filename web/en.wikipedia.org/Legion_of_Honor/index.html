Legion of Honor

The Legion of Honor (French: Légion d honneur) is the highest French order of merit, established by Napoleon Bonaparte in 1802. It is awarded for excellent civil or military conduct, and its insignia — a white enamelled five-armed cross suspended from a red ribbon — is instantly recognisable in French public life.

History

Napoleon created the Legion to reward merit regardless of birth, breaking with the orders of the old monarchy. It survived every regime change since, becoming the centrepiece of the French honours system under a Grand Chancellor answerable to the President of the Republic, who is its Grand Master.

Grades

The order has five grades: Chevalier (Knight), Officier, Commandeur, Grand Officier and Grand-Croix. Membership numbers are capped by grade, and foreigners may be admitted without becoming members.

Related uses

The name is shared by the San Francisco art museum, the Legion of Honor, modelled on the Palais de la Légion d Honneur in Paris, and by the Philippine Legion of Honor, a separate decoration.
