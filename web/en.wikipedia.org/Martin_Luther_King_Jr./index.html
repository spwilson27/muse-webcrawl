Martin Luther King Jr.

Martin Luther King Jr. (born Michael King Jr.; January 15, 1929 – April 4, 1968) was an American Baptist minister, activist, and political philosopher who was one of the most prominent leaders in the civil rights movement from 1955 until his assassination in 1968. He advanced civil rights for people of colour in the United States through the use of nonviolent resistance and nonviolent civil disobedience against Jim Crow laws and other forms of discrimination in the United States.

Early life

King was born in Atlanta, Georgia, the son of a prominent Baptist pastor. He graduated from Morehouse College in 1948, studied at Crozer Theological Seminary, and earned his doctorate in systematic theology from Boston University in 1955. In 1954 he became pastor of Dexter Avenue Baptist Church in Montgomery, Alabama.

Civil rights leadership

King rose to national prominence leading the Montgomery bus boycott in 1955–56. As founding president of the Southern Christian Leadership Conference (SCLC), he led nonviolent campaigns in Birmingham (1963), where he wrote the "Letter from Birmingham Jail", and the Selma to Montgomery marches (1965). On August 28, 1963, he delivered his "I Have a Dream" speech to over 250,000 people at the March on Washington. In 1964, aged 35, he became the youngest person at the time to receive the Nobel Peace Prize.

Later years and assassination

King later opposed the Vietnam War and launched the Poor People's Campaign against economic injustice. He was assassinated on April 4, 1968, in Memphis, Tennessee, where he had gone to support striking sanitation workers. His birthday is observed as a US federal holiday, Martin Luther King Jr. Day.
