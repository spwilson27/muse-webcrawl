Hanuman Chalisa
Author: attributed to Tulsidas
Language: Awadhi
Verses: 40 chaupais + framing dohas

The Hanuman Chalisa is a Hindu devotional hymn (stotra) in praise of the deity Hanuman, consisting of forty verses (chalisa derives from chalis, "forty" in Hindi). It is traditionally attributed to the 16th-century poet-saint Tulsidas, author of the Ramcharitmanas, and is written in the Awadhi language.

Content

The hymn is framed by opening and closing couplets (dohas). Its forty quatrains (chaupais) recount Hanuman's devotion to Rama, his strength, humility, learning, and exploits — carrying the life-giving herb from the Himalayas, burning Lanka, and serving as Rama's messenger — and promise devotees protection from evil, disease, and fear, and ultimately liberation through devotion.

Practice and influence

One of the most widely recited texts in Hinduism, the Chalisa is chanted daily in temples and homes, particularly on Tuesdays and Saturdays, days associated with Hanuman. It is said that reciting it 108 times is especially auspicious. Innumerable printed editions, recordings by singers such as Hari Om Sharan and Gulshan Kumar, and translations into many languages have spread its popularity; a 2011 rendition by Amitabh Bachchan with multiple singers was a notable modern recording.
