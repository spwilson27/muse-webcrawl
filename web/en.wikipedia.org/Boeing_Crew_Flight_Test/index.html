Starliner launches atop an Atlas V on the Crew Flight Test

The Boeing Crew Flight Test (Boe-CFT) was the first crewed mission of Boeing's CST-100 Starliner spacecraft, developed under NASA's Commercial Crew Program. NASA astronauts Barry "Butch" Wilmore and Sunita Williams launched aboard the Starliner capsule Calypso on June 5, 2024, on a United Launch Alliance Atlas V rocket from Cape Canaveral, and docked with the International Space Station the following day.

Mission background

The flight test was originally planned as a short mission of about eight days, following two uncrewed Orbital Flight Tests (OFT in 2019 and OFT-2 in 2022) that had encountered software and parachute and valve issues. Years of delays preceded the crewed launch; two launch attempts in May 2024 were scrubbed, one due to a valve issue on the Atlas V and another due to a ground computer fault, before the successful June 5 liftoff.

Thruster problems and extended stay

During rendezvous with the station, several of Starliner's reaction control thrusters malfunctioned, in addition to helium leaks in the propulsion system that had been detected before launch. After weeks of testing and analysis, NASA concluded that returning the crew aboard Starliner posed unacceptable risk. The spacecraft undocked and landed uncrewed at White Sands Missile Range on September 7, 2024.

Crew return

Wilmore and Williams remained aboard the ISS for about nine months, integrating into the station's long-duration expedition crew, and returned to Earth in March 2025 aboard the SpaceX Crew-9 Dragon capsule. The episode prompted widespread media coverage of the astronauts' extended stay and led NASA to assign Starliner's first operational mission to fly uncrewed cargo pending further review.
