Berlin Blockade

The Berlin Blockade (24 June 1948 – 12 May 1949) was one of the first major international crises of the Cold War. During the multinational occupation of post–World War II Germany, the Soviet Union blocked the Western Allies' railway, road, and canal access to the sectors of Berlin under Western control.

Background

The blockade was a response to the introduction of the new Deutsche Mark in the western zones and to Western moves toward creating a West German state. The Soviets hoped to force the Western powers to abandon West Berlin or to halt the formation of West Germany.

The Airlift

The Western Allies replied with the Berlin Airlift, a massive operation in which American and British aircraft flew more than 250,000 flights into West Berlin, delivering up to 12,941 tons of fuel and food per day at the operation's peak. Aircraft landed at Tempelhof, Gatow, and the newly built Tegel airport. The airlift also became famous for pilots such as Gail Halvorsen, the "Candy Bomber", who dropped small parachutes of sweets for Berlin children.

Outcome

Having failed to achieve its objectives, the Soviet Union lifted the blockade on 12 May 1949. The crisis accelerated the division of Germany: the Federal Republic of Germany (West Germany) and the German Democratic Republic (East Germany) were founded in 1949, and the Western Allies formed NATO.
