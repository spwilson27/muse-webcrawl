Grande Inter ("Great Inter") is the name given to the Inter Milan football team of the 1960s, widely regarded as one of the greatest club sides in the history of Italian and European football. Under the management of the Argentine coach Helenio Herrera and the presidency of Angelo Moratti, Inter dominated Italian football and won three consecutive European trophies between 1963 and 1967.

History

Angelo Moratti became president of Inter in 1955 and appointed Helenio Herrera, previously coach of Barcelona, in 1960. Herrera rebuilt the side around a rigorous tactical system and an exceptional group of players, including captain and sweeper Armando Picchi, playmaker Luis Suárez, winger Jair da Costa, forward Sandro Mazzola, left-back Giacinto Facchetti and goalkeeper Giuliano Sarti.

Tactics

The Grande Inter side is synonymous with catenaccio, the defensive system built around a sweeper (libero) playing behind a line of man-marking defenders, rapid counter-attacks and ruthless efficiency in front of goal. Herrera modified the older Swiss verrou system, adding devastating counter-attacking speed through Jair and Mario Corso on the wings.

Honours

The team won three Serie A titles (1962–63, 1964–65, 1965–66), two consecutive European Cups (1964, defeating Real Madrid 3–1 in Vienna; 1965, defeating Benfica 1–0 at San Siro) and two Intercontinental Cups (1964 and 1965, both against Independiente). The era is generally considered to have ended in 1967, when Inter lost the European Cup final to Celtic in Lisbon and the scudetto to Juventus in the final weeks of the season.

Legacy

Grande Inter set the standard for Italian club football for a generation. Its core players also formed the backbone of the Italian national team that won the 1968 European Championship, and Facchetti, Mazzola and others reached the 1970 World Cup final.
