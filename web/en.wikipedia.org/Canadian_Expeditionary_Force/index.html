The Canadian Expeditionary Force (CEF) was the entire overseas force fielded by Canada during the First World War. Some 619,000 Canadians and Newfoundlanders enlisted in the CEF, of whom about 424,000 served overseas; roughly 61,000 were killed and 172,000 wounded.

Formation and service

Raised from August 1914 under the direction of Minister of Militia Sam Hughes, the first contingent trained at Valcartier, Quebec, and sailed for England in October 1914. The CEF eventually fielded a four-division Canadian Corps on the Western Front from 1915, commanded from 1917 by General Sir Arthur Currie, along with cavalry, forestry, railway, tunnelling and other supporting units, and a separate contingent that served in Siberia in 1918–19.

Legacy

The Corps earned a reputation as elite assault troops, notably at Vimy Ridge in April 1917 and during the Hundred Days offensive of 1918. The wartime effort — and the political crisis over conscription in 1917 — profoundly shaped Canadian national identity. The CEF was disbanded in 1920, and its battle honours are perpetuated by modern Canadian Army regiments.
