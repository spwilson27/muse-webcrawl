Salade niçoise (French pronunciation: [saˈlad niˈswaz]; Occitan: salada nissarda) is a salad that originated in the French city of Nice. It is traditionally made of tomatoes, hard-boiled eggs, Niçoise olives and anchovies or tuna, dressed with olive oil, or in some historical versions, a vinaigrette. It has been popular worldwide since the early 20th century, and has been prepared and discussed by many chefs. Delia Smith called it "one of the best combinations of salad ingredients ever invented" and Gordon Ramsay said that "it must be the finest summer salad of all."

Traditional composition

The salad originally helped in the 19th century as a simple dish for poor people. It usually combined tomatoes, anchovies and olive oil. It evolved over time according to the tastes of chefs. A 1903 recipe by Henri Heyraud included tomatoes, artichokes, sweet peppers and black olives in addition to anchovies, tuna, hard-boiled eggs, Provençal herbs and a vinaigrette. An early champion of the salad was the mayor of Nice, Jacques Médecin, whose 1972 cookbook insisted that the authentic version should contain no cooked vegetables other than the eggs, and never potatoes or green beans in the most purist reading — though the inclusion of haricots verts and potatoes later became standard in many restaurants.

Contemporary versions

Modern versions frequently include boiled potatoes, blanched green beans, lettuce or other leaves, capers, and seared fresh tuna in place of canned. The dressing is usually a mustard vinaigrette of olive oil, lemon juice or wine vinegar, garlic, and basil. The dish is presented as a salade composée — arranged rather than tossed — so that each ingredient remains distinct on the plate.
