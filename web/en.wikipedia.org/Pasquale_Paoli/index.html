Pasquale Paoli (1725–1807)

Pasquale Paoli (6 April 1725 – 5 February 1807) was a Corsican patriot and military leader who led the island's struggle against Genoese and then French rule, and headed the Corsican Republic from 1755 to 1769. Revered in Corsica as U Babbu di a Patria ("Father of the Fatherland"), he is remembered as an early exponent of Enlightenment constitutional government and national self-determination.

Early life

Paoli was born at Morosaglia, the son of Giacinto Paoli, a leader of the Corsican revolt against the Republic of Genoa. When the revolt faltered, father and son went into exile in Naples, where Pasquale received a classical education and served as an officer in the Neapolitan army, absorbing the ideas of the Enlightenment.

The Corsican Republic

Returning to Corsica in 1755, Paoli was elected general-in-chief of the Corsican nation. That November, at the Consulta of Corte, he promulgated a constitution — often described as the first written constitution of a modern democratic state — which established universal male suffrage, a separation of powers and an elected Diet. His government founded a university at Corte, a printing press and a small navy, promoted agriculture and made Corsican (in its Italian literary form) the language of state. Jean-Jacques Rousseau admired the experiment and wrote of Corsica in The Social Contract; James Boswell's Account of Corsica (1768) made Paoli a celebrity across Europe.

French conquest and exile

In 1768 Genoa ceded its rights over Corsica to France, which invaded in force. Paoli's troops resisted but were defeated at the Battle of Ponte Novu in May 1769, and he fled into exile in London, where he lived for two decades as a celebrated pensioner of the British Crown. Returning during the French Revolution, he broke with Paris over the Terror and, with British support, established the short-lived Anglo-Corsican Kingdom (1794–1796) with Sir Gilbert Elliot as viceroy. Disillusioned by British policy, he was recalled to London and spent his final years there; he died in 1807 and was buried in St Pancras churchyard, his remains later honoured at Westminster Abbey with a memorial. In 1889 his body was repatriated to Corsica.
