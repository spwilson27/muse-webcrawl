Hjalmar Johansen

Fredrik Hjalmar Johansen (15 May 1867 – 3 January 1913) was a Norwegian polar explorer. He participated in Fridtjof Nansen's polar expedition of 1893–1896 on the ship Fram, and was Nansen's companion on the celebrated ski and kayak journey toward the North Pole that reached a record northern latitude of 86°14′N in 1895. He later took part in Roald Amundsen's South Pole expedition of 1910–1912.

Fram expedition

Johansen signed on to Nansen's Fram expedition despite having no seafaring experience, working as a stoker and then as meteorologist's assistant. When the drifting Fram failed to approach the Pole as hoped, Nansen chose Johansen as his companion for a dash north by dog sledge and ski. They left the ship on 14 March 1895, reached 86°13.6′N on 7 April — a new Farthest North record — and then endured a remarkable retreat to Franz Josef Land, wintering in a stone hut in 1895–96 and living on walrus and polar bear meat before being rescued by chance meeting with Frederick Jackson's expedition in June 1896.

South Pole expedition

Johansen joined Amundsen's South Pole expedition as an experienced dog driver. After the premature start of September 1911 ended in a dangerous retreat, Johansen publicly criticised Amundsen and was excluded from the final polar party, instead being assigned to a secondary exploration of King Edward VII Land. He became depressed after returning to Norway and died in Oslo in January 1913.

Legacy

Johansen has been increasingly recognised as one of the great polar travellers. The Cape Hjalmar Johansen in Franz Josef Land and the Johansen Islands are named after him, and his Fram diary was published posthumously.
