Pictogram

A pictogram (also called a pictograph or picto) is a graphical symbol that conveys meaning through its pictorial resemblance to a physical object. Pictograms are used in systems of writing and in standardized public signage where a message must be understood regardless of language.

Early writing systems such as Egyptian hieroglyphs, Sumerian cuneiform, and Chinese characters began as pictograms. In the modern world, pictograms are ubiquitous: road signs, airport and railway signage, laundry care symbols, hazard warnings, and the icons of computer and phone interfaces.

Modern standardization

In the 20th century, designers such as Otto Neurath (with the Isotype system) and later the designers of the 1964 Tokyo Olympics and the US Department of Transportation/AIGA symbol signs of 1974 developed standardized pictogram sets for transport, sport, and public information. ISO 7001 standardizes public information symbols internationally.

Pictograms differ from ideograms, which represent abstract ideas rather than depicting objects, and from logograms, which represent words or morphemes in a specific language.
