Heart of Neolithic Orkney

The Heart of Neolithic Orkney is a group of Neolithic monuments on Mainland, the largest island in the Orkney archipelago of Scotland. Inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1999, it comprises four major sites: the chambered tomb of Maeshowe, the standing stones of the Ring of Brodgar and the Stones of Stenness, and the excavated village of Skara Brae, together with associated burial and settlement remains.

The sites

Skara Brae, uncovered by a storm in 1850, is a remarkably preserved stone-built village occupied from about 3180 BC to 2500 BC, whose houses are furnished with stone beds, dressers, and hearths. Maeshowe is a monumental chambered cairn containing a corbelled central chamber; its entrance passage is aligned so the setting sun shines along it at the winter solstice, and its walls bear Norse runic graffiti from the 12th century. The Ring of Brodgar is a vast stone circle and henge, 104 metres across, of which 27 stones survive from perhaps 60. The Stones of Stenness are among the oldest henges in Britain, with stones up to six metres tall.

Significance

The monuments, built between roughly 3300 and 2500 BC, form a ceremonial landscape around the lochs of Stenness and Harray and testify to the social organisation and beliefs of Neolithic farming communities in northern Scotland. Later excavations at the nearby Ness of Brodgar, begun in 2003, revealed a complex of massive stone buildings between the two stone circles, further enriching the picture of the site.
