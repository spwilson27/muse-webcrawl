Piye

Reign: c. 744–714 BC
Predecessor: Kashta
Successor: Shabaqo
Dynasty: 25th Dynasty
Burial: el-Kurru, pyramid Ku 17

Piye (also spelled Piankhy or Piankhi) was a king of Kush who founded the Twenty-fifth Dynasty of Egypt. Ruling from Napata in Nubia, he invaded and conquered Egypt around 727 BC, uniting the Nile valley under Kushite rule for the first time in centuries.

Reign

Piye succeeded his father Kashta, who had already extended Kushite influence into Upper Egypt as far as Thebes. When the northern Egyptian prince Tefnakht of Sais began expanding southward, Piye launched a campaign down the Nile. His progress is recorded in detail on the Victory Stela, a granite slab erected in his twentieth regnal year at the temple of Amun at Jebel Barkal.

Conquest of Egypt

The stela describes the siege of Hermopolis, the surrender of its king Nimlot, and the submission of Memphis after an assault in which Piye's troops used ships' masts to scale the walls. Piye famously reproached Nimlot for the starvation of horses in the besieged city, reflecting his well-attested devotion to horses, which were buried near his own pyramid at el-Kurru.

Legacy

After receiving the submission of the Delta princes, Piye returned to Nubia and never again campaigned in Egypt, leaving local rulers largely in place. His brother and successor Shabaqo later consolidated Kushite rule over the whole of Egypt. Piye's reign marks the beginning of a century of Kushite pharaohs known for reviving Egyptian religious and artistic traditions.
