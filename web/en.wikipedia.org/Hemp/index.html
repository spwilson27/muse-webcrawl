Hemp

KingdomPlantae
FamilyCannabaceae
GenusCannabis
SpeciesCannabis sativa
THC contentTypically below 0.3% by dry weight (legal definition in many jurisdictions)

Hemp, or industrial hemp, is a plant in the botanical class of Cannabis sativa cultivars grown specifically for industrial and consumable use. It can be used to make a wide range of products, including fibres, textiles, paper, rope, building materials, food, and oils. Along with bamboo, hemp is among the fastest growing plants on Earth. It was also one of the first plants to be spun into usable fiber, some 50,000 years ago.

Hemp is distinguished legally and chemically from marijuana varieties of cannabis by its very low content of the psychoactive compound tetrahydrocannabinol (THC); many jurisdictions define hemp as cannabis containing no more than 0.3% THC by dry weight.

Uses

Hemp fibres, obtained from the bast of the stalk, have been used for millennia for rope, sailcloth, canvas (the word derives from cannabis), clothing, and paper. The woody inner core, the hurd or shive, is used in animal bedding and in hempcrete, a lightweight building material. Hemp seeds are a nutritious food containing protein, essential fatty acids, and minerals, and are pressed for hemp seed oil used in food, cosmetics, and industrial products. Refined hemp oil has also been used in paints, inks, and biofuel.

History

Hemp was cultivated in ancient China for fibre and seed, mentioned in early Chinese pharmacopeias, and spread across Asia and Europe. It was a strategic material in the age of sail, and colonial governments in North America encouraged or even mandated its cultivation. Production declined in the 20th century as hemp became entangled in anti-cannabis legislation, such as the United States' Marihuana Tax Act of 1937 and later prohibition regimes, despite its negligible psychoactive content.

Modern production and legality

From the 1990s onward many countries re-legalised low-THC hemp cultivation, led by producers such as China, Canada, and France. In the United States, the Agriculture Improvement Act of 2018 removed hemp from the Controlled Substances Act and established a federal regulatory framework, spurring rapid growth in hemp grown for CBD extraction as well as fibre and grain. The plant is also valued for phytoremediation and carbon sequestration owing to its fast growth and deep roots.
