Caesium

Caesium (IUPAC spelling; also spelled cesium in American English) is a chemical element with the symbol Cs and atomic number 55. It is a soft, silvery-golden alkali metal with a melting point of 28.4 °C, which makes it one of only five elemental metals that are liquid at or near room temperature.

Properties

Caesium is the most reactive of all metals and reacts explosively with cold water. It is also the most electropositive and most alkaline stable element. The metal tarnishes rapidly in air, forming oxides, and must be stored under inert conditions.

Discovery

Caesium was the first element discovered by spectroscopy: Robert Bunsen and Gustav Kirchhoff identified it in 1860 in mineral water from Dürkheim, Germany, naming it from the Latin caesius ("sky blue") for the bright blue lines in its emission spectrum.

Applications

The isotope caesium-133 defines the second in the International System of Units: since 1967 the second has been defined by the frequency of a transition in the caesium atom, and caesium atomic clocks provide the primary standard of time and frequency. Other uses include photoelectric cells, drilling fluids (caesium formate), and research in ion propulsion. The radioactive isotope caesium-137 is a notable product of nuclear fission and a major contaminant in nuclear accidents.
