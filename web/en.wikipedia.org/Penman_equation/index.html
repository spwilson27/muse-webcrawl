Penman equation

The Penman equation describes evaporation from an open water surface, developed by Howard Penman in 1948. Penman's equation requires daily mean temperature, wind speed, air pressure, and solar radiation to predict evaporation.

Formulation

The equation combines an energy-balance term, driven by net radiation, with an aerodynamic term driven by wind speed and the vapour pressure deficit of the air. In its common form, evaporation depends on the slope of the saturation vapour pressure curve, the psychrometric constant, net radiation, and ground heat flux.

Extensions

John Monteith extended the approach in 1965 to vegetated surfaces by adding surface resistance, producing the Penman–Monteith equation, which the FAO adopted as the standard method (FAO-56) for estimating reference evapotranspiration in irrigation scheduling and hydrology.
