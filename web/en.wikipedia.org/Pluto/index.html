Pluto

Pluto (minor-planet designation: 134340 Pluto) is a dwarf planet in the Kuiper belt, a ring of bodies beyond the orbit of Neptune. Discovered by Clyde Tombaugh on February 18, 1930, at the Lowell Observatory, it is the largest known trans-Neptunian object by volume but slightly less massive than Eris. From its discovery until 2006 it was considered the ninth planet of the Solar System, when the International Astronomical Union reclassified it as a dwarf planet.

Characteristics

Pluto is composed primarily of ice and rock. Its surface shows mountains of water ice, plains of nitrogen ice including the heart-shaped glacier Sputnik Planitia, and a thin atmosphere of nitrogen, methane, and carbon monoxide that freezes out as it moves away from the Sun. Its largest moon, Charon, is about half Pluto's diameter, and the two form a true binary system.

Exploration

NASA's New Horizons spacecraft flew by Pluto on July 14, 2015, becoming the first and so far only probe to explore it, returning detailed images and data before continuing onward to the Kuiper belt object Arrokoth in 2019.

Classification debate

The discovery of Eris and other large Kuiper belt objects prompted the 2006 IAU planet definition, under which Pluto — which has not "cleared its orbital neighbourhood" — is a dwarf planet. The decision remains a subject of public and some scientific debate.
