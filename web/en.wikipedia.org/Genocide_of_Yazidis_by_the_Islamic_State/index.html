Genocide of Yazidis by the Islamic State

The genocide of Yazidis by the Islamic State began in August 2014, when the Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant (ISIL) attacked the Sinjar region of northern Iraq, home to the Yazidis, a Kurdish-speaking ethno-religious minority. The United Nations and numerous governments have recognised the campaign of killings, forced conversion and sexual enslavement as genocide.

The Sinjar massacre

On 3 August 2014, ISIL forces overran Sinjar after the withdrawal of Kurdish Peshmerga. Tens of thousands of Yazidis fled to Mount Sinjar, where they were besieged without food or water; airdrops and a corridor opened by Kurdish forces eventually allowed many to escape. In the villages below, men and older women were massacred — in Kocho, several hundred men were killed on 15 August — and thousands of women and girls were abducted and sold into sexual slavery, while boys were taken for indoctrination and training as fighters. Mass graves were later documented across the district.

Scale and recognition

The UN estimated that around 5,000 Yazidis were killed and roughly 6,000–7,000 abducted; thousands remained missing years later, and some 200,000 were displaced, many to camps in the Kurdistan Region. In 2016 a UN Commission of Inquiry concluded that ISIL had committed genocide against the Yazidis, and the finding has been echoed by the US, British, German and other parliaments. The case of Nadia Murad, a survivor who received the 2018 Nobel Peace Prize, brought international attention to the enslavement. Iraqi courts and the German prosecution of ISIL members under universal jurisdiction have produced the first genocide convictions arising from the campaign.
