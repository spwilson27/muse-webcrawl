The Susquehanna River is a major river located on the East Coast of the United States, running 444 miles (715 km) from Otsego Lake in central New York south through Pennsylvania and into the Chesapeake Bay in Maryland. It is the longest river on the American East Coast that drains into the Atlantic Ocean, and the sixteenth largest river in the United States by flow. With the North Branch and West Branch joining at Northumberland, Pennsylvania, it forms the largest tributary of the Chesapeake Bay, supplying roughly half of the bay's freshwater inflow.

Geography

The main stem rises at Otsego Lake near Cooperstown, New York, flows generally south through the Appalachian Plateau — cutting dramatic water gaps through folded ridges — and passes Binghamton, Wilkes-Barre, Williamsport (via the West Branch), and Harrisburg before emptying into the upper Chesapeake Bay. The river is unusually wide and shallow, about a mile wide at Harrisburg, and is largely unnavigable, which historically pushed transport onto canals and railroads along its banks.

History

The river takes its name from the Susquehannock, an Iroquoian-speaking people who lived along its lower reaches in the 17th century. It was central to the anthracite coal trade of northeastern Pennsylvania in the 19th century. The river is also notorious for flooding: the great flood of June 1972, caused by Hurricane Agnes, remains one of the most destructive in the basin's recorded history. The Three Mile Island nuclear plant, site of the 1979 accident, sits on an island in the river near Harrisburg.

Environment

The Susquehanna is the largest single source of sediment and nutrient pollution to the Chesapeake Bay, and its watershed management is coordinated by the Susquehanna River Basin Commission. The lower river supports hydroelectric dams at Holtwood, Safe Harbor, and Conowingo.
