Los Angeles City Council

The Los Angeles City Council is the legislative body of the city of Los Angeles, California. It consists of 15 members, each elected from a single-member district for four-year terms, and is among the most highly paid city councils in the United States. The council meets at Los Angeles City Hall in the Civic Center district of downtown Los Angeles.

Powers

The council passes ordinances, sets the city budget together with the mayor, approves mayoral appointments, oversees land use and zoning, and manages city departments through its committee system. Under the city charter, Los Angeles has a "strong council" tradition balanced against a mayor with veto power, which the council can override by a two-thirds vote.

History

The council originated with the ayuntamiento of the Mexican-era pueblo and was reconstituted after American annexation; the modern 15-district structure dates to the charter of 1925, which replaced an at-large nine-member council. The council has been central to major civic controversies and reforms, including a 2022 scandal involving secretly recorded remarks that led to resignations, and subsequent ballot measures to expand the council and create an independent redistricting commission, approved by voters in 2024.
