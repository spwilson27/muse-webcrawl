Temperature

Temperature is a physical quantity that quantitatively expresses the attribute of hotness or coldness. Temperature is measured with a thermometer. Thermometers are calibrated in various temperature scales that historically have relied on various reference points and thermometric substances for definition. The most common scales are the Celsius scale (formerly called centigrade, denoted °C), the Fahrenheit scale (°F), and the Kelvin scale (K), the last being used predominantly for scientific purposes under the International System of Units (SI).

Thermodynamic definition

In thermodynamics, temperature is related to the average kinetic energy of the microscopic particles in a system: for an ideal gas, temperature is proportional to the mean translational kinetic energy of its molecules. The zeroth law of thermodynamics establishes that two systems in thermal equilibrium with a third are in equilibrium with each other and so share the same temperature. The lowest theoretical temperature is absolute zero, 0 K (−273.15 °C), at which particle motion is minimal, while no maximum temperature has been established.

Measurement

Instruments for measuring temperature include liquid-in-glass thermometers, thermocouples, resistance thermometers, bimetallic strips, and infrared pyrometers. Since 2019, the kelvin has been defined by fixing the numerical value of the Boltzmann constant.
