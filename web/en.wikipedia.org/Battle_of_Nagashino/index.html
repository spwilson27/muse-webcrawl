Battle of Nagashino

Battle of Nagashino
Date28 June 1575
LocationNagashino, Mikawa Province, Japan
ResultOda–Tokugawa victory
BelligerentsOda and Tokugawa clans; Takeda clan
CommandersOda Nobunaga, Tokugawa Ieyasu; Takeda Katsuyori

The Battle of Nagashino was fought on 28 June 1575 near Nagashino Castle in Mikawa Province. The combined forces of Oda Nobunaga and Tokugawa Ieyasu decisively defeated the Takeda clan under Takeda Katsuyori, in a battle famous for the massed use of arquebusiers.

Takeda Katsuyori had besieged Nagashino Castle. Nobunaga arrived with a relief army of about 38,000 men against some 15,000 Takeda troops, and positioned roughly 3,000 arquebusiers behind wooden stockades designed to blunt the famed Takeda cavalry charges. Traditional accounts describe the gunners firing in rotating volleys; modern historians debate the volley-fire detail, but agree that massed gunfire from prepared positions shattered successive Takeda attacks.

Significance

Nagashino is considered a turning point in Japanese warfare, demonstrating the power of firearms used by common footsoldiers against elite samurai cavalry. The Takeda clan never recovered from its losses of senior retainers and declined rapidly, being destroyed within seven years, while Nobunaga moved closer to unifying Japan.
