Sporadic group

In the mathematical field of group theory, a sporadic group is one of the 26 exceptional groups in the classification of finite simple groups. A simple group is a group which is not the trivial group and whose only normal subgroups are the trivial group and the group itself. The classification theorem states that the list of finite simple groups consists of 18 countably infinite families plus these 26 exceptions.

The 26 groups

The sporadic groups include the five Mathieu groups (M₁₁, M₁₂, M₂₂, M₂₃, M₂₄), discovered in the 1860s–70s, the three Conway groups, the Janko groups (J₁–J₄), the Fischer groups, and others such as the Higman–Sims, McLaughlin, Held, Rudvalis, Suzuki, O'Nan, Harada–Norton, Lyons, Thompson, and Baby Monster groups. The largest is the Monster group, with order about 8 × 10⁵³, constructed by Robert Griess in 1982.

Parias

Six of the sporadic groups — J₁, J₂, J₃, J₄, O'Nan, and Lyons (with Rudvalis sometimes counted) — are called "pariahs" because they are not subquotients of the Monster, unlike the other twenty, sometimes called the "Happy Family".

Monstrous moonshine

The Monster is connected to modular functions through the monstrous moonshine conjectures of Conway and Norton, proved by Richard Borcherds, linking group theory and number theory in a surprising way.
