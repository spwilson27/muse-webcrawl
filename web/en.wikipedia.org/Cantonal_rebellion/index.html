Cantonal rebellion

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Cantonal rebellion was a cantonalist insurrection that took place during the First Spanish Republic between July 1873 and January 1874. Its protagonists, the "intransigent" federal republicans, sought to establish a federation of autonomous cantons from below, without waiting for the Cortes to draw up a federal constitution.

Course of the rebellion

The rebellion began on 12 July 1873 in Cartagena, where insurgents seized the harbour and much of the Spanish fleet, and spread to many cities in Andalusia, Murcia, and Valencia, including Seville, Cádiz, Granada, Málaga, and Valencia itself. The government of the republic, first under Francisco Pi y Margall — himself a federalist, who resigned rather than use force — and then under Nicolás Salmerón and Emilio Castelar, moved to suppress the revolt.

End and consequences

Cartagena, the last stronghold, surrendered on 12 January 1874. The rebellion discredited the republic and federalism in Spain and contributed to the military coup of General Pavía and the eventual restoration of the Bourbon monarchy under Alfonso XII in December 1874.
