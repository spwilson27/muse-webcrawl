The Toyota Prius is a compact hybrid electric vehicle produced by Toyota since 1997. It was the world's first mass-produced petrol-electric hybrid car and became the best-selling hybrid vehicle globally, popularising hybrid technology worldwide.

History

The first-generation Prius (NHW10) was launched in Japan in December 1997 under the slogan "Just in time for the 21st century". Exported from 2000, it was the second generation (XW20, 2003) — a larger mid-size liftback with the distinctive triangular profile — that achieved mass success, especially in North America, where celebrity adoption and fuel prices boosted its profile. The third generation (2009) added solar ventilation and improved economy; the fourth (2015) adopted the TNGA platform. The fifth generation, revealed in 2022, adopted a much sleeker design and substantially more power, with a plug-in variant (Prius Prime) offering extended electric range.

Technology

The Prius uses Toyota's Hybrid Synergy Drive: a petrol engine operating on the Atkinson cycle combined with electric motors through a power-split device, allowing electric-only low-speed running, engine assistance and regenerative braking. Fuel economy figures around 50 mpg (US) made it for years the most efficient petrol car sold in the United States.

Impact

The Prius nameplate expanded into a family including the Prius v, Prius c and Prius Plug-in, and "Prius" became almost synonymous with hybrid cars. Cumulative sales passed five million units in the 2010s, though sales later declined as buyers shifted to SUVs and fully electric vehicles.
