Francis Xavier, "Apostle of the Indies"

Saint Francis Xavier (born Francisco de Jasso y Azpilicueta; Latin: Franciscus Xaverius; Basque: Frantzisko Xabierkoa; 7 April 1506 – 3 December 1552), venerated as Saint Francis Xavier, was a Navarrese Catholic missionary and saint who was a co-founder of the Society of Jesus (the Jesuits).

Born in Javier (Xavier, in modern Navarre, Spain), he was a companion of Ignatius of Loyola and one of the first seven Jesuits who took vows of poverty and chastity at Montmartre, Paris, in 1534. He led an extensive mission into Asia, mainly in the Portuguese Empire of the time, and was influential in evangelisation work, most notably in early modern India and Japan. He was also the first Christian missionary to venture into Japan, Borneo, the Maluku Islands, and other areas.

Mission to India

At the request of King John III of Portugal, who wanted missionaries for the Portuguese territories in Asia, Xavier sailed from Lisbon in April 1541 and arrived in Goa on 6 May 1542. He spent more than three years preaching among the Paravar pearl-fishers of the Fishery Coast of south India, then travelled to Malacca and the Maluku Islands (the "Spice Islands") in 1545–1547, where he laid foundations for Christian communities.

Japan and death

In Malacca Xavier met the Japanese fugitive Anjirō, whose accounts inspired him to travel to Japan. He landed at Kagoshima on 15 August 1549, spent two years preaching there and in Yamaguchi and Kyoto, and left behind small communities of converts. His accounts of Japan aroused great interest in Europe.

Convinced that reaching China was essential to evangelising East Asia, Xavier attempted to enter the country but died of fever on Shangchuan Island, off the Chinese coast, on 3 December 1552 while awaiting passage to the mainland. His incorrupt body was taken to Goa, where it is enshrined in the Basilica of Bom Jesus.

Veneration

Francis Xavier was beatified in 1619 and canonised by Pope Gregory XV on 12 March 1622, the same day as Ignatius of Loyola. He is known as the "Apostle of the Indies" and "Apostle of Japan" and is considered one of the greatest Christian missionaries since Paul the Apostle. In 1927 Pius XI named him patron saint of Catholic missions. His feast day is 3 December.
