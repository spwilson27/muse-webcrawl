Huineng

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Dajian Huineng (Chinese: 大鉴慧能; 638–713) was a Chinese Chan (Zen) Buddhist monk traditionally recognised as the Sixth and Last Patriarch of Chan Buddhism in China. The semi-legendary account of his life is preserved in the Platform Sutra of the Sixth Patriarch, the only Chinese Buddhist work traditionally designated a “sutra”, and he is credited with championing “sudden enlightenment”.

Born: 638, Xinzhou, Tang China
Died: 713 (aged 74–75), Shaoguan
Title: Sixth Patriarch of Chan
Predecessor: Hongren

Life according to tradition

Legend holds that Huineng was an illiterate firewood seller from the far south who, hearing a line of the Diamond Sutra (“Let your mind flow without dwelling on anything”), travelled to the monastery of the Fifth Patriarch, Hongren. Set to work pounding rice, he secretly won the patriarch’s succession contest with a verse asserting that there is no mirror-stand and no dust to wipe away — answering the head monk Shenxiu’s verse about polishing the mind like a mirror. Hongren transmitted to him the robe and bowl of Bodhidharma, and Huineng fled south, teaching only after years in hiding.

Teaching and legacy

Huineng taught that Buddha-nature is present in all beings and that enlightenment is sudden rather than gradually cultivated. His Southern school prevailed over Shenxiu’s Northern school, and all surviving Chan/Zen lineages trace their descent through him. His preserved body is enshrined at Nanhua Temple in Guangdong.
