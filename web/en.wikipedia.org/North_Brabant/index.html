North Brabant (Dutch: Noord-Brabant) is a province of the Netherlands in the south of the country. It borders Belgium to the south, the Meuse River to the north, and the provinces of Limburg to the east, Zeeland to the west, and South Holland and Gelderland to the north. The provincial capital is 's-Hertogenbosch (Den Bosch), while the largest city is Eindhoven.

History

Until the 17th century, the area formed the northern part of the Duchy of Brabant, most of which lies in present-day Belgium. During the Eighty Years' War, the northern part became a directly governed "generality land" of the Dutch Republic after the southern Netherlands remained under Spanish control, and its Catholic population faced restrictions under the Calvinist republic. North Brabant became a full province with equal status only in the Batavian era at the end of the 18th century, and it remains a predominantly Catholic region, in contrast to the historically Protestant north of the Netherlands.

Geography and cities

Major cities include Eindhoven — a major technology centre home to Philips, ASML and DAF — Tilburg, Breda, 's-Hertogenbosch, Helmond and Bergen op Zoom. The province is a largely flat, sandy lowland crossed by the Meuse (Maas) river; the Biesbosch, one of the largest national parks in the Netherlands, lies in its northwest. The region around Eindhoven, marketed as Brainport, is one of Europe's leading high-tech clusters.

Culture

North Brabant is known for its Carnival celebrations, held under temporary town names such as Oeteldonk ('s-Hertogenbosch), for its Brabantian dialect group, and for being the province of the painters Hieronymus Bosch and Vincent van Gogh, the latter born in Zundert in 1853.
