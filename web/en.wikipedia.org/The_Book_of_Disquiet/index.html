The Book of Disquiet (Portuguese: Livro do Desassossego: Composto por Bernardo Soares, ajudante de guarda-livros na cidade de Lisboa) is a work by the Portuguese author Fernando Pessoa (1888–1935), published posthumously in 1982. Attributed to the "semi-heteronym" Bernardo Soares, an assistant bookkeeper in Lisbon, it is a fragmentary, diaristic collection of reflections, aphorisms, prose poems, and autobiographical musings — described by Pessoa himself as a "factless autobiography". It is widely considered Pessoa's masterpiece and one of the defining works of literary modernism.

Composition and publication

Pessoa worked on the book from about 1913 until his death, leaving hundreds of fragments — many collected in the famous trunk of his unpublished papers — without a final ordering or completed structure. The project was first attributed to another heteronym, Vicente Guedes, before passing to Soares, who resembled Pessoa more closely. Editors have produced markedly different editions (notably those of Jacinto do Prado Coelho, 1982; Jerónimo Pizarro; and, in English, Richard Zenith's translation), so the book exists in multiple arrangements rather than a single definitive text.

Themes

The fragments meditate on tedium, dreams, solitude, the weariness of office routine, and the estrangement of the self; Soares finds in daydreaming and in Lisbon itself a richer life than action affords. Its melancholic lucidity and radical fragmentariness made it a cult book for later writers.
