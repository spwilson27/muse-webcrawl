Acroterion
Field: Classical architecture
Position: Apex and corners of a pediment
Forms: Palmette, statue, urn, disk

An acroterion (plural acroteria) is an architectural ornament placed on a flat base called the acroter or plinth, traditionally mounted at the apex or at the outer corners of the pediment of a building in the classical style.

Description

Acroteria may take the form of statues, tripods, palmettes, urns, disks, or other sculpted ornaments. On Greek temples such as the Parthenon they were often floral palmette designs or figural sculptures in marble, terracotta, or bronze.

Later use

The motif was revived in Renaissance, Neoclassical, and Beaux-Arts architecture, where acroteria appear as stone ornaments crowning pediments, parapets, and tombs.
