Trauma (psychology)

Psychological trauma is the damage done to a person's mind as a result of one or more events that are physically or emotionally harmful or life-threatening and that overwhelm the individual's ability to cope. Such events include violent assault, serious accidents, disasters, war, childhood abuse and neglect, and the sudden death of a loved one.

Responses and disorders

Common responses include intrusive memories and flashbacks, avoidance, hypervigilance, emotional numbing, sleep disturbance, and dissociation. When symptoms persist and cause significant impairment, diagnoses include post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and, in ICD-11, complex PTSD following prolonged or repeated trauma. Acute stress disorder describes similar symptoms in the first month after the event.

Types

Clinicians distinguish single-incident trauma from chronic or developmental trauma, such as adverse childhood experiences (ACEs), which are associated with long-term physical and mental health effects. Secondary (vicarious) trauma affects those who work closely with survivors.

Treatment

Evidence-based treatments include trauma-focused cognitive behavioural therapy, prolonged exposure, cognitive processing therapy, and eye movement desensitisation and reprocessing (EMDR). Trauma-informed care emphasises safety, trust, and avoiding retraumatisation in service settings.
