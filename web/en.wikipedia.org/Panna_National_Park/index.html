Panna National Park is a national park and tiger reserve in the Panna and Chhatarpur districts of Madhya Pradesh, India. Covering about 542 square kilometres of dry deciduous forest along the Ken River in the Vindhya range, it was declared a national park in 1981 and a tiger reserve in 1994 under Project Tiger. In 2020 it was added to UNESCO's World Network of Biosphere Reserves.

Wildlife

The park's habitats support Bengal tigers, leopards, sloth bears, wolves, striped hyenas, chital, sambar, chinkara, nilgai and wild boar, as well as gharials and mugger crocodiles in the Ken River. More than 200 bird species have been recorded, including vultures, the paradise flycatcher and the peregrine falcon. The terrain of gorges, plateaus and waterfalls such as the Pandav and Brihaspati falls was carved by the Ken, one of the least polluted rivers of the Yamuna system.

Tiger reintroduction

Panna became nationally prominent after its tiger population was found in 2009 to have been wiped out by poaching. A reintroduction programme began the same year with tigers translocated from Bandhavgarh and Kanha national parks, including a male brought from Pench. The founder animals bred successfully, and the population recovered to several dozen tigers within a decade, making Panna a widely cited example of tiger reintroduction.

Context

The park lies near the historic diamond-mining town of Panna, and the temples of Khajuraho, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, are about 25 km away. Threats to the reserve have included poaching, habitat pressure and the proposed Ken–Betwa river-linking project, which would submerge a significant part of the park.
