A nuclear-weapon-free zone (NWFZ) is an area in which states parties to a treaty agree not to develop, acquire, test, or station nuclear weapons, and in which the nuclear-weapon states undertake, through attached protocols, not to use or threaten to use nuclear weapons against them. The United Nations General Assembly endorsed the concept in 1975 as a contribution to non-proliferation and disarmament.

Existing zones

Treaties in force establish zones covering Latin America and the Caribbean (Treaty of Tlatelolco, 1967), the South Pacific (Treaty of Rarotonga, 1985), Southeast Asia (Treaty of Bangkok, 1995), Africa (Treaty of Pelindaba, 1996), and Central Asia (Treaty of Semipalatinsk, 2006). Mongolia's self-declared nuclear-weapon-free status has also been internationally recognised. Related regimes declare Antarctica, outer space, and the seabed free of nuclear weapons under the Antarctic Treaty, Outer Space Treaty, and Seabed Treaty. Together the zones cover most of the Southern Hemisphere.

Proposals

Zones have long been proposed for the Middle East — tied to a WMD-free zone repeatedly endorsed by UN conferences — as well as for Northeast Asia, the Arctic, and Central Europe, but none has yet been established, largely because of regional conflicts and security guarantees.
