American comic book

An American comic book is a thin periodical originating in the United States, on average 32 pages, containing comics. Since 1975, the standard format has been a saddle-stitched magazine printed in full color. Comic books are one of the primary forms of the comics medium in the United States and developed in the 1930s from reprints of newspaper comic strips.

History

The industry is conventionally divided into the Golden Age (1938–1956), beginning with the debut of Superman in Action Comics #1; the Silver Age (1956–1970), marked by renewed superhero titles from Marvel and DC; the Bronze Age; and the Modern Age. Famous Funnies (1933–34) is often considered the first true American comic book.

Industry

The market is dominated by superhero titles from DC Comics and Marvel Comics, distributed since the 1970s largely through the direct market of specialty comic shops, though sales of collected editions and graphic novels through bookstores have grown enormously.
