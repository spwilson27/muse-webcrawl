Fréchet derivative

In mathematics, particularly in functional analysis, the Fréchet derivative is a derivative defined on normed spaces or Banach spaces. Named after Maurice Fréchet, it generalizes the derivative of a real-valued function of a real variable to functions between vector spaces, by requiring that the function be approximated by a bounded linear map.

Definition

Let V and W be normed vector spaces and let U ⊂ V be an open set. A function f : U → W is called Fréchet differentiable at x ∈ U if there exists a bounded linear operator A : V → W such that the limit of ‖f(x + h) − f(x) − A(h)‖ / ‖h‖ as h → 0 equals zero. The operator A is called the Fréchet derivative of f at x and is written Df(x) or f′(x).

Properties

The Fréchet derivative, when it exists, is unique. It satisfies the usual rules of calculus, including the chain rule and linearity: the derivative of a sum is the sum of the derivatives, and scalar multiples behave as expected. Fréchet differentiability implies continuity and also implies the existence of the Gateaux derivative, though the converse is not true in general. In finite dimensions, Fréchet differentiability coincides with ordinary differentiability, and the derivative is represented by the Jacobian matrix.

Applications

The Fréchet derivative is foundational in nonlinear functional analysis, the calculus of variations, differential geometry of infinite-dimensional manifolds, and optimization on Banach spaces.
