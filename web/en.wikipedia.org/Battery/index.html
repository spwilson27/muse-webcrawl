Battery

A battery is a source of electric power consisting of one or more electrochemical cells with external connections for powering electrical devices. When supplying power, its positive terminal is the cathode and its negative terminal the anode.

History

Alessandro Volta invented the voltaic pile in 1800. Later milestones include the lead-acid battery (1859), alkaline cells and lithium-ion batteries (commercialised 1991).

Types

Primary batteries are single-use; secondary batteries are rechargeable. Chemistries include alkaline, lead-acid, nickel-metal hydride and lithium-ion.

Applications

Batteries power portable electronics, vehicles, grid storage and backup systems, and are central to electrification and renewable energy.
