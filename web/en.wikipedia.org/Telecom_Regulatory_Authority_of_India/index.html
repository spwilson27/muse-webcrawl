Telecom Regulatory Authority of India

The Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI) is the statutory regulatory body for telecommunications in India. It was established on 20 February 1997 by an Act of Parliament, the Telecom Regulatory Authority of India Act, 1997, to regulate telecom services, including the fixation and revision of tariffs, which had previously been overseen directly by the central government. Its mission is to create conditions for the growth of telecommunications in India and to protect the interests of both service providers and consumers.

Functions

TRAI makes recommendations to the government on matters such as licensing, spectrum allocation, and new service providers, and issues binding regulations and tariff orders on interconnection, quality of service, and consumer protection. Disputes between parties are heard by a separate appellate tribunal, the Telecom Disputes Settlement and Appellate Tribunal (TDSAT), created by a 2000 amendment. TRAI's remit was later extended to aspects of broadcasting and cable services.

Notable actions

TRAI played a prominent public role in the 2015–2016 net neutrality debate in India, receiving millions of public comments and ultimately issuing regulations in February 2016 that prohibited discriminatory tariffs for data services — effectively banning programmes such as Facebook's Free Basics in India. It has also driven consultations on spectrum pricing, mobile number portability, unsolicited commercial communications, and broadcast tariff frameworks, and publishes widely cited data on subscriber numbers and service quality.
