President of France

The president of France, officially the president of the French Republic (French: Président de la République française), is the executive head of state of France and the commander-in-chief of the French Armed Forces. The presidency is France's highest political office; the officeholder resides at the Élysée Palace in Paris. The current president is Emmanuel Macron.

History

The first French presidency was created by the Second Republic in 1848; its first and only president, Louis-Napoléon Bonaparte, made himself Emperor Napoleon III in 1852. The presidency took its modern, powerful form with the Fifth Republic founded by Charles de Gaulle in 1958, and direct election by universal suffrage was introduced in 1962. In 2000 the term was shortened from seven years to five.

Powers

The president appoints the prime minister, presides over the Council of Ministers, promulgates laws, may dissolve the National Assembly, calls referendums, commands the armed forces, controls France's nuclear deterrent, and appoints senior civil, military, and judicial officials. When the president's party lacks a parliamentary majority, power is shared with a prime minister of the opposing camp in a "cohabitation."

Election

The president is elected by a two-round majority vote for a five-year term, limited to two consecutive terms. Candidates require sponsorship by 500 elected officials ("parrainages") to appear on the ballot.
