Ethiopian Civil War

Ethiopia during the civil war, with insurgencies in Eritrea and Tigray

The Ethiopian Civil War was a civil war in Ethiopia and present-day Eritrea fought between the Ethiopian military junta known as the Derg and Ethiopian and Eritrean anti-government rebels from 12 September 1974 to 28 May 1991.

Background

The Derg, a committee of soldiers, deposed Emperor Haile Selassie in 1974 and established a Marxist-Leninist state under Mengistu Haile Mariam. The regime faced armed opposition almost immediately: the Eritrean People's Liberation Front (EPLF) fought for Eritrean independence, while within Ethiopia the Tigray People's Liberation Front (TPLF), the Oromo Liberation Front, and others waged guerrilla war. The Derg responded with campaigns including the Red Terror of 1977–78, in which tens of thousands of suspected opponents were killed.

Course of the war

The Soviet Union and Cuba backed the Derg on a massive scale, enabling it to repel a Somali invasion in the Ogaden War (1977–78), but the insurgencies in the north proved unwinnable. Government counter-offensives repeatedly failed to destroy the EPLF and TPLF, whose forces grew stronger through the 1980s. Drought and the regime's agricultural policies produced the famine of 1983–85, centred on Tigray and Wollo, which killed hundreds of thousands and drew global attention. In 1988 the EPLF inflicted a major defeat at Afabet and the TPLF won at Shire; the TPLF then formed the Ethiopian People's Revolutionary Democratic Front (EPRDF) coalition and advanced south.

Outcome

With Soviet support ending, Mengistu fled the country on 21 May 1991. EPRDF forces entered Addis Ababa on 28 May 1991, and the EPLF took Asmara, leading to Eritrean independence (recognised in 1993). Estimates of total deaths from the war, repression, and associated famines range from several hundred thousand to well over a million.
