The Minié ball is a type of muzzle-loading spin-stabilized bullet for rifled muskets, named after its French inventor, Claude-Étienne Minié. It came to prominence during the Crimean War and the American Civil War, where its range and accuracy greatly increased the lethality of infantry fire.

Design

Unlike earlier round balls, the Minié ball was a conical-cylindric bullet slightly smaller than the barrel bore, allowing it to be loaded quickly even in a fouled barrel. Its hollow base contained an iron cup; on firing, expanding gases forced the bullet's soft lead skirt outward to grip the rifling grooves, imparting spin and sealing the bore.

Impact

The Minié ball extended effective rifle range to several hundred yards, far beyond smoothbore muskets. Surgeons of the American Civil War recorded devastating wounds: the heavy, deforming lead bullet shattered bone, frequently necessitating amputation. Its widespread use is often cited as a factor that made Napoleonic-era frontal tactics catastrophically costly.

Successors

The Minié system was superseded in the late 1860s and 1870s by breech-loading rifles firing self-contained metallic cartridges.
