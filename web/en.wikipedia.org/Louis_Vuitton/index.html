Louis Vuitton

Louis Vuitton Malletier, commonly known as Louis Vuitton, is a French luxury fashion house and company founded in 1854 by Louis Vuitton. Its LV monogram appears on most of its products, ranging from luxury bags and leather goods to ready-to-wear, shoes, perfumes, watches, jewellery, and accessories. Louis Vuitton is one of the world's leading international fashion houses and the flagship brand of the LVMH group.

History

Louis Vuitton (1821–1892) arrived in Paris as a teenager and apprenticed as a box-maker and packer before opening his own workshop in 1854. He introduced flat-topped trunks covered in his distinctive Trianon canvas, which were lighter and more stackable than the rounded trunks then in use, and the house supplied luggage to wealthy travellers, including Empress Eugénie. His son Georges created the famous Monogram canvas in 1896, patterned with the LV initials, quatrefoils, and flowers, partly as a defence against counterfeiting.

Modern era

In 1987, Louis Vuitton merged with Moët Hennessy to form LVMH, and under Bernard Arnault the brand expanded from luggage into a global fashion label. Marc Jacobs became artistic director in 1997 and launched ready-to-wear lines and celebrated collaborations with artists such as Takashi Murakami. Later creative directors included Kim Jones, Virgil Abloh for menswear (2018–2021), and Pharrell Williams, appointed men's creative director in 2023, while Nicolas Ghesquière has directed womenswear since 2013.

Brand and stores

Louis Vuitton sells exclusively through its own boutiques and official website and is consistently ranked among the world's most valuable luxury brands. Its flagship on the Champs-Élysées and the Fondation Louis Vuitton art museum in Paris, designed by Frank Gehry and opened in 2014, are landmarks of the brand's cultural ambitions.
