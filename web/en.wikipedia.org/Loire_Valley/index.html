The Loire Valley (French: Val de Loire) is the valley of the middle stretches of the Loire River in central France, spanning some 280 kilometres between Sully-sur-Loire and Chalonnes-sur-Loire. It is famed for its abundance of historic towns, architecture, and wines, and the central part of the valley was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2000.

Châteaux

The valley contains more than 300 châteaux, built from the 10th century onward as fortresses and later as Renaissance residences of the French kings and nobility. Among the most famous are Chambord, Chenonceau, Amboise, Blois, Villandry, and Azay-le-Rideau. The concentration of royal residences earned the valley the nickname "the Garden of France."

Wine

The Loire Valley is one of France's major wine regions, producing white wines such as Sancerre, Pouilly-Fumé, Vouvray, and Muscadet, as well as reds like Chinon and sparkling Crémant de Loire, from grapes including Sauvignon blanc, Chenin blanc, Melon de Bourgogne, and Cabernet Franc.
