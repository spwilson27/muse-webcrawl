Webster–Hayne debate

DateJanuary 1830
PlaceUnited States Senate
ParticipantsDaniel Webster; Robert Y. Hayne

The Webster–Hayne debate was a famous exchange in the United States Senate in January 1830 between Senator Daniel Webster of Massachusetts and Senator Robert Y. Hayne of South Carolina. Ostensibly about a resolution on public lands, it became a landmark debate over the nature of the Union and states' rights.

Course of the debate

Hayne defended the Southern doctrine of nullification, arguing that the Constitution was a compact among sovereign states and that a state could declare federal laws void. Webster replied that the Constitution was made by the people, not the states, and that the federal Union was indestructible. His second reply to Hayne, delivered on January 26–27, 1830, is regarded as one of the greatest speeches in American history.

Famous conclusion

Webster closed with the words "Liberty and Union, now and for ever, one and inseparable!" — a phrase that became a rallying cry for Unionists in the decades before the Civil War and was widely reprinted and memorized by American schoolchildren.
