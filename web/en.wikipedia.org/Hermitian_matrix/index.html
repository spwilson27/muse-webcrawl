Hermitian matrix

In mathematics, a Hermitian matrix (or self-adjoint matrix) is a complex square matrix that is equal to its own conjugate transpose: A = AH. Equivalently, the entry in the i-th row and j-th column equals the complex conjugate of the entry in the j-th row and i-th column. Hermitian matrices generalize real symmetric matrices and are named after Charles Hermite.

Properties

The diagonal entries of a Hermitian matrix are real, and all of its eigenvalues are real. By the spectral theorem, every Hermitian matrix is unitarily diagonalizable, with an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors. Hermitian matrices that are positive-definite define inner products on complex vector spaces.

Applications

In quantum mechanics, observables are represented by Hermitian (self-adjoint) operators, guaranteeing that measured values are real. Hermitian matrices also appear in signal processing, statistics (covariance matrices), and the theory of Hermitian manifolds, where they provide the pointwise metric structure.
