Merkabah (Hebrew: מֶרְכָּבָה, "chariot") mysticism (or Chariot mysticism) is a school of early Jewish mysticism, c. 100 BCE – 1000 CE, centred on visions of the heavenly palaces and the throne-chariot of God described in the biblical visions of Ezekiel and Isaiah. Practitioners, known from their texts as the yoredei merkavah ("descenders to the chariot"), sought by fasting, prayer, hymns, and the recitation of divine and angelic names to ascend through seven heavenly palaces (hekhalot) to behold the glory of God enthroned.

Scriptural foundations

The starting point is Ezekiel 1, in which the prophet sees "the likeness of the glory of the LORD": a great storm-wind, four living creatures (hayyot) with four faces and four wings, gleaming wheels (ophanim) "full of eyes", and above them a throne of sapphire bearing a figure "like a man". Isaiah 6, with the seraphim crying "Holy, holy, holy", contributed the liturgy of the heavenly court (the Qedushah).

Hekhalot literature

The surviving texts, written in Hebrew and Jewish Aramaic mostly in late antiquity, include Hekhalot Rabbati, Hekhalot Zutarti, Ma'aseh Merkabah, Merkavah Rabba, and Sefer Hekhalot (3 Enoch), in which the patriarch Enoch is transformed into the archangel Metatron, God's scribe and "the lesser YHWH". The literature is pseudepigraphically attributed to tannaitic sages such as Rabbi Akiva and Rabbi Ishmael.

Dangers and restrictions

Rabbinic tradition surrounded the subject with warnings. The Mishnah forbids expounding the Work of the Chariot except to a sage who understands on his own. The Talmud tells of four who entered Pardes (the orchard/paradise): one died, one went mad, one became a heretic, and only Rabbi Akiva "entered in peace and departed in peace". The ascent was guarded by hostile angels at each palace gate, and the unprepared mystic could be destroyed.

Legacy

Merkabah mysticism is regarded by scholars such as Gershom Scholem as the earliest form of Jewish mysticism and the ancestor of medieval Kabbalah, whose symbolism of the divine throne and the angelic hierarchies it helped to shape.
