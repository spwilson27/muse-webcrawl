Usage-based insurance

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Usage-based insurance prices premiums partly on data recorded by an in-vehicle telematics device or smartphone app.

Usage-based insurance (UBI), also known as pay-as-you-drive (PAYD), pay-how-you-drive (PHYD) and mile-based auto insurance, is a type of vehicle insurance whereby the costs are dependent upon type of vehicle used, measured against time, distance travelled and behaviour. This differs from traditional insurance, which attempts to differentiate and reward "safe" drivers, giving them lower premiums by grouping them based on general criteria such as age, sex, location, prior accidents and vehicle type.

With UBI, premiums are calculated dynamically, sometimes in real time, using telematics data transmitted from the vehicle: mileage, time of day, road type, speed, and driving style measures such as hard braking, rapid acceleration and sharp cornering.

Types

  • Distance-based (pay-as-you-drive): premiums depend mainly on the number of miles or kilometres driven, recorded by odometer readings or GPS.
  • Behaviour-based (pay-how-you-drive): premiums also reflect how the vehicle is driven, using accelerometer and GPS data from a plug-in device, a factory-installed system, or a smartphone app.

History

The concept of mileage-based insurance dates to the early 20th century, but practical implementation awaited cheap telematics. Progressive Insurance began pilot programmes in the United States in the late 1990s and introduced its Snapshot programme nationally in 2011. In the United Kingdom, insurers such as insurethebox (launched 2010) marketed telematics "black box" policies heavily to young drivers facing very high conventional premiums. By the 2020s most large auto insurers in North America and Europe offered some form of UBI programme, and smartphone-based collection had largely replaced dedicated hardware.

Advantages

  • Low-mileage and careful drivers can receive substantially lower premiums than under class-based rating.
  • Feedback on driving behaviour may encourage safer driving; some studies report reductions in crash frequency among participants.
  • Per-mile pricing makes the cost of driving more transparent and may reduce total vehicle travel and emissions.
  • Telematics data can speed claims handling and assist in theft recovery and accident reconstruction.

Disadvantages and criticism

  • Privacy: continuous location and behaviour tracking raises concerns about surveillance, data resale and use of data by third parties or law enforcement.
  • Fairness: factors such as night driving may penalize shift workers; braking data may reflect road conditions rather than driver care.
  • Data security and accuracy: GPS gaps, device removal and smartphone attribution errors (distinguishing driver from passenger) can distort scores.
  • Penalties: some programmes can raise premiums above the standard rate for poor scores, which regulators in some jurisdictions restrict.
