Passkey

A passkey is a digital credential based on the FIDO2 standards (WebAuthn and CTAP) that allows a user to sign in to websites and applications without a password. A passkey consists of a public–private key pair: the private key stays on the user's device or in their synced keychain and is unlocked locally with biometrics, a PIN, or a device password, while the service stores only the public key. Because the credential is cryptographically bound to the legitimate site's domain, passkeys are inherently resistant to phishing.

How it works

When registering, the device generates a unique key pair for the service and domain. At sign-in, the service sends a challenge, which the authenticator signs with the private key after local user verification. No shared secret crosses the network, so server breaches do not expose usable credentials. Passkeys may be device-bound (stored on a hardware security key) or synced (backed up and shared across a user's devices through ecosystems such as iCloud Keychain, Google Password Manager, or password managers).

History and adoption

The FIDO Alliance and W3C standardised WebAuthn in 2019. In 2022 Apple, Google, and Microsoft jointly committed to the consumer-facing "passkey" branding and sync support across iOS, Android, and Windows. Major services including Google accounts (2023), Amazon, WhatsApp, PayPal, and many others have rolled out passkey sign-in, and Google made passkeys the default sign-in option for personal accounts in October 2023. Adoption studies report faster sign-ins and higher success rates than passwords, though account-recovery design and cross-ecosystem portability remain areas of active work.
