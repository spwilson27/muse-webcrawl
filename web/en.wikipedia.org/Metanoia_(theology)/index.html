Metanoia (theology)

Metanoia (from the Greek μετάνοια, "a change of mind") is a concept in Christian theology referring to repentance: a transformative change of heart and a turning away from sin toward God. In the New Testament, the word is used most often in connection with repentance, notably in the preaching of John the Baptist and Jesus.

Interpretation

Scholars have debated whether metanoia is best translated as "repentance", implying sorrow for sin, or more literally as a complete reorientation of one's thinking and way of life. Martin Luther criticised the Latin Vulgate's rendering poenitentiam agite ("do penance") and argued the Greek term meant an inner transformation rather than external acts of penance — a dispute that contributed to the Protestant Reformation.
