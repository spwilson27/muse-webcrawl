Ayodhya

Ayodhya
Country: India
State: Uttar Pradesh
Location: On the banks of the Sarayu River
Significance: Birthplace of Rama in Hindu tradition; one of the Sapta Puri

Ayodhya is a city situated on the banks of the Sarayu River in the Indian state of Uttar Pradesh. It is one of the seven sacred cities (Sapta Puri) of Hinduism and is revered as the birthplace of the god Rama, the hero of the epic Ramayana. In ancient times it was the capital of the Kosala kingdom and is also significant in Jainism and Buddhism.

History

Early sources identify Ayodhya with the ancient city of Saketa. According to tradition, it was founded by Manu and ruled by the solar dynasty (Suryavamsha), to which Rama belonged. The city finds mention in early Buddhist and Jain texts; the Buddha is said to have visited it, and several Jain Tirthankaras were born there. Under the Gupta empire it remained an important town, and in the medieval period it came under the Delhi Sultanate and then the Mughal Empire. The nearby city of Faizabad became the first capital of the Nawabs of Awadh in the 18th century.

Ayodhya dispute

For decades the city was the centre of the Ayodhya dispute over the Babri Masjid, a 16th-century mosque that many Hindus believed stood on the birthplace of Rama. The mosque was demolished in 1992, sparking widespread communal violence. In 2019, the Supreme Court of India awarded the disputed site for the construction of a Hindu temple and directed that land be allotted elsewhere for a mosque. The Ram Mandir was consecrated in January 2024.
