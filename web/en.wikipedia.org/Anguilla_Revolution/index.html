Anguilla Revolution

The Anguilla Revolution was a secessionist uprising on the Caribbean island of Anguilla in 1967–1969, in which Anguillians expelled the administration of the associated state of Saint Christopher-Nevis-Anguilla and resisted incorporation into an independent Saint Kitts, eventually returning to direct British rule and later becoming a separate British Overseas Territory.

Background

Anguilla had been administered from Saint Kitts since the 19th century, a union Anguillians resented, feeling neglected by the government in Basseterre. When Britain proposed granting the federation of St Christopher-Nevis-Anguilla internal self-government as an associated state in February 1967 — a step toward independence under Premier Robert Bradshaw — Anguillians feared permanent subordination to Saint Kitts.

Uprising

In May–June 1967 unrest escalated: the island's small police contingent from Saint Kitts was expelled, and on 11 July 1967 a referendum overwhelmingly endorsed secession. Anguilla declared itself an independent republic under leaders including Ronald Webster, though independence was never recognised. Negotiations with Britain failed, and Anguillians twice repelled attempts by Saint Kitts to reassert control.

British intervention

On 19 March 1969 British paratroopers and Metropolitan Police landed in Operation Sheepskin, meeting no resistance. The bloodless intervention restored British administration; the comic-opera character of the invasion drew wide press coverage. Under the Anguilla Act 1971 the island was placed under direct British rule, and in 1980 Anguilla was formally separated from Saint Kitts and Nevis.

Legacy

The revolution is commemorated annually on 30 May, Anguilla Day. Ronald Webster is honoured as the "Father of the Nation". Anguilla remains a British Overseas Territory with internal self-government.
