2023 Writers Guild of America strike

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The 2023 Writers Guild of America strike was a labor dispute between the Writers Guild of America (WGA)—representing some 11,500 screenwriters—and the Alliance of Motion Picture and Television Producers (AMPTP). The strike began on 2 May 2023 and ended on 27 September 2023, lasting 148 days, the joint second-longest in the guild's history.

Causes

Writers argued that the shift to streaming had eroded their pay and job security: shorter season orders, the disappearance of most residual income from reruns, and the growth of "mini-rooms" that hired small writing staffs for limited periods. The guild also sought rules governing the use of generative artificial intelligence, insisting that AI-generated material not count as source material and that writers not be required to rewrite AI drafts at lower pay rates.

Course of the strike

Late-night talk shows and productions across film and television shut down. On 14 July, the actors' union SAG-AFTRA joined the strike—the first joint strike by writers and actors since 1960—halting most remaining scripted production. Negotiations resumed in earnest in September, and a tentative agreement was announced on 24 September. Guild leadership voted to end the strike on 26 September, and members ratified the new contract in October.

Settlement

The new Minimum Basic Agreement included increased streaming residuals with a viewership-based bonus, minimum staffing levels for television writers' rooms, and landmark provisions on AI: AI-generated text is not considered literary or source material, companies must disclose if material given to writers was AI-generated, and the guild reserved the right to argue that training AI on writers' work violates the agreement.
