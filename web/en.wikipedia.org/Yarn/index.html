Yarn

Yarn is a long continuous length of interlocked fibres, used in the production of textiles, sewing, crocheting, knitting, weaving, embroidery, and rope making. Thread is a type of yarn intended for sewing by hand or machine. Modern manufactured sewing threads may be made of waxed cotton, or of synthetic fibres such as polyester, or of blends.

Manufacture

Yarn can be made from natural fibres (wool, cotton, flax/linen, silk, alpaca, cashmere, hemp), from synthetic fibres (acrylic, nylon, polyester, rayon), or from blends. Spun yarn is made by twisting staple fibres together; preparation involves cleaning, carding to align the fibres into roving, and drawing out and twisting on spinning frames — historically by hand with spindle and spinning wheel, in the industrial era with the spinning mule and ring-spinning machines. Filament yarn consists of long continuous strands, either natural (silk) or extruded synthetics, used with little or no twist. Two or more single yarns may be twisted together to form a plied yarn (for example, 2-ply or 4-ply), which improves strength and balance.

Measurement

Yarn thickness is specified by linear density: in the direct systems (tex, denier) a higher number means a heavier yarn per unit length; in the indirect cotton and worsted count systems a higher number means a finer yarn. Craft yarns for hand knitting and crochet are commonly grouped by weight categories, from laceweight and fingering through sport, double-knitting (DK), worsted/aran, chunky and super-chunky, each matched to typical needle or hook sizes and gauge (stitches per unit width). Yarn is sold in skeins, hanks, balls and cones, and is dyed either in the fibre, the yarn or the finished fabric stage.

Uses and properties

Yarns differ in warmth, elasticity, drape, durability and care requirements according to their fibre and construction: wool is warm, elastic and moisture-absorbent but can felt; cotton is cool, strong and inelastic; acrylic is inexpensive, light and machine-washable; silk is lustrous and strong. Beyond garment textiles, yarns are used for carpets, upholstery, industrial fabrics, twine and rope. The word "yarn" also colloquially denotes a long, embellished story — a usage derived from sailors' tales told while working on rope.
