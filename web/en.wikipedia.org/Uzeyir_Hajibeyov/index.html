Uzeyir bey Abdulhuseyn oglu Hajibeyov (Azerbaijani: Üzeyir bəy Əbdülhüseyn oğlu Hacıbəyov; 18 September 1885 – 23 November 1948) was a Soviet and Azerbaijani composer, conductor, musicologist and publicist. He is regarded as the founder of modern Azerbaijani classical music and opera, having composed the first opera in the Islamic world, and he composed the music for the national anthem of Azerbaijan.

Life

Hajibeyov was born in Aghjabadi and grew up in Shusha, the cultural centre of Karabakh, where he was immersed in Azerbaijani folk music and mugham. He studied at the Gori Teachers Seminary in Georgia, then taught in rural schools while writing satirical articles for Baku newspapers. Largely self-taught in composition, he later studied music in Moscow and St Petersburg with support from patrons. After the establishment of the Azerbaijan Democratic Republic in 1918 and then Soviet power, he headed the state music school that became the Baku Academy of Music — now named after him — and the Azerbaijan State Symphony Orchestra, also named in his honour.

Works

His opera Leyli and Majnun, premiered on 25 January 1908 in Baku, fused the improvised modal art of mugham with European operatic forms and is credited as the first opera of the Muslim East. He followed it with further mugham operas and with musical comedies, of which Arshin Mal Alan ("The Cloth Peddler", 1913) became internationally famous, translated into many languages and filmed repeatedly. His historical opera Koroghlu (1937) won a Stalin Prize. Hajibeyov's theoretical treatise Principles of Azerbaijani Folk Music (1945) systematised the modal foundations of mugham. With poet Ahmad Javad he wrote the music of the Azerbaijani national anthem, adopted in 1992, having originally been composed for the republic of 1918–1920.

Legacy

Hajibeyov's birthday, 18 September, is celebrated in Azerbaijan as National Music Day. His house-museums in Baku and Shusha, the Baku Academy of Music and the state symphony orchestra bear his name, and an international music festival in Baku is held in his honour. His synthesis of mugham and Western classical technique established the path followed by later Azerbaijani composers such as Gara Garayev and Fikret Amirov, who were his students.
