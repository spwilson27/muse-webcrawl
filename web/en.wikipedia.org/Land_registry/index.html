Land registry

A land registry is an official register (a repository of public records) of information relating to the ownership of land, kept by a state or federal authority. Land registries confer legal certainty on rights in land and are a foundational institution of property law.

Around the world, land registration systems fall broadly into two families: registration of deeds, which records documents evidencing transactions without guaranteeing their validity, and registration of title, on the Torrens model, in which the register itself is the authoritative record of ownership, backed by a state guarantee.

Torrens title

The Torrens system, introduced in South Australia in 1858 under Sir Robert Torrens and inspired by the registration of ships, aimed to make land transfer as simple as transferring shares. Its principles include the "mirror principle" (the register reflects all material facts), the "curtain principle" (buyers need not look behind the register) and the "insurance principle" (a compensation fund for errors). Torrens title now operates in Australia, New Zealand, Canada (western provinces), and in adapted forms elsewhere.

Examples

HM Land Registry maintains the register of title for England and Wales, covering the great majority of land. Scotland has Registers of Scotland; Germany records rights in the Grundbuch; many civil-law countries pair the registry with a cadastre that maps parcels. In the United States, recording is handled by county recorder's offices under a deeds system, with title insurance filling the resulting gaps.
