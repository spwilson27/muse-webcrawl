Snow (ship)

A snow (archaically snaw) is a type of two-masted sailing vessel that was widely used by the merchant services and navies of northern Europe from the 16th to the early 19th century. The snow is closely related to the brig, from which it differs in having a small additional mast — the snow mast or trysail mast — stepped immediately behind the mainmast to carry a trysail (spanker).

A snow under sail, showing the trysail mast abaft the mainmast

Rig

A snow carries square sails on both the fore and main masts, like a brig, but the fore-and-aft spanker is bent not to the mainmast itself but to the separate snow mast, a slender spar fixed just behind the mainmast. This arrangement kept the spanker's luff clear of the mainmast's rigging and allowed the squares'ails to be worked without interference. Snows were among the largest two-masted vessels of their time, some navies rating naval snows as sloops of war.

Use

Snows served as merchantmen in the Baltic and North Sea trades and as warships, dispatch vessels, and colliers. The type faded in the early 19th century as the simpler brig rig, using a mainmast-mounted spanker boom, became universal.
