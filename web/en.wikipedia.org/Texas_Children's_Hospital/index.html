Texas Children's Hospital is an American children's hospital located in the Texas Medical Center in Houston, Texas. Founded in 1954, it is one of the largest pediatric hospitals in the United States and is the primary pediatric teaching hospital of the Baylor College of Medicine. It consistently ranks among the top children's hospitals in national surveys, with highly rated programs in cardiology and heart surgery, neurology and neurosurgery, neonatology, and cancer.

History

The hospital opened in 1954 with 106 beds, the product of fundraising led by Houston civic figures including Leopold L. Meyer, for whom the hospital's Meyer Building is named. It grew steadily with the surrounding Texas Medical Center, adding specialty institutes in cardiology, cancer, and neurology. The Texas Children's Hospital Pavilion for Women opened in 2012, and the Lester and Sue Smith Legacy Tower, a high-rise for critical care and heart services, opened in 2018.

Facilities and programs

Beyond its main campus, Texas Children's operates hospitals in West Houston (Katy) and The Woodlands, a network of specialty care centres, and one of the largest pediatric primary care networks in Houston. Its research arm, the Jan and Dan Duncan Neurological Research Institute, opened in 2010 as the first dedicated institute for childhood neurological disease. The hospital's heart centre is among the busiest pediatric heart programs in the world, and its global health program, developed with Baylor, staffs pediatric HIV/AIDS centres in Africa and elsewhere.
