Theban hegemony

The Theban hegemony was the period in ancient Greek history, roughly 371–362 BC, when the Boeotian city of Thebes held the dominant position among the Greek city-states, having displaced Sparta as the leading military power in Greece.

Rise

Thebes rose to prominence under the generals Epaminondas and Pelopidas. At the Battle of Leuctra in 371 BC, Epaminondas used innovative tactics — a deepened left wing and an oblique advance — to crush the Spartan army, shattering the myth of Spartan invincibility. Theban armies then invaded the Peloponnese, liberating Messenia from Spartan control and permanently weakening Sparta.

Decline

Theban power rested heavily on Epaminondas personally. At the Battle of Mantinea in 362 BC the Thebans again defeated Sparta and its allies, but Epaminondas was killed, and Thebes could not sustain its ascendancy. Within a generation, the Greek cities fell under the domination of Macedon under Philip II. See also Battle of Leuctra and Epaminondas.
