Existence

FieldMetaphysics, ontology
Central questionsWhat exists? What does it mean to be?
Related notionsBeing, essence, reality, nothing

Existence is the state of having being, or of occurring in reality, as opposed to non-existence or nothingness. It is one of the most fundamental topics of metaphysics and ontology: to say that something exists is to affirm it as part of the inventory of the world. Philosophers disagree profoundly about what existence is — whether it is a property of things, a precondition rather than an attribute, or merely the satisfaction of a description — and about what kinds of things (abstract objects, fictional characters, possible objects) genuinely exist.

Existence as a predicate

In medieval philosophy, existence was often contrasted with essence: a thing's essence is what it is, its existence that it is. Aquinas built this distinction into his account of God, whose essence is identical with existence itself. Immanuel Kant famously objected that existence is not a "real predicate" — adding existence to the concept of a hundred thalers adds nothing to what the thalers are — an argument that undercuts the ontological argument for God's existence. Gottlob Frege and Bertrand Russell later treated existence as a second-order property of concepts: to say horses exist is to say the concept horse is instantiated, a view expressed in logic by the existential quantifier (∃).

Modes and kinds of existence

Debates continue over abstract objects: Platonists hold that numbers and sets exist timelessly and non-spatially; nominalists deny them. Alexius Meinong argued that objects can have "being" without existing (as with the golden mountain), a position Russell rejected. In the twentieth century, W. V. O. Quine reframed ontology around what a theory's quantifiers range over — "to be is to be the value of a bound variable". Existentialism, by contrast, focused on human existence: for Sartre, for human beings "existence precedes essence", meaning people are not given a fixed nature but make themselves through choices.
