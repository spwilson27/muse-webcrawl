eSIM

TypeEmbedded subscriber identity module
StandardGSMA Remote SIM Provisioning
Chip formMFF2 (soldered)
Introduced2016 (consumer specification)

An eSIM chip soldered onto a circuit board.

An eSIM (embedded SIM) is a form of SIM card that is embedded directly into a device and cannot be physically removed or swapped like a traditional SIM card. Instead, the subscriber's profile is downloaded and managed remotely over the air, using provisioning standards defined by the GSMA.

Technology

An eSIM uses an eUICC chip, commonly in the soldered MFF2 package, which can store multiple operator profiles, of which one is active at a time. Users typically activate a profile by scanning a QR code. Because no physical card is needed, manufacturers save internal space and can seal devices against water ingress more easily.

Adoption

eSIMs first appeared in consumer devices such as smartwatches and tablets in the mid-2010s, and in smartphones with devices such as the Google Pixel 2 and iPhone XS in 2017–2018. In 2022 Apple released US models of the iPhone 14 with no physical SIM tray at all, a milestone in eSIM-only design. The technology is also important in the Internet of Things, where remote provisioning lets large fleets of connected devices be managed without handling physical cards. A successor technology, iSIM, integrates the SIM functions into the device's main processor.
