The Internet (or internet) is the global system of interconnected computer networks that uses the Internet protocol suite (TCP/IP) to communicate between networks and devices. It is a network of networks that consists of private, public, academic, business, and government networks of local to global scope, linked by a broad array of electronic, wireless, and optical networking technologies, carrying information resources and services such as the World Wide Web, electronic mail, telephony, and file sharing.

History

The Internet grew out of the U.S. ARPANET, which connected its first four nodes in 1969 and pioneered packet switching, building on the theoretical work of Leonard Kleinrock and others. Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn designed the Transmission Control Protocol and Internet Protocol (TCP/IP), adopted as the ARPANET standard on 1 January 1983 — often considered the Internet's birthday. NSFNET linked universities in the 1980s, and commercial restrictions were lifted in the early 1990s. Tim Berners-Lee's invention of the World Wide Web at CERN (1989–1991) — hypertext documents accessed via browsers — triggered explosive growth in public use.

Technology

Data is broken into packets routed across networks by IP; the TCP protocol ensures reliable, ordered delivery. Every device has an IP address (IPv4, and increasingly IPv6), and the Domain Name System (DNS) translates human-readable names into addresses. Physical infrastructure includes fibre-optic submarine cables, satellites, and wireless links. Standards are developed openly by bodies such as the Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF) and the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C).

Governance and society

No single authority runs the Internet; coordination is shared among organizations such as ICANN (names and numbers), regional registries, and network operators. The Internet has transformed commerce, media, education, politics, and social life, while raising issues of access (the digital divide), privacy, security, censorship, and misinformation. By the 2020s, more than five billion people — over two-thirds of the world's population — were online.
