The Mercedes-AMG Petronas Formula One Team is a Formula One racing team and constructor based in Brackley, England, owned by the Mercedes-Benz Group together with team principal Toto Wolff and Ineos. Mercedes first competed in Formula One in 1954–1955, winning two Drivers' Championships with Juan Manuel Fangio, before withdrawing from motor racing after the 1955 Le Mans disaster. The modern team was created when Daimler bought the championship-winning Brawn GP team at the end of 2009 and relaunched it as Mercedes for 2010.

Return and hybrid-era dominance

After several rebuilding seasons with Michael Schumacher and Nico Rosberg, Mercedes rose to dominance when hybrid turbocharged power units were introduced in 2014. Its Brixworth-built engines, developed by Mercedes-AMG High Performance Powertrains, held a decisive advantage, and the team won eight consecutive Constructors' Championships (2014–2021) — a Formula One record. Lewis Hamilton, who joined in 2013, won six of his seven world titles with the team (2014, 2015, 2017–2020); Nico Rosberg won the 2016 championship before retiring days later. Valtteri Bottas (2017–2021) and George Russell (from 2022) subsequently partnered Hamilton, who left for Ferrari at the end of 2024, with Andrea Kimi Antonelli taking his seat for 2025.

Facilities

The team operates from a technology campus in Brackley, employing over a thousand staff, while its power units are designed and built in Brixworth, Northamptonshire. The cars race in the marque's traditional silver colours — the "Silver Arrows" — though the team raced in black livery in 2020–2021 in support of diversity initiatives before returning to silver.

Record

By the mid-2020s Mercedes had accumulated well over 120 Grand Prix victories in its modern incarnation, ranking among the most successful constructors in the sport's history. The team's record streak of double championships and Hamilton's individual titles made the Brackley operation the defining Formula One team of the 2010s.
