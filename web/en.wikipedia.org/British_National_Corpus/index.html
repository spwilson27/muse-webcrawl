British National Corpus

The British National Corpus (BNC) is a 100-million-word text corpus of samples of written and spoken English from a wide range of sources, compiled in the early 1990s. The corpus covers British English of the late 20th century and was designed to be a representative sample of the language as a whole. It is widely used in lexicography, language teaching, and natural language processing research.

Contents

The written component draws on newspapers, academic books, popular fiction, letters, essays, and other material; the spoken component includes recordings of everyday conversation (collected by volunteers across demographic groups) and context-governed speech such as meetings, lectures, and broadcasts. Every text is encoded with bibliographic and demographic metadata, and words are tagged for part of speech using the CLAWS tagger developed at Lancaster University.

Development and use

The BNC was built by a consortium including Oxford University Press, Longman, the British Library, and the universities of Oxford and Lancaster, with government and commercial funding. It underpinned major reference works such as the Oxford English Dictionary revisions and learner's dictionaries, and remains a standard benchmark for studies of British English usage. A successor, the BNC2014, compiled by Lancaster University and Cambridge University Press, provides a comparable corpus of contemporary British English.
