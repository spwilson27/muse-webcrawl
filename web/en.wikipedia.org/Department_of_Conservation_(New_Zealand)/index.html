The Department of Conservation (DOC; Māori: Te Papa Atawhai) is the public service department of New Zealand charged with the conservation of New Zealand's natural and historical heritage. It was formed on 1 April 1987 under the Conservation Act 1987.

Responsibilities

DOC administers most of New Zealand's public conservation land — around 8.5 million hectares, roughly 30% of the country — including 13 national parks, conservation parks, nature and scientific reserves, and marine reserves. It manages visitor facilities including Great Walks tracks, huts and campsites, and is responsible for protecting native species, controlling introduced pests such as possums, stoats and rats, and preserving historic sites on conservation land.

Organisation and history

The department was created by merging functions from the Lands and Survey Department, the Forest Service and the Wildlife Service. Its governing legislation, the Conservation Act 1987, also established the New Zealand Conservation Authority and regional conservation boards. DOC works in partnership with iwi under Treaty of Waitangi principles, reflected in section 4 of the Act.

Programmes

Major DOC initiatives include island pest eradications that created sanctuaries for birds such as the kākāpō and kiwi, the Predator Free 2050 programme in partnership with other agencies, and recovery plans for threatened species including Hector's and Māui dolphins.
