Marketing automation

Marketing automation is software that automates basic marketing tasks and workflows, such as email marketing, social media posting, lead generation and nurturing, and ad campaigns — not just for the sake of efficiency, but so that organizations can provide a more personalized experience for their customers. The technology of marketing automation makes these tasks easier and allows companies to streamline, automate and measure marketing tasks so they can increase operational efficiency and grow revenue faster.

Features

Typical platforms combine a customer database or link to a CRM system with tools for building landing pages and forms, sending triggered and drip email campaigns, scoring and segmenting leads according to their behaviour, and reporting on campaign performance. Workflow builders let marketers define rules — for example, sending a series of messages when a visitor downloads a white paper or abandons a shopping cart. Modern systems extend to SMS, push notifications, direct mail and advertising audiences.

History

Email service providers of the early 2000s added automation features, and dedicated business-to-business platforms such as Eloqua (founded 1999), Marketo (2006) and Pardot grew alongside HubSpot's inbound-marketing suite. Consolidation followed: Oracle acquired Eloqua in 2012, Adobe acquired Marketo in 2018, and Salesforce acquired Pardot as part of ExactTarget. Consumer-oriented tools such as Mailchimp and ActiveCampaign brought automation to small businesses. More recently, platforms have added machine-learning features for send-time optimisation, content recommendation and predictive lead scoring.

Criticism and regulation

Automation can produce impersonal or mistimed messages and, at scale, contributes to spam; it is regulated by laws such as the US CAN-SPAM Act, Canada's CASL and the European GDPR and ePrivacy rules, which require consent and opt-out mechanisms.
