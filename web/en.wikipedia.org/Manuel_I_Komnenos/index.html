Manuel I Komnenos

Reign: 1143–1180

Predecessor: John II Komnenos

Successor: Alexios II Komnenos

Dynasty: Komnenian

Manuel I Komnenos (1118–1180) was Byzantine emperor from 1143 to 1180, the last emperor under whom the Byzantine Empire acted as a great Mediterranean power.

Reign

Manuel pursued an ambitious western policy: alliances and campaigns in Italy, Hungary and the crusader states, hosting the Second Crusade's passage in 1147. He made Antioch acknowledge Byzantine suzerainty and campaigned deep into Anatolia against the Seljuks of Rum.

Myriokephalon and decline

His attempt to take Konya ended in defeat at Myriokephalon in 1176, often seen as a turning point; his death in 1180 was followed by regency turmoil, anti-Latin violence in Constantinople, and the empire's rapid weakening under the Angeloi — vulnerabilities his contemporary Frederick I would exploit in the Balkans.

Character

Chroniclers depict an energetic, personally brave ruler fond of western chivalry, whose court was a centre of learning and diplomacy.
