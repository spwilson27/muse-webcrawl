Iphigenia

Iphigenia (/ɪfɪˈdʒɪnaɪə/; Ancient Greek: Ἰφιγένεια, Iphigéneia) is a figure in Greek mythology, the daughter of King Agamemnon and Queen Clytemnestra, and the sister of Orestes and Electra.

Mythology

In the most famous version of the myth, Agamemnon's fleet is becalmed at Aulis, unable to sail for Troy, because he has offended the goddess Artemis. The seer Calchas declares that Artemis demands the sacrifice of Iphigenia. In some accounts she is slaughtered on the altar; in others, Artemis substitutes a deer at the last moment and carries Iphigenia off to Tauris, where she becomes a priestess of the goddess.

In literature

The story was dramatized by Euripides in two surviving plays, Iphigenia at Aulis and Iphigenia in Tauris. In the latter, Iphigenia is rescued by her brother Orestes and returns to Greece. Her sacrifice also motivates Clytemnestra's later murder of Agamemnon in Aeschylus's Oresteia. Gluck's opera Iphigénie en Tauride (1779) and Goethe's play Iphigenie auf Tauris (1787) are major later treatments.
