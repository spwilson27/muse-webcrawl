Provinces of Libya

The provinces of Libya were the historic first-level administrative divisions of Libya during the monarchy and the early republic. From 1934, Italian Libya was divided into provinces, and after independence the Kingdom of Libya (1951–1963) comprised three provinces: Tripolitania in the northwest, Cyrenaica in the east, and Fezzan in the southwest.

History

The three provinces reflected the historical regions of the country. In 1963 the federal system was abolished and Libya became a unitary state divided into ten governorates (muhafazat). These were later replaced by municipalities (baladiyat), and from the 1990s by districts (sha'biyat), the system retained after the 2011 civil war.

The three provinces

  • Tripolitania — the northwestern coastal region, containing the capital Tripoli.
  • Cyrenaica — the eastern region, centred on Benghazi.
  • Fezzan — the sparsely populated desert southwest, centred on Sabha.

The provincial division remains culturally and politically significant, and proposals to restore a federal structure based on the three provinces have been raised in post-2011 constitutional debates.
