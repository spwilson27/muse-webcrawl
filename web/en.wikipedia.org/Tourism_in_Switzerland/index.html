Tourism in Switzerland

Tourism in Switzerland is a major sector of the Swiss economy, built on the country's Alpine scenery, lakes, historic towns, ski resorts, and efficient rail network. Switzerland was one of the birthplaces of modern tourism: British travellers popularized mountaineering in the nineteenth century, and Thomas Cook brought his first conducted tour to the country in 1863.

Development

The Grand Tour brought aristocratic visitors to Geneva and the Lake Geneva region in the eighteenth century. In the Victorian era, British climbers and the Alpine Club (founded 1857) made peaks such as the Matterhorn famous; the first ascent of the Matterhorn in 1865, though ending in tragedy, fixed Zermatt in the public imagination. Grand hotels and mountain railways followed, and winter sports tourism developed around St. Moritz, Davos, and other resorts from the 1860s onward.

Destinations

Popular destinations include Zurich, Lucerne, Bern, Geneva, Interlaken, and Zermatt, as well as the Bernese Oberland, Valais, and Graubünden regions. Scenic rail journeys — the Glacier Express, Bernina Express, and GoldenPass lines — are attractions in themselves, and the Swiss Travel Pass gives visitors access to the integrated public transport network. Switzerland has more than a dozen UNESCO World Heritage Sites, including the old city of Bern, the Lavaux vineyard terraces, and the Jungfrau-Aletsch Alpine area.

Economic role

Tourism accounts for a significant share of Swiss GDP and employment, particularly in mountain cantons where it is often the dominant industry. Foreign visitors contribute a large portion of overnight stays, and Switzerland markets itself on quality, safety, cleanliness, and sustainability rather than low prices, as it is among the most expensive destinations in Europe.
