Quark (dairy product)

Quark is a type of fresh dairy product made by warming soured milk until the desired amount of curdling is achieved, and then straining it. It can be classified as a fresh acid-set cheese. Traditional quark can be made without rennet, but modern dairies may add small quantities. It is soft, white and unaged, and can be compared to products such as cottage cheese, fromage blanc and Icelandic skyr.

Production

Quark is made from pasteurised milk soured with lactic acid bacteria; the resulting curd is separated from the whey, traditionally by hanging in cheesecloth or, industrially, in a centrifuge separator. The finished product has a smooth, spreadable texture and a mild, slightly tart taste. Fat content ranges from skimmed (about 0.2%) to cream-enriched versions of 40% fat in dry matter.

Use

Quark is a staple in German-speaking countries (where it is called Quark or Topfen), the Netherlands (kwark), Scandinavia (kvarg), and Slavic countries (e.g. Polish twaróg, Russian tvorog). It is used in cheesecake (Käsekuchen), dumplings, spreads mixed with herbs or jam, and as a high-protein food eaten with fruit. In Central Europe it is also sold flavoured and as a dessert.

Nutrition

Skimmed quark is notable for its high protein content (around 12–14 g per 100 g) with very little fat, which has made it popular in fitness and diet contexts.
