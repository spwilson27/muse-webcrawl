Act of Accord

The Act of Accord was an act of the Parliament of England passed on 25 October 1460, during the Wars of the Roses. It declared that King Henry VI would remain king for life, but that Richard, Duke of York, and his heirs would succeed him, displacing Henry's son Edward of Westminster as heir.

Background

After the Yorkist victory at the Battle of Northampton in July 1460, the Duke of York returned from Ireland and claimed the throne by right of descent. Parliament instead brokered a compromise: Henry would keep the crown, while York was recognised as heir presumptive and made Protector of the Realm.

Aftermath

Queen Margaret of Anjou refused to accept the disinheritance of her son and raised an army in the north. York was killed weeks later at the Battle of Wakefield in December 1460, and the compromise collapsed; his son was proclaimed king as Edward IV the following year.
