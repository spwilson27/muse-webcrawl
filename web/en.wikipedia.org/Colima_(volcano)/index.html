Colima (volcano)

The Colima volcanic complex in western Mexico consists of two peaks: the older, extinct Nevado de Colima (4,339 m) and the younger, highly active Volcán de Colima, also called Volcán de Fuego (3,820 m). It is one of the most active volcanoes in North America and the most active in Mexico.

Activity

Volcán de Colima has erupted more than 40 times since 1576. Its activity includes dome growth, Vulcanian explosions, pyroclastic flows, and ash falls affecting nearby towns such as Ciudad Guzmán. Significant eruptions occurred in 1913 and during 2015–2017, when lava dome growth and collapses repeatedly forced evacuations.

Monitoring

Because hundreds of thousands of people live within the hazard zone, the volcano is continuously monitored by the University of Colima's observatory using seismometers, cameras, and gas measurements.
