The 1948 Czechoslovak coup d'état — in Communist historiography known as Victorious February (Czech: Vítězný únor) — was an event in late February 1948 in which the Communist Party of Czechoslovakia, supported by the Soviet Union, assumed undisputed control of the government of Czechoslovakia, marking the onset of four decades of communist rule in the country.

Events

The crisis was triggered when twelve non-communist ministers resigned on 20 February 1948, hoping to force early elections, after the communist Minister of the Interior Václav Nosek purged non-communist police commanders. Prime Minister Klement Gottwald mobilised party activists, armed workers' militias occupied Prague, and "action committees" purged opponents. On 25 February, the ailing President Edvard Beneš, fearing civil war or Soviet intervention, accepted a new government dominated by the KSČ.

Consequences

Foreign Minister Jan Masaryk was found dead below a window of the Foreign Ministry on 10 March 1948, officially ruled a suicide. Czechoslovakia was drawn firmly into the Soviet bloc, and the coup became a defining early episode of the Cold War, shocking Western opinion and spurring support for the Marshall Plan and the formation of NATO.
