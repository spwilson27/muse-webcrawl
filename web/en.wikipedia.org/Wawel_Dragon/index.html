Wawel Dragon

The Wawel Dragon (Polish: Smok Wawelski) is a famous dragon from Polish folklore, said to have lived in a cave beneath Wawel Hill in Kraków, beside the Vistula River. It is one of the best-known Polish legends and a symbol of the city.

Legend

According to the tale, recorded in versions by chroniclers such as Wincenty Kadłubek, the dragon terrorized the countryside during the reign of the legendary King Krakus, devouring livestock and demanding sacrifices. Knights who tried to slay it failed. In the most popular version, a clever cobbler's apprentice (or, in some tellings, the king's sons) fed the dragon a sheep stuffed with sulfur and tar; overcome by thirst, the dragon drank from the Vistula until it burst. The grateful king rewarded the hero, and the city of Kraków was said to be founded on the dragon's defeat.

Legacy

The Dragon's Den (Smocza Jama), a limestone cave under Wawel Castle, is a tourist attraction, and a bronze statue of the dragon by Bronisław Chromy, installed in 1972 at the cave's exit, periodically breathes real fire. The dragon's supposed bones hang by the entrance of Wawel Cathedral. The Wawel Dragon also appears in literature and popular culture, and lends its name to an award at the Kraków Film Festival.
