The Siege of Vienna (1529) was the first attempt by the Ottoman Empire, led by Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent, to capture the city of Vienna, capital of the Habsburg Archduchy of Austria. The siege marked the high point of Ottoman expansion into Central Europe and the beginning of some 150 years of conflict between the Habsburgs and the Ottomans.

The campaign

Suleiman's army, having taken Buda in Hungary, advanced on Vienna in the autumn of 1529, hampered by heavy rains that delayed its heavy artillery and by the campaigning season's late start. The city, defended by some 21,000–24,000 soldiers under Niklas Graf Salm and Wilhelm von Roggendorf, hastily strengthened its medieval walls and demolished suburbs to clear fields of fire.

The siege

Ottoman troops invested Vienna on 27 September. Mining and mass assaults in early October breached sections of the wall but were repulsed with heavy losses. With supplies dwindling, sickness spreading, and winter approaching, Suleiman ordered a final assault on 14 October, which failed; the Ottomans burned their camp and withdrew toward Hungary the following day.

Aftermath

The successful defence checked Ottoman ambitions in Austria for a generation. Suleiman campaigned again in 1532 but was halted at Güns (Kőszeg). Vienna would face its second, more famous siege in 1683, relieved at the Battle of Vienna.
