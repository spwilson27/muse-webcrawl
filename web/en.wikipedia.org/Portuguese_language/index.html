Portuguese (português) is a Romance language originating in what is now Galicia and northern Portugal, descended from the Vulgar Latin spoken in the western Iberian Peninsula. Spread by the Portuguese Empire, it is today the official language of nine countries and the sixth most spoken language in the world, with the great majority of speakers in Brazil.

History

Galician-Portuguese emerged as a distinct language in the medieval kingdom of Galicia and Portugal, the language of the famous cantigas lyric poetry. With 15th–16th-century exploration, Portuguese reached Africa, Asia and America, giving rise to creoles in places such as Cape Verde, Guinea-Bissau and Malacca. The work of Luís de Camões (Os Lusíadas, 1572) fixed its literary prestige.

Features

Portuguese is known for its rich vowel system including phonemic nasal vowels and diphthongs, a distinction between two verbs meaning "to be" (ser and estar), and the personal infinitive. The two major standards, European and Brazilian Portuguese, differ in pronunciation, vocabulary and some grammar; the 1990 Orthographic Agreement seeks to unify spelling across the Lusophone world.
