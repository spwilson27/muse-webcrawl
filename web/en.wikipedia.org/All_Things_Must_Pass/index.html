All Things Must Pass is the third studio album by the English rock musician George Harrison. Released as a triple album in November 1970, it was Harrison's first solo work after the break-up of the Beatles in April that year. It includes the hit singles "My Sweet Lord" and "What Is Life", as well as songs such as "Isn't It a Pity" and the title track that had been turned down for inclusion on releases by the Beatles. The album reflects the influence of Harrison's musical activities with artists such as Bob Dylan, the Band, Delaney & Bonnie and Eric Clapton, and addresses his spiritual search in songs influenced by Hindu philosophy.

Co-produced by Phil Spector, the album employs his Wall of Sound production technique to notable effect. It was recorded with a large group of musicians including Ringo Starr, Eric Clapton, members of the Band's circle, and the band Badfinger.

Background

During the Beatles' later years, Harrison's songwriting had matured rapidly, but his output was limited by the dominance of John Lennon and Paul McCartney in the group's albums; many of the songs on All Things Must Pass had been rehearsed or auditioned during the Get Back sessions in January 1969. Following the Beatles' break-up, Harrison began recording the album in May 1970, drawing on a stockpile of songs written over several years.

Music and lyrics

The album's themes include religious devotion ("My Sweet Lord", "Awaiting on You All", "Hear Me Lord"), acceptance of impermanence (the title track), lost love ("Isn't It a Pity", "Run of the Mill"), and friendship. The third disc, titled Apple Jam, consists of informal instrumental jams with musicians including Eric Clapton and members of what became Derek and the Dominos.

Release and reception

All Things Must Pass was an immediate commercial success, topping charts in the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, and elsewhere. It was certified seven-times platinum in the US. Critics received it warmly; it is widely regarded as the finest solo album by a former Beatle and frequently appears on lists of the greatest albums of all time. In 2020 it placed at number 79 on Rolling Stone's list of the 500 greatest albums. A 50th anniversary edition, with new mixes by Paul Hicks and executive producer Dhani Harrison, was released in 2021.
