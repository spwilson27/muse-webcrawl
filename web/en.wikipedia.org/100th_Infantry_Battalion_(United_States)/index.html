100th Infantry Battalion (United States)

100th Infantry Battalion
Active: 1942–1946; 1947–present (Reserve)
Country: United States
Branch: US Army
Motto: Remember Pearl Harbor
Nickname: Purple Heart Battalion

The 100th Infantry Battalion is a unit of the United States Army composed predominantly of Japanese American soldiers from Hawaii. Formed in June 1942 after the attack on Pearl Harbor, it was the first US Army unit of Nisei (second-generation Japanese Americans) to enter combat in World War II.

World War II

The battalion trained at Camp McCoy, Wisconsin, and Camp Shelby, Mississippi, then deployed to North Africa and Italy in 1943. It fought at Salerno, Monte Cassino and Anzio, suffering such heavy casualties — and earning so many Purple Hearts — that it became known as the "Purple Heart Battalion." In 1944 it was attached to the 442nd Regimental Combat Team while retaining its separate designation.

Legacy

Together with the 442nd, the 100th became among the most decorated American units of its size. Its motto, "Remember Pearl Harbor," reflected its soldiers' determination to prove their loyalty. Today the battalion continues as the only infantry unit in the US Army Reserve, headquartered in Hawaii.
