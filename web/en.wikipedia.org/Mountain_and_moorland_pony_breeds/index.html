Mountain and moorland pony breeds

Mountain and moorland ponies, often abbreviated M&M, are a group of native pony breeds of the British Isles adapted over centuries to upland and moorland environments. The grouping is especially used in British showing, where mountain and moorland classes form a distinct division judged on breed type, hardiness of conformation and natural, untrimmed presentation.

The breeds

Nine breeds are conventionally recognised as mountain and moorland: the Exmoor, Dartmoor, New Forest, Welsh (Sections A, B, C and D), Connemara, Highland, Fell, Dales and Shetland. They share characteristics shaped by harsh semi-feral life — compact bodies, deep girths, dense double coats, abundant mane and tail feather, sure-footedness and thriftiness on poor forage — while differing in size from the diminutive Shetland to the cob-sized Welsh Section D and Highland.

Use

Historically these ponies worked in mines, on farms, and as pack and draught animals in their home regions. Today they are valued as children's mounts, driving ponies and all-round family horses, and semi-feral herds still perform conservation grazing on moors and nature reserves. Several of the breeds are listed as rare or at risk by the Rare Breeds Survival Trust, and their showing standard traditionally requires them to be presented unplaited, with full mane and tail, to preserve their native character.
