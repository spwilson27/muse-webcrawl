The Who

Origin: London, England

Formed: 1964

Members: Roger Daltrey, Pete Townshend; John Entwistle (d. 2002), Keith Moon (d. 1978)

Genres: Rock, hard rock, art rock

The Who are an English rock band formed in London in 1964, comprising singer Roger Daltrey, guitarist and songwriter Pete Townshend, bassist John Entwistle and drummer Keith Moon. Regarded as one of the most influential rock bands of the twentieth century, they pioneered the rock opera and were famous for explosive live shows ending in smashed instruments.

Career

The band broke through with mod anthems "My Generation" (1965) and "I Can See for Miles", then released the concept albums Tommy (1969), a rock opera about a deaf, dumb and blind pinball player, Who's Next (1971), featuring "Baba O'Riley" and "Won't Get Fooled Again", and Quadrophenia (1973). Moon's death in 1978 and Entwistle's in 2002 left Daltrey and Townshend, who have continued to tour and record, releasing the album Who in 2019.

Legacy

The Who have sold over 100 million records, were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1990, and influenced punk, hard rock and power pop. Their dynamic interplay — Moon's frenzied drumming, Entwistle's virtuoso lead bass, Townshend's windmill guitar — remains a benchmark of live rock.
