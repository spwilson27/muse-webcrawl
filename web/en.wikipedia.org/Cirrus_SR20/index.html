Cirrus SR20

Cirrus SR20
Role: light aircraft
Manufacturer: Cirrus Aircraft
First flight: 21 March 1995
Introduction: 1999

The Cirrus SR20 is an American piston-engine, four- or five-seat composite monoplane built by Cirrus Aircraft of Duluth, Minnesota. It was the first production general aviation aircraft equipped with a parachute system to lower the aircraft safely to the ground after a loss of control or structural failure.

Design

The SR20 has a fixed tricycle landing gear, a Continental IO-360 engine of 200 hp in early models, and side-yoke controls. Later "G6" models use a Lycoming IO-390 engine. All Cirrus SR aircraft feature the Cirrus Airframe Parachute System (CAPS) and glass cockpit avionics. The SR20 is widely used in flight training, including by airline academies and the United States Air Force Academy (as the T-53A).

Development

The SR20 was certified by the FAA in October 1998 and first delivered in 1999. It was followed by the higher-performance SR22, which outsold it, but the SR20 remains popular with training fleets and as an entry-level model.
