Tugaloo River

Length45.7 mi (73.5 km)
SourceConfluence of the Tallulah and Chattooga Rivers
MouthLake Hartwell (Savannah River)
StatesGeorgia, South Carolina

The Tugaloo River is a 45.7-mile-long (73.5 km) river in the southeastern United States that forms part of the border between Georgia and South Carolina. It is created by the confluence of the Tallulah River and the Chattooga River near Lake Tugalo, and flows southwest to join the Seneca River, forming Lake Hartwell on the Savannah River system.

Course

From its head at Lake Tugalo, the Tugaloo flows through a largely forested valley along the Georgia–South Carolina line, passing near the town of Tugaloo and Tugaloo State Park in Georgia before emptying into Lake Hartwell.

History

The name derives from a Cherokee town, Tugaloo, once located near the river's mouth. The area was the setting of early Cherokee settlements and later of the Yonah Dam and hydroelectric developments by Georgia Power.

Recreation

The river and its impoundments support fishing, boating, and lakeside recreation, and the nearby Tallulah Gorge and Chattooga River attract whitewater enthusiasts.
