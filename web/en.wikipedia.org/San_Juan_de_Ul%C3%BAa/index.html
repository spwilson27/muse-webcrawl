San Juan de Ulúa is a large complex of fortresses, prisons and palaces built on an island of the same name in the Gulf of Mexico, overlooking the port of Veracruz, Mexico. The island was named in 1518 by the expedition of Juan de Grijalva, and Hernán Cortés landed here in 1519 at the start of the Spanish conquest of the Aztec Empire.

History

Construction of the fortress began in 1535 to protect the harbour from pirates and rival powers. Built largely of coral stone, it withstood attacks and served as the last Spanish stronghold in Mexico: its garrison did not surrender until 1825, four years after Mexican independence. It later served as a notorious prison under the regime of Porfirio Díaz, housing political prisoners such as the labour leader Ricardo Flores Magón-era inmates and, briefly, Jesús Arriaga ("Chucho el Roto").

Today

The fortress is now a museum administered by Mexico's National Institute of Anthropology and History and is one of Veracruz's principal historic attractions.
