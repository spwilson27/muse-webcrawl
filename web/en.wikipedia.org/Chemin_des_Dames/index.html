Chemin des Dames

The Chemin des Dames (French: [ʃə.mɛ̃ de dam]; literally "the ladies' path") is a road in the Aisne department in northern France, running along a ridge between the valleys of the Aisne and Ailette rivers. It acquired fame during the First World War, when its crest became one of the most bitterly contested sectors of the Western Front.

Chemin des Dames
Location:Aisne, Hauts-de-France, France
Route:Part of the RD 18, between Laon and Reims corridor
Known for:Battles of 1914, 1917 and 1918

Origin of the name

The road follows an 18th-century route associated with Adélaïde and Victoire, daughters of King Louis XV, who travelled this way to visit the Château de la Bove in Bouconville-Vauclair; the way was improved and paved for their journeys and took the name "Path of the Ladies".

First World War

German forces seized the ridge during the First Battle of the Aisne in September 1914 and fortified it with deep quarries and caverns such as the Caverne du Dragon. In April 1917 General Robert Nivelle launched his disastrous offensive here; the French army gained little ground at enormous cost, and the failure helped trigger the French Army mutinies of 1917. German forces retook the ridge in May 1918 during the Third Battle of the Aisne before it was finally recaptured in the Allied offensives of autumn 1918.

Memorials

The plateau is studded with cemeteries, monuments, and the Caverne du Dragon museum, which preserves an underground quarry used by both sides. The basin below the ridge holds the largest German war cemetery of the First World War in France at Laon.
