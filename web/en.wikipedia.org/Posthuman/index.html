Posthuman

A posthuman might be a being with capacities radically exceeding present-day humans.

A posthuman or post-human is a concept originating in the fields of science fiction, futurology, contemporary art, and philosophy that means a person or entity that exists in a state beyond being human. The concept addresses questions of ethics and justice, language and trans-species communication, social systems, and the intellectual aspirations of interdisciplinarity. A posthuman might be created through radical technological enhancement, such as genetic engineering, cybernetic augmentation, or mind uploading, or might emerge as an artificial intelligence or an evolved descendant of humanity.

In philosophy

Posthumanism, a philosophical movement distinct from transhumanism, critiques the anthropocentric assumptions of humanism and examines how the category of "the human" is constructed and destabilised by technology, evolution, and relations with other species. Thinkers associated with the term include Ihab Hassan, who used it in 1977, Donna Haraway, whose "A Cyborg Manifesto" (1985) employed the cyborg as a figure breaking down boundaries between human, animal, and machine, and N. Katherine Hayles, whose How We Became Posthuman (1999) analysed embodiment and information.

In fiction

Posthumans populate much science fiction: the genetically engineered castes of Brave New World, the uploaded minds and radically altered descendants in works by Greg Egan and Alastair Reynolds, and the far-future descendants of humanity in Olaf Stapledon's Last and First Men. Debates about posthumanity raise questions about personal identity, moral status, and the risks of unequal access to enhancement technologies.
