The Age of Sail is a period in European and world history, roughly from the mid-16th to the mid-19th century, during which international trade and naval warfare were dominated by sailing ships. It coincides broadly with the Age of Discovery and the rise of European colonial empires.

Development

Advances in shipbuilding produced full-rigged ships capable of ocean crossings, including the carrack, the galleon, and later the ship of the line — large warships carrying dozens of cannon in broadside batteries. Navies of Britain, France, Spain, and the Netherlands fought for control of trade routes across the Atlantic, Indian, and Pacific oceans.

Trade and empire

Sailing ships carried the goods — and the people, including the enslaved victims of the transatlantic slave trade — of the emerging global economy. Merchant vessels grew faster and larger, culminating in the clipper ships of the mid-19th century, which set speed records on tea and wool routes.

Decline

Steam engines, screw propellers, and iron construction made steamships increasingly practical from the 1840s onward; by the later 19th century sail survived mainly in bulk cargo trades before finally disappearing from commercial use in the early 20th century.
