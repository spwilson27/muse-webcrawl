Himawari (ひまわり, "sunflower") is a series of Japanese geostationary weather satellites operated by the Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA). The satellites provide continuous meteorological observation of East Asia, Australia and the western Pacific, and their imagery is a staple of weather broadcasts across the region.

History

The first satellite, GMS-1 (Himawari 1), was launched in 1977 from Cape Canaveral. Successive GMS spacecraft were followed by the Multifunctional Transport Satellites MTSAT-1R (Himawari 6, 2005) and MTSAT-2 (Himawari 7, 2006), which combined meteorological and air-traffic-control functions.

Himawari 8 and 9

Himawari 8, launched on 7 October 2014, and its backup Himawari 9, launched on 2 November 2016, represented a major advance: their Advanced Himawari Imager (AHI) observes in 16 spectral bands, delivering full-disk colour imagery every 10 minutes and imagery of the Japan area every 2.5 minutes. The satellites were built by Mitsubishi Electric and launched on H-IIA rockets from Tanegashima Space Center. Himawari 9 took over primary operations from Himawari 8 in December 2022.

Uses

Data from the Himawari satellites support weather forecasting, tropical-cyclone monitoring, volcanic-ash tracking, and disaster response across the Asia-Pacific. Imagery is distributed freely to meteorological agencies throughout the region.
