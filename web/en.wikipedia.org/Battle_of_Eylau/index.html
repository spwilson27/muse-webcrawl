Battle of Eylau

The Battle of Eylau, also known as the Battle of Preussisch-Eylau, was fought on 7–8 February 1807 during the Napoleonic Wars between the French Grande Armée under Napoleon Bonaparte and the Imperial Russian Army under General Levin August von Bennigsen. Fought in a blinding snowstorm in East Prussia, it was one of the bloodiest battles of the era and ended indecisively, with both sides claiming victory.

Background

After the destruction of Prussian resistance following Jena-Auerstedt in October 1806, Napoleon advanced into Poland and East Prussia in pursuit of the remaining Russian forces. Bennigsen attempted to catch the isolated French corps of Marshal Ney and then turned to stand at Eylau, hoping to overwhelm Napoleon before French reinforcements arrived.

The battle

Fighting began on the afternoon of 7 February over possession of the town of Eylau. On 8 February, in appalling weather, the Russians bombarded the French positions. Around midday Marshal Augereau's VII Corps advanced into a white-out blizzard, lost its bearings, and was nearly annihilated by Russian artillery and cavalry. To rescue the crumbling French centre, Napoleon ordered a massive cavalry charge of some 10,700 horsemen under Marshal Murat—one of the largest cavalry charges in history. The French cavalry smashed through two Russian lines and relieved the pressure. Marshal Ney's late arrival on the Russian right stabilized the French position by nightfall.

Aftermath

Casualties were horrific: estimates range from 15,000 to 25,000 on each side. Bennigsen withdrew during the night, leaving the field to Napoleon, who technically held the battlefield but was too battered to pursue. The slaughter at Eylau shocked Europe and dented Napoleon's aura of invincibility. The campaign resumed in the spring, culminating in the decisive French victory at Friedland in June 1807 and the Treaties of Tilsit.
