Valine (symbol Val or V) is an α-amino acid used in the biosynthesis of proteins. It is an essential amino acid in humans, meaning it must be obtained from the diet, and it is one of the three branched-chain amino acids, along with leucine and isoleucine.

Properties and sources

Valine has an aliphatic isopropyl side chain, making it nonpolar and hydrophobic. Dietary sources include meat, dairy products, soy, beans, and legumes. It was first isolated from casein in 1901 by Emil Fischer.

Biological role

Besides protein synthesis, valine is involved in muscle metabolism and energy production. In sickle cell disease, a valine residue replaces glutamic acid in the β-globin chain, causing haemoglobin to polymerize. Defects in valine catabolism are associated with maple syrup urine disease.
