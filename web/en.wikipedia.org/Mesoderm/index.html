Mesoderm

The mesoderm is the middle layer of the three primary germ layers in the very early embryo of bilaterian animals. It lies between the ectoderm (outer layer) and the endoderm (inner layer) and forms during gastrulation, in humans around the third week of development, as cells migrate through the primitive streak.

Subdivisions

The mesoderm differentiates into several regions. The paraxial mesoderm forms somites, which give rise to vertebrae, ribs, skeletal muscle, and dermis. The intermediate mesoderm forms the urogenital system, including the kidneys and gonads. The lateral plate mesoderm splits into somatic and splanchnic layers forming the body wall, limbs' connective tissue, heart, blood vessels, and the serous membranes. The axial mesoderm (notochord) patterns the neural tube and vertebral column.

Derivatives

Mesoderm gives rise to all muscle types (skeletal, cardiac, smooth), bone, cartilage, connective tissue, the circulatory and lymphatic systems, blood cells, kidneys, reproductive organs, the spleen, and much of the dermis. Mesodermal development is directed by signaling pathways including BMP, Wnt, Nodal, and FGF.
