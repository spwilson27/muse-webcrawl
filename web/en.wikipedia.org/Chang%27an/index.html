Chang'an was an ancient capital of China, located on the site of modern Xi'an. Serving as the capital under several dynasties — most notably the Western Han and the Tang — it was one of the largest and most cosmopolitan cities of the pre-modern world and the eastern terminus of the Silk Road.

Han Chang'an

Liu Bang, founder of the Han dynasty, established his capital here in 202 BCE. The walled city contained great palace complexes such as Changle and Weiyang — the latter the largest palace complex ever built in China — and the imperial city endured for two centuries before the capital moved east to Luoyang.

Tang Chang'an

Rebuilt by the Sui as Daxing and renamed Chang'an by the Tang, the city was laid out on a vast grid of wards and avenues, with the imperial palace in the north and East and West Markets drawing merchants from Persia, Central Asia, Korea, and Japan. With perhaps a million inhabitants within the walls, Tang Chang'an was a model for the Japanese capitals of Nara and Kyoto.
