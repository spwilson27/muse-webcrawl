Computer security

Computer security, also known as cyber security or IT security, is the protection of computer systems and networks from attacks by malicious actors that may result in unauthorised information disclosure, theft of or damage to hardware, software or data, as well as disruption or misdirection of the services they provide. The field is significant due to the expanding reliance on computer systems, the Internet, and wireless standards such as Wi-Fi, alongside the growth of smart devices including smartphones and the Internet of things.

Computer security protects systems and data from unauthorised access.

Core principles

Security practice is organised around the CIA triad: confidentiality (information is accessible only to those authorised), integrity (information is accurate and unaltered), and availability (systems and data are accessible when needed). Supporting concepts include authentication, authorisation, non-repudiation, and the principle of least privilege, which grants users only the access their tasks require.

Threats

Common threats include malware (viruses, worms, trojan horses, ransomware and spyware), phishing and social engineering, denial-of-service attacks, man-in-the-middle attacks, and exploitation of software vulnerabilities such as SQL injection and cross-site scripting. Attackers range from individual hackers and organised criminal groups to nation states conducting espionage and sabotage. A zero-day vulnerability is one unknown to the vendor, leaving no patch available when it is first exploited.

Defences

Defence in depth layers multiple controls: firewalls and intrusion detection systems guard networks; encryption protects data in transit and at rest; multi-factor authentication strengthens identity; regular patching closes known vulnerabilities; and backups limit the damage of ransomware. Organisations formalise these measures in security policies, penetration testing, and incident response plans. Cryptography — including public-key infrastructure and protocols such as TLS — underpins secure communication on the Internet.

History

Early milestones include the Creeper program of 1971, an experimental self-replicating program on ARPANET, and the Morris worm of 1988, one of the first worms distributed via the Internet, which prompted the creation of the first computer emergency response team, CERT/CC. The growth of e-commerce in the 1990s made security a commercial necessity, and major incidents in the 2010s — from large retail data breaches to the WannaCry ransomware attack of 2017 — made it a matter of public policy.
