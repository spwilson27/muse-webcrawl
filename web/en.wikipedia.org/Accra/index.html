Accra

Accra is the capital and largest city of Ghana, located on the southern coast at the Gulf of Guinea, part of the Atlantic Ocean. It serves as the country's political, economic and educational center. The city proper has a population of over two million, and the Greater Accra metropolitan area is home to roughly five million people, making it one of West Africa's major urban centers.

History

The Ga people founded settlements in the area from the late 15th century. Portuguese, Dutch, Danish, Swedish, French and British traders built forts along this stretch of the Gold Coast, including Fort James, Ussher Fort and Christiansborg Castle. When the British moved the capital of the Gold Coast colony from Cape Coast to Accra in 1877, the merged trading settlements began to grow into a modern capital. Accra became capital of independent Ghana under Kwame Nkrumah in 1957.

Geography and climate

Accra lies in the Dahomey Gap, where the coastline runs roughly east–west, and has a tropical savanna climate with two rainy seasons. Temperatures are warm year-round, typically 24–31 °C, moderated by sea breezes.

Economy and landmarks

Accra hosts the Bank of Ghana, the Ghana Stock Exchange and most national government institutions. Landmarks include Black Star Square (Independence Square) with its Black Star Gate, the Kwame Nkrumah Mausoleum, Jamestown with its lighthouse and fishing harbor, Osu's Oxford Street commercial district, and Makola Market. The University of Ghana's main campus is at Legon, about 12 km north of the city center.
