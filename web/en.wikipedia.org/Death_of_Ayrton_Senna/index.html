Death of Ayrton Senna

On 1 May 1994, Brazilian Formula One driver Ayrton Senna, a three-time world champion, was killed in a crash while leading the San Marino Grand Prix at the Autodromo Enzo e Dino Ferrari in Imola, Italy. His Williams FW16 left the track at the high-speed Tamburello corner and struck a concrete wall at about 211 km/h (131 mph). Senna's death, broadcast live to a worldwide television audience, was the second fatality of the weekend — Austrian driver Roland Ratzenberger had been killed in qualifying the previous day — and it transformed Formula One's approach to safety.

The weekend and the crash

The Imola weekend was marred by accidents from the start: Rubens Barrichello survived a heavy crash on Friday, and Ratzenberger's death on Saturday was the first fatality at a Grand Prix meeting in twelve years. Senna, deeply unsettled, nevertheless took pole position. On lap 7, shortly after a restart, his car failed to turn into Tamburello and ran straight into the unprotected concrete barrier. Debris and the suspension of the car struck Senna's helmet; he died from head injuries, and an Austrian flag was found in the cockpit, which he had intended to wave in Ratzenberger's honour.

Investigations and consequences

Italian prosecutors pursued a lengthy manslaughter case against members of the Williams team, focusing on a modified steering column that had broken, though whether the column broke before or in the crash was never conclusively resolved and all defendants were ultimately acquitted. The official findings did not change the sweeping reforms that followed: circuits were redesigned (including Tamburello itself, converted to a chicane), cockpit protection and crash testing were strengthened, the HANS device was eventually mandated, and the Grand Prix Drivers' Association was re-formed with Senna's death as its catalyst. Brazil declared three days of national mourning, and an estimated three million people lined the streets of São Paulo for his funeral.
