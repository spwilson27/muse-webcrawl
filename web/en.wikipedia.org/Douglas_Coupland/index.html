Douglas Coupland (born December 30, 1961) is a Canadian novelist, designer and visual artist. His first novel, Generation X: Tales for an Accelerated Culture (1991), popularised the term "Generation X" and made him a chronicler of post-Cold War youth culture.

Writing

Coupland's fiction combines pop culture, technology and existential themes. Notable novels include Microserfs (1995), set among employees at a software start-up; Girlfriend in a Coma (1998); Hey Nostradamus! (2003); and JPod (2006), a sequel-of-sorts to Microserfs. He has also written non-fiction, including a biography of Marshall McLuhan, and coined terms such as "McJob".

Visual art

Trained at the Emily Carr College of Art and Design in Vancouver, Coupland works in sculpture, installation and painting, often using found objects and digital imagery. He was appointed an Officer of the Order of Canada and has exhibited internationally.
