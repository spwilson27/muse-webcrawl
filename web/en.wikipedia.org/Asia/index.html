Asia is the largest and most populous continent on Earth, covering about 44.6 million square kilometres — roughly 30 per cent of the planet's land area — and home to some 4.8 billion people, around 60 per cent of humanity. It is conventionally bounded by the Ural Mountains, the Caucasus and the Caspian and Black Seas to the west, the Arctic Ocean to the north, the Pacific Ocean to the east, and the Indian Ocean to the south.

Geography

Asia contains the world's highest mountain system, the Himalayas, including Mount Everest (8,849 m), and vast lowlands, plateaus and deserts — the Siberian plain, the Tibetan Plateau (the "Roof of the World"), the Gobi and the Arabian deserts. Great rivers such as the Yangtze, Yellow, Ganges, Indus, Mekong, Lena and Ob drain its regions. Climates range from Arctic tundra to equatorial rainforest, with the monsoon system dominating South and East Asia.

Regions and peoples

The continent is usually divided into North, Central, East, South, Southeast and West Asia. It is the birthplace of the world's major religions — Hinduism, Buddhism, Judaism, Christianity, Islam, Sikhism, Jainism, Taoism and Confucian traditions among them — and of early civilisations in Mesopotamia, the Indus Valley, China and Persia. Asian languages include the Sino-Tibetan, Indo-European, Turkic, Semitic, Dravidian and many other families.

History and economy

Asia hosted ancient empires from Persia and Han China to the Mongol Empire, the largest contiguous land empire in history, and trade routes such as the Silk Road linked it to Europe and Africa. After centuries in which European colonial powers dominated much of the continent, the later twentieth century saw rapid industrialisation in Japan, the "Asian Tigers", China and India. Today Asia produces the largest share of world GDP and includes the two most populous countries, India and China.
