The Battle of Kadesh took place between the forces of the New Kingdom of Egypt under Ramesses II and the Hittite Empire under Muwatalli II at the city of Kadesh on the Orontes River, near the modern Syrian–Lebanese border. Conventionally dated to 1274 BC, it is one of the earliest battles in recorded history for which detailed tactical accounts survive, and probably the largest chariot battle ever fought, involving perhaps 5,000–6,000 chariots.

Background

Kadesh, a strategic city controlling trade routes between Egypt and Mesopotamia, had shifted between Egyptian and Hittite control for generations. Ramesses II marched north in his fifth regnal year with four divisions named for the gods Amun, Ra, Ptah and Seth, intending to recover the city and the province of Amurru for Egypt.

The battle

Deceived by two captured Hittite agents who claimed Muwatalli's army was far away at Aleppo, Ramesses advanced with only his household troops and the Amun division and camped northwest of Kadesh. In fact the Hittite army was hidden behind the city. Hittite chariots burst across the Orontes, smashed the marching Ra division, and stormed Ramesses' camp. The pharaoh, fighting almost surrounded, held on until the elite Ne'arin troops from Amurru arrived and struck the Hittites—who had stopped to plunder the Egyptian camp—in the flank. The Ptah division then came up, and the Hittites were driven back across the river with heavy losses. The next day's fighting was indecisive, and Muwatalli proposed a truce.

Aftermath

Ramesses claimed a great victory in inscriptions carved on temples across Egypt, including the famous "Poem of Pentaur," but strategically Kadesh and Amurru remained in Hittite hands. Continued stalemate eventually produced the Egyptian–Hittite peace treaty of about 1258 BC between Ramesses II and Hattusili III—one of the earliest surviving peace treaties, a copy of which is displayed at the United Nations headquarters.
