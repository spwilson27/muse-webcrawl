Gérard Henri de Vaucouleurs (25 April 1918 – 7 October 1995) was a French-American astronomer whose work on galaxies — their classification, brightness profiles, distances and distribution — shaped extragalactic astronomy in the twentieth century.

Early life and career

De Vaucouleurs was born in Paris and studied physics and mathematics at the Sorbonne, earning his doctorate in 1949. As a young man he had been an enthusiastic amateur astronomer and observer of the planet Mars. After the Second World War he worked in England and then in Australia at Mount Stromlo Observatory (1951–1957), where he studied southern galaxies, before moving to the United States. He worked at the Lowell Observatory and Harvard, and in 1960 joined the University of Texas at Austin, where he remained for the rest of his career, collaborating closely with his wife Antoinette de Vaucouleurs, also an astronomer.

Contributions

De Vaucouleurs refined the Hubble sequence of galaxy classification, introducing the detailed system of stages, families and varieties (including barred and ringed forms) used in the Reference Catalogue of Bright Galaxies, whose third edition (1991, with collaborators) remains a standard resource. In 1948 he showed that the brightness of elliptical galaxies follows a characteristic profile, now called de Vaucouleurs's law (the r1/4 law). In the 1950s he argued that the Milky Way belongs to a flattened local supercluster of galaxies — the Local Supercluster, centred on the Virgo Cluster — one of the first maps of large-scale structure.

The Hubble constant controversy

De Vaucouleurs led one side of a long-running dispute over the value of the Hubble constant and hence the scale and age of the universe, advocating values around 100 km/s/Mpc, against Allan Sandage's values near 50. The controversy persisted for decades before measurements converged on intermediate values.

Honours and death

He received the Henry Norris Russell Lectureship (1988) and was elected to the US National Academy of Sciences. De Vaucouleurs died in Austin, Texas, in 1995.
