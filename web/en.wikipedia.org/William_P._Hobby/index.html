William Pettus Hobby (March 26, 1878 – June 7, 1964) was an American politician and publisher who served as the 27th governor of Texas from 1917 to 1921 and later owned the Houston Post newspaper.

Career

Hobby worked in Texas journalism from his teens and became editor and part-owner of the Beaumont Enterprise. Elected lieutenant governor in 1914, he succeeded to the governorship in August 1917 when Governor James E. Ferguson was impeached and removed. Hobby won election in his own right in 1918. His administration enacted prohibition enforcement, suffrage measures, and highway and education reforms; he lost the Democratic primary in 1920 to Pat Neff. He later took control of the Houston Post and remained a major figure in Texas media and Democratic politics.

Family

His wife, Oveta Culp Hobby, became the first U.S. Secretary of Health, Education, and Welfare. Houston's Hobby Airport is named for him.
