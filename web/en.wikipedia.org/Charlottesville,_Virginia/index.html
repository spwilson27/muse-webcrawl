Charlottesville is an independent city in the Commonwealth of Virginia, United States, and the county seat of Albemarle County, which surrounds it. Named for Queen Charlotte, wife of George III, it was chartered in 1762. The city is best known as the home of the University of Virginia, founded by Thomas Jefferson in 1819, and of two U.S. presidents, Jefferson and James Monroe, whose homes — Monticello and Highland — stand nearby, as does James Madison's Montpelier in neighboring Orange County.

History

Charlottesville grew as a trading and court town along the Three Notch'd Road. During the Civil War it hosted a Confederate hospital and was raided in 1864, surrendering to Union forces in March 1865 with little destruction. Jefferson's university, designed around his Academical Village with the Rotunda modeled on the Pantheon, is together with Monticello a UNESCO World Heritage Site. In August 2017 the city drew international attention for the "Unite the Right" rally, a white supremacist gathering ostensibly protesting the removal of a statue of Robert E. Lee, during which a counter-protester, Heather Heyer, was killed by a car attack and two state police troopers died in a helicopter crash; the Lee statue was removed in 2021 and later melted down.

Geography and culture

Charlottesville sits in the foothills of the Blue Ridge Mountains along the Rivanna River, near the southern entrance to Shenandoah National Park and the Blue Ridge Parkway. Its pedestrian Downtown Mall is one of the longest in the United States. The local economy centers on the university, health care, wine production in the Monticello AVA, and a notable music scene that produced the Dave Matthews Band.
