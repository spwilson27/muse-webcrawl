Fertilizer

A fertilizer is any material of natural or synthetic origin applied to soil or plant tissues to supply plant nutrients essential for plant growth. The three primary macronutrients are nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), and potassium (K), the familiar NPK numbers on bags of fertilizer.

Types

Fertilizers are classed as organic (manure, compost, bone meal, green manure) and mineral/synthetic (ammonium nitrate, urea, superphosphate, potash).

History

The modern industry began with Justus von Liebig's mineral theory (1840) and Lawes's superphosphate. The Haber–Bosch process (1913) made nitrogen fixation industrial-scale, enabling the Green Revolution.

Environmental issues

Fertilizer runoff causes eutrophication of waterways; nitrogen fertilizers release nitrous oxide, a potent greenhouse gas.
