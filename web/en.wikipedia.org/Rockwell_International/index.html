Rockwell International was a major American manufacturing conglomerate involved in aircraft, the space industry, defence and commercial electronics, automotive products, and printing presses. It grew out of earlier companies including North American Aviation and Rockwell-Standard.

History

Rockwell's aerospace heritage included the P-51 Mustang and F-86 Sabre through North American Aviation, and later the B-1 Lancer bomber. Its Rocketdyne division built rocket engines, and Rockwell was the prime contractor for the Space Shuttle orbiter. In the 1990s the company divested its defence and aerospace businesses to Boeing (1996), spun off its automotive division as Meritor, and its semiconductor business as Conexant. The remaining automation business became Rockwell Automation, and the name Rockwell International ceased to exist in 2001.
