Moldova

Capital: Chișinău

Official language: Romanian

Area: 33,846 km²

Population: about 2.5 million (excluding Transnistria)

Currency: Moldovan leu (MDL)

Moldova, officially the Republic of Moldova, is a landlocked country in Eastern Europe, bordered by Romania to the west and Ukraine to the north, east and south. Its capital and largest city is Chișinău. Most of Moldova's territory lies between the Prut and Dniester rivers and formed part of the historical Principality of Moldavia before becoming the Russian province of Bessarabia in 1812.

History

After the Russian Revolution, Bessarabia united with Romania in 1918, but the Soviet Union annexed it in 1940 and again in 1944, organising it as the Moldavian Soviet Socialist Republic. Moldova declared independence from the Soviet Union on 27 August 1991. A brief war in 1992 left the breakaway region of Transnistria, along the east bank of the Dniester, outside government control, hosting a Russian military presence; the conflict remains frozen and unresolved.

Geography and economy

Moldova is a hilly country of fertile steppe and forest (the Codru), drained by the Dniester and Prut rivers. Its economy is the poorest in Europe by GDP per capita and depends heavily on agriculture — wine, fruit, vegetables, sunflower oil and tobacco — and on remittances from citizens working abroad. Moldovan wines are produced in large underground cellars such as Cricova and Mileștii Mici, the latter among the largest wine cellars in the world. The country is a candidate for membership of the European Union, having signed an Association Agreement in 2014 and gained candidate status in 2022.

Culture

Moldova's population is majority ethnic Romanian/Moldovan, with Ukrainian, Russian, Gagauz, Bulgarian and Roma minorities; Gagauzia is an autonomous region in the south. The country shares language, folk traditions and much of its history with Romania, including the veneration of Stephen the Great and the poet Mihai Eminescu.
