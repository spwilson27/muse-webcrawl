Esau is a figure in the Hebrew Bible, the elder twin son of Isaac and Rebecca and the brother of Jacob. He is described as a skilful hunter and his father's favourite, and he is remembered above all for selling his birthright to Jacob for a meal of lentil stew, and for losing his father's blessing through a deception. Esau is regarded as the ancestor of the Edomites, and is also called Edom, meaning "red", which the narrative connects both with his ruddy, hairy appearance at birth and with the red stew for which he traded his inheritance.

The birthright and the blessing

According to Genesis, Esau came home famished from the field and begged Jacob for some of the stew Jacob was cooking, agreeing to sell his birthright — the double portion and headship belonging to the firstborn — in exchange. Later, when Isaac was old and blind and intended to bless Esau, Rebecca helped Jacob disguise himself with goatskins, imitating his hairy brother, and Jacob received the blessing of the firstborn instead. Esau's anguished plea — "Have you only one blessing, my father?" — and Isaac's lesser blessing for him are among the most noted episodes in the patriarchal narratives.

Reconciliation and descendants

Esau settled in the hill country of Seir, south of the Dead Sea. When Jacob returned from Mesopotamia after twenty years, the brothers met in an emotional reconciliation: Esau ran to meet Jacob, embraced him, and kissed him, though Jacob insisted that Esau accept his gifts. Esau's descendants, listed in Genesis 36, became the kings and chiefs of Edom, a kingdom that later stood in a long and often hostile relationship with Israel. In later Jewish and Christian interpretation Esau variously symbolizes impulsive appetite, the rejected elder brother, and — in rabbinic tradition — Rome.
