Sun Ce

Sun Ce (175 – 5 May 200), courtesy name Bofu, was a Chinese military general, politician, and warlord who lived during the late Eastern Han dynasty of China. He was the eldest son of Sun Jian and laid the foundation for the state of Eastern Wu, one of the Three Kingdoms, which was formally established by his younger brother Sun Quan.

Conquest of Jiangdong

After Sun Jian's death in 191, Sun Ce served under the warlord Yuan Shu. In 195 he used his father's old troops, granted to him by Yuan Shu, to cross the Yangtze River and campaign in the Jiangdong region. In a series of swift victories against warlords such as Liu Yao, Yan Baihu, and Wang Lang, he conquered the commanderies of Wu, Kuaiji, and Danyang. His boldness and charisma earned him the nickname "Little Conqueror."

Death

In 200, while preparing to attack Cao Cao in the north, Sun Ce was ambushed and wounded by assassins sent by Xu Gong, a former official he had executed. He died of his wounds at the age of 25. On his deathbed he passed his seal to his younger brother Sun Quan, urging him to rely on advisers such as Zhang Zhao and Zhou Yu.

Legacy

Sun Quan posthumously honoured him as Prince Huan of Changsha. In the 14th-century novel Romance of the Three Kingdoms, Sun Ce's deeds are dramatised, including his famous duel with Taishi Ci, who later joined his service.
