Paramount Pictures Corporation is an American film production and distribution company and a subsidiary of Paramount Global. Founded in 1912 as the Famous Players Film Company by Adolph Zukor, it is the fifth-oldest surviving film studio in the world and the last of the classic "major" studios to remain headquartered in the Hollywood district of Los Angeles. Its logo, a rugged mountain peak surrounded by stars, is among the most recognisable in cinema.

History

Zukor's Famous Players Film Company merged in 1916 with Jesse L. Lasky's Feature Play Company and distributor W. W. Hodkinson's Paramount Pictures Corporation to form the Famous Players–Lasky Corporation, distributing films under the Paramount name — Hodkinson's company had been founded in 1914 and supplied the mountain logo, said to be inspired by Ben Lomond in Utah. Paramount dominated silent-era Hollywood with stars such as Mary Pickford, Rudolph Valentino and Gloria Swanson, and directors like Cecil B. DeMille. In 1927 its Wings won the first Academy Award for Best Picture.

The company survived bankruptcy in the 1930s, the 1948 United States v. Paramount Pictures antitrust decree that forced studios to divest their theatre chains, and acquisition by Gulf+Western in 1966. Under Barry Diller, Michael Eisner and Jeffrey Katzenberg in the 1970s it produced The Godfather, Chinatown and Saturday Night Fever, and launched the Star Trek and Indiana Jones franchises.

Recent era

Paramount was acquired by Viacom in 1994. Major franchises include Mission: Impossible, Transformers, Top Gun: Maverick (2022) and Sonic the Hedgehog, and its library spans The Ten Commandments, Sunset Boulevard, Psycho (distributed), Forrest Gump and Titanic (co-production). Following the 2024–25 Skydance Media merger with parent Paramount Global, the studio became part of the Paramount Skydance Corporation under David Ellison.
