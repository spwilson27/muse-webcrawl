Perseus of Macedon

Perseus (Greek: Περσεύς, Perseus; c. 212 – 166 BC) was the last king of the Antigonid dynasty, who ruled the Hellenistic kingdom of Macedonia from 179 BC until the abolition of the monarchy in 168 BC. He was the son and successor of Philip V. Perseus' defeat by the Roman Republic at the Battle of Pydna in 168 BC ended the Third Macedonian War and marked the effective end of Macedonian independence.

Early life and accession

Perseus was the eldest legitimate son of Philip V. In his youth he commanded Macedonian forces in campaigns against the Romans and their allies during and after the Second Macedonian War. Ancient sources hostile to Perseus alleged that he poisoned his younger brother Demetrius, who had been sent to Rome as a hostage and had gained favour there; Philip, believing reports of treason, had Demetrius executed in 180 BC. When Philip died the following year, Perseus succeeded without opposition.

Reign

On taking the throne, Perseus renewed Macedon's treaty with Rome but worked to restore Macedonian influence in Greece: he rebuilt the army and treasury, married Laodice, daughter of the Seleucid king Seleucus IV, and cultivated alliances with Greek states and Balkan peoples. These activities alarmed Rome, and complaints from Rome's ally Eumenes II of Pergamum helped precipitate war in 171 BC.

Third Macedonian War

Perseus won an early cavalry success at the Battle of Callinicus in 171 BC but failed to press his advantage. After several years of inconclusive campaigning, the Roman consul Lucius Aemilius Paullus took command in 168 BC and decisively defeated Perseus at Pydna on 22 June 168 BC, in a battle decided by the flexibility of the Roman legion against the Macedonian phalanx.

Captivity and death

Perseus fled to Samothrace but surrendered to the Romans. He was led in Paullus' triumph at Rome and then imprisoned at Alba Fucens, where he died in 166 BC. Macedonia was divided into four republics, ending the Antigonid kingdom founded more than a century earlier.
