Itihasa

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Itihasa (Sanskrit: इतिहास, itihāsa, literally "so it happened") refers to the collection of written descriptions of important events in Hinduism. The term is conventionally applied to the two Sanskrit epics, the Mahābhārata and the Rāmāyaṇa, which are central texts of Hindu tradition.

Meaning and scope

In the broader traditional classification of Sanskrit literature, itihasa is distinguished from the purāṇas; together with the epics, some traditions also count works such as the Harivaṃśa. Itihasa is regarded as recounting events that actually occurred, in contrast to purely mythological narrative, and is often grouped with the puranas as the "fifth Veda."

The epics

The Mahabharata, attributed to Vyasa, narrates the Kurukshetra War and contains the Bhagavad Gita. The Ramayana, attributed to Valmiki, tells the story of Rama, Sita, and the demon king Ravana. Both texts have profoundly influenced art, literature, and religious life across South and Southeast Asia.
