Sir Cameron Anthony Mackintosh (born 17 October 1946) is a British theatrical producer and theatre owner, widely regarded as the most commercially successful musical theatre producer in history. His productions of Les Misérables, The Phantom of the Opera, Cats and Miss Saigon defined the era of the global "mega-musical".

Career

Mackintosh began as a stagehand and assistant stage manager before producing touring shows in the late 1960s. His breakthrough came with Andrew Lloyd Webber's Cats (1981), followed by Les Misérables (London, 1985), developed with the Royal Shakespeare Company from the French concept album by Alain Boublil and Claude-Michel Schönberg, and Lloyd Webber's The Phantom of the Opera (1986). Miss Saigon (1989) and Mary Poppins (2004, co-produced with Disney) continued his run of international successes. He also championed the work of Stephen Sondheim in Britain, producing revivals including Follies and Side by Side by Sondheim.

Through Delfont Mackintosh Theatres he owns a portfolio of major West End playhouses, including the Prince Edward, Prince of Wales, Novello, Gielgud, Noël Coward, Wyndham's, Victoria Palace and Sondheim theatres. He co-produced the 2012 film of Les Misérables and the London production of Hamilton.

Honours

Mackintosh was knighted in 1996 for services to theatre. He founded the Cameron Mackintosh Foundation to support theatre education and charitable causes, and has consistently appeared on lists of the wealthiest figures in British entertainment.
