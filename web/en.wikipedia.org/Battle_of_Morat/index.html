Battle of Morat

The Swiss assault on the Burgundian positions outside Morat (Murten), 22 June 1476.

Date22 June 1476
LocationMorat (Murten), present-day Switzerland
ResultDecisive Swiss victory
BelligerentsOld Swiss Confederacy vs. Duchy of Burgundy

The Battle of Morat (German: Schlacht bei Murten) was fought on 22 June 1476 between the Old Swiss Confederacy and the forces of Charles the Bold, Duke of Burgundy. One of the great victories of Swiss arms, it destroyed Charles's second army in a single campaigning season and confirmed the reputation of Swiss infantry as the finest in Europe.

Background

In the Burgundian Wars, Charles the Bold sought to carve a middle kingdom between France and the Empire, bringing him into conflict with the Swiss and their allies. After his defeat at the Battle of Grandson in March 1476 — where he lost his artillery train and a fabulous camp treasure — Charles rebuilt his army and in June laid siege to the small Bernese town of Morat on the lake of the same name.

The battle

The Confederate relief army, some 25,000 strong including cavalry supplied by René II of Lorraine, approached through rain on 22 June. The Swiss main body charged the Burgundian palisade, the Grünhag, before Charles's army had fully deployed. After hard fighting around the guns, the Burgundian line collapsed and the army dissolved in flight toward Lake Morat. Swiss troops and their allies pursued relentlessly; thousands of Burgundians were killed in the rout or drowned in the lake. Burgundian losses may have reached 6,000–10,000 men; Swiss losses were comparatively small.

Consequences

Charles escaped with a remnant of his army but never recovered his power. Six months later he was killed at the Battle of Nancy (5 January 1477), and his duchy was partitioned between France and the Habsburgs. A Swiss saying summed up the wars: Charles lost his goods at Grandson, his blood at Morat, and his life at Nancy. The victory is still commemorated each year at Murten with a historical run and festival.
