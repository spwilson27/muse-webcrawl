Chocolate chip cookie
Type: Drop cookie
Origin: United States, 1938
Inventor: Ruth Graves Wakefield
Main ingredients: Flour, butter, sugar, eggs, chocolate chips

A chocolate chip cookie is a drop cookie made from a dough of flour, butter, sugar and eggs studded with small pieces of chocolate. It is among the most popular cookies in the United States and worldwide.

History

The cookie was invented in 1938 by Ruth Graves Wakefield, owner of the Toll House Inn in Whitman, Massachusetts, who added chopped Nestlé semi-sweet chocolate to butter cookie dough. The recipe, published as the Toll House Chocolate Crunch Cookie, became famous; Nestlé later printed it on its chocolate packaging and marketed pre-cut chocolate chips.

Variations

Common variations adjust texture from crisp to chewy or cakey, add nuts or oatmeal, or use different chocolates. The chocolate chip cookie is the official state cookie of Massachusetts.
