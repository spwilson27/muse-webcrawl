Loch Eil (Scottish Gaelic: Loch Iall) is a sea loch in Lochaber, in the Highland council area of Scotland. It extends roughly east–west for about 10 kilometres (6 miles) at the head of Loch Linnhe, with the town of Fort William lying just to its south-east at the junction with the River Lochy.

Geography

The loch is a fjord-like inlet carved by glacial action, steep-sided and wooded along much of its shore. At its western end it is joined to Loch Linnhe by a narrow channel at Annat and Corpach, where the Caledonian Canal begins its ascent through Neptune's Staircase, the flight of eight locks built by Thomas Telford. The freshwater Loch Shiel drains toward the sea nearby but empties into Loch Moidart's system via the River Shiel, not into Loch Eil. At the head of Loch Eil, the River Lochy flows in near its confluence zone by the village of Corpach.

Transport and industry

The A830 "Road to the Isles" and the West Highland Line railway run along the loch's northern shore from Banavie and Corpach toward Glenfinnan, Mallaig, and the ferry ports for Skye and the Small Isles; the station at Locheilside serves request stops along the route. Corpach has a basin and port facilities historically used by the pulp and paper mill at Annat, served by freight traffic on the railway. Along the southern shore, small settlements such as Duisky and Trislaig face the loch from the Ardgour side, linked by the Corran Ferry across Loch Linnhe.
