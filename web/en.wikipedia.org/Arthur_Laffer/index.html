Arthur Laffer

Arthur Betz Laffer (born 1940) is an American economist best known as the namesake of the Laffer curve, which illustrates the relationship between tax rates and tax revenue. A prominent advocate of supply-side economics, he advised President Ronald Reagan and influenced the tax cuts of the 1980s.

The Laffer curve

The curve plots revenue against the tax rate: revenue is zero at rates of both 0% and 100%, and rises to a maximum somewhere between, implying that beyond some point higher rates reduce revenue by discouraging economic activity. Laffer popularised the idea — famously, in his account, sketching it on a napkin in 1974 — though similar arguments go back centuries, including to Ibn Khaldun.

Career

Laffer received his doctorate from Stanford University, taught at the University of Chicago and the University of Southern California, and served on Reagan's Economic Policy Advisory Board. He has remained a prominent commentator and adviser on tax policy, and in 2019 received the Presidential Medal of Freedom.
