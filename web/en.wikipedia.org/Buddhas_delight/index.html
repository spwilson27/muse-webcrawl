Buddha's delight

Alternative namesLuohan zhai, jai
TypeVegetarian dish
Place of originChina
Main ingredientsTofu, mushrooms, bamboo shoots, water chestnuts, various vegetables

Buddha's delight, often transliterated as luohan zhai (Mandarin) or jai (Cantonese), is a well-known vegetarian dish of Chinese Buddhist cuisine. It is traditionally served by monks and at Chinese New Year, when it is considered good fortune to eat a vegetarian meal on the first day of the year. The dish is named for the luohan, or arhats, the enlightened disciples of the Buddha.

Ingredients

The dish is a complex medley of many different ingredients, with elaborate versions said to contain eighteen or more components, a number associated with the eighteen luohan. Common ingredients include several forms of tofu (such as dried bean curd sticks, tofu skin, and fried tofu puffs), bamboo shoots, black mushrooms, wood ear fungus, snow fungus, water chestnuts, lotus root and seeds, gingko nuts, daylily buds ("golden needles"), mung bean vermicelli, Napa cabbage, carrots, and fermented red tofu, which gives the sauce a distinctive colour and flavour.

Traditions

Because devout Buddhists traditionally eat vegetarian food on the first and fifteenth days of the lunar month and abstain from meat at the start of the new year, Buddha's delight is a fixture of Lunar New Year celebrations in many Cantonese and other Chinese households. Outside China, the dish became widely known in the West as a staple of Chinese-American restaurant menus.
