Banh mi

Banh mi is a Vietnamese sandwich consisting of a short baguette with a thin, crisp crust and a soft, airy interior, split lengthwise and filled with savoury ingredients. The bread itself, introduced during French colonial rule, is also called banh mi.

Ingredients

Typical fillings combine Vietnamese and French elements: sliced pork, chả lụa (pork sausage), liver pâté, mayonnaise or butter, pickled carrot and daikon, cucumber, coriander, and chillies or jalapeños.

History

The sandwich developed in Saigon in the 1950s, after the partition of Vietnam, blending the French baguette, pâté and mayonnaise with local pickles, herbs and meats. Vietnamese emigration after 1975 spread banh mi worldwide.

Variations

Regional and modern variations include banh mi thit (cold cuts), banh mi cha ca (fish cake), vegetarian versions with tofu, and breakfast versions with fried egg (banh mi op la).
