Pregnancy

Pregnancy is the time during which one or more offspring develops (gestates) inside a woman's uterus (womb). A multiple pregnancy involves more than one offspring, such as with twins. Pregnancy usually occurs by sexual intercourse, but can also occur through assisted reproductive technology procedures.

Timeline

A pregnancy may end in a live birth, a miscarriage, an induced abortion, or a stillbirth. Childbirth typically occurs around 40 weeks from the start of the last menstrual period (LMP), a span known as the gestational age; slightly more than 38 weeks after fertilisation. Pregnancy is divided into three trimesters of roughly three months each. The first trimester includes conception and carries the highest risk of miscarriage; in the second trimester the fetus becomes more readily monitored; by the beginning of the third trimester the fetus may be able to survive outside the uterus with modern medical care.

Development

After fertilisation, the zygote travels to the uterus and implants in the uterine wall. The developing embryo forms the placenta, which transfers nutrients and oxygen from the mother and removes waste. By the end of the embryonic period (week 8) the major organ systems have formed; thereafter the developing being is called a fetus, and the fetal period is marked mainly by growth and maturation.

Signs, diagnosis and care

Early signs and symptoms may include missed periods, tender breasts, nausea (often called morning sickness), hunger and frequent urination. Pregnancy may be confirmed with a test for the hormone human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) in blood or urine, and the fetus assessed by obstetric ultrasonography. Prenatal care, including folic acid supplementation, screening for complications such as pre-eclampsia and gestational diabetes, and avoidance of alcohol and certain drugs, improves outcomes. Around 213 million pregnancies occurred globally in 2012, of which about 190 million were in the developing world.

Complications

Complications of pregnancy may include high blood pressure disorders, gestational diabetes, iron-deficiency anaemia, severe nausea and vomiting, ectopic pregnancy and miscarriage. Globally about 287,000 women died of pregnancy-related causes in 2020, with the great majority of deaths in low-income countries.
