Tercet

A tercet is a stanza of poetry composed of three lines. Tercets can have various rhyme schemes — all three lines rhyming (AAA), a couplet plus a refrain, or interlocking rhymes — and appear both as independent stanzas and within larger forms.

Forms using tercets

The most famous use is Dante's terza rima, an interlocking scheme (ABA BCB CDC…) used throughout the Divine Comedy. The villanelle is built from five tercets and a concluding quatrain. The haiku and the senryū consist of a single three-line stanza, and tercets also appear in the sestina rotations and in modern free verse as the three-line stanza or "triplet".

Terminology

A tercet in which all three lines rhyme is sometimes called a triplet. The word derives from French and Italian diminutives of Latin tertius, "third".
