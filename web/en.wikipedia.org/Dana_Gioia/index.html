Dana Gioia

Dana Gioia
Born: Michael Dana Gioia, 24 December 1950, Hawthorne, California, U.S.
Occupation: Poet, essayist, critic
Known for: Chairman of the National Endowment for the Arts (2003–2009); Poet Laureate of California

Michael Dana Gioia (born December 24, 1950) is an American poet, essayist, and critic. He served as chairman of the National Endowment for the Arts from 2003 to 2009 and later as Poet Laureate of California. He is a prominent figure in the New Formalism movement in American poetry.

Career

Gioia earned degrees from Stanford University and Harvard and, unusually for a poet, spent fifteen years in business, becoming a vice president at General Foods while writing poetry at night. His 1991 essay "Can Poetry Matter?", published in The Atlantic, sparked wide debate about the marginalization of poetry in American culture. His poetry collections include Daily Horoscope (1986), The Gods of Winter (1991), Interrogations at Noon (2001), which won the American Book Award, and 99 Poems: New & Selected (2016).

Public service

As NEA chairman, Gioia launched major national initiatives including The Big Read, Poetry Out Loud, Shakespeare in American Communities, and Operation Homecoming, and is often credited with revitalizing the agency. He has also worked as a translator, librettist, and anthologist.
