Four Symbols

The Four Symbols: Azure Dragon (east), Vermilion Bird (south), White Tiger (west), Black Tortoise (north)

The Four Symbols (Chinese: 四象; pinyin: Sì Xiàng) are four mythological creatures appearing among the Chinese constellations along the ecliptic, and are viewed as the guardians of the four cardinal directions. They are the Azure Dragon of the East, the Vermilion Bird of the South, the White Tiger of the West, and the Black Tortoise (also called the Black Warrior) of the North. Each of these creatures gives its name to seven lunar mansions, dividing the sky into four groups of seven mansions each.

History

The Four Symbols were an important part of early Chinese astronomy and cosmology. By the Han dynasty they were firmly established in ritual, funerary art, and imperial symbolism, appearing on tomb walls, mirrors, roof tiles, and banners. The system spread throughout the Sinosphere, influencing Korean, Japanese, and Vietnamese culture, where the creatures remain familiar figures in art, flags, and popular media.

The four creatures

  • Azure Dragon (青龍, Qīnglóng) — the East, spring, and the element of wood.
  • Vermilion Bird (朱雀, Zhūquè) — the South, summer, and fire. It is often conflated with the fenghuang but is a distinct creature.
  • White Tiger (白虎, Báihǔ) — the West, autumn, and metal; a symbol of war and protection.
  • Black Tortoise (玄武, Xuánwǔ) — the North, winter, and water; usually depicted as a tortoise entwined with a snake, symbolising longevity.

In other cultures

In Japan the creatures are known as Seiryū, Suzaku, Byakko, and Genbu, and they famously guard the city of Kyoto in folklore. In Korea they appear in Goguryeo tomb murals. The Four Symbols also feature prominently in feng shui, where they describe ideal landforms around a site, and in modern popular culture, including anime, manga, and video games.
