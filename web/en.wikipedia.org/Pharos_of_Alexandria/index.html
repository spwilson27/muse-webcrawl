Pharos of Alexandria

The Pharos of Alexandria, sometimes called the Lighthouse of Alexandria, was a lighthouse built by the Ptolemaic Kingdom of Egypt between 280 and 247 BC on the island of Pharos at the harbour of Alexandria. Estimated to have stood between 100 and 130 metres (330–430 ft) tall, it was among the tallest man-made structures in the world for many centuries and was counted as one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World. The word "Pharos" became the generic term for a lighthouse in many languages (French phare, Italian and Spanish faro).

Construction

The tower was commissioned by Ptolemy I Soter and completed under his son Ptolemy II Philadelphus. Its architect was Sostratus of Cnidus. Later reconstructions, based on ancient descriptions and coins, show three tapering tiers: a square lower section, an octagonal middle section, and a cylindrical upper section, crowned by a statue, variously identified as Zeus or Poseidon. A fire burned at the top by night, reportedly amplified by mirrors, to guide ships into the busy harbour.

Destruction

The lighthouse was severely damaged by earthquakes in 956 and 1303 AD and finally ruined by 1323. Its remains were quarried for the Citadel of Qaitbay, built on the site in the 1480s by the Mamluk sultan. In 1994, underwater archaeologists located submerged blocks of the structure in Alexandria's eastern harbour.
