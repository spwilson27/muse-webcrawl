Thermodynamics

Thermodynamics is the branch of physics dealing with heat, work, temperature, and energy, and with the laws governing their conversion and transfer. It describes the bulk behaviour of matter without reference to individual atoms.

Laws

The first law states that energy is conserved: internal energy changes by heat added minus work done. The second law states that the entropy of an isolated system never decreases, limiting the efficiency of heat engines to the Carnot limit. The third law fixes entropy at zero for a perfect crystal at absolute zero, while the zeroth law defines thermal equilibrium and temperature.

History and applications

The science grew from Sadi Carnot's 1824 analysis of steam engines through Clausius, Kelvin, Gibbs, and Boltzmann's statistical mechanics. It underpins engineering, chemistry, meteorology, and cosmology — from power stations to the heat death of the universe.
