Carnitas

OriginMichoacán, Mexico
Main ingredientPork
Similar dishesChicharrón, cochinita pibil, carnitas estilo Uruapan

Carnitas (Spanish for "little meats") is a dish of Mexican cuisine that originated in the state of Michoacán. Carnitas are made by braising, confit-style simmering, or slow-roasting pork in lard until tender, then increasing the heat so the exterior crisps; the meat is then shredded or chopped.

Preparation

Traditionally, pork shoulder and other cuts are cooked for several hours in a large copper cazo (pot, in Michoacán typically from Santa Clara del Cobre) in their own fat, often seasoned simply with salt, sometimes with orange, bay leaves, garlic, milk, or cola depending on regional style. The result is tender, moist meat with browned, caramelized edges.

Serving

Carnitas are served in tacos with chopped onion, cilantro, salsa, and lime, as well as in tortas, burritos, tamales, and as a main platter accompanied by beans, guacamole, and pickled jalapeños. Towns such as Uruapan and Quiroga in Michoacán are famous for their carnitas; the dish is a centerpiece at celebrations and weekend markets across Mexico.

Variations

Regional variants include carnitas made with ribs, skin (cueritos), and offal offered at market stalls, and lighter oven-roasted adaptations made at home without full immersion in lard.
