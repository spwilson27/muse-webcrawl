Grappa

TypePomace brandy
Country of originItaly
Alcohol by volume35–60%
Main ingredientsGrape pomace (skins, pulp, seeds, stems)

Grappa is an alcoholic beverage, a fragrant grape-based pomace brandy of Italian origin that contains 35 to 60 percent alcohol by volume. It is made by distilling the pomace — the skins, pulp, seeds and stems left over from winemaking after pressing the grapes — which ferments and is then distilled to produce a clear spirit.

Production

Traditionally a way for farmers to use winery waste, grappa was once a rough peasant drink. Modern production, refined from the 1970s onward by producers in regions such as Friuli, Veneto, Piedmont and Trentino, uses fresh, high-quality pomace — often from a single grape variety (monovitigno) — and careful distillation in steam or bain-marie stills. Grappa may be unaged (bianca), or aged in wooden barrels (vecchia or riserva), which gives it a golden colour and softer flavour; aromatic varieties are made from grapes such as Moscato or Gewürztraminer.

Serving

Grappa is traditionally served as a digestivo after a meal, in small tulip-shaped glasses at around 9–13 °C for young grappas. In the Veneto it is also added to espresso to make a caffè corretto, and a small amount of grappa called the resentin is used to rinse the espresso cup.

Legal status

"Grappa" is a protected designation: within the European Union the name may be used only for pomace spirit produced in Italy (and the Italian-speaking part of Switzerland). Similar pomace brandies elsewhere include French marc, Spanish orujo and Portuguese aguardente bagaceira.
