Petrov classification

The Petrov classification (also known as the Petrov–Pirani–Penrose classification) describes the possible algebraic symmetries of the Weyl tensor at each event in a Lorentzian manifold. It is most often applied in studying exact solutions of Einstein's field equations, but in purely mathematical terms it can be applied to any pseudo-Riemannian manifold. The classification was developed by the Soviet physicist Aleksey Zinovievich Petrov, who published the foundational work in the 1950s; Felix Pirani and Roger Penrose independently arrived at related formulations.

The classification theorem

Interpreting the Weyl tensor as the action of a linear operator on the vector space of bivectors, or equivalently as a linear operator acting on a three-dimensional complex vector space of self-dual bivectors, one can classify its eigenvalue structure. The possible algebraic types are conventionally labelled I, II, D, III, N, and O, forming a hierarchy of decreasing generality: type I is the most general, while types III, N and O are the most special. Type O corresponds to a vanishing Weyl tensor (a conformally flat spacetime), and type N describes transverse gravitational radiation, with a quadruple principal null direction.

Principal null directions

Equivalently, the classification can be phrased in terms of the multiplicities of the principal null directions of the Weyl tensor, determined by the roots of a quartic polynomial. In type I all four principal null directions are distinct; in type D there are two pairs of repeated directions, the case that includes the Schwarzschild and Kerr solutions; in type N all four coincide.

Applications

The Petrov classification is a standard invariant tool in the analysis of black hole spacetimes, gravitational waves, and the peeling theorem. It underlies the Goldberg–Sachs theorem, which relates algebraically special spacetimes to shear-free null congruences.
