Heat shield

A heat shield is designed to protect a substance from absorbing excessive heat from an external source, either by dissipating, reflecting, or simply absorbing the heat. It is most often used in the context of spacecraft atmospheric entry, where a vehicle returning from orbit at hypersonic speed experiences intense aerodynamic heating, with surface temperatures exceeding 1,500 °C (2,730 °F). Heat shields are also used in automotive exhaust systems, industrial machinery, and firefighting equipment.

Types

Passive heat shields absorb or radiate heat. Ablative heat shields, such as the AVCOAT shield used on Apollo and Orion capsules and the PICA shield on SpaceX vehicles, char and erode away during entry, carrying heat away with the departing material. Thermal soak shields use materials with high heat capacity, such as the silica tiles of the Space Shuttle, which absorb heat and re-radiate it. Reusable ceramic tiles combine low thermal conductivity with high emissivity. Actively cooled shields circulate a fluid to remove heat, though they are rarely flown.

Spaceflight use

Every crewed spacecraft that has returned from orbit — Mercury, Gemini, Apollo, Soyuz, Shenzhou, Crew Dragon, and Orion — has relied on a heat shield for safe reentry. The Mars Science Laboratory's shield endured the highest temperatures of any Mars mission. Future vehicles such as SpaceX's Starship use stainless steel structure with ceramic tiles on the windward side for transpiration and radiative cooling.
