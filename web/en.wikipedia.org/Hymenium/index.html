Hymenium

The hymenium is the tissue layer on the hymenophore of a fungal fruiting body where the cells develop into basidia or asci, which produce spores. In some species all of the cells of the hymenium develop into basidia or asci, while in others some cells develop into sterile cells called cystidia (in basidiomycetes) or paraphyses (in ascomycetes).

Position

The position of the hymenium is a key character in fungal identification. In gilled mushrooms it coats the surfaces of the gills; in boletes it lines the inside of the tubes; in cup fungi it lines the inside of the cup; and in puffballs the spore-producing tissue, the gleba, is internal. Lichens bear their hymenium in apothecia or perithecia on the thallus surface.

Structure

The hymenium sits upon a supporting layer of hyphae called the subhymenium, beneath which lies the trama of the fruiting body. Under the microscope, the arrangement, size and shape of basidia, asci, spores and sterile elements in the hymenium are used to classify fungi to genus and species level.
