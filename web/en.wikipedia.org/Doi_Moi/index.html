Launched1986, 6th Congress of the CPV
CountryVietnam
ModelSocialist-oriented market economy

Đổi Mới (Vietnamese for "renovation") is the name given to the economic reforms initiated by the Communist Party of Vietnam at its 6th National Congress in December 1986. Modelled in part on China's reform and opening up under Deng Xiaoping, Đổi Mới shifted Vietnam from central planning toward a "socialist-oriented market economy" while preserving one-party rule.

Background

After reunification in 1976, Vietnam's collectivised economy stagnated; war with Cambodia and China, a US embargo, and hyperinflation exceeding several hundred percent in the mid-1980s produced a crisis. Local experiments with contract farming and market prices ("fence-breaking") from below pressured the leadership in Hanoi to reform from above. Nguyen Van Linh, elected general secretary in 1986, became the reform's face.

Reforms

Measures included decollectivising agriculture (Resolution 10 of 1988, returning land-use rights to households), freeing prices, devaluing the dong, opening to foreign investment (1987 Foreign Investment Law), legalising private enterprise, reforming state-owned enterprises and joining regional and global institutions — ASEAN (1995), normalisation with the US (1995), APEC, and the WTO (2007).

Results

Vietnam became a major exporter of rice, coffee, garments, footwear and electronics; growth averaged around 6–7% for decades and poverty fell dramatically from over half the population in the early 1990s to low single digits by World Bank measures. Critics note inequality, corruption, environmental costs and dependence on foreign-invested manufacturing — debates similar to those about state capitalism in the region.
