Respiration (physiology)

Hero image (placeholder):

In physiology, respiration is the movement of oxygen from the outside environment to the cells within tissues, and the removal of carbon dioxide in the opposite direction. It contrasts with cellular respiration, the metabolic process by which cells generate energy (ATP) using oxygen and glucose.

Mechanism

In air-breathing vertebrates, respiration involves ventilation — the inhalation and exhalation of air driven by the diaphragm and intercostal muscles — followed by gas exchange across the alveoli of the lungs, transport of gases in the blood, and exchange with the tissues. In fish, exchange occurs across the gills; insects use tracheal systems, and plants exchange gases through stomata. Respiratory rate and depth are regulated by the brainstem in response to blood carbon dioxide levels and pH.
