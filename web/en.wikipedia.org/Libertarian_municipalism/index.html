Libertarian municipalism

Libertarian municipalism is a political philosophy developed by the American theorist Murray Bookchin, proposing that political transformation begin at the local level through democratic citizens' assemblies in towns and city neighbourhoods, which then confederate into networks capable of countering and eventually replacing the centralised state.

Principles

The strategy calls for activists to run in local elections with a mandate to democratise municipal governments: creating legally empowered assemblies practising direct democracy, municipalising the economy, and linking communities in confederations bound by mandated, recallable delegates. It forms the political programme of Bookchin's broader philosophy of social ecology and, in its later form, communalism.

Influence

Ideas derived from libertarian municipalism influenced democratic confederalism as implemented in the Autonomous Administration of North and East Syria (Rojava), whose theorist Abdullah Öcalan read Bookchin's work in prison. See also direct democracy.
