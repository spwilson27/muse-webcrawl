Jamsetji Nusserwanji Tata (3 March 1839 – 19 May 1904) was an Indian pioneer industrialist who founded the Tata Group, India's largest conglomerate. Regarded as the father of Indian industry, he established the foundations of Indian steel, power, hospitality, and scientific research.

Early life and business

Born in Navsari, Gujarat, into a Parsi Zoroastrian family of priests, Tata broke with family tradition by going into trade. He graduated from Elphinstone College in Bombay and joined his father's export firm, working in Hong Kong, Shanghai, and London. In 1868 he struck out on his own, and in 1877 he founded the Empress Mills at Nagpur, building a successful textile business and introducing innovations such as the ring spindle and employee welfare schemes unusual for the time.

Visionary projects

Tata conceived four great projects for India: an integrated steel plant, a world-class institution of science, a hydroelectric power scheme for Bombay, and a hotel to rival Europe's best. Only the last was completed in his lifetime — the Taj Mahal Hotel in Bombay, opened in 1903. The others were realised by his successors: the Indian Institute of Science (founded 1909), Tata Steel at Sakchi — the city renamed Jamshedpur in his honour (steel production began in 1912) — and the Tata hydroelectric companies that lit Bombay from 1915.

Philanthropy and death

In 1892 Tata endowed the JN Tata Endowment to fund higher education abroad for Indian students regardless of caste or creed, an early model of organised Indian philanthropy carried forward by the Tata trusts. He died in 1904 at Bad Nauheim, Germany, while seeking technology and expertise for his steel project, and is buried in London. Jawaharlal Nehru later called him a "one-man planning commission" for his vision of India's industrial development.
