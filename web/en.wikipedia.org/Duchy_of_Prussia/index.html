Duchy of Prussia

The Duchy of Prussia (yellow) within the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth

The Duchy of Prussia (German: Herzogtum Preußen; Polish: Prusy Książęce) was a duchy in the region of Prussia established as a result of the secularization of the Monastic State of the Teutonic Knights in 1525. It was the first Protestant state when it adopted Lutheranism as the state religion that year.

Foundation

In 1525, Grand Master Albert of the Teutonic Order converted to Lutheranism, secularized the Order's Prussian lands, and, by the Treaty of Kraków and the Prussian Homage, received them as a hereditary duchy held as a fief of the Kingdom of Poland. Its capital was Königsberg, where Albert founded the university (the Albertina) in 1544.

Brandenburg union

After Albert's son Albert Frederick died without male heirs in 1618, the duchy passed to his son-in-law John Sigismund, Elector of Brandenburg, forming the personal union known as Brandenburg-Prussia. By the treaties of Wehlau and Bromberg (1657) and Oliva (1660), Frederick William obtained full sovereignty over the duchy from Poland.

Kingdom

Because the duchy lay outside the Holy Roman Empire, Elector Frederick III could crown himself “King in Prussia” as Frederick I in 1701, transforming Brandenburg-Prussia into the Kingdom of Prussia.
