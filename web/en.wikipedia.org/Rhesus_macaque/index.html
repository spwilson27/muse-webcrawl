Rhesus macaque

Scientific nameMacaca mulatta
FamilyCercopithecidae
RangeSouth, Central and Southeast Asia
StatusLeast Concern (IUCN)

The rhesus macaque (Macaca mulatta) is a species of Old World monkey native to Asia, ranging from Afghanistan across India and Nepal to southern China and mainland Southeast Asia. It is one of the best-known primates, famous for its role in scientific research.

Description

Rhesus macaques have brown or grey fur, pink bare faces and tails 19–30 cm long. Adults weigh roughly 5–12 kg, males being larger. They live in multi-male, multi-female troops of 20 to 200 individuals with matrilineal hierarchies, and are comfortable both in trees and on the ground; they are also strong swimmers.

Relation to humans

The species adapts readily to human environments and is common around temples and cities in India, where it is associated with the god Hanuman. In science it gave its name to the Rh blood group factor; rhesus monkeys were used in polio vaccine development, behavioural research, and early spaceflight tests. Introduced populations exist in Florida.
