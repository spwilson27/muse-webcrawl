Honeywell International Inc. is an American publicly traded, multinational conglomerate corporation headquartered in Charlotte, North Carolina. It primarily operates in four areas of business: aerospace, building automation, performance materials and technologies, and safety and productivity solutions. Honeywell is a Fortune 500 company and a component of major stock indices; its products range from aircraft engines, avionics, and auxiliary power units to thermostats, security systems, process-control software, advanced materials, and warehouse automation.

History

The company traces its origins to Albert Butz's 1885 patent for a furnace temperature regulator, and to the Honeywell Heating Specialty Company founded in Wabash, Indiana, in 1906 by Mark C. Honeywell. The thermostat business became famous for the round "T87" thermostat of 1953, designed by Henry Dreyfuss, an icon of industrial design now held in museum collections. In 1927 Honeywell merged with the Minneapolis Heat Regulator Company to form Minneapolis-Honeywell, expanding into industrial controls and later aerospace and computing — Honeywell was a major mainframe manufacturer and the "BUNCH" rival to IBM before selling its computer division.

The modern company was created in 1999 when AlliedSignal acquired Honeywell Inc. and adopted the better-known name. Under CEO David Cote (2002–2017) the conglomerate expanded through acquisitions while divesting slower businesses; in 2018 it spun off its homes business (Resideo) and transportation systems unit (Garrett Motion). Honeywell moved its corporate headquarters to Charlotte in 2019. In the 2020s it announced plans to separate into independent companies for aerospace, automation, and advanced materials.

Business segments

Honeywell Aerospace supplies engines, cockpit systems, and services to commercial, defence, and space customers; its auxiliary power units and avionics fly on most major airliner types. Building Automation sells HVAC controls, fire and security systems, and building management software — the descendant of the original thermostat business. Performance Materials and Technologies includes UOP process technology for refining and petrochemicals, refrigerants such as Solstice, and specialty chemicals. Safety and Productivity Solutions covers personal protective equipment, gas detection, and Intelligrated warehouse automation and handheld computing.
