Short-track speed skating

Short-track speed skating is a competitive form of ice speed skating in which four to six skaters race simultaneously on an oval ice track with a circumference of 111.12 metres (364.5 ft) — the size of an Olympic ice hockey rink — rather than on the 400 m oval used in long-track speed skating. Races are decided by finishing position rather than by time, and the combination of tight corners, high speeds (over 50 km/h), pack tactics and frequent crashes makes it one of the most dramatic Winter Olympic sports.

Racing

Events progress through heats, quarter-finals and semi-finals to A and B finals, with the first finishers advancing; skaters impeded by fouls may be advanced by the referee. Blocking, impeding and team skating (except in relays) are penalised by disqualification. In relay races, teammates exchange by pushing the incoming skater, and any member may take over except in the final two laps. Skaters lean at extreme angles in the turns, fingertips touching the ice, on boots with high stiff cuffs and blades offset to the left boot. South Korea is the historically dominant nation, with Canada, China, the Netherlands and Italy also strong; famous champions include Apolo Ohno (United States), Viktor Ahn (South Korea/Russia), Choi Min-jeong and Arianna Fontana.

Famous moments

The sport's most famous race came at Salt Lake City 2002, when Australia's Steven Bradbury — last and well behind in the 1000 m final — won gold after all four rivals crashed on the final corner, an upset so iconic that "doing a Bradbury" entered Australian English for an unlikely victory. The 2002 Games also saw the disputed disqualification of Kim Dong-sung in the 1500 m, heightening the sport's profile in South Korea.

History

Mass-start pack racing developed in North America in the early 20th century, where skating was held on hockey rinks, while European skating favoured the time-trial long track. The ISU staged the first World Short Track Championships in 1981 (unofficial events date to 1976), and the sport entered the Olympics as a demonstration at Calgary 1988 before full inclusion in 1992. The ISU Short Track World Tour (formerly World Cup) runs each season alongside world and continental championships.
