Dow theory

A price chart illustrating market trends.

Dow theory on stock price movement is a form of technical analysis derived from editorials written by Charles H. Dow (1851–1902), co-founder of Dow Jones & Company and editor of The Wall Street Journal. The theory holds that the stock market moves in trends that can be analysed to forecast future direction.

Principles

The theory's six basic tenets, as later formalised by William Hamilton, Robert Rhea and others, include: the market discounts everything; there are three kinds of trends (primary, secondary and minor); primary trends have three phases (accumulation, public participation and excess); the market averages—industrial and transportation—must confirm each other; volume must confirm the trend; and a trend is assumed to remain in effect until it gives definite signals of reversal.

Influence

Dow theory is regarded as the foundation of modern technical analysis, introducing concepts such as trend confirmation and market phases that remain in use, though its statistical effectiveness is debated by proponents of the efficient-market hypothesis.
