Collaborative filtering

Collaborative filtering (CF) is a technique used by recommender systems to make predictions about a user's interests by collecting preferences from many users. The underlying assumption is that if two users agreed in their evaluations in the past, they are more likely to agree again in the future.

Approaches

Memory-based methods work directly with the user–item rating matrix. User-based filtering finds users similar to the target user (using measures such as Pearson correlation or cosine similarity) and recommends items those neighbours rated highly. Item-based filtering, popularised by Amazon, computes similarities between items and recommends items similar to those the user already liked.

Model-based methods build a predictive model from the data. Matrix factorisation techniques, which represent users and items as vectors in a shared latent factor space, were brought to prominence by the Netflix Prize and remain widely used. Other model-based approaches include Bayesian networks, clustering and latent semantic models.

Challenges

Collaborative filtering suffers from data sparsity (most users rate only a tiny fraction of items), scalability problems with millions of users and items, synonymy, and the cold start problem for new users and items. It is also vulnerable to manipulation through fake profiles ("shilling attacks").
