The 2016 UEFA European Championship, commonly referred to as UEFA Euro 2016, was the 15th UEFA European Championship, the quadrennial international men's football championship of Europe organised by UEFA. It was held in France from 10 June to 10 July 2016. Portugal won the tournament for the first time, defeating hosts France 1–0 after extra time in the final at the Stade de France in Saint-Denis.

Expansion

For the first time, the European Championship was contested by 24 teams, expanded from the previous 16-team format used since 1996. Under the new format, teams contested a group stage of six groups of four, the top two of each group and the four best third-placed teams advancing to a round of 16.

Tournament highlights

Debutants Iceland produced the tournament's fairy-tale run, drawing with Portugal and Hungary in the group, then famously beating England 2–1 in the round of 16 before losing to France in the quarter-finals. Wales, also debutants, reached the semi-finals. Holders Spain were eliminated by Italy in the round of 16. Portugal drew all three group games and won the tournament with only one win inside 90 minutes — in the semi-final against Wales.

Final

In the final, Portugal's captain Cristiano Ronaldo was forced off injured in the first half, but Portugal held France at bay; substitute Éder scored the only goal in the 109th minute of extra time to give Portugal their first major international trophy.
