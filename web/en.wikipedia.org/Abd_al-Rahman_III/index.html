Abd al-Rahman III (891–961) was the Emir of Córdoba from 912 and, from 929, the first Caliph of Córdoba, ruling the Umayyad state of al-Andalus in Iberia for nearly half a century. His reign marked the political and cultural zenith of Muslim Spain: he reunified a fragmented emirate, proclaimed the Caliphate of Córdoba in rivalry with the Abbasids of Baghdad and the Fatimids of North Africa, and made Córdoba one of the largest and most cultured cities in the world.

Reign

Succeeding his grandfather Abd Allah at the age of about twenty, Abd al-Rahman inherited a state weakened by decades of rebellion, above all the long revolt of Umar ibn Hafsun. Through patient campaigning he subdued the rebels, taking Bobastro in 928, and brought the whole of al-Andalus under Córdoban rule. In 929, countering Fatimid expansion, he assumed the title of caliph and the regnal name al-Nasir li-Din Allah ("Defender of the Religion of God"). Although defeated by the Leonese king Ramiro II at Simancas in 939, his armies generally kept the Christian kingdoms of the north in tributary check, and his navy dominated the western Mediterranean.

Court and legacy

In 936 Abd al-Rahman began building the vast palace-city of Madinat al-Zahra outside Córdoba, the seat of his court and administration, renowned for its splendour. Córdoba under his rule supported great libraries, scholarship and diplomacy — receiving embassies from Byzantium and the Holy Roman Empire — and a notable degree of interfaith coexistence, employing officials such as the Jewish courtier Hasdai ibn Shaprut. He was succeeded by his son al-Hakam II.
