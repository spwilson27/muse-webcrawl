Gloster Meteor

The Gloster Meteor was the first British jet fighter and the Allies' only jet aircraft to enter service during the Second World War. Designed by George Carter of the Gloster Aircraft Company around Frank Whittle's turbojet engines, it first flew on 5 March 1943 and entered service with No. 616 Squadron RAF in July 1944.

Service history

Meteors were first used against V-1 flying bombs in the summer of 1944, accounting for a number of the weapons, and flew armed reconnaissance and ground-attack sorties over Europe in 1945 without meeting the Luftwaffe's Me 262 in air combat. Postwar marks — notably the F.8 — formed the backbone of RAF Fighter Command in the early 1950s. In the Korean War, Australian Meteors fought MiG-15s, suffering losses in air combat but performing well in ground attack. Night-fighter, reconnaissance, trainer (T.7) and target-tug versions served widely abroad.

Records and survivals

In November 1945 a specially prepared Meteor F.4 set a world air speed record of 606 mph (975 km/h). Nearly 4,000 Meteors were built, making it by far the most numerous early jet fighter, and numerous examples survive in museums; two Martin-Baker test aircraft long remained in use for ejection-seat trials.
