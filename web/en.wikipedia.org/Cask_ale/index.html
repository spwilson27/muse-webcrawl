Cask ale

Cask ale, or cask-conditioned beer, is unfiltered and unpasteurised beer which is conditioned (including secondary fermentation) and served from a cask, traditionally without the addition of extraneous carbon dioxide. The term "real ale" was coined by CAMRA for this style of beer in the 1970s.

Process

Cask ale is racked into the cask with live yeast, continues fermenting ("conditioning") in the pub cellar, and is vented and "tapped" before service using a hard and soft spile. It is served cool — around 11–13 °C — either by gravity or drawn with a hand pump (beer engine), and must typically be drunk within a few days of being put on sale.

Characteristics

Because it is a living product, cask ale has a shorter shelf life than keg beer but develops complex flavours from secondary fermentation. It is emblematic of the traditional British pub, and its survival was the founding cause of the Campaign for Real Ale.
