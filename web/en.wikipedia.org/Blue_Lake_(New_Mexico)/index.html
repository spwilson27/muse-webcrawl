Blue Lake (New Mexico)

Blue Lake (Taos: P'hala-iu, "lake") is a sacred alpine lake in the Sangre de Cristo Mountains of Taos County, New Mexico, at an elevation of about 11,409 feet (3,477 m). It is held sacred by Taos Pueblo, whose people believe it is the source of all life and the resting place of the souls of the dead. The lake feeds the Rio Pueblo de Taos, which flows through the pueblo.

Dispossession and return

In 1906, President Theodore Roosevelt proclaimed the lake and surrounding watershed part of the Taos National Forest (now Carson National Forest), taking some 48,000 acres of Taos Pueblo land into the national forest system and opening it to recreation and other uses. The Pueblo campaigned for 64 years for the land's return — one of the longest and most prominent Native American land struggles of the 20th century. In 1970, President Richard Nixon signed Public Law 91-550, returning the 48,000 acres, including Blue Lake, to Taos Pueblo in trust, the first time the United States returned land to an Indigenous people for religious reasons.

Access

The Blue Lake watershed is closed to the public, a restriction credited with preserving the lake's pristine character and the Pueblo's religious practices. The return of Blue Lake is considered a landmark in federal Indian policy and the free exercise of Native American religion.
