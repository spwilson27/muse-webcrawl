Battle of Valmy

The Battle of Valmy, also known as the Cannonade of Valmy, was fought on 20 September 1792 during the War of the First Coalition, part of the French Revolutionary Wars. A French army under Generals Charles François Dumouriez and François-Christophe de Kellermann halted the advance of a Prussian army under the Duke of Brunswick toward Paris. Little more than an artillery duel in the rain, Valmy was nonetheless one of the most consequential engagements of the age: it saved the French Revolution and, the following day, the National Convention abolished the monarchy and proclaimed the French Republic.

Background

Following the outbreak of war in April 1792 and the storming of the Tuileries in August, a Prussian-led army invaded France to restore Louis XVI, issuing the threatening Brunswick Manifesto. After the frontier fortresses of Longwy and Verdun fell, the road to Paris seemed open. Dumouriez and Kellermann united their armies in the Argonne and took up a strong position around the windmill at Valmy, astride the Prussian line of advance.

The cannonade

On 20 September, in rain and mist, the Prussians deployed and a heavy artillery exchange began. Brunswick probed the French position expecting the revolutionary army — a mixture of regulars and volunteers — to break. Instead Kellermann famously raised his hat on his sword and shouted "Vive la nation!", answered by his troops. The Prussian infantry advanced, hesitated under fire, and Brunswick declined to press the assault. Both sides disengaged; the Prussians, their supplies failing and dysentery spreading, began a retreat from France within days.

Significance

Johann Wolfgang von Goethe, present with the Prussian army, reportedly told his companions: "From here and today, a new epoch of world history begins." Valmy preserved the Revolution, vindicated the citizen-soldier ideal, and was followed by French offensives into the Austrian Netherlands and the Rhineland. The windmill at Valmy remains a national monument.
