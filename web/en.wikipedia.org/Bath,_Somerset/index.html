Bath, Somerset

Bath is a city in Somerset, England, known for and named after its Roman-built baths. It lies in the valley of the River Avon, about 97 miles west of London, and became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1987.

History

The Romans founded Aquae Sulis around the hot springs. Bath flourished in the Georgian era as a fashionable spa town, leaving celebrated Palladian architecture.

Architecture

Landmarks include the Royal Crescent, the Circus, Pulteney Bridge, Bath Abbey and the Roman Baths museum, largely built from honey-coloured Bath stone.

Today

Bath is a major tourist destination and university city.
