Avatar (computing)

TypeDigital representation of a user
First used1979 (PLATO game Avatar); popularized by Habitat (1985)
Forms2D icons, 3D models, profile pictures

In computing, an avatar is a graphical representation of a user, the user's character, or persona. Avatars can be two-dimensional images in Internet forums and other online communities, where they are also known as profile pictures or userpics, or three-dimensional models in games and virtual worlds.

History

The word derives from the Sanskrit avatāra, the descent of a deity to Earth. It was used for the player representation in the 1979 PLATO role-playing game Avatar, and Lucasfilm's 1985 graphical online world Habitat popularized the term for a user's on-screen embodiment. Neal Stephenson's 1992 novel Snow Crash cemented the term in popular culture for virtual-reality personas.

Uses

Avatars appear in video games (as the player character), virtual worlds such as Second Life, social media and forums (as static images), video conferencing (animated or AI-driven representations), and instant messaging services. Services such as Gravatar provide globally consistent avatar images across websites.

Design and identity

Studies of online identity examine how users choose avatars that resemble, idealize, or deliberately depart from their physical appearance, and how avatar behaviour affects social interaction in virtual environments.
