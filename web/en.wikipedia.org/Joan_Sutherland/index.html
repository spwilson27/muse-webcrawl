Joan Sutherland

Born7 November 1926, Sydney, Australia
Died10 October 2010, Geneva, Switzerland
OccupationOperatic soprano
Years active1947–1990

Dame Joan Alston Sutherland (7 November 1926 – 10 October 2010) was an Australian dramatic coloratura soprano noted for her contribution to the renaissance of the bel canto repertoire from the late 1950s through the 1980s. She was nicknamed La Stupenda ("the Stupendous One") after a performance in Venice in 1960.

Early life

Sutherland was born in Point Piper, Sydney, to Scottish parents. Her mother, a mezzo-soprano who had given up her career, gave her daily singing exercises. She attended St Vincent's College, Potts Point, and won a scholarship to study piano and singing.

Career

She began her career in Australia, winning the Sun Aria in 1949 and the Mobil Quest in 1950, which funded study in London at the Royal College of Music. She joined the Royal Opera, Covent Garden, in 1952, debuting as the First Lady in Mozart's The Magic Flute. Initially trained as a dramatic soprano with Wagnerian ambitions, she was persuaded by her husband, conductor Richard Bonynge, to specialise in coloratura roles.

Her breakthrough came in 1959 as Lucia in Donizetti's Lucia di Lammermoor at Covent Garden, in a production by Franco Zeffirelli. The performance established her as a leading exponent of bel canto. She went on to sing at La Scala, the Paris Opera, the Metropolitan Opera in New York (debut 1961 as Lucia), and opera houses worldwide.

Repertoire and recordings

Her repertoire included Donizetti's three Tudor queens, Bellini's Norma, I puritani and La sonnambula, Rossini's Semiramide, Handel's Alcina and Rodelinda, and Massenet's Esclarmonde. With Bonynge and tenor Luciano Pavarotti she made many complete opera recordings for Decca, including Lucia di Lammermoor, Rigoletto, La traviata and Turandot (as Liù's opposite, the title role's ice princess — she sang Turandot in the famous 1972 recording with Pavarotti and Caballé).

Later life and honours

Sutherland retired from the stage in 1990 after performances of Les Huguenots at the Sydney Opera House. She was appointed a Dame Commander of the Order of the British Empire in 1979 and a Companion of the Order of Australia in 1975, and received the Order of Merit in 1991. She died in Geneva in 2010 aged 83.
