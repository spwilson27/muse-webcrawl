Capitalization

Upper caseCapital letters (majuscules)
Lower caseMinuscules
Main usesSentence initials, proper nouns, acronyms, titles

Capitalization (or capitalisation) is the writing of a word with its first letter as a capital (upper-case) letter and the remaining letters in lower case, in languages that distinguish the two cases. English capitalizes the first word of a sentence, proper nouns and adjectives derived from them, the pronoun "I," many acronyms and initialisms, and words in certain titles.

Majuscule and minuscule forms of the Latin alphabet.

History

Classical Latin inscriptions were written entirely in what are now called capital letters; distinct lower-case letterforms developed from cursive scripts in late antiquity and the early Middle Ages (Carolingian minuscule). Early modern English printers capitalized nouns liberally, following German practice, before conventions narrowed in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries toward the modern system. The terms "upper case" and "lower case" come from the drawers (cases) in which printers stored type.

Rules in English

Style guides vary on details: title case capitalizes major words in headings, while sentence case capitalizes only the first word and proper nouns. German capitalizes all nouns. Some languages, such as Spanish and French, do not capitalize days and months or language names as adjectives in the English manner. In computing, capitalization is significant in case-sensitive identifiers, and stylized forms such as camelCase and PascalCase structure multi-word names.

Other meanings

In finance, capitalization refers to a company's capital structure or its market value (market capitalization); in accounting, to recording a cost as an asset rather than an expense. Those senses are treated in separate articles.
