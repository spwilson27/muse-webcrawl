Comic strip

A comic strip is a sequence of cartoons, arranged in interrelated panels to display brief humour or form a narrative, often serialised, with text in balloons and captions. Traditionally published in newspapers and magazines, comic strips are now also widely published on the web as webcomics.

History

Precursors include Rodolphe Töpffer's picture stories and Wilhelm Busch's Max and Moritz. The modern newspaper strip emerged in 1890s America with The Yellow Kid and The Katzenjammer Kids, followed by classics such as Krazy Kat, Little Nemo, Peanuts (1950–2000) by Charles M. Schulz, Calvin and Hobbes, and The Far Side. Adventure strips like Flash Gordon and Prince Valiant expanded the form.

Formats

Daily strips are typically black-and-white with three or four panels; Sunday strips are larger and in colour. Gag-a-day strips contrast with continuity strips carrying ongoing storylines. Syndicates such as King Features distribute strips to papers worldwide.

Influence

Comic strips shaped popular culture and graphic storytelling, feeding into comic books, graphic novels, animation, and internet memes.
