Margaret Thatcher

Margaret Hilda Thatcher, Baroness Thatcher (née Roberts; 13 October 1925 – 8 April 2013), was a British stateswoman who served as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom from 1979 to 1990 and Leader of the Conservative Party from 1975 to 1990. She was the longest-serving British prime minister of the twentieth century and the first woman to hold the office. A Soviet journalist dubbed her the "Iron Lady", a nickname that became associated with her uncompromising politics and leadership style. The policies associated with her premiership are known as Thatcherism.

Early life and entry into politics

Thatcher was born and raised in Grantham, Lincolnshire, where her father owned grocery shops. She read chemistry at Somerville College, Oxford, and later qualified as a barrister. She was elected MP for Finchley in 1959 and served as Secretary of State for Education and Science in Edward Heath's government (1970–74), where she earned the tabloid nickname "Thatcher the milk snatcher" for ending free school milk for older children. In 1975 she defeated Heath for the Conservative leadership.

Premiership

Thatcher won the 1979 general election on a programme of tackling inflation, curbing trade union power and reducing state intervention. Her government pursued monetarist policies, deregulated financial markets (the "Big Bang" of 1986), privatised nationalised industries including British Telecom, British Gas and British Airways, and introduced the Right to Buy council houses. In 1982 she led Britain to victory in the Falklands War. She survived an IRA assassination attempt at Brighton in 1984 and defeated the year-long miners' strike of 1984–85. Abroad she formed a close alliance with US President Ronald Reagan and played a role in easing Cold War tensions with Mikhail Gorbachev.

Downfall, later life and legacy

Thatcher's introduction of the Community Charge ("poll tax") provoked riots in 1990, and her increasingly hostile stance toward European integration split her party. After Michael Heseltine challenged her leadership in November 1990, she failed to win outright on the first ballot and resigned, succeeded by John Major. She left the Commons in 1992 and entered the House of Lords as Baroness Thatcher. She died in London in 2013 and was given a ceremonial funeral. Thatcher remains one of the most divisive and consequential figures in modern British politics.
