Alexa Internet

Alexa Internet, Inc. was an American web traffic analysis company based in San Francisco, California. Founded in 1996 and acquired by Amazon in 1999, it was best known for the Alexa Rank, a global ranking system that ordered websites by estimated popularity based on traffic data gathered from a panel of users with its browser toolbar and extensions. Amazon retired the service on 1 May 2022.

History

Alexa was founded by Brewster Kahle and Bruce Gilliat, and named in homage to the Library of Alexandria. Its original product suggested related links as users browsed. In 1999 Amazon acquired Alexa (along with the Internet Archive and Accept.com) for about $250 million in stock. Alexa supplied search results and site information to other engines over the years, including, at various times, Google and Microsoft.

Alexa Rank

The Alexa Traffic Rank combined estimated average daily unique visitors and pageviews over a rolling three-month period into a single number, with the most-visited site (usually Google) ranked number 1. Rankings below about 100,000 were widely regarded as statistically noisy, since they rested on a small, self-selected sample of toolbar users; the system was also criticised for bias toward technology-oriented audiences and for susceptibility to manipulation. Nevertheless, the rank became a commonly quoted, if rough, public metric of website popularity, and Alexa's lists underpinned services such as the Wayback Machine's crawl priorities.

Closure

In December 2021 Amazon announced that Alexa Internet would be retired; the website and API ceased operation on 1 May 2022, ending more than 25 years of service.
