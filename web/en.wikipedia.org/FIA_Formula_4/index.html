FIA Formula 4

FIA Formula 4, also called FIA F4, is an open-wheel racing car category intended for junior drivers. There is no global Formula 4 championship; instead, individual nations or regions host their own championships under a common set of FIA regulations. The category was created by the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA) — the international sanctioning body for motorsport — and Gerhard Berger, then president of the FIA Single-Seater Commission, to provide an affordable first step on the single-seater ladder between karting and Formula 3.

Background

The FIA Formula 4 regulations were first approved in March 2013. The concept standardised chassis safety requirements, capped costs, and allowed national series to select an engine supplier. The first championship run to the regulations was the Italian F4 Championship in 2014, won by Lance Stroll. Championships followed in Germany (ADAC), Britain, Spain, Japan, China, Australia, the United States, Brazil, India, and elsewhere, forming the base of the FIA's "Global Pathway" from karting to Formula One.

Cars

First-generation cars used carbon-fibre monocoque chassis from manufacturers such as Tatuus, Mygale, and Ligier, with engines of around 160 horsepower. A second generation, introduced in some series from 2022 (notably the Tatuus F4-T421), added the halo cockpit protection device and improved safety structures. Drivers earn FIA Super Licence points according to their championship results, with Formula 4 positioned below the FIA Formula 3 Championship and Formula Regional.

Notable graduates

Drivers who progressed through FIA Formula 4 championships include Lando Norris (British F4 champion), Mick Schumacher (ADAC F4 runner-up), Zhou Guanyu, and numerous later Formula 2 and Formula 3 competitors.
