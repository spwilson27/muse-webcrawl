Labour law (also spelled labor law in American English, and also known as employment law) is the body of law governing the rights and duties of workers, employers and trade unions, and the legal relationship between them. It arose from the industrial revolution's factory system, in response to the stark imbalance of bargaining power between individual workers and employers.

Branches

Labour law is conventionally divided into two broad fields. Individual labour law concerns the contract of employment and minimum standards: wages (including minimum-wage laws), working time and rest, paid leave, protection against unfair dismissal, workplace health and safety, and equality law prohibiting discrimination on grounds such as sex, race, disability, age, religion and sexual orientation. Collective labour law concerns trade unions and collective action: the right to organise and bargain collectively, works councils and employee representation, and the law of strikes, lockouts and picketing.

History

Early factory legislation, such as Britain's Factory Acts of the 19th century, limited child labour and working hours. Trade unions, long treated as criminal conspiracies or restraints of trade, were legalised across the industrialising world in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The International Labour Organization, founded in 1919 as part of the Treaty of Versailles settlement, set international conventions on hours, forced labour, child labour and union rights. Post-1945 welfare states expanded employment protection, while from the 1980s many systems moved toward greater labour-market "flexibility", a continuing tension with job security ("flexicurity" in the Nordic model).

Contemporary issues

Modern debates centre on the classification and rights of gig-economy and platform workers, the regulation of agency and zero-hours work, remote and hybrid working, algorithmic management, and the cross-border reach of supply-chain labour standards. At the international level, the ILO's 1998 Declaration of Fundamental Principles and Rights at Work — extended in 2022 to include a safe and healthy working environment — binds all member states regardless of ratification.
