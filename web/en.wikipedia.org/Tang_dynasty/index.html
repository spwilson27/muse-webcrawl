Tang dynasty

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Tang dynasty (Chinese: 唐朝; pinyin: Táng cháo), or the Tang Empire, was an imperial dynasty of China that ruled from 618 to 907, with an interregnum between 690 and 705. It was preceded by the Sui dynasty and followed by the Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms period. Historians generally regard the Tang as a high point in Chinese civilization, and a golden age of cosmopolitan culture. Tang territory, acquired through the military campaigns of its early rulers, rivalled that of the Han dynasty.

Founding and early reigns

The dynasty was founded by the Li family, who seized power during the decline and collapse of the Sui Empire. Li Yuan proclaimed himself Emperor Gaozu in 618. His son, Emperor Taizong (r. 626–649), expanded the empire deep into Central Asia and presided over a celebrated era of good government. The dynasty's sole female ruler, Wu Zetian, interrupted the Li line by proclaiming her own Zhou dynasty in 690, before the Tang was restored in 705.

Golden age and decline

Under Emperor Xuanzong (r. 712–756), the Tang reached its cultural zenith — the age of poets Li Bai and Du Fu — but the devastating An Lushan Rebellion (755–763) killed a large portion of the population and permanently weakened central control. Regional military governors (jiedushi) grew increasingly autonomous, and the dynasty never fully recovered. The last Tang emperor was deposed in 907 by the warlord Zhu Wen, ushering in a half-century of division.

Culture

The capital Chang'an was one of the largest cities in the world and a terminus of the Silk Road, receiving merchants, monks and fashions from Persia, India and beyond. Tang China saw advances in woodblock printing, cartography, medicine, and timekeeping, and its poetry is still regarded as the pinnacle of the Chinese verse tradition.
