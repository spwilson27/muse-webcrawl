Yavneh

Yavneh (יַבְנֶה) is a city in the Central District of Israel, south of Tel Aviv. In antiquity it was known as Jamnia (Greek: Ἰαμνία), a town with a famous role in Jewish history: after the Roman destruction of Jerusalem in 70 CE, the sage Yohanan ben Zakkai re-established the Sanhedrin at Yavneh, where rabbinic Judaism took shape.

History

The site has Bronze Age roots and appears in the Bible as Jabneh/Jabneel, a Philistine and later Judean border town. Under the Hasmoneans and Herodians it was a significant port town (its harbour was at Yavneh-Yam). According to rabbinic tradition, Yohanan ben Zakkai escaped besieged Jerusalem — legend says smuggled out in a coffin — and obtained Vespasian's permission to found an academy at Yavneh. There the sages reorganized Jewish law and liturgy after the loss of the Temple; Rabban Gamaliel II succeeded as patriarch, and tradition associates Yavneh with the fixing of the biblical canon and the calendar.

The town became the Arab village of Yibna, identified with Crusader Ibelin, whose castle and church stood there. Yibna's residents fled or were expelled in the 1948 Arab–Israeli War. Modern Yavneh was founded in 1949 as a transit camp for Jewish immigrants and grew into a city of over 50,000, known for national cultural prizes, hi-tech industry, and the Soreq Nuclear Research Center nearby.

Archaeology

Excavations at Tel Yavneh have uncovered a major Philistine and Byzantine industrial quarter, including one of the largest wine-production complexes of the Byzantine world (5th–7th centuries CE), with enormous warehouses and kilns.
