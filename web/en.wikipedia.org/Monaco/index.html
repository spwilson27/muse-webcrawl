Monaco, officially the Principality of Monaco, is a sovereign city-state and microstate on the French Riviera in Western Europe. Bordered by France on three sides and the Mediterranean Sea on the fourth, it is the second-smallest sovereign state in the world (after Vatican City) at about 2.08 square kilometres, and the most densely populated. Monaco is governed by the House of Grimaldi, which has ruled — with brief interruptions — since 1297.

History

The Rock of Monaco was settled in antiquity by Ligurian tribes and used by Phoenicians, Greeks, and Romans; its natural harbour was known as Monoikos. In 1297 Francesco Grimaldi seized the fortress disguised as a monk, founding the dynasty that still rules. Monaco's independence was recognised by France in successive treaties (notably 1861), while its survival was guaranteed in the 20th century by treaties under which it remains closely aligned with France, which is responsible for its defence. Prince Rainier III (r. 1949–2005) transformed Monaco into a financial and tourism centre and, through his 1956 marriage to American actress Grace Kelly, brought it worldwide celebrity. His son, Prince Albert II, succeeded in 2005.

Geography and districts

Monaco consists of a narrow coastal strip beneath the Maritime Alps. Its best-known districts are Monaco-Ville (the old town on the Rock, with the Prince's Palace and cathedral), Monte Carlo (famous for its casino), La Condamine (the port quarter), and Fontvieille, largely built on land reclaimed from the sea — reclamation has expanded Monaco's territory by roughly a fifth since the 19th century. A further extension, Mareterra, was inaugurated in 2024.

Economy

Monaco levies no personal income tax on most residents, a policy dating to 1869, and is a major banking and luxury-services centre with one of the world's highest GDPs per capita and an extraordinary concentration of millionaires. Tourism, the Monte Carlo Casino (opened 1863), conventions, and events such as the Monaco Grand Prix (held since 1929 on a street circuit), the Monte-Carlo Rally, and the Monaco Yacht Show drive the economy. Fewer than a quarter of residents are Monégasque citizens; most are French and other foreign residents, and tens of thousands commute in daily from France and Italy.

Culture and sport

The principality is home to the Monte Carlo Philharmonic Orchestra, the Ballets de Monte-Carlo, and the Oceanographic Museum founded by Prince Albert I. AS Monaco plays in the French football league system, and the Stade Louis II hosts athletics' Herculis meeting. Monaco joined the United Nations in 1993.
