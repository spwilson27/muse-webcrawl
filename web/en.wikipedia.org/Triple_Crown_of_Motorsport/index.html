Triple Crown of Motorsport
EventsIndianapolis 500; 24 Hours of Le Mans; Monaco Grand Prix
Alternative definitionIndianapolis 500; 24 Hours of Le Mans; Formula One World Championship

The Triple Crown of Motorsport is an unofficial motor racing achievement regarded as winning three of the most prestigious motor races in the world in one's career: the Indianapolis 500, the 24 Hours of Le Mans, and the Monaco Grand Prix. An alternative definition replaces the Monaco Grand Prix with the Formula One World Drivers' Championship.

Graham Hill

British driver Graham Hill is the only person to have achieved the Triple Crown under the Monaco-Grand-Prix definition. He won the Indianapolis 500 in 1966 on his first attempt, the Monaco Grand Prix five times (1963, 1964, 1965, 1968, 1969), and the 24 Hours of Le Mans in 1972 with Henri Pescarolo. Hill also won the Formula One World Championship in 1962 and 1968, making him the only driver to hold all four honours under either definition.

Near misses

Several drivers have won two of the three: A. J. Foyt, Mario Andretti, and Juan Pablo Montoya (Indy 500 and either Le Mans or Monaco), among others. Fernando Alonso won Monaco twice and Le Mans twice (2018, 2019) and contested the Indianapolis 500 in pursuit of the crown, leading the 2017 race before an engine failure. Ayrton Senna and others have won Monaco and championships but never attempted Indianapolis or Le Mans.
