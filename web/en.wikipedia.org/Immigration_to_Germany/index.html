Immigration to Germany

Immigration to Germany, both in the modern Federal Republic and historically, has made Germany one of the world's major destination countries. The United Nations has ranked Germany as having one of the largest immigrant populations of any country, second only to the United States in absolute numbers; roughly a quarter of Germany's population has a migration background.

History

West Germany recruited large numbers of foreign workers (Gastarbeiter) from the 1950s, especially from Turkey, Italy, Greece, and Yugoslavia, under bilateral recruitment agreements; recruitment stopped in 1973 but many workers stayed and were joined by their families. After reunification, Germany received ethnic German resettlers (Aussiedler) from Eastern Europe and the former Soviet Union, asylum seekers during the Yugoslav wars, and, as an EU member, free-movement migrants from other member states. In 2015–2016 Germany received over a million asylum seekers, largely from Syria, Afghanistan, and Iraq, and in 2022 more than a million refugees from Ukraine following the Russian invasion.

Law and policy

Citizenship law was historically based on descent (jus sanguinis); reforms in 2000 introduced elements of birthplace-based citizenship, and a 2024 reform further liberalised naturalisation and dual citizenship. The Skilled Immigration Act of 2020 and its successors aim to attract qualified workers to address demographic decline and labour shortages.
