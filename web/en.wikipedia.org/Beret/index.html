Beret

A beret is a soft, round, flat-crowned hat, usually made of woven or hand-knitted wool, felt, or (in modern versions) synthetic fibres. It is worn pulled to one side and has no brim. Berets have ancient antecedents in Europe and became strongly associated with peasants and artists in France and Spain, especially the Basque country, and with military uniform dress worldwide.

History

Flat wool caps of beret type were worn from the Bronze Age onward in parts of Europe. The modern beret was industrialised in the nineteenth century in south-western France, around Oloron-Sainte-Marie, and in the Basque regions of France and Spain. It became a symbol of French style in the twentieth century and an emblem of artists, intellectuals, and political movements.

Military use

Armies adopted berets from the early twentieth century — notably the French Chasseurs Alpins and the British Royal Tank Regiment — and coloured berets came to denote branches and elite units: maroon for airborne forces, green for commandos, and the light blue beret of United Nations peacekeepers.
