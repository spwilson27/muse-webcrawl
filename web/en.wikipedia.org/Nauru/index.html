Nauru, officially the Republic of Nauru, is an island country in Micronesia in the Pacific Ocean. It is the world's third-smallest country by area, covering just 21 square kilometres, and lies about 300 kilometres west of Banaba in Kiribati.

History

Nauru was first settled by Micronesian people at least 3,000 years ago. It was annexed by Germany in 1888, captured by Australian forces in 1914, and administered under a League of Nations mandate and later a United Nations trusteeship. Nauru became independent in 1968.

Economy

During the 20th century Nauru's economy rested on phosphate mining, which briefly made it one of the richest countries per capita in the world. Depletion of the reserves and environmental damage from mining led to severe economic decline; revenue later came in part from hosting an Australian offshore immigration processing centre.

Geography

The island is surrounded by a coral reef. A fertile coastal strip encircles a mined-out central plateau known as Topside. Nauru has no official capital; government offices are in the Yaren district.
