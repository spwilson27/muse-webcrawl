Widescreen

Widescreen images are displayed within a set of aspect ratios wider than the traditional 4:3 (1.33:1) of standard-definition television and early cinema. Common widescreen ratios include 16:9 (1.78:1), now the standard for television and online video, and the cinematic 2.39:1.

History in cinema

Although some widescreen experiments date to the 1920s, widescreen cinema became widespread in the 1950s as studios sought to distinguish films from the new medium of television: processes included CinemaScope (anamorphic, about 2.35:1), VistaVision and the three-projector Cinerama. The 1.85:1 "flat" ratio became the common standard for non-anamorphic theatrical films.

Television and computing

The 16:9 ratio was adopted as the shape of high-definition television because it lay between 4:3 and cinema ratios and could display both with modest letterboxing or pillarboxing. Computer monitors followed, with 16:10 common in the 2000s before 16:9 became dominant; "ultrawide" monitors use 21:9. When widescreen content is shown on a narrower screen it is letterboxed (black bars above and below); narrow content on a wide screen is pillarboxed.
