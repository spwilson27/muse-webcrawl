Qixi Festival

The Qixi Festival (Chinese: 七夕; pinyin: Qīxī), also known as the Qiqiao Festival, is a Chinese festival celebrating the annual meeting of Zhinü and Niulang in Chinese mythology. It is celebrated on the seventh day of the seventh lunisolar month of the Chinese calendar, and is sometimes called the Chinese Valentine's Day. The festival originated from the romantic legend of two lovers, Zhinü (the weaver girl, symbolised by the star Vega) and Niulang (the cowherd, symbolised by the star Altair).

Legend

According to the legend, the weaver girl Zhinü, daughter of the Heavenly Mother, fell in love with the mortal cowherd Niulang, and they married and had children. When the Heavenly Mother discovered the marriage, she separated the lovers by drawing the Milky Way across the sky. Moved by their devotion, she permitted them to meet once a year, on the seventh night of the seventh month, when a bridge of magpies forms across the Milky Way so the couple can be reunited for a single night.

Traditions

Traditionally, girls and young women would demonstrate their domestic skills, especially needlework and carving melons and fruit, and make wishes for a good husband, in a custom called "pleading for skills" (qǐqiǎo). Offerings of fruit, flowers and tea were made to Zhinü. In contemporary China, the festival is primarily celebrated as a romantic holiday: couples exchange gifts and flowers, and it is a popular date for weddings. Related festivals include Tanabata in Japan and Chilseok in Korea.
