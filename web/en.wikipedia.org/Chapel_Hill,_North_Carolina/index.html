Chapel Hill is a town in Orange and Durham counties in the U.S. state of North Carolina, with a small portion extending into Chatham County. Its population was 61,960 in the 2020 census, making it the 16th-largest municipality in the state. Chapel Hill, Durham, and the state capital, Raleigh, make up the corners of the Research Triangle, so named in 1959 with the creation of Research Triangle Park.

History

The town was founded in 1793 around the newly chartered University of North Carolina, the first public university in the United States to open its doors. The name derives from a chapel of the Church of England, New Hope Chapel, which stood at a crossroads on a hill. The town grew slowly as a university community and was incorporated in 1851.

Education

The University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill dominates the town's economy and culture. Its basketball team, the Tar Heels, is a major focus of local identity. The town is also served by the Chapel Hill-Carrboro City Schools district, regarded as among the best in the state.

Culture

Franklin Street, the town's main street, runs along the northern edge of the university campus and is lined with shops, restaurants, and music venues such as the Cat's Cradle in neighbouring Carrboro. Chapel Hill has a long-standing reputation as a centre of progressive politics in North Carolina.
