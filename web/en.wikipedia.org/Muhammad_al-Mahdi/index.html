Muhammad al-Mahdi


Born: c. 869 CE, Samarra, Abbasid Caliphate
Disappeared: c. 874 CE
Father: Hasan al-Askari
Religion: Twelver Shia Islam

Muhammad ibn Hasan al-Mahdi (born c. 869) is believed by Twelver Shia Muslims to be the twelfth and final Imam and the promised Mahdi, a messianic figure who will return at the end of time to establish justice and peace on Earth. He is the son of the eleventh Imam, Hasan al-Askari.

Birth and early life

According to Twelver tradition, Muhammad al-Mahdi was born in Samarra in 255 AH (c. 869 CE). His birth was kept secret because the Abbasid caliphs, fearing the prophecy of a descendant of the Prophet Muhammad who would overthrow tyranny, closely monitored the household of Hasan al-Askari. His mother is traditionally named as Narjis, said in some accounts to have been of Byzantine origin.

The Occultation

After the death of Hasan al-Askari in 874, the young Muhammad entered what Shia doctrine calls the Occultation (ghayba). The Minor Occultation (874–941) was a period during which he was said to communicate with his followers through four successive deputies known as the Four Gates. The Major Occultation, which continues to the present day, began in 941 after the death of the fourth deputy; no further intermediary was appointed, and the Mahdi is believed to remain hidden until God permits his reappearance.

Beliefs and expectations

Twelvers believe the Mahdi will return alongside Jesus (Isa) to fill the world with justice after it has been filled with oppression. The doctrine of intizar (awaiting) encourages believers to prepare for his return through pious conduct. The figure is commemorated especially on Sha'ban 15, the anniversary of his birth, and the Jamkaran Mosque near Qom in Iran is a major site associated with him.

In other traditions

Sunni Muslims also await a Mahdi, though he is generally not identified with the son of Hasan al-Askari, and the concept carries different theological weight. The Ahmadiyya community and various historical claimants have also shaped wider discourse about the Mahdi.
