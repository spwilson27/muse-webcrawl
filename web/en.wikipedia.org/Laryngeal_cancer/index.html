Laryngeal cancer

Laryngeal cancer is a cancer arising in the larynx (voice box), most commonly a squamous cell carcinoma. Tumours are classified by site: glottic (vocal cords), supraglottic and subglottic.

Signs and risk factors

Persistent hoarseness is the classic early symptom of glottic cancer; other signs include difficulty swallowing, ear pain, a neck lump and chronic cough. The dominant risk factors are tobacco smoking and heavy alcohol use, acting synergistically; HPV infection and occupational exposures also contribute.

Treatment

Early cancers may be treated with radiotherapy or transoral laser surgery preserving the voice. Advanced disease may require partial or total laryngectomy with chemotherapy and radiation, followed by voice rehabilitation.
