Thomas Wolsey

Bornc. March 1473, Ipswich, Suffolk, England
Died29 November 1530 (aged about 57), Leicester, Leicestershire, England
OccupationCardinal, Archbishop of York, Lord Chancellor
KingHenry VIII

Thomas Wolsey (c. March 1473 – 29 November 1530) was an English statesman and Catholic cardinal. When Henry VIII became King of England in 1509, Wolsey became the king's almoner. Wolsey's affairs prospered, and by 1514 he had become the controlling figure in virtually all matters of state. He also held important ecclesiastical appointments, including Archbishop of York — the second most important role in the English church — and, from 1515, cardinal, which gave him precedence over the Archbishop of Canterbury.

Rise to power

The son of an Ipswich tradesman, Wolsey was educated at Magdalen College, Oxford. His administrative brilliance in organising Henry's French campaigns brought him rapid promotion; he became Lord Chancellor in 1515 and a papal legate, and for some fifteen years he dominated English government, amassing enormous wealth and building Hampton Court Palace, which he later gave to the king. Abroad, he engineered the Treaty of London (1518) and the Field of the Cloth of Gold (1520), seeking to make England the arbiter of Europe.

Fall and death

Wolsey's failure to secure from Pope Clement VII an annulment of Henry's marriage to Catherine of Aragon destroyed the king's confidence. Deprived of the Great Seal and his palaces in 1529 and charged with praemunire, he retreated to York. In 1530 he was arrested on suspicion of treason and died at Leicester Abbey on the journey south to face trial. His fall cleared the way for Thomas Cromwell and the English Reformation. Wolsey founded Cardinal College, Oxford — refounded by Henry VIII as Christ Church — and a grammar school in his native Ipswich.
