Cardiff

Cardiff (Welsh: Caerdydd) is the capital and largest city of Wales, in the United Kingdom. Located on the River Taff near its mouth on the Bristol Channel, it has a population of roughly 360,000 in the city and over a million in the wider metropolitan area. Cardiff was proclaimed the capital of Wales in 1955 and is home to the Senedd, the Welsh Parliament.

History

The name derives from the Welsh Caerdydd — "fort on the Taff" — recalling the Roman fort established here around AD 75. The Normans built Cardiff Castle within the Roman walls, and the town grew as a port. Its explosive growth came in the 19th century, when the docks at Tiger Bay shipped coal from the South Wales Valleys to the world; for a time Cardiff was the world's largest coal-exporting port, and its Coal Exchange set global coal prices. The first £1 million cheque is said to have been signed there.

Modern Cardiff

After the decline of coal, the city reinvented itself through government, finance, media and education; Cardiff University and the BBC are major employers. The Cardiff Bay barrage created a freshwater lake and a regenerated waterfront home to the Wales Millennium Centre and the Senedd building. Sport is central to the city's identity: the Principality (Millennium) Stadium hosts Welsh rugby, and Cardiff City FC plays in English football leagues. Cardiff is also the birthplace of children's author Roald Dahl and singer Shirley Bassey.
