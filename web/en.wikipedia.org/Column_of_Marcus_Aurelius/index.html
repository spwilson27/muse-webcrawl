The Column of Marcus Aurelius (Latin: Columna Centenaria Divorum Marci et Faustinae; Italian: Colonna di Marco Aurelio) is a Roman victory column in Piazza Colonna, Rome. Modelled on Trajan's Column, it is a Doric column of Carrara marble carved with a spiralling relief that narrates Marcus Aurelius' Danubian wars, and it still stands on its original site.

Description

The hollow shaft rises about 29.6 metres and contains an internal spiral staircase of 190–200 steps, lit by narrow slits and once giving access to a viewing platform. The relief band winds around the column in 21 turns and depicts, in somewhat larger and more deeply cut figures than on Trajan's Column, the campaigns of the Marcomannic Wars of 166–180 AD, including the famous episodes of the "Rain Miracle" and the "Lightning Miracle". The emperor appears repeatedly at the head of his troops.

History

The column was completed around 193 AD, after the emperor's death in 180, and stood within a temple precinct on the Campus Martius. The original crowning statue of Marcus Aurelius (probably with Faustina) was lost; in 1589 Pope Sixtus V had Domenico Fontana restore the monument and place a bronze statue of Saint Paul on top, matching the statue of Saint Peter set on Trajan's Column. The relief figures show the increasingly schematic, expressive style characteristic of later second-century Roman art.
