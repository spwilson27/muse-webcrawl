Groupe PSA

Groupe PSA
Formerly: PSA Peugeot Citroën (1991–2016)
Founded: 1976 (as PSA)
Defunct: January 16, 2021 (merged into Stellantis)
Headquarters: Rueil-Malmaison, France
Brands: Peugeot, Citroën, DS, Opel, Vauxhall

Groupe PSA (known legally as Peugeot S.A.) was a French multinational automotive manufacturing company which produced automobiles and motorcycles under the Peugeot, Citroën, DS, Opel and Vauxhall brands. On January 18, 2016, PSA Peugeot Citroën announced its new corporate identity became Groupe PSA, effective April 5, 2016.

Peugeot was the largest PSA brand. PSA was listed on the Euronext Paris stock exchange and was a constituent of the CAC 40 index. Beginning in 2016, PSA began to outline a strategy which entailed the expansion of the company, through both geographic expansion and acquisitions of other car companies. PSA announced plans to enter the Indian, American, Canadian, Southeast Asian, and other markets in the coming years.

History

In December 1974 Peugeot S.A. acquired a 38.2% share of Citroën. In May 1976 they increased their stake of the then bankrupt company to 89.95%, thus creating the PSA Group (where PSA is short for Peugeot Société Anonyme). In 1978 the group purchased the European operations of Chrysler, which became Talbot. In March 2017, PSA agreed to buy Opel and Vauxhall from General Motors for €2.2 billion, making it Europe's second-largest automaker behind Volkswagen.

Merger into Stellantis

On October 31, 2019, Groupe PSA announced its intention to merge with Fiat Chrysler Automobiles. The merger was completed on January 16, 2021, creating Stellantis, one of the world's largest automakers, with Carlos Tavares — previously PSA's chairman — as its first CEO.
