Internet Archive

The Internet Archive is an American digital library founded on May 10, 1996, and chaired by free information advocate Brewster Kahle. It provides free access to collections of digitized materials including websites, software applications, music, audiovisual and print materials, and its collection surpassed 99 petabytes of data in 2024.

In addition to its archiving function, the Internet Archive is an activist organization advocating a free and open Internet. Its web archive, the Wayback Machine, contains hundreds of billions of web captures. The Archive also oversees numerous book digitization projects, collectively one of the world's largest book digitization efforts.

History

Brewster Kahle founded the Archive in 1996 at the same time as he started the for-profit web crawling company Alexa Internet. The earliest known archived page was saved on October 10, 1996. By 1999 the collection included the Prelinger Archives. In late 1999 the Archive expanded into a general collection with the opening of dedicated media archives.

In 2006 the Archive created the Wayback Machine's "Save Page Now" tool. In 2013 the organization moved into the former Fourth Church of Christ, Scientist building at 300 Funston Avenue in San Francisco's Richmond District, which serves as its headquarters.

Projects

The Archive hosts numerous projects, including the Wayback Machine, Open Library, the Software Library, the Moving Image and Audio archives, the NASA Images collection, and the Live Music Archive. Open Library aims to create a web page for every book ever published, and by 2023 held records for over 40 million books.

Legal issues

The Archive's book lending program, the National Emergency Library, launched during the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020, drew a lawsuit from major publishers. In Hachette v. Internet Archive, a federal court ruled in 2023 that the Archive's scanning and lending of books was not fair use, and the Archive subsequently removed hundreds of thousands of titles from its lending program.
