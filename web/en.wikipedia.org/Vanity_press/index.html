Vanity press

A vanity press, or vanity publisher, is a publishing house in which authors pay to have their books published, in contrast to traditional commercial publishing, in which the publisher bears the cost and risk and pays the author. Because the vanity publisher's profit comes from the author rather than from book sales, it has little incentive to select manuscripts for quality, edit them, or promote them effectively.

Characteristics

Vanity presses typically charge fees for editing, design, printing, and marketing packages, while offering low or no advances and minimal distribution. The term derives from the assumption that authors publish through such houses out of vanity. Some vanity publishers have been criticised for misleading marketing that disguises the arrangement as conventional acceptance of a manuscript.

The model is distinct from legitimate self-publishing, in which the author retains control and hires services transparently, and from academic subsidy publishing. Writers' organisations advise authors to ensure that money should normally flow toward the author in commercial publishing ("Yog's Law").
