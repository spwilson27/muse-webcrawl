Motocross des Nations

The Motocross des Nations (Motocross of Nations) is an annual team motocross race, where riders representing their country meet at what is billed as the "Olympics of Motocross". The event has been staged since 1947, where the team of Bill Nicholson, Fred Rist and Bob Ray, representing the United Kingdom, rode BSA motorcycles.

Format

The event is held over a weekend at a different venue each year. Each national team fields three riders, one in each of three classes (MXGP, MX2 and Open). Three races ("motos") are held, each combining two classes, and team scores are determined by adding the five best finishes from the six race results, with each rider's worst result dropped. The country with the lowest total wins the Chamberlain Trophy.

History

The first event was held in 1947 at Wassenaar in the Netherlands. The United States holds the record for the most victories, achieved largely during a dominant run from 1981 onwards, while Belgium, Great Britain and France have also been multiple winners. France won several consecutive editions in the mid-2010s. The event draws large crowds and is regarded as the most prestigious team competition in the sport.
