Sarnath (also Isipatana, "the place where holy men fall/land") is a place located about 10 km northeast of Varanasi in Uttar Pradesh, India. It is one of the most important sites in Buddhism: in the deer park here, Gautama Buddha first taught the Dharma after his enlightenment, delivering the Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta — the "Setting in Motion of the Wheel of Dhamma" — to his five former companions, and founding the Sangha.

History

The deer park, Migadaya, was an ancient sanctuary where deer could roam unharmed. After the sermon, Sarnath became a major monastic centre with dozens of monasteries. The Mauryan emperor Ashoka in the 3rd century BC erected a polished sandstone pillar here, crowned by the Lion Capital — now the national emblem of India — and built the original Dhamek Stupa, enlarged in the Gupta period to its present massive cylindrical form. Chinese pilgrims Faxian and Xuanzang described a flourishing community. The site declined after 12th-century invasions and was excavated in the 19th and early 20th centuries.

Today

Modern Sarnath hosts the Mulagandha Kuti Vihara (1931) with frescoes by Kosetsu Nosu, temples built by many Buddhist nations, an Archaeological Museum housing the Lion Capital, and the great white Chaukhandi Stupa. It remains a focus of pilgrimage and of the revival of Buddhism in India associated with the Maha Bodhi Society.
