IAI Kfir

The Israel Aircraft Industries Kfir (Hebrew: כפיר, "Lion Cub") is an Israeli all-weather multirole combat aircraft based on the French Dassault Mirage 5, with Israeli avionics and an Israeli-built version of the General Electric J79 turbojet engine. It entered service with the Israeli Air Force in 1975.

Development

After France embargoed the sale of Mirage 5 aircraft to Israel in 1967, Israel developed the IAI Nesher from acquired plans, and then re-engined the design with the J79 to create the Kfir, adding canard foreplanes in later variants to improve manoeuvrability and short-field performance. Variants included the Kfir C.2, TC.2 trainer, and the upgraded C.7.

Service and operators

The Kfir first saw combat in 1977 and was used extensively in the 1982 Lebanon War. Israel retired the type in the 1990s, but upgraded Kfirs were exported to Colombia, Ecuador and Sri Lanka, and a small number were leased to the United States Navy and Marine Corps as aggressor aircraft under the designation F-21A. Private aggressor companies have also operated the type.
