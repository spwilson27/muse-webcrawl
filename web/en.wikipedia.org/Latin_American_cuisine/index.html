Latin American cuisine

Latin American cuisine is the food and culinary traditions of the many countries of Latin America, a cuisine shaped by the fusion of Indigenous American ingredients and cooking techniques with the influences of European colonizers (chiefly Spanish and Portuguese), enslaved and immigrant Africans, and later arrivals from Europe, the Middle East and Asia. Its defining native ingredients include maize (corn), beans, potatoes, tomatoes, chili peppers, avocado, quinoa, cacao and cassava; the Columbian exchange carried these foods worldwide while introducing rice, wheat, citrus, sugarcane, coffee and livestock to the Americas.

Mexico and Central America

Mexican cuisine — inscribed by UNESCO on its list of Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2010 — centers on the corn tortilla and an immense repertoire of dishes: tacos, tamales, enchiladas, pozole and complex moles, alongside a greater variety of chili peppers than anywhere else in the world. Central American cooking shares the corn-and-beans foundation, expressed in Guatemalan pepián, Salvadoran pupusas, Nicaraguan gallo pinto and Honduran baleadas.

The Caribbean

Cuban, Puerto Rican and Dominican cuisines combine Spanish, African and Taíno roots: rice and beans, roast pork, fried plantains (tostones and mofongo), sofrito seasoning bases and dishes such as Cuba's ropa vieja. Root vegetables such as yuca and malanga are staples across the islands.

South America

Peruvian cuisine is internationally celebrated for ceviche, lomo saltado (a Chinese-influenced stir-fry exemplifying chifa), anticuchos, and hundreds of native potato varieties, as well as Nikkei (Japanese-Peruvian) cooking. Venezuela and Colombia are known for arepas and empanadas; Brazil for feijoada (black bean and pork stew), churrasco barbecue, moqueca and pão de queijo. Argentina, Uruguay and Paraguay center on asado (grilled beef), chimichurri, yerba mate and empanadas, with strong Italian influence in pasta and pizza. Chilean and Andean cuisines feature corn, squash, quinoa and seafood.

Drinks and desserts

Characteristic drinks include coffee (Colombia and Brazil are major producers), mate in the Southern Cone, Mexican aguas frescas and horchata, the pisco sour (claimed by both Peru and Chile), caipirinha in Brazil, and rum-based cocktails in the Caribbean. Desserts include tres leches cake, flan, dulce de leche, churros and alfajores.

Global spread

Latin American restaurants and street food — especially Mexican — have spread worldwide, and dishes such as the taco, burrito and ceviche have been widely adapted abroad, sometimes controversially, as in Tex-Mex cuisine in the United States.
