Harry Kupfer (August 12, 1935 – December 30, 2019) was a German opera director, one of the leading figures of post-war Regietheater. A pupil of Walter Felsenstein, he staged more than 175 productions worldwide, and his two-decade tenure at the Komische Oper Berlin and his Wagner productions at the Bayreuth Festival defined an era of psychologically penetrating, ensemble-centred opera direction.

Career

Kupfer studied theatre in Leipzig and began directing in Halle, Stralsund, Weimar and Karl-Marx-Stadt before becoming opera director in Weimar (1966–1972). In 1972 he became chief director of the Staatsoper Dresden, where his productions of Mozart, Strauss and contemporary works drew international attention. He made his Bayreuth debut in 1978 with a starkly anti-romantic Der fliegende Holländer.

Komische Oper Berlin

From 1981 to 2002 Kupfer was chief director of the Komische Oper Berlin, succeeding his teacher Felsenstein as the house's presiding spirit. There he developed his method of "realistic music theatre", rehearsing singer-actors intensively so that every gesture grew out of the score. Landmark Berlin productions included Die Meistersinger von Nürnberg, Boris Godunov, the Mozart Da Ponte operas and the world premiere of Siegfried Matthus's Judith (1985).

Bayreuth and beyond

At Bayreuth in 1988, Kupfer and conductor Daniel Barenboim presented a celebrated Ring des Nibelungen that read Wagner's cycle as a parable of industrial destruction, its second act of Die Walküre staged along a "road of history" littered with ruins. He staged operas in Vienna, Munich, Zurich, London, Copenhagen, San Francisco, Sydney and Tokyo, taught directing in Berlin, and continued working into his eighties; his production of Handel's Poro opened at the Komische Oper in 2019, the year of his death.

Style and honours

Kupfer's stagings combined textual fidelity to the score with unflinching contemporary readings, treating opera as theatre of ideas enacted by singing actors. He received numerous honours, including the National Prize of the GDR, the Order of Merit of the Federal Republic of Germany and honorary membership of the Komische Oper. He was married to the soprano and voice teacher Marianne Kupfer.
