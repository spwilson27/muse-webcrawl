Golden goal

SportsAssociation football, ice hockey, field hockey, lacrosse and others
EffectFirst goal in extra time ends the match immediately
In footballIntroduced 1993; abolished by IFAB in 2004

The golden goal is a rule used in several association football and other sports competitions in which, during extra time of a tied knockout match, the first goal scored immediately ends the game and gives victory to the scoring team. It is a form of sudden-death play.

In association football

FIFA introduced the golden goal in 1993, hoping to encourage attacking play in extra time and reduce the number of matches decided by penalty shoot-outs. In practice, teams often played more cautiously for fear of conceding. Famous golden goals decided major finals: Oliver Bierhoff's winner for Germany in the Euro 1996 final against the Czech Republic, and David Trezeguet's for France against Italy in the Euro 2000 final. A related variant, the silver goal — under which the team leading at half-time of extra time won — was used briefly, including at Euro 2004.

Abolition

The International Football Association Board removed both the golden and silver goal from the Laws of the Game in 2004, after which drawn knockout matches reverted to a full period of extra time followed, if necessary, by penalties.

Other sports

Sudden-death overtime goals remain standard in ice hockey playoffs (including the NHL) and are used in field hockey, lacrosse, and rugby league, where a golden point variant applies.
