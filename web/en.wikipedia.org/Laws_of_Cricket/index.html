Laws of Cricket

The Laws of Cricket are a code of rules that govern the game of cricket. First codified in 1744, they are now owned and maintained by the Marylebone Cricket Club (MCC), which is based at Lord's Cricket Ground in London. The Laws are divided into numbered sections covering the players, the match, the equipment and playing area, the innings, methods of dismissal, and the conduct of the game.

The Laws

The current edition of the Laws contains 42 laws. Law 1 deals with the players, Law 2 with substitutes and runners, and Law 5 covers the ball. Law 6 specifies the dimensions of the bat, and Law 7 the creases. Law 21 covers "no ball", Law 22 the wide ball, and Law 24 the over. The methods of dismissal are set out in Laws 28 to 39, including bowled, caught, leg before wicket (lbw), run out and stumped. Law 41 concerns unfair play and Law 42 the players' conduct.

History

The earliest known code of laws for cricket was drawn up in 1744, though cricket had been played for well over a century before that. The MCC, founded in 1787, assumed responsibility for the Laws shortly afterwards. Major revisions followed in 1947, 1980, 2000 and 2017. The 2017 Code, which came into force on 1 October 2017, replaced the separate Laws and the then-ICC Standard Playing Conditions for international matches in part, introduced a red-card system for player misconduct, removed the "handled the ball" dismissal by folding it into "obstructing the field", and removed the restriction on bat thickness in Law 5.
