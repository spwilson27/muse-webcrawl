Naloxone

Naloxone, sold under the brand name Narcan among others, is a medication used to reverse or reduce the effects of opioids, particularly life-threatening respiratory depression in opioid overdose. It is an opioid antagonist that works by displacing opioids from their receptors in the brain.

Medical use

Naloxone is given by injection into a muscle, under the skin, into a vein, or as a nasal spray. Effects begin within two to five minutes and last roughly 30 to 90 minutes, so repeat doses may be needed for long-acting opioids. In people dependent on opioids it can precipitate acute withdrawal. It has no effect on overdoses from non-opioid drugs and no potential for abuse itself.

History and access

First synthesised in 1961 and approved by the US Food and Drug Administration in 1971, naloxone is on the WHO List of Essential Medicines. In response to the opioid epidemic, distribution of naloxone kits to first responders and laypeople has expanded widely, and in 2023 the FDA approved over-the-counter sale of the nasal spray in the United States.
