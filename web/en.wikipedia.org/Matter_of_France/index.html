Matter of France

The Matter of France, also known as the Carolingian cycle, is a body of literature and legendary material associated with the history of France, in particular involving Charlemagne and his associates. It is one of the three great cycles of medieval Western narrative, alongside the Matter of Britain (Arthurian legend) and the Matter of Rome (stories of classical antiquity).

Content

The cycle comprises the Old French chansons de geste, epic poems celebrating the deeds of Charlemagne and his paladins — Roland, Oliver, Ogier the Dane, and Archbishop Turpin — in wars against Saracen foes in Spain and Italy. The most famous work is The Song of Roland (La Chanson de Roland), recounting the hero's death at the Battle of Roncevaux Pass in 778. Other poems deal with William of Orange, the rebellious barons, and the hero's youth and family.

Themes and influence

The poems emphasize loyalty, feudal duty, Christian–Muslim conflict, and heroic sacrifice. The material was retold across Europe, feeding into Italian Renaissance epics such as Boiardo's Orlando Innamorato and Ariosto's Orlando Furioso, and survives in the French national literary canon.
