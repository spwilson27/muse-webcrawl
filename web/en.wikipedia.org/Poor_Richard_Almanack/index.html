Poor Richard's Almanack (sometimes Almanac) was an annual almanac published by Benjamin Franklin from 1732 to 1758. Franklin wrote under the pseudonym of "Richard Saunders", a fictitious astrologer, with editorial asides from Saunders's equally fictitious wife Bridget. The almanack sold as many as 10,000 copies a year — an enormous circulation for the colonial press — and secured Franklin's fortune.

Contents

Like other almanacs of the period it contained calendars, weather predictions, astronomical data (sunrise, tides, eclipses), court days, road distances and practical advice. What set it apart was Franklin's wit: the margins and prefaces were filled with proverbs, aphorisms and humorous pieces celebrating thrift, industry and prudence — "Early to bed and early to rise, makes a man healthy, wealthy, and wise"; "A penny saved is a penny earned" (in Franklin's version, "a penny saved is two pence clear").

Father Abraham's speech

The preface to the 1758 edition, later published separately as The Way to Wealth, gathers dozens of the almanack's sayings into a speech by the character Father Abraham. It was reprinted hundreds of times, translated into many languages, and became one of the most popular works of the era.

Hoaxes and legacy

Franklin used the almanack for jokes at rivals' expense, most famously "predicting" the death of competing almanac-maker Titan Leeds and continuing the gag for years after Leeds protested that he was still alive. Poor Richard's maxims passed into the American language, and the Almanack remains a foundational document of early American print culture and of Franklin's public persona.
