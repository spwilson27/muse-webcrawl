Lewisian complex

The Lewisian complex or Lewisian gneiss is a suite of Precambrian metamorphic rocks that outcrop in the northwestern part of Scotland, forming part of the Hebridean Terrane and the North Atlantic Craton. These rocks are among the oldest in Europe, formed up to 3 billion years ago.

Geology

The complex is composed mainly of gneisses — tonalitic-trondhjemitic-granodioritic (TTG) gneisses with subordinate amphibolites and ultramafic rocks — that have experienced multiple episodes of deformation and high-grade metamorphism. Radiometric dating shows protolith ages from about 3.1 to 2.7 billion years (Archaean), reworked in Palaeoproterozoic events. The rocks are divided into assemblages including the Scourian, Inverian, and Laxfordian, recording successive orogenic episodes.

Distribution

Outcrops occur on the Scottish mainland from Cape Wrath to Loch Torridon, and throughout the Outer Hebrides — the "Islands" from which the complex takes its name (the Isle of Lewis). The complex forms the basement beneath the Torridonian sandstone and was thrust over younger rocks during the Caledonian orogeny in the Moine Thrust Belt region.

Significance

Because of their great age and complex history, the Lewisian rocks are a classic area for studying the early evolution of continental crust and Archaean tectonics, and have been a training ground for generations of geologists.
