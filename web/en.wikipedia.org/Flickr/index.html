Flickr

Flickr is an image hosting and video hosting service, as well as an online community, founded in Canada and headquartered in the United States. It was created by Ludicorp in 2004 and was a popular way for amateur and professional photographers to store, organize, and share digital photographs. In addition to being a repository for personal photos, the service is widely used by photo researchers and by bloggers to host images embedded in blogs and social media.

History

Flickr was launched on February 10, 2004 by Ludicorp, a Vancouver-based company founded by Stewart Butterfield and Caterina Fake. The service emerged from tools originally developed for Ludicorp's Game Neverending, a web-based massively multiplayer online game. Flickr proved far more successful than the game, which was eventually shelved. Early versions of the Flickr chat client, FlickrLive, featured live photo exchange.

In March 2005, Yahoo acquired Ludicorp and Flickr. During the week of June 28, 2005, all content was migrated from servers in Canada to servers in the United States. Flickr was one of the pioneering examples of the "Web 2.0" era and an early adopter of tagging, allowing users to categorize photographs with keywords that made them searchable.

In April 2018, SmugMug acquired Flickr from Verizon's Oath subsidiary (which had acquired Yahoo). Under SmugMug's ownership, changes were made to the free tier, limiting free accounts to 1,000 photos from 2019.

Features

Users can upload photographs and short videos, organize them into albums and collections, and annotate them with tags, titles, descriptions and geographic locations (geotagging). Flickr supports licensing options including Creative Commons licences, and its Commons program hosts public photograph collections from libraries, archives and museums around the world, including the Library of Congress. The site offers both free and paid "Pro" accounts; Pro subscribers receive unlimited storage and other benefits.
