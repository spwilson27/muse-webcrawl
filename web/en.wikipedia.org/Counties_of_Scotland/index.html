Counties of Scotland

The counties of Scotland (Siorrachdan na h-Alba) are historic subdivisions of Scotland, established in the Middle Ages and used for local government until 1975. There are 33 traditional counties, from Shetland and Orkney in the north to Wigtownshire and Kirkcudbrightshire in the south.

Counties originated as sheriffdoms — areas under the jurisdiction of a sheriff — and gradually acquired administrative functions through commissioners of supply, justices of the peace, and, from 1890, elected county councils under the Local Government (Scotland) Act 1889. That Act tidied many boundary anomalies, merging Ross-shire and Cromartyshire into Ross and Cromarty.

Abolition and modern use

The counties were abolished as administrative areas in 1975 by the Local Government (Scotland) Act 1973, which introduced regions and districts, and were succeeded in 1996 by the present 32 council areas. Historic counties remain in use for land registration (as registration counties), as lieutenancy areas in groups, and for cultural and sporting identity.
