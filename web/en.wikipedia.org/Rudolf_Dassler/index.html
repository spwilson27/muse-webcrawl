Rudolf "Rudi" Dassler (26 March 1898 – 27 October 1974) was a German cobbler and businessman. He was the founder of the sportswear company Puma. Rudolf was the brother of Adolf "Adi" Dassler, the founder of Adidas; the two brothers jointly founded the Dassler Brothers Shoe Factory in the 1920s before a bitter family feud led them to split in 1948.

Life

Dassler was born in Herzogenaurach, Bavaria, the son of a shoemaker. After serving in the First World War and working in the porcelain industry and leather wholesale trade, he joined his younger brother Adolf's shoe business in 1923. Together they founded the Gebrüder Dassler Schuhfabrik (Dassler Brothers Shoe Factory) in 1924. Their shoes gained fame when athletes wearing them won medals at the Olympic Games, most notably when Jesse Owens won four gold medals at the 1936 Berlin Olympics in Dassler shoes.

During the Second World War both brothers were members of the Nazi Party, and tensions between them and their wives grew. Rudolf was drafted and later arrested by American forces on suspicion of having worked for the SS; Adolf was also investigated. In 1948 the brothers dissolved their partnership and divided the company's assets. Rudolf moved to the other side of the Aurach river and founded his own firm, first called "Ruda" — from the first two letters of his name — before renaming it Puma later the same year. Adolf founded Adidas in 1949.

Rivalry and death

The split created one of business history's most famous feuds: Herzogenaurach became known as "the town of bent necks", where residents looked down at a person's shoes to determine which camp they belonged to. Adidas and Puma employees were divided by loyalty, and the brothers never reconciled. Rudolf Dassler died of lung cancer in 1974; his sons Armin and Gerd carried on the company. He is buried in Herzogenaurach, in the same cemetery as his brother but at the farthest possible distance.
