Street circuit

A street circuit is a motor racing circuit composed of temporarily closed-off public roads of a city, town, or village, used in motor races such as Formula One, Formula E, IndyCar, and touring car series. Facilities such as the paddock, pit boxes, fences, and grandstands are erected temporarily and removed soon after the race. Run-off areas are usually minimal, with concrete walls lining much of the track.

Characteristics

Street circuits tend to be bumpy, narrow, and low-grip compared with purpose-built tracks, with little room for error and frequent safety car periods. Overtaking is often difficult. Because they are assembled for a single weekend, the surface evolves rapidly as rubber is laid down.

Notable circuits

The Monaco Grand Prix circuit, used since 1929, is the most famous street circuit. Others include the Marina Bay Street Circuit in Singapore (Formula One's first night race), the Baku City Circuit, the Circuit Gilles Villeneuve (a semi-street layout), Long Beach, St. Petersburg, and the Adelaide Street Circuit. Formula E races almost exclusively on street circuits in city centres. See also Monaco Grand Prix, race track, and Formula One.
