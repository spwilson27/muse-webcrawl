Dick, Kerr Ladies F.C.

Dick, Kerr Ladies Football Club was an English women's association football team formed in Preston, Lancashire, in 1917 by women working at the Dick, Kerr & Co. munitions factory during the First World War. The team was one of the earliest and most famous women's football clubs.

History

The team began playing charity matches to raise money for wounded soldiers. On Boxing Day 1920, their match against St Helens Ladies at Goodison Park attracted a crowd of 53,000 spectators, with thousands more locked out — a record for women's football that stood for decades.

In December 1921 the Football Association banned women's football from its members' grounds, declaring the game "quite unsuitable for females". The ban, which lasted fifty years, forced teams such as Dick, Kerr Ladies to play on non-league grounds. The club, later known as Preston Ladies, continued playing until 1965.

Legacy

Star players included Lily Parr, one of the most celebrated early women's footballers. The team played over 800 matches, losing very few, and raised large sums for charity. They are commemorated by a memorial at Preston and were inducted into the English Football Hall of Fame's recognition of women's football pioneers.
