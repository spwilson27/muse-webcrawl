Polytrauma

Polytrauma (or multiple trauma) is a medical term describing a condition in which a person has sustained multiple traumatic injuries, at least one or a combination of which is life-threatening. A widely used definition requires injuries in at least two body regions with an Injury Severity Score (ISS) greater than 15 or 16. Polytrauma is a leading cause of death and long-term disability in young adults, most often resulting from road traffic collisions, falls from height, and, in military settings, blast injuries.

Polytrauma care requires coordinated trauma-center teams.

Pathophysiology

Beyond the direct injuries, polytrauma triggers a systemic response: hemorrhage and shock, the "lethal triad" of hypothermia, acidosis, and coagulopathy, and a dysregulated inflammatory response that can progress to systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS), acute respiratory distress syndrome, and multiple organ dysfunction syndrome. A "second hit" from surgery or infection can worsen this response.

Management

Modern management follows Advanced Trauma Life Support (ATLS) principles — airway, breathing, circulation — within trauma systems and designated trauma centers. Key strategies include damage control resuscitation (balanced blood-product transfusion, permissive hypotension, and correction of coagulopathy, often guided by viscoelastic tests) and damage control surgery, in which definitive repair is deferred in favor of rapid hemorrhage and contamination control, followed by staged reconstruction ("early appropriate care").

Military usage

The term gained prominence through the wars in Iraq and Afghanistan, where the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs established Polytrauma System of Care centers to treat service members with combinations of traumatic brain injury, amputation, burns, and psychological trauma.
