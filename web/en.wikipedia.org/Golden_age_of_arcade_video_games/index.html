Golden age of arcade video games

The golden age of arcade video games was a period of rapid growth, creative innovation and cultural influence for arcade games, generally placed from the late 1970s to the mid-1980s. It began with the release of Space Invaders in 1978 and reached its peak around 1981–1982 before the video game crash of 1983.

History

Taito's Space Invaders (1978) was a phenomenon that caused a temporary shortage of 100-yen coins in Japan. It was followed by Atari's Asteroids (1979), and in 1980 Namco's Pac-Man — the highest-grossing and most iconic arcade game of the era — and Defender. Donkey Kong (1981) introduced Mario, and 1982 brought Ms. Pac-Man, Dig Dug and Pole Position. At its height, the North American arcade industry earned billions of dollars in quarters annually, rivalling the film and popular music industries.

Technology and design

The era saw the transition from discrete-logic games to microprocessor-based systems with colour raster graphics, and innovations such as high-score tables, continues, and increasingly sophisticated sound. Vector-display games such as Tempest (1981) offered an alternative aesthetic. Arcades became social spaces for young people, and competitive high-score play produced early gaming celebrities and tournaments.

Decline

The arcade boom faded with the video game crash of 1983 and the growing capability of home consoles and computers. Arcades revived partially in the early 1990s with fighting games such as Street Fighter II, but never regained their golden-age centrality.
