Inca Trail to Machu Picchu

The Inca Trail to Machu Picchu (also called the Camino Inca or Camino Inka) is a hiking trail in Peru leading to the Inca citadel of Machu Picchu. It follows paved royal roads built by the Inca Empire as part of the vast Qhapaq Ñan road network connecting Cusco with the royal estate at Machu Picchu.

The classic trek

The classic four-day route covers about 42 kilometres (26 miles) from the trailhead near Ollantaytambo in the Sacred Valley, crossing the Dead Woman's Pass (Warmiwañusqa) at 4,215 metres — the route's highest point — and passing Inca sites including Runkurakay, Sayacmarca, Phuyupatamarca, and Wiñay Wayna. Trekkers enter Machu Picchu at dawn through Inti Punku, the Sun Gate.

Regulation

To protect the trail, Peru limits access to 500 permits per day (including guides and porters), requires trekkers to accompany licensed guides, and closes the trail each February for maintenance. The route was brought to wide attention after Hiram Bingham's explorations of the region beginning in 1911.
