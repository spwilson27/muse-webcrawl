Ernesto "Che" Guevara (14 June 1928 – 9 October 1967) was an Argentine Marxist revolutionary, physician, author, guerrilla leader and diplomat. A major figure of the Cuban Revolution, his stylised visage — Alberto Korda's 1960 photograph Guerrillero Heroico — became a ubiquitous countercultural symbol of rebellion.

Early life and travels

Guevara studied medicine at the University of Buenos Aires. Motorcycle journeys across South America, recorded in The Motorcycle Diaries, exposed him to poverty and radicalised him. In Guatemala he witnessed the 1954 CIA-backed overthrow of President Jacobo Árbenz, confirming his view that revolution required armed struggle.

Cuban Revolution and government

Joining Fidel Castro's 26th of July Movement, Guevara sailed on the Granma in 1956 and rose to comandante, leading a decisive column at the Battle of Santa Clara in 1958. After the revolution he directed revolutionary tribunals, served as Minister of Industries and president of the National Bank, and promoted his theory of the "new man" and foco guerrilla strategy in writings such as Guerrilla Warfare (1961).

Congo, Bolivia and death

Frustrated with Cuba's alignment with the Soviet Union, Guevara left in 1965 to foment revolution abroad, first in Congo, then Bolivia. There his small guerrilla band was hunted down; he was captured and executed by the Bolivian army near La Higuera on 9 October 1967. His remains, located in 1997, were reburied in Santa Clara, Cuba. Assessments of his legacy range from heroic liberator to ruthless ideologue.
