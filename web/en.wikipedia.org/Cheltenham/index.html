Overview

Cheltenham is a spa town and borough in Gloucestershire, England, on the edge of the Cotswolds. It is noted for its Regency architecture, its mineral springs — discovered in 1716, when pigeons were observed flocking to a salt spring — and for the Cheltenham Festival of National Hunt horse racing, which culminates in the Cheltenham Gold Cup each March.

History

Cheltenham appears in the Domesday Book of 1086 as Celteham. After the discovery of its spa waters, and especially following a visit by King George III in 1788, it grew into a fashionable resort; the Pittville Pump Room (1825–30) is the grandest survival of the era. Tree-lined Promenade and the terraces of Montpellier, Lansdown and Pittville display the town's Regency and early Victorian development. The town became a popular retirement home for officers of the British Indian Army.

Culture and education

Cheltenham hosts major festivals of literature, music, jazz and science. It is home to Cheltenham Ladies' College (founded 1854) and Cheltenham College, and to the headquarters of GCHQ, the British signals intelligence agency, whose circular "Doughnut" building stands in Benhall. The composer Gustav Holst was born here in 1874; his birthplace is now a museum.
