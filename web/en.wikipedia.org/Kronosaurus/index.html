Kronosaurus

Kronosaurus
Period: Early Cretaceous, ~120–100 million years ago
Length: about 9–10.5 m
Classification: Pliosaurid plesiosaur

Kronosaurus is an extinct genus of large pliosaurid plesiosaur that lived in the Early Cretaceous seas covering parts of present-day Australia and Colombia. Named in 1924 after the titan Kronos, it was among the largest marine reptiles of its time.

Description

Kronosaurus had a large, robust skull up to about 2.5 metres long with conical teeth, a short neck, a streamlined body and four powerful flippers. Early estimates exaggerated its size; modern studies put large specimens at roughly 9–10.5 metres and over 10 tonnes. It was an apex predator of fish, turtles, ichthyosaurs and other marine reptiles and ammonites.

Discovery

Fossils come from the Toolebuc and related formations of Queensland and New South Wales, and from Colombia's Paja Formation (Kronosaurus boyacensis, sometimes assigned to Monquirasaurus). A famous Harvard specimen was reconstructed with extra vertebrae, inflating its original reported length.
