John Erskine of Carnock

Born: 4 November 1695
Died: 1 March 1768 (aged 72)
Nationality: Scottish
Occupation: Jurist, professor of law
Known for: Institute of the Law of Scotland; Principles of the Law of Scotland

John Erskine of Carnock (1695–1768) was a Scottish jurist and professor of law, remembered as one of the great institutional writers of Scots law. His two principal works, the Principles of the Law of Scotland (1754) and the posthumously published An Institute of the Law of Scotland (1773), remain standard reference works cited in Scottish courts to this day.

Life

Erskine was the son of Colonel John Erskine of Carnock (1662–1743), deputy governor of Stirling Castle, and a descendant of the Earls of Mar. He was admitted as an advocate in 1719 and built a successful legal practice in Edinburgh. In 1737 he was appointed Professor of Scots Law at the University of Edinburgh, a chair he held until 1765, lecturing to generations of Scottish lawyers.

Works

Erskine's Principles of the Law of Scotland was written as a textbook for his students and arranged in a clear, didactic style. His larger work, the Institute of the Law of Scotland, was modelled on the earlier institutional writings of Stair and Mackenzie of Rosehaugh, but Erskine aimed for a comprehensive and systematic statement of the whole of Scots private law as it stood in the mid-eighteenth century. The work was completed shortly before his death and published in 1773. Numerous later editions, with notes by editors bringing the text up to date, kept Erskine's Institute in continuous use, and passages from it are still treated by Scottish courts as authoritative statements of the common law.

Legacy

Together with Stair's Institutions, Erskine's Institute ranks as one of the sources from which the modern Scots law derives. He is commemorated as part of the tradition of institutional writers whose works possess a quasi-authoritative status in Scotland's mixed legal system.
