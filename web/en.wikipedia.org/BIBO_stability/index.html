BIBO stability

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In signal processing and control theory, a system is bounded-input, bounded-output (BIBO) stable if every bounded input produces a bounded output. BIBO stability is a practical notion of stability for linear time-invariant (LTI) systems and filters, ensuring that no admissible signal can drive the output to grow without limit.

FieldControl theory, signal processing
Applies toLinear time-invariant systems

Conditions

For a continuous-time LTI system, BIBO stability holds if and only if the impulse response is absolutely integrable. For a discrete-time system, the condition is that the impulse response be absolutely summable. In transfer-function terms, given a reduced (pole-zero cancellations removed) rational transfer function, BIBO stability is equivalent to all poles lying strictly in the left half of the complex plane (continuous time) or strictly inside the unit circle (discrete time) — that is, all poles having negative real parts or magnitudes below one.

Remarks

BIBO stability is weaker than internal (asymptotic) stability of a state-space realization, since unobservable or uncontrollable unstable modes can be hidden by pole-zero cancellation; in state-space terms, a system can be BIBO stable while not asymptotically stable. The concept guides the design of digital filters, where BIBO stability guarantees that rounding errors and finite inputs cannot send the filter output diverging — the practical failure mode seen in unstable recursive (IIR) designs.
