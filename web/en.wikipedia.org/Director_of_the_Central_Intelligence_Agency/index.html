Director of the Central Intelligence Agency

The director of the Central Intelligence Agency (D/CIA) is a statutory office that serves as the head of the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA), one of the principal civilian foreign intelligence agencies of the United States federal government. The director reports to the Director of National Intelligence (DNI).

History

Before 2005, the analogous office was the Director of Central Intelligence (DCI), who served both as head of the CIA and as head of the entire U.S. Intelligence Community. The Intelligence Reform and Terrorism Prevention Act of 2004 split the role, creating the DNI to oversee the community and the D/CIA to run the CIA alone. The first director of central intelligence, in 1946, was Sidney Souers; Allen Dulles, the longest-serving, held the office from 1953 to 1961.

Appointment and duties

The director is nominated by the President of the United States and confirmed by the Senate. The director manages the CIA's operations, budget and personnel, oversees the collection and analysis of foreign intelligence and covert action, and serves as a principal intelligence adviser to the President and the National Security Council.

Order of succession

The deputy director of the CIA is the agency's second-in-command and acts as director in the director's absence.
