Saṃsāra

Saṃsāra (Sanskrit: संसार; Pali: saṃsāra) is a central concept in Indian religions — Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism and Sikhism — denoting the endless cycle of birth, death and rebirth in which beings are bound by karma and ignorance. The word literally connotes "wandering" or "flowing on".

In Hinduism

In Hindu thought, the individual self (ātman) transmigrates through successive lives according to karma until liberation (mokṣa) is attained, ending the cycle.

In Buddhism

Buddhism teaches that saṃsāra is characterised by suffering (duḥkha) and is perpetuated by craving and ignorance; beings are reborn among six realms of existence. Liberation (nirvāṇa) is reached by following the Noble Eightfold Path. Unlike Hinduism, Buddhism denies a permanent soul transmigrating, teaching rebirth without an unchanging self (anattā).

In Jainism

Jainism holds that karmic matter binds the soul and keeps it cycling through four states of existence; liberation comes through austerity and non-violence.
