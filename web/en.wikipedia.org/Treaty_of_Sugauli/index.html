Treaty of Sugauli

Signed2 December 1815
Ratified4 March 1816
LocationSugauli, India
PartiesEast India Company; Kingdom of Nepal

The Treaty of Sugauli (also spelled Segowlee), signed on 2 December 1815 and ratified on 4 March 1816, was the peace treaty that ended the Anglo-Nepalese War (1814–1816) between the East India Company and the Kingdom of Nepal. It was signed by Lieutenant Colonel Paris Bradshaw for the Company and Chandra Sekher Upadhyaya for Nepal.

Background

The war had broken out over border disputes in the Terai and Nepalese expansion into the hill states. Despite determined resistance by Nepalese forces under commanders such as Amar Singh Thapa and Balbhadra Kunwar, British forces under Major General David Ochterlony broke through in the west and advanced toward the Kathmandu Valley, compelling Nepal to negotiate.

Terms

Under the treaty, Nepal lost roughly one-third of its territory:

  • The whole region west of the Kali River — Kumaon, Garhwal, and parts of the Sutlej basin — was ceded to the British.
  • Most of the Terai lowlands between the Koshi and Rapti rivers went to the Company, and the Mechi River became Nepal's eastern boundary.
  • Nepal renounced all claims to the territories of the Raja of Sikkim that it had seized.
  • A British resident was to be stationed at Kathmandu.
  • Nepal agreed not to employ Europeans or Americans without British consent.

Some Terai lands were later partially returned in 1860 in recognition of Nepalese help during the Indian Rebellion of 1857.

Legacy

The treaty fixed the broad outline of Nepal's modern borders, though the exact course of the western boundary along the Kali (Mahakali) River remains a source of dispute between Nepal and India, notably over Kalapani and Susta.
