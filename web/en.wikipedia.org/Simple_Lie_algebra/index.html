Simple Lie algebra

In mathematics, a simple Lie algebra is a non-abelian Lie algebra whose only ideals are the trivial ones (zero and itself). Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, the finite-dimensional simple Lie algebras were completely classified by Wilhelm Killing and Élie Cartan into four infinite families and five exceptional types.

Classification

Every finite-dimensional semisimple Lie algebra is a direct sum of simple ones. The classification is encoded in Dynkin diagrams: the classical families correspond to special linear (A), orthogonal (B, D), and symplectic (C) algebras, while the five exceptional algebras — G2 (dimension 14) through E8 (dimension 248) — have no classical matrix description.

Representation theory

Finite-dimensional representations of simple Lie algebras are completely reducible (Weyl's theorem) and are classified by their highest weights. The structure theory — roots, weights, the Weyl group, and the Cartan matrix — is central to modern algebra and to gauge theories in physics, where groups such as SU(3) and E8 appear.
