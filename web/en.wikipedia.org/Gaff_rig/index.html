Gaff rig

TypeFore-and-aft sailing rig
Defining featureFour-sided mainsail controlled by a gaff spar
Common vesselsSchooners, ketches, cutters, yawls

A gaff rig is a sailing rig in which the principal sail is a four-sided fore-and-aft sail whose head is supported by a spar called a gaff. The gaff is hoisted at an angle, controlled by peak and throat halyards, and its jaws ride against the mast. Gaff rigs were the dominant rig on working sailing craft and yachts from the eighteenth century until the early twentieth century, when the triangular Bermuda (Marconi) rig largely displaced them.

Characteristics

The gaff mainsail's four sides — head, foot, luff, and leech — allow a large sail area on a relatively short mast, an advantage before modern strong, light spars and sailcloth. A gaff sail may be loose-footed or laced to a boom, and is often supplemented by a topsail set above the gaff on a jackyard or along the topmast. Gaff rigs deliver powerful drive off the wind and in light airs but are generally less efficient to windward than Bermuda rigs, whose tall, high-aspect sails point higher.

Handling

Gaffs are heavier and require more crew work than a Bermuda rig: two halyards plus sheets and vangs must be managed, and the sail plan's centre of effort is lower and further aft. Nevertheless, the rig remains popular on traditional boats, replicas, and classic yachts, and in regattas devoted to gaffers such as those raced by the Old Gaffers Association, founded in Britain in 1963.

Related rigs

The gaff rig is closely related to the sprit rig and the lug rig, which likewise carry four-sided sails; the gunter rig is a variant in which the gaff is set nearly vertical, approximating a Bermuda sail.
