Oilfield services

Oilfield services are the products and services that companies provide to the oil and gas industry to support the exploration, production and transportation of petroleum and natural gas. The sector encompasses firms that carry out tasks such as seismic surveying, drilling, well logging, hydraulic fracturing, cementing, well completion and production services, rather than companies that own and sell the hydrocarbons themselves.

Overview

Oil and gas operators, such as integrated energy companies and national oil companies, typically outsource much of the physical work of finding and producing hydrocarbons to oilfield services firms. The largest services companies — including SLB (formerly Schlumberger), Halliburton and Baker Hughes — are often described as the industry's "big three" and operate in dozens of countries.

Segments

Major segments of the oilfield services industry include:

  • Geophysical services — acquisition and processing of seismic data used to image subsurface structures.
  • Drilling services — provision and operation of drilling rigs, directional drilling, and measurement-while-drilling and logging-while-drilling tools.
  • Well construction — casing, cementing, wireline logging and completions equipment.
  • Stimulation and production services — hydraulic fracturing, pressure pumping, artificial lift and well intervention.
  • Subsea and offshore services — installation and maintenance of subsea equipment and production systems.

Business characteristics

Revenue in the sector is closely tied to the level of capital spending by operators, which in turn tracks the price of oil and gas. Because exploration and production companies reduce drilling activity when commodity prices fall, oilfield services is a highly cyclical industry. Much of its technology is developed by the services companies themselves, which hold large portfolios of patents in downhole tools, sensors and digital reservoir software.
