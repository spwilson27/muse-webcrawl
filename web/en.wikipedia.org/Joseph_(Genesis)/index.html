Joseph (Genesis)

Joseph (Hebrew: יוֹסֵף, Yosef) is a major figure in the Book of Genesis in the Hebrew Bible, and the eleventh son of Jacob and the first son of Rachel. His story, occupying Genesis chapters 37–50, is one of the longest and most developed narratives in the Pentateuch.

Story

Joseph was his father's favourite son, receiving a richly ornamented robe — traditionally rendered a "coat of many colours" — which provoked the jealousy of his ten older brothers. Joseph further angered them by recounting dreams in which his brothers' sheaves and the sun, moon, and eleven stars bowed down to him. The brothers sold him to Ishmaelite (Midianite) traders for twenty pieces of silver, and he was taken to Egypt, where he was sold to Potiphar, an officer of Pharaoh.

After being falsely accused by Potiphar's wife and imprisoned, Joseph interpreted the dreams of Pharaoh's cupbearer and baker, and later Pharaoh's own dreams of seven fat and seven lean cows and ears of grain, predicting seven years of plenty followed by seven years of famine. Pharaoh appointed Joseph vizier over Egypt, and he stored grain during the years of abundance. When famine struck Canaan, Joseph's brothers came to Egypt to buy grain; after testing them, Joseph revealed himself and forgave them, declaring that God had sent him ahead to preserve life. Jacob's whole household settled in the land of Goshen.

Death and legacy

Joseph died in Egypt aged 110, asking that his bones be carried up when the Israelites returned to Canaan — a request fulfilled in the Exodus, with his bones finally buried at Shechem. He is the father of Manasseh and Ephraim, whose descendants formed two of the Twelve Tribes of Israel. Joseph is also revered in Islam as the prophet Yusuf, the subject of the twelfth sura of the Quran.
