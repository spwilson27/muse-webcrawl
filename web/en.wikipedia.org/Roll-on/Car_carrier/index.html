Car carrier (roll-on/roll-off)

A car carrier in maritime transport is a type of roll-on/roll-off (ro-ro) cargo ship designed to carry wheeled vehicle cargo, principally new automobiles, that is driven on and off the vessel under its own power via built-in ramps. Dedicated ships are known as Pure Car Carriers (PCC) and, when they also carry trucks and other rolling cargo, Pure Car and Truck Carriers (PCTC).

Design

Car carriers resemble large floating multi-storey car parks, with a boxy superstructure running the full length of the hull, a stern or side ramp, and numerous internal decks — fixed and hoistable — connected by internal ramps. Modern PCTCs can carry 6,000 or more car-equivalent units. Vehicles are lashed down for sea passages, and the trade routes chiefly link manufacturing centres in Japan, South Korea, Europe and China with consumer markets worldwide.
