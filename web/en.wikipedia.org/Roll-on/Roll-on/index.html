Roll-on/roll-off

Roll-on/roll-off (RORO or ro-ro) ships are cargo ships designed to carry wheeled cargo, such as cars, motorcycles, trucks, semi-trailer trucks, buses, trailers, and railroad cars, that are driven on and off the ship on their own wheels or using a platform vehicle.

Design

RORO vessels have either built-in or shore-based ramps, usually at the stern and bow, allowing cargo to be efficiently rolled on and off. Large car carriers (Pure Car Carriers, PCCs) can carry thousands of automobiles on multiple enclosed decks. RORO ferries carry passengers as well as vehicles on shorter routes.

History

The concept descends from nineteenth-century train ferries; purpose-built RORO car carriers expanded rapidly after the Second World War as automobile exports grew, particularly from Japan and later South Korea and China.
