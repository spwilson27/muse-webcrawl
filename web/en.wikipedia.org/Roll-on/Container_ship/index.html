Container ship (roll-on context)

A container ship is a cargo ship that carries all of its load in intermodal containers, in a technique called containerisation. Unlike roll-on/roll-off vessels, whose cargo is driven aboard, container ships are "lift-on/lift-off" (LoLo) ships: containers are loaded and unloaded vertically by gantry cranes at container terminals.

Characteristics

Capacity is measured in twenty-foot equivalent units (TEU); the largest modern ultra-large container vessels exceed 24,000 TEU and are over 400 metres long. Containerisation, pioneered in the 1950s by Malcom McLean, drastically reduced the cost of international freight and reshaped global trade. Hybrid vessels combining container cells with ro-ro vehicle decks also exist for mixed trades.
