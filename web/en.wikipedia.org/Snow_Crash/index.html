Snow Crash

Snow Crash is a science fiction novel by American writer Neal Stephenson, published in 1992. Like many of Stephenson's novels, it covers history, linguistics, anthropology, archaeology, religion, computer science, politics, cryptography, memetics, and philosophy. The book popularized the term "metaverse" for a virtual-reality-based successor to the internet, and its use of "avatar" for a virtual body influenced later virtual worlds.

Plot summary

In a near-future Los Angeles fragmented into franchised sovereign enclaves ("burbclaves"), Hiro Protagonist — pizza delivery driver for the Mafia and elite hacker — teams with skateboard courier Y.T. ("Yours Truly") to investigate "Snow Crash", a drug in the real world and a computer virus in the Metaverse that crashes the minds of hackers. Their investigation leads to Sumerian mythology, the cult of the goddess Asherah, and media magnate L. Bob Rife, who seeks to control humanity through a neurolinguistic "nam-shub" derived from ancient Sumerian.

Themes and influence

The novel blends cyberpunk tropes with Sumerian myth, linguistics, and libertarian satire. Its depiction of the Metaverse — a persistent virtual street populated by user-controlled avatars — directly influenced the development of virtual worlds such as Second Life and later industry uses of the term "metaverse". Time magazine included Snow Crash in its list of the 100 best English-language novels from 1923 to 2005.
