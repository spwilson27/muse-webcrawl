Robert Redford

Charles Robert Redford Jr. (born August 18, 1936) is an American actor and filmmaker. He has received numerous accolades, including an Academy Award, a BAFTA Award, and two Golden Globe Awards, as well as the Cecil B. DeMille Award in 1994, the Screen Actors Guild Life Achievement Award in 1996, the Academy Honorary Award in 2002, the Kennedy Center Honors in 2005, the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2016, and the Honorary César in 2019.

Career

Redford began acting on television and stage before making his film debut in War Hunt (1962). He rose to prominence with roles in Barefoot in the Park (1967), Butch Cassidy and the Sundance Kid (1969), Jeremiah Johnson (1972), The Candidate (1972), The Sting (1973), The Way We Were (1973), The Great Gatsby (1974), and All the President's Men (1976), becoming one of the biggest box-office stars of the 1970s.

His directorial debut, Ordinary People (1980), won the Academy Award for Best Picture and earned Redford the Academy Award for Best Director. He went on to direct films including A River Runs Through It (1992) and Quiz Show (1994).

Sundance

Redford founded the Sundance Institute in 1981 and championed the Sundance Film Festival in Utah, which became the premier showcase for independent cinema in the United States. He is also a noted environmental activist.

Later work

Redford continued acting into the 2010s, appearing in All Is Lost (2013), Captain America: The Winter Soldier (2014), and The Old Man & the Gun (2018), after which he announced his retirement from acting.
