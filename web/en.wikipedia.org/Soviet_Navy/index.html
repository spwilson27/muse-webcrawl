Soviet Navy

A Soviet Navy destroyer underway

The Soviet Navy (Russian: Военно-морской флот СССР, Voyenno-morskoi flot SSSR, lit. "Military-Maritime Fleet of the USSR") was the naval warfare uniform service branch of the Soviet Armed Forces. Often referred to as the Red Fleet, it was a major component of the strategic planning of the Soviet Union during the Cold War.

Origins

The Soviet Navy was formed from the remnants of the Imperial Russian Navy following the Russian Revolution of 1917 and the Russian Civil War. In its early decades it was a small coastal-defence force, but a major shipbuilding programme began in the late 1930s under Stalin, interrupted by the Second World War, in which the navy fought in the Baltic, Black Sea, Arctic and Pacific theatres.

Cold War

Under Admiral Sergey Gorshkov, commander-in-chief from 1956 to 1985, the Soviet Navy was transformed into a blue-water fleet with a large submarine force, nuclear-powered ballistic-missile submarines forming part of the Soviet nuclear triad, and surface forces including Kiev-class aircraft-carrying cruisers and the Kirov-class battlecruisers. The navy was organised into four fleets: the Northern, Pacific, Black Sea and Baltic fleets, plus the Caspian Flotilla.

Dissolution

After the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991, the Soviet Navy ceased to exist and its assets were divided among the successor states, principally the Russian Navy and the Ukrainian Navy. Economic difficulties in the 1990s led to the rapid decline of the inherited fleet.
