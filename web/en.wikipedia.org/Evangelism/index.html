Evangelism

Evangelism
DefinitionPreaching the Christian gospel to convert others
Biblical basisThe Great Commission (Matthew 28:19–20)
Key figuresPaul the Apostle, Billy Graham, John Wesley
RelatedMissions, proselytism, revivalism

Evangelism is the act of preaching the gospel — the "good news" (euangelion in Greek) of Jesus Christ — with the intention of sharing the message and teachings of Christianity and converting others to the faith. Christians who specialise in evangelism are known as evangelists, whether in their home communities or as missionaries in foreign lands.

History

Evangelism begins in the New Testament with the ministry of Jesus and the missionary journeys of Paul the Apostle across the Roman world. It expanded with the monastic missions of the early medieval period, such as those of Augustine of Canterbury to England and Patrick to Ireland. The Protestant Reformation renewed emphasis on preaching, and in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries evangelical revivals — led by figures such as John Wesley, George Whitefield and later Charles Spurgeon and Dwight Moody — carried evangelism to mass audiences. The twentieth century added crusade evangelism (Billy Graham), radio, television and internet ministries, and the rapid growth of Pentecostalism in the Global South.

Methods

Methods include personal or friendship evangelism, open-air preaching, door-to-door visitation, tract distribution, mass crusades and media broadcasts, and "servant evangelism" through acts of service. The Lausanne Movement, founded in 1974, has coordinated global evangelical mission strategy, and the Alpha Course, developed in London, became a widely used introductory course format.

Evangelism and evangelicalism

Although related, evangelism (an activity) is distinct from evangelicalism (a movement within Protestantism emphasising the authority of the Bible, the new birth, and the atoning death of Christ). Roman Catholic and Orthodox churches also practise evangelism, increasingly framed since the Second Vatican Council as the "new evangelisation" of historically Christian societies.
