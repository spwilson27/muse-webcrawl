Bristol Sessions

The Bristol Sessions were a series of recording sessions held in 1927 in Bristol, Tennessee, by the Victor Talking Machine Company, led by record producer Ralph Peer. The sessions are widely regarded as the "Big Bang of Country Music" — a phrase attributed to Johnny Cash — because they produced the first recordings of the Carter Family and Jimmie Rodgers, two of the foundational acts of the genre, and demonstrated the commercial potential of rural "hillbilly" music.

Background

By the mid-1920s the major record companies were travelling to the American South with portable recording equipment to capture regional music for local markets. Ralph Peer, who had pioneered royalty arrangements with songwriters and publishers, arranged a session in Bristol, a town split between Tennessee and Virginia and accessible by rail from the Appalachian coalfields and farming country. Victor advertised auditions in local newspapers, offering musicians cash payments and the prospect of royalties.

The sessions

Over roughly ten days beginning on 25 July 1927, Peer recorded nineteen acts and dozens of performances in a converted warehouse on State Street. On 1 August, the Carter Family — A.P. Carter, his wife Sara, and his sister-in-law Maybelle — recorded for the first time, performing songs including "Single Girl, Married Girl" and "The Wandering Boy". Days later, Jimmie Rodgers, a former railroad brakeman from Mississippi, recorded "The Soldier's Sweetheart" and "Sleep, Baby, Sleep", beginning a career that would make him the "Father of Country Music". Other performers included the Stoneman family, the Johnson Brothers, and the West Virginia Coon Hunters.

Peer returned to Bristol in 1928 for a second round of sessions, though these produced no discoveries of comparable significance.

Legacy

The sessions launched commercial country music as a national industry and established the twin pillars of its early repertoire: the Carters' traditional family harmony singing and guitar style (including Maybelle Carter's influential "Carter scratch") and Rodgers's blues-inflected solo singing with yodels. In 1998 the Birthplace of Country Music organisation was founded in Bristol, and a museum dedicated to the sessions opened in 2014. The 1927 recordings were recognised by the Library of Congress, and Bristol was officially designated the "Birthplace of Country Music" by an act of the United States Congress in 1998.
