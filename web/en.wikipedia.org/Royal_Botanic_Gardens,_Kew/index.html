The Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew is a non-departmental public body in the United Kingdom, sponsored by the Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs, and an internationally important botanical research and education institution. Its gardens at Kew in south-west London house the largest and most diverse botanical and mycological collections in the world.

History

The gardens trace their origin to the exotic garden at Kew Park formed in 1759 by Augusta, Princess of Wales, for whom the nine-storey Great Pagoda was built in 1762. Under Sir Joseph Banks, Kew became a centre for the global exchange of useful plants. In 1840 the gardens were adopted as a national botanical garden, and under William Hooker and his son Joseph Dalton Hooker they expanded greatly; the Decimus Burton-designed Palm House (1844–48) and Temperate House remain landmarks.

Collections and research

Kew's living collections include more than 27,000 taxa, its herbarium holds over 8.5 million specimens, and the Millennium Seed Bank at Wakehurst preserves seeds from tens of thousands of wild plant species. Kew scientists conduct research on plant and fungal taxonomy, conservation and sustainable development, and publish the Plants of the World Online database.
