Laser cooling


Illustration: Laser cooling

Laser cooling is a set of techniques in which laser light is used to cool atoms and molecules to temperatures near absolute zero. The most common method, Doppler cooling, exploits the Doppler effect: atoms moving toward a red-detuned laser beam preferentially absorb photons and are slowed by the resulting radiation pressure, while spontaneous emission carries away energy.

Methods

Below the Doppler limit, sub-Doppler mechanisms such as Sisyphus cooling reach microkelvin temperatures. Further evaporative cooling in magnetic or optical traps produces nanokelvin samples used to create Bose–Einstein condensates.

Applications and recognition

Laser cooling underpins atomic clocks, atom interferometry and quantum simulation. The 1997 Nobel Prize in Physics was awarded to Steven Chu, Claude Cohen-Tannoudji and William D. Phillips for its development. Related cryogenic methods include magnetic refrigeration.
