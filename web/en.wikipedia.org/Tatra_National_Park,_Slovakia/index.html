Tatra National Park, Slovakia

Tatra National Park (Slovak: Tatranský národný park, TANAP) is a national park in northern Slovakia, protecting the Slovak portion of the High Tatra mountains along the border with Poland. Established in 1949, it is the oldest national park in Slovakia and, together with the adjoining Polish Tatra National Park, forms a transboundary UNESCO biosphere reserve.

Geography

The park covers the High Tatras and parts of the Western and Eastern Tatras, an area of roughly 738 square kilometres, plus a protective buffer zone. The High Tatras are the highest range of the Carpathians; Gerlachovský štít, at 2,655 metres, is the highest peak in Slovakia and the entire Carpathian chain. The landscape features sharp granite ridges, more than a hundred glacial lakes (plesá), waterfalls, and deep valleys carved by Ice Age glaciers.

Wildlife

Symbol of the park is the Tatra chamois, an endemic subspecies found nowhere else, alongside marmots, brown bears, lynx, wolves, and golden eagles. Forests of spruce and dwarf pine climb to the alpine zone of meadows and bare rock.

Tourism

The park is Slovakia's most visited mountain region, served by resorts such as Štrbské Pleso, Starý Smokovec, and Tatranská Lomnica, with marked hiking trails, mountain huts, and ski areas. A devastating windstorm in November 2004 flattened large areas of forest on the park's southern slopes.
