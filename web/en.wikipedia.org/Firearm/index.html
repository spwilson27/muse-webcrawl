Firearm

A firearm is any type of gun that uses an explosive charge to propel a projectile through a barrel, and which can be carried and used by an individual. The term legally encompasses a wide range of weapons, including handguns (pistols and revolvers), long guns such as rifles and shotguns, and, in many jurisdictions, machine guns and other automatic weapons.

History

The earliest firearms appeared in China, where gunpowder was invented; bamboo tubes and later metal-barrelled hand cannons were in use by the 13th century. The technology spread westward through the Middle East to Europe, where matchlock, wheellock and flintlock ignition systems successively improved reliability. The 19th century brought percussion caps, rifled barrels, breech-loading, metallic cartridges and repeating actions, transforming infantry warfare. In the 20th century, self-loading semi-automatic and automatic designs became standard.

Operation

In a typical firearm, pulling the trigger releases a firing pin or striker that ignites a primer; the primer ignites the propellant charge in the cartridge, and the rapidly expanding gases drive the bullet down the barrel, which is often rifled with spiral grooves to spin-stabilise the projectile. Shotguns fire a spread of shot or a single slug from a usually smooth barrel, while rifles fire single projectiles at higher velocity.

Regulation

Firearm ownership and use are regulated to widely varying degrees around the world, from permissive regimes with constitutional protections, as in the United States, to highly restrictive licensing systems in countries such as the United Kingdom, Japan and Australia.
