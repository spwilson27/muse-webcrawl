The Victorian Society

Formation1958
TypeCharity, amenity society
PurposeConservation of Victorian and Edwardian architecture
HeadquartersLondon, United Kingdom

The Victorian Society is a UK charity and amenity society that campaigns for the preservation of Victorian and Edwardian architecture and design in England and Wales. It is one of the statutory amenity societies consulted on changes to listed buildings, and it works to protect buildings and other structures built between 1837 and 1914.

History

The Society was founded in 1958, at a time when Victorian architecture was widely unfashionable and under threat of demolition. Its founders included the architectural historian Nikolaus Pevsner, who became its first chairman, and John Betjeman, the poet and broadcaster who became one of its most prominent champions. The founding meeting was held at 18 Stafford Terrace, London, a remarkably preserved Victorian artist's house.

Activities

The Society campaigns against the demolition or unsympathetic alteration of significant Victorian and Edwardian buildings, makes representations to local planning authorities and public inquiries, and publishes an annual list of the top ten most endangered Victorian and Edwardian buildings in England and Wales. It also organises lectures, walks, and study tours, and publishes a journal, The Victorian, as well as academic publications.

Notable campaigns

Early campaigns included efforts to save the Euston Arch in London (which was demolished in 1961–62 despite protests) and Midland Grand Hotel at St Pancras station, which was saved and later restored. The Society has also campaigned for the preservation of Victorian churches, libraries, town halls, pubs, and industrial buildings across the country.
