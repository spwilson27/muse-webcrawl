Problem-based learning

Problem-based learning (PBL) is a student-centered pedagogy in which students learn about a subject through the experience of solving an open-ended problem found in trigger materials. The PBL process focuses on developing students' collaborative problem-solving, self-directed learning, and critical thinking rather than on passive absorption of lectures.

History

PBL was pioneered at the medical school of McMaster University in Hamilton, Ontario, Canada, in the late 1960s under Howard Barrows and colleagues, who sought to make medical education mimic the reasoning clinicians use with real patients. It spread to other health-science programmes and then to engineering, law, economics, architecture, and school education; Aalborg University in Denmark built its entire model around it.

Process

Students, in small groups with a tutor acting as facilitator, receive a problem scenario. They clarify terms, define the problem, brainstorm explanations drawing on prior knowledge, formulate learning objectives, study individually, and return to discuss and apply what they found. Assessment typically combines tutor evaluation, self and peer assessment, and examinations.

Evidence

Research comparing PBL with traditional curricula finds slightly lower scores on tests of basic factual knowledge but better application of knowledge, retention, and student satisfaction, though results vary by discipline and implementation quality.
