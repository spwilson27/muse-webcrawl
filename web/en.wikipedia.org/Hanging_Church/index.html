The Hanging Church, formally the Church of the Holy Virgin Mary at Haret El-Roum, is one of the oldest and most famous churches in Egypt, located in Old Cairo. It is also known as Al-Kanisa Al-Muallaqa in Arabic.

Name and location

The church is called "hanging" or "suspended" because its nave is built above the gatehouse of the Roman-era Babylon Fortress, giving the impression that it hangs in mid-air. Visitors historically reached it by climbing a steep staircase of 29 steps, which led early travellers to nickname Cairo "the Babylon of Egypt".

History

The church may date to the third or fourth century, though it was substantially rebuilt over the centuries. It served for a time as the residence of the Coptic Patriarchs, beginning in the eleventh century when the patriarchal seat moved from Alexandria to Cairo. Many important councils and elections of patriarchs took place here.

Art and icons

The church contains numerous icons, the oldest dating to the eighth century, and a celebrated icon of the Virgin Mary known as the "Coptic Mona Lisa". Its carved wooden screen, inlaid with ivory and ebony, and its marble pulpit supported on thirteen columns are notable features.
