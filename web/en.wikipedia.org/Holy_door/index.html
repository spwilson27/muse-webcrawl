Holy door

A holy door (Latin: porta sancta) is a special door in certain Roman Catholic churches, most famously St Peter's Basilica in the Vatican, which is opened only during a Holy Year (jubilee). Passing through it symbolises the passage from sin to grace and is associated with a plenary indulgence.

The bronze Holy Door of St Peter's, opened only in jubilee years.

LocationSt Peter's Basilica, Vatican City (principal)
OpenedOnly during Holy Years
Other Roman doorsSt John Lateran, St Mary Major, St Paul Outside the Walls

History

The custom of a special jubilee door developed in the 15th and 16th centuries; the first recorded opening was at St John Lateran. The present bronze door of St Peter's, made by Vico Consorti and depicting scenes of salvation history, was installed for the Holy Year of 1950 under Pius XII.

Rite

Traditionally the pope struck the wall sealing the door with a hammer; for safety, the wall is now dismantled beforehand and the pope pushes the door open. At the close of the jubilee the door is sealed again with bricks and mortar, along with coins and documents of the year. During the Jubilee of Mercy (2015–16), Pope Francis authorised holy doors in cathedrals around the world, and for 2025 a holy door was also opened at Rome's Rebibbia prison.
