Rasam

Rasam is a spicy South Indian soup-like dish made with a tamarind or tomato base, flavoured with black pepper, cumin, chilli, mustard seeds, curry leaves, and other spices. It is eaten with rice or drunk on its own, especially in Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, and Kerala.

Preparation and varieties

A tempering of mustard seeds, cumin, dried chillies, and curry leaves in oil or ghee is added to a sour, peppery broth; common varieties include tomato rasam, pepper rasam (milagu rasam), garlic rasam, lemon rasam, and pineapple rasam. Rasam powder — a ground blend of pepper, cumin, coriander seed, and dried chillies — is a household staple.

Cultural notes

The black pepper and tamarind base is traditionally regarded as warming and is often served to people with colds. The British colonial soup mulligatawny is generally considered to derive from rasam-style preparations ("milagu tannir", pepper water).
