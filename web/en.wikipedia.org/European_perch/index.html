European perch
Perca fluviatilis Linnaeus, 1758

Family: Percidae
Order: Perciformes

The European perch (Perca fluviatilis), also known as the common perch, redfin perch or English perch, is a predatory species of the freshwater perch native to Europe and northern Asia. It is the type species of the genus Perca.

The European perch has a greenish body with red or orange pelvic, anal and caudal fins. Along its flanks it carries five to eight dark vertical bars. It has two dorsal fins, the first spiny and the second soft-rayed. A distinctive feature is the black blotch at the rear of the first dorsal fin.

Distribution and habitat

The species is widespread across Europe and Siberia, occurring in lakes, ponds, slow-flowing rivers and brackish estuaries. It has been introduced to Australia, New Zealand and South Africa, where it is regarded as an invasive pest because of its effect on native fish.

Behaviour and diet

Perch are carnivorous, feeding on invertebrates when young and increasingly on other fish — including smaller perch — as they grow. They are schooling fish, often forming loose groups that hunt cooperatively. Spawning takes place in spring, when females lay long ribbons of eggs over submerged vegetation and branches.

Fishing

The European perch is a popular target for anglers and is also fished commercially in parts of Europe. Its flesh is white, firm and well regarded as food. Typical adult size is 20–30 cm, though specimens over 50 cm and 4 kg have been recorded.
