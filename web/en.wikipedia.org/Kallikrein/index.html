Kallikrein

TypeSerine protease enzymes
GroupsPlasma kallikrein (KLKB1); tissue kallikreins (KLK1–KLK15)
Gene locus19q13 (tissue kallikrein cluster)
Best-known memberKLK3 — prostate-specific antigen (PSA)
SystemKallikrein–kinin system

Kallikreins are a subgroup of serine proteases, enzymes capable of cleaving peptide bonds in proteins. In humans, plasma kallikrein (encoded by KLKB1) and a family of fifteen tissue kallikrein-related peptidases (KLK1–KLK15) carry out diverse physiological functions, from blood-pressure regulation and inflammation to skin desquamation and semen liquefaction. The name derives from the Greek kallikreas, "pancreas", where the enzyme was first identified in the 1930s.

Plasma kallikrein

Plasma kallikrein circulates as the zymogen prekallikrein and is activated by factor XII (Hageman factor) in the contact system of blood coagulation. It reciprocally activates more factor XII and cleaves high-molecular-weight kininogen to release the peptide bradykinin, a potent vasodilator that increases vascular permeability. Excess bradykinin production, usually due to deficiency of its inhibitor C1-esterase inhibitor, causes hereditary angioedema; drugs targeting plasma kallikrein, such as ecallantide and lanadelumab, are used to treat and prevent attacks.

Tissue kallikreins

The fifteen tissue kallikrein genes form the largest contiguous protease cluster in the human genome, on chromosome 19q13. Tissue kallikrein proper (KLK1) releases kallidin (lysyl-bradykinin) in tissues. Other members process growth factors, extracellular matrix, and skin proteins: KLK5, KLK7, and KLK14 regulate shedding of corneocytes, and their overactivity (as in Netherton syndrome, caused by loss of the inhibitor LEKTI) produces severe skin disease.

Clinical significance

The best-known kallikrein is KLK3, prostate-specific antigen (PSA), whose blood level is widely used in screening and monitoring for prostate cancer. KLK2 is a related prostate marker. Several kallikreins are dysregulated in ovarian, breast, and other cancers and are studied as biomarkers, and members of the family expressed in the brain have been investigated in neurodegenerative disease.
