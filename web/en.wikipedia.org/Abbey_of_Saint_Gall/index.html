Abbey of Saint Gall

LocationSt. Gallen, Switzerland
Founded719 (cell from c. 612)
OrderBenedictine
StatusUNESCO World Heritage Site (1983)

The Abbey of Saint Gall (German: Fürstabtei St. Gallen) is a former Benedictine abbey in the city of St. Gallen in north-eastern Switzerland. For many centuries it was one of the chief monasteries of Europe, and its library remains one of the richest medieval collections in the world. The abbey precinct was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983.

History

The site originated in a hermit's cell founded around 612 by the Irish monk Gallus, a companion of Columbanus. In 719 the Alemannic priest Othmar founded a monastery on the site and is counted its first abbot. Under the Carolingians the abbey became an imperial abbey whose abbots ranked as princes of the Holy Roman Empire.

The abbey's school and scriptorium made it a great centre of learning. Its library preserves about 170,000 volumes and some 2,100 manuscripts, including the famous Plan of Saint Gall, an architectural drawing of an idealised monastery from around 820 that has never been identified with an actual building programme. The present cathedral, a late Baroque church, was built in 1755–1766. The abbey was secularised in 1805, and since 1847 its church has been the cathedral of the Diocese of St. Gallen.
