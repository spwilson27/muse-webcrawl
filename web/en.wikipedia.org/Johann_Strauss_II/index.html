Johann Baptist Strauss II (1825–1899) was an Austrian composer of light music, particularly dance music and operettas, known as "The Waltz King". He composed over 500 waltzes, polkas, quadrilles and other dance pieces, as well as several operettas and a ballet.

Career

Son of Johann Strauss I, he formed his own orchestra in 1844 and soon rivalled and then surpassed his father. His most famous waltz, "The Blue Danube" (1866), became one of the most popular pieces of classical music ever written; others include "Kaiser-Walzer", "Tales from the Vienna Woods" and "Wein, Weib und Gesang".

Operettas

Strauss's operettas, above all Die Fledermaus (1874) and Der Zigeunerbaron (The Gypsy Baron, 1885), remain in the standard repertoire. His music defined the sound of 19th-century Vienna.
