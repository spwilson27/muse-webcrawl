Red

Red
Wavelength: approx. 625–740 nm
Hex triplet: #FF0000
sRGB (r, g, b): (255, 0, 0)
Primary colour: additive and (in RYB) subtractive systems
Red is the colour of blood, fire and ripe fruit, and carries strong symbolic associations in most cultures.

Red is the color at the long wavelength end of the visible spectrum, next to orange and opposite violet. It has a dominant wavelength of approximately 625–740 nanometres. It is a primary color in the RGB color model and a secondary color (made from magenta and yellow) in the CMYK color model, and is the complementary color of cyan. Red is one of the most symbolically charged colours: it is associated with blood, fire, love, courage, danger and revolution.

Optics and perception

Human eyes perceive red when light dominated by wavelengths around 625–740 nm falls on the retina, stimulating chiefly the long-wavelength-sensitive (L) cones. Because red light is scattered least by air molecules among visible colours (after the short wavelengths are removed), distant objects at sunset and the Sun itself appear red. In additive mixing, red combined with green gives yellow and with blue gives magenta; all three together give white.

Pigments and dyes

Red pigments are among the oldest used by humans: prehistoric cave paintings used red ochre (iron oxide). Vermilion (mercuric sulfide), madder, and cochineal from scale insects were prized historically; modern artists' reds are largely synthetic, such as cadmium red and the quinacridone pigments. Red dyes coloured the uniforms of armies — the British "redcoats" — and the robes of cardinals and kings.

Symbolism

Red signals danger and prohibition in traffic lights and warning signs, and is used for stop signals because it is visible at distance and culturally linked with alertness. In politics, red has symbolised socialism and communism since the nineteenth century — the Red Army, the red flag — while in the United States it conventionally denotes the Republican Party in election graphics. In China, red signifies luck, prosperity and celebration, and brides traditionally wear red; in Western tradition it is associated with love and passion (Valentine's Day, red roses) as well as with debt ("in the red") and embarrassment. In finance, red denotes falling prices in Western markets but rising prices in East Asian ones.

In nature

Blood is red because of the iron-containing protein haemoglobin. Autumn leaves turn red through anthocyanin pigments; many fruits and flowers — apples, strawberries, poppies, roses — are red, the colour advertising ripeness to animals or attracting pollinators. The red planet, Mars, owes its colour to iron oxide dust.
