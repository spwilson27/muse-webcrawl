Corona discharge

A corona discharge is an electrical discharge brought on by the ionization of a fluid such as air surrounding a conductor carrying a high voltage. It represents a local region where the air (or other fluid) has undergone electrical breakdown and become conductive, allowing charge to leak continuously off the conductor into the surroundings. It occurs at locations where the strength of the electric field exceeds the dielectric strength of the fluid but conditions are insufficient for complete breakdown or arcing to nearby objects.

Properties

Corona is often visible as a faint bluish glow in the dark, audible as a crackling or hissing noise, and accompanied by the production of ozone. It is enhanced around sharp points, edges, and rough or dirty conductor surfaces where the local field is concentrated — the same physics behind St. Elmo's fire observed on ships' masts and aircraft.

Problems caused

On high-voltage transmission lines corona causes power loss, radio and television interference, audible noise, and chemical attack on insulators by ozone and nitrogen oxides. Utilities mitigate it by using larger-diameter conductors, bundled conductors, and corona rings that smooth the electric field around hardware.

Applications

Corona discharge is exploited in photocopiers and laser printers to charge the photoconductor drum, in electrostatic precipitators that remove particulates from exhaust gases, in ozone generators, ionizers and air purifiers, and in the corona treatment of plastic films to make them receptive to inks and adhesives.
