Lewy body

Lewy bodies are abnormal aggregates of protein that develop inside neurons in certain neurodegenerative diseases. They are composed primarily of the protein alpha-synuclein associated with other proteins such as ubiquitin. Lewy bodies are the pathological hallmark of Parkinson's disease and dementia with Lewy bodies.

History

They were first described by the neurologist Friedrich H. Lewy in 1912, while working in Alois Alzheimer's laboratory. In 1997, alpha-synuclein was identified as their main component.

Formation and significance

Lewy bodies appear as spherical masses in the cytoplasm that displace other cellular components. Their presence in the substantia nigra is associated with the loss of dopaminergic neurons in Parkinson's disease, while cortical Lewy bodies are linked to the cognitive symptoms of Lewy body dementia. Lewy neurites are related abnormal deposits in neuronal processes. The diseases characterised by these inclusions are collectively called synucleinopathies.
