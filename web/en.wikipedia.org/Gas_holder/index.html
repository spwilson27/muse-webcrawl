Gas holder

Gas holder
Other namesGasholder, gasometer
PurposeStore manufactured or natural gas near atmospheric pressure
Common typesWater-sealed telescoping; dry-seal piston

A gas holder or gasholder, sometimes called a gasometer, is a large container used to store gas at close to ambient temperature and pressure. It smooths differences between steady gas production and variable demand and helps maintain pressure in local distribution networks.

Water-sealed holders

The classic design has a telescoping steel bell whose lower edge is sealed in a circular water tank. As gas enters, sections rise; guide frames keep the bell vertical. Spiral-guided holders omit the external frame and use helical rails instead.

Dry-seal holders

Dry-seal designs use a piston sealed against a cylindrical shell by tar, oil or a flexible membrane. They avoid the large water tank but require careful sealing and maintenance.

Decline and reuse

High-pressure pipelines and underground storage reduced the need for town-gas holders. Many nineteenth- and twentieth-century holders became industrial landmarks; some frames have been retained in parks and housing developments.
