Category O

Category O is a mathematical category whose objects are certain representations of a semisimple Lie algebra over the complex numbers, and whose morphisms are the representation homomorphisms. It was introduced by Joseph Bernstein, Israel Gelfand, and Sergei Gelfand in the 1970s and has become a central object of study in representation theory.

Definition

Fix a finite-dimensional semisimple complex Lie algebra with a chosen Cartan subalgebra and Borel subalgebra. Category O consists of finitely generated modules over the universal enveloping algebra that are semisimple (diagonalizable) with respect to the Cartan subalgebra, have finite-dimensional weight spaces, and are locally finite with respect to the Borel subalgebra.

Key properties

Category O has enough projective objects, every object has finite length, and its simple objects are parametrized by weights. Central objects include the Verma modules and their simple quotients. The category exhibits a highest-weight structure, is closed under duality, and decomposes into blocks according to the action of the centre of the enveloping algebra, connected with the Weyl group and Kazhdan–Lusztig theory.

Applications

The structure of category O underlies the Bernstein–Gelfand–Gelfand (BGG) resolution and the proof of the Kazhdan–Lusztig conjectures on multiplicities of simple modules in Verma modules. Generalizations to quantum groups, affine Lie algebras, and Lie superalgebras remain active areas of research.
