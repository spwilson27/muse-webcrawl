Viral marketing

Viral marketing is a marketing strategy that encourages individuals to spread a message to others, creating the potential for exponential growth in the message's exposure and influence — analogous to the spread of a virus. In the internet era it typically relies on social media, video platforms and messaging apps, using content designed to be entertaining, surprising or emotionally engaging enough that audiences share it voluntarily.

TypeMarketing strategy
ChannelsSocial media, video, email, messaging
Key mechanismAudience-to-audience sharing
Prominent exampleHotmail (1996); ALS Ice Bucket Challenge (2014)

History

The term was popularized in the late 1990s, notably in discussions of Hotmail, whose free webmail service appended a promotional line to every outgoing message and grew to millions of users within months after its 1996 launch. With the rise of YouTube, Facebook and Twitter in the 2000s, viral distribution became a central goal of digital campaigns.

Mechanisms

Successful viral campaigns usually combine a low-friction sharing channel with content that provokes a strong reaction: humour, awe, outrage, surprise or social currency — the feeling that sharing makes the sharer look good. Marketers often "seed" content with influencers or communities, use incentives and referrals, and design formats (challenges, quizzes, user-generated content) that recruit participants into producing the message themselves. Metrics such as the viral coefficient (K-factor) estimate whether each user brings in more than one additional user, the threshold for self-sustaining growth.

Examples and criticism

Famous cases include the ALS Ice Bucket Challenge of 2014, which raised over $100 million for charity, the Old Spice "The Man Your Man Could Smell Like" campaign, and countless viral music videos and memes. Critics note that virality is largely unpredictable, that brands cannot control how audiences remix or mock messages, and that engagement-driven tactics can shade into clickbait, astroturfing or manipulation. Because success depends on the audience, most attempts never spread beyond their initial audience at all.
