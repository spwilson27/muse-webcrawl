Medical cannabis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Medical cannabis, or medical marijuana, is cannabis and cannabinoids that are prescribed by physicians for their patients. The use of cannabis as medicine has a long history, but it has not been as rigorously tested as other medicines due to legal restrictions in many countries. Preliminary evidence suggests cannabis may reduce nausea and vomiting during chemotherapy, improve appetite in people with HIV/AIDS, reduce chronic pain and muscle spasms, and treat severe forms of epilepsy.

Medical uses

Cannabinoid medicines such as dronabinol and nabilone are approved in some jurisdictions for chemotherapy-induced nausea. Nabiximols, an oromucosal spray containing THC and CBD, is used for spasticity in multiple sclerosis in a number of countries. Evidence for many other proposed uses remains limited.

Adverse effects

Short-term adverse effects may include dizziness, dry mouth, nausea, fatigue, somnolence and impaired short-term memory. Long-term risks, particularly from smoked cannabis, are debated and include possible effects on mental health and lung function.

Legal status

The legal status of medical cannabis varies widely by country and, in the United States, by state. A growing number of jurisdictions permit its medical use under regulated programmes.
