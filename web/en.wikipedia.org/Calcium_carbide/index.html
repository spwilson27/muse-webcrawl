Calcium Carbide

Calcium carbide, also known as calcium acetylide, is a chemical compound with the formula CaC2. Its main industrial use is in the production of acetylene and calcium cyanamide. The pure material is colourless, while technical-grade pieces are grey or brown.

Production

Calcium carbide is produced industrially in an electric arc furnace from a mixture of lime and coke at approximately 2,200 °C: CaO + 3C → CaC2 + CO. The process, developed in the 1890s, demands large amounts of electricity.

Reactions and uses

With water it yields acetylene gas and calcium hydroxide: CaC2 + 2H2O → C2H2 + Ca(OH)2. This reaction powered carbide lamps for miners, cyclists, and cavers, and supplied acetylene for welding before direct synthesis routes spread. With nitrogen at high temperature it forms calcium cyanamide, a fertiliser. It is also used in steel desulfurisation and, controversially, to ripen fruit artificially.

Structure

The solid adopts a distorted rock-salt structure with C22− dumbbells aligned in parallel.
