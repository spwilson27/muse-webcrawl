Hospicio Cabañas

The Hospicio Cabañas is a large neoclassical complex in Guadalajara, Jalisco, Mexico, one of the oldest and largest hospital and hospice complexes in the Americas. It was founded by Bishop Juan Cruz Ruiz de Cabañas, designed by the architect Manuel Tolsá, and built in the early 19th century (1805–1810) to serve as a workhouse and shelter for orphans, the elderly, the disabled, and the chronically ill.

Architecture

The complex is arranged around a series of courtyards on a single storey, a design intended to ease movement for its residents and to admit light and air. At its centre stands the chapel. The building functioned as an orphanage and hospital well into the 20th century.

Orozco murals

The Hospicio is most famous for the murals painted in its chapel in 1938–1939 by José Clemente Orozco, one of the leading Mexican muralists. The 57 frescoes include the celebrated Man of Fire (El hombre de fuego) in the dome, depicting a figure engulfed in flames, regarded as a masterpiece of modern Mexican art.

Recognition

The Hospicio Cabañas was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1997. It now serves as the Instituto Cultural Cabañas, a museum and cultural centre hosting exhibitions and performances.
