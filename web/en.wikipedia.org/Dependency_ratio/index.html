Dependency ratio

The dependency ratio is a measure of the number of dependents aged zero to 14 and over the age of 65, compared with the total population aged 15 to 64. It is an indicator used to measure the pressure on the productive population: the higher the ratio, the greater the economic burden that workers are expected to bear in supporting children and pensioners through taxes and social systems.

Formula

The total dependency ratio is calculated as (population aged 0–14 + population aged 65+) divided by population aged 15–64, often expressed per 100 working-age people. It is sometimes split into the child dependency ratio and the aged dependency ratio.

Implications

Rising old-age dependency ratios in ageing societies strain pension and healthcare systems, and are used by governments and institutions such as the World Bank and OECD to assess fiscal sustainability. A demographic dividend occurs when the dependency ratio falls as a relatively large working-age cohort supports fewer dependants.
