Eskimo–Aleut languages

The Eskimo–Aleut languages, also known as Eskaleut, are a language family native to Greenland, the Canadian Arctic, Alaska, and parts of Siberia. They consist of the Aleut (Unangam) language and the Eskimo languages, which divide into Yupik and Inuit branches.

Classification

The family comprises Aleut; the Yupik languages of Alaska and Siberia (Central Alaskan Yup'ik, Siberian Yupik, and others); and the Inuit language continuum, including Iñupiaq, Inuvialuktun, Inuktitut, and Greenlandic (Kalaallisut), the official language of Greenland.

Features

These languages are polysynthetic, building long words from a root and many suffixes, and are ergative in alignment. Many are endangered, though revitalisation efforts are under way.
