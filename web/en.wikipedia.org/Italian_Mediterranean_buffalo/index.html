Italian Mediterranean buffalo

Conservation status: Not at risk
Country of origin: Italy
Type: River buffalo
Use: Milk (mozzarella), meat

The Italian Mediterranean buffalo (Italian: Bufala Mediterranea Italiana) is an Italian breed of domestic water buffalo (Bubalus bubalis) of the river type. It is the only indigenous buffalo breed of Italy formally recognised as a breed, and it is raised principally for its milk, from which mozzarella di bufala campana is made.

History

The water buffalo is not native to Europe, and the route by which it reached Italy is debated: it may have arrived in Roman or late antique times, or been introduced during the early Middle Ages by the Goths, the Lombards, or through Norman and Arab contacts with Sicily. For centuries buffaloes were kept in the marshy coastal plains of central and southern Italy, where their hardiness in wetlands and their use as draught animals made them valuable. Drainage of the marshes and mechanisation reduced numbers in the twentieth century, but the rising value of buffalo mozzarella revived the breed. A national herd book was established in 1980, administered by the national breeders' association ANASB, and systematic selection for milk production began.

Distribution and numbers

The breed is concentrated in Campania — above all the provinces of Caserta and Salerno — with further populations in Lazio, Puglia, Basilicata, Molise and other regions. Italy now keeps one of the largest buffalo populations in Europe, numbering several hundred thousand head, and the Italian stock has been exported to many countries for cross-breeding and milk production programmes.

Characteristics

The skin is dark grey to nearly black and sparsely haired; the muzzle, socks and tail switch may show white. The horns are flattened in section and curve backwards and outwards. Cows weigh roughly 500–650 kg and bulls 700 kg to over 900 kg. The breed is noted for longevity, fertility and adaptation to hot climates.

Use

Buffalo milk is white, rich and sweet, containing around 8% fat and a high proportion of protein and casein, making it exceptionally suitable for cheese-making. Recorded cows average over 2,200 kg of milk per lactation, with elite animals far exceeding this. The milk is used almost entirely for mozzarella di bufala campana, which holds PDO (protected designation of origin) status in the European Union. Buffalo meat and leather are secondary products.
