Nagarjuna (c. 150 – c. 250 CE) was an Indian Mahayana Buddhist thinker, scholar-saint, and philosopher, widely considered one of the most important Buddhist philosophers. He was the founder of the Madhyamaka ("Middle Way") school of Buddhist philosophy and is credited with developing the philosophy of śūnyatā (emptiness).

Philosophy

In his masterpiece, the Mūlamadhyamakakārikā, Nagarjuna argued that all phenomena are empty of svabhāva — inherent, independent existence — because everything arises dependently (pratītya-samutpāda). His dialectical method refutes every position, including its opposite, without asserting a counter-thesis, pointing instead to the middle way between eternalism and nihilism. His doctrine of the two truths distinguishes conventional truth from ultimate truth.

Legend and works

Traditional biographies say he retrieved the Perfection of Wisdom sutras from the underwater kingdom of the nagas (serpent beings), whence his name. Many works are attributed to him, including the Ratnāvalī and the Vigrahavyāvartanī; his influence on Tibetan and East Asian Buddhism has been immense.
