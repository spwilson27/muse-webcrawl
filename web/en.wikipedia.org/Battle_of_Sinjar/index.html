Battle of Sinjar

DateAugust – November 2014 (initial assault); December 2014 – November 2015 (offensives)
LocationSinjar, Nineveh Governorate, Iraq
ResultKurdish and allied victory; Sinjar town retaken November 2015
BelligerentsIslamic State vs. Kurdish Peshmerga, PKK, YPG/YPJ and Yazidi forces, supported by US-led coalition airstrikes

The Battle of Sinjar refers to the fighting around the town of Sinjar and Mount Sinjar in northern Iraq beginning in August 2014, when the Islamic State (IS) overran the region, and continuing through the Kurdish-led offensives that retook the town in November 2015. The IS assault on Sinjar precipitated the Sinjar massacre and a humanitarian crisis that drew international intervention.

Mount Sinjar, refuge for tens of thousands of Yazidis in August 2014.

Background

Sinjar district, west of Mosul, was home to a large Yazidi population. After IS captured Mosul in June 2014, it advanced on Sinjar on 3 August 2014. The Kurdish Peshmerga forces defending the area withdrew, and IS fighters entered the town, beginning mass killings, abductions and enslavement of Yazidis — acts later recognised by the United Nations as genocide.

The siege of Mount Sinjar

Tens of thousands of Yazidis fled to Mount Sinjar, where they were besieged without adequate food or water. The United States and allies carried out humanitarian airdrops and began airstrikes against IS positions. Fighters of the Kurdistan Workers' Party (PKK) and the Syrian Kurdish YPG opened a corridor from Syria, evacuating many of the trapped civilians.

Kurdish offensives

In December 2014, Peshmerga forces, supported by coalition air power, broke the siege of the mountain and retook some surrounding areas. A larger offensive in November 2015, involving some 7,500 Peshmerga together with Yazidi and PKK-affiliated units, severed the IS supply route along Highway 47 and retook Sinjar town itself on 13 November 2015. Mass graves of Yazidi victims were subsequently discovered.

Aftermath

The town was left heavily damaged, and the question of Sinjar's administration remained contested between the Iraqi federal government, the Kurdistan Regional Government and local Yazidi groups. Thousands of Yazidi women and children remained missing or in captivity after the battle.
