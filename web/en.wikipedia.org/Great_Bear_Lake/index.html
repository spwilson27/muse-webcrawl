Great Bear Lake (Slavey: Sahtú; French: Grand lac de l'Ours) is a lake in the boreal forest of Canada. It is the largest lake entirely in Canada (Lake Superior and Lake Huron are larger but straddle the Canada–US border), the fourth largest in North America, and the eighth largest in the world. It lies on the Arctic Circle in the Northwest Territories, between 65 and 67 degrees northern latitude.

Geography and ecology

The lake's highly indented shoreline is about 2,700 km long, with five main arms — Dease, McTavish, McVicar, Keith and Smith — studded with islands. It is ice-covered for much of the year and supports lake trout, Arctic grayling and whitefish. The only community on its shore is Délı̨nę, at the lake's southwestern outlet.

History

The lake took its English name from the bears around its shores. In the 1930s, pitchblende (uranium and radium ore) was mined at Port Radium on the eastern shore; ore from the mine later supplied uranium to the Manhattan Project, a history bound up with the grievances of the Sahtú Dene carriers who transported the ore. In 2016 the Tsá Tué biosphere reserve, encompassing the lake and its watershed, was designated by UNESCO.
