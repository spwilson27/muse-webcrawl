Harold Gregory Moore Jr. (February 13, 1922 – February 10, 2017) was a United States Army lieutenant general and author. He is best known as the lieutenant colonel in command of the 1st Battalion, 7th Cavalry Regiment, at the Battle of Ia Drang in 1965, the first large-unit battle of the Vietnam War between the United States and the People's Army of Vietnam, which he co-authored the account of in the book We Were Soldiers Once… and Young (1992).

Early life and education

Moore was born in Bardstown, Kentucky, the eldest of five children. He grew up in Kentucky and later moved to Washington, D.C., where he worked in a bookstore while seeking an appointment to the United States Military Academy at West Point. Because Kentucky's appointments were filled, he obtained a nomination from a Georgia congressman, reasoning that Georgia had fewer applicants. He graduated from West Point in 1945, just as the Second World War was ending.

Military career

Commissioned as an infantry officer, Moore served in occupied Japan and then in the Korean War, where he commanded an infantry company and earned awards for valor. He later served as an instructor and studied airborne and air mobility tactics, helping to develop concepts of helicopter-borne warfare at Fort Benning, Georgia.

In November 1965, Moore led the 1st Battalion, 7th Cavalry Regiment into the Ia Drang Valley in the Central Highlands of South Vietnam. On November 14, his battalion was airlifted into Landing Zone X-Ray, where it fought a much larger North Vietnamese force for three days. Outnumbered roughly four to one, the battalion held its position; Moore was famous for his promise that he would be the first man on the battlefield and the last man off it. He was awarded the Distinguished Service Cross for his actions.

Moore later commanded the 3rd Brigade of the 1st Cavalry Division and the 7th Infantry Division at Fort Ord, California, where he was noted for strict discipline and intolerance of drug abuse. He retired in 1977 as a lieutenant general.

Author

With journalist Joseph L. Galloway, who had covered the Ia Drang battle as a reporter, Moore wrote We Were Soldiers Once… and Young (1992), a detailed account of the battle that became a bestseller. It was adapted into the 2002 film We Were Soldiers, starring Mel Gibson as Moore. A sequel, We Are Soldiers Still (2008), recounted a return trip to Vietnam. In later life Moore notably built a friendship with his former adversary, North Vietnamese general Nguyễn Hữu An.

Death

Moore died at his home in Auburn, Alabama, on February 10, 2017, three days before his 95th birthday. He was buried at Fort Benning (now Fort Moore; the post was renamed in honor of Moore and his wife Julia in 2023) beside his wife, Julia Compton Moore, who had died in 2004 and was known for personally delivering casualty notifications to families at Fort Benning during the war, a practice that led the Army to change its notification procedures.
