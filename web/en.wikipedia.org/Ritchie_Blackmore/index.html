Richard Hugh Blackmore (born 14 April 1945) is an English guitarist. He was a founding member of Deep Purple in 1968, and after leaving in 1975 formed Rainbow, which fused hard rock with baroque and classical influences. He is regarded as one of the most influential rock guitarists, known for the riffs of "Smoke on the Water" and his classically inspired soloing.

Career

Blackmore began as a session musician in London in the early 1960s. With Deep Purple he recorded landmark albums including Machine Head (1972). He left in 1975, formed Ritchie Blackmore's Rainbow with singer Ronnie James Dio, and scored success with albums such as Rising (1976) and the more commercial Down to Earth (1979). He rejoined Deep Purple from 1984 to 1993.

Blackmore's Night

Since 1997 Blackmore has performed with his partner Candice Night in Blackmore's Night, a Renaissance-inspired folk rock project. In 2016 he briefly returned to rock with a re-formed Rainbow for live dates. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame with Deep Purple in 2016.
