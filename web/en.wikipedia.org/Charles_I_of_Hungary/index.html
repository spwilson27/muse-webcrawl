Charles I, also known as Charles Robert (Hungarian: Károly Róbert; 1288–1342), was King of Hungary and Croatia from 1308 until his death. A member of the Angevin dynasty and great-grandson of Charles I of Naples, he claimed the Hungarian throne after the extinction of the Árpád dynasty in 1301.

Struggle for the throne

Charles arrived in Hungary as a boy claimant backed by the papacy, but rival kings and powerful oligarchs — provincial lords who ruled much of the kingdom as near-independent princes — resisted him for two decades. His decisive victory over the oligarchs came at the Battle of Rozgony in 1312, and by the early 1320s he had reunified the kingdom under royal authority.

Reign

Charles rebuilt royal power on new foundations: he replaced the old baronial guard with loyal officials, reformed taxation and the currency (issuing a stable gold florin), and promoted mining of gold and silver, making Hungary a leading European producer. In 1335 he hosted the Congress of Visegrád, where the kings of Hungary, Bohemia, and Poland agreed trade and alliance terms. He was succeeded by his son Louis I, under whom Hungary reached its medieval height.
