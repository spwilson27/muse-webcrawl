Citroën (French pronunciation: [sitʁɔɛn]) is a French automobile brand. The "Automobiles Citroën" manufacturing company was founded in March 1919 by André Citroën. Citroën has been owned by Stellantis since 2021 and was previously part of the PSA Group after Peugeot acquired 89.95% of the shares in 1976. Citroën's brand name and logo derive from founder André Citroën's surname, with the double-chevron logo referencing the herringbone gears his earlier company produced.

History

André Citroën founded the company in 1919, converting his wartime munitions factory to mass production of automobiles; the Type A 10 HP, launched that year, was the first mass-produced car in Europe with electric starting and lighting. Citroën pioneered modern advertising — including writing the company's name in lights on the Eiffel Tower — and built networks of dealers and service across Europe.

Though bankrupted in 1934 and taken over by Michelin, its largest creditor, Citroën produced landmark cars: the Traction Avant (1934), among the first mass-produced front-wheel-drive cars with a unitary body; the utilitarian 2CV (1948), designed to motorise rural France; and the DS (1955), celebrated for its aerodynamic styling and hydropneumatic self-levelling suspension. The SM grand tourer followed in 1970.

Later years

Financial strain from the Maserati acquisition and development costs led to the 1976 merger with Peugeot into PSA Peugeot Citroën. In 2014 the premium DS line was spun off as the separate DS Automobiles brand. Following the 2021 merger of PSA with Fiat Chrysler Automobiles, Citroën became one of fourteen brands of Stellantis, positioned as a comfort-focused, value-oriented marque; its range includes the C3, C4, C5 Aircross, and electric models such as the ë-C3 and the microcar Ami.

Motorsport

Citroën has a strong rallying record: its World Rally Team won the WRC manufacturers' championship eight times between 2003 and 2012, and Sébastien Loeb won nine consecutive drivers' titles with Citroën cars.
