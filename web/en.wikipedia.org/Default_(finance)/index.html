Default (finance)

Default
FieldFinance, contract law
OppositePerformance / repayment
Related termsDelinquency, bankruptcy, insolvency, credit event

In finance, default is failure to meet the legal obligations (or conditions) of a loan, for example when a home buyer fails to make a mortgage payment, or when a corporation or government fails to pay a bond which has matured. A default may also occur through breach of other covenants of the debt contract, such as maintaining financial ratios, even where payments are current (a "technical default").

Types

Debt service default is failure to make a scheduled payment of interest or principal by its due date (or after any contractual grace period). Technical default is a breach of a covenant or condition other than payment. A sovereign default is the failure of a government to repay its national debt; a strategic default occurs when a borrower who is able to pay deliberately chooses not to, typically where the debt exceeds the value of the underlying asset.

Consequences

Default is an event of default under the loan documents and typically entitles the lender to accelerate the debt (declare the whole balance due), enforce security, and pursue remedies in court or through insolvency proceedings such as bankruptcy. For the borrower, default damages credit ratings, raises future borrowing costs, and may lead to foreclosure or repossession of collateral. Cross-default clauses can cause a default under one instrument to trigger defaults under others.

Distinction from delinquency and bankruptcy

Delinquency means a payment is late; default is the formal failure to perform according to the contract, often declared after a delinquency persists beyond a set period. Bankruptcy is a legal process that may follow default but is not synonymous with it.

Credit derivatives and ratings

Default is the principal "credit event" referenced by credit default swaps. Credit rating agencies track default rates and assign their lowest grades (e.g., "D") to issuers in default. The probability of default is a core input to credit risk models and to regulatory capital frameworks such as Basel.
