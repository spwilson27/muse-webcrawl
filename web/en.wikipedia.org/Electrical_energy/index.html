Electrical energy

Electrical energy is energy derived from the electric potential energy or kinetic energy of charged particles. In everyday usage it refers to the energy delivered by electricity, generated at power stations and transmitted through power grids to consumers.

Electrical energy is measured in joules, or in practice in kilowatt-hours (kWh): one kilowatt-hour equals 3.6 megajoules. It is converted to other forms — light, heat, motion, and sound — by appliances and machines, and can be generated from mechanical energy by generators, from chemical energy by batteries and fuel cells, from light by photovoltaics, and from heat by thermoelectric devices.

Because it is versatile and readily transmitted, electrical energy underpins modern industry, transport electrification, and information technology, though storage at grid scale remains a major engineering challenge.
