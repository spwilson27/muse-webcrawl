Natural language processing (NLP) is a subfield of artificial intelligence and linguistics concerned with enabling computers to process, analyse, and generate human language. Applications range from machine translation and speech recognition to chatbots, search engines, and text summarisation.

History

NLP began in the 1950s with machine translation experiments such as the Georgetown–IBM demonstration of 1954. Early systems were rule-based; Joseph Weizenbaum's ELIZA (1966) simulated conversation with simple pattern matching. From the late 1980s, statistical methods using large corpora displaced hand-written rules. The 2010s brought deep learning: word embeddings such as Word2vec, sequence-to-sequence models, and then the transformer architecture introduced in the 2017 paper "Attention Is All You Need". Transformers underpin large language models such as BERT and the GPT series, which achieved dramatic gains across NLP benchmarks.

Tasks

Core NLP tasks include tokenisation, part-of-speech tagging, parsing, named entity recognition, sentiment analysis, coreference resolution, machine translation, speech recognition and synthesis, information retrieval, question answering, and text generation. Evaluation relies on shared benchmarks, though benchmark saturation and dataset bias are persistent concerns.

Challenges

Human language is ambiguous, context-dependent, and rich in idiom, sarcasm, and world knowledge. NLP systems can reproduce biases in their training data and generate fluent but false statements ("hallucinations"), issues that remain active areas of research in alignment, interpretability, and evaluation.
