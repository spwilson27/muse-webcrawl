Wire (band)

Wire are an English rock band, formed in London in 1976 by Colin Newman (vocals, guitar), Graham Lewis (bass, vocals), Bruce Gilbert (guitar) and Robert Grey (aka Robert Gotobed; drums). They are considered a major influence on post-punk and alternative rock, and are known for their concise, artful songs and willingness to experiment throughout their long career.

History

Wire formed in Watford and London in 1976, emerging from an art-school background rather than the pub-rock circuit. Their debut album, Pink Flag (1977), was a landmark of the punk era: 21 short, fragmented songs that stripped rock down to its essentials while displaying considerable wit and abstraction.

The follow-ups Chairs Missing (1978) and 154 (1979) expanded their sound with keyboards, atmospherics and more complex arrangements, prefiguring post-punk and influencing generations of musicians. Tensions with their label EMI led to a hiatus in 1980, during which members pursued solo and collaborative projects such as Dome and Duet Emmo.

The band re-formed in 1985, embracing electronic production on albums such as The Ideal Copy (1987) and A Bell Is a Cup (1988), including the alternative hit "Eardrum Buzz". After another break in the early 1990s, Wire returned in 1999, and with the Read & Burn EPs and Send (2003) reasserted their punk roots with a harsh, minimal sound.

Later work

Guitarist Bruce Gilbert left in 2004 and was eventually replaced by Matthew Simms. Albums including Object 47 (2008), Red Barked Tree (2011), Change Becomes Us (2013), Wire (2015), Nocturnal Koreans (2016), Silver/Lead (2017) and Mind Hive (2020) have been released on the band's own Pinkflag label. Wire are perennially cited as an influence by bands from R.E.M. and The Cure to Elastica, Franz Ferdinand and Protomartyr.
