Battle of the Camel

DateDecember 656 CE
LocationNear Basra, Iraq
ResultVictory for Ali's forces
BelligerentsRashidun Caliphate (Ali) vs. forces of Aisha, Talha and Zubayr
CommandersAli ibn Abi Talib vs. Aisha bint Abi Bakr; Talha ibn Ubayd Allah (killed); Zubayr ibn al-Awwam (killed)

The Battle of the Camel (Arabic: معركة الجمل), also known as the Battle of Jamal or the Battle of Basra, was fought in December 656 CE near Basra in present-day Iraq. It was the first battle of the First Fitna, the first Muslim civil war, and pitted the forces of the caliph Ali ibn Abi Talib against an army led by Aisha, the widow of Muhammad, together with the companions Talha and Zubayr. The battle takes its name from the camel on which Aisha rode in the centre of her army.

The melee around Aisha's camel gave the battle its name.

Background

Following the murder of the caliph Uthman in 656, Ali was chosen as caliph in Medina. Aisha, Talha and Zubayr rejected his authority, demanding vengeance for Uthman, and seized Basra. Ali marched from Arabia into Iraq, raised support in Kufa, and advanced to meet them. Negotiations nearly produced a settlement before fighting broke out — tradition holds that extremists on both sides provoked the clash.

The battle

The fighting centred on Aisha's camel, around which her followers rallied and died in large numbers as they successively seized its reins. Talha was killed by an arrow and Zubayr withdrew from the field, to be killed shortly afterwards. When Ali's men hamstrung the camel and it fell, the battle ended. Casualty figures in the sources are large and uncertain, running into the thousands.

Aftermath

Ali treated the defeated with notable leniency: Aisha was escorted honourably back to Medina, and the dead of both sides were buried. The victory secured Iraq for Ali, who made Kufa his capital, but it did not settle the larger conflict — Muawiyah in Syria remained in revolt, leading to the confrontation at Siffin the following year.
