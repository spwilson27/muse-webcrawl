French (français, [fʁɑ̃sɛ]) is a Romance language of the Indo-European family. It descended from the Vulgar Latin of the Roman Empire, as did all Romance languages, and evolved from Gallo-Romance, the Latin spoken in Gaul, and more specifically in Northern Gaul.

French is an official language in 27 countries, as well as one of the most geographically widespread languages. Estimates of native speakers range around 80 million, with total speakers around 310 million worldwide, spread across Europe, Africa, the Americas and Oceania. It is a working language of the United Nations, the European Union, the International Olympic Committee and many other international organisations.

History

French evolved from the Gallo-Romance dialects spoken after the Roman conquest of Gaul, heavily influenced by the Celtic Gaulish substrate and the Frankish superstrate. The Strasbourg Oaths of 842 are often considered the earliest text in a form of early French. Old French gave rise to Middle French during the Renaissance, when the Ordinance of Villers-Cotterêts (1539) made French the language of French law and administration. The Académie Française, founded in 1635, was charged with standardising and purifying the language. French became the dominant language of European diplomacy and culture in the 17th and 18th centuries.

Geographic distribution

French is the sole official language of France and Monaco, and co-official in Belgium, Switzerland, Luxembourg, Canada, and numerous African states including Senegal, Ivory Coast, Mali and the Democratic Republic of the Congo, which has the largest French-speaking population of any country. Overseas, it is spoken in Quebec, Louisiana, Haiti, French Guiana and the French Pacific territories.

Phonology

French is noted for its nasal vowels, front rounded vowels, the uvular /ʁ/, and liaison — the pronunciation of normally silent final consonants before vowels. Word stress falls regularly on the final syllable of a phrase. The spelling is conservative and preserves many letters no longer pronounced, a legacy of Latin orthography.

Grammar

French nouns have two genders (masculine and feminine) and articles agree in gender and number. Verbs conjugate for tense, aspect, mood and person, and are grouped traditionally into three conjugation classes. Word order is generally subject–verb–object. The formal second-person pronoun vous contrasts with informal tu.

Vocabulary

The core vocabulary is Latin in origin, with Celtic and Frankish contributions and later borrowings from Italian, Spanish, Arabic and, increasingly, English — a trend the Académie and laws such as the Toubon Law seek to resist in official usage.
