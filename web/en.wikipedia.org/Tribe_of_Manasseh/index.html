Tribe of Manasseh

According to the Hebrew Bible, the Tribe of Manasseh (Hebrew: מְנַשֶּׁה, Menaše) was one of the Tribes of Israel. It is one of the ten lost tribes. Together with the Tribe of Ephraim, Manasseh formed the Tribe of Joseph. The tribe was named after Manasseh, the elder son of Joseph, who was born in Egypt to Asenath.

Territory

Uniquely among the tribes, Manasseh's territory was divided into two portions by the Jordan River: a western half in the central hill country north of Ephraim (sometimes called the half-tribe of Manasseh), and an eastern half in Transjordan, in the regions of Gilead and Bashan, granted to the families of Machir. This made Manasseh one of the geographically largest tribal territories.

Biblical figures

Gideon, the judge who defeated the Midianites, was a Manassite from Ophrah, and the judge Jephthah came from Gilead in eastern Manasseh. After the Assyrian conquest of the northern kingdom (c. 722 BCE), the tribe is counted among the Ten Lost Tribes.
