Mehmed Kâmil Pasha (1833 – 14 November 1913), also spelled Kamil Pasha, was an Ottoman statesman of Turkish Cypriot origin who served four times as Grand Vizier of the Ottoman Empire during the reigns of Abdülhamid II and Mehmed V.

Early life and career

Born in Nicosia, Cyprus, Kâmil Pasha entered government service and rose through provincial administration, serving as governor of several provinces including Jerusalem and Aleppo. His competence brought him to the attention of Sultan Abdülhamid II, who appointed him Grand Vizier for the first time in 1885.

Grand vizierates

His first term (1885–1891) was marked by administrative reform and a generally pro-British orientation in foreign policy. He returned to office briefly in 1895, again in 1908 after the Young Turk Revolution restored the constitution, and finally in October 1912 during the First Balkan War.

Downfall and death

His last government was overthrown in the 1913 Ottoman coup d'état (the Raid on the Sublime Porte), carried out by officers of the Committee of Union and Progress, partly in reaction to his willingness to negotiate over Edirne. He retired to Cyprus, where he died later that year.

Legacy

Kâmil Pasha is remembered as one of the last great grand viziers of the old regime — an able administrator whose career spanned the empire's transformation from autocracy to constitutional politics.
