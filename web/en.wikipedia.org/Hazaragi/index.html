Hazaragi

Hazaragi (Persian: هزارگی) is a variety of Persian spoken by the Hazara people, primarily in the Hazarajat region of central Afghanistan, with large speaker communities in Pakistan (notably Quetta) and Iran, and in a worldwide diaspora. It is closely related to Dari, the Afghan standard of Persian, but is distinguished by a substantial vocabulary borrowed from Turkic and Mongolic languages — a legacy of the Hazara's history — alongside archaisms lost in other Persian dialects.

Classification and features

Linguists generally treat Hazaragi as a dialect of Persian within the Dari group, though it is not fully mutually intelligible with standard Dari in its most divergent rural forms. Its lexicon includes many words of Mongolian and Turkic origin — estimates of the non-Persian share run to a fifth of everyday vocabulary — reflecting the Hazara's descent in part from Mongol and Turkic soldiers settled in the region from the 13th century onward. Phonologically it preserves diphthongs and vowel distinctions found in classical Persian.

Speakers and status

Hazaragi is the home language of most Hazaras in Afghanistan, where speakers are concentrated in Bamyan, Daykundi, Ghazni, Uruzgan and parts of Maidan Wardak provinces. In Quetta, Pakistan, a long-established Hazara community maintains the language, as do refugee communities in Iran and diaspora populations in Australia, Europe and North America. Hazaragi has no official status; education and media use standard Dari. It is nevertheless written informally in the Perso-Arabic script, and it has a rich oral tradition of proverbs, folktales and religious poetry. Because of language shift toward Dari and Urdu among younger urban speakers, some varieties are considered vulnerable.

Culture

Hazaragi oral literature includes the epic traditions and elegies (marsia) of Shia Islam, the faith of most Hazaras. The language appears in contemporary Hazara music and cinema, and diaspora organisations run weekend schools teaching it to children.
