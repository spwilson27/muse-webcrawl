Stramenopile

The stramenopiles, also called heterokonts, are a major lineage of eukaryotes containing more than 100,000 known species, many of them of great ecological and economic importance. Most are algae, ranging from the giant multicellular kelps to unicellular diatoms, which are a primary component of plankton. Others include the oomycetes (water moulds), which contain notorious plant pathogens such as Phytophthora infestans, the cause of potato blight.

The name "stramenopile" means "straw hair", referring to the stiff tripartite hairs (mastigonemes) that coat the forward flagellum of motile cells. The alternative name "heterokont" refers to the two dissimilar flagella — one smooth and one hairy — characteristic of the group's swimming cells.

Characteristics

Stramenopiles are united by the structure of their flagellar hairs: each mastigoneme is made of three parts — a basal section, a tubular shaft, and terminal filaments. Photosynthetic stramenopiles (the ochrophytes) carry chloroplasts derived from a red alga by secondary endosymbiosis, surrounded by four membranes; their main accessory pigment is fucoxanthin, which gives brown and golden algae their colour. Many non-photosynthetic groups, such as oomycetes, were once classified with fungi because of their filamentous, absorptive way of life, but molecular phylogenetics places them firmly within the stramenopiles.

Ecology and importance

Diatoms are responsible for a large fraction — often estimated at around a fifth to a quarter — of the world's primary production and oxygen generation. Brown algae form kelp forests that are among the most productive marine ecosystems. Oomycetes include devastating crop pathogens (Phytophthora, Pythium) and the downy mildews. Stramenopiles also include the causal agents of some fish diseases and economically important decomposers in aquatic food webs.

Classification

Within the SAR supergroup, stramenopiles are the sister group of the alveolates and Rhizaria. Major sublineages include the Ochrophyta (photosynthetic groups such as diatoms, brown algae, golden algae, yellow-green algae, raphidophytes and eustigmatophytes), the Pseudofungi (oomycetes and hyphochytriomycetes), and the "Bigyra" (opalines, labyrinthulids, bicosoecids and others). The classification was proposed by Thomas Cavalier-Smith and refined by molecular studies.
