Ralt was a British manufacturer of single-seater racing cars founded in 1974 by Ron Tauranac, the designer who had previously co-founded Brabham with Jack Brabham. The company name derives from Ron and Lewis Tauranac — Ron and his brother Austin Lewis Tauranac. Ralt became one of the most successful constructors in Formula Three and Formula Two during the late 1970s and 1980s.

History

After leaving Brabham in the early 1970s, Ron Tauranac established Ralt to build customer racing cars. The early RT1 was produced in large numbers for Formula Three, Formula Atlantic and Formula Super Vee, with variants tailored to each category's regulations. Ralt cars won numerous championships, establishing the marque as a mainstay of junior single-seater racing.

The RT3 dominated Formula Three in the early 1980s; drivers such as Ayrton Senna won the British Formula Three Championship in a Ralt RT3 in 1983. The RT4 was a highly successful Formula Atlantic and Formula Mondial design, particularly in Australia and North America. In Formula Two, the works Ralt-Honda team won the European championship in 1981 with Geoff Lees, and Ralt RH6 cars won further titles in the mid-1980s.

Later years

As Formula 3000 replaced Formula Two in 1985, Ralt produced the RB20 and RT23 among other designs, but faced growing competition from Reynard and Lola. In 1989 the company was taken over by March Engineering. Tauranac continued designing cars for some years, including Formula Holden designs in Australia.

Notable models

  • RT1 — Formula Three / Atlantic / Super Vee (1975–)
  • RT2 — Formula Two (1979)
  • RT3 — Formula Three (1979–1984)
  • RT4 — Formula Atlantic (1980–)
  • RT5 — Formula Super Vee
  • RH6 — Formula Two (1981–1984)
  • RT20 / RB20 — Formula 3000
