Electroacoustic music

Electroacoustic music is a genre of Western art music in which composers use technology to manipulate the timbres of acoustic sounds, sometimes by electronic means. It originated around the middle of the 20th century.

History

The genre grew out of two post-war schools: musique concrète, developed in Paris by Pierre Schaeffer using recorded sounds on tape, and elektronische Musik, developed in Cologne by composers such as Karlheinz Stockhausen using electronically generated tones. American composers including Edgard Varèse and the Columbia-Princeton Electronic Music Center also pioneered the field.

Techniques

Composers record, edit, and transform sounds using tape splicing, looping, speed changes, filtering, and later digital sampling and computer processing. The finished work is typically fixed on a recording medium and diffused through loudspeakers in concert, often without live performers.

Acousmatic music

Works intended to be heard without seeing their sound sources are termed acousmatic, a practice central to Schaeffer's theory of the sound object and reduced listening.
