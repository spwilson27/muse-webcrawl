Master of Business Administration

The Master of Business Administration (MBA) is a graduate degree in business administration and management. Originating in the United States in the early 20th century, it is now offered worldwide in full-time, part-time, executive, and online formats.

Curriculum

Core courses typically cover accounting, finance, marketing, organizational behaviour, operations, strategy, economics, and business ethics, with electives and concentrations. Many programmes use the case method, pioneered by Harvard Business School (founded 1908, which awarded the first MBA in 1910).

Admissions and formats

Admission usually requires an undergraduate degree, work experience, and often the GMAT or GRE. Executive MBA programmes serve experienced managers; one-year formats are common in Europe (e.g., INSEAD), while US programmes traditionally run two years.

Criticism and value

Debate surrounds tuition cost, return on investment, and whether MBA teaching adequately covers ethics and entrepreneurship. Accreditation bodies include AACSB, EQUIS, and AMBA.
