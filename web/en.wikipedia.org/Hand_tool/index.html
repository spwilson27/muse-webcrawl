Hand tool

TypeTool powered by the user's muscles
ExamplesHammer, screwdriver, saw, wrench, pliers
ContrastPower tool

A hand tool is any tool that is powered by the hand rather than by a motor or other external power source. Hand tools include hammers, screwdrivers, wrenches, pliers, chisels, files, hand saws, planes, and knives, among many others used in trades, crafts, and households.

History

Hand tools are among the oldest technologies: stone handaxes date back over a million years, and bronze and iron tools transformed agriculture and craft in antiquity. Guilds of toolmakers flourished in the medieval and early modern periods, and the Industrial Revolution standardised tool production even as powered machinery took over many tasks. Many traditional designs have changed little in centuries.

Categories

Hand tools are commonly grouped as cutting tools (saws, chisels, shears), striking tools (hammers, mallets), gripping tools (pliers, vises), fastening tools (screwdrivers, wrenches), measuring and marking tools (rules, squares, levels), and shaping tools (files, rasps, planes). Safety and ergonomics — handle design, vibration, and repetitive-strain risks — are important considerations in modern tool design.
