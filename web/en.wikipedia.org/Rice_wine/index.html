Type: Alcoholic beverage
Origin: East and Southeast Asia
Main ingredient: Fermented rice

Rice wine

Rice wine is a family of alcoholic beverages made from fermented rice, produced across East, Southeast, and South Asia. Unlike grape wine, which is made by fermenting fruit sugar directly, rice wine requires the starches in rice to be converted into sugars by moulds or enzymes before yeast can ferment them into alcohol.

Varieties

Well-known rice wines include Japanese sake and shōchū (distilled), Chinese huangjiu (such as Shaoxing wine) and baijiu (distilled, though often grain-based), Korean makgeolli and cheongju, and Vietnamese rượu gạo. Rice wines range from milky and lightly alcoholic to clear, aged, and strong.

Production

Production typically begins with polished, steamed rice. A starter culture — koji mould (Aspergillus oryzae) in Japan, or yeast-and-mould cakes in China and Southeast Asia — converts starch to sugar while yeast ferments the sugar, often simultaneously. The result is pressed, filtered, pasteurised, and sometimes aged.

Culinary use

Besides drinking, rice wines such as Shaoxing wine and mirin are widely used in cooking for marinades, sauces, and deglazing.
