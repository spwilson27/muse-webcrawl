Chernobyl Exclusion Zone

The Chernobyl Exclusion Zone is an officially designated exclusion area around the site of the Chernobyl nuclear reactor disaster in northern Ukraine. It covers an area of approximately 2,600 square kilometres surrounding the Chernobyl Nuclear Power Plant, within which radioactive contamination from fallout is highest and public access and habitation are restricted.

Chernobyl Exclusion Zone

Location: Kyiv Oblast and Zhytomyr Oblast, Ukraine

Area: approx. 2,600 km²

Established: 1986

Administered by: State Agency of Ukraine on Exclusion Zone Management

Establishment

The zone was established by the Soviet armed forces soon after the disaster of 26 April 1986. Initially a restricted area of 30 km radius around the plant, it was later expanded to cover the territory most heavily contaminated by caesium-137 and strontium-90. More than 100,000 people were evacuated from the zone, including the entire population of the city of Pripyat and the town of Chernobyl.

Present day

Radiation levels have fallen substantially since 1986, and the zone has become an inadvertent wildlife sanctuary, home to wolves, lynx, elk and reintroduced Przewalski's horses. Several hundred self-settlers, mostly elderly people, returned to their villages and live in the zone's less contaminated areas. Regulated tourism began in the 2000s, and the ruined Reactor No. 4 was enclosed by the New Safe Confinement structure completed in 2016.
