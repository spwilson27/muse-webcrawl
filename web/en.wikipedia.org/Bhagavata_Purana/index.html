Bhagavata Purana

The Bhagavata Purana (Sanskrit: Bhāgavata Purāṇa), also known as the Srimad Bhagavatam, is one of Hinduism's eighteen great Puranas (Mahapuranas). Composed in Sanskrit and traditionally attributed to the sage Vyasa, it promotes devotion (bhakti) towards Krishna, regarded by devotees as the supreme God and the source of all avatars of Vishnu. It is one of the most widely studied and influential texts of the Vaishnava tradition.

Contents

The text consists of twelve books (skandhas) containing some 18,000 verses. After opening books on creation, cosmology, and the stories of devotees such as Dhruva and Prahlada, the work culminates in the tenth book, which narrates the life of Krishna in detail: his birth in Mathura, childhood pastimes in Vrindavan with the gopis — including the celebrated Rasa Lila — his slaying of Kamsa, and his career as a prince of Dvaraka. The eleventh book contains the Uddhava Gita, Krishna's final teachings, and the twelfth prophesies the evils of the Kali Yuga.

Date and influence

Scholars generally date the text's composition to roughly the 9th or 10th century CE, probably in South India, though its traditions are older. The Bhagavata shaped the devotional cultures of Gaudiya Vaishnavism (the tradition of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu), the Vallabha sampradaya, and the Ekasarana dharma of Assam, and its stories have been retold in countless regional-language versions, paintings, dances such as Kathakali and Bharatanatyam, and the week-long public recitations known as Bhagavata saptaha.
