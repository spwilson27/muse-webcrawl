National Diet

The National Diet (国会, Kokkai) is the bicameral legislature of Japan. It is composed of a lower house, the House of Representatives, and an upper house, the House of Councillors. Both houses are directly elected under a parallel voting system.

TypeBicameral
HousesHouse of Representatives (465 seats); House of Councillors (248 seats)
Meeting placeNational Diet Building, Tokyo
Established1890 (as Imperial Diet); 1947 in current form

History

The Diet was first convened in 1890 under the Meiji Constitution as the Imperial Diet. The 1947 Constitution made it "the highest organ of state power" and the sole law-making organ of the state, with the House of Representatives able to override the upper house on budgets, treaties, and designation of the prime minister.

Powers

The Diet enacts legislation, approves the budget, ratifies treaties, designates the prime minister, and can initiate constitutional amendments. Ordinary sessions run 150 days from January; extraordinary and special sessions may be called.

Building

The National Diet Building, completed in 1936 in Nagatachō, Tokyo, is built of Japanese materials and is crowned by a pyramid-topped central tower.
