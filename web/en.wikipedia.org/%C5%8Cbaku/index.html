Ōbaku

Ōbaku-shū is one of the three main schools of Japanese Zen Buddhism, alongside Sōtō and Rinzai. It was introduced to Japan in the mid-17th century by the Chinese monk Ingen Ryūki (Yinyuan Longqi) and his disciples fleeing the fall of the Ming dynasty.

History

Ingen arrived in Nagasaki in 1654 and in 1661 was granted land at Uji, near Kyoto, where he founded the temple Manpuku-ji, built in contemporary Ming Chinese style. Ōbaku practice combined Zen meditation with Pure Land recitation of the nembutsu, and the school introduced Ming-era culture — including sencha tea, calligraphy and architecture — to Edo-period Japan.

Today

Manpuku-ji remains the head temple of Ōbaku, which maintains a number of temples and practitioners across Japan.
