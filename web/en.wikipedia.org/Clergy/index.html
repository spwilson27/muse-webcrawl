Clergy

Clergy
Role: Formal religious leadership
Examples: priests, bishops, imams, rabbis, monks
Counterpart: Laity

Clergy are formal leaders within established religions. Their functions vary by religion and denomination, but generally include conducting worship, teaching doctrine, administering sacraments or rites of passage such as marriages and funerals, and providing pastoral care to members of a faith community.

Christianity

In the Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, and Anglican traditions, clergy are ordained to the orders of bishop, priest, and deacon. Catholic clergy in the Latin Church are generally required to be celibate. Many Protestant churches recognize pastors, elders, and deacons, and some traditions place greater emphasis on the "priesthood of all believers," blurring the distinction between clergy and laity.

Other religions

In Islam, religious leaders include imams who lead prayer, scholars (ulama), and muftis who issue legal opinions, though Islam has no ordained priesthood in the Christian sense. In Judaism, rabbis serve as teachers and interpreters of the law. Hindu priests (pujari) perform temple rituals, and Buddhist monks and nuns (the sangha) follow monastic discipline while lay teachers may also lead communities.

Legal status

Many countries grant clergy particular legal privileges or duties, such as the confidentiality of confession, the authority to solemnize marriages, tax provisions for clergy housing, and exemptions or special provisions regarding military service.
