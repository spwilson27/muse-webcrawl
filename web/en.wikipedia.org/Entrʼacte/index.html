Entr'acte

Entr'acte (French: "between acts"; also spelled entracte and sometimes called an interlude in English usage) is a piece of music performed between the acts of a play or opera, or more broadly any performance or entertainment presented during an interval. In theatre practice, the entr'acte keeps the audience engaged while scenery is changed and gives the orchestra or other performers a featured role between portions of the drama.

An orchestra performing during the interval of an opera.

Usage in opera and theatre

In Baroque and classical opera, instrumental sinfonias and ballet divertissements filled the spaces between acts; composers of the nineteenth century produced celebrated entr'actes that became concert pieces in their own right. Famous examples include the entr'acte music in Bizet's Carmen (notably the intermezzo before Act III), the intermezzo of Mascagni's Cavalleria rusticana, the "Méditation" from Massenet's Thaïs (performed between scenes), and the entr'actes of Schubert's incidental music to Rosamunde. Rossini and Donizetti wrote sparkling overture-like entr'actes; in ballet, divertissements inserted between acts allowed displays of virtuoso dancing loosely connected to the plot.

In spoken theatre, entr'acte music historically covered scene changes and maintained atmosphere; the modern musical theatre retains the practice with an "Entr'acte" number opening the second act, usually a medley of the show's themes played by the orchestra as the audience returns to their seats. Film exhibition once borrowed the practice, with intermission music in roadshow presentations of epics.

The 1924 film

Entr'acte is also the title of a famous French Dada short film directed by René Clair with music by Erik Satie, made to be shown during the interval of the ballet Relâche (1924) by Francis Picabia for the Ballets Suédois. Its playful, non-narrative images — including a chess game between Marcel Duchamp and Man Ray, a runaway hearse, and figures filmed on Paris rooftops — made it a landmark of avant-garde cinema, and it exemplifies the original sense of the word: entertainment between the acts.
