Desdemona

Desdemona is a character in William Shakespeare's tragedy Othello (c. 1601–1604). She is the Venetian wife of Othello, the Moorish general.

Role

Desdemona elopes with Othello, defying her father Brabantio. In Cyprus, Iago manipulates Othello into believing she has been unfaithful with Cassio; her dropped handkerchief becomes false evidence. Othello smothers her in their bed, then kills himself on learning her innocence.

Interpretation

Desdemona is traditionally read as a figure of love, loyalty and innocence, though modern criticism also stresses her assertiveness in choosing her marriage. Her name derives from the Greek for "ill-starred".
