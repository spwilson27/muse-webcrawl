Kingdom of Israel (united monarchy)

The Kingdom of Israel, often called the United Monarchy, is the kingdom described in the Hebrew Bible as uniting the Israelite tribes under the kings Saul, David, and Solomon in approximately the 11th–10th centuries BC, with its capital at Jerusalem.

Biblical account

According to the biblical narrative, the prophet Samuel anointed Saul as the first king, around 1020 BC. After Saul's death at Mount Gilboa, David — who had defeated the Philistines — was proclaimed king, conquered Jerusalem, and made it his capital and the home of the Ark of the Covenant. David's son Solomon (c. 970–931 BC) is remembered for building the First Temple, expanding trade, diplomacy and monumental building, and legendary wisdom. On Solomon's death the kingdom split into the northern Kingdom of Israel (Samaria) and the southern Kingdom of Judah.

Historicity and archaeology

The extent and even existence of a united monarchy is debated among archaeologists and historians. The Tel Dan Stele (9th century BC) refers to the "House of David," supporting a historical Davidic dynasty. Scholars in the "minimalist" camp argue that 10th-century Jerusalem was a modest highland chiefdom, while others point to fortified sites such as Khirbet Qeiyafa and large-scale building in the City of David as evidence of a genuine early state. Mainstream views generally accept an historical David and Solomon ruling a smaller kingdom than the Bible describes.

Legacy

The united monarchy occupies a central place in Jewish tradition as a golden age of Israelite unity, and its division is treated in the Bible as a consequence of Solomon's idolatry in old age.
