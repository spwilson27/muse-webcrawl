Dronten is a municipality and town in the central Netherlands, in the province of Flevoland. It stands on land reclaimed from the IJsselmeer as part of the Zuiderzee Works: the Eastern Flevoland polder was drained in 1957, and the town of Dronten, designed from scratch by planners, received its first inhabitants in 1962. The municipality, formally established in 1972, also comprises the villages of Biddinghuizen and Swifterbant.

Geography and development

Dronten lies several metres below sea level, ringed by the bordering lakes Veluwemeer, Ketelmeer, and Drontermeer. Its street plan is a notable example of post-war Dutch planning, with a radial layout, a central meeting church (De Meerpaal), and separated traffic for cars and bicycles. The surrounding polder is devoted mainly to arable farming, for which the new land was originally intended, and the municipality is among the largest in the Netherlands by area.

Economy and culture

The area hosts the Walibi Holland theme park at Biddinghuizen and the Lowlands music festival, one of the Netherlands' largest outdoor festivals, held annually near Biddinghuizen. The agricultural university of applied sciences Aeres Hogeschool has a campus in Dronten, and the town is connected by the Hanzelijn railway to Zwolle and Lelystad, with a station opened in 2012. Swifterbant gives its name to the Neolithic Swifterbant culture, whose sites in the polder revealed some of the oldest farming settlements in the region.
