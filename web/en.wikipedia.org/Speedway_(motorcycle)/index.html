Motorcycle speedway

Motorcycle speedway, usually referred to simply as speedway, is a motorcycle sport involving four and sometimes up to six riders competing over four anti-clockwise laps of an oval circuit. The motorcycles are specialist machines that use only one gear and have no brakes; racing takes place on a flat oval track usually consisting of dirt, loosely packed shale, or crushed rock.

History

The sport originated in Australia and the United Kingdom in the 1920s, with the first meetings held at West Maitland, New South Wales, in 1923 and in England at Droylsden in 1927. League racing became enormously popular in Britain after the Second World War, and the sport later spread strongly to Poland, Sweden, Denmark and Russia, where it remains a major spectator sport.

The bikes

Speedway machines are powered by 500 cc single-cylinder four-stroke engines running on methanol. They accelerate faster than most road cars over short distances, yet have no brakes and no gearbox; riders slow by easing off the throttle and by sliding the machine sideways ("broadsiding") into corners.

Competitions

The premier individual event is the Speedway Grand Prix series, which since 1995 has decided the world championship through rounds across Europe. Major team competitions include the British Premiership, Poland's Ekstraliga — the most lucrative league in the sport — and the Speedway World Cup and Speedway of Nations.
