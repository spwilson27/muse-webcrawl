Medinet Habu (Arabic: مدينة هابو) is an archaeological locality on the west bank of Luxor, Egypt, at the southern end of the Theban Necropolis. It is best known for the Mortuary Temple of Ramesses III, a massive and remarkably well-preserved New Kingdom temple complex enclosed within a fortified mudbrick wall. The site's ancient name was Djanet.

The temple of Ramesses III (reigned c. 1186–1155 BC) is modelled on the Ramesseum of Ramesses II and covers some 210 by 300 metres, with about 7,000 square metres of decorated wall reliefs. Its pylons and walls record Ramesses III's wars, most famously his victories over the Sea Peoples, the Libyans and the Nubians — inscriptions that are a primary historical source for the upheavals at the end of the Bronze Age. A unique fortified gatehouse (migdol) imitates a Syrian fortress.

Other structures

The enclosure also contains the earlier Small Temple of Amun, begun in the 18th Dynasty under Hatshepsut and Thutmose III and enlarged over many centuries; chapels of the God's Wives of Amun of the Third Intermediate Period; and remains of a Coptic town whose church occupied the temple's second court. The site was excavated and recorded extensively by the Oriental Institute of the University of Chicago in the 1920s and 1930s.
