The hero's journey, also known as the monomyth, is a common narrative pattern identified by the comparative mythologist Joseph Campbell in his 1949 book The Hero with a Thousand Faces. Campbell argued that myths from around the world share a fundamental structure in which a hero ventures from the ordinary world into a realm of supernatural wonder, wins a decisive victory, and returns transformed with the power to bestow boons on others.

Structure

Campbell organised the journey into three acts — Departure, Initiation and Return — subdivided into seventeen stages, including the call to adventure, refusal of the call, supernatural aid, crossing the threshold, trials, the ordeal, the ultimate boon, and the return across the threshold as master of two worlds.

Influence and criticism

The framework profoundly influenced modern storytelling; George Lucas credited it in shaping Star Wars, and screenwriting guides such as Christopher Vogler's The Writer's Journey popularised simplified versions for film and fiction. Folklorists and mythologists have criticised Campbell for over-generalising, selectively reading myths, and forcing diverse traditions into a single template.
