Nagarjunakonda (meaning "Nagarjuna Hill") is a historical site and island located near Nagarjuna Sagar in Nalgonda district of Telangana, India. It was the capital of the Ikshvaku dynasty (c. 225–325 CE), successors of the Satavahanas in the eastern Deccan, and one of the most important centres of Buddhism in ancient India. The site is named in modern times after Nagarjuna, the 2nd-century Mahayana philosopher believed to have lived in the region.

History

Known in inscriptions as Vijayapuri, the city flourished under Ikshvaku kings such as Virapurushadatta, whose queens and royal ladies patronised dozens of Buddhist monasteries, stupas and temples. Excavations revealed more than 30 monasteries, as well as Hindu temples, a palace complex, baths, an amphitheatre-like stadium and wharves on the Krishna River — evidence of a prosperous port city trading as far as the Roman world.

Salvage and submergence

The valley was excavated in campaigns from the 1920s to 1960. When the Nagarjuna Sagar dam was built across the Krishna in the 1960s, the original site was submerged by the reservoir. Monuments were carefully dismantled and reconstructed on the hilltop, which became an island in the lake, and a site museum displays the celebrated sculpted panels depicting scenes from the life of the Buddha in the distinctive Amaravati style.

Tourism

Nagarjunakonda is accessible by ferry from Vijayapuri. The island preserves reconstructed stupas, viharas and a museum; nearby attractions include the Nagarjuna Sagar dam and the Ethipothala Falls.
