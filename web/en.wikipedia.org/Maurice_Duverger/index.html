Maurice Duverger

Maurice Duverger (5 June 1917 – 16 December 2014) was a French jurist, political scientist and politician, known for his work on political parties and electoral systems.

Duverger's law

Duverger is best known for Duverger's law, the proposition that single-member plurality electoral systems tend to produce two-party systems, while proportional representation tends to favour multiparty systems. The idea, developed in his 1951 book Les partis politiques (Political Parties), explained the effect both mechanically (smaller parties are under-represented) and psychologically (voters and elites avoid wasting votes).

Life and career

Born in Angoulême, Duverger taught law and political science at Bordeaux and at the Sorbonne in Paris, and helped establish political science as an academic discipline in France. He wrote extensively for Le Monde and served as a member of the European Parliament from 1989 to 1994, elected on the Italian Communist Party list. Among his other concepts is the "semi-presidential" regime, a term he coined for systems such as France's Fifth Republic.
