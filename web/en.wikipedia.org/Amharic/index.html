Amharic (Amharic: አማርኛ, Amarəñña) is an Ethiopian Semitic language, which is a subgrouping within the Semitic branch of the Afroasiatic languages. It is spoken as a first language by the Amharas and as a lingua franca by other populations residing in major cities and towns of Ethiopia.

Amharic is the official working language of the Ethiopian federal government and is also the working language of several of Ethiopia's federal regions. With over 33 million native speakers and more than 25 million second-language speakers, it is the most widely spoken language in Ethiopia and the second most spoken Semitic language in the world after Arabic.

Writing system

Amharic is written left-to-right using a system that grew out of the Ge'ez script, known as an abugida: each character represents a consonant+vowel syllable, and characters are grouped in "orders" sharing a consonantal base. The script has 33 basic characters, each with seven forms depending on the vowel.

Grammar

Unlike most Semitic languages, Amharic typically uses subject–object–verb word order. It marks definiteness and case with suffixes, and it encodes gender, number, and respect in its pronouns and verb agreements.
