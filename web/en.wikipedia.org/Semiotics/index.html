Semiotics

Semiotics (also called semiology or semiotic studies) is the systematic study of sign processes and the communication of meaning. A sign is anything that communicates a meaning not present in itself — words, images, sounds, gestures, traffic lights, articles of clothing and works of art can all function as signs. Semiotics asks how meaning is made and understood, and how representations are created, shared and interpreted within culture.

Founders

The discipline has two principal founding traditions. The Swiss linguist Ferdinand de Saussure proposed a dyadic model of the sign as the union of a signifier (the form the sign takes) and a signified (the concept it represents), and stressed that the link between them is arbitrary and determined by the system of a language. The American philosopher Charles Sanders Peirce developed a triadic model — a representamen (sign vehicle) stands for an object by generating an interpretant in the mind of an interpreter — and classified signs as icons (which resemble their objects), indexes (connected causally or physically, like smoke to fire) and symbols (related by convention).

Development

In the 20th century semiotics was applied far beyond language. Roland Barthes analysed the "mythologies" of modern culture — advertisements, wrestling, fashion — showing how second-order meanings (connotations) naturalise ideology. Umberto Eco made semiotics central to theories of interpretation and the "open work"; A. J. Greimas, Juri Lotman and the Tartu–Moscow school extended it to narrative and culture as a whole. Zoosemiotics and biosemiotics study sign processes in animals and in living systems generally.

Branches and applications

Semiotic analysis is used in linguistics, literary theory, media and film studies, anthropology, marketing and design (for example, in analysing branding), architecture, and the study of human–computer interaction and emoji. Its three classical branches are semantics (signs and what they stand for), syntactics (relations among signs) and pragmatics (signs in use). Critics have charged semiotics with jargon and over-extension — in its broadest form treating all culture as text — but its core concepts (denotation/connotation, codes, and the arbitrariness of the sign) remain standard tools of cultural analysis.
