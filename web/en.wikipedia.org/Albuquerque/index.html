Albuquerque is the most populous city in the US state of New Mexico. Its nicknames, "The Duke City" and "Burque", both reference its founding in 1706 as a Spanish colonial villa named in honour of the Duke of Alburquerque, then viceroy of New Spain. Located in the Albuquerque Basin of the Rio Grande valley, flanked by the Sandia Mountains to the east, the city is one of the highest-elevation major cities in the United States.

History

The area has been inhabited for millennia; the petroglyphs preserved in Petroglyph National Monument attest to early settlement along the Rio Grande. The Spanish villa of Alburquerque was founded in 1706 along El Camino Real, growing around a plaza and the San Felipe de Neri Church in what is now Old Town. The city became part of the United States in 1846, expanded rapidly with the arrival of the railroad in 1880 and Route 66 in the twentieth century, and grew further during and after the Second World War with Kirtland Air Force Base and Sandia National Laboratories.

Culture and economy

Albuquerque hosts the Albuquerque International Balloon Fiesta, the world's largest hot-air balloon festival, held every October since 1972. Its economy is anchored by federal research laboratories, the University of New Mexico, and a growing film industry. The city's cuisine centres on New Mexican fare built around red and green chile — diners combining both order "Christmas". In popular culture the city is the setting of the television series Breaking Bad and Better Call Saul.
