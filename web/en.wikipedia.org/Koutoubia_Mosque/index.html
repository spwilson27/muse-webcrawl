Koutoubia Mosque

Koutoubia Mosque
Location: Marrakech, Morocco
Founded: 1147; rebuilt c. 1158
Minaret height: 77 m

The Koutoubia Mosque (Arabic: جامع الكتبية) is the largest mosque in Marrakech, Morocco. Its name derives from the Arabic for booksellers, recalling the manuscript sellers who once traded nearby.

History

A first mosque was begun after the Almohad conquest of Marrakech in 1147 under Abd al-Mu'min. Because its qibla orientation was judged incorrect, a second mosque was built beside it around 1158; the minaret was completed under Yaqub al-Mansur in the 1190s.

Architecture

The mosque is built of brick and sandstone in Almohad style, with a vast prayer hall of horseshoe arches. Its 77-metre minaret, decorated with carved bands and topped by a lantern and gilded orbs, served as a model for the Giralda in Seville and the Hassan Tower in Rabat. Non-Muslims may not enter the prayer hall, but the surrounding gardens are open.
