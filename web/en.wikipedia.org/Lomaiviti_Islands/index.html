Lomaiviti Islands

The Lomaiviti Islands (Fijian pronunciation: [lomaiˈβiti], meaning "middle Fiji") are an archipelago of Fiji, lying between the country's two main islands, Viti Levu and Vanua Levu, in the Koro Sea. Administratively they form Lomaiviti Province, part of Fiji's Eastern Division. The group consists of seven main islands and a number of smaller islets.

Geography

The principal islands are Ovalau, Koro, Wakaya, Makogai, Moturiki, Nairai and Vanua Balavu's neighbour within the group, Gau — Fiji's fifth largest island. Ovalau is the largest and most populous, and its town of Levuka was Fiji's first capital in the 19th century; Levuka was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2013 as the country's first listed site, recognised for its early colonial port townscape.

The islands are of volcanic origin, ringed by coral reefs, and are noted for marine biodiversity; the surrounding waters include sites such as the Namena Marine Reserve. Tourism, including diving and small resorts on Wakaya and other islands, is an important activity, alongside copra, fishing and subsistence agriculture.

History

Europeans first sighted the group in the 17th and 18th centuries, and Bligh passed through after the mutiny on the Bounty in 1789. In the 19th century Levuka became a busy port for traders and settlers, and it was at Levuka that the Deed of Cession was signed in 1874, by which Fiji became a British colony.
