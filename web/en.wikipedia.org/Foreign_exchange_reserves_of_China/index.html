The foreign exchange reserves of China are the state assets held by the People's Bank of China (PBOC) and administered by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE). China holds the largest foreign exchange reserves of any country in the world, a position it has maintained since 2006, when it overtook Japan.

Growth

China's reserves grew rapidly in the 2000s as the central bank bought dollars generated by large trade surpluses and capital inflows in order to manage the value of the renminbi. Reserves passed trillion in 2006 and trillion in 2011, peaking at just under trillion in June 2014.

Drawdown and stabilization

After 2014 the reserves declined as the PBOC sold dollars to support the renminbi amid capital outflows and a slowing economy; by early 2017 they had fallen to about trillion. Since then they have remained broadly stable in the .0–3.3 trillion range.

Composition

The exact currency composition is not fully disclosed, but the reserves are known to be held mainly in US dollar assets, including large holdings of US Treasury securities, along with euros, yen, pounds, and other assets. Part of the reserves has been used to capitalize the China Investment Corporation, the country's sovereign wealth fund, established in 2007.
