The Klezmatics

OriginNew York City, United States
GenresKlezmer
Years active1986–present
LabelsRounder, Jewish Music Group

The Klezmatics are an American klezmer music group based in New York City, formed in 1986. Widely credited as leaders of the klezmer revival, the band blends traditional Yiddish wedding and folk music with jazz, Latin rhythms, Balkan styles, rock and other contemporary influences, and its lyrics frequently engage with social and political themes sung in Yiddish, English and other languages.

History

The group was founded by trumpeter Frank London, violinist Alicia Svigals and vocalist/clarinetist Lorin Sklamberg, among others, in the mid-1980s downtown New York scene. Their early albums, including Shvaygn = Toyt (1988) and Rhythm and Jews (1990), helped bring klezmer to a wide audience beyond the Jewish community.

In 2006 the band won the Grammy Award for Best Contemporary World Music Album for Wonder Wheel, a collection of songs setting lyrics by folk singer Woody Guthrie, recorded in collaboration with the Woody Guthrie Foundation. It remains the only Grammy ever awarded for a klezmer album. Other notable recordings include The Well (1998) with Israeli singer Chava Alberstein, and Brother Moses Smote the Water (2004), featuring gospel singer Joshua Nelson.

Style and themes

The Klezmatics are known for treating klezmer as a living tradition rather than a museum piece. Their repertoire draws on the repertoire of early twentieth-century Jewish-American dance bands while incorporating improvisation, odd meters and contemporary arrangements. Songs address workers' rights, gay rights, religious tolerance and other progressive causes, often with humour.

Members

Long-standing members have included Lorin Sklamberg (vocals, accordion), Frank London (trumpet), Lisa Gutkin (violin), Matt Darriau (clarinet, saxophone), Paul Morrissett (bass, tsimbl) and Richie Barshay (drums). Alicia Svigals, a founding violinist, departed in the early 2000s.
