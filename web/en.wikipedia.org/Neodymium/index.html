Neodymium

Neodymium is a chemical element with the symbol Nd and atomic number 60. It is a soft, silvery rare-earth metal of the lanthanide series, discovered in 1885 by the Austrian chemist Carl Auer von Welsbach, who separated it — with praseodymium — from the material then called didymium.

Properties and compounds

Neodymium tarnishes readily in air. Its salts are characteristically lilac-pink, and neodymium-doped glass (didymium glass) changes colour strikingly with the type of lighting and is used in astronomers' and glassblowers' filters. The ion Nd³⁺ is the active species in the widely used Nd:YAG laser.

Applications

By far its most important application is the neodymium magnet — the alloy Nd₂Fe₁₄B, developed in 1984, is the strongest type of permanent magnet known. These magnets are essential to electric-vehicle motors, wind-turbine generators, hard disk drives, headphones, and cordless tools, making neodymium a strategically significant element whose production is concentrated in China.
