East Africa

East Africa (or Eastern Africa) is the eastern subregion of the African continent. In the United Nations geoscheme it comprises 18 territories including Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda, Ethiopia, Somalia, Rwanda, Burundi, South Sudan, Mozambique, Madagascar and Zimbabwe. The region is considered the cradle of humankind, where the earliest hominins evolved.

Geography

The region is defined by the East African Rift, a continental rift zone whose lakes — Victoria, Tanganyika and Malawi among them — are among the world's largest and deepest. High volcanic peaks include Kilimanjaro (5,895 m) and Mount Kenya. The Horn of Africa juts into the Indian Ocean and the Arabian Sea.

History

Fossils from Ethiopia's Afar region, including "Lucy" (Australopithecus afarensis), document early human evolution. The Swahili Coast traded with Arabia, Persia, India and China for over a millennium, producing city-states such as Kilwa and Mombasa. In the 19th and 20th centuries the region was colonised by Britain, Germany, Italy, France and Portugal; most states gained independence in the 1960s.

Regional integration

The East African Community, revived in 2000, pursues a customs union, common market and eventual political federation among its partner states, which now include the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Somalia.
