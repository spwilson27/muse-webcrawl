Hannes Kolehmainen

Juho Pietari "Hannes" Kolehmainen (9 December 1889 – 11 January 1966) was a Finnish middle- and long-distance runner. He was the first of a generation of great Finnish long-distance runners, often called the "Flying Finns", who dominated Olympic distance running in the early 20th century.

Career

Kolehmainen won three gold medals and one silver medal at the 1912 Summer Olympics in Stockholm, triumphing in the 5,000 metres, 10,000 metres and the individual cross country event, and taking silver in the team cross country. His most celebrated victory was in the 5,000 metres final, where he defeated France's Jean Bouin in a dramatic duel, setting a world record of 14:36.6. At the 1920 Antwerp Olympics he added a fourth gold medal by winning the marathon.

Kolehmainen lit the Olympic flame at the 1952 Summer Olympics in Helsinki together with Paavo Nurmi. He is regarded as one of the pioneers of Finnish distance running and one of the greatest long-distance runners of the pre-World War I era.
