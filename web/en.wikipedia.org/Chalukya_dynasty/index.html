Chalukya dynasty

Empirec. 543 – 753 CE (Early Chalukyas of Badami)
CapitalBadami (Vatapi)
Common languagesKannada, Sanskrit
ReligionHinduism, Jainism, Buddhism
Greatest rulerPulakeshin II (c. 610–642)

The Chalukya dynasty was a Classical Indian dynasty that ruled large parts of southern and central India between the 6th and 12th centuries. During this period, it ruled as three related yet individual dynasties: the "Badami Chalukyas", who ruled from Vatapi (modern Badami) from the middle of the 6th century; the "Eastern Chalukyas" of Vengi; and the "Western Chalukyas" of Kalyani, who rose to power in the 10th century after the fall of the Rashtrakutas.

Early Chalukyas of Badami

The dynasty was founded by Pulakeshin I, who fortified Badami around 543 CE. His grandson Pulakeshin II became the dynasty's most celebrated ruler, defeating the northern emperor Harshavardhana on the banks of the Narmada River — a victory recorded in the Aihole inscription of the court poet Ravikirti — and extending Chalukya power across much of the Deccan. His reign also saw the visit of the Persian ambassador of Khosrau II and a long, exhausting rivalry with the Pallavas of Kanchipuram, who eventually captured and sacked Badami in 642.

Architecture

The Chalukyas made foundational contributions to Indian architecture. Their rock-cut cave temples at Badami, the experimental structural temples at Aihole — often called the "cradle of Indian architecture" — and the great temple complexes at Pattadakal, now a UNESCO World Heritage Site, blended northern Nagara and southern Dravidian styles into the distinctive Vesara idiom.

Later branches

The Eastern Chalukyas ruled the Vengi region of coastal Andhra for some five centuries, fostering Telugu literature. The Western Chalukyas, founded by Tailapa II in 973 after he overthrew the Rashtrakutas, ruled from Kalyani and are remembered for the patronage of Kannada poets such as Ranna and for refined later architecture. Their empire fragmented in the late 12th century among the Hoysalas, Kakatiyas, and Seunas.

Legacy

Chalukya rule consolidated the Kannada-speaking Deccan as a political and cultural region, advanced temple architecture across South India, and supported Hindu, Jain, and Buddhist communities alike.
