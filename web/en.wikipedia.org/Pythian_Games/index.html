The Pythian Games (Greek: Πύθια, Pythia) were one of the four Panhellenic Games of ancient Greece. They were held in honour of the god Apollo at his sanctuary at Delphi, every four years, two years after the Olympic Games, traditionally in the third year of each Olympiad. The games commemorated Apollo's slaying of the serpent Python, which guarded the site.

History

Originally the Pythia featured only musical contests, centred on singing to the kithara a hymn about Apollo's combat with Python. After the First Sacred War, the Amphictyonic League reorganised the festival in 582 BC, adding athletic and equestrian events modelled on those at Olympia, and it became a stephanitic ("crown") game whose victors received a wreath of laurel from Tempe. The poet Pindar composed victory odes (Pythians) for winners.

Events

Musical and artistic contests remained central and distinctive: competitions for kithara players, aulos players, singers, and later for poets, actors and artists. Athletic events included the stadion sprint, diaulos, dolichos, wrestling, boxing, pankration, pentathlon and the hoplitodromos, together with chariot and horse races in the hippodrome on the Crisaean plain below Delphi. Victors were crowned with laurel sacred to Apollo.

End

The games continued under Roman rule and were last celebrated in the late 4th century AD, in the era when the emperor Theodosius I suppressed pagan festivals and the Delphic oracle fell silent.
