Pope Eugene III (Latin: Eugenius III; died 8 July 1153), born Bernardo da Pisa, was head of the Catholic Church from 15 February 1145 to his death. A monk of Clairvaux and a disciple of Bernard of Clairvaux, he was the first Cistercian to become pope. He was beatified in 1872. Much of his pontificate was spent in exile from Rome, which was controlled by a revolutionary commune.

Early life and election

Bernardo entered the Cistercian order at Clairvaux in 1135 and became abbot of the monastery of Saints Vincent and Anastasius near Rome in 1140. On the death of Lucius II he was elected pope — reportedly to his own dismay — and consecrated at Farfa Abbey because the Roman commune barred the city to him.

The Second Crusade

News of the fall of Edessa to Zengi prompted Eugene to proclaim a new crusade in the bull Quantum praedecessores (December 1145), and he commissioned Bernard to preach it; Louis VII of France and Conrad III of Germany took the cross. The Second Crusade (1147–1149) ended in failure before Damascus, to the great disappointment of Christendom. Eugene also authorised crusading activity in Iberia and against the pagan Wends beyond the Elbe.

Exile and reform

Driven repeatedly from Rome by the commune led by Arnold of Brescia's followers, Eugene spent years in France, Germany and northern Italy, holding reforming synods (notably at Reims in 1148) and intervening in disputes such as the case of Gilbert de la Porrée and the contested election at York. He confirmed the rule of the Knights Templar with the bull Milites Templi and supported the young Frederick Barbarossa's accession by the Treaty of Constance (1153). He died at Tivoli in July 1153, and Romans — reconciled at the end — mourned him as a father.
