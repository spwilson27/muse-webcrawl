Richard Brent (scientist)


Richard P. Brent, mathematician and computer scientist

Richard Peirce Brent (born 20 April 1946) is an Australian mathematician and computer scientist known for his contributions to numerical analysis, algorithms and computational number theory. He is the co-discoverer, with John Pollard, of the Pollard–Brent rho factorisation algorithm, and the author of Brent's method for finding roots of equations.

Education and career

Brent was born in Melbourne, Australia. He studied at Monash University and completed his PhD at Stanford University in 1971 under George Forsythe. He worked at IBM's T. J. Watson Research Center and at the University of Oxford, where he was Professor of Computing Science, before returning to Australia to join the Australian National University in Canberra.

Work

Brent's 1973 book Algorithms for Minimization Without Derivatives presented the root-finding procedure now called Brent's method, combining bisection, the secant method and inverse quadratic interpolation; it remains the basis of widely used software routines such as MATLAB's fzero. With John Pollard he developed a variant of Pollard's rho algorithm that factored the eighth Fermat number, F8 = 2256 + 1, in 1980. Brent has also worked on parallel algorithms (including Brent's theorem on parallel computation), the analysis of the Euclidean algorithm, high-precision computation of constants such as the Euler–Mascheroni constant, and the search for large prime numbers and factorisations of Fermat and Cunningham numbers.

Honours

He was elected a Fellow of the Australian Academy of Science in 1982 and of the Royal Society in 2008, and received the Hannan Medal of the Australian Academy of Science.
