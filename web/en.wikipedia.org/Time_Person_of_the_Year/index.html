Time Person of the Year

Person of the Year (called Man of the Year or Woman of the Year until 1999) is an annual issue of the American news magazine Time featuring a person, group, idea or object that "for better or for worse... has done the most to influence the events of the year". The tradition began in 1927, when aviator Charles Lindbergh was named Man of the Year — partly, by Time's own account, to make amends for not putting him on the cover after his transatlantic flight.

Notable selections

US presidents have frequently been chosen, including every president from Franklin D. Roosevelt onward at least once, usually in election years. Controversial choices reflecting the "for better or worse" criterion include Adolf Hitler (1938) and Joseph Stalin (1939 and 1942). Non-individual selections include "the American fighting-man" (1950), the personal computer as "Machine of the Year" (1982), the endangered Earth as "Planet of the Year" (1988), "You" — the mass creators of user-generated content (2006), and groups such as the Ebola fighters (2014) and the silence breakers of the #MeToo movement (2017).

Selection process

The choice is made solely by Time's editors, though the magazine also runs an online readers' poll whose winner is announced separately as the readers' choice. Recent honorees have included Volodymyr Zelenskyy (2022) and singer Taylor Swift (2023).
