People's Budget

The People's Budget was the British government budget of 1909, introduced by Chancellor of the Exchequer David Lloyd George in H. H. Asquith's Liberal government. It proposed unprecedented taxes on the wealthy — including a supertax on high incomes, increased death duties, and duties on land values and unearned increments — to fund old-age pensions, labour exchanges, and naval rearmament.

Constitutional crisis

The Conservative-dominated House of Lords rejected the budget in November 1909, breaking a convention dating back centuries that the Lords did not block money bills. The resulting constitutional crisis led to two general elections in 1910 and the Parliament Act 1911, which removed the Lords' power to veto budgets and limited their veto over other legislation. The budget itself passed in April 1910.

Legacy

The People's Budget is regarded as a landmark in the development of the British welfare state and redistributive taxation, laying groundwork for the National Insurance Act 1911 and the broader Liberal welfare reforms.
