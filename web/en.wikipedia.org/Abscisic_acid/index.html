Abscisic acid

Abscisic acid (ABA) is a plant hormone. ABA functions in many plant developmental processes, including seed and bud dormancy, the control of organ size and stomatal closure. It is especially important for plants in the response to environmental stresses, including drought, soil salinity, cold tolerance, freezing tolerance and heat stress. Chemically it is a sesquiterpenoid with molecular formula C15H20O4.

Discovery

In the 1960s two substances were isolated independently: "abscisin II", which accelerated leaf abscission in cotton, and "dormin", which induced dormancy in buds of woody plants. They proved to be the same compound, named abscisic acid — although it was later found that ABA is not the primary signal for abscission, which is mainly driven by ethylene.

Functions

  • Stomatal closure: in response to water stress, ABA accumulates in leaves and causes guard cells to lose turgor, closing stomata and reducing transpiration.
  • Seed dormancy and germination: ABA maintains dormancy and inhibits germination, antagonising gibberellins; it promotes synthesis of seed storage proteins and desiccation tolerance.
  • Bud dormancy and growth inhibition: ABA accumulates in buds entering winter dormancy and counteracts growth-promoting hormones.
  • Stress responses: ABA signalling activates gene expression for tolerance of drought, salt and cold.

Biosynthesis and signalling

ABA is synthesised mainly in plastids from carotenoid precursors (via zeaxanthin and the cleavage product xanthoxin, the step catalysed by NCED enzymes). Its core signalling pathway — PYR/PYL/RCAR receptors, PP2C phosphatases and SnRK2 kinases — is one of the best-characterised hormone pathways in plants, and is exploited in developing drought-tolerant crops and ABA-mimicking agrochemicals.
