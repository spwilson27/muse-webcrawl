Parti Québécois

The Parti Québécois (pronounced [paʁti kebekwa]; PQ) is a sovereigntist and social-democratic political party in the Canadian province of Quebec. Its primary goals are the independence of Quebec from Canada and the promotion of the French language and Quebec culture. Founded in 1968 by René Lévesque, the party has formed the government of Quebec several times and twice held referendums on sovereignty, in 1980 and 1995 — the second being narrowly lost by 50.58% to 49.42%.

History

The PQ was formed from the merger of Lévesque's Mouvement Souveraineté-Association with the Ralliement national, soon joined by much of the Rassemblement pour l'indépendance nationale. After early electoral breakthroughs, the party won the 1976 provincial election under Lévesque, becoming the first Quebec government in Canadian history to advocate independence. Its first term enacted the Charter of the French Language (Bill 101, 1977), electoral financing reform, and automobile insurance nationalisation, and held the 1980 referendum on sovereignty-association, which lost 60–40.

Under Jacques Parizeau, the PQ returned to power in 1994 and held the 1995 referendum, which the federalist side won by barely one percentage point — a result followed by Parizeau's resignation after controversial remarks on the night of the vote. Lucien Bouchard and Bernard Landry subsequently led majority and minority PQ governments that balanced budgets while shelving an immediate third referendum. In 2012 Pauline Marois became Quebec's first female premier at the head of a PQ minority government; her government's proposed Charter of Quebec Values proved divisive, and the party lost the 2014 election. The PQ fell to fourth place in 2018 and won only a handful of seats in 2022, before recovering momentum under leader Paul St-Pierre Plamondon, who committed the party to holding a referendum in a first mandate.

Policies

Core PQ commitments are achieving Quebec sovereignty by referendum, defending and extending the French language (from Bill 101 to later laws such as Bill 96 under the rival CAQ, which the PQ sought to strengthen), secularism of the state, and social-democratic measures including public childcare and environmental protection. The party is associated federally with the Bloc Québécois, founded in 1991 by defecting federal MPs, though the two are organisationally separate.

Leaders

Leaders have included René Lévesque (1968–1985), Pierre-Marc Johnson, Jacques Parizeau (1988–1996), Lucien Bouchard (1996–2001), Bernard Landry, André Boisclair, Pauline Marois (2007–2014), Pierre Karl Péladeau, Jean-François Lisée, and Paul St-Pierre Plamondon (from 2020).
