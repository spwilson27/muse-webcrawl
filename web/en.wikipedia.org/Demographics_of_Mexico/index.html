Demographics of Mexico

Mexico is the most populous Spanish-speaking country in the world, with a population of roughly 130 million people, making it about the tenth most populous country overall. The population is concentrated in the central highlands, in and around the Valley of Mexico, where the capital, Mexico City, anchors a metropolitan area of more than 20 million inhabitants. Other major urban areas include Guadalajara, Monterrey, Puebla, and Tijuana. Urbanisation is high, with around four-fifths of Mexicans living in towns and cities.

Ethnic and linguistic composition

Most Mexicans are of mixed Indigenous and European (predominantly Spanish) ancestry, commonly termed mestizo, though Mexican censuses do not ask about race in the way some other countries do. Indigenous peoples make up a significant minority: the 2020 census counted several million speakers of Indigenous languages aged three and over, with Nahuatl the most widely spoken, followed by Yucatec Maya, Tzeltal, Tzotzil, Mixtec, and Zapotec. Mexico officially recognises 68 national languages, including Spanish, which is the de facto national language and the mother tongue of the great majority. There are also communities of Afro-Mexicans, and of European, Asian, and Middle Eastern descent.

Religion, growth, and migration

Mexico is predominantly Roman Catholic — roughly three-quarters to four-fifths of the population identified as Catholic in recent censuses — though the share has been declining, with growing Protestant and evangelical minorities and a rising proportion reporting no religion. Population growth, very rapid in the mid-20th century, has slowed markedly as fertility has fallen to near or below replacement level. Mexico has historically been a major country of emigration, above all to the United States, which hosts more than ten million Mexican-born residents; in recent decades net migration has at times reversed, and Mexico has also become a transit and destination country for migrants from Central America and elsewhere.
