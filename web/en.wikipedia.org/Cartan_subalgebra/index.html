Cartan subalgebra

A root system, whose structure is defined relative to a Cartan subalgebra

In mathematics, a Cartan subalgebra, often abbreviated as CSA, is a nilpotent, self-normalizing subalgebra of a Lie algebra, named after the French mathematician Élie Cartan, who developed the theory in his doctoral thesis of 1894. Cartan subalgebras are a basic tool in the classification of semisimple Lie algebras and in representation theory.

Definition

A subalgebra h of a Lie algebra g over a field is a Cartan subalgebra if it is nilpotent — its lower central series eventually reaches zero — and self-normalizing: its normalizer in g is itself. For a finite-dimensional semisimple Lie algebra over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, Cartan subalgebras are exactly the maximal toral subalgebras: abelian subalgebras consisting of semisimple elements that are maximal with these properties.

Properties

Over an algebraically closed field, all Cartan subalgebras of a Lie algebra are conjugate under the automorphism group, so they share a common dimension, called the rank of the algebra. Choosing a Cartan subalgebra yields the root space decomposition: the algebra decomposes into the Cartan subalgebra itself and one-dimensional root spaces indexed by the roots, elements of the dual space. The weights of representations, the Dynkin diagram classification of semisimple Lie algebras, and the theory of the Weyl group are all built on this decomposition.

Examples

In the algebra sl2 of 2×2 traceless matrices, the diagonal matrices form a one-dimensional Cartan subalgebra. More generally, the diagonal matrices in sln form a Cartan subalgebra of dimension n−1. In matrix algebras, Cartan subalgebras correspond (over algebraically closed fields) to sets of simultaneously diagonalizable commuting matrices maximal with this property.

Real forms and physics

Over the real numbers, Cartan subalgebras need not be conjugate; classifying their conjugacy classes is a key step in the classification of real semisimple Lie algebras, with applications in theoretical physics, where Cartan subalgebras of Lie algebras of symmetry groups label states by mutually commuting observables — the origin of terms such as the Cartan generators of su(3) in quark flavor symmetry.
