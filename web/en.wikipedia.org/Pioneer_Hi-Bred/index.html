Pioneer Hi-Bred International, Inc. is a major American producer of seeds for agriculture, headquartered in Johnston, Iowa. Founded in 1926 by Henry A. Wallace — later Secretary of Agriculture and Vice President of the United States — it was one of the first companies devoted to developing and selling hybrid seed corn, and it became the world's largest seed company.

History

Wallace began experimenting with hybrid maize in the 1910s and, convinced that crossbred inbred lines could dramatically raise yields, organized the Hi-Bred Corn Company in 1926. Hybrid seed spread rapidly through the American Corn Belt in the 1930s and 1940s, transforming farm productivity. The company was renamed Pioneer Hi-Bred in the 1930s after disputes over the "Hi-Bred" name.

Pioneer expanded internationally and into other crops, including soybeans, sorghum, alfalfa, canola and wheat, and became a major developer of genetically modified seed traits. In 1999 the chemical company DuPont acquired full ownership of Pioneer; following the Dow–DuPont merger and subsequent breakup, Pioneer's seed business became part of the agricultural company Corteva, Inc. in 2019, where Pioneer remains its flagship seed brand.

Business

Pioneer sells seed in more than 90 countries and maintains an extensive network of research stations and local agronomy advisers. Its products are marketed under the Pioneer brand, known for its green-and-white signage along Midwestern roads.
