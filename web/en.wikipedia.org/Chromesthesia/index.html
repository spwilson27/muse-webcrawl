Chromesthesia

Chromesthesia or sound-to-color synesthesia is a type of synesthesia in which sound involuntarily evokes an experience of color, shape, and movement. A person with chromesthesia hears ordinary sounds — music, voices, everyday noises — and simultaneously perceives colours that may appear as shapes or textures moving in their visual field or mind's eye.

Characteristics

The associations are automatic, consistent over time for a given individual, and idiosyncratic: two chromesthetes rarely agree on which colour a note produces. Higher pitches often evoke lighter or brighter colours, and louder sounds larger or more vivid shapes. The pairings can be influenced by learned associations, such as the colours of letters or of musical notation.

Prevalence and mechanisms

Chromesthesia is among the more commonly reported forms of synesthesia. It is thought to arise from increased cross-activation or reduced inhibition between auditory and visual areas of the brain. Composers and musicians who reported the trait include Franz Liszt, Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov, Olivier Messiaen, and Duke Ellington; contemporary artists such as Pharrell Williams and Billie Eilish have also described sound–colour experiences.
