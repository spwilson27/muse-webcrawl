The DejaVu fonts are a superfamily of fonts designed for broad coverage of the Unicode Universal Character Set. The family was derived from the Bitstream Vera fonts, released in 2003 under a permissive license. Their stated goal, as of the project's later releases, is "to provide a wider range of characters ... while maintaining the original look and feel through the process of collaborative development." The fonts are developed by a community of contributors, with the DejaVu name borrowed from the French phrase meaning "already seen."

Typefaces

The family includes DejaVu Sans (with oblique, bold, condensed, and extra-light variants), DejaVu Serif, and DejaVu Sans Mono for programming and terminal use. Because the parent Vera design was widely used in free and open-source software but covered only a limited character set, DejaVu added glyphs for many scripts — including Greek, Cyrillic, Arabic, Hebrew, Armenian, Georgian, mathematical symbols, and technical characters — making it a default font in many Linux distributions and in software such as LibreOffice.

Usage

DejaVu Sans was for years the default interface font in several Linux environments and remains the fallback typeface behind much mathematical and symbolic text rendering on free systems. Its wide coverage has also made it a common choice for embedding symbols in documents; its metrics and appearance are sometimes criticized as wider and heavier than alternatives such as Liberation Sans.
