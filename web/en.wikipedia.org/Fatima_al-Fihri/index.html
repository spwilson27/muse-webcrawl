Fatima bint Muhammad al-Fihriya al-Qurashiyya (Arabic: فاطمة بنت محمد الفهري القرشي), better known as Fatima al-Fihri (c. 800 – c. 880), was an Arab Muslim woman credited with founding the al-Qarawiyyin mosque and madrasa in Fez, Morocco, in 859. The institution later developed into the University of al-Qarawiyyin, recognized by UNESCO and Guinness World Records as the oldest existing, continually operating educational institution in the world.

Life

Fatima was born around 800 in Kairouan, in present-day Tunisia, into a wealthy merchant family of Quraysh descent that had migrated from the Hejaz. Her family moved to Fez during the reign of the Idrisid ruler Idris II. After the deaths of her father and husband left her a large inheritance, Fatima — who was well educated and deeply devout — resolved to spend her fortune on a work of public benefit. According to tradition, she purchased land in Fez and vowed to fast throughout the construction of the mosque, which began in Ramadan 245 AH (November–December 859 CE).

Legacy

The al-Qarawiyyin began as a mosque with an associated teaching institution, grew into one of the leading spiritual and educational centres of the Islamic world, and attracted scholars such as Ibn Khaldun and Leo Africanus. Fatima's sister Mariam is credited with founding the al-Andalus mosque in Fez in the same period. Fatima al-Fihri is celebrated in Morocco and beyond as an early example of female philanthropy and educational endowment (waqf), and prizes and academic programmes in Europe and the Arab world bear her name.
