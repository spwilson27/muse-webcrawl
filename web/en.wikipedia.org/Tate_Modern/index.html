Tate Modern is a national museum of international modern and contemporary art in London, England, and part of the Tate group (with Tate Britain, Tate Liverpool, and Tate St Ives). It is housed in the former Bankside Power Station on the south bank of the River Thames and is one of the most visited museums of modern art in the world, with free admission to its permanent collection displays.

Building

The museum occupies the Bankside B power station, designed by Sir Giles Gilbert Scott and built between 1947 and 1963, which closed in 1981. The Swiss architects Herzog & de Meuron converted the vast brick building for the Tate, retaining its central 99-metre chimney and turning the turbine hall into a dramatic entrance and exhibition space. The museum opened in May 2000. A further Herzog & de Meuron extension — the ten-storey Switch House (now the Blavatnik Building), built over the station's former oil tanks — opened in June 2016, increasing gallery space by about 60 percent; the underground Tanks became dedicated spaces for performance and film.

Collection and displays

Tate Modern holds the national collection of international art from 1900 to the present, including major works by Picasso, Matisse, Dalí, Warhol, Rothko (whose Seagram murals are a highlight), and many contemporary artists. Rather than a strict chronology, the permanent collection is arranged in thematic wings and named displays. The Turbine Hall hosts a celebrated series of large commissioned installations, beginning with Louise Bourgeois in 2000 and including Olafur Eliasson's The Weather Project (2003) and Ai Weiwei's Sunflower Seeds (2010).
