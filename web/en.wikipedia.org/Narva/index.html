Narva

Narva
Country: Estonia
County: Ida-Viru
First mentioned: 1172
Population: ~53,000
River: Narva River
Border: Russia (Ivangorod, across the river)

Narva is the third-largest city in Estonia, situated at the country's northeastern extremity on the Narva River, which forms the border with Russia. Directly across the river stands the Russian town of Ivangorod and its medieval fortress, facing Narva's own Hermann Castle.

History

Narva grew up under Danish and then Teutonic Order rule — the castle was founded around 1256 — and became a prosperous Hanseatic trading town. Sweden took the city in 1581, and in 1700 the Battle of Narva opened the Great Northern War with a spectacular Swedish victory by Charles XII over a much larger Russian army; Peter the Great captured the city in 1704. Tsarist and Soviet industrialization made Narva a center of textile production at the vast Kreenholm mills and of oil-shale power generation.

Destruction and modern city

Narva was almost completely destroyed in 1944 during fighting between German and Soviet forces; its celebrated Baroque old town was largely left in ruins and replaced by Soviet-era housing. After the war the population became overwhelmingly Russian-speaking, and today roughly 95% of residents are ethnic Russians or Russian speakers — unique within Estonia. Since Estonia joined the EU and NATO in 2004, Narva has been a frontier city of the European Union, and its border crossing is a major checkpoint between the EU and Russia.
