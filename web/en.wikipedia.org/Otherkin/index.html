Otherkin

TypeSubculture / identity community
Emergence1990s, online communities

Otherkin are a subculture of people who identify as partially or entirely non-human, typically as animals, mythological creatures, or fictional beings, in some sense — whether spiritually, psychologically, or as a metaphorical framework for self-understanding. Those who identify as animals are sometimes called therians.

History

The community grew out of online discussion groups in the early 1990s, initially among people identifying with elves, and expanded through mailing lists, forums, and later social media. The word combines "other" with "kin", suggesting kinship with something other than humanity.

Beliefs and identity

Explanations vary widely within the community: some members understand their identity in spiritual terms such as reincarnation, while others take a psychological view. Experiences described include "shifts" in mindset toward one's kintype and a sense of phantom limbs such as tails or wings.

Reception

Scholars of religion and sociology have studied otherkin as an internet-enabled identity community and an example of contemporary alternative spirituality. The subculture has also attracted media curiosity and occasional ridicule.
