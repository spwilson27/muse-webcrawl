Whaam! is a 1963 diptych painting by the American artist Roy Lichtenstein. It is one of the best-known works of pop art, and represents the artist's monumental adaptation of imagery from comic books. The two panels show, at left, a fighter aircraft firing a rocket and, at right, the target aircraft exploding in flames beneath the onomatopoeic word "WHAAM!" in bold yellow letters.

Background and creation

Lichtenstein based the composition on a panel drawn by Irv Novick from the DC Comics war story "Star Jockey", published in All-American Men of War no. 89 (January–February 1962). As in his other early pop works, Lichtenstein enlarged, simplified and regularised the comic-book image, rendering it in his signature style of primary colours, heavy black outlines and hand-painted Ben-Day dots.

The painting was first exhibited at the Leo Castelli Gallery in New York in 1963. The Tate Gallery in London purchased it in 1966 — the first Lichtenstein and one of the first major American pop works to enter the Tate collection — a decision that attracted considerable public debate at the time. It is now displayed at Tate Modern.

Interpretation

Whaam! was painted during the Cold War, shortly after the Cuban Missile Crisis, and critics have read it both as a celebration and as a deadpan critique of American militarism and the glamorisation of aerial combat in popular media. Lichtenstein, who had served in the US Army during the Second World War and trained as a pilot before deployment, painted a series of war and aviation subjects in 1962–64, including Bratatat!, Okay Hot-Shot, Okay! and As I Opened Fire.
