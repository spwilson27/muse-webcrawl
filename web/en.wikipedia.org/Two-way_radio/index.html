Two-way radio

A typical handheld two-way radio ("walkie-talkie")
TypeRadio transceiver
ModesSimplex, half-duplex; trunked and repeater systems
Common servicesLand mobile, FRS, GMRS, marine, amateur

A two-way radio is a radio transceiver (a radio that can both transmit and receive radio waves) that is used for bidirectional person-to-person voice communication with other users with similar radios, in contrast to broadcast receivers which only receive transmissions. Two-way radios usually use a half-duplex communication channel, where only one radio transmits at a time although any user can listen; operators take turns speaking, typically using a push-to-talk (PTT) button.

Handheld two-way radios are commonly called walkie-talkies, a name dating to the backpack radios developed for infantry use during the Second World War. Two-way radios are widely used by police, fire, and emergency services, by the military, and in industries such as construction, transportation, hospitality, and retail. They are also used for recreation through services such as the Family Radio Service (FRS) and by radio amateurs.

Systems

Simple radios communicate directly ("simplex"), limiting range to a few kilometres. Range can be extended with repeaters, which receive on one frequency and retransmit on another, and with trunked radio systems that dynamically assign channels to user groups. Digital standards such as DMR, P25, and TETRA add encryption, data, and improved spectral efficiency.

History

Two-way radio communication developed in the 1920s and 1930s for police and military use. The first truly portable hand-held unit, the SCR-536 "handie-talkie", was produced by Motorola during World War II.
