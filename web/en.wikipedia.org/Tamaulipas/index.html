Tamaulipas

Tamaulipas (Spanish pronunciation: [tamawˈlipas]), officially the Free and Sovereign State of Tamaulipas, is one of the 31 states of Mexico. It lies in the northeast of the country, bordering the United States (Texas) to the north along the Rio Grande, the Gulf of Mexico to the east, and the Mexican states of Veracruz, San Luis Potosí, and Nuevo León. Its capital is Ciudad Victoria, and its largest city is Reynosa.

Geography

The state includes coastal plains along the Gulf, the Sierra de Tamaulipas, and part of the Sierra Madre Oriental in the southwest. The El Cielo Biosphere Reserve, a UNESCO-recognized reserve, protects cloud forest and a remarkable diversity of ecosystems where temperate and tropical species meet.

History

The region was colonized by Spain in the 18th century under José de Escandón, who founded the province of Nuevo Santander, from which modern Tamaulipas largely descends. The state's name is generally derived from the Huastec Tamaholipa. The port of Tampico became a major oil and shipping centre in the 20th century.

Economy

Tamaulipas has a significant manufacturing sector driven by maquiladoras clustered in border cities such as Reynosa, Matamoros, and Nuevo Laredo — the latter one of the busiest inland ports in North America for trade with the United States. Agriculture (sorghum, citrus, sugar cane), fishing, cattle ranching, and petrochemicals around Tampico and Altamira are also important.

Municipalities and cities

The state is divided into 43 municipalities. Major cities include Reynosa, Matamoros, Nuevo Laredo, Tampico, Ciudad Victoria, Ciudad Madero, and Miramar (Altamira).
