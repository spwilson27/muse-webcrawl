Seawaymax is the informal name for the largest vessels able to transit the locks of the St. Lawrence Seaway, the system of canals and locks linking the Atlantic Ocean to the Great Lakes of North America. The size class is defined by the dimensions of the smallest lock chambers on the route, principally those of the Welland Canal and the Montreal–Lake Ontario section.

Dimensions

A Seawaymax vessel may be no more than about 225.6 metres (740 feet) long, 23.8 metres (78 feet) in beam, and 8.1 metres (26.5 feet) in draft, with a maximum air draft of 35.5 metres to clear bridges. Ships built to these limits — often called "lakers" when they operate within the Great Lakes system — typically carry between 25,000 and 30,000 deadweight tons when loaded to Seaway draft.

The Seaway

The St. Lawrence Seaway opened in 1959 as a joint Canadian–American project. Its fifteen locks lift ships roughly 180 metres between Montreal and Lake Erie, mainly through the eight locks of the Welland Canal bypassing Niagara Falls. Grain, iron ore, coal, and general cargo dominate its traffic. Because Seawaymax locks are smaller than those of the Panama Canal (Panamax), ship designers treat Seawaymax as a distinct, more restrictive size category in the family of ship size names that also includes Handysize, Capesize, and Suezmax.

Vessels

Many Seawaymax ships are geared bulk carriers able to unload their own cargoes with onboard conveyor systems, suited to Great Lakes ports. Some are built as combined lake-and-ocean traders ("saltie" trade), while the largest lakers exceed Seaway dimensions and are confined to the upper lakes, passing only through the Soo Locks.
