Trendelenburg position

The Trendelenburg position is named after the German surgeon Friedrich Trendelenburg (1844–1924). It involves the patient being positioned supine on an operating table with their head at a lower level than their feet at an incline of about 16 degrees. The feet are at the highest point and head at the lowest.

Uses

The position is used during certain surgical procedures, especially abdominal and genitourinary surgery, because it allows better access to the pelvic organs: gravity pulls the intestines away from the pelvis, allowing the surgeon better access to the organs of the pelvic cavity. It was once thought to be helpful for treating shock by increasing venous return to the heart, though evidence for this is weak and the position is no longer recommended for that purpose. It may also be used during central venous catheter placement to reduce the risk of air embolism and to increase venous filling in the neck veins.

Reverse Trendelenburg

In the reverse Trendelenburg position, the patient's head is elevated above the feet. This variation is used for some head and neck, upper abdominal, and bariatric procedures, and to reduce intracranial pressure.
