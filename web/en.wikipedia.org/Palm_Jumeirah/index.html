The Palm Jumeirah is an artificial archipelago in Dubai, United Arab Emirates, created using land reclamation and extending into the Persian Gulf. It is shaped like a palm tree enclosed by a crescent-shaped breakwater, and is the smallest and first completed of Dubai's three planned Palm Islands, developed by the state-owned company Nakheel. Construction began in June 2001, and the first residents moved in from 2006–2007.

Construction

The island was built almost entirely from sand dredged from the floor of the Persian Gulf — some 94 million cubic metres — held in place with the help of GPS-guided "rainbowing" techniques, and protected by an 11 km breakwater of about 7 million tonnes of rock. No concrete or steel was used for the island body itself. The crescent's breakwater was engineered to allow water circulation through deliberate gaps after ecological studies.

Layout and attractions

The "trunk" carries apartment buildings, hotels and the Palm Jumeirah Monorail, which opened in 2009 and connects to the mainland. Sixteen "fronds" carry private villas, while the outer crescent hosts resort hotels, most famously Atlantis, The Palm (opened 2008) and Atlantis The Royal (opened 2023). The island added roughly 78 km of coastline to Dubai and is visible from space.

Environmental concerns

Reclamation altered local currents and marine habitats; Nakheel and Dubai authorities have funded monitoring and artificial reef programmes. Studies have also noted the island's slow subsidence and the vulnerability of low-lying reclaimed land to sea-level rise.
