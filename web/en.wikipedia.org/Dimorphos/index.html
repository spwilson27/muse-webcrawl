Dimorphos

Dimorphos is a natural satellite orbiting the near-Earth asteroid 65803 Didymos; it is the smaller member of the Didymos binary system. Discovered in 2003 and nicknamed "Didymoon", it is about 160 metres (525 ft) in diameter and orbits Didymos in roughly 11 hours and 55 minutes originally. Dimorphos was the target of NASA's Double Asteroid Redirection Test (DART), which intentionally collided a spacecraft with it on 26 September 2022 in the first demonstration of asteroid deflection by a kinetic impactor.

Discovery and naming

The satellite was discovered on 20 November 2003 by Petr Pravec and colleagues at Ondřejov Observatory, through photometric observations of mutual events in the Didymos system. Its name, approved by the International Astronomical Union in 2020, is Greek for "having two forms", referring to its shape before and after the DART impact and to its nature as a paired body.

DART impact

DART struck Dimorphos at about 6.6 km/s. Observations from Earth-based telescopes and the Italian Space Agency's LICIACube showed the impact shortened Dimorphos's orbital period by about 33 minutes — far exceeding the 73-second success threshold — and excavated a large plume and tail of debris. The momentum enhancement factor (beta) was estimated at roughly 3–5, meaning ejecta recoil contributed significantly to the deflection. The European Space Agency's Hera mission, launched in October 2024, is travelling to the system to survey the crater and the moonlet's altered properties in detail.

Physical characteristics

Images from DART's DRACO camera revealed a rubble-pile surface strewn with boulders, consistent with an aggregate held together by weak gravity. Dimorphos is an oblate spheroid; the impact may have reshaped it and put it into a tumbling or tidally disturbed rotation state.
