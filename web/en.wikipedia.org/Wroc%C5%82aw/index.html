Wrocław is a city in southwestern Poland and the largest city in the historical region of Silesia. Situated on the banks of the Oder River, it is the capital of the Lower Silesian Voivodeship and the third-largest city in Poland. Historically known by its German name, Breslau, the city has passed between Polish, Bohemian, Austrian, Prussian, and German rule over its long history.

History

A settlement existed at the site by the 10th century; a bishopric was founded in the year 1000, and the city became an important trading centre of the Piast dynasty. Wrocław joined the Hanseatic League and prospered as a centre of trade and, later, industry. After the Second World War, the Potsdam Agreement transferred the city to Poland; its German population was expelled and replaced largely by Poles displaced from Lwów and other eastern territories.

Landmarks

Landmarks include the Gothic Old Town Hall on the Market Square (Rynek), one of the largest town squares in Europe; the Cathedral of St. John the Baptist on Ostrów Tumski, the oldest part of the city; and the Centennial Hall (1913), a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The city is famous for its many bridges, its islands on the Oder, and its hundreds of small dwarf statues (krasnale) scattered through the streets. Wrocław was a European Capital of Culture in 2016.
