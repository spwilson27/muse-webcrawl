Tsumeb (from Khoekhoegowab !Tsṭmẻb, "place of moss") is a mining town in the Oshikoto Region of northern Namibia, and the closest town to the Etosha National Park. It was founded and developed around the Tsumeb Mine — a world-famous polymetallic orebody mined from 1907 to 1996 — and grew under German and then South African administration before Namibian independence in 1990.

The mine

The Tsumeb orebody is one of the most mineralogically remarkable deposits on Earth: of the roughly 290 mineral species recorded at the mine, 72 are type-locality species first described from Tsumeb, including fine specimens of azurite, dioptase, cerussite, duftite, and the copper-lead minerals that made "Tsumeb" a byword among collectors. The mine produced mainly copper, lead, zinc, and silver, with accessory germanium and gallium.

Modern town

After the mine's closure in 1996 the town's economy shifted toward tourism and services, serving visitors to Etosha. The former mine grounds were redeveloped; a mineral museum commemorates the town's heritage. A separate modern zinc-copper operation, Tschudi, opened nearby in the 2010s. Tsumeb also hosts the Tsumeb Copper Festival and is known for the nearby Otjikoto Lake, a flooded sinkhole and dive site.
