Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty

CTBT
Signed: 24 September 1996, New York
Location: Vienna (CTBTO headquarters)
Status: Not yet in force
Signatories: 187 states (approx.)

The Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty (CTBT) is a multilateral treaty that bans all nuclear weapon test explosions and any other nuclear explosions in all environments. It was adopted by the United Nations General Assembly and opened for signature on 24 September 1996.

Terms

The treaty prohibits any nuclear explosion, whether for weapons or peaceful purposes, underground, underwater, in the atmosphere, or in space. It establishes the Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty Organization (CTBTO) in Vienna, together with a global International Monitoring System of seismic, hydroacoustic, infrasound, and radionuclide stations to detect violations.

Status

The treaty enters into force only after ratification by all 44 "Annex 2" states that possessed nuclear power or research reactors at the time of negotiation. Several of these, including the United States, China, India, Pakistan, and North Korea, have not ratified; Russia ratified but revoked its ratification in 2023. Despite not being formally in force, a near-universal moratorium on nuclear testing has held since the 1990s, broken only by India and Pakistan (1998) and North Korea (2006–2017).

Verification

The monitoring network, largely complete, detected all of North Korea's announced nuclear tests, demonstrating that even small underground explosions can be identified.
