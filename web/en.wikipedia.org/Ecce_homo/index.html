Ecce homo

Ecce homo (Latin for "behold the man") is the phrase spoken by Pontius Pilate in the Gospel of John (19:5) when he presented Jesus, bound and wearing the crown of thorns, to the hostile crowd before his crucifixion. It is also the name of a major motif in Christian art depicting that moment.

In art

Ecce homo scenes became common in Western art from the fifteenth century onward, showing Christ wearing the crown of thorns and sometimes a reed sceptre and purple robe, often flanked by Pilate gesturing toward him. Artists including Bosch, Titian, Caravaggio, and Rembrandt treated the subject. In modern times the phrase gained renewed fame through a 2012 incident in Borja, Spain, where an elderly parishioner's amateur restoration of a small Ecce Homo fresco by Elías García Martínez went viral.

Other uses

The phrase was used by Friedrich Nietzsche as the title of his autobiography, Ecce Homo: How One Becomes What One Is (written 1888), and appears in various literary and musical works.
