National Cabinet (Australia)

Formed: 13 March 2020

Members: Prime Minister; state premiers; territory chief ministers

Preceded by: Council of Australian Governments (COAG, for most purposes)

The National Cabinet is an intergovernmental decision-making forum in Australia composed of the prime minister of Australia and the premiers and chief ministers of the Australian states and territories. It was established on 13 March 2020 in response to the COVID-19 pandemic in Australia, and on 29 May 2020 Prime Minister Scott Morrison announced that it would permanently replace the Council of Australian Governments (COAG) as the country's principal intergovernmental forum.

Role and operation

Unlike COAG, National Cabinet was designed to meet more frequently and to focus on a narrower set of national priorities, initially the health and economic response to the pandemic, and subsequently matters such as energy policy, health funding, housing, skills and the National Disability Insurance Scheme. Its meetings are chaired by the prime minister, and decisions are generally made by consensus. Because it was constituted as a committee of the federal Cabinet, its deliberations were initially claimed to be covered by cabinet confidentiality, though a 2021 decision of the Administrative Appeals Tribunal held that it is not a committee of the Cabinet for the purposes of freedom-of-information law.

Criticism

Commentators have debated the transparency of National Cabinet, its accountability to parliaments, and whether its consensus model blurs responsibility between the Commonwealth and the states. Nevertheless, it has remained the central forum for federal-state coordination under successive governments.
