Mircea Eliade

BornMarch 13, 1907, Bucharest, Romania
DiedApril 22, 1986, Chicago, United States
OccupationHistorian of religion, philosopher, novelist
Known forThe Sacred and the Profane; theory of hierophany; eternal return

Mircea Eliade (1907–1986) was a Romanian historian of religion, fiction writer and philosopher, and one of the most influential scholars of religion of the twentieth century. After studying in Bucharest and spending formative years in India researching yoga and Indian philosophy, he worked as a diplomat, then emigrated after the Second World War, eventually becoming a professor at the University of Chicago.

Eliade's scholarship centred on patterns he believed recurred across world religions: the distinction between the sacred and the profane, the manifestation of the sacred in objects and places (hierophany), sacred space organised around an axis mundi, and the cyclical "eternal return" by which traditional societies regenerated time through myth and ritual — ideas set out in works such as Cosmos and History, The Sacred and the Profane and the monumental A History of Religious Ideas. He also wrote on shamanism, yoga and alchemy, and edited the sixteen-volume Encyclopedia of Religion.

Literary work and controversy

In Romanian, Eliade was a significant novelist and diarist of the interwar generation. His youthful journalism expressed sympathy for the far-right Iron Guard, a chapter of his biography that has been debated by scholars alongside assessments of his scholarly legacy. His fiction, much of it fantastic and metaphysical in character, has been increasingly translated into English.
