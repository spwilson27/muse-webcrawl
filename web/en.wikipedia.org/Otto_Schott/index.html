Otto Schott


Born: 17 December 1851, Witten, Prussia
Died: 27 August 1935 (aged 83), Jena, Germany
Occupation: Chemist, glass technologist
Known for: Borosilicate glass; founding Schott AG

Friedrich Otto Schott (17 December 1851 – 27 August 1935) was a German chemist and glass technologist who systematically investigated the relationship between the chemical composition of glass and its properties, effectively founding modern glass science. His work made possible precision optical instruments, laboratory glassware and heat-resistant glass.

Work in Jena

Schott, the son of a glassmaker, earned his doctorate at Jena and began melting experimental glasses in his own laboratory. From 1882 he collaborated with physicist Ernst Abbe and optician Carl Zeiss, whose microscope optics needed glasses with precisely controlled properties. In 1884 the three founded the Jenaer Glaswerk Schott & Genossen in Jena, which became the company Schott AG. Schott's catalogue of optical glasses, including his famous letter-numbered melts, let designers correct lens aberrations as never before.

Borosilicate glass

In 1893 Schott introduced borosilicate glass, marketed as Duran, with outstanding resistance to heat and thermal shock and to chemical attack; related products such as Jenaer Glas became standard for laboratories and households. His numbering system for glass compositions and his statistical approach to experiments made glassmaking a quantitative science. The company he founded remains one of the world's leading specialty glass manufacturers.
