George I (George Louis; 7 June 1660 – 22 June 1727) was King of Great Britain and Ireland from 1 August 1714 and ruler of the Electorate of Hanover from 1698 until his death. He was the first British monarch of the House of Hanover.

Accession

Under the Act of Settlement 1701, the English throne was settled on the Protestant descendants of Sophia, Electress of Hanover. When Queen Anne died in 1714, Sophia's son George, though only 52nd in line by primogeniture, was the nearest Protestant heir and was proclaimed king in London.

Reign

George spoke little English and spent much time in Hanover, facts that encouraged the development of cabinet government led by a prime minister — in practice Robert Walpole. His reign saw the Jacobite rising of 1715, the South Sea Bubble financial crisis of 1720, and the consolidation of Whig supremacy. His marriage to Sophia Dorothea of Celle ended in divorce and her lifelong confinement after the Königsmarck affair.

Death

George I died of a stroke while visiting Hanover in 1727 and was buried there. He was succeeded by his son, George II.
