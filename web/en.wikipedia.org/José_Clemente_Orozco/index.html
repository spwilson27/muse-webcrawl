José Clemente Orozco

José Clemente Orozco
Born: 23 November 1883, Zapotlán, Jalisco
Died: 7 September 1949, Mexico City
Movement: Mexican muralism

José Clemente Orozco (23 November 1883 – 7 September 1949) was a Mexican painter best known for his large public murals, and one of "the big three" of Mexican muralism alongside Diego Rivera and David Alfaro Siqueiros.

Life

Orozco lost his left hand in a childhood gunpowder accident. He studied at the Academy of San Carlos and worked as a political cartoonist during the Mexican Revolution, developing a dark, expressionistic style focused on human suffering and social conflict.

Major works

His murals include the cycle at the National Preparatory School in Mexico City, Prometheus (1930) at Pomona College in California, the Epic of American Civilization at Dartmouth College, and the Hospicio Cabañas in Guadalajara, considered his masterpiece, with the dramatic Man of Fire dome painting.

Legacy

Orozco's unsparing, universal vision of violence and redemption influenced muralists and abstract expressionists alike; the Hospicio Cabañas murals are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
