Sabre Dance

The "Sabre Dance" is a movement in the final act of Aram Khachaturian's ballet Gayane (1942), in which the dancers dance with sabres. It is Khachaturian's best-known and most widely recognised work worldwide. The music combines an ostinato figure with an Armenian folk song theme and is noted for its frenzied, whirling character.

Popularity

The "Sabre Dance" achieved enormous popularity in the United States in 1948, when three recordings by major orchestras — the Chicago Symphony Orchestra, the New York Philharmonic, and the Boston Pops — appeared on the Billboard classical charts, and jukebox versions circulated widely. Khachaturian himself reportedly had mixed feelings about the piece's fame, feeling it distracted from his other compositions.

In popular culture

The music is frequently used in films, television, advertising, and sporting events to accompany scenes of frantic or comic activity, and is a stock accompaniment for plate-spinning acts — it was famously used by Ed Sullivan's variety show for the plate spinner Erich Brenn. It has been covered by artists ranging from the rock band Love Sculpture, whose 1968 version reached the UK top five, to numerous orchestras and brass ensembles.
