The Siege of Zutphen was a military campaign fought from 19 to 30 May 1591 during the Eighty Years' War. Forces of the Dutch Republic under Maurice of Nassau, supported by English troops under Sir Francis Vere, captured the strategically important town of Zutphen on the IJssel river from its Spanish garrison.

Background

Zutphen had been unsuccessfully attacked in 1586 — the battle in which Sir Philip Sidney was mortally wounded — and remained a Spanish outpost threatening the eastern Dutch provinces. In 1591 Maurice opened the first of the offensives that would win him fame, moving with a mobile siege train by river.

The siege

Maurice first seized a sconce (fort) on the opposite bank of the IJssel using a stratagem: soldiers disguised as peasants and women selling produce overpowered its small guard, allowing Dutch troops to establish batteries commanding the town. Bombardment began, a breach was made, and on 30 May the Spanish garrison surrendered and was permitted to march out.

Aftermath

The capture of Zutphen opened Maurice's successful 1591 campaign, in which he also took Deventer, Delfzijl, Nijmegen, and other towns, dramatically extending the Republic's eastern frontier.
