Rule of thumb

A rule of thumb is a principle with broad application that is not intended to be strictly accurate or reliable for every situation. It is an easily learned and easily applied procedure or standard, based on practical experience rather than theory. Rules of thumb develop over time from similar experiences: they are simple empirical guides that work in most ordinary cases even though they may fail in unusual ones.

A thumb used as an informal unit of measurement, one folk origin suggested for the phrase.

Etymology

The exact origin of the phrase is uncertain. One popular explanation is that carpenters and builders measured roughly with the thumb, whose width is approximately one inch; in several languages the words for "thumb" and "inch" are closely related. Another often-repeated story claims the phrase derives from an old English common-law statement about the thickness of a stick a husband might legally use to beat his wife, but historians have found no evidence that such a rule ever existed in law, and the story appears to be a later invention. The earliest known print appearance of "rule of thumb" is in a 1658 posthumously published work by James Durham, who used it in a religious context meaning a rough practical method as opposed to exact measurement.

Examples

Rules of thumb appear in many fields. In investing, a common rule is to subtract one's age from 100 to find the percentage of a portfolio that should be in stocks. In navigation, the "rule of twelfths" estimates tidal height between high and low water. In cooking, a rule of thumb suggests resting roasted meat for roughly half its cooking time. In computing, similar practical guides are called heuristics.

Relationship to heuristics

In psychology and decision science, related concepts are studied as heuristics: mental shortcuts that usually produce serviceable answers quickly but can lead to systematic errors or biases. A rule of thumb is essentially a socially transmitted heuristic, passed on through practice and proverb rather than formal derivation.
