Loch Ness

Loch Ness from the south shore

Loch Ness (Scottish Gaelic: Loch Nis) is a large freshwater loch in the Scottish Highlands, extending for approximately 37 kilometres (23 miles) southwest of Inverness. Its surface is 16 metres (52 feet) above sea level. Loch Ness is best known for the alleged sightings of the cryptozoological Loch Ness Monster, also known affectionately as "Nessie".

It is the second-largest Scottish loch by surface area at 56 km2 (22 sq mi) after Loch Lomond, but due to its great depth it is the largest by volume in Great Britain. Its deepest point is 230 m (755 ft), making it the second deepest loch in Scotland after Loch Morar. It contains more water than all the lakes of England and Wales combined.

Geography

Loch Ness lies along the Great Glen Fault, which runs from Inverness in the northeast to Fort William in the southwest. The Caledonian Canal, which links the loch to the North Sea at its northeastern end and to the other lochs of the Great Glen, uses Loch Ness as part of its route. The loch is fed by several rivers, including the Oich and the Enrick, and drains through the River Ness into the Moray Firth.

The only island on Loch Ness is Cherry Island, visible at its southwestern end near Fort Augustus. It is an artificial island, a crannog dating from the Iron Age.

The Loch Ness Monster

Loch Ness is renowned worldwide for the Loch Ness Monster, a creature said to inhabit the loch. The first written account appeared in Adomnán's Life of St Columba in the 7th century AD, describing an encounter in the River Ness. Interest surged in 1933 after a road was built along the loch shore, with several reported sightings and the famous "surgeon's photograph" of 1934, later revealed to be a hoax. Despite numerous sonar explorations and scientific studies, no conclusive evidence of a large unknown animal has been found.

Settlements and tourism

Drumnadrochit, on the western shore, is home to the Loch Ness Centre and Exhibition. Urquhart Castle, a ruined medieval fortress, stands on the northwestern shore and is one of the most visited castles in Scotland. The loch forms part of a popular tourist route through the Highlands.
