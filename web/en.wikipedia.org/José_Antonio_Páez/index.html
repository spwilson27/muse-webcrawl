José Antonio Páez

José Antonio Páez Herrera
Born: 13 June 1790, Curpa, Venezuela
Died: 6 May 1873, New York City
Office: President of Venezuela (1831–1835, 1839–1843, 1861–1863)

José Antonio Páez Herrera (13 June 1790 – 6 May 1873) was a Venezuelan leader who fought in the Spanish American wars of independence and served as president of Venezuela three times.

Independence war

A llanero (plains cowboy) of humble origin, Páez rose to command the feared llanero cavalry for the patriot cause, winning key battles including Carabobo (1821) under Simón Bolívar, and earning the nickname "El Centauro de los Llanos" — the Centaur of the Plains.

Presidency

Páez led Venezuela's separation from Gran Colombia in 1830 and dominated the new republic's early decades, building a conservative order. Overthrown and exiled after the Federal War, he returned briefly as dictator (1861–1863), then lived in exile in New York, where he died and was later repatriated with national honours.
