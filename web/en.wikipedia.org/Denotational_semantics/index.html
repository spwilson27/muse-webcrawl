Denotational semantics

Scott domains are central to denotational semantics

Denotational semantics is an approach in formal semantics for programming languages in which each phrase of a language is assigned a mathematical object, its denotation, representing its meaning. It was developed by Christopher Strachey and Dana Scott in the late 1960s and early 1970s.

Approach

The meaning of a composite phrase is defined as a function of the meanings of its parts (compositionality). Because loops and recursion require fixed points, Scott constructed domains — partially ordered sets with limits of chains — in which programs denote continuous functions.

Relation to other semantics

Denotational semantics contrasts with operational semantics, which describes execution step by step, and axiomatic semantics, which describes programs by logical assertions.
