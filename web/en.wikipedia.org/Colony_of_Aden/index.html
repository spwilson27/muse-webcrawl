The Colony of Aden was a British Crown colony from 1937 to 1963, located around the port city of Aden at the southern tip of the Arabian Peninsula. It had previously been administered as part of British India, first as the Aden Settlement from 1839.

History

British forces captured Aden in 1839 to secure the sea route to India and suppress piracy in the Red Sea. After the opening of the Suez Canal in 1869, Aden grew into one of the world's busiest bunkering ports, where steamships stopped to take on coal and later oil.

On 1 April 1937 Aden became a Crown colony, administered directly from London rather than through the Government of India. During the Second World War the port was a vital Allied naval base. In the postwar decades Aden's refinery at Little Aden and its free-port trade made it prosperous, but nationalist agitation grew through the 1950s.

In 1963 the colony was merged into the Federation of South Arabia as the State of Aden. Following the Aden Emergency, British forces withdrew in 1967 and the territory became part of the People's Republic of South Yemen.
