A national park is a nature park designated for conservation purposes, created and protected by a national government. National parks preserve outstanding natural landscapes, wildlife and cultural heritage for the benefit and enjoyment of the public, generally under stricter protections than other parks.

History

Yellowstone National Park, established by the United States Congress in 1872, is widely regarded as the world's first national park, though earlier protected areas such as Bogotá's forests and the Bogd Khan Uul in Mongolia had legal protection. The idea spread worldwide: Australia's Royal National Park followed in 1879, Canada created Banff in 1885, and by the twentieth century most nations had park systems. The International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) classifies many national parks as Category II protected areas.

Management and challenges

Definitions vary by country — in England and Wales, national parks include lived-in landscapes of farms and villages, while in the United States they are largely uninhabited wilderness. Common challenges include visitor pressure, invasive species, boundary conflicts, funding, and the effects of climate change. Famous examples include Serengeti, Kruger, Fiordland, Banff, Yosemite and the Galápagos.
