Portfolio investment

Portfolio investment is investment in the form of a group (portfolio) of assets, including transactions in equity securities, such as common stock, and debt securities, such as banknotes, bonds, and debentures. Internationally, it is recorded in the balance of payments as investment in foreign securities without seeking control of the issuing enterprise.

Distinction from direct investment

Portfolio investment differs from foreign direct investment in that the investor does not acquire a controlling interest in the enterprise; conventionally, ownership of less than 10 percent of voting shares counts as portfolio investment. Portfolio holdings are generally liquid — they can be bought and sold quickly on financial markets — whereas direct investment is longer-term and illiquid.

Characteristics

Investors hold portfolios to diversify risk across assets, sectors, and countries, balancing return against risk as described by modern portfolio theory. Because portfolio flows can reverse rapidly in response to changing sentiment or interest rates, economists sometimes describe large inflows into emerging markets as volatile "hot money" with implications for exchange rates and financial stability.
