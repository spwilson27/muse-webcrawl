The Voskhod programme (Russian: Восход, "Sunrise") was the second Soviet human spaceflight programme, carried out in 1964–1965. It adapted the one-person Vostok capsule to carry multiple cosmonauts, scoring two spectacular firsts in the Space Race against the United States: the first multi-person spaceflight and the first spacewalk.

Flights

Voskhod 1 (12 October 1964) carried Vladimir Komarov, Konstantin Feoktistov, and Boris Yegorov — the first crew of three, and the first flight by a scientist and a physician. To fit three crew, the capsule had no ejection seats and the cosmonauts flew without spacesuits or a launch escape system, accepting significant risk.

Voskhod 2 (18 March 1965) carried Pavel Belyayev and Alexei Leonov. Using an inflatable airlock, Leonov performed the first extravehicular activity (EVA), spending about twelve minutes outside the spacecraft. His spacesuit ballooned in vacuum, forcing him to bleed off pressure to re-enter the airlock, and the crew later survived a manual re-entry and landed in deep snow in the Urals.

Cancellation

Further Voskhod missions — including long-duration and artificial-gravity flights — were cancelled as the USSR concentrated on the Soyuz programme and the race to the Moon. Although the programme achieved its propaganda goals, its improvisations were widely regarded as dangerous even at the time.
