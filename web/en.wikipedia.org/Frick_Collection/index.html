The Frick Collection is an art museum in New York City, housed in the former residence of the industrialist Henry Clay Frick on Fifth Avenue in Manhattan. It is known for its distinguished collection of Old Master paintings and decorative arts, displayed in the intimate setting of a Gilded Age mansion.

History

Henry Clay Frick (1849–1919), who made his fortune in coke and steel as a partner of Andrew Carnegie, built the house between 1913 and 1914 to designs by Thomas Hastings of the firm Carrère and Hastings. Frick bequeathed the house and its art collection to the public. After the death of his wife Adelaide, the residence was converted into a museum and opened to the public in 1935. A major renovation and expansion in the early 2020s added new galleries and amenities while restoring the historic interiors.

Collection

The collection includes works by Bellini, Rembrandt, Vermeer (three paintings, one of the largest groups in a single collection), Turner, Whistler, Goya, El Greco, Fragonard, Boucher, and Gainsborough. The Fragonard Room holds the celebrated series The Progress of Love. The museum also preserves fine French furniture, Limoges enamels, sculpture, and Chinese porcelain.

Frick Art Reference Library

Adjacent to the museum is the Frick Art Reference Library, founded in 1920 by Helen Clay Frick, one of the major art history research libraries in the United States.
