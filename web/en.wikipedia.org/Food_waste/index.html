Food waste

Roughly one third of food produced for human consumption is lost or wasted globally.

Food waste (or food loss) is food that is discarded or lost uneaten at stages of production, processing, retailing, and consumption. Global food loss and waste amount to between one third and one half of all food produced. The Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) has estimated that about 14% of the world's food is lost between harvest and retail, while the UN Environment Programme's Food Waste Index estimated that a further 19% of food available to consumers was wasted at retail, food service, and household level in 2022 — roughly 1.05 billion tonnes. In the EU alone, over 59 million tonnes of food waste were generated in 2021.

Definitions

The FAO distinguishes "food loss", occurring in the supply chain from harvest up to (but excluding) retail, from "food waste", occurring at retail and consumption. Both are grouped together as food loss and waste (FLW). The definition may be limited to food originally intended for human consumption, or may include food diverted to animal feed, bioenergy, or other non-food uses as a form of loss.

Causes

Causes differ by development level. In low-income countries, losses concentrate early in the chain because of inadequate storage, cold chains, transport, and processing infrastructure, and because of pests and spoilage in hot climates. In high-income countries, waste concentrates at retail and consumer level: cosmetic standards cause edible produce to be rejected, large stocks and promotions encourage over-purchasing, and confusion over date labels ("best before" versus "use by") leads households to discard safe food. In the home, over-preparation, poor storage, and forgotten leftovers are common causes.

Environmental and social impact

Food loss and waste account for an estimated 8–10% of global greenhouse gas emissions, because the land, water, fertilizer, and energy used to produce the food are wasted and because food decomposing in landfill generates methane. The global economic cost has been estimated at around one trillion US dollars per year. The issue is ethically charged because hundreds of millions of people face hunger and food insecurity while vast quantities of edible food are discarded.

Reduction

Sustainable Development Goal 12.3 commits countries to halve per-capita retail and consumer food waste by 2030 and reduce losses along supply chains. Measures include improved storage and logistics, redistribution of surplus food through food banks and charities, date-label reform, consumer awareness campaigns, animal feed and industrial uses, anaerobic digestion, and composting; the waste hierarchy places prevention first and landfill last. Some countries, including France, have legislated to require supermarkets to donate unsold edible food.
