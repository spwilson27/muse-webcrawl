The Bulldog is a British breed of dog of mastiff type, also known as the English Bulldog or British Bulldog. It is a medium-sized, muscular dog with a distinctive pushed-in nose, heavily wrinkled face, drooping lips and an undershot jaw. Bulldogs were originally bred for the blood sport of bull-baiting, in which dogs seized and held a tethered bull, but after the sport was banned in England in 1835 the breed was refashioned by fanciers into a calm companion animal.

Characteristics

Modern Bulldogs are stocky and low-slung, weighing around 18–25 kilograms, with a short smooth coat in colours including brindle, fawn, white and red, often in combination. Bred for companionship, they are typically gentle, affectionate and patient with children, and famously stubborn. The Bulldog is a national symbol of Britain, associated with determination and pluck, and is a popular mascot for universities, sports teams and the United States Marine Corps.

Health

The breed's extreme brachycephalic (short-faced) conformation causes well-documented health problems, including breathing difficulties (brachycephalic obstructive airway syndrome), overheating, skin-fold infections, hip dysplasia and reproductive problems — most puppies are delivered by caesarean section because of their large heads. These concerns have led to criticism of breed standards and, in some countries, legal restrictions on breeding dogs with extreme features.
