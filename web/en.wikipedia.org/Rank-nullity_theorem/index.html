Rank–nullity theorem

The rank–nullity theorem is a fundamental theorem of linear algebra. It states that for a linear transformation T : V → W between vector spaces, where V is finite-dimensional, the dimension of V equals the sum of the rank and the nullity of T:

dim V = rank(T) + nullity(T)

Here the rank is the dimension of the image (range) of T, and the nullity is the dimension of the kernel (null space) of T — the set of vectors mapped to zero. For a matrix A, the statement becomes: the number of columns equals the rank of A (dimension of the column space) plus the dimension of its null space.

Proof idea

A standard proof extends a basis of the kernel of T to a basis of V. The images under T of the added basis vectors are then shown to form a basis of the image of T. Counting vectors on both sides gives the result. The theorem can also be derived from the first isomorphism theorem for vector spaces, which states that V/ker(T) is isomorphic to im(T).

Consequences

The theorem implies that a linear map between spaces of equal finite dimension is injective if and only if it is surjective. For a system of linear equations Ax = b, it quantifies how many free variables a solution has: nullity counts the degrees of freedom of the solution set. It also underlies statements about invertibility of square matrices and appears in generalisations such as the dimension formula for sums of subspaces.
