741 operational amplifier

The 741 operational amplifier is a classic general-purpose operational amplifier integrated circuit, introduced by Fairchild Semiconductor in 1968. Designed by Dave Fullagar, it improved on the earlier 709 op-amp by adding internal frequency compensation, making it stable and easy to use. It became one of the most widely used analogue ICs of all time.

Characteristics

The 741 is typically supplied in an 8-pin package with two input pins (inverting and non-inverting), an output, dual supply rails (commonly ±15 V), and offset-null pins. Typical figures include an open-loop gain of about 200,000, an input impedance around 2 MΩ, a slew rate of about 0.5 V/µs, and a gain-bandwidth product near 1 MHz. These modest specifications are nevertheless adequate for many audio, instrumentation, and educational applications.

Applications

The 741 has been used in amplifiers, filters, oscillators, comparators, integrators, and signal-conditioning circuits. Although modern op-amps surpass it in speed, noise, and supply range, the 741 remains a standard teaching device and is still manufactured in large numbers.
