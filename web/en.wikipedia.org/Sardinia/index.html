Sardinia (Italian: Sardegna; Sardinian: Sardigna) is the second-largest island in the Mediterranean Sea, after Sicily, and one of the twenty regions of Italy. It is an autonomous region with special statute under the Italian constitution. Its capital and largest city is Cagliari, on the southern coast.

Geography

Sardinia lies west of the Italian mainland, south of Corsica (from which it is separated by the Strait of Bonifacio) and north of Tunisia. Much of the interior is mountainous or hilly, dominated by the Gennargentu massif, whose Punta La Marmora (1,834 m) is the island's highest summit. The Campidano plain in the south-west is the principal lowland and agricultural area. The island is noted for its long coastline, clear waters and resorts such as the Costa Smeralda in the north-east.

History

Sardinia was settled in prehistory and is famous for the Bronze Age Nuragic civilisation, whose inhabitants built more than 7,000 stone towers (nuraghi) — the best known, Su Nuraxi di Barumini, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The island was successively controlled by Phoenicians and Carthaginians, Rome (from 238 BCE), Vandals, Byzantines, and local independent kingdoms called giudicati. Aragonese and then Spanish rule lasted from the 14th century until 1718, when Sardinia passed to the House of Savoy; the Kingdom of Sardinia, based in Turin, became the nucleus of Italian unification in 1861.

Culture and languages

Sardinian (sardu) is a Romance language recognised as co-official and considered among the most conservative descendants of Latin; Catalan survives in Alghero, and Gallurese and Sassarese are spoken in the north. The island has distinctive traditions of polyphonic singing (cantu a tenore, recognised by UNESCO), horsemanship festivals such as the Sartiglia of Oristano, and a notable longevity hotspot in its mountain villages.

Economy

Tourism, agriculture (including Cannonau wine and pecorino cheese), sheep farming — the island supports several million sheep — and mining have historically dominated the economy, supplemented by petrochemicals, services and technology in Cagliari.
