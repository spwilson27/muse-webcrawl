Drive-through

A drive-through, or drive-thru, is a type of take-out service provided by a business that allows customers to purchase products without leaving their cars. The format is best known from fast food restaurants but is also used by banks, pharmacies, coffee shops, liquor stores and even wedding chapels in Las Vegas.

History

Drive-in services appeared in the United States in the 1920s and 1930s; the Red's Giant Hamburg stand in Springfield, Missouri, is often credited with an early drive-through window in 1947, and Jack in the Box built its 1951 model on drive-through service with a two-way intercom. McDonald's opened its first drive-through in 1975 in Sierra Vista, Arizona, near a military base whose soldiers could not leave their cars in uniform. By the late 20th century drive-throughs accounted for the majority of fast-food sales in the United States.

Operation

A typical lane routes customers past a menu board and speaker or touchscreen for ordering, a payment window, and a pick-up window; larger sites use dual lanes, order confirmation screens and licence-plate or app-based ordering. Speed of service is measured closely and layouts are engineered around the kitchen. Drive-throughs have been criticised for encouraging car dependence, idling emissions and noise, and some cities restrict new ones, while operators have added EV charging, walk-up windows and AI voice ordering.
