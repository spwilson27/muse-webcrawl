Dominick Labino

Dominick Labino (1910–1987) was an American glass artist, engineer, and inventor who was a central figure in the founding of the studio glass movement. A vice president and director of research at Johns Manville Fiber Glass, Labino held over sixty patents related to glass composition and fiber glass technology.

Role in studio glass

In 1962, Harvey Littleton held two glassblowing workshops at the Toledo Museum of Art using a small furnace based on Labino's design and glass marbles of Labino's #475 composition, which melted at a low temperature suitable for small studios. These workshops are regarded as the birth of the studio glass movement, demonstrating that individual artists could melt and work glass outside factories.

Artwork

Labino's own blown-glass works, often featuring interior veiling and aerial forms, are held in museum collections internationally. He built a studio on his farm near Grand Rapids, Ohio, and lectured and demonstrated widely.
