National Guard Bureau
Formed1903 (as Militia Bureau)
HeadquartersThe Pentagon, Arlington, Virginia
Parent agencyDepartment of Defense
ChiefChief of the National Guard Bureau

The National Guard Bureau (NGB) is the federal instrument responsible for the administration of United States National Guard personnel and activities in all 50 states, three territories and the District of Columbia. It is a joint activity of the Department of Defense, serving as the channel of communications between the Department of the Army, the Department of the Air Force and the state National Guards.

History

The bureau traces its origins to the Militia Act of 1903, which created a Militia Bureau in the War Department to improve the readiness of state militias. Reorganized several times, it became the National Guard Bureau in 1933, jointly administering Army and (from 1947) Air National Guard affairs. The National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2008 elevated the chief of the National Guard Bureau to four-star rank, and in 2012 the chief became a member of the Joint Chiefs of Staff.

Leadership

The Chief of the National Guard Bureau is the senior uniformed National Guard officer, responsible for ensuring that the more than 400,000 Army and Air National Guard personnel are accessible, capable and ready. A Vice Chief and directors of the Army National Guard and Air National Guard support the bureau's work.
