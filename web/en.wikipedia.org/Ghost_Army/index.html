Ghost Army

Active1944–1945
CountryUnited States
BranchUnited States Army
Official name23rd Headquarters Special Troops
SizeAbout 1,100 men
RoleTactical military deception

The Ghost Army was the popular name of the United States Army's 23rd Headquarters Special Troops, a tactical deception unit of about 1,100 men that operated in Europe during the Second World War from shortly after D-Day until the end of the war in 1945. Using inflatable tanks and artillery, sound effects, fake radio traffic, and theatrical impersonation, the unit impersonated far larger American formations — sometimes simulating forces of 30,000 men — to deceive the German Army about Allied strength and intentions.

Organisation and methods

The unit was recruited heavily from art schools and advertising agencies and included future notables such as fashion designer Bill Blass and painter Ellsworth Kelly. Its sub-units specialised in visual deception (inflatable vehicles and camouflage), sonic deception (recordings of armoured and infantry activity played through powerful speakers, developed with Bell Labs), radio deception ("spoof radio" imitating real operators' traffic), and "atmosphere" — soldiers wearing the insignia of impersonated units, driving convoys in loops, and frequenting cafés to spread disinformation.

Operations

The Ghost Army carried out more than twenty deception operations in France, Luxembourg, Belgium, and Germany. Its largest and most consequential mission supported the crossing of the Rhine in March 1945 (Operation Viersen), drawing German attention away from the actual crossing sites; the deception is credited with saving many lives. The unit's activities remained classified for more than forty years and were not officially acknowledged until 1996. In 2022 the surviving members were awarded the Congressional Gold Medal.
