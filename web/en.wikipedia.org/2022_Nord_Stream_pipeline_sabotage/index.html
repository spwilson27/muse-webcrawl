2022 Nord Stream pipeline sabotage

The 2022 Nord Stream pipeline sabotage was a series of underwater explosions and consequent gas leaks that damaged the Nord Stream 1 and Nord Stream 2 pipelines in the Baltic Sea on 26 September 2022. The pipelines, built to carry natural gas from Russia to Germany, were ruptured at several points near the Danish island of Bornholm. The blasts occurred amid the energy crisis following Russia's invasion of Ukraine; investigations by Denmark, Sweden, and Germany concluded the damage was deliberate sabotage, but responsibility has remained officially unattributed and politically contested.

The explosions

Seismologists recorded two blasts in the early hours and afternoon of 26 September, measuring about magnitude 2.3 and 2.1. Both strings of Nord Stream 1 and one string of Nord Stream 2 were breached; the second Nord Stream 2 string remained intact. Neither pipeline was delivering gas at the time — Russia had halted Nord Stream 1 flows weeks earlier and Nord Stream 2 had never entered service — but both were pressurised, and the leaks released an estimated several hundred thousand tonnes of methane over the following days, one of the largest single methane releases ever recorded. Gas bubbling zones up to a kilometre wide were observed by Danish and Swedish patrols.

Investigations

Sweden's investigation found traces of explosives on recovered debris and confirmed "gross sabotage"; Denmark and Sweden closed their inquiries in 2024 without naming suspects, citing jurisdiction and evidentiary limits. Germany's federal investigation continued, focusing on a chartered sailing yacht, the Andromeda, allegedly used by a small team of divers. In August 2024 German prosecutors obtained an arrest warrant for a Ukrainian diving instructor, who left Poland before he could be detained; in 2025 Italian police arrested another Ukrainian suspect in the case. Ukraine has denied state involvement.

Competing claims

Russia blamed the United States and United Kingdom, and in February 2023 journalist Seymour Hersh published an account alleging a US Navy operation with Norwegian assistance — a claim the White House dismissed as "utterly false". Western officials initially speculated about Russian responsibility. No government has formally attributed the attack, and the episode remains a prominent unresolved mystery of the Ukraine war era, with the pipelines abandoned and their operator pursuing insurance and arbitration claims.
