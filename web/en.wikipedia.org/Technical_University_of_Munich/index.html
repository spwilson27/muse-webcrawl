Technical University of Munich

The Technical University of Munich (German: Technische Universität München, TUM) is a public research university in Munich, Germany, specialising in engineering, technology, medicine, and the natural and social sciences. Founded in 1868 as a polytechnic school by King Ludwig II of Bavaria, it is consistently ranked among the best universities in Germany and Europe and has been designated a "University of Excellence" under the German Excellence Initiative and its successor strategy.

Campuses and schools

TUM's main campus is in central Munich, with a large science campus at Garching — home to departments of physics, chemistry, informatics, mathematics, and mechanical engineering, as well as the FRM II research neutron source — a life sciences campus at Weihenstephan in Freising, a medical campus around the Klinikum rechts der Isar hospital, and additional sites including Heilbronn and Singapore (TUM Asia). In the 2020s the university reorganised its faculties into interdisciplinary schools.

People and research

The university has more than 50,000 students and a strong international profile. Numerous Nobel laureates have studied or worked at TUM, including chemist Hans Fischer and physicist Rudolf Mössbauer. TUM is known for close ties to industry and for a prolific start-up scene, supported by its affiliated entrepreneurship centre UnternehmerTUM.
