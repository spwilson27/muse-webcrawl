Rock and Roll Hall of Fame

Established1983 (foundation); museum opened 1995
LocationCleveland, Ohio, United States
Building architectI. M. Pei

The Rock and Roll Hall of Fame is a museum and hall of fame located in downtown Cleveland, Ohio, on the shore of Lake Erie. It documents the history of rock music and honours the artists, producers, engineers and others who have influenced it. The museum's distinctive glass pyramid building was designed by architect I. M. Pei and opened on 2 September 1995.

Induction

The Rock and Roll Hall of Fame Foundation was established in 1983 by Atlantic Records founder Ahmet Ertegun and others. Cleveland was chosen as the museum's home partly because local disc jockey Alan Freed popularised the term "rock and roll". Artists become eligible for induction 25 years after the release of their first record. Inductees are chosen by a voting body of historians, artists and industry figures, in categories including Performers, Early/Musical Influence, Non-Performers (the Ahmet Ertegun Award) and Musical Excellence. The first class, inducted in 1986, included Elvis Presley, Chuck Berry, Little Richard, Buddy Holly and Jerry Lee Lewis.

Museum

The museum holds extensive exhibits on the roots and development of rock and roll, major artefacts from artists' careers, and a permanent hall displaying inductees' signatures. Annual induction ceremonies, long held in New York, have rotated to Cleveland regularly since 2012.

Criticism

The Hall has been criticised for the opacity of its selection process, under-representation of women and of some genres, and the long exclusion of popular acts, prompting fan campaigns and, in some cases, inductees' public protests.
