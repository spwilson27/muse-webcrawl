Facial muscles

The facial muscles are a group of striated skeletal muscles supplied by the facial nerve (cranial nerve VII) that, among other things, control facial expression. These muscles are also called mimetic muscles. They are only found in mammals.

Structure

Unlike most skeletal muscles, the facial muscles attach to skin and fascia rather than moving joints, allowing subtle movements of the face. Major muscles include the frontalis, orbicularis oculi, orbicularis oris, zygomaticus major, buccinator, platysma, and the corrugator supercilii.

Development and innervation

The facial muscles develop from the second pharyngeal arch and are all innervated by branches of the facial nerve. Damage to the facial nerve, as in Bell's palsy, causes paralysis of the muscles on the affected side.
