Peter of Colechurch

Peter of Colechurch (died 1205) was a priest and architect of medieval London, remembered as the builder of the first stone London Bridge. He was chaplain and head of the Fraternity of the Brethren of London Bridge, the organisation responsible for the bridge's maintenance, and had earlier overseen repairs to the old wooden bridge from around 1163.

The stone bridge

Construction of the stone bridge, a few metres upstream of the wooden one, began in 1176 under Peter's direction. It was an ambitious work for its time: nineteen pointed arches on piers founded on starlings (timber-piled islands) in the Thames, carrying a roadway lined eventually with houses and a chapel of St Thomas Becket at its centre. Peter did not live to see it finished — he died in 1205, four years before completion in 1209 — and was buried in the bridge chapel.

Legacy

The bridge Peter began stood for over 600 years, until replaced in 1831. When the medieval bridge was demolished, remains believed to be his were reinterred; a plaque in the crypt of St Magnus-the-Martyr church, at the bridge's northern end, commemorates him. The Bridge House Estates charity that he helped shape still maintains London's bridges today.
