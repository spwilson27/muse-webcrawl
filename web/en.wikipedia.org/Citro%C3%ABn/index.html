Citroën (French pronunciation: [sitʁo.ɛn]) is a French automobile brand. The "Automobiles Citroën" manufacturing company was founded on 4 June 1919 by André Citroën. After being part of the PSA Group (Peugeot Société Anonyme) from 1976, Citroën became a marque of Stellantis in 2021 following the merger of PSA and Fiat Chrysler Automobiles. Its logo — the double chevron — derives from the herringbone gears André Citroën had produced since 1900.

History

Citroën pioneered mass production in Europe, modelled on Henry Ford's methods, and built a reputation for innovation: the Traction Avant (1934) combined front-wheel drive with unitary construction; the 2CV (1948) became an icon of basic French motoring; the DS (1955) introduced hydropneumatic self-levelling suspension and striking aerodynamic styling; the SM and CX followed. André Citroën was also a flamboyant marketer — he rented advertising space on the Eiffel Tower itself from 1925 to 1934.

Motorsport and recent years

Citroën achieved major rally success, dominating the World Rally Championship with Sébastien Loeb, who won nine consecutive drivers' titles (2004–2012) with the Xsara, C4 and DS3 WRC cars. Contemporary Citroën models emphasise comfort and distinctive styling, including the C3, C4, C5 X and ë-C3 electric car.
