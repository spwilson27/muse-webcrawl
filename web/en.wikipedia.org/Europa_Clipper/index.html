ObjectiveInvestigate Europa's habitability

Europa Clipper is a NASA interplanetary mission launched in October 2024 to study Jupiter's moon Europa while orbiting Jupiter. Its goal is to assess Europa's potential habitability: the moon is believed to hide a global saltwater ocean beneath its icy crust, containing perhaps twice the volume of Earth's oceans.

Mission profile

The spacecraft — the largest NASA has built for a planetary mission, with solar arrays spanning about 30 m — flew by Mars in 2025 and will use an Earth gravity assist in 2026 before arriving at Jupiter in 2030. To limit radiation exposure, it will orbit Jupiter and make about 49 close flybys of Europa rather than orbiting the moon itself.

Instruments

Nine instruments include the EIS cameras, the E-THEMIS thermal imager, the REASON ice-penetrating radar, the Europa-UVS ultraviolet spectrograph, the MASPEX mass spectrometer and SUDA dust analyser (to sample plume material), and a magnetometer to characterise the subsurface ocean's depth and salinity through induction.

Background

Galileo data in the 1990s first indicated Europa's ocean; Hubble observations suggested intermittent water plumes. The mission is managed by JPL with APL; ESA's JUICE spacecraft, launched 2023, will study the neighbouring moon Ganymede in the same era.
