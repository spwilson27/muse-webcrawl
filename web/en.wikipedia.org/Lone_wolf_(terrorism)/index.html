Lone wolf (terrorism)

A lone wolf, lone-wolf terrorist, or lone actor is an individual who prepares and commits violent acts alone, outside of any command structure and without material assistance from any group, though they may be influenced or motivated by the ideology and beliefs of an external group and may act in support of such a group.

Terminology and origins

The term “lone wolf” was popularised in the 1990s by white supremacists in the United States who promoted “leaderless resistance”, a strategy also described earlier by Louis Beam. Law-enforcement and academic usage has since broadened to Islamist, far-right, and other ideological attackers who self-radicalise, often online.

Characteristics

Researchers note lone actors often leak intent, have personal grievances fused with ideology, and may have mental-health difficulties, though profiles vary widely. Because they do not communicate with networks, they are harder to detect, but they also tend to lack training and capability compared with organised groups.

Responses

Counter-terrorism strategies emphasise community reporting, threat-assessment teams, and monitoring of online radicalisation, while scholars debate how “lone” such actors truly are given online communities that encourage them.
