White Sands National Park

LocationOtero and Doña Ana counties, New Mexico, US
Area146,344 acres (592 km²)
Established18 January 1933 (national monument); 20 December 2019 (national park)
Governing bodyNational Park Service

White Sands National Park is an American national park located in the state of New Mexico, surrounded by the White Sands Missile Range. The park covers about 145,762 acres in the Tularosa Basin and protects the southern part of the world's largest gypsum sand dune field, which spans 275 square miles.

Geology

The dunes are composed of gypsum crystals — a mineral rarely found as sand because it dissolves in water. Rain and snowmelt in the surrounding San Andres and Sacramento mountains dissolve gypsum and carry it to Lake Lucero, where evaporation leaves selenite crystals that weather into sand and are blown into dunes up to 60 feet high.

Ecology

More than 600 species of invertebrates and numerous reptiles, mammals, and birds inhabit the dune field. Many animals, such as the bleached earless lizard and the Apache pocket mouse, have evolved unusually pale coloration to blend with the white sand.

Human history and recreation

Fossilised human footprints at White Sands, dated to roughly 21,000–23,000 years ago, are among the earliest evidence of human habitation in the Americas. Visitors today hike the dunes, picnic, and sled on the gypsum slopes; the park was redesignated from a national monument to a national park in December 2019.
