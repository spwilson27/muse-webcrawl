Ozark National Scenic Riverways

LocationOzarks, Missouri, United States
Area80,785 acres (32,691 ha)
Established27 August 1964
Governing bodyNational Park Service

The Ozark National Scenic Riverways is a national park in the Ozarks of southern Missouri, in the United States, protecting the Current River and its tributary the Jacks Fork. It was the first national park area created specifically to protect a river system, authorised by Congress in 1964 and dedicated in 1971.

Features

The park preserves spring-fed, free-flowing rivers famed for canoeing, kayaking, fishing, and camping. The region contains some of the largest freshwater springs in the United States, including Big Spring, as well as caves, bluffs, and historic structures such as Alley Spring Mill.

Ecology

The riverways protect Ozark forests and aquatic habitats supporting diverse fish, mussels, and the endangered Ozark hellbender salamander. Former farms in the valley were bought out as the park was assembled, a process that generated lasting local controversy.
