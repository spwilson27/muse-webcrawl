Karl Paul August Friedrich Liebknecht (13 August 1871 – 15 January 1919) was a German socialist and anti-militarist. A member of the Social Democratic Party of Germany (SPD), he co-founded, with Rosa Luxemburg, the anti-war Spartacus League during the First World War, and at the end of 1918 the Communist Party of Germany (KPD). He is remembered for his lone vote in the Reichstag against war credits in December 1914 and for his murder, together with Luxemburg, by Freikorps troops in January 1919.

Early life

Liebknecht was the son of Wilhelm Liebknecht, a co-founder of the SPD. Trained as a lawyer, he specialised in defending socialists in political trials and became a leading figure in the socialist youth movement and a member of the Reichstag from 1912.

First World War and revolution

On 2 December 1914 Liebknecht was the only Reichstag deputy to vote against further war credits, breaking SPD party discipline. He was imprisoned from 1916 to 1918 for organising anti-war demonstrations. Released during the November Revolution of 1918, he proclaimed a "Free Socialist Republic" from the Berlin Palace on 9 November, hours after Philipp Scheidemann proclaimed the republic for the SPD.

Death

Following the Spartacist uprising of January 1919, Liebknecht and Luxemburg were captured and murdered by members of the Freikorps Guards Cavalry Rifle Division on 15 January 1919. Their deaths made them enduring martyrs of the German left.
