Decoy (intercontinental ballistic missile)

An intercontinental ballistic missile decoy is a penetration aid carried by a ballistic missile to confuse enemy missile defence systems during the midcourse phase of flight. Decoys are released along with the actual warheads in the post-boost phase and are designed to mimic the radar or infrared signature of a reentry vehicle.

Types

The simplest decoys are lightweight inflatable balloons coated with a reflective film, which in the vacuum of space travel on the same trajectory as the much heavier warheads. More sophisticated "replica" decoys attempt to reproduce the warhead's signature across multiple sensor bands, while anti-simulation techniques deliberately make the warhead look different from decoys. Chaff, jammers, and the missile's own booster fragments also serve as incidental decoys.

Strategic significance

Because midcourse discrimination between warheads and decoys is extremely difficult, decoys are a major challenge for exoatmospheric missile defence interceptors such as the US Ground-Based Midcourse Defense system. Decoys are far less effective inside the atmosphere, where drag slows lightweight objects, so terminal-phase defences are less vulnerable to them.

Arms control

The development of decoys and other penetration aids has been a recurring issue in arms control negotiations, since improvements in missile defence tend to spur offensive countermeasures, complicating efforts to limit strategic arsenals.
