Salento is a town and municipality in the department of Quindío, Colombia, in the heart of the Eje Cafetero, the country's coffee-growing region. Founded in 1842, it is one of the oldest municipalities in the department and a leading tourist destination.

Tourism

Salento is known for its colourful bahareque colonial architecture, the craft shops of Calle Real, and its trout restaurants. It is the gateway to the Cocora Valley, famous for its towering wax palms — Colombia's national tree — and for hiking in Los Nevados National Natural Park.

Geography

The town sits at about 1,895 metres in the Cordillera Central of the Andes. Earthquakes, notably in 1999, damaged parts of the region, and many buildings were rebuilt in traditional style.
