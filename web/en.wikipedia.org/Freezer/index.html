Freezer

Freezer
TypeHome appliance / refrigeration equipment
Typical temperature−18 °C (0 °F) or below
Common formsChest freezer, upright freezer, refrigerator compartment
PrincipleVapour-compression refrigeration

A freezer is a device that preserves food and other perishable items by keeping them at temperatures below the freezing point of water, typically at or below −18 °C (0 °F). At such temperatures, bacterial growth is essentially halted and chemical reactions slow dramatically, extending storage life from days to many months. Freezers may be standalone units or compartments of a refrigerator.

Types

Chest freezers open from the top, retain cold air well when opened, and are usually the most energy-efficient per unit volume. Upright freezers resemble refrigerators, with shelves and door storage for easier organization, and are available in manual-defrost and frost-free versions. Drawer and under-counter freezers serve compact kitchens, while industrial blast freezers freeze goods rapidly to protect texture and quality.

How it works

Most freezers use the vapour-compression cycle: a compressor pressurizes a refrigerant gas, which condenses and releases heat at the back or base of the unit, then expands and evaporates inside coils, absorbing heat from the cabinet. A thermostat cycles the compressor to maintain the set temperature.

Food storage

Regulators recommend keeping freezers at −18 °C (0 °F) or colder. Frozen food remains safe indefinitely in principle, though quality declines over time; freezer burn results from dehydration at exposed surfaces and is a quality, not safety, defect. Freezers are also used for ice making, laboratory samples, and medical storage — ultra-low-temperature freezers reach −80 °C for vaccines and biological specimens.
