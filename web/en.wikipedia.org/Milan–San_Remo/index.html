Milan–San Remo is an annual road cycling race between Milan and Sanremo in northern Italy. Nicknamed La Primavera ("the Spring Classic") because it is held in mid-March, it is the longest professional one-day race of the modern era at about 298 kilometres and is traditionally the first of the five Monuments of cycling.

Course

First held in 1907, the race crosses Lombardy and Piedmont, climbs the Passo del Turchino to reach the Ligurian coast, then follows the Via Aurelia westward. The decisive climbs come late: the Cipressa, added in 1982, and especially the Poggio di San Remo, introduced in 1960, whose summit lies just a few kilometres from the finish on the Via Roma. The combination of great distance and the short final climbs makes the race a contest between sprinters who can survive the Poggio and attackers who escape on it.

Records

Eddy Merckx holds the record with seven victories between 1966 and 1976; Italy's Costante Girardengo won six editions in the 1910s and 1920s. Because of its length the race is considered one of the hardest to win and the easiest to lose, and its list of winners includes most of the great one-day riders in the sport's history.
