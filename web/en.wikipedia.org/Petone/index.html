Petone (Māori: Pito-one) is a large suburb of Lower Hutt in the Wellington Region of New Zealand's North Island. It stands at the southern end of the Hutt Valley, on the northern shore of Wellington Harbour, at the mouth of the Hutt River. Its name derives from the Māori pito-one, meaning "end of the sand beach".

Jackson Street, Petone's main shopping street

Petone was the site of the first organised European settlement in the Wellington region: New Zealand Company settlers arrived aboard the Aurora in January 1840 and built Britannia township on the foreshore. Flooding from the Hutt River soon drove most settlers across the harbour to Thorndon in Wellington. A first signing context of the Treaty of Waitangi also has associations with the area, and the Te Puni memorial on the foreshore commemorates the Ngāti Toa chief Honiana Te Puni.

Industry and character

In the late nineteenth and twentieth centuries Petone became one of New Zealand's principal industrial suburbs, home to woollen mills, meat processing, railway workshops, and later motor vehicle assembly and tobacco manufacture. As heavy industry declined, Jackson Street was revitalised as a heritage shopping and café precinct, and the Petone Settlers Museum on the Esplanade tells the story of early migration. The suburb is served by Petone railway station on the Hutt Valley Line.
