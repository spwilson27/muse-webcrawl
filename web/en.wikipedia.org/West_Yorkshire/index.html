West Yorkshire is a metropolitan and ceremonial county in the Yorkshire and the Humber region of England. It covers an area of 2,029 square kilometres (783 sq mi) and is the most populous county in Yorkshire, with a population of around 2.4 million. Its largest settlement is the city of Leeds, and other major settlements include Bradford, Huddersfield, Halifax and Wakefield.

The county was created on 1 April 1974 under the Local Government Act 1972, largely from the urbanised parts of the historic West Riding of Yorkshire. It is governed by five metropolitan district councils; the West Yorkshire Combined Authority coordinates transport, economic development and, since 2021, an elected mayor.

History

West Yorkshire grew rapidly during the Industrial Revolution as a centre of the woollen textile industry, coal mining and engineering. Mill towns such as Halifax, Huddersfield and Dewsbury were built on worsted and woollen manufacture, while Leeds became a major commercial and financial centre.

Geography

The county rises westward toward the Pennine hills, reaching its highest point at Black Hill on the border with Greater Manchester. Rivers including the Aire, Calder and Colne drain the region eastward.

Economy and culture

Leeds is one of the United Kingdom's largest financial and legal centres outside London. West Yorkshire is home to the Yorkshire Sculpture Triangle — the Hepworth Wakefield, Yorkshire Sculpture Park, Leeds Art Gallery and the Henry Moore Institute — and to the National Science and Media Museum in Bradford, the world's first UNESCO City of Film.
