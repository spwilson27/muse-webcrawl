Maxim (philosophy)

A maxim is a principle or rule of conduct. In moral philosophy, the term is used most prominently by Immanuel Kant, for whom a maxim is the subjective principle on which an agent acts — roughly, the rule the agent gives herself, incorporating her intention and circumstances.

Kantian maxims

In the Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals (1785), Kant defines a maxim as "the subjective principle of volition." The categorical imperative tests maxims: one may act only on a maxim that one can at the same time will to become a universal law. Maxims thus connect general moral law to particular actions.

Interpretation

Scholars debate how maxims should be formulated and individuated — the so-called "generality problem" — since the same action can be described under many possible maxims.
