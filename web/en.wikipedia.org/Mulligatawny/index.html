Mulligatawny is a soup which originated from South Indian cuisine but became a classic of Anglo-Indian cooking. The name originates from the Tamil words miḷagu (மிளகு, "pepper") and taṇṇi (தண்ணி, "water"), i.e. "pepper-water". The original dish was a thin, spicy pepper rasam, served by Tamil servants to British officials of the East India Company who wanted a soup course; their cooks enriched it with meat, coconut, rice and other ingredients to suit British tastes.

By the mid-19th century, recipes for mulligatawny appeared widely in British cookbooks, and it became a staple in Britain, commonly made with chicken or mutton stock, curry powder, rice, and sometimes apple and coconut milk. Heinz sold a tinned version in the UK from the 1960s. Modern versions range from Anglo-Indian curry soups to vegetarian lentil-based interpretations closer to the South Indian original.

Variations

There is no single canonical recipe: the soup may be yellow with turmeric and curry powder, thickened with rice or red lentils, sharpened with lemon or tamarind, and finished with coconut milk. It remains popular in Britain, Australia and in Indian restaurants abroad.
