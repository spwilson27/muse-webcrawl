Junkers Ju 88

Role: Multirole combat aircraft

Manufacturer: Junkers Flugzeug- und Motorenwerke

First flight: 21 December 1936

Number built: about 15,000

The Junkers Ju 88 was a German World War II Luftwaffe twin-engined multirole combat aircraft. Designed by Junkers Flugzeug- und Motorenwerke (JFM) in the mid-1930s as a so-called Schnellbomber ("fast bomber"), it was one of the most versatile combat aircraft of the war, serving as a bomber, dive bomber, night fighter, torpedo bomber, reconnaissance aircraft, heavy fighter and tank destroyer.

Design and development

The Ju 88 originated in a 1935 Reich Air Ministry requirement for a fast bomber. The prototype, designed under W. H. Evers and American engineer Alfred Gassner, first flew on 21 December 1936. Early versions suffered from structural and engine troubles, and the addition of dive-bombing capability and defensive armament increased weight and reduced the originally hoped-for performance, but the aircraft matured into a capable and adaptable design.

Operational history

The Ju 88 entered service shortly before the outbreak of war and fought in nearly every theatre, including the Battle of Britain, the Eastern Front, North Africa and the Atlantic. In the night-fighter role (Ju 88C, R and G series) it became the Luftwaffe's most important night fighter. Production totalled roughly 15,000 aircraft, second among German bombers only to the Heinkel He 111 in some counts, and the type remained in production throughout the war.

Variants

Major families included the Ju 88A bomber, the Ju 88C/R/G night fighters, the Ju 88D reconnaissance version, the Ju 88H long-range version, the Ju 88P tank destroyer with large cannon, and the Ju 88S high-speed bomber. The enlarged Ju 188 and Ju 388 were developed from the basic design.
