SMS

SMS (Short Message Service) is a text messaging service component of most telephone, Internet and mobile device systems. It uses standardised communication protocols that let mobile devices exchange short text messages, and is the technical foundation of "texting". SMS was originally designed as part of the GSM standard in the 1980s, and the first text message — "Merry Christmas" — was sent on 3 December 1992 by engineer Neil Papworth to a mobile phone on the Vodafone UK network.

Technical overview

SMS transmits messages over the signalling channels of the mobile network rather than the voice/data channels, via a Short Message Service Centre (SMSC) that stores and forwards messages. A single SMS segment carries 140 bytes, allowing 160 characters in the GSM 7-bit alphabet, 140 in 8-bit data, or 70 characters in UCS-2 (used for many non-Latin scripts). Longer messages are split into concatenated segments. Delivery reports can inform the sender when a message reaches the recipient's handset.

History and adoption

The concept was developed in the Franco-German GSM cooperation by Friedhelm Hillebrand and Bernard Ghillebaert in 1984; Hillebrand's analysis of typical sentence lengths motivated the 160-character limit. Commercial services launched in the mid-1990s, and SMS use exploded in the late 1990s and 2000s, especially among young users. By 2010, trillions of messages were sent annually. Although person-to-person SMS has since been overtaken in many markets by Internet messaging apps (such as WhatsApp and iMessage), SMS remains universal — requiring no data plan or specific app — and is heavily used for two-factor authentication, alerts, and application-to-person (A2P) messaging.

Successors

MMS extended SMS to multimedia content. Rich Communication Services (RCS) is positioned as its modern successor, adding read receipts, typing indicators and high-quality media over data connections while retaining telephone-number addressing.
