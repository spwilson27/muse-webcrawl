Iodine deficiency

EffectsGoitre, hypothyroidism, intellectual disability, congenital iodine deficiency syndrome
CauseInsufficient dietary iodine
PreventionIodised salt, iodine-rich foods, supplements

Iodine deficiency is a lack of the trace element iodine, which is essential for the production of thyroid hormones. It is the world's leading preventable cause of intellectual disability, and its consequences in a population are collectively called iodine deficiency disorders.

Signs and effects

The thyroid gland enlarges (goitre) as it attempts to trap more iodine. Deficiency during pregnancy and early childhood impairs brain development and can cause congenital iodine deficiency syndrome (formerly called cretinism), characterised by stunted growth and severe intellectual impairment. In adults, deficiency may cause hypothyroidism, fatigue, and weight gain.

Causes and distribution

Iodine is scarce in soils leached by glaciation, flooding, or erosion, so mountainous regions such as the Himalayas, Alps, and Andes, and inland flood plains, have historically been most affected. Seafood, dairy products, and foods grown in iodine-rich soil are the main dietary sources.

Prevention

Universal salt iodisation — adding small amounts of potassium iodate or iodide to table salt — is the principal public health strategy, adopted in most countries during the 20th century. It has dramatically reduced the global burden of deficiency, though hundreds of millions of people remain at risk where iodised salt coverage is incomplete.
