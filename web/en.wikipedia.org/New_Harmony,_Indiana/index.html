New Harmony is a historic town in Posey County, Indiana, United States, on the Wabash River. It was the site of two early 19th-century utopian communities and became an important early scientific and educational centre of the American West.

Harmonie Society

The town was founded in 1814 as Harmony by the German pietist Harmony Society led by George Rapp, whose members built a prosperous communal settlement before deciding to return to Pennsylvania in 1824.

Owen's community

In 1825 the Welsh industrialist Robert Owen purchased the town for an experiment in communal, egalitarian living, renamed New Harmony. The experiment dissolved by 1827, but the community attracted scientists and educators including naturalist Thomas Say and geologist David Dale Owen, and hosted one of the first free public libraries and coeducational schools in the United States.

Today

Many Harmonist and Owenite buildings survive, and the town is a National Historic Landmark district with a small population of under 1,000.
