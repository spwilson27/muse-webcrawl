Cosimo I de' Medici (12 June 1519 – 21 April 1574) was the second Duke of Florence from 1537 and the first Grand Duke of Tuscany from 1569 until his death. Coming to power at seventeen after the assassination of his predecessor, he transformed Florence from a fragile duchy into a consolidated territorial state, and his descendants ruled Tuscany for two centuries.

Rise to power

Cosimo was the son of the celebrated condottiere Giovanni de' Medici, known as Giovanni delle Bande Nere, and Maria Salviati, a granddaughter of Lorenzo the Magnificent. When Duke Alessandro de' Medici was murdered in January 1537, the Florentine senate, expecting the distant and inexperienced seventeen-year-old Cosimo to be a pliable figurehead, elected him head of the republic and then duke. He quickly proved otherwise: he defeated an army of Florentine exiles and their French backers at Montemurlo in August 1537, punished his opponents, and secured recognition of his hereditary title from the Emperor Charles V.

Rule

Cosimo centralised administration, built an efficient bureaucracy and treasury, and pursued an expansionist policy in Italy. His greatest acquisition was the Republic of Siena, conquered after a brutal siege (1554–1555) and formally ceded to him by Spain in 1557, uniting most of modern Tuscany under Medici rule. He founded the Order of Saint Stephen to fight Barbary corsairs, developed the port of Livorno, and, on 27 August 1569, received the title of Grand Duke of Tuscany from Pope Pius V, a dignity later recognised by the Emperor.

Patronage

A major patron of the arts and sciences, Cosimo employed Giorgio Vasari — who built the Uffizi and redecorated the Palazzo Vecchio — and Bronzino, Cellini, and Bartolomeo Ammannati. He founded the Accademia delle Arti del Disegno, laid out the Boboli Gardens with his wife Eleanor of Toledo, revived the University of Pisa, and supported Galileo's father Vincenzo and the botanical gardens of Pisa and Florence.

Death and succession

Cosimo abdicated much of the government to his son Francesco in 1564 and died at the Villa di Castello in 1574. The grand ducal line he founded ruled Tuscany until the extinction of the Medici in 1737.
