The TecDAX is a stock index of the 30 largest German technology companies by market capitalisation and trading volume, traded on the Frankfurt Stock Exchange. It is operated by Deutsche Börse and sits alongside the blue-chip DAX, the mid-cap MDAX, and the small-cap SDAX.

History

The TecDAX was introduced on 24 March 2003 as the successor to the NEMAX 50 index of the Neuer Markt, which had collapsed after the dot-com bubble burst. The new index was designed with stricter admission and transparency rules to restore confidence in listed German technology shares. In 2018 the index system was reorganised so that TecDAX constituents could also be members of the DAX or MDAX, ending the former strict separation between "classic" and technology segments.

Composition

Companies are selected according to free-float market capitalisation and exchange turnover, with the composition reviewed regularly. Well-known constituents over the years have included SAP, Infineon, Siemens Healthineers, Deutsche Telekom, TeamViewer, and Nemetschek.
