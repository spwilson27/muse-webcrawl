National Film Development Corporation of India

Founded: 24 April 1975

Headquarters: Mumbai, Maharashtra, India

Parent: Ministry of Information and Broadcasting, Government of India

Abbreviation: NFDC

The National Film Development Corporation of India (NFDC) is a public sector undertaking under the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting of the Government of India. Established in 1975, it was formed to promote and organise an integrated development of the Indian film industry and to foster the growth of quality cinema, particularly the parallel or art cinema movement. The NFDC is based in Mumbai.

Activities

The NFDC finances, produces and co-produces films in various Indian languages, often supporting first-time and independent filmmakers whose work might not find commercial backing. Films backed by the NFDC include Gandhi (1982, as co-producer), Jaane Bhi Do Yaaro (1983), Mirch Masala (1987), Salaam Bombay! (1988), Miss Beatty's Children and works by directors such as Satyajit Ray, Mrinal Sen, Shyam Benegal, Govind Nihalani and Mira Nair. Many NFDC films have won National Film Awards and international festival honours.

Film Bazaar and festivals

The NFDC organises Film Bazaar, a major co-production and distribution market held annually alongside the International Film Festival of India in Goa, as well as the NFDC Film Market events. Following a government decision to rationalise film bodies, the activities of the Films Division, the Directorate of Film Festivals, the National Film Archive of India and the Children's Film Society were merged into the NFDC in the early 2020s, making it India's nodal agency for film promotion and preservation.
