Roaming

Roaming is a wireless telecommunication term typically used with mobile devices (mobile phones, mobile computers, routers, etc.). It refers to the extension of connectivity service to a location different from the home location where the service was registered.

Roaming ensures that a traveling wireless device is kept connected to a network without losing the connection. In more technical terms, roaming refers to the ability for a cellular subscriber to automatically make and receive voice calls, send and receive data, or access other services, including home data services, when travelling outside the geographical coverage area of the home network, by means of using a visited network.

Types of roaming

Regional roaming is the ability of cellular subscribers to use their phones outside the geographic area in which they live but inside their home country, where roaming partners provide coverage. National roaming is a subset where subscribers roam within their home country outside their provider's coverage. International roaming (or global roaming) allows subscribers to use their phones on foreign networks in other countries.

Process

When a mobile phone is switched on in a visited network, the network registers the device, exchanges identifying information with the home network, and authenticates the subscriber. The visited network periodically sends location updates to the home network so that incoming calls and messages can be routed. The home operator typically charges the subscriber retail roaming rates, while wholesale charges are settled between operators.

Regulation

High international roaming charges have drawn regulatory attention. The European Union capped roaming charges within the European Economic Area and, from 15 June 2017, abolished retail roaming surcharges under its "Roam like at home" rules, allowing subscribers to use their domestic allowances across the EU at no extra cost, subject to fair-use policies.
