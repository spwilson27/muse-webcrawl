Sir

Sir is a formal honorific address in English for men, derived from the Middle French sire and ultimately from the Latin senior ("elder"). It is used in several contexts: as a respectful form of address to a man, as the style of knights and baronets, and in certain official and military settings.

Knights and baronets

In the United Kingdom and some Commonwealth realms, men appointed to certain orders of knighthood, and baronets, are entitled to prefix "Sir" to their forename (for example, Sir Winston Churchill). The equivalent for women is "Dame". The title is used with the given name, or given name and surname, never with the surname alone.

General usage

In everyday speech "sir" is a polite form of address to a man whose name is unknown or as a mark of respect, common in customer service, formal correspondence ("Dear Sir"), and disciplined environments such as schools and the armed forces, where subordinates address male officers as "sir". Similar usages occur in South Asian English in education and officialdom.
