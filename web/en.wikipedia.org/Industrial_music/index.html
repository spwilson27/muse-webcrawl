Industrial music

OriginsMid-1970s, United Kingdom and United States
Key actsThrobbing Gristle, Cabaret Voltaire, Einstürzende Neubauten, Skinny Puppy, Nine Inch Nails
LabelIndustrial Records

Industrial music is a genre of experimental music that emerged in the mid-1970s, drawing on avant-garde, electronic and noise traditions and on provocative themes of technology, control and transgression. The term derives from Industrial Records, the label founded in 1976 by the English group Throbbing Gristle, whose slogan was "industrial music for industrial people".

Characteristics

Industrial music employs harsh mechanical sounds, tape loops, found objects and metal percussion, distorted electronics, and confrontational performance art. Influences include musique concrète, krautrock, dub, and the writings of William S. Burroughs and J. G. Ballard. Early groups such as Throbbing Gristle, Cabaret Voltaire and SPK rejected rock conventions in favour of abrasive, conceptual work.

Development and offshoots

In the 1980s the genre diversified: Einstürzende Neubauten built instruments from scrap metal; Coil and Psychic TV explored occult themes; and North American acts like Skinny Puppy and Ministry fused industrial textures with dance beats, producing electro-industrial and industrial metal. Nine Inch Nails brought the style to mass popularity in the 1990s with albums such as The Downward Spiral (1994).

Legacy

Industrial music influenced techno, EBM, power electronics, dark ambient and mainstream pop production, and its aesthetics — dystopian imagery, sampling, DIY media manipulation — remain embedded in electronic music culture.
