Dmitry Belyayev

Born17 July 1917, Protasovo, Russia
Died14 November 1985, Novosibirsk, USSR
FieldsGenetics, zoology, evolutionary biology
Known forDomesticated silver fox experiment

Dmitry Konstantinovich Belyayev (17 July 1917 – 14 November 1985) was a Soviet geneticist and the director of the Institute of Cytology and Genetics in Novosibirsk, Siberia. He is best known for the long-running silver fox domestication experiment, begun in 1959, which showed that selecting animals solely for tameness produced, within a few generations, a suite of other changes in appearance, physiology and behaviour—evidence for the role of domestication in evolution and for the connections between behaviour and development.

Career and the Lysenko era

Belyayev was a student of genetics at a time when Trofim Lysenko's pseudoscientific doctrines dominated Soviet biology and genetics was officially suppressed. Like many Soviet geneticists of his generation, he was forced to disguise his work, presenting his fox research as fur-breeding experiments of economic value, while quietly pursuing his real questions about heredity and domestication.

The silver fox experiment

At a fur farm in Novosibirsk, Belyayev and his colleague Lyudmila Trut selected silver foxes (Vulpes vulpes) for a single trait: tameness, measured by the foxes' lack of fear and aggression toward humans. Within about a decade, some foxes became as docile and affectionate as dogs. Strikingly, these tame foxes also developed changes that had not been selected for: floppy ears, mottled coats, curly tails, shorter snouts, and altered reproductive cycles. Belyayev interpreted these as evidence that selection on behaviour could change developmental pathways, acting through hormonal and neural-crest mechanisms, and that similar processes may have occurred during the domestication of dogs from wolves. The experiment continues at Novosibirsk, making it one of the longest-running experiments in evolutionary biology.

Legacy

Belyayev's work has had lasting influence on evolutionary developmental biology, the study of domestication, and popular understanding of how selection works. His findings are discussed in textbooks, popular science books such as Lee Alan Dugatkin and Lyudmila Trut's How to Tame a Fox (and Build a Dog) (2017), and documentaries. A street in the Akademgorodok science town of Novosibirsk bears his name.
