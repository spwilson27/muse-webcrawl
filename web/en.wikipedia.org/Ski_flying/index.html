Ski flying

Ski flying Governing body: FIS
Major hills: Planica, Oberstdorf, Vikersund, Bad Mitterndorf, Harrachov
World record: 254.5 m (Stefan Kraft, 2017)

Ski flying is a winter sport and a discipline of ski jumping in which skiers jump from specially designed, extra-large hills, achieving distances far beyond those of ordinary ski jumping. Only five ski flying hills are in regular use worldwide: at Planica (Slovenia), Oberstdorf (Germany), Vikersund (Norway), Bad Mitterndorf (Austria) and Harrachov (Czech Republic).

History

Ski flying developed in Planica, where in 1936 Sepp Bradl became the first man to jump over 100 metres, landing at 101.5 metres. Successive hill enlargements pushed distances past 200 metres in 1994, when Toni Nieminen first exceeded the mark at Planica. The FIS Ski Flying World Championships have been held since 1972, and a Ski Flying World Cup classification runs within the Ski Jumping World Cup.

Technique and records

Ski flyers descend a steep inrun reaching speeds around 100 km/h and launch into a gliding flight using the V-style, riding air pressure for as long as possible before landing on the steep outrun slope. The standing world record is 254.5 metres, set by Austria's Stefan Kraft at Vikersundbakken in 2017. Because of the extreme speeds and heights, ski flying is considered more dangerous than regular ski jumping, and competitions are often shortened or cancelled in adverse wind conditions.
