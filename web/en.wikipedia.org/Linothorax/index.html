Linothorax

The linothorax is a type of upper body armour used by the Ancient Greeks, as well as other Mediterranean peoples, made from layers of linen glued or stitched together, sometimes combined with scales or plates.

Depicted on vase paintings and worn, according to tradition, by hoplites and by Alexander the Great, the linothorax was lighter and cooler than bronze cuirasses. Modern reconstructions have tested its resistance to arrows and blades, though no complete ancient example survives.
