Original six frigates of the United States Navy

The United States Congress authorized the construction of six frigates on 27 March 1794 with the Naval Act of 1794. These were the first ships of the newly created United States Navy. Designed by Joshua Humphreys, they were larger, more heavily armed, and more strongly built than standard frigates of the period, intended to outgun any frigate they could not outrun and to outrun any ship of the line.

The six ships

The six frigates were: United States, Constellation, Constitution, Congress, Chesapeake, and President. Construction was spread among shipyards from Portsmouth, New Hampshire, to Norfolk, Virginia. They were built of live oak and other durable timbers, with diagonal riders strengthening their hulls. USS Constitution's exploits in the War of 1812—particularly her victories over HMS Guerriere and HMS Java—earned her the nickname "Old Ironsides."

Service

The frigates saw action in the Quasi-War with France, the First Barbary War, and the War of 1812. Chesapeake was captured by HMS Shannon in 1813 and President by a British squadron in 1815. Constitution alone survives: she remains a commissioned ship of the U.S. Navy, berthed in Boston as the world's oldest commissioned naval vessel afloat.
