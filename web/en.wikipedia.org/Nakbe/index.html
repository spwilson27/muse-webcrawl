Nakbe

Nakbe (also spelled Nakbé) is an ancient Maya archaeological site in the Mirador Basin of the Petén department of northern Guatemala. It is one of the earliest known Maya cities, with occupation dating from the Middle Preclassic period (from about 1000–800 BCE), and it reached prominence during the Late Preclassic (about 400 BCE – 200 CE).

Site description

Nakbe is divided into eastern and western groups linked by a raised causeway (sacbe). The site contains some of the earliest monumental architecture in the Maya lowlands, including triadic pyramid complexes and an early ballcourt. Stelae found at the site, such as Stela 1, are among the oldest carved stone monuments in the lowlands. A codex-style painted pottery tradition has been linked to Nakbe and the Mirador Basin.

Regional context

Nakbe is closely related to the much larger neighbouring city of El Mirador, roughly 13 km to the northwest, which rose to dominate the basin in the Late Preclassic. Both cities were largely abandoned around 150 CE, along with other Mirador Basin centres, possibly due to environmental degradation and warfare.
