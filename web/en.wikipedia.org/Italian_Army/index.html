Italian Army

The Italian Army traces its origins to the Royal Army of 1861.

The Italian Army (Italian: Esercito Italiano) is the land-based component of the Italian Armed Forces. It was formed on 4 May 1861 as the Royal Army (Regio Esercito) following the unification of Italy, and assumed its present name when Italy became a republic in 1946. Its headquarters is in Rome, and it numbers roughly 95,000–100,000 active personnel.

History

The Royal Army fought the Third Italian War of Independence, the colonial campaigns in Eritrea, Libya and Ethiopia, and both World Wars — suffering severe losses on the Alpine front in 1915–18 and in North Africa and Russia in 1940–43. After the 1943 armistice, Italian co-belligerent forces fought alongside the Allies. The post-war army was rebuilt within NATO, and from the 1990s has deployed extensively on peacekeeping and combat missions abroad, including Iraq and Afghanistan.

Organisation and equipment

The Army is organised into operational commands and brigades, including the Alpine brigades Taurinense and Julia, the Folgore parachute brigade, and the Bersaglieri and Lagunari regiments. Principal equipment includes the Ariete main battle tank, the Centauro tank destroyer, the Dardo and Freccia infantry fighting vehicles, and Mangusta attack helicopters.
