Police body camera

TypeWearable audio/video recording device
UsersLaw enforcement agencies worldwide
PurposesEvidence, accountability, training

A police body camera or body-worn camera (BWC) is a wearable audio, video, or photographic recording system used by police to record their interactions with the public or gather video evidence at crime scenes.

Adoption

Body cameras were first trialled in the United Kingdom in the mid-2000s and adopted widely in the United States in the 2010s, following high-profile incidents and public demands for police accountability. Major manufacturers include Axon (formerly TASER International). By the 2020s most large US and UK police forces had equipped frontline officers with cameras.

Evidence and debate

Research on cameras' effects on use of force and complaints is mixed: some early studies found large reductions in complaints, while larger later trials found smaller or context-dependent effects. Debates continue over when officers may activate or deactivate cameras, data retention, public access to footage, and the privacy of victims and bystanders.
