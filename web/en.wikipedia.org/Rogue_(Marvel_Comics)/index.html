Rogue (Marvel Comics)

Real name: Anna Marie

Rogue is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics, commonly in association with the X-Men. Created by writer Chris Claremont and artist Michael Golden, she first appeared in Avengers Annual #10 (1981). A mutant from Mississippi, Rogue's powers cause her to absorb the psyche and abilities of anyone she touches, making physical contact dangerous and leaving her unable to touch others safely.

Fictional character biography

Born Anna Marie, Rogue ran away from home as a teenager and was taken in by the mutants Mystique and Destiny, joining the Brotherhood of Evil Mutants. After a fight with Ms. Marvel (Carol Danvers), Rogue permanently absorbed Danvers' powers and memories, a trauma that drove her to seek help from Professor Charles Xavier and join the X-Men, where she became one of the team's core members and later a leader.

Powers and abilities

Rogue's mutation allows her to absorb the memories, personality traits, physical skills, and—if her target is superhuman—powers of others through skin-to-skin contact. Prolonged contact can be fatal. From Ms. Marvel she long retained superhuman strength, durability, and flight. Her inability to control the absorption has been a defining source of conflict in her relationships, notably with her husband, Gambit.

In other media

Rogue was a main character in X-Men: The Animated Series (1992–1997) and its revival X-Men '97, where her Southern drawl and catchphrases made her popular. In the 20th Century Fox X-Men films she was played by Anna Paquin.
