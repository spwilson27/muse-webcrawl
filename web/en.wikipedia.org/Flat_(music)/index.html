Flat (music)

In music, flat (♭) is an accidental that lowers the pitch of a note by one semitone. The symbol itself derives from a stylised lowercase "b". A note is made flat by adding the flat sign before it in notation; the effect lasts for the remainder of the measure unless cancelled by a natural sign or another accidental.

Notation and terminology

In key signatures, flats follow a fixed order — B♭, E♭, A♭, D♭, G♭, C♭, F♭ — each a perfect fifth apart. A double flat (𝄫) lowers a note by two semitones. In intonation and performance contexts, "flat" also describes a pitch that is lower than intended or than the reference pitch.

Enharmonic equivalents

In twelve-tone equal temperament, a flattened note is enharmonically equivalent to another note: for example, D♭ sounds the same as C♯. Composers choose one spelling or the other according to harmonic context and readability.
