Acid–base disorder

Acid–base disorder
Specialty: Nephrology, critical care
Types: Acidosis, alkalosis (metabolic / respiratory)
Diagnosis: Arterial blood gas

An acid–base disorder is an abnormality of the human body's normal balance of acids and bases that causes the plasma pH to deviate from the normal range (7.35 to 7.45). Disorders are classified by their primary cause: respiratory or metabolic.

Classification

Acidosis is an excess of acid (or loss of base), and alkalosis an excess of base (or loss of acid). Respiratory disorders arise from changes in carbon dioxide controlled by the lungs; metabolic disorders arise from changes in bicarbonate, regulated by the kidneys and metabolism. The body compensates through buffering, ventilation, and renal adjustment.

Causes

Metabolic acidosis can result from kidney failure, diabetic ketoacidosis, lactic acidosis, or toxins. Respiratory acidosis results from hypoventilation, as in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease. Alkaloses may follow vomiting, diuretic use, or hyperventilation.

Diagnosis

Arterial blood gas analysis with electrolytes and the anion gap is used to identify the primary disorder and compensation.
