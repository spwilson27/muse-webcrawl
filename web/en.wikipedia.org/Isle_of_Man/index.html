Isle of Man

The Isle of Man (Manx: Ellan Vannin) is a self-governing British Crown Dependency in the Irish Sea, between Great Britain and Ireland. It is not part of the United Kingdom, but its monarch is the British sovereign (as Lord of Mann) and the UK is responsible for its defence. Its capital is Douglas.

Government

The island's parliament, the Tynwald, claims to be the world's oldest continuous parliamentary body, founded in 979 AD. It is bicameral, comprising the elected House of Keys and the Legislative Council. The Isle of Man has its own laws, taxes, stamps and coins, and is known for low-tax policies that have made it a financial services centre.

Culture and geography

The island covers 572 km²; its highest point is Snaefell (620 m), from whose summit six kingdoms are said to be visible on a clear day. Manx Gaelic, once near extinction, has been revived. The island is famous for the TT (Tourist Trophy) motorcycle races, held on public roads since 1907, for tailless Manx cats, and for the triskelion symbol of three armoured legs on its flag.
