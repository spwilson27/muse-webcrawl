Great Hall of the People
Chinese人民大会堂 (Rénmín Dàhuìtáng)
LocationTiananmen Square, Beijing, China
OpenedSeptember 1959
ArchitectZhang Bo

The Great Hall of the People is a state building situated at the western edge of Tiananmen Square in Beijing. It is used for legislative and ceremonial activities by the government of China. The People's Great Hall functions as the meeting place for the full sessions of China's legislature, the National People's Congress, which occurs every year during March along with the national session of the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference.

History

The hall was one of the "Ten Great Buildings" constructed for the 10th anniversary of the founding of the People's Republic of China. Designed by architect Zhang Bo, it was built in just ten months by a huge volunteer workforce and opened in September 1959. It covers about 171,800 square metres and stands 46.5 metres tall.

Architecture and interior

The building contains three main sections: the central Great Auditorium, which seats more than 10,000 people under a ceiling decorated with a galaxy of lights centred on a large red star; the State Banquet Hall, which can host banquets of 5,000 guests (used for state visits, including US President Richard Nixon's visit in 1972); and offices for the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress. Its 34 reception halls are named after, and decorated in the styles of, China's provinces and regions.

Use

Besides NPC sessions, the hall hosts party congresses of the Chinese Communist Party, state funerals and commemorations, diplomatic receptions, concerts and conferences. When not in official use, parts of it are open to tourists.
