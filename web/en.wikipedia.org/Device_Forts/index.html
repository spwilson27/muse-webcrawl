Commissioned byHenry VIII of England
PurposeCoastal defence against French and Spanish invasion

Deal Castle in Kent, one of the finest of the Device Forts.

The Device Forts are a series of artillery fortifications built across the south coast of England by Henry VIII between 1539 and 1547, following his break with the Roman Catholic Church and fears of invasion by France and Spain. Their name derives from the king's "device"—his written plan or programme of 1539—which ordered the defence of the coastline, harbours, and likely landing places.

Design

The forts were built to mount and withstand cannon fire, with squat rounded bastions and curved walls intended to deflect shot, marking the transition from medieval castles to modern artillery fortification. Early forts such as Deal and Walmer in Kent have a distinctive clover-leaf plan of concentric rounded tiers, while later designs moved toward angular bastions in the continental style. They guarded strategic anchorages including the Downs, Portsmouth, Southampton Water, and the Thames estuary.

History

The invasion scare passed, and many forts saw little action, but several were garrisoned and fought over during the English Civil War. Some remained in military use for centuries: Walmer Castle became the official residence of the Lord Warden of the Cinque Ports, occupied by figures including the Duke of Wellington and Queen Elizabeth the Queen Mother. Others, such as Pendennis Castle in Cornwall, were refortified in later wars up to the Second World War.

Survival

Many Device Forts survive, several in the care of English Heritage and open to the public, and they are considered among the finest coastal defences of the Tudor period.
