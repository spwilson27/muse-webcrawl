Will Rogers

BornWilliam Penn Adair Rogers, 4 November 1879, near Oologah, Cherokee Nation, Indian Territory
Died15 August 1935, near Point Barrow, Alaska Territory
OccupationHumorist, actor, entertainer, social commentator

William Penn Adair Rogers (1879–1935) was an American stage and film actor, vaudeville performer, cowboy, humorist, newspaper columnist, and social commentator from Oklahoma. A citizen of the Cherokee Nation, he began his career as a trick roper in Wild West shows, rose to stardom in the Ziegfeld Follies, and became one of the most beloved public figures in the United States during the 1920s and 1930s.

Career

Rogers's homespun wit — summarised in his famous line, "I never met a man I didn't like" — reached audiences through the stage, some 71 films (silent and sound), radio broadcasts, and a widely syndicated newspaper column. His gentle satire of politicians, Prohibition, and Congress made him a de facto spokesman for ordinary Americans during the Great Depression, and he was a major fundraiser for disaster relief.

Death and legacy

Rogers died on 15 August 1935 when the small plane piloted by aviator Wiley Post crashed shortly after takeoff near Point Barrow, Alaska; both men were killed. Tributes poured in nationwide. He is commemorated by the Will Rogers Memorial Museum in Claremore, Oklahoma, by Will Rogers World Airport in Oklahoma City, and by a statue in the United States Capitol representing Oklahoma.
