The Battle of San Pasqual, also spelled San Pascual, was a battle of the Mexican–American War fought on 6 December 1846 in the San Pasqual Valley of present-day San Diego County, California. United States dragoons under Brigadier General Stephen W. Kearny were defeated by Californio lancers commanded by Andrés Pico.

Background

Kearny's Army of the West, reduced to about 160 men after crossing from New Mexico, was marching to join American forces at San Diego. Warned that a Californio force was nearby, Kearny ordered a dawn attack despite exhausted men and mounts, many riding worn-out mules.

The battle

The American charge became strung out, and Pico's lancers — skilled horsemen armed with long lances — turned on the leading dragoons. In close fighting the lancers inflicted severe casualties: 21 Americans were killed, including Captain Abraham Johnston and Captain Benjamin Moore, and Kearny himself was wounded. The Americans claimed the field after the Californios withdrew, but the bloodied column was besieged on Mule Hill until relieved by sailors and marines from San Diego.

Aftermath

San Pasqual was the bloodiest battle fought in California during the war and the sharpest reverse suffered by U.S. forces there. The site is preserved as San Pasqual Battlefield State Historic Park.
