Internalism and externalism

Internalism and externalism are opposing positions in several areas of philosophy, including the philosophy of mind, epistemology and ethics. Broadly, internalists hold that the relevant facts are internal to the subject, while externalists hold that they partly depend on the subject's environment or relations to the world.

Philosophy of mind

Semantic externalism, associated with Hilary Putnam's Twin Earth thought experiment and Tyler Burge, holds that the content of a person's thoughts depends partly on their physical and social environment — in Putnam's phrase, meanings "just ain't in the head".

Epistemology

Internalists about justification argue that a belief's justification must be accessible to the believer, while externalists, such as reliabilists, allow that a belief may be justified by reliable processes the subject cannot introspect.
