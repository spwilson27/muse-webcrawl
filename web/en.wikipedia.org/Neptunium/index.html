Neptunium

Symbol: Np

Atomic number: 93

Group: Actinide

Discovery: 1940 (McMillan and Abelson)

Neptunium is a chemical element; it has symbol Np and atomic number 93. A radioactive actinide metal, neptunium is the first transuranium element. It is named after the planet Neptune, just as uranium was named after Uranus, since it follows uranium in the periodic table. Its longest-lived isotope, neptunium-237, has a half-life of about 2.14 million years.

Discovery

Neptunium was discovered in 1940 by Edwin McMillan and Philip Abelson at Berkeley, California, who produced neptunium-239 by bombarding uranium with neutrons. Claims in the 1930s — including Enrico Fermi's supposed "ausenium" — were later recognized as misidentifications of fission products. Trace quantities of neptunium occur naturally in uranium ores through neutron capture.

Properties and uses

Neptunium is a hard, silvery, ductile metal with an unusually complex crystal chemistry, adopting several allotropes. Its isotope 237Np is a by-product of nuclear reactors and spent fuel, a long-lived component of nuclear waste, and the precursor for producing plutonium-238 used in radioisotope power systems. Neptunium-237 is fissile and has been considered as a potential weapons and fast-reactor material, though it has never been used in a deployed weapon.
