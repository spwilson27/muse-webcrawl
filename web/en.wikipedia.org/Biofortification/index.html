Biofortification

Biofortification is the process of increasing the nutritional value of food crops — particularly their vitamin and mineral content — through conventional plant breeding, agronomic practices, or modern biotechnology. Its goal is to reduce "hidden hunger," the micronutrient deficiencies affecting billions of people.

Approaches

Breeding programmes select crop varieties naturally richer in iron, zinc, or vitamin A precursors. Agronomic biofortification applies mineral fertilisers, such as zinc or selenium, to soils. Transgenic approaches include Golden Rice, engineered to produce beta-carotene (provitamin A) in the grain.

Examples

Biofortified crops released in developing countries include orange-fleshed sweet potato rich in vitamin A, iron beans, zinc rice and wheat, and quality protein maize. Organisations such as HarvestPlus have coordinated the breeding and dissemination of these varieties.

Impact

Studies indicate biofortified crops can improve micronutrient status in consuming populations, complementing dietary diversification, supplementation, and industrial food fortification.
