The Betrothed (Italian: I promessi sposi) is an Italian historical novel by Alessandro Manzoni, first published in 1827. It is widely considered the most important novel in Italian literature and a foundational work in the development of the modern Italian language.

Plot

The story is set in Lombardy between 1628 and 1630, during Spanish rule. Renzo Tramaglino and Lucia Mondella, two young silk workers engaged to be married, are prevented from wedding by Don Rodrigo, a local nobleman who desires Lucia. Forced to flee, the couple are separated: Lucia takes refuge in a convent in Monza and later in Milan, while Renzo becomes caught up in the bread riots of Milan and flees to Bergamo. Their trials unfold against the backdrop of war, famine, and the plague of 1630, and the lovers are finally reunited and married.

Background and publication

Manzoni first drafted the novel as Fermo e Lucia in 1821–1823. The first published edition appeared in 1827. Dissatisfied with its language, Manzoni famously "rinsed" the text in the Arno — revising it into Florentine Italian — and published the definitive edition in 1840–1842, accompanied by his History of the Column of Infamy.

Themes and legacy

The novel explores providence, justice, and the lives of humble people caught in the machinery of power. Its 1840 edition effectively established Tuscan-based Italian as the literary standard, and the book remains a staple of Italian schools.
