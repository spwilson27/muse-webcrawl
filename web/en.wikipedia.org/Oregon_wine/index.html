Oregon wine

Vineyards in the Willamette Valley, Oregon's best-known wine region, noted for Pinot noir.

The U.S. state of Oregon has an established international reputation for its production of wine, ranking fourth in the United States by production. Oregon's modern wine industry began in the 1960s, when pioneers such as Richard Sommer, David Lett, and Charles Coury planted vinifera grapes — especially Pinot noir — in the Willamette Valley, on latitude roughly comparable to Burgundy, France.

Regions

Oregon contains more than twenty American Viticultural Areas (AVAs). The largest and best known is the Willamette Valley AVA, which produces the majority of the state's wine and contains nested sub-appellations such as Dundee Hills, Eola-Amity Hills, and Yamhill-Carlton. Southern Oregon includes the Umpqua Valley and Rogue Valley, while the Columbia Gorge and Walla Walla Valley straddle the Washington border.

Varieties

Pinot noir accounts for the largest share of plantings and is the variety most associated with Oregon, winning international acclaim since the 1980s. Other significant grapes include Pinot gris, Chardonnay, Riesling, and Syrah. Oregon law requires wines labelled by variety to contain at least 90% of the named grape, stricter than the United States federal standard.

Industry character

Oregon wineries tend to be small and family-owned, with a strong emphasis on sustainable, organic, and biodynamic farming. Wine is one of the state's most valuable agricultural products and a major draw for tourism.
