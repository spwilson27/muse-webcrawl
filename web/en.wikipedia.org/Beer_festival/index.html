Beer festival

A beer festival is an event at which a variety of beers are available for purchase and tasting. Festivals range from traditional folk celebrations such as Germany's Oktoberfest to specialist events organized by consumer groups, such as the Campaign for Real Ale (CAMRA) in Britain, where drinkers sample many small-producer and cask-conditioned beers in one venue.

Notable festivals

The world's largest beer festival is Oktoberfest in Munich, held annually from mid-September to the first Sunday in October since 1810; it attracts around six million visitors who drink beer served by Munich's major breweries in enormous tents. Other major German Volksfeste include the Cannstatter Volksfest in Stuttgart and the Starkbierfest strong-beer season in Munich. In Britain, CAMRA's Great British Beer Festival, held in London since 1977, showcases hundreds of real ales. In the United States, the Great American Beer Festival in Denver (founded 1982) is the country's largest ticketed beer event and competition, reflecting the craft-beer movement; Belgium hosts events such as the Zythos Beer Festival celebrating its diverse brewing traditions.

Format

Typical features include entry by ticket or token systems, small tasting measures that allow sampling many styles, brewery stalls, food vendors, live music, and commemorative glassware. Specialist festivals often publish tasting notes, run judging competitions, and focus on particular styles or regions, from lambic and Trappist ales to Bavarian lagers and American IPAs.
