Angra do Heroísmo

Angra do Heroísmo (Portuguese pronunciation: [ˈɐ̃ɡɾɐ ðu eɾuˈiʒmu]), or simply Angra, is a city and municipality on Terceira Island in the Portuguese autonomous region of the Azores. The population in 2011 was 35,402, in an area of 239.00 square kilometres.

Founded in the late fifteenth century, Angra was an important port of call for fleets sailing between Europe, Africa, and the Americas. Its historic centre is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, designated in 1983. The city is also the seat of the Roman Catholic Diocese of Angra.

History

Colonisation of the bay of Angra began around 1450 under the Flemish entrepreneur Jácome de Bruges. The settlement's sheltered anchorage made it a compulsory stop for Portuguese carracks returning from India and Brazil and for Spanish treasure fleets, and it was formally elevated to a city in 1534. Its epithet "do Heroísmo" ("of Heroism") was granted by Queen Maria II in recognition of the city's role in the Liberal Wars of the early nineteenth century. In 1980 an earthquake severely damaged the city, and the subsequent restoration underpinned the UNESCO listing.

Heritage

Landmarks include the sixteenth-century Sé Cathedral, the fortresses of São João Baptista and São Sebastião guarding the bay under Monte Brasil, the Palácio dos Capitães-Generais, and the Angra Museum housed in the former Franciscan convent. The Sanjoaninas festival in June is the largest popular festival in the Azores.
