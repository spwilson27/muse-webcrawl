Hearing dog

A hearing dog is a type of assistance dog specifically selected and trained to alert people who are deaf or hard of hearing to important sounds, such as doorbells, smoke alarms, ringing telephones, oven timers, alarm clocks and a baby's cry.

Training and work

When the dog hears a target sound, it makes physical contact with its owner — for example by pawing or nudging — and then leads the owner toward or away from the source; for a smoke alarm the dog may drop to the floor to signal danger. Training typically takes several months and builds on the dog's natural sound reactivity, often using rescue dogs of alert, responsive temperaments. In public, hearing dogs wear identifying jackets or leads, and in many jurisdictions their handlers have legal rights of access similar to those of guide dog users.

History

Organised training began in the United States in the 1970s with the founding of programmes such as Dogs for the Deaf (now Dogs for Better Lives). In the United Kingdom, Hearing Dogs for Deaf People was founded in 1982. Hearing dogs are one of several categories of service dog, alongside guide dogs for the blind and mobility assistance dogs.
