United Nations Security Council

United Nations Security Council
Established17 January 1946 (first session)
HeadquartersUnited Nations Headquarters, New York City
Membership15 states (5 permanent, 10 elected)
Permanent membersChina, France, Russia, United Kingdom, United States
Legal basisChapters V–VII, UN Charter

The United Nations Security Council (UNSC) is one of the six principal organs of the United Nations and is charged with ensuring international peace and security. It is the only UN body whose decisions — adopted as Security Council resolutions — are legally binding on all member states, and it can authorize sanctions, peacekeeping operations and the use of military force under Chapter VII of the UN Charter.

Membership

The Council has 15 members. Five are permanent — China, France, Russia, the United Kingdom and the United States (the "P5") — and each holds the power of veto over substantive resolutions. The remaining ten seats are filled by the General Assembly for staggered two-year terms, distributed among regional groups. The presidency rotates monthly among members in alphabetical order.

Powers

Under Chapter VI the Council investigates disputes and recommends peaceful settlements. Under Chapter VII, determining a "threat to the peace, breach of the peace, or act of aggression," it may impose diplomatic and economic sanctions, arms embargoes, travel bans and asset freezes, and as a last resort authorize "all necessary means," i.e. military action — as in Korea (1950) and the Gulf War (1990–91). It establishes and mandates peacekeeping operations, recommends the admission of new UN members, and, together with the General Assembly, elects the judges of the International Court of Justice.

History

The Council first met on 17 January 1946 in London. Cold War rivalry frequently paralysed it through vetoes, though it acted decisively during the Korean War while the Soviet Union boycotted its sessions. The People's Republic of China replaced the Republic of China (Taiwan) in the Chinese seat in 1971, and Russia succeeded the Soviet Union's seat in 1991. A surge of activism followed the Cold War — authorizing the liberation of Kuwait, creating war-crimes tribunals for Yugoslavia and Rwanda, and expanding peacekeeping — though vetoes over Syria and Ukraine in the 2010s–2020s revived charges of paralysis.

Reform debate

The Council's composition, reflecting the power balance of 1945, is widely debated. The G4 nations (Brazil, Germany, India, Japan) seek permanent seats; the Uniting for Consensus group opposes new permanent members; and African states demand representation through the Ezulwini Consensus. No amendment of the Charter has yet achieved the required ratifications, including by all five permanent members.
