The Beijing Coup (Chinese: 北京政變) was a coup d'état on 23 October 1924 in which the warlord Feng Yuxiang, commander of the Third Army of the Zhili clique, betrayed his own faction during the Second Zhili–Fengtian War, seized the capital Beijing, and forced President Cao Kun to resign.

Events

Feng's troops occupied key points in the capital, imprisoned Cao Kun, and issued telegrams calling for peace. Feng expelled the deposed Qing emperor Puyi from the Forbidden City, invited Sun Yat-sen north to discuss reunification, and installed a provisional government under Huang Fu, soon replaced by Duan Qirui as chief executive with Zhang Zuolin's support.

Consequences

The coup turned the tide of the war against Wu Peifu's Zhili forces and reshaped the balance of power among the warlords, but it did not end China's fragmentation; Feng's Guominjun, Zhang's Fengtian clique and Duan's government soon fell into new conflicts.
