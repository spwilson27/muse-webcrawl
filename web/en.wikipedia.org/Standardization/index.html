Standardization

Standardized shipping containers revolutionized global trade.

Standardization (American English) or standardisation (British English) is the process of implementing and developing technical standards based on the consensus of different parties, including firms, users, interest groups, standards organizations, and governments. Standardization can help maximize compatibility, interoperability, safety, repeatability, efficiency, and quality.

History

The implementation of standards in industry and commerce became highly important with the onset of the Industrial Revolution and the need for high-precision machine tools and interchangeable parts. Henry Maudslay developed the first industrially practical screw-cutting lathe in 1800, allowing standardized screw thread sizes for the first time. In the 20th century, national and international standards bodies proliferated, including the International Organization for Standardization (ISO), founded in 1947.

Types of standards

Standards may be classified as de facto standards, which arise through widespread use; de jure standards, established by law or formal agreement; and voluntary consensus standards, developed by standards development organizations. Examples include the SI system of units, ISO 9000 quality management standards, and technical standards for the Internet maintained by bodies such as the IETF and W3C.
