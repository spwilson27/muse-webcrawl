The Czechoslovak Socialist Republic (Czech and Slovak: Československá socialistická republika, ČSSR) was the official name of Czechoslovakia from 1960, when a new socialist constitution was adopted, until 1990, shortly after the Velvet Revolution. The state had been ruled by the Communist Party of Czechoslovakia since the coup of February 1948, and was a member of the Warsaw Pact and Comecon within the Soviet-led Eastern Bloc.

History

The 1960 constitution declared that socialism had been achieved in Czechoslovakia. In 1968 the reform movement known as the Prague Spring, led by Alexander Dubček, sought to liberalise the system; it was crushed by the Warsaw Pact invasion of August 1968, justified by the Brezhnev Doctrine. From 1969 the country became a federation of the Czech Socialist Republic and the Slovak Socialist Republic, while political life was dominated by the hardline "normalization" regime of Gustáv Husák. Dissidents organised around Charter 77, and mass protests in November 1989 — the Velvet Revolution — brought communist rule to an end. In March 1990 the word "Socialist" was dropped from the country's name, and in 1993 Czechoslovakia peacefully dissolved into the Czech Republic and Slovakia.
