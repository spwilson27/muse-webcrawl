Football in the Netherlands

Governing bodyKNVB (Royal Dutch Football Association)
Top leagueEredivisie
Main cupKNVB Cup
Historic clubsAjax, PSV, Feyenoord

Football is the most popular sport in the Netherlands, governed by the Royal Dutch Football Association (KNVB), founded in 1889. The Dutch game is historically renowned for "Total Football" (totaalvoetbal), the fluid tactical system developed at Ajax and the national team in the 1970s by Rinus Michels and Johan Cruyff.

League system

The top division is the Eredivisie (18 clubs), founded in 1956, followed by the Eerste Divisie. Three clubs — Ajax (Amsterdam), PSV (Eindhoven), and Feyenoord (Rotterdam) — have dominated domestically and all have won European trophies: Ajax won the European Cup four times (1971, 1972, 1973, 1995), Feyenoord in 1970, and PSV in 1988. The KNVB Cup is the main domestic knockout competition.

National team

The Netherlands national team, known as Oranje, reached three FIFA World Cup finals (1974, 1978, 2010), losing all three, and won the UEFA European Championship in 1988, when Marco van Basten's volley in the final against the Soviet Union became one of the tournament's most famous goals. The Dutch side also finished third at the 2014 World Cup.

Women's football

The Netherlands women's national team won UEFA Women's Euro 2017 on home soil under Sarina Wiegman and reached the final of the 2019 FIFA Women's World Cup. The Eredivisie Vrouwen is the top women's league.
