The National Science Foundation (NSF) is an independent agency of the United States federal government that supports fundamental research and education in all the non-medical fields of science and engineering. Its medical counterpart is the National Institutes of Health.

Mission and structure

Created by the National Science Foundation Act of 1950, signed by President Harry S. Truman, NSF funds roughly a quarter of all federally supported basic research conducted by America's colleges and universities. Grants are awarded through a competitive merit-review process. The Foundation is led by a director and the 24-member National Science Board, and is organized into disciplinary directorates — including Biological Sciences, Computer and Information Science and Engineering, Geosciences, Mathematical and Physical Sciences, and STEM Education — plus offices running the U.S. Antarctic Program and major facilities.

Facilities and achievements

NSF funds large research infrastructure such as the LIGO gravitational-wave observatories, the National Optical-Infrared Astronomy Research Laboratory, ocean drilling vessels and the Amundsen–Scott South Pole Station. Research it supported underlies advances from internet protocols and web browsers to magnetic resonance imaging and ecological long-term research networks.
