Syrianus

Bornc. 375 AD, Alexandria
Diedc. 437 AD, Athens
SchoolNeoplatonism

Syrianus (c. 375 – c. 437 AD) was a Greek Neoplatonist philosopher and head of the Academy in Athens. He was the teacher of Proclus, who succeeded him, and of Hermias.

Life

Born in Alexandria, Syrianus studied under Plutarch of Athens and became scholarch of the Platonic school. His lectures on Plato and Aristotle shaped the curriculum followed by later Neoplatonists.

Works

His surviving major work is a commentary on books B, Γ, M, and N of Aristotle's Metaphysics, in which he defends Platonic metaphysics against Aristotle's criticisms. Commentaries on Plato's Timaeus and Phaedrus survive only in fragments quoted by Proclus.

Influence

Through Proclus, Syrianus's interpretations of Plato and his doctrine of divine principles deeply influenced late antique and Byzantine philosophy.
