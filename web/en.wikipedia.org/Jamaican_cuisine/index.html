Jamaican cuisine

Jerk chicken, a signature Jamaican dish

Jamaican cuisine includes a mixture of cooking techniques, flavours and spices influenced by the island's Amerindian, West African, European (especially Spanish and British), Indian, Chinese and Middle Eastern inhabitants. It is known internationally for jerk seasoning, curried goat, ackee and saltfish — Jamaica's national dish — and patties.

Influences

The Taíno people contributed cassava, sweet potato and the method of slow-smoking meat over pimento wood. Spanish settlers brought escovitch (pickled fish) and citrus; enslaved Africans contributed okra, callaloo, yams and one-pot stews; the British introduced the pastry for patties and Easter bun; Indian indentured workers brought curry powders and roti; and Chinese and Lebanese communities added their own dishes and seasonings. The result is summed up in the national motto, "Out of Many, One People".

Jerk

Jerk is a style of cooking native to Jamaica in which meat is dry-rubbed or wet-marinated with a hot spice mixture also called jerk. Its principal ingredients are allspice (called "pimento" in Jamaica) and Scotch bonnet peppers. Traditionally applied to pork and chicken, jerk seasoning is now also used on fish, goat, beef, vegetables and tofu. The technique is traced to the Jamaican Maroons, descendants of escaped enslaved Africans who hunted wild hogs in the interior mountains and preserved the meat with spices and slow smoking.

Notable dishes

  • Ackee and saltfish — salted cod sautéed with ackee fruit, onions and peppers; the national dish.
  • Jerk chicken and jerk pork — grilled over pimento wood.
  • Curry goat — a festive dish served with rice or roti.
  • Rice and peas — rice cooked with kidney beans or gungo peas in coconut milk.
  • Jamaican patty — a flaky pastry filled with spiced beef, chicken or vegetables, its crust coloured with turmeric.
  • Oxtail stew, escovitch fish, pepperpot soup, run down (rundown), callaloo, festival and bammy (cassava flatbread).

Beverages

Jamaica is known for Blue Mountain coffee, rum (including Appleton Estate), Red Stripe beer, sorrel drink made at Christmas, ginger beer, and "roots" tonics. The island's food culture is celebrated internationally wherever the Jamaican diaspora has settled, particularly in London, New York and Toronto.
