Young Frankenstein is a 1974 American comedy film directed by Mel Brooks and written by Brooks and Gene Wilder. A parody of the classic Universal horror films, it follows Dr. Frederick Frankenstein (pronounced "Fronkensteen"), a respected neurosurgeon who inherits his grandfather's Transylvanian estate and resumes his experiments reanimating the dead.

Production

The film was shot in black and white, a rare choice for the era, and used the original 1930s laboratory equipment created for the earlier Frankenstein films. Wilder conceived the story and insisted that Brooks not appear on screen.

Reception and legacy

A critical and commercial success, the film is regularly ranked among the greatest comedies ever made. It was adapted into a Broadway musical in 2007 and was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry in 2003.
