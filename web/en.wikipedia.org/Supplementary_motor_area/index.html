Supplementary motor area

The supplementary motor area (SMA) is a part of the cerebral cortex involved in the planning and coordination of movement. It lies in the superior frontal gyrus, just anterior to the primary motor cortex representation of the legs, on the medial surface of the hemisphere (Brodmann area 6).

Function

The SMA contributes to internally generated sequences of movements, bimanual coordination, and postural stabilization. It is divided into a posterior SMA proper, more closely tied to movement execution, and an anterior pre-SMA, more involved in planning, learning sequences, and aspects of speech initiation. It has dense connections with the primary motor cortex, premotor areas, and the basal ganglia.

Clinical significance

Damage to the SMA, for example after surgery in the region, can produce the supplementary motor area syndrome: a temporary reduction of spontaneous movement and speech (mutism in dominant-hemisphere cases) that typically recovers over weeks.
