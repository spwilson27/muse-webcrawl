Abdullah II (born 30 January 1962) is the king of Jordan, reigning since the death of his father, King Hussein, on 7 February 1999. A member of the Hashemite dynasty, which has ruled Jordan since 1921 and claims descent from the prophet Muhammad, he is the 43rd-generation direct descendant of Muhammad according to the Hashemite genealogy. In the West he is widely regarded as a moderate, reform-minded monarch who has kept Jordan stable amid regional turmoil.

Early life and accession

Abdullah was born in Amman to King Hussein and his British-born wife Princess Muna. He was educated at St Edmund's School in England, Deerfield Academy in the United States, the Royal Military Academy Sandhurst, and Pembroke College, Oxford, and studied at Georgetown University's School of Foreign Service. He pursued a military career, serving in the Royal Jordanian Army and eventually commanding Jordan's special forces. Although his uncle Hassan had long been crown prince, Hussein changed the succession shortly before his death, naming Abdullah king and his half-brother Hamzah crown prince (a position later transferred to Abdullah's son Hussein in 2009).

Reign

Abdullah's reign has focused on economic liberalisation, free-trade agreements (including with the United States), and maintaining the 1994 peace treaty with Israel. Jordan absorbed large waves of refugees from the Iraq War and the Syrian civil war. The kingdom faced protests during the Arab Spring of 2011–2012, met with limited political reforms, and periodic unrest over austerity measures and taxation. Abdullah has also promoted interfaith initiatives, most notably the 2004 Amman Message defining orthodox Islam and rejecting extremism.
