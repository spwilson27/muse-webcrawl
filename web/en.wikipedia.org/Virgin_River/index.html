The Virgin River is a tributary of the Colorado River in the U.S. states of Utah, Nevada and Arizona. About 162 miles (261 km) long, it was designated Utah's first wild and scenic river in 2009. Its dramatic canyon in Zion National Park, including the famous Narrows, is among the best-known landscapes of the American Southwest.

Course

The river rises on the Markagunt Plateau in southwestern Utah and flows south through Zion National Park, where the North Fork carves the Zion Narrows — a slot canyon with walls up to 2,000 feet high and a riverbed as narrow as 20 feet. It continues past St. George, crosses the northwestern corner of Arizona, and enters Nevada, joining the Colorado River at Lake Mead near Overton.

History

The Virgin River valley was inhabited for centuries by Southern Paiute people. Jedediah Smith traversed the region in 1826, and the river's name is often attributed to his party, honouring trapper Thomas Virgin. Mormon pioneers settled the valley from the 1850s, founding communities such as St. George; the Old Spanish Trail and later routes followed the river through the Arizona Strip.

Ecology

The river supports endemic and threatened species including the woundfin, Virgin River chub and southwestern willow flycatcher, as well as desert bighorn sheep in its canyons. Its riparian corridor contrasts sharply with the surrounding Mojave and Great Basin deserts, and its flows are contested among municipal, agricultural and environmental users.
