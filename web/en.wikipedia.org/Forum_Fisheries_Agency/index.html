Pacific Islands Forum Fisheries Agency

The Pacific Islands Forum Fisheries Agency (FFA) is an intergovernmental organisation established in 1979 to help its Pacific Island member countries manage and develop their offshore fisheries, especially the highly migratory tuna stocks of the western and central Pacific Ocean — the world's largest tuna fishery, supplying more than half of the global tuna catch.

Formation1979
HeadquartersHoniara, Solomon Islands
Membership17 Pacific Island countries and territories
Parent bodyPacific Islands Forum

Role and history

The FFA was created under the South Pacific Forum Fisheries Agency Convention, signed in 1979 as Pacific Island states extended their jurisdiction with 200-nautical-mile exclusive economic zones. By acting collectively, small island states gained bargaining power over distant-water fishing nations seeking access to their tuna-rich waters. The agency provides legal, economic, and technical advice; coordinates licensing and access arrangements; and runs the regional vessel register and vessel monitoring system.

Surveillance and cooperation

The FFA's Regional Fisheries Surveillance Centre in Honiara coordinates maritime patrols and aerial surveillance among members, working with Australia, New Zealand, France, and the United States against illegal, unreported and unregulated fishing. Members also cooperate in sub-groupings such as the Parties to the Nauru Agreement (PNA), whose vessel day scheme limits purse-seine effort and has greatly increased access revenues for island governments. The FFA works alongside the Secretariat of the Pacific Community and the Western and Central Pacific Fisheries Commission, the regional fisheries management organisation established in 2004.
