Assassination of John F. Kennedy

DateNovember 22, 1963, 12:30 p.m. CST
LocationDealey Plaza, Dallas, Texas
TargetJohn F. Kennedy, 35th president of the United States
PerpetratorLee Harvey Oswald (per Warren Commission)

The assassination of John F. Kennedy occurred on Friday, November 22, 1963, in Dallas, Texas. Kennedy, the 35th president of the United States, was fatally shot while riding in a presidential motorcade through Dealey Plaza in an open-top limousine during a political trip to Texas.

The shooting

As the motorcade passed the Texas School Book Depository, shots were fired. Kennedy was struck in the neck and head, and Texas governor John Connally, riding in front of him, was seriously wounded. Kennedy was rushed to Parkland Memorial Hospital, where he was pronounced dead about half an hour later. Vice President Lyndon B. Johnson was sworn in as president aboard Air Force One that afternoon.

Oswald and the investigations

Former Marine Lee Harvey Oswald, an employee of the depository, was arrested that day for the murder of police officer J. D. Tippit and later charged with Kennedy's assassination. Two days later Oswald himself was shot and killed on live television by nightclub owner Jack Ruby while in police custody. The Warren Commission (1964) concluded that Oswald acted alone; persistent public doubt has fuelled decades of conspiracy theories, and a 1979 House committee concluded a conspiracy was probable, largely on acoustic evidence later disputed.

Legacy

The assassination shocked the world and remains a defining cultural and political event of the 20th century, endlessly analysed in books, films, and declassified document releases.
