Vowel

Vowel
Field: Phonetics, linguistics
Articulation: Voiced, unobstructed airflow
Contrasts with: Consonant

A vowel is a speech sound made by the vocal tract with the mouth open and without audible friction, forming the nucleus of a syllable. In phonetic terms, vowels are produced with a relatively open vocal tract and are typically voiced.

Classification

Vowels are classified by the position of the tongue (high/low, front/back), lip rounding, and length. The International Phonetic Alphabet represents vowel qualities on a quadrilateral diagram, with cardinal vowels such as [i], [a] and [u] marking the extremes of the space.

In writing systems

The word "vowel" also refers to letters representing such sounds, such as A, E, I, O and U in English. Alphabets differ in how completely they write vowels: abjads such as Arabic and Hebrew historically omitted most vowel signs, while alphabets like Greek and Latin assigned vowels their own letters.

Vowel systems

Languages vary greatly in their vowel inventories, from as few as two or three vowel qualities to more than a dozen, and many languages also distinguish long and short vowels, nasalised vowels and diphthongs.
