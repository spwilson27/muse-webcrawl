Donn Beach

Born: Ernest Raymond Beaumont Gantt, February 22, 1907, Limestone County, Texas, U.S.
Died: June 7, 1989 (aged 82), Honolulu, Hawaii, U.S.
Known for: Founder of Don the Beachcomber; inventor of tiki drinks

Donn Beach (born Ernest Raymond Beaumont Gantt; February 22, 1907 – June 7, 1989) was an American adventurer, businessman, and World War II veteran who was the "founding father" of tiki culture. He is known for opening the first prototypical tiki bar, Don the Beachcomber, during the 1930s in Hollywood, California. A series of spin-off restaurants and competitors soon spread the style across the United States and beyond.

Early life

Gantt grew up in Texas and New Orleans and travelled widely as a young man, including work as a bootlegger during Prohibition and journeys through the Caribbean and the South Pacific. Those travels supplied both the exotic decor and the rum-based drinks with which he later made his name.

Don the Beachcomber

In 1933, at the end of Prohibition, Gantt opened a small bar in Hollywood called Don's Beachcomber Café, soon renamed Don the Beachcomber. Decorated with flotsam from his travels — fishing nets, bamboo, tropical plants, and rain effects on the roof — it served elaborate rum cocktails of his own invention, including the Zombie (1934) and the Missionary's Downfall. Beach kept his recipes secret, mixing syrups in coded bottles so that no bartender could easily reproduce them elsewhere.

His great rival Victor "Trader Vic" Bergeron built a competing chain; together their establishments defined Polynesian-themed popular culture in mid-century America.

Later life

Beach served as a major in the U.S. Army Air Forces during World War II. In the 1950s he moved to Hawaii, where he developed the International Market Place in Waikiki. He legally changed his name to Donn Beach. He died in Honolulu in 1989, a decorated veteran holding the Purple Heart and the Bronze Star.
