Jacqueline du Pré

Jacqueline Mary du Pré (1945 – 1987) was an English cellist whose intense, emotive playing made her one of the most celebrated classical musicians of the twentieth century, before multiple sclerosis ended her performing career at the age of 28.

Born26 January 1945, Oxford, England
Died19 October 1987, London
InstrumentCello
SpouseDaniel Barenboim

Career

A prodigy taught by William Pleeth and heard by Pablo Casals and Mstislav Rostropovich, du Pré made her formal debut at Wigmore Hall in 1961. Her 1965 recording of Elgar's Cello Concerto with Sir John Barbirolli became a landmark, defining the work for a generation.

Illness and legacy

Du Pré married pianist-conductor Daniel Barenboim in 1967 and performed widely with him and with the "Jackie generation" of musicians. Symptoms of multiple sclerosis forced her retirement in 1973; she died in 1987. Her Stradivarius and Davidov cellos passed to other players, and her life inspired the film Hilary and Jackie.
