Novum Organum
Author: Francis Bacon
Language: Latin
Published: 1620
Subject: Scientific method
Full title: Novum Organum Scientiarum

Novum Organum, fully Novum Organum Scientiarum ("new instrument of science"), is a philosophical work by the English philosopher and statesman Francis Bacon, published in 1620. The title is a reference to Aristotle's work Organon, which was his treatise on logic and syllogism. In Novum Organum, Bacon details a new system of logic he believes to be superior to the old ways of syllogism. This is now known as the Baconian method.

Contents

The work was published in Latin in 1620 as the second part of Bacon's projected Instauratio Magna ("Great Instauration"), a six-part scheme for reforming all knowledge. It consists of two books of aphorisms. In Book One, Bacon attacks the "Idols of the Mind" — the idols of the tribe, the cave, the marketplace, and the theatre — false notions that obstruct true understanding. In Book Two, he sets out a method of inductive reasoning based on careful observation and experiment, including tables of presence, absence, and degrees for investigating the "forms" of natural phenomena.

Influence

The Novum Organum influenced the development of the scientific method and the philosophy of science, and it helped inspire the founding ethos of the Royal Society. Its inductive method was later refined by John Stuart Mill and others.
