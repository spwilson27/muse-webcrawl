Matching engine

A matching engine is the software system at the core of an electronic exchange that matches buy and sell orders for securities, commodities, derivatives or cryptocurrencies. It receives orders from market participants, maintains the order book, and executes trades according to defined priority rules.

Operation

Matching engines typically apply price-time priority: orders at the best price are executed first, and among orders at the same price, the earliest submitted are filled first. Other algorithms include pro-rata matching, common in some futures markets, and price-size priority. Modern engines process millions of messages per second with latencies measured in microseconds.

Design

Engines are usually built around a single-threaded, deterministic event loop to guarantee consistent ordering, with in-memory order books for speed. They disseminate market data feeds and order acknowledgements through protocols such as FIX. High-frequency trading has made matching-engine performance and fairness — including co-location of trading servers — important regulatory topics.
