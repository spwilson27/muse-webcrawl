Vulture

Vulture
Groups: Old World vultures (Accipitridae); New World vultures (Cathartidae)
Diet: Carrion
Notable trait: Soaring flight, keen eyesight or sense of smell

A vulture is a large bird of prey that feeds almost entirely on carrion. Vultures are found on every continent except Australia and Antarctica. They are divided into two groups that evolved similar traits convergently: the Old World vultures of Africa, Asia and Europe, and the New World vultures of the Americas.

Characteristics

Vultures have broad wings adapted for soaring for hours with minimal effort while searching for carcasses. Most have bald heads, which stay cleaner when feeding and help regulate temperature, and exceptionally strong stomach acid that destroys pathogens such as anthrax and botulinum toxin, giving them an important ecological role in disease control.

Conservation

Many vulture species have suffered catastrophic declines. In South Asia, populations of Gyps vultures crashed by more than 95% in the 1990s owing to poisoning by the veterinary drug diclofenac, prompting bans and captive-breeding programmes; African species face poisoning and habitat pressures.
