Nudge theory

Nudge theory is a concept in behavioural economics and public policy proposing that small changes to the environment in which people make choices — the "choice architecture" — can predictably influence behaviour without forbidding options or significantly changing economic incentives.

Origins

The idea was popularised by Richard Thaler and Cass Sunstein in their 2008 book Nudge: Improving Decisions About Health, Wealth, and Happiness. Classic examples include making organ donation or pension enrolment the default option, placing healthier food at eye level, and the famous etched fly in the urinals at Amsterdam's Schiphol Airport to improve aim.

Applications and criticism

Governments established dedicated units, such as the UK's Behavioural Insights Team (2010), to apply nudges to tax compliance, energy use and public health. Critics question the size and durability of nudge effects, publication bias in the literature, and whether nudging is a legitimate form of state influence — a debate Thaler and Sunstein addressed through their idea of "libertarian paternalism".
