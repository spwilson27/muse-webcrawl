The 1935 German Grand Prix was a Grand Prix motor race held at the Nürburgring on 28 July 1935. It is widely remembered as one of the greatest upsets in motor racing history: the Italian veteran Tazio Nuvolari, driving an obsolete Alfa Romeo P3 run by Scuderia Ferrari, defeated the lavishly funded German teams of Mercedes-Benz and Auto Union on their home ground, before a crowd that included senior figures of the Nazi regime.

Race summary

The German cars — the Mercedes W25 and the mid-engined Auto Union Type B — were far more powerful than Nuvolari's ageing Alfa Romeo, which produced roughly 100 horsepower less. In wet and drying conditions, however, Nuvolari drove relentlessly, clawing back time through the corners of the 22.8 km Nordschleife.

A botched pit stop cost Nuvolari over a minute, yet he fought back through the field, passing rivals one by one. On the final lap, Manfred von Brauchitsch's leading Mercedes suffered a burst tyre, and Nuvolari swept past to win by just over half a minute from Hans Stuck's Auto Union.

Legacy

The victory, achieved in inferior machinery against the combined might of the German racing programme, is frequently cited as Nuvolari's greatest drive and one of the finest performances in the history of Grand Prix racing.
