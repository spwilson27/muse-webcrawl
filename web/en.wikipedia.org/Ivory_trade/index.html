Ivory trade

The ivory trade is the commercial trade in ivory, chiefly elephant tusks, and also from walrus, narwhal, mammoth and other animals. Ivory has been valued for carvings, piano keys, billiard balls and ornaments for centuries.

History and decline of elephants

Demand drove severe poaching, especially in Africa in the 1970s–1980s, halving elephant populations. CITES banned international commercial trade in African elephant ivory in 1989, with limited one-off sales later.

Modern trade

Illegal poaching surged again in the 2000s–2010s driven by demand in Asia. China banned its domestic ivory trade at the end of 2017, and other markets tightened rules. Mammoth ivory from Siberia remains legal in many places.
