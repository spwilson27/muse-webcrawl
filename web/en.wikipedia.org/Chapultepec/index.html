Chapultepec

LocationMiguel Hidalgo, Mexico City, Mexico
AreaAbout 686 hectares (1,695 acres)
Elevation of hill2,325 m (7,628 ft)
Name meaning"Grasshopper hill" (Nahuatl)

Chapultepec, more commonly called the "Bosque de Chapultepec" (Chapultepec Forest), is one of the largest city parks in Mexico City and in the Western Hemisphere, covering some 686 hectares. Centered on a rocky hill — Chapultepec means "grasshopper hill" in Nahuatl — the park is divided into four sections and contains some of Mexico's most visited cultural and recreational sites.

History

The hill was a refuge and ceremonial site long before the Aztec era, and Aztec rulers built retreats and the aqueducts that carried its springs into Tenochtitlan; the carved portraits of emperors once adorned the rock. In the colonial era the Spanish viceroys used it as a residence. At its summit stands Chapultepec Castle, begun in 1785, later the home of Emperor Maximilian I and, until 1939, the official residence of Mexico's presidents. In 1847, during the Mexican–American War, the castle — then a military academy — was stormed by U.S. forces in the Battle of Chapultepec, an event commemorated in Mexico by the legend of the Niños Héroes, six cadets who died defending it.

Attractions

The first section of the park contains the National Museum of Anthropology, the Museo Tamayo, the Museum of Modern Art, the Chapultepec Zoo, the Los Pinos cultural complex (the former presidential residence), and the castle itself, now the National Museum of History. Lakes, monuments such as the Monumento a los Niños Héroes, and miles of paths make the park the principal green lung of the capital — often called the "lungs of Mexico City".
