Vltava

The Vltava (German: Moldau) is the longest river in the Czech Republic, running southeast along the Bohemian Forest and then north across Bohemia, through Český Krumlov, České Budějovice, and Prague, before merging with the Elbe at Mělník. It is 430 km (270 mi) long and drains about 28,090 km².

Course and features

The river rises from two headstreams in the Šumava mountains, the Warm and Cold Vltava. The "Vltava Cascade" of nine dams, including Lipno and Orlík, regulates flow, generates hydroelectricity, and supplies drinking water; the Slapy reservoir is a major recreation area. In Prague the river is spanned by 18 bridges, most famously the Charles Bridge (begun 1357).

Culture

The river is celebrated in Bedřich Smetana's symphonic poem Vltava (1874), the second of Má vlast, which traces its journey from two springs through forests, a peasant wedding, rapids, and past Prague's Vyšehrad. The devastating flood of August 2002 forced mass evacuations in Prague and remains the worst in the city's modern history.
