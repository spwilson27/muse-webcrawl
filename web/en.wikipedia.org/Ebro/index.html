The Ebro (Spanish: Ebro; Catalan: Ebre) is the largest river in Spain by discharge volume and the second-longest river on the Iberian Peninsula, after the Tagus. Rising in Cantabria in northern Spain, it flows 930 kilometres south-east through La Rioja, Navarre, Aragon and Catalonia before emptying into the Mediterranean Sea through the Ebro Delta in the province of Tarragona.

Course

The Ebro rises at the Pico Tres Mares ("Peak of the Three Seas") in the Cantabrian Mountains. It flows through the city of Logroño, then past Zaragoza, the capital of Aragon, where it passes the Basilica of Our Lady of the Pillar. Major tributaries include the Aragón, Gállego and Segre from the Pyrenees to the north, and the Jalón, Guadalope and Jalón from the Iberian System to the south. Below Tortosa the river enters its delta, a major wetland and rice-growing area protected as the Ebro Delta Natural Park.

History

The river's Latin name, Iberus, gave the Iberian Peninsula its name. In 217 BC the Treaty of the Ebro between Rome and Carthage set the river as the boundary between their spheres of influence in Iberia, a boundary whose crossing by Hannibal's ally Saguntum helped precipitate the Second Punic War. In 1938 the Battle of the Ebro, the longest and largest battle of the Spanish Civil War, was fought along the lower course of the river.
