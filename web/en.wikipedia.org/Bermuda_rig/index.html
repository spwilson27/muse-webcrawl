Bermuda rig

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A Bermuda rig, Bermudian rig, or Marconi rig is a configuration of mast and rigging for a type of sailboat and is the typical configuration for most modern sailboats. This configuration was developed in Bermuda in the 17th century, the name Marconi being a later reference to the similarity of the tall mast and its wire rigging to early radio masts.

Description

The rig consists of a triangular mainsail set behind the mast, its luff attached to the mast and its foot held out by a boom, together with one or more triangular headsails (jibs or genoas) set on stays forward of the mast. The triangular sail develops lift in the same manner as an aircraft wing, giving Bermuda-rigged vessels excellent ability to sail to windward compared with older square-rigged and gaff-rigged craft.

History

Fore-and-aft triangular sails evolved in Bermuda from earlier lateen and leg-of-mutton designs, suited to the local sloops used around the islands. By the early 20th century the Bermuda rig, refined in racing yachts, had supplanted the gaff rig on most recreational and racing sailing vessels, and it remains the standard rig for modern production sailboats.
