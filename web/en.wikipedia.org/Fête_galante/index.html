Fête galante

A fête galante (French: "courtship party") is a category of painting specially created by the French Academy in 1717 to describe the work of Antoine Watteau. Such paintings depict elegantly dressed figures in idyllic outdoor settings — parklands and gardens — engaged in music, dance, conversation, and amorous flirtation.

Characteristics

Fêtes galantes blend landscape, genre scene, and theatre, often with a dreamy, melancholic atmosphere beneath the apparent gaiety. Watteau's Pilgrimage to Cythera (1717) was the reception piece for which the category was invented. Other notable practitioners include Nicolas Lancret and Jean-Baptiste Pater.

Context

The genre flourished in early 18th-century France during the Régence, standing between Baroque grandeur and Rococo lightness, and it influenced later artists and composers — Debussy's music and Verlaine's poetry collection Fêtes galantes both drew on its mood.
