History of Poland

The history of Poland begins with the arrival of Slavic peoples and the establishment of the Piast state in the 10th century. Poland grew into one of Europe's largest states in the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, was partitioned and erased from the map in 1795, regained independence in 1918, suffered devastation in the Second World War, and emerged from communist rule in 1989.

The Piast kingdom

Duke Mieszko I of the Piast dynasty accepted baptism in 966, aligning Poland with Latin Christianity. His son Bolesław the Brave was crowned the first king in 1025. After a period of fragmentation, the kingdom was reunified in the 14th century; Casimir III the Great strengthened it and founded the university at Kraków in 1364.

The Commonwealth

The marriage of Queen Jadwiga to Jogaila of Lithuania (1386) began a dynastic union, sealed as the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth by the Union of Lublin in 1569. The Commonwealth was an elective monarchy with a powerful nobility, religious tolerance and vast territory stretching from the Baltic towards the Black Sea. Its decline in the 17th and 18th centuries, through wars with Sweden, Russia and the Ottomans and internal paralysis, ended with the three partitions by Russia, Prussia and Austria (1772–1795).

Partitioned Poland and independence

Poles rose repeatedly against the partitioning powers, notably in 1830 and 1863, and many emigrated. Poland regained independence in November 1918 under Józef Piłsudski, and defended it against Soviet Russia in 1920. The Second Republic was a parliamentary state until Piłsudski's coup of 1926.

Second World War and communism

Germany and the Soviet Union invaded in September 1939, beginning the Second World War. Poland suffered immense losses, including the murder of most of its Jewish population in the Holocaust and the destruction of Warsaw after the 1944 uprising. After 1945 Poland fell within the Soviet sphere as a communist People's Republic, its borders shifted westward.

Modern Poland

The Solidarity movement, founded in 1980 and led by Lech Wałęsa, forced round-table talks in 1989 and the first partially free elections, beginning the transition to democracy and a market economy. Poland joined NATO in 1999 and the European Union in 2004.
