Income tax

An income tax is a tax imposed on individuals or entities (taxpayers) in respect of the income or profits earned by them, usually computed as the product of a tax rate and taxable income. Income taxes are a major source of revenue for most modern governments and are used to fund public services and redistribution.

History

Early income-like taxes existed in several pre-modern states, but the modern income tax is generally traced to Britain, where William Pitt the Younger introduced it in 1799 to finance the Napoleonic Wars; it was reintroduced permanently by Robert Peel in 1842. In the United States, a federal income tax was first levied during the Civil War, and the Sixteenth Amendment (1913) settled Congress's power to tax incomes. Many other countries adopted income taxes in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

Structure

Systems differ in the definition of taxable income, allowable deductions and credits, and the treatment of capital gains, dividends and business income. Rates may be progressive (rising with income), flat or regressive. Collection is commonly by withholding at source on wages, supplemented by self-assessment.

Corporate income tax

Companies are typically taxed on profits, with rules on depreciation, loss relief and international allocation (transfer pricing) that are major areas of policy and dispute.
