Dom Casmurro

Dom Casmurro is an 1899 novel by Brazilian author Joaquim Maria Machado de Assis. It is widely considered one of the greatest works of Brazilian literature and a masterpiece of world fiction, often compared to the works of Flaubert and Sterne.

Plot

The novel is narrated in the first person by Bento Santiago, nicknamed "Dom Casmurro" (Lord Gloomy), who in old age recounts his life in order to "tie together the two ends of life." He recalls being destined by his widowed mother for the priesthood, his seminary years, and his love for his neighbour Capitu. He escapes the seminary, marries Capitu, and forms a close friendship with the seminary companion Escobar. After the birth of his son Ezequiel, Bento becomes consumed by jealousy and suspects that Capitu betrayed him with Escobar, whom the boy increasingly resembles. The marriage collapses in separation, though the narrator never resolves whether the adultery actually occurred.

Themes and legacy

The central ambiguity — whether Capitu was unfaithful — has generated over a century of critical debate. The novel's unreliable narration, ironic tone, and psychological depth make it a landmark of literary realism.
