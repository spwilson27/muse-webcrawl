Subculture

A subculture is a group of people within a larger culture who differentiate themselves through distinct styles, beliefs, interests, or behaviours. Subcultures often form around shared music, fashion, or pastimes—such as punks, goths, skaters, and gamers—and may define themselves partly in opposition to mainstream norms.

Subculture
Field: Sociology
Key texts: Hebdige, Subculture: The Meaning of Style (1979)
Examples: Punk, goth, hip-hop, mod

Theory

The Chicago School of sociology first studied subcultures in deviant groups. In the 1970s the Birmingham Centre for Contemporary Cultural Studies analysed British youth subcultures as symbolic resistance by working-class youth; Dick Hebdige argued that subcultural style works as a form of communication and defiance.

Formation and identity

Shared taste in music and clothing, specialized slang, and gathering places give members a sense of belonging. Media exposure can commercialize and mainstream a subculture's style, a process called incorporation.

Digital subcultures

The internet has enabled subcultures to form around highly specialized interests, with online communities sustaining scenes that are geographically dispersed.
