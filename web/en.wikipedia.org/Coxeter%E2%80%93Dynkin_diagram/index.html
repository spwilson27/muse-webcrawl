Coxeter–Dynkin diagram

In geometry, a Coxeter–Dynkin diagram (or Coxeter diagram, Coxeter graph) is a graph with numerically labelled edges representing the spatial relations between a collection of mirrors (or reflecting hyperplanes). It describes a kaleidoscopic construction: each graph "node" represents a mirror and the label attached to a branch encodes the dihedral angle between two mirrors.

History

The diagrams are named after H. S. M. Coxeter, who used them systematically in the classification of regular polytopes and honeycombs, and Ludwig Dynkin, who independently used similar diagrams in the study of Lie algebras; they are widely called Coxeter diagrams in geometry and Dynkin diagrams in algebra.

Finite and affine diagrams

The connected finite diagrams correspond to the finite reflection groups and underlie the classification of simple Lie algebras: the infinite families An, Bn, Cn, Dn and the exceptional diagrams E6, E7, E8, F4, and G2, together with the non-crystallographic cases H3, H4, and I2(p). Affine diagrams add one node and classify affine Weyl groups.

Construction

From a Coxeter–Dynkin diagram one can construct a Coxeter group by assigning a generator (a reflection) to each node, with relations determined by branch labels: a branch labelled m imposes (st)m = 1 on the corresponding generators, and unconnected nodes correspond to commuting reflections.
