Fruit tree

Fruit tree
DefinitionTree bearing edible fruit for human use
ExamplesApple, pear, peach, cherry, plum, citrus, mango
PropagationUsually grafting onto rootstocks

A fruit tree is a tree cultivated — or valued — for fruit that is eaten by people. The category is a horticultural one rather than a botanical family: most commercial fruit trees belong to the rose family (Rosaceae), including apple, pear, peach, plum, and cherry, but it also covers citrus, olive, fig, mango, and many others.

Cultivation

Because most fruit varieties do not grow true from seed, named cultivars are propagated vegetatively, usually by grafting a scion onto a rootstock chosen for size control, soil tolerance, and disease resistance. Orchards use dwarfing rootstocks and training systems such as espalier to increase yield and ease harvesting. Fruit trees require appropriate chilling hours, pollination (many varieties need a compatible polliniser and insect pollinators), pruning, and pest management.

History

Fruit trees were among the earliest domesticated plants: figs were cultivated over 11,000 years ago, and apples spread along the Silk Road from Central Asia, their grafting techniques described by the Romans. Modern breeding has produced thousands of cultivars adapted to climates and markets worldwide.
