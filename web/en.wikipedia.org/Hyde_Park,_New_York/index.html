Hyde Park is a town in Dutchess County, New York, United States, situated on the eastern bank of the Hudson River, roughly 90 miles north of New York City and just north of the city of Poughkeepsie. It is best known as the hometown of Franklin D. Roosevelt, the 32nd President of the United States.

History

The settlement was originally called Stoutenburgh, after an early landowner. In the mid-18th century it was renamed Hyde Park in honour of Edward Hyde, Earl of Clarendon, a former governor of New York. The town was officially formed in 1821 from part of the town of Clinton.

Historic sites

Hyde Park contains an unusual concentration of nationally significant historic sites. Springwood, the Roosevelt family estate, is preserved as the Home of Franklin D. Roosevelt National Historic Site, together with the Franklin D. Roosevelt Presidential Library and Museum — the first presidential library, established by a sitting president. Eleanor Roosevelt's cottage, Val-Kill, is a separate National Historic Site. The Vanderbilt Mansion National Historic Site preserves a grand Gilded Age estate nearby, and the town is also home to the main campus of the Culinary Institute of America, housed in a former Jesuit seminary.

Geography

The town covers about 40 square miles along the Hudson River valley. It includes the hamlets of Hyde Park, Staatsburg, and Haviland. U.S. Route 9 runs north–south through the town.
