Jet Propulsion Laboratory Development Ephemeris

The Jet Propulsion Laboratory Development Ephemeris (generally abbreviated as JPL DE number) is a fundamental ephemeris of the Solar System produced by the Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) in Pasadena, California. Each version is a high-precision numerical integration of the equations of motion of the Sun, planets, Moon and (in later versions) asteroids, fitted to centuries of astronomical observations.


Solar System ephemeris computation

Construction

A Development Ephemeris is generated by numerically integrating the post-Newtonian equations of motion of the major bodies, with parameters adjusted by least-squares fitting to observations including optical astrometry, radar ranging to planets and the Moon, spacecraft tracking, lunar laser ranging and very-long-baseline interferometry. Positions are stored as Chebyshev polynomial coefficients, allowing the position of any body at any covered epoch to be interpolated accurately.

Versions

Notable versions include DE102 (a long ephemeris spanning 1141 BC to AD 3000), DE200 (the basis of the Astronomical Almanac from 1984 to 2003), DE405 (released 1997, widely distributed on CD-ROM), DE421 and DE430/DE431 (2013), and DE440/DE441 (2021). The DE440 ephemeris covers the years 1550–2650, while its companion DE441 extends from 13,200 BC to AD 17,191.

Uses

The JPL ephemerides underpin spacecraft navigation throughout the Solar System, the reduction of astronomical observations, pulsar timing, and the fundamental celestial reference frames. Extracts are used by almanac offices and by software such as planetarium programs.
