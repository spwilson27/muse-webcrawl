Fireman's pole

A fireman's pole (also called a sliding pole or fire pole) is a brass or steel pole installed in multi-storey fire stations, allowing firefighters to descend rapidly from upper-floor dormitories to the appliance bay when an alarm sounds.

History

The pole is credited to David Kenyon of Chicago's Engine Company 21, who in 1878 adapted a wooden hay-fork pole; a brass version followed, and the device spread quickly through American and then worldwide fire services. It cut response times compared with stairs or the spiral staircases once used to stop horses wandering upstairs.

Modern use

Many modern stations are single-storey, and poles have declined; some services removed them over injury risks, while others retain them with safety features such as gap guards and landing mats. The pole remains an enduring symbol of the fire service.
