Hair

Hair is a protein filament that grows from follicles in the skin of mammals.

Hair is a protein filament that grows from follicles found in the dermis. Hair is one of the defining characteristics of mammals. The human body, apart from areas of glabrous skin, is covered in follicles which produce thick terminal and fine vellus hair. Most common interest in hair is focused on hair growth, hair types, and hair care, but hair is also an important biomaterial primarily composed of protein, notably alpha-keratin.

Attitudes towards different forms of hair, such as hairstyles and hair removal, vary widely across different cultures and historical periods, but it is often used to indicate a person's personal beliefs or social position, such as their age, gender, or religion.

Structure

Each strand of hair is made up of the medulla, cortex, and cuticle. The innermost region, the medulla, is not always present and is an open, unstructured region. The highly structural and organized cortex, or second of three layers, is the primary source of mechanical strength and water uptake. The cortex is composed of elongated cells containing melanin granules that give hair its colour. The cuticle is the outermost covering, made of overlapping scales.

Hair grows from a follicle, a pocket of epidermis containing the hair root. At the base of the root is the bulb, which receives nutrients from blood vessels in the dermal papilla. Hair growth follows a cycle of three phases: anagen (active growth, lasting years on the scalp), catagen (a short transitional phase), and telogen (a resting phase after which the hair is shed). Humans have roughly 100,000 hair follicles on the scalp and normally shed 50–100 hairs per day.

Functions

In mammals, hair serves insulation, protection against ultraviolet light and abrasion, camouflage, sensory functions (through whiskers or vibrissae), and social and sexual signalling. In humans, scalp hair retains a role in thermoregulation and protection, while body hair is greatly reduced compared to other primates — a trait sometimes explained by ectoparasite reduction or the demands of endurance running and sweating.

Hair care and culture

Hair care involves hygiene and cosmetology, including washing, cutting, colouring, and styling. Hairstyles have carried social, religious, and political meaning throughout history — from the tonsures of monks and the wigs of early modern European courts to punk and other subcultural styles. Hair loss (alopecia) can result from genetics, hormones, illness, or medication; androgenetic alopecia is the most common form. Treatments and transplantation are a substantial industry.
