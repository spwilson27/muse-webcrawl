Florida Agricultural and Mechanical University (Florida A&M University, commonly FAMU) is a public historically Black land-grant university in Tallahassee, Florida, United States. Founded in 1887, it is the only public historically Black university in Florida and one of the largest HBCUs in the nation by enrolment.

History

The university began as the State Normal College for Colored Students, opening in 1887 with 15 students, and became a land-grant institution under the Second Morrill Act of 1890. It gained university status in 1953. FAMU is a member institution of the State University System of Florida.

Academics and culture

FAMU offers undergraduate and graduate degrees including pharmacy, law, nursing, and engineering (through a joint college with Florida State University). Its marching band, the Marching "101", is nationally renowned and has performed at Super Bowls and presidential inaugurations. The university's athletic teams, the Rattlers, compete in NCAA Division I.
