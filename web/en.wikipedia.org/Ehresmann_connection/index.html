Ehresmann connection

In differential geometry, an Ehresmann connection (after the French mathematician Charles Ehresmann, who first formalised the concept in 1951) is a version of the notion of a connection that is defined on arbitrary fibre bundles. It consists of a choice, at each point of the total space, of a horizontal subspace complementary to the vertical subspace (the tangent space to the fibre), varying smoothly from point to point.

Definition and properties

Formally, if π : E → M is a fibre bundle, an Ehresmann connection is a smooth distribution H on E such that TE = H ⊕ V, where V is the vertical bundle ker(dπ). Equivalently it can be given by a connection form or a horizontal lift of tangent vectors and curves. Unlike the covariant-derivative connections of principal bundles, a general Ehresmann connection need not be linear or flat; its failure to be integrable is measured by its curvature, an obstruction to the horizontal distribution being tangent to a foliation.

Uses

Ehresmann connections allow the definition of parallel transport and holonomy for general fibre bundles, and they underlie the geometric treatment of nonholonomic mechanics and control theory. A classical (linear) connection on the frame bundle induces an Ehresmann connection, and a complete connection (one for which parallel transport exists for all curves) is guaranteed when fibres are compact.
