John Harvard (statue)

ArtistDaniel Chester French
Year1884
MediumBronze sculpture
LocationHarvard Yard, Harvard University, Cambridge, Massachusetts

John Harvard is a sculpture in bronze by Daniel Chester French at Harvard University in Cambridge, Massachusetts. It honors John Harvard (1607–1638), whose bequest of his library and half his estate led the college to take his name, and it is one of the university's best-known landmarks.

Description

The statue depicts a seated young clergyman reading a book resting on his knee, with a pile of books beside his chair. It was unveiled in 1884 and stands in Harvard Yard in front of University Hall.

The "Statue of Three Lies"

The statue is jokingly called the "Statue of Three Lies": the figure is not actually John Harvard (no likeness of him survives; a student, Sherman Hoar, served as the model); the inscription "Founder" is contested, since Harvard was a benefactor rather than the founder; and the date given as 1638 is the year of Harvard's bequest, whereas the college was founded in 1636.

Traditions

Tourists and visitors traditionally rub the toe of the statue's left shoe for luck, keeping it polished bright; students, however, have their own less savoury prank traditions involving the statue.
