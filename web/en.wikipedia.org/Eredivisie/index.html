Eredivisie

The Eredivisie (Dutch for "Honour Division") is the highest professional football league in the Netherlands. Founded in 1956, two years after the introduction of professional football in the country, it consists of 18 clubs. Each club meets every other club twice during the season, once at home and once away. At the end of the season, the bottom club is automatically relegated to the second level, the Eerste Divisie, while play-offs determine further promotion and relegation and qualification for European competitions.

History

Before 1956, Dutch champions were decided by play-offs between the winners of regional competitions. The first Eredivisie champion was Ajax, and together with PSV Eindhoven and Feyenoord the Amsterdam club forms the traditional "big three" (grote drie) that have dominated the league: no club outside these three won the title between 1965 and 1981, and only AZ Alkmaar (1981, 2009) and FC Twente (2010) have broken their hold since. Ajax is the most successful club, with more than 35 league titles. The league is renowned as a development ground for world-class players, with alumni including Johan Cruyff, Marco van Basten, Ruud Gullit, Dennis Bergkamp, and Arjen Robben.

European performance

Dutch clubs have won the European Cup and Champions League six times (Feyenoord 1970, Ajax 1971, 1972, 1973 and 1995, PSV 1988), along with UEFA Cup and Cup Winners' Cup titles. In UEFA's league coefficient rankings the Eredivisie has generally stood among the top six to seven leagues in Europe.
