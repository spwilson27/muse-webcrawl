Marination

Meat marinating in a seasoned liquid (image placeholder).

Marination is the process of soaking foods in a seasoned, often acidic, liquid — a marinade — before cooking, in order to add flavour and, in some cases, to tenderise. Marinades typically combine an acid (vinegar, citrus juice, wine or yogurt), oil, salt, herbs and spices.

Mechanism

Acids denature proteins on the surface of meat, which can improve moisture retention in thin cuts but, if prolonged, makes the surface mushy; contrary to popular belief, marinades penetrate only a few millimetres into meat. Enzymatic marinades containing pineapple (bromelain), papaya (papain) or kiwifruit actively break down proteins and can quickly over-tenderise. Marinating in yogurt or buttermilk, as for tandoori dishes, works more gently through mild acidity and calcium.

Safety

Because raw-meat marinades can carry pathogens, used marinade should be boiled before being served as a sauce, and marination is generally carried out under refrigeration.
