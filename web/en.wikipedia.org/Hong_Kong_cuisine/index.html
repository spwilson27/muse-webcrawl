Hong Kong cuisine

Hong Kong cuisineBase: Cantonese cuisine
Influences: British, Shanghainese, Japanese, Southeast Asian
Signature venues: cha chaan teng, dai pai dong

Hong Kong cuisine is built on Cantonese culinary traditions and enriched by a century of exposure to British colonial rule and regional Chinese and Asian influences, making the city famous as a culinary destination. Its food culture spans refined banquet cooking, dim sum teahouses, noodle shops and the hybrid Western-Chinese fare of the cha chaan teng.

Dim sum and Cantonese classics

Dim sum — small steamed or fried dishes such as har gow (prawn dumplings), siu mai, char siu bao and cheung fun — is served with tea in the yum cha tradition. Cantonese staples include roast meats (siu mei) such as char siu and roast goose, congee, wonton noodles, double-boiled soups and claypot rice.

Cha chaan teng fare

The Hong Kong-style café created localised Western dishes: Hong Kong-style milk tea, pineapple buns, egg tarts, macaroni in broth with ham, satay beef instant noodles, French toast with condensed milk, and yin-yeung (a coffee-tea mix). Open-air dai pai dong stalls and dai pai dong-style dishes like typhoon-shelter crab remain beloved institutions, as do desserts such as mango pudding, tofu fa and egg waffles.
