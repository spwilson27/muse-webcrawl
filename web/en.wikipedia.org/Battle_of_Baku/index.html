The Battle of Baku (26 August – 14 September 1918) was the final major engagement of the Caucasus campaign of the First World War, in which Ottoman and Azerbaijani forces of the Army of Islam, commanded by Nuri Pasha, captured the oil-rich city of Baku on the Caspian Sea from a coalition of Bolshevik, Armenian, and British-supported forces.

Background

Baku's oil fields made the city a prize for all parties. After the March Days violence of 1918, the city was controlled first by the Bolshevik Baku Commune and then by the Centro-Caspian Dictatorship, which invited in a small British force under Major-General Lionel Dunsterville ("Dunsterforce").

The battle

The Ottoman-Azerbaijani Army of Islam advanced on Baku through the summer of 1918. The final assault began in late August; after heavy fighting on the hills west of the city, the British evacuated by ship across the Caspian on 14 September, and the defenders collapsed. Baku fell on 15 September, followed by reprisals against the Armenian population. Ottoman control proved brief: the Armistice of Mudros (30 October 1918) forced withdrawal, and British troops reoccupied Baku in November.
