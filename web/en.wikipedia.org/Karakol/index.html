Karakol is a city in eastern Kyrgyzstan, the administrative centre of the Issyk-Kul Region, situated near the eastern shore of Lake Issyk Kul beneath the Teskey Ala-Too range of the Tian Shan mountains. Founded in 1869 as a Russian military and administrative outpost, it is today Kyrgyzstan's fourth-largest city and a principal base for trekking, mountaineering, and skiing tourism.

History

The town grew where caravan routes met the lake, attracting Russian, Dungan (Chinese Muslim), Tatar, and Kyrgyz settlers. It was renamed Przhevalsk in honour of the explorer Nikolay Przhevalsky, who died there in 1888 and is buried nearby. Karakol's diverse heritage is visible in its grid of Russian colonial streets, the wooden Holy Trinity Orthodox Cathedral (rebuilt in the 1890s), and the Dungan Mosque, built in Chinese style without a single nail.

Tourism and culture

Karakol is famed for its Sunday livestock market, its local speciality ashlan-fu (a cold Dungan noodle dish), and access to the Karakol valley, Altyn Arashan hot springs, and the Karakol ski base. The explorer Przhevalsky's museum and memorial lie at nearby Pristan-Przhevalsk on the lake shore.
