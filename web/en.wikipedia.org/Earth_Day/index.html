Earth Day

Observed by: More than 190 countries

Date: April 22

First observed: April 22, 1970

Significance: Support for environmental protection

Earth Day is an annual event on April 22 to demonstrate support for environmental protection. First held on April 22, 1970, it now includes a wide range of events coordinated globally by earthday.org (formerly the Earth Day Network) including 1 billion people in more than 193 countries. The official theme for 2024 was "Planet vs. Plastics" and for 2025 "Our Power, Our Planet".

History

In 1969 at a UNESCO Conference in San Francisco, peace activist John McConnell proposed a day to honor the Earth and the concept of peace, to first be observed on March 21, 1970, the first day of spring in the northern hemisphere. A month later a separate Earth Day was founded by United States Senator Gaylord Nelson, who was inspired by the anti-war teach-ins and the 1969 Santa Barbara oil spill. Nelson recruited Harvard student Denis Hayes as national coordinator, and the first Earth Day on April 22, 1970 mobilized an estimated 20 million Americans.

The event strengthened support for legislation such as the Clean Air Act, the Clean Water Act, and the Endangered Species Act, and contributed to the creation of the United States Environmental Protection Agency later in 1970.

International expansion

In 1990, Denis Hayes organized a major international campaign that mobilized 200 million people in 141 countries, giving a huge boost to recycling efforts worldwide and helping pave the way for the 1992 United Nations Earth Summit in Rio de Janeiro. Earth Day 2000 combined the ambitions of the first Earth Day with international grassroots activism.

Themes and activities

Activities include cleanups, tree planting, marches, teach-ins, and campaigns on issues such as climate change, deforestation, pollution, and plastic waste. The Canopy Project, the Great Global Cleanup, and climate literacy campaigns are among the network's ongoing initiatives.
