Stephen Decatur Jr. (1779–1820) was a United States naval officer celebrated as a hero of the early republic. He is famed for the 1804 raid that burned the captured frigate USS Philadelphia in Tripoli harbour — an exploit Lord Nelson reportedly called "the most bold and daring act of the age."

Career

Born in Maryland and raised in Philadelphia, Decatur entered the Navy in 1798 and served in the Quasi-War with France. In the First Barbary War he led the daring night raid into Tripoli. During the War of 1812, commanding USS United States, he captured HMS Macedonian. In 1815 he commanded the squadron that defeated Algiers in the Second Barbary War, ending American tribute payments.

Death

Decatur settled in Washington, famous for his toast "Our Country! In her intercourse with foreign nations may she always be in the right; but our country, right or wrong!" He was killed in a duel with Commodore James Barron in 1820. Numerous towns, counties and ships bear his name.
