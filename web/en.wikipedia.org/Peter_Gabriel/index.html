Peter Gabriel

Peter Brian Gabriel (born 13 February 1950) is an English singer, songwriter and human-rights activist. He rose to fame as the original lead singer of the progressive rock band Genesis, which he co-founded in 1967, becoming known for theatrical costumes and stagecraft. After leaving in 1975 he built a major solo career, achieving his greatest commercial success with the 1986 album So, which included the hit singles "Sledgehammer", "Big Time" and "Don't Give Up".

Solo career

Gabriel's first four solo albums — all titled Peter Gabriel — moved from art rock toward rhythm-driven experimentation, producing songs such as "Solsbury Hill", "Games Without Frontiers" and "Biko", the last written about the murdered South African activist Steve Biko. So (1986) made him a global star; its "Sledgehammer" video became one of the most played in MTV history. Later albums include Us (1992), Up (2002), orchestral projects, and i/o (2023), his first album of new original material in over two decades.

World music and activism

Gabriel founded the WOMAD festival in 1982 and Real World Records to promote world music, and his work with artists such as Youssou N'Dour popularised the genre in the West. A long-standing supporter of Amnesty International, he co-founded the human-rights organisation WITNESS and The Elders' initiative with Nelson Mandela and Richard Branson. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame both with Genesis (2010) and as a solo artist (2014).
