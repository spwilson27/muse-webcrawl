Tailings

Also called: Mine tailings, mine dumps, slimes
Produced by: Ore processing (milling)
Storage: Tailings dams and ponds; dry stacking
Risks: Dam failure, acid drainage, contamination

Tailings are the materials left over after the process of separating the valuable fraction of an ore from the uneconomical fraction (gangue) in mining. Usually ground to a fine powder and mixed with water into a slurry, tailings are the waste product of the mill and are distinct from waste rock and overburden removed to reach the ore body.

Composition and storage

Tailings typically consist of crushed rock, water and residual process chemicals, and may contain trace quantities of metals and sulfides. Because ores are mostly waste by mass, tailings volumes are enormous — the world's mines produce billions of tonnes annually. The most common disposal method is impoundment behind tailings dams in ponds where solids settle; increasingly, tailings are dewatered ("dry stacked") to reduce risk, or used as backfill underground.

Environmental and safety issues

Failures of tailings dams have caused some of the worst mining disasters, releasing floods of toxic slurry — as at Mariana (2015) and Brumadinho (2019) in Brazil. Sulfide-bearing tailings exposed to air and water generate acid mine drainage, and pond surfaces can produce contaminated dust. These hazards led to the Global Industry Standard on Tailings Management (2020) and growing interest in reprocessing old tailings for remaining metals and critical minerals.
