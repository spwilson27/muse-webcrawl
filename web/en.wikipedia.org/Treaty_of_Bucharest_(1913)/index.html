Treaty of Bucharest (1913)

The treaty ended the Second Balkan War and stripped Bulgaria of most of its gains from the first.

Signed10 August 1913
LocationBucharest, Romania
PartiesBulgaria, Romania, Serbia, Greece, Montenegro
ContextSecond Balkan War

The Treaty of Bucharest (Romanian: Tratatul de la București; Serbian, Greek and Bulgarian equivalents) was concluded on 10 August 1913, in Bucharest, Romania, by the delegates of Bulgaria, Romania, Serbia, Greece and Montenegro. The Treaty concluded the Second Balkan War and was a revision of the earlier Treaty of London, which had concluded the First Balkan War.

Terms

Bulgaria, defeated on all fronts, lost most of the territory it had gained only months before. Romania obtained Southern Dobruja (the Cadrilater), including Silistra. Serbia gained central and northern Macedonia (Vardar Macedonia). Greece gained southern Macedonia (Aegean Macedonia), including Salonika, Kavala and part of Epirus. Bulgaria retained Western Thrace with an Aegean outlet at Dedeagach (Alexandroupoli) and the Pirin region, while the Ottoman Empire recovered Edirne (Adrianople) and Eastern Thrace under the separate Treaty of Constantinople (1913).

Aftermath

The settlement left Bulgaria deeply revisionist and was a major cause of its decision to join the Central Powers in World War I in the hope of recovering Macedonia. Serbia's enlargement alarmed Austria-Hungary and fed the tensions that erupted in 1914. The treaty's Macedonian partition largely endured, forming the basis of the region's modern borders.
