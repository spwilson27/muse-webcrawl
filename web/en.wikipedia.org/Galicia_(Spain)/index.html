Galicia (Spain)

Galicia (Galician: Galiza)
Country: Spain
Capital: Santiago de Compostela
Largest city: Vigo
Provinces: A Coruña, Lugo, Ourense, Pontevedra
Area: 29,574 km²
Population: ~2.7 million
Official languages: Galician and Spanish

Galicia (Galician: Galiza; Spanish: Galicia) is an autonomous community of Spain and a historic nationality under the Spanish constitution, located in the northwest of the Iberian Peninsula. It is bordered by Portugal to the south, the Spanish autonomous communities of Castile and León and Asturias to the east, and the Atlantic Ocean to the west and north. Galicia is known for its green, rainy landscape, its deeply indented estuaries (the rías), and the pilgrimage city of Santiago de Compostela.

History

The region was inhabited in Roman times by the Gallaeci, from whom the name derives; Roman Gallaecia became a separate province under Diocletian. After Rome's fall, Galicia became the centre of the Suebi kingdom, one of the first independent kingdoms of post-Roman Europe, before absorption into the Visigothic kingdom. In the Middle Ages Galicia belonged for long periods to the Kingdom of León; the discovery in the 9th century of the supposed tomb of the apostle St. James made Santiago de Compostela one of Christendom's great pilgrimage destinations, reached by the Camino de Santiago. A Statute of Autonomy approved in 1936 was suppressed under Franco; the current Statute dates to 1981.

Geography and economy

Galicia's coast, about 1,500 km long, is cut by the Rías Altas and Rías Baixas, which shelter major ports (Vigo, A Coruña, Ferrol) and support Europe's largest mussel-farming industry. The interior is a rolling plateau of small farms, forests, and eucalyptus plantations. The economy rests on fishing and seafood processing, shipbuilding, automobiles (the Stellantis plant in Vigo), textiles (Inditex, founded in Arteixo, is headquartered in the region), dairy and wine production (Albariño, Ribeiro), and tourism linked to the Camino.

Culture and language

Galician, a Romance language closely related to Portuguese, is co-official with Spanish and spoken by the majority of the population. Galician culture preserves Celtic-tinged traditions — the gaita bagpipe, stone hórreos (granaries), and festivals such as the Apostle's Day on 25 July. Heavy emigration to the Americas in the 19th and 20th centuries left a large Galician diaspora, and in parts of Latin America "gallego" became a generic word for a Spaniard.
