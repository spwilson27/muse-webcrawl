Uber Eats

Uber Eats is an online food ordering and delivery platform launched by Uber in 2014. Users can browse menus from local restaurants and merchants, place orders through its mobile app or website, and have food delivered by couriers using cars, bicycles, scooters or on foot. The service operates in thousands of cities across dozens of countries and is one of the largest food delivery platforms in the world, competing with services such as DoorDash and Deliveroo.

History

Uber launched the service as UberFRESH in Los Angeles in 2014, offering a small set menu delivered by its drivers, and rebranded it as Uber Eats with a standalone app in 2015, beginning in Toronto. The platform expanded rapidly internationally and added grocery, alcohol and convenience delivery. In 2020 Uber acquired the rival service Postmates in the United States.

Business model

Restaurants pay a commission on orders, historically around 15–30 percent, while customers pay delivery and service fees; couriers are engaged as independent contractors in most markets, a classification that has been contested in several jurisdictions. Dynamic pricing, subscriptions such as Uber One, and advertising for merchants form additional revenue streams. The model has drawn debate over restaurant margins, courier pay and conditions, and the effect of "dark kitchens" and platform competition on local food businesses.

Criticism

The platform has faced criticism over commission levels, the treatment of gig-economy couriers, and regulatory disputes over licenses and food safety in some cities, alongside praise for convenience and for giving small restaurants access to delivery infrastructure.
