Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Friedrich Hirzebruch (1927–2012)

The Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem is a far-reaching generalisation of the classical Riemann–Roch theorem from algebraic curves to all dimensions. Proved by Friedrich Hirzebruch in 1954, it computes the holomorphic Euler characteristic of a holomorphic vector bundle on a smooth projective (or compact complex) variety in terms of characteristic classes.

Statement

For a holomorphic vector bundle E over a smooth projective variety X, the theorem states:

χ(X, E) = ∫X ch(E) · td(X)

where χ is the holomorphic Euler characteristic (the alternating sum of the dimensions of the sheaf cohomology groups), ch(E) is the Chern character of E, and td(X) is the Todd class of X. Hirzebruch's signature theorem is a special case.

History and influence

Hirzebruch's proof, in his 1953 Bonn dissertation and subsequent work, used Thom's cobordism theory and helped ignite the post-war flowering of algebraic topology and algebraic geometry. Alexander Grothendieck soon recast the result as the Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch theorem for morphisms (1957), and the Atiyah–Singer index theorem (1963) provided a differential-geometric generalisation unifying it with analysis.
