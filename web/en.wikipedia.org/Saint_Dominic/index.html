Saint Dominic

Saint Dominic (Spanish: Santo Domingo; Latin: Dominicus de Guzmán; 8 August 1170 – 6 August 1221), also known as Dominic of Caleruega, was a Castilian Catholic priest and the founder of the Dominican Order (the Order of Preachers). He is the patron saint of astronomers and of the Dominican Republic.

Early life

Dominic was born in Caleruega, in the province of Burgos in Old Castile, to Felix de Guzmán and Joan of Aza, who was later beatified. He studied the arts and theology at the University of Palencia, where he became known for his charity: during a famine he is said to have sold his books and furniture to help the poor. He was ordained a priest and became a canon regular at the cathedral of Osma, following the Rule of St. Augustine.

Mission in Languedoc

In 1203 or 1204 Dominic accompanied his bishop, Diego de Acebo, on a diplomatic mission to Denmark for King Alfonso VIII of Castile. Travelling through southern France, they encountered the Cathar heresy spreading through Languedoc. Dominic and Diego adopted a new model of evangelization: instead of the pomp of the Cistercian legates, they preached on foot, in apostolic poverty, and through public debate. Dominic remained in Languedoc for about a decade, preaching against Catharism and founding a community for converted women at Prouille in 1206, often considered the first Dominican house.

According to tradition, in 1208 at Prouille Dominic received a vision of the Virgin Mary, who gave him the Rosary — a form of prayer that the Dominicans later popularized throughout the Church.

Founding of the Order of Preachers

In 1215 Dominic gathered a small community of followers in Toulouse. The order received preliminary approval from Bishop Foulques of Toulouse and, on 22 December 1216, formal confirmation from Pope Honorius III in the bull Religiosam Vitam. The Order of Preachers was dedicated to preaching and the salvation of souls, combining contemplation, study, and an itinerant apostolate. Dominic dispersed his friars across Europe — to Paris, Bologna, Spain, Rome, and England — trusting in the order's growth through university cities. The first general chapter, held at Bologna in 1220, organized the order's democratic constitution.

Death and canonization

Dominic died at Bologna on 6 August 1221, exhausted by his travels and austerities. He was canonized by Pope Gregory IX — his friend, formerly Cardinal Ugolino — in 1234. His remains rest in the Basilica of San Domenico in Bologna, in a shrine (the Arca di San Domenico) later adorned by Nicola Pisano and the young Michelangelo.

Legacy

The Dominican Order became one of the great intellectual forces of the medieval Church, producing theologians such as Albertus Magnus and Thomas Aquinas. Dominic's feast day is celebrated on 8 August. The capital of the Dominican Republic, Santo Domingo, bears his name.
