The Brownie was a series of inexpensive simple cameras made by Eastman Kodak. First introduced in February 1900, the Brownie made photography affordable and practical for the general public — Kodak's slogan was "You press the button, we do the rest" — and popularised the snapshot.

The original Brownie

Designed by Frank A. Brownell, the first Brownie was a small cardboard box camera with a simple meniscus lens that took 2¼-inch square pictures on 117 roll film. Priced at one dollar and marketed heavily to children — its name borrowed from Palmer Cox's popular "Brownie" cartoon characters — it sold about 150,000 units in its first year.

Later models

The Brownie line continued for decades in many forms: the No. 2 Brownie (1901) using 120 film, folding Brownies, Bakelite models of the 1930s, the Brownie Hawkeye (1949), and the Brownie 127, which sold millions in Britain. Kodak and licensed makers produced Brownie-branded cameras into the 1980s.

Impact

By putting a camera in ordinary households, the Brownie transformed photography from a professional craft into a mass social practice, documenting everyday life, family events and travel, and creating the vast vernacular photographic record of the 20th century.
