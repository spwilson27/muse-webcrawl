Fasting

Fasting is the voluntary abstention from food, and sometimes drink, for a period of time.

Fasting is the act of abstaining from food, drink, or both for a period of time, usually for religious, medical, or health-related reasons. A fast may be total (consuming nothing) or partial (restricting certain foods or eating only during certain hours), and it may last from a few hours to several weeks. Fasting is practised in many of the world's religions, including Christianity, Islam, Judaism, Buddhism, and Hinduism, and it has also become popular in modern health culture in forms such as intermittent fasting.

Religious fasting

Fasting is one of the oldest and most widespread religious practices. In Islam, fasting from dawn to sunset during the month of Ramadan (sawm) is one of the Five Pillars of Islam. In Judaism, Yom Kippur and Tisha B'Av are major fast days on which observant Jews abstain from food and drink for about 25 hours. In Christianity, fasting and abstinence are traditionally observed during Lent, the forty days preceding Easter, and Roman Catholic and Orthodox churches retain various fasting rules. Hindu devotees fast on days associated with particular deities, and Buddhist monks commonly refrain from eating after midday.

Physiological effects

During a fast, the body first draws on glycogen stores in the liver, then increasingly on fat reserves, producing ketone bodies which the brain can use as fuel. Short-term fasting is generally safe for healthy adults, but prolonged fasting can lead to electrolyte imbalances, loss of lean muscle, and, on resuming eating, the potentially dangerous refeeding syndrome. Fasting is used medically before surgery and certain blood tests, and supervised fasting has been studied in relation to metabolic health and longevity.

Intermittent fasting

Intermittent fasting is an eating pattern that cycles between periods of fasting and eating, such as the 16:8 method (fasting sixteen hours and eating within an eight-hour window) or alternate-day fasting. It gained popularity in the 2010s as a weight-management strategy; studies suggest its weight-loss effects are broadly comparable to continuous calorie restriction.

Political fasting

Fasting has also been used as a method of political protest, most notably by Mahatma Gandhi, who undertook numerous hunger strikes during the Indian independence movement, and by suffragettes and prisoners who used hunger strikes to draw attention to their causes.
