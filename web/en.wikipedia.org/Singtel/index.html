Singapore Telecommunications Limited, commonly known as Singtel, is a Singaporean multinational telecommunications conglomerate and one of the country's largest corporations. The dominant provider of fixed-line, mobile, broadband and pay-TV services in Singapore, Singtel also has major stakes in regional operators, including Optus in Australia, Bharti Airtel in India, Telkomsel in Indonesia, Globe Telecom in the Philippines and AIS in Thailand, giving it a combined mobile customer base of several hundred million across Asia-Pacific.

History

Telephony in Singapore began in 1879 with a small private exchange. The service passed through British colonial and then Singaporean government hands as the Singapore Telephone Board and the Telecommunication Authority of Singapore. In 1992 the operating business was corporatised as Singapore Telecommunications, and in 1993 its initial public offering was the largest in Singapore's history at the time, designed to broaden share ownership among citizens. The state investment firm Temasek Holdings remains the majority shareholder.

Operations

In Singapore, Singtel provides mobile and fixed broadband networks, the mio TV/Singtel TV pay television service, and enterprise ICT services through its NCS subsidiary. Internationally, its wholly owned Australian subsidiary Optus is the country's second-largest carrier; Optus suffered a major data breach in 2022 affecting millions of customers and a nationwide outage in November 2023. The group also operates data centres and digital services, and has been restructuring to focus on 5G, enterprise digitalisation and infrastructure assets.
