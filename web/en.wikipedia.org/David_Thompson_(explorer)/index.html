David Thompson (explorer)

David Thompson (30 April 1770 – 10 February 1857) was a British-Canadian fur trader, surveyor and cartographer who mapped more of North America than any other explorer. Working for the Hudson's Bay Company and later the North West Company, he travelled some 90,000 km across the continent and mapped about one-fifth of its area.

Explorations

Thompson crossed the Rocky Mountains in 1807, explored the Columbia River basin, and in 1811 became the first European to travel the entire length of the Columbia River to the Pacific. After retiring from the fur trade he produced his great map of western Canada (1814), which remained the most accurate for decades, and later surveyed the Canada–United States boundary. He died in obscurity in 1857, and his achievements were only widely recognised in the twentieth century.
