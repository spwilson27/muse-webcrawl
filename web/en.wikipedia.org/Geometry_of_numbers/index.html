The geometry of numbers is the part of number theory that uses geometry to study algebraic numbers and Diophantine problems. It was created by Hermann Minkowski, whose book Geometrie der Zahlen (1910) founded the subject. Its central objects are lattices — discrete subgroups of Euclidean space — and the interaction between lattices and convex bodies.

Minkowski's theorem

The foundational result, Minkowski's convex body theorem, states that any convex set in Rn symmetric about the origin with volume greater than 2n times the determinant of a lattice must contain a non-zero lattice point. From this simple statement flow elegant proofs of deep results, including Lagrange's four-square theorem and the finiteness of the class number of a number field.

Successive minima and packing

The theory also studies successive minima of convex bodies with respect to lattices, and lattice packing and covering problems: how densely congruent copies of a body can be arranged with centres on a lattice. These questions connect the geometry of numbers to coding theory and to lattice-based post-quantum cryptography, where the hardness of finding short lattice vectors underlies proposed cryptosystems.

Later developments

Twentieth-century work by Mahler, Cassels, and others extended Minkowski's ideas, while C. L. Siegel's mean value theorem linked lattice counting to integration over the space of lattices. The subject remains active, notably through the work of the Fields medallist Maryna Viazovska on sphere packing in dimensions 8 and 24.
