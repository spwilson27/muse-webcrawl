Anchorage is the largest city in the U.S. state of Alaska, with a population of about 288,000 in the municipality and roughly 40 percent of the state's population. Located in Southcentral Alaska on Cook Inlet, it is a major port, transport hub, and centre for the state's government services, oil industry, and military.

History

Anchorage was founded in 1914 as a railroad construction camp for the Alaska Railroad and was named after the anchorage at the mouth of Ship Creek. It grew rapidly during the Second World War and the Cold War as a strategic military location, and again after the discovery of oil at Prudhoe Bay in 1968. The city was severely damaged by the 1964 Good Friday earthquake, one of the most powerful ever recorded in North America.

Geography and economy

The city lies between Cook Inlet's Knik and Turnagain Arms and the Chugach Mountains. Ted Stevens Anchorage International Airport is among the world's busiest cargo airports, owing to Alaska's position on great-circle routes between Asia and North America. Joint Base Elmendorf–Richardson is a major employer, and tourism, health care, and transportation anchor the economy.
