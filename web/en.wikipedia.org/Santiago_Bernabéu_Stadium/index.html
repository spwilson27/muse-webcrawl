The Santiago Bernabéu Stadium (Spanish: Estadio Santiago Bernabéu) is a football stadium in the Chamartín district of Madrid, Spain, and the home of Real Madrid since its completion in 1947. It is one of the world's most famous football venues and has a capacity of over 83,000 following its extensive 21st-century renovation.

History

Built on the site of the old Campo de Chamartín and inaugurated as the Nuevo Estadio Chamartín, the ground was renamed in 1955 for Santiago Bernabéu, the club's legendary president. It hosted the 1982 FIFA World Cup final, in which Italy beat West Germany, as well as the 1964 European Championship final and four European Cup / Champions League finals.

Renovation

Between 2019 and the mid-2020s Real Madrid carried out a radical remodelling — sometimes branded the "Bernabéu of the 21st century" — wrapping the stadium in a futuristic metallic shell with a fixed roof, a 360-degree video scoreboard ring, and a retractable pitch that can be stored in an underground greenhouse so the venue can host concerts and events year-round. The project cost well over a billion euros and transformed the stadium into a year-round entertainment complex.
