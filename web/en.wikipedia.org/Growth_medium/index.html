Growth medium

Colonies of bacteria growing on an agar plate.

A growth medium or culture medium is a solid, liquid, or semi-solid designed to support the growth of microorganisms or cultured cells. Media contain the nutrients — sources of carbon and nitrogen, minerals, vitamins and growth factors — that the organisms cannot synthesise themselves, and they are a fundamental tool of microbiology, cell biology, and biotechnology.

Types

  • Liquid media (broths) support growth suspended in fluid, used for large cultures and fermentation.
  • Solid media are liquids gelled with agar (introduced through the work of Fanny Hesse in Koch's laboratory) and poured into Petri dishes; colonies grow on the surface, allowing isolation of pure cultures.
  • Defined (synthetic) media have known chemical composition; complex media contain extracts (e.g., yeast extract, peptone) of uncertain exact composition.
  • Selective media contain agents (antibiotics, dyes, bile salts) that suppress unwanted organisms while permitting target species.
  • Differential media contain indicators so that organisms with different metabolisms produce visibly different colonies — for example, lactose fermenters turn pink on MacConkey agar.
  • Enrichment media favour the growth of a particular organism from a mixed sample.

Cell culture

Animal and plant cells require richer formulations — media such as DMEM and RPMI-1640 with amino acids, glucose, salts, buffers and often serum — and are grown in controlled incubators at the organism's body temperature with regulated CO2. Media for different purposes range from minimal salts media for genetics to the sophisticated broths used to produce antibiotics and vaccines at industrial scale.
