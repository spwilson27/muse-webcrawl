Intubation

Intubation is a medical procedure in which a tube is placed into the body. Most commonly the term refers to tracheal intubation, the insertion of a flexible plastic tube through the mouth or nose into the trachea to secure an open airway, deliver oxygen and mechanical ventilation, and protect the lungs from aspiration.

Tracheal intubation is performed during general anaesthesia, in emergency medicine, and in intensive care for patients unable to maintain their own airway — for example, when the Glasgow Coma Scale score falls to 8 or below ("GCS 8, intubate"). The tube is usually placed with the aid of a laryngoscope or video laryngoscope, and correct placement is confirmed by capnography. Risks include hypoxia, dental and airway trauma, and unrecognised oesophageal placement.
