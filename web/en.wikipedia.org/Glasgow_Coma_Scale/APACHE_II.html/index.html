APACHE II

APACHE II (Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation II) is a severity-of-disease classification system used in intensive care medicine. It was described by Knaus and colleagues in 1985 and is applied within the first 24 hours of admission to an intensive care unit to estimate the risk of hospital mortality.

The score, ranging from 0 to 71, is computed from twelve physiological variables — temperature, mean arterial pressure, heart rate, respiratory rate, oxygenation, arterial pH, sodium, potassium, creatinine, hematocrit, white blood cell count, and the Glasgow Coma Scale — plus points for age and for chronic health status. Higher scores correspond to more severe disease and a higher predicted risk of death. APACHE II is used for risk adjustment, benchmarking ICU performance and stratifying patients in research.
