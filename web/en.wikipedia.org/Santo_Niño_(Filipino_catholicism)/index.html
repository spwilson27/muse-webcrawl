Santo Niño

TitleSanto Niño (Holy Child)
Original imageSanto Niño de Cebú, dated to 1521
ShrineBasilica del Santo Niño, Cebu City
FeastThird Sunday of January
PatronageCebu; the Philippines

The Santo Niño (Spanish for "Holy Child") is a title of the Child Jesus particularly venerated in the Philippines, where devotion to the Santo Niño is among the oldest and most widespread expressions of Filipino Catholicism. The original image, the Santo Niño de Cebú, is a wooden statue of the Christ Child about 30 centimetres (12 in) tall, dressed in elaborate garments and holding a globus cruciger and sceptre.

History

Catholic tradition holds that Ferdinand Magellan presented the image as a baptismal gift to Rajah Humabon's wife, baptised as Queen Juana, in Cebu in 1521. After Magellan's death the image was lost to view until 1565, when a soldier of Miguel López de Legazpi's expedition found it remarkably preserved in a burnt nipa hut. Legazpi founded a church on the spot; today's Basilica Minore del Santo Niño, run by Augustinian friars, stands on the site.

Devotion and festivals

The feast of the Santo Niño is celebrated on the third Sunday of January. In Cebu the Sinulog festival draws millions in fluvial and street processions honouring the image; the Ati-Atihan of Kalibo, Aklan — often called the "Mother of All Philippine Festivals" — and Iloilo's Dinagyang likewise honour the Santo Niño. Images of the Child, in forms such as the Santo Niño de Atocha and the Santo Niño de Praga, are kept in homes, shops and jeepneys across the country. In 2021 the Philippines marked the 500th anniversary of the image's arrival and of Christianity in the archipelago.
