Christian Dior SE, commonly known as Dior, is a French luxury goods company founded in 1946 by the fashion designer Christian Dior. Through its principal subsidiary it controls a major stake in LVMH, and its own fashion house, Parfums Christian Dior, and retail operations together represent one of the world's largest luxury brands.

History

The house of Dior opened in Paris in December 1946, backed by textile magnate Marcel Boussac. Its first collection, presented in February 1947, was dubbed the "New Look" by Harper's Bazaar editor Carmel Snow: with nipped-in waists, soft shoulders, and full calf-length skirts, it repudiated wartime austerity and re-established Paris as the capital of fashion. After Christian Dior's sudden death in 1957, the young Yves Saint Laurent took over as artistic director.

Creative directors and business

Subsequent creative directors have included Marc Bohan, Gianfranco Ferré, John Galliano, Raf Simons, and Maria Grazia Chiuri for womenswear. The company is controlled by the Arnault family, and Dior's couture, ready-to-wear, leather goods (such as the Lady Dior bag), and fragrance lines (including J'adore and Sauvage) generate tens of billions of euros in annual revenue.
