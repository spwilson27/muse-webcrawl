Siméon Denis Poisson

Siméon Denis Poisson
Born: 21 June 1781, Pithiviers, France
Died: 25 April 1840, Paris, France
Fields: Mathematics, physics
Known for: Poisson distribution, Poisson's equation, Poisson bracket

Siméon Denis Poisson (1781–1840) was a French mathematician and physicist who made fundamental contributions to mathematical physics, probability theory and analysis.

Work

Poisson extended Laplace's work on potential theory; Poisson's equation governs the potential of gravitational and electrostatic fields. In probability, his 1837 work introduced the Poisson distribution, which models the number of events occurring in a fixed interval. He also contributed to elasticity, fluid mechanics and the theory of heat, and introduced the Poisson bracket in Hamiltonian mechanics.

The Poisson spot

In 1818, evaluating Fresnel's wave theory of light for a prize competition, Poisson showed that the theory implied a bright spot at the centre of the shadow of a circular obstacle — a result he considered absurd. François Arago then observed the spot experimentally, providing striking evidence for the wave theory; it is now called the Arago (or Poisson) spot.
