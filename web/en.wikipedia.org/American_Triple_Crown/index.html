American Triple Crown

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Triple Crown of Thoroughbred Racing, often shortened to Triple Crown or the American Triple Crown, is a series of horse races for three-year-old Thoroughbreds, consisting of the Kentucky Derby, Preakness Stakes, and Belmont Stakes. The three races were inaugurated in different years, the last being the Kentucky Derby in 1875. The Triple Crown Trophy, commissioned in 1950 but awarded to all previous winners as well as those after 1950, is awarded to a horse who wins all three races.

History and winners

Sir Barton won all three races in 1919, before the term "Triple Crown" was in common use; sportswriter Charles Hatton of the Daily Racing Form popularised the name in the 1930s. Only thirteen horses have achieved the feat, including Gallant Fox (1930), Omaha (1935), War Admiral (1937), Whirlaway (1941), Count Fleet (1943), Assault (1946), Citation (1948), Secretariat (1973), Seattle Slew (1977), and Affirmed (1978). After Affirmed, a 37-year drought followed until American Pharoah won in 2015, and Justify followed in 2018. Secretariat's 1973 Belmont victory by 31 lengths, in a still-standing record time, is considered one of the greatest performances in sports.

Only two fillies have won individual races prominently in the modern era, and no filly has won the Triple Crown itself. The compressed five-week schedule and escalating distances make the sweep a severe test of the three-year-old thoroughbred.
