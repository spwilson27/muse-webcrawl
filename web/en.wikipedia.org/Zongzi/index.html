Zongzi

TypeRice dumpling
Place of originChina
Main ingredientsGlutinous rice, various fillings, bamboo or reed leaves
OccasionDragon Boat Festival (Duanwu)

Zongzi (Chinese: 粽子; pinyin: zòngzi) is a traditional Chinese rice dish made of glutinous rice stuffed with different fillings and wrapped in bamboo leaves (or sometimes reed or other large flat leaves). They are cooked by steaming or boiling. In the Western world, zongzi are also known as rice dumplings or sticky rice dumplings.

Zongzi are traditionally eaten during the Dragon Boat Festival (Duanwu), held on the fifth day of the fifth month of the Chinese lunisolar calendar, to commemorate the death of the poet and minister Qu Yuan (c. 340–278 BCE). According to legend, packets of rice were thrown into the Miluo River to prevent fish from eating Qu Yuan's body, and the custom evolved into eating zongzi and racing dragon boats.

Varieties

Regional varieties differ greatly. In northern China, zongzi tend to be sweet, filled with red bean paste or jujubes, and are typically pyramidal. In the south, savoury fillings predominate: marinated pork belly, salted egg yolk, chestnuts, mushrooms and peanuts. Jiaxing zongzi from Zhejiang province, filled with pork, are especially famous. In Taiwan, Malaysia, Singapore and elsewhere in the Chinese diaspora, local styles such as the Nonya chang of Peranakan cuisine are popular.

Preparation

Glutinous rice is soaked, sometimes mixed with lye water for a firmer texture, then wrapped with fillings into a triangular or tetrahedral bundle of leaves and bound with plant stems or string. The parcels are boiled for several hours, which cooks the rice and melds the flavour of the leaves into the dumpling.
