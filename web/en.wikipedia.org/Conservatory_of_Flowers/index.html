Conservatory of Flowers

Conservatory of Flowers
Location: Golden Gate Park, San Francisco, California
Completed: 1879
Style: Victorian glasshouse
Status: California Historical Landmark; National Register of Historic Places

The Conservatory of Flowers is a botanical garden and Victorian greenhouse in Golden Gate Park, San Francisco, California. Opened in 1879, it is the oldest public conservatory of its kind in the United States and the oldest building in the park. Its whitewashed wood-and-glass structure, crowned by a central dome, was modelled on the Palm House at Kew Gardens in London.

History

The building materials were originally purchased as a kit, reportedly by businessman James Lick, and after his death the unassembled materials were bought by a group of prominent San Franciscans who donated them to the city. Erected in 1878–79, the conservatory survived the 1906 earthquake but was damaged by fires and storms over the years. A severe storm in 1995 shattered many panes and damaged the structure, forcing a five-year closure; a major restoration campaign reopened it in 2003.

Collections

The conservatory houses some 1,700 plant species in five galleries: Lowland Tropics, Highland Tropics, Aquatic Plants, Potted Plants, and a special-exhibitions gallery. Highlights include a giant imperial philodendron dating to the early 20th century, renowned orchid and carnivorous plant collections, and the giant water lilies of the aquatic gallery. Seasonal displays of butterflies and corpse flowers draw large crowds.
