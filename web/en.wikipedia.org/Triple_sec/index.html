Triple sec

Triple sec, a clear orange-flavoured liqueur

TypeLiqueur
Alcohol by volume15–40%
ColourClear or golden
FlavourOrange
VariantsCuraçao

Triple sec is an orange-flavoured liqueur that originated in France. It usually has an alcohol content between 15% and 40%, depending on the brand. It is an important ingredient in many classic cocktails, including the Margarita, the Cosmopolitan, the Sidecar, the Long Island iced tea, and the Mai Tai.

History

The Combier distillery in Saumur, France, claims that triple sec was invented in 1834 by distiller Jean-Baptiste Combier and his wife Josephine. Orange liqueurs were already popular: Dutch traders had long shipped curaçao made from the bitter Laraha oranges of the island of Curaçao. The name triple sec has been taken to refer to a triple distillation ("sec" meaning "dry" in French), though the Combier family has also suggested it referred to the third type of liqueur the firm produced. Cointreau, the best-known premium brand, was created in Angers in 1875 and was originally called "Curaçao Blanco Triple Sec".

Production

Triple sec is made from a base of neutral spirit flavoured with the dried peels of bitter and sweet oranges, which are macerated and redistilled in copper pot stills, then sweetened with sugar. Under European Union law, the term may be used for liqueurs meeting minimum standards for orange flavouring and sweetness.

Use in cocktails

Because of its balance of sweetness, dryness, and orange aroma, triple sec functions as the sweetening and citrus component in a wide family of mixed drinks. It features in the Margarita (with tequila and lime juice), the White Lady, the Between the Sheets, and the Corpse Reviver No. 2. Premium expressions such as Cointreau (40% ABV) are also drunk neat or on ice.
