Opera Philadelphia

Opera Philadelphia
Founded: 1975 (as Opera Company of Philadelphia)
Home: Academy of Music, Philadelphia
Known for: Festival O, new American works

Opera Philadelphia is an American opera company in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, and the city's only producer of grand opera. Founded in 1975 as the Opera Company of Philadelphia through the merger of the Philadelphia Lyric Opera Company and the Philadelphia Grand Opera Company, it adopted its current name in 2013.

Performances and programming

The company performs principally at the Academy of Music, the oldest opera house in the United States still used for its original purpose (opened 1857). It also presents works at the Kimmel Center's Perelman Theater and other venues. Under general director David B. Devan (2011–2024) and music director Corrado Rovaris, the company reoriented itself around new work and adventurous programming.

Festival O and new works

In 2017 the company launched Festival O, a citywide autumn festival built around world premieres and unconventional stagings, which drew international attention. Its commissions and premieres have included Kevin Puts's Silent Night (co-production), Charlie Parker's Yardbird (2015), Breaking the Waves by Missy Mazzoli (2016), Written on Skin-style contemporary imports, We Shall Not Be Moved (2017), and George Benjamin and others' works, as well as the Pulitzer Prize-winning The Central Park Five composer Anthony Davis's projects. Opera Philadelphia has been widely credited with reshaping the American opera business model toward festivals and commissions.
