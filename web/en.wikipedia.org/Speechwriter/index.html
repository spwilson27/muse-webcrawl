Speechwriter

A speechwriter is a person who is hired to prepare and write speeches that will be delivered by another person. Speechwriters are employed by many senior-level elected officials and executives in the government and private sectors. They may also be hired to write for weddings and other social occasions, or by celebrities for award ceremonies.

Role

A speechwriter works closely with a client to understand their voice, positions and audience. The work typically involves researching the topic, producing drafts, and revising in consultation with the speaker. Political speechwriters often work under tight deadlines, preparing remarks for campaigns, legislatures and state occasions.

Notable speechwriters

Famous examples include Ted Sorensen, who wrote for John F. Kennedy; Peggy Noonan, who wrote for Ronald Reagan and George H. W. Bush; and Jon Favreau, who served as chief speechwriter to Barack Obama. In antiquity, professional logographers such as Lysias wrote courtroom speeches for litigants in Athens.

Process

Speechwriters commonly study recordings and writings of their principal to imitate cadence and diction, and they must usually accept that credit for the finished speech belongs to the speaker. Many later publish memoirs about their time in the role.
