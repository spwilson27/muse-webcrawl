Marcus Aurelius Valerius Maxentius (c. 283 – 28 October 312) was a Roman emperor who ruled Italy and North Africa from 306 to 312. The son of the emperor Maximian, he seized power in Rome after being passed over in the Tetrarchic succession, and was eventually defeated and killed by Constantine the Great at the Battle of the Milvian Bridge.

Rise to power

When Diocletian and Maximian abdicated in 305, Maxentius — though an emperor's son and son-in-law of the new emperor Galerius — was excluded from the succession. In October 306, resentment in Rome at taxation and the threatened dissolution of the Praetorian Guard led to his proclamation as emperor, supported by his retired father. Maxentius repelled invasions by Severus (who surrendered and was executed) and by Galerius, and suppressed a revolt in Africa.

Rule in Rome

Maxentius presented himself as the defender and restorer of Rome, undertaking an ambitious building programme: the enormous Basilica of Maxentius in the Forum, the restoration of the Temple of Venus and Roma, a villa and circus on the Via Appia, and work on the Aurelian Walls. He tolerated Christians in his territories and returned confiscated church property, though later Christian tradition under Constantine condemned him as a tyrant.

Downfall

In 312 Constantine invaded Italy, defeating Maxentius's forces at Turin and Verona. Outside Rome, at the Battle of the Milvian Bridge on 28 October 312, Maxentius's army was routed; he drowned in the Tiber when the bridge of boats collapsed. His head was paraded through Rome. Constantine's victory — associated with the famous vision of the Cross — transformed the religious history of the empire.
