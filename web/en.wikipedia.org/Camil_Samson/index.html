Camil Samson

Camil Samson (born January 3, 1935 – died December 18, 2012) was a Canadian politician from Quebec. A dentist by profession, he became the leader of the Ralliement créditiste du Québec, the provincial branch of the Social Credit movement, and was a prominent figure in Quebec politics during the 1970s.

Political career

Samson was first elected to the National Assembly of Quebec in the 1970 election, representing the riding of Rouyn-Noranda. As leader of the créditistes he held the balance of attention during the decline of the Union Nationale. In the 1973 election his party won 12 seats, its best showing.

Internal divisions weakened the party in the later 1970s. Samson sat as an independent and later joined the Parti Québécois, under whose banner he was re-elected in 1981, before retiring from politics in 1985.

Later life

After leaving politics Samson returned to private life in the Abitibi-Témiscamingue region. He died in 2012 at the age of 77.
