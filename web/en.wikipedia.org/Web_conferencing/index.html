Web conferencing

Web conferencing is a type of real-time teleconferencing and videoconferencing service that allows conferences, meetings, training and presentations to be conducted over the Internet, connecting individuals and groups in different locations. Participants typically join through a web browser or a dedicated application, and features commonly include live video and audio, screen sharing, text chat, file sharing, slideshows, polling and session recording.

History

Real-time text chat services such as IRC appeared in the late 1980s, and early web-based chat and conferencing products emerged in the mid-1990s. Software such as CU-SeeMe and the Microsoft NetMeeting application popularised desktop videoconferencing. Dedicated web conferencing platforms grew in the 2000s with products such as Webex and GoToMeeting, and usage expanded dramatically in the 2010s and especially during the COVID-19 pandemic, when tools such as Zoom, Microsoft Teams and Google Meet became central to remote work and education.

Webinar

A webinar (web seminar) is a largely one-to-many form of web conferencing, in which a presenter or panel addresses a large audience, often with limited audience interaction through questions, chat and polls. Webinars are commonly used for marketing, training and lectures.
