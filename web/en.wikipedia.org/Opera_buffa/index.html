Opera buffa

Opera buffa (Italian for comic opera) is a genre of opera. It was first used as an informal description for Italian comic operas variously classified by their authors as dramma giocoso, commedia in musica and other terms. Opera buffa is mainly associated with developments in Naples and Venice in the first half of the 18th century, from which it gained popularity in Rome and northern Italy.

Unlike the heroic opera seria, opera buffa features everyday characters, servants often outwitting masters, spoken-like recitative, ensemble finales, and plots of love and trickery. Early landmarks include Pergolesi's La serva padrona (1733), originally an intermezzo. The genre culminated in Mozart's Le nozze di Figaro, Don Giovanni (a dramma giocoso) and Così fan tutte, and later in Rossini's Il barbiere di Siviglia and Donizetti's Don Pasquale.

Characteristics

Features include bass voices for comic old men, rapid patter singing, and social satire, reflecting Enlightenment challenges to aristocratic opera seria.
