Henry I (4 May 1008 – 4 August 1060) was King of the Franks from 1031 to 1060. A member of the Capetian dynasty, he was the son of Robert II and Constance of Arles. His reign, like those of the other early Capetians, was marked by feudal struggles as the crown sought to maintain authority over a small royal domain centred on Paris and Orléans while great magnates ruled much of the kingdom in practice.

Reign

Henry was crowned co-king at Reims in 1027 during his father's lifetime, following Capetian practice. On Robert II's death in 1031, his mother Constance supported the claim of his younger brother Robert, and Henry had to fight to secure his throne, aided by Robert II of Normandy. In gratitude, and to purchase support, he conceded territory and privileges that further weakened the royal domain.

Henry campaigned repeatedly against rebellious vassals. In Burgundy he supported his brother Robert's installation as duke. In the 1050s he came into conflict with William, Duke of Normandy, whose growing power alarmed him; Henry invaded Normandy twice but was defeated at the battles of Mortemer (1054) and Varaville (1057).

Marriages and children

Henry's first wife, Matilda of Franconia, died in 1044. In 1051 he married Anne of Kiev, daughter of Yaroslav the Wise, Grand Prince of Kiev, who brought the name Philip into the Capetian family for their eldest son. Their children included Philip I, who succeeded Henry, and Hugh, founder of the Vermandois line.

Death

Henry died at Vitry-aux-Loges on 4 August 1060 and was buried in the Basilica of Saint-Denis. His seven-year-old son Philip was crowned in his lifetime and succeeded him under the regency of Anne of Kiev and Baldwin V of Flanders.
