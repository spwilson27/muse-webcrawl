Ankle-foot orthosis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An ankle-foot orthosis (AFO) is an orthotic brace worn on the lower leg and foot to support the ankle, hold the foot and ankle in a correct position, and improve walking. AFOs are commonly used to treat foot drop, in which weakness of the dorsiflexor muscles causes the toes to drag during walking.

Uses

AFOs are prescribed for conditions including stroke, cerebral palsy, multiple sclerosis, spinal cord injury, peripheral nerve injury (such as peroneal nerve damage), muscular dystrophy and Charcot–Marie–Tooth disease. By controlling ankle motion they improve gait stability, reduce the risk of falls and compensate for weak or spastic muscles.

Types

Designs range from flexible posterior leaf-spring AFOs, which assist toe clearance, to solid AFOs that immobilise the ankle, hinged AFOs that permit controlled movement, and ground-reaction AFOs used to stabilise the knee. They may be prefabricated or custom-moulded from thermoplastic, metal and leather, or carbon fibre. Fitting is typically done by an orthotist, often informed by gait analysis.
