Fuenteovejuna

Fuenteovejuna is a play by the Spanish playwright Lope de Vega, written in 1612–1614 and first published in 1619 in Madrid. It is one of the most famous works of the Spanish Golden Age theatre and is based on a real historical incident in 1476 in the village of Fuente Obejuna in the province of Córdoba.

Plot

The village suffers under the tyranny of Fernán Gómez de Guzmán, Commander of the Order of Calatrava, who abuses his power and repeatedly assaults the village women, including Laurencia, daughter of the mayor. When the Commander abducts Laurencia on her wedding day, she escapes and shames the village men into action. The entire village rises, storms the Commander's house, and kills him. When royal investigators torture the villagers — men, women, and children — to find the culprit, every one of them answers only: "Fuenteovejuna did it." Impressed and unable to single out a guilty party, the Catholic Monarchs pardon the village.

Themes and legacy

The play dramatizes collective responsibility, resistance to tyranny, and the dignity of peasants — remarkable themes for its time. Its famous interrogation scene has made "Fuenteovejuna" a byword for solidarity. The work was embraced in the 20th century by socialist and anti-fascist movements, and a Soviet ballet, Laurencia (1939), was based on it.
