Boris I of Bulgaria

Reign: 852–889 (Knyaz of Bulgaria)

Born: early 9th century

Died: 2 May 907

Baptismal name: Michael

Successor: Vladimir-Rasate; then Simeon I

Venerated: Orthodox Church (saint)

Boris I (died 907), also known after his baptism as Michael, was the ruler (knyaz) of the First Bulgarian Empire from 852 to 889. He is remembered above all for the Christianisation of Bulgaria in 864/865, a decision that bound his realm to Orthodox Christianity, reshaped its culture, and made Bulgaria a centre of Slavic letters. Boris was canonised by the Orthodox Church and is regarded as one of the most significant Bulgarian rulers.

Conversion to Christianity

Boris inherited wars with the Franks, Serbs, and Byzantines, and a state divided between Bulgar and Slav populations with separate religions. Seeking unity and recognition, he negotiated with both Rome and Constantinople before accepting baptism from Byzantine clergy in 864 or 865, taking the name Michael after the Emperor Michael III. A revolt of pagan boyars was crushed. His famous correspondence with Pope Nicholas I — the Pope's "Responses to the Questions of the Bulgars" (866) — sought guidance for the new church, but Bulgaria ultimately remained in the Byzantine orbit, gaining an autonomous archbishopric in 870.

Slavonic letters

In 886 Boris welcomed the disciples of Saints Cyril and Methodius — Clement, Naum, and Angelarius — expelled from Moravia. Under his patronage the schools of Ohrid and Preslav developed the Slavonic liturgy and literature, and the Cyrillic script took shape, spreading literacy and a distinct Bulgarian church culture.

Abdication and final years

In 889 Boris abdicated in favour of his eldest son Vladimir-Rasate and retired to a monastery. When Vladimir attempted to restore paganism, Boris emerged from retirement in 893, deposed and blinded him, placed his younger son Simeon on the throne, and moved the capital to Preslav. He then returned to monastic life and died on 2 May 907.
