Sambar

Sambar is a lentil-based vegetable stew, cooked with pigeon peas (toor dal) and tamarind broth, originating from South India and now popular throughout India, Sri Lanka and wherever South Indian food is eaten. It is seasoned with a distinctive spice blend called sambar powder and finished with a tempering of mustard seeds, curry leaves and dried red chillies in oil.

Place of originIndia

Preparation and serving

Toor dal is boiled until soft and combined with tamarind extract and vegetables such as drumstick (moringa pods), okra, brinjal (aubergine), pumpkin, carrots, shallots and tomatoes. Sambar powder — typically ground coriander seed, cumin, fenugreek, dried chillies, chana dal and turmeric — gives the dish its characteristic flavour, and a pinch of asafoetida (hing) is traditional. Sambar is a staple accompaniment to steamed rice and to the breakfast dishes idli, dosa, vada and pongal, and forms part of the banana-leaf meal known as sadya.

History

According to a popular account, sambar was created in the 17th or 18th century in the Maratha court kitchen at Thanjavur, and named after Sambhaji, son of Shivaji. Whatever its precise origin, tamarind-dal stews have deep roots in Tamil cuisine, and regional variants across Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh and Kerala differ in their spice blends, vegetables and sweetness.
