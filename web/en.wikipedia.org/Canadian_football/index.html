Canadian football

Canadian football (French: football canadien) is a sport played in Canada in which two teams of 12 players compete for territorial control of a field 110 yards (101 m) long and 65 yards (59 m) wide, attempting to advance a pointed oval ball into the opposing team's end zone. It developed from rugby football in the nineteenth century and is closely related to American football, but retains distinctive rules.

Differences from American football

The Canadian game uses a larger field, 12 players per side rather than 11, three downs instead of four to advance the ball ten yards, a one-point score called the single or rouge for a ball kicked into or through the end zone that is not returned, unlimited motion before the snap, and a neutral zone a full yard wide at the line of scrimmage. Goal posts stand on the goal line rather than at the back of the end zone, and there is no fair catch rule.

History and leagues

Rugby-derived football was played in Canada from the 1860s; the Canadian Rugby Union was founded in 1882, and the Grey Cup was donated in 1909 by Earl Grey, the Governor General, for the amateur championship and became the trophy of the professional Canadian Football League (CFL), formed in 1958. The CFL, with nine teams today, is the highest level of the sport; its championship game is one of Canada's largest annual sporting events. University football (U Sports) and junior leagues also play under Canadian rules, administered by Football Canada.

Play

The wider field and three-down format favour the passing game and wide-open play. Teams feature specialized kicking plays because of the rouge, and the larger end zones (20 yards deep) affect goal-line strategy. Professional rosters must include a minimum number of Canadian ("national") players, a rule intended to develop domestic talent.
