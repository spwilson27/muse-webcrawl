Canis Minor

Canis Minor is a small constellation in the northern celestial hemisphere. In the second century, it was included in Ptolemy's 48 constellations, and it is counted among the modern 88 constellations. Its name is Latin for "lesser dog" or "smaller dog"; it represents one of the dogs following Orion, the hunter, the other being the larger Canis Major.

Stars

The constellation's brightest star is Procyon, the eighth-brightest star in the night sky, a binary system only 11.5 light-years away consisting of a yellow-white main-sequence star and a faint white dwarf companion. The second-brightest star, Gomeisa (Beta Canis Minoris), is a blue-white star about 160 light-years away. Canis Minor contains few deep-sky objects of note, though it includes some faint galaxies.

Mythology

In Greek mythology Canis Minor was variously identified as one of Orion's hunting dogs or as Maera, the dog of Icarius. The star Procyon's name means "before the dog", as it rises before Sirius, the "dog star" of Canis Major.
