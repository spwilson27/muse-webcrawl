Human trafficking

Human trafficking
Forms: Forced labour, sexual exploitation, forced marriage, organ removal, child soldiers
Key treaty: Palermo Protocol (2000)
Global estimates: Tens of millions in modern slavery (ILO/Walk Free)

Human trafficking is the recruitment, transportation, transfer, harbouring or receipt of people through force, fraud or coercion for the purpose of exploitation. Exploitation includes forced labour, sexual exploitation, slavery-like practices, servitude and the removal of organs. It is regarded as a form of modern slavery and a serious violation of human rights.

Definition

The 2000 UN Protocol to Prevent, Suppress and Punish Trafficking in Persons, Especially Women and Children — the Palermo Protocol — established the widely used three-element definition: an act (recruitment, transport and so on), a means (force, deception, abuse of vulnerability), and a purpose (exploitation). For children, no coercive means need be shown.

Forms

Labour trafficking occurs in agriculture, construction, fishing, domestic work and manufacturing. Sex trafficking forces victims into prostitution or pornography. Other forms include forced begging, forced criminality, child soldiering, forced marriage and trafficking for organ removal. The ILO estimated in 2022 that about 27.6 million people were in forced labour on any given day.

Causes and methods

Poverty, discrimination, conflict and demand for cheap labour and commercial sex create vulnerability. Traffickers recruit through false job offers, romantic manipulation, family networks and online platforms, and control victims through debt, confiscated documents, threats and violence.

Responses

Anti-trafficking policy is commonly framed as the “3 Ps” — prevention, protection of victims, and prosecution — with a fourth P, partnership. The US State Department publishes an annual Trafficking in Persons Report ranking governments' efforts.
