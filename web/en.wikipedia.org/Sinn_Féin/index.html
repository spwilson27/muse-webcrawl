Sinn Féin (Irish pronunciation: [ʃɪn̞ʲ fʲeːnʲ]; "We Ourselves") is an Irish republican and democratic socialist political party active in both the Republic of Ireland and Northern Ireland. Its central goal is the reunification of Ireland as a 32-county republic, ending British jurisdiction in Northern Ireland.

History

The party was founded by Arthur Griffith in 1905 as a movement advocating Irish self-reliance and abstention from Westminster. After the 1916 Easter Rising — for which it was popularly (if inaccurately) blamed by the authorities — Sinn Féin won a landslide in Ireland at the 1918 general election, declared an Irish republic and convened the First Dáil. The party subsequently split repeatedly, over the Anglo-Irish Treaty (producing Fine Gael and Fianna Fáil traditions) and again in 1970, when the modern Provisional Sinn Féin emerged alongside the Troubles in Northern Ireland.

Modern party

Under Gerry Adams's long leadership, Sinn Féin moved from being regarded as the political wing of the Provisional IRA towards electoral politics, supporting the 1998 Good Friday Agreement. Mary Lou McDonald succeeded Adams in 2018. In the 2020 Irish general election Sinn Féin won the largest share of first-preference votes, and in the 2022 Northern Ireland Assembly election it became the largest party at Stormont for the first time, with Michelle O'Neill becoming First Minister in 2024 — the first Irish nationalist to hold the post.
