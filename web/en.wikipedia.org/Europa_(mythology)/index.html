In Greek mythology, Europa was a Phoenician princess abducted by Zeus, who, in the form of a gentle white bull, carried her across the sea to Crete. The continent of Europe is named after her; her story is among the most depicted myths in Western art.

The myth

Zeus, enamoured of Europa, transformed himself into a tame white bull and mingled with her father's herds at Sidon or Tyre. Europa, drawn to the beautiful animal, climbed onto its back; the bull plunged into the sea and swam to Crete. There Zeus revealed himself, and Europa became the first queen of Crete. Their sons — Minos, Rhadamanthus, and Sarpedon — became famed judges and kings; Minos and Rhadamanthus later judged the dead in Hades. Zeus gave her gifts including the bronze guardian Talos, the hound Laelaps, and a javelin that never missed. She later married King Asterion of Crete.

Sources and legacy

The tale appears in the Iliad, Hesiodic fragments, Moschus's epyllion Europa, and Ovid's Metamorphoses. Herodotus offered a rationalised version with Cretan sailors kidnapping a king's daughter. Titian's The Rape of Europa is a canonical painting of the subject. The name "Europe" plausibly derives from her name in Greek tradition; Jupiter's moon Europa also bears her name.
