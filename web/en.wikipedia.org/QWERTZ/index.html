QWERTZ

The QWERTZ keyboard layout used in Central Europe

QWERTZ, or QWERTZU, is a keyboard layout widely used in Central Europe for typewriters and computer keyboards. The name comes from the first six letters of the top row of keys: Q, W, E, R, T, Z. It differs from the English-language QWERTY layout mainly by interchanging the Z and Y keys and by providing dedicated keys for the umlauted letters Ä, Ö and Ü.

Usage

The QWERTZ layout is standard in Germany and Austria (with national variants), and variants are used in Switzerland (with additional accented letters for French and Italian), Hungary, the Czech Republic and Slovakia, Poland, Romania, and several Balkan countries, most of which make further local modifications. Because German text uses Z much more often than Y — the reverse of English — the Z occupies the more accessible position in the top row, while Y moves to the bottom row.

Differences from QWERTY

Besides the Z/Y swap, the German layout places brackets, braces and other symbols at different positions, moves the ß key, and uses AltGr combinations for characters such as @, € and the backslash. Swiss keyboards print QWERTZ with letters for all three national languages on the same keycaps.
