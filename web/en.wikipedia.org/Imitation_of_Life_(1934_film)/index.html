Imitation of Life (1934 film)

Imitation of Life is a 1934 American drama film directed by John M. Stahl, adapted from Fannie Hurst's 1933 novel of the same name. It stars Claudette Colbert as Bea Pullman, a widowed white businesswoman, and Louise Beavers as Delilah Johnson, her Black housekeeper and business partner, alongside Warren William and Fredi Washington.

Plot

Bea and Delilah build a fortune marketing Delilah's pancake recipe under the "Aunt Delilah" brand. The parallel storylines follow Bea's daughter Jessie and Delilah's light-skinned daughter Peola, who rejects her mother and attempts to pass for white — an unusually frank treatment of race, colourism and passing for a Hollywood film of its era, made just as the Production Code began to be enforced.

Reception and legacy

The film was a commercial success and was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Picture. Washington's portrayal of Peola drew attention (and controversy) for its realism. The story was remade in 1959 by Douglas Sirk with Lana Turner and Juanita Moore. In 2005 the 1934 version was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry as culturally, historically or aesthetically significant, and it is widely discussed in scholarship on race in early American cinema.
