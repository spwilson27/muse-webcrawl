Player character

A player character (PC) is a fictional character in a role-playing game whose actions are controlled by a player, as distinct from a non-player character (NPC) controlled by the gamemaster or, in video games, by the software. The term originated in tabletop games such as Dungeons & Dragons and is now used across tabletop, live-action, and video role-playing games.

In tabletop games

Players typically create their characters by choosing attributes such as race, class, and abilities, often recorded on a character sheet, and then portray them through speech and decisions during play. Character advancement — gaining experience, levels, and equipment — is a central reward structure of most campaigns.

In video games

In video games the player character is the avatar through which the player acts: it may be a fixed, authored protagonist, a silent character, or one created in a character editor. Player characters are sometimes classified as "pre-generated" or "custom", and in massively multiplayer games they are commonly called avatars, toons, or mains.
