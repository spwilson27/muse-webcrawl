Talent management

Talent management — the anticipation of required human capital for an organization and the planning to meet those needs; the recruitment, development, and retention of skilled employees.

Talent management (TM) refers to the anticipation of required human capital for an organization and the planning to meet those needs. Talent management is the science of using strategic human resource planning to improve business value and to make it possible for companies and organizations to reach their goals. Everything done to recruit, retain, develop, reward and make people perform forms a part of talent management as well as strategic workforce planning.

History

The term was coined by McKinsey & Company following a 1997 study; it was popularized by the 2001 book The War for Talent. As competition for skilled knowledge workers intensified, human-resource departments expanded beyond administration into talent acquisition, onboarding, performance management, learning and development, succession planning, and compensation.

Scope

A talent-management system typically covers workforce planning, job profiling, sourcing and recruiting, performance appraisal, leadership development, and retention programmes. Two philosophies compete: an exclusive approach focused on identified "high potentials", and an inclusive approach that treats everyone's strengths as talent to be developed. Critics note that "talent" is socially constructed within organizations and that labelling can become a self-fulfilling prophecy.
