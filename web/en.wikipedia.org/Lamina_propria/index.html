Lamina propria

The lamina propria is a thin layer of connective tissue that forms part of the moist linings known as mucous membranes or mucosa, which line various tubes and organs in the body, including the digestive tract, respiratory tract and urogenital tract.

Structure

The lamina propria lies beneath the epithelium and, together with the epithelium and the muscularis mucosae, constitutes the mucosa. It is loose connective tissue, typically more cellular and less fibrous than the deeper submucosa. It contains blood vessels, lymphatics, nerves, and often glands that open onto the epithelial surface.

Function

It supplies the epithelium with nutrients and oxygen, anchors it to underlying tissue, and provides an irregular surface that increases contact between epithelium and deeper layers. Its vessels allow substances absorbed through the epithelium — such as nutrients in the intestine — to enter the circulation.

Immune role

The lamina propria is a major site of mucosal immunity, rich in lymphocytes, plasma cells (which secrete IgA), macrophages and mast cells. In the gut it is part of the gut-associated lymphoid tissue (GALT).
