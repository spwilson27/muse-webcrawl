Standing stone

A standing stone, or menhir, erected in prehistoric times.

A standing stone, or menhir (from the Breton words men, "stone", and hir, "long"), is a large upright stone erected in prehistoric times, typically dating from the European Neolithic and Bronze Ages. Standing stones may be found singly as monoliths, in rows, or arranged in circles.

Distribution

Standing stones are found across Europe, Africa, and Asia, with especially dense concentrations in Britain, Ireland, and Brittany in France. Famous examples include the stone circles of Stonehenge and Avebury in England, the Callanish Stones in Scotland, and the Carnac stones in Brittany, where more than a thousand menhirs stand in alignments.

Purpose

The purpose of standing stones remains uncertain. Proposed functions include territorial markers, commemorative monuments, components of astronomical observatories, and elements of religious or funerary rituals. Many are associated with burials and other ceremonial landscapes. In later periods, some standing stones were incorporated into folklore as the petrified remains of dancers, giants, or saints.
