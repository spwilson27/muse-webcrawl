Story arc

A story arc (also called a narrative arc) is an extended or continuing storyline in episodic storytelling media such as television, comic books, video games and novel series. Within a story arc, a plot or subplot develops and is resolved over multiple instalments rather than within a single episode, following the shape of a conventional narrative — exposition, rising action, climax, falling action and resolution — stretched across the arc's length.

Television

Story arcs contrast with the self-contained "episodic" format and with serial storytelling, in which the whole series is one continuous story. Many modern dramas combine episodic plots with season-long arcs: examples include the mythology arcs of The X-Files and the season arcs of Buffy the Vampire Slayer, each built around a season's principal antagonist. Streaming-era series frequently consist of a single arc spanning an entire season.

Comics and other media

In comics, story arcs gather multi-issue storylines within an ongoing title, later often collected as trade paperbacks. Character arcs — the inner transformation of a character across a work — are a related but distinct concept, frequently discussed alongside plot arcs.
