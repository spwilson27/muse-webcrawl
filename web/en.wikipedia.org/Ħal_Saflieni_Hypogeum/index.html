The Ħal Saflieni Hypogeum is a Neolithic subterranean structure in Paola, Malta, dating from the Saflieni phase of Maltese prehistory (c. 3300–3000 BC). It is the only known prehistoric underground temple in the world, and it is believed to have served first as a sanctuary and later as a necropolis, containing the remains of more than 7,000 individuals. The site was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1980.

Discovery and excavation

The Hypogeum was accidentally discovered in 1902 during the digging of house foundations, when workmen broke through its roof. The site was initially kept quiet by the builders, but it soon came to the attention of the authorities. Excavations were directed first by Father Emmanuel Magri and, after his death, by Sir Themistocles Zammit, Malta's first director of museums. The complex was opened to the public in 1908.

Structure

The Hypogeum is carved into soft globigerina limestone over three levels descending to a depth of about 10.6 metres. The upper level consists of a series of burial chambers; the middle level contains the most elaborately carved rooms, including the so-called Holy of Holies, the Oracle Room — notable for a small niche that produces a powerful acoustic resonance — and the Main Chamber, whose ceiling is decorated with red ochre spiral paintings, the only prehistoric paintings found in Malta. The lowest level appears to have been used for storage. Many of the chambers imitate the architecture of Malta's above-ground megalithic temples, with carved facades, trilithon doorways and corbelled ceilings executed in solid rock.

Artefacts

Excavations recovered pottery, stone and clay figurines, beads, amulets and carved animals. The most famous find is the "Sleeping Lady", a clay figurine of a reclining woman, now displayed at the National Museum of Archaeology in Valletta, along with the headless "Venus of Malta".

Conservation

Because of its fragile microclimate, the Hypogeum was closed to the public between 1992 and 2000 for conservation works, and visitor numbers remain strictly limited. Management of the site is the responsibility of Heritage Malta.
