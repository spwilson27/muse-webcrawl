Environmental criminology

Environmental criminology studies how places, routines, and opportunities shape crime events. Instead of asking only why offenders are motivated, it asks where and when crimes occur, how targets are encountered, and how environments can be altered to reduce harm.

Key theories

Routine activity theory holds that crime is likely when a motivated offender, a suitable target, and the absence of a capable guardian converge. Rational choice perspective treats offending as decision-making influenced by effort, risk, and reward. Crime pattern theory explains how offenders develop awareness spaces along familiar routes and nodes.

Methods and prevention

Researchers map hot spots, analyze journey-to-crime distances, and examine facilities such as bars, stations, and parking lots. Practical applications include situational crime prevention, improved lighting and sightlines, place management, and problem-oriented policing.

Debates

Critics warn that place-based measures may displace crime, intensify surveillance of marginalized communities, or neglect social causes. Defenders respond that concentrated prevention can reduce victimization without simply moving it elsewhere. See also routine activity theory and crime prevention through environmental design.
