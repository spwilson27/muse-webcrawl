Angoulême

Angoulême is a commune and the prefecture (capital) of the Charente department in the Nouvelle-Aquitaine region of southwestern France, with a population of around 42,000. Set on a limestone plateau above a bend of the Charente River, the old town is ringed by ramparts and dominated by its Romanesque cathedral. Angoulême is internationally known for the Angoulême International Comics Festival, the largest comics festival in Europe, held every January since 1974.

History

Known in Roman times as Iculisma, the city became the seat of counts of Angoulême in the early Middle Ages; its most famous count's daughter, Isabella of Angoulême, married King John of England in 1200, binding the Angoumois to Anglo-French rivalry for centuries. The city later gave its name to a cadet line of the French royal house — Francis I, born at nearby Cognac as Count of Angoulême, became king in 1515, and his sister Marguerite of Angoulême became a noted Renaissance writer. Angoulême was long a centre of papermaking, using the Charente's waters, and later of printing and foundries.

Sights and culture

The Cathedral of Saint-Pierre, consecrated in 1128, is famed for its sculpted façade depicting the Last Judgment. The old town's ramparts offer panoramic views over the river. Beyond the comics festival — which leaves painted murals, including characters from Franco-Belgian comics, on walls across the city — Angoulême hosts the French national comics museum (Cité Internationale de la Bande Dessinée et de l'Image) and is recognized by UNESCO as a Creative City of Literature.

Economy

Modern industries include naval engineering (a major naval defence site), electrical equipment, and animation and image-production studios clustered around the comics sector.
