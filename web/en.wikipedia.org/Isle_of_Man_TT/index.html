The Isle of Man TT (Tourist Trophy) is an annual motorcycle racing event held on the Isle of Man in late spring or early summer. For many decades the most prestigious motorcycle race in the world, it is run in a time-trial format on public roads closed for the purpose, over the Snaefell Mountain Course of 37.73 miles — a circuit of more than 200 corners through villages, glens, and over the island's mountain. It is widely regarded as one of the most dangerous racing events in motorsport.

History

The TT began in 1907, when racing on public roads was prohibited in Great Britain but permitted under Manx law. The first races used the shorter St John's Course; the Mountain Course was adopted in 1911. The event formed part of the Motorcycle World Championship from 1949 to 1976, when its dangerous reputation led riders to boycott and the championship round moved to mainland Britain. The TT nevertheless continued, sustained by its prestige and by amateur and specialist road racers.

The course

Riders set off at intervals from the TT Grandstand in Douglas, racing against the clock through landmarks such as Bray Hill, Quarter Bridge, Ballacraine, Kirk Michael, Ramsey, the Bungalow, and the mountain section over Snaefell. Leading superbikes lap at average speeds above 135 mph (217 km/h), with top speeds exceeding 200 mph on the Sulby Straight — on roads lined with stone walls, houses, and telegraph poles.

Race classes

The modern TT programme includes Superbike, Supersport, Superstock, Lightweight (Supertwin), and Sidecar races, plus the blue-riband Senior TT. Race distances are typically four to six laps — up to 226 miles. The Manx Grand Prix and Classic TT, held in late summer, use the same course for amateurs and historic machines.

Danger and fatalities

More than 260 riders have been killed on the Mountain Course in the TT, Manx Grand Prix, and related events since 1907, making safety a permanent controversy. Organisers have introduced measures including improved marshalling, air fencing, rider licensing standards, and live timing, but the event's fundamental character — very high speeds on ordinary roads — remains. Supporters defend it as a unique test of skill and freedom; critics question whether it should continue. The event was cancelled in 2020 and 2021 because of the COVID-19 pandemic, returning in 2022.

Notable riders

Joey Dunlop's 26 victories stood as the record for decades before his nephew Michael Dunlop surpassed it in the 2020s. Other greats include Mike Hailwood, Giacomo Agostini, John McGuinness, Ian Hutchinson, and Peter Hickman, holder of the outright lap record.
