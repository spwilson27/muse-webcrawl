China Railway High-speed


A Fuxing (CR400AF) trainset

China Railway High-speed (CRH) is the high-speed rail service operated by China Railway, the national passenger rail operator of the People's Republic of China. The CRH brand was introduced in April 2007 along with the sixth national railway speed-up programme, initially covering trains running on upgraded conventional lines as well as the first dedicated passenger lines.

Development

Under joint-venture agreements, foreign manufacturers including Siemens, Alstom, Kawasaki Heavy Industries and Bombardier supplied technology for the first generation of CRH trainsets, which were progressively localised. The CRH380 series, introduced from 2010, was designed for speeds of up to 380 km/h, though operating speeds were capped at 300–350 km/h. A second family, the Fuxing ("Rejuvenation") trains developed to fully domestic standards, entered service in 2017 and are marketed separately from the CRH (Hexie, "Harmony") fleet, although both operate within the same high-speed network.

Network and services

China has built the world's largest high-speed rail network, exceeding 40,000 km of dedicated high-speed lines by the early 2020s — more than the rest of the world combined. CRH services operate on flagship routes such as the Beijing–Shanghai high-speed railway and the Beijing–Guangzhou line. Train names are prefixed with G (high-speed), D (electric multiple unit) or C (intercity) depending on service class and maximum speed.

Safety

The programme's safety record was marred by the 2011 Wenzhou collision, in which two high-speed trains collided, killing 40 people. The crash led to speed reductions, a major anti-corruption investigation of the railway ministry, and the eventual restructuring of railway administration, with operating functions transferred to the China Railway Corporation (now China State Railway Group) in 2013.
