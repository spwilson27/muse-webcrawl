Anthony Charles Lynton Blair (born 6 May 1953) is a British politician who served as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom from 1997 to 2007 and Leader of the Labour Party from 1994 to 2007. He led Labour to three consecutive general election victories under the banner of "New Labour", modernising the party's image and policies.

Premiership

Blair's governments introduced the national minimum wage, devolution to Scotland and Wales, the Human Rights Act and civil partnerships, oversaw the Northern Ireland peace process culminating in the Good Friday Agreement, and increased investment in health and education. His premiership was defined internationally by close alignment with the United States, intervention in Kosovo and Sierra Leone, and above all the 2003 invasion of Iraq, which proved deeply controversial and permanently damaged his reputation at home.

After office

Blair resigned in 2007 and served as envoy of the Middle East Quartet until 2015. He later founded the Tony Blair Institute for Global Change and remains an influential, if polarising, voice in British politics.
