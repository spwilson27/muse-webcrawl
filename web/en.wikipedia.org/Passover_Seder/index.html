Passover Seder

The Passover Seder is a ritual feast at the beginning of the Jewish holiday of Passover. It is conducted throughout the world on the eve of the 15th day of Nisan in the Hebrew calendar (i.e., at the start of the 15th; a second night is observed outside Israel in traditional communities). The Seder is a ritual involving a retelling of the story of the liberation of the Israelites from slavery in ancient Egypt, as told in the Book of Exodus.

The Seder plate, whose symbolic items anchor the retelling of the Exodus.

The ceremony

The Seder follows the order set out in the Haggadah, a text that prescribes fifteen steps, from the opening blessing (Kadesh) through to the concluding songs (Nirtzah). Participants drink four cups of wine, eat matzah (unleavened bread), and taste the symbolic foods arranged on the Seder plate: a roasted shank bone, an egg, bitter herbs, a second bitter herb, a sweet paste of fruit and nuts called charoset, and a green vegetable. The youngest child traditionally asks the Four Questions, beginning "Why is this night different from all other nights?", prompting the telling of the Exodus story.

Customs

Families recline at the table in the manner of free people of the ancient world, hide and search for the afikoman (a piece of matzah eaten as the last morsel), open the door for the prophet Elijah, and sing songs including Dayenu and Chad Gadya. The Seder is among the most widely observed of all Jewish rituals.
