Canticle

A canticle (from the Latin canticulum, a diminutive of canticum, "song") is a hymn, psalm, or song of praise taken from the Bible or from a biblical imitation, other than the Psalms. Canticles form a core part of the Liturgy of the Hours and of Anglican morning and evening prayer.

Biblical canticles

The Old Testament contains numerous canticles, including the Song of Moses (Exodus 15), the Song of Deborah (Judges 5), the Song of Hannah (1 Samuel 2), and songs in Isaiah, Jonah, Habakkuk, and Daniel, notably the Song of the Three Young Men. Medieval breviaries traditionally counted a cycle of Old Testament canticles sung at Lauds through the week.

Gospel canticles

Three canticles from the Gospel of Luke anchor the daily office: the Benedictus (the Song of Zechariah, Luke 1:68–79), sung at morning prayer; the Magnificat (the Song of Mary, Luke 1:46–55), sung at evening prayer; and the Nunc dimittis (the Song of Simeon, Luke 2:29–32), sung at night prayer. In the Anglican Book of Common Prayer these appear at Mattins, Evensong, and Compline respectively, often in alternation with psalms.

Other uses

The term also applies to the Song of Songs, traditionally called the Canticle of Canticles, and to Saint Francis of Assisi's Canticle of the Sun, a foundational work of Italian poetry. Composers from Schütz and Charpentier to Britten — whose five Canticles span his career — have set canticle texts to music.
