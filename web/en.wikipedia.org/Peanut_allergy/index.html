Peanut allergy

Peanut allergy is a type of food allergy to peanuts, distinct from tree nut allergies. It is an abnormal immune response, usually mediated by immunoglobulin E (IgE) antibodies to peanut proteins. Symptoms can range from mild hives and itching to life-threatening anaphylaxis.

Peanut allergy
Specialty: Allergy and immunology
Onset: Usually childhood
Management: Avoidance; epinephrine for anaphylaxis

Symptoms

Reactions typically begin within minutes of exposure and may include skin flushing, swelling, vomiting, difficulty breathing and a drop in blood pressure. Peanut allergy is one of the most common causes of fatal food-related anaphylaxis.

Diagnosis and management

Diagnosis rests on clinical history supported by skin-prick testing, specific IgE blood tests, or supervised oral food challenge. Management centres on strict avoidance and carrying epinephrine auto-injectors. The early-introduction LEAP study (2015) showed that feeding peanut to high-risk infants markedly reduced the development of the allergy, changing prevention guidelines in several countries.

Immunotherapy

Oral immunotherapy products—standardized peanut protein given in escalating doses—have been approved in some jurisdictions to reduce the severity of reactions to accidental exposure.
