Judea Pearl (born 1936) is an Israeli-American computer scientist and philosopher, best known for developing the probabilistic approach to artificial intelligence and the study of causality. He is a professor at the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA) and received the 2011 ACM Turing Award for fundamental contributions to artificial intelligence through the development of a calculus for probabilistic and causal reasoning.

Life and career

Pearl was born in Tel Aviv and studied electrical engineering at the Technion, later earning a doctorate from the Polytechnic Institute of Brooklyn (now NYU Tandon). He worked in industry on superconductors and electronic memory before joining UCLA's computer science department in 1970. He is the father of the journalist Daniel Pearl, who was kidnapped and murdered in Pakistan in 2002; Judea Pearl subsequently became a prominent public voice on extremism and co-founded the Daniel Pearl Foundation.

Work

In his 1988 book Probabilistic Reasoning in Intelligent Systems, Pearl introduced Bayesian networks — graphical models that represent probabilistic dependencies among variables — transforming how machines reason under uncertainty. He then turned to causal inference, developing do-calculus and structural causal models, summarized for a general audience in The Book of Why (2018, with Dana Mackenzie). His "ladder of causation" distinguishes association, intervention and counterfactual reasoning. He has also written on the philosophy of science and on Jewish thought.
