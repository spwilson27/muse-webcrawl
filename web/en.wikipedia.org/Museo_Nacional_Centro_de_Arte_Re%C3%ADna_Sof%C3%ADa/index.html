The Museo Nacional Centro de Arte Reina Sofía (MNCARS), commonly called the Reina Sofía, is Spain's national museum of 20th- and 21st-century art, located in Madrid near the Atocha railway station. Together with the Prado and the Thyssen-Bornemisza Museum it forms Madrid's "Golden Triangle of Art".

The museum is housed principally in a former hospital, the Hospital General de San Carlos, an 18th-century neoclassical building; distinctive glass lift towers were added on its exterior, and a major extension designed by Jean Nouvel opened in 2005.

Collection

The Reina Sofía is best known as the home of Pablo Picasso's monumental anti-war painting Guernica (1937), which returned to Spain in 1981 and moved to the museum in 1992. Its collection also includes major works by Salvador Dalí, Joan Miró, Juan Gris, Antoni Tàpies and many contemporary artists, with particular strength in Spanish modern art and international contemporary art.
