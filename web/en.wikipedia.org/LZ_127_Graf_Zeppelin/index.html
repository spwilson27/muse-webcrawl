LZ 127 Graf Zeppelin

LZ 127 Graf Zeppelin was a German hydrogen-filled rigid airship that operated commercially from 1928 to 1937, the most successful airship of its era. It was named for Count Ferdinand von Zeppelin.

Service

Under commander Hugo Eckener, Graf Zeppelin made the first intercontinental passenger airship flights, circumnavigated the globe in 1929, flew an Arctic research expedition in 1931, and ran regular services to South America. It covered over 1.7 million kilometres in 590 flights.

Retirement

After the Hindenburg disaster in 1937 the ship was grounded and later scrapped in 1940, its metal used for the German war effort.
