Lindbergh kidnapping

Date1 March 1932
LocationHopewell Township, New Jersey
VictimCharles Augustus Lindbergh Jr.
PerpetratorBruno Richard Hauptmann (convicted)

The Lindbergh kidnapping refers to the abduction and murder of Charles Augustus Lindbergh Jr., the 20-month-old son of aviator Charles Lindbergh and Anne Morrow Lindbergh. The child was taken from his nursery at the family home near Hopewell, New Jersey, on the evening of 1 March 1932. The crime became one of the most publicised in American history, dubbed the "Crime of the Century" by the press.

Abduction and ransom

A handwritten ransom note demanding $50,000 was left on the nursery windowsill. Over the following weeks, intermediary John F. Condon delivered $50,000 in gold certificates to a man in a Bronx cemetery. On 12 May 1932, the child's body was found in woods about 4.5 miles from the Lindbergh home; he had died of a head injury, apparently on the night of the kidnapping.

Investigation and trial

In September 1934, Bruno Richard Hauptmann, a German immigrant carpenter, was arrested after passing a ransom gold certificate. More than $14,000 of the ransom money was found in his garage. His trial in Flemington, New Jersey, in early 1935 was a media sensation. Hauptmann was convicted of felony murder and executed in the electric chair on 3 April 1936, maintaining his innocence.

Consequences

The case prompted Congress to pass the Federal Kidnapping Act, known as the "Lindbergh Law", making it a federal offence to transport a kidnapping victim across state lines. The Lindberghs later left the United States for a period to escape publicity.
