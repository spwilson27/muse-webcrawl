Canvassing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Canvassing, also known as door-to-door or direct canvassing, is the systematic initiation of direct contact with individuals, commonly used during political campaigns. Canvassing can be done for many reasons: political campaigning, grassroots fundraising, community awareness, public surveys, and membership drives such as union organising.

Political canvassing

In electoral politics, campaigners visit voters at home to identify supporters, persuade undecided voters, register voters, and, near election day, encourage supporters to turn out and vote. Modern campaigns use voter databases and "turf cutting" software to assign canvassers lists of targeted households and record the results of each contact, a practice sometimes called the "deep canvass" when it involves extended persuasive conversations.

Other uses

In the United Kingdom, the official canvass is the annual process by which electoral registration officers update the electoral roll. The term is also used in sales (cold calling door-to-door) and in research surveys.

Effectiveness

Field experiments in political science generally find face-to-face canvassing among the most effective methods of increasing voter turnout, considerably more so than direct mail or phone banking.
