Spanish conquest of the Inca Empire

Spanish conquest of the Inca Empire
Date: 1532–1572
Location: Western South America
Commanders: Francisco Pizarro; Atahualpa
Result: Spanish victory; end of the Inca Empire

The Spanish conquest of the Inca Empire was one of the most consequential campaigns in the Spanish colonisation of the Americas. Between 1532 and 1572, expeditions led by Francisco Pizarro and his brothers overthrew the Inca state — the largest empire in the pre-Columbian Americas — and incorporated its Andean territories into the Spanish Empire as the Viceroyalty of Peru.

Background

In the late 1520s the Inca Empire (Tawantinsuyu) was weakened by the arrival of Eurasian diseases, including smallpox, which killed the emperor Huayna Capac and his heir and precipitated a civil war between his sons Atahualpa and Huáscar. Atahualpa had just won that war when Pizarro's third expedition reached Peru in 1532.

Cajamarca and collapse

On 16 November 1532, Pizarro's force of fewer than 200 men ambushed Atahualpa's retinue at Cajamarca, killing thousands and capturing the emperor. Atahualpa offered a famous ransom — a room filled with gold and twice over with silver — but was nevertheless executed in July 1533 after a show trial. Spanish forces entered Cusco in November 1533 and installed puppet rulers, first Manco Inca, whose great rebellion of 1536 besieged Cusco and Lima but ultimately failed.

Consolidation and resistance

The conquistadors then fought among themselves; Diego de Almagro and the Pizarro brothers were all dead by 1541. Royal authority was consolidated under Viceroy Francisco de Toledo in the 1570s. A Neo-Inca state survived at Vilcabamba until 1572, when its last ruler, Túpac Amaru, was captured and executed — the conventional end of the conquest. Epidemic disease, warfare and exploitation caused catastrophic population decline among Andean peoples.
