McDonald's Corporation is an American multinational fast food chain founded in 1940 by Richard and Maurice McDonald in San Bernardino, California. It is the world's largest restaurant chain by revenue, serving over 69 million customers daily in over 100 countries. McDonald's is best known for its hamburgers — including the Big Mac — as well as french fries, chicken products, breakfast items, and soft drinks.

History

The McDonald brothers introduced the "Speedee Service System" in 1948, applying assembly-line principles to food preparation. Ray Kroc joined as a franchise agent in 1955, bought the company in 1961, and drove its global expansion. The chain introduced the Big Mac in 1968 and the Happy Meal in 1979.

Business model

Most McDonald's restaurants are operated by franchisees; the corporation earns revenue from rent and royalties as well as sales in company-operated stores. The golden arches are among the most recognisable logos in the world.

Criticism

McDonald's has faced criticism over the nutritional content of its food, labour practices, and environmental impact, including the 2004 documentary Super Size Me and the long-running "McLibel" case in the United Kingdom.
