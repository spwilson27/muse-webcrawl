USCGC Stratton (WMSL-752)

USCGC Stratton (WMSL-752)
Class: Legend-class cutter
Launched: 23 July 2010
Commissioned: 31 March 2012
Homeport: Alameda, California

USCGC Stratton (WMSL-752) is the third Legend-class cutter of the United States Coast Guard. She is named for Captain Dorothy C. Stratton, who served as director of the SPARS, the Coast Guard Women's Reserve, during World War II and was the first woman commissioned as an officer in the Coast Guard.

Construction and commissioning

Stratton was built by Huntington Ingalls Industries at Pascagoula, Mississippi. Her keel was laid in 2009, she was launched on 23 July 2010 and christened by Michelle Obama, the first time a First Lady christened a Coast Guard cutter. She was commissioned on 31 March 2012 at Alameda, California.

Service

Homeported in Alameda, Stratton has conducted counter-narcotics patrols in the Eastern Pacific, fisheries enforcement, search and rescue, and Arctic and Western Pacific deployments, including operations in support of U.S. Indo-Pacific Command.

Characteristics

The Legend-class cutters displace about 4,500 tons, are 418 feet long, and can reach speeds over 28 knots. Armament includes a 57 mm gun, a Phalanx CIWS, and machine guns; the class carries helicopters and small boats for boarding operations.
