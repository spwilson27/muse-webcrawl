Dione (Titaness)

Dione, the Titaness associated with the oracle of Dodona.

Dione (Ancient Greek: Διώνη, "she-Zeus" or "she who belongs to Zeus") is a figure in Greek mythology, variously counted among the Titans and the Oceanids. In some traditions she is the mother of the goddess Aphrodite by Zeus, and she was worshipped alongside Zeus at the ancient oracle of Dodona.

Mythology

Hesiod does not list Dione among the Titans in the Theogony, but he names her among the Oceanids, the three thousand daughters of Oceanus and Tethys. Apollodorus, by contrast, includes Dione among the Titan children of Uranus and Gaia. Homer gives her a prominent role in the Iliad: when Aphrodite is wounded by the mortal hero Diomedes, she flees to Mount Olympus and is comforted by her mother Dione, who heals her and recounts how other gods have suffered at mortal hands.

Dodona

At Dodona in Epirus — one of the oldest oracles in Greece, second in fame only to Delphi — Dione was worshipped as the consort of Zeus Naios. Priests and priestesses interpreted the rustling of the sacred oak and other signs. Inscriptions and votive offerings from the sanctuary attest to her cult there into the Roman period.

Legacy

Dione's name is the feminine form of Zeus (Dios). Her Roman counterpart is sometimes associated with the goddess Diana's name etymologically, though the two figures are distinct. The name Dione is also borne by one of Saturn's moons, though that moon is named for a different figure of the same name in some accounts, and by the figure Dione in the Dionysiaca of Nonnus.
