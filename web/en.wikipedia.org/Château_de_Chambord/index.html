Château de Chambord

The Château de Chambord is the largest château in France's Loire Valley, a masterpiece of French Renaissance architecture blending medieval fortress forms with classical Italian design.

Construction

King Francis I began building in 1519 as a hunting lodge and symbol of royal power. Its distinctive skyline of towers and chimneys crowns a keep with a famous double-helix staircase, traditionally attributed to the influence of Leonardo da Vinci, who died nearby in 1519. The château has 440 rooms and stands in a vast walled park.

Later history

Used intermittently by the court, it sheltered artworks from the Louvre during the Second World War and is now a major tourist site and UNESCO World Heritage property.
