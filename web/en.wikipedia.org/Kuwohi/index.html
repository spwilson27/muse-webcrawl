Kuwohi (Cherokee: ᎫᏩᎯ, "mulberry place"), formerly known as Clingmans Dome, is a mountain in the Great Smoky Mountains of Tennessee and North Carolina, in the southeastern United States. At an elevation of 6,643 feet (2,025 m), it is the highest mountain in the Smokies, the highest point in Tennessee, and the third-highest point in the Appalachian Mountains, as well as the highest point along the Appalachian Trail.

Name change

The mountain's Cherokee name, Kuwohi, was restored in September 2024 when the U.S. Board on Geographic Names voted to approve the name change from Clingmans Dome, following a request by the Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians. The peak had been named in 1859 for Thomas Lanier Clingman, a U.S. senator and Confederate general. Kuwohi is a sacred place in Cherokee tradition, where medicine people sought guidance and where part of the tribe hid during the Trail of Tears.

Geography and access

Kuwohi is protected as part of Great Smoky Mountains National Park. A paved road (Kuwohi Road, formerly Clingmans Dome Road) leads to a parking area near the summit, from which a half-mile paved trail ascends to a 45-foot (14 m) concrete observation tower built in 1959. On clear days the tower offers views of over 100 miles. The summit's spruce-fir forest has been heavily damaged by the balsam woolly adelgid and air pollution.
