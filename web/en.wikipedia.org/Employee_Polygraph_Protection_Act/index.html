Employee Polygraph Protection Act

The Employee Polygraph Protection Act of 1988 (EPPA) is a United States federal law, codified at 29 U.S.C. §§ 2001–2009, that generally prohibits private-sector employers from using lie detector tests — including polygraphs — either for pre-employment screening or during the course of employment.

Provisions

The act makes it unlawful for an employer engaged in interstate commerce to:

  • require, request, suggest, or cause an employee or prospective employee to take a lie detector test;
  • use, accept, refer to, or inquire about the results of such a test; or
  • discharge, discipline, or discriminate against an employee who refuses to take a test or who exercises rights under the act.

Employers are required to post a notice of the act's protections in the workplace. The Secretary of Labor enforces the act, and employees may also bring civil actions, with remedies including reinstatement, lost wages, and damages.

Exemptions

The EPPA does not apply to federal, state, or local government employers, nor to lie detector tests administered by the federal government to certain intelligence and security personnel. Limited exemptions allow private employers to request polygraph tests in connection with specific investigations of workplace theft or other economic loss, and cover employers in the security services and pharmaceutical industries under strict conditions. Many states impose restrictions that are stricter still.
