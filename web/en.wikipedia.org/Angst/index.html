Angst

Angst is a feeling of anxiety, apprehension, or insecurity. It is a German word (capitalised as a noun in German) borrowed into English, where it also carries the specific philosophical sense of existential dread developed in the works of Søren Kierkegaard and later existentialist thinkers.

In philosophy

Kierkegaard introduced the concept in The Concept of Anxiety (1844), describing Angest as the "dizziness of freedom" — the anxiety a person feels when confronting possibility and the responsibility of choice, distinct from fear, which has a definite object. Martin Heidegger later made Angst central to Being and Time (1927), where anxiety reveals the nothingness at the heart of existence and individuates Dasein by confronting it with death. Jean-Paul Sartre adapted the idea as anguish (angoisse), the recognition of one's radical freedom.

In psychology

Sigmund Freud distinguished forms of anxiety, and existential psychologists such as Rollo May drew directly on Kierkegaard in treating anxiety as an unavoidable feature of human existence that can be either destructive or a spur to growth. In ordinary clinical usage, however, angst is not a diagnosis; related conditions are classified as anxiety disorders.

In popular culture

In English vernacular, "angst" often denotes the brooding emotional turmoil associated with adolescence, and has lent its name to a strain of dark, confessional themes in music, film, and literature, from German Expressionist cinema to grunge and emo.
