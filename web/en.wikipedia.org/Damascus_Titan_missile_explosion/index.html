Damascus Titan missile explosion

The Damascus Titan missile explosion was a 1980 incident at a Titan II intercontinental ballistic missile complex near Damascus, Arkansas, United States. A fuel leak led to an explosion that destroyed the missile in its silo and killed one US Air Force airman, Senior Airman David Livingston, while injuring 21 others. The missile's 9-megaton W-53 nuclear warhead was ejected from the silo but did not detonate, and no radioactive material was released beyond the immediate site.

Incident

On the evening of 18 September 1980, during routine maintenance, a technician dropped a socket from a ratchet wrench, which fell about 80 feet, bounced, and punctured the skin of the missile's first-stage fuel tank. Aerozine 50 fuel began leaking and vapour filled the silo. The complex was evacuated, and disaster-response teams worked through the night. At about 3 a.m. on 19 September the accumulated fuel vapours exploded, blowing off the 740-ton silo closure door and hurling the second stage and warhead into the air; the warhead landed about 100 feet from the complex gate.

Aftermath

The explosion intensified public and congressional debate about the safety of the ageing, liquid-fuelled Titan II force. The damaged complex was filled in and never reused; the Titan II fleet was retired during the 1980s. The incident was documented in Eric Schlosser's 2013 book Command and Control and a 2016 documentary film of the same name.
