Syrah

Syrah, also known as Shiraz, is a dark-skinned grape variety grown throughout the world and used primarily to produce red wine. In 1999, Syrah was found to be the offspring of two obscure grapes from southeastern France, Dureza and Mondeuse Blanche.

History

The grape is believed to have originated in the Rhône valley of France, where it is the principal grape of appellations such as Hermitage and Côte-Rôtie. DNA profiling in 1999 settled long-running legends that linked the grape to Persia or Sicily.

Viticulture

Syrah is a vigorous, mid-ripening variety that produces small, thick-skinned berries. It thrives in warm climates but can lose acidity and fruit character if conditions become too hot.

Wine styles

Wines range from powerful, full-bodied Australian Shiraz to the more savoury, peppery styles of the northern Rhône. Common flavours include blackberry, plum, black pepper, and smoked meat.

Regions

Major plantings are found in France, Australia, the United States, South Africa, Chile, and Argentina.
