Juneau, Alaska

Juneau (officially the City and Borough of Juneau) is the capital city of the U.S. state of Alaska, located in the Alaskan Panhandle on the Gastineau Channel. It is the third-most populous city in the state, with a population of about 32,000, and is notable as the only U.S. state capital with no road connections to the rest of the state or the continent — access is by air or sea only.

History

The area was home to the Tlingit people for thousands of years. In 1880, prospectors Richard Harris and Joe Juneau, guided by the Tlingit chief Kowee, discovered gold in Gold Creek, sparking a rush that founded the town. Juneau became the capital of the Territory of Alaska in 1906 and remained capital after statehood in 1959, despite its remote location; the Alaska–Juneau (A-J) Mine was once among the world's largest gold mines. Debates over moving the capital closer to Anchorage recur but have never succeeded.

Geography and tourism

The unified city-borough is larger in area than Delaware and Rhode Island combined, much of it rugged mountain wilderness in the Tongass National Forest. The Mendenhall Glacier, just outside downtown, is a major attraction, as are whale watching, fishing, and the Mount Roberts Tramway. Juneau is a popular cruise ship port, receiving over a million visitors in peak seasons.
