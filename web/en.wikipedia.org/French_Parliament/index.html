The French Parliament (French: Parlement français) is the bicameral legislature of the French Republic, comprising the National Assembly (lower house) and the Senate (upper house). Under the Constitution of the Fifth Republic (1958), Parliament votes laws, controls government action, and evaluates public policy, though its powers are deliberately limited compared with the executive — a system often called "rationalized parliamentarism".

Houses

The National Assembly has 577 deputies elected by direct universal suffrage in single-member constituencies for five-year terms; it sits at the Palais Bourbon. It has the last word on most legislation and alone can bring down the government by a motion of censure. The Senate, meeting in the Luxembourg Palace, represents the territorial collectivities; its 348 senators are elected for six years by an electoral college of local officials, with half renewed every three years.

Legislative procedure

Bills may originate in either house (except finance bills, which start in the National Assembly). A shuttle (navette) between the houses seeks agreement; failing that, a joint committee tries to settle a text, and ultimately the National Assembly can decide definitively at the government's request. Parliament may also be convened in Congress at Versailles to revise the Constitution, and it sits as a High Court for the impeachment of the president.

Sessions and control

Parliament sits in an ordinary session from October to June. It questions the government, conducts inquiries through commissions, and votes the budget within strict constitutional deadlines.
