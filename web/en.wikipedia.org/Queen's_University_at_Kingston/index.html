Queen's University at Kingston

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Queen's University at Kingston, commonly known as Queen's University or simply Queen's, is a public research university in Kingston, Ontario, Canada. Founded on 16 October 1841 via a royal charter issued by Queen Victoria, the university predates Canada's founding by 26 years and was modelled on the Scottish universities of Edinburgh and Glasgow.

Overview

Queen's enrols roughly 25,000 students and is organised into faculties including Arts and Science, Engineering, Business (the Smith School of Business), Law, Medicine, and Education. Its limestone main campus sits near downtown Kingston on the shore of Lake Ontario. The university is a member of the U15 group of Canadian research universities and the Matariki Network of Universities.

Traditions and notable alumni

Queen's is known for strong student traditions — including its engineering students' purple-dyed jackets, the Tricolour colours (blue, gold, and red), and the Gaelic motto Sapientia et Doctrina Stabilitas ("Wisdom and knowledge shall be the stability of thy times"). Its alumni include Nobel laureate Arthur McDonald, co-recipient of the 2015 Nobel Prize in Physics for work on neutrino oscillations carried out with the Sudbury Neutrino Observatory, in which Queen's played a leading role.
