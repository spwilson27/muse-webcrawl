The Kamchatka Peninsula (Russian: полуостров Камчатка) is a 1,250-kilometre-long peninsula in the Russian Far East, with an area of about 270,000 km². The Pacific Ocean and the Sea of Okhotsk make up the peninsula's eastern and western coastlines, respectively. Immediately offshore along the Pacific coast runs the 10,500-metre-deep Kuril–Kamchatka Trench.

Geography and volcanism

Kamchatka is one of the most volcanically active regions on Earth, forming part of the Pacific Ring of Fire. It contains about 300 volcanoes, of which some 29 are active, including Klyuchevskaya Sopka, the largest active volcano in the Northern Hemisphere. The Valley of Geysers, discovered in 1941, is the second-largest geyser field in the world. The Volcanoes of Kamchatka are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Wildlife and history

The peninsula is famous for dense populations of brown bears, Steller's sea eagles and some of the world's richest salmon runs. Explored by Russian Cossacks under Vladimir Atlasov in the 1690s and used as the starting point of Vitus Bering's expeditions, Kamchatka was closed to foreigners during the Soviet era owing to military installations, including the Pacific Fleet submarine base at Vilyuchinsk. Indigenous peoples include the Itelmens, Koryaks and Evens.
