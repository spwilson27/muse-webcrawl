San Luis Potosí (state)


Image placeholder: San Luis Potosí (state)

San Luis Potosí (Spanish pronunciation: [san ˈlwis potoˈsi]) is one of the 32 federal entities of Mexico, located in north-central Mexico. Its capital and largest city is San Luis Potosí. The state is bordered by Coahuila, Nuevo León, Tamaulipas, Veracruz, Hidalgo, Querétaro, Guanajuato, Jalisco and Zacatecas. It had a population of about 2.8 million at the 2020 census.

Geography and economy

The state spans the arid Mexican Plateau in the west, the Sierra Madre Oriental, and the lush tropical Huasteca region in the east, home to the Tamul and Micos waterfalls and the surrealist garden of Las Pozas at Xilitla. The state's name combines San Luis, for King Louis IX of France, and Potosí, a reference to the fabulously rich silver mines of Potosí in Bolivia, to which the region's gold and silver deposits were compared. Mining, manufacturing (including automotive plants of General Motors and BMW), agriculture and tourism drive the modern economy.

History

The area was inhabited by Chichimeca peoples, including the Guachichil, who fiercely resisted Spanish colonization during the Chichimeca War (1550–1590). The capital was founded in 1592 after gold and silver were discovered at Cerro de San Pedro. San Luis Potosí played roles in the Mexican War of Independence, the Reform War, and the Mexican Revolution — the Plan of San Luis Potosí, issued by Francisco I. Madero in 1910, called for the uprising that began the Revolution.
