Marie Smith Jones

Marie Smith Jones
Born: 14 May 1918, Cordova, Alaska
Died: 21 January 2008 (aged 89), Anchorage, Alaska
Eyak name: Udach' Kuqax*a'a'ch
Known for: Last native speaker of the Eyak language

Marie Smith Jones (14 May 1918 – 21 January 2008) was an Alaska Native activist and the last known native speaker of the Eyak language, as well as the last full-blooded Eyak. Her Eyak name, Udach' Kuqax*a'a'ch, means "a sound that calls people from afar."

Life and work

Jones was born in Cordova, Alaska, one of a small number of children raised speaking Eyak, a Na-Dené language of the Copper River delta region. As her siblings and other speakers died, she became, from the 1990s, the language's sole remaining native speaker. She worked for decades with linguist Michael Krauss of the University of Alaska Fairbanks, helping to compile an Eyak dictionary and grammar and to preserve texts and recordings for future generations.

Activism and legacy

A lifelong advocate for Alaska Native rights and environmental protection, Jones spoke publicly about language loss worldwide — the Eyak became a textbook example of language extinction. Although the language was declared extinct on her death, her documentation work made revival efforts possible, and learners have since worked with the archived materials; an Eyak language revitalisation programme continues in Cordova.
