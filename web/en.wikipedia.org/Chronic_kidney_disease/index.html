Chronic kidney disease

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is a type of long-term kidney disease, in which either there is a gradual loss of kidney function over a period of months to years, or an abnormal kidney structure. Initially generally no symptoms are seen, but later symptoms may include leg swelling, feeling tired, vomiting, loss of appetite, and confusion. Complications can relate to hormonal dysfunction of the kidneys and include high blood pressure, bone disease, and anemia. Additionally CKD patients have markedly increased cardiovascular complications with increased risks of death and hospitalization.

Causes and diagnosis

Causes that increase the risk of CKD include diabetes, high blood pressure, a family history of kidney failure, and older age. Other causes include glomerulonephritis, polycystic kidney disease, infections, and prolonged obstruction of the urinary tract. Diagnosis is by blood tests to measure the estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), and a urine test to measure albumin. CKD is classified into five stages (G1–G5) based on eGFR, with stage 5—kidney failure—being the most severe; the diagnosis requires abnormalities persisting for at least three months to distinguish it from acute kidney injury.

Treatment

Treatment aims to slow progression and manage complications: blood pressure control, especially with ACE inhibitors or angiotensin receptor blockers; blood sugar control in diabetes; dietary measures such as salt and, in some cases, protein restriction; treatment of anemia and mineral and bone disorders; and avoidance of nephrotoxic drugs. When kidney failure develops, treatment options are haemodialysis, peritoneal dialysis, kidney transplantation, or conservative care. Vaccination, smoking cessation, and weight management reduce cardiovascular risk.

Epidemiology

Chronic kidney disease affects roughly 10% of the world's population—more than 800 million people—and caused about 1.5 million deaths in 2019, making it one of the leading causes of death globally; its prevalence rises with age and with the global burden of diabetes and hypertension. The related condition chronic kidney disease of unknown etiology (CKDu) has severely affected agricultural workers in Central America and Sri Lanka.
