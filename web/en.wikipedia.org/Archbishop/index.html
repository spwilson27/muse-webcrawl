Archbishop

An archbishop is a bishop of higher rank or office. In many Christian denominations, including the Roman Catholic Church, Eastern Orthodox Church, Oriental Orthodox Churches, and Anglican Communion, archbishops oversee an archdiocese or hold a senior position within the church hierarchy.

Terminology and insignia

The term derives from the Greek archiepiskopos, meaning "chief overseer." In the Latin Church, a metropolitan archbishop is traditionally invested with the pallium, a band of white wool worn over the chasuble, symbolizing communion with the Pope and the shepherd's care for his flock. An archbishop's coat of arms is distinguished by a processional cross with two horizontal bars and a galero with ten tassels on each side.

Metropolitan and other archbishops

A metropolitan archbishop heads an ecclesiastical province and has limited supervisory authority over the suffragan dioceses within it. Other archbishops may be titular, holding the title of a defunct see while serving, for example, as papal nuncios; personal archbishops, granted the title ad personam; or primatial archbishops whose sees enjoy precedence within a nation.

Eastern Christianity

In Eastern Orthodoxy the rank of archbishop varies: in the Greek tradition it can denote the head of an autocephalous church, while in the Slavic tradition a metropolitan ranks above an archbishop. The Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople holds the title of archbishop of Constantinople, New Rome.

Anglican Communion

The Archbishop of Canterbury is the senior bishop of the Church of England and spiritual leader of the worldwide Anglican Communion; the Archbishop of York is the second-ranking. Other provinces of the Communion are likewise headed by archbishops or primates.
