5G

5G is the fifth generation of cellular network technology, standardised by the 3GPP as 5G NR (New Radio) and deployed commercially from 2019. It is designed to deliver much higher data rates, lower latency and greater connection density than its predecessor, 4G LTE.

Technology

5G operates in two frequency ranges: sub-6 GHz bands (FR1), which offer broad coverage, and millimetre-wave bands (FR2, roughly 24–40 GHz), which provide very high capacity over short distances. Key techniques include massive MIMO antenna arrays, beamforming, wider channel bandwidths and network slicing, which allows operators to create multiple virtual networks with different characteristics on one physical infrastructure. Target performance includes peak download rates of up to 10–20 Gbit/s and air-interface latency as low as one millisecond for ultra-reliable low-latency communication (URLLC).

Use cases

Beyond faster smartphones, 5G is intended to support the Internet of Things (massive machine-type communications), industrial automation, connected vehicles, telemedicine and fixed wireless access for home broadband. The ITU's IMT-2020 requirements define three pillars: enhanced mobile broadband (eMBB), URLLC and mMTC.

Deployment

South Korea and the United States launched the first commercial services in April 2019; China built the world's largest network, with millions of base stations. Deployment also became entangled in geopolitics, with several countries restricting equipment from Chinese vendor Huawei on security grounds.
