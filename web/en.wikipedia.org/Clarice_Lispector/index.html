Clarice Lispector

Born: 10 December 1920, Chechelnyk, Ukraine
Died: 9 December 1977, Rio de Janeiro, Brazil
Occupation: Novelist, short-story writer, journalist
Notable works: Near to the Wild Heart, The Passion According to G.H., The Hour of the Star

Clarice Lispector, regarded as one of Brazil's greatest writers.

Clarice Lispector (born Chaya Pinkhasivna Lispector; December 10, 1920 – December 9, 1977) was a Ukrainian-born Brazilian novelist and short-story writer. Acclaimed internationally for her innovative novels and stories, she is considered one of the most important literary figures of the 20th century in Brazil and a central figure in world modernism. Her work, characterised by interior monologue, epiphanic moments and the exploration of female consciousness, has been translated widely, and her reputation has grown substantially in the English-speaking world in recent decades.

Life

Lispector was born to a Jewish family in Chechelnyk, in present-day Ukraine, during the pogroms that followed the Russian Civil War; her family emigrated to Brazil in 1922, settling first in Maceió and then in Recife, where she grew up in poverty. She studied law in Rio de Janeiro and published her first novel, Near to the Wild Heart (1943), at the age of 23; its stream-of-consciousness technique drew comparisons with Joyce and Woolf and won the Graça Aranha Prize. After marrying a diplomat, she spent some fifteen years abroad, in Europe and the United States, before returning to Rio in 1959. She wrote newspaper columns (crônicas), children's books and translations alongside her fiction.

Major works

Her short-story collection Family Ties (1960) and the novel The Passion According to G.H. (1964) — in which a woman's encounter with a cockroach becomes a mystical and philosophical crisis — are regarded as masterpieces of introspective fiction. Água Viva (1973) pushed her style toward pure meditation, and her final novel, The Hour of the Star (1977), the story of the impoverished typist Macabéa, is her most widely read book in Brazil. She died of cancer in Rio de Janeiro on the eve of her 57th birthday.

Legacy

Lispector's work has been the subject of major biographies, notably Benjamin Moser's Why This World (2009), and of new English translations in the 2010s that consolidated her international reputation. See also Brazilian literature.
