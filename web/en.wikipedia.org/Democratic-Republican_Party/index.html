The Democratic-Republican Party was an American political party formed by Thomas Jefferson and James Madison in the early 1790s that championed republicanism, individual liberty, equal rights, decentralization, and states' rights, in opposition to the nationalist Federalist Party led by Alexander Hamilton. Historians often call it the Jeffersonian Republican Party.

The party grew out of opposition to Hamilton's financial program, including the assumption of state debts and the creation of the First Bank of the United States. It dominated American politics from Jefferson's election in 1800 — the "Revolution of 1800" — through the 1820s.

Presidents

Four consecutive Democratic-Republicans held the presidency: Thomas Jefferson (1801–1809), James Madison (1809–1817), James Monroe (1817–1825), and John Quincy Adams (1825–1829). Under Monroe, the collapse of the Federalists produced the one-party "Era of Good Feelings".

Split

The disputed election of 1824 shattered the party's unity. Followers of Andrew Jackson eventually formed the modern Democratic Party, while supporters of John Quincy Adams and Henry Clay became the National Republicans and later the Whigs.
