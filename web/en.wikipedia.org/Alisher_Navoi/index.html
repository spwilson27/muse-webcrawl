Alisher Navoi (9 February 1441 – 3 January 1501), also known as Nizām al-Dīn ʿAlī Shīr, was a Timurid poet, writer, statesman and patron of the arts, regarded as the greatest representative of Chagatai Turkic literature and a national hero of Uzbekistan. His work made Chagatai a major literary language, rivalling Persian.

Life

Born in Herat, then capital of the Timurid Empire, Navoi was educated alongside the future sultan Husayn Bayqara, whose court he served in high office, including keeper of the seal and governor of Astarabad. A fabulously wealthy man, he used his fortune to endow mosques, madrasas, libraries, hospitals and caravanserais — over 370 buildings are attributed to his patronage — and to support scholars, artists and musicians at the brilliant court of Herat.

Works

Writing under the pen name Navoi ("the melodious one") in Turkic and Fānī in Persian, he composed some 30 works. His masterpiece is the Khamsa (1483–85), a quintet of epic poems modelled on Nizami's, including Farhad and Shirin and Layli and Majnun. Four divans collect his Turkic lyric poetry, and the allegorical poem Lisan al-Tayr ("Language of the Birds") adapts Attar's Sufi classic. In Muhakamat al-Lughatayn ("Judgment of Two Languages", 1499) he argued the superiority of Turkic over Persian as a literary medium.

Legacy

Navoi's influence shaped Uzbek and wider Turkic literature for five centuries. The city of Navoiy in Uzbekistan, the national theatre and library, and numerous monuments bear his name.
