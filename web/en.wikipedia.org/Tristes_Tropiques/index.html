Tristes Tropiques

AuthorClaude Lévi-Strauss
Published1955 (Paris, Plon)
LanguageFrench
GenreMemoir, travel writing, anthropology

Tristes Tropiques ("Sad Tropics") is a memoir by the French anthropologist Claude Lévi-Strauss, published in 1955. Blending travel narrative, philosophical reflection and ethnography, it documents his expeditions in Brazil in the 1930s among the Caduveo, Bororo, Nambikwara and Tupi-Kawahib peoples, and helped establish his reputation and the structuralist movement in anthropology.

Content

The book famously opens: "I hate travelling and explorers." Lévi-Strauss recounts his departure from France, his academic posting in São Paulo, and journeys into the Brazilian interior, interspersed with reflections on the decline of indigenous cultures under the pressure of Western civilisation, the nature of myth, and the melancholy of a world whose diversity is being destroyed by monoculture. Its final chapters turn to Asia, Islam and Buddhism before a concluding meditation on humanity's place in nature.

Reception

Tristes Tropiques was an immediate literary sensation; the jury of the Prix Goncourt publicly regretted that, as a work of non-fiction, it was ineligible for the prize. Susan Sontag called it one of the great books of the century. Critics have praised its prose and its pioneering critique of colonialism and the ethnographer's own position, while some later anthropologists have questioned its pessimism and its portrayal of the Nambikwara. The English translation by John and Doreen Weightman appeared in 1973.
