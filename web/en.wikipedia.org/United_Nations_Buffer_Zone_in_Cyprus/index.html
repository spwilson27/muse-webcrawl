United Nations Buffer Zone in Cyprus

The United Nations Buffer Zone in Cyprus is a demilitarised zone, patrolled by the United Nations Peacekeeping Force in Cyprus (UNFICYP), that was established in 1964 and extended in 1974 after the Turkish invasion of Cyprus. It stretches for about 180 kilometres from Kokkina in the west to Famagusta in the east, separating the internationally recognised Republic of Cyprus from the Turkish Cypriot-administered north, and is popularly known as the "Green Line" because a British officer, Major-General Peter Young, first drew the ceasefire line on a map with a green chinagraph pencil in 1963.

Description

The zone varies in width from just 3.3 metres in central Nicosia — where it runs between the Greek Cypriot and Turkish Cypriot sectors of the divided capital — to 7.4 kilometres in the countryside. It covers roughly 3.3 per cent of the island's area, including the abandoned Nicosia International Airport and the eastern portion of the ghost resort town of Varosha. Around 10,000 people live in villages inside the zone, such as Pyla, the island's only remaining mixed Greek–Turkish Cypriot village.

History

UNFICYP was created in 1964 to prevent renewed intercommunal violence. After the Greek junta-backed coup and the Turkish military intervention of July–August 1974, the force supervised the new ceasefire lines, and the buffer zone took its present form. Crossings were closed for decades; the first, at the Ledra Palace Hotel, opened for pedestrians, and in 2008 the symbolic Ledra Street crossing in old Nicosia reopened. UNFICYP's mandate has been renewed by the UN Security Council at regular intervals ever since, making it one of the longest-running peacekeeping missions in the world.
