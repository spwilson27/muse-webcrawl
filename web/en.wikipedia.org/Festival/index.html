Festival

Festival
TypesReligious, seasonal, harvest, arts, music, film, food
EtymologyFrom Latin festivus, via Old French festival

A festival is an event celebrated by a community, centring on some characteristic aspect of that community and its traditions. Festivals often serve to fulfil specific communal purposes, especially with regard to commemoration or thanksgiving, and are frequently tied to religious observance, seasonal change, or harvest cycles. Many festivals have ancient origins and form part of the intangible cultural heritage of a society.

Types

Religious festivals mark sacred events or seasons: Christmas and Easter in Christianity, Ramadan and Eid in Islam, Diwali and Holi in Hinduism, Passover in Judaism, and Vesak in Buddhism. Seasonal and harvest festivals — such as the Mid-Autumn Festival, Oktoberfest, or Thanksgiving — celebrate agricultural cycles. Arts festivals showcase performance and creativity: the Edinburgh Festival Fringe, the Cannes Film Festival, and music festivals from Glastonbury to Coachella.

History

Festive gatherings date to the earliest agrarian societies, whose calendars of feasts regulated religious and agricultural life. In ancient Greece, festivals such as the Panathenaea and the Dionysia combined ritual, athletic competition, and theatre. Medieval Europe maintained an extensive calendar of saints' days, fairs, and carnivals, many of which survive in modern forms.

Cultural significance

Festivals reinforce communal identity, mark the passage of time, and license behaviour — feasting, dressing up, inversion of hierarchy — outside everyday norms. In the modern era they are also major economic events, driving tourism and the creative industries.
