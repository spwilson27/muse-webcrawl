Elephant Island

Elephant Island is an ice-covered, mountainous island off the coast of Antarctica in the outer reaches of the South Shetland Islands. It is uninhabited apart from visiting research and expedition parties.

Shackleton expedition

The island is famous as the refuge of Ernest Shackleton's Imperial Trans-Antarctic Expedition after its ship Endurance was crushed by ice in 1915. In April 1916 the crew reached Elephant Island; Shackleton and five companions sailed a small open boat, the James Caird, some 1,300 km to South Georgia to fetch rescue. The remaining 22 men survived at Point Wild under Frank Wild until they were rescued in August 1916 — all lived.

Geography and wildlife

The island's terrain offers little shelter and no natural harbour of consequence. Its shores host chinstrap penguins, gentoo penguins and seals, and a bust of the Chilean captain Luis Pardo at Point Wild commemorates the rescue.
