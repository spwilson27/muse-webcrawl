Moral relativism

[Image: Scales of justice]
FieldMeta-ethics
Key claimMoral judgements are true or false only relative to a standpoint
VariantsDescriptive, meta-ethical, normative
Opposed byMoral universalism, moral realism

Moral relativism is the family of philosophical views holding that moral judgements are true or false only relative to some particular standpoint — for instance, that of a culture or a historical period — and that no standpoint is uniquely privileged over all others. Relativists generally contend that there is no universal or absolute set of moral principles binding on all people.

Forms

Descriptive relativism

Descriptive relativism simply records the empirical fact that different cultures and societies hold differing moral beliefs and practices. It is an anthropological observation and does not by itself entail any normative conclusion.

Meta-ethical relativism

Meta-ethical relativism holds that the truth or falsity of a moral judgement is relative to the traditions, beliefs or practices of a group. On this view, "stealing is wrong" may be true in one culture and false in another, in much the way statements about what is "to the left" depend on a frame of reference.

Normative relativism

Normative relativism goes further, claiming that we ought to tolerate the behaviour of others even when it conflicts with our own moral standards, because no standard has authority beyond its own culture.

Criticism

Critics argue that moral relativism cannot condemn practices such as slavery or genocide when they are accepted within a culture, that it makes moral reformers by definition mistaken (since they oppose their own society's norms), and that normative relativism is self-undermining because its principle of tolerance is itself asserted universally.

History

Relativistic ideas appear in the ancient sophist Protagoras's dictum that "man is the measure of all things". In the modern era, anthropologists such as Franz Boas and Ruth Benedict documented moral diversity, and philosophers including Gilbert Harman and David Wong have defended sophisticated relativist theories.
