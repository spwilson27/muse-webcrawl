Iran–Iraq War

The Iran–Iraq War was an armed conflict between Iran and Iraq that began in September 1980 and ended in August 1988, making it the 20th century's longest conventional war. It began when Iraq, under President Saddam Hussein, invaded Iran following the Iranian Revolution, seeking to annex the oil-rich Khuzestan province and to contain the spread of revolutionary Shia Islam.

Causes

The war had its roots in a long history of border disputes, most notably over the Shatt al-Arab waterway, and in Saddam Hussein's fear that Ayatollah Khomeini's revolutionary government would incite Iraq's Shia majority against his Ba'athist regime. Iraq abrogated the 1975 Algiers Agreement and launched a full-scale invasion on 22 September 1980.

Course of the war

After initial Iraqi gains, Iran halted the advance and by 1982 had liberated most of its territory. Iran then rejected a ceasefire and carried the war into Iraq, launching offensives toward Basra. The conflict degenerated into trench warfare reminiscent of the First World War, with extensive use of chemical weapons by Iraq, human wave attacks by Iranian Basij volunteers, and the "War of the Cities" missile campaigns against civilian centres. The Tanker War in the Persian Gulf drew in the United States Navy, which escorted Kuwaiti tankers and clashed directly with Iranian forces in 1988.

Conclusion and consequences

On 20 August 1988 the war ended with a ceasefire under United Nations Security Council Resolution 598, restoring the status quo ante bellum. Casualty estimates range from several hundred thousand to over a million dead across both sides, with enormous economic damage. Iraq emerged heavily indebted, a factor in Saddam Hussein's 1990 invasion of Kuwait.
