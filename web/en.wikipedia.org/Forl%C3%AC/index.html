Forlì is a city and commune in Emilia-Romagna, Italy, and the capital of the province of Forlì-Cesena. It is situated on the Via Aemilia at the foot of the Apennines, near the Montone River, and has a population of about 117,000, making it a significant economic and cultural centre of the Romagna region.

History

The city was founded by the Romans in 188 BC as Forum Livii, named after the consul Marcus Livius Salinator, and it lay on the Via Aemilia connecting Rimini and Piacenza. In the Middle Ages Forlì was a contested prize between Guelphs and Ghibellines; it later came under the rule of the Ordelaffi family and, famously, of Caterina Sforza in the late fifteenth century, who defended the city's fortress against Cesare Borgia. Forlì subsequently formed part of the Papal States until Italian unification.

Landmarks

The city's monuments include the Abbey of San Mercuriale, with its tall Romanesque bell tower overlooking the central Piazza Saffi; the Rocca di Ravaldino fortress; and the cathedral, largely rebuilt after wartime damage. During the Fascist era, Forlì—near Benito Mussolini's birthplace of Predappio—received notable monumental buildings, including the Palazzo delle Poste and the war memorial in Piazza della Vittoria.

Economy

Forlì is a centre for agriculture, food processing, furniture manufacture, and engineering, and hosts a campus of the University of Bologna with schools of economics, engineering, political science, and translation.
