Aircraft flight dynamics

Flight dynamics is the science of air vehicle orientation and control in three dimensions. The three critical flight dynamics parameters are the angles of rotation in three dimensions about the vehicle's center of gravity, known as pitch, roll and yaw.

Axes and rotations

An aircraft in flight is free to rotate in three dimensions: yaw, nose left or right about an axis running up and down; pitch, nose up or down about an axis running from wing to wing; and roll, rotation about an axis running from nose to tail. These rotations are controlled respectively by the rudder, elevator, and ailerons. The orientation of the aircraft relative to its flight path is described by angles such as angle of attack and sideslip angle.

Stability and control

Flight dynamics analyzes static and dynamic stability — how an aircraft responds to disturbances. Longitudinal modes include the phugoid and short-period oscillations; lateral-directional modes include Dutch roll, spiral divergence, and roll subsidence. Control derivatives quantify the effect of control surface deflections on forces and moments, and the resulting equations of motion underpin flight simulator models and autopilot design.

Reference frames

Several frames are used: the body frame fixed to the aircraft, the stability frame aligned with the airflow, and the Earth-fixed (inertial) frame. Transformations between them use Euler angles or quaternions.
