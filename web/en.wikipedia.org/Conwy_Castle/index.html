Conwy Castle

Conwy Castle (Welsh: Castell Conwy) is a medieval fortress in Conwy, on the north coast of Wales. It was built by Edward I during his conquest of Wales between 1283 and 1289, to designs by the master mason James of St George, as part of a wider project that included the walled town of Conwy and a ring of castles intended to secure English control of the principality.

Architecture

The castle stands on a rocky ridge above the River Conwy. Its rectangular plan is divided into an outer and an inner ward, defended by eight massive towers and two barbicans. The inner ward contained the royal apartments, built for Edward and his queen Eleanor of Castile, including a great hall, chambers and a chapel — among the best-preserved medieval royal suites in Wales. Unlike Edward's concentric castles such as Harlech and Beaumaris, Conwy's defences follow the linear shape of its rock.

History

During the revolt of Madog ap Llywelyn in 1294–95, Edward was besieged in the castle and supplied by sea until relieved. In 1399 Richard II sheltered at Conwy before his capture by Henry Bolingbroke. Owain Glyndŵr's forces seized the castle by stealth in 1401. In the English Civil War it was held for Charles I, besieged and taken by Parliamentary forces in 1646, and later slighted. The ruin passed through private hands; the painterly ruin attracted artists including J. M. W. Turner.

Modern status

The castle is managed by Cadw and forms part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Castles and Town Walls of King Edward in Gwynedd", inscribed in 1986. It is one of the most visited historic attractions in Wales.
