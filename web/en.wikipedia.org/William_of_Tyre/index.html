William of Tyre

Bornc. 1130, Jerusalem
Died29 September 1186
OccupationArchbishop of Tyre; Chancellor of the Kingdom of Jerusalem; historian

William of Tyre (c. 1130 – 1186) was a medieval prelate and chronicler. As Archbishop of Tyre, he is sometimes known as the finest historian of the Crusades, and his account remains the principal narrative source for the 12th-century Kingdom of Jerusalem.

Life

William was born in Jerusalem to a family of settlers and educated there before spending roughly twenty years studying in France and Italy, at Paris and Bologna, under some of the leading scholars of the age. Returning east in 1165, he became a canon of Tyre cathedral, then archdeacon, and in 1175 Archbishop of Tyre. He served as chancellor of the kingdom and tutor to the young king Baldwin IV.

History

His great work, the Historia in 23 books, covers the First Crusade through to 1184 and is prized for its relatively measured judgements and its author's first-hand knowledge of the kingdom's politics. William attributed the kingdom's troubles in part to a generation born in the East, a thesis long debated. His description of the rise of Saladin and of the leper king Baldwin IV remains indispensable. He also wrote a history of the Islamic world, now lost.
