The 7.62×39mm (also called 7.62 Soviet or M43) is a rimless, bottlenecked, intermediate rifle cartridge developed in the Soviet Union during the Second World War. It is the cartridge of the AK-47 and its countless derivatives, making it one of the most widely used military rifle rounds in history.

History

Soviet designers studied the German 7.92×33mm Kurz and developed their own intermediate round, adopted in 1943–44 and first fielded in the SKS carbine. With the adoption of the AK-47 in 1949 the cartridge spread across the Warsaw Pact, China, and much of the developing world.

Characteristics

The steel-cased, tapered cartridge fires a bullet of about 7.9 grams at roughly 715 m/s. Its pronounced case taper aids reliable extraction in adverse conditions. Effective range is generally considered about 300–400 metres. The Soviet Union later supplemented it with the smaller, high-velocity 5.45×39mm, but 7.62×39mm remains in mass production worldwide for military, hunting, and sporting use.
