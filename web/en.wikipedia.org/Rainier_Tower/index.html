Rainier Tower

Rainier Tower's distinctive tapered pedestal in downtown Seattle.

Rainier Tower is a 41-story, 156-metre (512 ft) skyscraper in the Metropolitan Tract area of downtown Seattle, Washington. Completed in 1977 and designed by architect Minoru Yamasaki — who also designed the World Trade Center in New York — with NBBJ, it is famous for rising from a slender concrete pedestal that is only a fraction of the tower's floor width, creating the impression that the building floats or is balanced on a tapered stalk.

Design

The inverted-pyramid pedestal, about 37 metres tall, was intended to free up ground-level space for the Rainier Square retail plaza while still providing a large, secure base for the tower's core. The unusual form led locals to nickname it the "golf tee" building. The tower contains office space above the pedestrian plaza and shops.

Context

Rainier Tower was built for Rainier National Bank (later part of Bank of America). It stands adjacent to Yamasaki's earlier Rainier Bank Tower site and, since 2020, the much taller Rainier Square Tower, one of Seattle's tallest buildings, as part of the redeveloped Rainier Square complex.
