Björk

Björk Guðmundsdóttir (born 21 November 1965) is an Icelandic singer, songwriter, composer, record producer, and actress. Noted for her distinct voice, three-octave vocal range, and eclectic musical style, she has developed an avant-garde body of work over a four-decade career, drawing on electronic, pop, experimental, trip hop, classical, and avant-garde music. She rose to prominence as lead singer of the alternative rock band the Sugarcubes before achieving international solo success.

Career

Björk released a first album as a child in 1977, then passed through punk and post-punk bands including Tappi Tíkarrass and Kukl before co-founding the Sugarcubes in 1986, whose single "Birthday" brought international attention. Her adult solo Debut (1993) launched a string of influential albums: Post (1995), Homogenic (1997), Vespertine (2001), Medúlla (2004), Volta (2007), Biophilia (2011, released with a suite of apps), Vulnicura (2015), Utopia (2017) and Fossora (2022). Hit singles include "It's Oh So Quiet", "Army of Me" and "Hyperballad".

Acting

She starred in Lars von Trier's Dancer in the Dark (2000), winning the Best Actress award at the Cannes Film Festival; her song "I've Seen It All" was nominated for an Academy Award, and her swan dress worn at the 2001 Oscars became an iconic image.

Legacy

Björk is widely regarded as one of the most innovative figures in popular music, recognized for integrating technology, nature imagery and visual art into her work. She has sold tens of millions of records worldwide and received numerous awards, including multiple BRIT Awards and Grammy nominations.
