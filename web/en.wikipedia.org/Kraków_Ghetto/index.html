Kraków Ghetto

LocationPodgórze, Kraków, German-occupied Poland
OperatedMarch 1941 – March 1943
PopulationUp to about 20,000 Jews

The Kraków Ghetto was one of five major metropolitan Jewish ghettos created by Nazi Germany in the General Government territory of occupied Poland during the Holocaust in World War II. It was established for the purpose of exploitation, terror and persecution of Polish Jews, and was a staging point for deportations to extermination camps.

History

The ghetto was formally established on 3 March 1941 in the Podgórze district, across the Vistula from the historic Jewish quarter of Kazimierz. Some 15,000–20,000 Jews were crowded into an area previously housing about 3,000 people, enclosed by a wall whose tops were shaped like Jewish tombstones. Within the ghetto stood the pharmacy of Tadeusz Pankiewicz, "Under the Eagle", a centre of aid and resistance communication, and nearby was Oskar Schindler's enamelware factory (Emalia).

Liquidation

Deportations to Bełżec and Auschwitz began in 1942. The ghetto was liquidated on 13–14 March 1943 under SS-Hauptsturmführer Amon Göth: about 8,000 remaining Jews were sent to the Kraków-Płaszów concentration camp, some 2,000 were deported to Auschwitz, and others were murdered in the streets of the ghetto.
