Nordic Council

Nordic Council
Founded: 1952
Members: Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway, Sweden, plus Åland, Faroe Islands, Greenland
Seat: Copenhagen, Denmark

The Nordic Council is the official body for formal inter-parliamentary co-operation among the Nordic countries. Formed in 1952, it has 87 representatives from Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway, and Sweden as well as from the autonomous territories of the Faroe Islands, Greenland, and Åland. The representatives are members of parliament in their respective countries and are elected by those parliaments.

Work

The Council issues recommendations to the Nordic Council of Ministers, the intergovernmental forum formed in 1971. Nordic co-operation underpins the Nordic Passport Union, a common labour market, and joint initiatives in culture, environment and welfare. The Council awards prominent prizes in literature, music, film, children's literature and the environment.

History

Founded after the failure of Scandinavian defence union talks, the Council's first session met in Copenhagen in 1953. Finland joined in 1955. Despite members' differing affiliations — EU membership for Denmark, Finland and Sweden; NATO membership changes after 2022 — the Council remains the parliamentary anchor of the world's oldest regional co-operation of its kind.
