Muzzleloader

A muzzleloader is any firearm in which the user loads the projectile and the propellant charge into the muzzle end of the gun (from the forward, open end of the gun's barrel). This is distinct from the modern designs of breech-loading firearms, in which user loads the weapon at the breech. The term applies to both rifled and smoothbore weapons, from hand cannons to modern in-line hunting rifles.

Loading procedure

Traditional loading involves measuring a charge of black powder (or a substitute) into the muzzle, seating a wad or patch, ramming the projectile home with a ramrod, and priming the ignition system — a percussion cap, a charge in a flintlock pan, or a match for a matchlock. Because each shot must be loaded from the front, muzzleloaders have a slow rate of fire compared with breechloaders.

History

Muzzleloading weapons dominated warfare and hunting from the fourteenth century until the mid-nineteenth century, when breech-loading rifles, metallic cartridges, and repeating arms displaced them. Famous muzzleloading arms include the Brown Bess musket, the Kentucky long rifle, and the Pattern 1853 Enfield rifle-musket.

Modern use

Today muzzleloaders are used mainly for hunting — many jurisdictions hold special primitive-weapons seasons — as well as historical reenactment and competitive target shooting. Modern in-line designs use shotgun primers and pelletized powder while retaining muzzle loading.
