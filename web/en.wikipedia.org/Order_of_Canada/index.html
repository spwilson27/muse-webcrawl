Order of Canada

The Order of Canada (French: Ordre du Canada) is a Canadian national order and the second-highest honour for merit in the system of orders, decorations and medals of Canada, after the Order of Merit. It recognises outstanding achievement, dedication to the community and service to the nation.

SovereignMonarch of Canada

MottoDesiderantes meliorem patriam ("They desire a better country")

History

The order was instituted in 1967, Canada's centennial year, by Queen Elizabeth II on the advice of Prime Minister Lester B. Pearson, as Canada moved to replace British honours with its own system. The first appointments were announced on 1 July 1967, with Pearson among the inaugural Companions.

Grades and insignia

There are three grades: Companion (C.C.), the highest, limited in number; Officer (O.C.); and Member (C.M.). The insignia is a stylised white snowflake with a red maple leaf at its centre — gold for Companions, silver for Officers and Members. Appointments are made by the Governor General on the recommendation of an advisory council.

Eligibility

Canadian citizens are eligible; honorary appointments may be made to non-Canadians. Sitting politicians and judges are generally not appointed for their official work. More than 7,000 people have been invested into the order since its creation.
