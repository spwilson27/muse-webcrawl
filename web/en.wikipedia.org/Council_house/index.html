Council house

Council house
TypePublic housing (United Kingdom)
ProvidersLocal authorities; housing associations
Key legislationHousing Act 1919; Housing Act 1980 (Right to Buy)

A council house is a form of British public housing built by local authorities. A council house is part of a council estate — a housing development of houses and flats built and owned by a local council, let to tenants at below-market rents, typically on the basis of housing need.

History

Large-scale council housing began with the Housing, Town Planning, &c. Act 1919, promoted by David Lloyd George's promise of "homes fit for heroes" after the First World War. Building expanded greatly after the Second World War, when councils rebuilt bombed cities and cleared slums; at its peak in the 1950s, over 300,000 homes a year were being completed in Britain, many of them council homes. By the late 1970s roughly a third of British households lived in council housing.

Right to Buy

The Housing Act 1980 introduced the Right to Buy, allowing tenants to purchase their council homes at a discount. More than two million homes were sold, and because councils were largely prevented from using receipts to build replacements, the council stock shrank sharply. Much remaining stock was later transferred to housing associations.
