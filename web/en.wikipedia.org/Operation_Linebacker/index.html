Operation Linebacker was the codename for a United States air interdiction campaign conducted against North Vietnam from 9 May to 23 October 1972, during the Vietnam War. It was launched in response to North Vietnam's Easter Offensive, a massive conventional invasion of South Vietnam begun on 30 March 1972, and was the first sustained bombing of North Vietnam since President Lyndon Johnson halted bombing in November 1968.

Background

President Richard Nixon sought to blunt the Easter Offensive and coerce Hanoi back to negotiations by unleashing air power with far fewer restrictions than had applied during Operation Rolling Thunder (1965–1968). Unlike its predecessor, Linebacker had clear military objectives, targeted North Vietnam's war-making logistics — bridges, rail yards, petroleum storage, power plants, and supply lines from China — and made extensive use of precision-guided munitions.

The campaign

The operation opened with Operation Pocket Money on 9 May 1972, in which US Navy A-6 and A-7 aircraft mined Haiphong and other North Vietnamese harbours, effectively blockading imports by sea. Strike aircraft then systematically destroyed key infrastructure: the Paul Doumer Bridge in Hanoi and the Thanh Hóa Bridge — which had survived years of earlier attacks — were dropped by laser-guided bombs in May 1972. US forces flew roughly 41,000 sorties during the campaign and dropped about 155,000 tons of bombs; North Vietnamese air defences, built around SA-2 missiles, MiG fighters, and dense anti-aircraft artillery, shot down dozens of US aircraft.

Effects and Linebacker II

Linebacker badly strained North Vietnam's logistics, contributed to the failure of the Easter Offensive to achieve decisive gains, and helped bring Hanoi back to the Paris negotiations, which produced a near-agreement in October 1972. When talks broke down in December, Nixon ordered Operation Linebacker II (18–29 December 1972) — the intense "Christmas bombing" by B-52s — after which the Paris Peace Accords were signed in January 1973, ending direct US involvement in the war.
