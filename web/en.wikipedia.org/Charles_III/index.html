Charles III (Charles Philip Arthur George; born 14 November 1948) is King of the United Kingdom and the 14 other Commonwealth realms. He acceded to the throne on the death of his mother, Elizabeth II, on 8 September 2022, having been heir apparent and Prince of Wales for over 70 years — the longest-serving heir and Prince of Wales in British history. At his accession he was the oldest person ever to assume the British throne.

Early life and education

Charles was born at Buckingham Palace, the eldest child of Princess Elizabeth and Philip, Duke of Edinburgh. He attended Hill House and Cheam schools, then Gordonstoun in Scotland, spending two terms at Timbertop in Australia, an experience he later spoke of positively. He read archaeology, anthropology, and history at Trinity College, Cambridge, taking a 2:2 degree in 1970 — the first heir apparent to earn a university degree. He also studied Welsh at Aberystwyth ahead of his investiture as Prince of Wales at Caernarfon Castle in 1969. From 1971 to 1976 he served in the Royal Navy, commanding the coastal minehunter HMS Bronington.

Prince of Wales

As Prince of Wales, Charles undertook official duties across the Commonwealth and pursued wide-ranging charitable and campaigning interests. In 1976 he founded The Prince's Trust, which has helped over a million disadvantaged young people into work and education. He became known — sometimes controversially — for advocacy on environmental protection, sustainable farming and organic agriculture (including his Duchy Originals brand and the village of Poundbury), for criticism of modernist architecture, and for an interest in complementary medicine and interfaith dialogue. His private letters to government ministers (the "black spider memos") prompted litigation over their release.

In 1981 Charles married Lady Diana Spencer in St Paul's Cathedral; the "fairytale wedding" was watched worldwide. The couple had two sons, William (born 1982) and Harry (born 1984), but the marriage broke down publicly and they divorced in 1996. Diana died in a car crash in Paris the following year. In 2005 Charles married Camilla Parker Bowles in a civil ceremony at Windsor.

Reign

Charles was proclaimed king by the Accession Council on 10 September 2022. His coronation, shared with Queen Camilla, took place at Westminster Abbey on 6 May 2023, in a scaled-back service. Early in his reign he combined continuity with signals of a "slimmed-down" monarchy, undertaking state visits to Germany and France and receiving heads of state. In February 2024 Buckingham Palace announced that the King had been diagnosed with a form of cancer (discovered during treatment for benign prostate enlargement; not prostate cancer) and that he would step back from public-facing duties during treatment, though he continued state business and returned to public engagements in spring 2024.

As head of the Commonwealth, Charles is sovereign of 15 realms, though republican debates continue in several Caribbean and Pacific realms.
