Neutron cross section

The neutron cross section of a nuclide quantifies the probability that a neutron will interact with an atomic nucleus of that nuclide. It is expressed as an effective area, conventionally measured in barns (1 barn = 10−28 m²), and depends strongly on both the target nuclide and the neutron's energy.

Types of interaction

Cross sections are defined for specific reactions: scattering (elastic and inelastic), capture (radiative capture, (n,γ)), fission, and particle-producing reactions such as (n,p) and (n,α). The total cross section is the sum over all possible interactions. Slow (thermal) neutrons often have much larger capture and fission cross sections than fast neutrons — for example, uranium-235's fission cross section is about 585 barns at thermal energies but only about 1 barn for fast neutrons.

Applications

Tabulated neutron cross sections (libraries such as ENDF) are fundamental to nuclear reactor design, radiation shielding, neutron activation analysis and nuclear medicine.
