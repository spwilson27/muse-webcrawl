Luigi is a fictional character featured in video games and related media released by Nintendo. Created by Japanese video game designer Shigeru Miyamoto, Luigi is portrayed as the younger fraternal twin brother and sidekick of Mario. He first appeared in the 1983 arcade game Mario Bros. as the character controlled by the second player, originally distinguished from Mario only by a palette swap.

Characteristics

Luigi is taller and thinner than Mario, wears green overalls and a green cap bearing the letter "L", and is characterised as kind-hearted but timid and easily frightened. His higher jump and lower traction became staples of his gameplay identity beginning with the Japanese Super Mario Bros. 2 (1988), released in the West as Super Mario Bros.: The Lost Levels, and the international Super Mario Bros. 2.

Appearances

Although long cast in a supporting role in the Super Mario series, Luigi headlines his own games, most notably the Luigi's Mansion series (beginning 2001), in which he explores haunted mansions and captures ghosts with the Poltergust vacuum devised by Professor E. Gadd. He is a regular playable character in spin-off series including Mario Kart, Mario Party, and Super Smash Bros. Nintendo designated 2013 the "Year of Luigi" to mark the thirtieth anniversary of the character's debut.

In The Super Mario Bros. Movie (2023), Luigi was voiced by Charlie Day. The character is frequently parodied and affectionately celebrated in fan culture as an underdog counterpart to his more famous brother.
