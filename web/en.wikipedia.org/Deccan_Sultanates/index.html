Deccan Sultanates


Golconda Fort

The Deccan Sultanates were five late-medieval Indian Muslim kingdoms on the Deccan Plateau: Ahmadnagar, Berar, Bidar, Bijapur, and Golconda. They emerged in the late 15th and early 16th centuries from the disintegration of the Bahmani Sultanate and were notable for the syncretic Indo-Persian Deccani culture they fostered.

History

As Bahmani power waned after 1480, provincial governors carved out independent kingdoms: the Nizam Shahi dynasty at Ahmadnagar, the Adil Shahis at Bijapur, the Qutb Shahis at Golconda, the Barid Shahis at Bidar, and the Imad Shahis at Berar. The sultanates frequently warred among themselves but combined in 1565 to defeat the Vijayanagara Empire at the Battle of Talikota. One by one they were absorbed by the Mughal Empire in the 17th century, culminating in Aurangzeb's conquests of Bijapur (1686) and Golconda (1687).

Culture

The sultanates patronized architecture, painting, and literature, including the Dakhini language. Landmarks include the Gol Gumbaz at Bijapur, one of the largest domes of the pre-modern world, and the Charminar in Hyderabad, founded by the Qutb Shahis in 1591.
