Scaling law

A scaling law is a functional relationship in which one quantity varies as a power of another, f(x) = a xk, so that the relationship is invariant under changes of scale. Scaling laws appear throughout physics, biology, geology, economics and computer science, and on a log–log plot they appear as straight lines.

A power-law scaling relation on logarithmic axes

Examples

Classic examples include the square–cube law described by Galileo, relating surface area and volume; Kepler's third law of planetary motion; Kleiber's law, by which animals' metabolic rates scale with body mass to roughly the three-quarters power; and the Gutenberg–Richter law for earthquake frequencies. In physics, scaling and critical exponents organise the behaviour of systems near phase transitions.

Neural scaling laws

In machine learning, "neural scaling laws" describe how the performance of language models improves predictably as power laws of model size, dataset size and training compute. Work published in 2020 showed smooth power-law improvements over many orders of magnitude, and the 2022 "Chinchilla" results refined the trade-off between parameters and training tokens, shaping how large models are built.
