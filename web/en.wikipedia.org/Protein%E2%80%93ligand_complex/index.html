Protein–ligand complex

A protein–ligand complex is a non-covalent association between a protein and a smaller molecule (its ligand), such as a substrate, inhibitor, metabolite, ion, or drug. Formation of the complex is the molecular event underlying enzyme catalysis, receptor signaling, transport, and most drug action.

Binding

Ligand binding typically occurs at a defined binding site or pocket on the protein and is driven by hydrogen bonds, ionic interactions, van der Waals forces, and the hydrophobic effect. Affinity is quantified by the dissociation constant (Kd); binding may involve conformational change (induced fit) or selection among pre-existing conformations. Cooperative systems, such as hemoglobin binding oxygen, show sigmoidal binding curves described by the Hill equation.

Study methods

Complexes are characterized structurally by X-ray crystallography, NMR spectroscopy, and cryo-electron microscopy, and thermodynamically by isothermal titration calorimetry and surface plasmon resonance. Computational docking predicts complex geometry and is a mainstay of structure-based drug design.
