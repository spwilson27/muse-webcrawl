Macaronesia

Macaronesia is a biogeographical region in the North Atlantic Ocean comprising four volcanic archipelagos off the coasts of Europe and Africa: the Azores and Madeira (Portugal), the Canary Islands (Spain), and Cabo Verde. The name derives from the Greek makárōn nêsoi, "Islands of the Blessed" or "Fortunate Isles", a term used by ancient geographers for islands west of the Strait of Gibraltar.

Geography and climate

The archipelagos are all of volcanic origin but geologically diverse: the Azores straddle the Mid-Atlantic Ridge, while the Canaries and Cabo Verde lie off the African coast. Climates range from the humid, temperate Azores to the arid, trade-wind-cooled Canaries and tropical Cabo Verde. Elevation varies enormously, from sea level to Mount Teide on Tenerife (3,715 m), Spain's highest peak.

Flora and fauna

Macaronesia is recognised as a biodiversity hotspot with very high endemism. Its laurel forests (laurisilva), remnants of Tertiary subtropical forests once widespread around the Mediterranean, survive in Madeira, the Azores, and the Canaries and are protected as UNESCO World Heritage Sites. Characteristic flora include the dragon tree, Canary Island pine, and many endemic species of genera such as Aeonium and Echium.

Status

Politically, the Azores and Madeira are autonomous regions of Portugal and outermost regions of the European Union, as are the Canary Islands for Spain; Cabo Verde is an independent republic. Macaronesia should not be confused with Micronesia in the Pacific.
