Charles Dow

Charles Henry Dow (November 6, 1851 – December 4, 1902) was an American journalist and economist who co-founded Dow Jones & Company and The Wall Street Journal. He created the Dow Jones Industrial Average and laid the foundations of modern technical analysis through the editorials later systematised as Dow theory.

Career

Dow was born in Sterling, Connecticut, and worked as a reporter in New England before coming to New York, where he specialised in financial news for the Kiernan News Agency. In 1882 he founded Dow Jones & Company with Edward Jones and Charles Bergstresser, publishing the hand-delivered Customers' Afternoon Letter. In 1889 the firm launched The Wall Street Journal, with Dow as its first editor.

Stock averages

To give readers a simple measure of the market's direction, Dow compiled an average of leading railway stocks in 1884 and in 1896 created the Dow Jones Industrial Average, initially of twelve industrial companies. He believed the averages should confirm each other and that price movements reflected all available information.

Dow theory

In a series of editorials written between 1900 and 1902, Dow argued that the market moves in primary trends punctuated by secondary reactions, that trends proceed through phases of accumulation, public participation and distribution, and that trends persist until clear reversal signals appear. His ideas were collected and named "Dow theory" by later writers such as William Peter Hamilton and Robert Rhea.
