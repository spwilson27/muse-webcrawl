Artificial neural network

An artificial neural network (ANN) is a computational model inspired by the structure and function of biological neural networks in animal brains. It consists of connected units called artificial neurons, which model the neurons in a brain. These are connected by edges, which model the synapses. Artificial neurons receive signals from connected neurons, process them, and send a signal to other connected neurons. The output of each neuron is computed by a non-linear function of the sum of its inputs, called the activation function. The strength of the signal at each connection is determined by a weight, which adjusts during the learning process.

Typically, neurons are aggregated into layers. Different layers may perform different transformations on their inputs. Signals travel from the first layer (the input layer) to the last layer (the output layer), possibly passing through multiple intermediate (hidden) layers. Neural networks are a central technology of modern artificial intelligence and machine learning, with applications in computer vision, speech recognition, machine translation, game playing, and medical diagnosis.

History

The foundations of neural networks were laid in 1943, when Warren McCulloch and Walter Pitts created a computational model for neural networks based on mathematics and algorithms. Their model, the threshold logic unit, is regarded as the first work in the field of artificial intelligence. In 1958, psychologist Frank Rosenblatt invented the perceptron, a single-layer neural network capable of learning simple patterns, which generated great excitement. However, in 1969 Marvin Minsky and Seymour Papert showed that single-layer perceptrons were incapable of processing the exclusive-or (XOR) circuit, contributing to a decline in research funding known as the "AI winter".

Interest revived in the 1980s with the popularisation of the backpropagation algorithm for training multi-layer networks, notably through the 1986 paper by David Rumelhart, Geoffrey Hinton and Ronald Williams. The development of convolutional neural networks (building on Kunihiko Fukushima's neocognitron of 1980) and long short-term memory networks in the 1990s further expanded capabilities. From around 2012, deep learning networks trained on large datasets with graphics processing units achieved breakthrough results, beginning with AlexNet's victory in the ImageNet competition.

Models

An artificial neural network is characterised by its architecture (the number of neurons and layers and how they connect), its activation functions, and its learning algorithm. Neurons compute a weighted sum of their inputs plus a bias term, then apply an activation function such as the sigmoid function, the hyperbolic tangent, or the rectified linear unit (ReLU). The network's behaviour is determined by its weights; learning consists of adjusting these weights to reduce the difference between the network's outputs and desired outputs, as measured by a loss function.

Learning

The most common form of learning is supervised learning, in which the network is trained on labelled examples. Errors are propagated backwards through the network using backpropagation, and weights are updated by gradient descent or one of its variants, such as stochastic gradient descent or Adam. Other paradigms include unsupervised learning, where the network finds structure in unlabelled data, and reinforcement learning, where an agent learns from rewards and penalties.

Key practical concerns include overfitting (when a network memorises training data rather than generalising), addressed by techniques such as regularisation, dropout, and early stopping, and the choice of hyperparameters such as the learning rate.

Types

Major types of neural networks include feedforward neural networks, convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for grid-like data such as images, recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and long short-term memory (LSTM) networks for sequences, generative adversarial networks (GANs), and transformers, which underpin large language models.

Applications

Neural networks are used in image and speech recognition, natural language processing, recommender systems, autonomous vehicles, drug discovery, financial modelling, and scientific research. Their rapid advance since the 2010s has made them one of the most influential technologies of the twenty-first century.
