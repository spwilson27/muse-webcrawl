The Eagle Forum is a conservative political advocacy organisation in the United States, founded in 1972 by activist and author Phyllis Schlafly. It advocates conservative positions on issues such as national sovereignty, constitutional government, education, immigration, and family policy, and it publishes the Phyllis Schlafly Report (formerly the Eagle Forum Report).

History

Schlafly founded the organisation as the Eagle Forum (the eagle symbolising national defence) after her campaigns against the Equal Rights Amendment (ERA). The organisation's predecessor, the National Committee to Defend America, had mobilised grassroots opposition; Eagle Forum grew out of the "STOP ERA" network of state-level volunteers. Schlafly led the organisation until her death in 2016, when her daughter-in-law Anne Schlafly Cori and later Ed Martin successively took leadership roles, and internal disputes over direction led to rival claims to the Eagle Forum name and archives.

Activities and positions

Eagle Forum's most famous campaign was the successful grassroots mobilisation that prevented ratification of the Equal Rights Amendment before the 1982 deadline, arguing that the amendment would harm traditional family structures and subject women to the military draft. The organisation also opposed the Immigration Reform and Control Act of 1986's amnesty provisions, supported English-only policies, promoted "phonics"-based reading instruction in public schools, and campaigned against international treaties it viewed as threats to American sovereignty, including the Convention on the Rights of the Child and the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea.

Organisation

Eagle Forum is headquartered in Alton, Illinois, with chapters in many states. It conducts lobbying, voter education, and its annual Eagle Council leadership conference. Its publications include the monthly Phyllis Schlafly Report and educational pamphlets on legislation.
