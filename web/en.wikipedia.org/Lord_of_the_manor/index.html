Lord of the manor

A lord of the manor in England and Wales is the holder of a manorial lordship, a form of landholding under the feudal manorial system. The lord held the manor — an estate with land, rights over tenants, and a manorial court — originally in return for service to a greater lord or the Crown.

Manorial system

From the Norman Conquest, land was organised into manors. Tenants held land by various tenures, including copyhold, owing rents and services; the lord's demesne was worked for his own benefit. The lord presided over the court baron and court leet, regulating agriculture, transfers of land and minor offences.

Modern status

Copyhold was abolished by the Law of Property Act 1922 (effective 1926), and most manorial incidents were extinguished, but lordships of the manor survive as incorporeal property that can be bought and sold. The title is a form of property, not a title of nobility, and confers no seat in the House of Lords. Manorial rights such as minerals and sporting rights had to be registered with the Land Registry by October 2013 to bind purchasers in overriding-interest cases.
