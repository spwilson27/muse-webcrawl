The Worshipful Company of Stationers and Newspaper Makers (usually shortened to the Stationers' Company) is one of the livery companies of the City of London. Historically the guild of the book trade — the scribes, illuminators, booksellers, and later printers and publishers of London — it held a near-monopoly over printing in England for centuries and administered the first register of copyrights.

History

The guild of stationers (so called because they sold from fixed "stations" around St Paul's Cathedral) was formed in 1403 and received a royal charter from Queen Mary I in 1557, granting it a monopoly on printing and the power to search out and burn unlicensed books. The company's register, the Stationers' Register, recorded members' rights in copies; entries for works by William Shakespeare and his contemporaries make it a key source for literary history, and its system underpinned the development of copyright, eventually codified in the Statute of Anne (1710).

Modern company

In 1934 the company merged with the Newspaper Makers' Company, and today its members come from the communications and content industries — publishing, printing, journalism, packaging, and digital media. Located at Stationers' Hall in Ave Maria Lane, the Grade I listed hall (rebuilt after the Great Fire of London) hosts company ceremonies and events. The company supports education, including Stationers' Crown Woods Academy and a bursary scheme for postgraduate publishing students, and ranks 47th in the order of precedence of the livery companies.
