Graceful degradation

Graceful degradation is the ability of a computer, machine, electronic system, or network to maintain limited functionality even when a large portion of it has been destroyed or rendered inoperative. The purpose of graceful degradation is to prevent catastrophic failure. It is a property associated with fault tolerance: the system continues to operate, at reduced capability, in proportion to the surviving components.

Examples

In mechanical engineering, an example is a structure that loses strength gradually as components fail rather than collapsing suddenly. In computing, a website designed with graceful degradation presents its full features in modern browsers while remaining usable — core content accessible, forms functional — in older browsers or with JavaScript disabled. Load shedding by power grids and websites during traffic spikes is another example: a service may disable non-essential features to keep the essential ones running.

Graceful degradation vs. progressive enhancement

In web design, graceful degradation is often contrasted with progressive enhancement. Graceful degradation starts with a full-featured site designed for the newest browsers and then adapts it so it still works acceptably in older environments. Progressive enhancement takes the opposite approach: it begins with a baseline of essential content and functionality that works everywhere, then adds layers of presentation and behaviour for browsers that support them. Both aim at broad accessibility, differing mainly in design workflow and emphasis.

Related concepts

The concept is related to fault tolerance, fail-safe and fail-soft design, resilience engineering, and "graceful degradation" in telecommunications, where a signal's quality falls off gradually rather than cutting out entirely — as in analogue versus early digital radio reception.
