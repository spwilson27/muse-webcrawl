Spartacus (ballet)

Spartacus (Russian: «Спартак», Spartak) is a ballet by Aram Khachaturian (1903–1978). The work follows the exploits of Spartacus, the leader of the slave uprising against the Romans known as the Third Servile War, although the ballet's storyline takes considerable licence with historical record. Khachaturian composed Spartacus in 1954, and was awarded a Lenin Prize for the composition that same year.

History

The ballet was first staged in Leningrad on 27 December 1956 by the Kirov Ballet, choreographed by Leonid Yakobson, with a libretto by Nikolai Volkov based on the 1874 novel by Raffaello Giovagnoli. It was revised and restaged at the Bolshoi Theatre in Moscow in 1968 in the famous production choreographed by Yury Grigorovich, which became the definitive version and a signature work of the Bolshoi, celebrating virtuoso male dancing.

Synopsis

The principal characters are Spartacus, a Thracian slave; his wife Phrygia; Crassus, the Roman commander; and Aegina, Crassus's courtesan mistress. Captured and enslaved, Spartacus leads the gladiators in revolt, builds an army of escaped slaves, and wins victories over the Roman legions, but internal divisions, the treachery of Aegina, and the might of Crassus lead to the uprising's defeat. Spartacus dies in the final battle, and Phrygia mourns over his body — the ballet's celebrated closing requiem.

Music

The score is among Khachaturian's best known, especially the lyrical "Adagio of Spartacus and Phrygia" from Act III, which was used as the theme music for the BBC drama series The Onedin Line (1971–80) and entered popular culture in the UK. Music from the ballet also appears in the 2006 animated film Ice Age: The Meltdown and the Coen brothers' The Hudsucker Proxy (1994).
