7th Cavalry Regiment

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The 7th Cavalry Regiment is a United States Army cavalry regiment formed in 1866. Its official nickname is "Garryowen", after the Irish air of the same name adopted as the regiment's marching song. The regiment is most famous for its role in the American Indian Wars, particularly the destruction of Lieutenant Colonel George Armstrong Custer's battalion at the Battle of the Little Bighorn in 1876.

American Indian Wars

Organised at Fort Riley, Kansas, in 1866, the regiment campaigned on the Great Plains. Under Custer, it attacked Black Kettle's Cheyenne village at the Battle of Washita River in 1868. On 25 June 1876, during the Great Sioux War, Custer and five companies—about 210 men—were annihilated at the Little Bighorn in Montana by Lakota, Northern Cheyenne and Arapaho warriors. Companies under Major Marcus Reno and Captain Frederick Benteen survived a two-day siege on the bluffs above the river.

Later service

The regiment fought in the Spanish–American War's aftermath in the Philippines, patrolled the Mexican border, and served in the Second World War in the Pacific as infantry of the 1st Cavalry Division. In the Korean War, the regiment was implicated in the No Gun Ri massacre of South Korean refugees in July 1950. Reorganised as an armoured cavalry and later infantry formation, squadrons of the 7th Cavalry served in Vietnam—including at the Battle of Ia Drang in 1965—and in the Iraq War.

Legacy

The regiment's history has been widely depicted in film and literature, from They Died with Their Boots On (1941) to We Were Soldiers (2002), which portrays the 1st Battalion, 7th Cavalry at Ia Drang.
