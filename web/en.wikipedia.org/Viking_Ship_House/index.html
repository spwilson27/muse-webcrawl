Viking Ship House

The Viking Ship House can refer to buildings shaped like or housing Viking ships. The best-known examples are the Viking Ship House (Vikingskipshuset) at Bygdøy in Oslo, Norway — now part of the Museum of Cultural History, University of Oslo — built to house the Oseberg, Gokstad and Tune ships, and various ship-shaped houses elsewhere.

Oslo Viking Ship House

Designed by architect Arnstein Arneberg, the Oslo building's first wing for the Oseberg ship opened in 1926, with wings for the Gokstad ship and Tune ship completed in the following years. The three burial ships, excavated from mounds around the Oslo Fjord in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, are among the finest surviving Viking Age artefacts.

The museum closed for a complete rebuilding and is being transformed into the new Museum of the Viking Age, planned to reopen in the mid-2020s with greatly expanded exhibition space.
