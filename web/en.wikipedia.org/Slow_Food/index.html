Slow Food is an international movement and organisation that promotes local food traditions, gastronomic pleasure and a slower pace of life, in opposition to the standardisation associated with fast food. It campaigns for food that is "good, clean and fair": tasty and culturally rooted, produced without harming the environment, and accessible at fair prices to consumers and producers alike.

History

The movement began in 1986 in Italy, when journalist Carlo Petrini helped organise a protest against a planned McDonald's restaurant near the Spanish Steps in Rome. Slow Food was formally founded in Paris in 1989 with the signing of its manifesto. Its headquarters are in Bra, Piedmont, Petrini's home town.

Activities

Slow Food maintains the Ark of Taste, a catalogue of endangered heritage foods, and supports Presidia — projects that help small producers of threatened foods survive. Its biennial Terra Madre gathering in Turin brings together food communities from around the world, and it founded the University of Gastronomic Sciences in Pollenzo. Local chapters, called convivia, organise tastings, markets and school gardens.

Influence and criticism

Slow Food helped inspire related movements including slow cities (Cittaslow) and the broader "slow movement". Admirers credit it with defending biodiversity and regional cuisines; critics have at times called it elitist or nostalgic, charges the organisation answers by stressing food access and producers' rights in the developing world.
