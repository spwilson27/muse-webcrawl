The Ballon d'Or ("Golden Ball") is an annual football award presented by the French magazine France Football since 1956, honouring the player judged to have performed best over the preceding year (initially the calendar year, later the European season). Voted on by football journalists from around the world, it is widely regarded as the most prestigious individual award in the sport.

History

Conceived by writers Gabriel Hanot and Jacques Ferran, the award was originally restricted to European players at European clubs, and became known as the European Footballer of the Year. In 1995 eligibility opened to all players at European clubs — George Weah of Liberia won that year — and in 2007 to all professional footballers worldwide. From 2010 to 2015 the award merged with FIFA's World Player award as the FIFA Ballon d'Or before the partnership ended. A women's award, the Ballon d'Or Féminin, was introduced in 2018.

Record holders

Lionel Messi has won the award a record eight times, followed by Cristiano Ronaldo with five. Johan Cruyff, Michel Platini and Marco van Basten each won three times; Platini's three came in consecutive years (1983–85), a feat Messi surpassed with four in a row (2009–12).
