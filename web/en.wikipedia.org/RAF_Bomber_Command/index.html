RAF Bomber Command

RAF Bomber Command controlled the Royal Air Force's bomber forces from 1936 to 1968. Together with the United States Army Air Forces, it played a central role in the strategic bombing of Germany during the Second World War. From 1942, under Air Chief Marshal Sir Arthur Harris, it pursued a policy of area bombing of German cities, culminating in raids such as those on Hamburg (1943) and Dresden (1945).

Second World War

Early-war daylight raids suffered heavy losses, and the Butt Report (1941) showed that night bombing was grossly inaccurate; the response was a shift to night area attacks supported by navigation aids such as Gee, Oboe, and H2S radar, and the famous specialist operations of No. 617 Squadron, the "Dambusters". Aircraft included the Avro Lancaster, Handley Page Halifax, Short Stirling, and de Havilland Mosquito. The Command suffered the highest casualty rate of any British service arm: of some 125,000 aircrew, about 55,573 were killed.

Post-war

In the Cold War, Bomber Command operated the V-bombers — Valiant, Vulcan, and Victor — as Britain's nuclear deterrent until the role passed to the Royal Navy's Polaris submarines in 1969. In 1968 Bomber Command was merged into the new RAF Strike Command. A national memorial to Bomber Command in Green Park, London, was unveiled in 2012.
