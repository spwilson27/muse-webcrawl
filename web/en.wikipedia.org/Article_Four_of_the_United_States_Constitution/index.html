Article Four of the United States Constitution

Article Four of the United States Constitution outlines the relationship between the various states, as well as between the states and the federal government. It contains the Full Faith and Credit Clause, the Privileges and Immunities Clause, provisions on the admission of new states, and the guarantee to every state of a republican form of government.

Section 1: Full faith and credit

Each state must give "full faith and credit" to the public acts, records and judicial proceedings of every other state, so that judgments, marriages and other official acts are recognised across state lines. Congress may prescribe the manner of proving such acts and their effect.

Section 2: Rights of state citizens

The Privileges and Immunities Clause provides that citizens of each state are entitled to the privileges and immunities of citizens in the several states. The section also originally required the return of fugitive slaves — a provision rendered obsolete by the Thirteenth Amendment — and still provides for interstate extradition of persons charged with crimes.

Section 3: New states and federal property

Congress may admit new states into the Union, but no new state may be formed within, or by the junction of, existing states without their consent as well as that of Congress. The Property Clause gives Congress power to dispose of and regulate the territory and property of the United States.

Section 4: Republican form of government

The United States guarantees every state a republican form of government and protection against invasion and, on application, against domestic violence. The Supreme Court has generally treated questions under the Guarantee Clause as nonjusticiable political questions.
