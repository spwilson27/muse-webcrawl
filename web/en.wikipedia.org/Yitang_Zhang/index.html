Yitang Zhang (Chinese: 张益唐; born 5 February 1955) is a Chinese-American mathematician working in number theory. In 2013, while a little-known lecturer at the University of New Hampshire, he submitted a proof to the Annals of Mathematics establishing the first finite bound on the gaps between prime numbers.

Bounded prime gaps

Zhang proved that there are infinitely many pairs of primes differing by at most 70 million — the first result of its kind toward the twin prime conjecture, which concerns the case of gap 2. His breakthrough was rapidly improved by the collaborative Polymath8 project and by James Maynard, reducing the bound to the hundreds. Zhang's work earned him a MacArthur "Genius" Fellowship in 2014, and he subsequently moved to the University of California, Santa Barbara.

Early life

Zhang's education was interrupted by the Cultural Revolution, during which he worked in fields and a factory. He later studied at Peking University and earned his doctorate at Purdue University in 1991, but struggled for years to find an academic position, at times working in restaurants and motels — a story that made his late breakthrough one of the most celebrated in modern mathematics.
