Gebirgsjäger

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Gebirgsjäger (German: [ɡəˈbɪʁksˌjɛːɡɐ]) are the light infantry part of the alpine or mountain troops (Gebirgstruppe) of Germany, Austria and Switzerland. The word Jäger (hunter) is a characteristic term for light infantry in German-speaking countries. Mountain troops as a distinct branch arose in the German and Austro-Hungarian empires before the First World War, drawing personnel largely from alpine regions.

History

German mountain divisions served extensively in the Second World War in Norway, Finland, the Caucasus, the Balkans, Italy and on the Eastern Front. The first mountain division was raised in 1938 from Bavarian and Austrian personnel after the Anschluss. Gebirgsjäger units were recognisable by the edelweiss insignia worn on the cap and sleeve, and by their distinctive mountain boots and equipment designed for high-altitude warfare, including pack animals for transport.

Modern forces

In the modern Bundeswehr, the Gebirgsjäger form part of the 23rd Mountain Infantry Brigade (Gebirgsjägerbrigade 23 "Bayern"), headquartered at Bad Reichenhall, with battalions in Bad Reichenhall, Bischofswiesen and Mittenwald. Austrian Gebirgsjäger serve in the 6th Mountain Brigade, and Swiss mountain troops likewise continue the tradition. Troops are trained in skiing, climbing, and operations in mountainous and arctic conditions, and have deployed on international missions including in Afghanistan and Kosovo.

Edelweiss

The edelweiss, a flower growing at high altitude in the Alps, is the traditional badge of German and Austrian mountain troops, symbolising the alpine environment in which they operate. It is worn on the side of the mountain cap (Bergmütze) and, in the German forces, on the upper sleeve of the uniform.
