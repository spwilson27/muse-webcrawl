Eurasia is the largest continental area on Earth, comprising all of Europe and Asia. It covers about 54.76 million square kilometres — roughly 36% of Earth's total land area — and is home to more than 5 billion people, over 70% of the world's population. Physically, Europe and Asia form a single continuous landmass; their division is historical and cultural rather than geographical, conventionally running along the Ural Mountains, the Ural River, the Caspian and Black Seas, and the waterways connecting the Black Sea to the Aegean.

Geography

Eurasia stretches from Portugal in the west to the Bering Strait in the east, and from the Arctic Ocean to the Indian Ocean. It contains the world's highest point (Mount Everest) and lowest land point (the Dead Sea shore). The Eurasian Plate carries most of the landmass. The vast Eurasian Steppe, running from Hungary to Manchuria, historically served as a corridor for trade and migration, notably along the Silk Road.

History and geopolitics

The term "Eurasia" combines "Europe" and "Asia". In geopolitics, Eurasia has been central to Halford Mackinder's "Heartland" theory and to later concepts of Eurasian integration, including the Eurasian Economic Union and China's Belt and Road Initiative. Some political movements — notably Russian Eurasianism — treat Eurasia as a distinct civilization.
