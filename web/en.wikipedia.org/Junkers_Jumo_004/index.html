The Junkers Jumo 004 was the world's first production turbojet engine in operational use, and the first successful axial-flow turbojet. Developed by Junkers Motorenbau in Germany under Dr Anselm Franz, it powered the Messerschmitt Me 262 fighter and the Arado Ar 234 reconnaissance bomber during the Second World War.

Design and development

Work began in 1939. The engine used an eight-stage axial compressor, six individual combustion chambers ("cans"), and a single-stage turbine. It first ran in 1940 and first flew in 1942 under a Messerschmitt Bf 110. To start the engine, a small two-stroke Riedel starter motor housed in the intake bullet was used. The Jumo 004 produced about 8.8 kN (1,980 lbf) of thrust.

Operational history

Because Germany faced shortages of strategic metals such as nickel and chromium, the engine was built largely from mild steel coated with aluminium, and its service life was short — often only 10 to 25 hours before overhaul. Careful throttle handling was essential, as rapid throttle movements could cause flame-outs or compressor stalls. Around 8,000 units were produced. After the war, copies were built in the Soviet Union (as the RD-10) and Czechoslovakia, and the design influenced early jet development worldwide.
