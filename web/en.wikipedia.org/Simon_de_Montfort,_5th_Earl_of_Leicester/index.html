Simon de Montfort, 5th Earl of Leicester (c. 1175 – 25 June 1218), also known as Simon IV de Montfort, was a French nobleman and soldier who took part in the Fourth Crusade and was a prominent leader of the Albigensian Crusade. He gained notoriety and criticism for his campaigns in southern France, most of all at Béziers and Carcassonne, and died at the siege of Toulouse in 1218. He was also lord of Montfort-l'Amaury and a claimant to the earldom of Leicester in England.

Early life

Simon was the son of Simon IV de Montfort and Amicia de Beaumont, sister and co-heir of Robert de Beaumont, 4th Earl of Leicester. Through his mother he inherited a claim to the earldom of Leicester, which was formally recognized in 1207, though the English estates were later seized by King John.

Fourth Crusade

Montfort joined the Fourth Crusade in 1202 but refused to participate in the attack on the Christian city of Zara and withdrew from the expedition rather than take part in the diversion against Constantinople.

Albigensian Crusade

In 1209 Montfort joined the crusade against the Cathars of Languedoc. After the capture of Carcassonne he was chosen to govern the conquered territories and became Viscount of Albi, Béziers and Carcassonne. His campaigns were marked by military skill and by episodes of extreme severity, including the massacres that followed the capture of Béziers in 1209.

In 1213 he defeated Peter II of Aragon at the Battle of Muret, in which the king was killed. The Fourth Lateran Council of 1215 confirmed him in possession of Toulouse, but the city revolted in 1217. Montfort was killed during the ensuing siege, struck on the head by a stone from a mangonel, on 25 June 1218.

Family

Montfort married Alix de Montmorency, who actively supported his campaigns. Their younger son, Simon de Montfort, 6th Earl of Leicester, became a major figure in English politics and led the baronial revolt against Henry III.
