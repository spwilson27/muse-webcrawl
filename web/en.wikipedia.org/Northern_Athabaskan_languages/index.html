Northern Athabaskan languages

The Northern Athabaskan languages are a large subgroup of the Athabaskan language family spoken in the interior of Alaska and northwestern Canada, including the Yukon, Northwest Territories, and northern parts of British Columbia, Alberta, Saskatchewan, and Manitoba. Together with the Pacific Coast and Southern Athabaskan (Apachean) groups, they form the Athabaskan branch of the Na-Dene family.

Classification and languages

The subgroup includes around 30 languages and dialect complexes. Among the better known are:

  • Gwich'in (Kutchin), spoken in Alaska, Yukon, and the Northwest Territories
  • Slavey (North and South Slavey), in the Northwest Territories, Alberta, and British Columbia
  • Dogrib (Tłı̨chǫ), between Great Slave and Great Bear lakes
  • Chipewyan (Dëne Sųłıné), the most widely spoken of the group
  • Carrier (Dakelh) and Chilcotin (Tsilhqot'in) in British Columbia
  • Koyukon, Tanana, Ahtna, Dena'ina, and other languages of Alaska
  • Tahltan, Kaska, Beaver (Dane-zaa), and Sarcee (Tsuut'ina) near the Rockies and plains

Characteristics

Like other Athabaskan languages, the Northern languages are known for complex verb morphology: verbs are built from many prefixes layered around a stem, and stems are classified by the shape and handling of objects (the classificatory verb system). The languages are tonal or pitch-accented, with tone developed from Proto-Athabaskan constriction. Noun classification and area-based dialect continua make the internal classification of the group debated; Eung-Do Cook and others have proposed subgroupings based on phonological innovations.

Status

Most Northern Athabaskan languages are endangered because of the effects of colonization, residential schools, and the dominance of English, though the number of speakers varies from a few individuals to several thousand. Revitalization programs, immersion schools, and community documentation projects are under way across the region, and several languages have official status in the Northwest Territories.
