Whitechapel is a district of East London, England, in the London Borough of Tower Hamlets, about 3.4 miles (5.5 km) east of Charing Cross. It takes its name from a small white painted chapel of St Mary Matfelon that once stood there. The area is traversed by Whitechapel Road and is served by Whitechapel station.

History

By the Victorian era, Whitechapel had become synonymous with poverty and overcrowding; Charles Booth's poverty maps classified many of its streets among London's poorest. In 1888 the district gained grim notoriety as the scene of the Whitechapel murders attributed to Jack the Ripper. Successive waves of immigration — Huguenot weavers, Irish and Jewish communities, and later a large Bangladeshi community — have shaped its character.

Culture and landmarks

Landmarks include the Royal London Hospital, the Whitechapel Gallery (founded 1901), the East London Mosque, and Petticoat Lane Market nearby. The Whitechapel Bell Foundry, which cast Big Ben and the Liberty Bell, operated on Whitechapel Road from the 18th century until 2017 and was among Britain's oldest manufacturing companies.

Transport

Whitechapel station is served by the District and Hammersmith & City lines of the London Underground, the London Overground, and the Elizabeth line, which opened its Whitechapel stop in 2022.
