The Salzburg Easter Festival (German: Osterfestspiele Salzburg) is an annual festival of opera and classical concerts held in Salzburg, Austria, during the Easter holiday period. Founded in 1967 by the conductor Herbert von Karajan, it is a sister event to the summer Salzburg Festival and one of the most prestigious — and most expensive — classical music festivals in the world.

History

Karajan created the Easter Festival as a vehicle for his own productions with the Berlin Philharmonic, of which he was chief conductor. The centrepiece each year was a new opera production, performed in the Grosses Festspielhaus, surrounded by orchestral and choral concerts. After Karajan's death in 1989, guest conductors and orchestras gradually took a larger role; Sir Georg Solti, Claudio Abbado and others led editions in the 1990s.

From 1994 the Berlin Philharmonic remained the resident orchestra until 2012, when it moved its Easter residency to Baden-Baden. From 2013 the Staatskapelle Dresden under Christian Thielemann became the festival's orchestra in residence, a partnership that ran until 2022. In November 2022 the conductor Nikolaus Bachler took over as artistic director, with the festival returning to a model of rotating guest orchestras, including the Berlin Philharmonic once more.

Programme and character

The festival runs for roughly ten days, presenting one opera production (often revived at the summer festival), several orchestral concerts, chamber music and sacred works appropriate to the Easter season. Its subscription-based ticketing and high prices have long made it a byword for exclusivity in classical music, while its productions frequently tour or transfer to major houses.
