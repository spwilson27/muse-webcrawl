Abu ʾl-Qasim Ferdowsi Tusi (c. 940 – 1019/1025), commonly known as Ferdowsi, was a Persian poet and the author of the Shahnameh ("The Book of Kings"), which is the longest epic poem ever written by a single author and the national epic of Greater Iran. Ferdowsi is celebrated as one of the most important figures in Persian literature and one of the greatest in the history of literature generally; he is often credited with preserving the Persian language and Iranian identity after the Arab conquest.

Life

Ferdowsi was born in the village of Paj near Tus in Khorasan, into a family of landowners (dehqans), the class that preserved pre-Islamic Iranian tradition. Little is known with certainty about his life; much comes from the Shahnameh itself and later biographical tradition. He began versifying the national history around 977, drawing on a prose Shahnameh compiled for Abu Mansur and on the incomplete verse version of the poet Daqiqi, whose thousand couplets he incorporated.

The Shahnameh

Completed around 1010, the Shahnameh contains some 50,000 rhyming couplets recounting the mythical and historical past of the Persian Empire from the creation of the world until the Arab conquest of the seventh century. Its heroes include Rostam, the mightiest of champions, and it features famous episodes such as the tragedies of Rostam and Sohrab and of Siyavash. Ferdowsi presented the work to Sultan Mahmud of Ghazni; legend holds that the reward was insultingly small and that the poet, embittered, gave the money away, though the story comes from later sources.

Legacy

Ferdowsi's deliberate use of Persian with a minimum of Arabic loanwords shaped the development of modern Persian. His tomb at Tus is a national shrine, and the millenary of his birth was celebrated in 1934 with an international congress in Tehran. See also: Shahnameh, Rostam, Persian literature, Tus.
