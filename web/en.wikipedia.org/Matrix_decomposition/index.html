Matrix decomposition

In linear algebra, a matrix decomposition or matrix factorization is a factorisation of a matrix into a product of matrices with special properties. Decompositions simplify computations such as solving linear systems, finding inverses and determinants, estimating eigenvalues, and compressing data, and they are the workhorses of numerical linear algebra.

Common decompositions

The LU decomposition writes a matrix as a product of lower and upper triangular matrices (with pivoting, PLU), used to solve linear systems. The QR decomposition factors a matrix into an orthogonal matrix Q and an upper triangular R, used for least squares. The Cholesky decomposition applies to symmetric positive-definite matrices. Eigendecomposition expresses a diagonalisable matrix in terms of its eigenvectors and eigenvalues, while the singular value decomposition (SVD), A = UΣV*, exists for every matrix and underlies principal component analysis and low-rank approximation. Other forms include the Schur, Jordan, polar and nonnegative matrix factorisations.

Applications

Decompositions are used in statistics, signal processing, computer graphics, recommender systems, quantum mechanics and the training and analysis of machine-learning models.
