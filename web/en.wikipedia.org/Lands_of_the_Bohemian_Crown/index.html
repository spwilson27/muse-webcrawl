Lands of the Bohemian Crown

Established1348 (Charles IV)
Core landsBohemia, Moravia, Silesia, Lusatia
Dissolved1918

The Lands of the Bohemian Crown (Czech: Země Koruny české; German: Böhmische Kronländer; Latin: Corona regni Bohemiae) were the states in Central Europe connected by feudal relations under the Bohemian kings. The legal concept was established by King Charles IV on 7 April 1348, declaring the crown lands an indivisible entity joined to the Crown of Saint Wenceslas.

Composition

The core lands were the Kingdom of Bohemia, the Margraviate of Moravia, the Duchies of Silesia, and the Lusatias (Upper and Lower Lusatia). Their composition changed over time: most of Silesia was lost to Prussia in 1742, and the Lusatias passed to Saxony in 1635.

Later history

From 1526 the lands were ruled by the Habsburgs, and after the Battle of White Mountain in 1620 their autonomy was sharply curtailed, though the crown lands retained a distinct legal identity within the Habsburg monarchy and later Cisleithanian Austria. The term remained in use until the collapse of Austria-Hungary in 1918, when Bohemia, Moravia, and Czech Silesia became part of Czechoslovakia.
