Shuttle–Mir program

Type: Crewed space programme
Countries: United States, Russia
Duration: 1994–1998

The Shuttle–Mir program was a collaborative space programme between Russia and the United States involving American Space Shuttles visiting the Russian space station Mir, with Russian cosmonauts flying on the Shuttle and American astronauts undertaking long stays aboard Mir.

Background

Agreed in 1992 by Presidents George H. W. Bush and Boris Yeltsin, the programme was designated Phase One of the International Space Station programme, preparing the way for joint construction of the ISS.

Missions

From 1994 to 1998 there were eleven Shuttle flights to Mir, beginning with a rendezvous by Discovery in 1995 and the first docking by Atlantis later that year. Seven American astronauts lived aboard Mir for extended expeditions, despite incidents including a fire and a collision with a Progress cargo craft in 1997.

Legacy

The programme gave NASA experience in long-duration flight and international operations, though it was criticised for safety risks. It ended in 1998 as ISS assembly began.
