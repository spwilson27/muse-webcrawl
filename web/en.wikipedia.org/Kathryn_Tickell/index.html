Kathryn Tickell

Born8 June 1967, Wylam, Northumberland, England
InstrumentsNorthumbrian smallpipes, fiddle
GenresFolk, traditional Northumbrian music
Years active1984–present

Kathryn Tickell (born 8 June 1967) is an English folk musician, best known for her mastery of the Northumbrian smallpipes and fiddle. She has recorded more than a dozen albums and is regarded as the foremost exponent of the smallpipes, an instrument native to North East England.

Early life

Tickell was born in Wylam, Northumberland, into a family deeply rooted in the traditional music of the region; her father Mike Tickell was a singer and her uncle was involved in the local piping scene. She began playing the smallpipes at the age of nine, inspired by pipers such as Billy Pigg, and by thirteen was winning piping competitions. She released her first album, On Kielder Side, in 1984 at the age of sixteen.

Career

Tickell has performed worldwide, both solo and with ensembles including the Kathryn Tickell Band and Kathryn Tickell & The Darkening. She has collaborated with Sting, a fellow Northumbrian, and her playing features in theatre productions, including the Royal Shakespeare Company. In 2013 she won Musician of the Year at the BBC Radio 2 Folk Awards.

Education and advocacy

Tickell was instrumental in founding Folkworks, the folk music development agency for the North East, and helped establish the traditional music degree programme at Newcastle University. From 2018 to 2022 she was artistic director of the Music at Oxford... In 2015 she was appointed a Deputy Lieutenant of Northumberland, and she was awarded an OBE in 2009 for services to folk music.
