Proton nuclear magnetic resonance

Proton NMR (1H NMR)
TechniqueNMR spectroscopy
Nucleus1H (spin ½, ~99.98% natural abundance)
Typical field300–1000 MHz instruments
ReferenceTetramethylsilane (TMS), δ = 0 ppm
YieldsChemical shift, integration, spin–spin coupling

Proton nuclear magnetic resonance (proton NMR, 1H NMR or 1H-NMR) is the application of nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy to hydrogen-1 nuclei within the molecules of a substance, in order to determine the structure of its molecules. Because hydrogen atoms occur in nearly all organic compounds and the 1H nucleus is highly sensitive, proton NMR is the most commonly used form of NMR in chemistry.

Principles

In a strong magnetic field, 1H nuclei (spin ½) absorb radio-frequency radiation at a resonance frequency that depends slightly on their chemical environment: electrons shield nuclei to different degrees, so chemically distinct protons resonate at different frequencies. This displacement is reported as the chemical shift δ in parts per million (ppm) relative to tetramethylsilane. Typical ranges include 0.9 ppm for alkyl CH3, 3–4 ppm for protons on carbons bonded to oxygen, 6.5–8 ppm for aromatic protons and 9–12 ppm for aldehyde and carboxylic-acid protons.

Information content

Three features make the spectrum structurally informative. The number of signals counts distinct proton environments; the integration (area under each signal) is proportional to the number of protons in that environment; and spin–spin coupling splits signals into multiplets — commonly following the n + 1 rule for n neighbouring non-equivalent protons — revealing which groups are adjacent. Coupling constants (J, in Hz) and two-dimensional techniques such as COSY extend the analysis to complex molecules, complemented by carbon-13 NMR.

Practice

Samples are usually dissolved in deuterated solvents (such as CDCl3 or DMSO-d6) so the solvent's own protons do not overwhelm the spectrum. Modern spectrometers use superconducting magnets operating at 300–1000 MHz for protons and Fourier-transform acquisition. Proton NMR is indispensable in organic synthesis, pharmaceutical analysis and metabolomics for confirming identity and purity.
