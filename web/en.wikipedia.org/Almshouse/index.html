Almshouse

Purpose: Charitable housing for the poor and elderly
Funded by: Private endowments, guilds, parishes
Oldest (England): Hospital of St Cross, Winchester (c. 1132)
Related: Almsgiving, workhouse

An almshouse (also called a bede-house or poorhouse hospital) is charitable housing provided to people in a particular community, typically the elderly poor, supported by a private endowment. Almshouses have existed since the medieval period in Europe, founded by monasteries, guilds, wealthy merchants, and landowners as acts of almsgiving.

History

The tradition in England dates to the tenth century; the Hospital of St Cross in Winchester, founded around 1132, is often cited as England's oldest continuing almshouse. Wealthy benefactors endowed almshouses with land or rents, and residents — called almsmen or almswomen, or bedesmen where they were expected to pray (bede) for the founder's soul — received a small stipend, fuel, and clothing such as a distinctive gown. Many surviving almshouses are arranged around a courtyard with a chapel at the centre.

Modern almshouses

Roughly 1,600 almshouse charities still operate in the United Kingdom, providing homes for around 36,000 people, coordinated by the Almshouse Association. In the United States, "almshouse" historically referred to county poorhouses, which by the twentieth century were largely replaced by other welfare institutions.
