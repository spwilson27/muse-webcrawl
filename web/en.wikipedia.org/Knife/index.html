A knife is a tool or weapon with a cutting edge or blade, usually attached to a handle. One of humanity's oldest tools, knives have been made for at least two million years from stone, bone, bronze, iron and steel, and are used as utensils, tools and weapons worldwide.

Construction

A knife consists of a blade, which may be fixed or folding, and a handle. Blades are commonly made of carbon steel, stainless steel or ceramic, ground to an edge and sometimes serrated. The part of the blade extending into the handle is the tang; handles are made of wood, plastic, bone, horn or metal.

History

Stone knives and hand axes are among the earliest human tools. Metallurgy produced bronze and later iron blades; Roman, Viking and medieval smiths refined forging and tempering. Japanese swordsmiths developed laminated steels, and industrial production in centres such as Sheffield and Solingen made knives inexpensive household goods.

Types

Kitchen knives include chef's, paring, bread and boning knives. Utility and craft knives, pocket knives such as the Swiss Army knife, hunting and survival knives, and combat knives serve specialised purposes. Ceremonial and ritual knives include the kukri, kirpan and athame.

Legal aspects

Many jurisdictions regulate the carrying of knives in public, restricting blade length, automatic (switchblade) mechanisms or concealed carry, while exempting tools used for work or sport.
