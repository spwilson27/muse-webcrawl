United States of the Ionian Islands

The United States of the Ionian Islands (Greek: Enōmenon Kratos tōn Ioniōn Nēsōn) was a British protectorate in the Ionian Islands from 1815 to 1864. Formed under the Treaty of Paris in 1815, it comprised the seven principal islands of Corfu, Paxos, Lefkada, Cephalonia, Ithaca, Zakynthos, and Kythira, together with their dependencies, and was governed by a Lord High Commissioner appointed by the British Crown.

History

The Ionian Islands had passed from Venetian rule to French, then to a Russo-Ottoman-backed Septinsular Republic, and back to French control before British forces occupied them between 1809 and 1814. The 1815 settlement placed them under British "protection" as a nominally free and independent state. The first Lord High Commissioner, Sir Thomas Maitland, imposed the authoritarian constitution of 1817, which islanders nicknamed the "Maitland Constitution."

Government and society

The protectorate had a Senate and a Legislative Assembly, but real power lay with the Lord High Commissioner, who could dissolve the Assembly and control the press. Britain undertook major public works, including roads and the Palace of St. Michael and St. George in Corfu. Greek was the language of the population, and agitation for enosis — union with the Kingdom of Greece — grew throughout the period, encouraged by figures such as the radical democratic movements of Cephalonia.

Union with Greece

In 1863 Britain agreed to cede the islands following the accession of the Danish prince William as King George I of Greece, partly to cement the new monarchy. The Treaty of London (1864) transferred sovereignty, and the islands were formally incorporated into Greece on June 1, 1864, after British forces demolished fortifications at Corfu.
