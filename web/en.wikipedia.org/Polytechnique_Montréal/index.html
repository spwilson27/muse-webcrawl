Polytechnique Montréal

Polytechnique Montréal (previously École Polytechnique de Montréal) is a public research university affiliated with the Université de Montréal in Montreal, Quebec, Canada. Founded in 1873, it is the oldest engineering school in Canada and one of the most successful engineering schools in the country in terms of research funding.

History

The École Polytechnique was founded in 1873 by Urgel-Eugène Archambault and began teaching the following year. It became affiliated with the Université de Montréal in 1920, and moved to its present site on the northern slope of Mount Royal in 1958. Polytechnique offers undergraduate and graduate programs in twelve engineering specialties, including chemical, civil, computer, electrical, industrial, mechanical, mining and software engineering, teaching primarily in French.

1989 massacre

On December 6, 1989, a gunman entered the school and murdered fourteen women in an antifeminist attack before killing himself. The event, known as the École Polytechnique massacre, became a turning point in Canadian debates about gun control and violence against women; December 6 is observed as the National Day of Remembrance and Action on Violence Against Women. A memorial, Nef pour quatorze reines, stands near the campus.

Campus and research

The main pavilion (1958) and the Claudette-MacKay-Lassonde and Pierre-Lassonde pavilions (2005) house teaching laboratories and research centres in areas such as biomedical engineering, nanotechnology, aerospace, energy and cybersecurity. Polytechnique is noted for its close ties to industry and for research chairs in partnership with Quebec and Canadian companies.
