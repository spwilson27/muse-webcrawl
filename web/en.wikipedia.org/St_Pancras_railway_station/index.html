St Pancras railway station

St Pancras railway station, officially known since 2007 as London St Pancras International, is a major central London railway terminus on Euston Road in the London Borough of Camden. Opened in 1868 by the Midland Railway, it is famous for its Victorian Gothic Midland Grand Hotel frontage and for William Henry Barlow's train shed, which was the largest single-span roof in the world when completed.

History

The station was built as the London terminus of the Midland Railway, whose trains previously used other companies' lines into the capital. Threatened with demolition in the 1960s, the station was saved after a campaign led by poet John Betjeman and granted Grade I listed status in 1967.

St Pancras International

Between 2001 and 2007 the station was extended and rebuilt as the London terminus of High Speed 1, the Channel Tunnel rail link. Since November 2007 it has been the home of Eurostar services to Paris, Brussels and Amsterdam, alongside East Midlands Railway services, Southeastern high-speed trains and Thameslink services. A statue of John Betjeman and the sculpture The Meeting Place stand on the upper concourse.
