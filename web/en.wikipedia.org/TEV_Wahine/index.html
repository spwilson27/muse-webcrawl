TEV Wahine

TEV Wahine was a twin-screw, turbo-electric roll-on/roll-off passenger and vehicle ferry built in 1966 for the Union Steam Ship Company of New Zealand. She operated the Wellington–Lyttelton route across Cook Strait and was lost on 10 April 1968 when she foundered at the entrance to Wellington Harbour during Cyclone Giselle, in what remains New Zealand's worst modern maritime disaster.

Service

Wahine (a Māori word meaning "woman") was built by the Fairfield Shipbuilding and Engineering Company at Govan, Glasgow, and entered service in August 1966. She could carry around 900 passengers and 200 vehicles, and her overnight sailings between Wellington and Lyttelton were a vital link between New Zealand's North and South Islands.

Loss

On the morning of 10 April 1968, as Wahine approached Wellington in winds gusting to over 270 km/h — among the strongest ever recorded in New Zealand — she was driven onto Barrett Reef at the harbour entrance. She lost a propeller and steering, drifted into the harbour, and anchored, but flooding caused her to list progressively. The order to abandon ship was given in the early afternoon; the list then worsened suddenly and she capsized and sank near Steeple Rock. Of the 734 people aboard, 51 died on the day, most by drowning and exposure; two further victims died later from injuries, bringing the toll to 53.

Aftermath

A court of inquiry found the grounding was caused by the extreme weather but criticised aspects of the decision-making and the delayed abandonment. The disaster led to reforms in ferry safety, lifeboat design and weather warning procedures. The wreck, initially a hazard in the harbour entrance, was cut up and removed over the following years. Memorials at Seatoun and Eastbourne commemorate those who died, and the Wahine disaster remains a defining event in Wellington's history.
