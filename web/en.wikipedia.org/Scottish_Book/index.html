Scottish Book

The Scottish Book (Polish: Księga szkocka) was a thick notebook used in the 1930s and 1940s by mathematicians of the Lwów School of Mathematics in the city of Lwów (now Lviv, Ukraine) to record mathematical problems, their solutions, and prizes offered for solving them. The problems were recorded during meetings at the Scottish Café (Kawiarnia Szkocka).

History

Mathematicians such as Stefan Banach, Stanisław Ulam, and Hugo Steinhaus gathered regularly at the Scottish Café to discuss problems, first writing on the marble tabletops before switching to a notebook kept at the café. Contributors offered prizes for solutions, ranging from a bottle of wine to, famously, a live goose offered by Stanisław Mazur.

Contents

The book contains 193 problems posed between 1935 and 1941, spanning functional analysis, topology, set theory, and measure theory. Several problems influenced the development of Banach space theory and related fields.

Legacy

Ulam translated and annotated the book, and it was published in English in 1981, edited by R. Daniel Mauldin. In 1972, Mazur presented the promised live goose to Per Enflo for solving problem 153, concerning the approximation property of Banach spaces.
