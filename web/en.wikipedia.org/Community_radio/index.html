Community radio

Community radio is a radio service offering a third model of radio broadcasting in addition to commercial and public broadcasting. Community stations serve geographic communities and communities of interest. They broadcast content that is popular and relevant to a local, specific audience but is often overlooked by commercial or mass-media broadcasters. Community radio stations are operated, owned, and influenced by the communities they serve, and are generally non-profit organisations funded by listeners, grants and limited advertising.

History

The community radio movement grew out of post-war experiments with alternative media. In Latin America, miners' radio stations in Bolivia and Radio Sutatenza in Colombia pioneered participatory broadcasting in the 1940s. In the United States, Pacifica Radio's KPFA (founded 1949) established the listener-sponsored model. In the 1970s and 1980s, legal community radio sectors were established in Australia, Canada and much of Europe; the United Kingdom licensed its first full-time community stations in the mid-2000s.

Principles

Community radio emphasises access and participation: members of the community are trained to produce and present programmes, and governance typically includes community representatives. Programming commonly includes local news, minority-language content, specialist music and educational material.

By country

Australia

Australia has one of the largest community radio sectors in the world, with more than 350 licensed stations, coordinated by the Community Broadcasting Association of Australia.

United Kingdom

The Community Radio Order 2004 created a licensing regime requiring stations to deliver "social gain", restrict advertising and sponsorship, and be accountable to their target communities.

United States

The Local Community Radio Act of 2010 expanded Low Power FM (LPFM) licensing, allowing thousands of small non-commercial stations to broadcast.
