Treviso (Italian pronunciation: [treˈviːzo]) is a city and comune in the Veneto region of northern Italy. It is the capital of the province of Treviso and the municipality has 84,669 inhabitants (as of September 2017). Some 3,000 people live within the Venetian walls (le Mura) or in the historic and monumental centre; some 80,000 people live in the city proper, the surrounding urban area.

History

Known in Roman times as Tarvisium, Treviso joined the Lombard duchy in the 6th century and later became part of the Republic of Venice in 1339. It was badly damaged by bombing in the Second World War; on 7 April 1944, Allied bombers struck the city, killing about 1,000 people and destroying much of the historic centre, which was later rebuilt.

Economy and culture

Treviso is the home of the clothing company Benetton and the appliance manufacturer De'Longhi, and is near the centre of the Prosecco wine region. The city is also associated with the dessert tiramisù, which local tradition claims was invented at the restaurant Le Beccherie in the 1960s–70s. Radicchio Rosso di Treviso is a prized local chicory.

Main sights

Notable sights include the Piazza dei Signori with the Palazzo dei Trecento, the cathedral (Duomo), the church of San Nicolò, and the canals of the old town, which give Treviso a resemblance to a small Venice.
