League of Blood Incident

The League of Blood Incident (Ketsumeidan jiken) was a series of assassinations in Japan in 1932 carried out by members of the League of Blood (Ketsumeidan), an ultranationalist group led by the Nichiren Buddhist priest Nisshō Inoue.

The group, which included young naval officers and students, drew up a list of politicians and business leaders blamed for Japan's economic difficulties and for compromising with the West. Each member was assigned one target — one man, one kill. In February 1932 former finance minister Junnosuke Inoue was shot, and in March Dan Takuma, head of the Mitsui zaibatsu, was assassinated; a series of bomb attacks was also planned or attempted.

The arrested conspirators received relatively lenient sentences amid public sympathy, and the incident, together with the May 15 Incident of the same year, marked the weakening of party government and the rise of militarism in 1930s Japan.
