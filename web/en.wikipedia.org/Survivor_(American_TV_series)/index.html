Survivor is the American version of the international Survivor reality competition television franchise, itself derived from the Swedish television series Expedition Robinson created by Charlie Parsons and first broadcast in 1997. The American series premiered on 31 May 2000 on CBS. It is hosted by Jeff Probst, who is also an executive producer along with Mark Burnett and the original creator Parsons.

Survivor places a group of strangers in an isolated location, where they must provide food, fire, and shelter for themselves. The contestants compete in challenges including physical and mental endurance for rewards and immunity from elimination. The contestants are progressively eliminated from the game as they are voted out by their fellow contestants until only one remains, who is given the title of "Sole Survivor" and is awarded the grand prize of US$1,000,000 (US$2,000,000 in the all-winners season, Winners at War).

Format

Each season, 16 to 20 contestants ("castaways") are marooned in a remote location and split into tribes. They compete in reward and immunity challenges; the losing tribe attends Tribal Council, where they vote one member out. Around the midpoint, the tribes merge into one, and challenges become individual. Eliminated players from a certain point onward form a jury, which votes between the finalists to select the winner. Twists that have become fixtures of the format include hidden immunity idols, tribe swaps, the Edge of Extinction, and, in the shortened "new era" format introduced with season 41, a 26-day game and new advantages such as "beware advantages" and the shot in the dark.

History

The first season, Survivor: Borneo (2000), was a ratings phenomenon and is credited with launching the modern reality-competition era of American television; its finale drew over 51 million viewers. The show has filmed in locations including Malaysia, Australia, Kenya, Brazil, China, and, for most seasons since 2016, the Mamanuca Islands of Fiji, which serves as its semi-permanent base. Notable winners include Richard Hatch (Borneo), Tina Wesson, Ethan Zohn, Sandra Diaz-Twine — the first two-time winner — and Tony Vlachos, winner of Cagayan and Winners at War.

Reception and influence

Survivor has been nominated for numerous Emmy Awards, and Probst won the Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Host for a Reality or Reality-Competition Program four consecutive times. The franchise has spawned versions in more than 50 countries. Critics and scholars regard the series as a landmark of reality television whose social-strategy gameplay has influenced numerous later shows.
