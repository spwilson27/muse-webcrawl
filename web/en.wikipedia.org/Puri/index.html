Puri is a coastal city and a municipality in the state of Odisha in eastern India. It is the headquarters of Puri district and is situated on the Bay of Bengal, about 60 kilometres south of the state capital Bhubaneswar. It is one of the original Char Dham pilgrimage sites for Hindus.

Jagannath Temple and Ratha Yatra

Puri is famous for the 12th-century Jagannath Temple, dedicated to Jagannath, a form of Vishnu/Krishna, worshipped together with his siblings Balabhadra and Subhadra. Non-Hindus are not permitted inside the temple. The temple's annual Ratha Yatra (chariot festival) draws millions of devotees who pull the deities' enormous wooden chariots through the streets; the English word "juggernaut" derives from Jagannath and this festival. The temple kitchen is renowned as one of the largest in the world, preparing mahaprasad for thousands daily.

History

Puri has been a major pilgrimage center for over a thousand years and is associated with the saint-scholar Adi Shankaracharya, who founded one of his four mathas (the Govardhana Matha) here in the 8th century. The present Jagannath Temple was built in the 12th century under the Eastern Ganga dynasty. The region later came under Maratha and then British rule.

Geography and tourism

Puri's sandy beach on the Bay of Bengal makes it a popular resort as well as pilgrimage town. Nearby attractions include the Konark Sun Temple (a UNESCO World Heritage Site), Chilika Lake — Asia's largest brackish-water lagoon, famous for migratory birds and Irrawaddy dolphins — and the artist village of Raghurajpur, known for pattachitra painting.
