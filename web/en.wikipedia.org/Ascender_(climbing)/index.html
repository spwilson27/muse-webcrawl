Ascender (climbing)

An ascender is a mechanical device used in rock climbing, mountaineering, caving and rope rescue that allows a climber to ascend a fixed rope. Ascenders grip the rope when loaded in one direction and slide freely when moved in the other, typically by means of a spring-loaded cam with angled teeth that bite into the rope sheath.

Types

Handled ascenders have an ergonomic grip and are used in pairs — one for each hand, often combined with foot loops — for ascending long fixed lines, as in big-wall climbing and caving. Smaller, handle-less devices serve as compact ascenders or progress-capture pulleys. Well-known models include the Petzl Ascension and the devices historically sold under the Jumar name, after the Swiss climbers Jüsi and Marti who developed an early popular design in the 1950s; "jumar" is often used generically for the technique of ascending a rope with such devices ("jumaring").

Use

To ascend, the climber alternately weights and slides each ascender upward, standing in attached foot loops. Because the toothed cam can damage a rope if shock-loaded or used on icy or muddy ropes, ascenders are intended for body-weight loads only and are not designed to arrest a fall; climbers commonly back them up with knots or a second device. Before mechanical ascenders, climbers used friction hitches such as the Prusik knot, which is still taught as a backup technique.
