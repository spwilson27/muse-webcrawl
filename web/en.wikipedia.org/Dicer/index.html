Dicer, also known as endoribonuclease Dicer or helicase with RNase motif, is an enzyme that in humans is encoded by the DICER1 gene. As a member of the RNase III family, Dicer cleaves double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) and precursor microRNA (pre-miRNA) into short double-stranded RNA fragments — small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) and microRNAs (miRNAs) of about 21–23 nucleotides — that drive RNA interference.

Function

Dicer's PAZ domain binds the end of a double-stranded substrate while its two RNase III domains, positioned a fixed distance away, cut both strands, so the enzyme acts as a molecular ruler producing fragments of characteristic length with two-nucleotide 3' overhangs. In the cytoplasm, Dicer processes pre-miRNA exported from the nucleus (where the Drosha complex has trimmed the primary transcript) into mature miRNA duplexes. One strand is loaded into an Argonaute protein within the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC), which then represses or cleaves complementary messenger RNAs.

Variation across organisms

Many organisms have multiple Dicer homologues with specialised roles: Drosophila Dicer-1 processes miRNA while Dicer-2 handles siRNA from long dsRNA, an antiviral defence; plants such as Arabidopsis have four Dicer-like proteins generating small RNAs of distinct sizes. Some organisms, such as the yeast Saccharomyces cerevisiae, lack Dicer entirely.

Clinical significance

Germline mutations in DICER1 cause DICER1 syndrome, a tumour predisposition condition associated with pleuropulmonary blastoma, cystic nephroma, multinodular goitre and other rare tumours. Altered Dicer expression is also reported in many cancers, and experimental knockdown of Dicer is a standard tool in functional genomics.
