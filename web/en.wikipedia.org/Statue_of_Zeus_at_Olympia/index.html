Statue of Zeus at Olympia

The Statue of Zeus at Olympia was a giant seated figure of the god Zeus, about 12.4 m (41 ft) tall, made by the Greek sculptor Phidias around 435 BC at the sanctuary of Olympia, Greece. A sculpture of ivory plates and gold panels on a wooden framework, it occupied the entire width of the aisle of the temple built to house it and "seemed to fill the temple." It was one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World.

Description

According to the geographer Strabo and the travel writer Pausanias, the seated Zeus held a small statue of Nike, goddess of victory, in his right hand and a sceptre topped with an eagle in his left. The god's skin was carved from ivory and his robes and beard from gold. The throne was decorated with ebony, ivory, gold, and precious stones. Strabo wrote that if Zeus had stood up, he would have unroofed the temple.

History

The temple of Zeus at Olympia was built in the fifth century BC, and Phidias set up a workshop west of the temple — excavated in the 1950s, it yielded moulds, tools, and ivory-working debris — where the statue was assembled piece by piece. The Roman emperor Caligula reportedly attempted to have the statue moved to Rome; later, it may have been transported to Constantinople, where it is believed to have been destroyed in the great fire of the Palace of Lausus in AD 475, though accounts vary.

Legacy

The bearded, majestic image of Zeus established by Phidias influenced later representations of gods and rulers, and is thought to have shaped early Christian depictions of Christ enthroned. No copy of the statue survives; its appearance is known from descriptions, coins of Elis, and Roman-era imitations.
