Actors Studio

The Actors Studio is a membership organisation for professional actors, theatre directors and playwrights, located in the Hell's Kitchen neighbourhood of Manhattan, New York City. It is famous for teaching and refining the practice of method acting, developed from the system of Konstantin Stanislavski. Membership is gained by audition, and members attend sessions to develop their craft without the pressure of commercial production.

History

The Studio was founded in 1947 by Elia Kazan, Cheryl Crawford, and Robert Lewis, former members of the Group Theatre. In 1951 Lee Strasberg became its artistic director, a position he held until his death in 1982; under him the Studio became the leading centre of method acting in the United States. Notable members and alumni include Marlon Brando, James Dean, Marilyn Monroe, Paul Newman, Al Pacino, and Robert De Niro.

Education

The Studio's work is conducted in sessions moderated by experienced members, in which actors present scenes and receive critique. Since 1994 the Actors Studio Drama School has offered graduate degrees, and the television series Inside the Actors Studio, hosted by James Lipton, brought its interview format to a wide audience.
