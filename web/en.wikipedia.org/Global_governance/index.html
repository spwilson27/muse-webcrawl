Global governance

Global governance refers to the system of rules, institutions and cooperative arrangements through which states, international organisations, corporations and civil-society actors manage shared problems that transcend national borders. It is not a world government, but a patchwork of governance mechanisms operating without a single overarching authority.

Institutions and mechanisms

The central institutions include the United Nations and its specialised agencies, the World Bank and International Monetary Fund, the World Trade Organization, and forums such as the G7 and G20. Alongside treaties and formal organisations, global governance works through transnational networks, public–private partnerships, technical standards bodies and informal norms, addressing areas from trade and finance to climate, health and internet policy.

Debates

Scholars debate the effectiveness and legitimacy of global governance: critics point to democratic deficits, unequal influence of powerful states, and gridlock in multilateral negotiations, while supporters note successes in areas such as ozone protection, disease eradication and aviation safety. The concept gained prominence after the Cold War as globalisation intensified cross-border interdependence.
