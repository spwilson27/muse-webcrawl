Greenmail

Greenmail is a corporate finance practice in which an investor accumulates a large block of shares in a company, threatens a hostile takeover, and is then bought out by the company at a substantial premium over the market price in return for abandoning the bid and often for agreeing not to acquire further shares for a period of years. The word blends "greenbacks" (money) with "blackmail". The targeted buyback is typically made only to the greenmailer rather than to all shareholders.

Greenmail flourished during the hostile takeover wave of the 1980s

Criticism and responses

Critics argue that greenmail transfers wealth from remaining shareholders to the raider and entrenched management without creating value, and that managers use shareholders' money to protect their own positions. Famous cases in the 1980s involved raiders such as T. Boone Pickens and Carl Icahn receiving large payments. In response, many companies adopted anti-greenmail charter provisions, the United States imposed a 50 percent excise tax on greenmail profits in 1987, and some states passed anti-greenmail statutes. The practice declined markedly thereafter, though standstill agreements and targeted repurchases remain common elements of takeover defence.
