Eastern Visayas, designated Region VIII, is an administrative region of the Philippines occupying the eastern Visayan islands of Leyte, Samar and Biliran. Its regional center is Tacloban City. The main languages are Waray in Samar and northern Leyte, Cebuano in southern and western Leyte, and Abaknon on Capul Island.

Geography

The region comprises three main islands — Samar, Leyte and Biliran — separated by straits including the San Juanico Strait, spanned since 1973 by the San Juanico Bridge between Tacloban and Santa Rita, Samar. The terrain is largely mountainous and forested; Samar Island Natural Park protects one of the largest remaining lowland forests in the Philippines. The region faces the Pacific Ocean and is among the country's most exposed to typhoons.

History

Leyte Gulf was the site of major Second World War events: General Douglas MacArthur's landing at Palo in October 1944 and the Battle of Leyte Gulf, one of the largest naval battles in history. In November 2013, Super Typhoon Yolanda (Haiyan), one of the strongest tropical cyclones ever recorded at landfall, devastated Tacloban and surrounding areas, killing thousands and prompting a vast relief and reconstruction effort.

Economy

Agriculture (rice, coconut, abacá and sugarcane), fishing and forestry dominate the regional economy; the Leyte Industrial Development Estate hosts a copper smelter and fertilizer plants, and geothermal fields near Ormoc and Kananga supply power. Poverty incidence has historically been among the higher in the country.
