Armageddon (from Ancient Greek Ἁρμαγεδών, Harmagedṓn, from Hebrew הַר מְגִדּוֹ, "mountain of Megiddo") is, according to the New Testament Book of Revelation, the prophesied location of the gathering of armies for the final battle at the end of the world. The word has since passed into general use for any world-ending catastrophe or decisive final conflict.

Biblical reference

Armageddon is mentioned only once in the Bible, in Revelation 16:16: the kings of the world are gathered "to the place that in Hebrew is called Armageddon". The associated battle forms part of the eschatological sequence of Revelation, culminating in the defeat of the Beast and Satan, the Last Judgment and the New Jerusalem. Scholars debate whether the term is literal or symbolic; the plain of Megiddo (Jezreel Valley) in northern Israel was the scene of famous battles in the Hebrew Bible, including the deaths of kings Saul's opponents, Ahaziah and Josiah.

Interpretations

Interpretations vary by tradition. Historicist and futurist readings in parts of evangelical Christianity — including dispensationalist theology — expect a literal military campaign in the Middle East before Christ's return. Jehovah's Witnesses understand Armageddon as God's war against worldly governments. Preterist and idealist readings treat it symbolically as the recurring conflict between good and evil. In Bahá'í and Ahmadiyya thought the term is interpreted allegorically.

Popular usage

"Armageddon" is widely used for scenarios of total destruction, notably nuclear war, and titles works such as the 1998 film Armageddon, about an asteroid impact.
