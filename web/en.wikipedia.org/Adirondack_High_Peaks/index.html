Adirondack High Peaks

The Adirondack High Peaks are a set of 46 mountain summits in the Adirondack Mountains of northeastern New York, United States, traditionally considered to be all of the peaks in the range with an elevation of 4,000 feet (1,219 m) or higher — although later surveys showed that a few fall slightly below that mark while some unlisted peaks exceed it. The High Peaks lie mainly in Essex County, within the High Peaks Wilderness Area of Adirondack Park.

The list

The highest is Mount Marcy at 5,344 feet (1,629 m), the highest point in New York State, followed by Algonquin Peak (5,114 ft), Mount Haystack, Mount Skylight and Whiteface Mountain. The original list derives from surveys by brothers Robert and George Marshall and their guide Herbert Clark in the 1920s. Four peaks — Blake Peak among them — were later found to be under 4,000 feet, and at least one, MacNaughton Mountain, exceeds 4,000 feet but was not on the original list; tradition preserves the count of 46.

Forty-Sixers

Hikers who climb all 46 peaks may join the Adirondack Forty-Sixers, an organization founded by the Marshall brothers and Clark — the first three finishers — whose members receive a sequential finisher number. Thousands of hikers have completed the round; a smaller number have done so in winter ("Winter 46ers"). Popularity has raised concerns about trail erosion and alpine vegetation damage, leading to permit and education programmes.
