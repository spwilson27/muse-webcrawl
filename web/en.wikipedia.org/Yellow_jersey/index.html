Yellow jersey

The yellow jersey (French: maillot jaune) is the garment worn by the leader of the general classification in the Tour de France, the world's most famous cycling race. It is the most prized jersey in professional road cycling, awarded after each stage to the rider with the lowest cumulative time, and ultimately to the winner of the Tour.

History

The jersey was introduced in 1919, midway through the first post-war Tour, so that spectators could easily identify the race leader. Race director Henri Desgrange chose yellow because the organising newspaper, L'Auto, was printed on yellow paper. The first rider to wear it was Eugène Christophe on 19 July 1919 on the stage from Grenoble to Geneva; the 1919 winner, Firmin Lambot, was the first to wear it in Paris.

Record holders

Eddy Merckx wore the yellow jersey for a record 96 days (in some counts 111 stages including half-stages). Bernard Hinault (75 days), Miguel Indurain (60) and Chris Froome (59) follow. Five-time Tour winners Jacques Anquetil, Merckx, Hinault and Indurain are synonymous with the jersey. Lance Armstrong's 83 days in yellow were expunged after his doping disqualification.

Other races

Many other stage races award a yellow leader's jersey in imitation (e.g., the Critérium du Dauphiné), though the Giro d'Italia uses pink (maglia rosa) and the Vuelta a España red (la roja).
