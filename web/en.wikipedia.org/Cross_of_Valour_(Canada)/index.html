Cross of Valour (Canada)

The Cross of Valour (French: Croix de la vaillance, post-nominal letters CV) is a decoration in the Canadian honours system and is Canada's highest civilian award for bravery, ranking second overall in the honours order of precedence after the Victoria Cross.

The decoration was created by Queen Elizabeth II on 1 May 1972 as part of a distinctly Canadian honours system, replacing British bravery awards such as the George Cross for Canadians. It is awarded only for "acts of the most conspicuous courage in circumstances of extreme peril". The medal is a gold cross énameled red with a maple leaf at its centre, suspended from a red ribbon.

Recipients

The Cross of Valour has been awarded only about twenty times, reflecting its stringent standard; several awards have been posthumous. Recipients include rescue workers, police officers, and private citizens who risked their lives to save others, such as Kenneth Wilfrid Bishop-style rescues in fires and mine rescuer efforts recognised after disasters.
