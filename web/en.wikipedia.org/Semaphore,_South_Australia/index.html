Semaphore is a north-western seaside suburb of Adelaide, South Australia, located on the Lefevre Peninsula on the Gulf St Vincent about 14 kilometres from the city centre. It adjoins Port Adelaide and is known for its wide sandy beach, historic jetty and main street of cafes.

History

The suburb takes its name from a signal station — a semaphore mast — erected in the 1850s to signal the arrival of ships at the entrance to Port Adelaide. Semaphore developed as a fashionable seaside resort for Adelaide residents; the jetty was built in 1860, and a railway connection to Adelaide opened in 1882. A substantial time ball tower was erected near the jetty in 1875 to signal the time to ships, and later served as a war memorial precinct.

Attractions

Landmarks include the Semaphore jetty, the beachfront carousel, a steam train miniature railway along the foreshore, pubs dating to the nineteenth century, and the nearby Fort Glanville conservation park. The suburb hosts community events such as the annual Kite Festival and regular markets on the Esplanade.
