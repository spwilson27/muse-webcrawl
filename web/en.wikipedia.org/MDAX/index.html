The MDAX is a German stock market index of mid-cap companies traded on the Frankfurt Stock Exchange. It comprises the 50 stocks ranked below the 40 blue chips of the DAX by free-float market capitalisation and trading volume.

History

The MDAX was introduced on 19 January 1996 with values back-calculated to 1987. It originally contained 70 companies; in 2003 it was reduced to 50. When the DAX expanded from 30 to 40 members in September 2021, the MDAX correspondingly shrank from 60 to 50 constituents. Composition is reviewed quarterly, and members must meet transparency requirements of the exchange's Prime Standard.

Characteristics

The index is heavily weighted toward Germany's industrial Mittelstand — engineering, chemicals, logistics and media companies — and is widely used as an underlying for index funds and derivatives. Like the DAX, it is calculated both as a price index and as a total-return ("performance") index that reinvests dividends.
