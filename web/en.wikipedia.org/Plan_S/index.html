Plan S requires research funded by participating funders to be published open access.

Plan S is an initiative for open access scholarly publishing launched in September 2018 by cOAlition S, a consortium of national research funders, charitable foundations and international organisations, principally coordinated through the European Science Foundation. Its central requirement is that scientific publications arising from research funded by participating organisations must be published in open access journals or platforms, or made immediately available in open repositories, without embargo.

Principles

Plan S rests on ten principles announced at launch. The headline principle states that, from 2021, publicly or privately funded research must be published in compliant open access venues with no paywall and no embargo period. Publications must be released under an open licence, preferably CC BY, allowing reuse with attribution. Funders committed to cover open access publication fees only for compliant venues, to cap those fees, and to sanction non-compliance. So-called "hybrid" journals — subscription journals charging optional open access fees — were not considered compliant except under approved transformative arrangements.

Implementation

Implementation guidance followed in 2019, with requirements taking effect from 1 January 2021. Compliance routes include publishing in fully open access journals, depositing the accepted manuscript in a repository under the "Rights Retention Strategy" (by which authors retain sufficient rights to share the manuscript under CC BY regardless of publisher terms), or publishing under transformative agreements. The Journal Checker Tool was provided so researchers could verify a journal's compliance. Funders that joined include UK Research and Innovation, the French ANR, national funders across Europe, and private funders such as the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation and Wellcome.

Reception

Plan S drew both strong support and criticism. Open access advocates praised its ambition, while some researchers and learned societies warned about article processing charges shifting costs to authors, effects on early-career researchers, and restrictions on journal choice. Publishers responded with transformative agreements that gradually converted subscription journals to open access models. The initiative is widely credited with accelerating the global transition to open access in the early 2020s.
