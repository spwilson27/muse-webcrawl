Fujiwara no Kamatari

Fujiwara no Kamatari (614 – November 14, 669), born Nakatomi no Kamako, was a Japanese statesman, courtier, and politician during the Asuka period. He was the founder of the Fujiwara clan, which would dominate Japanese court politics for centuries.

Life

Kamatari was born into the Nakatomi family, hereditary priests of Shinto ritual at court. Allied with Prince Naka no Ōe (later Emperor Tenji), he planned the assassination of Soga no Iruka in 645, an event known as the Isshi Incident that destroyed the Soga clan's grip on power. Kamatari and the prince then carried out the Taika Reform, a sweeping programme modelled on Tang Chinese institutions that centralised power in the imperial state, reorganised landholding, and reshaped the bureaucracy.

As the emperor's closest adviser, Kamatari held the highest court ranks and effectively directed government. On his deathbed in 669, Emperor Tenji granted him the surname Fujiwara ("wisteria field") and the rank of Taishokan, the highest of the court ranks. He was later deified as a Shinto kami and enshrined at Tanzan Shrine in Nara Prefecture. His son Fujiwara no Fuhito consolidated the family's position and compiled legal codes.
