The Beatles were an English rock band formed in Liverpool in 1960, comprising John Lennon, Paul McCartney, George Harrison and Ringo Starr. Widely regarded as the most influential band of all time, they were integral to the development of 1960s counterculture and the recognition of popular music as an art form.

History

Rooted in skiffle and 1950s rock and roll, the group honed their craft in Liverpool and Hamburg clubs before manager Brian Epstein secured them a recording contract with producer George Martin at Parlophone. Their 1963 singles "Please Please Me" and "She Loves You" ignited "Beatlemania" in Britain, and their February 1964 appearance on The Ed Sullivan Show launched the British Invasion of America.

Studio years

Abandoning touring in 1966, the Beatles produced increasingly sophisticated studio work: Rubber Soul (1965), Revolver (1966), Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band (1967) — a landmark of psychedelic rock and album-as-art — the double White Album (1968) and Abbey Road (1969). Internal tensions, business disputes over Apple Corps and manager Allen Klein, and Lennon's partnership with Yoko Ono led McCartney to announce his departure in April 1970; the band's dissolution was legally finalised in 1974.

Legacy

The Beatles are the best-selling music act in history, with estimated sales of 600 million units. They hold the record for most number-one albums on the UK charts and most number-one hits on the US Billboard Hot 100. Their innovations in songwriting, recording technique (multitracking, tape loops, sampling-like effects), album packaging and music film reshaped popular music. The three Anthology volumes (1995–96) included two posthumous singles built on Lennon demos; a third, "Now and Then", was completed with AI-assisted audio restoration and released in 2023.
