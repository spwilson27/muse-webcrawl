Pierre Laval

Pierre Jean Marie Laval (28 June 1883 – 15 October 1945) was a French politician. During the Third Republic, he served as Prime Minister of France in the early 1930s and again in 1935–36. During the Second World War, Laval became a leading figure in the collaborationist Vichy regime, serving as its head of government from 1942 to 1944.

Born in Châteldon, Puy-de-Dôme, Laval trained as a lawyer and began in politics as a socialist before moving to the right. As prime minister in 1935 he pursued deflationary economic policies and signed the Laval–Hoare Pact concerning Abyssinia, which damaged his reputation abroad. After France's defeat in 1940 he engineered the vote granting full powers to Marshal Philippe Pétain, ending the Third Republic.

Vichy and trial

As head of government under Pétain, Laval pursued active collaboration with Nazi Germany, famously declaring in June 1942 that he desired a German victory, and organizing the Service du travail obligatoire, which sent French workers to Germany. He was also complicit in the deportation of Jews from France. After the liberation of France he fled to Spain and then Austria, was extradited, and tried for high treason in October 1945. His trial was chaotic and his defense largely boycotted it; convicted and sentenced to death, he attempted suicide by poison before being revived and executed by firing squad at Fresnes Prison on 15 October 1945.
