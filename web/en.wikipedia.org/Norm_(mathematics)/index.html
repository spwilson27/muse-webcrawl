Norm (mathematics)

In mathematics, a norm is a function from a real or complex vector space to the non-negative real numbers that behaves in certain ways like the distance from the origin: it commutes with scaling, obeys a form of the triangle inequality, and is zero only at the origin.

Definition

A norm on a vector space V is a function p : V → ℝ satisfying: p(u + v) ≤ p(u) + p(v) (triangle inequality); p(sv) = |s| p(v) (absolute homogeneity); and if p(v) = 0 then v = 0. A vector space equipped with a norm is called a normed vector space.

Examples

The most familiar example is the Euclidean norm, corresponding to ordinary length. More generally, the p-norms on ℝⁿ are defined by raising the sum of the p-th powers of the absolute values of the components to the power 1/p. The case p = 1 gives the taxicab or Manhattan norm; p = ∞ gives the maximum norm.

Properties

Every norm defines a metric and hence a topology. In finite-dimensional spaces all norms are equivalent, inducing the same topology. Norms are continuous and convex functions.
