Lucius Tarquinius Superbus (died 495 BC), also known as Tarquin the Proud, was the legendary seventh and final king of Rome, reigning 25 years until the popular uprising that led to the establishment of the Roman Republic. He is commonly dated to have reigned from 535 BC until the revolution in 509 BC.

Rise to power

Tarquin was the son or grandson of Lucius Tarquinius Priscus, the fifth king. According to Livy, Tarquin, urged on by his wife Tullia Minor, murdered his father-in-law, King Servius Tullius, and seized the throne without the sanction of the Senate or the people.

Reign

Tarquin earned his surname Superbus ("proud, haughty") through tyrannical rule: he abolished the privileges of the patricians, executed senators, and disbanded the assembly. Yet he also completed the Temple of Jupiter Optimus Maximus on the Capitoline Hill, undertook great building works including the Cloaca Maxima, founded colonies, and extended Roman power through war and diplomacy in Latium. He is said to have purchased three of the Sibylline Books from the Cumaean Sibyl for the price originally asked for all nine.

Overthrow

The rape of the noblewoman Lucretia by Tarquin's son Sextus Tarquinius provoked a revolt led by Lucius Junius Brutus and Lucius Tarquinius Collatinus. The royal family was expelled, and Rome established a republic governed by two annually elected consuls. Tarquin sought to regain his throne with the aid of Lars Porsena of Clusium and the Tarquinian conspiracy, but died in exile at Cumae in 495 BC, according to tradition.

Historicity

The traditional account is largely legendary, and modern historians debate the details of the monarchy's fall, but the reign of an Etruscan-influenced Tarquin dynasty in 6th-century Rome is widely accepted.
