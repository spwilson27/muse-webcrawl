Anthony Howard Wilson (20 February 1950 – 10 August 2007) was a British record label owner, radio and television presenter, nightclub manager, journalist and impresario. He was a central figure in the Manchester music scene, co-founding the independent label Factory Records and the Haçienda nightclub, and promoting bands such as Joy Division, New Order and Happy Mondays.

Early life and broadcasting

Wilson was born in Salford and educated at De La Salle Grammar School and Jesus College, Cambridge, where he read English. He began his media career as a trainee news reporter at ITN before joining Granada Television in Manchester in the early 1970s. There he presented the regional news programme Granada Reports and, from 1976, the music show So It Goes, which gave early television exposure to punk and new wave acts including the Sex Pistols, The Clash and Buzzcocks.

Factory Records and the Haçienda

In 1978 Wilson co-founded Factory Records with Alan Erasmus, Rob Gretton, Peter Saville and Martin Hannett. The label released music by Joy Division, New Order, A Certain Ratio, Orchestral Manoeuvres in the Dark and later Happy Mondays, and became noted for Saville's minimalist sleeve design and its practice of cataloguing everything — posters, buildings, even a cat — with FAC numbers. In 1982 Factory opened the Haçienda nightclub (FAC 51), which became a focal point of the late-1980s acid house and "Madchester" scenes despite persistent financial losses.

Later career and death

Factory Records collapsed in 1992, but Wilson remained a prominent civic and media figure in the North West, presenting political programmes for the BBC, chairing conferences and promoting Manchester's cultural regeneration. A fictionalised version of Wilson, played by Steve Coogan, appears in Michael Winterbottom's 2002 film 24 Hour Party People, whose title Wilson himself had suggested. He died of a heart attack on 10 August 2007 while being treated for kidney cancer; New Order and other Factory artists had campaigned for funding of his medication. A gravestone designed by Peter Saville and Ben Kelly, inscribed with a rotating epitaph, was installed at Southern Cemetery, Manchester, in 2010.
