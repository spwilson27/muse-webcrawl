Levinson recursion

Levinson recursion, or the Levinson–Durbin algorithm, is a procedure in linear algebra to recursively calculate the solution to an equation involving a Toeplitz matrix. The algorithm runs in Θ(n²) time, which is a strong improvement over Gaussian elimination, which runs in Θ(n³) time. It was described by Norman Levinson in 1947 and refined for the important special case of stationary time-series by James Durbin in 1960.

Applications

The Levinson–Durbin recursion is widely used in digital signal processing to solve the Yule–Walker equations and to compute the coefficients of all-pole linear predictive models, as in linear predictive coding (LPC) of speech. It also yields the reflection (partial correlation) coefficients of the model, whose magnitudes indicate filter stability.

Related methods

Generalizations include the Schur algorithm, the split Levinson recursion, and extensions to block Toeplitz systems. Analogous recursions exist for Toeplitz-plus-Hankel and other structured matrices, forming part of the broader field of fast solvers for structured linear systems.
