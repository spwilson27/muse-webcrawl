Technical writing

Technical writing is a form of communication that conveys complex information about technical or specialized topics in a clear, accurate, and accessible way. The primary goal of technical writing is to enable readers to understand a subject or complete a task. Technical writers produce documents such as manuals, guides, specifications, reports, and online help.

Characteristics

Technical writing is distinguished from other forms of writing by its emphasis on the audience's needs, factual accuracy, a direct and unambiguous style, and structured organization. Documents are typically organized with headings, lists, and graphics so that readers can find information quickly and use it without reading the whole document.

History

Although technical documents have existed since antiquity, technical writing emerged as a distinct profession during and after the Second World War, driven by the growth of military, industrial, and later computer documentation. The spread of personal computers in the 1980s created large demand for software manuals and online help, shaping the modern profession.

Process and tools

Technical writers plan, interview subject-matter experts, draft, edit, and test documents. Common tools include word processors, help authoring tools, content management systems, and structured authoring standards such as DITA (Darwin Information Typing Architecture), which supports topic-based writing and reuse.

Related roles

Technical writers often work alongside editors, illustrators, instructional designers, and user-experience researchers. The broader discipline encompassing their work is known as technical communication.
