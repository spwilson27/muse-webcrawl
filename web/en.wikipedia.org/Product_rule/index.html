Product rule

In calculus, the product rule (or Leibniz rule) is a formula used to find the derivatives of products of two or more functions. For two differentiable functions u and v, it states:

(uv)′ = u′v + uv′

or, in Leibniz notation, d(uv)/dx = u·dv/dx + v·du/dx.

Discovery

The rule is credited to Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz, who demonstrated it in a 1675 manuscript using differentials, though a rigorous proof in terms of limits came later. Isaac Newton had an equivalent formulation in his fluxional calculus.

Intuition and proof

Geometrically, if u and v are the sides of a rectangle, the change in its area when both sides grow consists of two thin strips plus a negligible corner rectangle; dropping the second-order term gives the rule. A standard proof applies the limit definition of the derivative to (u+Δu)(v+Δv) − uv and discards the product of increments.

Generalisations

The rule extends to products of three or more functions, to higher derivatives via the general Leibniz rule (a sum resembling the binomial expansion), and to bilinear operations such as dot and cross products. Its integral counterpart is integration by parts. Abstractly, any operation satisfying the product rule is called a derivation, a central concept in differential algebra.
