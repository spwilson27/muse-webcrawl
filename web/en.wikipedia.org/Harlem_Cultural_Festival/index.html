Harlem Cultural Festival

DatesJune 29 – August 24, 1969 (six Sunday concerts)
LocationMount Morris Park (now Marcus Garvey Park), Harlem, New York City
OrganiserTony Lawrence
AttendanceAbout 300,000 total
GenresSoul, gospel, blues, jazz, funk, Afrobeat influences

The Harlem Cultural Festival was a series of six free music concerts held in Mount Morris Park (now Marcus Garvey Park) in Harlem, Manhattan, during the summer of 1969. Celebrating African American music and culture and promoting Black pride, it drew a total audience of roughly 300,000 and was dubbed the "Black Woodstock", taking place the same summer as the Woodstock festival.

Performers

The line-up included Stevie Wonder, Nina Simone, Sly and the Family Stone, Gladys Knight & the Pips, Mahalia Jackson, B.B. King, the 5th Dimension, the Staple Singers, David Ruffin, Clara Walker, and Hugh Masekela, along with speakers and activists including Jesse Jackson. The July 20 concert took place the day of the Apollo 11 Moon landing; one famous moment captured the crowd's greater excitement for the music than the broadcast announcement. The festival was supported by Mayor John Lindsay's administration and community figures in the tense period following the assassinations of Martin Luther King Jr. and Robert F. Kennedy.

Legacy

Producer Hal Tulchin filmed some 40 hours of the concerts hoping for a television special, but the footage went largely unseen for fifty years, stored in his basement. It was finally assembled by Ahmir "Questlove" Thompson into the documentary Summer of Soul (...Or, When the Revolution Could Not Be Televised), released in 2021 to critical acclaim and winner of the Academy Award for Best Documentary Feature, which brought the festival to wide recognition for the first time.
