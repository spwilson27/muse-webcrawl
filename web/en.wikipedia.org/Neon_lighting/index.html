Neon lighting

Neon lighting consists of brightly glowing, electrified glass tubes or bulbs that contain rarefied neon or other gases. Neon lights were first demonstrated by Georges Claude at the Paris Motor Show in December 1910. While neon lights are used worldwide, their popularity in the United States waned from the 1960s to the 1980s before enjoying renewed interest.

History

Neon is a noble gas that gives off a distinctive reddish-orange glow when used in a discharge tube. The gas was discovered in 1898 by the British chemists Sir William Ramsay and Morris W. Travers. French engineer and chemist Georges Claude patented the neon lamp in 1915 in the United States, and his company, Claude Neon, introduced the first neon signs to America in 1923, selling two signs to a Packard car dealership in Los Angeles.

Colour production

Neon itself produces an orange-red light. Other colours are obtained by using different gases — argon gives a lavender colour, while mercury vapour gives blue — or by coating the inside of the tube with phosphors that fluoresce under ultraviolet light from a mercury discharge. More than 150 colours are possible in principle.

Applications

Neon lighting is used in signage, in artistic installations, and in high-voltage indicator lamps and nixie tubes. Because the tubes can be bent into shapes and letters, neon became a defining feature of roadside advertising, theatres, and urban nightlife districts such as Las Vegas and Hong Kong.
