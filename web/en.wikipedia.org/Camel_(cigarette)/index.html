Camel (cigarette)


Placeholder image: A pack of Camel cigarettes

Camel is an American brand of cigarettes, currently owned and manufactured by the R. J. Reynolds Tobacco Company in the United States and by Japan Tobacco outside the US. Most current Camel cigarettes contain a blend of Turkish tobacco and Virginia tobacco.

History

Richard Joshua "R. J." Reynolds launched the brand in 1913 in Winston-Salem, North Carolina. Camel was notable as the first cigarette sold in a pre-packaged form rather than rolled to order, and its launch was accompanied by a teaser campaign featuring camels — the pack design, showing a dromedary camel against pyramids and palm trees, was based on a photograph of a camel named "Old Joe" from a visiting circus.

By 1919 Camel had become the best-selling cigarette in the United States. Later advertising included the long-running slogan "I'd walk a mile for a Camel" and, from 1987, the cartoon mascot Joe Camel, which was withdrawn in 1997 amid criticism that it appealed to children.
