Comcast Corporation is an American multinational telecommunications and media conglomerate headquartered in Philadelphia. Founded in 1963 by Ralph J. Roberts as a small cable operator in Tupelo, Mississippi, it grew through acquisitions to become the largest American multinational telecommunications conglomerate, the largest cable television company and home Internet service provider in the United States, and the nation's third-largest home telephone service provider. It provides services to residential and commercial customers in 40 states and the District of Columbia.

Operations

Comcast's consumer services are marketed primarily under the Xfinity brand, offering cable television, broadband Internet, home phone, and mobile service. Its media division, NBCUniversal, operates the NBC broadcast network, numerous cable channels (including USA Network, MSNBC and CNBC), the Universal Pictures film studio, and the Universal theme parks. In 2018 Comcast acquired the British broadcaster Sky, extending its reach into European media and pay television.

History

The company was incorporated in 1969 under the name Comcast, a portmanteau of "communications" and "broadcast". It went public in 1972 and expanded steadily, acquiring Group W Cable, Storer Communications, and AT&T Broadband (in 2002), which made it the largest cable operator in the United States. In 2011 it completed the acquisition of a controlling stake in NBCUniversal from General Electric, buying the remainder in 2013. In 2025 the company announced the spin-off of most of its cable networks into a separate company, Versant.

Criticism

Comcast has frequently ranked poorly in customer-satisfaction surveys and has been a prominent participant in debates over net neutrality in the United States. Its proposed 2014 merger with Time Warner Cable was abandoned in 2015 amid regulatory opposition.
