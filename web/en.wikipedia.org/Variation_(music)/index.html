Variation (music)

In music, variation is a formal technique where material is repeated in an altered form. The changes may involve melody, rhythm, harmony, counterpoint, timbre, orchestration, or any combination of these. A composition built on this principle is often organized as a theme and variations: a theme is presented and then restated in a succession of varied forms.

Types of variation

  • Melodic variation — the tune is ornamented or reshaped while the harmony remains largely intact.
  • Harmonic variation — the chords beneath a melody change, as in variations built on a repeating bass line such as the passacaglia and chaconne.
  • Rhythmic variation — metre or rhythmic patterns are altered between statements.
  • Character variation — each variation adopts a distinct mood, style, or genre.

Notable examples

Famous variation sets include Bach's Goldberg Variations, the finale of Beethoven's Eroica Symphony, Brahms's Variations on a Theme by Haydn, Elgar's Enigma Variations, and Rachmaninoff's Rhapsody on a Theme of Paganini. Variation technique also underlies much jazz improvisation, in which soloists elaborate repeatedly over a fixed harmonic progression.
