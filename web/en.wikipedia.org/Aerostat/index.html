Aerostat


A tethered aerostat carrying a radar payload

An aerostat is a lighter-than-air aircraft that gains its lift through buoyancy, using a buoyant gas held inside one or more gas bags or envelopes. The term covers free balloons, moored (tethered) balloons, and steerable airships (dirigibles), although in modern military and technical usage "aerostat" most often refers specifically to a tethered balloon system.

Types

Free balloons drift with the wind and include hot air balloons and gas balloons used for sport, research and weather observation. Tethered aerostats are held in place by one or more cables and are widely used to lift radar, communications and camera payloads to altitudes of a few thousand feet for persistent surveillance; an example is the US Tethered Aerostat Radar System (TARS) used for border and airspace monitoring. Airships are powered, steerable aerostats such as blimps and rigid airships like the Zeppelins.

Principle

By Archimedes' principle, the buoyant lift on the envelope equals the weight of the air displaced minus the weight of the lifting gas and structure. Common lifting gases are helium and hot air; hydrogen was historically used but is flammable.
