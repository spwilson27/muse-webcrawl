Arnold Dolmetsch

Born: 24 February 1858, Le Mans, France

Died: 28 February 1940, Haslemere, England

Occupation: Musician, instrument maker

Arnold Dolmetsch (24 February 1858 – 28 February 1940) was a French-born musician and instrument maker who settled in England and played a leading role in the early music revival of the 20th century. He built copies of historical instruments including harpsichords, clavichords, lutes, viols and recorders, and trained a generation of performers in historically informed performance.

Life and work

Dolmetsch was born in Le Mans to a family of piano makers. He studied at the Brussels Conservatoire and later at the Royal College of Music in London. In 1905 he settled in Haslemere, Surrey, where he established a workshop producing early instruments. His book The Interpretation of the Music of the XVIIth and XVIIIth Centuries (1915) was a landmark in performance practice scholarship.

Haslemere Festival

In 1925 Dolmetsch founded the Haslemere Festival of Early Music, which brought together performers and instrument makers from around the world and helped establish early music as a serious concert discipline. Members of his family, including his wife Mabel and children Natalie, Rudolph and Carl, carried on the workshop after his death.

Legacy

Dolmetsch is widely regarded as the father of the modern early music movement. The Dolmetsch workshop and the Dolmetsch Foundation continue to promote the study and performance of early music.
