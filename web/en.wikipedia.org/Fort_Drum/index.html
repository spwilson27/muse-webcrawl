Fort Drum

The 10th Mountain Division trains in Fort Drum's harsh winter climate.

Fort Drum is a United States Army installation in Jefferson County, New York, near Watertown. Covering about 107,000 acres in the North Country, it is the home of the 10th Mountain Division (Light Infantry) and one of the most frequently deployed installations in the US Army. It is named for Lieutenant General William C. Drum, a former First Army commander.

History

Military training began in the area in 1908, when the site known as Pine Camp hosted summer training for the New York National Guard. The Army purchased the land and greatly expanded it in 1941–42 as Camp Drum, training hundreds of thousands of soldiers during the Second World War; German and Italian prisoners of war were also held there. It became Fort Drum in 1974, and in 1985 the reactivated 10th Mountain Division was assigned there, prompting a billion-dollar construction programme that built a small city of barracks, ranges and the Wheeler-Sack Army Airfield.

Role and units

The post's principal unit is the 10th Mountain Division headquarters with its infantry brigade combat teams, combat aviation brigade and sustainment brigade. Fort Drum also hosts the 91st Military Police Battalion, elements of the New York National Guard and Army Reserve, and the Mission Training Complex. Its large training area, heavy snow — lake-effect storms routinely deposit over 200 inches — and varied terrain make it a premier cold-weather and light-infantry training site, and one of the largest employers in northern New York.

Deployments

Since the 1990s Fort Drum units have deployed to Somalia, Haiti, Bosnia, Kosovo, Iraq and Afghanistan; the 10th Mountain Division was among the most deployed divisions of the post-9/11 era.
