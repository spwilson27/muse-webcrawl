Earle Brown (26 December 1926 – 2 July 2002) was an American composer who established his own formal and notational systems. Along with John Cage, Morton Feldman, and Christian Wolff, he is considered one of the leaders of the New York School of experimental composers.

Life

Brown studied at Northeastern University and the Schillinger House of Music, intending to become a jazz trumpeter and arranger. He performed in big bands, then served in the US Army Air Corps. After settling in Denver and studying at Boston University, he moved to New York City in the early 1950s, where he worked at Capitol Records and produced, with John Cage and David Tudor, the avant-garde concert series on the New York scene.

Open form and graphic notation

Inspired by Alexander Calder's mobiles and Jackson Pollock's action paintings, Brown developed "open form" (also called "mobile") composition: the score gives the performer a degree of choice in the ordering, layering, or transformation of musical material. His graphic scores include December 1952, consisting of horizontal and vertical lines of varying thickness on a page, intended to suggest musical events without conventional pitch notation. He distinguished his approach from Cage's indeterminacy: Brown's works have fixed materials whose forms the performer may reorder, whereas Cage often used chance operations to fix elements.

Works

Major works include Folio (1952), Twenty-five Pages (1953), Available Forms I for 18 players (1961), Available Forms II (1962), Modules I & II, Centering, and large orchestral pieces such as Cross Sections and Color Fields (1975). His scores were published by his own company, Associated Music Publishers.

Legacy

Brown taught at the Peabody Institute and Tanglewood, and his music was championed by conductors including Leonard Bernstein. His innovations in performer choice influenced subsequent generations of composers of aleatoric music, graphic notation, and interactive electronic works. The Earle Brown Music Foundation preserves his archives and scores.
