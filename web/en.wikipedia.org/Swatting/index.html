Swatting

Swatting is a criminal harassment tactic in which a person deceives emergency services into dispatching an armed police response — often a SWAT team, from which the practice takes its name — to another person's address by falsely reporting a serious incident such as a hostage situation, shooting or bomb threat. The practice is dangerous, wastes emergency resources, and has resulted in deaths.

Method

Perpetrators typically conceal their identity using caller ID spoofing, relay services, or internet-based telephony, and may obtain the victim's address through doxing. Swatting is frequently associated with online gaming and livestreaming communities, where it is used to harass streamers, sometimes live on camera, as well as with campaigns against celebrities, politicians, schools and public officials.

Notable incidents

In December 2017, a swatting incident in Wichita, Kansas, led to the death of Andrew Finch, an uninvolved man shot by a police officer responding to a false hostage report arising from a dispute between gamers; the caller, Tyler Barriss, was later sentenced to 20 years in federal prison. Other incidents have targeted the homes of public figures and have forced evacuations of schools and public buildings.

Legal response

Swatting is prosecuted under laws against false emergency reports, wire fraud and interstate threats in the United States, with penalties increased where injury or death results. Legislators in several jurisdictions have introduced specific anti-swatting statutes, and police departments have adopted verification measures, such as registries allowing residents at risk to flag their addresses.
