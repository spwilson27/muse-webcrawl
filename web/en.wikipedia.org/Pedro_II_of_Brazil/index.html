Pedro II (2 December 1825 – 5 December 1891), nicknamed "the Magnanimous", was the second and last emperor of the Empire of Brazil, reigning for over 58 years. Born in Rio de Janeiro, he was the son of Emperor Pedro I and Empress Maria Leopoldina of Austria.

Early reign

Pedro became emperor at the age of five when his father abdicated in 1831. Brazil was governed by regents during his minority, a turbulent period marked by regional revolts. In 1840 the parliament declared him of age, and he was crowned in 1841 at fifteen. The young emperor worked to consolidate national unity and strengthen central authority.

Achievements

Under Pedro II, Brazil enjoyed decades of political stability, economic growth and expanding civil liberties. He promoted education, science and the arts, corresponded with scholars and inventors around the world, and personally encouraged immigration, railways and telegraphs. Brazil emerged victorious from the Paraguayan War (1864–1870), the largest conflict in South American history. Slavery was progressively dismantled — the slave trade was suppressed in 1850, the Law of the Free Womb passed in 1871, and slavery was finally abolished by the Golden Law of 13 May 1888, signed by his daughter Princess Isabel as regent.

Overthrow and exile

The abolition of slavery without compensation alienated the planter class, while the military and republican movements grew disaffected. On 15 November 1889 a military coup proclaimed the Republic, and Pedro, by then elderly and in poor health, accepted exile without resistance. He died in Paris in 1891. His remains were returned to Brazil in 1921 and later interred in Petrópolis.

Legacy

Historians generally regard Pedro II as an unusually conscientious, liberal and scholarly monarch whose long reign shaped modern Brazil; he consistently ranks among the country's most admired historical figures.
