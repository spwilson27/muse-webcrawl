Press release

A press release (also called a news release or media release) is an official statement delivered to members of the news media for the purpose of providing information, making an announcement, or taking a position. Press releases are a primary tool of public relations, used by corporations, governments, nonprofits, and public figures to generate media coverage.

Structure

The conventional press release is written in the style of a news article, following the inverted pyramid: a headline, a dateline, and a lead paragraph summarizing who, what, when, where, and why, followed by supporting detail, a quotable statement from a spokesperson, and a boilerplate paragraph describing the issuing organization. Releases are often distributed through wire services such as PR Newswire or Business Wire, or sent directly to journalists under an embargo specifying when the information may be published. The first modern press release is generally credited to Ivy Lee, who issued one on behalf of the Pennsylvania Railroad after a 1906 train wreck.

Use and criticism

While press releases provide journalists with verifiable, attributable information, media critics have long complained about "churnalism"—the verbatim republication of releases as news without independent reporting. In the digital era, releases are also published directly online for search visibility and to reach audiences without journalistic intermediaries.
