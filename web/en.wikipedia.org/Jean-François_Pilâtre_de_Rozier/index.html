Jean-François Pilâtre de Rozier

Born: 30 March 1754, Metz, France

Died: 15 June 1785, Wimereux, France

Known for: First manned free balloon flight

Jean-François Pilâtre de Rozier (30 March 1754 – 15 June 1785) was a French chemistry and physics teacher and one of the first pioneers of aviation. On 21 November 1783 he and the Marquis d'Arlandes made the first manned free flight in a Montgolfier hot air balloon, travelling about 9 kilometres across Paris.

Early life

Born in Metz, Pilâtre de Rozier moved to Paris, where he taught physics and chemistry and founded a technical museum, the Musée de Monsieur, patronized by the court. His demonstrations of scientific experiments brought him to the attention of the Montgolfier brothers' circle.

Ballooning

After earlier tethered ascents in October 1783, Pilâtre de Rozier and d'Arlandes rose from the Château de la Muette in the first untethered manned flight, staying aloft about 25 minutes. In June 1784 he flew before the King of Sweden in a balloon named in honour of Marie Antoinette.

Death

Attempting to cross the English Channel from France, Pilâtre de Rozier used a hybrid balloon combining a hydrogen cell above a hot-air bag — the Rozière balloon. On 15 June 1785 the balloon deflated and fell near Wimereux, killing him and his companion Pierre Romain. They were the first known fatalities in an air crash.
