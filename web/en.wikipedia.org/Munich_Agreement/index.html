Munich Agreement

Signed: 30 September 1938
Location: Munich, Germany
Signatories: Neville Chamberlain, Édouard Daladier, Adolf Hitler, Benito Mussolini
Effect: Sudetenland ceded to Germany

The Munich Agreement was a settlement reached in the early hours of 30 September 1938 by Germany, the United Kingdom, France and Italy, permitting Nazi Germany's annexation of the Sudetenland, the largely German-speaking border regions of Czechoslovakia. Czechoslovakia itself was not invited to the negotiations. The agreement is the most famous product of the policy of appeasement and has become a byword for the futility of conceding to dictators' demands.

Background

After Austria's Anschluss in March 1938, Hitler pressed claims on behalf of the three million ethnic Germans in the Sudetenland, whose grievances were championed by Konrad Henlein's Sudeten German Party. Czechoslovakia possessed strong border fortifications and alliances with France and the Soviet Union, but Britain's prime minister Neville Chamberlain, determined to avoid war, flew to meet Hitler three times in September 1938 — at Berchtesgaden, Bad Godesberg, and finally Munich — each time accepting expanded German demands.

Terms and aftermath

Germany was to occupy the Sudetenland between 1 and 10 October 1938; the four powers guaranteed the remainder of Czechoslovakia, a guarantee never honoured. Chamberlain returned to London waving the Anglo-German declaration and proclaiming "peace for our time." Poland and Hungary also seized Czechoslovak border territories in the weeks that followed. In March 1939 Germany occupied the rest of Bohemia and Moravia, destroying the Munich settlement outright. The loss of the Sudeten fortifications and the Škoda armaments works left Czechoslovakia indefensible and transferred major industrial capacity to Germany.

Legacy

"Munich" entered political language as a synonym for dishonourable capitulation, and the "lessons of Munich" have been invoked in debates over foreign crises ever since. Historians continue to debate whether the agreement bought valuable time for British rearmament or simply strengthened Germany at the cost of a democratic ally. Less than a year later, on 1 September 1939, Germany invaded Poland and the Second World War began.
