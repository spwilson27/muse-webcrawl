Samwise Gamgee, usually called Sam, is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's novel The Lord of the Rings. A hobbit of the Shire, he is the gardener and loyal companion of the Ring-bearer Frodo Baggins, whom he accompanies on the quest to destroy the One Ring. Tolkien regarded Sam as one of the chief heroes of the story, and critics widely read him as the novel's emotional centre and an everyman figure modelled on the English working-class soldiers Tolkien had known in the First World War.

Role in the story

Sam, son of Hamfast "the Gaffer" Gamgee, works as gardener at Bag End. Caught eavesdropping on Gandalf and Frodo's discussion of the Ring, he is assigned to accompany Frodo into exile. He becomes a member of the Fellowship of the Ring, and after the Fellowship breaks at Amon Hen he follows Frodo toward Mordor, sharing the burden of the journey, guiding the treacherous Gollum, and defending Frodo from the giant spider Shelob — briefly bearing the Ring himself when he believes Frodo dead, then freely returning it, one of very few characters to surrender the Ring willingly.

In Mordor, Sam rescues Frodo from the Tower of Cirith Ungol and, in one of the book's most famous scenes, carries Frodo up the slopes of Mount Doom. After the War of the Ring he helps scour the Shire, marries Rosie Cotton, fathers thirteen children, and is elected Mayor of the Shire seven times. Having been a Ring-bearer, he is at last permitted to sail into the West.

Character and reception

Tolkien called Sam "the chief hero" of the tale in a 1951 letter, describing his arc from rustic simplicity to reflective dignity — including his vision of the Star of Eärendil in Mordor — as one of the most moving in the book. Scholars have analysed Sam's servant-master relationship with Frodo, his rooted Englishness, and his role as the story's principal link between the humble Shire and the high romance of the Elves. In Peter Jackson's film trilogy Sam is played by Sean Astin, whose performance is frequently singled out among the cast.
