Online shopping

Online shopping is a form of electronic commerce which allows consumers to directly buy goods or services from a seller over the Internet using a web browser or a mobile app. Consumers find a product by visiting a retailer's website directly or by searching among alternative vendors using a shopping search engine.

Online shopping evolved with the World Wide Web in the 1990s. Michael Aldrich is credited with inventing online shopping in 1979 by connecting a modified television to a transaction computer via telephone. The first secure retail transaction on the web, a sale by NetMarket in 1994, and the launches of Amazon and eBay in 1995, popularised the model.

Business models

Models include business-to-consumer (B2C), business-to-business (B2B), consumer-to-consumer (C2C) marketplaces, dropshipping and subscription commerce. Payment is typically by credit card, debit card, digital wallet or buy-now-pay-later services, with goods delivered by post, courier, or collected in store.

Advantages and issues

Advantages include convenience, wider selection, price comparison and home delivery. Issues include inability to inspect goods, delivery delays, fraud, privacy, returns logistics and the environmental cost of packaging and transport. Global e-commerce sales exceeded several trillion US dollars annually in the 2020s, accelerated by the COVID-19 pandemic.
