Gettysburg National Military Park preserves the landscape of the Battle of Gettysburg, fought on 1–3 July 1863 during the American Civil War and widely regarded as the war's turning point. Located around the town of Gettysburg in Adams County, Pennsylvania, the park protects most of the battlefield, its supporting areas, and many noncontiguous sections, and contains well over a thousand monuments and markers — one of the largest collections of outdoor sculpture in the world.

History of the park

Preservation began in 1864 with the Gettysburg Battlefield Memorial Association, and the battlefield became a federal military park in 1895 under the War Department, passing to the National Park Service in 1933. Veterans placed most of the monuments marking their units' positions. The park adjoins Gettysburg National Cemetery, where President Abraham Lincoln delivered the Gettysburg Address in November 1863.

Features

Key sites include Little Round Top, Cemetery Ridge, the High Water Mark of the Confederacy, the Peach Orchard, the Wheatfield, Devil's Den and the ground of Pickett's Charge. A museum and visitor centre, opened in 2008, displays the restored Gettysburg Cyclorama painting of the battle. Ranger-led programmes and a licensed battlefield guide service operate year-round; the park draws roughly a million visitors annually.
