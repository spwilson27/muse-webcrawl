Cyclohexane

The chair conformation of cyclohexane, the classic example of a puckered ring.

Cyclohexane is a cycloalkane with the molecular formula C6H12. It is a colourless, flammable liquid with a detergent-like odour, produced in very large quantities as a precursor to nylon. Global production runs to several million tonnes per year.

Production and uses

Almost all cyclohexane is made by catalytic hydrogenation of benzene. Controlled oxidation of cyclohexane yields "KA oil" — a mixture of cyclohexanone and cyclohexanol — which is converted to adipic acid and caprolactam, the monomers for nylon-6,6 and nylon-6 respectively. Smaller amounts serve as a solvent, a paint remover component, and a calibration standard in calorimetry and chromatography.

Conformation

Cyclohexane is the textbook example of conformational analysis. The ring is not planar: it adopts a chair conformation in which all C–C–C bond angles are near the ideal tetrahedral angle and torsional strain is minimised. Each carbon bears one axial and one equatorial hydrogen. At room temperature the ring rapidly flips between two equivalent chairs, interchanging axial and equatorial positions — a motion observable by NMR. Substituted cyclohexanes prefer the conformer placing the largest group equatorial, and the energy cost of axial placement (the "A-value") is a standard measure of steric size. Half-chair, twist-boat and boat forms lie higher in energy along the interconversion pathway.

Safety

Cyclohexane is highly flammable and an aspiration hazard; chronic exposure affects the central nervous system. It occurs naturally in crude oil and is released by volcanoes and forest fires as well as by industry.
