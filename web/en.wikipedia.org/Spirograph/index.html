Spirograph

A Spirograph is a geometric drawing device that produces mathematical curves known as hypotrochoids and epitrochoids. It consists of plastic gears with teeth that mesh with fixed rings; a pen inserted in a hole of a rotating gear traces intricate repeating patterns as the gear rolls around the ring.

History

The toy was developed by the British engineer Denys Fisher and first sold in 1965; it was marketed in the United States by Kenner from 1966 and has been reissued several times since. Similar geometric drawing instruments had existed since the nineteenth century.

Mathematics

The curves produced are roulette curves: the path of a point fixed relative to a circle rolling inside (hypotrochoid) or outside (epitrochoid) another circle. The pattern depends on the ratio of the gear and ring sizes and the position of the pen hole.
