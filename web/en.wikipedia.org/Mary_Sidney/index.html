Mary Sidney Herbert, Countess of Pembroke (née Sidney; 27 October 1561 – 25 September 1621) was one of the first English women to achieve a major reputation as a poet and literary patron. She was the sister of Sir Philip Sidney, at whose death in 1586 she inherited his literary circle, and the centre of the Wilton House salon. By completing her brother's metrical translation of the Psalms she created one of the most influential devotional works of the English Renaissance.

Life

Mary Sidney was the daughter of Sir Henry Sidney and Mary Dudley. Highly educated in languages, music, and literature, she married Henry Herbert, 2nd Earl of Pembroke, in 1577 and made Wilton House a centre of literary patronage. Her sons William and Philip Herbert were later the dedicatees of Shakespeare's First Folio. She also survived smallpox that scarred her — her mother had been similarly disfigured nursing Queen Elizabeth I through the disease.

Works

After Philip Sidney's death at the Battle of Zutphen, Mary completed the Sidney Psalms: Sidney had translated Psalms 1–43, and she rendered the remaining 107 in a dazzling variety of stanza forms and metres, drawing on the Geneva Bible and French models. She also translated Robert Garnier's French tragedy as Antonius (1592), helping found the English Senecan closet-drama tradition, and translated Petrarch's Triumph of Death and Philippe de Mornay's Discourse of Life and Death. Her original poems include the pastoral dialogue "A Dialogue between two shepherds, Thenot and Piers" and the dedication poem "To the Angel Spirit of the most excellent Sir Philip Sidney."

Legacy

Long read mainly as her brother's editor and memorialist, Sidney Herbert is now recognised by scholars as a major Tudor poet and a pioneering woman of letters; John Donne praised the Sidney Psalms in verse. She died in London in 1621 and was buried in Salisbury Cathedral.
