Audiology

An audiologist conducting a pure-tone audiometry test.

Audiology is a branch of science that studies hearing, balance, and related disorders. Audiologists treat those with hearing loss and proactively prevent related damage. Employing various testing strategies — such as pure tone audiometry, otoacoustic emission testing, and electrophysiologic tests — audiologists aim to determine whether someone has normal sensitivity to sounds. If hearing loss is identified, audiologists counsel on hearing aids, cochlear implants, and other assistive technologies, and provide rehabilitation.

Scope of practice

Audiologists work in hospitals, clinics, schools, universities, and private practice. The profession emerged as a distinct discipline during and after the Second World War, initially to rehabilitate soldiers with noise-induced hearing loss. In the United States, audiology is a doctoral-level profession, with the Doctor of Audiology (Au.D.) the standard entry qualification; practice is regulated by state licensure.

Areas of study

The field encompasses paediatric and geriatric hearing assessment, vestibular (balance) disorders, tinnitus management, occupational hearing conservation, and the fitting and verification of hearing devices.
