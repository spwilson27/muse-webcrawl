Amoraim

Amoraim (Aramaic: plural of amora, "speaker" or "expounder") were Jewish scholars of the period from about 200 to 500 CE who commented on and transmitted the teachings of the earlier Tannaim. Their discussions form the Gemara, which together with the Mishnah constitutes the Talmud.

Period and centers

The Amoraic period followed the redaction of the Mishnah by Judah the Prince (c. 200 CE) and lasted until the completion of the Babylonian Talmud around 500 CE. Two great centers produced the two Talmuds: the academies of Roman Palestine (Tiberias, Sepphoris, Caesarea), producing the Jerusalem Talmud, and the Babylonian academies of Sura, Pumbedita, and Nehardea, producing the Babylonian Talmud.

Notable Amoraim

Prominent Amoraim include Rav and Samuel in Babylonia; Rabbi Yochanan and Resh Lakish in Palestine; and later Babylonian masters such as Rav Ashi, traditionally credited with beginning the redaction of the Babylonian Talmud, and Ravina. The Amoraim were succeeded by the Savoraim, who gave the Babylonian Talmud its final form.
