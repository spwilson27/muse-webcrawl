Partitions of Poland

The Partitions of Poland were three partitions of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth carried out by the Russian Empire, the Kingdom of Prussia and the Habsburg monarchy in 1772, 1793 and 1795, which together eliminated sovereign Poland and Lithuania until 1918.

Background

By the 18th century the Commonwealth, paralysed by the liberum veto and foreign interference (Silent Sejm 1717; Repnin Sejm 1767–68), had become a Russian protectorate in practice.

The three partitions

1772: After the Bar Confederation's civil war, the three powers annexed about 30% of territory and 35% of population; the Partition Sejm ratified under duress (Tadeusz Rejtan's protest). 1793: Following the Constitution of 3 May and the Targowica Confederation, Russia and Prussia took further vast territories; the Grodno Sejm ratified. 1795: After the Kościuszko Uprising failed, the remainder was divided; King Stanisław August abdicated, Warsaw went to Prussia, Kraków to Austria, Vilnius to Russia.

Aftermath

For 123 years no Polish state existed, though uprisings (1830, 1863), Napoleon's Duchy of Warsaw (1807–15) and cultural resistance kept the question alive until independence in 1918.
