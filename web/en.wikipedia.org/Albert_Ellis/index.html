Albert Ellis

Albert Ellis (September 27, 1913 – July 24, 2007) was an American psychologist who in 1955 developed Rational Emotive Behavior Therapy (REBT). Originally trained in psychoanalysis, he became disillusioned with its pace and passivity and went on to become one of the founders of the cognitive-behavioural therapy movement.

Early life and career

Ellis was born in Pittsburgh and raised in New York City. A shy young man, he famously overcame his fear of speaking to women by forcing himself to approach strangers in the Bronx Botanical Garden. He earned his doctorate in clinical psychology from Columbia University in 1947 and initially practised psychoanalysis before breaking with it.

Rational Emotive Behavior Therapy

REBT holds that emotional disturbance is caused not by events themselves but by the irrational beliefs people hold about them — Ellis's "ABC" model: an Activating event triggers Beliefs, which produce emotional and behavioural Consequences. Therapy focuses on actively disputing rigid demands ("musts") and replacing them with rational, flexible preferences. Ellis founded the Institute for Rational Living in 1959 (later the Albert Ellis Institute) and promoted his approach through books such as A Guide to Rational Living (1961).

Legacy

Surveys of psychologists have ranked Ellis among the most influential psychotherapists of the twentieth century. His energetic, confrontational style and Friday-night workshops in New York made REBT widely known, and his work paved the way for Aaron Beck's cognitive therapy.
