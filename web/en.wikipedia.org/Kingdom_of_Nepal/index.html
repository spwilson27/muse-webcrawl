The Kingdom of Nepal was a Hindu kingdom in South Asia, formed in 1768 by the unification of Nepal under Prithvi Narayan Shah of the Gorkha Kingdom. It lasted for 240 years until the abolition of the monarchy in 2008, when Nepal became a federal democratic republic, ending the world’s last Hindu monarchy.

Unification and expansion

Prithvi Narayan Shah conquered the Kathmandu Valley in 1768–69 and made Kathmandu his capital. His successors expanded the kingdom until it stretched from Sikkim to Garhwal. Defeat in the Anglo-Nepalese War (1814–16) fixed much of Nepal’s modern boundary under the Treaty of Sugauli, and the kingdom supplied the famous Gurkha soldiers to British service.

The Shah and Rana eras

From 1846 to 1951 the hereditary Rana prime ministers ruled Nepal in fact while the Shah kings reigned as figureheads. King Tribhuvan’s restoration in 1951 opened the country to the world. King Mahendra imposed the partyless Panchayat system in 1960, which lasted until the 1990 People’s Movement restored multiparty democracy under a constitutional monarchy.

End of the monarchy

A Maoist insurgency from 1996 to 2006, the 2001 royal massacre in which Crown Prince Dipendra killed King Birendra and much of the royal family, and King Gyanendra’s 2005 takeover eroded the monarchy’s legitimacy. The Constituent Assembly abolished the kingdom on 28 May 2008.
