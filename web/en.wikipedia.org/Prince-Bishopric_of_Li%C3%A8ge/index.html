The Prince-Bishopric of Liège was an ecclesiastical principality of the Holy Roman Empire in the present-day Belgium. It was created in 980, when Bishop Notger received secular power over the County of Huy from Emperor Otto II, and it endured until the French Revolutionary Wars, when it was annexed by France in 1795.

History

The diocese of Liège had been founded in the 4th century, with its seat moved to Liège by Saint Hubert in the 8th century. Under Notger and his successors, the prince-bishops accumulated territory across the Meuse valley. The principality was never coterminous with the diocese, which extended much further.

During the later Middle Ages the citizens of Liège repeatedly clashed with their prince-bishops, and the city was burned by Charles the Bold of Burgundy in 1468. The principality remained a battleground in the wars between France and the Habsburgs. In 1789 the Liège Revolution overthrew the prince-bishop, foreshadowing annexation by revolutionary France and eventual incorporation into the United Kingdom of the Netherlands and then Belgium.
