The aardwolf (Proteles cristatus) is an insectivorous hyaenid native to East and Southern Africa. Its name means "earth-wolf" in Afrikaans and Dutch. Unlike the other three hyena species, it does not hunt large prey but feeds almost entirely on termites, licking them up with a long, sticky tongue — a single aardwolf may consume up to 250,000–300,000 termites in one night.

Description

The aardwolf resembles a small, striped hyena, with a yellowish-white to red-brown coat marked with dark vertical stripes and a prominent mane of long hair along its back that can be raised when threatened. Its molars are reduced to small pegs, an adaptation to its insect diet, though it retains large canine teeth used in territorial disputes.

Behaviour and habitat

Aardwolves are shy and nocturnal, sheltering by day in burrows — often disused aardvark burrows. They live mainly in arid, open plains and bushland in two distinct populations, one in southern Africa and one in East and Northeast Africa. Monogamous pairs defend territories scent-marked with anal gland secretions, and rear cubs in dens. When attacked, an aardwolf may mimic the larger striped hyena by raising its mane to appear bigger.
