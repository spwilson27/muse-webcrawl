Register (sociolinguistics)

Martin Joos's five clocks: a classic model of register.

In sociolinguistics, a register is a variety of language used for a particular purpose or in a particular communicative situation. For example, speakers typically use formal language in a job interview and informal language among friends, even though both use the same language. Register is usually analysed along dimensions of formality, and manifests in vocabulary, grammar, pronunciation and discourse structure.

Joos's five styles

Martin Joos (1961) proposed five speech styles, sometimes called the "five clocks": frozen (fixed texts such as oaths and prayers), formal (one-way, complete sentences, as in a lecture), consultative (two-way professional talk, as between doctor and patient), casual (conversation among peers, with slang and ellipsis) and intimate (private codes between close partners or family).

Halliday's framework

In systemic functional linguistics, M. A. K. Halliday analysed register through three situational variables: field (what is happening), tenor (who is taking part and their relationships) and mode (the channel and role of language). A register, in this view, is the configuration of semantic choices typically associated with a given situation type; specialised registers such as legal, medical or scientific English are common examples.

Register and dialect

Register contrasts with dialect: dialect varies according to the user (region, class, ethnicity), whereas register varies according to the use — the same speaker commands many registers but normally speaks one dialect. Sudden register shifts can be comic, rude or marked, and control of prestigious registers is a form of linguistic capital in education and employment.
