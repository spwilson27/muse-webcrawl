Maximilian Bircher-Benner

Maximilian Oskar Bircher-Benner
Born: 22 August 1867, Aarau, Switzerland
Died: 24 January 1939 (aged 71), Zurich, Switzerland
Occupation: Physician, nutritionist
Known for: Inventing muesli (Birchermüesli)

Maximilian Oskar Bircher-Benner (22 August 1867 – 24 January 1939) was a Swiss physician and pioneering nutritionist best known as the inventor of muesli.

Life and work

Bircher-Benner studied medicine at the University of Zurich. As a young doctor suffering from jaundice, he reported curing himself with a diet of raw apples, which convinced him of the healing power of raw fruits and vegetables. In 1897 he opened a small sanatorium in Zurich, and in 1904 the famous "Lebendige Kraft" sanatorium on the Zürichberg, where patients — who over the years included prominent figures from across Europe — followed a regimen of raw-food diet, strict daily routine, exercise, and sun and air baths.

Birchermüesli

Around 1900 he developed the dish he called "Apfeldiätspeise" — grated raw apple mixed with rolled oats soaked in water, lemon juice, condensed milk, and ground nuts — inspired by a dish served in the Swiss Alps. Known as Birchermüesli, it was served as the first course of each meal at the clinic and eventually spread worldwide as muesli.

Theory and legacy

Bircher-Benner organised foods by their content of what he called "living energy" from sunlight, ranking raw plant foods highest — a system pre-dating modern nutritional science and partly at odds with it, but influential in popularising whole foods, and his clinic's philosophy anticipated much of today's whole-food movement.
