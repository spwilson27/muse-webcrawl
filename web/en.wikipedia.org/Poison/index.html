A poison is any chemical substance that is harmful or lethal to living organisms when introduced in sufficient quantity — a concept summed up in Paracelsus's dictum that "the dose makes the poison." In biology, poisons are substances that cause death, injury or harm to organs, usually by chemical reaction or other activity at the molecular scale, when an organism absorbs a sufficient quantity. The legal and industrial senses of the term are somewhat narrower, covering substances officially labelled as such.

Terminology

Poisons delivered by bite or sting are conventionally called venoms: a venom is injected, while a poison is typically ingested, inhaled or absorbed through the skin — animals may be "poisonous" (harmful to eat or touch) or "venomous" (harmful when they bite). Toxins are poisons produced by living organisms, such as the botulinum toxin of Clostridium botulinum, the most acutely lethal toxin known. The study of poisons is toxicology.

Types and effects

Poisons act by diverse mechanisms: neurotoxins disrupt nerve impulses; cytotoxins destroy cells; hemotoxins damage blood; enzyme inhibitors such as cyanide block cellular respiration; heavy metals such as lead, mercury and arsenic poison enzymes and accumulate in tissue. Effects range from irritation and nausea to organ failure and death, and exposure may be acute or chronic. Many poisons have antidotes or treatments, from activated charcoal and chelation therapy to specific agents such as naloxone for opioid overdose and antivenoms.

Uses and history

Poisons have been used throughout history for hunting (arrow poisons such as curare), execution (hemlock, used to kill Socrates), murder (arsenic, the "inheritance powder"), warfare (chemical weapons) and pest control. Famous poisonings include those of Alexander Litvinenko (polonium-210) and the attempted poisoning of Sergei Skripal with a Novichok nerve agent. Conversely, many poisons are valuable medicines at controlled doses, a heritage reflected in the fact that numerous drugs derive from plant alkaloids.

Regulation

Poisons are regulated under national poisons laws and the Globally Harmonized System of classification and labelling, which uses the skull-and-crossbones pictogram for acute toxicity. Poison control centres provide emergency advice worldwide.
