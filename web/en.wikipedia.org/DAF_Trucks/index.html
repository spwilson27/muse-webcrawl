DAF Trucks

DAF Trucks is a Dutch truck manufacturer headquartered in Eindhoven, Netherlands, and a subsidiary of the American company Paccar since 1996. Its principal products are the LF, CF (now XD) and XF series of commercial trucks, sold widely across Europe and exported worldwide.

History

The company traces its origins to 1928, when Hub van Doorne founded a machine repair shop in Eindhoven. The initials DAF derive from Van Doorne's Aanhangwagenfabriek (trailer factory); it began building trailers and, after the Second World War, trucks. DAF also produced passenger cars from 1958, notable for the Variomatic continuously variable transmission, before selling its car division to Volvo in 1975. After bankruptcy in 1993, the truck business was relaunched and acquired by Paccar in 1996.

Operations

DAF builds trucks in Eindhoven, in Westerlo, Belgium, and through its Leyland Trucks plant in the United Kingdom, and produces engines at Eindhoven. Its XF model has won the International Truck of the Year award, most recently the new-generation XF, XG and XG+ range launched for 2022.
