Group f/64

Group f/64 was a group of seven 20th-century San Francisco photographers who shared a common photographic style characterised by sharply focused, carefully framed images made with large-format cameras stopped down to small apertures. Formed in 1932, the group included Ansel Adams, Imogen Cunningham, John Paul Edwards, Preston Holder, Consuelo Kanaga, Willard Van Dyke and Edward Weston. The name refers to f/64, then the smallest aperture setting available on large-format lenses, which maximised depth of field.

History and philosophy

The group emerged in reaction against the soft-focus, painterly pictorialism then dominant in art photography. Its 1932 manifesto declared that pure photography "possesses no qualities of technic, composition or idea, derivative of another art form" and championed direct, unmanipulated printing of negatives made with maximum sharpness. Their first exhibition opened at the M. H. de Young Memorial Museum in San Francisco in November 1932.

Legacy

Group f/64 is credited with establishing "straight photography" as the dominant modernist idiom on the American West Coast, influencing generations of landscape and documentary photographers. The group effectively dissolved by the end of the 1930s as members' careers diverged.
