Kingdom of Galicia and Lodomeria

The Kingdom of Galicia and Lodomeria was a crownland of the Habsburg Monarchy, the Austrian Empire, and Austria-Hungary from 1772 to 1918. It comprised lands taken from the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth in the Partitions of Poland, in what is now southeastern Poland and western Ukraine.

Creation

Austria seized the territory in the First Partition of Poland (1772), reviving medieval Hungarian claims to the principalities of Halych (Galicia) and Volodymyr (Lodomeria) to legitimate the annexation. The Third Partition (1795) added "West Galicia" around Kraków and Lublin, though those lands were lost in 1809; Kraków became a free city until annexed in 1846. The capital was Lemberg (Polish Lwów, Ukrainian Lviv).

Society

Galicia was the largest, most populous, and poorest crownland of the empire — "Galician misery" became a byword. Its population was multiethnic: Poles predominated in the west, Ukrainians (Ruthenians) in the east, alongside a large Jewish population and German officials and colonists. After 1867 the province enjoyed autonomy under Polish-dominated self-government, and Poles held ministerial posts in Vienna, while Ukrainian national movements grew in the east around Lemberg and the Greek Catholic Church. Galicia was a major source of emigration to the Americas and site of early oil fields around Borysław and Drohobycz.

First World War and dissolution

Galicia was a principal Eastern Front battleground in 1914–1917, fought over in the Battle of Galicia and the Gorlice–Tarnów offensive, with immense destruction. In November 1918 the collapsing empire's Galician lands were claimed by both the new Polish republic and the West Ukrainian People's Republic, whose war ended with Polish control; the territory was divided between interwar Poland and, after 1945, Poland and the Ukrainian SSR.
