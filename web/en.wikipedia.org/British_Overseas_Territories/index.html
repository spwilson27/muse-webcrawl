British Overseas Territories

The British Overseas Territories (BOTs) are fourteen territories with constitutional links to the United Kingdom that do not form part of the UK itself. They are the surviving remnants of the British Empire that have not been granted independence or have voted to remain under British sovereignty. Although largely self-governing, they are represented by the UK in foreign affairs and defence, and most have the British monarch as head of state.

The territories

The fourteen territories are Anguilla, Bermuda, the British Antarctic Territory, the British Indian Ocean Territory, the British Virgin Islands, the Cayman Islands, the Falkland Islands, Gibraltar, Montserrat, the Pitcairn Islands, Saint Helena, Ascension and Tristan da Cunha, South Georgia and the South Sandwich Islands, the Turks and Caicos Islands, and the Sovereign Base Areas of Akrotiri and Dhekelia on Cyprus. Their combined population is roughly 270,000, the largest communities being Bermuda, the Cayman Islands, and Gibraltar. Several are major offshore financial centres, while others are valued chiefly for scientific research and conservation; together they hold a large share of the world's penguins, albatrosses, and other seabirds.

Governance

Until 2002 they were officially termed British Dependent Territories; the British Overseas Territories Act 2002 renamed them and restored full British citizenship to their inhabitants. Each inhabited territory has its own constitution, legislature, and elected government, with a governor appointed by London responsible for external affairs, defence, and internal security. Sovereignty disputes persist over Gibraltar (claimed by Spain), the Falkland Islands (claimed by Argentina), and the Chagos Archipelago of the British Indian Ocean Territory (claimed by Mauritius).
