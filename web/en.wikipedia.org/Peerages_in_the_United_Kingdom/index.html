Peerages in the United Kingdom

The peerages in the United Kingdom are titles of nobility held under the Crown, divided historically among the Peerage of England, the Peerage of Scotland, the Peerage of Ireland, the Peerage of Great Britain and the Peerage of the United Kingdom. The ranks, in descending order, are duke, marquess, earl, viscount and baron (in Scotland, lord of Parliament).

Hereditary and life peerages

Most historic peerages are hereditary. Since the Life Peerages Act 1958, the Crown has also created life peers—almost always barons—who sit in the House of Lords for their own lifetimes only.

Role in Parliament

Until 1999 hereditary peers generally had an automatic right to sit in the House of Lords. The House of Lords Act 1999 removed that right for most, leaving 92 hereditary peers elected to remain. Life peers continue to form the bulk of the chamber's membership.

Creation today

New hereditary peerages are now created only rarely, generally for members of the royal family; new life peerages are announced through honours lists and resignation or dissolution lists on the advice of the prime minister.
