The Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA) is a worldwide study by the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) intended to evaluate educational systems by measuring 15-year-old school pupils' performance in mathematics, science and reading.

Design

First conducted in 2000 and repeated every three years, PISA tests students' ability to apply knowledge to real-life problems rather than simply reproduce curriculum content. Each cycle emphasises one major domain — reading, mathematics or science — while assessing the others, and also collects background questionnaires from students and schools. Recent cycles have added domains such as financial literacy, collaborative problem solving and creative thinking.

Results and influence

Consistently high-performing systems have included Singapore, Japan, South Korea, Canada, Finland and Estonia. PISA results attract intense media and policy attention and have prompted education reforms in many countries — most famously Germany's "PISA shock" after 2001 — though critics question cross-cultural comparability, the tests' influence on curricula, and the narrowing of educational goals to what PISA measures.
