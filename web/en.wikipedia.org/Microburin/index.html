Microburin

A microburin is a small, characteristic waste product of the manufacture of microliths — tiny flint tools of Mesolithic and Neolithic hunter-gatherers — produced using the microburin technique. Archaeologists use microburins as diagnostic evidence that microliths were made at a site, since the microburin itself was usually discarded.

The technique

To standardise the size of microliths, a knapper cut a notch into the edge of a flint blade and then snapped the blade at the notch, producing two pieces. The piece with the trihedral point (the "krukowski microburin" is a related accidental type) — typically the proximal waste end — is the microburin, while the remaining segment was further retouched into a geometric microlith such as a crescent, triangle or trapeze. The method is documented worldwide but is especially characteristic of the European Mesolithic and the Natufian culture of the Levant.

Identification

A true microburin shows a flat facet formed by the snap, meeting the notched edge of the original blade segment. Because they are waste, microburins are often far more numerous at a site than finished microliths, making them a reliable marker of tool production loci and occupation intensity.
