Anchovy

FamilyEngraulidae
Species144 in 17 genera
SizeTypically 12–20 cm
HabitatCoastal marine and brackish waters worldwide
DietPlankton, fish larvae

An anchovy is a small, common forage fish of the family Engraulidae. Most species are found in marine waters, but several enter brackish water, and some in South America are restricted to fresh water. Anchovies are small, green fish with blue reflections due to a silver-coloured stripe, ranging from about 2 cm to 40 cm in length, most commonly around 12–20 cm.

Ecology

Anchovies feed on plankton and recently hatched fish and are in turn a crucial food source for larger fish, seabirds, and marine mammals. The Peruvian anchoveta (Engraulis ringens) supports the largest single-species fishery in the world by weight, yielding several million tonnes annually, mostly reduced to fishmeal — though catches collapse dramatically during El Niño events. Anchovy-rich waters such as the Andaman Sea similarly support important regional fisheries.

As food

Anchovies have a strong, distinctive flavour when cured in salt and packed in oil, and are used in Caesar salad, Worcestershire sauce, pizza toppings, and the fermented fish sauces of Southeast Asia. Fresh anchovies — such as the white anchovies (boquerones) of Spain — are milder and are marinated or fried.
