Federal Court of Justice

The Federal Court of Justice (Bundesgerichtshof) in Karlsruhe is Germany's highest court for civil and criminal matters.

The Federal Court of Justice (German: Bundesgerichtshof, BGH) is the highest court of the Federal Republic of Germany in the area of ordinary jurisdiction (civil and criminal law). It is the German equivalent of a supreme court for private and criminal law. It is located in Karlsruhe, with one senate sitting in Leipzig.

Established on 1 October 1950, the court reviews decisions of the higher regional courts (Oberlandesgerichte) on points of law only (Revision); it does not re-hear evidence. Alongside the Federal Constitutional Court, the Federal Administrative Court, the Federal Fiscal Court, the Federal Labour Court, and the Federal Social Court, it is one of Germany's federal supreme courts.

Organisation

The court is divided into senates (panels) specialising in civil or criminal matters — twelve civil senates and six criminal senates at various times, plus specialised senates for matters such as cartel law and patent law. Judges are selected by the Richterwahlausschuss, a body composed of state justice ministers and members appointed by the Bundestag. The court's decisions have shaped German private law, including rulings on contract, tort, and consumer protection.
