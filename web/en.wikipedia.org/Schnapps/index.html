Schnapps

Schnapps (German: Schnaps) is a term applied to several kinds of distilled alcoholic beverages. True schnapps in the German tradition is a clear, colorless spirit made by fermenting and distilling fruit — commonly apples, pears, plums, cherries, or apricots — without added sugar or flavoring. The result is similar to a fruit brandy or eau de vie, usually bottled at around 40% alcohol by volume, and is traditionally drunk neat at room temperature.

European schnapps

In Germany, Austria, and neighboring countries, Obstler or Obstbrand is distilled from fermented fruit; varieties include Kirschwasser (cherry), Williams (pear), and Zwetschgenwasser (plum). Grain-based schnapps include Korn, a clear spirit distilled from rye or wheat. Herbal liqueur-like products such as Kräuterlikör are also colloquially grouped under the term. In Scandinavia, related clear spirits include aquavit and the various flavored brännvin drinks.

American schnapps

In the United States, "schnapps" usually denotes a flavored, sweetened liqueur — for example peppermint, peach, or butterscotch schnapps — made by combining neutral spirits with fruit or other flavors and sugar. These are typically lower in proof (15–25% ABV) than European schnapps and are used in cocktails, shots, and mixed drinks.

Etymology

The word derives from Low German snappen, "to snap", referring to the way the drink is quickly downed from a small glass, and later to the small shot itself.
