Robert III (c. 1337 – 4 April 1406), born John Stewart, was King of Scots from 1390 until his death in 1406. He was the eldest son of King Robert II and Elizabeth Mure, and had been created Earl of Carrick in 1368. Although he formally succeeded his father, infirmity from a horse-riding injury and the ambitions of his younger brother Robert Stewart, Duke of Albany, meant that effective power lay largely in others' hands during his reign.

Reign

John Stewart took the regnal name Robert—partly to avoid the unlucky associations of King John Balliol and of John of England—when he was crowned at Scone in August 1390. By 1399 his lameness and ill health led the council to appoint his eldest son, David, Duke of Rothesay, as lieutenant of the realm. The rivalry between Rothesay and Albany ended in the young duke's death in custody at Falkland Palace in 1402, one of the great scandals of the reign.

Conflict with England continued throughout the period, including the Scottish defeat at the Battle of Homildon Hill (1402). Fearing for the safety of his younger son and heir James, Robert arranged for him to be sent to France; the boy was captured by English sailors in 1406 and imprisoned by Henry IV. Robert III died at Rothesay Castle on 4 April 1406, shortly after hearing of the capture, and was buried at Paisley Abbey. He is reported to have asked to be remembered as "the worst of kings and the most miserable of men".

Issue

By his wife Annabella Drummond, Robert had seven children, including David, Duke of Rothesay, and the future James I of Scotland.
