Chorionic villus sampling

Chorionic villus sampling (CVS) is a form of prenatal diagnosis in which a sample of chorionic villi — placental tissue — is removed and tested for chromosomal abnormalities such as Down syndrome and for certain inherited genetic disorders. Because the placenta and fetus arise from the same fertilized egg, the genetic makeup of the villi normally matches that of the fetus. CVS is usually performed between the 10th and 13th weeks of pregnancy, earlier than amniocentesis.

Procedure

Under continuous ultrasound guidance, a small sample of placental tissue is obtained either through the cervix with a thin catheter (transcervical CVS) or through the abdominal wall with a needle (transabdominal CVS). The tissue is analyzed by karyotyping, chromosomal microarray, or targeted DNA testing. Results from direct preparations may be available quickly, with cultured-cell results following in one to two weeks.

Indications

CVS may be offered when screening tests indicate an increased risk of a chromosomal condition, when a parent carries a balanced chromosome rearrangement or a known genetic disorder, or when there is a family history of a condition diagnosable from placental DNA. Unlike amniocentesis, CVS cannot measure alpha-fetoprotein and therefore does not detect open neural tube defects, which are screened separately.

Risks and limitations

CVS carries a small risk of miscarriage, slightly higher than that of amniocentesis in older studies and approaching it in experienced centers. In about 1–2 percent of cases, confined placental mosaicism — a chromosome abnormality present in the placenta but not the fetus — makes results ambiguous and requires follow-up amniocentesis. CVS performed before 10 weeks has been associated with fetal limb abnormalities and is avoided.
