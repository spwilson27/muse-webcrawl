Parkinson's disease dementia

Parkinson's disease dementia
Specialty: Neurology
Onset: Typically years after Parkinson's motor symptoms
Related: Dementia with Lewy bodies

Parkinson's disease dementia (PDD) is a dementia that develops in the course of Parkinson's disease. A majority of people with Parkinson's disease will eventually develop cognitive impairment; estimates suggest dementia affects roughly 30–40% of patients at any given time and most over a long course of disease.

Features

PDD is characterised by impaired executive function, attention and visuospatial ability, fluctuating alertness, apathy, depression and visual hallucinations. By convention, dementia arising more than a year after the onset of Parkinson's motor symptoms is classified as PDD, while dementia preceding or coinciding with parkinsonism suggests dementia with Lewy bodies; both involve abnormal deposits of alpha-synuclein (Lewy bodies).

Treatment

There is no cure. Cholinesterase inhibitors such as rivastigmine modestly help cognition; management focuses on reviewing medications that worsen cognition, treating mood and sleep problems, and supporting carers.
