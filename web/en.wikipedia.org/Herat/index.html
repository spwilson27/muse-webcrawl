Herat (Persian/Dari: هرات) is a city in western Afghanistan, the capital of Herat Province, situated in the fertile valley of the Hari River. It is one of the oldest cities in the region and was historically a major centre of trade, learning, and Persian culture on the Silk Road, linking Persia, Central Asia, and the Indian subcontinent.

History

The area was part of the Achaemenid satrapy of Aria; Alexander the Great founded or refounded a city here (Alexandria Ariana) around 330 BC. Herat passed through Parthian, Sasanian, Arab, Tahirid, Saffarid, Ghaznavid, Seljuk, and Khwarazmian hands. The Mongols devastated it in 1221–1222, but it revived under the local Kart dynasty.

Herat reached its golden age under the Timurids in the 15th century, when Shah Rukh and his wife Gawhar Shad made it a capital of empire and a centre of Persian miniature painting, calligraphy, and architecture. The Musalla complex, the Friday Mosque (Masjid-i Jami), and the shrines of poets and saints such as Jami and Navai date in large part from this era; the Herat school of painting, associated with Bihzad, is famed across the Islamic world.

Contested between Safavid Persia and the Uzbeks, the city became part of the Durrani Afghan Empire in the 18th century. In the 19th century it was a focus of the "Great Game" between Britain and Russia; Persian attempts to seize it in 1837–38 and 1856 helped trigger the Anglo-Persian War. In the 20th and 21st centuries Herat endured the Soviet–Afghan War, civil war, Taliban rule (1995–2001), and — as an economic hub — rapid growth after 2001, before returning to Taliban control in August 2021.

Geography and economy

Herat lies near the borders with Iran and Turkmenistan and remains Afghanistan's principal western trading city, known for saffron, carpets, glassblowing, and agriculture in the Hari Rud valley. Landmarks include the Citadel (Qala Iktyaruddin), said to date in part to Alexander's time and repeatedly rebuilt, the Great Mosque, and the Musalla minarets.
