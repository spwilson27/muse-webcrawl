The Maltese Falcon is a 1941 American film noir directed and written by John Huston in his directorial debut, based on the 1930 novel of the same name by Dashiell Hammett. It stars Humphrey Bogart as private investigator Sam Spade and Mary Astor as his femme fatale client. The story follows a San Francisco private detective and his dealings with three unscrupulous adventurers, all of whom are competing to obtain a jewel-encrusted falcon statuette.

Plot

Private detectives Sam Spade and Miles Archer are hired by the alluring Ruth Wonderly to follow a man she claims has run off with her sister. Archer is murdered that night, and Spade becomes the prime suspect. Wonderly — actually Brigid O'Shaughnessy — confesses the story was a lie: she is searching for a priceless black statuette of a falcon, pursued also by Joel Cairo and the corpulent Kasper Gutman. When the falcon finally arrives, it proves to be a worthless lead fake. Spade, though attracted to Brigid, turns her over to the police for Archer's murder, remarking that the falcon is "the stuff that dreams are made of."

Production

Huston adapted Hammett's novel with unusual fidelity, retaining much of its dialogue. The film was shot inexpensively at Warner Bros. and completed in about two months. It was the third screen adaptation of the novel, following versions in 1931 and 1936 (Satan Met a Lady).

Reception and legacy

The film was nominated for three Academy Awards, including Best Picture, and launched Huston's career while cementing Bogart's star persona. It is widely regarded as the first major film noir and one of the greatest films of all time; in 1989 it was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry as "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant."
