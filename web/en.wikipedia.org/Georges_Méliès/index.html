Georges Méliès

Died21 January 1938 (aged 76), Paris, France

Marie-Georges-Jean Méliès (8 December 1861 – 21 January 1938) was a French stage magician, actor and film director. He led many technical and narrative developments in the earliest days of cinema and is regarded as one of the medium's first visionaries, pioneering the use of special effects, multiple exposures, time-lapse photography, dissolves and hand-coloured film.

Early life and theatre

Méliès was born in Paris to a family of shoemakers. Against his parents' wishes he pursued the stage, and after working in London, where he studied stage magic, he bought the Théâtre Robert-Houdin in Paris in 1888 and became a celebrated illusionist, designing elaborate stage tricks and mechanical automata.

Entry into cinema

Méliès attended the first public screening of the Lumière brothers' Cinématographe on 28 December 1895. Unable to buy a camera from them, he acquired equipment in London and began filming in 1896. He founded his company, Star Film, and built one of the first film studios at Montreuil, a glass construction that admitted sunlight while sheltering elaborate sets.

Trick films and masterpieces

Discovering the "stop trick" by accident when his camera jammed, Méliès made films in which objects appeared, vanished and transformed. Between 1896 and 1913 he directed more than 500 films. His most famous work is A Trip to the Moon (Le Voyage dans la Lune, 1902), in which a group of astronomers is fired to the Moon in a shell; its image of the capsule landing in the eye of the Man in the Moon became one of the most iconic in film history. Other celebrated works include The Impossible Voyage (1904) and The Kingdom of the Fairies (1903).

Decline and rediscovery

Unable to compete with larger studios and hurt by piracy of his films, Méliès fell into debt and stopped filmmaking in 1913; his studio was requisitioned during the First World War and many of his negatives were destroyed. He was rediscovered in the late 1920s working in a toy shop at the Gare Montparnasse, and was honoured with the Legion of Honour in 1931. He died in Paris in 1938.

Legacy

Méliès is honoured as the father of narrative and fantasy cinema. His life and the restoration of A Trip to the Moon inspired Martin Scorsese's film Hugo (2011).
