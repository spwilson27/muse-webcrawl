Acrylic paint

Acrylic paint
Medium: Pigment in acrylic polymer emulsion
Drying: Fast, water-soluble when wet
Introduced: 1930s–1950s (artists' paints)

Acrylic paint is a fast-drying paint made of pigment suspended in acrylic polymer emulsion. Acrylic paints are water-soluble but become water-resistant when dry.

History

Acrylic resins were developed in the 1930s; the first commercially available artists' acrylics appeared in the late 1940s and 1950s, marketed by firms such as Liquitex and Golden. They were quickly adopted by painters seeking the versatility of oil with faster drying and easier cleanup.

Characteristics

Depending on dilution and mediums, finished acrylic works can resemble watercolor, gouache, or oil paintings, or have their own unique appearance. Acrylics adhere to many surfaces, remain flexible, and resist yellowing.

Use in art

Artists associated with acrylics include the Mexican muralists, and later David Hockney, Helen Frankenthaler, and the color field and pop art movements.
