Charles River

SourceEcho Lake, Hopkinton, Massachusetts
MouthBoston Harbor, Massachusetts Bay
Length80 mi (129 km)
Basin size308 sq mi (798 km²)
Named forKing Charles I of England

The Charles River is an 80-mile-long river in eastern Massachusetts, flowing from Hopkinton northeastward through 23 cities and towns to Boston Harbor. It separates Boston from Cambridge and Charlestown, and its lower basin forms one of the most heavily used recreational waterways in the United States.

Course and watershed

The river begins at Echo Lake in Hopkinton and winds generally northeast through suburbs including Dedham, Newton, and Waltham before reaching the tidal basin at Watertown. Its watershed covers about 308 square miles. Despite its modest length, the river drops and meanders extensively, and it is crossed by numerous dams originally built for mills and flood control.

History

Indigenous peoples knew the river as Quinobequin, "meandering". Captain John Smith explored and mapped the coast in 1614, and Prince Charles — later King Charles I — gave the river its English name. In the 19th century the river's banks were industrialized, its tidal flats filled, and its pollution notorious. The construction of the Charles River Dam in 1910 created the freshwater basin flanked today by the Esplanade in Boston and Memorial Drive in Cambridge.

Recreation and cleanup

The basin hosts the Head of the Charles Regatta, the world's largest two-day rowing event, along with sailing, kayaking, and the Fourth of July concerts at the Hatch Shell. Once so polluted that swimming was unthinkable, the river has been the target of a major federal cleanup since 1995; combined-sewer overflows have been sharply reduced and water quality grades have improved dramatically, with organized public swims resuming in the 2010s.
