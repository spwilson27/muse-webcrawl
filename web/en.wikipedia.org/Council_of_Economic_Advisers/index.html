Council of Economic Advisers
Agency overview
Formed: 1946
Headquarters: Eisenhower Executive Office Building, Washington, D.C.
Agency executive: Chair of the Council of Economic Advisers
Parent agency: Executive Office of the President

The Council of Economic Advisers (CEA) is a United States agency within the Executive Office of the President established by the Employment Act of 1946, which provides the president of the United States with economic advice based on economic research. The CEA is composed of three members, appointed by the president with the advice and consent of the Senate, one of whom is designated chair. The council's staff of professional economists produces the annual Economic Report of the President.

History

The council was established by the Employment Act of 1946, a compromise measure that fell short of guaranteeing full employment but committed the federal government to promoting maximum employment, production, and purchasing power. The act created both the CEA in the executive branch and the Joint Economic Committee in Congress. Edwin G. Nourse served as the first chair, from 1946 to 1949, and Leon Keyserling, a more activist New Dealer, succeeded him and shaped the council's early role under President Harry S. Truman.

Organization and duties

The chair of the CEA is a cabinet-level position often accorded significant influence, though its prominence varies by administration. The council's members are typically academic economists. Its duties include assisting in the preparation of the annual Economic Report of the President, studying national economic developments, appraising federal programs, and making policy recommendations. The CEA also contributes to the administration's economic forecasts, working alongside the Treasury Department and the Office of Management and Budget in the so-called "troika."

Notable chairs

Chairs of the council have included Arthur F. Burns, Walter Heller, Arthur Okun, Alan Greenspan, Martin Feldstein, Laura Tyson, Joseph Stiglitz, Janet Yellen, Ben Bernanke, Christina Romer, Austan Goolsbee, Jason Furman, Kevin Hassett, Cecilia Rouse, and Jared Bernstein. Several went on to chair the Federal Reserve.
