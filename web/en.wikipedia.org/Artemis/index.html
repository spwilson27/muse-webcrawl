Artemis

Artemis is the ancient Greek goddess of the hunt, the wilderness, wild animals, the Moon, and archery. She was also often associated with childbirth, care of children, and chastity. In poetry and myth she was regularly described as the huntress of the moon, the lady of the forest. Her Roman equivalent is Diana.

Origins and worship

Artemis was born on the island of Delos to Zeus and the Titan Leto, twin sister of Apollo; according to one tradition she was born first and helped her mother deliver Apollo. Her worship was widespread throughout Greece; her most famous sanctuary was the Temple of Artemis at Ephesus, one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, and her festival of Brauronia was celebrated at Brauron in Attica.

Mythology

Artemis was a virgin goddess who fiercely punished those who transgressed against her retinue: the hunter Actaeon, who spied on her bathing, was turned into a stag and torn apart by his own hounds; the nymph Callisto was expelled after breaking her vow of chastity; and the hunter Orion was killed by a scorpion or by Artemis herself, depending on the account. She demanded the sacrifice of Iphigenia when Agamemnon's fleet was becalmed at Aulis, and hunted the Calydonian boar's slayers in fury after the insult to her altar.

Ephesian Artemis

The Ephesian Artemis, venerated at Ephesus, was depicted with multiple breast-like ornaments and a mural crown, a form quite distinct from the huntress of Greek myth, blending Anatolian mother-goddess traditions with the Olympian deity.
