Food safety

Food safety (or food hygiene) is a scientific and regulatory discipline describing the handling, preparation, and storage of food in ways that prevent foodborne illness. It covers the entire food chain, "from farm to fork", including agricultural practices, processing, transport, retail, and home cooking.

Hazards

Food hazards are classified as biological (bacteria such as Salmonella and Listeria, viruses such as norovirus, and parasites), chemical (pesticide residues, toxins such as aflatoxin, allergens), and physical (foreign objects). Two or more cases of a similar illness resulting from ingestion of a common food is known as an outbreak of foodborne illness.

Prevention

Key consumer practices are commonly summarised as: clean (wash hands and surfaces), separate (avoid cross-contamination), cook (to safe internal temperatures), and chill (refrigerate promptly). At industry level, the Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points (HACCP) system identifies points in production where hazards can be controlled, and is mandated for many food sectors worldwide.

Regulation

Governments regulate food safety through agencies such as the U.S. Food and Drug Administration and Department of Agriculture, the European Food Safety Authority, and national food standards bodies. International standards are coordinated through the Codex Alimentarius Commission, established by the FAO and WHO in 1963. Recalls of contaminated products are a major enforcement tool.
