The prime minister of Iraq is the head of government of Iraq and the commander-in-chief of the Iraqi Armed Forces. Under the Constitution of Iraq adopted in 2005, the prime minister is the country's chief executive, while the president serves as head of state with largely ceremonial powers.

Selection

Following parliamentary elections, the president asks the nominee of the largest bloc in the Council of Representatives to form a government. The prime minister-designate has 30 days to name a cabinet and present a governing program, which must win the confidence of an absolute majority of the Council. Since 2005, an informal power-sharing convention known as muhasasa has distributed the leading offices among Iraq's communities: the prime minister is conventionally a Shia Arab, the president a Kurd, and the speaker of parliament a Sunni Arab.

History

The office was created in 1920 under the British Mandate, with Abd al-Rahman al-Gillani as its first holder. During the monarchy, prime ministers served at the pleasure of the king. After the 14 July Revolution of 1958, the office continued under successive republican regimes, though real power rested with presidents such as Saddam Hussein. The 2003 invasion of Iraq and the fall of the Ba'athist government led to a transitional period under the Coalition Provisional Authority and an appointed Governing Council. The first post-2003 elected prime minister was Ibrahim al-Jaafari, followed by Nouri al-Maliki (2006–2014), Haider al-Abadi (2014–2018), Adil Abdul-Mahdi (2018–2020), and Mustafa Al-Kadhimi (2020–2022). Mohammed Shia' al-Sudani took office in October 2022.
