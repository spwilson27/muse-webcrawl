Wushu (sport)

Governing bodyInternational Wushu Federation (IWUF)
Founded (IWUF)1990
DisciplinesTaolu and Sanda

Wushu is a modern sport derived from traditional Chinese martial arts (collectively also called kung fu), developed in the People's Republic of China after 1949 as a standardized competitive form. It is governed internationally by the International Wushu Federation, founded in 1990, and features at the Asian Games and the World Wushu Championships.

Disciplines

Wushu competition has two main components. Taolu consists of choreographed forms — routines with prescribed movements drawn from styles such as changquan (northern long fist), nanquan (southern fist), and taijiquan, and weapon routines using the broadsword, straight sword, spear, and staff, judged on difficulty, execution, and presentation. Sanda (also called sanshou) is a full-contact fighting sport combining punches, kicks, throws, and sweeps, fought on a raised platform (leitai).

History and recognition

The sport was systematized in the 1950s under China's sports authorities. A special wushu tournament was held alongside the 2008 Beijing Olympics, though wushu has not become an official Olympic medal sport; it is scheduled to appear at the 2026 Dakar Youth Olympic Games.
