Immigration Act of 1990

The Immigration Act of 1990 (Pub. L. 101-649) is a United States federal law signed by President George H. W. Bush on November 29, 1990. It was the first major overhaul of U.S. immigration law since 1965, substantially increasing legal immigration and restructuring the preference system for family-sponsored and employment-based visas.

Provisions

The Act raised the annual worldwide level of immigration to a flexible cap of roughly 675,000–700,000, increased employment-based visas to 140,000 per year, and created new categories including the EB-5 immigrant investor program. It established the Diversity Visa Program (the "green card lottery"), reserving visas for nationals of countries with low rates of immigration to the United States. The Act also created Temporary Protected Status (TPS) for nationals of countries experiencing armed conflict or natural disaster, revised grounds of deportation and exclusion, and limited judicial review in certain immigration cases.

Impact

Legal immigration to the United States rose markedly in the 1990s, and the Act's employment and diversity categories remain central features of the modern U.S. immigration system. The Diversity Visa Program has periodically been the subject of legislative debate and proposed elimination.
