Şanlıurfa (often simply Urfa), known in ancient and medieval times as Edessa, is a city in southeastern Turkey and the capital of Şanlıurfa Province. It lies on a plain about 80 kilometres east of the Euphrates River, near the Syrian border. The city is one of the oldest continuously inhabited settlements in the region and holds a central place in the religious traditions of the Abrahamic faiths; in Turkish tradition it is known as the "City of Prophets".

History

The wider region was settled in the Neolithic period: the monumental site of Göbekli Tepe, about 15 kilometres northeast of the city and dated to around 9500 BC, is among the oldest known temple complexes in the world, and the Neolithic settlement of Nevalı Çori lay nearby. The city itself was refounded as Edessa by Seleucus I Nicator around 303 BC. It later became a frontier city contested between Rome and Parthia (and then Persia), an early centre of Syriac Christianity, and the capital of the Crusader County of Edessa from 1098 until its fall to Zengi in 1144, an event that helped trigger the Second Crusade. Ottoman rule began in the 16th century. During the Turkish War of Independence the city resisted French occupation, and in 1984 the Turkish parliament added the honorific prefix "Şanlı" ("glorious") to its name.

Religious significance

Urfa is traditionally identified with the biblical Ur of the Chaldees and as the birthplace of Abraham (İbrahim). The Balıklıgöl ("Pool of the Sacred Fish"), set in a landscaped complex of mosques including the Halil-ür Rahman Mosque, marks the place where, according to legend, Nimrod cast Abraham into a fire that God turned into water, its burning logs becoming carp; the fish in the pool are considered sacred and are never eaten. A cave near the pool is venerated as Abraham's birthplace.

Sights and culture

Above the old town rises Urfa Castle, traditionally dated to the Abgar dynasty of Osroene. The Şanlıurfa Museum, opened in a large new complex in 2015, displays the region's extraordinary archaeology, including material from Göbekli Tepe and the "Urfa Man" (Balıklıgöl statue), considered the world's oldest life-sized human statue. The old bazaars, among them the Gümrük Han, remain a centre of trade in copperware, fabrics and spices. Urfa is famous across Turkey for its spicy cuisine, especially Urfa kebab (the milder counterpart to Adana kebab), çiğ köfte, and dishes flavoured with the local isot pepper.

Economy

The Southeastern Anatolia Project (GAP), a vast irrigation and hydroelectric scheme on the Euphrates and Tigris, transformed agriculture in the province, making it a major producer of cotton, wheat, lentils and pistachios.
