X-linked agammaglobulinemia

GeneBTK (Xq21.3–q22)
InheritanceX-linked recessive
First described1952, by Ogden Bruton
TreatmentImmunoglobulin replacement therapy

X-linked agammaglobulinemia (XLA), also called Bruton's agammaglobulinemia, is a rare genetic primary immunodeficiency characterized by the near absence of mature B lymphocytes and of all classes of immunoglobulins (antibodies), leading to recurrent bacterial infections. It was the first primary immunodeficiency disease to be described, by Ogden Bruton in 1952.

Cause

XLA is caused by mutations in the BTK gene on the X chromosome, which encodes Bruton tyrosine kinase, an enzyme essential for B-cell development in the bone marrow. Without functional BTK, development arrests at the pre-B-cell stage. Because inheritance is X-linked recessive, the condition almost always affects males; female carriers are typically healthy.

Signs and diagnosis

Affected boys are usually healthy for the first months of life while protected by maternal antibodies, then develop recurrent infections of the ears, sinuses, lungs, and bloodstream with encapsulated bacteria such as Streptococcus pneumoniae and Haemophilus influenzae; they also lack visible tonsils and lymph nodes. Diagnosis rests on very low immunoglobulin levels, absent or nearly absent B cells in blood, and genetic testing.

Treatment

There is no cure; lifelong immunoglobulin replacement therapy (intravenous or subcutaneous) plus prompt antibiotic treatment of infections greatly improves survival and quality of life. Live vaccines are avoided, and patients are at risk of severe enterovirus infections.
