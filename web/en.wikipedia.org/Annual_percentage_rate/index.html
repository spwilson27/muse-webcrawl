Annual percentage rate

The term annual percentage rate of charge (APR), corresponding sometimes to a nominal APR and sometimes to an effective APR (EAPR), is the interest rate for a whole year (annualised), rather than just a monthly fee or rate, as applied on a loan, mortgage loan, credit card, etc. It is a finance charge expressed as an annual rate.

Definition

APR is intended to give borrowers a standardised measure of the cost of credit so that offers from different lenders can be compared. Depending on the jurisdiction, APR may include certain fees and costs in addition to interest. In the United States, APR disclosure for consumer credit is governed by the Truth in Lending Act of 1968 and Regulation Z; in the European Union, a harmonised APRC definition applies under consumer credit directives.

Nominal and effective APR

The nominal APR is the simple interest rate multiplied by the number of periods in a year, ignoring compounding. The effective APR accounts for compounding within the year, so it is higher whenever interest compounds more often than annually. For example, a nominal 12% APR compounded monthly corresponds to an effective annual rate of about 12.68%.

Limitations

APR calculations rest on assumptions about the loan's term and repayment pattern, so APRs for open-ended credit, adjustable-rate mortgages, or loans repaid early may not reflect the true cost. Fees excluded by local rules can also cause APR to understate total charges.
