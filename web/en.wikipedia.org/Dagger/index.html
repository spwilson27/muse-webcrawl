Dagger

A dagger is a fighting knife with a very sharp point and usually two sharp edges, typically designed or capable of being used as a thrusting or stabbing weapon. Daggers have been used throughout human history for close combat confrontations, and many cultures have used adorned daggers in ritual and ceremonial contexts. The distinctive shape and historic usage of the dagger have made it iconic and symbolic.

A wide variety of thrusting knives have been described as daggers, including knives that feature only a single cutting edge, such as the European rondel dagger or the Afghan pesh-kabz, or, in some instances, no cutting edge at all, such as the stiletto of the Renaissance. However, in the last hundred years or so, in most contexts, a dagger has certain definable characteristics, including a short blade with a sharply tapered point, a central spine or fuller, and usually two cutting edges sharpened the full length of the blade.

History

The earliest daggers were made of materials such as flint, ivory or bone in Neolithic times. Copper daggers appeared first in the Early Bronze Age, in the 3rd millennium BC, and copper daggers of Early Minoan III (2400–2000 BC) have been recovered at Knossos. In the course of the Bronze and Iron Ages, daggers became standard sidearms and symbols of status; Tutankhamun's tomb contained both a gold dagger and one of meteoritic iron.

Medieval and Renaissance use

During the Middle Ages, forms such as the rondel, ballock, and baselard dagger were carried by knights and common soldiers alike, as sidearms and as tools for finishing armored opponents through gaps in plate. The stiletto emerged in Italy as a slender thrusting weapon. In many cultures, daggers retained ceremonial importance — examples include the kris of Southeast Asia, the jambiya of Yemen, and the Scottish dirk and skean-dhu.

Modern use

By the 20th century, daggers were issued in specialized military forms such as the Fairbairn–Sykes fighting knife of British commandos, trench knives of the World Wars, and modern combat/utility knives. Most jurisdictions regulate the carrying of daggers as offensive weapons, though they remain common as dress and ceremonial items.

Cultural symbolism

The dagger is a recurring motif in literature and heraldry, from the assassination of Julius Caesar to Shakespeare's Macbeth ("Is this a dagger which I see before me?"). The typographic dagger symbol (†) derives its name from the weapon's shape and is used to mark footnotes and obsolete terms.
