Hypothalamic hamartoma

Coronal MRI showing a hypothalamic hamartoma attached to the tuber cinereum.

A hypothalamic hamartoma (HH) is a rare benign brain tumour-like malformation of the hypothalamus, consisting of disorganised neurons and glia in a location where they do not belong. It occurs in roughly one in 200,000 children and typically presents in infancy or early childhood. Lesions are attached to the tuber cinereum or mammillary bodies and are classified as sessile (broad-based, often with more severe epilepsy) or pedunculated (stalk-like, more often associated with endocrine effects).

Symptoms

The classic presentation is gelastic seizures—brief, stereotyped laughing episodes that arise directly from the lesion, which is intrinsically epileptogenic. Over time, other seizure types often develop, and many patients progress to a severe epileptic encephalopathy with cognitive decline and behavioural problems, including rage attacks. A second major manifestation is central precocious puberty, caused by the lesion driving premature activation of the hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis; this is especially common with pedunculated lesions.

Diagnosis

MRI shows a non-enhancing, isointense mass in the suprasellar region that does not change on follow-up. EEG may be normal or show secondary generalised discharges, since the primary focus is deep.

Treatment

Gelastic seizures respond poorly to anti-seizure medications. Definitive treatment is removal or disconnection of the lesion, accomplished by open surgery (including transcallosal approaches), endoscopic resection, stereotactic radiofrequency thermocoagulation, laser interstitial thermal therapy (LITT), or Gamma Knife radiosurgery. Precocious puberty is treated with GnRH analogues. Surgical series show that seizure freedom is achievable in a large proportion of patients, with risks including memory impairment (from mammillary body injury), endocrine deficits, and weight gain.
