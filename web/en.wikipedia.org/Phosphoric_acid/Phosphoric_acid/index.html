Phosphoric acid

Phosphoric acid (orthophosphoric acid, phosphoric(V) acid) is a colorless, odorless phosphorus-containing solid, and inorganic compound with the chemical formula H3PO4. It is commonly encountered as an 85% aqueous solution, which is a colourless, odourless and non-volatile syrupy liquid. It is a major industrial chemical, being a component of many fertilizers.

Production

Phosphoric acid is produced industrially by two general routes. In the wet process, phosphate rock (apatite) is treated with sulfuric acid, yielding phosphoric acid and gypsum. In the thermal process, elemental phosphorus is burned and the oxide hydrated, giving a purer acid used in food applications.

Properties

Phosphoric acid is a triprotic acid, dissociating stepwise with pKa values of about 2.1, 7.2 and 12.3. Its salts are the phosphates, important in biology as components of DNA, RNA and ATP.

Uses

The dominant use is in phosphate fertilizers. Food-grade phosphoric acid (E338) acidifies soft drinks such as colas; it is also used in rust removal, dental etchants, and detergents.
