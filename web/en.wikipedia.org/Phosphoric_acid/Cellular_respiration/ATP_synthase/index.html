ATP synthase

ATP synthase is an enzyme that catalyzes the formation of the energy storage molecule adenosine triphosphate (ATP) using adenosine diphosphate (ADP) and inorganic phosphate (Pi). ATP synthase is a molecular machine that is used to produce energy. It can also work in reverse, hydrolyzing ATP to build a proton gradient. ATP synthases are found in the inner membrane of mitochondria in eukaryotes, in the thylakoid membrane of chloroplasts in plants (as CF1CFO-ATP synthase), and in the plasma membrane of bacteria and archaea.

Energy is often released in the form of hydrogen ions (H+), moving down an electrochemical gradient, such as from the lumen into the stroma of chloroplasts or from the intermembrane space into the matrix of mitochondria. The flow of protons through the enzyme's membrane-embedded FO portion drives rotation of a central stalk, which causes conformational changes in the catalytic F1 portion that bind ADP and phosphate and release ATP.

Structure

ATP synthase consists of two main subunits: the FO portion, which is embedded in the membrane and conducts protons, and the F1 portion, a spherical catalytic head containing three α and three β subunits arranged around a central γ subunit. The binding change mechanism, proposed by Paul Boyer, explains how rotation of the γ subunit drives the sequential binding, conversion, and release steps at the catalytic sites.

Role in cellular respiration

During oxidative phosphorylation, the final stage of cellular respiration, the electron transport chain pumps protons across the inner mitochondrial membrane, creating the proton-motive force. ATP synthase harnesses this gradient, synthesizing the majority of the ATP produced from a glucose molecule.
