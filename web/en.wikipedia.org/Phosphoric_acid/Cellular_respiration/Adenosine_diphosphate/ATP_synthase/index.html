Type: Enzyme complex (EC 7.1.2.2)
Location: Inner mitochondrial membrane; thylakoid membrane; bacterial membrane

ATP synthase

ATP synthase is an enzyme that creates the energy storage molecule adenosine triphosphate (ATP). ATP is the most commonly used "energy currency" of cells for all organisms. ATP synthase is formed from adenosine diphosphate (ADP) and inorganic phosphate (Pi), using energy from a transmembrane electrochemical gradient of protons.

Structure and mechanism

The enzyme consists of two main regions: a membrane-embedded FO portion, a rotary motor driven by protons flowing through it, and a catalytic F1 portion that protrudes into the matrix or stroma and synthesises ATP. Rotation of the central shaft deforms the catalytic subunits in turn — Paul Boyer's binding-change mechanism — forcing ADP and phosphate together and releasing ATP. The rotary mechanism was confirmed by direct observation in experiments by Masasuke Yoshida's group in 1997.

Role in respiration and photosynthesis

In cellular respiration, the proton gradient is generated by the electron transport chain; in photosynthesis, by the light reactions in the thylakoid. ATP synthase is ancient and nearly universal in life, and it can also run in reverse, hydrolysing ATP to pump protons, as in some bacteria.
