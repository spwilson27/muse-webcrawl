Eutrophication

Eutrophication is the process by which a body of water becomes increasingly enriched with nutrients, typically nitrogen and phosphorus, leading to excessive growth of algae and other aquatic plants. It is a major cause of water quality impairment worldwide.

Causes

The principal sources are runoff of fertilisers and manure from farmland, and sewage discharges. Phosphorus is often the limiting nutrient in fresh waters, so additions of phosphate—ultimately derived from phosphoric acid and phosphate rock—can trigger algal blooms even where nitrogen (fixed industrially by the Haber process) is abundant.

Effects

Decomposition of algal blooms consumes oxygen, creating hypoxic "dead zones". Eutrophication also reduces water clarity, harms fisheries, and can produce toxic cyanobacterial blooms.
