Phosphorus cycle

The phosphorus cycle is the biogeochemical cycle by which phosphorus moves through rocks, soil, water and living organisms. Phosphorus is an essential nutrient for plants and animals, forming part of DNA, RNA, ATP and cell membranes.

Natural cycle

Phosphate is released slowly by the weathering of rocks and is taken up by plants. It returns to the soil through decomposition and eventually washes into the oceans, where it is buried in sediments; geological uplift can return it to land over timescales of millions of years.

Human impact

Mining of phosphate rock for phosphoric acid production and fertiliser manufacture has greatly accelerated phosphorus flows. Runoff from fields fertilised with both phosphate and nitrogen from the Haber process contributes to eutrophication.
