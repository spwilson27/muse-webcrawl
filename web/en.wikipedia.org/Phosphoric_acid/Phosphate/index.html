Phosphate

FormulaPO₄³⁻
Conjugate acidPhosphoric acid, H₃PO₄
StructureTetrahedral: one phosphorus atom bonded to four oxygen atoms
Main sourcesMined phosphate rock (apatite), chiefly in Morocco, China, and the US

In chemistry, a phosphate is an anion, salt, functional group or ester derived from a phosphoric acid. It most commonly means orthophosphate, a derivative of orthophosphoric acid, H₃PO₄. The phosphate ion consists of one central phosphorus atom surrounded by four oxygen atoms in a tetrahedral arrangement and carries a 3− charge.

Occurrence and production

Phosphate is obtained industrially from phosphate rock, which consists largely of the mineral apatite, Ca₅(PO₄)₃(F,Cl,OH). Morocco and Western Sahara hold the majority of world reserves. Treating the rock with sulfuric acid produces phosphoric acid and phosphogypsum, forming the basis of the phosphate fertilizer industry.

Biological importance

Phosphorus, delivered as phosphate, is an essential element for all life. Phosphate groups form the backbone of DNA and RNA, the energy carrier ATP, and the phospholipids of cell membranes; calcium phosphate (hydroxyapatite) is the principal mineral of bones and teeth. In ecosystems, phosphate is often the limiting nutrient for plant and algal growth, which is why fertilizer runoff triggers eutrophication.

Uses

Most mined phosphate goes into fertilizers (such as mono- and diammonium phosphate and triple superphosphate). Other uses include food additives, water treatment, metal finishing, and detergents — though detergent phosphates have been widely phased out because of water-pollution concerns.
