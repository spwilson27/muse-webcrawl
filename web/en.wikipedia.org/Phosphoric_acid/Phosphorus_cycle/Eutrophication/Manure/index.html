Manure

Manure is organic matter, mostly derived from animal faeces, that is used as an organic fertiliser in agriculture. Manures contribute to soil fertility by adding organic matter and nutrients, such as nitrogen, potassium and phosphorus, that are used by bacteria, fungi and other organisms in the soil.

Types

Farmyard manure consists of faeces, urine and bedding such as straw. Slurry is a liquid mixture stored in tanks or lagoons, while poultry litter and composted manures are drier products that are easier to transport.

Environmental issues

Phosphorus in manure is part of the phosphorus cycle: crops take it up from soil built from plant feed and fertilisers, including those made with phosphoric acid. Over-application can lead to nutrient runoff and eutrophication of nearby waters.
