Haber process

The Haber process, also called the Haber–Bosch process, is an artificial nitrogen fixation process and the main industrial procedure for the production of ammonia. It is named after its inventors, the German chemists Fritz Haber and Carl Bosch, who developed it in the first decade of the 20th century.

Process

Nitrogen gas from the air is combined with hydrogen, typically derived from natural gas, over an iron catalyst at high pressure (around 200 atmospheres) and moderate temperature (around 450 °C) to produce ammonia: N2 + 3H2 → 2NH3.

Significance

Ammonia from the Haber process is the basis of most synthetic nitrogen fertilisers, which together with phosphate fertilisers made from phosphoric acid sustain modern crop yields. Nutrient losses from fertilised fields link the process indirectly to eutrophication of waterways.
