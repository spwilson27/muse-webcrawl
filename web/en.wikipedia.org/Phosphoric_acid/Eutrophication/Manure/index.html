Manure

Manure is organic matter that is used as organic fertilizer in agriculture. Most manure consists of animal feces; other sources include compost and green manure. Manures contribute to the fertility of soil by adding organic matter and nutrients, such as nitrogen, that are utilised by bacteria, fungi and other organisms in the soil. Higher organisms then feed on the fungi and bacteria in a chain of life that comprises the soil food web.

Types

Manure from farm animals — cattle, horses, pigs, sheep, chickens and others — is the most common source. "Green manure" refers to crops grown specifically to be ploughed back into the soil to add organic matter and nutrients. Composted manure has been decomposed by microbial action, which reduces its volume and stabilizes its nutrients.

Nutrient content and use

Animal manures supply nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium (the primary plant macronutrients) as well as micronutrients and organic carbon that improves soil structure, water retention and drainage. Manure is typically applied by spreading on fields, sometimes after storage in pits or lagoons as liquid slurry.

Environmental issues

When applied in excess of crop needs, or before heavy rain, nutrients in manure can run off into surface waters. The phosphorus and nitrogen in this runoff are major drivers of eutrophication, causing algal blooms and oxygen depletion in lakes and coastal waters. Manure management is therefore regulated in many jurisdictions.
