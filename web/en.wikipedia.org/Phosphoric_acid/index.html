Phosphoric acid, H3PO4.

Phosphoric acid (orthophosphoric acid, phosphoric(V) acid, or phosphoric acid) is a colourless, odourless phosphorus-containing solid and a weak acid with the chemical formula H3PO4. It is commonly encountered as an 85% aqueous solution, which is a colourless, odourless and non-volatile syrupy liquid. It is a major industrial chemical, being a component of many fertilizers.

Phosphoric acid is a triprotic acid, donating up to three protons in successive equilibria, giving the ions H2PO4− (dihydrogen phosphate), HPO42− (hydrogen phosphate) and PO43− (phosphate). Its salts — phosphates — are essential to life, forming the backbone of DNA and RNA and the energy currency ATP.

Production

About 90% of phosphoric acid is made by the wet process, in which phosphate rock (fluorapatite) is treated with concentrated sulfuric acid, producing phosphoric acid and gypsum (calcium sulfate) as a byproduct. The purer thermal process burns elemental phosphorus to phosphorus pentoxide, which is then hydrated; it accounts for a smaller share of production and serves food- and pharmaceutical-grade markets.

Uses

  • Fertilizers — the dominant use, in products such as monoammonium phosphate (MAP), diammonium phosphate (DAP) and triple superphosphate.
  • Food additive (E338) — acidulant giving a tangy taste to soft drinks, notably colas, and used in processed foods.
  • Rust removal and metal treatment — converts iron oxide to a protective black ferric phosphate coating; a standard ingredient in rust converters and naval jelly.
  • Detergents and water treatment — formerly widely used in detergents as sodium tripolyphosphate; restricted in many regions because phosphate runoff causes eutrophication.
  • Dentistry and medicine — etchant for tooth enamel; buffer in pharmaceuticals.

Safety

Concentrated phosphoric acid is corrosive to skin and eyes, though it is far less toxic than many mineral acids; dilute solutions are common food ingredients. Large-scale wet-process production generates vast stacks of slightly radioactive phosphogypsum, an ongoing environmental management issue in producing regions such as Florida and Morocco.
