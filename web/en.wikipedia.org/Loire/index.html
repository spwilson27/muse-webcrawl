Loire

The Loire at Amboise

The Loire (French: [lwaʁ]) is the longest river in France and the 171st longest in the world. With a length of 1,012 kilometres (629 mi), it drains an area of 117,054 km2 (45,195 sq mi), roughly one-fifth of France's land area. It rises in the Massif Central at Mont Gerbier de Jonc and flows north and then west through Roanne, Nevers, Orléans, Tours, Angers and Nantes before emptying into the Bay of Biscay at Saint-Nazaire.

The Loire Valley

The central part of the river valley, known as the Jardin de la France ("Garden of France"), is famed for its vineyards and its concentration of historic châteaux, including Chambord, Chenonceau, Amboise, Blois and Villandry. In 2000, UNESCO added the central part of the Loire valley, between Sully-sur-Loire and Chalonnes, to its list of World Heritage Sites. The valley was a major centre of the French Renaissance, when the royal court resided in its castles.

Navigation and floods

The Loire is only partly navigable and is notorious for dramatic changes in flow: its discharge can range from a few tens of cubic metres per second in drought to over 8,000 m³/s in flood. Major floods in 1856 and 1866 led to the construction of extensive levees (levées) along its banks. The river's sandbanks and shifting channels support distinctive ecosystems and it is an important flyway for migratory birds.
