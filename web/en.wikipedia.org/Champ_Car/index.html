Champ Car was the trade name for the series of open-wheel racing cars and the sanctioning organization — descended from Championship Auto Racing Teams (CART) — that governed top-level American championship car racing from 1979 until its collapse and reunification with the rival IndyCar Series in 2008. The term "champ car" itself dates to the early 20th century, when it denoted the big cars of the AAA/USAC national championship.

History

CART era (1979–2003)

Team owners, frustrated with USAC's governance, formed CART in 1979, modelled partly on Formula One's commercial structure. CART sanctioned the PPG Indy Car World Series, racing on ovals, road courses, and street circuits, with turbocharged engines from suppliers such as Cosworth, Ilmor/Chevrolet, Honda, and Toyota, and chassis from Lola, Reynard, Penske, and March. The series flourished in the 1980s and early 1990s, rivalling NASCAR in U.S. popularity with stars like Mario Andretti, Emerson Fittipaldi, Bobby Rahal, Al Unser Jr., and Nigel Mansell (who won the 1993 title after switching from Formula One).

The split and decline

In 1996 Indianapolis Motor Speedway president Tony George founded the rival Indy Racing League, centring on the Indianapolis 500, which CART teams largely boycotted. The "split" divided fans, sponsors, and teams for twelve years. CART went public, then bankrupt after 2003; its assets were bought by team owners who relaunched the series as the Champ Car World Series (2004), standardizing on Cosworth engines and, from 2007, Panoz chassis.

Reunification

In February 2008 Champ Car and the Indy Racing League agreed to unify under IndyCar sanctioning. Champ Car's final race was held at Long Beach in April 2008, and its leading teams (e.g., Newman/Haas, Forsythe, KV Racing) joined the unified IndyCar Series, ending the schism.
