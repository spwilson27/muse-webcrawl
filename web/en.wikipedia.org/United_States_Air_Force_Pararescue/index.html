United States Air Force Pararescue

United States Air Force Pararescue (PJ) is the elite personnel recovery specialty of the United States Air Force. Pararescuemen are trained to rescue and medically treat downed aircrew and other isolated personnel in hostile or denied territory, on land or at sea, under the motto "That Others May Live."

Training

The pararescue training pipeline, sometimes called "the Pipeline" or "Superman School," lasts roughly two years and includes parachute (including freefall), combat diving, survival (SERE), mountain, and advanced paramedic training. Attrition is high, and graduates are among the most comprehensively trained rescue specialists in the world.

Missions

PJs deploy by parachute, helicopter, boat or on foot to reach isolated personnel, provide trauma care under fire, and coordinate extraction. They have also supported NASA recovery operations, disaster relief and civilian rescues, including during hurricanes and in remote terrain.

History

The specialty grew out of Second World War rescue efforts in the China–Burma–India theater and was formally shaped during the Korean and Vietnam Wars, where pararescuemen flying aboard rescue helicopters became known for extraordinary heroism.
