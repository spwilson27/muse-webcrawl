Essex County, Ontario

Essex County in southwestern Ontario, on Lake Erie and the Detroit River

Essex County is a county in the Canadian province of Ontario, the southernmost county in Canada. It is bounded by Lake St. Clair to the north, the Detroit River to the west, and Lake Erie to the south, and contains the city of Windsor as well as Pelee Island, the southernmost inhabited part of Canada.

Geography and communities

The county comprises seven local municipalities: Amherstburg, Essex, Kingsville, Lakeshore, LaSalle, Leamington, and Tecumseh, along with the geographically separate city of Windsor and the township of Pelee. The region is exceptionally flat and low-lying, much of it reclaimed from wetlands and protected by dykes. Its southerly latitude gives it one of the warmest climates in Canada, supporting vineyards, tomato and greenhouse agriculture — Leamington was long known as the "Tomato Capital of Canada" — and major greenhouse vegetable production.

History

Essex County was established in 1792 as part of Upper Canada. The region saw action in the War of 1812, and Fort Malden at Amherstburg is preserved as a national historic site. Point Pelee National Park, established in 1918, is the southernmost point of mainland Canada and a noted stopover for migrating birds and monarch butterflies. The county shares an international border with Detroit, Michigan, across the Detroit River, linked by the Ambassador Bridge and the Windsor–Detroit tunnel, making Windsor one of Canada's busiest border crossings and an automotive manufacturing centre.
