Yan Xishan

Yan Xishan (閻錫山; 8 October 1883 – 22 May 1960) was a Chinese warlord who effectively controlled Shanxi province from the 1911 Xinhai Revolution until the Communist victory in 1949 — one of the longest tenures of any warlord. Known as the "Model Governor," he kept Shanxi comparatively stable and prosperous while neighbouring provinces were wracked by war, balancing among larger powers with the maxim of "carrying on in the eye of a needle" between rivals.

Early life and rise

Born into a banking family in Wutai County, Shanxi, Yan studied at the Taiyuan military academy and then in Japan, where he joined Sun Yat-sen's Tongmenghui. He led the Shanxi uprising in October 1911 and became military governor. Under Yuan Shikai and the Beiyang government he consolidated control, keeping Shanxi out of most conflicts by neutrality and cunning diplomacy.

Model province

Yan promoted village reorganization, opium suppression, anti-footbinding campaigns, universal primary education, road building, and industrial development, including the Taiyuan Arsenal and a narrow-gauge railway network built to a non-standard gauge partly to hinder invasion. Shanxi's relative order was praised by foreign observers, though his rule was authoritarian and paternalistic.

Wars and later career

Yan allied with the Kuomintang in the Northern Expedition (1927–28) but then joined Feng Yuxiang and Li Zongren in the Central Plains War against Chiang Kai-shek in 1930, which he lost. He returned to Shanxi and fought both the Japanese invasion from 1937 — Taiyuan fell that November — and the Communists, cooperating uneasily with each against the other. In 1949, after the fall of Taiyuan, he fled to Guangzhou and then Taiwan, briefly serving as premier of the Republic of China (1949–50). He died in Taipei in 1960.
