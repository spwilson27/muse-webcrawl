Peshwa

The Peshwa was the appointed, and later hereditary, prime minister of the Maratha Empire of India. Originally the chief of the council of ministers (Ashta Pradhan) under the Maratha king, the Peshwa became the effective ruler of the empire in the 18th century, with the Chhatrapati (king) reduced largely to a ceremonial figurehead.

Rise of the office

Shivaji created the office in the 17th century; the first Peshwa was Moropant Trimbak Pingle. The office became hereditary in the Bhat family with Balaji Vishwanath (1713–1720). His son Baji Rao I (1720–1740), a brilliant cavalry commander who never lost a battle, made the Peshwas supreme and moved the seat of power to Pune, where the palace of Shaniwar Wada was built.

Peak and decline

Under Balaji Baji Rao (Nanasaheb), Maratha power reached its greatest extent, stretching across most of the subcontinent, but suffered a catastrophic defeat at the Third Battle of Panipat (1761) against Ahmad Shah Durrani. The gifted Madhavrao I restored Maratha fortunes before his early death in 1772. Later Peshwas struggled against internal rivalries and the rising East India Company.

End of the Peshwaship

The last Peshwa, Baji Rao II, was defeated in the Third Anglo-Maratha War and deposed by the British in 1818, ending the office. His adopted son, Nana Saheb, became a leading figure of the Indian Rebellion of 1857.
