Diriyah

Diriyah (Arabic: الدِرْعِيّة, romanised ad-Dir'īyah), formerly romanised as Dereyeh and Dariyya, is a city and governorate in Saudi Arabia located on the north-western outskirts of the Saudi capital, Riyadh. Diriyah was the home of the House of Saud, and was the capital of the Emirate of Diriyah, the first Saudi dynasty, from 1727 to 1818. It is the ancestral home of the Saudi royal family and one of the most historically significant sites in the Kingdom.

History

Diriyah was founded in 1446 by Mani' ibn Rabiah al-Muraidi, an ancestor of the Saudi family, who settled in the fertile Wadi Hanifa valley. The oasis town prospered on agriculture and trade. In 1744 the local ruler Muhammad ibn Saud formed an alliance with the religious reformer Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab, who had taken refuge in Diriyah — a pact that launched the first Saudi state (Emirate of Diriyah), which expanded across most of the Arabian Peninsula.

Ottoman-Egyptian forces under Ibrahim Pasha besieged and destroyed Diriyah in 1818 after a long campaign, ending the first Saudi state; its ruler, Abdullah ibn Saud, was taken to Constantinople and executed. The ruined mud-brick city was largely abandoned, with settlement shifting to Riyadh, though a newer town grew beside the ruins.

At-Turaif and restoration

The historic At-Turaif district, with the great mud-brick Salwa Palace complex, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2010 as the first capital of the Saudi dynasty and a fine example of the Najdi architectural style. Extensive restoration has turned the district into a museum quarter. Under Saudi Vision 2030, the Diriyah Gate Development Authority is developing the area as a major cultural and tourism destination, including the Bujairi Terrace dining district and large-scale heritage-themed construction.

Geography

Diriyah lies along the seasonal Wadi Hanifa valley, whose palm groves and farms historically supported the settlement. Its climate is arid desert, with very hot summers and cool winters, typical of the Najd plateau.
