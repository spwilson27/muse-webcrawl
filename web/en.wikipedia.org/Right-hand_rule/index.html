Right-hand rule

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics and physics, the right-hand rule is a common mnemonic for understanding orientation of axes in three-dimensional space and for determining the direction of the cross product of two vectors. It is also used to relate the direction of an electric current to the magnetic field it produces, and the direction of rotation to angular velocity and torque vectors.

Cross products

To find the direction of a × b, point the fingers of the right hand in the direction of a and curl them toward b; the extended thumb points in the direction of the cross product. Equivalently, with the index finger along a and the middle finger along b, the thumb gives a × b. This convention underlies the definition of torque (τ = r × F) and angular momentum (L = r × p).

Electromagnetism

Grip a current-carrying wire with the right hand, thumb pointing in the direction of conventional current: the curled fingers show the direction of the encircling magnetic field (Ampère's right-hand grip rule). The same grip applied to a coil gives the direction of its magnetic moment and north pole. Fleming's left-hand rule is the corresponding mnemonic for the force on a conductor in a magnetic field.

Rotation and screws

Curling the fingers of the right hand in the direction of rotation makes the thumb point along the angular velocity vector. A standard screw or bolt advances in the direction of the thumb when turned in the direction of the curled fingers, which is why ordinary threads are called "right-handed".
