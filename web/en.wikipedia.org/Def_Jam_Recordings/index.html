Def Jam Recordings (often styled Def Jam Recordings or simply Def Jam) is an American multinational record label owned by Universal Music Group. Founded in 1984 by Rick Rubin in his New York University dorm room and soon joined by promoter Russell Simmons, it became the most influential label in the commercial breakthrough of hip hop.

History

Def Jam's early releases included singles by T La Rock, LL Cool J, and the Beastie Boys, whose 1986 debut Licensed to Ill was the first rap album to top the Billboard 200. The label's roster expanded to include Public Enemy, Slick Rick, and EPMD. A distribution deal with Columbia Records, and later PolyGram's purchase of a controlling stake, carried the label through the 1990s.

Later years

As part of Universal Music Group, Def Jam absorbed or spawned imprints such as Roc-A-Fella and was home to artists including Jay-Z (who served as the label's president from 2005 to 2007), Kanye West, Rihanna, Justin Bieber, and Logic. The label remains one of the defining institutions of hip hop and contemporary R&B.
