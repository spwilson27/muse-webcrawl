Jäger (infantry)

[Image: Prussian Jäger in the field, 19th century]

Jäger (singular [der] Jäger, plural [die] Jäger, German pronunciation: [ˈjɛːɡɐ]) is a German military term adopted in English to denote a type of light infantry or skirmisher. In modern usage, it generally refers to light infantry units specialising in fighting in difficult terrain. The word Jäger means "hunter" in German, and originally described soldiers recruited from foresters and gamekeepers.

Origins

The first Jäger units were raised in the Landgraviate of Hessen-Kassel in 1631 and in Prussia under Frederick the Great, who valued the marksmanship and fieldcraft of hunters armed with rifled hunting weapons. Unlike line infantry, Jäger fought in open order, aimed individual shots, and used cover — tactics well suited to the wooded terrain of Central Europe and to service in the American War of Independence, where Hessian Jäger served in British employ.

Development

During the Napoleonic Wars, Jäger battalions became standard components of German armies, and the type spread to Austria, Russia and Scandinavia. In the twentieth century the term was applied to mountain troops (Gebirgsjäger), paratroops (Fallschirmjäger), and in Finland the Finnish Jägers influenced the creation of the independent Finnish Army.

Modern usage

In the modern Bundeswehr, Jäger designates light infantry distinct from mechanised (Panzergrenadier) and armoured troops, and the Jäger tradition is preserved in specialised battalions. In Finnish and Estonian forces the term (Finnish jääkäri, Estonian jääger) remains a generic rank and unit designation for infantry.
