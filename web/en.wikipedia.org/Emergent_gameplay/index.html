Emergent gameplay

Emergent gameplay refers to complex situations in video, board, or tabletop roleplaying games that emerge from the interaction of relatively simple game mechanics, rather than from the developer's intent. Designers have attempted to encourage emergent play by providing tools to players such as placement of weapons in a 3D environment.

Intentional emergence

Intentional emergence occurs when some creative uses of the game are intended by the game designers. From the 1970s and 1980s board games and tabletop role playing games such as Cosmic Encounter or Dungeons & Dragons featured intentional emergence, where the rules deliberately allow players to combine tools and actions in unexpected ways. Video game series such as Deus Ex and System Shock are famous for encouraging problem solving through emergent combinations of systems.

Unintentional emergence

Unintentional emergence occurs when creative uses of the video game were not intended by the game designers. Examples include rocket jumping in Quake, combos in Street Fighter II that arose from a system quirk and became central to the series, and the wavedash technique in Super Smash Bros. Melee. In simulation games such as Dwarf Fortress and Minecraft, entire stories and strategies arise from procedural systems interacting in ways their creators did not script.

Emergent narrative

Warren Spector, designer of Deus Ex, has defended the concept of emergent narrative, arguing that designers should provide players with rich simulation systems and let stories grow out of play rather than forcing linear plots. Games such as The Sims and Crusader Kings are often cited for generating unscripted stories from their systems and AI characters.
