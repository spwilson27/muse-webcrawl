Müller-Lyer illusion

TypeGeometric optical illusion
Described byFranz Carl Müller-Lyer (1889)

The Müller-Lyer illusion is an optical illusion consisting of two line segments of identical length, one terminating in arrowheads pointing outward and the other in fins pointing inward. The segment with outward-pointing fins appears longer, although the two are exactly the same length.

Explanation

Several explanations have been proposed, including low-level neural filtering of the junctions, misapplied size-constancy (the "carpentered world" hypothesis, in which the fins are interpreted as depth cues from rectangular architecture), and centroid-of-mass effects. Susceptibility to the illusion varies across cultures, which has been taken as evidence that visual perception is shaped by environment and experience.
