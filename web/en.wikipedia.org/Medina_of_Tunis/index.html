Medina of Tunis

The Medina of Tunis is the medina quarter of Tunis, the capital of Tunisia. It has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1979. The Medina contains some 700 monuments, including palaces, mosques, mausoleums, madrasas and fountains, dating from the Almohad and Hafsid periods. It covers about 270 hectares and is home to roughly 100,000 people, making it one of the largest and best-preserved historic medinas in the Arab world.

History

Tunis was founded in the late 7th century during the Arab conquest of North Africa and grew around the Zitouna Mosque, founded around 698–732, which remains the spiritual heart of the medina. The city flourished under the Aghlabids, Fatimids, and especially the Hafsids (13th–16th centuries), when Tunis became a major centre of trade and learning. Under Ottoman rule from 1574, Turkish-influenced monuments such as the Sidi Mahrez Mosque were added. The medina's dense network of covered souks (markets) — each traditionally specializing in a trade, such as the Souk el Attarine (perfumers), Souk el Berka (jewellers), and the chechia makers — developed around the Zitouna Mosque.

Architecture

The medina is characterized by a labyrinth of narrow streets and covered passages lined with whitewashed houses, grand palaces such as Dar Ben Abdallah and Dar Lasram, ornate madrasas, and numerous mosques and zaouias (religious complexes). Two historic suburbs, Bab Souika to the north and Bab El Jazira to the south, extend the old city beyond its former walls, most of which were demolished in the 19th and 20th centuries.

Conservation

The Association de Sauvegarde de la Médina, founded in 1967, has led restoration efforts, and the medina remains a living city rather than an open-air museum, with continuing programmes to restore monuments and improve residents' living conditions.
