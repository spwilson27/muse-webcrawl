Louis Riel, founder of Manitoba and leader of two Métis resistances.

Louis Riel (October 22, 1844 – November 16, 1885) was a Canadian politician, a founder of the province of Manitoba, and a political leader of the Métis people. He led two resistance movements against the Government of Canada, seeking to defend Métis rights and land in the Red River Colony and the North-West Territories as Canadian settlement expanded westward.

Red River Resistance

Educated in part in Montreal, Riel returned to Red River in 1868 as Canada prepared to acquire Rupert's Land from the Hudson's Bay Company without consulting its inhabitants. In 1869–70 he led the Red River Resistance, forming a provisional government that negotiated Manitoba's entry into Confederation under the Manitoba Act of 1870. The execution of Thomas Scott by Riel's government made him a fugitive in Ontario, though he was elected three times to the House of Commons while in exile or hiding.

Exile and the North-West Resistance

Riel spent years in the United States, suffered religious visions and a period of confinement in asylums, married Marguerite Monet dit Bellehumeur, and became an American citizen. In 1884 Métis delegates asked him to return to the Saskatchewan valley to present their grievances. When Ottawa failed to respond adequately, a provisional government was proclaimed at Batoche in March 1885, leading to the North-West Resistance. Métis and allied Indigenous forces under Gabriel Dumont won early engagements at Duck Lake and Fish Creek but were defeated at Batoche in May 1885.

Trial and execution

Riel surrendered and was tried for high treason in Regina before an English-speaking Protestant jury. Despite a defence of insanity and a jury recommendation of mercy, he was hanged on November 16, 1885. His execution bitterly divided English and French Canada, damaged the Conservative government in Quebec, and made Riel an enduring symbol — variously of resistance, nationhood, and martyrdom — for the Métis and for French Canadians.

Legacy

Manitoba formally recognises Riel as its founder; a provincial holiday, Louis Riel Day, is observed in February, and in 2024 Manitoba legislation named him the province's honorary first premier. Statues of Riel stand at the Manitoba Legislative Building and in Regina.
