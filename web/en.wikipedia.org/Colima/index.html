Colima

Colima is one of the 31 states of Mexico, located on the central Pacific coast. It is the fourth-smallest state in the country and one of the least populous, with a population of roughly 730,000. Its capital and largest city is also called Colima; its most important port and tourist centre is Manzanillo. The state also administers the Revillagigedo Islands far offshore in the Pacific Ocean.

Geography

Colima is bordered by Jalisco to the north and east and Michoacán to the south, with a short Pacific coastline. Its landscape is dominated by the Colima volcano complex: the older, extinct Nevado de Colima (4,339 m) and the younger, highly active Volcán de Colima (3,820 m), one of Mexico's most active volcanoes. The state's climate ranges from hot and humid on the coast to temperate in the northern highlands.

History

The region was home to important pre-Hispanic cultures, known for the shaft-tomb tradition and the distinctive ceramic "Colima dogs". After Spanish conquest in the 16th century, the area was administered from New Galicia and later Jalisco; Colima became a separate state in 1856. Its capital was founded as a Spanish villa in 1523, making it one of the oldest cities in Mexico.

Economy

Manzanillo is one of Mexico's busiest ports and a major resort destination. Agriculture includes limes — the region is famous for Mexican limes — coconuts, bananas, and melons, alongside mining of iron ore and the Tecomán agricultural valley.
