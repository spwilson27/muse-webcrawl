Howiesons Poort

The Howiesons Poort (also spelled Howieson's Poort) is a lithic technology cultural phase of the Middle Stone Age in southern Africa, named after the Howiesons Poort Shelter rock shelter near Grahamstown (Makhanda) in the Eastern Cape province of South Africa. It is dated to roughly 65,000–60,000 years ago and is notable for early evidence of complex, modern human behaviour.

Characteristics

The Howiesons Poort is characterised by small, standardised backed tools — crescents (segments), trapezoids, and backed blades — produced from fine-grained materials such as silcrete, hornfels, and quartz, often heat-treated to improve flaking. These segments were hafted into composite tools, likely as barbs or tips of arrows and spears, making the phase a candidate for some of the earliest evidence of bow-and-arrow technology.

Important sites include Klasies River, Sibudu Cave, Diepkloof Rock Shelter, and Rose Cottage Cave. Engraved ostrich eggshell containers from Diepkloof, associated with the phase, are among the earliest examples of graphic representation and storage technology.

Significance

Together with the preceding Still Bay industry, the Howiesons Poort is frequently cited in debates about the origins of behavioural modernity, because its standardised composite tools and symbolic artefacts appear, disappear, and are replaced by later industries in a pattern suggesting pulses of innovation among early Homo sapiens populations in Africa.
