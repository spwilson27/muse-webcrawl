Focus (optics)

In geometrical optics, a focus, also called an image point, is a point where light rays originating from a point on an object converge. Although the focus is conceptually a point, physically it has size, caused by aberrations and diffraction; this blur circle determines how sharp an image can be. The related concepts of focal length, focal point, and focal plane describe where this convergence occurs for rays arriving parallel to the optical axis.

In and out of focus

An image, or image point, is said to be in focus if light from a point on the object is converged almost as much as possible at the image plane, and out of focus if it is not well converged. Near the focus, the ray bundle's convergence region — its caustic — has a characteristic shape. The acceptable amount of defocus defines the depth of field and depth of focus of an imaging system.

Eye and camera focusing

The human eye focuses by changing the shape of its lens (accommodation); cameras focus by moving lens elements relative to the sensor. Autofocus systems measure focus error using phase detection or contrast detection and drive the lens until the image is sharp.

Other uses

In telescopes and microscopes, focusing adjusts the distance between objective and eyepiece or specimen. The term also extends beyond light: electron microscopes focus electron beams with magnetic lenses, and acoustic mirrors focus sound at a focal point.
