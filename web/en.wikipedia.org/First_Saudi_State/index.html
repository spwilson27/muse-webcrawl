First Saudi State

The First Saudi State, also known as the Emirate of Diriyah, was established in 1727 when the ruler of Diriyah, Muhammad ibn Saud, concluded the Diriyah pact with the religious reformer Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab. The alliance between the Al Saud family and the Wahhabi movement expanded Saudi rule across most of the Arabian Peninsula before the state was destroyed by an Ottoman-Egyptian invasion in 1818.

Expansion

From its base in the Najd, the emirate absorbed Riyadh and the surrounding towns, then extended its power into eastern Arabia and, at the start of the 19th century, into the Hejaz: Wahhabi forces captured Mecca in 1803 and Medina shortly thereafter, and raided as far as Karbala in Ottoman Iraq (1802), where the shrine of Husayn was sacked. Imam Saud ibn Abdulaziz (r. 1803–1814) presided over the state's greatest extent.

Destruction

Alarmed by the loss of the holy cities, the Ottoman sultan directed Muhammad Ali of Egypt to recover them. After a long campaign, Ibrahim Pasha advanced into the Najd and besieged Diriyah in 1818; the last imam, Abdullah ibn Saud, surrendered and was later executed in Constantinople, and Diriyah was razed. A Second Saudi State was re-established in the Najd a few years later.
