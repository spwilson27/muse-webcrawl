Vapour pressure

Vapour pressure is the pressure of a vapour in equilibrium with its liquid or solid phase at a particular temperature. The spelling "vapour" is standard in British English, while "vapor" is used in American English; the physical quantity is identical.

Explanation

Molecules at the surface of a liquid continually escape into the gas phase, while vapour molecules collide with the surface and re-enter the liquid. When the rates of evaporation and condensation balance in a closed container, the vapour is saturated and its pressure is the equilibrium vapour pressure. This pressure depends only on the substance and the temperature — not on the amount of liquid present — and it increases approximately exponentially with temperature.

Applications

Vapour pressure data underpin the design of distillation processes, the prediction of boiling points, meteorology (through dew point and humidity calculations), and the assessment of chemical volatility and environmental fate.
