Thomas John Sargent (born July 19, 1943) is an American economist and the W.R. Berkley Professor of Economics and Business at New York University. He shared the 2011 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences with Christopher A. Sims "for their empirical research on cause and effect in the macroeconomy."

Contributions

Sargent is a central figure of the rational expectations revolution in macroeconomics, following Robert Lucas. His work on the policy ineffectiveness proposition, on "unpleasant monetarist arithmetic" (with Neil Wallace), and on the ends of several European hyperinflations shaped modern thinking about the credibility of monetary and fiscal policy.

Works

His graduate textbooks, including Recursive Macroeconomic Theory (with Lars Ljungqvist) and Dynamic Macroeconomic Theory, helped define the modern macroeconomic curriculum. He has taught at the University of Pennsylvania, the University of Minnesota, the University of Chicago, and Stanford University.
