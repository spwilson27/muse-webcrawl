Alsace wine

Alsace wine (French: Vin d'Alsace) is produced in the Alsace region of northeastern France, along a narrow strip of vineyards on the eastern foothills of the Vosges mountains. Unlike most French wine regions, Alsace labels its wines primarily by grape variety and is renowned for aromatic, mostly dry white wines — Riesling, Gewürztraminer, Pinot Gris, Muscat, and Pinot Blanc — sold in the tall, slender "flûte d'Alsace" bottle. The region also produces Crémant d'Alsace sparkling wine, the rosé and light red Pinot Noir, and the prestigious late-harvest Vendanges Tardives and Sélection de Grains Nobles dessert wines.

Geography and climate

The Vosges shelter the vineyards from Atlantic rain, making Alsace one of France's driest and sunniest wine areas (Colmar averages roughly 600 mm of rain a year). Soils vary dramatically over short distances — granite, schist, limestone, marl, sandstone, and volcanic rock — a diversity that growers express through single-vineyard wines and the 51 Grand Cru designations.

Appellations and wine types

  • Alsace AOC covers varietal and blended wines (the traditional blend is called Edelzwicker; Gentil is a higher-quality blend).
  • Alsace Grand Cru AOC applies to wines from 51 named sites, restricted to Riesling, Gewürztraminer, Pinot Gris, and Muscat (with a notable exception for Sylvaner from Zotzenberg).
  • Crémant d'Alsace, made by the traditional method, chiefly from Pinot Blanc, is France's second-best-selling sparkling AOC after Champagne.
  • Vendanges Tardives ("late harvest") and Sélection de Grains Nobles (berry-by-berry selection affected by noble rot) are rich, sweet wines from the four noble varieties.

History

Viticulture in Alsace dates to Roman times. The region's wine trade flourished in the Middle Ages and Renaissance, declined during the Thirty Years' War and after phylloxera, and revived in the 20th century with an emphasis on quality varietal wines. Alsace's years under German rule (1871–1918, 1940–44) distorted its production toward bulk wine, a reputation the postwar AOC system sought to repair. The Alsace Wine Route (Route des Vins d'Alsace), established in 1953 and running some 170 km from Marlenheim to Thann, is among France's oldest wine tourism itineraries.
