Red Square

Red Square (Russian: Красная площадь, romanized: Krasnaya ploshchad) is one of the oldest and largest squares in Moscow, Russia. It is located in Moscow's historic centre, in the eastern walls of the Kremlin. It is very rich in history and considered the central square of Moscow. Its name derives not from the colour of the bricks or from communism, but from the Russian word krasnaya, which in older usage meant "beautiful".

History

The square originated in 1493 when Ivan III ordered the clearing of wooden buildings outside the Kremlin walls to reduce fire risk and create a marketplace. Over the centuries it served as Moscow's main market, a site for public ceremonies, proclamations and executions. Saint Basil's Cathedral was built at its southern end in 1555–1561 by order of Ivan the Terrible.

Landmarks

The square is bounded by the Kremlin wall and Lenin's Mausoleum to the west, the State Historical Museum to the north, the GUM department store to the east, and Saint Basil's Cathedral to the south. The Iberian Gate and Chapel, demolished under Stalin in 1931, were rebuilt in the 1990s. Kazan Cathedral, also demolished, was likewise reconstructed.

Soviet and modern era

Under the Soviet Union, Red Square became a ceremonial centre for state parades, most famously the annual October Revolution and Victory Day parades; the 1945 Moscow Victory Parade was held here. In 1987 the West German teenager Mathias Rust landed a small Cessna aircraft near the square, humiliating the Soviet military. Today the square hosts concerts, festivals and the annual 9 May Victory Day parade, and is closed to traffic as a pedestrian zone.
