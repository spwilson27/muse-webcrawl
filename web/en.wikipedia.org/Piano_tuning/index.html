Piano tuning

Piano tuning is the act of adjusting the tension of a piano's strings — more than 200 of them, held at a combined tension of many tonnes — so that the musical intervals between their tones are in tune, conventionally in equal temperament with A4 at 440 Hz.

Practice

A tuner uses a tuning hammer (lever) to turn the tuning pins, setting a temperament octave by ear using beat rates between intervals, then tuning the remaining octaves outward. Because piano strings are stiff, their overtones are sharp (inharmonicity), so tuners "stretch" octaves slightly wide. Pianos typically need tuning at least once or twice a year, as humidity changes swell or shrink the soundboard.

Profession

Piano technicians also perform regulation (adjusting the action), voicing (adjusting hammer felt) and repairs. Electronic tuning devices are now widely used alongside aural tuning.
