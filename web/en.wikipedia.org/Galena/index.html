Galena, also called lead glance, is the natural mineral form of lead(II) sulfide (PbS). It is the most important ore of lead and an important source of silver. Galena is one of the most abundant and widely distributed sulfide minerals. It crystallizes in the cubic crystal system, often showing octahedral forms. It is often associated with the minerals sphalerite, calcite and fluorite.

Occurrence

Galena is the primary ore mineral of lead, worked since ancient times for its high lead content and ease of smelting. Deposits occur worldwide, notably in the Mississippi Valley of the United States (the historic Lead Belt of Missouri), the Pennines of England, Broken Hill in Australia, and Freiberg in Germany.

Uses

Beyond its role as lead ore, galena was historically used in kohl, an ancient Egyptian eye cosmetic, and its semiconducting properties made galena crystals ("cat's whiskers") useful in early crystal radio receivers. Galena deposits frequently carry substantial silver, recovered as a by-product of lead refining.

Structure

Galena belongs to the rock salt structure group, with lead and sulfur atoms arranged in a regular cubic lattice. Its perfect cubic cleavage reflects this internal symmetry.
