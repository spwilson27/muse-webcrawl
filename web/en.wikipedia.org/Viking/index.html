Viking

Vikings were seafaring people originally from Scandinavia (present-day Denmark, Norway and Sweden), who from the late 8th to the late 11th centuries raided, pirated, traded and settled throughout parts of Europe, and as far as North Africa, the Middle East and North America.

[Image: A reconstructed Viking longship with a striped square sail and dragon prow at sea.]

The Viking Age

The Viking Age is conventionally dated from the raid on Lindisfarne in 793 to the Battle of Stamford Bridge in 1066. Viking longships enabled deep penetration of rivers and coasts: Norsemen founded settlements in Iceland and Greenland, reached Newfoundland at L'Anse aux Meadows, ruled the Danelaw in England and Normandy in France, and served as the Varangian Guard in Constantinople.

Society and legacy

Viking society was organized around chieftains and assemblies (things), with a rich Norse mythology and a literature of sagas. The word "Viking" itself likely derives from Old Norse víkingr, denoting a pirate or overseas expedition.
