Small business

A small business is a privately owned corporation, partnership or sole proprietorship that has fewer employees and lower annual revenue than a regular-sized business or corporation. Definitions vary by country and industry. In the United States, the Small Business Administration sets size standards by industry — commonly up to 500 employees for manufacturing and mining and under a defined revenue threshold for services — while the European Union defines small enterprises as those with fewer than 50 employees and turnover or balance-sheet totals below €10 million, with micro-enterprises smaller still.

Economic role

Small businesses constitute the great majority of firms in most economies and are a major source of employment and new job creation. In the United States, small businesses account for about 99.9% of all firms and employ roughly half of the private workforce. They include corner shops, restaurants, trades, professional practices, small manufacturers and technology startups. Economists credit small firms with flexibility, local responsiveness and innovation, though collectively they also account for most business failures, with a large share of new businesses closing within their first five years.

Challenges

Access to finance is a persistent constraint: small businesses rely heavily on owner savings, bank loans and trade credit, and are more vulnerable to cash-flow interruptions and economic downturns. They bear proportionately higher regulatory and administrative costs than large firms and may struggle to compete for skilled labor or to adopt new technology. Marketing has been transformed by e-commerce platforms and social media, which lower the cost of reaching customers but expose small firms to global competition.

Support

Governments support small businesses through loan guarantees, set-asides in public procurement, simplified tax regimes, advisory services and insolvency frameworks that permit restructuring. In the United States, the SBA guarantees loans made by private lenders and runs disaster assistance; many countries maintain analogous agencies, development banks and local enterprise partnerships. "Shop local" campaigns and events such as Small Business Saturday encourage consumer support.
