Moët & Chandon

Moët & Chandon
Founded: 1743, Épernay, France
Founder: Claude Moët
Parent: LVMH (Moët Hennessy)
Products: Champagne, incl. Dom Pérignon

Moët & Chandon (French pronunciation: [mɔ.ɛt e ʃɑ̃dɔ̃]) is a French fine-winery and part of the luxury group LVMH. Founded in 1743 by Claude Moët in Épernay, in the Champagne region, it is one of the world's largest and best-known champagne producers, producing approximately 28 million bottles annually.

History

Claude Moët began shipping wine from Champagne to Paris in 1743, and the house won favour at the court of Louis XV; Madame de Pompadour was a notable admirer. His grandson Jean-Rémy Moët expanded the house internationally and hosted Napoleon Bonaparte, after whom the flagship cuvée Impérial is named. In 1833 the name became Moët & Chandon when Pierre-Gabriel Chandon, who had married into the family, joined as partner. In 1971 the house merged with Hennessy, and in 1987 that group merged with Louis Vuitton to form LVMH.

Dom Pérignon

The house's prestige cuvée, Dom Pérignon, is a vintage champagne named after the 17th-century Benedictine monk traditionally — though mythically — credited with pioneering champagne production. It is produced only in declared vintage years from Chardonnay and Pinot Noir grapes. The brand was acquired by Moët & Chandon in 1937, and the first vintage, 1921, was released in 1936.

Estates and production

Moët & Chandon owns some 1,200 hectares of vineyards — among the largest estates in Champagne — and maintains 28 kilometres of cellars beneath Épernay, the largest in the region. Its best-known wines include Moët Impérial, Rosé Impérial, Ice Impérial, and the Grand Vintage collection.
