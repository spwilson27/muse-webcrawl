Headquarters of the United Nations

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

United Nations Headquarters
Location: 760 United Nations Plaza, Manhattan, New York City
Construction: 1948–1952
Architects: Board of Design (incl. Le Corbusier, Oscar Niemeyer), Wallace Harrison
Status: International territory (extraterritorial)

The United Nations headquarters is a complex in the Turtle Bay neighbourhood of Manhattan, New York City, serving as the official seat of the United Nations since its completion in 1951. Built on an 18-acre site along the East River purchased with an $8.5 million donation from John D. Rockefeller Jr., the complex is considered international territory: it is administered by the UN, flies only UN and member-state flags, and has its own security and postal services, though it remains subject to most local and federal laws by agreement.

Buildings

The complex centres on the 39-storey Secretariat Building, a glass-curtain-wall slab that became an icon of the International Style; the General Assembly Building, containing the General Assembly Hall seating all 193 member states; the Conference Building, housing the Security Council, ECOSOC, and Trusteeship Council chambers; and the Dag Hammarskjöld Library. The design was produced by an international Board of Design including Le Corbusier and Oscar Niemeyer, whose combined scheme (based largely on Niemeyer's plan and Le Corbusier's building forms) was coordinated by director of planning Wallace Harrison. The cornerstone was laid in 1949 and the first staff moved in during 1951; the General Assembly first met in the new hall in 1952.

Features

Grounds include the UN rose garden, sculptures donated by member states, the Japanese Peace Bell, and a display of flags of all member nations in alphabetical order. A major renovation, the Capital Master Plan (roughly 2008–2014), modernised the complex for safety, security, and energy efficiency. The UN also maintains major offices in Geneva, Vienna, and Nairobi.
