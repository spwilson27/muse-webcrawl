Shawarma

Shawarma
RegionLevant and Middle East (Ottoman origins)
TypeMeat dish, wrap sandwich
Main ingredientsMarinated meat (lamb, chicken, beef, turkey) roasted on a vertical spit; flatbread; tahini or garlic sauce; pickles; vegetables
Related dishesDöner kebab, gyros, al pastor

Shawarma (Arabic: شاورما) is a popular Middle Eastern dish of meat — traditionally mutton or lamb, today commonly chicken, beef, or turkey — marinated, stacked in a cone-like shape on a vertical rotisserie, and roasted slowly as the spit turns. Thin slices are shaved off the outer layer as it cooks and are typically served wrapped in flatbread such as pita or laffa with tahini or garlic sauce (toum), pickles, onions, tomatoes, and fries. The name derives from the Turkish çevirme, "turning".

History

Roasting stacked meat on a vertical spit developed in the Ottoman Empire in the 19th century; the related döner kebab is often associated with Bursa, Turkey. From the Levant the technique spread across the Arab world, where it became shawarma, and with migration it reached global popularity. Lebanese emigrants to Mexico in the early 20th century adapted the method to pork cooked with pineapple, creating tacos al pastor, while Greek gyros and German-style döner sandwiches developed in parallel.

Preparation

Slices of seasoned meat and fat are threaded onto a tall skewer — restaurant spits may hold tens of kilograms — and cooked for hours before a vertical grill. The cook shaves the browned surface with a long knife, catching the falling strips on bread or a tray. Marinades vary by region and meat: chicken shawarma in the Levant is often scented with cardamom, cinnamon, and paprika, and is served with garlic sauce and pickles; beef and lamb versions may come with tahini, sumac onions, and parsley. In Saudi Arabia and the Gulf, small wraps dominate; in parts of the Levant, platters with rice or bread are also common.

Regional variations

Shawarma is a staple street food in Lebanon, Syria, Jordan, Palestine, Israel, Egypt (where it is often beef or chicken with tahini), Iraq, and the Arabian Peninsula, and is ubiquitous in cities worldwide through Middle Eastern restaurants and food trucks. Regional touches include Iraqi amber (mango) sauce, pomegranate molasses in some Levantine shops, and saj bread wraps.
