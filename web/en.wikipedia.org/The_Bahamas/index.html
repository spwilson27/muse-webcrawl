The Bahamas

The Bahamas, officially the Commonwealth of The Bahamas, is an island country within the Lucayan Archipelago of the Atlantic Ocean. It consists of more than 3,000 islands, cays and islets north of Cuba and southeast of Florida. The capital is Nassau, on the island of New Providence. The country covers about 13,943 square kilometres with a population of roughly 400,000.

History

The islands were first inhabited by the Lucayan people, a branch of the Taíno. Christopher Columbus made his first New World landfall at San Salvador island in 1492; the Lucayans were soon enslaved and the population destroyed. The English settled Eleuthera in 1648 and the islands became a British crown colony in 1718, when piracy was suppressed by Woodes Rogers. After the American Revolution, Loyalists resettled with enslaved Africans, whose descendants form the majority of the population. The Bahamas gained independence from the United Kingdom on 10 July 1973, remaining a Commonwealth realm with Charles III as head of state.

Geography and climate

The archipelago is low-lying, formed of coral limestone, with the highest point only 64 metres at Mount Alvernia on Cat Island. The climate is tropical savanna, warmed by the Gulf Stream, and the islands lie in the Atlantic hurricane belt; Hurricane Dorian devastated Abaco and Grand Bahama in 2019.

Economy

Tourism accounts for roughly half of GDP, drawing millions of visitors to resorts such as Atlantis Paradise Island, followed by offshore financial services. The Bahamian dollar is pegged at par to the US dollar. The country is known for Junkanoo festivals, conch cuisine, and as the home of actor Sidney Poitier.
