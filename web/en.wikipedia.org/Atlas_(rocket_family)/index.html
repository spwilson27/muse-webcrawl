Atlas (rocket family)

Atlas is a family of US missiles and space launch vehicles that originated with the SM-65 Atlas intercontinental ballistic missile. The Atlas intercontinental ballistic missile was developed by General Dynamics (now part of Lockheed Martin) for the United States Air Force, and first flew in 1957.

An Atlas V launching from Cape Canaveral.

Design

The original Atlas used a distinctive "stage-and-a-half" design with pressure-stabilised stainless-steel balloon tanks, jettisoning two of its three engines during ascent. Converted missiles launched the Mercury astronauts — John Glenn's Friendship 7 flight in 1962 — and, paired with Agena and Centaur upper stages, sent many early probes and satellites to orbit.

Later versions

The Atlas II and Atlas III modernised the vehicle in the 1990s. The Atlas V, developed by Lockheed Martin and operated by United Launch Alliance from 2002, uses a Russian-built RD-180 first-stage engine and the Centaur upper stage; it has carried missions including New Horizons to Pluto, the Curiosity and Perseverance Mars rovers, and Boeing's Starliner spacecraft. Atlas V is being retired in favour of the Vulcan Centaur.
