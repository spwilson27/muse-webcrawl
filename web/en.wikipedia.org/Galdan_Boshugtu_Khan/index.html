Galdan Boshugtu Khan

Galdan Boshugtu Khan
Born: 1644
Died: 1697
Reign: Khan of the Dzungar Khanate, 1671–1697
Predecessor: Sengge
Successor: Tsewang Rabtan

Galdan Boshugtu Khan (1644–1697) was a Choros-Oirat prince and the khan of the Dzungar Khanate from 1671 until his death. The last great steppe conqueror of Central Asia, Galdan expanded Dzungar power from the Altai across the Tarim Basin and into Mongolia, bringing him into a climactic war with Qing China under the Kangxi Emperor, which ended in his defeat and death.

Early life

Galdan was a son of Erdeni Batur, founder of the Dzungar Khanate. Sent to Tibet as a youth, he studied as a Buddhist monk in Lhasa under the Fifth Dalai Lama and the Fourth Panchen Lama. When his brother Sengge was assassinated in 1671, Galdan renounced his monastic vows, returned to Dzungaria, avenged his brother, and took the throne, receiving the title Boshugtu Khan ("Khan by Heaven's mandate") from the Dalai Lama.

Conquests

Galdan conquered the oasis cities of the Tarim Basin (Kashgar, Yarkand, Turfan, and Hami) between 1678 and 1680, installing the Afaqi Khojas as vassals, and subdued the Kazakhs to the west. Turning east, he invaded the Khalkha Mongols in 1688, driving the Tüsheet Khan and the Jebtsundamba Khutuktu into flight to the Qing. In 1690 his army fought Qing forces to a draw at Ulan Butung, close to Beijing, but the Kangxi Emperor's great expedition of 1696 crushed the Dzungars at the Battle of Jao Modo. Deserted by many followers, and opposed by his nephew Tsewang Rabtan, Galdan died in 1697 in the Altai region — by suicide according to some accounts — as Qing armies closed in.

Legacy

Galdan was the last ruler to bid for a unified Mongol steppe empire in the tradition of Genghis Khan. His wars fixed the boundary between the Qing sphere and the Dzungars for a generation; the Dzungar Khanate itself survived until its destruction by the Qianlong Emperor in the 1750s, which brought Xinjiang and Mongolia permanently into the Chinese empire.
