Second Battle of Ypres

Date22 April – 25 May 1915
LocationYpres, Belgium
ResultGerman gains limited; Ypres salient held by the Allies
BelligerentsGerman Empire vs. France, British Empire (including Canada), Belgium

The Second Battle of Ypres was fought from 22 April to 25 May 1915 during the First World War, around the Ypres salient in western Belgium. It is remembered above all as the first mass use of poison gas on the Western Front.

The Ypres salient during the battle, spring 1915.

The gas attack

On 22 April 1915, German troops released about 168 tons of chlorine gas from thousands of cylinders opposite French colonial and territorial divisions north of Ypres. The greenish-yellow cloud, carried by the wind, opened a gap several kilometres wide in the Allied line. German infantry advanced behind it but, lacking reserves and protective equipment of their own, did not exploit the breakthrough fully. Canadian troops on the flank held their ground, improvising respirators from cloth soaked in water or urine, and counterattacked at Kitchener's Wood that night.

Course of the battle

Further gas attacks followed, including against Canadian positions at St. Julien on 24 April. Fighting continued around Gravenstafel Ridge, St. Julien, Frezenberg Ridge, and Bellewaarde Ridge into late May. The British Second Army, under Sir Horace Smith-Dorrien and then Herbert Plumer, shortened its line by withdrawing to a tighter perimeter around Ypres. Total casualties were roughly 59,000 British and Commonwealth and about 35,000 German, with heavy French losses as well.

Legacy

The battle marked the beginning of large-scale chemical warfare in the war, prompting rapid development of gas masks by all sides. It is also commemorated in the poem "In Flanders Fields", written by Canadian medical officer John McCrae during the battle, and in the valour of the 1st Canadian Division in its first major engagement.
