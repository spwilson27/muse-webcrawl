The Lamiales are an order of flowering plants in the asterid clade of the dicotyledons, containing about 23,800 species in roughly 24 families and more than 1,000 genera. Well-known members include lavender, lilac, olive, jasmine, snapdragon, sesame, psyllium, and many culinary herbs, as well as carnivorous plants such as butterworts and bladderworts.

Characteristics

Species in the order typically have opposite leaves, bilaterally symmetrical flowers with a fused corolla, and often four (or two) stamens. Many families bear glandular hairs producing aromatic compounds. Molecular phylogenetics has greatly reshaped the order, expanding families such as Plantaginaceae and Orobanchaceae.

Major families

The largest families are Lamiaceae (mints), Acanthaceae, Gesneriaceae, Plantaginaceae (plantains and foxgloves), Scrophulariaceae, Oleaceae (olives), Bignoniaceae, and Lentibulariaceae. The parasitic broomrape family Orobanchaceae is also notable.
