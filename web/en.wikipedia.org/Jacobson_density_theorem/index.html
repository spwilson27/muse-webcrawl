Jacobson density theorem

Jacobson density theorem
FieldRing theory, module theory
Proved byNathan Jacobson (1945)
Consequence ofSchur's lemma

In mathematics, the Jacobson density theorem is a result in ring theory and module theory, named after Nathan Jacobson, concerning the structure of simple modules over a ring. It generalises the Wedderburn–Artin theorem and shows that a ring acting on a simple module is "dense" in the ring of all linear transformations of the module considered as a vector space over its endomorphism division ring.

Statement

Let R be a ring and let M be a simple right R-module. By Schur's lemma, the endomorphism ring D = EndR(M) is a division ring, and M is a vector space over D. The theorem states that the image of R in EndD(M) is dense in the finite topology: for any D-linearly independent elements x1, …, xn of M and arbitrary elements y1, …, yn in M, there exists r in R with xir = yi for all i. In particular, if M is finite-dimensional over D, then R acts as the full matrix ring of D-linear transformations.

Chevalley–Jacobson density theorem

A version due to Chevalley extends the statement: the density result holds for a set of endomorphisms commuting with the action, a formulation often called the Chevalley–Jacobson density theorem. It underlies the treatment of semisimple modules: a semisimple ring is a finite product of full matrix rings over division rings.

Applications

The theorem is a key step in the proof of the Wedderburn–Artin theorem classifying semisimple rings, in the theory of primitive rings (a ring is primitive if and only if it is a dense ring of linear transformations over a division ring), and in the study of the Jacobson radical, which Jacobson developed in the same period.
