Nautical chart

A nautical chart or hydrographic chart is a graphic representation of a sea area and adjacent coastal regions. Depending on the scale, charts may show depths of water and heights of land (soundings and contours), natural features of the seabed, details of the coastline, navigational hazards, locations of natural and human-made aids to navigation, and information on tides and currents. Nautical charts are essential tools for marine navigation; by international law, vessels must carry up-to-date charts for the waters in which they operate.

Production and content

Charts are produced by national hydrographic offices — such as the United Kingdom Hydrographic Office (Admiralty charts), NOAA's Office of Coast Survey in the United States — from hydrographic survey data. Depth information is referenced to a chart datum, typically a low water level; soundings may be in metres, feet, or fathoms depending on the chart. Charts employ standard symbology published in documents such as INT1, and most modern charts use the Mercator projection, on which a rhumb line plots as a straight line. Corrections are promulgated through Notices to Mariners.

Electronic charts

Paper charts are increasingly supplemented or replaced by electronic systems: the Electronic Chart Display and Information System (ECDIS), which displays official Electronic Navigational Charts (ENCs) in the IHO S-57/S-100 vector formats, and raster navigational charts that reproduce paper chart images. IMO carriage requirements have been phased in so that most large commercial vessels must operate ECDIS with official ENCs, retaining paper charts or a second ECDIS as backup.

History

Precursors include medieval portolan charts of the Mediterranean. Systematic national charting began in the 18th and 19th centuries with the expansion of naval hydrographic offices and global seaborne trade.
