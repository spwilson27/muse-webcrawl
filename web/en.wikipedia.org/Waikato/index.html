Waikato (Māori: Waikato) is a region of the upper North Island of New Zealand. It covers the Waikato, Waipā, Thames-Coromandel, Hauraki, Matamata-Piako, South Waikato and Waitomo districts, and includes the city of Hamilton and the towns of Cambridge, Te Awamutu, Matamata, Morrinsville, Huntly, Thames, Coromandel, Whitianga, Tokoroa, Putāruru and Te Kūiti. The name is taken from the Waikato River, New Zealand's longest river, which flows through the region.

Geography

The region stretches from the Coromandel Peninsula and the Hauraki Plains in the east to the rugged King Country in the west, and from the Bombay Hills north of Hamilton to the slopes of Mount Ruapehu in the south. Its fertile alluvial plains, drained by the Waikato River and its tributaries, support some of New Zealand's most productive dairy farming. The west coast includes the harbours of Raglan, Aotea and Kawhia.

History

The Waikato was home to the Tainui confederation of Māori iwi. In the 1860s the region was a central theatre of the New Zealand Wars: the invasion of the Waikato in 1863–64 by British and colonial forces led to the defeat of the Kīngitanga (Māori King movement) and the confiscation (raupatu) of large areas of land, a grievance addressed by the Waikato Raupatu Claims Settlement Act 1995. Hamilton, founded in 1864 as a military settlement, grew into New Zealand's fourth-largest city.

Economy

Agriculture — especially dairy farming, along with thoroughbred horse breeding around Matamata and Cambridge — dominates the economy. Hamilton hosts the University of Waikato (founded 1964) and is a centre for agricultural research and technology. Tourism centres on the Coromandel Peninsula beaches, the Waitomo Caves glowworm grottos, and the Hobbiton movie set near Matamata.
