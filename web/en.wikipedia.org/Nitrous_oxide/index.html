Nitrous oxide

Nitrous oxide (dinitrogen oxide), commonly known as laughing gas, nitrous, or nangs, is a chemical compound, an oxide of nitrogen with the formula N2O. At room temperature it is a colourless, non-flammable gas with a slightly sweet odour and taste.

History

The gas was first synthesised in 1772 by English chemist Joseph Priestley. Humphry Davy experimented with it at the end of the 18th century, noting its euphoric and analgesic effects and coining the name "laughing gas". In 1844 the American dentist Horace Wells demonstrated its use as an anaesthetic in dentistry.

Uses

Nitrous oxide has been used in surgery and dentistry for its anaesthetic and pain-reducing effects since the 19th century; it is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines. In motor racing and automotive applications it is used as an oxidiser to increase engine power ("nitrous"). In the food industry it serves as a propellant in whipped cream chargers. It is also used recreationally for its euphoric effects, which has raised public-health concerns, including vitamin B12 deficiency and nerve damage with heavy use.

Environmental impact

Nitrous oxide is a potent greenhouse gas, with a global warming potential roughly 270 times that of carbon dioxide over 100 years, and it is now the most significant ozone-depleting substance emitted through human activities. Major sources include agricultural soils fertilised with nitrogen, fossil fuel combustion and industrial processes.
