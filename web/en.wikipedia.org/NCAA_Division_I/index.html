NCAA Division I

Division I men's basketball is among the most watched college sports

NCAA Division I (D-I) is the highest level of intercollegiate athletics sanctioned by the National Collegiate Athletic Association (NCAA) in the United States, which appoints schools to the division based on their athletic budgets and facilities, as well as the popularity of the sports they sponsor. D-I schools include the major collegiate athletic powers, with large budgets, more elaborate facilities and more scholarships than Divisions II and III.

The division was created in 1973 when the NCAA's predecessor University Division was renamed. As of recent seasons it comprises more than 350 member institutions and over 6,700 teams, with roughly 180,000 student-athletes competing.

Football subdivisions

For football only, Division I is split into the Football Bowl Subdivision (FBS) and the Football Championship Subdivision (FCS). FBS schools compete in bowl games and, since 2014, the College Football Playoff determines the FBS national champion. FCS schools determine their champion through a 24-team playoff tournament. The FBS level requires schools to average at least 15,000 in attendance once every two years and to sponsor a minimum number of sports and scholarships.

Conferences and media

Division I schools are grouped into conferences for most sports. The most prominent are the "Power" conferences — the ACC, Big Ten, Big 12, Pac-12 (largely dissolved in 2024 realignment), and SEC — whose members dominate television contracts and revenues. Media rights deals for Division I sports are worth billions of dollars; the NCAA men's basketball tournament ("March Madness") is one of the largest sporting events in the United States.

Scholarships

Division I schools may award full athletic scholarships in "head-count" sports such as FBS football and basketball, and partial scholarships in "equivalency" sports. In the 2020s the division has been transformed by the introduction of name, image and likeness (NIL) compensation for athletes (2021) and the transfer portal.
