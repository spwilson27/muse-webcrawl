Bifidobacterium

Bifidobacterium
Phylum: Actinomycetota
Family: Bifidobacteriaceae
Type species: Bifidobacterium bifidum

Bifidobacterium is a genus of gram-positive, non-motile, often branched anaerobic bacteria. They are ubiquitous inhabitants of the gastrointestinal tract, vagina, and mouth of mammals. Bifidobacteria are one of the major genera of bacteria that make up the gastrointestinal tract microbiota in mammals.

History

The bacteria were first isolated from the faeces of breast-fed infants by Henry Tissier at the Pasteur Institute in 1899, who called them Bacillus bifidus because of their bifurcated (Y-shaped) morphology. They were later assigned to their own genus.

Metabolism

Bifidobacteria ferment sugars through a distinctive pathway, the fructose-6-phosphate phosphoketolase pathway (the "bifid shunt"), producing acetate and lactate. Several species can metabolise human milk oligosaccharides, which supports their early colonisation of the infant gut.

Health role

Species such as B. longum, B. breve, B. infantis, and B. animalis are widely used as probiotics in foods and supplements. Research links bifidobacteria to immune modulation and gut health, though many claimed benefits remain under investigation. The genus contains dozens of described species found in humans, other animals, and even wastewater and insect guts.
