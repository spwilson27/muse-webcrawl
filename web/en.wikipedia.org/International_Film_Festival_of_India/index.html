International Film Festival of India

The International Film Festival of India (IFFI) is one of the oldest and most significant film festivals in Asia. Founded in 1952, it is organised by the Government of India's Directorate of Film Festivals under the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting, together with the state government hosting it. Since 2004 the festival's permanent venue has been Goa, where it is held annually in late November.

History

The first edition of IFFI was held in Bombay (now Mumbai) in January and February 1952 under the patronage of India's first prime minister, Jawaharlal Nehru, and inaugurated by the Minister for Information and Broadcasting. It travelled to Madras, Delhi and Calcutta in its early years and was non-competitive initially. The festival is accredited by the International Federation of Film Producers' Associations (FIAPF) as a competitive feature film festival, one of the so-called "A" festivals.

Awards

The festival's awards include the Golden Peacock for Best Film, the Silver Peacock awards for Best Director, Best Actor and Best Actress, and a Special Jury Award. The Satyajit Ray Lifetime Achievement Award, formerly the IFFI Lifetime Achievement Award, has been presented to filmmakers such as Martin Scorsese, Carlos Saura and Michael Douglas. Sections of the festival include the international competition, Indian Panorama, and Cinema of the World, alongside retrospectives and country focus programmes.

Alongside the festival, the Film Bazaar — a co-production and distribution market organised by the National Film Development Corporation — runs in parallel in Goa, promoting South Asian cinema to international buyers and festival programmers.
