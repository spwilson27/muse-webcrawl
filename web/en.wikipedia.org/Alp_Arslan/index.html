Alp Arslan (c. 1029–1072), whose name means "Heroic Lion" in Turkish, was the second sultan of the Seljuk Empire and the great-nephew of its founder Tughril. He consolidated Seljuk power and is best known for his victory over the Byzantine Empire at the Battle of Manzikert in 1071.

Reign

After succeeding his uncle in 1063, Alp Arslan, advised by the vizier Nizam al-Mulk, subdued rivals, annexed Armenia and Georgia, and campaigned in Syria. At Manzikert (19 August 1071) his forces defeated and captured the Byzantine emperor Romanos IV Diogenes, whom he released for ransom and a treaty. The victory opened Anatolia to Turkish settlement and migration, a turning point that eventually led to the Sultanate of Rum and, centuries later, the Ottoman Empire.

Death

Alp Arslan died in December 1072 during a campaign in Transoxiana, reportedly stabbed by a captured fortress commander he had ordered executed. He was succeeded by his son Malik-Shah I, under whom the Seljuk Empire reached its greatest extent.
