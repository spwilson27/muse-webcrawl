Annie Jump Cannon

Born: 11 December 1863, Dover, Delaware, US
Died: 13 April 1941, Cambridge, Massachusetts, US
Known for: Harvard classification of stellar spectra

Annie Jump Cannon (11 December 1863 – 13 April 1941) was an American astronomer whose cataloguing work was central to the development of modern stellar classification. Working at Harvard College Observatory, she created the Harvard Classification Scheme — the O, B, A, F, G, K, M sequence still used today — and classified more stars in her lifetime than anyone before her, some 350,000.

Life and career

Cannon was born in Dover, Delaware, and studied physics and astronomy at Wellesley College, graduating in 1884. Scarlet fever left her almost completely deaf. After her mother's death she returned to astronomy, taking graduate work at Radcliffe College and joining Edward Pickering's staff at Harvard in 1896 as one of the "Harvard Computers," the women employed to analyse astronomical data. She became curator of astronomical photographs in 1911 and received a regular Harvard appointment only in 1938, as the William Cranch Bond Astronomer.

Stellar classification

Simplifying the earlier Draper classification, Cannon rearranged and merged spectral classes into a single temperature sequence — remembered by the mnemonic "Oh, Be A Fine Girl/Guy, Kiss Me" — and subdivided each class decimally. Her classifications, published in the nine-volume Henry Draper Catalogue (1918–24) and its extension, covered the spectra of hundreds of thousands of stars, which she could classify at a rate of about three per minute with remarkable consistency. She also discovered some 300 variable stars and five novae.

Honours

Cannon was the first woman elected an officer of the American Astronomical Society, received the Henry Draper Medal in 1931 (the first woman to do so), and held honorary doctorates including one from Oxford, the first awarded to a woman there. The American Astronomical Society's Annie J. Cannon Award recognises distinguished women astronomers.
