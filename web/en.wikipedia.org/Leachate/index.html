Leachate

Leachate is any liquid that, in passing through matter, extracts soluble or suspended solids or other components of the material through which it has passed. The term is most widely used in connection with landfills, where leachate is water that has percolated through decomposing waste, picking up dissolved organic matter, inorganic salts, ammonia, heavy metals and other contaminants.

Landfill leachate composition varies with the age of the landfill and the type of waste; young leachate tends to be acidic and rich in biodegradable organic compounds, while older, stabilised leachate contains more ammonia and refractory compounds. Uncontrolled leachate can pollute groundwater and surface water, so modern landfills use impermeable liners, drainage collection systems and treatment — by biological processes, recirculation, or discharge to sewer — to manage it.

The term also applies in agriculture and mining, for example to liquids draining from compost, silage, or ore heaps in heap leaching.
