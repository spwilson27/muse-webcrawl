SeaTac, Washington

SeaTac is a city in southern King County, Washington, United States. It is an inner-ring suburb of Seattle and surrounds Seattle–Tacoma International Airport, from which its name — a portmanteau of Seattle and Tacoma — is derived. The population was about 31,000 at the 2020 census.

History

The city was incorporated in 1990 after a campaign by residents of the unincorporated neighbourhoods around the airport who sought local control over development and services. The area had grown with the airport, which opened in 1944, and with adjoining highways.

Geography and economy

SeaTac lies between Seattle to the north and Tacoma to the south, bordered by Tukwila, Burien, Des Moines and Kent. Its economy is dominated by the airport and associated hotels, parking, rental car and logistics businesses. The city is served by Interstate 5, State Route 99 and State Route 518, and by Link light rail stations at the airport and Angle Lake.

Government

SeaTac operates under a council–manager government with a seven-member elected city council.
