Cabinet of the United Kingdom

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Cabinet of the United Kingdom is the senior decision-making body of His Majesty's Government. It is chaired by the prime minister and consists of the most senior government ministers, most of them heads of government departments, chosen by the prime minister. Cabinet ministers are bound by the convention of collective ministerial responsibility, under which they must publicly support cabinet decisions or resign.

Composition

Members typically include the Chancellor of the Exchequer, the Foreign Secretary, the Home Secretary, and the secretaries of state for defence, health, education and other departments, together with figures such as the Leader of the House of Commons and the Chief Whip, some of whom attend rather than sit as full members. The cabinet usually numbers around twenty to twenty-five people. The Cabinet Secretary, the country's most senior civil servant, attends to record decisions.

Meetings and committees

The cabinet meets regularly, traditionally on Thursday mornings in the Cabinet Room at 10 Downing Street. Much detailed work is done in cabinet committees and sub-committees; decisions reached there are often simply ratified by the full cabinet. Cabinet government evolved from the Privy Council during the eighteenth century as the office of prime minister emerged.

Role debate

Political scientists debate how far real power lies with the full cabinet as opposed to the prime minister and small inner groups — critics of "prime ministerial government" point to episodes in which major decisions were taken by informal "sofa government" rather than through full cabinet discussion.
