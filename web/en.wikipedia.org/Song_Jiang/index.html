Song Jiang (Chinese: 宋江) is a Chinese historical figure of the Song dynasty who became the central protagonist of Water Margin, one of the Four Great Classical Novels of Chinese literature. In the novel he leads the 108 outlaws who gather at Liangshan Marsh.

Historical background

Historical records in the History of Song mention a bandit leader named Song Jiang who was active during the reign of Emperor Huizong, around 1119–1121. His band roamed the region around modern Shandong and Henan, and was eventually pacified by the imperial official Zhang Shuye. Little else is firmly documented about the historical Song Jiang, and later storytellers greatly expanded his legend.

In Water Margin

In the novel, Song Jiang is a clerk in Yuncheng County, famed for his generosity and righteousness, earning him the nickname "Timely Rain". After killing his concubine Yan Poxi, he flees and eventually joins the Liangshan bandits, succeeding Chao Gai as their chief. Song Jiang repeatedly advocates seeking amnesty from the Song court, and once pardoned, leads his men on campaigns against the Liao dynasty and the rebels Fang La and Tian Hu — campaigns that destroy most of the brotherhood. He is ultimately poisoned by corrupt officials, and dies having refused to betray the throne that betrayed him.

Legacy

Song Jiang embodies the Confucian tension between loyalty to the emperor and brotherhood with outlaws. His character has been debated for centuries: a folk hero to some, a compromiser to others.
