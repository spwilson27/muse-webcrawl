RFC 9309

RFC 9309 standardises the robots exclusion protocol

RFC 9309, titled Robots Exclusion Protocol, is an Internet Standard published by the Internet Engineering Task Force in September 2022. It formalises the conventions of the robots.txt file, which had been an informal de facto standard since 1994, specifying how web site owners indicate which parts of their sites should not be accessed by web crawlers and other automated clients.

Background

The robots exclusion protocol was proposed by Martijn Koster in 1994 and rapidly adopted by search engines, but for nearly three decades it rested on convention rather than a formal specification. Drafts to standardise it were submitted to the IETF in 2019 by Google and collaborators, resulting in RFC 9309.

Content

The RFC defines the file's location at /robots.txt, its text format of groups of user-agent, allow, and disallow rules, matching semantics based on path prefixes, caching guidance (crawlers may cache the file, normally for up to 24 hours), and limits such as a maximum parsed file size of 500 kibibytes. It reiterates that the protocol is advisory: it is neither a security mechanism nor a means of access control, and non-compliant crawlers may ignore it.
