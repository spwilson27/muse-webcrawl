Modal analysis

Modal analysis is the study of the dynamic properties of structures and fluids under vibrational excitation. It determines a system's modal parameters — its natural frequencies, damping factors, and mode shapes — which describe how the structure vibrates when disturbed.

Principles

Any structure's complex vibration can be decomposed into a set of simple modes, each with a characteristic frequency and shape. Near a natural frequency, small inputs can produce large responses (resonance), so engineers use modal analysis to predict and avoid destructive vibration, validate finite element models, and diagnose machinery faults.

Methods

  • Experimental modal analysis (EMA) — the structure is excited with an instrumented hammer or shaker while accelerometers measure responses; frequency response functions are fitted to extract modal parameters.
  • Operational modal analysis (OMA) — natural (ambient) excitation such as wind or traffic is used, common for large civil structures like bridges and buildings where artificial excitation is impractical.
  • Analytical modal analysis — performed computationally, typically as part of finite element analysis, solving an eigenvalue problem for the mass and stiffness matrices.

Applications

Modal analysis is used in aerospace and automotive engineering (for example, ground vibration testing of aircraft), civil engineering structural health monitoring, rotating machinery diagnostics, and product design for noise and vibration reduction.
