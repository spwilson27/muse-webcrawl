GLONASS

GLONASS
Country/region: Russia (formerly Soviet Union)
Operator: Roscosmos / Russian Aerospace Forces
Type: Satellite navigation system
Status: Operational
Nominal constellation: 24 satellites

GLONASS (Russian: ГЛОНАСС, IPA: [ɡɫɐˈnas]; Russian: Глобальная навигационная спутниковая система, tr. Global'naya Navigatsionnaya Sputnikovaya Sistema, lit. 'Global Navigation Satellite System') is a Russian satellite navigation system operating as part of a radionavigation-satellite service. It provides an alternative to Global Positioning System (GPS) and is the second navigational system in operation with global coverage and of comparable precision.

Satellite navigation devices supporting both GPS and GLONASS have become common, since the two systems together provide improved coverage, accuracy, and reliability, especially in dense urban areas and at high latitudes, where GLONASS's orbital inclination gives it an advantage.

History

Development began in the Soviet Union in 1976; the first satellite was launched in October 1982, and the system was declared operational with a full 24-satellite constellation in 1995. After the collapse of the Soviet Union, underfunding caused the constellation to decay to only a handful of usable satellites by 2001. A federal restoration programme returned GLONASS to global coverage in 2011, and it has been maintained since with successive generations of satellites.

System description

The nominal constellation consists of 24 satellites in three orbital planes at an altitude of about 19,100 km, inclined at 64.8°, each completing an orbit in roughly 11 hours 15 minutes. Historically GLONASS used frequency-division multiple access (FDMA) signals around 1.6 GHz and 1.25 GHz, with newer satellites adding CDMA signals shared in structure with other GNSS systems. Ground control stations are centred at Krasnoznamensk near Moscow, with a monitoring network across Russia and abroad.

Accuracy and use

GLONASS positioning accuracy is a few metres for civilian users, comparable to GPS. It is used by the Russian military, in Russian commercial and consumer equipment (where support is mandated by law), and in most modern smartphones, which combine GLONASS with GPS, Galileo, and BeiDou for faster fixes.
