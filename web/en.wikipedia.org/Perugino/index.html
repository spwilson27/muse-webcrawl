Perugino

Pietro Perugino (c. 1446/1452 – 1523), born Pietro Vannucci, was an Italian Renaissance painter of the Umbrian school. He was the teacher of Raphael and one of the most successful and sought-after painters in Italy in the late 15th century, famous for the serene, sweet-faced Madonnas and Saints set in spacious, luminous landscapes that epitomised the early High Renaissance style.

Life and career

Born in Città della Pieve near Perugia — from which he took his nickname "Perugino" — he trained in Florence, possibly in the workshop of Andrea del Verrocchio alongside Leonardo da Vinci. In 1481–82 he was a leading member of the team that frescoed the walls of the Sistine Chapel in Rome; his Christ Giving the Keys to St Peter there is among his most celebrated works.

Major works

Perugino ran busy workshops in Florence and Perugia, producing altarpieces across Italy. His fresco cycle in the Collegio del Cambio, Perugia (1496–1500), is a masterpiece of the period, and his Crucifixion for Santa Maria Maddalena dei Pazzi in Florence and the Vision of St Bernard (Munich) exemplify his limpid, balanced style. Around 1500 the young Raphael entered his workshop and absorbed his manner.

Later years

As tastes shifted toward the more dynamic styles of Michelangelo and the mature Raphael, Perugino's graceful but repetitive art fell from favour in Florence, though he remained busy in Umbria until his death from plague in 1523 at Fontignano.
