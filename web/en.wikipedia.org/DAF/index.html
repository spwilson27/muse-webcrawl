DAF Trucks is a Dutch truck manufacturing company and a division of the American PACCAR group. Its name derives from Van Doorne's Automobiel Fabriek, the company founded by Hub van Doorne in Eindhoven. DAF is one of Europe's largest truck manufacturers, with production plants in Eindhoven, Westerlo (Belgium), Leyland (United Kingdom), and Ponta Grossa (Brazil).

History

Hub van Doorne founded the business in 1928 as a small engineering workshop; it began building trailers and, after the Second World War, trucks (1949) and cars (1958). DAF cars were famous for the Variomatic continuously variable transmission, an automatic gearbox driven by rubber belts, pioneering a technology now common in small cars and scooters. The car division was sold to Volvo in 1975 (becoming Volvo Cars' Dutch operation), while the truck division continued independently. After bankruptcy in 1993, DAF was relaunched and acquired by PACCAR in 1996; it also acquired Britain's Leyland Trucks.

Products and motorsport

DAF's truck range has included the XF flagship for long-haul transport — twice voted International Truck of the Year — and the smaller CF and LF models; a new generation of XG, XG+, XD, and XF trucks launched in 2021 to exploit new EU cab-dimension rules. DAF also supplies diesel engines to other manufacturers and has a notable history in truck racing and the Dakar Rally, where Jan de Rooy won the truck category in 1987 with the twin-engined TurboTwin.
