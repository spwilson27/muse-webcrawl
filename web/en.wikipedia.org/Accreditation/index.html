Accreditation

Accreditation is the independent, third-party evaluation of a conformity assessment body (such as a testing laboratory, certification body, or educational institution) against recognised standards, conveying formal demonstration of its competence to carry out specific tasks. Accreditation differs from certification: certification attests that a product, process, or person meets requirements, while accreditation attests that the certifier itself is competent and impartial.

Process

An accreditation body assesses an applicant organisation's management system, technical competence, personnel, and procedures, typically including document review and on-site assessment by peer experts. Accreditation is granted for a defined scope and a limited period, with surveillance visits and periodic reassessment. International arrangements operated by the International Laboratory Accreditation Cooperation (ILAC) and the International Accreditation Forum (IAF) allow accreditations to be recognised across borders under the principle "accredited once, accepted everywhere".

Common standards

  • ISO/IEC 17025 — testing and calibration laboratories
  • ISO/IEC 17020 — inspection bodies
  • ISO/IEC 17065 — product certification bodies
  • ISO 15189 — medical laboratories

Educational accreditation

In education, accreditation is a quality-assurance process under which schools, universities, and programmes are evaluated by external bodies. In the United States, institutional accreditation determines eligibility for federal student aid and strongly influences the transferability of credits and the recognition of degrees. Accrediting agencies are themselves recognised by the Department of Education or the Council for Higher Education Accreditation.

Other uses

The term is also used for the formal recognition of journalists, diplomats, and event personnel, where accreditation grants access and establishes credentials.
