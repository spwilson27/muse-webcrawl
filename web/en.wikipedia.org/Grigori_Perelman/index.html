Grigori Perelman

Grigori Yakovlevich Perelman (born 13 June 1966), Russian mathematician.

Grigori Yakovlevich Perelman (born 13 June 1966) is a Russian mathematician who is known for his contributions to the fields of geometric analysis, Riemannian geometry, and geometric topology. In the 1990s, partly in collaboration with Yuri Burago, Mikhael Gromov, and Anton Petrunin, he made influential contributions to the study of Alexandrov spaces. In 1994 he proved the soul conjecture in Riemannian geometry, which had been open for more than twenty years. In 2005 Perelman resigned from his research post at the Steklov Institute.

The Poincaré conjecture

In 2002 and 2003 Perelman posted three preprints on the arXiv in which he outlined a proof of William Thurston's geometrisation conjecture, of which the century-old Poincaré conjecture is a special case. His approach extended Richard Hamilton's programme using Ricci flow, introducing new techniques — notably a monotonic entropy functional and surgery to continue the flow past singularities — to overcome the obstacles that had stalled the programme. Over the following three years, teams of mathematicians verified the details; the proof was accepted as complete.

Declined honours

In August 2006 Perelman was awarded the Fields Medal, mathematics' highest honour, "for his contributions to geometry and his revolutionary insights into the analytical and geometric structure of the Ricci flow" — but he declined to accept it, saying he was not interested in money or fame. In March 2010 the Clay Mathematics Institute awarded him the Millennium Prize of one million dollars for solving the Poincaré conjecture; in July 2010 he rejected the money as well, arguing that Hamilton's contribution was no less than his own and criticising what he regarded as unfairness in the mathematical community. Perelman subsequently withdrew from public mathematics and has lived reclusively in Saint Petersburg.

Early life

Perelman was born in Leningrad (now Saint Petersburg) in 1966, won a gold medal with a perfect score at the 1982 International Mathematical Olympiad, and earned his doctorate at Leningrad State University before working in the United States in the early 1990s.
