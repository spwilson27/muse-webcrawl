William Osler

Sir William Osler, 1st Baronet
Born: July 12, 1849, Bond Head, Canada West
Died: December 29, 1919, Oxford, England
Known for: Founding figure of Johns Hopkins; modern medical residency

Sir William Osler, 1st Baronet (July 12, 1849 – December 29, 1919) was a Canadian physician and one of the "Big Four" founding professors of the Johns Hopkins Hospital. He created the first residency programme for training physicians, introduced the practice of bedside clinical teaching for medical students, and is frequently described as the "Father of Modern Medicine."

Career

Osler studied medicine at McGill University, graduating in 1872, and after further study in Europe joined McGill's faculty, becoming professor in 1878. He moved to the University of Pennsylvania in 1884, then in 1889 became physician-in-chief of the new Johns Hopkins Hospital in Baltimore and professor of medicine at its medical school, which opened in 1893. In 1905 he became Regius Professor of Medicine at the University of Oxford, a post he held until his death.

Contributions

Osler's The Principles and Practice of Medicine (1892) was the leading medical textbook of its era. He championed learning at the bedside, remarking that the student who studies medicine without books "sails an uncharted sea," while one who studies books without patients "does not go to sea at all." He founded residency training in which young physicians lived in the hospital while gaining supervised experience, and he promoted the study of medical history and literature — his personal library formed the Bibliotheca Osleriana at McGill.

Osler died in Oxford in 1919 during the influenza pandemic era, of pneumonia following an attack of influenza. His aphorisms, collected in works such as Aequanimitas (1904), remain staples of medical education.
