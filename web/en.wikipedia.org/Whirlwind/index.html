Whirlwind

TypeWeather phenomenon
ExamplesDust devils, waterspouts, tornadoes, landspouts

A whirlwind is a weather phenomenon in which a vortex of wind — a vertically oriented rotating column of air — forms due to instabilities and turbulence created by heating and flow gradients. Whirlwinds occur all over the world and in any season.

Types

Minor whirlwinds include dust devils, which form on sunny days over hot ground, and steam devils over warm water. Larger whirlwinds include waterspouts over water and landspouts. A tornado is sometimes classified as a major whirlwind, though it is generally treated separately because it descends from a thunderstorm's mesocyclone rather than rising from surface heating.

Formation

Whirlwinds form when a pocket of rapidly rising warm air moves through cool air, creating rotation. As the rising air stretches the vortex vertically, conservation of angular momentum spins it faster, much as a figure skater spins faster by drawing in their arms.

Similar phenomena

Fire whirls occur within intense fires, and rotating columns of debris or snow are known as dust whirls and snow devils respectively. Whirlwind-like vortices have also been observed on Mars by orbiting spacecraft and rovers.
