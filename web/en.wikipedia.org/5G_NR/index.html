5G NR (New Radio)
Developed by3GPP
First releaseRelease 15 (2018)
Frequency rangesFR1: 410 MHz – 7.125 GHz; FR2: 24.25 – 71 GHz
WaveformCP-OFDM (downlink and uplink), DFT-s-OFDM (uplink)
Deployment modesNon-standalone (NSA) and standalone (SA)

5G NR (New Radio) is the radio access technology — the air interface — developed by 3GPP for fifth-generation mobile networks. It is the global standard for 5G wireless, designed to support enhanced mobile broadband, ultra-reliable low-latency communication, and massive machine-type communication.

Frequency ranges

NR operates in two ranges. Frequency Range 1 (FR1) spans sub-6 GHz bands (extended in later releases to 7.125 GHz) and provides wide-area coverage. Frequency Range 2 (FR2) covers millimeter-wave spectrum from 24.25 to 52.6 GHz, extended to 71 GHz in Release 17, offering very wide channels and high capacity over short distances. Channel bandwidths range from 5 MHz up to 400 MHz per carrier.

Technical features

NR uses scalable OFDM numerologies: subcarrier spacing scales as 15 × 2^μ kHz, letting the same design serve low-band coverage and mmWave low-latency links. It relies on massive MIMO and beamforming — essential at millimeter waves — LDPC codes for data channels, and polar codes for control channels. Early networks used non-standalone mode anchored to an LTE core; standalone NR connects directly to the 5G core, enabling network slicing and the lowest latencies.
