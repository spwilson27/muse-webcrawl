Kitchen Confidential: Adventures in the Culinary Underbelly is a 2000 nonfiction book by American chef Anthony Bourdain, expanded from his 1999 New Yorker article "Don’t Eat Before Reading This". A frank, funny and often shocking account of life in professional kitchens, it became a bestseller and launched Bourdain’s second career as a writer and television presenter.

Content

The book traces Bourdain’s path from a childhood summer in France — where a raw oyster on a fishing boat awakened his love of food — through a disastrous adolescence, washing dishes in Provincetown, training at the Culinary Institute of America, and drug-fuelled years working the lines of New York restaurants to his position as executive chef at Brasserie Les Halles. Along the way he exposes kitchen practices with practical warnings: never order fish on a Monday, beware brunch, and respect the cooks — many of them Latin American immigrants — who actually run America’s kitchens.

Reception and legacy

Praised for its voice — literate, macho, confessional and darkly comic — the book is credited with demystifying restaurant cooking and inspiring a generation of chef memoirs. It was adapted into a short-lived 2005 television sitcom starring Bradley Cooper. Bourdain later revisited the book in Medium Raw (2010), softening some of its judgements.
