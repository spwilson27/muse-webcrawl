Metropolitan area of the Valley of Mexico

The Metropolitan Area of the Valley of Mexico (Zona Metropolitana del Valle de México) is the metropolitan area centred on Mexico City, the capital of Mexico. It is one of the largest urban agglomerations in the world, with a population of roughly 22 million, and comprises Mexico City's 16 boroughs, some 59 municipalities in the State of Mexico and one municipality in Hidalgo, covering about 7,900 km² in and around the high Valley of Mexico basin.

Geography and growth

The valley is a closed basin at about 2,240 metres elevation, once occupied by the lakes of the Aztec capital Tenochtitlan. Explosive growth in the 20th century spread the built-up area far beyond the Federal District into surrounding states, onto drained lakebeds prone to subsidence and flooding, and up surrounding hillsides. Air pollution, trapped by the basin's thermal inversions, became notorious in the 1980s-90s and has been reduced by fuel, vehicle and industrial controls, though ozone remains a problem.

Administration and transport

No single government runs the metropolis; coordination is handled by commissions among the federal government, Mexico City and the neighbouring states. The area is served by the Mexico City Metro, Metrobús, suburban rail, the Mexibús BRT and two airports (Benito Juárez and the Felipe Ángeles International Airport, opened 2022).
