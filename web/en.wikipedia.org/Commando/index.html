Commando

A commando is a combatant, or operative of an elite light infantry or special operations force, specially trained to carry out raids. Originally the term referred to the Boer commandos of South Africa, mounted infantry units raised during the Second Boer War. During the Second World War the name was adopted by British special forces units, and it has since become associated with elite raiding troops in many armed forces.

History

The word derives from the Afrikaans kommando, a militia unit raised from settlers. After the British Army's experience against Boer commandos, Prime Minister Winston Churchill proposed in 1940 the creation of specially trained troops "of the hunter class" to conduct raids on the coasts of occupied Europe. The British Commandos carried out operations including the St Nazaire Raid of 1942.

Modern usage

Today the term is applied to units such as the British Royal Marines Commandos, the Australian Army's 2nd Commando Regiment, and similar forces worldwide. Commando training typically emphasises physical endurance, amphibious operations, demolitions, and small-unit tactics, and graduates are often distinguished by the green beret.

In popular culture

The commando image — a lightly equipped raider striking behind enemy lines — has been a staple of war films, novels, and video games since the 1940s.
