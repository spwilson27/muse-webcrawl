European Arrest Warrant

European Arrest Warrant
Legal basisCouncil Framework Decision 2002/584/JHA
In force1 January 2004
ScopeEU member states
ReplacesTraditional extradition between member states

The European Arrest Warrant (EAW) is an arrest warrant valid throughout the European Union, requiring a member state to arrest and transfer a criminal suspect or sentenced person to the issuing state. Based on mutual recognition of judicial decisions, it replaced slow, politically mediated extradition within the EU.

Operation

For 32 categories of serious offence (including terrorism, trafficking, and murder), the traditional double-criminality check is abolished: surrender follows if the offence carries at least three years' imprisonment in the issuing state. Decisions are made by judicial authorities under strict deadlines — normally within 60 days of arrest, or 10 days if the person consents.

Criticism and case law

The EAW has been criticised for use in minor cases, for surrender to states with poor prison conditions, and for tension with fundamental rights. Court of Justice rulings (e.g., Aranyosi and Căldăraru, 2016) allow refusal where there is a real risk of inhuman detention conditions, and German and Irish courts have tested its limits regarding judicial independence in Poland. After Brexit, the UK participates in a similar surrender arrangement under the EU–UK Trade and Cooperation Agreement.

Statistics

Tens of thousands of warrants are issued annually, though only a fraction result in surrender; the system dramatically shortened average surrender times compared with pre-2004 extradition.
