Ekiden (駅伝) is a Japanese term for a long-distance relay running race, typically held on roads, in which teams of runners each cover a leg of the course and pass a cloth sash called a tasuki to the next runner instead of a baton. Ekiden races are hugely popular in Japan, where the biggest events draw enormous television audiences.

History

The first ekiden was held in 1917, organised by the Yomiuri Shimbun newspaper to commemorate the 50th anniversary of Tokyo becoming the capital; it ran over three days from Kyoto to Tokyo, a distance of about 500 kilometres. The name derives from the old ekiden system of relay stations on the Tōkaidō road, where horses and messengers were exchanged along the route.

Major races

The most prestigious ekiden is the Hakone Ekiden, a two-day university race between Tokyo and Hakone held on 2–3 January, in which ten runners per team cover roughly 217 kilometres. Other leading events include the Izumo Ekiden and the All-Japan University Ekiden (the "university triple crown"), the New Year Ekiden for corporate teams, and numerous high-school and women's ekidens. The Chiba International Ekiden, in which national teams competed, helped popularise the format abroad.

Format

Stage lengths vary by event and by leg, from a few kilometres to more than a marathon half-distance. Because the tasuki symbolises the accumulated effort of the team, runners traditionally wear it across the chest, and dropping or losing it is considered a serious failure of duty to one's team-mates.
