Counterfactual conditional

Counterfactual conditional
FieldPhilosophy of language; logic
Form"If A had been the case, B would have been the case"
Key theoristsDavid Lewis, Robert Stalnaker, Nelson Goodman

A counterfactual conditional (abbreviated counterfactual) is a conditional statement whose antecedent is false or contrary to fact, typically expressed with the subjunctive mood: "If Oswald had not shot Kennedy, someone else would have." Counterfactuals are central to philosophical discussions of causation, laws of nature, free will, and dispositions.

Semantics

The possible-worlds analyses of Robert Stalnaker and David Lewis evaluate a counterfactual "if A were the case, B would be" by considering the closest possible worlds in which A is true and checking whether B holds there. Lewis's 1973 book Counterfactuals is the classic treatment. Counterfactuals differ from material implication: a material conditional with a false antecedent is trivially true, whereas counterfactuals can be false even when their antecedents are false.

Problems and applications

Nelson Goodman raised the problem of stating which background facts are held fixed when evaluating a counterfactual. Counterfactual reasoning is used in history (alternative history), law (the "but-for" test of causation), and causal inference in statistics and machine learning.
