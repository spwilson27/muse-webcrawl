The Oberammergau Passion Play (German: Oberammergauer Passionsspiele) is a passion play performed since 1634 by the inhabitants of the village of Oberammergau in Bavaria, Germany. It dramatises the Passion of Jesus, from his entry into Jerusalem to the Crucifixion and Resurrection, in a production running about five hours with a cast of more than 2,000 actors, singers and musicians drawn entirely from the village.

Origin

According to tradition, during the Thirty Years' War the plague reached Oberammergau, and the villagers vowed that if God spared them they would perform a play depicting the suffering and death of Jesus every ten years. Deaths ceased, and the first performance was held in 1634.

Modern performances

The play is staged in years ending in zero, in an open-air theatre seating some 4,700. Special editions have marked anniversaries (1934, 1984); the 2020 season was postponed to 2022 because of the COVID-19 pandemic. Recent productions have revised the text to remove anti-Jewish elements criticised in earlier versions. The event draws hundreds of thousands of visitors and dominates the village's economy and calendar.
