Impulse (physics)

In classical mechanics, impulse (symbolized by J) is the change in linear momentum of an object. It is defined as the integral of a force, F, over the time interval, t, for which it acts. Since force is a vector quantity, impulse is also a vector quantity. Impulse applied to an object produces an equivalent vector change in its linear momentum, also in the same direction.

Definition

Mathematically, J = ∫ F dt = Δp = m Δv, where Δp is the change in momentum, m is mass (assumed constant), and Δv is the change in velocity. The SI unit of impulse is the newton-second (N·s), dimensionally identical to the unit of momentum, the kilogram-metre per second (kg·m/s). For a constant force, impulse simplifies to J = F Δt.

Impulse–momentum theorem

The relationship between impulse and momentum follows directly from Newton's second law: the impulse applied to an object equals its change in momentum. This principle explains why extending the time of a collision — with airbags, crumple zones, or a boxer's "rolling" with a punch — reduces the peak force for the same change in momentum.

Applications

Impulse is central to the analysis of collisions, rocket propulsion (where total impulse characterizes a motor's performance), ballistics, and safety engineering. In rocketry, the total impulse is the integral of thrust over burn time, and specific impulse measures propellant efficiency.
