Digital audio workstation

A digital audio workstation (DAW) is an electronic device or application software used for recording, editing, and producing audio files. DAWs range from a single laptop-based software suite to integrated standalone units, and they are central to modern music production, film sound, podcasting, and broadcasting.

Features

A typical DAW provides multitrack recording and non-destructive editing of audio and MIDI, a mixing console with automation, and hosting of virtual instruments and effect plug-ins in formats such as VST and Audio Units. Common elements include a timeline arranged in tracks, piano-roll MIDI editors, and tools for time-stretching, pitch correction, and mastering.

Examples

Widely used DAWs include Pro Tools, long a studio standard for audio recording; Logic Pro and GarageBand on macOS; Ableton Live, oriented toward electronic music and live performance; FL Studio, popular in hip-hop and EDM; Cubase; and the free and open-source Audacity and Ardour.

History

Early digital audio workstations of the late 1970s and 1980s were expensive dedicated systems. The spread of powerful personal computers in the 1990s, together with affordable audio interfaces, moved professional production onto general-purpose machines and reshaped the recording industry.
