The Green Bank Observatory (GBO) is a major radio astronomy observatory located in Green Bank, West Virginia, in the United States. It is operated by Associated Universities, Inc. under a cooperative agreement with the National Science Foundation (NSF). The observatory is best known for the Green Bank Telescope (GBT), the world's largest fully steerable radio telescope, whose dish measures 100 meters (328 feet) in diameter.

History

The site was established in 1956 as the National Radio Astronomy Observatory (NRAO), the first national observatory built by the NSF. Its location in the Allegheny Mountains was chosen because the surrounding terrain shields it from radio interference, and it lies within the National Radio Quiet Zone, a large area where radio transmissions are restricted by law.

The first major instrument at Green Bank was the 300-foot (91 m) transit telescope, completed in 1962. It famously collapsed on 15 November 1988 when a gusset plate failed. Its replacement, the Green Bank Telescope, was dedicated in 2000 and entered regular scientific operation soon after. The GBT's unblocked aperture and active surface of 2,004 panels allow it to observe at frequencies from about 100 MHz to 116 GHz.

Research

Green Bank has been central to several landmark discoveries. In 1960, astronomer Frank Drake conducted Project Ozma there, the first modern search for extraterrestrial intelligence (SETI). The observatory has also played a key role in pulsar astronomy, including studies of the first known binary pulsar, and in astrochemistry, detecting many interstellar molecules. Its extremely quiet radio environment makes it a preferred site for sensitive observations of the early universe and for testing theories of gravity.
