The British Army is the principal land warfare force of the United Kingdom, a part of the British Armed Forces along with the Royal Navy and the Royal Air Force. It is managed by the Ministry of Defence and commanded by the Chief of the General Staff.


British soldiers on exercise; the Army is an all-volunteer professional force.

The British Army has seen major combat in the Seven Years' War, the Napoleonic Wars, the Crimean War, the two World Wars, the Korean War, the Falklands War, the Gulf War, and the wars in Iraq and Afghanistan. Since the end of the Cold War it has also frequently deployed on peacekeeping and humanitarian operations.

History

Before the Acts of Union of 1707, separate English and Scottish armies existed. The English standing army traces its origins to the New Model Army of 1645 and the regiments raised after the Restoration of 1660. The modern British Army dates from the union of the English and Scottish armies in 1707. Conscription was introduced only in the two world wars (1916–1920 and 1939–1960, the latter as National Service); otherwise the Army has been an all-volunteer force.

Empire and the world wars

During the 18th and 19th centuries the Army defended and expanded the British Empire, fighting in North America, India, and Africa. In the First World War the British Expeditionary Force grew into a mass army of millions that fought on the Western Front, at Gallipoli, and in the Middle East. In the Second World War British and Commonwealth forces fought in Europe, North Africa, and Asia, including the campaigns in Normandy, Italy, and Burma.

Post-1945

After 1945 the Army fought in Korea, Malaya, Cyprus, and Northern Ireland, and maintained the British Army of the Rhine in West Germany throughout the Cold War. It recaptured the Falkland Islands in 1982, fought in the Gulf War of 1991, and deployed for long campaigns in Iraq (2003–2009) and Afghanistan (2001–2014, with combat operations ending in 2014).

Structure

The Army is organised into corps and regiments that recruit and administer soldiers, while operational formations are built from brigades and divisions. Major elements include the infantry, the Royal Armoured Corps, the Royal Artillery, the Royal Engineers, the Army Air Corps, and combat service support corps for logistics, signals, intelligence, and medicine. The regular Army is supplemented by the Army Reserve and by the Brigade of Gurkhas, recruited from Nepal.

Equipment

Principal equipment includes the Challenger 2 main battle tank (being replaced by Challenger 3), the Warrior and Boxer armoured vehicles, Ajax reconnaissance vehicles, Apache attack helicopters, and the SA80 (L85) rifle family as the standard individual weapon.
