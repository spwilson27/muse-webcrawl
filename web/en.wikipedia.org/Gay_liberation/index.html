Gay liberation

Gay liberation is a social and political movement of the late 1960s and 1970s that urged gay people to come out, reject shame, and engage in direct action against legal and social oppression. It emerged from the earlier homophile movement but adopted a more radical, confrontational style influenced by the civil-rights, feminist, and anti-war movements of the period.

Origins

The Stonewall uprising of June 1969 in New York City is widely regarded as a catalyst. Within weeks, activists founded the Gay Liberation Front (GLF), and chapters spread across the United States and abroad. On the first anniversary of Stonewall in 1970, marches in several cities inaugurated the tradition of Pride parades.

Aims and legacy

Liberationists demanded decriminalisation of homosexuality, an end to police harassment, removal of homosexuality from psychiatric classifications (achieved in the United States in 1973), and broader transformation of social institutions. Though the movement fragmented in the mid-1970s, it laid the groundwork for later LGBT rights activism and for the community's response to the AIDS crisis.
