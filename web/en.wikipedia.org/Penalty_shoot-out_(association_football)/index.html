Penalty shoot-out (association football)

A penalty shoot-out (officially kicks from the penalty mark) is a tie-breaking method in association football to determine which team is awarded victory in a match that cannot end in a draw, when the score is tied after regular playing time and any extra time. It is governed by Law 10 of the Laws of the Game.

Procedure

Each team takes five kicks from the penalty mark, taken alternately by different players, with the opposing goalkeeper defending. The team scoring more goals wins. If one team reaches an insurmountable lead before five rounds are complete, the shoot-out ends early. If the scores are level after five rounds each, the shoot-out proceeds to sudden-death rounds until one team scores and the other fails to equalise from the same number of kicks. Only players on the field at the end of the match (or temporarily absent) are eligible, and a coin toss decides which goal is used and, under current rules, the team winning a second toss chooses whether to kick first or second.

History

Before shoot-outs, drawn knockout matches were settled by replays, drawing of lots or coin tosses — Italy reached the Euro 1968 final after a coin toss. The modern shoot-out was proposed in the late 1960s, among others by Israeli administrator Yosef Dagan and German referee Karl Wald, and was adopted by IFAB in 1970. The first major tournament decided on penalties was the Euro 1976 final, in which Czechoslovakia beat West Germany, Antonín Panenka scoring the decisive kick with his famous chipped penalty. The first World Cup shoot-out came in the 1982 semi-final between West Germany and France; the 1994 and 2006 World Cup finals were decided by shoot-outs.

Records and tactics

Shoot-out success rates for individual penalties are around 70–75%. Research has debated whether the team kicking first enjoys an advantage, which led to trials of an ABBA sequence borrowed from the tennis tie-break, though the traditional alternating format was retained. Goalkeepers study takers' habits, while takers employ techniques such as the panenka and stutter-step run-ups.

Criticism

Shoot-outs are often criticised as a lottery that does not reflect the preceding play, but defenders note they are more satisfactory than coin tosses and provide a decisive, dramatic conclusion within the stadium schedule.
