Political campaign

A rally during an election campaign.

A political campaign is an organized effort to influence the decision-making of a specific group, most commonly a candidate's attempt to win election to public office, or a campaign for or against a ballot measure. Campaigns combine messaging, fundraising, advertising, and grassroots organizing to persuade voters and mobilize supporters.

Elements

A modern campaign typically develops a message and slogan, raises money, builds a field organization to register and turn out voters, buys advertising in broadcast and digital media, commissions opinion polls, and prepares the candidate for debates and press appearances. Research on opposing candidates ("opposition research") and rapid-response communications are standard features.

Techniques and change

Campaign methods have evolved with media technology: from posters, radio, and whistle-stop tours, to television advertising in the mid-20th century, to internet fundraising, social media, microtargeting based on voter data, and text-message outreach in the 21st. The professional campaign consultant emerged as a distinct occupation, and national campaigns in the United States routinely cost billions of dollars.

Regulation

Most democracies regulate campaign finance, disclosure, and advertising to some degree. In the United States, the Federal Election Campaign Act and subsequent reforms, together with Supreme Court decisions such as Citizens United v. FEC (2010), shape how money may be raised and spent.
