Merseybeat

Merseybeat is a British popular music genre and scene that emerged in Liverpool, England, in the late 1950s and early 1960s, named after the River Mersey. Merseybeat bands combined rock and roll, skiffle, rhythm and blues, doo-wop and soul into an energetic sound built on close harmonies, jangling guitars and a strong backbeat, typically in a guitar-bass-drums line-up.

The scene

Liverpool's port brought American records into the city via merchant seamen ("Cunard Yanks") before they reached the rest of Britain. Hundreds of local groups — by some counts more than 300 — played clubs such as the Cavern Club and the Casbah, village halls and ballrooms, and many honed their craft in the clubs of Hamburg, Germany. The local music paper Mersey Beat, founded in 1961 by Bill Harry, gave the scene its name.

Principal groups

The Beatles became the scene's defining act and, from 1963, its global phenomenon. Other Merseybeat successes included Gerry and the Pacemakers, the Searchers, Cilla Black, Billy J. Kramer and the Dakotas, the Swinging Blue Jeans, the Merseybeats and Freddie and the Dreamers (from nearby Manchester). Manager Brian Epstein and producer George Martin brought several of these acts to national fame.

Legacy

Merseybeat launched the "British Invasion" of the American charts in 1964 and established the self-contained guitar group — writing and performing its own material — as the template for modern pop and rock bands.
