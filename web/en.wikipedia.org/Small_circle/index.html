Small circle

[Image: A sphere showing a great circle through the centre and small circles parallel to it]
Small circle
FieldSpherical geometry
DefinitionCircle on a sphere whose plane does not pass through the sphere's centre
Contrasts withGreat circle
ExamplesParallels of latitude (except the Equator); polar circles; tropics

A small circle of a sphere is the circle formed by the intersection of the sphere with a plane that does not pass through the centre of the sphere — in contrast to a great circle, whose plane passes through the centre and which is the largest possible circle on the sphere. The radius of a small circle is always smaller than the radius of the sphere itself.

On Earth

On the globe, the circles of latitude (parallels) are small circles, with one exception: the Equator, which is a great circle because its plane passes through Earth's centre. The Arctic and Antarctic Circles and the Tropics of Cancer and Capricorn are notable small circles. Small circles matter in navigation and geodesy: unlike a great circle, a small circle is not the shortest path (geodesic) between two of its points, and sailing along a parallel (a rhumb-line course of constant bearing) is longer than the great-circle route except along the Equator.

Properties

For a sphere of radius R, a cutting plane at distance d from the centre produces a small circle of radius √(R² − d²). Every small circle has two "poles" on the sphere where the perpendicular axis through the plane's centre meets the surface; points on the small circle are at constant angular distance from the nearer pole. As d approaches 0 the small circle approaches a great circle; as d approaches R it shrinks to a point.
