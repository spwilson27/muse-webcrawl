Hamming window

FieldSignal processing
Named forRichard Hamming
FamilyRaised-cosine windows (Hann window family)

The Hamming window is a window function used in signal processing to taper a signal before spectral analysis, reducing spectral leakage when computing a discrete Fourier transform. It was proposed by Richard Hamming and belongs to the family of raised-cosine windows.

Definition

The Hamming window is defined as w(n) = 0.54 − 0.46 cos(2πn/(N−1)) for n = 0 … N−1. Its coefficients were chosen to cancel the first sidelobe of the frequency response, giving a maximum sidelobe level of about −43 dB. Unlike the closely related Hann window, the Hamming window does not reach zero at its endpoints, having a value of 0.08 there.

Applications

Hamming windows are widely used in speech processing, filter design, and spectrum analysis, where their balance of main-lobe width and sidelobe suppression is suitable for analysing signals with closely spaced frequency components.
