Van Kampen theorem

In algebraic topology, the van Kampen theorem — also called the Seifert–van Kampen theorem — gives a method for computing the fundamental group of a topological space from the fundamental groups of two open, path-connected subspaces that cover the space. It is named after the Dutch mathematician Egbert van Kampen, who introduced it in 1933, building on work of Herbert Seifert.

Statement

Let X be a topological space that is the union of two open sets U and V, where U, V and their intersection U ∩ V are all path-connected. Then the fundamental group of X is the free product of the fundamental groups of U and V, amalgamated over the fundamental group of the intersection: loops coming from the intersection are identified in the two groups. In categorical language, the fundamental group of X is the pushout of the groups of U and V over that of U ∩ V.

Applications

The theorem is the principal elementary tool for computing fundamental groups. Applied to a wedge sum of circles it yields a free group; applied to the torus and other surfaces, to knot complements (via the Wirtinger presentation), and to configuration spaces, it produces presentations of their fundamental groups. A standard corollary is that the fundamental group of a space formed by gluing two simply connected open sets with path-connected intersection is trivial, and the theorem underlies the standard computation that the fundamental group of the n-sphere is trivial for n ≥ 2.

Generalizations

Versions of the theorem exist for groupoids — the fundamental groupoid on a set of base points — which handle cases where the intersection is not path-connected, and higher-dimensional analogues relate to the theory of higher van Kampen theorems developed by Ronald Brown and others.
