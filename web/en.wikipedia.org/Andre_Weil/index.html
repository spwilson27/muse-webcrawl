André Weil (6 May 1906 – 6 August 1998) was a French mathematician, known for his foundational work in number theory and algebraic geometry. He was one of the most influential mathematicians of the twentieth century and a founding member of the Bourbaki group.

Life

Weil was born in Paris to a Jewish family; his younger sister was the philosopher Simone Weil. He studied at the École Normale Supérieure and traveled widely, including to India, where he taught at Aligarh Muslim University. A conscientious objector at the outbreak of the Second World War, he was imprisoned in Finland and France before emigrating to the United States, where he taught at the University of Chicago and then at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton.

Work

Weil's proof of the Riemann hypothesis for curves over finite fields and his formulation of the Weil conjectures transformed algebraic geometry and number theory; the conjectures were finally proved by Pierre Deligne in 1974. He also contributed to the theory of algebraic groups, abelian varieties, and the foundations of algebraic geometry in his Foundations of Algebraic Geometry (1946).

Honours

Weil received the Wolf Prize in Mathematics in 1979 and was a member of numerous academies. His autobiography, The Apprenticeship of a Mathematician, appeared in 1992.
