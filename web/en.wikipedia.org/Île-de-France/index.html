Île-de-France

Île-de-France is the most populous of the eighteen regions of France, located in the north-central part of the country. It is centred on Paris, the national capital, which is also its prefecture and largest city. With roughly 12 million inhabitants — about a fifth of France's population — on only about 2% of its territory, it is by far the country's most densely populated region and its principal economic centre, accounting for roughly 30% of national GDP.

Geography and departments

The region comprises eight departments: Paris, Seine-et-Marne, Yvelines, Essonne, Hauts-de-Seine, Seine-Saint-Denis, Val-de-Marne, and Val-d'Oise. The inner three departments plus Paris form the petite couronne (small ring) of dense suburbs, surrounded by the more spacious grande couronne. The region is largely a low plateau drained by the Seine and its tributaries, the Marne and the Oise.

History

The name, first attested in the late Middle Ages, reflects the region's position as the historic heartland of the French royal domain, bounded roughly by the Seine, Marne, Oise and Beuvronne rivers — an "island" of royal territory. Created as an administrative region in the 20th century from the former province of the same name, it inherited the central role Paris has played in French political, cultural and economic life for a millennium.

Transport and economy

Île-de-France has one of the world's most extensive urban transport networks — Métro, RER, Transilien suburban rail, trams and buses, coordinated by Île-de-France Mobilités — and is served by Charles de Gaulle and Orly airports. Its economy is dominated by services, finance, technology, tourism, and corporate headquarters, alongside major research universities and cultural institutions.
