Brownie (camera)

The Brownie was a long-running series of simple, inexpensive cameras made by Eastman Kodak. Introduced in February 1900, the original Brownie was a cardboard box camera costing one dollar, putting photography within reach of the general public under the slogan 'You press the button, we do the rest.'

Designed by Frank A. Brownell, the Brownie used roll film and helped popularise the snapshot. Dozens of models followed over more than 70 years, including Bakelite models of the 1930s–50s and the Brownie 127, one of the best-selling cameras of all time.

The Brownie's cultural impact was immense: it created the mass market for amateur photography and gave its name to the Girl Guides' Brownie troops, supposedly after the camera's association with children.
