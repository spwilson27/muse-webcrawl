Tashkent Declaration

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Tashkent Declaration was a peace agreement between India and Pakistan signed on 10 January 1966 following the Indo-Pakistani War of 1965. The summit conference in Tashkent, in the Uzbek Soviet Socialist Republic, was mediated by Soviet Premier Alexei Kosygin, and attended by Indian Prime Minister Lal Bahadur Shastri and Pakistani President Muhammad Ayub Khan.

Terms

The declaration committed both countries to withdraw their armed forces to positions held before 5 August 1965, to restore normal diplomatic relations, to exchange prisoners of war, and to settle their disputes by peaceful means without recourse to force. It also restored the status quo ante on the Kashmir issue, which disappointed expectations in both countries — Pakistanis had hoped for a settlement on Kashmir, while many Indians objected to returning captured territory such as the Haji Pir pass.

Aftermath

Prime Minister Shastri died suddenly in Tashkent in the early hours of 11 January 1966, the day after the declaration was signed, officially of a heart attack; his death gave rise to persistent speculation in India. The agreement was criticized in Pakistan as a diplomatic defeat, contributing to the fall of Ayub Khan's foreign minister Zulfikar Ali Bhutto and, eventually, Ayub himself. Nevertheless, the declaration did restore diplomatic relations and is remembered as a landmark of Soviet mediation in South Asia.
