Herringbone gear

A herringbone gear, also called a double helical gear, is a gear with two rows of helical teeth cut at equal and opposite helix angles on the same blank, producing a distinctive V- or chevron-shaped pattern resembling the bones of a herring. The design was popularized by André Citroën, whose company logo derives from the chevron teeth.

Advantages

Like a helical gear, a herringbone gear engages gradually, running more smoothly and quietly than a spur gear and capable of transmitting greater loads. Its key advantage is that the axial thrust generated by one helix is cancelled by the opposite helix, eliminating the net side thrust that helical gears impose on bearings and shafts.

Manufacture and uses

Herringbone gears are difficult and costly to manufacture because the two helices must meet precisely at the center, often requiring specialized gear-shaping machinery or a center groove. They are used in heavy machinery, marine propulsion, rolling mills, and large power-transmission drives where smooth, high-torque operation justifies the expense. See also gear.
