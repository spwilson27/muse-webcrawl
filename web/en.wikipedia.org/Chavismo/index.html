Chavismo

Chavismo (Spanish: chavismo) is a left-wing political ideology and movement in Venezuela based on the ideas, programmes and style of government of Hugo Chávez, president of Venezuela from 1999 until his death in 2013. The movement combines elements of democratic socialism, social democracy, socialist patriotism and Bolivarianism — the political thought inspired by the South American liberator Simón Bolívar — and is officially promoted by the United Socialist Party of Venezuela (PSUV).

Policies

Under Chávez, chavismo drove the "Bolivarian Revolution": a new constitution (1999), large-scale social programmes known as Bolivarian missions aimed at poverty, literacy, health care and subsidised food, nationalisations of oil, telecommunications, electricity and other industries, and an assertive foreign policy opposing United States influence, including the formation of the ALBA alliance. State control over the petroleum company PDVSA provided the revenue underpinning these programmes during the 2000s oil boom.

After Chávez

Chávez's successor, Nicolás Maduro, has continued to govern in the movement's name. His presidency coincided with a severe economic crisis marked by hyperinflation, shortages and mass emigration, which the government attributed to an "economic war" and sanctions, while critics blamed corruption, mismanagement and authoritarian consolidation. Adherents distinguish between loyal chavistas and critical "dissident chavismo", and the movement remains the central dividing line of Venezuelan politics.
