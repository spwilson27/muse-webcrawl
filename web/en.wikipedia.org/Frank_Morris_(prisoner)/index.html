Frank Lee Morris (September 1, 1926 – disappeared June 11, 1962) was an American bank robber who, together with brothers John and Clarence Anglin, carried out the famous June 1962 escape attempt from Alcatraz Federal Penitentiary. The three men were never found, and whether they survived the crossing of San Francisco Bay remains one of the most debated mysteries in American penal history.

Early life and criminal career

Morris was born in Washington, D.C., and spent much of his childhood in foster homes and orphanages. By his teens he had been convicted of narcotics possession and armed robbery, and he cycled through prisons in Louisiana, Florida, and Georgia, escaping from the Louisiana State Penitentiary. Reputed to have a very high IQ, he was regarded by authorities as an habitual escape artist. Sentenced for a bank robbery in Louisiana, he was transferred to Alcatraz in January 1960 — the prison reserved for inmates considered escape risks elsewhere.

The 1962 escape

Over many months, Morris and fellow inmates John Anglin, Clarence Anglin, and Allen West widened the ventilation grilles at the back of their cells using improvised tools, including a drill powered by a vacuum-cleaner motor. They concealed the work with painted cardboard, climbed into an unguarded utility corridor, built a raft and life vests from stitched raincoats, and fashioned lifelike dummy heads from soap, toilet paper, and real hair to fool patrolling guards.

On the night of June 11, 1962, Morris and the Anglin brothers (West failed to get out of his cell in time) climbed to the roof, descended to the shore, and launched the raft from the northeast side of the island. They were never seen again. A massive manhunt found pieces of the raft and a paddle on nearby Angel Island, but no bodies were ever recovered.

Aftermath and theories

The FBI officially concluded that the men drowned, closing its case in 1979, but the U.S. Marshals Service kept the warrants open. The escape was dramatized in the 1979 film Escape from Alcatraz, starring Clint Eastwood as Morris. A 2015 History Channel documentary publicized a photograph, provided by friends of the Anglin family, allegedly showing the brothers alive in Brazil in 1975; facial-recognition analysis was reported as inconclusive-to-supportive, and the claim remains disputed. If alive, Morris would have evaded capture for the rest of his life.
