Mahabali

Mahabali (Sanskrit: महाबली, "the great Bali"), also known simply as Bali, is a benevolent daitya (asura) king in Hindu mythology, grandson of Prahlada and descendant of the sage Kashyapa. Celebrated for his generosity and devotion, he is best known for the story of the Vamana avatar of Vishnu, who humbled Bali's conquest of the three worlds by measuring them in three strides. In Kerala, Mahabali's annual return to visit his former subjects is the basis of the Onam festival.

The story of Vamana

Bali, though an asura, ruled justly and by devotion and conquest gained dominion over heaven, earth, and the netherworld. The devas appealed to Vishnu, who was born as the dwarf Brahmin Vamana. During a great sacrifice, Vamana asked Bali for just three paces of land. Despite his guru Shukracharya's warning, Bali granted the request; Vamana then grew to cosmic size, covering the earth with one step and heaven with the second. With no space left, Bali offered his own head for the third step and was pressed down to Sutala, a netherworld realm, where Vishnu granted him sovereignty and promised he would become Indra in a future age. The tale is a favourite illustration of devotion, humility, and generosity.

Onam and Kerala tradition

In Kerala the story takes a gentler turn: Mahabali's reign is remembered as a golden age when "all men were equal", and Vishnu granted him the boon of visiting his people once a year. During the ten-day Onam harvest festival (month of Chingam, August–September), Malayalis welcome him with flower carpets (pookalam), new clothes, grand vegetarian feasts (sadhya), and snake-boat races. The related festival of Balipratipada, on the fourth day of Diwali in other parts of India, also honours his return.
