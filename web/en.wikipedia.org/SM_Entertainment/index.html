SM Entertainment Co., Ltd. (Korean: SM에테테인먼트) is a South Korean multinational entertainment agency established in 1989 by record executive and music producer Lee Soo-man. It is one of South Korea's largest entertainment companies and has been responsible for fostering and popularizing the careers of many K-pop stars garnering huge global fanbases. The company operates as a record label, talent agency, music production company, event management and concert production company, and music publishing house.

SM Entertainment headquarters in Seoul.

History

After graduating from Seoul National University and pursuing a master's degree in computer engineering in the United States, Lee Soo-man returned to Korea and in 1989 founded what became SM Entertainment. The company signed its first successful acts in the mid-1990s, and in 1996 debuted H.O.T., widely regarded as the first modern K-pop idol group. They were followed by the girl group S.E.S. in 1997 and the boy band Shinhwa in 1998. In the 2000s the company debuted TVXQ, Super Junior and Girls' Generation, acts central to the Korean Wave (Hallyu) across Asia.

Artists

SM's roster has included BoA, TVXQ, Super Junior, Girls' Generation, Shinee, f(x), EXO, Red Velvet, NCT and its sub-units, aespa, and Riize. Its artists perform collectively as SM Town. The company pioneered the systematic trainee system — recruiting, training and marketing performers over several years — that became the industry standard for K-pop.

Corporate governance dispute

In 2023, SM became the object of a high-profile takeover battle between its founder Lee Soo-man, Kakao, and Hybe Corporation. Kakao and Kakao Entertainment ultimately secured control, and Lee sold his stake and departed the company he had founded, ending an era in the K-pop industry.
