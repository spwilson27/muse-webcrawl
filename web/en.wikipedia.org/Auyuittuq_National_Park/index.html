Auyuittuq National Park (Inuktitut: ᐊᐅᔪᐃᑦᑐᖅ, "the land that never melts") is a national park located on the Cumberland Peninsula of Baffin Island in Nunavut, Canada. It features many terrains of Arctic wilderness, such as fjords, glaciers, and ice fields. Although Auyuittuq (formerly Baffin Island National Park) was established in 1976 as a national park reserve, it received full national park status in 2001.

Geography

The park covers the Penny Ice Cap and the dramatic granite peaks of the Arctic Cordillera along the Akshayuk Pass, a traditional Inuit travel corridor linking the communities of Pangnirtung and Qikiqtarjuaq. Mount Thor (1,675 m), whose west face drops 1,250 metres at an average angle of 105 degrees — often cited as the Earth's greatest purely vertical drop — and Mount Asgard, with its twin cylindrical towers, are famed among mountaineers and appeared in the James Bond film The Spy Who Loved Me.

Flora and fauna

Little vegetation is found in Auyuittuq because of its northern latitude and severe climate, though Arctic willow, heather, and lichens grow in sheltered valleys. Wildlife includes polar bears (a significant safety consideration for hikers), caribou, Arctic foxes, lemmings, and marine mammals in the adjacent fjords.
