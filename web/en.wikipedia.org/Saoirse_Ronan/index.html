Saoirse Una Ronan (born 12 April 1994) is an Irish actress. Born in New York City to Irish parents and raised in Ireland, she is the recipient of various accolades, including a Golden Globe Award, in addition to nominations for four Academy Awards and five British Academy Film Awards. Primarily known for her work in period dramas since adolescence, she has been recognised as one of the most accomplished actresses of her generation.

Early life

Ronan was born in the Bronx, New York City, the only child of Monica (née Brennan) and Paul Ronan, both from Ireland. Her father worked in construction and in bars before training as an actor. The family moved back to Ireland when she was three, settling in County Carlow. She was raised Catholic, though she has described herself as questioning the faith in adulthood.

Career

Ronan made her acting debut in 2003 on the Irish medical drama series The Clinic. Her breakthrough came at age 13 with the role of Briony Tallis in Joe Wright's Atonement (2007), which earned her an Academy Award nomination for Best Supporting Actress, making her one of the youngest nominees in that category. She went on to star in The Lovely Bones (2009), Hanna (2011), and Wes Anderson's The Grand Budapest Hotel (2014).

Her portrayal of an Irish immigrant in 1950s New York in Brooklyn (2015) earned her a second Oscar nomination, this time for Best Actress. She won a Golden Globe for her title role in Greta Gerwig's coming-of-age film Lady Bird (2017), and received further Oscar nominations for Gerwig's Little Women (2019), in which she played Jo March, and for her performance as Mary Stuart in Mary Queen of Scots (2018). She made her Broadway debut in 2016 as Abigail Williams in The Crucible, and her West End debut in 2021 as Lady Macbeth.
