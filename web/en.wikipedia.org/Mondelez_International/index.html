Mondelez International, Inc. (stylized as Mondelēz International) is an American multinational confectionery, food, and beverage company based in Chicago, Illinois. It is one of the world's largest snack companies, with annual revenues of roughly US$36 billion and operations in about 160 countries.

History

The company was created on 1 October 2012 when Kraft Foods Inc. split into two publicly traded companies: a North American grocery business, which retained the Kraft name, and a global snack foods business, which took the new name Mondelez. The name is a coinage combining the Latin word mundus ("world") and delēz, a fanciful rendering of "delicious".

Mondelez inherited much of Kraft's international portfolio, including the Cadbury chocolate business acquired in 2010. Its major brands include Oreo (the world's best-selling cookie), Milka, Cadbury Dairy Milk, Toblerone, Ritz, Wheat Thins, belVita, Halls, and Trident gum. In recent years the company has expanded by acquisition, taking stakes in businesses such as Perfect Snacks, Tate's Bake Shop, and the Mexican confectioner Ricolino.

Operations

The company reports its business across regions including Latin America, Asia–Middle East–Africa, Europe, and North America. Its stated strategy, "Snacking Made Right", centres on growth in biscuits, chocolate, and baked snacks.
