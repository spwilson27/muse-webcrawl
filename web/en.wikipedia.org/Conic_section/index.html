Conic section

A conic section is a curve obtained as the intersection of the surface of a cone with a plane. The three classic types are the ellipse (of which the circle is a special case), the parabola and the hyperbola, distinguished by the angle of the cutting plane relative to the cone. Conic sections were studied intensively in ancient Greek geometry, culminating in the Conics of Apollonius of Perga (c. 200 BC).

Definitions and equations

Each conic can also be defined by its eccentricity: a circle has eccentricity 0, an ellipse between 0 and 1, a parabola exactly 1, and a hyperbola greater than 1. Equivalently, a conic is the locus of points whose distance from a fixed point (the focus) is a constant multiple — the eccentricity — of the distance from a fixed line (the directrix). In Cartesian coordinates every conic is given by a quadratic equation in two variables.

Applications

Conics describe the paths of bodies under gravity: Kepler showed that planets move in elliptical orbits with the Sun at one focus, and comets may follow parabolic or hyperbolic trajectories. Parabolic reflectors focus parallel rays to a point and are used in telescopes, satellite dishes and headlights; elliptical reflectors underlie whispering galleries and some medical devices.
