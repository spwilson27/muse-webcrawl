The Independent Media Center, also known as Indymedia, is a worldwide participatory network of activist and alternative media organisations. It was founded in November 1999 to cover the protests against the World Trade Organization ministerial conference in Seattle, and became a flagship example of open publishing: anyone could post text, photographs, audio and video directly to its websites without prior editorial approval, under the slogan "Don't hate the media, become the media."

History

The first Independent Media Center was set up in Seattle by activists from several alternative media projects to provide grassroots coverage of the 1999 WTO protests. Its website received hundreds of thousands of visits during the protests, and the model spread rapidly: within a few years there were more than 150 local IMCs on six continents, most run by volunteer collectives using consensus decision-making and open-source software such as the Active codebase. Indymedia collectives covered anti-globalisation, anti-war and other protest movements throughout the 2000s, often providing the first reports and images from demonstrations.

Controversies and decline

Indymedia's open newswires also attracted criticism for carrying conspiracy theories, hate speech and unverified claims, and the network repeatedly clashed with authorities. In 2004, servers hosting many IMC sites were seized from a hosting company in London by US authorities, and in 2017 German police raided and banned the linksunten.indymedia.org site. From the late 2000s the network declined as social media platforms absorbed much of its role, though a number of local centres remain active.
