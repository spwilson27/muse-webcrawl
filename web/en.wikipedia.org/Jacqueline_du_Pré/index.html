Jacqueline du Pré, British cellist

Born26 January 1945, Oxford, England
Died19 October 1987 (aged 42), London, England
OccupationCellist

Jacqueline Mary du Pré OBE (26 January 1945 – 19 October 1987) was a British cellist, widely regarded as one of the greatest cellists of the 20th century. Her career, though cut short by multiple sclerosis, left an enduring legacy through her recordings and her intensely emotional style of playing, most famously in Edward Elgar's Cello Concerto in E minor.

Early life and education

Du Pré was born in Oxford, England, the second child of Iris (née Greep), a concert pianist and piano teacher, and Derek du Pré, a banker. At the age of four she heard the sound of the cello on the radio and asked her mother for "one of those". She began lessons with her mother, who composed little pieces accompanied by illustrations for her daughter. At five she won a scholarship to the London Violoncello School, and at seven she began studying with William Pleeth, whom she called her "cello daddy". She later studied in Paris with Paul Tortelier, in Switzerland with Pablo Casals, and in Moscow with Mstislav Rostropovich.

Career

Du Pré made her formal debut at the Wigmore Hall in London in March 1961, at the age of 16. Her performance of the Elgar Cello Concerto with the BBC Symphony Orchestra in 1962 caused a sensation, and she was soon performing the work regularly. In 1965, at age 20, she recorded the Elgar concerto with Sir John Barbirolli and the London Symphony Orchestra for EMI; the recording became a landmark and remains one of the best-selling classical recordings of all time.

She converted to Judaism and married the pianist and conductor Daniel Barenboim in 1967. The couple became one of the most celebrated partnerships in classical music, performing and recording widely with friends such as Itzhak Perlman, Pinchas Zukerman and Zubin Mehta. Their performance of Schubert's "Trout" Quintet at Queen Elizabeth Hall in 1969 was filmed by Christopher Nupen.

Illness and death

In 1971 du Pré began to lose feeling in her fingers, an early symptom of multiple sclerosis. She took a sabbatical in 1971–72 and returned to performing, but her last concert was in New York in February 1973. She was formally diagnosed with multiple sclerosis in October 1973 and never performed publicly again. She died in London on 19 October 1987, aged 42.

Legacy

Du Pré's playing is remembered for its passion, warmth and technical command. She played the 1672 Stradivarius cello known as the "Davidov", later owned by Yo-Yo Ma, and a 1973 Sergio Peresson cello, purchased for her by Barenboim, which was loaned after her death to the young cellist Alison Eldridge and others. The 1998 film Hilary and Jackie, based on her siblings' memoir, renewed public interest in her life, though it was controversial among her friends and colleagues.
