Human papillomavirus infection (HPV infection) is caused by a DNA virus from the Papillomaviridae family. Many HPV infections cause no symptoms and resolve spontaneously, but some persist and result in warts or precancerous lesions. More than 200 types are known; about 40 infect the genital area. HPV is the most common sexually transmitted infection worldwide.

Signs and symptoms

Low-risk types such as HPV 6 and 11 cause most genital warts and common skin warts. Most infections are asymptomatic and are cleared by the immune system within one to two years.

Cancer

Persistent infection with high-risk types — especially HPV 16 and 18 — can cause cancers of the cervix, anus, penis, vulva, vagina, and oropharynx. HPV is responsible for nearly all cases of cervical cancer, the discovery of which earned Harald zur Hausen a share of the 2008 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.

Prevention and screening

Prophylactic vaccines (Gardasil, Cervarix) protect against the most dangerous types and are recommended for adolescents. Cervical screening by Pap smear or HPV DNA testing detects precancerous changes, and vaccination programmes have sharply reduced cervical cancer rates in several countries.
