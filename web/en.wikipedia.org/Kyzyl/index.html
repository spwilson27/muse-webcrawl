Kyzyl is the capital city of the Tuva Republic in southern Siberia, Russia, situated where the Bolshoy Yenisey and Maly Yenisey rivers join to form the Yenisei. It claims to lie at the exact geographical centre of Asia, marked by an obelisk on the riverbank.

History

The settlement was founded in 1914 as Belotsarsk ("White Tsar's Town") when Tuva became a Russian protectorate, renamed Khem-Beldyr in 1918 and Kyzyl in 1926. Tuva existed as the nominally independent Tuvan People's Republic from 1921 until its annexation by the Soviet Union in 1944, with Kyzyl as its capital throughout.

Geography and culture

Kyzyl sits in a mountain-ringed basin with an extreme continental climate — summers above 35 °C and winters below −40 °C. The population is majority Tuvan, and the city is a centre of Tuvan culture, known for khöömei (throat singing); its National Museum holds the gold treasure of the Scythian-era Arzhan burials. The city has no railway connection and is reached by road and by air.
