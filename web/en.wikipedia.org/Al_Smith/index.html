Al Smith

Born: 30 December 1873, New York City

Died: 4 October 1944, New York City

Office: 42nd Governor of New York (1919–1920, 1923–1928)

Known for: Democratic nominee for President, 1928

Alfred Emanuel Smith (30 December 1873 – 4 October 1944) was an American politician who served four terms as Governor of New York and was the Democratic Party's candidate for president in 1928. He was the first Roman Catholic nominated for president by a major party.

Early life and career

Smith grew up in poverty on Manhattan's Lower East Side, the grandson of Irish, German, and Italian immigrants, and left school as a teenager to support his family after his father's death. Backed by Tammany Hall, he rose through the New York State Assembly, where as a leader of the commission investigating the Triangle Shirtwaist Factory fire of 1911 he championed workplace-safety and labour reforms.

Governor and presidential run

As governor (1919–1920 and 1923–1928), Smith was a leading progressive of his era, reorganizing state government, expanding housing, parks, and social welfare, and opposing Prohibition. Nominated for president in 1928, he lost to Herbert Hoover in a landslide, in a campaign marked by anti-Catholic prejudice and prosperity under Republican rule; he carried only eight states, though he cracked the Democratic "Solid South" in reverse — Republican gains in the South foreshadowed realignments to come.

Smith later broke with Franklin D. Roosevelt, whose New Deal he came to oppose as a founder of the American Liberty League. The annual Al Smith Dinner, a white-tie charity event in New York attended by presidential candidates, is named in his honour.
