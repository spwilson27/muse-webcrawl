Deva (Hinduism)

Deva (Sanskrit: देव, "shining, divine") is the Sanskrit word for a deity in Hinduism. Devas are benevolent supernatural beings associated with various forces of nature, moral values, and cosmic functions. They are contrasted with the asuras, who are often depicted as power-seeking beings associated with chaos and material desires.

Etymology

The word deva derives from the Proto-Indo-European root *dei-, meaning "to shine". It is cognate with Latin deus, Greek theos (in part), and the English word "divine" via Latin.

The Vedic devas

In the Vedas, the oldest Hindu scriptures, the devas include Agni (fire), Indra (rain and thunder), Surya (the sun), Vayu (wind), Varuna (the cosmic order and waters), and Ushas (dawn). The Rigveda hymns are largely addressed to these deities, who are invoked in sacrifice (yajna) to sustain the cosmic order, known as rita.

Later Hinduism

In later Hindu thought, the pantheon came to be organized around major deities such as Vishnu, Shiva, and Devi (the Goddess), with the devas forming a celestial assembly (often said to number 33, or 330 million in popular interpretation). Devas reside in Svarga, the heavenly realm ruled by Indra.
