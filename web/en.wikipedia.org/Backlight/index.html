Backlight

A backlight is a form of illumination used in liquid-crystal displays (LCDs). Because liquid crystals do not emit light themselves, a backlight shines light through the display panel from behind so the image can be seen; in dim conditions it is what makes the screen readable at all.

Types

Early LCDs used electroluminescent panels or small incandescent lamps; colour LCDs later standardised on cold-cathode fluorescent lamps (CCFLs) arranged at the edge of a light guide or directly behind the panel. Modern displays overwhelmingly use white LEDs, either edge-lit through a diffuser or as a full array behind the screen. Full-array designs enable "local dimming", in which zones of the backlight are darkened in dark parts of the image to improve contrast; mini-LED backlights extend this to thousands of zones.

Other uses

Backlighting is also used for illuminated keyboards, advertising signs, film and photography (where a back light separates a subject from the background), and backlit mirrors and instrument panels.
