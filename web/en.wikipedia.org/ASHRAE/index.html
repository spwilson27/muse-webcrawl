ASHRAE (pronounced "ash-ray"), founded in 1894, is an American professional association seeking to advance heating, ventilation, air conditioning and refrigeration (HVAC&R) systems design and construction. Its full name is the American Society of Heating, Refrigerating and Air-Conditioning Engineers. ASHRAE is an international organization with members in over 130 countries, headquarters in Atlanta, Georgia, and a network of regional chapters.

History

ASHRAE was formed in 1959 from the merger of the American Society of Heating and Ventilating Engineers (founded 1894 in New York) and the American Society of Refrigerating Engineers (founded 1904). The society maintains a global headquarters in Atlanta, renovated in 2020 as a net-zero-energy demonstration project.

Standards and publications

ASHRAE is best known for its consensus standards, many of which are incorporated into building codes. Major examples include Standard 90.1, Energy Standard for Buildings, the principal U.S. benchmark for building energy efficiency; Standard 62.1 on ventilation for acceptable indoor air quality; Standard 55 on thermal environmental conditions for human occupancy; Standard 34, which designates and classifies refrigerants (the familiar "R-" numbers); and Standard 15 on safety for refrigeration systems. Guideline 36 standardizes high-performance HVAC control sequences. The four-volume ASHRAE Handbook — Fundamentals, Refrigeration, HVAC Applications, and HVAC Systems and Equipment — is the field's central reference, updated on a rotating cycle. The society also publishes the ASHRAE Journal and research through its extensive technical-committee structure, and it played a prominent role during the COVID-19 pandemic through its Epidemic Task Force guidance on airborne transmission and building ventilation.

Activities

ASHRAE holds two large conferences each year (winter and annual/summer meetings), administers professional certifications such as the Building Energy Assessment Professional, funds research projects through member contributions, and runs student branches and design competitions. It cooperates with other bodies including IES and the U.S. Department of Energy on joint standards such as Standard 189.1 for high-performance green buildings.
