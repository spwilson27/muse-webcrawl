Mathematical constant

Examplespi, e, golden ratio, square root of 2
FieldMathematics

A mathematical constant is a number with a fixed, well-defined value that arises naturally in mathematics, as opposed to a variable or a physical constant measured by experiment. Famous examples include pi, the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter; e, the base of natural logarithms; the golden ratio; and the square root of 2.

Notable constants

Pi (about 3.14159) and e (about 2.71828) are transcendental numbers appearing throughout analysis and geometry. Other celebrated constants include the imaginary unit i, Euler's constant, Apery's constant, and the Feigenbaum constants from chaos theory.

Notation

Important constants are usually denoted by a single letter or symbol, and many remain subjects of research regarding their irrationality, transcendence, and digit distribution.
