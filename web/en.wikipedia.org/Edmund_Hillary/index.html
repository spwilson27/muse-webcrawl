Sir Edmund Percival Hillary (20 July 1919 – 11 January 2008) was a New Zealand mountaineer, explorer, and philanthropist. On 29 May 1953, Hillary and Sherpa mountaineer Tenzing Norgay became the first climbers confirmed to have reached the summit of Mount Everest. They were part of the ninth British expedition to Everest, led by John Hunt.

Life and expeditions

Hillary, a beekeeper from Tuakau, developed his climbing in New Zealand's Southern Alps and the Himalayas. After Everest he climbed ten other Himalayan peaks and, in 1958, led the New Zealand section of the Commonwealth Trans-Antarctic Expedition, reaching the South Pole overland — the first party since Scott and Shackleton, and the first to do so in motor vehicles. In 1977 he led a jet-boat expedition up the Ganges.

Philanthropy

Hillary devoted much of his later life to the Sherpa people of Nepal through the Himalayan Trust, building schools, hospitals, bridges, and airstrips in the Solukhumbu region. He served as New Zealand's High Commissioner to India. His face appears on the New Zealand five-dollar note. A 2008 state funeral in Auckland honoured him as New Zealand's most famous son.
