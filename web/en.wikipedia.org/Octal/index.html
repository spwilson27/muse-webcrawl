Octal

Octal is a numeral system with eight as the base. In the decimal system, its counterpart is the counting numbers, and in the binary system it is three bits. The octal numeral system, or oct for short, uses the digits 0 to 7.

Conversions between decimal, octal, and binary.

In computing

Octal became widely used in computing when systems employed word sizes divisible by three, such as 12-, 24-, and 36-bit machines. Each octal digit corresponds exactly to three binary digits, making it a convenient shorthand for binary. It is still encountered in Unix file permissions, where three octal digits encode read, write, and execute rights for user, group, and others.

Octal literals in programming languages are often prefixed with a leading zero, a convention that can cause confusion when numbers with leading zeros are parsed as octal. Data grouped in eights also appears in the octet of networking.

History

Counting in base eight has been used by some cultures, including the Yuki people of California, who counted using the spaces between the fingers, and has been proposed as a natural system for certain Swedish and German traditions of measurement.
