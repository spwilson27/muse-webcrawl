Argyll

Argyll (Gaelic: Earra-Ghàidheal, "coastland of the Gaels") is a historic county and registration county of western Scotland. It stretches along the Atlantic coast of the Scottish Highlands and includes many of the Inner Hebridean islands. Since 1996, most of the mainland area has formed part of the council area of Argyll and Bute.

Geography

Argyll is a region of mountains, glens, and deeply indented sea lochs, including Loch Fyne, noted for its herring and oysters, and Loch Awe. Principal towns include Oban, the "Gateway to the Isles", Campbeltown on the Kintyre peninsula, Lochgilphead, Inveraray (the seat of the Duke of Argyll), and Dunoon. The area also includes the islands of Mull, Islay, Jura, Tiree, Coll, and Lismore, among others.

History

Argyll was central to the Gaelic kingdom of Dál Riata, founded by Irish settlers from about the 5th century AD, and the nearby island of Iona became a centre of early Christianity after St Columba's arrival in 563. In the later Middle Ages the Lords of the Isles and then Clan Campbell, whose chiefs became Earls and later Dukes of Argyll, dominated the region. The area was affected by the Jacobite risings and, in the 18th and 19th centuries, by emigration and the Highland Clearances in parts of the county.

Economy

Today the economy is based on tourism, fishing and fish farming, forestry, and whisky distilling — Islay alone is home to nine distilleries. Oban is a major ferry port for the Hebrides.
