Group N

In motor sport, Group N was a category of production-based cars defined by the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA) for rallying and touring car racing. Group N cars were required to be based on production models built in quantities of at least 2,500 units in twelve consecutive months, and permitted modifications were strictly limited, mostly confined to safety equipment, suspension and gearing.

History

Group N was introduced in the early 1980s as part of the FIA's restructuring of vehicle categories alongside Group A and Group B. Following the abolition of Group B in 1986, Group N became the entry-level category in rallying, feeding the Production World Rally Championship (PWRC), which ran from 1987 to 2012. Iconic Group N rally cars included the Mitsubishi Lancer Evolution and Subaru Impreza WRX STI, whose all-wheel-drive turbocharged rivalry defined the class through the 1990s and 2000s.

Regulations

Engines had to remain essentially standard, with the standard air intake and exhaust routing largely retained; turbocharger boost was controlled by restrictors. Body shells could not be materially altered, and interior trim could be removed only to fit safety equipment such as roll cages and competition seats. Limited-slip differentials and close-ratio gearboxes were permitted within defined limits.

Modern equivalents

The FIA progressively replaced Group N in rallying with the R-group regulations, particularly R4 and the Rally2-Kit formula, and from 2019 homologation of new Group N cars in rallying ceased. National championships in several countries continue to run Group N-type classes.
