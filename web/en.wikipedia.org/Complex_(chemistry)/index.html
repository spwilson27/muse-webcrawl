Complex (chemistry)

A complex in chemistry, or coordination complex, consists of a central atom or ion, usually a metal, bound to surrounding molecules or ions called ligands. The ligands donate electron pairs to the metal, forming coordinate covalent bonds.

Structure

Complexes adopt geometries such as octahedral, tetrahedral, and square planar, determined by the coordination number. Their colours and magnetic properties are explained by crystal field and ligand field theory.

Importance

Coordination complexes are vital in biology — haemoglobin, chlorophyll, and vitamin B12 are examples — and in industry, including catalysis, electroplating, and chelation therapy for metal poisoning.
