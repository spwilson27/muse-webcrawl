Lions Gate Bridge

The Lions Gate Bridge, opened in 1938 and officially known as the First Narrows Bridge, is a suspension bridge that crosses the first narrows of Burrard Inlet in Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada. It connects the City of Vancouver to the North Shore municipalities of the District of North Vancouver, the City of North Vancouver, and West Vancouver. The term "Lions Gate" refers to the Lions, a pair of mountain peaks north of Vancouver. The bridge is a National Historic Site of Canada and a well-known landmark of the city.

Carries: Highway 99 / Highway 1A
Crosses: Burrard Inlet (First Narrows)
Total length: 1,823 m
Main span: 473 m
Opened: 1938

History

The bridge was financed by the Guinness family, which had purchased large tracts of land in West Vancouver (the British Properties) and needed access to sell them. Construction began in 1937; the bridge opened to pedestrians in November 1938 and to traffic in May 1939, and King George VI and Queen Elizabeth participated in the formal opening during their royal tour. Originally a private toll bridge, it was purchased by the Province of British Columbia in 1955, and tolls were removed in 1963. Between 2000 and 2001 the original deck, worn and too narrow, was replaced in a major retrofit, widening the lanes and sidewalks while preserving the bridge's historic appearance. The two lion statues guarding the south approach, sculpted by Charles Marega, were installed in 1941.
