Leigh Van Valen

Leigh Maiorana Van Valen
Born: 12 August 1935, Albany, New York, U.S.
Died: 16 October 2010 (aged 75)
Known for: Red Queen hypothesis, Law of Extinction
Fields: Evolutionary biology, paleontology

Leigh Maiorana Van Valen (12 August 1935 – 16 October 2010) was an American evolutionary biologist at the University of Chicago. He is best known for proposing the Red Queen hypothesis in 1973 and for his "Law of Extinction", which observed that the probability of extinction for taxa within a group appears roughly constant over time.

Red Queen hypothesis

In his 1973 paper "A New Evolutionary Law", Van Valen proposed that organisms must continually adapt simply to maintain their relative fitness as competing organisms also evolve — an idea named after the Red Queen in Lewis Carroll's Through the Looking-Glass, who runs to stay in the same place. The hypothesis emphasises biotic interactions, especially competition and predation, as drivers of evolution.

Other work

Van Valen worked extensively on mammalian evolution and taxonomy, classification of fossil mammals, and evolutionary theory. He founded and edited the journal Evolutionary Theory. His views were sometimes unorthodox; he proposed, for example, that some early cetaceans were related to mesonychians, an idea broadly consistent with later findings, and he defended controversial classifications of early primates.
