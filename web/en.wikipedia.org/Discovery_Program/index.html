Discovery Program

The Discovery Program is a series of Solar System exploration missions funded by NASA and managed by the Planetary Missions Program Office at Marshall Space Flight Center. Established in 1992 under Administrator Daniel Goldin, the program follows a "faster, better, cheaper" philosophy: missions are proposed competitively by scientist-led teams, must be completed within strict cost caps, and are led by a principal investigator from a university or research institution.

Missions

Discovery missions have included NEAR Shoemaker, the first spacecraft to orbit and land on an asteroid (433 Eros); Mars Pathfinder, which landed the first rover, Sojourner, on Mars in 1997; Stardust, which returned comet dust samples to Earth; MESSENGER, the first orbiter of Mercury; Dawn, which orbited two protoplanets, Vesta and Ceres; Kepler, which discovered thousands of exoplanets; InSight, which studied the interior of Mars; and Lucy, launched in 2021 to tour Jupiter's Trojan asteroids. The twin metal-world and Venus missions Psyche (launched 2023) and DAVINCI and VERITAS continue the program.

Selection

Missions are chosen through periodic Announcements of Opportunity. Proposals compete on scientific merit and feasibility; cost caps (historically around $450–500 million excluding launch) force focused science objectives. The program sits between the larger flagship missions and the medium-class New Frontiers Program in cost and scope.
