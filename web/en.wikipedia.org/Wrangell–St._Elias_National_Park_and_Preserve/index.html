Mount St. Elias and the park's glacier-covered ranges.

Wrangell–St. Elias National Park and Preserve is the largest national park in the United States, covering more than 13 million acres of wilderness in southeastern Alaska — an area larger than Switzerland. It was established in 1980 under the Alaska National Interest Lands Conservation Act, and its name honours the Wrangell Mountains and Mount St. Elias, at 18,008 feet (5,489 m) the second-highest peak in both the United States and Canada.

Geography

The park contains the convergence of the Chugach, Wrangell, and St. Elias mountain ranges, the largest assemblage of glaciers and peaks over 16,000 feet in North America; nine of the sixteen highest peaks in the United States lie within or on its borders. The Bagley Icefield stretches nearly 90 miles, and the Malaspina Glacier, larger than Rhode Island, spreads across the coastal plain in a piedmont lobe. Rivers such as the Copper and Chitina carve deep valleys through the park.

History and wildlife

The Ahtna and other Alaska Native peoples have inhabited the Copper River basin for centuries. Copper mining at Kennecott — once among the richest copper mines in the world — boomed between 1911 and 1938 and left a preserved ghost town now managed as a national historic landmark. Wildlife includes grizzly and black bears, Dall sheep, moose, caribou, mountain goats, wolves, and lynx, while coastal waters host sea lions and migrating whales.

The park, together with Canada's Kluane National Park and Alaska's Glacier Bay and Tatshenshini-Alsek parks, forms a UNESCO World Heritage Site recognized for its spectacular glacial and mountain scenery and grizzly bear, caribou, and Dall sheep habitat.
