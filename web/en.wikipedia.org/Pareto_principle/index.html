Pareto principle

The Pareto principle, also known as the 80/20 rule, states that for many outcomes roughly 80% of consequences come from 20% of causes — the "vital few" as opposed to the "trivial many". It is named after the Italian economist Vilfredo Pareto.

Origin

Pareto observed that about 80% of the land in Italy was owned by 20% of the population, and found similar distributions in other datasets. The management consultant Joseph Juran popularised the principle in quality management in the 1940s, applying it to defects: most problems stem from a small number of causes.

Applications

The rule is invoked in business (20% of customers generating 80% of revenue), software engineering, economics (wealth distributions often follow a Pareto distribution), and productivity advice. It is a rule of thumb, not a law: actual ratios vary and need not sum to 100.

Relation to Pareto distribution

The principle is associated with power-law distributions; a specific underlying Pareto distribution with a particular index yields an exact 80/20 split.
