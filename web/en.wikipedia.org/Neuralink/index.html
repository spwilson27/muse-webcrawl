Neuralink

Neuralink Corp. is an American neurotechnology company founded in 2016 by Elon Musk and a team of scientists and engineers. It develops implantable brain–computer interfaces (BCIs) intended to help people with paralysis control external devices, and in the long term to explore human–AI symbiosis.

The Link device

The company's device, the Link, is a coin-sized implant placed in the skull, with more than a thousand fine electrode threads fanning into the cerebral cortex. A custom surgical robot inserts the threads, avoiding blood vessels. Signals are transmitted wirelessly to an external computer that decodes intended movement.

Human trials

After animal studies in pigs and monkeys, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration authorised human trials in 2023. The first participant, a man with quadriplegia from a spinal cord injury, received an implant in January 2024 and demonstrated control of a computer cursor by thought; some electrode threads later retracted, reducing performance, which the company addressed with software changes. Further participants joined the PRIME study in the United States, Canada, and other countries.

Criticism

Neuralink has faced scrutiny over animal welfare in its testing programme and over the risks of its ambitious claims, while researchers note both the promise of high-bandwidth BCIs and competition from other firms and academic groups.
