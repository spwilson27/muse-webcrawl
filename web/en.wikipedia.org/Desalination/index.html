Desalination is a process that removes mineral components from saline water, generally seawater or brackish groundwater, to produce fresh water suitable for human consumption or irrigation. More than 17,000 desalination plants operate in about 150 countries, supplying water to over 300 million people.

Technologies

The dominant technology is reverse osmosis, in which water is forced under pressure through a semipermeable membrane that blocks dissolved salts. Thermal methods, especially multi-stage flash distillation and multi-effect distillation, remain common in the Middle East where energy is cheap and plants are often co-located with power stations. Electrodialysis is mainly used for brackish water.

Costs and environment

Desalination is energy-intensive, and its cost has historically been higher than conventional water supplies, though falling membrane costs have made reverse osmosis competitive in water-scarce coastal regions. Environmental concerns include the disposal of concentrated brine, harm to marine life at intakes, and greenhouse gas emissions from the energy used.

Notable plants

Israel's Sorek plant and Saudi Arabia's Ras Al-Khair plant are among the largest in the world. Australia built major plants around Perth, Sydney, and Melbourne during the Millennium Drought. Singapore and Malta rely heavily on desalination as part of national water strategies.
