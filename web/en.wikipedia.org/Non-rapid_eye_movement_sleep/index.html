Non-rapid eye movement sleep

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Non-rapid eye movement sleep (NREM sleep), also known as quiescent sleep, is, collectively, sleep stages 1–3, previously divided into stages 1–4. It is distinguished from rapid eye movement (REM) sleep by the absence of rapid eye movements, lower brain activity, and progressively deeper thresholds for arousal. NREM and REM sleep alternate in cycles of roughly 90 minutes throughout the night, with NREM — especially its deepest stage — predominating in the first half of the night.

Stages

Under the American Academy of Sleep Medicine scoring system, N1 is light transitional sleep with theta activity on the EEG; N2, the largest share of the night, is marked by sleep spindles and K-complexes; N3, or slow-wave sleep, is deep sleep characterized by high-amplitude delta waves, during which heart rate, blood pressure and breathing are slow and regular, and arousal is most difficult. Dreaming occurs in NREM sleep but is typically less vivid and narrative than REM dreaming.

Functions and disorders

Slow-wave sleep is associated with physical restoration, growth hormone release, declarative memory consolidation and the proposed clearance of metabolic waste from the brain. Disorders arising mainly from deep NREM sleep include the NREM parasomnias — sleepwalking, sleep terrors and confusional arousals — in which partial arousal produces complex behaviour with little subsequent recall.
