Drop D tuning

Drop D tuning is an alternative guitar tuning in which the lowest (sixth) string is tuned down by one whole step from E to D, giving the notes D–A–D–G–B–E from low to high. The remaining five strings keep their standard tuning.

Characteristics

Lowering the sixth string extends the guitar's range by two semitones and, crucially, places the three lowest strings a fourth apart in a pattern that lets a guitarist play power chords (root–fifth–octave) on the bottom three strings with a single finger barré. This ease of one-finger power chords, together with the heavier, lower sound, has made drop D a staple of hard rock and heavy metal.

History and use

The tuning has roots in blues and country playing and was used in classical guitar repertoire as well. It became widespread in rock from the late 1980s and 1990s onward: examples include songs by Rage Against the Machine, Tool, Alice in Chains and many nu-metal bands. Variants tune all strings down further while retaining the dropped bottom string, such as drop C.

Related tunings

Double drop D lowers the high E string to D as well. Drop D is also a common tuning for the bass guitar in heavy genres, where it allows the bass to match guitar riffs in unison.
