The Palatine Hill (Latin: Collis Palatinus) is the centremost of the seven hills of Rome and one of the most ancient areas of the city, standing some 40 metres above the Roman Forum on one side and the Circus Maximus on the other. In Roman mythology it was the site of the Lupercal cave where Romulus and Remus were suckled by the she-wolf, and where Romulus founded Rome in 753 BC. From the age of Augustus it became the seat of the emperors, and its Latin name gave the world the word "palace" (French palais, Italian palazzo).

History

Excavations have found huts and burials from as early as the 10th century BC on the hill, lending a kernel of plausibility to the foundation legends. During the Republic the Palatine was Rome's most fashionable residential district, home to Cicero and other notables; Augustus, born on the hill, built a comparatively modest house there (still visitable, with celebrated frescoes, near the House of Livia), and his successors progressively covered the hilltop with an enormous imperial palace complex. Tiberius, Caligula and Nero extended it, and Domitian's architects built the definitive Domus Augustana and Domus Flavia (completed AD 92), whose towering ruins still dominate the hill.

The site today

The Palatine is now a vast open-air museum administered with the Colosseum and Roman Forum. Highlights include the imperial palace ruins and their stadium-shaped garden, the painted rooms of the House of Augustus, the Temple of Apollo Palatinus, and the Farnese Gardens — laid out in the 16th century over the palace ruins as among Rome's first botanical gardens. Views from the hill take in the Forum, the Circus Maximus and the Aventine.
