FieldGraph theory
OriginSeven Bridges of Königsberg, 1736

In graph theory, an Eulerian trail (or Eulerian path) is a trail in a finite graph that visits every edge exactly once (allowing for revisiting vertices). Similarly, an Eulerian circuit or Eulerian cycle is an Eulerian trail that starts and ends on the same vertex. They were first discussed by Leonhard Euler while solving the famous Seven Bridges of Königsberg problem in 1736.

Existence

Euler proved that a necessary condition for the existence of Eulerian circuits is that all vertices with nonzero degree belong to a single connected component of the graph, and every vertex has even degree. For an Eulerian trail that is not a circuit, exactly zero or two vertices have odd degree. These conditions are also sufficient for finite graphs.

Counting Eulerian circuits

The number of Eulerian circuits in directed graphs can be computed using the BEST theorem (named after de Bruijn, van Aardenne-Ehrenfest, Smith and Tutte). Counting Eulerian circuits in undirected graphs is more complicated; the problem is #P-complete.

Applications

Eulerian trails are used in bioinformatics to reconstruct fragments of DNA from their constituent parts, in CMOS circuit design, and in algorithms such as Hierholzer's algorithm for finding an Eulerian trail in linear time.
