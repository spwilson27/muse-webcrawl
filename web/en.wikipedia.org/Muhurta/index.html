Muhurta

Muhūrta is a Hindu unit of measurement for time, and more commonly an auspicious moment or period chosen according to Vedic astrology (jyotiṣa) for undertaking an important action, such as a wedding, the start of a journey, or the founding of a building.

As a unit of time

Classical texts divide the day and night into 30 muhūrtas, so that one muhūrta equals 48 minutes. Each muhūrta is subdivided into smaller units (such as the kāla and the kāṣṭhā). The earliest reference is in the Ṛgveda, where muhūrta denotes a brief moment or instant.

Electional astrology

In the branch of astrology called muhūrta śāstra, an astrologer examines the panchāṅga (almanac) — the tithi (lunar day), vāra (weekday), nakṣatra (lunar mansion), yoga and karaṇa — together with the horoscopes of the parties, to select a moment free of malefic influences for an undertaking. Certain daily periods are traditionally inauspicious, such as Rāhu-kālam; others, such as the early-morning Brahma muhurta, are held to be universally favourable, especially for meditation and study.
