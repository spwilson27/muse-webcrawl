Estonia in World War II

Estonia in World War II covers the period from the Soviet occupation of 1940 to the reoccupation of 1944. Estonia was occupied successively by the Soviet Union, Nazi Germany, and again the Soviet Union, and lost a large share of its population to deportations, executions, war, and flight.

Soviet occupation 1940–1941

Under the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact, the USSR forced a mutual assistance treaty on Estonia in 1939 and occupied and annexed it in 1940. Mass deportations to Siberia followed in June 1941.

German occupation 1941–1944

Germany invaded in 1941 and Estonia became part of Reichskommissariat Ostland. The Holocaust in Estonia murdered nearly all Jews who had remained, and Estonians served on both sides — in German units including the 20th Waffen Grenadier Division, and in the Red Army's Estonian Rifle Corps.

1944 and aftermath

In 1944 fierce fighting, including the Battle of Tannenberg Line and the Battle of Narva, preceded the Soviet recapture. Many Estonians fled west by sea. Soviet rule lasted until independence was restored in 1991.
