Ross Sea

The Ross Sea is a deep bay of the Southern Ocean in Antarctica, between Victoria Land and Marie Byrd Land, bounded by the Ross Ice Shelf — the largest ice shelf on Earth — which covers much of its southern extent. It is often described as one of the last largely intact marine ecosystems.

Geography and ecology

Discovered by James Clark Ross in 1841, the sea extends to about 85°S behind the ice shelf. Its nutrient-rich waters support krill, Antarctic toothfish, Adélie and emperor penguins, Weddell seals and orca ecotypes found nowhere else. McMurdo Sound, at its southwest corner, hosts McMurdo Station and Scott Base.

Conservation and exploration

The Ross Sea was the departure point for the heroic-age expeditions of Scott, Shackleton and Amundsen. In 2016 the CCAMLR established the Ross Sea region Marine Protected Area, then the world's largest, covering about 1.55 million km². See also Ross Ice Shelf and McMurdo Station.
