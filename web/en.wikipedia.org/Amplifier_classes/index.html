Power amplifier classes

In electronics, power amplifier classes are letter symbols applied to different power amplifier types. The class gives a broad indication of an amplifier's characteristics and performance, principally the portion of the input signal cycle during which the active device conducts (its "conduction angle").

Classes A, B, and AB

In Class A, the device conducts for the full 360° of the cycle, giving excellent linearity but low efficiency (theoretically at most 50%, typically much less). In Class B, two devices each conduct for half the cycle in push-pull, raising efficiency to a theoretical 78.5% but introducing crossover distortion. Class AB biases the devices to conduct slightly more than half the cycle, trading a little efficiency for much lower distortion, and dominates audio power amplifier design.

Class C and switching classes

Class C conducts for less than half the cycle and is used with tuned loads in radio-frequency transmitters. Class D amplifiers use pulse-width modulation and switching output devices, achieving efficiencies above 90%, and are widespread in audio and motor control. Classes E and F use shaped switching waveforms and harmonic tuning for very high RF efficiency.

Other classes

Class G and H amplifiers vary their supply rails with the signal to improve on Class AB efficiency while retaining linear output stages.
