Beat (music)

In music and music theory, the beat is the basic unit of time, the pulse (regularly repeating event) that listeners entrain to when they tap their feet or dance. Beats are grouped into measures by the time signature, and faster or slower subdivisions and multiples of the beat generate the rhythm of a piece. The speed of the beat is the tempo, usually measured in beats per minute (BPM).

Structure

Beats within a measure are not all equal: the downbeat is the first beat of the measure, typically felt as the strongest, while upbeats are the weaker beats preceding it, notably the last beat of the previous measure. In popular music, the backbeat—an accent on beats two and four in 4/4 time, often played by the snare drum—is a hallmark of rock, funk, and hip hop. An offbeat falls between the main beats, while syncopation deliberately displaces accents onto weak beats or offbeats.

Beat and meter

Meter organizes beats into recurring patterns of strong and weak pulses: duple (two beats per measure), triple (three), and quadruple (four) meters, each simple or compound depending on whether the beat divides into two or three. Listeners perceive a metric hierarchy from the level of subdivisions up through measures and hypermeasures.

Perception

Beat perception is a fundamental human capacity: people spontaneously synchronize movement to a musical pulse, and neuroscientific research links beat processing to motor areas of the brain even when a listener is still. The subjective beat continues through brief silences and can be inferred from syncopated patterns—a phenomenon known as subjective rhythmization.
