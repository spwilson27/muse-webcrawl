Navya-Nyāya

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Navya-Nyāya ("new Nyāya" or "new logic") is a school of Indian philosophy and logic that developed principally in Mithila and Bengal from the 13th century onwards. It systematised and refined the realist Nyāya tradition, developing an extraordinarily precise technical language for expressing logical and epistemological relations, which scholars compared with modern formal logic.

History

The school is generally traced to Gaṅgeśa Upādhyāya's Tattvacintāmaṇi ("Thought-Jewel of Reality," c. 1325), which re-founded the Nyāya theory of knowledge around the four means of valid cognition (perception, inference, comparison, and testimony). Later centres at Mithila and Navadvīpa produced masters such as Vāsudeva Sārvabhauma, Raghunātha Śiromaṇi — whose radical revisions of categories earned him epithets of praise and horror — and Gadādhara Bhaṭṭācārya.

Technical language

Navya-Nyāya logicians analysed cognition in terms of qualifier–qualificand structure, relations such as contact and inherence, and carefully defined relational properties like "limitorhood" (avacchedakatā). This apparatus let them resolve paradoxes of negation, quantification, and indirect reference, and it influenced other schools across Sanskrit intellectual culture. Modern scholars such as Bimal Krishna Matilal and Jonardon Ganeri have drawn detailed parallels with analytic philosophy. See also Nyāya.
