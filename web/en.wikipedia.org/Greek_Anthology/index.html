Greek Anthology

The Greek Anthology (Anthologia Graeca) is a collection of poems, mostly epigrams, that span the classical and Byzantine periods of Greek literature. Most of the material of the Greek Anthology comes from two manuscripts, the Palatine Anthology of the 10th century and the Anthology of Planudes of 1301.

History

The tradition of anthologising Greek epigrams began with the Garland (Stephanos) of Meleager of Gadara (1st century BC), which collected poems by dozens of earlier poets and arranged them alphabetically by the first letters of their opening words. Later garlands were compiled by Philip of Thessalonica (1st century AD) and Agathias (6th century AD), whose Cycle included contemporary Byzantine poets.

Around AD 900 the Byzantine scholar Constantine Cephalas compiled a comprehensive anthology drawing on Meleager, Philip, Agathias, and many other sources. Two descendants of Cephalas's collection survive: the Palatine Anthology, a manuscript discovered in the Palatine Library at Heidelberg in 1606, which preserves Cephalas more fully, and the anthology of Maximus Planudes, which for centuries was the only version in circulation in the West.

Contents

The Palatine Anthology contains some 3,700 epigrams arranged in fifteen books, including Christian epigrams, love poems (Book V), dedicatory offerings (Book VI), epitaphs (Book VII), convivial and satirical poems, epideictic pieces, riddles and oracles (Book XIV), and a book of homoerotic verse (Book XII, the Musa Puerilis of Strato). Poets represented range from archaic figures such as Sappho and Simonides to Hellenistic masters like Callimachus, Asclepiades, and Leonidas of Tarentum, and late antique writers such as Palladas and Paul the Silentiary.

Influence

The epigrams of the Anthology influenced Renaissance and later European poetry extensively; the genre of the short, pointed epigram in modern literatures derives largely from this collection. Many poems survive only here, making the Anthology a principal source for Greek lyric and epigrammatic poetry.
