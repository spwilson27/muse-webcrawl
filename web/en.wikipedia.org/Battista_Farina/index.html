Battista Farina

Giovanni Battista Farina (2 November 1893 – 3 April 1966), later Battista "Pinin" Farina and from 1961 Battista Pininfarina, was an Italian automobile designer and founder of the Carrozzeria Pinin Farina coachbuilding company, which became the Pininfarina design house. He is regarded as one of the most influential car designers of the twentieth century.

Life and work

Born in Turin, the tenth of eleven children, Farina acquired the nickname "Pinin" (roughly "little one" in Piedmontese). He began work at age twelve in his brother Giovanni's coachbuilding firm, Stabilimenti Farina. After visiting the United States in 1920 — where Henry Ford offered him a job, which he declined — he founded his own firm in Turin in 1930, specialising in elegant one-off and small-series bodies.

After the Second World War his designs brought him international fame: the Cisitalia 202 of 1947, now in the Museum of Modern Art in New York, pioneered the smooth, integrated fender line of modern cars. His firm bodied cars for Ferrari (a partnership beginning in 1952 that lasted decades), Lancia, Alfa Romeo, Maserati, Peugeot, Fiat, and many others, and produced such landmarks as the Alfa Romeo Giulietta Spider and the Ferrari Dino. In 1961 the President of Italy authorised him to change his surname to Pininfarina. He received the Compasso d'Oro in 1957 and was inducted into the Automotive Hall of Fame in 2004.
