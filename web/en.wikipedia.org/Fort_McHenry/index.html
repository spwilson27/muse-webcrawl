Fort McHenry

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fort McHenry National Monument and Historic Shrine

Location: Locust Point, Baltimore, Maryland

Built: 1798–1805

Type: Star fort

Administered by: National Park Service

Fort McHenry is a historical American coastal pentagonal bastion fort on Locust Point, now a neighbourhood of Baltimore, Maryland. It is best known for its role in the War of 1812, when it successfully defended Baltimore Harbor from an attack by the British navy from the Chesapeake Bay on 13–14 September 1814.

The bombardment and the national anthem

During the Battle of Baltimore, British warships bombarded the fort for about 25 hours but failed to subdue it. The lawyer Francis Scott Key, detained aboard a British vessel while negotiating a prisoner's release, watched the attack and, seeing the large American garrison flag — the "Star-Spangled Banner" — still flying over the fort at dawn on 14 September, wrote the poem "Defence of Fort M'Henry", which became the lyrics of the national anthem of the United States, "The Star-Spangled Banner". The original flag is now preserved at the Smithsonian's National Museum of American History.

Later history

The fort, named for James McHenry, a signer of the U.S. Constitution and former Secretary of War, served as a prison for Confederate soldiers during the American Civil War and as a hospital in the First World War. It was designated a national park in 1925 and re-designated in 1939 as a National Monument and Historic Shrine — the only such double designation in the National Park System.
