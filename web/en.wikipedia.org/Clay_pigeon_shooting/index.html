Clay pigeon shooting

Clay pigeon shooting, also known as clay target shooting, is the art of shooting a firearm at special flying targets known as "clay pigeons" or "clay targets". The terminology originally derived from competitions that used live pigeons released from traps; these were gradually replaced in the late 19th century by standardized disc-shaped targets thrown from spring-loaded machines called traps.

Targets and equipment

Modern clay targets are typically 110 mm (4.3 in) discs made of pitch and pulverised limestone, designed to break easily when struck by even a few pellets. Shotguns — usually over-and-under double-barrelled guns with interchangeable chokes — are used, and shooters wear eye and ear protection. Targets are launched at speeds of up to 100 km/h at angles and trajectories that vary by discipline.

Disciplines

The main disciplines include trap, skeet and sporting clays. In trap, targets fly away from the shooter from a single house; in skeet, targets cross between two houses at either side of a semicircular field; sporting clays simulates field shooting with varied presentations across a course. Trap and skeet are Olympic events, governed internationally by the ISSF, with national bodies such as the CPSA in the United Kingdom and the ATA and NSSA in the United States.

History

Live-pigeon shooting was a popular gambling sport in the 19th century and an event at the 1900 Paris Olympics. Glass ball targets and then clay discs replaced birds, and the sport spread worldwide as a leisure pursuit and competitive discipline.
