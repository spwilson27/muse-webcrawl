Regiment

A regiment is a military unit whose size and role vary considerably between countries and historical periods. In modern armies a regiment typically consists of several hundred to a few thousand soldiers, commanded by a colonel, and may be an administrative and ceremonial grouping of battalions or an operational formation in its own right.

History

Regiments emerged in the 16th and 17th centuries as standing units raised and maintained by a colonel under contract to a state. In the British Army the regiment became the focus of soldiers' identity, traditions, and recruitment, while the battalion served as the tactical unit. In other armies, such as those of the United States and Russia, regiments have functioned more directly as combat formations, usually composed of three or four battalions.

Organization

Depending on the army, a regiment may comprise companies, squadrons, or batteries, and regiments of artillery, armor, and engineers may be organized differently from infantry regiments. Many historic regiments perpetuate battle honors and customs dating back centuries.
