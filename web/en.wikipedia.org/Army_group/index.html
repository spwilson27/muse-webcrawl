Army group

An army group is a military organization consisting of several field armies, typically commanded by a field marshal or a senior general. It is one of the largest operational formations, often comprising several hundred thousand to over a million troops.

Role

Army groups are usually responsible for a distinct theatre or major sector of a front, coordinating the operations of their subordinate armies toward strategic objectives. The echelon above the army group is typically a theatre command or general headquarters; below it are armies, corps, divisions, and smaller units.

Historical use

The term came into widespread use in the First World War (German Heeresgruppe, e.g. Army Group Rupprecht of Bavaria). In the Second World War, German army groups (North, Centre, South, and others) directed the campaigns on the Eastern Front, while the Western Allies fielded the 12th, 21st, and 6th Army Groups in Europe. The Soviet equivalent echelon was the front, itself a group of armies.

Modern usage

Few armies maintain army groups today; NATO doctrine retains the concept for very large coalition land operations.
