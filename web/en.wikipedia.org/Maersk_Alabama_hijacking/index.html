Maersk Alabama hijacking

The Maersk Alabama hijacking was a series of events in April 2009 in the Indian Ocean off Somalia, in which four Somali pirates seized the United States–flagged container ship Maersk Alabama and took its captain, Richard Phillips, hostage in a lifeboat. It ended on 12 April 2009 when United States Navy SEAL snipers killed three of the pirates; the fourth was captured. It was the first successful pirate seizure of a US-registered vessel since the early 19th century.

Background

The Danish-owned, US-operated Maersk Alabama was carrying relief food aid to Mombasa, Kenya, when pirates boarded on 8 April 2009, some 240 nautical miles southeast of Eyl, Somalia. The crew, trained in anti-piracy measures, disabled the ship's systems and resisted; chief engineer Mike Perry ambushed and captured the pirate leader, Abduwali Muse.

Hostage crisis

In exchange for Muse, the crew released him to the pirates, but the pirates reneged on the exchange and fled in a covered lifeboat with Captain Phillips, demanding a $2 million ransom. The destroyer USS Bainbridge shadowed the drifting lifeboat for days while negotiators attempted a resolution. Fearing Phillips's life was in imminent danger, US President Barack Obama authorised force; on 12 April, SEAL snipers positioned on the Bainbridge's fantail simultaneously killed all three pirates in the lifeboat. Phillips was rescued unharmed.

Aftermath

Muse was tried in New York and sentenced to over 33 years in prison. The incident intensified international counter-piracy operations off the Horn of Africa. It was dramatised in the 2013 film Captain Phillips, starring Tom Hanks.
