Women's Health Initiative

The Women's Health Initiative (WHI) is a long-term United States health research programme launched in 1991 by the National Institutes of Health. It was designed to study the leading causes of death and disability in postmenopausal women — cardiovascular disease, cancer and osteoporotic fractures — and enrolled more than 161,000 women aged 50 to 79 at some 40 clinical centres across the United States.

Components

The WHI combined a set of randomised clinical trials with a large observational study. The trials examined hormone therapy (oestrogen alone, and oestrogen plus progestin), dietary modification with a low-fat eating pattern, and calcium and vitamin D supplementation. The observational study followed a further cohort to identify predictors of disease.

Findings

The combined hormone therapy trial was stopped early in 2002 when researchers found that the risks — including increased rates of breast cancer, coronary heart disease, stroke and blood clots — outweighed the benefits for chronic disease prevention. The results transformed medical practice, leading to sharp declines in the routine long-term use of hormone therapy for preventing chronic disease, while later analyses refined understanding of how risks vary with age and timing of treatment. The dietary trial found no significant reduction in breast or colorectal cancer or cardiovascular disease from the low-fat dietary pattern, and calcium with vitamin D did not significantly reduce hip fractures overall.

Legacy

WHI data and biological samples remain a major resource, and follow-up studies of participants continue to produce findings on ageing, cancer and chronic disease in women.
