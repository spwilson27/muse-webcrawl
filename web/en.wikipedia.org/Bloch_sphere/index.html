In quantum mechanics and computing, the Bloch sphere is a geometrical representation of the pure state space of a two-level quantum mechanical system (qubit). It is named after physicist Felix Bloch. Every pure state of a qubit corresponds to a unique point on the surface of a unit sphere.

Geometry

A qubit state written as cos(θ/2)|0⟩ + e^(iφ)·sin(θ/2)|1⟩ maps to the point with spherical coordinates (θ, φ). The north and south poles are the basis states |0⟩ and |1⟩; antipodal points represent orthogonal states, so states that are orthogonal — a full 180° apart in Hilbert space — appear on opposite sides of the sphere.

Uses

Single-qubit unitary operations correspond to rotations of the sphere, making the Bloch representation a standard visualization tool in quantum computing education and in NMR. Mixed states lie inside the sphere, with the maximally mixed state at its center.
