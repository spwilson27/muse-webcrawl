Language education

Language education — the process and practice of teaching a second or foreign language — is primarily a branch of applied linguistics, an interdisciplinary field. It encompasses the teaching of languages in schools, universities, and private institutions, and is usually divided into school-based education and self-study. Traditionally, tasks included instruction in vocabulary, grammar, pronunciation, reading, writing, listening, and speaking.

History of methods

Language teaching has swung between competing approaches for centuries. The grammar–translation method, long used for Latin and Greek, emphasized reading literary texts and memorizing rules. The direct method, arising in the late 19th century, banned translation and taught through spoken interaction in the target language. The audiolingual method, developed during and after the Second World War, relied on drills and habit formation informed by behaviorist psychology.

From the 1970s, communicative language teaching became dominant, prioritizing meaningful communication and fluency over mechanical accuracy. Later approaches include task-based language teaching, content and language integrated learning (CLIL), the lexical approach, and various humanistic methods such as Suggestopedia and the Silent Way.

Key concepts

A central distinction is between language acquisition — subconscious pickup through exposure — and conscious learning, a distinction popularized by Stephen Krashen's Monitor Model, which emphasized comprehensible input. Researchers study the roles of motivation, aptitude, age (including the critical period hypothesis), first-language transfer, and individual differences. Proficiency is widely described using frameworks such as the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR), with levels from A1 (beginner) to C2 (mastery), and the ACTFL scale in the United States.

Language education today

Language programs range from primary-school foreign language classes to intensive immersion programs, bilingual education, study abroad, and computer-assisted language learning (CALL), including mobile apps and online tutoring platforms used by hundreds of millions of learners. English is by far the most widely taught foreign language worldwide, followed by languages such as Spanish, French, German, Mandarin Chinese, and Japanese. Assessment is provided by standardized tests including TOEFL, IELTS, DELE, DELF, the HSK, and the JLPT.
