Developed market

In investing, a developed market is a country that is most developed in terms of its economy and capital markets. Such countries are characterized by high income, openness to foreign ownership, ease of capital movement, and efficiency of market institutions. Developed markets contrast with emerging markets and frontier markets.

Classification

Index providers such as MSCI, FTSE Russell, and S&P Dow Jones Indices maintain classifications of countries as developed, emerging, or frontier markets, reviewed periodically. Criteria typically include economic development (such as sustained high gross national income per capita), market size and liquidity, and market accessibility, including regulatory quality and the absence of significant capital controls.

Characteristics

Developed markets generally offer investors greater liquidity, more transparent financial reporting, stronger legal protections, and more stable political and economic institutions. Their stock markets include many of the world's largest publicly traded companies, though returns may be lower and less volatile than in faster-growing emerging economies.
