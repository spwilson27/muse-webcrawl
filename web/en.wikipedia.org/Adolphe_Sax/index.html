Adolphe Sax

Antoine-Joseph "Adolphe" Sax (1814–1894) was a Belgian inventor and musical instrument designer best known for inventing the saxophone, patented in 1846.

Life and work

Born in Dinant, Belgium, Sax studied flute and clarinet and began making instruments as a young man. Moving to Paris in 1842, he developed families of instruments including the saxhorn and the saxophone, designed originally for military bands and orchestras. Despite legal challenges from rivals and periods of bankruptcy, his workshop became influential, and he taught saxophone at the Paris Conservatoire.

Legacy

The saxophone later became central to jazz and popular music, far beyond Sax's original intended uses. His hometown Dinant honours him with a museum and statues.
