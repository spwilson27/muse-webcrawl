Knowledge representation and reasoning


Knowledge representation and reasoning (KR&R) is a field of artificial intelligence.

Knowledge representation and reasoning (KR, KR&R) is the field of artificial intelligence (AI) dedicated to representing information about the world in a form that a computer system can use to solve complex tasks such as diagnosing a medical condition or having a dialog in a natural language. It incorporates findings from psychology about how humans represent and reason with knowledge in order to design formalisms that will make complex systems easier to design and build.

Formalisms

Knowledge representation includes formalisms such as semantic networks, frames, rules, logic-based representations (including first-order logic and description logics) and ontologies. Reasoning mechanisms include deduction, induction, abduction, inheritance in semantic networks, and automated theorem proving. Description logics underpin the Web Ontology Language (OWL) used in the Semantic Web.

History

The field emerged in the 1970s alongside expert systems, which encoded expert knowledge as rules. Debates over declarative versus procedural representation, the frame problem, and non-monotonic reasoning shaped its development. Modern applications include knowledge graphs, question answering and intelligent assistants.
