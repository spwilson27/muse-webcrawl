David Robert Joseph Beckham (born 2 May 1975) is an English former professional footballer, the president and co-owner of Inter Miami CF and co-owner of Salford City. Renowned for his crossing, free kicks, and passing range as a right midfielder, he played for Manchester United, Real Madrid, LA Galaxy, AC Milan, and Paris Saint-Germain, winning league titles in four countries.

Club career

One of Alex Ferguson's "Class of '92", Beckham won the Premier League six times and the 1999 Champions League as part of Manchester United's Treble. His halfway-line goal against Wimbledon in 1996 made him famous. In 2003 he joined Real Madrid's Galácticos, winning La Liga in 2007, then signed a landmark deal with LA Galaxy that transformed Major League Soccer's profile; loans to AC Milan and a final season with Paris Saint-Germain, with whom he won Ligue 1 in 2013, preceded his retirement.

International career

Beckham earned 115 caps for England, captaining the side from 2000 to 2006. Sent off against Argentina at the 1998 World Cup, he redeemed himself with a last-minute free kick against Greece that sent England to the 2002 World Cup, where he scored the winning penalty against Argentina. He played at three World Cups and two European Championships.

Personal life and business

His marriage to Victoria Beckham (née Adams of the Spice Girls) made the couple global celebrities. Beyond endorsements, Beckham has been a UNICEF ambassador and founded Inter Miami CF, whose signing of Lionel Messi in 2023 was a landmark for US soccer. He was knighted in 2025.
