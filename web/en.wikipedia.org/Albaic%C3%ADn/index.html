Albaicín

Albaicín
LocationGranada, Andalusia, Spain
UNESCO statusWorld Heritage Site (1994)

The Albaicín (also spelled Albayzín) is a historic district of Granada, Spain, built on a hill opposite the Alhambra. It preserves the street layout and much of the atmosphere of the Moorish city of the Nasrid period, with narrow winding lanes, whitewashed houses, and walled gardens called cármenes.

History

The quarter grew under Muslim rule, reaching its height in the Nasrid era, when it was a densely populated suburb with numerous mosques, cisterns, and baths. After the Christian conquest of 1492, many mosques were converted into churches, and the district remained home to Morisco residents until their expulsion in the early seventeenth century.

Landmarks

Notable sights include the Mirador de San Nicolás, famous for its view of the Alhambra, the Banuelo Arab baths, and the remains of the Zirid-era city walls. Together with the Alhambra and Generalife, the Albaicín is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
