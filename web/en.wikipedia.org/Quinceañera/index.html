Quinceañera

A quinceañera in her formal gown

A quinceañera (Spanish for "one who is fifteen") is a celebration of a girl's 15th birthday, common in Latin America and among Hispanic communities in the United States. The festivity marks the transition from childhood to womanhood, in a tradition thought to blend indigenous Mesoamerican coming-of-age rites with Spanish Catholic customs introduced during colonisation.

The celebration

The day typically begins with a thanksgiving Mass, attended by the girl, her parents, godparents (padrinos), and her court of honour: damas (maids) and chambelanes (escorts). The quinceañera wears a formal ball gown and carries symbolic items, including a tiara, a cross, and a last doll (la última muñeca).

Customs

Symbolic moments include the changing of shoes, in which the father replaces his daughter's flat shoes with high heels, the crowning with a tiara, and the ceremonial first waltz with her father, followed by a choreographed dance with her court. A reception with food, music, and dancing follows, sometimes lasting late into the night.

Regional variations

In Mexico the celebration may include mariachi music and a surprise dance (baile sorpresa). In Cuba, group celebrations for many girls at once were historically organised, and in Brazil the analogous event is the festa de debutantes.
