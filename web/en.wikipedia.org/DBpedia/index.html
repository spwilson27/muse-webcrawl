DBpedia

DBpedia extracts structured information from Wikipedia and publishes it as Linked Open Data.

DBpedia is a project aiming to extract structured content from the information created in the Wikipedia project. This structured information is made available on the World Wide Web using RDF and Linked Data principles, allowing users to query relationships and properties of Wikipedia resources, including links to other datasets. DBpedia was one of the first and most central datasets in the Linked Open Data cloud; Tim Berners-Lee has described it as one of the most famous parts of the decentralized Linked Data effort.

Overview

The project began in 2007, developed by researchers at the Free University of Berlin and the University of Leipzig in collaboration with OpenLink Software. DBpedia extracts data chiefly from Wikipedia infoboxes, category hierarchies, article links, and (through community-maintained mappings) from the many language editions of Wikipedia. The resulting knowledge base describes millions of entities — people, places, organizations, species, works, and more — classified in a consistent ontology.

Uses

DBpedia data can be explored through a public SPARQL endpoint, faceted browsers, and lookup services, and is downloaded or mirrored by researchers for natural-language processing, question answering, entity linking, and semantic web applications. Early adopters ranged from the BBC, which used DBpedia identifiers for content annotation, to numerous research prototypes.
