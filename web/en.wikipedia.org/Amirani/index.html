Amirani

Amirani or Amiran is the name of a culture hero of a Georgian epic who resembles the Classical Prometheus. Amirani challenges God, is chained on a mountain peak in the Caucasus, and has his liver eaten daily by an eagle — a punishment renewed each morning as the organ regrows.

The epic

The legend circulated orally for centuries and was recorded in numerous versions across Georgia. Unlike Prometheus, who stole fire for humankind, Amirani's defiance is more ambiguous: he battles demons and monsters, defies the heavens, and in some versions is a figure of pride as much as benefaction.

Origins and parallels

Scholars have suggested the myth may date to the second or first millennium BC, and it is often compared not only with Prometheus but also with the Armenian hero Artavazd and the Ossetian Abrskil. The name itself has been linked to the Iranian hero tradition.

In culture

Amirani remains a symbol of resistance and endurance in Georgian culture; a volcano on Jupiter's moon Io is named Amirani after him and is one of the largest active volcanoes in the Solar System.
