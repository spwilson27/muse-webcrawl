Mīrzā Muhammad Tāraghay bin Shāhrukh (22 March 1394 – 27 October 1449), better known as Ulugh Beg (Persian: الغ‌بیگ), was a Timurid sultan, as well as an astronomer and mathematician. He is best remembered as the founder of the Ulugh Beg Observatory in Samarkand, one of the finest observatories in the Islamic world, and as the patron behind the Zij-i Sultani, a star catalogue that remained authoritative for centuries.

Early life and rule

Ulugh Beg was born in Sultaniyeh during his grandfather Timur's invasion of Persia. The eldest son of Shah Rukh, he was appointed governor of Transoxiana (Mawarannahr) with his capital at Samarkand in 1409, at the age of sixteen. Under his governorship Samarkand became a leading center of learning: he founded a madrasa on the Registan (built 1417–1420) and invited scholars such as Qadi-zada al-Rumi, Ghiyath al-Din Jamshid al-Kashi, and later Ali Qushji to his court. On Shah Rukh's death in 1447, Ulugh Beg became ruler of the Timurid Empire, but his reign was brief and troubled by rebellion.

Astronomy

The observatory, built in the 1420s, housed a giant meridian sextant with a radius of about 36 metres, at the time the largest astronomical instrument in the world. Its staff produced the Zij-i Sultani (1437), cataloguing 1,018 stars and measuring the sidereal year as 365 days, 6 hours, 10 minutes and 8 seconds — an error of less than a minute compared with modern values. They also computed tables of sines and tangents to high accuracy.

Death and legacy

Defeated in civil war by his own son Abd al-Latif, Ulugh Beg was assassinated on 27 October 1449 while traveling to Mecca after abdication. The observatory was destroyed by religious zealots and its site lost until rediscovered by the Russian archaeologist Vasily Vyatkin in 1908. A lunar crater and the asteroid 2439 Ulugbek are named after him, and his observatory's sextant arc survives as a museum in Samarkand.
