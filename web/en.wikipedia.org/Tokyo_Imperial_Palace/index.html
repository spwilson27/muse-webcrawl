Tokyo Imperial Palace

Tokyo Imperial Palace
Location: Chiyoda, Tokyo, Japan
Site: former Edo Castle
Residence of: Emperor of Japan

The Tokyo Imperial Palace (皇居, Kōkyo) is the main residence of the Emperor of Japan. It occupies the site of the former Edo Castle in central Tokyo, a large park-like area of about 1.15 square kilometres surrounded by moats and massive stone walls, in the Chiyoda ward near Tokyo Station.

History

After the Meiji Restoration in 1868, the shogun's Edo Castle became the imperial residence. The old palace, built in 1888, was destroyed by bombing in the Second World War; the present palace buildings, a blend of modern construction and traditional styling, were completed in the 1960s.

Grounds

The inner grounds, including the Fukiage Palace where the Emperor lives, are generally closed to the public, though guided tours are offered on weekdays. The public may enter freely the East Gardens (Kokyo Higashi Gyoen), on the site of the castle's honmaru and ninomaru enclosures. Twice a year, on the Emperor's birthday and on 2 January, crowds gather to receive the Imperial Family's balcony greetings. Iconic views include the Nijūbashi double bridge and the Fushimi-yagura keep across the moat.
