Indo-Aryan peoples

LanguagesIndo-Aryan languages (Hindi, Bengali, Punjabi, Marathi, etc.)
RegionsSouth Asia and diaspora
PopulationOver 1 billion

Indo-Aryan peoples are a diverse ethnolinguistic grouping of peoples speaking Indo-Aryan languages, the easternmost major branch of the Indo-European language family. They predominate across northern and central South Asia — India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal and Sri Lanka — and form large diasporas worldwide.

History

Indo-Aryan languages descend from Old Indo-Aryan, the language of the Vedas, composed in the northwestern subcontinent in the 2nd millennium BCE. Mainstream scholarship associates their spread with migrations of Indo-European-speaking pastoralists from the Eurasian steppe (the Indo-Aryan migration), who mixed with local populations including those of the declining Indus Valley Civilisation. Middle Indo-Aryan Prakrits gave rise to modern languages such as Hindi, Bengali, Marathi, Punjabi, Gujarati, Odia, Assamese, Nepali and Sinhala.

Culture

Indo-Aryan societies historically developed institutions such as the varna system and were the matrix for Hinduism, Buddhism and Jainism, while later centuries brought extensive interaction with Persianate and Islamic cultures. Genetic studies show modern South Asian populations descend from mixtures of ancestral groups including steppe pastoralists, Iranian-related farmers and indigenous South Asian hunter-gatherers, in varying proportions.

Modern distribution

Today Indo-Aryan language speakers number well over a billion, making the branch the largest of Indo-European by speakers. Large diaspora communities live in the Caribbean, Fiji, Mauritius, South Africa, Europe and North America.
