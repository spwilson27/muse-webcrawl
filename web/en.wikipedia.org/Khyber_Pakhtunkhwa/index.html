Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (often abbreviated KP or KPK) is a province of Pakistan in the northwest of the country, bordering Afghanistan. Known until 2010 as the North-West Frontier Province (NWFP), it is the smallest province by area but home to roughly 40 million people, predominantly Pashtuns, with Peshawar as its capital.

Geography

The province ranges from the high Hindu Kush mountains in the north — including Tirich Mir, Pakistan's highest peak outside the Karakoram — through the valleys of Chitral, Swat, and Dir, to the Peshawar plain and the arid south around Dera Ismail Khan. The historic Khyber Pass links Peshawar to Afghanistan. Major rivers include the Indus, Swat, and Kabul.

History

The region formed part of ancient Gandhara, a centre of Buddhist learning, and was contested by empires from the Achaemenids to the Mughals and Sikhs. Britain annexed it after the Second Anglo-Sikh War (1849) and constituted it as the North-West Frontier Province in 1901. After 1947 it joined Pakistan following a referendum. The province was renamed Khyber Pakhtunkhwa by the 18th Amendment in 2010, honouring the Pashtun identity ("Pakhtunkhwa" means "land of the Pashtuns"). In 2018 the Federally Administered Tribal Areas were merged into the province, extending its administration to the Afghan border.

Economy and culture

Agriculture, remittances, trade with Afghanistan, tourism in the northern valleys, and hydroelectric power from dams on the Indus and Swat are economic mainstays. The province has borne a heavy burden from militancy and military operations since the 2000s, as well as from floods, notably in 2022. Pashto language, music, and the code of Pashtunwali shape its culture.
