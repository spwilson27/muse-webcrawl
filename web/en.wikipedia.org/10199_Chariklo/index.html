10199 Chariklo

10199 Chariklo
Discovery: 15 February 1997
Discoverer: Spacewatch
Mean diameter: ~248 km
Orbital period: 62.9 years
Rings: Two, discovered 2013

10199 Chariklo is the largest confirmed centaur in the Solar System — a class of icy minor planets orbiting between Jupiter and Neptune. It is named after Chariclo, the wife of Chiron in Greek mythology.

Discovery and properties

Chariklo was discovered on 15 February 1997 by the Spacewatch programme. It is roughly 248 kilometres in diameter, dark and reddish, and follows an orbit that crosses that of Saturn while remaining mostly outside Uranus's orbit.

Rings

In June 2013 a stellar occultation observed from South America revealed that Chariklo is surrounded by two narrow, dense rings — the first ring system ever discovered around a minor planet. The rings, officially named Oiapoque and Chuí in 2023, lie about 386 and 400 km from Chariklo's centre and are only a few kilometres wide, possibly shepherded by small unseen moons.
