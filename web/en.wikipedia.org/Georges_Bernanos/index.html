Georges Bernanos

Georges Bernanos (20 February 1888 – 5 July 1948) was a French author and a soldier in World War I. Among the most important French Catholic writers of the 20th century, he is best known for the novels Under the Sun of Satan (1926) and The Diary of a Country Priest (1936), the latter adapted into a celebrated film by Robert Bresson.

Life and themes

Bernanos served and was wounded several times in the Great War. A monarchist in youth associated with Action Française, he broke with it and became a fierce critic of totalitarianism and bourgeois complacency. Living in Mallorca during the Spanish Civil War, he denounced Francoist repression in Les grands cimetières sous la lune (1938). His fiction explores grace, despair, and spiritual combat in ordinary lives. His final work, the Dialogues des Carmélites, was adapted into Poulenc's 1957 opera.
