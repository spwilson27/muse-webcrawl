Vicente Ramón Guerrero Saldaña (10 August 1782 – 14 February 1831) was a Mexican military officer and statesman who became the second president of Mexico in 1829. One of the leading insurgent generals of the Mexican War of Independence, he fought alongside José María Morelos and, after Morelos's death, kept the insurgency alive in the southern mountains when the movement seemed all but defeated.

Early life

Guerrero was born in Tixtla, in what is now the state of Guerrero, to a family of mixed African, Indigenous and Spanish descent. He worked as a mule driver and merchant before joining the insurgent forces of Hermenegildo Galeana in 1810, soon coming to the attention of Morelos, who commissioned him to campaign in the south.

War of Independence

After Morelos was captured and executed in 1815, Guerrero refused royalist pardons and sustained guerrilla resistance in the Sierra del Sur. His persistence made possible the eventual alliance with the royalist officer Agustín de Iturbide, sealed in the famous Embrace of Acatempan in February 1821 and embodied in the Plan of Iguala, which achieved Mexican independence later that year.

Presidency

Guerrero contested the election of 1828; when the result favoured Manuel Gómez Pedraza, a revolt brought Guerrero to the presidency in April 1829. His administration is remembered above all for the decree of 15 September 1829 abolishing slavery throughout Mexico, a measure that contributed to tensions with Anglo-American slaveholders in Texas. Facing financial crisis and opposition, he was deposed in December 1829 after Congress declared him unfit to govern.

Death and legacy

Betrayed and captured by the Genoese sea captain Francisco Picaluga, Guerrero was tried by a court-martial and executed by firing squad at Cuilápam, Oaxaca, on 14 February 1831. He is honoured as a national hero; the Mexican state of Guerrero, created in 1849, bears his name, and his famous declaration — "La patria es primero" ("The fatherland comes first") — remains a celebrated patriotic motto.
