Paper clip

A paper clip (or paperclip) is a simple device made of bent wire, usually steel, used to hold sheets of paper together without damaging them. It is one of the most ubiquitous office supplies in the world; billions are produced each year.

History

Straight pins, string, and wax seals preceded the paper clip. The familiar double-oval "Gem" design was being sold by the 1890s by the Gem Manufacturing Company in Britain, though it was never patented in that exact form. The Norwegian Johan Vaaler obtained patents in Germany and the United States around 1900–1901 for a less complete design, and he is popularly celebrated in Norway as the inventor — during the Second World War the paper clip became a Norwegian symbol of resistance to the German occupation. Other early designs included the "Fay", "Eureka" and "Proco" clips, and later plastic-coated and coloured variants.

Use and symbolism

Paper clips are prized because they bind documents reversibly without piercing them. Beyond the office they serve as improvised tools — ejecting SIM cards and resetting electronics — and as symbols: the giant paper clip sculptures in several cities, the Microsoft "Clippy" assistant, and the red paperclip traded up to a house by blogger Kyle MacDonald in 2005–2006.
