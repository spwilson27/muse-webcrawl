Sprinkler system

A sprinkler system may refer to systems that discharge water for fire protection or for irrigation. A fire sprinkler system is an active fire protection method, consisting of a water supply system providing adequate pressure and flowrate to a water distribution piping system, on which fire sprinklers are connected. Irrigation sprinklers distribute water over lawns, gardens, and crops.

Fire sprinklers

Each sprinkler head is held closed by a heat-sensitive element — typically a glass bulb filled with liquid or a fusible link — that releases at a set temperature, allowing water to spray over the fire below. Contrary to common depiction, only sprinklers near the fire activate. Modern systems were developed in the 19th century by inventors including Henry S. Parmalee and Frederick Grinnell, and are credited with greatly reducing fire deaths and property loss.

Types

Wet pipe systems keep water in the pipes at all times; dry pipe systems hold pressurised air and are used where pipes might freeze. Deluge and pre-action systems serve special hazards.

Irrigation sprinklers

Irrigation systems range from simple oscillating lawn sprinklers to centre-pivot systems watering circular fields hundreds of metres across. Drip irrigation, by contrast, delivers water directly to roots and uses far less water.
