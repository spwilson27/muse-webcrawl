Nepal

Nepal (Nepali: नेपाल, romanized: Nepāl), officially the Federal Democratic Republic of Nepal, is a landlocked country in South Asia, situated mainly in the Himalayas between China to the north and India to the south, east and west. It has an area of 147,516 square kilometres (56,956 sq mi) and a population of around 30 million. Its capital and largest city is Kathmandu.

Nepal is home to eight of the world's ten tallest mountains, including Mount Everest (Sagarmāthā), the highest point on Earth at 8,848.86 m (29,031.7 ft). The country is multi-ethnic, multilingual and multi-religious; Nepali is the official language, and Hinduism is the majority religion, followed by Buddhism. Lumbini, in southern Nepal, is revered as the birthplace of Siddhartha Gautama, the Buddha.

History

The modern state traces its origins to the unification campaign of King Prithvi Narayan Shah of Gorkha in the mid-18th century, which created the Kingdom of Nepal. The Shah dynasty ruled until 2008, interrupted in effective power by the Rana oligarchy (1846–1951). A decade-long civil war between government forces and Maoist insurgents ended with the Comprehensive Peace Accord of 2006; in 2008 the Constituent Assembly abolished the monarchy and declared Nepal a federal democratic republic. A new constitution was promulgated in 2015, establishing seven provinces.

Geography

Nepal's terrain rises from the flat, subtropical Terai plains in the south, through the hill region, to the Himalayan range in the north. Despite its small size it has an extraordinary range of climates and ecosystems, from tropical jungle to arctic conditions. Major rivers — the Koshi, Gandaki and Karnali — originate in the Himalayas and flow south into the Ganges plain. Nepal's national parks, including Chitwan and Sagarmatha, protect species such as the Bengal tiger and the one-horned rhinoceros.

Economy

Nepal is one of the least developed countries in Asia, with an economy based on agriculture, remittances from workers abroad, and tourism. Mountaineering and trekking in the Himalayas are major sources of foreign income. A devastating earthquake in April 2015 killed nearly 9,000 people and caused widespread destruction in the Kathmandu Valley.
