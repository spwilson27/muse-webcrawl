The Conservative and Unionist Party, commonly the Conservative Party and colloquially known as the Tories, is one of the two main political parties in the United Kingdom, along with the Labour Party. The party sits on the centre-right to right-wing of the political spectrum. It is the current party of opposition, having lost the 2024 general election after fourteen years in government.

The Conservative Party was founded in 1834 from the Tory Party and was one of two dominant political parties in the nineteenth century, along with the Liberal Party. Under Benjamin Disraeli it defined a program of "One Nation" conservatism, and under Robert Gascoyne-Cecil, 3rd Marquess of Salisbury, it was in power for much of the late Victorian era.

History

In the twentieth century the party governed for long stretches under leaders including Winston Churchill, Anthony Eden, Harold Macmillan, and Margaret Thatcher, Britain's first female prime minister, whose governments from 1979 to 1990 pursued privatization, trade-union reform, and market liberalization. After defeat in 1997 the party spent thirteen years in opposition before returning to power in 2010 under David Cameron, first in coalition and then with a majority. The 2016 Brexit referendum and its aftermath dominated the following governments of Theresa May, Boris Johnson, Liz Truss, and Rishi Sunak, before the party's heavy defeat in 2024.

Ideology and factions

British conservatism combines support for free markets and private property with unionism — commitment to the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland — and gradualist reform. Factions include One Nation conservatives, Thatcherites, and various socially conservative and Eurosceptic groupings.

Electoral performance

Often described as the most successful political party in the democratic world, the Conservatives were in government for most of the twentieth century and for 14 consecutive years from 2010 to 2024.
