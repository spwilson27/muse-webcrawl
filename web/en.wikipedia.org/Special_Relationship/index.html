Special Relationship

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


British and American flags

The Special Relationship is a term used to describe the unusually close political, diplomatic, cultural, economic, military, and historical relations between the United Kingdom and the United States. The phrase was popularised by Winston Churchill in his 1946 "Sinews of Peace" speech at Fulton, Missouri, which also introduced the phrase Iron Curtain.

Dimensions

The relationship has encompassed close cooperation in the Second World War, the UK–US intelligence alliance (including the UKUSA Agreement and Five Eyes), nuclear collaboration under the 1958 Mutual Defence Agreement, and joint military operations in Korea, the Gulf War, Afghanistan, and Iraq. Personal chemistry between presidents and prime ministers — Roosevelt and Churchill, Reagan and Thatcher, Blair and Clinton/Bush — has often been emphasised.

Debate

Commentators periodically debate whether the relationship is as special to Washington as to London, especially after Brexit, but defence, intelligence, and financial ties remain exceptionally deep.
