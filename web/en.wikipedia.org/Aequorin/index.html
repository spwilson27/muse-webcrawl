Aequorin

Aequorin is a calcium-activated photoprotein isolated from the hydrozoan jellyfish Aequorea victoria. It emits blue light in the presence of calcium ions and, in the living jellyfish, its light is converted to green by energy transfer to green fluorescent protein (GFP).

Aequorin
Type: Photoprotein
Source: Aequorea victoria
Activated by: Calcium ions (Ca2+)

Discovery

Aequorin was isolated by Osamu Shimomura in the early 1960s during his work on bioluminescence at Friday Harbor, Washington. Shimomura's studies of aequorin and GFP later earned a share of the 2008 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

Applications

Because its luminescence is triggered by calcium, aequorin is used as a reporter to measure intracellular calcium concentrations in cells and organisms, including genetically engineered plants and animals.
