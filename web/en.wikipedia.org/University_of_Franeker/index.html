University of Franeker

Type: University (defunct)
Active: 1585–1811
Location: Franeker, Friesland, Netherlands

The University of Franeker (Dutch: Universiteit van Franeker) was a university in Franeker, Friesland, in the Dutch Republic. Founded in 1585, shortly after Leiden University, it was the second university of the Netherlands and became an important centre of Reformed theology and learning.

History

The university attracted students from across Protestant Europe, notably from Hungary and Transylvania. Notable professors and students included the theologian Franciscus Gomarus and, among its students, William IV, Prince of Orange. In the 17th and 18th centuries it taught theology, law, medicine, philosophy, mathematics and languages.

Closure and legacy

Declining enrolment led Napoleon to close the university by decree in 1811; a successor athenaeum (1815–1843) also failed. In 2018 a new Campus Fryslân graduate school of the University of Groningen was established in nearby Leeuwarden, reviving academic life in the province.
