The American Revolutionary War (1775–1783), also known as the Revolutionary War or American War of Independence, was the military conflict of the American Revolution in which American Patriot forces, supported by France, Spain and the Dutch Republic, defeated the British Army and secured the independence of the United States of America.

Origins

Resistance to British taxation and control — the Stamp Act (1765), the Townshend Acts, the Boston Massacre (1770) and the Boston Tea Party (1773) — culminated in the Intolerable Acts and the meeting of the First Continental Congress in 1774. Fighting began on 19 April 1775 at the Battles of Lexington and Concord.

Course of the war

On 4 July 1776 the Second Continental Congress adopted the Declaration of Independence. Early campaigns saw the British occupy New York City and Philadelphia, while the American victory at Saratoga (1777) persuaded France to enter the war in 1778; Spain followed in 1779 and the Dutch Republic in 1780, turning a colonial rebellion into a global war. In the south, British forces won battles but could not control the countryside. In October 1781 a Franco-American army under George Washington, supported by a French fleet, trapped Lord Cornwallis at Yorktown, Virginia, effectively ending major land operations in North America.

Treaty of Paris

The Treaty of Paris, signed on 3 September 1783, recognised the independence of the United States and set its boundaries at the Mississippi River, the Great Lakes and Spanish Florida.

Legacy

The war established the United States as an independent republic and inspired revolutionary movements abroad, notably in France and Haiti, while leaving unresolved questions — slavery, relations with Native American nations, and the debts of the new republic — that shaped the Constitution of 1787.
