A/B testing

A/B testing (also called split testing or bucket testing) is a method of comparing two versions of a webpage, app screen, email or other experience to determine which performs better against a defined metric. Users are randomly assigned to the control (A) or the variant (B), and statistical analysis of their behaviour — such as click-through or conversion rates — decides whether the change is genuinely effective.

Method

A typical test states a hypothesis, selects a primary metric, and calculates a sample size sufficient to detect an effect of the expected size with adequate statistical power. Because results are checked repeatedly, practitioners guard against pitfalls such as peeking (stopping early on a random high), multiple comparisons across many variants, and novelty or seasonality effects. Tests may be analysed with classical significance testing (for example a two-proportion z-test or chi-squared test) or with Bayesian methods.

Applications

A/B testing is a standard tool in web analytics, product management and online marketing: technology companies run thousands of concurrent experiments to tune headlines, layouts, pricing pages and recommendation algorithms. The approach descends from randomised controlled trials in medicine and agriculture, and from direct-mail marketers who tested alternative copy on split mailing lists. Related methods include multivariate testing, which varies several elements at once, and multi-armed bandit algorithms, which shift traffic toward better-performing variants while the experiment runs.
