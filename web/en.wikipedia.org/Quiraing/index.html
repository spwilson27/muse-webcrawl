The Quiraing (Scottish Gaelic: A' Chuith-Raing) is a landslip on the eastern face of Meall na Suiramach, the northernmost summit of the Trotternish ridge on the Isle of Skye in Scotland. It forms part of the same great series of landslips as the Storr, to the south, and is one of the most photographed landscapes in Britain.

Geology

The Trotternish ridge is built of thick basalt lava flows resting on weaker Jurassic sedimentary rocks. Where the sediments give way under the lavas' weight, the escarpment collapses eastward in enormous rotational slips. At the Quiraing the slip is still moving: the road that crosses it below the escarpment has to be repaired annually as it shifts. The collapse has produced a dramatic maze of pinnacles, cliffs and hidden plateaux.

Features

Named features include the Needle, a 37-metre jagged pinnacle; the Prison, a pyramidal block that from some angles resembles a medieval keep; and the Table, a flat grassy plateau high in the slip, reached by a steep scramble, where local tradition holds that islanders hid cattle from Viking raiders. A circular walking route of about 6.8 km from the car park on the Staffin–Uig road passes through the formations and is among Skye's most popular hikes.
