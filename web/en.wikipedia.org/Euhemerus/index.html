Euhemerus

Born: c. 330 BC, Messene (Messina), Sicily
Died: c. 260 BC
Known for: Euhemerism — interpreting myths as distorted history

Euhemerus (c. 330 – c. 260 BC) was a Greek mythographer at the court of Cassander, the king of Macedon. He is chiefly known for a theory of mythology, now called euhemerism, according to which myths are the distorted records of real historical events and the gods were originally great men, kings and benefactors, who came to be worshipped after their deaths.

The Sacred Record

Euhemerus presented his ideas in a work called the Sacred Record (Hiera Anagraphē), which survives only in fragments and in an account by Diodorus Siculus. Framed as the narrative of a voyage to a fictional island called Panchaea in the Indian Ocean, the work described a golden pillar in a temple of Zeus recording the deeds of Uranus, Cronus, and Zeus — who were presented not as gods but as mortal kings of great antiquity.

Influence

Euhemerus was controversial in antiquity and was accused of atheism by some writers, but his method of rationalizing myth proved durable. It was later used by Christian apologists to argue that pagan gods were merely deified men, and the term "euhemerism" remains standard in the comparative study of religion and mythology.
