Changing of the Guard

The Changing of the Guard is the formal ceremony at which sentries providing the guard at royal palaces and other locations are relieved by a new detachment. The term is most famous for the ceremony at Buckingham Palace in London, formally the King's Guard change, in which the Old Guard hands over responsibility for guarding Buckingham Palace and St James's Palace to the New Guard.

The Buckingham Palace ceremony

The guard is provided by the five regiments of Foot Guards of the Household Division — the Grenadier, Coldstream, Scots, Irish and Welsh Guards — recognizable by their scarlet tunics and bearskin caps, although other Commonwealth and British units (including the Royal Navy, Royal Marines, RAF Regiment and Gurkhas) occasionally mount the guard, and visiting foreign units have done so on special occasions. The ceremony, accompanied by a band playing a mixture of military marches and popular music, takes place on scheduled days, normally in the late morning at Buckingham Palace and Windsor Castle, and involves detachments marching between Wellington Barracks, St James's Palace and Buckingham Palace.

The King's Guard

The King's Guard consists of the detachments posted at Buckingham Palace and St James's Palace, commanded by the Captain of the King's Guard. Sentries are posted at their sentry boxes for two-hour spells within a 24- or 48-hour tour of duty. A similar mounted ceremony, the Changing of the King's Life Guard, takes place at Horse Guards, where the Household Cavalry (Life Guards and Blues and Royals) exchange duties.

Worldwide

Changing of the guard ceremonies are performed in many countries at presidential palaces, royal residences, war memorials and tombs of unknown soldiers — notable examples include the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier at Arlington National Cemetery in the United States, the Evzones at Syntagma Square in Athens, the guards at the Kremlin and Alexander Garden in Moscow, and ceremonies in Stockholm, Oslo, Ottawa, New Delhi (Rashtrapati Bhavan) and Taipei.
