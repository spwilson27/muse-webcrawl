Grenfell Tower fire

Grenfell Tower, a 24-storey residential block in North Kensington, London.

The Grenfell Tower fire broke out on 14 June 2017 in the 24-storey Grenfell Tower block of flats in North Kensington, West London, England. It caused 72 deaths, including one person who died in hospital the following year, making it the worst residential fire in the United Kingdom since the Second World War.

The fire

The fire started shortly before 1 a.m. in a fridge-freezer on the fourth floor of the tower, which contained 129 flats and housed a diverse, largely working-class community. Firefighters initially fought the flat fire, but flames spread rapidly up the exterior of the building through the cladding system installed during a 2015–16 refurbishment: aluminium composite material (ACM) panels with a polyethylene core, combined with combustible insulation. The fire engulfed the tower within hours. The building's "stay put" advice to residents — based on the assumption that fires would be contained within individual flats — broke down as compartmentation failed, and many residents were unable to escape the smoke-filled single staircase.

Inquiry and aftermath

The Grenfell Tower Inquiry, chaired by Sir Martin Moore-Bick, published its Phase 1 report in October 2019, concluding that the cladding did not comply with building regulations and was the principal reason for the rapid spread. Its Phase 2 report, published in September 2024, examined the decades of failures by governments, the construction industry and regulators that led to the disaster, and found the deaths were avoidable.

The fire triggered a national building-safety crisis: hundreds of high-rise buildings across Britain were found to have unsafe cladding, leading to remediation programmes costing billions of pounds, changes in law including the Building Safety Act 2022, and an ongoing dispute over who should pay. A police investigation into possible criminal charges has continued alongside the inquiry. Commemorations are held each year on 14 June, and the tower, covered in green sheeting bearing a heart symbol, stood as a memorial pending a decision on its demolition.
