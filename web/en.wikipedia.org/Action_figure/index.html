Action figure

An action figure is a poseable character model figure made most commonly of plastic, representing a character from film, television, comic books, video games or original toy lines. The term was coined by Hasbro in 1964 for its G.I. Joe line, to market a doll-like toy to boys without using the word "doll".

History

Hasbro's original G.I. Joe (1964) was 11.5 inches tall with 21 moving parts. In 1977 Kenner launched its 3.75-inch Star Wars figures, establishing the smaller scale that allowed extensive vehicle and playset lines and revolutionised licensed toys. Successful franchises followed, including Masters of the Universe, Transformers and Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles. From the 1990s, manufacturers such as McFarlane Toys and NECA increasingly targeted adult collectors with detailed, display-oriented figures, while Japanese companies developed high-end articulated lines such as figma and S.H.Figuarts.

Characteristics

Action figures are defined by articulation — ball joints, hinges and swivel joints that allow posing — and often include accessories, interchangeable hands or heads, and soft-goods clothing. Common scales include 1:18 (3.75 in), 1:12 (6 in) and 1:6 (12 in). Mint-in-package vintage figures can command high prices on the collectors' market.
