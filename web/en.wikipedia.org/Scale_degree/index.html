Scale degree

In music theory, a scale degree is the position of a particular note within a musical scale relative to the tonic. Degrees of the diatonic scale are conventionally given names and numbers: the first is the tonic, the second the supertonic, the third the mediant, the fourth the subdominant, the fifth the dominant, the sixth the submediant, and the seventh the subtonic or, when a semitone below the tonic, the leading tone.

The seven degrees of the major scale

Function

Scale degrees carry characteristic melodic and harmonic tendencies: the leading tone pulls strongly toward the tonic, while the dominant supports the principal chord of the dominant (V). In Roman numeral analysis, chords are labelled by the degree on which they are built. Movable-do solfège assigns syllables (do, re, mi...) to degrees rather than fixed pitches, reflecting their functional relationships.

Major and minor

In the natural minor scale the seventh degree is a whole step below the tonic and is called the subtonic; raising it produces the leading tone of harmonic minor. Degrees may also be altered chromatically, indicated with raised or lowered symbols.
