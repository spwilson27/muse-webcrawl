Water scarcity

Water scarcity is the lack of sufficient available water resources to meet the demands of water usage within a region. It already affects every continent and, according to the United Nations, around four billion people experience severe water scarcity during at least one month of the year. Water scarcity can result from physical absence of water, from inadequate infrastructure and institutions to ensure a regular supply (economic water scarcity), or from pollution that renders available water unusable.

Measurement

The Falkenmark indicator classifies countries by renewable water resources per capita: below 1,700 m³ per person per year indicates water stress; below 1,000 m³, scarcity; below 500 m³, absolute scarcity. Other measures consider seasonal variability, water quality and the environmental flows ecosystems require. Agriculture accounts for about 70 per cent of global freshwater withdrawals, followed by industry (about 20 per cent) and domestic use (about 10 per cent).

Causes

Drivers include population growth, urbanisation, rising consumption of water-intensive foods and goods, groundwater over-extraction, and the degradation of catchments and wetlands. Climate change alters precipitation patterns, melts glaciers that buffer dry-season flows, and intensifies droughts and floods. Notable crises have included Cape Town's "Day Zero" scare of 2018, chronic depletion of the Ogallala Aquifer in the United States, groundwater decline in northern India, and the shrinkage of the Aral Sea following Soviet-era irrigation diversions.

Impacts and responses

Scarcity undermines food security, health, ecosystems and economic development, and can contribute to social tension and conflict, especially where rivers and aquifers cross borders. Responses include improved irrigation efficiency, leakage reduction, wastewater reuse, rainwater harvesting, desalination, aquifer recharge, water pricing and allocation reform, and international cooperation on shared basins. Sustainable Development Goal 6 commits the world to ensuring availability and sustainable management of water and sanitation for all by 2030.
