Water cycle

The water cycle is the continuous movement of water within the Earth and atmosphere. It is a complex system that includes many different processes. Liquid water evaporates into water vapour, condenses to form clouds, and precipitates back to earth in the form of rain and snow. Water in different phases moves through the atmosphere. Liquid water flows across land (runoff), into the ground (infiltration and percolation), and through the ground (groundwater).

Main stages

The cycle's stages are evaporation, transpiration, condensation, precipitation, infiltration, runoff, and subsurface flow. Water also moves between stores: oceans, glaciers and ice caps, groundwater, lakes, rivers, soil moisture, and the atmosphere.

Fluxes

Each year roughly 500,000 cubic kilometres of water evaporate, mostly from the oceans, and a slightly smaller amount falls as precipitation over the oceans; the difference is carried over land as atmospheric moisture and returns via rivers and groundwater discharge. This net transport sustains land ecosystems and human water use.

Change

Warming intensifies the cycle: a warmer atmosphere holds about 7 percent more moisture per degree Celsius, tending to make wet regions and wet seasons wetter and to lengthen dry spells — changes that directly affect regional water availability.
