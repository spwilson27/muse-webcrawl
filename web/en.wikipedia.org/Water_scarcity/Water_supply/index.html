Water supply

Water supply is the provision of water by public utilities, commercial organisations, community endeavours or by individuals, usually via a system of pumps and pipes. The quality and continuity of supply vary enormously worldwide, from continuous treated tap water available around the clock to intermittent systems where water flows for only a few hours a day.

Sources and distribution

Supplies are sourced from surface waters (rivers, lakes, reservoirs) and groundwater. After treatment, water is pumped into distribution networks of trunk and branch mains, with storage tanks balancing daily demand. Utilities also supply irrigation, industry, and firefighting, though drinking-water quality is required only for the potable network.

Supply under stress

Where demand outgrows resources, suppliers ration deliveries, drill deeper wells, transfer water between basins, or build desalination and reuse plants. Non-revenue water — lost through leaks, theft, and metering errors — can exceed 40 percent in stressed systems, and reducing it is often the cheapest "new" source.

Governance

Supply is typically a natural monopoly run by municipal utilities or regulated private companies. Tariffs balance affordability with the need to fund maintenance and expansion, a tension at the heart of water policy in both rich and poor countries.
