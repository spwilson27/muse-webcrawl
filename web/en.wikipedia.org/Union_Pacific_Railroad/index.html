The Union Pacific Railroad (reporting mark UP) is a Class I freight-hauling railroad that operates more than 8,300 locomotives over 32,000 route miles in 23 states west of Chicago and New Orleans. It is the largest railroad network in the United States by route mileage, and along with BNSF Railway forms a duopoly over transcontinental freight rail lines in the western United States.

History

The Union Pacific was chartered by the Pacific Railroad Act of 1862, signed by Abraham Lincoln. Building westward from Council Bluffs, Iowa, its crews met the Central Pacific Railroad at Promontory Summit, Utah, on 10 May 1869, completing the first transcontinental railroad in North America; the event was marked by the driving of a golden spike. The construction was financed in part by federal bonds and land grants, and was tainted by the Crédit Mobilier scandal of 1872. After bankruptcy in the Panic of 1893, the railroad was reorganised and rebuilt under E. H. Harriman, who made it one of the most profitable properties in the country.

Modern era

The twentieth-century Union Pacific absorbed numerous rivals, including the Missouri Pacific and Western Pacific in 1982, the Chicago and North Western in 1995, and the Southern Pacific in 1996 — creating the modern system. Its Bailey Yard in North Platte, Nebraska, is the world's largest rail classification yard. Union Pacific also preserves notable heritage equipment, including the steam locomotive Big Boy No. 4014, restored to excursion service in 2019.

Operations

The railroad carries bulk commodities (grain, coal, chemicals), intermodal containers, automobiles and industrial products. Its parent company, Union Pacific Corporation, is headquartered in Omaha and listed on the New York Stock Exchange.
