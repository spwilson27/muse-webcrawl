Bluetooth Low Energy

Bluetooth Low Energy (Bluetooth LE, colloquially BLE, formerly marketed as Bluetooth Smart) is a wireless personal area network technology designed and marketed by the Bluetooth Special Interest Group. It is aimed at novel applications in healthcare, fitness, beacons, security and home entertainment, and is not backward-compatible with classic Bluetooth, although dual-mode devices implement both.

History

The technology originated as Wibree, announced by Nokia in 2006, and was merged into the Bluetooth standard in 2009. It was adopted as a hallmark feature of Bluetooth Core Specification version 4.0 in 2010. Apple was among the first major adopters, and BLE quickly became standard in smartphones, tablets and computers.

Technical details

BLE operates in the same 2.4 GHz ISM band as classic Bluetooth but uses 40 channels and a simpler protocol stack optimised for brief, infrequent data transfers. Devices spend most of their time asleep, waking for short connection events, which allows peripherals such as coin-cell-powered sensors to run for months or years. Data is organised through the Generic Attribute Profile (GATT) into services and characteristics. Bluetooth 5 (2016) added higher throughput and a long-range coded mode, and later releases added direction finding and LE Audio.

Applications

Common uses include fitness trackers, heart-rate monitors, smart watches, proximity beacons for indoor positioning and retail, glucose monitors, hearing aids and wireless sensors in the Internet of Things.
