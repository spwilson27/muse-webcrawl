Panah Ali Khan Javanshir (1693–1763; Azerbaijani: Pənahəli xan Cavanşir) was the founder and first ruler of the Karabakh Khanate in the South Caucasus, ruling from 1748 until his death. A chieftain of the Javanshir Turkic tribe, he rose amid the collapse of Afsharid Persia after the assassination of Nader Shah in 1747.

Rise to power

Panah Ali had served Nader Shah but fallen out of favour and fled; after Nader's death he returned to Karabakh, gathered the Javanshir and allied tribes, and repelled attempts by rival khans and by the Persian claimant Adel Shah to subdue the region. Between 1748 and 1752 he built the fortresses of Bayat and Shahbulag, and in 1752 founded a new capital fortress at Shusha — originally called Panahabad after him — in a naturally defensible mountain stronghold.

Reign

From Shusha, Panah Ali Khan consolidated control over Karabakh's five Armenian melikdoms and neighbouring territories, fought wars with the Sheki and Shamakhi khanates, and maneuvered between Persia, the Georgian kingdoms, and local rivals. In 1763 he was summoned (or travelled) to Shiraz by Karim Khan Zand and died there the same year; his son Ibrahim Khalil Khan succeeded him and ruled the khanate into the era of Russian expansion.

Legacy

Panah Ali Khan is remembered as the founder of Shusha, which became the cultural capital of Karabakh, and of the line of khans that governed Karabakh until its incorporation into the Russian Empire in the early 19th century.
