Margaret Fell

Margaret Fell
Born: 1614, Dalton-in-Furness, Lancashire
Died: 23 April 1702 (aged 87)
Known for: Co-founder of the Religious Society of Friends (Quakers)
Spouses: Thomas Fell (d. 1658); George Fox (m. 1669)

Margaret Fell or Margaret Fox (1614 – 23 April 1702) was one of the founders of the Religious Society of Friends. Known popularly as the "mother of Quakerism," she was a leading figure among the early Quaker preachers and organisers called the Valiant Sixty.

Conversion

Margaret Askew married Thomas Fell, a barrister and later judge and MP, and lived at Swarthmoor Hall near Ulverston. In 1652 the itinerant preacher George Fox visited Swarthmoor; Margaret was convinced by his preaching, while her husband, though never a Quaker himself, protected the meetings held at the hall, which became a headquarters of the early movement.

Activism and writings

Fell organised support networks for imprisoned Friends, corresponded with hundreds of congregations, and petitioned Parliament and the king against persecution. She was herself imprisoned in Lancaster Castle from 1664 to 1668 for refusing to take an oath and for holding meetings. Her pamphlet Women's Speaking Justified (1666/67) is a foundational defence of women's preaching, arguing from scripture that women may prophesy and teach. She also wrote an account of Hebrew learning and corresponded in an effort to convert Jews.

Later life

After Thomas Fell's death she married George Fox in 1669. She continued travelling and organising into old age and died at Swarthmoor in 1702, leaving an enormous correspondence that remains a principal source for early Quaker history.
