The Empire of Nicaea (Greek: Βασιλεία Ῥωμαίων) was the largest of the Greek successor states founded after the Fourth Crusade seized Constantinople in 1204. Ruled from Nicaea in Bithynia (north-western Anatolia) by the Laskaris family, it successfully claimed continuity with the Byzantine Empire and, under Michael VIII Palaiologos, retook Constantinople in 1261, restoring the Byzantine Empire.

History

Theodore I Laskaris, son-in-law of the deposed emperor Alexios III, established the state in 1204–1205 and was crowned emperor in 1208. His successors — the soldier-emperor John III Vatatzes (1222–1254) and Theodore II Laskaris — expanded Nicaean rule across western Anatolia and deep into the Balkans, defeated the Latin Empire and rival Greeks of Epirus, and deliberately cultivated Byzantine administration, court culture and Orthodoxy, making Nicaea a centre of Greek learning in exile.

Restoration of Byzantium

In 1259 Michael VIII Palaiologos usurped the throne from the child John IV. On 25 July 1261, a Nicaean force under Alexios Strategopoulos recovered Constantinople almost by accident from the distracted Latins, and Michael entered the city on 15 August, dissolving the Empire of Nicaea into the restored Byzantine Empire.
