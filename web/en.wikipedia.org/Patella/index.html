Patella

The patella, commonly known as the kneecap, is a flat, roughly triangular bone which articultes with the femur (thigh bone) and covers and protects the front of the knee joint. It is the largest sesamoid bone in the human body — a bone embedded within a tendon — sitting inside the tendon of the quadriceps femoris muscle.

Structure and function

The patella's posterior surface is covered with articular cartilage and glides in the trochlear groove of the femur as the knee flexes and extends. By holding the quadriceps tendon away from the joint axis, the patella increases the leverage of the quadriceps by up to about 30–50%, and it shields the front of the knee.

Development and clinical significance

The patella ossifies from a cartilaginous precursor between roughly ages three and six. Common problems include patellar fractures from direct blows, dislocation (most often lateral), patellar tendinitis ("jumper's knee"), and chondromalacia patellae, a softening of the cartilage behind the kneecap.
