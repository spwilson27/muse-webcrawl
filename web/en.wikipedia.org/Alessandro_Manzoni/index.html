Alessandro Manzoni

Alessandro Francesco Tommaso Antonio Manzoni (7 March 1785 – 22 May 1873) was an Italian poet, novelist and philosopher. He is famous for the novel The Betrothed (1827), generally ranked among the masterpieces of world literature. The novel is also a symbol of the Italian Risorgimento, both for its patriotic spirit and for its contribution to the formation of a modern, unified Italian language.

Life

Manzoni was born in Milan, then part of the Austrian-ruled Kingdom of Lombardy–Venetia. His grandfather was Cesare Beccaria, the celebrated jurist and author of On Crimes and Punishments. Educated in religious schools he initially disliked, Manzoni spent formative years in Paris, where he moved in liberal intellectual circles. Initially sceptical in religion, he returned to Catholicism around 1810 and remained a devout Catholic thereafter.

Works

Manzoni's early works include the sacred hymns (Inni sacri), the odes Marzo 1821 and Il cinque maggio (on the death of Napoleon), and the tragedies Il Conte di Carmagnola and Adelchi. His masterpiece, I promessi sposi (The Betrothed), first published in 1827 and thoroughly revised for the definitive 1840 edition, is set in Lombardy during the Spanish occupation of the 1620s–30s and follows the peasant lovers Renzo and Lucia through plague, famine, and injustice.

Language and legacy

Manzoni deliberately rewrote the novel in a language modelled on educated Florentine usage, an effort he described as rinsing his clothes in the Arno. This choice decisively influenced the standardization of modern Italian. Giuseppe Verdi's Requiem was composed in his memory and first performed on the first anniversary of his death. Manzoni died in Milan in 1873 after a fall, and his funeral was a national event.
