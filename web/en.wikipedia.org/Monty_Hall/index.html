Monty Hall (born Monte Halparin; 25 August 1921 – 30 September 2017) was a Canadian-American television host, actor and producer, best known as the long-time host and co-creator of the game show Let's Make a Deal, which premiered in 1963 and made him a fixture of American daytime television.

Hall as host of Let's Make a Deal.

Early life and career

Hall was born in Winnipeg to Jewish parents and studied chemistry and zoology at the University of Manitoba, where he emceed campus shows. He worked in radio in Winnipeg and Toronto before moving into Canadian television in the 1950s and then to the United States, hosting shows in New York before relocating to Los Angeles.

Let's Make a Deal

With partner Stefan Hatos, Hall created Let's Make a Deal (1963), in which costumed audience members ("traders") made deals for prizes hidden behind doors or in boxes — risking a good prize for a chance at a better one, or ending with a booby prize ("zonk"). Hall hosted the original run through 1977, later revivals, and remained a consultant on the version hosted by Wayne Brady from 2009.

The Monty Hall problem

Hall's name is attached to the Monty Hall problem, a famous probability puzzle modelled on the show: after a contestant picks one of three doors, the host opens another door revealing a goat, and the contestant does better by switching, winning with probability 2/3. Popularised by Marilyn vos Savant's column in 1990, the puzzle generated widespread controversy before its counterintuitive answer was broadly accepted.

Later life

Hall was active in philanthropy, raising large sums for charities. He received stars on the Hollywood Walk of Fame and Canada's Walk of Fame and was made an Officer of the Order of Canada. He died of heart failure in 2017 at the age of 96.
