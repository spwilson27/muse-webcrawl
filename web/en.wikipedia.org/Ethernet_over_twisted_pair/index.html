Ethernet over twisted pair

Ethernet over twisted pair technologies use twisted-pair cables for the physical layer of an Ethernet computer network. They became the dominant form of Ethernet cabling in local area networks from the 1990s onward, replacing coaxial cable, using standardised 8P8C (RJ45) connectors and cable categories such as Cat 5e, Cat 6 and Cat 6a.

History and standards

10BASE-T, standardised in IEEE 802.3i (1990), carried 10 Mbit/s over two pairs of Category 3 cable up to 100 metres. 100BASE-TX (Fast Ethernet) used Category 5 cable; 1000BASE-T (Gigabit Ethernet) uses all four pairs bidirectionally; 10GBASE-T requires Cat 6a for the full 100-metre reach. Later standards include 2.5GBASE-T and 5GBASE-T for existing cabling.

Cabling and wiring

Pairs are wired to the T568A or T568B pinouts. Twisting reduces electromagnetic interference and crosstalk. Early equipment required crossover cables between like devices; modern interfaces implement Auto MDI-X, automatically detecting the needed configuration. Power over Ethernet (PoE) delivers DC power on the same cable for phones, cameras and wireless access points.

Operation

Links are typically point-to-point between a device and a switch, operating full-duplex so collisions do not occur. Autonegotiation selects speed and duplex. Link integrity pulses / Fast Link Pulses signal connection status.
