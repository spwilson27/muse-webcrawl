Peugeot (French pronunciation: [pø.ʒo]) is a French automobile brand, part of the Stellantis group. The Peugeot family business began in 1810 as a steel foundry making tools, springs, and later coffee mills, pepper grinders, and bicycles, before becoming one of the pioneering manufacturers of automobiles. Its emblem is a lion, in use since 1858.

History

The Peugeot family of Valentigney, in eastern France, converted a mill into a steel works in 1810. The company made a wide range of steel products and began producing bicycles in 1882. Armand Peugeot, convinced of the future of the motor car, produced a steam-powered three-wheeler in 1889 and soon switched to petrol engines supplied by Daimler, making Peugeot one of the world's earliest car manufacturers.

Twentieth century

Peugeot cars won early motor races and became known for robust, popular models such as the 201, 202, 203, and the elegant Pininfarina-styled 404 and 504. In 1976 Peugeot took over Citroën to form the PSA Peugeot Citroën group, and in 1978 it bought Chrysler's European operations (the former Rootes and Simca businesses). Successful later models included the 205 hatchback (1983), credited with saving the company, the 206, and the 208.

Modern era

In 2021 PSA merged with Fiat Chrysler Automobiles to form Stellantis, making Peugeot one of fourteen brands in the group. Peugeot has committed to electrifying its range, offering battery-electric and hybrid versions of models such as the e-208 and e-2008, and unveiled the 9X8 hybrid hypercar for endurance racing.

Motorsport

Peugeot Sport has won the World Rally Championship five times, the Dakar Rally multiple times, the 24 Hours of Le Mans (1992, 1993, 2009), and set record times at Pikes Peak, including Sébastien Loeb's 2013 run in the 208 T16.

Other products

The separate Peugeot family company continues to be associated with pepper and salt mills, and Peugeot-branded bicycles and scooters remain in production under licence arrangements.
