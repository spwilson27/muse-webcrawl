Criminal justice

Criminal justice is the delivery of justice to those who have committed crimes. The criminal justice system is a series of government agencies and institutions whose goals include the rehabilitation of offenders, preventing other crimes, and moral support for victims. The main institutions of the criminal justice system are the police, prosecution and defence lawyers, the courts, and the prison system.

The criminal justice system is commonly symbolised by the scales of justice

Components

Law enforcement agencies, typically the police, investigate suspected criminal activity and refer the results of investigations to prosecutors. Prosecutors decide whether to bring charges and conduct the case for the state; defence lawyers represent the accused. Courts adjudicate, applying the law and ensuring fair procedures, and determine guilt or innocence at trial. Where an offender is convicted, the corrections system administers the sentence, which may include imprisonment, probation, fines, or community service.

Goals

The aims conventionally attributed to criminal justice and sentencing include retribution, deterrence, incapacitation, rehabilitation, and restoration. Different jurisdictions and eras weight these goals differently: retributive approaches emphasise deserved punishment, while rehabilitative and restorative approaches emphasise repairing harm and reintegrating offenders.

Models of the process

Herbert Packer's influential analysis contrasted two models: the crime control model, which prioritises the efficient suppression of crime and resembles an assembly line, and the due process model, which prioritises the rights of the accused and the reliability of fact-finding, resembling an obstacle course. Real systems combine elements of both.

Justice around the world

Criminal justice systems are usually classified by legal tradition: common law (adversarial) systems, in which parties present competing cases to a neutral judge or jury; civil law (inquisitorial) systems, in which judges play a more active investigative role; and various hybrid and religious legal traditions. International criminal justice is administered by bodies such as the International Criminal Court, which prosecutes genocide, crimes against humanity, and war crimes.
