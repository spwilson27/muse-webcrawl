Digital currency

Digital currency (digital money, electronic money or electronic currency) is any currency, money, or money-like asset that is stored, managed and exchanged primarily on digital computer systems, especially over the internet. Types of digital currencies include cryptocurrency, virtual currency and central bank digital currency. Digital currency may be recorded on a distributed database on the internet, a centralised electronic computer database owned by a company or bank, within digital files, or even on a stored-value card.

History

Precursors of digital currency date to the beginnings of the internet: in 1983 David Chaum introduced the concept of cryptographic electronic cash with his ecash system, commercialised through DigiCash in the 1990s. Early 2000s services such as e-gold and Liberty Reserve allowed transferable digital balances before legal actions shut them down. In 2009 Bitcoin became the first decentralised cryptocurrency, combining a peer-to-peer network with a proof-of-work blockchain to prevent double-spending without a central issuer.

Types

Cryptocurrencies are digital currencies secured by cryptography and usually maintained on a public blockchain, with new units created by a defined issuance schedule. Virtual currencies are unregulated digital currencies controlled by their developers or a governing organisation, often used within online games and communities. Central bank digital currencies (CBDCs) are digital forms of fiat money issued and regulated by a central bank; pilots and launches include the Bahamas' Sand Dollar, Nigeria's eNaira and China's e-CNY.

Regulation and concerns

Regulators treat digital currencies variously as property, commodities, securities or money, affecting taxation and anti-money-laundering obligations. Debates focus on consumer protection, price volatility, energy use of proof-of-work mining, the risk of financial exclusion or surveillance with CBDCs, and the implications for monetary policy and the banking system.
