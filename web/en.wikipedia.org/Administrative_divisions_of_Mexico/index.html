Administrative divisions of Mexico

The administrative divisions of Mexico consist of 31 states and Mexico City, which together form the United Mexican States. Each state is free and sovereign in its internal regime under the federal constitution.

States

The states range from Baja California and Chihuahua in the north to Yucatan, Quintana Roo and Chiapas in the south-east. Mexico City, the capital, gained state-like autonomy under the 2016 political reform and is divided into boroughs (alcaldias).

Municipalities

States are subdivided into municipalities — more than 2,400 nationwide — each governed by an elected municipal president and council. Oaxaca has the most municipalities, largely governed under indigenous customary law (usos y costumbres).
