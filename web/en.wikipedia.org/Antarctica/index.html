Antarctica is Earth's southernmost continent, containing the geographic South Pole and surrounded by the Southern Ocean. It is the fifth-largest continent, the coldest, driest, windiest and highest on average, and the only continent without a native human population. About 98% of its surface is covered by the Antarctic ice sheet, which averages about 1.9 km in thickness and holds roughly 61% of the world's fresh water.

Geography and climate

The Transantarctic Mountains divide the continent into East Antarctica, a high ice plateau, and the smaller West Antarctica, including the Antarctic Peninsula reaching towards South America. Antarctica is a polar desert: annual precipitation is minimal in the interior, temperatures have fallen below −89 °C, and katabatic winds pour off the plateau at great speed. The surrounding ocean, with its Antarctic Circumpolar Current, supports rich ecosystems of krill, penguins, seals and whales, while the interior supports only mosses, lichens and microscopic life, plus subglacial lakes such as Lake Vostok.

Exploration

The continent was first sighted in 1820 by Russian, British and American expeditions (Bellingshausen, Bransfield and Palmer are all credited), and first landed on in the 1820s–40s. The "Heroic Age" of exploration culminated in the race for the South Pole: Roald Amundsen's Norwegian party reached it on 14 December 1911, five weeks before Robert Falcon Scott's British party, which perished on the return journey. Ernest Shackleton's expeditions became legendary for endurance and survival. The first crossing of the continent was achieved by the Commonwealth Trans-Antarctic Expedition in 1957–58.

Governance and research

Seven states maintain territorial claims, but the Antarctic Treaty (signed 1959, in force 1961) sets the continent aside for peaceful purposes and scientific research, suspends sovereignty disputes, bans military activity and (under the 1991 Madrid Protocol) prohibits mineral extraction and designates Antarctica a natural reserve. Dozens of research stations operate year-round, including McMurdo Station (USA), Amundsen–Scott South Pole Station, and Concordia (France/Italy). Research covers glaciology, climate history from ice cores, astronomy and astrophysics, and biology.

Environment and climate change

Antarctica drives global ocean circulation and, through its ice sheet, holds the largest potential contribution to future sea-level rise. Parts of West Antarctica and the Peninsula have warmed and lost ice rapidly, while ozone depletion over the continent (the "ozone hole") is slowly recovering under the Montreal Protocol.
