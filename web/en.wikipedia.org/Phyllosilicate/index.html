Phyllosilicate

Phyllosilicates, or sheet silicates, are silicate minerals whose crystal structure consists of parallel sheets of silicate tetrahedra (Si₂O₅ units) linked in two dimensions. Their layered structure gives many of them perfect basal cleavage and soft, flaky habits.

Structure and groups

Layers may be 1:1 (one tetrahedral and one octahedral sheet, as in kaolinite) or 2:1 (an octahedral sheet sandwiched between two tetrahedral sheets, as in talc and the micas). Major groups include the serpentine group, clay minerals (kaolinite, smectite, illite), the mica group (muscovite, biotite) and chlorite.

Occurrence and uses

Phyllosilicates form by weathering and hydrothermal alteration and dominate soils and sedimentary rocks. They are used in ceramics, paper, cosmetics, drilling fluids and agriculture; swelling clays such as bentonite are important industrial materials.
