Book publishing is the industry concerned with the acquisition, editing, production, marketing, and distribution of books. Publishers act as intermediaries between authors and readers: they select manuscripts, finance production, and assume the commercial risk of bringing a title to market, in exchange for the right to publish it under a contract with the author.

Process

A book typically passes through acquisition (often involving a literary agent), developmental and copy editing, design and typesetting, proofreading, printing or digital conversion, and distribution through wholesalers and retailers. Authors usually earn royalties — a percentage of sales — against an advance paid on signing.

Industry structure

The trade market is dominated by the "Big Five" English-language publishers: Penguin Random House, HarperCollins, Simon & Schuster, Hachette, and Macmillan. Alongside them operate independent and university presses, educational and professional publishers, and a growing self-publishing sector enabled by print-on-demand and e-book platforms.

Digital change

E-books and audiobooks have reshaped the industry since the 2000s, while online retail and direct-to-consumer models have altered marketing and distribution. Print remains the majority of trade revenue in most major markets.
