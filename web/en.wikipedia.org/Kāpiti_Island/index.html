Kāpiti Island

Kāpiti Island, a predator-free bird sanctuary off New Zealand's lower North Island.

Kāpiti Island is an island about 5 kilometres off the west coast of the lower North Island of New Zealand, in the Tasman Sea opposite Paraparaumu. It is approximately 10 kilometres long and 2 kilometres wide, covering some 1,965 hectares, and rises to 521 metres at Tuteremoana, its highest point. The island is one of New Zealand's most important nature reserves.

History

The island was occupied by Māori iwi including Ngāti Toa under Te Rauparaha, who used it as a stronghold in the early nineteenth century and from there dominated the Cook Strait region. European whaling stations operated there later in the century. In 1897 the island was set aside as a bird sanctuary, one of New Zealand's earliest conservation reserves, championed by naturalist Richard Henry and others as a refuge for native species.

Conservation

Possums, goats, and other introduced mammals were progressively removed, and the island was declared free of possums in 1986 and of rats (kiore and Norway rats) after an eradication programme in 1996–1998. Freed of predators, the regenerating forest now supports abundant birds, including little spotted kiwi — for which Kāpiti is the principal stronghold — North Island kōkako, takahe, stitchbird (hihi), saddleback (tīeke), and bellbirds. Access is strictly limited by permit, with visitor numbers capped daily, to protect the sanctuary's biosecurity.
