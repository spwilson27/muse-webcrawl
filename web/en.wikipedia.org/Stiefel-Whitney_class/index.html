Stiefel–Whitney class

In algebraic topology, a Stiefel–Whitney class is a characteristic class associated to a real vector bundle. The classes are named after Eduard Stiefel and Hassler Whitney, who introduced them in the 1930s. They take values in cohomology with coefficients in the field with two elements, Z/2Z.

Definition

For a vector bundle E over a space B, the Stiefel–Whitney classes wi(E) lie in the cohomology groups Hi(B; Z/2). They are packaged into the total Stiefel–Whitney class w(E) = 1 + w1(E) + w2(E) + …, which has degree-zero component 1. The classes are characterised by axioms: naturality under pullback, the Whitney product formula w(E ⊕ F) = w(E)w(F), and normalisation on the tautological line bundle over the real projective plane.

Interpretation

The classes measure obstructions to finding linearly independent sections of a bundle. w1(E) vanishes exactly when E is orientable; w2 is related to the existence of a spin structure. For the tangent bundle of a smooth manifold, the Stiefel–Whitney classes are topological invariants of the manifold itself.

Stiefel–Whitney numbers

Evaluating products of the classes on the fundamental class of a closed manifold gives Stiefel–Whitney numbers, which are invariants of cobordism: by a theorem of René Thom, two closed manifolds are unorientedly cobordant if and only if all their Stiefel–Whitney numbers agree.
