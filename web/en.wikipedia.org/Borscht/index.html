Borscht

Borscht is a sour soup, commonly made with beetroot as the main ingredient giving it a deep red color, that is popular across Eastern Europe and Northern Asia. It is strongly associated with Ukrainian cuisine, where it originated, and is also a staple in Russia, Poland, Belarus, and other countries of the region.

Ingredients and preparation

The classic version combines beetroot with a meat or bone stock and sautéed vegetables such as cabbage, carrots, onions, potatoes, and tomatoes. It is typically served hot, garnished with sour cream and fresh dill. Numerous regional and seasonal variants exist, including meatless versions for Lent and cold borscht served in summer.

History

Borscht evolved from a soup originally made from the stems, leaves, and umbels of common hogweed (borshchevik), which lent the dish its name. Over centuries the recipe changed, and the beet-based version became dominant. In 2022, UNESCO placed "the culture of Ukrainian borscht cooking" on its List of Intangible Cultural Heritage in Need of Urgent Safeguarding.

Cultural significance

Borscht is regarded as a national dish in Ukraine and a symbol of Ukrainian culinary identity, and it remains one of the best-known dishes of Eastern European cooking worldwide.
