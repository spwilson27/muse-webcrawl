Liu Zongyuan

Liu Zongyuan (Chinese: 柳宗元; 773 – November 28, 819), courtesy name Zihou, was a Chinese writer, poet and government official of the Tang dynasty. He is counted among the Eight Masters of Tang and Song prose (唐宋八大家) and, together with Han Yu, was a leading figure of the Classical Prose Movement (古文運動), which sought to revive a plainer, more direct style modelled on pre-Han writing.

Life

Liu Zongyuan was born in 773 in the region of modern Yuncheng, Shanxi. A precocious student, he passed the jinshi civil service examination at the age of twenty and rose quickly in the bureaucracy. He became associated with the reformist faction around Wang Shuwen, which attempted sweeping changes during the brief reign of Emperor Shunzong in 805. When the reformers fell, Liu was demoted and exiled — first to Yongzhou in Hunan, and later to the even more remote Liuzhou in Guangxi, where he eventually died in 819.

Works

Liu's exile years were his most productive. In Yongzhou he wrote the celebrated "Eight Records of Yongzhou" (永州八記), landscape essays that combine precise observation of nature with reflections on his frustrated political career. His fables and satirical prose, such as "The Tale of the Snake Catcher" (捕蛇者說), criticised oppressive taxation and misgovernment. In Liuzhou he is remembered for benevolent administration and for releasing bondservants; local people later honoured him with a temple and citadel.

His poetry, like that of his friend Liu Yuxi, often expresses sorrow and quiet endurance in exile. Together, the two Lius were remembered in the phrase "Liu-Liu" (劉柳).
