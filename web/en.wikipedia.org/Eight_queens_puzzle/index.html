Eight queens puzzle

The eight queens puzzle is the problem of placing eight chess queens on an 8×8 chessboard so that no two queens threaten each other — that is, no two share the same row, column, or diagonal. The puzzle was proposed by chess composer Max Bezzel in 1848, and many mathematicians, including Carl Friedrich Gauss, worked on it and its generalization, the n queens problem.

Solutions

The eight queens puzzle has 92 distinct solutions. If solutions differing only by rotations and reflections of the board are considered the same, there are 12 fundamental solutions. The first published solutions appeared in 1850, in the chess magazine Schachzeitung, by Franz Nauck, who also extended the problem to n queens on an n×n board.

Counting solutions

For the general n queens problem, the number of solutions grows rapidly: n = 1 has 1 solution, n = 4 has 2, n = 8 has 92, and n = 26 has over 22 trillion. No simple formula is known. Exhaustive computation for larger boards is a notable benchmark in parallel computing and constraint satisfaction.

In computer science

The problem is a classic example used to teach backtracking algorithms: queens are placed one row at a time, retreating whenever a conflict arises. Edsger Dijkstra used it to illustrate structured programming. Variants include placing queens on boards with blocked squares, using other chess pieces, and the n-queens completion problem, which was shown to be NP-hard in 2017.
