Boeing KC-135 Stratotanker

Role: Aerial refuelling tanker / transport

Manufacturer: Boeing

First flight: 31 August 1956

Introduced: June 1957

Number built: 803

The Boeing KC-135 Stratotanker is an American military aerial refuelling aircraft developed from the Boeing 367-80 prototype, alongside the Boeing 707 airliner. It was the United States Air Force's first jet-powered refuelling tanker, replacing the piston-engined KC-97 Stratofreighter. Initially tasked with supporting strategic bombers, the KC-135 came to be used to extend the range of fighters and other tactical aircraft as well.

Development and service

The KC-135 first flew in August 1956 and was delivered to Strategic Air Command beginning in 1957. A total of 803 airframes were built between 1955 and 1965. The aircraft transfers fuel through a flying boom operated from a prone position in the tail; probe-and-drogue adapters can be fitted for aircraft using that system.

Variants and derivatives

Most surviving aircraft are KC-135R models, re-engined with CFM56 turbofans, which greatly improved fuel efficiency and reduced noise. Related variants include the RC-135 reconnaissance family, the E-3 Sentry and E-8 Joint STARS (based on the related 707/C-135 family), and specialised test and command-post versions. The KC-135 remains in service with the USAF and several allied air forces, and is being supplemented — and eventually replaced — by the KC-46 Pegasus.
