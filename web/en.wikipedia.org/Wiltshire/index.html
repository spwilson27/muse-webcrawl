Wiltshire is a ceremonial county in South West England, known for its rolling chalk downland, its prehistoric monuments and its military training areas. It borders Gloucestershire, Oxfordshire, Berkshire, Hampshire, Dorset and Somerset. The county town is Trowbridge and its largest town is Swindon; its only city is Salisbury.

Geography

Much of Wiltshire is chalk downland, including Salisbury Plain — the largest remaining area of chalk grassland in north-west Europe and a major British Army training area — and the Marlborough Downs. The county is the source of several rivers, including the Bristol Avon and the Hampshire Avon, and of the River Kennet, a tributary of the Thames. The Kennet and Avon Canal crosses the county from west to east.

History and landmarks

Wiltshire contains some of Britain's most famous prehistoric landscapes. Stonehenge and Avebury, together a UNESCO World Heritage Site, date from the Neolithic and Bronze Age, alongside Silbury Hill and West Kennet Long Barrow. The county town in Saxon and medieval times was Wilton, from which the county takes its name. Salisbury Cathedral, built in the 13th century, has the tallest church spire in Britain and an original copy of Magna Carta. The old hillfort and abandoned settlement of Old Sarum was a notorious "rotten borough" until 1832. Swindon grew as a railway town built around the Great Western Railway works.

Economy

The economy mixes agriculture, tourism, technology and defence: the M4 corridor hosts electronics and financial-services offices in Swindon, while a large share of the Army's personnel are based on Salisbury Plain at Tidworth, Bulford and Larkhill.
