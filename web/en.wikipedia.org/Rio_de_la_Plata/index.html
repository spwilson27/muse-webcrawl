Río de la Plata

The Río de la Plata (Spanish for "River of Silver"), also called the River Plate or La Plata River in English, is the estuary formed by the confluence of the Uruguay River and the Paraná River at Punta Gorda. It empties into the Atlantic Ocean, forming a funnel-shaped indentation on the southeastern coastline of South America between Argentina and Uruguay.

Depending on the geographer, the Río de la Plata may be considered a river, an estuary, a gulf, or a marginal sea. It is about 290 kilometres (180 mi) long and widens from about 2 kilometres (1.2 mi) at its source to about 220 kilometres (140 mi) at its mouth, making it among the widest river estuaries in the world. The cities of Buenos Aires and Montevideo sit on its western and northern shores respectively.

History

The estuary was first reached by Europeans in 1516, when the Spanish navigator Juan Díaz de Solís explored it while searching for a passage to the Pacific; he was killed by the local Charrúa people. Ferdinand Magellan's expedition passed through in 1520. The name derives from early explorers' hopes of finding silver and from silver objects obtained from Indigenous peoples.

The estuary is also remembered for the Battle of the River Plate in December 1939, the first major naval battle of the Second World War, in which the German heavy cruiser Admiral Graf Spee was scuttled off Montevideo.
