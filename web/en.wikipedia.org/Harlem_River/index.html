Harlem River

LocationNew York City, New York, USA
Length8 mi (13 km)
ConnectsHudson River and East River
SeparatesManhattan and the Bronx

The Harlem River is an eight-mile (13 km) tidal strait in New York City flowing between the Hudson River and the East River, separating the boroughs of Manhattan and the Bronx. Despite its name, it is not a true river but a tidal channel influenced by the tides of Long Island Sound and New York Harbor.

Course

The river runs from the confluence of Spuyten Duyvil Creek with the Hudson at the northern tip of Manhattan, southeast past Inwood, Washington Heights, and Harlem, then east along the South Bronx to the East River at Wards Island. The Harlem River Ship Canal, completed in 1895, straightened and deepened its northern reach and detached Marble Hill from Manhattan.

Bridges

Numerous bridges cross the river, including the Henry Hudson, Broadway, Washington, Alexander Hamilton, High Bridge (the city's oldest bridge, now a pedestrian span), Macombs Dam, Triborough (Harlem River Lift), and Hell Gate bridges.

Recreation

The river hosts rowing, including the Peter Jay Sharp boathouse and collegiate crew programmes, and its shores include Harlem River Park and Roberto Clemente State Park. Industrial use and pollution historically degraded water quality, which has improved in recent decades.
