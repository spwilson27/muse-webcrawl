Giza

Giza is a city in Egypt, located on the west bank of the Nile, about 5 kilometres southwest of central Cairo. It is the capital of the Giza Governorate and, with a population of several million, forms part of the Greater Cairo metropolis. Giza is world-famous for the Giza Plateau, site of the most celebrated monuments of ancient Egypt.

The Giza Plateau

On the desert plateau above the city stand the three great pyramids of the Fourth Dynasty (c. 2600–2500 BCE): the Great Pyramid of Khufu, the largest and oldest, which was the tallest human-made structure in the world for more than 3,800 years; the Pyramid of Khafre, still capped with its original limestone casing; and the smaller Pyramid of Menkaure. Beside them stands the Great Sphinx, a colossal lion-bodied statue carved from a single ridge of bedrock, together with temples, causeways, boat pits, and vast cemeteries of nobles' mastaba tombs. The necropolis was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1979.

The modern city

Modern Giza grew rapidly during the twentieth century as Cairo expanded across the river. It is home to the University of Cairo, Egypt's oldest modern university precinct in its present location, the Giza Zoo, and major transport links including the road to Alexandria. The Grand Egyptian Museum, built near the pyramids to house the treasures of Tutankhamun and other collections, is one of the largest museum projects in the world.

Tourism centred on the pyramids is a mainstay of the local economy, drawing millions of visitors annually to one of the most recognisable archaeological landscapes on Earth.
