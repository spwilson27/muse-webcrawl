Angora rabbit

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Angora rabbit (Turkish: Ankara tavşanı) is a variety of domestic rabbit bred for the long fibres of its coat, known as Angora wool. One of the oldest types of domestic rabbit, it is thought to have originated in Ankara (historically Angora), Turkey, the region also famed for the Angora cat and Angora goat.

History

Angora rabbits were popular pets in the French royal court in the mid-18th century and spread across Europe by the century's end, reaching the United States in the early 20th century. Breeders developed distinct breeds valued for wool production, fibre quality and show points.

Breeds

Recognised breeds include:

  • English Angora — covered in dense wool including "furnishings" over the face and ears.
  • French Angora — cleaner-faced, with a coarser guard hair that makes its wool easier to spin.
  • German Angora — bred for exceptionally high, uniform wool yield.
  • Giant Angora — the largest breed, developed in the United States.
  • Satin Angora — with a translucent hair shaft giving the fibre a distinctive sheen.

Wool

Angora wool is prized for its softness, warmth and hollow fibres; it is some seven to eight times warmer than sheep's wool, though often blended with other fibres for durability. The wool is harvested every three to four months by shearing, combing or plucking loose fibres, a healthy rabbit yielding several hundred grams a year. China dominates commercial production. Harvesting methods have attracted animal-welfare scrutiny, leading many retailers to demand certified humane sourcing or to drop angora altogether.

Care

The long coat requires frequent grooming to prevent mats, and owners must guard against "wool block", a potentially fatal digestive obstruction caused by ingested fur.
