Chabad

Chabad, also known as Lubavitch or Chabad-Lubavitch, is an Orthodox Jewish Hasidic dynasty and one of the largest Hasidic movements in the world. Founded in 1775 by Rabbi Schneur Zalman of Liadi in the town of Lyubavichi (Lubavitch) in present-day Russia, its name is a Hebrew acronym of the three intellectual faculties chochmah (wisdom), binah (understanding) and da'at (knowledge).

History

Schneur Zalman's Tanya (1797) became the movement's foundational text, presenting a systematic philosophy of Hasidism. Seven hereditary leaders (Rebbes) headed the dynasty; the seventh, Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson (1902–1994), transformed Chabad after the Holocaust into a global outreach movement from its headquarters in Crown Heights, Brooklyn.

Activities

Chabad is known for its network of emissaries (shluchim) who operate thousands of Chabad Houses, synagogues, schools, camps and community centres in more than 100 countries, offering Jewish education and services to Jews of all backgrounds. Its mitzvah campaigns, public menorah lightings and the tefillin campaign are prominent features of contemporary Jewish life.
