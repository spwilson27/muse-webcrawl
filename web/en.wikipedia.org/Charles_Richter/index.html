Charles Richter

Charles Francis Richter (April 26, 1900 – September 30, 1985) was an American seismologist who, with Beno Gutenberg, developed the Richter scale for measuring earthquake magnitude, introduced in 1935.

Career

Richter was born in Ohio and educated at Stanford University and the California Institute of Technology, where he earned his doctorate in theoretical physics in 1928 and spent nearly his whole career. Working at the Carnegie Institution's seismological laboratory in Pasadena and later as a Caltech professor, he catalogued Southern California earthquakes and sought a consistent way to compare their sizes.

The Richter scale

Building on an idea by Kiyoo Wadati, Richter defined magnitude logarithmically from the maximum amplitude recorded on a standard Wood–Anderson seismograph, corrected for distance from the epicentre. Each whole-number increase corresponds to a tenfold increase in measured amplitude and roughly 31.6 times more energy released. With Gutenberg he extended the method to earthquakes worldwide and published the standard work Seismicity of the Earth (1949; revised 1954). The Richter scale has since been largely superseded by the moment magnitude scale for large earthquakes, but Richter's name remains popularly attached to earthquake magnitudes.

Other interests

Richter campaigned for earthquake-resistant building codes in California. He was also known for translating and writing poetry, and for his naturist lifestyle. He died in Pasadena in 1985.
