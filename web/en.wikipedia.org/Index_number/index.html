Index number

An index number is a statistical measure designed to show changes in a variable, or a group of related variables, over time or between places. The best-known examples are price indices such as the consumer price index, which summarises the movement of the prices of a "basket" of goods and services relative to a chosen base period, conventionally set to 100.

Construction

Constructing an index involves selecting the items to be included, choosing weights reflecting their relative importance, and selecting a formula. Classical formulas include the Laspeyres index (base-period weights), the Paasche index (current-period weights) and the Fisher index, the geometric mean of the two, which Irving Fisher called "ideal" because it satisfies several desirable tests. Statistical agencies frequently use chained indices, updating weights periodically to limit substitution bias.

Uses

Index numbers are used to measure inflation, deflate nominal values into real terms, index wages, pensions and contracts, and compare economic output, trade and productivity between periods and countries.
