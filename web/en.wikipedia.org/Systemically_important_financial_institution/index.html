Systemically important financial institution

A systemically important financial institution (SIFI) is a bank, insurance company, or other financial institution whose failure might trigger a financial crisis. Such institutions are informally described as "too big to fail".

Regulation

Following the 2008 financial crisis, the Financial Stability Board began publishing lists of global systemically important banks (G-SIBs), which face higher capital surcharges, enhanced supervision, and resolution planning requirements.

Categories

Regulators distinguish global systemically important banks, domestic systemically important banks (D-SIBs), and systemically important insurers, each designated on the basis of size, interconnectedness, complexity, and cross-border activity.

Criticism

Critics argue designation implies an implicit government guarantee, creating moral hazard, while supporters contend the additional capital and supervision reduce the likelihood and impact of failure.
