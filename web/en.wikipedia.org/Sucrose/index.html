Sucrose

FormulaC12H22O11
TypeDisaccharide (glucose + fructose)
AppearanceWhite crystalline solid
SourcesSugarcane, sugar beet

Sucrose is common sugar — the white crystalline substance used worldwide as a sweetener. It is a disaccharide composed of the monosaccharides glucose and fructose joined by a glycosidic bond, with molecular formula C12H22O11. Plants make it as a transport and storage form of energy; it is extracted commercially from sugarcane (about 70% of world production) and sugar beet (about 30%).

Properties and digestion

Sucrose is highly soluble in water and melts and caramelises on heating, decomposing around 186 °C. In the small intestine the enzyme sucrase splits it into glucose and fructose, which are absorbed into the blood. Because it yields glucose rapidly, sucrose has a glycaemic index of about 65. Heated with acid or enzymes it "inverts" into invert sugar, a sweeter, non-crystallising syrup used in confectionery.

Production and use

World production is roughly 180 million tonnes per year; the largest producers include Brazil, India, the European Union, China and Thailand. Beyond table sugar, sucrose is used in baking, beverages, preserves and fermentation (for ethanol and rum), and as a feedstock in some industrial processes.

Health

Excessive consumption is linked to tooth decay — oral bacteria ferment sucrose to acids — and contributes to obesity and type 2 diabetes risk through excess energy intake. The World Health Organization recommends limiting free sugars to less than 10% of daily energy intake. People with rare sucrase-isomaltase deficiency cannot digest sucrose properly.
