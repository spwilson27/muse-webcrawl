Fireball (meteor)

A fireball crossing the night sky.

A fireball is an unusually bright meteor, one that reaches a visual apparent magnitude of −4 or brighter — at least as bright as the planet Venus in the morning or evening sky. The International Astronomical Union defines a fireball as "a meteor brighter than any of the planets" (magnitude −4 or greater). Still brighter events, reaching magnitude −14 or brighter, are called bolides, and an exploding bolide is sometimes termed a superbolide.

Characteristics

Fireballs are produced by meteoroids, typically from a few centimetres to a metre or more across, entering the Earth's atmosphere at speeds of 11 to 72 km/s. Friction with the air heats and ablates the object's surface, producing a brilliant trail of ionised gas. Spectacular fireballs may fragment, flare, cast shadows, and leave persistent trains, and the largest events can generate audible sonic booms.

Fireballs occur daily over the whole Earth, though most fall over oceans or uninhabited areas and are never reported. Networks of cameras, such as those operated by fireball networks and space agencies, track bright events to estimate trajectories and, occasionally, to recover meteorites — as in the case of meteorites recovered after tracked falls.

Notable events

The Chelyabinsk meteor of 15 February 2013 was a superbolide with an estimated energy of about 500 kilotons of TNT; its airburst injured over a thousand people, mostly from broken glass. The Tunguska event of 1908, which flattened some 2,000 km² of Siberian forest, is believed to have been the airburst of a much larger object.
