Hugo Rafael Chávez Frías (28 July 1954 – 5 March 2013) was a Venezuelan politician and military officer who was the president of Venezuela from 1999 until his death in 2013, previously serving as the leader of the Fifth Republic Movement political party from its foundation in 1997 until 2007, when it merged with several other parties to form the United Socialist Party of Venezuela (PSUV), which he led until 2012. Following his own political ideology of Bolivarianism and "socialism of the 21st century", he focused on implementing socialist reforms in the country as a part of a social project known as the Bolivarian Revolution.

Early life and military career

Born into a lower-middle-class family in Sabaneta, Barinas, Chávez became a career officer in the Venezuelan Army, graduating from the Venezuelan Academy of Military Sciences in 1975. He founded the clandestine Revolutionary Bolivarian Movement-200 (MBR-200) in the early 1980s and led its failed coup d'état against the Democratic Action government of President Carlos Andrés Pérez in 1992, for which he was imprisoned. Pardoned in 1994, he founded the Fifth Republic Movement and was elected president in 1998 with 56.2% of the vote.

Presidency

Chávez was re-elected in 2000, 2006 and 2012. His administration oversaw a new constitution in 1999, the Bolivarian Missions social programmes, nationalisations funded by oil revenues, and opposition to United States foreign policy; he survived a coup attempt in 2002 and a recall referendum in 2004. He promoted Latin American integration through alliances such as ALBA and Petrocaribe.

Death and legacy

After a two-year battle with cancer, Chávez died in Caracas on 5 March 2013. His supporters viewed him as a champion of the poor, while critics blamed his policies for Venezuela's economic distortions. He was succeeded by his vice president, Nicolás Maduro.
