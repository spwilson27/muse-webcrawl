Punch line

Punch line
Field: comedy, joke structure
Preceded by: set-up
Mechanism: incongruity, surprise, reinterpretation
Related: tag, callback, rule of three

A punch line (also punch-line or punchline) is the final part of a joke, usually the word, sentence or exchange of sentences which triggers the listener's laughter or realisation. It typically follows a set-up that establishes a situation and an expectation, which the punch line then subverts.

Structure

In the classic two-part joke, the set-up creates a plausible framework and the punch line forces the audience to reinterpret that framework in a second, usually absurd, sense. This is the basis of incongruity theory in humour research: laughter arises from the sudden resolution of an unexpected mismatch. Comedians refer to the words that carry the joke as the "punch words", which are normally placed at the very end of the line so that no following material dilutes the laugh.

Variants

Variants on the basic form include the tag (an additional joke appended to a punch line without a new set-up), the callback (a punch line that refers back to an earlier joke), and anti-humour jokes in which the expected punch line is replaced by a deliberately mundane statement. In some forms — shaggy-dog stories — the joke consists precisely in the punch line's weakness after a long build-up.

History of the term

The expression is American, recorded from the early twentieth century in the context of comic songs and burlesque, and derives from the notion of a line that delivers the "punch" — denoting strength or impact — of the act.
