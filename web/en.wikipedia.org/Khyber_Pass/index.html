Khyber Pass

Khyber Pass
Elevation: 1,070 m (3,510 ft)
Length: about 53 km (33 mi)
Location: Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan, to Nangarhar, Afghanistan

The Khyber Pass (Pashto: د خیبر دره) is a mountain pass in the Spin Ghar mountains connecting Peshawar in Pakistan with Jalalabad in Afghanistan. Historically the most important route between Central Asia and the Indian subcontinent, it formed a branch of the Silk Road and has been traversed by traders, pilgrims and invading armies for more than two thousand years.

History

Persian, Greek, Turkic and Mughal forces all entered India through or near the Khyber. Alexander the Great sent part of his army through the pass in 326 BC, and later conquerors including Mahmud of Ghazni, Babur and Nadir Shah used the route. During the 19th century it was a central theatre of the "Great Game" between Britain and Russia, guarded by the Khyber Rifles, a locally recruited militia. The pass featured in all three Anglo-Afghan Wars.

Geography and route

The modern road and the now-disused Khyber Pass Railway climb from Jamrud, near Peshawar, past the Shagai and Ali Masjid forts to Landi Kotal and the Torkham border crossing. The monumental Bab-e-Khyber ("Khyber Gate") arch was built in 1964. The surrounding tribal areas were administered as part of Pakistan's Federally Administered Tribal Areas until their 2018 merger into Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province.
