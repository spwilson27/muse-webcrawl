Leonhard Seppala

Leonhard Seppala (1877–1967) was a Norwegian-American musher who became famous in the 1925 serum run to Nome, Alaska, when dog-sled teams relayed diphtheria antitoxin across the territory in a blizzard.

Serum run

Seppala drove the longest and most dangerous leg of the relay — nearly 400 kilometres out and back across Norton Sound sea ice — with his lead dog Togo. Although Gunnar Kaasen and Balto ran the final leg and got the fame, Seppala and Togo covered the critical stretch, and Togo is now widely credited as the run true hero dog.

Legacy

Seppala popularised the Siberian Husky in North America, racing successfully in New England and helping establish the breed. He spent his last years in Seattle.
