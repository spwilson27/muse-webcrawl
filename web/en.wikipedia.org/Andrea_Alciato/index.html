Andrea Alciato

Born8 May 1492, Alzate Brianza, Duchy of Milan
Died12 January 1550 (aged 57), Pavia
OccupationJurist, humanist, writer

Andrea Alciato (8 May 1492 – 12 January 1550), commonly known as Alciati (Andreas Alciatus), was an Italian jurist and writer, regarded as the founder of the French school of legal humanism and celebrated as the originator of the emblem book.

Life and legal career

Alciato studied law at Pavia and Bologna and taught at Avignon, Bourges, Pavia, Bologna and Ferrara. His lectures applied humanist philological methods to Roman law, challenging the medieval commentators and helping to establish the mos gallicus tradition of jurisprudence.

The Emblemata

Alciato's Emblematum liber, first published in Augsburg in 1531, collected short Latin epigrams each paired (in later editions) with a symbolic woodcut image and a motto. The book launched the immensely popular genre of the emblem book, which shaped European art, literature and visual culture for two centuries, with hundreds of editions and imitations.

Legacy

Alciato's legal writings and his emblems alike reflect Renaissance humanism's fusion of classical learning, jurisprudence and imagery. His name is commemorated in the plant genus Alchemilla's relatives and in scholarship on iconography.
