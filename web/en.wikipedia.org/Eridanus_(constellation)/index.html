Eridanus (constellation)

Eridanus
Latin: Eridanus ("the River")
Area: 1,138 sq. deg. (6th largest)
Brightest star: Achernar (α Eri)
Eridanus, the celestial river, winds south from Orion to Achernar

Eridanus is a constellation in the southern celestial hemisphere. It is represented as a river and is one of the 48 constellations listed by the 2nd-century astronomer Ptolemy, and remains one of the 88 modern constellations. It is the sixth largest of the modern constellations, winding from the foot of Orion far southward.

Notable stars

Its brightest star is Achernar (Alpha Eridani), a hot blue star whose rapid rotation makes it one of the least spherical stars known; its name means "End of the River" in Arabic. Other notable stars include Epsilon Eridani, one of the nearest Sun-like stars and a target in the search for exoplanets, and 40 Eridani (Omicron² Eridani), a triple system containing one of the first discovered white dwarfs.

Mythology

In Greek mythology Eridanus was identified with the river into which Phaethon, son of Helios, fell after losing control of the Sun's chariot. The constellation was also associated by various cultures with earthly rivers including the Po, the Nile, and the Euphrates.

Deep-sky objects and features

Eridanus contains the galaxy NGC 1300, a prominent barred spiral, and the Witch Head Nebula. A large, unusually cold region of the cosmic microwave background, the CMB cold spot, lies in the direction of Eridanus and has been associated with the putative Eridanus Supervoid, one of the largest known voids in the universe.
