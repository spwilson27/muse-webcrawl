The Alaska Volcano Observatory (AVO) is a joint program of the United States Geological Survey, the Alaska Division of Geological & Geophysical Surveys, and the Geophysical Institute of the University of Alaska Fairbanks. It was founded in 1988 to monitor Alaska's volcanoes and issue warnings of eruptions.

Monitoring

AVO monitors more than 50 historically active volcanoes along the Aleutian Arc, much of it beneath heavily traveled North Pacific air routes where volcanic ash poses a severe hazard to jet aircraft. Instruments include seismic networks, GPS deformation stations, web cameras, and satellite remote sensing.

Notable eruptions

The observatory was created following the 1989–1990 eruption of Mount Redoubt, during which a KLM airliner encountered an ash cloud and temporarily lost all engines. AVO has since tracked eruptions at Augustine, Redoubt, Kasatochi, Pavlof, and Bogoslof, issuing aviation color codes and alert levels.
