Intention in English law

Intention in English law refers to the mental state with which a person acts, and is a central element in both criminal law and the law of obligations. In criminal law, intention is usually the highest form of mens rea, distinguishing the deliberate wrongdoer from one who is merely reckless or negligent.

Criminal law

English courts distinguish direct intention, where the defendant's purpose is to bring about a result, from oblique intention, where the result is a virtually certain consequence of the defendant's actions and the defendant appreciates that. The leading authority is R v Woollin (1999), which refined the test first developed in R v Nedrick.

Intention to create legal relations

In contract law, an agreement is enforceable only if the parties intended to create legal relations. Commercial agreements carry a presumption of such intention, while domestic and social arrangements — as in Balfour v Balfour (1919) — are presumed not to.
