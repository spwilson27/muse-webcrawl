Knot

Knot
PurposeFastening, joining, securing rope or cord
Related termsBend, hitch, lashing, splice
StudyKnot theory (mathematics)

A knot is an intentional complication in cordage, which may be practical, decorative or both. Knots are essential in sailing, climbing, fishing, surgery, camping and countless crafts, and have been used since prehistory — knotted cords served as records, such as the Inca quipu.

Categories

Strictly, a knot ties a rope to itself, a bend joins two ropes, and a hitch attaches a rope to another object such as a post or ring. Common knots include the reef (square) knot, bowline, figure-eight knot, sheet bend, clove hitch and fisherman's knot. Knots reduce rope strength by roughly 25–50 per cent depending on type.

Properties

A good practical knot is secure under load, easy to tie and inspect, and releasable when required. Some knots jam after loading; others, like the bowline, remain easy to untie. Friction, rope material and dressing (neat arrangement of the parts) all affect performance.

History and culture

Knots appear in ancient Egyptian, Greek and Chinese art and technology. The Gordian knot legend symbolises an intractable problem. Decorative knotwork features in Celtic and Chinese art, macramé and maritime crafts. The mathematics of knots developed into knot theory in the 19th and 20th centuries.

Safety

In life-critical uses such as climbing and rescue, knot choice, correct tying and backup knots are matters of formal training and standards.
