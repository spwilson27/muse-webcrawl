Vickrey–Clarke–Groves auction

VCG auction
Type: sealed-bid, multi-item auction
Property: truthful bidding is a dominant strategy

A Vickrey–Clarke–Groves (VCG) auction is a multi-item auction in which bidders submit sealed bids for bundles of goods, the allocation maximises total reported value, and each winner pays the opportunity cost their presence imposes on the other bidders. It is the auction form of the more general VCG mechanism.

How it works

Suppose several items are for sale. Each bidder states a value for every bundle they might receive. The auctioneer picks the value-maximising assignment of items. A winner who receives a bundle pays the difference between the best total value the others could have achieved without them and what the others actually achieve with the winner present. In the single-item case this reduces to the familiar second-price (Vickrey) auction: the winner pays the second-highest bid.

Properties

Bidding one's true values is a dominant strategy, and the outcome is efficient. However, VCG auctions can yield low revenue, are not collusion-proof, and computing the optimal allocation for arbitrary bundle bids is NP-hard, so practical implementations restrict the bidding language.
