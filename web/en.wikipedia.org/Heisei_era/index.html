Heisei era

The Heisei era (Japanese: 平成, "achieving peace") is the period of Japanese history corresponding to the reign of Emperor Akihito, from 8 January 1989 — the day after the death of Emperor Hirohito — until his abdication on 30 April 2019. It was preceded by the Shōwa era and followed by the Reiwa era of Emperor Naruhito. Under Japan's era-name (nengō) system, 1989 is Heisei 1 and 2019 is Heisei 31.

Economy and society

The era opened with the collapse of Japan's asset price bubble, ushering in the prolonged economic stagnation known as the "Lost Decades". Japan nevertheless remained a leading technological and cultural power: the period saw the worldwide spread of Japanese popular culture — anime, manga, and video games — alongside demographic ageing and a shrinking population. Politically, the long dominance of the Liberal Democratic Party was twice interrupted, in 1993–94 and 2009–2012.

Disasters

Heisei Japan endured major disasters: the Great Hanshin earthquake of 1995, which devastated Kobe, and the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami, which triggered the Fukushima Daiichi nuclear disaster. The era also saw the 1995 Tokyo subway sarin attack by the Aum Shinrikyo cult. Akihito's unprecedented televised address and the 2017 abdication legislation led to the first imperial abdication in about two centuries, closing the era on 30 April 2019.
