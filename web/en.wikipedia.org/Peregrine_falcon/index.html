Peregrine falcon

Scientific name: Falco peregrinus (Tunstall, 1771)
Family: Falconidae
Order: Falconiformes
Wingspan: 74–120 cm (29–47 in)
Conservation status: Least Concern (IUCN)

The peregrine falcon (Falco peregrinus), historically known as the duck hawk in North America, is a cosmopolitan bird of prey in the family Falconidae. A large, crow-sized falcon, it has a blue-grey back, barred white underparts, and a black head. The peregrine is renowned for its speed: it can reach over 320 km/h (200 mph) during its characteristic hunting stoop (high-speed dive), making it the fastest animal on the planet.

Description

The peregrine falcon has a body length of 34 to 58 cm (13–23 in) and a wingspan from 74 to 120 cm (29–47 in). Males and females have similar markings and plumage, but as in many birds of prey the female is up to 30% larger than the male. Adults have steel-blue upperparts, pale barred underparts, a black "moustache" contrasting with a white throat, and yellow feet, cere and eye-rings.

Distribution and habitat

The peregrine is one of the most widespread birds, found on every continent except Antarctica, breeding in habitats from Arctic tundra to tropical coasts. It nests typically on cliff edges in a scrape, but has adapted readily to cities, nesting on tall buildings and bridges and hunting feral pigeons.

Diet and hunting

Peregrines feed almost exclusively on medium-sized birds, caught in flight. The hunter climbs high, then stoops at extreme speed, striking its prey with a clenched foot and often killing it on impact. Doves, pigeons, shorebirds, ducks and songbirds dominate the diet.

Conservation

Peregrine populations collapsed in the mid-20th century because the pesticide DDT accumulated in prey and caused eggshell thinning. Following bans on DDT and intensive captive-breeding and reintroduction programmes, the species recovered strongly and was removed from the US endangered species list in 1999. It has been used in falconry for more than 3,000 years.
