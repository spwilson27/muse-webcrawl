Real estate bubble

House prices diverging from rents and incomes in a bubble

A real estate bubble or housing bubble is a type of economic bubble that occurs periodically in local, national or global real estate markets, typically following a land boom. It is characterised by rapid increases in the market price of real property, such as housing, until prices reach unsustainable levels relative to incomes and rents, followed by declining prices and, frequently, financial distress for owners and lenders.

Causes and identification

Housing bubbles are driven by combinations of low interest rates, easy credit, speculative buying in expectation of further price rises, and beliefs that property is a safe investment. Economists identify possible bubbles using ratios such as price-to-income and price-to-rent compared with their long-run trends, though in real time it is often difficult to distinguish a bubble from prices justified by fundamentals.

Consequences

When housing bubbles burst, falling prices leave borrowers with negative equity, raise mortgage defaults, and can trigger banking crises because property loans form a large share of bank assets. Examples include the Japanese asset price bubble that burst in the early 1990s, the United States housing bubble whose collapse in 2006–2008 precipitated the global financial crisis of 2007–2008, and the Irish and Spanish property bubbles of the 2000s.
