Universal Soil Loss Equation

The Universal Soil Loss Equation (USLE) is a widely used mathematical model that describes soil erosion processes. It was developed by the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA) based on erosion plot data collected since the 1930s, and published in Agriculture Handbook 282 in 1965 (updated as Handbook 537 in 1978).

The equation

The USLE estimates the average annual rate of erosion on a field slope as:

A = R · K · L · S · C · P

  • A – computed soil loss per unit area, usually in tons per acre per year
  • R – rainfall and runoff erosivity factor
  • K – soil erodibility factor
  • L – slope length factor
  • S – slope steepness factor
  • C – cover and management factor
  • P – support practice factor (e.g. contouring, terracing)

Development and use

The model grew out of decades of rainfall-simulation and runoff-plot experiments conducted by the USDA's Agricultural Research Service, with W. H. Wischmeier and D. D. Smith as principal authors. It became a standard tool for conservation planning on farms, for predicting sediment delivery, and for land-management policy worldwide.

Successors

The Revised Universal Soil Loss Equation (RUSLE, and later RUSLE2) computerized the model, refined its factors with additional data, and remains in wide use. The USLE predicts only rill and interrill (sheet) erosion; it does not account for gully erosion, wind erosion, or mass wasting, and it estimates long-term averages rather than single-storm losses.
