Oliver Otis Howard

Oliver Otis Howard
Born: 8 November 1830, Leeds, Maine
Died: 26 October 1909, Burlington, Vermont
Allegiance: United States (Union)
Rank: Major general
Commands: XI Corps, Army of the Tennessee

Oliver Otis Howard (November 8, 1830 – October 26, 1909) was a career United States Army officer and Union general in the American Civil War. Known as the "Christian General" for his deep religious devotion, he lost his right arm at the Battle of Seven Pines and later headed the Freedmen's Bureau during Reconstruction.

Civil War

A West Point graduate (class of 1854), Howard commanded a brigade at First Bull Run and a division at Antietam. Given command of the XI Corps, he was surprised and routed by Stonewall Jackson's flank attack at Chancellorsville in May 1863, and his corps was again driven back on the first day at Gettysburg, though he had wisely secured Cemetery Hill as a fallback position. Transferring west, he succeeded William T. Sherman in command of the Army of the Tennessee for the March to the Sea and the Carolinas Campaign. He received the Medal of Honor in 1893 for his actions at Seven Pines.

Reconstruction and later service

In 1865 President Andrew Johnson appointed Howard commissioner of the Bureau of Refugees, Freedmen, and Abandoned Lands. He worked to provide education and legal protection to formerly enslaved people, though the bureau's land-redistribution hopes were thwarted. He was a founder of Howard University in Washington, D.C. (chartered 1867), serving as its president from 1869 to 1874, and later helped found Lincoln Memorial University in Tennessee.

In the Indian Wars, Howard conducted the 1877 pursuit of the Nez Perce under Chief Joseph across the Northwest. He later served as superintendent of West Point (1881–1882) and commanded the Department of the East before retiring in 1894.
