Advanced Audio Coding


Illustration: Advanced Audio Coding

Advanced Audio Coding (AAC) is an audio coding standard for lossy digital audio compression. Designed to be the successor of the MP3 format, AAC generally achieves higher sound quality than MP3 encoders at the same bit rate. It was standardized by ISO and IEC as part of the MPEG-2 and MPEG-4 specifications.

History

AAC was developed with the cooperation of companies including AT&T Bell Laboratories, Dolby, Sony and Nokia. It was officially declared an international standard by the Moving Picture Experts Group in April 1997. Apple adopted AAC as the default format for the iTunes Store, and it later became the standard audio format for YouTube, streaming services and digital radio standards such as DAB+.

Technology

AAC uses a modified discrete cosine transform (MDCT) with perceptual noise shaping, temporal noise shaping and intensity stereo coding. Profiles include AAC-LC (Low Complexity), HE-AAC (High Efficiency, using spectral band replication) and HE-AAC v2 (adding parametric stereo). AAC supports up to 48 full-bandwidth audio channels and sampling rates from 8 to 96 kHz.
