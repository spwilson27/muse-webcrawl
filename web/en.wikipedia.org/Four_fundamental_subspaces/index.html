In linear algebra, the four fundamental subspaces of a matrix are the four vector spaces associated with it: the column space, the null space (kernel), the row space, and the left null space (the null space of the transpose). They form the core of the fundamental theorem of linear algebra, a unifying description popularized by Gilbert Strang.

Definitions

For an m × n matrix A of rank r: the column space C(A) lies in m-dimensional space and has dimension r; the null space N(A) lies in n-dimensional space and has dimension n − r; the row space C(AT) lies in n-dimensional space with dimension r; and the left null space N(AT) lies in m-dimensional space with dimension m − r.

Orthogonality

The row space and null space are orthogonal complements in n-dimensional space, and the column space and left null space are orthogonal complements in m-dimensional space. This structure explains the conditions for the solvability of Ax = b and underlies least-squares approximation and the singular value decomposition.
