Magnetic dipole

A magnetic dipole is the limit of either a closed loop of electric current or a pair of magnetic poles as the size of the source is reduced to zero while keeping the magnetic moment constant. It is a magnetic analogue of the electric dipole. No isolated magnetic pole (magnetic monopole) has been observed, so the dipole is the simplest and most common type of magnetic field source.

Field of a dipole

At distances large compared with the size of the source, the magnetic field of a dipole with moment m at position r falls off as 1/r³ and has the characteristic form proportional to (3(m·r̂)r̂ − m)/r³. The same pattern describes a bar magnet, a current loop, and the field of the Earth to first approximation.

Dipoles in nature

Elementary particles such as the electron carry intrinsic magnetic dipole moments associated with their spin, and many atomic nuclei have dipole moments that underlie nuclear magnetic resonance and magnetic resonance imaging. Anomalies in the measured dipole moments of particles are a sensitive test of the Standard Model of particle physics.
