Spontaneous parametric down-conversion

Spontaneous parametric down-conversion (also known as SPDC, parametric fluorescence or parametric scattering) is a nonlinear optical process that converts a photon of high energy (namely, a pump photon) into a pair of photons (namely, a signal photon and an idler photon) of lower energy, in accordance with the law of conservation of energy and law of conservation of momentum. It is an important process in quantum optics, for the generation of entangled photon pairs, and of single photons.

Process

A nonlinear crystal, commonly beta barium borate (BBO) or potassium titanyl phosphate (KTP), splits incoming photons. Conservation requirements are expressed as ωp = ωs + ωi and a corresponding phase-matching condition on the wavevectors. In type-I down-conversion the pair have the same polarisation; in type-II they are orthogonal, which allows the production of polarisation-entangled states.

Applications

SPDC sources are used in quantum key distribution, quantum computing experiments, tests of Bell inequalities, and heralded single-photon sources, where detection of one photon of a pair announces the presence of the other.
