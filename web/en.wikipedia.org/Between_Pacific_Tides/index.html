Between Pacific Tides

Between Pacific Tides is a 1939 book by marine biologist Edward F. Ricketts and Jack Calvin describing the intertidal ecology and marine invertebrates of the Pacific coast of North America. Organised by habitat and shore type rather than by taxonomy, it was a pioneering work of ecological natural history and remains a classic of marine biology writing.

Content and approach

Instead of arranging species taxonomically, Ricketts organised the book by environment — rocky shores, sandy beaches, mud flats, wharf pilings and kelp beds — and by the zonation of organisms between tide levels. This community-based approach, influenced by his philosophical collaboration with Joseph Campbell and John Steinbeck, anticipated the ecosystem emphasis of later ecology. The first edition described several hundred species, and successive editions, revised after Ricketts's death by Joel W. Hedgpeth, expanded the work substantially.

Legacy

The book helped popularise tide-pooling on the Pacific coast and shaped generations of marine biologists. Ricketts's laboratory on Cannery Row in Monterey and his collecting work also formed the background to Steinbeck's novels Cannery Row and The Log from the Sea of Cortez. Stanford University Press has kept the book in print through multiple editions.
