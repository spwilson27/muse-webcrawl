Culture of South Korea

The contemporary culture of South Korea developed from the traditional culture of Korea, which has been shared by the Korean Peninsula for millennia but diverged from North Korean culture since the division of Korea in 1945. South Korean culture has been shaped by Confucian values of hierarchy, filial piety, and education, by Buddhism and shamanic traditions, and by rapid industrialization and urbanization since the 1960s.

Korean Wave

Since the late 1990s, South Korean popular culture has achieved global prominence through the Korean Wave (Hallyu), encompassing K-pop music — with groups such as BTS and Blackpink — television dramas, and films such as Bong Joon-ho's Parasite (2019), the first non-English-language film to win the Academy Award for Best Picture. The television series Squid Game (2021) became one of the most-watched programs on Netflix. The Korean Wave has boosted tourism, exports of cosmetics (K-beauty), and interest in the Korean language worldwide.

Traditional culture

Traditional elements include the hanbok garment, worn on holidays such as Seollal (Lunar New Year) and Chuseok (the autumn harvest festival), when families honor ancestors in the charye rite. Korean cuisine centers on rice, soups, and fermented side dishes, most famously kimchi, whose preparation tradition (kimjang) is recognized by UNESCO. Other hallmarks include taekwondo, traditional music (gugak), celadon pottery, and the ondol underfloor heating system found in traditional hanok houses.

Modern society

South Korea is one of the world's most technologically connected societies, with leading electronics and shipbuilding industries, near-universal broadband, and a large gaming and esports culture centered on PC bangs (gaming cafés). At the same time, demographic pressures — including the world's lowest fertility rate — and debates over gender, work culture, and mandatory military service shape contemporary cultural discourse.
