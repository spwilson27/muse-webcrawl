Marrakesh Agreement

The Marrakesh Agreement, officially the Marrakesh Agreement Establishing the World Trade Organization, is an international treaty signed in Marrakesh, Morocco, on April 15, 1994. It concluded the Uruguay Round of multilateral trade negotiations and established the World Trade Organization (WTO), which came into existence on January 1, 1995.

The signing of the Marrakesh Agreement in 1994 concluded the Uruguay Round and created the WTO.

Background

The Uruguay Round, launched in Punta del Este in 1986 under the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade (GATT), was the most ambitious round of trade negotiations yet attempted. It extended multilateral trade rules beyond goods into services and intellectual property, tackled agriculture and textiles, and addressed weaknesses in GATT's dispute-settlement procedures.

Contents

The Marrakesh Agreement is the umbrella treaty of the WTO system. Its annexes contain the substantive agreements adopted by all WTO members as a "single undertaking", including:

  • The General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade 1994 (GATT 1994)
  • The General Agreement on Trade in Services (GATS)
  • The Agreement on Trade-Related Aspects of Intellectual Property Rights (TRIPS)
  • The Agreement on Agriculture and other goods agreements
  • The Understanding on dispute settlement (DSU)
  • The Trade Policy Review Mechanism

The agreement also defines the structure of the WTO, including its Ministerial Conference, General Council, and Secretariat, and provides that the WTO is the common institutional framework for the conduct of trade relations among its members.

Significance

By replacing the provisional GATT arrangements with a permanent international organization with binding dispute settlement, the Marrakesh Agreement marked a major step toward a rule-based multilateral trading system. The WTO it created began with 76 members and grew to more than 160.
