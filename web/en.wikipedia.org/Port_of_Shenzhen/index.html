The Port of Shenzhen is a collective name for a number of ports along the coastline of Shenzhen, Guangdong Province, China. It is one of the busiest and fastest growing container ports in the world, consistently ranking among the top five container ports globally by throughput, alongside Shanghai, Singapore, and Ningbo-Zhoushan.

Geography and terminals

The port facilities are spread along Shenzhen's roughly 260 kilometres of coastline on both sides of the Pearl River estuary. The western port area includes the terminals at Shekou, Chiwan, Mawan, and Dachan Bay, which face the Pearl River Delta, while the eastern port area is centred on Yantian, on Mirs Bay (Dapeng Bay), with additional facilities at Longgang. The Yantian International Container Terminals are among the largest single container terminals in the world, known for deep-water berths capable of handling the largest container vessels afloat.

History

Before the designation of Shenzhen as a Special Economic Zone in 1980, the area had only small fishing wharves. Port construction accompanied the explosive growth of manufacturing in the Pearl River Delta. Shekou was developed beginning in the late 1970s and 1980s, and Yantian's deep-water terminals opened in the 1990s. Throughput passed 20 million TEU (twenty-foot equivalent units) in the mid-2000s and exceeded 30 million TEU in the early 2020s.

Operations

The port serves as a principal gateway for exports from the manufacturing heartland of southern China, handling electronics, machinery, textiles, and consumer goods. Major terminal operators include Hutchison Ports (at Yantian), China Merchants Port, and Shenzhen Yantian Port Group. The port competes and cooperates with the neighbouring ports of Hong Kong and Guangzhou (Nansha).
