Mirrorless camera

A mirrorless camera (formally a mirrorless interchangeable-lens camera, MILC) is a digital camera that, unlike a digital single-lens reflex camera (DSLR), has no reflex mirror or optical viewfinder assembly. Instead, light passes through the lens directly onto the image sensor, and the photographer composes using an electronic viewfinder (EVF) or the rear LCD screen. Removing the mirror allows smaller, lighter bodies and shorter flange focal distances, enabling more compact lens designs.

History

The category began with the Micro Four Thirds system: the Panasonic Lumix G1 of 2008 was the first mirrorless interchangeable-lens camera, followed by the Olympus PEN E-P1 (2009). Sony's NEX series (2010) brought APS-C sensors, and the Sony α7 (2013) was the first full-frame mirrorless camera. Canon and Nikon, long dominant in DSLRs, launched full-frame mirrorless systems (EOS R and Nikon Z) in 2018; by the early 2020s mirrorless shipments surpassed DSLRs, and both manufacturers wound down DSLR development.

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages include compact size, silent electronic shutters, in-body image stabilisation, real-time exposure preview, fast burst rates without mirror blackout, and sophisticated on-sensor autofocus with eye and subject tracking. Drawbacks have included shorter battery life (due to the constantly powered sensor and EVF), viewfinder lag or blackout in early models, and smaller initial lens catalogues compared with mature DSLR systems.

Market

Mirrorless designs now dominate the interchangeable-lens camera market, spanning entry-level vlogging models to professional sports cameras and medium-format systems such as Fujifilm's GFX line.
