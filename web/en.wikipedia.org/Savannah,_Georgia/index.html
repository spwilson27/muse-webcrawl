Savannah is a city in the US state of Georgia and the county seat of Chatham County. Founded in 1733 by General James Oglethorpe, it was the first capital of the colonial Province of Georgia and is renowned for its planned grid of public squares, cobblestone streets and one of the largest historic districts in the United States.

History

Oglethorpe laid out the city in a distinctive ward pattern of residential lots around open squares, of which 22 survive. Savannah prospered as a cotton port; during the American Revolutionary War it was captured by the British in 1778, and in the Civil War it was presented to President Lincoln as a "Christmas gift" by General Sherman at the end of his March to the Sea in 1864, sparing it the destruction visited on other Southern cities.

Geography and economy

The city lies on the Savannah River near the Atlantic coast. The Port of Savannah is one of the busiest container ports in the United States, and tourism, manufacturing and the military are major employers. Savannah's architecture — Federal, Greek Revival, Gothic Revival and Victorian — together with its squares and cemeteries, draws millions of visitors annually.
