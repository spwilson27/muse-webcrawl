Retreat (spiritual)

A spiritual retreat is a period of withdrawal from ordinary routines for prayer, meditation, reflection, religious instruction, or renewal. Retreats may last a few hours, several days, or months and may be solitary or communal, silent or guided.

Christian traditions

Christian retreat practice draws on biblical accounts of withdrawal for prayer, desert monasticism, and later devotional movements. Ignatius of Loyola's Spiritual Exercises organise an extended retreat around meditation, self-examination, and discernment, traditionally lasting about thirty days but often adapted to daily life. Catholic, Orthodox, Anglican, and Protestant communities conduct retreats in monasteries, retreat houses, parishes, and schools.

Other traditions

Buddhist meditation retreats may involve intensive sitting and walking meditation, silence, interviews with teachers, and observance of precepts; some Tibetan practices include long secluded retreats. Hindu ashrams, Sufi practices, Jewish study and reflection programmes, and Indigenous ceremonial withdrawals have their own forms and purposes, not all appropriately described by a single model.

Structure

A retreat often removes distractions and imposes a simple timetable for waking, practice, meals, work, and sleep. Teachers may give talks or individual guidance. Silence can heighten attention but may also be psychologically demanding, so contemporary organisers increasingly provide screening, opt-outs, and support for intensive meditation.

Modern usage

Wellness, creative, corporate, and recovery retreats borrow the language of spiritual withdrawal even when their aims are secular. The commercial expansion of retreats has raised questions about cost, cultural appropriation, teacher authority, and the difference between a temporary restorative break and an established contemplative discipline.
