RS-232

RS-232, later formalised as TIA-232, is a standard originally introduced in 1960 for serial communication transmission of data between data terminal equipment (DTE), such as a computer terminal, and data circuit-terminating equipment (DCE), such as a modem. It defines electrical signalling characteristics, timing, and the physical connector, with signals carried over single-ended voltages typically between ±3 and ±15 volts relative to a common ground.

Characteristics

The standard supports asynchronous and synchronous communication at modest bit rates (originally up to 20 kbit/s over cable runs of about 15 metres; faster and longer links are possible in practice). Common connectors are the 25-pin and 9-pin D-subminiature. Signals include transmitted data (TxD), received data (RxD), and control lines for hardware flow control such as RTS/CTS and DTR/DSR, plus modem status lines.

Legacy

RS-232 serial ports were standard on personal computers from the 1980s until USB displaced them in the 2000s. The standard remains in use for console ports on network equipment, industrial automation, laboratory instruments, and legacy hardware. Related standards include RS-422 and RS-485, which use differential signalling for longer distances.
