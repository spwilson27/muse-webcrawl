Tabasco sauce

Tabasco is an American brand of hot sauce made from tabasco peppers, vinegar and salt. It is produced by the McIlhenny Company of Avery Island in southern Louisiana, founded by Edmund McIlhenny, who developed the recipe in 1868. The peppers are mashed with salt, aged for up to three years in oak barrels previously used for whiskey, then blended with vinegar and strained before bottling.

History

According to company tradition, Edmund McIlhenny, a Maryland-born banker who had moved to Louisiana, received tabasco pepper seeds from a traveller returning from Mexico or Central America around the time of the American Civil War. He planted them on Avery Island, which his wife's family owned and where salt was mined, and began commercial production in 1868, selling 658 bottles in the first year. The company remains family-owned, and Avery Island is still the site of production; some pepper seed production has moved to Latin America while the mash is shipped back to Louisiana.

Varieties and heat

The original red sauce measures between 2,500 and 5,000 Scoville heat units. The brand has expanded to include green jalapeño sauce, chipotle, habanero (the hottest standard variety), garlic, sweet and spicy, sriracha and scorpion pepper sauces. Tabasco is sold in more than 195 countries and territories and packaged in 36 languages and dialects.

Cultural presence

Tabasco has long been included in United States military rations, and miniature bottles accompany meals in the MRE. The sauce holds a Royal Warrant as a supplier to the British royal household. The brand name derives from a Mexican Indigenous word often glossed as "land where the soil is humid" or "flickering", after the Mexican state of Tabasco.
