Field ration

A field ration is a type of preprepared, ready-to-eat meal that is easily prepared and eaten in the field by soldiers who lack access to a field kitchen. Field rations are designed to be nutritionally balanced, shelf-stable, and robust enough to survive airdrops and rough handling.

History

Armies have issued preserved field food since antiquity, from Roman grain rations to the canned goods of the nineteenth century. The United States' K-ration and C-ration of the Second World War were followed by the Meal, Ready-to-Eat (MRE), introduced in 1981, each pouch containing an entrée, side dishes, snacks, and an accessory pack.

Modern examples

Many nations issue their own packs: the British 24-hour Operational Ration Pack, the French RCIR, and the Australian Combat Ration Pack. Typical contents supply roughly 3,600–4,000 kilocalories per day of operations.
