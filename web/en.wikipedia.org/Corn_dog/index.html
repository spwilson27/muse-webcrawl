Corn dog

A corn dog is a hot dog sausage coated in a thick layer of cornmeal batter and deep-fried, traditionally served on a wooden stick. It is a quintessential food of American state fairs, carnivals and boardwalks.

History

The origins of the corn dog are disputed. German immigrants in Texas in the 1920s are sometimes credited with early versions, and a United States patent for a combined cooking, frying and article-holding apparatus for battered foods on sticks was filed in 1927 and granted in 1929. The "Pronto Pup" — which uses a pancake-style batter rather than cornmeal — was demonstrated at the Minnesota State Fair in 1941, and the "Cozy Dog" was served in Springfield, Illinois, from 1946. The corn dog became a fixture of fairs nationwide in the postwar era and is celebrated on National Corn Dog Day, an informal holiday held in March.

Preparation

A frankfurter is dried, skewered, dipped in a thick, slightly sweet cornmeal batter, and deep-fried in oil until the coating is crisp and golden. Commercial frozen versions are widely sold in supermarkets and are reheated by baking or frying. Variations include mini "corn dog nuggets," cheese-filled versions, and similar battered items using other sausages.

Regional and international variants

In Argentina, a similar item is called a panchuker. In South Korea, hot dogs coated in batter — sometimes with french-fry cubes pressed into the coating or dusted with sugar — are popular street food known as hatdogeu.
