The Tennessee Valley Authority (TVA) is a federally owned electric utility corporation in the United States. Created by Congress in 1933 as part of President Franklin D. Roosevelt's New Deal, it was originally charged with providing navigation, flood control, electricity generation, and economic development in the Tennessee Valley, a region badly affected by the Great Depression.

History

TVA built a system of dams on the Tennessee River and its tributaries, bringing electricity, fertilizer, and flood control to a largely rural region. It became the nation's largest public power provider.

Operations

Today TVA supplies electricity to about 10 million people across Tennessee and parts of six surrounding states, operating fossil fuel, nuclear, hydroelectric, and renewable generating facilities. Its nuclear fleet includes the Sequoyah, Browns Ferry, and Watts Bar plants.
