Religious pluralism

Religious pluralism is an attitude or policy regarding the diversity of religious belief systems co-existing in society. It can describe the condition of religious diversity itself, legal frameworks granting equal rights to different faiths, or theological positions holding that multiple religions may contain truth.

Meanings

As a social condition, pluralism simply denotes the presence of many religions in one society. As a civic value, it implies tolerance, mutual respect, and equal treatment under law. In theology, pluralism — associated with thinkers such as John Hick — is distinguished from exclusivism (only one religion is true) and inclusivism (one religion is fullest, but others contain partial truth).

Practice

Pluralist arrangements range from state neutrality and disestablishment, as in the United States, to official recognition and support of several religions. Interfaith dialogue and shared civic institutions are often cited as practical expressions of pluralism.
