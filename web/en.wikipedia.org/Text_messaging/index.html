Text messaging

Text messaging, or texting, is the act of composing and sending electronic messages, typically consisting of alphabetic and numeric characters, between two or more users of mobile phones, tablet computers, smartwatches, desktops/laptops, or another type of compatible computer. Text messages may be sent over a cellular network or via an Internet connection. The term originally referred to messages sent using the Short Message Service (SMS) on GSM networks, with a limit of 160 characters per message.

History

The SMS concept was developed in the 1980s within the GSM standards process; Friedhelm Hillebrand and Bernard Ghillebaert are credited with key contributions. The first text message was sent on 3 December 1992 by British engineer Neil Papworth, who transmitted "Merry Christmas" to a mobile phone on the Vodafone network. Texting grew slowly in the 1990s — hampered by billing and interoperability issues — before exploding in the 2000s as prices fell and predictive text (such as T9) made typing easier. At its peak in the early 2010s, trillions of SMS messages were sent annually worldwide.

Language and culture

Constraints of character limits and keypad entry produced distinctive texting language: abbreviations ("u", "gr8", "LOL"), acronyms, and later emoji. Texting reshaped social communication, enabling asynchronous, low-cost exchanges, and became a major channel for alerts, marketing, two-factor authentication, and charitable donation campaigns.

Over-the-top services

From the early 2010s, internet-based ("over-the-top") messaging applications such as WhatsApp, iMessage, Facebook Messenger, WeChat, and Telegram displaced SMS in many markets, offering group chats, media sharing, and end-to-end encryption, though SMS remains universal on basic phones and widely used for authentication codes.
