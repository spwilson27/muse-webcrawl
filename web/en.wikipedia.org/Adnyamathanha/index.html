Adnyamathanha

The Adnyamathanha are an Aboriginal Australian people of the northern Flinders Ranges in South Australia. Their name derives from their language: adnya meaning "rock" and mathanha meaning "people" — "rock people". Their traditional lands encompass the rugged ranges around Nepabunna, Balcanoona, and the Vulkathunha-Gammon Ranges National Park.

Language and culture

The Adnyamathanha language is one of the Thura-Yura languages. Traditional culture is organised around two moieties, Ararru and Mathari, and a rich body of Dreaming (Ngarrnydjang) stories associated with features of the landscape, including the travels of ancestral beings such as Yurlu the kingfisher and the two Akurra (serpents).

History

European pastoral settlement from the 1850s brought conflict and disease; many Adnyamathanha worked on sheep stations. The Nepabunna Mission was established in 1931 and became a focal community. In 2016 the Federal Court recognised Adnyamathanha native title over much of the northern Flinders Ranges. The community played a prominent role in the successful campaign against a proposed national radioactive waste facility near Hawker in 2023.
