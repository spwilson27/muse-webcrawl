Ludwig van Beethoven (baptised 17 December 1770 – 26 March 1827) was a German composer and pianist. He is one of the most revered figures in the history of Western music; his works rank among the most performed of the classical music repertoire and span the transition from the Classical period to the Romantic era in classical music.

Life

Early life in Bonn

Beethoven was born in Bonn, capital of the Electorate of Cologne. His first music teacher was his father Johann, a court singer; he later studied with Christian Gottlob Neefe, who published Beethoven's first composition, a set of keyboard variations, in 1783. He worked as an assistant organist and viola player in the court orchestra, and in 1792 moved to Vienna to study with Joseph Haydn.

Vienna and rising fame

In Vienna Beethoven established himself as a virtuoso pianist and composer of piano sonatas, chamber music, and his first two symphonies. From the late 1790s his hearing began to deteriorate; the crisis is documented in his Heiligenstadt Testament of 1802. Despite progressive deafness, his "middle" or "heroic" period produced works of unprecedented scale, including the Eroica Symphony (No. 3), the Fifth and Sixth Symphonies, the opera Fidelio, and the "Razumovsky" string quartets.

Late period

The works of Beethoven's late period, composed when he was almost totally deaf, are noted for their intellectual depth and formal innovation: the last five piano sonatas, the Missa solemnis, the Diabelli Variations, the late string quartets, and the Ninth Symphony, whose finale sets Schiller's "Ode to Joy" for voices and chorus. The Ninth premiered in Vienna on 7 May 1824 to great acclaim.

Death and legacy

Beethoven died in Vienna on 26 March 1827 during a thunderstorm; his funeral was attended by thousands. His music has remained central to concert life ever since. The "Ode to Joy" melody was adopted as the anthem of the European Union. His works are catalogued by opus numbers and the WoO system, and Bonn's Beethoven House preserves his birthplace as a museum.
