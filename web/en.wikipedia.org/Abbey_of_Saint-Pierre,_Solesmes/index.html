Abbey of Saint-Pierre, Solesmes

The Abbey of Saint-Pierre, Solesmes (French: Abbaye Saint-Pierre de Solesmes) is a Benedictine monastery in Solesmes, Sarthe, France. It is internationally known as the centre of the restoration of Gregorian chant in the 19th and 20th centuries, and the mother house of the Solesmes Congregation of Benedictine monasteries.

History

A priory existed at Solesmes by about 1010. Suppressed during the French Revolution, the buildings were bought in 1832 by Dom Prosper Guéranger, who refounded the monastery in 1833 and was appointed its first abbot in 1837. Guéranger restored the Benedictine life in France after its Revolutionary suppression and made Solesmes a centre of liturgical scholarship. Expelled by the anti-religious laws of 1901, the community lived in exile on the Isle of Wight, at Appuldurcombe and then Quarr Abbey, returning to Solesmes in 1922.

Gregorian chant

The monks of Solesmes undertook a scholarly programme of comparing chant manuscripts to restore the authentic melodies of Gregorian chant. Their publications, including the Paléographie musicale facsimile series begun under Dom André Mocquereau and the official Vatican Edition of the chant books, established the Solesmes method of chant performance, characterised by a free, speech-like rhythm. Scholars such as Dom Joseph Pothier and later Dom Eugène Cardine, who developed Gregorian semiology, shaped chant studies worldwide.

The Abbey today

The community continues the monastic round of prayer and publishes chant editions, recordings, and liturgical books through its associated institutions. The abbey church contains noted Renaissance sculptures, including the saints of Solesmes in the transepts.
