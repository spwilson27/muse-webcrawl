State-recognized tribes in the United States

State-recognized tribes in the United States are Native American tribes, nations, and heritage groups that have been recognized by a process established under the law of various US states, but not by the federal government.

Background

Federal recognition, administered by the Bureau of Indian Affairs, confers a government-to-government relationship and eligibility for federal services. Separately, states such as Virginia, North Carolina, Louisiana, Alabama and Vermont have their own recognition processes, often reflecting tribes' continuous presence within state borders.

Effects

State recognition may bring eligibility for certain state programs, representation on state commissions, and some federal benefits (for example through certain education and arts statutes), but it does not confer federal tribal sovereignty, trust land, or gaming rights under the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act. Some state-recognized groups have later gained federal recognition.
