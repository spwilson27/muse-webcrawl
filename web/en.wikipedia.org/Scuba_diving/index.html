Scuba diving

Equipment: Scuba set, mask, fins, wetsuit or drysuit

Governing training agencies: PADI, NAUI, SSI, CMAS

Scuba diving is a mode of underwater diving whereby divers use breathing equipment that is completely independent of a surface breathing gas supply, and therefore has a limited but variable endurance. The name "scuba" is an acronym for "self-contained underwater breathing apparatus".

History

Modern scuba began with the Aqua-Lung, developed in 1943 by Jacques-Yves Cousteau and Émile Gagnan, which combined a demand regulator with high-pressure air cylinders and freed divers from surface-supplied hoses. Recreational diving grew rapidly after the Second World War, and training and certification systems were established by organisations such as NAUI (1960) and PADI (1966).

Equipment

Recreational divers typically use an open-circuit set: a cylinder of compressed air (or enriched air nitrox), a regulator, a buoyancy compensator, a mask, fins and an exposure suit. Rebreathers, which recycle exhaled gas, are used in technical and military diving.

Risks and training

Risks include decompression sickness, nitrogen narcosis, barotrauma and oxygen toxicity, which are managed through training, dive tables or dive computers, and conservative ascent procedures. Certification levels range from entry-level open water qualifications to professional divemaster and instructor ratings.
