November Uprising
Date29 November 1830 – 21 October 1831
LocationCongress Poland, Russian Empire
ResultRussian victory; Polish autonomy curtailed

The November Uprising (1830–31), also known as the Polish–Russian War of 1830–31 or the Cadet Revolution, was an armed rebellion in Congress Poland against the Russian Empire. It began on 29 November 1830 in Warsaw when young Polish officers from the military academy rose against Grand Duke Constantine's regime.

Course of the war

The uprising's leaders formed a National Government, and in January 1831 the Sejm dethroned Tsar Nicholas I as King of Poland. The Polish army fought larger Russian forces under Field Marshal Diebitsch and then Paskevich at battles including Stoczek, Grochów and Ostrołęka. Warsaw fell in September 1831 after a two-day assault.

Aftermath

Defeat brought repression: the Polish constitution was replaced by the Organic Statute, the army and Sejm dissolved, and thousands of Poles emigrated in the "Great Emigration" to France and elsewhere, where figures such as Chopin and Mickiewicz sustained Polish culture in exile.
