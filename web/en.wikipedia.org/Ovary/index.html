The ovary is a gonad in the female reproductive system that produces ova (egg cells). In most vertebrates there are two ovaries, one on each side of the uterus, connected to the fallopian tubes. The ovaries also secrete the hormones estrogen and progesterone, and so function as endocrine glands as well as reproductive organs.

Structure

In humans, the ovaries lie on either side of the uterus against the wall of the pelvis, held in place by ligaments including the suspensory ligament of the ovary and the ovarian ligament. Each ovary is roughly almond-shaped, about 3–5 cm long in adults. Internally it consists of an outer cortex, containing ovarian follicles at various stages of development, and an inner medulla of connective tissue and blood vessels. The surface is covered by a layer of simple cuboidal epithelium.

Function

From puberty until menopause, the ovaries undergo a monthly cycle in which a cohort of follicles develops and usually one mature oocyte is released at ovulation. Females are born with a finite pool of primordial follicles — roughly one to two million at birth, declining throughout life — and only about 400–500 oocytes are ovulated during a reproductive lifetime. After ovulation, the ruptured follicle forms the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone; if fertilisation does not occur, it regresses.

Hormones

Under the influence of follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) and luteinizing hormone (LH) from the pituitary gland, the ovaries produce estrogens, progesterone, and smaller amounts of androgens. These hormones regulate the menstrual cycle, maintain pregnancy, and drive the development of secondary sexual characteristics.

Clinical significance

Disorders of the ovary include polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), ovarian cysts, premature ovarian insufficiency, endometriosis involving the ovary, and ovarian cancer. Ovarian torsion — twisting of the ovary on its ligaments — is a surgical emergency. Removal of an ovary is called oophorectomy.

In botany

The term "ovary" is also used in botany for the enlarged basal portion of the pistil of a flower, containing the ovules; after fertilisation it typically develops into the fruit. See Ovary (botany).
