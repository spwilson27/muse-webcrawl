A chimichanga is a deep-fried burrito that is common in Tex-Mex and other Southwestern U.S. cuisine. The dish is typically prepared by filling a flour tortilla with various ingredients, most commonly rice, cheese, beans, and a meat such as machaca (dried meat), carne adobada, carne seca, or shredded chicken, and folding it into a rectangular package. It is then deep-fried, and can be accompanied by salsa, guacamole, sour cream, or carne asada.

Origins

The chimichanga's origins are disputed between Arizona and the Mexican state of Sonora. The most famous legend credits Monica Flin of El Charro Café in Tucson, Arizona, who in 1922 accidentally dropped a burrito into a deep fryer and began to exclaim a Spanish profanity ("¡Chi...!"), catching herself mid-word and substituting "¡chimichanga!" — a nonsense word roughly equivalent to "thingamajig". Another claimant is Macayo's Mexican Kitchen in Phoenix, whose founder Woody Johnson said he invented the dish in 1946 by frying leftover burritos. Tucson boosters have promoted the city as the chimichanga's birthplace, and the dish is emblematic of Arizona's Sonoran-style Mexican food.

Preparation

Unlike a standard burrito, which is eaten hand-held, the fried chimichanga is crisp and usually served on a plate with a knife and fork, often "enchilada style" — smothered in sauce and melted cheese, in which case it may also be cooked soft (a "chivichanga" style in some regions). Fillings vary widely, from shredded beef and chicken to seafood and vegetarian versions.

In popular culture

The word became widely known beyond the Southwest partly through the Marvel character Deadpool, whose fondness for chimichangas — and for the sound of the word itself — is a recurring gag in comics and films.
