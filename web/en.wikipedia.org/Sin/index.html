Sin

In a religious context, sin is a transgression against divine law or a law of God. Each culture has its own interpretation of what it means to commit a sin. While sins are generally considered actions, any thought, word, or act considered immoral, selfish, shameful, harmful, or alienating might be termed "sinful".

Etymology

The English word "sin" derives from Old English synn, cognate with German Sünde and Old Norse synd. The Greek term used in the New Testament, hamartia, is sometimes glossed as "missing the mark", though this is debated among scholars.

Religious views

Christianity

Christian hamartiology describes sin as an act of offence against God by despising his persons and biblical law, and by injuring others. The doctrine of original sin holds that human nature was corrupted by the first sin of Adam and Eve in the Garden of Eden. Catholic tradition distinguishes mortal sins, which destroy the life of grace, from venial sins, which merely wound it. The seven deadly sins — pride, greed, wrath, envy, lust, gluttony, and sloth — are a traditional grouping of vices.

Judaism

In Judaism, sin is understood as a violation of the commandments (mitzvot) and is addressed through repentance (teshuvah), prayer, and acts of charity, with Yom Kippur as the annual day of atonement.

Islam

Islam teaches that sin is an act of disobedience to God's law; major sins (kaba'ir) are distinguished from minor ones, and sincere repentance (tawba) is held to bring forgiveness.
