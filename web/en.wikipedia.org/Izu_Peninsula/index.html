Izu Peninsula

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Izu Peninsula (Japanese: 伊豆半島, Izu-hantō) is a large mountainous peninsula with deeply indented coasts in Shizuoka Prefecture, Japan. Surrounded by Suruga Bay to the west and the Sagami Sea to the east, it lies south of Mount Fuji and is a popular resort area for the Tokyo metropolis.

Geography and tourism

The peninsula's interior is mountainous, formed by ancient volcanoes; the Amagi mountains run across its center. It is famous for hot springs (onsen) at Atami, Itō, and Shimoda, for wasabi cultivation in its clear streams, and for the port of Shimoda, where Commodore Matthew Perry's "black ships" arrived in 1854 and one of the first American consulates in Japan was opened.

Geology

The peninsula sits on the northernmost part of the Philippine Sea Plate, which collided with Honshu; its ongoing uplift and seismicity make it a focus of Japanese earthquake research. Much of it lies within Fuji-Hakone-Izu National Park.
