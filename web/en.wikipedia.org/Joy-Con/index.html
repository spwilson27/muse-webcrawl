Joy-Con

Joy-Con are the primary game controllers for the Nintendo Switch video game console. They consist of two individual units, each containing an analog stick, a set of buttons, and motion-sensing hardware. The controllers can be attached to the sides of the Switch console, used separately as a pair, or detached and held one in each hand; a single Joy-Con turned sideways can also serve as a standalone controller for multiplayer games.

Features

Each Joy-Con includes an accelerometer and gyroscope for motion control. The right Joy-Con additionally contains an infrared depth camera that can detect shapes and distance, and an NFC reader for amiibo. Both feature "HD Rumble," a form of precise haptic feedback produced by linear actuators, which Nintendo demonstrated could simulate sensations such as ice cubes moving in a glass. Joy-Con are sold in pairs and individually, in colours including grey, neon red, neon blue, and many special editions.

Drift controversy

Joy-Con became the subject of widespread complaint over "drifting" — analog sticks registering input when untouched, attributed to wear of the stick's internal contacts and contamination. Class-action lawsuits were filed in several countries; Nintendo apologised in 2020 and offered free repairs for drifting controllers in many regions, including units out of warranty.
