Mount Hood

Mount Hood is a potentially active stratovolcano in the Cascade Volcanic Arc in northern Oregon. At 11,249 feet (3,429 m) it is the highest point in Oregon and a prominent landmark visible from Portland, some 50 miles to the west. It hosts twelve named glaciers and year-round skiing at Timberline Lodge.

Geology and climbing

Hood last erupted in the late 18th century, shortly before the arrival of Lewis and Clark, who named the Sandy River for its volcanic sediment. The mountain, called Wy'east by the Multnomah people, was named in 1792 after British Admiral Samuel Hood. It is one of the most climbed glaciated peaks in the world, attracting some 10,000 climbers a year, most via the south side route; despite its accessibility, its changeable weather has caused numerous fatalities.
