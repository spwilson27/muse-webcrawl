Kumaragupta I

Kumaragupta I
Reign: c. 415 – 455 CE
Predecessor: Chandragupta II
Successor: Skandagupta
Dynasty: Gupta

Kumaragupta I (Gupta script: Ku-ma-ra-gu-pta; r. c. 415–455 CE) was an emperor of the Gupta Empire of ancient India, son of Chandragupta II and queen Dhruvadevi.

Reign

Kumaragupta maintained the vast empire he inherited, from Gujarat in the west to Bengal in the east, issuing extensive gold and silver coinage. His coins depict him as a devotee of the war god Kartikeya (Kumara), after whom he was named; he also performed the Ashvamedha horse sacrifice, commemorated on his coins. Tradition credits him with founding Nalanda Mahavihara, the great Buddhist monastic university, though its growth continued under later kings.

Towards the end of his reign the empire faced invasion by the Pushyamitras and the Hunas (Hephthalites). His son Skandagupta repelled these threats and succeeded him in about 455 CE.
