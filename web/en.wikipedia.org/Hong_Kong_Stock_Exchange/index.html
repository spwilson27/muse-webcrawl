Hong Kong Stock Exchange

Hong Kong Stock ExchangeOperator: Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing (HKEX)
Location: Central, Hong Kong
Founded: 1891 (as the Association of Stockbrokers)
Main index: Hang Seng Index

The Stock Exchange of Hong Kong (SEHK) is a stock exchange based in Hong Kong and one of the largest in Asia by market capitalisation. It is operated by Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Limited (HKEX), which was formed in 2000 through the merger of the exchange with the Hong Kong Futures Exchange and the clearing houses.

History

Formal securities trading in Hong Kong dates to 1891 with the Association of Stockbrokers in Hong Kong. For much of the twentieth century several rival exchanges operated; they were unified in 1986 into today's exchange. The floor-based open-outcry system gave way to electronic trading, and the trading hall in Exchange Square closed in 2017.

Market

The benchmark is the Hang Seng Index, launched in 1969. The exchange lists many mainland Chinese enterprises, including H shares, red chips and, increasingly, large technology companies; it runs the Shanghai–Hong Kong and Shenzhen–Hong Kong Stock Connect schemes allowing cross-border trading. Major developments include the 2018 listing reforms admitting weighted-voting-rights companies and pre-revenue biotech firms, and Alibaba's secondary listing in 2019.
