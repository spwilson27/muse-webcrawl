Employment

Employment is a relationship between two parties, usually based on a contract, where work is paid for: an employer, which may be a business, non-profit, or government, and an employee who performs work in return for wages or a salary.

Employer–employee relationship

The relationship is typically governed by employment law, organizational policies, and contracts, including collective bargaining agreements. Employers provide compensation, and often benefits such as health insurance and paid leave; employees owe duties of performance, loyalty, and obedience to lawful instructions. Employment may be full-time or part-time, permanent or temporary, and at-will in much of the United States.

Economics

Economists study employment levels, unemployment, wages, and labor markets. Governments track employment statistics as key indicators and regulate minimum wages, working time, safety, and discrimination.
