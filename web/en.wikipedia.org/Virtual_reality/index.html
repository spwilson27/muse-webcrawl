Virtual reality

Virtual reality (VR) is a simulated experience that employs 3D near-eye displays and pose tracking to give the user an immersive feel of a virtual world. Applications include entertainment (particularly video games), education (such as medical or military training), and business (such as virtual meetings).

[Image: A person wearing a modern virtual reality headset, holding motion controllers in both hands.]

Technology

A typical VR system uses a head-mounted display that tracks the user's head movements and renders stereoscopic images for each eye, creating the sensation of "presence" in a computer-generated environment. Modern standalone headsets such as the Meta Quest series integrate inside-out tracking, hand tracking, and mixed-reality passthrough.

History

Precursors include Morton Heilig's Sensorama (1962) and Ivan Sutherland's "Sword of Damocles" head-mounted display (1968). The term was popularized by Jaron Lanier in the 1980s; consumer VR revived after the 2012 Oculus Rift Kickstarter and its acquisition by Facebook (now Meta).
