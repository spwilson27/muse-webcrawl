Poisson summation formula

FieldHarmonic analysis
Named afterSiméon Denis Poisson
RelatesA sum over integers to a sum over Fourier transforms

In mathematics, the Poisson summation formula is an equation that relates the Fourier series coefficients of the periodic summation of a function to values of the function's continuous Fourier transform.

Statement

For a suitable function f on the real line with Fourier transform f̂, the formula states that the sum of f over all integers equals the sum of f̂ over all integers: Σn∈ℤ f(n) = Σn∈ℤ f̂(n). More generally, rescaled versions relate sums sampled at one spacing to sums of the transform at the reciprocal spacing.

Applications

The formula is a cornerstone of harmonic analysis. It is used to prove the functional equation of the Riemann zeta function and of theta functions, in the theory of lattice sums and crystallography, and in signal processing, where it underlies the Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem: periodizing a signal in time corresponds to sampling it in frequency.
