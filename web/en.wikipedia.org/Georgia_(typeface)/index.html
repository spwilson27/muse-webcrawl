Georgia (typeface)

Georgia
Category: Serif
Designer: Matthew Carter
Foundry: Microsoft / Carter & Cone
Date released: 1996

Georgia is a serif typeface designed in 1993–94 by Matthew Carter and hinted by Tom Rickner for the Microsoft Corporation. It was released in 1996 as part of Microsoft's Core fonts for the Web package and became one of the most widely used screen typefaces.

Design

Georgia was designed for clarity on low-resolution screens: it has a large x-height, open letterforms, and old-style (text) figures in its default setting. Although inspired by Scotch Roman types of the nineteenth century, its construction was adapted for screen rendering, with sturdy serifs and generous spacing. Carter named it after a tabloid headline about a girl named Georgia, following a series of names in the project that referenced American states and places.

Use

Bundled with Windows and macOS, Georgia became a default choice for web typography seeking a bookish, editorial feel, and has been used by major news websites. An expanded "Georgia Pro" version with additional weights was released later.
