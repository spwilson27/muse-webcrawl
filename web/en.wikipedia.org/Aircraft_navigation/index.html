Aircraft navigation

Aircraft navigation is the process by which pilots, navigators and flight management systems determine an aircraft's position and plan and follow a route to a destination. It combines techniques of position fixing, dead reckoning and course guidance, and is supported by ground-based and satellite-based navigation aids.

Methods

Traditional methods include pilotage (navigation by visual reference to landmarks), dead reckoning using heading, speed and wind estimates, and celestial navigation, once standard on long overwater flights. Radio navigation aids such as NDB, VOR and DME allowed instrument navigation along airways, and inertial navigation systems provided self-contained position data.

Modern navigation

Today most aircraft navigate using satellite systems such as GPS within performance-based navigation (PBN) frameworks, with flight management systems steering the aircraft along programmed routes. Air traffic control provides radar surveillance and clearances, while systems such as ADS-B broadcast the aircraft's position for tracking.
