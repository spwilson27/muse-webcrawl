National emblem

A national emblem is an emblem or seal that is reserved for use by a nation state or multi-national state as a symbol of that nation. Many nations have a seal or emblem in addition to a national flag and a national coat of arms. National emblems may appear on currency, passports, official documents, and government buildings.

Types

Some national emblems are heraldic coats of arms, following European heraldic traditions, such as the coat of arms of the United Kingdom. Others are non-heraldic seals or devices, such as the National Emblem of China or the Imperial Seal of Japan — a stylized chrysanthemum. Some states use emblems drawn from indigenous or ideological symbolism, including socialist heraldry used by several 20th-century states.

Usage

National emblems function as marks of sovereignty and authentication. Their use is often regulated by law, restricting them to official purposes. Along with the national anthem and flag, the emblem forms part of the core symbolic repertoire through which a state represents itself at home and abroad.
