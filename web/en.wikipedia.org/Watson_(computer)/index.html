Watson is a question-answering computer system developed by IBM, capable of answering questions posed in natural language. It was named after IBM's founder and first CEO, Thomas J. Watson. The system was initially developed as part of IBM's DeepQA project by a research team led by principal investigator David Ferrucci.

The Jeopardy! match

In 2011, Watson competed on the American quiz show Jeopardy! against Brad Rutter and Ken Jennings, the two most successful contestants in the show's history. Watson received the questions as electronic text, searched an enormous corpus of documents, and generated and ranked possible answers, speaking its responses using speech synthesis. Over three games broadcast in February 2011, Watson won the first-place prize of $1 million, half of which IBM donated to charity. The match was seen as a landmark demonstration of machine natural-language understanding.

Technology

Watson combined natural language processing, information retrieval, knowledge representation, automated reasoning and machine learning. The competition system ran on 90 IBM Power 750 servers with a total of 2,880 processor cores and 15 terabytes of RAM, and could process data at roughly 500 gigabytes per second. For the television matches it was not connected to the Internet; its knowledge base of some 200 million pages of content was stored locally.

Later applications

After Jeopardy!, IBM commercialised Watson for applications such as healthcare decision support (notably the Watson for Oncology and Watson for Genomics products), finance, customer service and education. The Watson brand was later applied to IBM's broader portfolio of artificial intelligence products and services, and in 2023 IBM launched watsonx, its enterprise AI and data platform building on the Watson name.
