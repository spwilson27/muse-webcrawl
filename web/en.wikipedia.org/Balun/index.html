Balun

A simple balun wound on a toroidal ferrite core. [Image placeholder]

A balun (a portmanteau of balanced and unbalanced) is an electrical device that allows a signal to be transmitted between a balanced transmission line or load and an unbalanced one, or vice versa. A balanced line has two conductors with equal impedances to ground (such as twin-lead or a dipole antenna), while an unbalanced line has one conductor at ground potential (such as coaxial cable). In converting between the two, a balun also commonly performs impedance transformation.

Why a balun is needed

Connecting an unbalanced feed line (e.g. coaxial cable) directly to a balanced antenna (e.g. a dipole) allows radio-frequency current to flow on the outside of the cable shield — "common-mode" current. This distorts the antenna's radiation pattern, makes the feed line radiate and receive noise, and can cause interference. A balun at the feed point forces equal and opposite currents in the two conductors and suppresses the common-mode current.

Types

Transformer baluns

The classic balun is an RF transformer with separate (galvanically isolated) windings, often wound on a ferrite toroid or rod. Autotransformer baluns use a single tapped winding and provide ratios such as 1:1 or 4:1. Transmission-line transformer baluns (e.g. Guanella and Ruthroff designs) wind transmission lines on ferrite cores to achieve wide bandwidth.

Choke baluns

A "current" or choke balun does not transform impedance but presents a high impedance to common-mode current — for example by coiling the coaxial cable or passing it through ferrite beads — thereby choking off current on the outside of the shield.

Other forms

At microwave frequencies, baluns can be realised as printed structures such as the Marchand balun or tapered microstrip transitions. In video and networking, small baluns adapt signals between twisted pair and coaxial cable (e.g. CCTV over Cat-5 cable).

Applications

  • Feeding dipole and other balanced antennas from coaxial cable in amateur radio and broadcasting
  • Connecting balanced audio lines to unbalanced equipment
  • Television antenna matching (300 Ω twin-lead to 75 Ω coax, a 4:1 balun)
  • Video, CCTV and Ethernet media conversion
