Deputy of the Governor General of Canada

The Deputy of the Governor General of Canada (French: suppléant du gouverneur général du Canada) is a person, appointed by commission under the Great Seal of Canada by the governor general of Canada, who is empowered to act on the governor general's behalf in specified matters, most commonly granting royal assent to bills passed by the Parliament of Canada and signing formal documents and orders in council.

Constitutional basis

The Constitution Act, 1867 permits the sovereign to authorise the governor general to appoint deputies to exercise any of the governor general's powers. In practice, the governor general's commission appoints the judges of the Supreme Court of Canada as deputies, together with certain senior officials such as the secretary to the governor general.

Practice

Deputies most visibly exercise the function of granting royal assent. By long-standing practice, the Chief Justice of Canada (or another justice of the Supreme Court) may give royal assent in the Senate chamber as the governor general's deputy. This duty became especially prominent during periods when the governor general is absent from Canada or the office is vacant, at which time the chief justice serves as administrator of the government of Canada under a separate commission.
