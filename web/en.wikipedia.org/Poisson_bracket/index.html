Poisson bracket

In mathematics and classical mechanics, the Poisson bracket is an important binary operation in Hamiltonian mechanics, playing a central role in Hamilton's equations of motion, which govern the time evolution of a Hamiltonian dynamical system. The Poisson bracket also distinguishes a certain class of coordinate transformations, called canonical transformations, which map canonical coordinate systems into canonical coordinate systems. A canonical coordinate system consists of canonical position and momentum variables (denoted qi and pi respectively) that satisfy fundamental canonical Poisson bracket relations. A general class of coordinate transformations that also preserve the Poisson bracket relation structure is possible and called canonical transformations. This preservation of the Poisson bracket is the mathematical reason for the invariance of Hamilton's equations under canonical transformations.

The Poisson bracket is a special case of the Lie bracket and provides an elegant, coordinate-independent formulation of Hamiltonian mechanics in terms of symplectic geometry. It was introduced by Siméon Denis Poisson in 1809 in the context of his work on the variation of orbital elements in celestial mechanics.

Definition

Let (M, ω) be a symplectic manifold. That is, ω is a 2-form which is nondegenerate. For any smooth functions f, g, h in C∞(M) (that is, smooth functions from M to ℝ), the Poisson bracket {f, g} is defined in terms of the Hamiltonian vector fields associated to f and g. In canonical coordinates (qi, pi), the Poisson bracket takes the form:

{f, g} = Σi ( ∂f/∂qi ∂g/∂pi − ∂f/∂pi ∂g/∂qi )

Properties

The Poisson bracket is bilinear, antisymmetric (skew-symmetric), satisfies the Leibniz rule (making it a derivation in each argument), and satisfies the Jacobi identity. These properties make the space of smooth functions on a symplectic manifold into a Lie algebra, and in fact a Poisson algebra when combined with pointwise multiplication of functions.

For a function f of the canonical coordinates and time, its total time derivative along the flow generated by a Hamiltonian H is given by df/dt = {f, H} + ∂f/∂t, which recovers Hamilton's equations when f is chosen to be a coordinate function.

Lie algebra structure

The Poisson bracket is closely related to the structure of Hamiltonian mechanics as a Lie algebra. In quantum mechanics, the Poisson bracket is replaced by the commutator of operators, divided by iħ, an analogy central to canonical quantization and Dirac's formulation of quantum mechanics.
