Color gamut

In color reproduction, including computer graphics and photography, the gamut, or color gamut, is a complete subset of colors that can be accurately represented in a given circumstance, such as within a given color space or by a certain output device. The gamut is commonly shown on the CIE 1931 chromaticity diagram as a convex polygon (often a triangle for additive RGB displays) inside the horseshoe-shaped region of all colours visible to the human eye.

Device gamuts

No physical device can reproduce every colour the eye can see. Monitors using red, green and blue primaries can only display the colours inside the triangle defined by those primaries, while printers using cyan, magenta, yellow and black inks have gamuts of a different, irregular shape. Colours outside a device's gamut are described as "out of gamut" and must be mapped to nearby reproducible colours, a process called gamut mapping.

Standard gamuts

The sRGB color space, standard for the web and most displays, covers roughly a third of the visible gamut. Wider gamuts such as Adobe RGB and DCI-P3 are used in professional photography and cinema; the Rec. 2020 standard for ultra-high-definition television defines a still larger gamut.
