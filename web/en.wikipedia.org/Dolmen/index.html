Dolmen

A dolmen is a type of single-chamber megalithic tomb, usually consisting of two or more vertical megaliths supporting a large flat horizontal capstone or "table". Most date from the Early Neolithic period (4000–3000 BCE) and were sometimes covered with earth or smaller stones to form a tumulus.

Distribution

Dolmens are found across western Europe, including Brittany, Ireland, Wales, and Iberia, and also in large numbers on the Korean Peninsula, which contains a large share of the world's dolmens and is protected by UNESCO World Heritage designations. Similar structures occur in India, the Levant, and North Africa.

Interpretation

Many dolmens contained human remains and grave goods, and they are generally interpreted as tombs or ossuaries. In earlier centuries, folklore attributed them to giants or druids, and the name itself comes from Breton words for "stone table," popularized by 18th-century antiquarians.
