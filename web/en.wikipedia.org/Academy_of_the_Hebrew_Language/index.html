Academy of the Hebrew Language

Founded1953 (by Knesset law)
PredecessorHebrew Language Committee (1890)
HeadquartersHebrew University, Givat Ram, Jerusalem
PurposeRegulation of the Hebrew language

The Academy of the Hebrew Language (Hebrew: הָאָקָדֶמְיָה לַלָּשׁוֹן הָעִבְרִית, HaAkademya laLashon ha'Ivrit) is the central institution for the Hebrew language in Israel. Founded by law of the Knesset in 1953, it is the supreme authority that determines standards for Hebrew grammar, spelling, transliteration and terminology, and its decisions on matters of language are binding on government agencies in Israel.

History

The Academy succeeded the Hebrew Language Committee (Va'ad ha-Lashon), founded in 1890 by Eliezer Ben-Yehuda, the driving force behind the revival of Hebrew as a spoken language. The Committee coined thousands of new words for modern life; the Academy continued this work systematically after the establishment of the State of Israel.

Activities

The Academy publishes dictionaries and terminology lists for fields ranging from medicine and law to technology, operates the Historical Dictionary Project documenting Hebrew usage across the ages, and answers public inquiries about usage. Well-known coinages include words for computer (maḥshev), airplane (matos) and ice cream (glida), though many proposed coinages compete with loanwords in everyday speech.

Organisation

The Academy's plenum consists of scholars of Hebrew linguistics, literature and related fields, elected for life. Its campus is located at the Givat Ram campus of the Hebrew University of Jerusalem.
