Tripartism
Cooperation among government, employers and trade unions

Tripartism is an economic system of neo-corporatism based on a mixed economy and tripartite contracts between the interests of the state, employers and employees (through trade unions) within an economy. Each is to act as a social partner to create economic policy through cooperation, consultation, negotiation and compromise.

Practice

Tripartite arrangements are characteristic of many European countries, notably the Nordic states, the Netherlands and Ireland, where national wage agreements and social pacts were negotiated among the three partners. The International Labour Organization, founded in 1919, is itself structured tripartitely: its delegations include representatives of governments, employers and workers.

Related concepts

The term also describes political systems and, in a different sense, the three-part division of government. See also corporatism and social partnership.
