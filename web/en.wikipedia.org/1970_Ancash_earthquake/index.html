1970 Ancash earthquake

The 1970 Ancash earthquake (also known as the Great Peruvian earthquake) occurred on 31 May 1970 off the coast of Peru in the Pacific Ocean at 15:23 local time. The magnitude 7.9 megathrust earthquake, combined with the catastrophic landslide it triggered from Mount Huascarán, makes it the deadliest natural disaster in Peruvian history and one of the deadliest earthquakes and avalanches on record.

Earthquake and damage

The undersea earthquake struck on a Sunday afternoon, lasting about 45 seconds. The shaking devastated the Ancash Region and neighbouring areas: the coastal city of Chimbote was badly damaged, and towns throughout the Callejón de Huaylas valley in the Cordillera Blanca suffered heavy destruction of adobe buildings. Estimates of the death toll range from about 66,000 to 70,000, with some 25,000 people missing and around 150,000 injured; it remains the deadliest earthquake in the history of South America.

The Huascarán landslide

The earthquake destabilised the northern wall of Mount Huascarán, Peru's highest peak. An enormous mass of glacier ice and rock — estimated at tens of millions of cubic metres — fell from an altitude of over 5,500 metres and travelled some 14.5 kilometres at speeds estimated up to 335 km/h. The debris flow buried the town of Yungay and the village of Ranrahirca, killing more than 20,000 people in minutes. Only a few hundred inhabitants of Yungay survived, most of them at an elevated cemetery and stadium; the ruined site is preserved as a memorial, and a new Yungay was built nearby.

Aftermath

The disaster prompted an enormous international relief effort and led Peru's military government to create a national civil defence system (INDECI). Reconstruction in the Callejón de Huaylas took years; the Yungay burial ground, marked by a giant statue of Christ with outstretched arms, is visited annually on the anniversary of the earthquake.
