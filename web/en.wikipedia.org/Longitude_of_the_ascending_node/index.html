TypeOrbital element

The longitude of the ascending node (symbol ☊ or Ω) is one of the orbital elements used to specify the orbit of an object in space. It is the angle from a chosen reference direction, called the origin of longitude, to the direction of the ascending node — the point where the orbiting body crosses the plane of reference from south to north — measured in the plane of reference.

Reference planes

For bodies orbiting the Sun, the reference plane is usually the ecliptic and the origin of longitude is the March equinox (the First Point of Aries). For satellites of Earth, the reference plane is Earth's equatorial plane and the angle is called the right ascension of the ascending node (RAAN). For objects outside the Solar System, the plane of the sky through the primary is used.

Use

Together with inclination and the argument of periapsis, the longitude of the ascending node fixes the orientation of an orbit in three-dimensional space. The node itself precesses over time under gravitational perturbations, an effect exploited in mission design — for example, in sun-synchronous orbits, where nodal precession of about one degree per day keeps the orbit plane aligned with the Sun.
