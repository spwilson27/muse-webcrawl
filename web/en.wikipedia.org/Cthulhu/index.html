Cthulhu

Cthulhu is a fictional cosmic entity created by writer H. P. Lovecraft. It was introduced in his short story "The Call of Cthulhu", published in the American pulp magazine Weird Tales in 1928. Considered a Great Old One within the pantheon of Lovecraftian cosmic entities, Cthulhu is described as an octopus-like head with tentacles, a massive scaly humanoid body, and dragon-like wings.

Description

In the story, Cthulhu lies in a deathlike sleep in the sunken city of R'lyeh beneath the Pacific Ocean, awaiting the time when the stars are right for its return. The famous couplet from the fictional Necronomicon reads: "That is not dead which can eternal lie, / And with strange aeons even death may die." Cthulhu is also associated with the phrase "Ph'nglui mglw'nafh Cthulhu R'lyeh wgah'nagl fhtagn" ("In his house at R'lyeh dead Cthulhu waits dreaming").

Legacy

Cthulhu has become one of the most recognizable figures in horror fiction, inspiring games such as the Call of Cthulhu role-playing game, films, music, and countless references in popular culture. The shared fictional universe built around it is known as the Cthulhu Mythos.
