The Battle of Eniwetok was a battle of the Pacific campaign of World War II, fought between 17 and 23 February 1944 on Eniwetok Atoll in the Marshall Islands. The invasion, codenamed Operation Catchpole, followed the American success in the Battle of Kwajalein and was intended to give the Americans an airfield and harbour to support attacks on the Mariana Islands to the northwest.

Background

Eniwetok is a large coral atoll of 40 islands in the northwestern Marshall Islands, about 525 km (326 mi) northwest of Kwajalein. After the fall of Kwajalein, Admiral Raymond Spruance ordered the atoll taken quickly, before the Japanese could reinforce it. The Japanese garrison, commanded by Major General Yoshimi Nishida, numbered about 2,700 men, most of them from the 1st Amphibious Brigade, which had only recently arrived in the atoll and had little time to prepare elaborate defences.

The battle

Engebi

On 17 February, naval bombardment began with fire support from Task Group 51.11 under Rear Admiral Harry W. Hill. The main assault, on Engebi Island in the north of the atoll, went in on 18 February, carried by the 22nd Marine Regiment under Colonel John T. Walker. Engebi contained the atoll's airfield, the principal objective. Despite pockets of resistance, the island was declared secure by the evening of 18 February, though mopping-up continued the next day.

Eniwetok Island

On 19 February, the 106th Infantry Regiment landed on Eniwetok Island itself, in the southeast of the atoll. Progress was slower than expected: the preliminary bombardment had been lighter than at Engebi, and the Japanese defenders held strongpoints in the island's coconut groves. The island was secured on 21 February after bitter fighting.

Parry Island

The last major action came on Parry Island, where the bulk of the remaining Japanese garrison was concentrated. After a heavy bombardment, the 22nd Marines landed on 22 February. The fighting was intense, but the island was taken by the end of 23 February, completing the capture of the atoll.

Aftermath

American casualties were 348 killed and 866 wounded; Japanese losses were 2,677 killed and only 16 prisoners taken. The capture of Eniwetok provided the U.S. Navy with an advanced anchorage and an airfield from which to support the subsequent invasions of the Marianas in June 1944, including the Battle of Saipan and Battle of Tinian. The atoll later became a major fleet anchorage, and Eniwetok was used after the war as a site for nuclear weapons tests.
