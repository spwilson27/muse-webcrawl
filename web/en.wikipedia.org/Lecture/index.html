Lecture

A lecture (from Latin lectura, "reading") is an oral presentation intended to present information or teach people about a particular subject, for example by a university or college teacher. Lectures are used to convey critical information, history, background, theories, and equations.


A university lecture in progress

History

The lecture dates to medieval universities, when books were scarce and a scholar would read aloud from a text while students took notes. Despite the advent of print and digital media, the lecture has remained a central teaching method in higher education worldwide.

Criticism and alternatives

Educational researchers have criticized the traditional lecture for passively engaging students and for poor retention compared with active learning methods such as discussions, problem-based learning, and the flipped classroom. Studies commonly show students' attention declines significantly during long lectures. Nonetheless, lectures remain efficient for presenting structured material to large audiences, and modern practice often blends lecturing with questions, demonstrations, and media.

Public lectures

Many learned societies and institutions host public lectures, such as the Royal Institution Christmas Lectures in Britain, founded by Michael Faraday, and inaugural and memorial lectures at universities.
