Computational intelligence

Neural networks are a core computational intelligence technique

Computational intelligence (CI) is an umbrella term for nature-inspired, adaptive problem-solving methods that tolerate imprecision, uncertainty, and partial truth, in contrast to traditional "hard computing" based on binary logic and exact models. The IEEE Computational Intelligence Society defines the field around neural networks, fuzzy systems, and evolutionary computation.

Main branches

  • Artificial neural networks — models loosely inspired by biological neurons that learn from data; they underpin modern deep learning.
  • Fuzzy systems — reasoning with fuzzy sets and linguistic variables, pioneered by Lotfi Zadeh (1965), widely used in control systems.
  • Evolutionary computation — optimization by simulated evolution, including genetic algorithms, genetic programming, and evolution strategies.
  • Swarm intelligence — collective methods such as particle swarm optimization and ant colony optimization.

Relationship to artificial intelligence

Computational intelligence is usually regarded as a subfield of, or complement to, artificial intelligence: where classical AI emphasized symbolic reasoning and expert systems, CI emphasizes learning and adaptation from data and interaction. Hybrid systems combine CI methods with probabilistic reasoning and symbolic techniques. Applications include control engineering, pattern recognition, data mining, robotics, and financial forecasting.
