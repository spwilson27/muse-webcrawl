Coursera Inc. is an American online learning platform founded in 2012 by Stanford University computer science professors Andrew Ng and Daphne Koller. It partners with universities and organisations worldwide to offer massive open online courses (MOOCs), specialisations, professional certificates, and full online degrees.

History

Coursera grew out of Stanford's experiments with online classes in 2011, when Ng's machine learning course and other Stanford offerings drew hundreds of thousands of online students. The company launched in April 2012 with courses from Stanford, Princeton, the University of Michigan, and the University of Pennsylvania, and grew rapidly, raising substantial venture funding. It went public on the New York Stock Exchange in March 2021.

Offerings

Courses typically consist of video lectures, auto-graded and peer-reviewed assignments, and discussion forums. Many courses can be audited free, while verified certificates, degree programmes, and enterprise offerings (Coursera for Business and Coursera for Government) generate revenue. Partners include institutions such as Yale, Imperial College London, Google, and IBM; popular offerings include Ng's Machine Learning and Yale's The Science of Well-Being.

Criticism

Like other MOOC platforms, Coursera has faced questions about low course completion rates, the recognition of its certificates, and academic integrity in unproctored assessments.
