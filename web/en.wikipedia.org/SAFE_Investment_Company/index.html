SAFE Investment Company

The SAFE Investment Company Limited is an investment company controlled by the People's Republic of China. It is a branch of the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) and was established in Hong Kong in 1997 to manage part of China's foreign exchange reserves through investments abroad.

Role and operations

China's foreign exchange reserves — the largest in the world — are managed primarily by SAFE in Beijing. The SAFE Investment Company serves as one of its overseas investment arms, deploying a portion of the reserves into foreign equities and other assets with the aim of achieving higher returns than the conservative central-bank portfolio of government bonds.

The company attracted international attention in 2007–2008 when it acquired minority stakes in major listed companies, including holdings in Australian banks and in the French energy company Total. Such investments were generally passive and below disclosure thresholds in the markets concerned.

Context

The SAFE Investment Company operates alongside the China Investment Corporation (CIC), the sovereign wealth fund established in 2007, which took over the Central Huijin Investment Company and a portion of the reserves capitalization. Together the two entities reflect China's strategy of diversifying its reserve assets beyond United States Treasury securities while keeping the bulk of the portfolio liquid and low-risk.
