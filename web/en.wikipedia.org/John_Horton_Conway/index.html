John Horton Conway

Born: 26 December 1937, Liverpool, England
Died: 11 April 2020 (aged 82), New Brunswick, New Jersey, U.S.
Fields: Mathematics
Institutions: University of Cambridge; Princeton University
Known for: Game of Life; surreal numbers; Conway groups; Doomsday algorithm; Free Will theorem

John Horton Conway (26 December 1937 – 11 April 2020) was an English mathematician active in the theory of finite groups, knot theory, number theory, combinatorial game theory and coding theory. He also made contributions to many branches of recreational mathematics, most notably the invention of the cellular automaton called the Game of Life.

Cambridge years

Conway studied at Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge, and became a fellow and lecturer there. Working on the ATLAS of Finite Groups, he discovered the Conway groups among the sporadic simple groups. His analysis of impartial games produced the surreal numbers, described in Donald Knuth's novella and in Conway's On Numbers and Games (1976).

Game of Life

Published by Martin Gardner in Scientific American in 1970, the Game of Life — a zero-player cellular automaton whose evolution is determined by simple rules — brought Conway wide public fame and spawned a field of study.

Princeton and later work

From 1987 Conway was John von Neumann Professor at Princeton. He devised the Doomsday algorithm for computing the day of the week, co-authored Winning Ways for Your Mathematical Plays, and with Simon Kochen proved the Free Will theorem (2004–2006). He died of COVID-19 in April 2020.
