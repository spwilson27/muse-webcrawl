Heteronym (linguistics)

Heteronyms share a spelling but differ in sound and meaning

A heteronym (also known as a heterophone) is a word that has the same spelling as another word but a different pronunciation and meaning. Heteronyms are a subclass of homographs — words with identical spelling but different meanings — restricted to those whose pronunciations differ.

Examples

English examples include read (present /riːd/ vs past /rɛd/), lead (to guide vs the metal), wind (moving air vs to turn), bass (the fish vs the low voice or instrument), tear (to rip vs a drop from the eye), bow (to bend vs a weapon or knot), desert (arid land vs to abandon), polish (to shine vs relating to Poland), and minute (a unit of time vs tiny). Many heteronyms differ only in stress placement between noun and verb forms, as in record, present, object and invalid.

Terminology and other languages

The term should not be confused with Fernando Pessoa's literary "heteronyms" — fully developed alternative authorial personas — which borrowed the same word. Heteronyms occur in many languages with non-phonetic orthographies or tonal systems; in Mandarin Chinese, characters with multiple readings (多音字) form an analogous phenomenon.

Practical significance

Heteronyms pose a recognised challenge for speech synthesis and text-to-speech systems, which must use context to select the correct pronunciation, and they feature frequently in wordplay, poetry and language teaching.
