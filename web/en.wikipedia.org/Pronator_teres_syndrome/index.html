Pronator teres syndrome

Pronator teres syndrome is a compression neuropathy of the median nerve as it passes through or near the pronator teres muscle in the proximal forearm.

Symptoms

Patients report aching forearm pain, paresthesia in the median distribution including the palmar cutaneous branch territory (thenar eminence) — distinguishing it from carpal tunnel syndrome — and weakness of pronation and median-innervated muscles. Symptoms worsen with resisted pronation. Causes include a hypertrophied or fibrous pronator, anatomical bands, and repetitive forearm rotation.

Management

Conservative care — rest, splinting, physiotherapy, NSAIDs — helps many; surgical decompression is used for persistent cases. Diagnosis is clinical, supported by electrodiagnostic studies, and must be distinguished from carpal tunnel syndrome and anterior interosseous nerve syndrome.
