The Changthang (Tibetan for "northern plains") is a high-altitude plateau stretching across western and northern Tibet into south-eastern Ladakh in India. Lying mostly above 4,500 metres, it is the western extension of the Tibetan Plateau and one of the most sparsely populated landscapes on Earth.

People

The plateau is home to the Changpa, Tibetan nomadic pastoralists who herd yaks, sheep, and the Changthangi goat, whose fine undercoat produces pashmina (cashmere) wool. Since the 1962 Sino-Indian War divided the plateau's grazing lands, many Changpa have faced shrinking pastures, and some have settled near Leh or in refugee settlements.

Wildlife and lakes

The Changthang Cold Desert Sanctuary in Ladakh and the Changtang Nature Reserve in Tibet protect the Tibetan wild ass (kiang), snow leopard, Tibetan antelope (chiru), and black-necked crane. Great brackish lakes, including Pangong Tso and Tso Moriri, dot the plateau.
