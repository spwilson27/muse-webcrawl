Kabul

Kabul (Pashto/Dari: کابل)
Capital of Afghanistan
Elevation: about 1,790 m (5,870 ft)
Population: roughly 4–5 million (estimates vary)

Kabul is the capital and largest city of Afghanistan. Situated in a narrow valley along the Kabul River high in the Hindu Kush mountains of the country's east, it has been a strategic crossroads of Central and South Asian trade routes for millennia and is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the region.

History

Kabul was part of the Achaemenid Empire, was conquered by Alexander the Great, and lay within the Greco-Bactrian, Kushan, and Hindu Shahi spheres before its gradual Islamization. It was an important city under the Ghaznavids and later the Mughals; Babur, founder of the Mughal Empire, loved Kabul and was buried there in the Bagh-e Babur gardens. In 1776 Timur Shah Durrani made it the capital of the Afghan Empire. The city was largely destroyed and rebuilt many times: British forces occupied it during the Anglo-Afghan wars of the 19th century, and it was devastated again in the civil wars of the 1990s following the Soviet withdrawal, when rival mujahideen factions fought over it. The Taliban ruled Kabul from 1996 to 2001 and again took the city in August 2021 as the Islamic Republic collapsed.

The city today

The historic old city lies south of the Kabul River; modern districts spread across the surrounding hills, and rapid population growth has produced extensive informal housing on the mountainsides. Landmarks include the Darul Aman Palace, the Bala Hissar fortress, Chicken Street bazaars, and the National Museum of Afghanistan. Kabul faces severe challenges of air pollution, water scarcity, and security, yet remains Afghanistan's political, economic, and cultural center.
