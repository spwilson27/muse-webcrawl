Immune thrombocytopenic purpura

Other name: Immune thrombocytopenia (ITP)
Specialty: Haematology
Hallmark: Low platelet count with bleeding tendency

Immune thrombocytopenic purpura, now more commonly called immune thrombocytopenia (ITP), is an autoimmune disorder in which antibodies destroy blood platelets and suppress their production, leading to a low platelet count (thrombocytopenia) and an increased risk of bleeding. It may occur on its own (primary ITP) or in association with other illnesses (secondary ITP).

Signs and symptoms

Many patients have no symptoms. When present, signs include petechiae (tiny red or purple spots on the skin), purpura and easy bruising, nosebleeds, bleeding gums, and, in severe cases, internal bleeding. Childhood ITP often follows a viral infection and usually resolves by itself; adult ITP is more often chronic.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is one of exclusion, based on an isolated low platelet count on a blood count and examination of the blood smear, after other causes have been ruled out. Treatment depends on the platelet count and bleeding: options include observation, corticosteroids, intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG), thrombopoietin receptor agonists such as eltrombopag and romiplostim, rituximab, and, in refractory cases, splenectomy.
