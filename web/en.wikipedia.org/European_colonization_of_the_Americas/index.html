The European colonization of the Americas began with the voyages of Christopher Columbus in 1492 and continued for centuries, as Spain, Portugal, England, France, the Netherlands, and other powers conquered territory, founded colonies, and transformed the societies, populations, and ecosystems of the Americas.

Course of colonization

Spain conquered the Aztec and Inca empires in the early sixteenth century and built vast viceroyalties in Mexico and Peru, extracting silver from Potosí and Zacatecas. Portugal colonised Brazil under the Treaty of Tordesillas (1494). England established colonies along North America's east coast from Jamestown (1607), France in Canada and Louisiana, and the Dutch briefly in New Netherland and the Caribbean. Plantation economies relied first on Indigenous labour and then on millions of enslaved Africans transported in the Atlantic slave trade.

Consequences

Introduced diseases such as smallpox caused catastrophic Indigenous population decline — often estimated above 50–90% in affected regions. The Columbian exchange moved crops (potato, maize, tomato) and livestock worldwide. Colonisation imposed European languages, Christianity, and legal systems, provoked centuries of Indigenous resistance, and reshaped global trade and ecology.

Decolonization

From the American Revolution (1776) and Haitian Revolution (1804) through the Spanish American wars of independence (1810s–20s) and Brazilian independence (1822), most American colonies became independent states; a few Caribbean territories remain linked to European powers today.
