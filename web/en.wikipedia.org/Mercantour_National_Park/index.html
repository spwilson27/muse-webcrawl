Mercantour National Park (French: Parc national du Mercantour) is a national park in the French Maritime Alps, created in 1979. Its protected core covers some 685 square kilometres across 28 communes, rising from Mediterranean valleys near the Côte d'Azur to Mont Gélas at 3,143 metres, with the historic royal hunting reserve of Victor Emmanuel II of Italy at its heart; it adjoins Italy's Alpi Marittime Nature Park.

Natural heritage

The park's steep climatic gradient supports an exceptional diversity of plants — around 2,000 species, including many endemics and the ancient saxifrage — and animals such as ibex, chamois, mouflon, marmots, golden eagles, and bearded vultures. Wolves returned naturally from Italy in the 1990s, making Mercantour the first place in France where the species re-established itself, a development celebrated by conservationists and contested by shepherds.

Cultural heritage

The Vallée des Merveilles (Valley of Wonders), around Mont Bégo, contains tens of thousands of Bronze Age rock engravings, among the largest such collections in Europe. Mountain villages, chapels, and transhumance traditions add to the park's cultural landscape.
