Mind–body problem

The mind–body problem is a central problem in the philosophy of mind concerning the relationship between thought and consciousness (the mind) and the physical body (the brain). It asks how mental phenomena relate to physical phenomena, and whether and how they can affect one another.

Dualism

René Descartes famously argued for substance dualism: mind and body are distinct kinds of substance, interacting (he suggested) through the pineal gland. This "Cartesian dualism" faces the interaction problem: how can an immaterial mind causally affect a material body?

Monism

Monist responses include physicalism (everything is physical), idealism (everything is mental), and neutral monism. Twentieth-century physicalists proposed identity theory, functionalism, and eliminative materialism. David Chalmers formulated the "hard problem of consciousness": why physical processes are accompanied by subjective experience at all.
