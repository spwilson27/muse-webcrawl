Hamilton Gardens is a public garden park in the south of Hamilton owned and managed by Hamilton City Council in New Zealand. The 54-hectare park is based on the banks of the Waikato River and includes enclosed gardens, open lawns, a lake, a nursery, a convention centre and the Hamilton East Cemetery. It is the Waikato region's most popular visitor attraction, attracting more than 1 million people annually, and was named International Garden of the Year in 2014.

History

The site was originally a Māori pā and later a British military post, rubbish dump and sand quarry. Development of the gardens began in the 1960s, and the concept of themed "garden rooms" was developed from the 1980s under director Dr Peter Sergel. Rather than focusing on plant collections like a traditional botanic garden, Hamilton Gardens tells "the story of gardens" through different civilisations and historical periods.

Garden collections

The gardens are organised into five collections: Paradise, Productive, Fantasy, Cultivar and Landscape. The Paradise Collection includes an Indian Char Bagh Garden, an Italian Renaissance Garden, a Japanese Garden of Contemplation, a Chinese Scholars' Garden, an English Flower Garden and a Modernist Garden. The Productive Collection includes a Māori Te Parapara garden, an herb garden, a kitchen garden and a sustainable backyard garden. The Fantasy Collection features a Tudor Garden, a Tropical Garden, the Chinoiserie Pavilion, a Concept Garden and a Surrealist Garden, opened in 2019.

Events

The gardens host the annual Hamilton Gardens Arts Festival each summer and serve as a venue for concerts, weddings and community events.
