Poul William Anderson (November 25, 1926 – July 31, 2001) was an American science fiction and fantasy author. Of Danish heritage, he earned a degree in physics from the University of Minnesota and brought a rigorous scientific sensibility to his fiction. He won seven Hugo Awards and three Nebula Awards and was named a Grand Master by the Science Fiction and Fantasy Writers of America in 1998.

Work

Anderson's extensive output spans hard science fiction, space opera, fantasy, and historical fiction. His future-history series include the Polesotechnic League stories featuring merchant adventurer Nicholas van Rijn and the later Terran Empire tales of Dominic Flandry. His novel Tau Zero (1970), about a relativistic starship whose crew cannot slow down, is a landmark of hard SF. In fantasy, The Broken Sword (1954) drew on Norse mythology and influenced later writers; he also wrote the historical novel The Golden Slave and, with his wife Karen Anderson, works based on Norse and classical themes. Other notable works include Brain Wave (1954), Three Hearts and Three Lions (1961), and The Boat of a Million Years (1989).

Life

Anderson was active in science fiction fandom and filk music, served as president of the Science Fiction and Fantasy Writers of America, and was a founding figure of the Society for Creative Anachronism. He lived in the San Francisco Bay Area and died in 2001.
