Lake Sentarum National Park (Indonesian: Taman Nasional Danau Sentarum) is a national park protecting one of the world's most biodiverse lake systems, in the upper Kapuas River basin of West Kalimantan, Indonesian Borneo, near the border with the Malaysian state of Sarawak. Its roughly 132,000 hectares encompass a remarkable seasonal floodplain of interconnected lakes, flooded forest, and peat swamp forest.

Ecology

The lakes fill during the wet season when the Kapuas River rises and backs up into the basin, and they largely drain in the dry season, leaving a mosaic of shallow pools and mudflats. This dramatic seasonal pulse supports an exceptionally rich freshwater fish fauna — more than 240 recorded species, including the prized Asian arowana and clown loach — and serves as a vital dry-season refuge and wet-season spawning ground. The park is also home to one of the largest inland populations of proboscis monkeys, orangutans, estuarine crocodiles, false gharials, and over 140 mammal and 300 bird species.

People and conservation

Around ten thousand people, principally Iban Dayak and Malay communities, live in and around the park in floating houses and stilt villages, depending on traditional fisheries, rattan, and honey production. The area was declared a wildlife reserve in 1982 and a Ramsar site in 1994, and it was inaugurated as a national park in 1999. Together with Malaysia's adjoining protected areas it forms part of the Heart of Borneo conservation landscape.
