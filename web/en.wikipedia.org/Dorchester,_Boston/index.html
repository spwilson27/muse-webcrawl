Dorchester, Boston

Dorchester
City: Boston, Massachusetts, U.S.
Founded: 1630
Annexed to Boston: 1870
Area: Largest neighborhood of Boston

Dorchester is a neighborhood comprising more than six square miles in the city of Boston, Massachusetts, United States. Founded in 1630 by Puritan settlers from Dorchester, Dorset, England, it is Boston's largest and most populous neighborhood, and was an independent town until annexed by Boston in 1870.

History

Dorchester's settlers arrived aboard the Mary and John in 1630, among the first English settlements in New England. The neighborhood hosted the first public school supported by taxes (the Mather School, 1639) and, in the 19th century, mills and chocolate factories along the Neponset River, including the Walter Baker chocolate company.

Today

Dorchester is ethnically diverse, with long-established Irish-American, African-American, Caribbean, Vietnamese, and Cape Verdean communities. Notable institutions include the John F. Kennedy Presidential Library and Museum at Columbia Point and the University of Massachusetts Boston. Areas such as Savin Hill, Fields Corner, Ashmont, and Codman Square each have distinct characters.
