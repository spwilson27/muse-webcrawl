Grand Canal (Ireland)

The Grand Canal at Dublin, with a houseboat moored along the towpath

The Grand Canal (Irish: An Chanáil Mhór) is the southernmost of a pair of canals that connect Dublin, in the east of Ireland, with the River Shannon in the west, together with the Royal Canal. The canal is owned by the Irish government through Waterways Ireland and remains open to navigation.

Route

The main line runs 132 kilometres from Grand Canal Dock in Dublin to Shannon Harbour in County Offaly, passing through suburbs and towns including Inchicore, Clondalkin, Lucan, Sallins, Robertstown, Tullamore, and Daingean. It crosses the watershed of the Irish midlands on a summit level at the Bog of Allen. A branch once ran to Athy, where it joined the River Barrow navigation, and other branches served Mountmellick and Naas; the Naas branch is partially navigable and its extension to Corbally remains a focus of restoration campaigns.

History

Construction began in 1756 under engineer Thomas Omer. The canal reached Sallins by 1779 and the Shannon in 1804, after chronic financial difficulties, competition with the rival Royal Canal, and disputes over water supply from the Bog of Allen. At its peak the canal carried passengers in fly-boats and heavy cargo, notably Guinness stout and turf. Commercial traffic declined with rail and road competition; the last commercial boat ran in 1960. The canal has since been maintained for leisure boating, fishing, and walking along its towpaths.
