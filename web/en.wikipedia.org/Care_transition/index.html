Care transition

FieldHealth care quality
Related conceptsHandover, discharge planning, medication reconciliation

A care transition is the movement of a patient between health care settings or providers—for example, from a hospital to home, a rehabilitation facility, or a nursing home—as their condition and care needs change. Transitions of care are recognized as vulnerable points in the care process, where communication failures can lead to medication errors, missed follow-up, and avoidable readmissions.

Risks

Studies have found that a substantial share of patients experience an adverse event shortly after hospital discharge, many of them preventable or related to medications. Incomplete discharge summaries, unclear instructions, and unreconciled medication lists are common contributing factors.

Interventions

Evidence-based approaches include structured discharge planning, medication reconciliation at every transition, teach-back education for patients and caregivers, timely follow-up appointments, and dedicated "transition coach" or pharmacist-led programs. Frameworks such as Eric Coleman's Care Transitions Intervention and Project RED (Re-Engineered Discharge) have reduced readmission rates in trials.

Policy

In the United States, financial penalties for excess hospital readmissions and the adoption of transitional care management billing codes have made care transitions a national quality priority.
