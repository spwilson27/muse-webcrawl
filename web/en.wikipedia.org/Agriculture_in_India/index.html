Agriculture in India

The history of agriculture in India dates back to the Neolithic period. Today India ranks among the world's largest producers of many crops: it is the largest producer of milk, pulses and jute, and one of the largest producers of rice, wheat, sugarcane, cotton, groundnuts, fruits and vegetables. Agriculture and allied sectors employ roughly 45 per cent of the Indian workforce and contribute around 18 per cent of gross value added.

History

Farming began in the Indus Valley and other regions by around 9000 BCE, with wheat, barley, rice, cotton and livestock domesticated early. Irrigation was developed in the Indus Valley Civilisation. In the medieval period Indian textiles and spices were traded worldwide. Under British rule, commercial cropping expanded while food-crop productivity stagnated.

After independence in 1947 India initially depended on food imports. The Green Revolution of the 1960s, led by scientists such as M. S. Swaminathan and drawing on Norman Borlaug's dwarf wheat varieties, introduced high-yielding seeds, fertilisers and irrigation, transforming India into a food-surplus country. Later "revolutions" included the White Revolution (Operation Flood), which made India the world's largest milk producer.

Major crops and production

India has two main cropping seasons: kharif (sown with the onset of the monsoon) and rabi (sown in winter). Major crops include rice, wheat, maize, millets such as jowar and bajra, pulses, oilseeds, sugarcane, cotton, tea, coffee and spices – India is the world's largest spice producer. Most farms are small: the average holding is barely over one hectare, and a large share of cultivation depends on monsoon rainfall, though irrigation has steadily expanded.

Issues

Challenges include fragmented landholdings, groundwater depletion, soil degradation, dependence on the monsoon, indebtedness among farmers, and climate change. Government policies include minimum support prices, subsidised inputs, crop insurance and periodic farm-loan waivers; proposed market reforms in 2020 triggered large farmer protests and were repealed in 2021.
