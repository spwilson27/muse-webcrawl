National Bureau of Economic Research

Founded: 1920

Founder: Wesley Clair Mitchell

Headquarters: Cambridge, Massachusetts, US

Type: Nonprofit research organization

The National Bureau of Economic Research (NBER) is an American private nonprofit research organization "committed to undertaking and disseminating unbiased economic research among public policymakers, business professionals, and the academic community." Founded in 1920 by Wesley Clair Mitchell, it is headquartered in Cambridge, Massachusetts, and is the largest economics research organization in the United States, with over 1,700 affiliated university economists organized into some twenty research programs.

Business cycle dating

The NBER is best known in public life for its Business Cycle Dating Committee, which is considered the official arbiter of the start and end dates of U.S. recessions. The committee defines a recession as a significant decline in economic activity spread across the economy, lasting more than a few months, normally visible in real GDP, real income, employment, industrial production, and wholesale-retail sales. Its declarations — for example, dating the end of the Great Recession to June 2009 and identifying the brief COVID-19 recession of February–April 2020 — often arrive many months after the fact.

Research output

NBER working papers, circulated before peer review, are among the most widely read economics manuscripts; its scholars have included many Nobel Prize laureates. Research spans macroeconomics, labor, health, development, public economics, and economic history.
