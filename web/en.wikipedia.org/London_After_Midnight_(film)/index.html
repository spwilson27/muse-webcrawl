London After Midnight (film)

London After Midnight, also known as The Hypnotist, is a 1927 American silent mystery horror film directed by Tod Browning and starring Lon Chaney. It is one of the most famous lost films.

Plot

Five years after Roger Balfour is found shot in his London home — an apparent suicide — his abandoned house is occupied by a mysterious, vampire-like figure (Chaney, in grotesque makeup with sharpened teeth and a beaver hat) and a ghostly woman. Scotland Yard inspector Burke, who investigated the death, returns; in the climax the "vampire" is revealed to be Burke himself in disguise and hypnosis, staging the haunting to expose the real murderer.

Production and loss

The Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer film, adapted from Browning's story "The Hypnotist", was a commercial success and became notorious for Chaney's striking makeup. The last known print was destroyed in the 1965 MGM vault fire, and no copy has since surfaced.

Reconstructions

In 2002, Turner Classic Movies aired a reconstruction by Rick Schmidlin using surviving still photographs and the original script. The film remains a holy grail for film historians and frequently tops lists of sought-after lost films, though contemporary critical reaction to its plot was mixed.
