Osogbo

Osogbo (also Oshogbo) is the capital city of Osun State in south-western Nigeria. It is a major Yoruba city, traditionally founded beside the Osun River, and is known for its indigo dyeing tradition, its art scene, and the Osun-Osogbo Sacred Grove.

Culture

The Osun-Osogbo Festival, held each August, draws devotees of the goddess Osun and tourists from around the world; the Sacred Grove, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2005, preserves one of the last remnants of primary high forest in southern Nigeria, adorned with sculptures by the New Sacred Art movement associated with Austrian artist Susanne Wenger.

Economy

Osogbo is a trading centre for yams, cassava, and other farm produce, with industries including textiles. It lies on the railway from Lagos to Kano and became state capital when Osun State was created in 1991.
