Apollo–Soyuz

Apollo–Soyuz Test Project
Mission dates15–24 July 1975
Apollo crewThomas Stafford, Vance Brand, Deke Slayton
Soyuz crewAlexei Leonov, Valeri Kubasov
Docking17 July 1975, lasting ~44 hours
SignificanceFirst joint U.S.–Soviet crewed spaceflight

The Apollo–Soyuz Test Project (ASTP), conducted in July 1975, was the first joint human spaceflight mission between the United States and the Soviet Union. A U.S. Apollo spacecraft docked with a Soviet Soyuz capsule in Earth orbit, and the crews conducted joint experiments, exchanged gifts and shared meals. The mission was a powerful symbol of the policy of détente and marked the end of the Space Race that had begun in 1957.

Background

The project arose from improving U.S.–Soviet relations in the early 1970s. An agreement signed in Moscow in May 1972 by President Richard Nixon and Premier Alexei Kosygin committed both nations to a joint docking mission. Engineers had to reconcile incompatible systems: the two spacecraft used different atmospheres (pure oxygen at low pressure in Apollo, a nitrogen–oxygen mix in Soyuz), different measurement units and incompatible docking hardware. A purpose-built docking module, carried aloft by Apollo, served as both airlock and adapter.

The mission

Soyuz 19 launched from Baikonur on 15 July 1975, followed seven and a half hours later by the Apollo spacecraft from Kennedy Space Center — the final flight of Apollo hardware. The two craft docked on 17 July. Commander Thomas Stafford and Alexei Leonov shook hands in the docking module hatchway, a moment televised worldwide. The crews spent about 44 hours docked, conducting experiments and ceremonial activities, before separating and later re-docking once more. Soyuz landed on 21 July; Apollo splashed down on 24 July, its crew exposed to toxic nitrogen tetroxide fumes during descent — a near-disaster attributed to a checklist error — but recovered.

Legacy

Apollo–Soyuz was the last crewed U.S. mission until the Space Shuttle's first flight in 1981 and the final mission of the Apollo program. Despite criticism that it was largely symbolic, the project established technical and diplomatic groundwork for later cooperation, including the Shuttle–Mir programme and the International Space Station.
