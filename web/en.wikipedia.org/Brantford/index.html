Brantford

Brantford is a city in Ontario, Canada, founded on the Grand River in southwestern Ontario. It is the seat of Brant County and is surrounded by, but politically separate from, the county. The population was about 104,000 at the 2021 census. The city is named for Joseph Brant (Thayendanegea), the Mohawk leader who led his people to the area after the American Revolutionary War.

History

In 1784 the British Crown granted land along the Grand River to the Six Nations of the Haudenosaunee in compensation for losses in the war; a settlement grew where the river could be forded, on the trail between Hamilton and London. Brantford was incorporated as a town in 1847 and as a city in 1877. With railways and the Grand River Navigation Company it became a major 19th-century manufacturing centre for farm implements, engines and bicycles, earning the nickname "Telephone City."

Notable associations

Alexander Graham Bell conceived the telephone at his family's home, Tutela Heights, in 1874, and the first long-distance call was made to nearby Paris, Ontario, in 1876; the Bell Homestead is a National Historic Site. Brantford is also the birthplace of hockey legend Wayne Gretzky and the home of the Wayne Gretzky Sports Centre. Other noted residents include artist Lawren Harris and comedian Phil Hartman.

Today

Modern Brantford has campuses of Wilfrid Laurier University and Conestoga College, a manufacturing and logistics economy, and attractions including Her Majesty's Royal Chapel of the Mohawks (1785), the oldest surviving church in Ontario, and the Woodland Cultural Centre.
