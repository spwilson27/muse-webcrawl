Dingo Fence

The Dingo Fence or Dog Fence is a pest-exclusion fence in Australia that was built in the 1880s and completed in 1885 to keep dingoes out of the relatively fertile south-east part of the continent (where they had largely been exterminated) and protect the sheep flocks of southern Queensland. It is one of the longest structures in the world.

A section of the Dingo Fence in outback Australia.

Extent and construction

The fence stretches 5,614 kilometres (3,488 miles) from Jimbour on the Darling Downs near Dalby through thousands of kilometres of arid land, ending west of the Eyre Peninsula on cliffs of the Nullarbor Plain above the Great Australian Bight. It consists of wire mesh about 180 cm high supported by wooden and steel posts, with patrol roads running alongside. Parts of it were originally built as rabbit-proof fences.

Ecological effects

By excluding dingoes, the fence has profoundly altered ecosystems on either side: kangaroo and emu populations are higher on the dingo-free side, vegetation patterns differ, and smaller native species have declined where mesopredators such as foxes and cats are released from dingo predation. The fence requires constant maintenance by patrols who repair damage from floods, camels, and kangaroos.
