Food and Agriculture Organization

The Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO) is a specialized agency of the United Nations that leads international efforts to defeat hunger and improve nutrition and food security. Founded on 16 October 1945 in Quebec City, Canada, it is headquartered in Rome, Italy. Its Latin motto, fiat panis, translates to "let there be bread".

FAO headquarters in Rome, Italy

Work

The FAO serves both developed and developing countries, providing technical assistance, collecting and disseminating agricultural statistics, and setting international standards such as those of the Codex Alimentarius for food safety. It maintains major databases, including FAOSTAT, and issues global assessments such as The State of Food Security and Nutrition in the World. With 195 members — 194 countries and the European Union — the FAO also works in forestry, fisheries, livestock health, and emergency relief after natural disasters and conflicts.

Structure

The organization is governed by a conference of member nations that meets every two years and elects a Director-General. Since 2019 the post has been held by Qu Dongyu of China, the first Chinese national to lead the agency. The FAO operates regional, subregional, and country offices around the world in addition to its Rome headquarters.
