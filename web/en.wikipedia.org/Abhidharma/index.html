Abhidharma

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Abhidharma (Sanskrit: अभिधर्म; Pali: Abhidhamma) are ancient Buddhist texts, dating roughly from the 3rd century BCE onwards, which contain detailed scholastic presentations of doctrinal material appearing in the Buddhist sutras. The term also refers to the scholastic method itself and to the higher teaching it embodies.

Content and method

Abhidharma works analyze experience into its ultimate components (dharmas) — momentary mental and physical events — and set out exhaustive lists and matrices (mātṛkā) of these phenomena and their causal relations. Rather than narrative teaching addressed to particular listeners, Abhidharma is systematic, definitional and psychological in character, aimed at a precise account of how perception, consciousness and liberation work.

Schools and texts

Two complete Abhidharma collections survive: the Theravāda Pali Abhidhamma Piṭaka (seven books, including the Dhammasaṅgaṇī and Paṭṭhāna) and the Sarvāstivāda Abhidharma preserved in Chinese translation. Vasubandhu's Abhidharmakośa ("Treasury of Abhidharma", 4th–5th century CE) became the standard compendium studied across Mahāyāna traditions, while later Theravāda commentaries such as the Visuddhimagga shaped Southeast Asian practice.
