North Atlantic Gyre

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The North Atlantic Gyre is one of the Earth's five major oceanic gyres — great rotating systems of ocean currents driven by the global wind patterns and the Coriolis effect. Located in the North Atlantic Ocean, it circulates clockwise between North America, Europe, and Africa, and encloses the Sargasso Sea, a region of calm, clear water famous for its floating Sargassum seaweed and its accumulation of marine debris.

Currents

The gyre consists of four principal currents:

  • The Gulf Stream, a powerful warm current flowing north-eastward from Florida along the North American coast — one of the fastest currents in the world ocean.
  • The North Atlantic Current (or North Atlantic Drift), its continuation toward northwestern Europe, moderating the climate of Britain, Ireland, and Scandinavia.
  • The Canary Current, a cool current flowing southward along the coasts of Portugal and northwest Africa.
  • The North Equatorial Current, flowing westward across the tropics, completing the circuit; part of its water feeds the Caribbean and the Gulf Stream via the Loop Current in the Gulf of Mexico.

Ekman transport and the garbage patch

Wind-driven Ekman transport pushes surface water toward the gyre's centre, where it converges and downwells. The same convergence concentrates floating debris; the North Atlantic garbage patch, first documented in 1972 and studied intensively since 2010, consists largely of microplastic particles spread over hundreds of kilometres of the Sargasso Sea region. Because the gyre's interior is nutrient-poor (an oceanic "desert"), it supports low biological productivity except where Sargassum mats provide habitat for specialised communities of fish, turtles, and invertebrates — including the spawning grounds of the European and American eels.

Climate role

The gyre transports enormous quantities of heat poleward; variability in its strength, together with the Atlantic meridional overturning circulation, influences weather and climate around the North Atlantic basin. The subpolar gyre to the north, circulating anticlockwise, is a key site of deep-water formation.
