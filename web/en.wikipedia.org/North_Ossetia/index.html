North Ossetia, officially the Republic of North Ossetia–Alania, is a republic of Russia situated in the North Caucasus of Eastern Europe, on the northern slopes of the Greater Caucasus mountains. Its capital is Vladikavkaz. It borders the Russian republics of Kabardino-Balkaria, Ingushetia and Chechnya, Stavropol Krai, and the countries of Georgia (including the disputed territory of South Ossetia) to the south.

Geography and people

The republic spans mountain gorges — including the Darial Gorge on the Georgian Military Road — and fertile foothill plains watered by the Terek River. The majority ethnic group, the Ossetians, speak an Iranian language descended from that of the medieval Alans, and are predominantly Orthodox Christian. The name "Alania" was added to the republic's official title in the 1990s.

History

The region came under Russian control in the late eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. Under the Soviet Union it became the North Ossetian Autonomous Oblast (1924) and then an ASSR (1936). In 2004 the republic was the site of the Beslan school siege, in which more than 330 hostages, most of them children, were killed. The status of the Prigorodny District remains a source of tension with neighbouring Ingushetia.
