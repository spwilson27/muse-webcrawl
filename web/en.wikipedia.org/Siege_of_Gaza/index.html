Siege of Gaza

The siege of Gaza was a military confrontation that took place during Alexander the Great's campaigns against the Persian Empire, from September to November 332 BC. Gaza, a strongly fortified city on the route from Phoenicia to Egypt, was held by the Persian commander Batis (Batis of Gaza). After a stubborn two-month siege, Alexander's forces stormed the city.

Background

After defeating Darius III at the Battle of Issus and capturing the Phoenician ports, including Tyre after a long siege, Alexander advanced south toward Egypt. Gaza, situated on a high mound surrounded by strong walls, controlled the main road into Egypt. Its Persian garrison under Batis refused to surrender, expecting to replicate Tyre's resistance.

The siege

Alexander's engineers built a massive assault mound (agger) against the southern wall, higher than at Tyre, and employed siege engines and catapults brought by sea. The soft sand initially hindered the works, and Alexander was wounded in the shoulder during one assault. After several attacks, Macedonian troops breached the walls and stormed Gaza. According to ancient sources, the surviving male population was killed and the women and children sold into slavery. Batis was captured and, per some accounts, dragged behind a chariot in imitation of Achilles' treatment of Hector.

Aftermath

With Gaza taken, the way to Egypt lay open. Alexander entered Egypt without resistance and was welcomed as a liberator from Persian rule, founding Alexandria early the following year.
