Signal (messaging app)

Signal is an encrypted instant messaging service developed by the non-profit Signal Foundation and its subsidiary, Signal Messenger LLC. It uses the Internet to send one-to-one and group messages, which can include files, voice notes, images, and videos. Its mobile apps can also make one-to-one and group voice and video calls, and the Android version can optionally function as an SMS app.

Signal uses mobile telephone numbers for identification, but since 2024 users can also connect via usernames while keeping their number private. All communications are protected by end-to-end encryption using the open-source Signal Protocol, which has been adopted by other messengers including WhatsApp and Skype. The software is free and open-source, and its development is supported by donations.

History

Signal originated in the work of cryptographer Moxie Marlinspike and his company Open Whisper Systems, which released the TextSecure and RedPhone apps. Signal was launched as a unified app for iOS in 2014 and Android in 2015. In 2018 Marlinspike and WhatsApp co-founder Brian Acton founded the Signal Foundation with $50 million from Acton. The app saw explosive growth in early 2021 amid controversy over WhatsApp's privacy policy changes.

Features

Signal offers disappearing messages, screen security, sealed sender (which hides message metadata from Signal's servers), registration lock PINs, payments in some regions, stories, and group chats of up to 1,000 members. The service is designed to collect as little user data as possible; a 2016 US federal subpoena produced only an account creation date and last connection time.

Reception

Signal is widely recommended by security researchers, journalists, and privacy advocates, and is used by activists, government officials, and the general public. It has been blocked or restricted in some countries, including China, Iran, and Venezuela.
