Computer-aided engineering

Not to be confused with computer-aided manufacturing.

Computer-aided engineering (CAE) is the general usage of technology in engineering to assist engineers with many different tasks. It includes computer-aided design (CAD) software, computer-aided analysis (CAA), computer-integrated manufacturing (CIM), computer-aided manufacturing (CAM), material requirements planning (MRP), and computer-aided planning (CAP). In its most common usage for analysis, CAE refers to the use of computer software to simulate the performance of a product design in order to improve it or to help solve engineering problems.

Finite element analysis, a core CAE technique

Overview

CAE tools analyse the robustness and performance of components and assemblies. The workflow typically involves preprocessing (building a geometric model and mesh), solving (computing the physical behaviour), and post-processing (visualising and interpreting results). By replacing physical prototypes with virtual tests, CAE reduces development time and cost and allows engineers to explore more design alternatives.

Areas of application

  • Finite element analysis (FEA) — stress, vibration, heat transfer and other structural behaviour.
  • Computational fluid dynamics (CFD) — flow of liquids and gases around and through products.
  • Multibody dynamics — motion of interconnected rigid and flexible bodies.
  • Durability and optimisation — fatigue prediction and automated design improvement.

Relation to CAD and CAM

CAE sits within the wider product lifecycle alongside computer-aided design, which creates the geometry, and computer-aided manufacturing, which uses the design to drive production machinery. Integrated CAD/CAE/CAM suites form the backbone of modern digital product development and digital twin strategies.
