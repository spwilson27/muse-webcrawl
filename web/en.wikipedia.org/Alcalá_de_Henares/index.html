Alcalá de Henares

Alcalá de Henares (Spanish pronunciation: [alkaˈla ðe eˈnaɾes]) is a city in the Community of Madrid, Spain, about 35 kilometres northeast of the capital, with a population of roughly 200,000. Its name derives from the Arabic al-qalʿa, "the fortress", on the Henares River. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and the birthplace of Miguel de Cervantes.

History

The Roman town of Complutum stood nearby. In 1499 Cardinal Cisneros founded the University of Alcalá, which became one of Spain's great centres of learning during the Renaissance; the Complutensian Polyglot Bible was printed here in 1514–1517. The university moved to Madrid in 1836, but a new University of Alcalá was founded in 1977, and the historic campus forms part of the World Heritage listing of 1998.

Cervantes

Miguel de Cervantes, author of Don Quixote, was born in Alcalá in 1547; his birthplace is now a museum. Each year the city hosts the presentation of the Cervantes Prize, the most prestigious literary award in the Spanish-speaking world, at the university.

Landmarks

Highlights include the Plaza de Cervantes, the arcaded Calle Mayor, the Magistral Cathedral, and the Archbishop's Palace, where the first meeting between Christopher Columbus and the Catholic Monarchs took place in 1486.
