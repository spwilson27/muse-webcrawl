51 Pegasi b, officially named Dimidium and unofficially nicknamed Bellerophon, is an exoplanet orbiting the Sun-like star 51 Pegasi, about 50 light-years away in the constellation Pegasus. Announced in 1995 by Michel Mayor and Didier Queloz, it was the first exoplanet discovered around a main-sequence star, a breakthrough that launched modern exoplanet science and earned its discoverers the 2019 Nobel Prize in Physics.

Discovery

Using the ELODIE spectrograph at the Haute-Provence Observatory, Mayor and Queloz measured periodic shifts in the star's spectral lines with a period of 4.23 days — the signature of a massive planet in a very close orbit. The announcement in October 1995 was initially met with skepticism, because a Jupiter-mass planet orbiting closer than Mercury contradicted formation theories, but independent confirmation followed within weeks.

Characteristics

51 Pegasi b has a minimum mass of about half that of Jupiter and orbits only 0.05 AU from its star, completing a revolution in just over four days. It is the prototype of the "hot Jupiter" class — gas giants whose existence so close to their stars implies inward migration through the protoplanetary disk after forming farther out.
