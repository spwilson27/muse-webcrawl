The Bogd Khan, eighth Jebtsundamba Khutuktu and last khan of Mongolia

Bogd Khan (1869 – 20 May 1924) was the ruler of Mongolia from 1911 to 1924. As the eighth Jebtsundamba Khutuktu, the spiritual leader of Tibetan Buddhism in Mongolia, he was enthroned as khan when Outer Mongolia declared independence from the Qing dynasty during the Xinhai Revolution, becoming head of state of the Bogd Khanate of Mongolia. He was the last khan of Mongolia; after his death the Mongolian People's Republic was proclaimed.

Early life

Born in Sichuan in 1869 to Tibetan parents, he was recognized as the reincarnation of the Jebtsundamba Khutuktu and brought to Urga (present-day Ulaanbaatar) as a child. As spiritual head of Mongolia's Buddhists, he wielded enormous religious authority, though Qing officials repeatedly sought to limit his political influence.

Reign

In December 1911, Mongolian princes and lamas proclaimed independence and enthroned him as Bogd Khan. The new state initially won only partial international recognition: the 1915 Treaty of Kyakhta reduced Mongolia to autonomy under Chinese suzerainty. In 1919, Chinese troops under Xu Shuzheng occupied Urga and forced the abrogation of autonomy; the Bogd Khan was placed under house arrest. In 1921 the White Russian forces of Roman von Ungern-Sternberg expelled the Chinese and restored him, but real power soon passed to the Mongolian People's Party, backed by Soviet Russia. He remained a constitutional monarch until his death from laryngeal cancer in 1924, after which the communist government forbade the recognition of a new reincarnation and declared a republic.

Legacy

His winter residence in Ulaanbaatar is preserved as the Bogd Khan Palace Museum. His reign is remembered as the bridge between theocratic Qing-era Mongolia and the modern independent state.
