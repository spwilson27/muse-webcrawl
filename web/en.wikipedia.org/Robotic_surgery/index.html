Robotic surgery

Robotic surgery, or robot-assisted surgery, is a form of minimally invasive surgery in which the surgeon controls robotic arms holding instruments and a camera from a console, rather than holding the instruments directly. The systems translate the surgeon's hand movements into scaled, tremor-filtered motions inside the patient.

Systems and use

The best-known platform is the da Vinci Surgical System, approved for clinical use from 1999–2000 and now used worldwide for prostatectomy, hysterectomy, cardiac valve repair, and many general and thoracic procedures. Other systems include platforms for orthopaedic joint replacement and endovascular intervention. Advantages claimed include three-dimensional magnified vision, wristed instruments with greater range of motion than laparoscopy, smaller incisions, and shorter recovery times, weighed against high capital and per-procedure costs.

History

Early surgical robots included the PUMA 560, used in a neurosurgical biopsy in 1985, and ROBODOC for hip replacement. Telesurgery was demonstrated in the 2001 "Lindbergh operation", a transatlantic cholecystectomy performed from New York on a patient in Strasbourg.
