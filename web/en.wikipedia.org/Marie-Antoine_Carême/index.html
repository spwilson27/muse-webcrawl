Marie-Antoine Carême

Marie-Antoine Carême (8 June 1784 – 12 January 1833), known as Antonin Carême, was a French chef who is regarded as the founder of haute cuisine and one of the first celebrity chefs. Nicknamed "the king of chefs, and the chef of kings", he cooked for Talleyrand, the future George IV of Britain, Tsar Alexander I of Russia, and the banker James de Rothschild.

Early life

Carême was born in Paris into poverty and abandoned by his parents during the French Revolution. Apprenticed to the pâtissier Sylvain Bailly, he taught himself to read and studied architecture in prints at the national library, an education he applied to elaborate edible centrepieces (pièces montées) modelled on temples, ruins and pavilions.

Career and codification

Working for the diplomat Talleyrand at the Château de Valençay, Carême prepared the celebrated diplomacy-through-dining of the Congress of Vienna era. He systematised French cooking: he is credited with standardising the mother sauces, refining service à la russe, popularising the soufflé, and designing the tall chef's hat, the toque. His multi-volume works, including L'Art de la cuisine française au dix-neuvième siècle (1833–35), codified grand cuisine for professional kitchens.

Death and legacy

Carême died in Paris aged 48, his health broken, it was said, by years over charcoal stoves. His writings and classifications shaped Auguste Escoffier and professional cookery worldwide, and his life inspired books and the television series Carême.
