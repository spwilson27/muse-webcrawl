Range of a projectile

In physics, a projectile launched with an initial speed and angle will travel a certain horizontal distance before returning to the launch height; this distance is called the range of the projectile. Neglecting air resistance and assuming a flat surface with constant gravitational acceleration, the range depends only on the initial speed and the launch angle.

Ideal projectile motion

For a projectile launched with initial velocity v0 at an angle θ above the horizontal, the range is

R = (v02 sin 2θ) / g

where g is the acceleration due to gravity (about 9.81 m/s² on Earth). The range is greatest when sin 2θ is maximal, i.e. when the launch angle is 45°. Complementary angles (such as 30° and 60°) give the same range, although the trajectories differ in height and flight time.

Derivation

The horizontal position is x = v0 cos θ · t, and the vertical position is y = v0 sin θ · t − ½ g t². Setting y = 0 (excluding the trivial solution at launch) gives the total flight time t = 2 v0 sin θ / g. Substituting this into the expression for x yields the range formula above.

Corrections

In practice, air resistance reduces the range and makes the optimal launch angle slightly less than 45°. Launching from an elevated position increases the range. For very long ranges, the curvature of the Earth and the Coriolis effect must also be considered.
