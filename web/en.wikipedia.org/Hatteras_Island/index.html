Hatteras Island

Hatteras Island is a barrier island located off the coast of North Carolina in the United States, forming part of the Outer Banks. The long, narrow island runs roughly 42 miles (68 km) from Bodie Island in the north to Ocracoke Inlet in the south, separating Pamlico Sound from the Atlantic Ocean. It lies mostly within Dare County, with its southwestern tip in Hyde County, and is largely protected as part of the Cape Hatteras National Seashore.

Communities

The island's villages, from north to south, are Rodanthe, Waves, Salvo, Avon, Buxton, Frisco, and Hatteras Village. North Carolina Highway 12 runs the length of the island, and the Hatteras Inlet Ferry connects its southern end to Ocracoke Island. The Marc Basnight Bridge carries the highway over Oregon Inlet at the island's northern end.

Landmarks and history

The Cape Hatteras Lighthouse, near Buxton, is the tallest brick lighthouse in the United States and a symbol of the island; in 1999 it was famously moved inland to escape shoreline erosion. The waters off the cape, where the warm Gulf Stream meets colder currents, were long notorious as the "Graveyard of the Atlantic" for their many shipwrecks. During the Civil War, Forts Hatteras and Clark guarded Hatteras Inlet. The island is also known for the surf, fishing, and wind-sports tourism centred on places such as the Canadian Hole near Avon.

Storms and erosion

Hatteras Island is highly exposed to hurricanes and nor'easters. Hurricane Isabel in 2003 cut a new inlet through the island near Hatteras Village, and Hurricane Irene in 2011 breached Highway 12 near Rodanthe, repeatedly reshaping the island's geography.
