Kingdom of Judah

Capital: Jerusalem
Established: c. 930 BCE
Disestablished: 586 BCE
Religion: Yahwism / ancient Judaism

The Kingdom of Judah was an Iron Age kingdom of the Southern Levant, centred on Jerusalem and the Judean hills. According to the Hebrew Bible, it emerged after the division of the united Kingdom of Israel around 930 BCE, with the tribes of Judah and Benjamin forming the southern kingdom.

History

Judah was ruled by the Davidic dynasty from its capital, Jerusalem, where the First Temple stood. For much of its history the kingdom was a vassal of larger powers — Egypt, Assyria, and finally Babylon. King Hezekiah famously withstood the Assyrian siege of 701 BCE under Sennacherib. King Josiah is credited with religious reforms in the late 7th century BCE.

Destruction

After revolts against Babylonian rule, Nebuchadnezzar II besieged and destroyed Jerusalem and the Temple in 586 BCE, deporting much of the population to Babylonia in the Babylonian captivity. The kingdom ceased to exist, and Judah became the Babylonian, then Persian, province of Yehud.
