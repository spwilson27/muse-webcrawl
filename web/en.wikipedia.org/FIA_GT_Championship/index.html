FIA GT Championship

GT1 and GT2 cars shared the grid in the FIA GT Championship.

The FIA GT Championship was a sports car racing series for grand touring cars, organised by the Stéphane Ratel Organisation (SRO) under the sanction of the FIA. It ran from 1997 to 2009, succeeding the BPR Global GT Series, before evolving into the FIA GT1 World Championship in 2010.

Format

The championship featured two classes: GT1, for highly developed racing versions of supercars such as the McLaren F1 GTR, Mercedes-Benz CLK GTR, Maserati MC12, and Chevrolet Corvette, and GT2 (initially N-GT), for cars closer to production specification, dominated for years by Porsche and Ferrari entries. Races were endurance events, typically 500 km or two to three hours, including the blue-riband Spa 24 Hours, which joined the calendar in 2001. Performance balancing, success ballast, and driver categorisation were used to keep competition close.

History

The early seasons were dominated by Mercedes-Benz and then by the Chrysler Viper GTS-R. In the 2000s the Maserati MC12 became the benchmark GT1 car, winning multiple teams' and drivers' titles with Vitaphone Racing and drivers Michael Bartels and Andrea Bertolini. Rising costs and shrinking GT1 grids at the end of the decade led the FIA and SRO to restructure the series: GT1 became a standalone sprint-format World Championship in 2010, while GT2 machinery fed into new GT3-based series and the Blancpain Endurance Series.
