The University of Georgia (UGA) is a public land-grant research university in Athens, Georgia. Chartered by the state of Georgia in 1785, it is the first state-chartered university in the United States and one of the oldest public universities in the country. Its main campus covers 762 acres in Athens, about 70 miles northeast of Atlanta.

History

The university was chartered in 1785 and its first president, Abraham Baldwin, drafted its charter. Land was granted near the Oconee River, and the first building, Old College, was completed in 1805. The university closed during the Civil War and reopened in 1866. Under the Morrill Act it became a land-grant institution. Women were first admitted in 1918, and the university was desegregated in 1961 when Charlayne Hunter and Hamilton Holmes enrolled.

Academics

UGA comprises eighteen schools and colleges, including the Franklin College of Arts and Sciences, the Terry College of Business, the Grady College of Journalism and the School of Law. It is classified as a top-tier research university and administers the Georgia Museum of Art and the State Botanical Garden of Georgia. The campus's North Campus is listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

Athletics

The Georgia Bulldogs compete in the Southeastern Conference (SEC). The football team, playing at Sanford Stadium, won national championships in 1942, 1980, 2021 and 2022. The university's live mascot, an English bulldog named Uga, is one of the most recognisable in college sports.
