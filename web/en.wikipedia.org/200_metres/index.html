200 metres

The 200 metres, or 200-meter dash, is a sprint running event. On an outdoor 400 metre track, the race begins on the curve and ends on the home straight, so a combination of techniques is needed to successfully run the race. A slightly shorter race, called the stadion and run on a straight track, was the first recorded event at the ancient Olympic Games. The 200 m places more emphasis on speed endurance than shorter sprint distances as athletes predominantly rely on anaerobic energy system during the race.

History

The event was added to the Olympic programme for men in 1900 and for women in 1948. The standard 200 m track configuration involves running half a lap of a standard 400 m stadium, starting on the bend so that all athletes cover the same distance.

Technique

Athletes use starting blocks and run in staggered lanes. The race is typically divided into phases: the drive phase out of the blocks, acceleration around the bend, maximum speed on the straight, and speed maintenance to the finish line. Running the bend efficiently requires leaning into the curve.

World records

The men's world record is 19.19 seconds, set by Usain Bolt of Jamaica at the 2009 World Championships in Berlin. The women's world record is 21.34 seconds, set by Florence Griffith-Joyner of the United States at the 1988 Summer Olympics in Seoul.

Continental records

Area records are maintained by the continental athletics associations under World Athletics. The event is contested at the World Athletics Championships, the Olympic Games, and numerous Diamond League meetings.
