Khalsa

The Khalsa (Punjabi: ਖ਼ਾਲਸਾ, "the pure") is the collective body of initiated Sikhs, founded by Guru Gobind Singh, the tenth Sikh Guru, on Vaisakhi in 1699 at Anandpur Sahib. The founding of the Khalsa was a landmark in Sikh history, giving the Sikh community a distinct religious and martial identity and a code of discipline.

Founding

According to Sikh tradition, on 30 March 1699 Guru Gobind Singh addressed a large gathering and called for volunteers willing to give their heads. Five men — the Panj Pyare, or Five Beloved Ones — came forward and were initiated by the Guru with amrit (sweetened water stirred with a double-edged sword), after which they in turn initiated the Guru, symbolising the equality of the community. Initiates received new names — Singh ("lion") for men and Kaur ("princess") for women.

The Five Ks

Khalsa Sikhs commit to wearing the Five Ks, articles of faith: Kesh (uncut hair), Kangha (a wooden comb), Kara (a steel bracelet), Kachera (cotton undergarments), and Kirpan (a curved sword or dagger). Initiates follow the Rahit, the Sikh code of conduct, abstaining from tobacco, intoxicants, and ritual practices condemned by the Gurus.

Role

The Khalsa embodies the Sikh ideal of the sant-sipahi (saint-soldier), devoted both to spiritual discipline and to the defence of the weak. The Khalsa Panth collectively holds temporal authority in Sikh tradition, complementing the eternal spiritual authority of the Guru Granth Sahib.
