Bank holiday

Bank holidays are traditional days for leisure trips in the UK. [Image placeholder]

A bank holiday is a public holiday in the United Kingdom and the Crown Dependencies on which banks — and, by custom, most businesses and schools — are closed. The term derives from the days on which the Bank of England was closed for its staff's holidays, originally the many saints' days observed in the early 19th century. The term has also been used more loosely for public holidays in Ireland and some other Commonwealth countries.

History

Before 1834, the Bank of England observed about 33 saints' days and religious festivals as holidays. In 1834 these were reduced to four. The Bank Holidays Act 1871, promoted by Sir John Lubbock, standardised the holidays in law: 26 days in England, Wales and Ireland and 40 in Scotland were replaced by a smaller fixed list, adding days such as Easter Monday, Whit Monday and the first Monday in August. The Act also had a financial function: bills of exchange falling due on a bank holiday were payable the following business day.

Current arrangements

Bank holidays in the UK are now governed by the Banking and Financial Dealings Act 1971, and additional holidays can be proclaimed by royal proclamation (as was done for royal weddings, jubilees and the state funeral of Elizabeth II). The constituent countries have somewhat different lists: England and Wales usually have eight bank holidays a year, Scotland nine (including 2 January and St Andrew's Day), and Northern Ireland ten (including St Patrick's Day and the Battle of the Boyne/Orangemen's Day). When a holiday falls on a weekend, a substitute weekday is normally granted.

Workers' rights

There is no automatic statutory right in the UK to time off or extra pay on bank holidays; entitlement depends on the employment contract, although most employers close or pay a premium. The standard bank holidays in England and Wales are New Year's Day, Good Friday, the early May bank holiday, the spring bank holiday, the summer bank holiday (last Monday in August), Christmas Day and Boxing Day, plus Easter Monday.
