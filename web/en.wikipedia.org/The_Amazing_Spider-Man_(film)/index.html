The Amazing Spider-Man is a 2012 American superhero film based on the Marvel Comics character Spider-Man. It is a reboot of the Spider-Man film series after Sam Raimi's trilogy, and the first instalment in The Amazing Spider-Man series. Directed by Marc Webb from a screenplay by James Vanderbilt, Alvin Sargent, and Steve Kloves, the film stars Andrew Garfield as Peter Parker / Spider-Man, alongside Emma Stone, Rhys Ifans, Denis Leary, Campbell Scott, Irrfan Khan, Martin Sheen, and Sally Field.

Plot

Teenager Peter Parker, raised by his Uncle Ben and Aunt May after his parents' mysterious disappearance, finds his father's old briefcase and investigates his connection to Oscorp scientist Curt Connors. Sneaking into an Oscorp laboratory, Peter is bitten by a genetically engineered spider and gains arachnid-like powers. Seeking a cure for his missing arm, Connors tests a regenerative serum on himself and transforms into the monstrous Lizard, planning to release the formula across New York. Peter, having learned responsibility through Ben's death at the hands of a robber he had let escape, defeats Connors atop the Oscorp tower with help from police captain George Stacy, who is killed in the battle.

Production and release

Development began after Sony cancelled Spider-Man 4 in 2010, opting to reboot with a younger cast. The film was shot largely in Los Angeles and New York and was notable as the first feature shot on the RED Epic digital camera. Premiering in Tokyo on 13 June 2012 and released in the United States on 3 July, it grossed $758 million worldwide and received generally positive reviews, with praise for Garfield's performance and Webb's grounded tone. A sequel, The Amazing Spider-Man 2, followed in 2014.
