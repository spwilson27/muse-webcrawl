Irish War of Independence

The Irish War of Independence (Irish: Cogadh na Saoirse), also known in Britain as the Anglo-Irish War, was a guerrilla war fought in Ireland from 1919 to 1921 between the Irish Republican Army (IRA, the army of the Irish Republic) and British forces: the Royal Irish Constabulary (RIC) and its paramilitary auxiliaries the Black and Tans and the Auxiliaries, alongside the British Army.

Course of the war

The war is generally dated from 21 January 1919, when the First Dáil declared Irish independence in Dublin and an IRA unit killed two RIC constables at Soloheadbeg, County Tipperary. Under the political leadership of Éamon de Valera and the military direction of Michael Collins, the IRA waged a campaign of ambushes and assassinations, including the killings of British intelligence agents on Bloody Sunday, 21 November 1920. Over 2,300 people were killed.

Outcome

A truce came into effect on 11 July 1921. Negotiations in London produced the Anglo-Irish Treaty of December 1921, establishing the Irish Free State as a self-governing dominion while Northern Ireland remained in the United Kingdom. The settlement split the republican movement and led directly to the Irish Civil War (1922–23).
