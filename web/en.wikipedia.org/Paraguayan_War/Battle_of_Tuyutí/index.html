The Battle of Tuyutí was a Paraguayan offensive battle fought on 24 May 1866 in the Paraguayan War. The result of the battle was an overwhelming Allied victory; it is considered a turning point in the war. Tuyutí is regarded as the biggest and bloodiest battle in the military history of South America, and the largest battle ever fought in the Americas at the time.

Background

After the Allied invasion of Paraguay and the fall of the Itapirú position, Paraguayan president Francisco Solano López concentrated his main army in the marshy ground around Tuyutí. López planned a massive surprise attack against the Allied encampment, aiming to destroy the invading armies of Brazil, Argentina, and Uruguay in a single blow.

The battle

On the morning of 24 May, roughly 23,000 Paraguayan troops attacked the Allied camp, which held around 35,000 men. The Paraguayan columns had to cross difficult swampy terrain — the Estero Bellaco wetlands — and were delayed and disordered by the mud and by defensive ditches. After initial advances, the attacks were met by concentrated artillery and infantry fire and were driven back with enormous losses. Paraguay lost perhaps 6,000–13,000 men killed and wounded, while Allied casualties were roughly 4,000.

Aftermath

Although the Allies won, they were too exhausted to pursue, and the war continued for nearly four more years. The Paraguayan army never recovered its offensive strength, and the conflict settled into the long siege campaigns around the fortress of Humaitá.
