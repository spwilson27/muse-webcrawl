History of Paraguay

The history of Paraguay stretches from the pre-Columbian Guaraní peoples through Spanish colonization, independence in 1811, the catastrophic Paraguayan War of the 1860s, the Chaco War of the 1930s, the long Stroessner dictatorship, and the transition to democracy after 1989.

Colonial period

Asunción was founded in 1537 by Spanish explorers and became a center of colonial administration and of Jesuit missions among the Guaraní. The Jesuit reductions created a distinctive mission economy until the order's expulsion in 1767. Paraguay's relative isolation and its Guaraní-speaking population shaped a strongly mestizo, bilingual culture that persists today: Paraguay remains officially bilingual in Spanish and Guaraní.

Independence and the nineteenth century

Paraguay declared independence from Spain in 1811. José Gaspar Rodríguez de Francia ruled as a strict isolationist dictator until 1840; his successor Carlos Antonio López opened the country to trade and modernization. Under his son, Francisco Solano López, Paraguay went to war against Brazil, Argentina, and Uruguay in 1864. The Paraguayan War ended in 1870 with the death of López at Cerro Corá and the loss of a large portion of the population and territory.

Twentieth century to the present

Paraguay defeated Bolivia in the Chaco War (1932–1935), gaining most of the disputed Gran Chaco. Decades of political instability were followed by the authoritarian rule of Alfredo Stroessner (1954–1989). Since his overthrow, Paraguay has held regular elections, though the Colorado Party has dominated politics for most of the period.
