Isotype (picture language)

Full name: International System of Typographic Picture Education
Creators: Otto Neurath, Marie Neurath, Gerd Arntz
Period: 1920s–1930s, Vienna

ISOTYPE (International System of Typographic Picture Education) is a method of showing social, biological, and historical information in pictorial form, developed in Vienna in the 1920s and 1930s by the Austrian philosopher and economist Otto Neurath, his wife Marie Neurath, and the German artist Gerd Arntz.

Principles

Quantities are shown by repeating identical pictograms — a larger number means more symbols, never a bigger symbol. Arntz designed over 4,000 signs in a clear woodcut-like style, and Marie Neurath acted as "transformer", turning data into visual form according to strict rules. The system grew out of Neurath's work at Vienna's Museum of Society and Economy and his Vienna Circle philosophy of unified science and public education.

Dissemination and legacy

After the Austrofascist suppression of 1934 the team worked in The Hague and then Oxford, where the Isotype Institute was founded in 1942. ISOTYPE influenced modern infographics, pictogram signage (such as Olympic symbols and transport icons), and the data-visualisation theories of Edward Tufte.
