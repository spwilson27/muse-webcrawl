Brian Deer

Brian Deer (born 1949) is a British investigative journalist best known for his reporting that exposed the fraudulent research behind claims of a link between the MMR vaccine and autism. Writing principally for The Sunday Times, he has twice been named British Press Awards specialist journalist of the year and has received other honours for investigative reporting.

MMR investigation

Beginning in 2003–2004, Deer's investigation of Andrew Wakefield's 1998 Lancet paper showed that the research had been funded by lawyers suing vaccine manufacturers, that Wakefield had undisclosed financial conflicts of interest including a patent application for a rival vaccine, and that data in the paper had been misrepresented. His reporting contributed to the paper's partial retraction in 2004 and full retraction in 2010, and to Wakefield being struck off the medical register by the General Medical Council. A series of articles in the BMJ in 2011, based on Deer's work, described the episode as an "elaborate fraud". Deer later wrote a book, The Doctor Who Fooled the World (2020), about the affair.

Other work

Deer's earlier investigations included inquiries into pharmaceutical marketing, the drug industry and social work controversies, and he has made television documentaries, including a Channel 4 Dispatches film on the MMR controversy in 2004. Wakefield filed libel actions in response to the reporting; the claims were eventually struck out or abandoned.
