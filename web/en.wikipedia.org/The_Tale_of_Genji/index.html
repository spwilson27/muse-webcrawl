The Tale of Genji

The Tale of Genji (Genji monogatari)
Author: Murasaki Shikibu
Country: Japan
Language: Late Old Japanese
Genre: Monogatari
Publication date: before 1021

The Tale of Genji (Japanese: 源氏物語, Genji monogatari) is a classic work of Japanese literature created by the noblewoman, poet and lady-in-waiting Murasaki Shikibu around the peak of the Heian period, in the early 11th century. It is sometimes considered the world's first novel, and remains a touchstone of Japanese culture.

Plot

Across 54 chapters the work follows Hikaru Genji, the beautiful, talented son of an emperor removed from succession, through his romantic life, court career, exile at Suma, and old age; the final "Uji chapters" focus on his son Kaoru and grandson Niou. Some 400 characters appear, and hundreds of waka poems are exchanged.

Language and style

Written in court women's kana script in an archaic, allusive style, the tale is noted for its psychological depth and its aesthetic of mono no aware, a gentle sadness at the transience of things.

Translations and influence

Major English translations include those by Arthur Waley, Edward Seidensticker, Royall Tyler and Dennis Washburn. The tale has inspired picture scrolls, Noh plays, manga, opera and film, and Genji scenes appear on the Japanese 2,000-yen note.
