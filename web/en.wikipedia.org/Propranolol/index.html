Propranolol

Propranolol
Trade name: Inderal, others
Class: Beta blocker
Formula: C16H21NO2
Routes: Oral, intravenous

Propranolol is a medication of the beta blocker class, sold under the brand name Inderal among others. It is used to treat high blood pressure (hypertension), a number of types of irregular heart rate, thyrotoxicosis, capillary hemangiomas, performance anxiety, and essential tremors, and to prevent migraine headaches and further heart problems in those with angina or previous heart attacks.

History

Propranolol was developed by the Scottish scientist Sir James Black at Imperial Chemical Industries in the early 1960s and patented in 1964. It was the first successful beta blocker and revolutionized the treatment of cardiovascular disease; Black later received the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1988 for his work.

Mechanism of action

Propranolol is a non-selective beta blocker: it blocks both β1 and β2 adrenergic receptors, reducing the effects of adrenaline and noradrenaline. This slows heart rate, reduces the force of contraction, and lowers blood pressure. It is also highly lipophilic, readily crossing the blood–brain barrier, which underlies both some of its central effects and its use for anxiety and migraine prophylaxis.

Medical uses

  • Hypertension and angina
  • Arrhythmias, including rate control in atrial fibrillation
  • Secondary prevention after myocardial infarction
  • Essential tremor
  • Migraine prevention
  • Symptoms of hyperthyroidism
  • Performance anxiety (off-label)
  • Infantile hemangioma (oral solution)

Side effects

Common side effects include fatigue, dizziness, nausea, cold extremities, and slow heart rate. Because it blocks β2 receptors, propranolol can worsen asthma and mask the warning symptoms of low blood sugar in people with diabetes.
