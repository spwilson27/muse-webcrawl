Yoknapatawpha County is a fictional county in northeastern Mississippi created by the American author William Faulkner as the setting for most of his novels and short stories. Faulkner called it his "little postage stamp of native soil", and drew a detailed map of it, signed as "sole owner and proprietor".

Setting and name

The county is based on Faulkner's home of Lafayette County and its seat, Oxford, which becomes the fictional town of Jefferson. The name derives from Chickasaw words rendered as "water flows slow through flat land". In Faulkner's map the county covers about 2,400 square miles with a population of some 15,000, whites and Black residents roughly balanced.

Works set in Yoknapatawpha

Fourteen of Faulkner's novels and many short stories are set in the county, including The Sound and the Fury (1929), As I Lay Dying (1930), Light in August (1932), and Absalom, Absalom! (1936). Recurring families — the Compsons, Sartorises, Snopeses, McCaslins, and Sutpens — carry the county's history from the Chickasaw era through the Civil War, Reconstruction, and the twentieth century, and characters pass between works, making the county a single interconnected fictional world.

Themes

Through Yoknapatawpha, Faulkner examined the American South's burdens of slavery, race, memory, and decay, treating the county as a microcosm in which the past is never dead — "it's not even past".
