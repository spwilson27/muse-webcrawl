GABAB receptor

The GABAB receptor is an obligate heterodimer of GB1 and GB2 subunits

GABAB receptors (GABABR) are G protein-coupled receptors for gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA), the chief inhibitory neurotransmitter in the mammalian central nervous system. They are metabotropic transmembrane receptors connected via G proteins to potassium channels, and they mediate slow, prolonged inhibitory signals, in contrast to the fast ionotropic GABAA receptors.

Structure

The functional GABAB receptor is an obligate heterodimer composed of two subunits, GABAB1 (GB1) and GABAB2 (GB2). The GB1 subunit contains the extracellular "Venus flytrap" domain that binds GABA and other ligands, while GB2 couples to the G protein. GB1 alone is retained in the endoplasmic reticulum and cannot reach the cell surface unless it assembles with GB2, whose coiled-coil interaction masks GB1's retention signal. The receptor belongs to class C of the GPCR family, which also includes metabotropic glutamate receptors and the calcium-sensing receptor.

Function

Activation of GABAB receptors produces slow inhibitory postsynaptic potentials by opening inwardly rectifying potassium channels (GIRKs), hyperpolarising the neuron. Presynaptic GABAB receptors inhibit neurotransmitter release by suppressing voltage-gated calcium channels. These mechanisms modulate neuronal excitability, synaptic plasticity, pain perception, and muscle tone.

Pharmacology

Baclofen, a GABAB agonist, is used clinically as a muscle relaxant and antispastic agent, and is also used in the treatment of alcohol dependence in some countries. Other ligands include the agonist phenibut and antagonists such as phaclofen and saclofen, which are mainly research tools. Positive allosteric modulators of the receptor are under investigation for anxiety, addiction, and pain.
