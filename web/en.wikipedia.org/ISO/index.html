ISO

Full name: International Organization for Standardization
Founded: 23 February 1947
Headquarters: Geneva, Switzerland
Members: 170 national standards bodies

ISO is the short name of the International Organization for Standardization, an independent, non-governmental international body that develops and publishes voluntary consensus standards for products, services, and systems. The name ISO, rather than an acronym, derives from the Greek isos, "equal".

History and structure

ISO began work in 1947, succeeding earlier federations of national standardising associations. Its members are the national standards bodies of member countries, one per country. Technical committees draft standards, which are adopted by ballot; well-known outputs include ISO 9001 (quality management), ISO 14001 (environmental management), ISO/IEC 27001 (information security), ISO 3166 country codes, ISO 8601 date and time format, and the ISO 216 paper sizes.

Standards process

Standards progress through proposal, preparatory, committee, enquiry, approval, and publication stages, and are reviewed at least every five years. ISO works alongside the IEC for electrotechnology and the ITU for telecommunications. Certification to management standards is performed by independent bodies, not by ISO itself.
