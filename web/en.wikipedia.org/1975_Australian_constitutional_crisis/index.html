1975 Australian constitutional crisis

DateOctober – 11 November 1975
LocationCanberra, Australia
Key figuresGough Whitlam, Sir John Kerr, Malcolm Fraser

The 1975 Australian constitutional crisis, sometimes known as "the Dismissal", culminated on 11 November 1975 when Governor-General Sir John Kerr dismissed the government of Prime Minister Gough Whitlam and appointed opposition leader Malcolm Fraser as caretaker prime minister.

Background

The crisis arose when the opposition-controlled Senate deferred supply (appropriation) bills to force the Whitlam Labor government to an election. With the government unable to guarantee funds, Kerr used the reserve powers to withdraw Whitlam's commission.

Aftermath

Fraser's caretaker government passed supply and called a double-dissolution election, which the Liberal–National Country coalition won in a landslide in December 1975. The dismissal remains one of the most controversial events in Australian political history and a touchstone in debates about the powers of the governor-general.
