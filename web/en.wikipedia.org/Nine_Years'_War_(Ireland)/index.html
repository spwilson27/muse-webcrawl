Nine Years' War (Ireland)

The Nine Years' War in Ireland (1593–1603), sometimes called Tyrone's Rebellion, was a major uprising against English rule led by Hugh O'Neill, Earl of Tyrone, and Hugh Roe O'Donnell of Tyrconnell. It was the largest conflict of Elizabethan Ireland and came close to ending English control of the island.

Course of the war

The Ulster lords, resisting the extension of English law and sheriffs into their territories, built alliances across Ireland and won striking victories, notably at Clontibret (1595) and the Yellow Ford (1598), where an English army was destroyed. Spain intervened in support, landing troops at Kinsale in 1601, but the Irish and Spanish were defeated there. Lord Mountjoy's scorched-earth campaigns and garrisons then ground down the rebellion; O'Neill submitted in March 1603, days after Elizabeth I's death, under the Treaty of Mellifont.

Aftermath

The war devastated Ireland, especially famine-struck Ulster. Four years later O'Neill and O'Donnell left Ireland in the Flight of the Earls (1607), paving the way for the Plantation of Ulster.
