Turning

Turning a metal shaft on an engine lathe

Turning is a machining process in which a cutting tool, typically a non-rotary tool bit, removes material from a rotating workpiece, following a helical toolpath. Turning is used to produce rotational, typically axially symmetric parts with features such as cylindrical and conical surfaces, grooves, threads, tapers and contoured profiles.

Process

Turning is performed on a lathe, which rotates the workpiece about an axis while the cutting tool is fed along or across it. Common turning operations include straight turning, facing (machining the end of the workpiece), parting (cutting a piece off), boring (enlarging an internal hole), grooving, knurling, and thread cutting. The main process parameters are cutting speed (surface speed of the workpiece), feed rate, and depth of cut.

Tools and materials

Cutting tools are made of high-speed steel, cemented carbides, ceramics, cubic boron nitride or diamond, often as indexable inserts clamped in tool holders. Turning can be carried out on metals, plastics, wood (woodturning) and composites. Hard turning, performed on hardened steels, can substitute for grinding in some applications.

Types of lathes

Turning machines range from small bench lathes and traditional engine lathes to turret lathes, automatic screw machines, and CNC turning centres, which may combine turning with milling in mill-turn machines. Vertical turning lathes handle very large, heavy workpieces.
