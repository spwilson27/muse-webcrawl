Greeks (finance)

Sensitivity curves for an option position — the "Greeks" measure risk exposures.

In mathematical finance, the Greeks are quantities representing the sensitivity of the price of a derivative, such as an option, to changes in the underlying parameters of the pricing model. They are mostly named after Greek letters and are the standard tools by which traders measure and hedge the risk of options portfolios, a practice known as delta hedging and related strategies.

Principal Greeks

GreekMeasures sensitivity to
Delta (Δ)Price of the underlying asset
Gamma (Γ)Rate of change of delta with the underlying price
Theta (Θ)Passage of time (time decay)
Vega (ν)Volatility of the underlying (not actually a Greek letter)
Rho (ρ)Interest rate

Second- and third-order Greeks

More refined measures include vanna (sensitivity of delta to volatility, and of vega to the underlying price), volga or vomma (sensitivity of vega to volatility), charm (rate of change of delta over time), color (rate of change of gamma over time), and speed (third derivative of price with respect to the underlying). In the Black–Scholes model the Greeks have closed-form expressions; for other models they are computed numerically. Because a portfolio's Greeks can be aggregated, dealers routinely hedge delta to zero and manage gamma and vega within set limits.
