2017 Atlantic hurricane season

The 2017 Atlantic hurricane season was a hyperactive and catastrophic Atlantic hurricane season. It featured 17 named storms, 10 hurricanes, and 6 major hurricanes, and was one of the costliest seasons on record, with damage estimated at over US$294 billion, mostly from hurricanes Harvey, Irma and Maria.

Major storms

Hurricane Harvey struck Texas in August, stalling over the Houston area and producing catastrophic flooding with rainfall totals exceeding 60 inches locally. Hurricane Irma, one of the strongest Atlantic hurricanes ever recorded, devastated parts of the Caribbean and Florida in September. Hurricane Maria struck Puerto Rico later that month, causing a prolonged humanitarian crisis and thousands of deaths.

Seasonal summary

The season officially ran from June 1 to November 30, 2017, though Tropical Storm Arlene formed in April. Accumulated cyclone energy reached one of the highest values on record. The names Harvey, Irma, Maria and Nate were retired after the season.

Impacts

More than 3,000 deaths were attributed to the season's storms, the majority in Puerto Rico following Hurricane Maria. Recovery efforts in Texas, Florida and the Caribbean continued for years.
