Orisha

An altar dedicated to Ṣàngó (Shango), orisha of thunder and lightning.

An orisha (Yoruba: òrìṣà; Spanish: oricha, Portuguese: orixá) is a spirit or divinity in the religion of the Yoruba people of West Africa, embodying manifestations of the supreme god Olodumare. Orishas govern forces of nature and aspects of human life, and are venerated with offerings, festivals, divination, and possession rituals.

The pantheon

Tradition speaks of hundreds of orishas — "401 divinities" is a conventional figure. Among the most widely venerated are Ṣàngó (thunder and kingship), Ògún (iron and war), Ọ̀ṣun (rivers, love, and fertility), Yemọja (the sea and motherhood), Ọbàtálá (creation and purity), Èṣù (the trickster messenger who opens the way between humans and gods), and Ifá/Ọ̀rúnmìlà (divination and wisdom). Each orisha has distinctive colours, foods, songs, praise names, and sacred objects.

Spread to the Americas

Through the transatlantic slave trade, Yoruba religion travelled to the Caribbean and the Americas, where it survived — often fused with Roman Catholic saints and other African traditions — as Santería (Regla de Ocha) in Cuba, Candomblé in Brazil, and related traditions in Trinidad, Puerto Rico, and the wider diaspora. In these traditions the orishas remain the central focus of worship.

Beliefs and practice

Worship centres on maintaining a balanced relationship with the orishas through sacrifice (ebo), divination through the Ifá oracle or cowrie shells, and initiation into priesthoods. The concept of àṣẹ — the vital force that makes things happen — underlies Yoruba religious thought.
