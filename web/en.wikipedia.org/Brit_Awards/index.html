Brit Awards

The Brit Awards (stylised BRIT Awards) are the annual popular music awards of the British Phonographic Industry (BPI), the United Kingdom's recorded-music trade association. First presented in 1977 as the British Rock and Pop Awards to mark the Queen's Silver Jubilee, the ceremony became an annual event from 1982 and is widely regarded as the British equivalent of the American Grammy Awards.

History and format

The awards are voted on by an academy of more than a thousand music industry figures, journalists, and retailers. Major categories include British Album of the Year, British Artist of the Year, Best British Song, and international awards, alongside genre categories that have been reorganised several times — in 2022, the Brits made artist categories gender-neutral. The statuette, depicting Britannia, has been redesigned for many ceremonies by artists and designers including Vivienne Westwood, Damien Hirst, and Anish Kapoor.

Notable moments

The ceremony, broadcast live on ITV and held at The O2 Arena since 2011, is famous for unscripted incidents: Jarvis Cocker's stage invasion during Michael Jackson's 1996 performance, Deputy Prime Minister John Prescott being doused with water by Chumbawamba in 1998, and the rivalry years of the Britpop era between Blur and Oasis. Robbie Williams is the most-awarded artist overall, with wins as a solo artist and with Take That, while Adele holds the record for most wins in a single night on two occasions, and Raye won a record six awards in 2024.
