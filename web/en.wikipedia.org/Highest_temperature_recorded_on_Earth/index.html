Highest temperature recorded on Earth

The highest temperature recorded on Earth has been measured in several ways, most recently as direct air-temperature readings taken at weather stations. The current record, recognised by the World Meteorological Organization (WMO), is 56.7 °C (134.1 °F), recorded on 10 July 1913 at Furnace Creek Ranch (then Greenland Ranch) in Death Valley, California, United States.

The Death Valley record

The 1913 reading was taken during a heat wave in which five consecutive days reached 54 °C (129 °F) or above. Although some modern climatologists have questioned the plausibility of the 1913 observation and the reliability of the observer and equipment, a WMO review left the record standing, and it remains the official world air-temperature record. Death Valley also holds the record for the highest reliably measured modern temperatures: 54.4 °C (130 °F) was recorded at Furnace Creek on 16 August 2020 and again on 9 July 2021, verified by the WMO.

The former El Azizia record

For decades the accepted record was 57.8 °C (136 °F), measured on 13 September 1922 at El Azizia, Libya. In 2012, after a two-year investigation, the WMO decertified this reading, finding that an inexperienced observer had misread a thermometer by roughly 7 °C, compounded by an unrepresentative asphalt-like surface beneath the instrument. Decertification restored the Death Valley reading of 1913 as the world record.

Satellite surface measurements

Satellites measuring land surface temperature (rather than air temperature) have recorded higher values: up to 70.7 °C (159.3 °F) in Iran's Lut Desert in 2005. These skin temperatures exceed air readings because sun-baked ground heats well above the air above it, and they are not comparable to the standard meteorological record, which requires air temperature measured 1.25–2 metres above ground in a ventilated shelter.
