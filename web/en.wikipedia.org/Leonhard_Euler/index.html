Leonhard Euler (15 April 1707 – 18 September 1783) was a Swiss mathematician, physicist, astronomer, engineer and logician, widely regarded as one of the greatest and most prolific mathematicians of all time. He spent most of his adult life in Saint Petersburg and Berlin, and made fundamental contributions to calculus, graph theory, number theory, analysis and mechanics. Much of modern mathematical notation, including e, f(x), Σ and the use of π and i, was introduced or popularised by Euler.

Life

Euler was born in Basel and educated at the University of Basel, where he was mentored by Johann Bernoulli. In 1727 he moved to the Imperial Academy in Saint Petersburg; in 1741 he accepted an invitation from Frederick the Great to the Berlin Academy, where he remained for 25 years. In 1766 he returned to Saint Petersburg at the invitation of Catherine the Great. He lost the sight of one eye in 1738 and of the other in 1771, yet his output scarcely diminished; he dictated much of his later work. He died in 1783 after a day spent calculating balloon-flight laws and the orbit of Uranus.

Contributions

Analysis and number theory

Euler developed the theory of infinite series, solved the Basel problem (Σ 1/n² = π²/6), and proved the relation now called Euler's identity, eiπ + 1 = 0. He introduced the Euler–Mascheroni constant, the totient function φ(n), and proved the generalisation of Fermat's little theorem now known as Euler's theorem. His works Introductio in analysin infinitorum (1748) and the Institutiones calculi differentialis (1755) systematised analysis.

Graph theory and topology

His 1736 solution of the Seven Bridges of Königsberg problem founded graph theory, and his polyhedron formula V − E + F = 2 was an early landmark of topology.

Mechanics and physics

Euler formulated the equations of motion for rigid bodies and inviscid fluids (the Euler equations), contributed to optics, lunar theory and the calculus of variations (Euler–Lagrange equation), and wrote the influential textbook Mechanica (1736).

Legacy

Euler's collected works, the Opera Omnia, fill more than 80 volumes. Two numbers bear his name — e ≈ 2.71828 and γ ≈ 0.57721 — and Laplace famously advised students to "read Euler, read Euler; he is the master of us all."
