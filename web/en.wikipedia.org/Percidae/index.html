The Percidae are a family of ray-finned fishes in the order Perciformes, found mainly in fresh waters of the Northern Hemisphere. The family contains about 240 species in some 11–12 genera, including the true perches, walleye, sauger, ruffe, and the large group of small colourful darters.

Characteristics

Percids typically have two dorsal fins (the first spiny, the second soft-rayed), ctenoid scales, and a lateral line extending onto the tail. Darters (subfamily Etheostomatinae) are mostly small, bottom-dwelling fishes of North American streams, many brilliantly coloured in the breeding season; the perch lineage includes important food and sport fishes such as the European perch, yellow perch, walleye, and zander (pike-perch).

Distribution and importance

The family is centred in North America, where darters are exceptionally diverse, and Eurasia. Several species support commercial and recreational fisheries; the Eurasian ruffe has become invasive in the North American Great Lakes.
