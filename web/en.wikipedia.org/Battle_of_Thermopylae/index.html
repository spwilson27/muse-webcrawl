The Battle of Thermopylae was fought in 480 BC between an alliance of Greek city-states, led by King Leonidas I of Sparta, and the invading Persian Empire of Xerxes I, during the second Persian invasion of Greece. It took place at the narrow coastal pass of Thermopylae ("the Hot Gates") simultaneously with the naval battle at Artemisium.

Background

Xerxes invaded Greece with an enormous army and fleet to avenge the Persian defeat at Marathon a decade earlier. The Greeks chose to block the narrow pass at Thermopylae, where Persian numbers could not be brought to bear, with a force of about 7,000 men.

The battle

For two days the Greeks repelled Persian attacks in the confined pass. On the second night, a local man named Ephialtes showed the Persians a mountain path that outflanked the position. Leonidas dismissed most of the army and remained with 300 Spartans, 700 Thespians, 400 Thebans and others; they were surrounded and annihilated on the third day.

Legacy

The last stand became one of the most famous in history, a symbol of courage against overwhelming odds. The epitaph attributed to Simonides — "Go tell the Spartans, passerby, that here by Spartan law we lie" — commemorates the fallen. The delay helped the Greek cause, which triumphed later at Salamis and Plataea.
