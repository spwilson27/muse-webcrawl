Nucleic acid

TypesDNA, RNA
MonomersNucleotides
ComponentsPentose sugar, phosphate group, nitrogenous base
FunctionStorage and expression of genetic information

Nucleic acids are large biomolecules essential to all known forms of life. They are polymers of nucleotides, each consisting of a five-carbon sugar, a phosphate group, and a nitrogenous base. The two main classes are deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) and ribonucleic acid (RNA). Nucleic acids store, transmit, and help express hereditary information, directing the synthesis of proteins.

Structure

A nucleotide's bases are adenine, guanine, and cytosine in both DNA and RNA, plus thymine in DNA or uracil in RNA. Nucleotides are joined by phosphodiester bonds between the 3′ and 5′ carbons of successive sugars, giving each strand directionality. DNA usually forms a double helix of two antiparallel strands held together by hydrogen bonds between complementary base pairs (A–T and G–C), as described by Watson and Crick in 1953 using Rosalind Franklin's diffraction data. RNA is usually single-stranded but folds into complex shapes, as in transfer RNA and ribosomal RNA.

Function and history

DNA constitutes the genome of cellular organisms; RNA carries messages (mRNA), brings amino acids (tRNA), forms ribosomes (rRNA), and performs regulatory and catalytic roles. Nucleic acid was first isolated by Friedrich Miescher in 1869, who called it "nuclein"; experiments by Avery, Hershey and Chase, and others established DNA as the genetic material. Artificial nucleic acid analogues such as PNA and XNA are studied in synthetic biology and in research on the origin of life.
