Airbus A350

The Airbus A350 is a long-range, wide-body twin-engine airliner developed and produced by Airbus. Marketed as the A350 XWB ("extra wide body"), it was designed to compete with the Boeing 787 and 777. More than half of its airframe is made of carbon-fibre-reinforced polymer, and it is powered exclusively by Rolls-Royce Trent XWB engines.

Development

Airbus initially proposed a derivative of the A330, but after airline criticism it launched an all-new design in 2006. The A350 first flew in June 2013 and entered commercial service with Qatar Airways in January 2015. Two main passenger variants are in production: the A350-900, seating about 300–350 passengers over roughly 15,000 km, and the stretched A350-1000, seating about 350–410. An ultra-long-range version of the -900 operates flights of nearly 18,000 km, among the longest in the world, including Singapore Airlines' Singapore–New York service.

Design

The aircraft features a spacious cabin with high ceilings, large windows, and lower cabin altitude for passenger comfort, plus a distinctive black "mask" around the cockpit windows. Composite construction and new engines give substantial fuel savings over previous-generation widebodies. A freighter version, the A350F, was launched in 2021.
