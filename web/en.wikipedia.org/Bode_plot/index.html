Bode plot

In electrical engineering and control theory, a Bode plot is a graph of the frequency response of a system. It is usually a combination of a Bode magnitude plot, expressing the magnitude (usually in decibels) of the frequency response, and a Bode phase plot, expressing the phase shift. Both quantities are plotted against frequency on a logarithmic scale.

History

The method was developed by Hendrik Wade Bode while working at Bell Labs in the United States in the 1930s. Bode devised a set of graphical rules that allow engineers to approximate the plots by hand using straight-line segments, making feedback amplifier design much more tractable before computers.

Overview

For a transfer function expressed as a product of poles and zeros, the magnitude plot in decibels is formed by summing the contributions of each term, each of which changes slope at its corner (break) frequency. A pole contributes a slope of −20 dB per decade beyond its corner frequency; a zero contributes +20 dB per decade. The phase plot similarly sums contributions, with each pole shifting phase by −90 degrees across roughly two decades around its corner frequency.

Applications

Bode plots are used to assess system stability through gain margin and phase margin: the phase margin is read from the phase plot at the frequency where the magnitude crosses 0 dB, and the gain margin from the magnitude plot at the frequency where the phase reaches −180 degrees. They are fundamental tools in designing feedback control systems, electronic amplifiers, and filters.
