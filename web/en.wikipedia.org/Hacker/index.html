Hacker

A hacker is a person skilled in information technology who uses creative, unconventional methods to explore, modify, or gain access to computer systems and networks. The term originated in the 1960s at MIT, where it described playful, ingenious programmers; its meaning has since diverged between a tradition that celebrates technical virtuosity and the popular usage equating hackers with computer criminals.

Security hackers

In computer security, hackers are categorised informally by intent: white hat hackers test systems with permission to find vulnerabilities, black hat hackers break in for malicious purposes or personal gain, and grey hat hackers fall in between. Skilled security hackers work as penetration testers, bug bounty hunters, and security researchers; their techniques include social engineering, exploiting software vulnerabilities, and writing exploits.

Hacker culture

The original hacker culture, documented in Steven Levy's Hackers (1984), prized the "hacker ethic": hands-on imperatives, free access to information, decentralisation, and judging people by their work. This culture contributed to free and open-source software, the internet's early development, and organisations such as the Electronic Frontier Foundation. Because many in this tradition reject the criminal connotation, they prefer the term "cracker" for malicious intruders.
