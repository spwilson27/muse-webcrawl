Black Mirror: Bandersnatch

Black Mirror: Bandersnatch is a 2018 interactive film in the anthology series Black Mirror, written by Charlie Brooker and directed by David Slade. Released on Netflix on 28 December 2018, it lets viewers make choices that branch the narrative, with multiple endings. It won two Primetime Emmy Awards, including Outstanding Television Movie.

Plot

In 1984, young programmer Stefan Butler (Fionn Whitehead) is adapting Jerome F. Davies's choose-your-own-adventure novel Bandersnatch into a video game for the company Tuckersoft, run by Mohan Thakur and employing the celebrated designer Colin Ritman. As deadlines mount, Stefan's sense of free will unravels: he becomes aware he is being controlled — by the viewer, via Netflix — and the story branches into outcomes ranging from commercial failure to violence, revelation, and metafictional loops.

Interactivity and production

Netflix built a bespoke tool, Branch Manager, to script the branching story, which contains roughly five hours of footage across its paths; a typical viewing runs about 90 minutes. If viewers hesitate, the film chooses for them. The production deliberately thematises free will, determinism and the illusion of choice in interactive media.

Reception

Critics praised the ambition and Whitehead's performance, though some found the storytelling thinner than the concept. The film prompted a lawsuit from Chooseco, owner of the "Choose Your Own Adventure" trademark, later settled.
