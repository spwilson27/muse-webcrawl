The Council of Representatives (Arabic: مجلس النواب, Majlis an-Nuwwāb) is the principal legislature of the Republic of Iraq. Under the 2005 constitution it forms one chamber of a bicameral legislature alongside a Federation Council that has never been established, leaving the Council of Representatives as the effective parliament.

Composition

The council has 329 seats. Members are elected for four-year terms by proportional representation in multi-member constituencies based on the governorates. A quota reserves at least one quarter of seats for women, and a small number of seats are reserved for minority communities, including Christians, Yazidis, Shabaks, Sabean-Mandaeans, and Feyli Kurds.

Powers

The council enacts federal law, approves the budget, oversees the executive, elects the President of the Republic, and gives confidence to the Prime Minister and cabinet proposed by the largest bloc.

History

The first elections under the permanent constitution were held in December 2005, succeeding the transitional National Assembly elected in January of that year. Parliament buildings in the Green Zone have repeatedly been stormed by protesters, most notably in 2016 and 2022.
