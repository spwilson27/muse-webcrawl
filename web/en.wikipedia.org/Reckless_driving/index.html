Reckless driving

Reckless driving is a major moving traffic violation in which a driver drives a vehicle with a conscious and intentional disregard for the safety of persons or property, or without due caution and circumspection. It is generally a more serious offence than careless driving or a simple traffic infraction, and in many jurisdictions it is a criminal misdemeanour rather than a civil violation.

Excessive speed and aggressive manoeuvres are typical forms of reckless driving.

Definition

Definitions vary by jurisdiction. In the United States, state statutes typically define reckless driving as operating a vehicle with "willful or wanton disregard for the safety of persons or property". Some states specify qualifying conduct, such as driving at or above a certain speed over the limit (for example, more than 20 mph over the posted limit, or faster than 80 or 85 mph regardless of the limit, in Virginia), street racing, fleeing from police, or passing a stopped school bus.

Examples of conduct

  • Driving at speeds far in excess of the limit or too fast for conditions
  • Street racing or speed contests on public roads
  • Weaving aggressively through traffic or tailgating
  • Running red lights or stop signs at speed
  • Driving under the influence of alcohol or drugs (charged separately in most jurisdictions)
  • Evading or fleeing a police officer

Penalties

Penalties may include fines, imprisonment (commonly up to 90 days to one year for a misdemeanour conviction), licence suspension or revocation, demerit points, and increased insurance premiums. Where reckless driving causes serious injury or death, it may support charges such as vehicular assault or vehicular homicide, which are felonies in many jurisdictions.

Related offences

Reckless driving is distinguished from careless driving, which usually requires only negligence rather than wanton disregard, and from driving under the influence, which is a separate offence concerning impairment. In England and Wales, the analogous offences are dangerous driving and careless driving under the Road Traffic Act 1988; the offence of causing death by dangerous driving carries a maximum sentence of life imprisonment.
