Transposition (music)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In music, transposition refers to the process or operation of moving a collection of notes (pitches or pitch classes) up or down in pitch by a constant interval. Transposing a piece from one key to another preserves all internal interval relationships while shifting the overall pitch level, which may suit a singer's vocal range or a particular instrument.

Types

Chromatic transposition shifts every note by the same number of semitones, preserving the exact interval qualities. Diatonic (scalar) transposition shifts notes by a fixed number of scale degrees within a key, which alters some interval sizes. Sight-transposition is a skill expected of accompanists and orchestral musicians playing transposing instruments.

Transposing instruments

Some instruments are conventionally written at a pitch different from their sounding pitch. A clarinet in B♭ sounds a major second below its written notes; a horn in F sounds a perfect fifth below written pitch. Composers write parts at the appropriate transposition so the player can use standard fingerings across an instrument family.

In twelve-tone and set theory

In pitch-class set theory, transposition is one of the fundamental operations (along with inversion) applied to sets and tone rows; Tn denotes transposition by n semitones modulo 12.
