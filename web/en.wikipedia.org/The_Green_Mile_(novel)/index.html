The Green Mile (novel)

The Green Mile is a 1996 serial novel by American author Stephen King. It tells the story of death row supervisor Paul Edgecombe and his encounter with John Coffey, a gentle giant convicted of a terrible crime who possesses a mysterious healing gift.

Publication

The novel was originally released in six monthly paperback installments: The Two Dead Girls, The Mouse on the Mile, Coffey's Hands, The Bad Death of Eduard Delacroix, Night Journey, and Coffey on the Mile. It was later published as a single volume.

Plot

Set in 1932 at Cold Mountain Penitentiary in Louisiana, the story is narrated in flashback by an elderly Paul Edgecombe. The "Green Mile" is the lime-green linoleum corridor leading to the electric chair. John Coffey, a 6-foot-8 Black man convicted of raping and murdering two young white girls, heals Paul's urinary infection and resurrects a dead mouse, Mr. Jingles, revealing supernatural abilities. Paul comes to believe Coffey is innocent — the real killer was another inmate, William "Wild Bill" Wharton. Coffey nevertheless chooses to die, weary of the world's cruelty.

Adaptation

The novel was adapted into the acclaimed 1999 film directed by Frank Darabont, starring Tom Hanks as Paul and Michael Clarke Duncan as Coffey. See also The Shining and Stephen King bibliography.
