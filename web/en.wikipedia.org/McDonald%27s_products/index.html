The McDonald's fast food restaurant chain sells a wide range of products across its tens of thousands of restaurants worldwide. Its core menu centres on hamburgers, chicken products, french fries, breakfast items, soft drinks, milkshakes and desserts, with substantial local variation between countries.

Signature products

The Big Mac, introduced in 1967 in Pittsburgh and sold nationwide from 1968, consists of two beef patties, a three-part sesame seed bun, "special sauce", lettuce, cheese, pickles and onions; its ingredients inspired a famous jingle and The Economist's Big Mac index of purchasing power parity. Other long-running items include the Quarter Pounder (1971), the Filet-O-Fish (invented in 1962 for Catholic customers abstaining from meat on Fridays), the Egg McMuffin breakfast sandwich (1971) and Chicken McNuggets (1983).

International variation

Menus adapt to local tastes and religious requirements: the McAloo Tikki and McSpicy Paneer in India, where beef and pork are absent; the Teriyaki McBurger in Japan; poutine in Canada; and McFalafel historically in Israel and Sweden. Many products are offered for limited periods, such as the McRib.

Nutrition and criticism

McDonald's publishes nutritional information for its products and has reformulated items over time — removing trans fats from its fries, for example — while continuing to face criticism over calorie, sodium and sugar content and over marketing to children.
