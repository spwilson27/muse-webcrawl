Pisoid

A pisoid is a small, roughly spherical or pea-shaped sedimentary grain, typically composed of concentric layers of calcium carbonate around a nucleus. Pisoids resemble ooids but are larger — conventionally more than 2 millimetres in diameter — the name deriving from the Latin pisum, meaning pea.

Formation

Pisoids form by the accretion of carbonate layers around a nucleus such as a shell fragment or sand grain. Marine pisoids grow in agitated, supersaturated shallow waters, while many pisoids form in non-marine settings: in caliche soils (where they are called caliche pisolites), in caves as "cave pearls" in rimstone pools, and around hot springs. Vadose pisoids in soils often show asymmetric coatings, thicker on the underside, reflecting growth in place rather than rolling.

Pisolite

A rock composed predominantly of pisoids is called a pisolite. Pisolitic limestones and iron-rich pisolites (such as some bauxites and ironstones) can be economically significant. In sedimentary petrology, pisoids help geologists infer ancient environments of deposition.
