Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2003

Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2003
ParliamentScottish Parliament
Royal assent25 February 2003
PartsAccess rights; community right to buy; crofting community right to buy

The Land Reform (Scotland) Act 2003 is an Act of the Scottish Parliament that established statutory public access rights to land, a community right to buy land, and a crofting community right to buy. It was one of the signature measures of the early devolved Parliament, responding to Scotland's highly concentrated pattern of private landownership.

Access rights

Part 1 gives everyone statutory rights of access to most land and inland water for recreation, education, and passage, provided they are exercised responsibly. The Scottish Outdoor Access Code, approved under the Act, sets out the responsibilities of both access-takers and land managers.

Community right to buy

Part 2 allows defined community bodies to register an interest in land and to buy it when it comes up for sale, subject to ministerial approval and a public-interest test. Part 3 gives crofting communities an absolute right to buy their croft land and salmon fishings. The Act paved the way for notable community buyouts and for later, wider land reform legislation.
