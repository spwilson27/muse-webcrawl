The Golden Retriever is a Scottish breed of retriever dog of medium size. It is characterised by a gentle and affectionate nature and a striking golden coat, and is among the most frequently registered and popular dog breeds in many Western countries. Bred originally as a gundog to retrieve shot waterfowl during hunting, it is now widely kept as a family pet and is commonly trained as an assistance, guide, therapy and search-and-rescue dog.

History

The breed was developed in the Scottish Highlands in the late nineteenth century, principally by Dudley Marjoribanks, later Lord Tweedmouth, at his Guisachan estate near Loch Ness. Marjoribanks crossed a yellow wavy-coated retriever with the now-extinct Tweed Water Spaniel, and later introduced bloodhound and setter blood, keeping meticulous stud-book records from 1835 to 1890. The breed was recognised by The Kennel Club in 1913.

Characteristics

Golden Retrievers are well-proportioned, strongly built dogs with a dense, water-repellent double coat that sheds seasonally. They are intelligent, eager to please and tolerant, which makes them highly trainable but poor guard dogs. Typical health concerns include a high incidence of cancer — notably haemangiosarcoma and lymphoma — hip and elbow dysplasia, and certain heart and eye conditions. Regular exercise and grooming are required, and the breed readily gains weight if overfed.
