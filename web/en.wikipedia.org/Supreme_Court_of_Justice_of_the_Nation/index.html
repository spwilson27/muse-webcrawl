The Supreme Court of Justice of the Nation (Spanish: Suprema Corte de Justicia de la Nación, SCJN) is the Mexican institution serving as the country's federal high court and the spearhead organisation for the judiciary of the Mexican Federal Government. It consists of eleven magistrates, known as ministers of the court, one of whom is designated the president of the court.

Members of the Supreme Court are appointed by the President of Mexico and then ratified by the Senate of the Republic. The ministers chosen serve 15-year terms, which are not renewable. From among their number, the ministers select the president of the court to serve a four-year period; any given minister may only serve as president once.

Requirements to hold office

The Constitution requires that justices of the Court must be at least 35 years of age, must possess a professional degree in law, must have a good reputation, and must not have been convicted of certain crimes. Candidates must also have resided in the country for the two years preceding their nomination and must not have held certain senior political offices in the year before their appointment.

The building

The court itself is located just off the main plaza of Mexico City, on the southeast corner of the Zócalo, in what was the location of the ritual centre of the Aztec Tenochtitlan. The edifice was built between 1935 and 1941 by the Mexican architect Antonio Muñoz Garcia, on the site formerly occupied by the Mercado del Volador. Inside, four large murals painted by José Clemente Orozco and others adorn the stairwell and corridors.

Jurisdiction

As well as serving as a court of last resort on legal issues of federal importance, the Supreme Court is charged, among other duties, with:

  • Deciding constitutional controversies between different levels of government;
  • Ruling on the constitutionality of laws and acts upon challenges brought through the acción de inconstitucionalidad;
  • Resolving certain amparo cases of special significance; and
  • Ensuring the uniformity of interpretation of federal law.

Landmark decisions

In December 2009, the court released a facultad de atracción decision, exercising its power to directly request a review of the lower court decisions for the release of the three women arrested in 2006 for the kidnapping of six federal agents in Querétaro; the women were later released. In a series of decisions from 2010 onward, the court upheld the constitutionality of same-sex marriage legislation in Mexico City, and in 2015 it issued a jurisprudential thesis holding that state laws restricting marriage to heterosexual couples were unconstitutional. Beginning in 2018, the court issued a series of rulings on the personal use of cannabis, and in 2021 it decriminalised private recreational use of marijuana in a general declaration of unconstitutionality. In September 2023, the court decriminalised abortion at the federal level.

Presidents of the Court

The president of the Supreme Court presides over the plenary sessions, represents the Federal Judiciary before the other branches of government, and administers the court's affairs. Notable recent presidents include Arturo Zaldívar (2019–2022) and Norma Lucía Piña Hernández, who took office in January 2023 as the first woman to preside over the court.
