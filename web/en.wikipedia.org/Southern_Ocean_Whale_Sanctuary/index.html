Southern Ocean Whale Sanctuary

A southern right whale in the Southern Ocean

The Southern Ocean Whale Sanctuary is an area of 50 million square kilometres surrounding the continent of Antarctica where the International Whaling Commission (IWC) has banned all types of commercial whaling. At present, this and the Indian Ocean Sanctuary, adopted in 1979, are the only two whale sanctuaries designated by the IWC.

History

The establishment of IWC sanctuaries has a long history. The idea of a whale sanctuary was first proposed in 1975 at a meeting of non-governmental organizations attended by the IWC. The Southern Ocean Sanctuary was formally adopted by the IWC in 1994, with 23 member states supporting the proposal, although some whaling nations opposed it. The sanctuary came into force in December 1994, and under IWC rules it is subject to review at ten-year intervals.

Area

The boundaries of the sanctuary are as follows: following the coast of Antarctica south of 40°S latitude where it meets the borders of the Indian Ocean Sanctuary, then following the borders of the IWC management areas around the continent. The Antarctic Treaty area south of 60°S is also included. The sanctuary covers the circumpolar waters in which many of the world's great whales feed during the austral summer.

Whaling dispute

Japan conducted whaling inside the sanctuary under scientific permit for many years, a practice that was challenged by other member states. In March 2014 the International Court of Justice ruled that Japan's JARPA II programme was not "for purposes of scientific research" within the meaning of the International Convention for the Regulation of Whaling, and ordered Japan to revoke existing permits. Japan subsequently ended its Antarctic whaling programme.
