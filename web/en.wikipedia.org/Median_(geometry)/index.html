Median (geometry)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In geometry, a median of a triangle is a line segment joining a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side, thus bisecting that side. Every triangle has exactly three medians, one from each vertex, and they all intersect each other at the triangle's centroid.

Properties

The three medians intersect at a single point, the centroid, which divides each median in the ratio 2:1, with the longer part towards the vertex. Each median divides the triangle into two smaller triangles of equal area. The length of a median can be computed using Apollonius's theorem.

Relation to the centroid

The centroid is the centre of mass of a uniform triangular lamina. In coordinates, it is the average of the three vertices' coordinates.

Median in a tetrahedron

In a tetrahedron, a median is a segment joining a vertex to the centroid of the opposite face. The four medians of a tetrahedron are concurrent at the tetrahedron's centroid.
