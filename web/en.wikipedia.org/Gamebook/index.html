Gamebook

A gamebook is a work of printed fiction that allows the reader to participate in the story by making choices. The narrative branches along various paths, typically through the use of numbered paragraphs or pages. Once narrative-based role-playing reached a wider audience in the 1980s, gamebooks achieved mass popularity through series such as Choose Your Own Adventure, Fighting Fantasy, and Lone Wolf.

Types

Branching-plot gamebooks, like Choose Your Own Adventure, present pure choices with no game mechanics. Solitaire role-playing gamebooks, exemplified by Fighting Fantasy (begun 1982 with The Warlock of Firetop Mountain by Steve Jackson and Ian Livingstone), add dice-based combat, statistics, and inventories, importing the machinery of tabletop role-playing games into book form. Adventure sheet and codeword systems track the reader's progress across paragraph references.

History

Precursors include Jorge Luis Borges's story "The Garden of Forking Paths" (1941) and early instructional branching texts. The format's commercial breakthrough came with Bantam's Choose Your Own Adventure (from 1979), which sold hundreds of millions of copies. After a decline in the 1990s, gamebooks experienced revivals through reprints, mobile apps, and digital interactive fiction.
