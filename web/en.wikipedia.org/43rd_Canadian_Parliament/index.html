The 43rd Canadian Parliament was in session from December 5, 2019, to August 15, 2021. Its membership was set by the 2019 federal election that had been held on October 21, 2019. The governing Liberal Party under Prime Minister Justin Trudeau formed a minority government.

Composition

The House of Commons consisted of 157 Liberals, 121 Conservatives, 32 members of the Bloc Québécois, 24 New Democrats, 3 Greens, and 1 independent at the opening of the Parliament. Anthony Rota served as Speaker of the House of Commons.

Major events

The Parliament was dominated by the federal response to the COVID-19 pandemic, including emergency benefit legislation such as the Canada Emergency Response Benefit (CERB). It also saw the WE Charity scandal, the prorogation of Parliament in August 2020, and a throne speech that survived a confidence vote in October 2020. Finance Minister Bill Morneau resigned in August 2020 and was succeeded by Chrystia Freeland.

Dissolution

The Parliament was dissolved on August 15, 2021, when Prime Minister Trudeau advised Governor General Mary Simon to call a snap election, which was held on September 20, 2021, producing the 44th Canadian Parliament.
