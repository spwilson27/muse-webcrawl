El Capitan

El Capitan is a vertical rock formation in Yosemite National Park, on the north side of Yosemite Valley, near its western end.

El Capitan is a vertical rock formation in Yosemite National Park, in the U.S. state of California. The granite monolith rises about 3,000 feet (900 m) from base to summit above Yosemite Valley and is one of the world's most famous rock-climbing challenges.

Geology

El Capitan is composed almost entirely of granite, part of the intrusive suite formed around 100 million years ago and later exposed by glaciation. Its sheer face was sculpted by glaciers that carved Yosemite Valley, and its name — Spanish for "the captain" — was given by the Mariposa Battalion in 1851 as a loose translation of a Native American name.

Climbing history

The formation was long considered unclimbable until Warren Harding, Wayne Merry and George Whitmore ascended The Nose in 1958 after 47 days of expedition-style effort. In 1975 The Nose was climbed in a day. The wall became the crucible of modern free climbing: Lynn Hill freed The Nose in 1993, and in June 2017 Alex Honnold made the first free solo ascent of El Capitan via the Freerider route, one of the most celebrated feats in climbing history. Speed records on The Nose now stand under two hours.

In popular culture

El Capitan is one of the most photographed features of Yosemite and lent its name to Mac OS X 10.11 "El Capitan." Its summit can also be reached by hiking trails from the valley and Tamarack Flat.
