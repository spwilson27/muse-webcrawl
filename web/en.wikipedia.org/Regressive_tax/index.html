Regressive tax

A regressive tax is a tax in which the effective rate decreases as the amount subject to tax increases, so that lower-income taxpayers pay a larger share of their income than higher-income taxpayers. It is the opposite of a progressive tax.

Examples

Sales taxes and excise duties are often considered regressive in effect because lower-income households spend a larger fraction of their income on taxed consumption. Payroll taxes capped at a maximum wage base, such as the United States Social Security tax, are regressive above the cap. Flat charges such as poll taxes are the extreme case, taking the same absolute amount from everyone.

Analysis

Whether a fiscal system is regressive overall depends on the combined effect of all taxes and transfers. Economists and policymakers debate regressivity when designing consumption taxes, often pairing them with exemptions for necessities or refundable credits to offset the burden on poorer households.
