Bayezid II (3 December 1447 – 26 May 1512) was the eldest son of Mehmed II and the eighth sultan of the Ottoman Empire, reigning from 1481 to 1512. Nicknamed Bayezid the Just (Adlî) and, for his piety, Bayezid Veli ("the Saint"), he consolidated the empire after his father's rapid conquests and is remembered as a patron of culture.

Reign

Bayezid's accession was contested by his brother Cem, whose long exile — eventually in the hands of the Knights Hospitaller and then the Papacy — was used by European powers as leverage against the Ottomans. Bayezid fought wars with Venice (1499–1503), gaining strongholds in the Morea, campaigned against the Mamluks in the 1480s, and annexed Karaman and other Anatolian principalities. He struggled meanwhile with the rising Safavid power in the east and the Qizilbash movement among Anatolian Turcomans.

Jewish refugees from Spain

After the Alhambra Decree of 1492 expelled the Jews of Spain, Bayezid welcomed the refugees — famously remarking that Ferdinand was impoverishing his own realm while enriching the Ottoman one. Sephardic Jews settled in Constantinople, Salonica, Edirne and elsewhere, bringing printing, medicine and trade skills.

Abdication and death

In his last years his sons Ahmed and Selim fought for the succession. Selim, backed by the Janissaries, forced his father's abdication in April 1512; Bayezid died a month later on the road to exile at Didymoteicho.
