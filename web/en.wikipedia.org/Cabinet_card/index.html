Cabinet card

A cabinet card is a style of photograph that was widely used for photographic portraiture after 1870. It consists of a thin albumen photographic print mounted on a card typically measuring 108 by 165 mm (about 4¼ by 6½ inches).

The cabinet card superseded the smaller carte de visite in the 1870s and remained the dominant commercial portrait format through the 1890s. The larger image area allowed sharper prints, more elaborate painted backdrops and props, and better retouching. Photographers usually printed their studio name and address on the front margin and an ornate advertisement on the back, which helps modern collectors date and attribute cards.

Decline

By the early 1900s the cabinet card was displaced by snapshot photography, amateur cameras such as the Kodak Brownie, and real-photo postcards, though formal studio cabinet portraits continued into the 1920s. Today cabinet cards are collected as ephemera and are valuable primary sources for genealogy, costume history and local history.
