The Kashi Vishwanath Temple is one of the most sacred Hindu temples, dedicated to Shiva in his form as Vishwanath, Lord of the Universe, in the ancient city of Varanasi (Kashi) on the banks of the Ganga. It houses one of the twelve revered Jyotirlinga shrines, and Hindus believe that a visit to the temple and a bath in the Ganga lead to liberation (moksha).

History

The temple has been destroyed and rebuilt repeatedly over the centuries. The present structure was built in 1780 by the Maratha queen Ahilyabai Holkar of Indore; Maharaja Ranjit Singh of Punjab later donated the gold that plates its spires, giving it the popular name "the Golden Temple." The original lingam is said to have been hidden for protection in the adjacent Jnana Vapi (Well of Wisdom) during periods of iconoclasm, and the neighbouring Gyanvapi Mosque, built in the Mughal era on part of the earlier temple site, has been the subject of long-running dispute and litigation.

The Kashi Vishwanath Corridor

In 2021 the Kashi Vishwanath Dham Corridor project was inaugurated, creating a grand paved complex linking the temple directly to the Ganga ghats, easing access for the hundreds of thousands of pilgrims who visit each year, especially during Mahashivaratri and the month of Shravana.
