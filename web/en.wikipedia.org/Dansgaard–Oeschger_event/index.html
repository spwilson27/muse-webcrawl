Dansgaard–Oeschger event

Named forWilli Dansgaard and Hans Oeschger
PeriodLast glacial period, ~115,000–12,000 years ago
Number known25 (DO 1–25)
Key evidenceGreenland ice cores (GRIP, GISP2, NGRIP)

Dansgaard–Oeschger events (often abbreviated D–O events) are rapid climate fluctuations that occurred 25 times during the last glacial period. Some scientists say the events occur quasi-periodically with a recurrence time of about 1,470 years, though this is debated. Greenland ice cores show warming of as much as 8–16 °C within a few decades at the start of each event, followed by gradual cooling over centuries.

Characteristics

Each event has a characteristic sawtooth shape: extremely abrupt warming — in some cases detectable within years — then a plateau (interstadial) lasting centuries to over a millennium, then a slower return to full glacial (stadial) cold. The warming appears largest around the North Atlantic and Greenland; signals of opposite sign in Antarctica (the "bipolar seesaw") suggest changes in ocean heat transport. D–O events occur bundled with the larger Bond cycles and are followed, in some cases, by Heinrich events — massive iceberg discharges into the North Atlantic.

Causes

The leading explanation involves reorganizations of the Atlantic meridional overturning circulation (AMOC) and North Atlantic sea ice: when sea ice retracts and deep-water formation reinvigorates, heat is rapidly released to the atmosphere over Greenland and the Nordic Seas. Triggers proposed include freshwater forcing, internal ocean–sea-ice oscillations, and solar or tidal modulation; no single mechanism is settled. Climate models reproduce similar abrupt shifts, which is why D–O events are studied as past analogues for potential future tipping points in the climate system.

Discovery

The events were identified in the 1980s–90s in isotope records from the Camp Century, Dye 3, GRIP, and GISP2 ice cores, and are named for Willi Dansgaard and Hans Oeschger, pioneers of ice-core isotope paleoclimatology.
