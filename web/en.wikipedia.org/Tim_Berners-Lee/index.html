Tim Berners-Lee

Sir Timothy John Berners-Lee (born 8 June 1955), also known as TimBL, is an English computer scientist best known as the inventor of the World Wide Web, the HTML markup language, the URL system and HTTP. He proposed an information management system on 12 March 1989, and by the end of 1990 had implemented the first web browser and web server while working at CERN, the European Organization for Nuclear Research, in Geneva.

Early life and career

Berners-Lee was born in London to mathematicians Mary Lee Woods and Conway Berners-Lee, who worked on the Ferranti Mark 1, one of the first commercial computers. He studied physics at Oxford, graduating in 1976, and built his first computer from a television set and a microprocessor while at university. After work at Plessey and D. G. Nash, he arrived at CERN in 1980 as a contractor, where he wrote a program called Enquire — a personal hypertext notebook for keeping track of people, projects and machines — which foreshadowed the Web.

Inventing the Web

In March 1989 Berners-Lee circulated a proposal, "Information Management: A Proposal", for a decentralised hypertext system built on the internet; his supervisor Mike Sendall famously annotated it "vague but exciting". With Robert Cailliau he refined the idea, and by December 1990 he had created the first website, at info.cern.ch, the first browser-editor (called WorldWideWeb, running on a NeXT computer), the HTTP protocol and HTML. On 30 April 1993, CERN announced that the Web's technology would be placed in the public domain, royalty-free — a decision Berners-Lee has called essential to its explosive growth. The Web subsequently reached a billion websites and transformed publishing, commerce and communication.

Later work

In 1994 Berners-Lee founded the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C) at MIT to steward web standards. He has been a leading advocate of the Semantic Web and of open, linked data, coining the five-star deployment scheme for open government data. He co-founded the World Wide Web Foundation in 2009, has campaigned for net neutrality and affordable internet access, and launched the "Contract for the Web" in 2019. His later technical project, Solid (developed with Inrupt, the company he co-founded), aims to let users store their data in personal "pods" and control who accesses it. He was knighted in 2004, appointed to the Order of Merit in 2007, and received the ACM Turing Award in 2016 "for inventing the World Wide Web, the first web browser, and the fundamental protocols and algorithms allowing the Web to scale".
