Don Quixote

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Don Quixote (full title: The Ingenious Gentleman Don Quixote of La Mancha, Spanish: El ingenioso hidalgo don Quixote de la Mancha) is a Spanish novel by Miguel de Cervantes, published in two parts, in 1605 and 1615. It is widely considered the first modern novel and one of the greatest works of world literature.

Plot

Alonso Quixano, a minor nobleman (hidalgo) from La Mancha, reads so many chivalric romances that he loses his mind and decides to become a knight-errant under the name Don Quixote. He recruits the peasant Sancho Panza as his squire, and together they ride out in search of adventure. Quixote mistakes inns for castles, flocks of sheep for armies and windmills for giants; the gap between his fantasy and reality, and the interplay between the idealist knight and his pragmatic squire, generate the novel's comedy and pathos. In Part II (1615), written after a spurious sequel by another author appeared, the characters know they are famous from Part I, and the tale moves toward Quixote's disillusionment, return home and death.

Significance

Don Quixote parodied the romances of chivalry while pioneering the modern novel's techniques: realistic dialogue, multiple narrative perspectives and the fictional "found manuscript" of Cide Hamete Benengeli. It has been translated into more than 140 languages and has influenced writers from Fielding and Sterne to Dostoevsky and Flaubert. In a 2002 poll of notable authors organised by the Norwegian Book Clubs, it was voted the best book of all time.
