Lady Sings the Blues (film)

Lady Sings the Blues
Directed by: Sidney J. Furie
Released: 1972
Starring: Diana Ross, Billy Dee Williams, Richard Pryor

Lady Sings the Blues is a 1972 American biographical drama film about jazz singer Billie Holiday, based on her 1956 autobiography of the same name. Produced by Motown, it marked the film debut of Diana Ross as Holiday, with Billy Dee Williams as her lover Louis McKay and Richard Pryor as the pianist.

The film follows Holiday from her troubled youth in Baltimore through her rise as a singer, her struggles with racism on tour in the South, and her heroin addiction and arrests. Though critics noted liberties with the facts, Ross's performance was widely praised and she was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Actress. Ross also recorded the soundtrack album, which reached number one on the Billboard albums chart.
