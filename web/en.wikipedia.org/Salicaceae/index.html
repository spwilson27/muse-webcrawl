Salicaceae

The Salicaceae are the willow family of flowering plants. The traditional family (Salicaceae sensu stricto) included the willows, poplars, aspens, and cottonwoods. Genetic studies summarised by the Angiosperm Phylogeny Group (APG) have greatly expanded the circumscription of the family to include many genera previously classified in the Flacourtiaceae. In the APG IV system, Salicaceae contains 56 genera and about 1,220 species, and is placed in the order Malpighiales.

Characteristics

Members of the family in its narrow sense are trees and shrubs with simple, alternate, stipulate leaves and flowers borne in catkins. The classical genera Salix (willows, about 350 species) and Populus (poplars and aspens, about 35 species) are dioecious, wind- or insect-pollinated, and produce small seeds with tufts of hair for wind dispersal. The expanded family includes many tropical trees with diverse floral structures.

Uses

Poplars are grown for timber, paper pulp, and windbreaks, and willows for basketry, cricket bats, and landscape planting. Willow bark contains salicin, the natural precursor from which aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid) was developed, and has a long history of use as an analgesic.
