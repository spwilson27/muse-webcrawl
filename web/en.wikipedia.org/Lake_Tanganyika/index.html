Lake Tanganyika is an African Great Lake. It is the world's second-largest freshwater lake by volume and the world's second-deepest, after Lake Baikal in Siberia. It is also the world's longest freshwater lake, stretching 673 km in the western branch of the East African Rift. Its waters are shared by Burundi, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Tanzania, and Zambia.

Geography

The lake averages 50 km in width and plunges to 1,436 metres; its surface lies 773 metres above sea level, so its floor is far below sea level. Warm surface water overlies permanently anoxic deep water. The lake drains westward through the Lukuga River into the Congo River system, though the outflow has varied historically.

Biodiversity

Tanganyika holds some 250 species of cichlid fish, nearly all endemic, plus hundreds of other endemic invertebrates — crabs, shrimps, snails, and jellyfish. Its fisheries, based on the sardine-like kapenta (Limnothrissa miodon) and Nile perch relatives, employ tens of thousands of people around its shores.

History

Richard Burton and John Hanning Speke reached the lake in 1858 while searching for the source of the Nile. In 1871 Henry Morton Stanley met David Livingstone at Ujiji on its eastern shore. During the First World War, German and Allied forces fought a small naval war on the lake, famously involving the German vessel Graf von Götzen (scuttled 1916, later raised, and still in service as the MV Liemba).
