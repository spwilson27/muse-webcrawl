Cirrostratus cloud

Cirrostratus
Abbreviation: Cs
Altitude: above 6,000 m (high cloud)

Cirrostratus is a high-level cloud genus characterized by a thin, generally uniform sheet of ice crystals that can cover the whole sky. The cloud is often so transparent that the Sun or Moon shines through it, frequently producing halo phenomena.

Characteristics

Cirrostratus forms at altitudes above about 6,000 metres in the troposphere. Its species include cirrostratus fibratus and cirrostratus nebulosus. Because it is composed of ice crystals, it refracts light to form halos around the Sun or Moon—an observation long used in folk weather lore. A thickening and lowering cirrostratus deck often precedes a warm front and approaching precipitation by 12–24 hours.

Differentiation

Unlike altostratus, cirrostratus does not prevent objects on the ground from casting shadows, and unlike cirrus it forms a continuous sheet rather than detached filaments.
