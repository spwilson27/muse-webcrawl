Laxdæla saga

Laxdæla saga
Author: Unknown (attributed to a man of the Laxárdalur region)
Language: Old Icelandic
Genre: Family saga
Date: c. 1230–1260

Laxdæla saga is one of the Icelandic family sagas (Íslendingasögur), probably written in the mid-13th century. It tells the story of the people of Laxárdalur, a valley in western Iceland, over several generations from the late 9th century to the early 11th century.

Synopsis

The saga follows the descendants of Ketill Flatnose and his daughter Unnr the Deep-Minded, who settled in Iceland after leaving Norway and Scotland. Its central narrative concerns the love triangle between Guðrún Ósvífursdóttir and the foster-brothers Kjartan Óláfsson and Bolli Þorleiksson, which ends in Kjartan's death at Bolli's hands and Bolli's subsequent killing by Kjartan's brothers. Guðrún, married four times, ends her life as a nun and anchoress, famously telling her son that she was worst to the one she loved most.

Themes and style

The saga is noted for its strong female characters, its tragic treatment of love and honour, and its occasional romantic and chivalric colouring, which has led some scholars to suspect influence from continental romance. It survives complete in the 14th-century Möðruvallabók manuscript.
