Casting (manufacturing)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Casting is a manufacturing process in which a liquid material — usually metal, but also plaster, concrete, resin or plastic — is poured into a mould containing a hollow cavity of the desired shape, and then allowed to solidify. The solidified part, also known as a casting, is removed from the mould and may be finished by machining, grinding or polishing. Casting is one of the oldest shaping processes known: the earliest surviving casting, a copper frog from Mesopotamia, dates to about 3200 BC.

Processes

Casting processes divide into two main categories. Expendable-mould processes use a mould that is destroyed to release each part; these include sand casting (by far the most common, used for engine blocks and large machinery), investment (lost-wax) casting, used since antiquity for jewellery and now for turbine blades, and plaster and shell moulding. Permanent-mould processes reuse metal moulds thousands of times and include die casting (low- and high-pressure), permanent mould casting, and centrifugal casting for pipes and cylinders.

Principles

Sound castings depend on mould design, gating and risering (channels that feed metal and compensate for shrinkage on solidification), and control of defects such as porosity, shrinkage cavities, inclusions and cold shuts. Solidification follows Chvorinov's rule, relating freezing time to the volume-to-surface-area ratio of the casting. Modern foundries use computer simulation of metal flow and solidification to design moulds before production.

Applications

Cast parts range from gram-scale dental implants and jewellery to propellers, press frames and turbine housings weighing tonnes. Roughly 90 per cent of all manufactured goods contain at least one cast component, making casting a foundational process of industry.

History

Lost-wax casting was practised in the Indus Valley and Near East by the 4th millennium BC; China developed sophisticated bronze piece-mould casting for ritual vessels in the Shang dynasty, and cast iron was produced in China by the 5th century BC, more than a millennium before it appeared in Europe. Abraham Darby's coke-smelted cast iron at Coalbrookdale (1709) helped launch the Industrial Revolution.
