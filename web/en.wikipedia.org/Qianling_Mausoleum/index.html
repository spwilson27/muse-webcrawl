Qianling Mausoleum

The spirit way at Qianling

The Qianling Mausoleum (Chinese: 乾陵; pinyin: Qiánlíng) is a Tang dynasty tomb complex in Qian County, Shaanxi, China, built in 684 for the Emperor Gaozong of Tang and his wife, Wu Zetian — China's only female emperor. It is unique among Chinese imperial tombs in holding the burials of two emperors (Wu Zetian ruled in her own name after Gaozong's death).

The complex

The mausoleum occupies Mount Liang and follows the Tang practice of using a natural mountain as the tomb mound. A long spirit way lined with monumental stone sculptures — including ostriches, winged horses, court officials and the famous statues of 61 foreign ambassadors (whose heads were later removed) — leads to the tomb. Near it stand the Wordless Stele, a large blank tablet associated with Wu Zetian, and the Seven-joints (Qijie) Stele recording Gaozong's achievements. The complex includes 17 satellite tombs of imperial family members and officials, several of which — those of Princess Yongtai, Crown Prince Zhanghuai and Crown Prince Yide — have been excavated and are famous for their vivid wall paintings, now preserved in the Shaanxi History Museum.

Preservation

The main tomb itself has never been excavated and is believed to be intact, having resisted attempted robberies over the centuries, including a large-scale attempt during the late Tang rebellions. Surveys suggest the burial chamber remains sealed; the Chinese government has so far chosen not to excavate it.
