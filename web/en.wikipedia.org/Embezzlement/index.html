Embezzlement

Embezzlement is the act of withholding assets for the purpose of conversion (theft) of such assets, by one or more persons to whom the assets were entrusted, either to be held or to be used for specific purposes. Embezzlement is a type of financial fraud; for example, an employee who steals money from their employer's accounts, or a lawyer who misappropriates funds held in trust for a client, commits embezzlement.

Legal elements

In many jurisdictions, embezzlement requires that the defendant lawfully possessed the property — distinguishing it from theft or larceny, where possession is unlawful from the outset — and then fraudulently converted it to their own use. The crime may be committed by employees, public officials, treasurers or others in positions of trust. Penalties typically scale with the amount taken and the offender's position.

Notable cases

Large-scale embezzlement has featured in major corporate frauds and in schemes such as that perpetrated by Bernard Madoff, whose Ponzi scheme also involved the misappropriation of client funds.
