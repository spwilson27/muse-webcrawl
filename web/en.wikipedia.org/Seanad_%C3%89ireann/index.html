Seanad Éireann

Seanad Éireann (English: Senate of Ireland) is the upper house of the Oireachtas, the legislature of Ireland. It consists of 60 members, known as senators. Unlike the lower house, Dáil Éireann, senators are not directly elected by the general public; most are elected by panels, by university graduates, or nominated by the Taoiseach.

Composition

Of the 60 senators, 43 are elected from five vocational panels (Administrative, Agricultural, Cultural and Educational, Industrial and Commercial, and Labour), 6 are elected by graduates of the National University of Ireland and the University of Dublin, and 11 are nominated by the Taoiseach, ensuring the government of the day normally has a majority in the house.

Powers

The Seanad's powers are considerably weaker than those of the Dáil. It can debate and amend legislation, but it can only delay most bills rather than veto them, and it has no role in money bills beyond making recommendations. Its debates often focus on scrutiny and revision of legislation.

History and reform

A senate existed in the Irish Free State from 1922 until its abolition in 1936; the present Seanad was established under the Constitution of Ireland in 1937. In 2013 a referendum proposed abolishing the Seanad, but voters narrowly rejected the proposal, and debates over reform of its election and role have continued since.
