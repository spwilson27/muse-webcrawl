The Mosuo are an ethnic group living in China's Yunnan and Sichuan provinces, near the shores of Lugu Lake on the Tibetan plateau frontier. They are renowned for their matrilineal social organisation and the practice of "walking marriages", and are often described in popular literature as one of the world's last matrilineal societies. Chinese authorities classify them as part of the Naxi nationality, though the Mosuo regard themselves as distinct.

Social organisation

Traditional Mosuo households are headed by a matriarch (the dabu), with property, surnames and children passing through the female line. Children are raised in their mother's household; brothers remain there throughout their lives, acting as maternal uncles to their sisters' children.

Walking marriage

In the tisese or "walking marriage" custom, partners do not cohabit: a man visits a woman at night in her family's house and returns to his own maternal home before dawn. Unions are based on mutual affection, may last a night or a lifetime, and can be ended by either partner. Children belong to the mother's household, and biological fathers have no necessary social role, though many are acknowledged and involved.

Culture

Mosuo religion blends the indigenous Dongba shamanic tradition with Tibetan Buddhism. In recent decades tourism at Lugu Lake has brought income and attention, along with pressure on traditional customs, which are gradually changing as younger Mosuo migrate for work and education.
