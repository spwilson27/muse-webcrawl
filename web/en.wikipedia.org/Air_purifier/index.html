Air purifier

An air purifier or air cleaner is a device that removes contaminants — such as dust, pollen, mould spores, pet dander, smoke particles and some gases — from the air in a room. Purifiers are marketed for allergy and asthma sufferers and for reducing second-hand smoke and wildfire smoke indoors.

Techniques

  • Filtration: A fan draws air through filters. High-efficiency particulate air (HEPA) filters trap 99.97% of particles of 0.3 micrometres. Activated carbon filters adsorb odours and volatile organic compounds.
  • Ultraviolet germicidal irradiation (UVGI): UV-C light used to inactivate bacteria and viruses, usually combined with filtration.
  • Ionisation: Ionisers charge particles so they settle or are collected; some generate ozone, a lung irritant, and are discouraged by regulators such as the U.S. EPA and California Air Resources Board.
  • Photocatalytic oxidation and other gas-phase technologies of varying demonstrated effectiveness.

Performance ratings

In the United States, portable purifiers are rated by Clean Air Delivery Rate (CADR), measured under ANSI/AHAM standards, which indicates how much filtered air the unit delivers for dust, pollen and tobacco smoke. Mechanical HEPA filtration has strong evidence for reducing indoor particle concentrations; benefits for health outcomes are more modest and depend on the pollutant.

History

Industrial air cleaning developed in the mid-twentieth century, and the HEPA filter itself originated in the 1940s for containing radioactive contaminants. Domestic purifiers became widespread with the growth of allergy awareness, and demand surged during the COVID-19 pandemic and severe wildfire seasons, when agencies also promoted do-it-yourself filter-fan boxes such as the Corsi–Rosenthal Box.
