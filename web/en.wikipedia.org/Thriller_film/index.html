Thriller film

Thriller film, also known as a suspense film or suspense thriller, is a broad film genre that evokes excitement and suspense in the audience. The thriller film creates a moody, tense atmosphere, often through the protagonist's confrontation with danger, mystery, or an antagonist. Tension is built through delaying what the audience expects, red herrings, cliffhangers, and plot twists. Alfred Hitchcock, the "Master of Suspense", is the genre's most famous practitioner.

Characteristics

Thrillers typically feature an ordinary protagonist thrust into extraordinary danger, a race against the clock, resourceful villains, and high stakes involving life and death. The audience often knows more than the characters — the classic example being Hitchcock's bomb-under-the-table theory of suspense — or, conversely, shares the protagonist's uncertainty. Common devices include the MacGuffin, mistaken identity, the wrong-man plot, and unreliable narration.

Subgenres

  • Psychological thriller — conflict between characters' minds rather than physical action (Se7en, Black Swan).
  • Crime thriller — centred on criminal acts and their investigation (Heat, The Silence of the Lambs).
  • Spy thriller — espionage and intelligence agencies (the James Bond and Jason Bourne series).
  • Action thriller — combines suspense with action-movie set pieces (Die Hard, Mad Max: Fury Road).
  • Horror thriller / supernatural thriller — blends suspense with horror elements.
  • Legal and political thrillers — courtroom and governmental intrigue.
  • Erotic thriller — prominent in the 1980s–90s (Fatal Attraction, Basic Instinct).
  • Techno-thriller — advanced technology and military hardware, adapted from writers such as Tom Clancy.

History

Early thrillers included Harold Lloyd's Safety Last! (1923) and Hitchcock's silent The Lodger (1927). Hitchcock's British and Hollywood films of the 1930s–1960s — The 39 Steps, Rear Window, Vertigo, North by Northwest, and Psycho — defined the form. Film noir overlapped heavily with the thriller in the 1940s. The 1970s brought paranoid conspiracy thrillers such as The Conversation and Marathon Man, and modern masters include David Fincher, Denis Villeneuve, and Bong Joon-ho. Successful recent thrillers such as Gone Girl and Knives Out show the genre's continued popularity across theatrical and streaming platforms.
