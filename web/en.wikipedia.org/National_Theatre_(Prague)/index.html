National Theatre (Prague)

National Theatre

Location: Prague, Czech Republic

Architect: Josef Zítek

Opened: 11 June 1881; reopened 18 November 1883

The National Theatre (Czech: Národní divadlo) in Prague is known as the alma mater of Czech opera, and as the national monument of Czech history and art. It stands on the banks of the Vltava River, opposite Prague Castle, and is one of the most important and recognizable buildings in the Czech capital.

History

The idea of building a dignified national theatre for the Czech nation took shape in the mid-19th century, during the Czech National Revival. Funds were raised by public collection from the whole nation; the foundation stones were laid in 1868, with stones brought from significant Czech sites. The theatre, designed by architect Josef Zítek in the Neo-Renaissance style, first opened on 11 June 1881 with a performance of Bedřich Smetana's opera Libuše, composed for the occasion.

Shortly after opening, the building was severely damaged by fire. The nation again collected funds, and the theatre, restored under architect Josef Schulz, reopened on 18 November 1883, again with Libuše. The motto above the stage curtain reads "Národ sobě" — "The Nation Unto Itself."

Artistic ensembles

The National Theatre houses three artistic ensembles: opera, drama, and ballet. Performances also take place at affiliated stages such as the Estates Theatre, the State Opera, and the New Stage (Nová scéna), a distinctive glass building opened in 1983.
