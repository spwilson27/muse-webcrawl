Army Ranger Wing

The Army Ranger Wing (ARW; Irish: Sciathán Fianóglach an Airm) is the special operations force of the Irish Defence Forces. Established in 1980, it is based at the Curragh Camp in County Kildare and serves as Ireland's principal counter-terrorism and special reconnaissance unit. Its motto is Glaine ár gcroí, neart ár ngéag agus beart de réir ár mbriathar — "purity of our hearts, strength of our limbs and commitment to our promise".

Role and selection

The ARW undertakes special operations at home and overseas, including counter-terrorism, hostage rescue, special reconnaissance, direct action and the protection of dignitaries, as well as support to the Garda Síochána. Selection and training are extremely demanding, covering parachuting, combat diving, counter-terrorism tactics, survival and intelligence work; only a small fraction of candidates complete the course. Members wear a green beret and the distinctive Fianóglach shoulder tab.

Operations

The unit has deployed on peace-support and evacuation missions with Irish contingents abroad, including in East Timor, Liberia, Chad and Lebanon, and has carried out non-combatant evacuations of Irish citizens from conflict zones. Domestically it provides specialist support in armed incidents and has been involved in high-profile operations against organised crime and dissident republican groups.
