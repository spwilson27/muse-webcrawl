From Dictatorship to Democracy

From Dictatorship to Democracy is a short book-length essay on the theory of nonviolent action by political scientist Gene Sharp, first published in 1993 in Bangkok at the request of exiled Burmese dissidents. Subtitled "A Conceptual Framework for Liberation", it has been translated into dozens of languages and circulated widely among pro-democracy movements.

Content

Sharp argues that the power of any dictator depends on the obedience and cooperation of subjects and institutions — bureaucracies, security forces, courts, and media. If that cooperation is withdrawn through organised nonviolent resistance, the regime's pillars of support collapse and dictatorship can disintegrate without armed struggle. The book outlines strategies for planning a nonviolent campaign, selecting methods from Sharp's catalogue of 198 methods of nonviolent action, and managing the transition to democracy without creating a vacuum that a new dictator could fill.

Influence

The work, along with Sharp's other writings, has been credited with influencing resistance movements in Serbia, Ukraine, Georgia, Egypt, and elsewhere, and is frequently distributed as a free PDF by the Albert Einstein Institution. Critics note that translations and dissemination were sometimes funded by Western organisations, which regimes have cited when accusing activists of foreign direction; Sharp maintained the work is a generic framework usable by anyone.
