White Sands Missile Range

White Sands Missile Range
Location: Tularosa Basin, New Mexico
Area: about 8,300 km² (3,200 sq mi)
Established: 1945
Operator: United States Army

White Sands Missile Range (WSMR) is a United States Army military testing area and restricted airspace in the Tularosa Basin of south-central New Mexico. At roughly 3,200 square miles, it is the largest military installation in the United States. The range includes the Trinity Site, where the first atomic bomb was detonated on July 16, 1945.

History

Established in 1945 as White Sands Proving Ground, the range hosted tests of captured German V-2 rockets under scientists including Wernher von Braun, and early American sounding rockets. NASA has operated the White Sands Test Facility on the range, and the site was an alternate landing location for the Space Shuttle; Columbia landed there in 1982 on mission STS-3.

Geography

The range overlaps White Sands National Park's gypsum dune field and contains the San Andres Mountains. U.S. Route 70 crossing the area is periodically closed during missile tests.
