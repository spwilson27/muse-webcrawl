Ol Doinyo Lengai is an active volcano located in the Gregory Rift, south of Lake Natron in the Arusha Region of Tanzania. Its name means "Mountain of God" in the Maasai language. Standing about 3,188 metres above sea level, it is notable as the only volcano in the world known to erupt natrocarbonatite lava.

Geology

Unlike most lavas, which are silicate-rich, the lava of Ol Doinyo Lengai is rich in sodium and potassium carbonates, particularly nyerereite and gregoryite. This natrocarbonatite lava is relatively cool, erupting at temperatures of roughly 510 °C, and is fluid enough to flow almost like water. Fresh black lava rapidly weathers to a white colour on exposure to moisture in the air.

Eruptive history

The volcano has erupted repeatedly in recorded history, with significant explosive eruptions in 1917, 1940, 1960–1966, and 2007–2008, the latter forcing the evacuation of nearby villages and disrupting air traffic. Between major events, lava quietly fills the northern crater.

Cultural significance

The Maasai regard the mountain as the home of the god Engai, and its eruptions are traditionally interpreted as signs of the god's displeasure.
