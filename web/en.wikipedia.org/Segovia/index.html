Segovia is a city in the autonomous community of Castile and León, Spain, northwest of Madrid. It is famous for its well-preserved Roman aqueduct, one of the most significant Roman structures in Spain, built around the end of the 1st century AD.

Landmarks

The aqueduct of Segovia carries water some 15 kilometres from the mountains into the city and rises nearly 30 metres above the Plaza del Azoguejo. Other landmarks include the Alcázar of Segovia, a medieval fortress said to have inspired the design of Cinderella's castle, and Segovia Cathedral, a late Gothic church consecrated in 1768 and sometimes called the "Lady of Cathedrals."

History

Originally a Celtiberian settlement, Segovia passed under Roman rule and later was a favoured residence of the monarchs of Castile. Isabella I was proclaimed queen in Segovia in 1474. The old town and aqueduct were declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1985.
