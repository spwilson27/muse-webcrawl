Monashee Mountains

RangeColumbia Mountains
Highest pointMount Monashee, 3,274 m
LocationBritish Columbia, Canada

The Monashee Mountains are a mountain range in British Columbia, Canada, forming the westernmost subrange of the Columbia Mountains. They run roughly 530 kilometres from north to south, lying between the Okanagan Valley to the west and the Columbia River and Arrow Lakes to the east.

Geography

The range is bordered by the Shuswap Highland to the west and the Selkirk Mountains across the Columbia River to the east. Its northern end reaches toward the Rocky Mountain Trench near Valemount, while in the south it extends close to the Canada–United States border. Major peaks include Mount Monashee, Hallam Peak, and Mount Begbie near Revelstoke.

History

The name derives from a Gaelic word meaning "mountain of peace," given by a prospector from the Scottish Highlands. The region saw gold rush activity in the 1860s along the Columbia and its tributaries.

Recreation

The Monashees are known for deep powder skiing; several heli-skiing and cat-skiing operations are based near Revelstoke, and the range is traversed by the Trans-Canada Highway at Rogers Pass's western approaches and by Eagle Pass.
