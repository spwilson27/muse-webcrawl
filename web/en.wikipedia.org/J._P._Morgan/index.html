J. P. Morgan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

John Pierpont Morgan (April 17, 1837 – March 31, 1913) was an American financier and investment banker who dominated corporate finance on Wall Street throughout the Gilded Age and Progressive Era. As head of the banking firm that became J.P. Morgan and Co., Morgan was a driving force behind the formation of many multinational corporations.

Career

Morgan reorganized and consolidated railroads, and financed industrial consolidations that created General Electric, United States Steel, and International Harvester. In the Panic of 1893 he helped replenish the U.S. Treasury's gold reserves; in the Panic of 1907, acting as a lender of last resort, he organized bankers to stem the crisis — an episode that spurred the creation of the Federal Reserve System.

Legacy

Morgan was a major collector of art and books; his collection forms the core of the Morgan Library & Museum in New York. He died in Rome in 1913, leaving his bank and fortune to his son, J. P. Morgan Jr.
