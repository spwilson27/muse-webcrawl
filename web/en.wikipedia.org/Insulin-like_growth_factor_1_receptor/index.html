The IGF-1 receptor, a disulfide-linked α2β2 receptor tyrosine kinase

The insulin-like growth factor 1 receptor (IGF-1R) is a transmembrane receptor tyrosine kinase activated by the hormone insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1) and, with lower affinity, by IGF-2. It mediates most of the growth-promoting and survival effects of IGF-1 and is essential for normal prenatal and postnatal growth, as well as for the development and maintenance of many tissues. IGF-1R is structurally and evolutionarily close to the insulin receptor, with which it can form hybrid receptors; broadly, IGF-1R signalling drives growth and anti-apoptosis while insulin-receptor signalling drives metabolic regulation, though the pathways overlap.

Structure and signalling

The receptor is synthesised as a single-chain precursor cleaved into α and β subunits, assembled as a disulfide-linked α2β2 tetramer. The extracellular α subunits bind ligand; the transmembrane β subunits contain the intracellular tyrosine kinase domain. Ligand binding triggers autophosphorylation, recruiting adaptor proteins (IRS-1/2, Shc) that activate the PI3K–AKT pathway (cell survival, growth, protein synthesis via mTOR) and the Ras–Raf–MEK–ERK (MAPK) pathway (proliferation and differentiation).

Biology and medicine

Mice lacking IGF-1R die at birth of respiratory failure at about 45% of normal birth weight, demonstrating its centrality to growth; in humans, heterozygous IGF1R mutations cause intrauterine and postnatal growth retardation with microcephaly and developmental delay, while the receptor also influences longevity in model organisms. Because many tumours overexpress IGF-1R or its ligands and depend on its survival signalling, the receptor became a major oncology drug target: monoclonal antibodies (such as figitumumab, ganitumab, dalotuzumab and the approved agent teprotumumab — used, notably, for thyroid eye disease) and tyrosine-kinase inhibitors (linsitinib and others) have been tested, though results in common cancers were largely disappointing, partly because of compensatory insulin-receptor signalling and metabolic side effects such as hyperglycaemia.
