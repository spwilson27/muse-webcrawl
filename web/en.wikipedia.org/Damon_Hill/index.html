Damon Hill

Damon Graham Devereux Hill (born 17 September 1960) is a British former racing driver and broadcaster, who competed in Formula One from 1992 to 1999. Hill won the Formula One World Drivers' Championship in 1996 with Williams. He is the son of two-time world champion Graham Hill, making them the first father-and-son pair to both win the title (a feat later matched by Keke and Nico Rosberg).

Early life and career

Hill was 15 when his father Graham was killed in an air crash in 1975, which left the family in greatly reduced circumstances. He worked as a motorcycle courier and labourer while racing motorbikes, winning the Yamaha production championship, before switching to single-seater cars in 1983 at the relatively late age of 23. He progressed through Formula Ford and Formula 3, finishing third in the 1988 British F3 championship, then Formula 3000, before becoming test driver for Williams in 1991 while racing for the struggling Brabham team in 1992.

Formula One

Promoted to the Williams race seat for 1993 alongside Alain Prost, Hill won his first Grand Prix in Hungary and took three wins that season. In 1994, after the death of teammate Ayrton Senna at Imola, Hill became team leader and took the championship fight with Michael Schumacher to the final race in Adelaide, where a controversial collision decided the title for Schumacher. He challenged again in 1995, and in 1996 won eight races and the World Championship. Despite this, Williams replaced him for 1997; he moved to Arrows, nearly winning the 1997 Hungarian Grand Prix, then to Jordan, where he won the rain-soaked 1998 Belgian Grand Prix — Jordan's first victory. He retired at the end of 1999.

After racing

Hill was president of the British Racing Drivers' Club from 2006 to 2011, overseeing Silverstone's redevelopment and a new long-term British Grand Prix contract. He has worked as a pundit for Sky Sports F1 and published an autobiography, Watching the Wheels, in 2016.
