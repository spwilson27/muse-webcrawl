Air defense

Air defense (or air defence), also called anti-aircraft warfare, is the set of measures designed to defend a military force or territory against attacks from the air, particularly by aircraft, helicopters, drones and missiles. It includes detection (radar and other sensors), command and control, fighter aircraft, surface-to-air missiles (SAMs), anti-aircraft artillery and electronic warfare.

History

Anti-aircraft fire began in earnest during the First World War, when guns were adapted to engage balloons and aeroplanes. In the Second World War, integrated systems such as Britain's Dowding system combined radar, observers, plotting rooms and fighters to defeat the Luftwaffe in the Battle of Britain, while massed flak defended German cities. The proximity fuze and radar-directed guns greatly increased lethality.

After 1945 the surface-to-air missile transformed air defence: Soviet-built S-75 (SA-2) systems shot down a U-2 spyplane in 1960 and inflicted heavy losses in Vietnam and the 1973 Arab-Israeli War, driving the development of suppression of enemy air defences (SEAD) tactics, stealth aircraft and precision weapons. Modern integrated air defence systems (IADS) network long-, medium- and short-range SAMs – such as Patriot, S-400 and Iron Dome – with airborne early warning and data links.

Layers and components

  • Long range: area defence SAMs and interceptor aircraft against high-altitude threats
  • Medium range: SAMs covering formations and installations
  • Short range: SHORAD missiles, MANPADS (man-portable systems such as Stinger) and anti-aircraft guns, including counter-drone and counter-rocket systems
  • Ballistic missile defence: specialised interceptors such as THAAD and Aegis BMD
