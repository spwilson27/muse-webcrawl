Glavlit

Glavlit (Russian: Главлит), formally the Main Directorate for the Protection of State Secrets in the Press under the Council of Ministers of the USSR, was the official censorship and state-secrets protection agency of the Soviet Union. Founded in 1922, it exercised preventive censorship over virtually all printed matter, broadcasting and publishing in the USSR until its dissolution in 1991.

Functions

Glavlit reviewed books, newspapers, journals, radio and television scripts, and other material before publication or broadcast, removing content deemed to reveal state secrets or to conflict with official ideology. It maintained extensive lists of prohibited information — the so-called "Talmud" of banned topics — and employed tens of thousands of censors. It also controlled access to foreign literature and supervised the spetskhran, restricted collections of banned works in libraries.

History

Established by decree on 6 June 1922 as the Main Directorate for Literature and Publishing, Glavlit's name and mandate were later reframed around protecting state secrets. Under glasnost in the late 1980s its powers were curtailed, and the 1990 press law formally ended censorship; the agency was abolished in October 1991.
