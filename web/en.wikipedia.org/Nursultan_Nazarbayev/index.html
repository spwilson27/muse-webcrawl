Nursultan Abishuly Nazarbayev (born 6 July 1940) is a Kazakh politician who served as the first president of Kazakhstan, from the country's independence in 1991 until his resignation in 2019. He was named First Secretary of the Communist Party of the Kazakh SSR in 1989 and was elected as the nation's first president following its independence from the Soviet Union.

Early life and career

Nazarbayev was born in Chemolgan, near Alma-Ata, to a peasant family. He trained as a metallurgist and worked at the Karaganda steel mill before rising through the Communist Party apparatus, becoming prime minister of the Kazakh SSR in 1984 and First Secretary in 1989.

Presidency

As president, Nazarbayev oversaw Kazakhstan's transition to a market economy, attracted large foreign investment into its oil and gas sector, and moved the capital from Almaty to Akmola, renamed Astana. He voluntarily renounced Kazakhstan's inherited Soviet nuclear arsenal, closing the Semipalatinsk test site. His long rule was criticized by international observers for authoritarianism, restrictions on the press and opposition, and elections that were not considered free and fair, though supporters credited him with stability and economic growth.

Resignation and later role

Nazarbayev announced his resignation on 19 March 2019 and was succeeded by Kassym-Jomart Tokayev. He retained significant influence as chairman of the Security Council, head of his party, and holder of the title "Leader of the Nation" (Elbasy); the capital Astana was renamed Nur-Sultan in his honor. Following the unrest of January 2022, Tokayev removed Nazarbayev from his remaining posts, and the capital's name reverted to Astana later that year, while constitutional privileges attached to the Elbasy title were removed.
