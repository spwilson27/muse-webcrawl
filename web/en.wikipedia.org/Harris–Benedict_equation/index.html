Harris–Benedict equation

The Harris–Benedict equation is a formula that uses a person's sex, age, height, and weight to estimate their basal metabolic rate (BMR). Multiplying the BMR by an activity factor gives an estimate of daily calorie needs. It was published by James Arthur Harris and Francis Gano Benedict in 1918–1919 and revised by Roza and Shizgal in 1984.

The equations

The original equations (with weight in kg, height in cm, and age in years) are: for men, BMR = 66.5 + (13.75 × weight) + (5.003 × height) − (6.75 × age); for women, BMR = 655.1 + (9.563 × weight) + (1.850 × height) − (4.676 × age). Total energy expenditure is estimated by multiplying BMR by a factor from about 1.2 (sedentary) to 1.9 (very active).

Limitations

The equation does not account for body composition, so it can overestimate calorie needs in people with high body fat and underestimate them in very muscular people. The Mifflin–St Jeor equation, published in 1990, is often preferred today as slightly more accurate for modern populations.
