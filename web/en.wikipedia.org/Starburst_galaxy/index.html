Starburst galaxy

Messier 82, the Cigar Galaxy, a prototypical starburst galaxy.

A starburst galaxy is one undergoing an exceptionally high rate of star formation, often so intense that the galaxy would quickly exhaust its supply of star-forming gas if the rate continued. Starbursts are commonly triggered by gravitational interactions or mergers between galaxies, which compress gas and drive it toward galactic centres.

Characteristics

Starburst galaxies emit much of their energy in the infrared, because the light of young, massive stars is absorbed by dust and re-radiated at longer wavelengths. Many drive powerful galactic winds of outflowing gas, visible as filaments extending thousands of light-years. Nearby examples include Messier 82 (the Cigar Galaxy) and the Antennae Galaxies, a colliding pair.

Starburst regions

Localized starbursts also occur within otherwise normal galaxies; the Tarantula Nebula in the Large Magellanic Cloud is the most active star-forming region in the Local Group. In the distant universe, luminous and ultraluminous infrared galaxies (LIRGs and ULIRGs) are extreme starbursts often associated with major mergers.
