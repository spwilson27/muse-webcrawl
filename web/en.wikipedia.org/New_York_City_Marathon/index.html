The New York City Marathon, currently branded the TCS New York City Marathon, is an annual marathon (42.195 km / 26.219 mi) that courses through the five boroughs of New York City. It is the largest marathon in the world, with 53,627 finishers in 2019 and 98,247 applicants for the 2017 race. Along with the Boston Marathon and Chicago Marathon, it is among the pre-eminent long-distance annual running events in the United States and is one of the World Marathon Majors.

History

The first New York City Marathon was held in 1970, organised by Fred Lebow and the New York Road Runners, with 127 entrants running laps of Central Park. In 1976, to celebrate the United States Bicentennial, the course was moved to the streets of all five boroughs, beginning on Staten Island, crossing the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge into Brooklyn, then passing through Queens and the Bronx before finishing in Manhattan's Central Park. The race's popularity exploded, and it became a model for big-city marathons worldwide.

Course

The course is known for its bridges, its hills in the later miles, and the roar of spectators along First Avenue in Manhattan. Runners enter Central Park near mile 24 and finish near Tavern on the Green. The 2012 race was cancelled in the aftermath of Hurricane Sandy, and the 2020 race was cancelled because of the COVID-19 pandemic.
