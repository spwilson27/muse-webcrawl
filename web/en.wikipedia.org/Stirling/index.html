Stirling

Stirling (Scots: Stirlin; Scottish Gaelic: Sruighlea) is a city in central Scotland, 26 miles northeast of Glasgow and 37 miles northwest of Edinburgh. The market town, surrounded by rich farmland, grew up connecting the royal citadel, medieval old town with its merchants and tradesmen, the bridge and the port. Located on the River Forth, it forms a historic gateway between the Scottish Highlands and Lowlands.

History

Stirling has long occupied a position of strategic importance: whoever held Stirling controlled the crossing of the Forth and the route north. Stirling Castle was a favoured residence of the Stewart monarchs, and Mary, Queen of Scots was crowned there in 1543. Nearby were fought the Battle of Stirling Bridge (1297), where William Wallace defeated an English army, and the Battle of Bannockburn (1314), where Robert the Bruce secured Scottish independence.

Landmarks

Stirling Castle dominates the old town from its volcanic crag. Other landmarks include the Church of the Holy Rude, where James VI was crowned in 1567, the Wallace Monument on the Abbey Craig, and the Old Stirling Bridge. The University of Stirling, founded in 1967, is set in the grounds of Airthrey Castle.

Modern city

Stirling was granted city status in 2002 as part of Queen Elizabeth II's Golden Jubilee celebrations. Today it is a centre for government, education, retail, and tourism. See also Stirling Castle, River Forth, and Scotland.
