Place and route

Place and route (also called PnR or P&R) is a stage of electronic design automation (EDA) for integrated circuits, in which the components of a synthesized design are assigned physical locations on the die (placement) and the interconnect wiring between them is laid out (routing). Together with logic synthesis before it and verification after it, place and route converts a gate-level netlist into a manufacturable layout.

Steps

Floorplanning

The outline of the chip and the arrangement of large blocks (macros, memories, IP cores) are decided, along with the location of input/output pads and power distribution structures.

Placement

Standard cells are positioned in rows across the die. The placement tool optimizes for wire length, timing, congestion, and power. Global placement produces an approximate arrangement, after which legalization and detailed placement refine it.

Clock tree synthesis

A balanced network of buffers and wiring is inserted so that the clock signal reaches all sequential elements with minimal skew.

Routing

Global routing assigns connections to routing regions and layers; detailed routing then draws the actual wires and vias, respecting design rules. Timing-driven routing prioritizes critical nets.

Optimization and signoff

After routing, the layout is extracted and analyzed for timing, signal integrity, and manufacturability. Engineering change orders (ECOs) fix violations, and the final database is checked with design rule checking (DRC) and layout-versus-schematic (LVS) before tape-out.
