Narration

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Narration is the use of a written or spoken commentary to convey a story to an audience. Narration is conveyed by a narrator: a specific person, character, or voice created by the author to tell the story to the audience. It is a key component of narrative fiction, film, theatre, and other storytelling media.

Point of view

Narrative point of view describes the perspective from which a story is told. Common forms include first-person narration, in which the narrator is a character in the story ("I"); second-person narration, which addresses the reader as "you" and is comparatively rare; and third-person narration, in which the narrator stands outside the story. Third-person narration may be omniscient, with access to every character's thoughts, or limited, restricted to one character's perspective.

The narrator

Narrators may be reliable or unreliable: an unreliable narrator is one whose credibility is compromised, whether through deception, ignorance, or mental instability, a device famously used in works such as The Catcher in the Rye. In film, narration often takes the form of a voice-over, while documentaries conventionally employ an authoritative off-screen narrator.

Narratology

The study of narrative structure and narration is called narratology. Scholars such as Gérard Genette distinguished between the story (the events) and the discourse (the way they are presented), and classified narrators by their relation to the story and their level within it.
