Edmund Landau

Edmund Georg Hermann Landau (14 February 1877 – 19 February 1938) was a German mathematician who founded modern analytic number theory. His name is attached to the Landau notation (big O notation), the Landau prime ideal theorem, and Landau's problems on prime numbers.

Edmund Landau

Life

Born in Berlin into a wealthy Jewish family, Landau studied at the University of Berlin, receiving his doctorate in 1899 under Frobenius. After teaching in Berlin, he moved in 1909 to the University of Göttingen, then the world centre of mathematics, where he held a chair until the Nazi regime forced him out in 1933. He died in Berlin in 1938.

Work

Landau's 1909 two-volume Handbuch der Lehre von der Verteilung der Primzahlen was the first systematic treatise on analytic number theory, presenting the subject as a unified field. He gave proofs of the prime number theorem and extended the theory to algebraic number fields with the prime ideal theorem of 1903. His Vorlesungen über Zahlentheorie (1927) and Grundlagen der Analysis (1930) became standard texts prized for their austere, rigorous style. At the 1912 International Congress of Mathematicians he listed four problems about primes — Goldbach's conjecture, the twin prime conjecture, Legendre's conjecture, and primes of the form n² + 1 — as "unattackable at the present state of knowledge"; they remain open.

Notation

The big O symbol O(·), originally introduced by Paul Bachmann, was popularised worldwide through Landau's textbooks, and the notation f = O(g) for asymptotic bounds is often called Landau notation.
