Sketch comedy

Sketch comedy Origins: Vaudeville; music hall; radio
Notable shows: Saturday Night Live; Monty Python's Flying Circus; Mr. Show

Sketch comedy comprises a series of short, humorous scenes or vignettes, called "sketches", commonly between one and ten minutes long, performed by a group of comic actors or comedians. The sketches themselves are typically scripted but are often rehearsed and performed with improvisation involved, and the form is used on stage, on radio, and in film, but most commonly on television.

History

Sketch comedy has its roots in vaudeville and music hall, where short comic turns were interspersed among musical and variety acts, and was adapted to radio in the early twentieth century. In Britain, programmes such as The Goon Show and later Monty Python's Flying Circus (1969–74) transformed the form with surreal, stream-of-consciousness writing. In the United States, Sid Caesar's Your Show of Shows established television sketch in the 1950s.

Modern era

The premiere of Saturday Night Live in 1975 made sketch comedy a durable institution of American television, launching generations of film and television stars, while Canadian series such as SCTV and The Kids in the Hall and later shows like Mr. Show, Chappelle's Show, Key & Peele and I Think You Should Leave expanded the genre. Since the 2000s, short sketches have also flourished online, where troupes distribute individual pieces through video platforms.
