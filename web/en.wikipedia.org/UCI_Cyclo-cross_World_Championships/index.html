UCI Cyclo-cross World Championships

Elite racing at the Cyclo-cross World Championships.

The UCI Cyclo-cross World Championships are the annual world championship races for the discipline of cyclo-cross, organised by the Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI). Held each year in late January or early February at the end of the cyclo-cross season, the championships crown world champions in elite, under-23 and junior categories for men and women, together with a mixed team relay added in 2023. Winners earn the right to wear the rainbow jersey in their category for the following season.

History

The first men's world championship was held in Paris in 1950 and won by Jean Robic, better known as a Tour de France champion. A separate amateur championship ran from 1967 to 1993 before the categories were unified; a women's championship was introduced in 2000, first won by Germany's Hanka Kupfernagel. The event is traditionally hosted in the sport's heartlands of Belgium and the Netherlands, though it has also visited the United States, the Czech Republic, Germany, France and Denmark.

Notable champions

Belgium's Erik De Vlaeminck won a record seven elite men's titles between 1966 and 1973. In the modern era, Mathieu van der Poel of the Netherlands and Wout van Aert of Belgium have dominated the men's race, while Dutch riders including Marianne Vos — an eight-time world champion — have led the women's event. Marianne Vos's tally makes her the most successful rider in the championships' history across the men's and women's events of her era.

The championships form, with the season-long UCI Cyclo-cross World Cup and the Superprestige and X²O Badkamers Trofee series, the central pillars of the professional cyclo-cross calendar.
