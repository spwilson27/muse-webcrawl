Ablation

Meaning: Removal or destruction of material or tissue

Fields: Medicine, glaciology, aerospace, meteorology

Ablation (Latin: ablatio, "carrying away") is the removal or destruction of material from the surface of an object by vaporisation, erosion or other processes. The term is used across several disciplines with related but distinct meanings.

Medicine

In medicine, ablation is the removal or destruction of tissue, usually by surgical or minimally invasive means. Catheter ablation uses radiofrequency energy or cryotherapy to destroy small areas of heart tissue causing arrhythmias; laser and radiofrequency ablation treat tumours, varicose veins and abnormal uterine tissue; and endometrial ablation is a procedure for heavy menstrual bleeding. Surface ablation of the cornea, as in photorefractive keratectomy, reshapes the eye to correct vision.

Glaciology and earth sciences

In glaciology, ablation is the loss of ice or snow from a glacier through melting, sublimation, evaporation, calving and wind erosion; the ablation zone is where annual loss exceeds accumulation. In meteorology the term describes the removal of snow or ice by melting or evaporation from the air, and in geology, differential ablation sculpts features such as penitentes.

Aerospace

In spacecraft engineering, ablative materials protect vehicles during atmospheric entry: the heat shield chars, melts and vaporises, carrying away heat. Ablative shields protected the Apollo capsules and are used on sample-return missions; ablative nozzles and laser ablation are also used in propulsion research and materials processing.
