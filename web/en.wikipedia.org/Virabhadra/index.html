Virabhadra

Virabhadra (Sanskrit: Vīrabhadra, "auspicious hero") is a fierce and fearsome form of the Hindu god Shiva, created from Shiva's wrath. He is principally known for disrupting and destroying Daksha's yajna (fire sacrifice) after the self-immolation of Shiva's wife Sati.

Mythology

According to the Puranas, Daksha insulted Shiva and excluded him from a great sacrifice; Sati, Daksha's daughter and Shiva's wife, immolated herself in protest. Enraged, Shiva tore out a lock of his matted hair and dashed it to the ground, from which sprang Virabhadra and Bhadrakali. Virabhadra led Shiva's gana army to destroy the sacrifice and behead Daksha; Daksha was later restored with a goat's head.

Virabhadra is widely worshipped in South India, notably at the Veerabhadra Temple in Lepakshi, Andhra Pradesh. The yoga pose Vīrabhadrāsana (Warrior Pose) is named for him.
