South Georgia and the South Sandwich Islands

South Georgia and the South Sandwich Islands (SGSSI)
Status: British Overseas Territory
Administrative centre: King Edward Point
Currency: Pound sterling

South Georgia and the South Sandwich Islands is a British Overseas Territory in the southern Atlantic Ocean. It comprises the island of South Georgia, a group of smaller islands nearby, and the remote volcanic South Sandwich Islands, about 700 km to the southeast. There is no permanent population; presence consists of government officers, British Antarctic Survey scientists, and museum staff.

History

The territory was formed in 1985, having previously been administered as the Falkland Islands Dependencies. Argentina's claim to the islands led to their occupation during the 1982 Falklands War and British recovery operations. The South Sandwich Islands, discovered by Cook in 1775, are an active volcanic arc and were the site of an Argentine station on Thule Island from 1976 to 1982.

Government and economy

The territory is governed by a Commissioner (concurrently the Governor of the Falkland Islands) with a small administration at King Edward Point. Revenue comes from fishing licences for Patagonian toothfish and krill, tourism from expedition cruise ships, postage stamps, and research support. In 2012 the territory declared one of the world's largest marine protected areas, refined in later reviews into a fully protected zone of over 1.2 million km².
