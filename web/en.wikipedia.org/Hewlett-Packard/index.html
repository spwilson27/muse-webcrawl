Hewlett-Packard

The Hewlett-Packard Company, commonly known as HP, was an American multinational information technology company headquartered in Palo Alto, California. Founded in 1939 by Bill Hewlett and Dave Packard in a garage — an origin story regarded as the birth of Silicon Valley — HP grew from making electronic test equipment into one of the world's largest manufacturers of personal computers and printers. In 2015 the company split into HP Inc. (PCs and printers) and Hewlett Packard Enterprise (servers, storage, and services).

History

HP's first product was an audio oscillator, the Model 200A, famously bought by Walt Disney Studios for work on Fantasia. The company expanded into test and measurement instruments, then minicomputers, scientific calculators (including the HP-35, 1972), LaserJet and DeskJet printers, and — through the 2002 merger with Compaq — leadership in personal computers. Its corporate culture, the "HP Way," emphasised informality, engineering excellence, and management by walking around. The measurement and life-sciences businesses were spun off as Agilent Technologies in 1999, and the contentious Compaq acquisition under CEO Carly Fiorina reshaped the company.

Split and legacy

On 1 November 2015, HP separated into HP Inc., which retained the PC and printing businesses and the HP name, and Hewlett Packard Enterprise, focused on enterprise hardware, software, and services. The original garage at 367 Addison Avenue in Palo Alto is preserved as a California Historical Landmark, "the birthplace of Silicon Valley."
