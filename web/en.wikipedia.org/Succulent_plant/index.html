Succulent plant

Succulents store water in their thickened leaves, stems or roots.

In botany, succulent plants, also known as succulents, are plants with parts that are thickened, fleshy, and engorged, usually to retain water in arid climates or soil conditions. The word succulent comes from the Latin sucus, meaning "juice" or "sap". Succulent plants may store water in various structures, such as leaves and stems.

Characteristics

Many succulents use crassulacean acid metabolism (CAM) photosynthesis, opening their stomata at night to reduce water loss. Succulence has evolved independently many times across plant families: well-known groups include the cacti (family Cactaceae, almost all of which are stem succulents), agaves, aloes, echeverias, haworthias, jade plants and the ice plants of the Aizoaceae. The defining trait is convergent: not all succulents are closely related, and cacti are only one family among many.

Cultivation

Succulents are popular houseplants and garden subjects in dry climates because of their striking forms and low water requirements. They need sharply drained soil, bright light and infrequent watering; overwatering and rot are the commonest causes of failure in cultivation. Many are easily propagated from leaf or stem cuttings.
