Chemical Weapons Convention

The Chemical Weapons Convention (CWC) is an arms control treaty prohibiting the development, production, stockpiling and use of chemical weapons and requiring the destruction of existing stockpiles. It entered into force on 29 April 1997.

Provisions

States parties undertake never to develop, produce, acquire, stockpile, retain, transfer or use chemical weapons, and to destroy weapons and production facilities under international verification. Toxic chemicals are listed in three schedules according to their risk and legitimate uses.

Implementation

The Organisation for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons (OPCW), based in The Hague, verifies compliance; it received the Nobel Peace Prize in 2013. With 193 states parties, the convention is near-universal. Declared stockpiles — more than 72,000 metric tonnes of chemical agent — were verified as destroyed by 2023.
