Decebalus

DecebalusKing of Dacia
Reign: 87 – 106 AD
Predecessor: Duras
Successor: Roman province of Dacia
Died: 106 AD

Decebalus (died 106 AD) was the last king of Dacia, remembered for his long resistance to the expansion of the Roman Empire under the emperors Domitian and Trajan. He ruled from 87 AD until his death during Trajan's second Dacian War.

Early reign and war with Domitian

Decebalus succeeded the Dacian king Duras in 87 AD, at a time when Roman forces under Domitian were pressing against the Danube frontier. Dacian armies had earlier raided the Roman province of Moesia, killing its governor. Decebalus defeated a Roman expedition under Cornelius Fuscus in 87, destroying a legion and capturing its standards. A subsequent campaign under Tettius Julianus won a victory at Tapae in 88, but Roman difficulties on other frontiers led Domitian to negotiate a peace in 89. Under its terms Decebalus became a client king, receiving Roman subsidies, engineers and military equipment — much of which he used to strengthen his own kingdom.

Wars with Trajan

The emperor Trajan, unwilling to tolerate a powerful and subsidised kingdom beyond the Danube, invaded Dacia in 101 AD. After hard fighting, including the battle of Tapae and a winter counterattack across the Danube, Decebalus sued for peace in 102 on harsh terms. He used the interval to rearm and rebuild his fortresses, and in 105 he attacked Roman garrisons, provoking a second invasion. Trajan's forces, supported by the great bridge over the Danube built by Apollodorus of Damascus, advanced methodically on the Dacian capital Sarmizegetusa, which fell in 106.

Death and legacy

Decebalus fled into the mountains but, cornered by Roman cavalry, took his own life rather than be captured. His head was sent to Rome and displayed during Trajan's triumph. His campaigns are commemorated on Trajan's Column in Rome, and Dacia became a Roman province whose gold mines enriched the empire. In modern Romania Decebalus is honoured as a national hero; a colossal rock carving of his face, completed in 2004 near the Iron Gates of the Danube, is the tallest rock sculpture in Europe.
