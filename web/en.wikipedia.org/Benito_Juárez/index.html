Benito Juárez

Benito Pablo Juárez García (21 March 1806 – 18 July 1872) was a Mexican liberal lawyer and statesman who served as the 26th president of Mexico from 1858 until his death in 1872. A Zapotec from Oaxaca, he was the first Indigenous president of Mexico and of any North American country. Juárez is remembered as a progressive reformer dedicated to democracy, equal rights for the nation's Indigenous peoples, and the reduction of the power of the Roman Catholic Church and the military; his reforms, known as La Reforma, reshaped the Mexican state and its 1857 constitution. His famous maxim, "Among individuals, as among nations, respect for the rights of others is peace", remains a cornerstone of Mexican political thought.

Early life

Juárez was born to Zapotec peasant parents in the village of San Pablo Guelatao, Oaxaca, and orphaned at a young age. He walked to the city of Oaxaca at age 12, speaking only Zapotec, and was educated with the help of a Franciscan layman, Antonio Salanueva. He studied law at the Institute of Sciences and Arts, entered politics, and rose to become governor of Oaxaca (1847–1852). Exiled by Santa Anna, he worked in a cigar factory in New Orleans before returning to help overthrow the dictatorship in the Revolution of Ayutla.

Reform and civil war

As Minister of Justice in the liberal government, Juárez authored the Juárez Law (1855) abolishing special courts for clergy and military. The Reform Laws that followed disentailed church property and separated church and state. When conservatives revolted, the War of Reform (1858–1861) ensued; Juárez, as president by constitutional succession, led the liberal government from Veracruz to victory.

French intervention and restoration

After Juárez suspended debt payments, France invaded and installed Maximilian of Habsburg as emperor in 1864. Juárez led the republican resistance from the north — famously from El Paso del Norte, now Ciudad Juárez — and after French withdrawal under pressure from the United States and Mexican arms, Maximilian was captured and executed in 1867. Juárez was re-elected in 1867 and 1871, but his health failed and he died of a heart attack in office in 1872. He is revered in Mexico as the "Benemérito de las Américas", and his birthday, 21 March, is a national holiday.
