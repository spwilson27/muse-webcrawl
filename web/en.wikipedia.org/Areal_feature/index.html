Areal feature

In geolinguistics and dialectology, an areal feature is a linguistic feature shared by languages or dialects over an area that results from diffusion and language contact rather than from inheritance from a common ancestor. In biology, it may refer to a characteristic that occurs among the members of a group of closely-related organisms. Features historically known as effluvia are also known as shared areal features.

Linguistics

Regardless of whether borrowing, drift, areal diffusion or coincidence causes them, stages of convergence that are integral to a language scenario are usually accompanied by emergence and convergence of areal features. Sprachbunds (linguistic areas) are defined by the occurrence of many areal features that are shared by most or all languages in the area, resulting in a high degree of structural similarity between otherwise unrelated languages.

Well-known examples include the Balkan sprachbund, the South Asian (Indic) linguistic area, and the Mainland Southeast Asia linguistic area. In each case, languages of different families have come to share phonological, grammatical and lexical traits through prolonged multilingual contact.

Distinguishing areal features

To establish that a feature is areal, linguists generally require that the feature be found in several languages of the area, be absent or rare in related languages outside the area, and not be attributable to independent development or universal tendencies. Historical evidence of contact strengthens the case.
