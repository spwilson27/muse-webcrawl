Stapleton International Airport

Stapleton International Airport, Denver, before its 1995 closure.

Stapleton International Airport (IATA: DEN, ICAO: KDEN) was Denver, Colorado's primary airport from 1929 until its closure in 1995. Located about 10 kilometres east of downtown Denver, it began as Denver Municipal Airport and was renamed in 1944 for Benjamin F. Stapleton, the mayor who championed its construction.

History

The airport opened on October 17, 1929, and grew into a major hub, serving for decades as a base for United Airlines, Continental Airlines and the original Frontier Airlines. Its distinctive control tower and terminal hosted famous events, including Charles Lindbergh visits in its early years. A notable feature was a runway that crossed over Interstate 70 on a bridge.

Closure and replacement

By the 1980s Stapleton was hemmed in by residential development, plagued by noise complaints and lacking room to expand. It was replaced by Denver International Airport on February 28, 1995; the last flight departed for London Gatwick. The IATA code DEN transferred to the new airport.

Redevelopment

After closure, the site was redeveloped as a large mixed-use neighbourhood, renamed Central Park in 2020, with housing, offices, parks and schools built around the preserved control tower. Stapleton is often cited as a landmark case of airport relocation and brownfield-to-neighbourhood redevelopment.
