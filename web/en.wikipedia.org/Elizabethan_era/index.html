Elizabethan era

The Elizabethan era is the epoch in the Tudor period of the history of England during the reign of Queen Elizabeth I (1558–1603). Historians often depict it as the golden age in English history: the height of the English Renaissance, which saw the flowering of English poetry, music and literature.

Elizabeth's reign was marked by the defeat of the Spanish Armada in 1588, the growth of English exploration and trade, and the settlement of the Church of England. The era is remembered for the work of William Shakespeare, Christopher Marlowe and Edmund Spenser, and for a flourishing of theatre, notably in London's public playhouses such as the Globe.

It was also an age of religious tension, plots against the queen, and harsh treatment of Catholics and dissenters. The period ended with Elizabeth's death in 1603 and the accession of James VI of Scotland as James I of England.
