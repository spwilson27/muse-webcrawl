Sustainable Development Goal 8

Sustainable Development Goal 8 (SDG 8 or Global Goal 8) is about "decent work and economic growth" and is one of the 17 Sustainable Development Goals adopted by the United Nations in 2015. Its official mission statement is to "promote sustained, inclusive and sustainable economic growth, full and productive employment and decent work for all".

Targets

SDG 8 has twelve targets to be achieved by 2030, including: sustaining per-capita economic growth (with at least 7 percent annual GDP growth in the least developed countries); achieving higher economic productivity through diversification and innovation; promoting decent job creation, entrepreneurship and the formalisation of small and medium enterprises; improving resource efficiency and decoupling growth from environmental degradation; achieving full and productive employment and equal pay for work of equal value; reducing youth not in employment, education or training (NEET); ending child labour and protecting labour rights and safe workplaces, including for migrant workers; promoting sustainable tourism, access to finance, and Aid for Trade.

Indicators and monitoring

Progress is measured with indicators such as the growth rate of real GDP per capita, the unemployment rate by sex, age and disability status, the proportion of informal employment, the incidence of child labour, and occupational injury rates. The International Labour Organization is custodian for many of the labour-market indicators, and UN reporting has highlighted setbacks from the COVID-19 pandemic, which caused sharp losses in working hours and incomes worldwide.

Debates

Scholars and campaigners debate an internal tension in SDG 8: whether continued aggregate economic growth can be reconciled with the environmental targets of other goals, a discussion associated with "green growth" and degrowth perspectives.
