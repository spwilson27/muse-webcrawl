Hitsville U.S.A.


Hitsville U.S.A., Motown's first headquarters and studio

Hitsville U.S.A. is the nickname given to Motown Records' first headquarters and recording studio, a converted house at 2648 West Grand Boulevard in Detroit, Michigan. Berry Gordy Jr. purchased the property in 1959, converting its rear garage into the famous "Snake Pit" studio. From 1959 to 1972, Hitsville was the creative centre of the "Motown Sound", producing hundreds of hit recordings by artists including the Supremes, the Temptations, Marvin Gaye, Stevie Wonder and the Jackson 5. Today the building houses the Motown Museum, founded in 1985 by Esther Gordy Edwards.

History

Gordy, a former songwriter and autoworker, founded Tamla Records in 1959 with an $800 loan from his family. He bought the two-storey house at 2648 West Grand Boulevard that August, placing a sign reading "Hitsville U.S.A." in conscious imitation of Nashville's country-music branding. The garage became Studio A, engineered with an echo chamber built in the bathroom ceiling space; offices, a control room and Gordy's flat occupied the rest. As Motown grew, Gordy acquired seven neighbouring houses on the boulevard for administration, finance, artist development and sales.

Studio A and the Motown Sound

Recording in Studio A ran almost around the clock, powered by the Funk Brothers, Motown's uncredited house band of Detroit jazz and R&B musicians, whose contributions were only widely recognised after the 2002 documentary Standing in the Shadows of Motown. Songs passed through Gordy's Friday "quality control" meetings, where staff voted on releases judged by whether a hungry listener would buy the record or a hot dog. Classic recordings made at Hitsville include "My Girl", "I Heard It Through the Grapevine", "Dancing in the Street" and "What's Going On".

Relocation and museum

In 1968 Gordy bought the Donovan Building downtown as Motown's Detroit offices, and in 1972 the company moved its headquarters to Los Angeles, effectively ending Hitsville's studio era; Studio A's last sessions took place that year. Esther Gordy Edwards later used the building for the Motown Historical Museum, opened in 1985. The museum preserves Studio A largely as it was, along with Gordy's apartment and exhibits on Motown's artists. In 2022 the museum broke ground on a major expansion ("Hitsville NEXT"). The original building is a Michigan State Historic Site and one of Detroit's most visited landmarks.
