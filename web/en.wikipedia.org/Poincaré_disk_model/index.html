Poincaré disk model

In geometry, the Poincaré disk model, also called the conformal disk model, is a model of 2-dimensional hyperbolic geometry in which all points are inside the unit disk, and straight lines are either circular arcs contained within the disk that are orthogonal to the unit circle, or diameters of the unit circle.

Properties

The model is conformal: it preserves angles, so the angle between two hyperbolic lines equals the Euclidean angle between their representing arcs. However, distances are distorted: the metric ds = 2|dz| / (1 − |z|²) grows without bound toward the boundary circle, which represents points at infinity ("ideal points"). The entire infinite hyperbolic plane fits inside the finite disk.

History

The construction traces to Eugenio Beltrami and was popularized by Henri Poincaré. It is closely related to the Poincaré half-plane model, to which it can be mapped by a conformal transformation.

Art and visualizations

M. C. Escher's Circle Limit woodcuts are famous renderings of hyperbolic tessellations in the disk model, showing angels, demons, and fish shrinking toward the boundary.
