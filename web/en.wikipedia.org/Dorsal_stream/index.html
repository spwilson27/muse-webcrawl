Dorsal stream

Dorsal stream
System: Visual system
Pathway: Occipital lobe → parietal lobe
Function: Spatial awareness and guidance of action ("where"/"how")
Counterpart: Ventral stream

The dorsal stream is one of two main pathways of the cerebral visual system, projecting from the primary visual cortex (V1) in the occipital lobe forward into the parietal lobe. In the influential two-streams hypothesis of Ungerleider and Mishkin, and its refinement by Goodale and Milner, the dorsal stream processes the location and motion of objects — the "where" (or "how") pathway — supporting visually guided action such as reaching and grasping.

Function and evidence

Evidence comes from lesion studies: damage to the dorsal stream in the parietal cortex can produce optic ataxia, an inability to reach accurately toward objects while still recognizing them. This contrasts with ventral stream damage, which impairs object recognition while sparing visually guided movement. The dorsal stream includes areas MT/V5 for motion processing and regions of the intraparietal sulcus involved in transforming vision into motor coordinates.
