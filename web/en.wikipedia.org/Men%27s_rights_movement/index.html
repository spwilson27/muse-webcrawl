Men's rights movement

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The men's rights movement (MRM) is a branch of the men's movement that emerged in the 1970s, partly in response to second-wave feminism. Its adherents, known as men's rights activists, argue that men face systematic disadvantages and forms of discrimination in areas such as family law, child custody, military conscription, education, workplace deaths and injuries, suicide rates, and the treatment of male victims of violence and sexual assault.

History

The movement grew out of the broader men's liberation movement of the 1970s, from which it split as some groups adopted an explicitly anti-feminist stance, holding that feminist reforms had institutionalized advantages for women. Organizations such as the National Coalition for Men in the United States campaigned on custody, paternity, and conscription issues. From the 2000s the movement became prominent online, overlapping with the wider "manosphere" of blogs, forums, and video channels.

Reception

Critics, including the Southern Poverty Law Center, which has listed some men's rights groups under male supremacy, argue that parts of the movement promote misogyny and harassment of feminists. Supporters respond that they advocate equal treatment under law and draw attention to genuine problems affecting men.
