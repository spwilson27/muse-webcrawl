Common gamma chain

The common gamma chain (γc), also known as the interleukin-2 receptor subunit gamma or CD132, is a cytokine receptor subunit that is common to the receptor complexes for at least six different interleukin receptors: interleukin-2 (IL-2), IL-4, IL-7, IL-9, IL-15, and IL-21. It is a component of the type I cytokine receptor family and is expressed mainly on immune cells.

Function

When a cytokine binds its specific receptor subunits, the gamma chain recruits the Janus kinase JAK3, which associates with its cytoplasmic tail. Signalling proceeds largely through the JAK–STAT pathway, influencing the growth, differentiation, and survival of lymphocytes. Because the gamma chain is shared by receptors essential for lymphocyte development, its loss disables signalling by all of them simultaneously.

Clinical significance

Mutations in the gene encoding the gamma chain, IL2RG, which is located on the X chromosome, cause X-linked severe combined immunodeficiency (X-SCID), the most common form of SCID. Affected infants lack functional T cells and natural killer cells, and have non-functional B cells. X-SCID was one of the first diseases successfully treated with gene therapy, as well as with haematopoietic stem cell transplantation.
