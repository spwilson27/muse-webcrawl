Rheometer

A rheometer is a laboratory instrument used to measure the rheological properties of materials — how they flow and deform under applied forces — particularly fluids and soft solids whose viscosity cannot be described by a single value, such as polymer melts, gels, pastes and suspensions.

Types

Rotational rheometers shear the sample between rotating geometries (cone-and-plate, parallel plate, or concentric cylinders) and measure torque and displacement, yielding viscosity, storage and loss moduli, and yield stress. Capillary rheometers force the sample through a narrow die at high shear rates typical of processing. Extensional rheometers stretch the sample to measure extensional viscosity, relevant to fibre spinning and spraying.

Measurements

Modern instruments operate in controlled-stress or controlled-strain modes and perform oscillatory tests to probe viscoelastic behaviour: the storage modulus G′ reflects elastic response, the loss modulus G″ viscous response. Temperature sweeps and time sweeps reveal curing, gelation and glass transitions.

Applications

Rheometers are used in the polymer, food, cosmetics, pharmaceutical, paint and oil industries for quality control and product development.
