Padma Bhushan

Padma Bhushan
Type: Civilian award
Country: India
Established: 1954
First awarded: 1954
Precedence: Below Padma Vibhushan; above Padma Shri

The Padma Bhushan is the third-highest civilian award in the Republic of India, preceded by the Bharat Ratna and the Padma Vibhushan and followed by the Padma Shri. Instituted on 2 January 1954, the award is given for "distinguished service of a high order" in any field, including service rendered by government servants — though it is most often conferred upon citizens in fields such as art, education, industry, literature, science, social service, medicine, sports, and public affairs.

The award recommendations are made by the Padma Awards Committee, constituted by the Prime Minister every year; recipients are announced annually on Republic Day (26 January) and conferred by the President of India at a ceremonial function at Rashtrapati Bhavan. The decoration is a circular medallion with a lotus flower in the centre and the words "Padma" and "Bhushan" in Devanagari script.

History

The Padma Bhushan was established alongside the Padma Vibhushan and Padma Shri in 1954. The awards were briefly suspended from 1977 to 1980 and again from 1992 to 1995 during litigation and a change of government. More than 1,300 people have received the award to date, including a small number of foreign recipients.
