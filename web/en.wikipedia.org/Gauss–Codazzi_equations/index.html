Gauss–Codazzi equations

In Riemannian geometry, the Gauss–Codazzi equations (also called the Gauss–Codazzi–Mainardi equations or the fundamental equations of surface theory) are the compatibility conditions that must be satisfied by the first and second fundamental forms of a surface immersed in three-dimensional Euclidean space. Together they guarantee, by the fundamental theorem of surface theory (Bonnet's theorem), that the two forms actually arise from a surface, unique up to rigid motion.

Statement

The Gauss equation expresses the intrinsic Gaussian curvature of the surface in terms of the coefficients of the first and second fundamental forms; its most famous consequence is Gauss's Theorema Egregium, which shows that Gaussian curvature depends only on the first fundamental form and is therefore intrinsic. The Codazzi equations, named for Delfino Codazzi (with related work by Gaspare Mainardi and Karl Peterson), are a pair of partial differential equations relating the derivatives of the coefficients of the second fundamental form to the Christoffel symbols of the induced metric.

Generalisation

In the modern language of differential geometry the equations are the pullback of the flatness (structure) equations of Euclidean space to a submanifold. They generalise to submanifolds of arbitrary dimension and codimension in a space of constant sectional curvature, where they are joined by the Ricci equation governing the normal bundle, and appear as the constraint equations relating the geometry of a hypersurface to that of its ambient space — including in the initial-value formulation of general relativity.
