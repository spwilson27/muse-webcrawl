Reflex

A reflex is an automatic, involuntary response to a stimulus that does not require conscious thought. Reflexes are mediated by neural pathways called reflex arcs, which typically run through the spinal cord or brainstem and produce a rapid, stereotyped reaction such as withdrawing a hand from a hot surface.

Reflex arc

A typical reflex arc consists of a sensory receptor, a sensory neuron, one or more interneurons in the central nervous system, a motor neuron, and an effector muscle or gland. Because the signal does not need to travel to the cerebral cortex, reflexes are much faster than voluntary movements.

Types

Well-known examples include the patellar (knee-jerk) reflex, the pupillary light reflex, the gag reflex, and the withdrawal reflex. Some reflexes are innate, while others, such as those involved in posture and balance, are refined through experience. Clinicians test reflexes to assess the integrity of the nervous system.
