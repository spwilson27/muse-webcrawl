Education in Norway

Education in Norway is mandatory for all children aged from 6 to 16 and is almost entirely public and free, including higher education at public institutions for all students regardless of nationality. The system is governed by the Ministry of Education and Research, with primary and lower secondary schools run by municipalities and upper secondary schools by counties.

Structure

Primary and lower secondary education (grunnskole) lasts ten years: primary school (barneskole, grades 1–7, ages 6–13) and lower secondary school (ungdomsskole, grades 8–10, ages 13–16). There is no streaming or tracking, and pupils receive no formal grades until grade 8. Nearly all six-year-olds attend kindergarten beforehand, and kindergarten for ages 1–5 is heavily subsidized though not compulsory.

Upper secondary education

All young people who complete lower secondary school have a statutory right to three years of upper secondary education (videregående skole), either in study-preparation programmes (general studies, and specialized lines in sports, music, or media) or in vocational programmes, which typically follow a model of two years in school followed by two years of paid apprenticeship leading to a trade certificate (fagbrev).

Higher education

Higher education is provided by universities — including the University of Oslo (founded 1811), the Norwegian University of Science and Technology (NTNU) in Trondheim, and universities at Bergen, Tromsø, and elsewhere — and by university colleges and specialized institutions. Degrees follow the Bologna 3+2+3 pattern. The State Educational Loan Fund (Lånekassen) provides loans and grants to students. Norway has one of the highest rates of tertiary attainment in the OECD.

Sámi and minority education

In the Sámi administrative district, pupils have the right to instruction in and through the Sámi languages. Kven and other national minorities also have certain language rights. Norway also maintains a network of folk high schools for non-formal adult education.
