The Four Great Classical Novels (Chinese: 四大名著, sì dà míngzhù) are the four novels of pre-modern Chinese literature most highly regarded by critics and most influential on later Chinese fiction. Grouped together since the 20th century, they are Romance of the Three Kingdoms, Water Margin, Journey to the West, and Dream of the Red Chamber. Earlier groupings of "four masterworks" (四大奇書) of the Ming dynasty substituted the erotic novel Jin Ping Mei for Dream of the Red Chamber.

The novels

Romance of the Three Kingdoms (14th century), attributed to Luo Guanzhong, is a historical epic of the fall of the Han dynasty and the rival kingdoms of Wei, Shu, and Wu, celebrated for its strategy, heroism, and the figure of the strategist Zhuge Liang. Water Margin recounts the exploits of 108 outlaws who gather at Liangshan Marsh to rebel against a corrupt Song-dynasty government. Journey to the West (16th century), attributed to Wu Cheng'en, is a comic-fantastic account of the monk Xuanzang's pilgrimage to India, escorted by the monkey king Sun Wukong and other disciples. Dream of the Red Chamber (18th century), by Cao Xueqin, is a vast, semi-autobiographical portrayal of the rise and decline of the aristocratic Jia family, widely considered the pinnacle of Chinese fiction.

Influence

The four novels shaped the Chinese language's stock of proverbs, characters, and stories, and have been endlessly adapted into opera, film, television, comics, and video games. Their grouping as a canon reflects both their literary quality and their immense popularity across centuries of readers.
