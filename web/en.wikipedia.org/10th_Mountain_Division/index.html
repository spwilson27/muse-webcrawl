10th Mountain Division

The 10th Mountain Division (Light Infantry) is an elite light infantry division in the United States Army based at Fort Drum, New York. Originally constituted during the Second World War as a unit specialised in mountain and winter warfare, it is the most deployed division in the United States Army since 2001.

Second World War

The division was activated in 1943 at Camp Hale, Colorado, recruiting experienced skiers, climbers and outdoorsmen, many of them recruited with the help of the National Ski Patrol. After training in Colorado and Texas, the division deployed to Italy in early 1945. In February 1945 its soldiers made the celebrated night-time climb of Riva Ridge in the North Apennines, surprising German defenders, and subsequently broke through German lines in the Po Valley campaign, reaching Lake Garda by the war's end. The division suffered some 5,000 casualties in Italy, including nearly 1,000 killed.

Many veterans went on to found much of the American ski industry, establishing or developing resorts such as Vail and Aspen.

Post-war

The division was inactivated in 1945, briefly reactivated as a training division (1948–1958), and reactivated again in 1985 at Fort Drum as a light infantry division. Its rapid-deployment design made it the spearhead of numerous operations: relief after Hurricane Andrew (1992), Somalia (1992–94), Haiti (1994–95), and Bosnia.

Global War on Terror

Since 2001 the 10th Mountain Division has deployed repeatedly to Afghanistan and Iraq. It was among the first conventional forces in Afghanistan, fighting in the Shahikot Valley during Operation Anaconda in 2002, and its brigades served numerous rotations in both theatres, as well as later deployments to Europe and elsewhere.
