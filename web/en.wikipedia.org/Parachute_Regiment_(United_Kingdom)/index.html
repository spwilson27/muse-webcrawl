The Parachute Regiment, colloquially known as the Paras, is the airborne and infantry regiment of the British Army. The regiment, formed in 1942 during the Second World War, is structured into three battalions: the 1st Battalion (1 PARA), which forms the core of the Special Forces Support Group; the 2nd and 3rd Battalions (2 and 3 PARA), which form part of 16 Air Assault Brigade Combat Team; and the 4th Battalion (4 PARA), an Army Reserve unit. Paratroopers are distinguished by their maroon beret and by the demanding selection process of P Company, which precedes parachute training.

History

British airborne forces were raised in 1940 at the urging of Winston Churchill, inspired by German parachute successes early in the Second World War. The Parachute Regiment was formally established on 1 August 1942 and its battalions fought in North Africa, Sicily, Italy, Normandy (on D-Day), and at Arnhem during Operation Market Garden, where the 2nd Battalion famously held the northern end of the Arnhem road bridge. After the war the regiment served in Palestine, Malaya, Cyprus, Suez, Borneo, Aden, and the Falklands War of 1982, where 2 and 3 PARA fought at Goose Green, Mount Longdon, and Wireless Ridge; two members received the Victoria Cross posthumously.

Northern Ireland and recent operations

The regiment served extensively in Northern Ireland during the Troubles; its 1st Battalion was responsible for the Bloody Sunday shootings in Derry in 1972, which the Saville Inquiry later found unjustified, leading to a formal apology by the British government in 2010. In the twenty-first century the regiment deployed repeatedly to Iraq and Afghanistan, where 2 and 3 PARA undertook heavy fighting in Helmand Province.
