KLM

KLM Royal Dutch Airlines, legally Koninklijke Luchtvaart Maatschappij N.V., is the flag carrier of the Netherlands. Founded on 7 October 1919, it is the world's oldest airline still operating under its original name. KLM is headquartered in Amstelveen, with its hub at Amsterdam Airport Schiphol, and has been part of the Air France–KLM group since 2004.

History

KLM was founded by Albert Plesman and received its "Royal" predicate from Queen Wilhelmina. Its first flight, from London to Amsterdam, took place in May 1920. The airline opened the Amsterdam–Batavia (Jakarta) route in 1924, for years the world's longest scheduled service, and was an early operator of the Douglas DC-2 and DC-3. Plesman rebuilt the airline after the Second World War into a global carrier.

Operations

KLM and its subsidiaries KLM Cityhopper, Martinair and Transavia serve a worldwide network from Schiphol. The airline is known for its blue livery, Delft Blue miniature houses given to business-class passengers, and pioneering work in airline alliances, including its early partnership with Northwest Airlines. Since 2004 it has operated as one half of the Air France–KLM holding while retaining its own brand and Dutch identity.
