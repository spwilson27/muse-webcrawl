Margaret Mead (1901–1978) was an American cultural anthropologist and one of the most famous anthropologists of the twentieth century. A student of Franz Boas and Ruth Benedict at Columbia University, she became a curator at the American Museum of Natural History and a widely read popular author.

Fieldwork

Mead's 1928 book Coming of Age in Samoa, based on fieldwork among adolescent girls on the island of Ta'u, argued that adolescent experience is shaped largely by culture rather than by universal biological turmoil. Her subsequent studies in Papua New Guinea, including Sex and Temperament in Three Primitive Societies (1935), examined how cultures construct gender roles differently. With Gregory Bateson she also conducted pioneering visual ethnography in Bali.

Legacy

Mead's popular writings influenced debates about child-rearing, sexuality and education. Her Samoan work was later fiercely criticised by Derek Freeman, sparking the long-running "Mead–Freeman controversy".
