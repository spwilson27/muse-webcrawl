The Committee of Public Safety (French: Comité de salut public) was a committee of the National Convention during the French Revolution. Formed in April 1793 to oversee the government and defend the young republic against foreign invasion and internal rebellion, it became, under the influence of Maximilien Robespierre and his allies, the organ most closely associated with the Reign of Terror.

Origins and powers

The Convention created the committee amid military disaster: war with most of Europe, counter-revolutionary revolt in the Vendée, and the defection of General Dumouriez. Initially led by Georges Danton, the committee was reconstituted in July 1793 with Robespierre among its members, and its size stabilised at twelve. Granted authority over ministers, generals, and internal security, it worked alongside the Committee of General Security and the Revolutionary Tribunal.

The Terror

Through the Law of Suspects (September 1793) and the levée en masse, the committee mobilised France for total war while directing repression of perceived enemies. Under the Law of 22 Prairial (June 1794), trials were accelerated and defences curtailed; thousands were guillotined in Paris and far more died in provincial repression. Lazare Carnot's military organisation — "the organiser of victory" — simultaneously rebuilt the French armies, which by 1794 were winning on the frontiers, undercutting the emergency rationale for the Terror.

Fall

On 9 Thermidor (27 July 1794) the Convention turned against Robespierre, who was arrested and executed the next day with Saint-Just and others. The committee was stripped of its extraordinary powers, reorganised with rotating membership, and finally abolished with the Convention itself in 1795.
