Secretary of State for Defence


Ministry of Defence Main Building, Whitehall

The Secretary of State for Defence, also known as the Defence Secretary, is a Secretary of State in the Government of the United Kingdom, with responsibility for the Ministry of Defence. As a senior minister, the incumbent is a member of the Cabinet of the United Kingdom.

The postholder works alongside the other Defence ministers and is supported by the Permanent Under-Secretary of State for Defence and the Chief of the Defence Staff, the professional head of the British Armed Forces. The Defence Secretary is the chair of the Defence Council.

History

The office was created in 1964 with the unification of the Ministry of Defence, bringing together the Admiralty, War Office, and Air Ministry, which had previously been headed by their own ministers. The first holder was Peter Thorneycroft. The modern unified ministry was fully established in 1971, absorbing the Ministry of Aviation Supply.

Responsibilities

The Defence Secretary is responsible for defence policy, the strategic direction of the armed forces, defence procurement and spending, and the United Kingdom's nuclear deterrent, including authorizing arrangements for the Trident programme. The postholder represents UK defence interests in NATO and other international forums and is accountable to Parliament for the Ministry of Defence.
