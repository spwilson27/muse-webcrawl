Tehran Conference

The Tehran Conference (codenamed Eureka) was a strategy meeting of Joseph Stalin, Franklin D. Roosevelt and Winston Churchill from 28 November to 1 December 1943, held at the Soviet Union's embassy in Tehran, Iran. It was the first of the World War II conferences of the "Big Three" Allied leaders and followed the Cairo Conference, which had taken place on 22–26 November 1943.

Decisions

The central decisions reached at Tehran were a commitment to open a second front against Nazi Germany — the cross-Channel invasion of France, Operation Overlord, set for May 1944 — and agreement that the Soviet Union would launch a simultaneous offensive on the Eastern Front to coincide with it. The leaders also discussed the postwar division of Germany, Poland's borders (moving them westward), and Iran's independence and territorial integrity, which the three powers guaranteed in the Declaration of the Three Powers Regarding Iran. Yugoslav partisans under Tito were to receive full Allied support.

Significance

The conference marked the high point of Allied cooperation and set the strategic direction of the war in Europe. It also foreshadowed tensions over the postwar settlement that would surface more openly at the Yalta and Potsdam conferences in 1945.
