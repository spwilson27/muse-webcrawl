Ice

Ice is water that is frozen into a solid state, typically forming at or below temperatures of 0 °C (32 °F). It occurs naturally as snow, hail, glaciers, ice caps, frozen lakes and sea ice, and makes up much of Earth's cryosphere. Pure ice is transparent or slightly blue, depending on impurities and thickness.

Physical properties

Unusually, ice is less dense than liquid water — about 0.917 g/cm³ — so it floats. This anomaly results from hydrogen bonding, which holds water molecules in an open hexagonal lattice (ordinary ice, ice Ih) that takes up more space than the liquid. Ice has at least 18 known crystalline and amorphous phases, formed at different temperatures and pressures, several of which occur in the interiors of icy moons and planets.

Roles and uses

Ice regulates climate through its high albedo and through sea-level effects when land ice melts. Humans use ice for cooling and food preservation, for winter sports such as skating and hockey, for ice sculpture, and historically in the ice trade, when natural ice was harvested and shipped worldwide in the 19th century.
