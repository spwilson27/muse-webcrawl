Second impeachment of Donald Trump

The second impeachment of Donald Trump, the 45th president of the United States, took place in January and February 2021. On 13 January 2021, one week after the attack on the United States Capitol by his supporters, the House of Representatives impeached Trump on a single article charging "incitement of insurrection", making him the only US president to be impeached twice. The Senate tried him after he had left office and acquitted him on 13 February 2021, voting 57–43 to convict, short of the two-thirds majority required.

Background

Following his defeat in the 2020 presidential election, Trump repeatedly claimed the result was fraudulent. On 6 January 2021, as Congress met to certify the Electoral College results, he addressed a rally of supporters near the White House; a mob then stormed the Capitol, disrupting the count for hours and causing deaths and injuries. Trump had previously been impeached in December 2019 over the Ukraine affair and acquitted in February 2020.

Impeachment and trial

The article of impeachment charged that Trump had incited the insurrection through his statements and his efforts to overturn the election. Ten House Republicans joined Democrats in voting to impeach — the most bipartisan presidential impeachment vote in US history. Because Trump left office on 20 January, Chief Justice John Roberts did not preside; Senate president pro tempore Patrick Leahy did. House managers, led by Jamie Raskin, presented video evidence from the attack; Trump's lawyers argued the trial of a former president was unconstitutional and that his speech was protected by the First Amendment. Seven Republican senators voted to convict, the most of a president's own party in any impeachment trial, but the 57–43 vote fell ten short of conviction. A separate vote on disqualifying Trump from future office was therefore never held.
