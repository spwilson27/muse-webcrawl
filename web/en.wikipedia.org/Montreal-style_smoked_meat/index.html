Montreal-style smoked meat

Montreal-style smoked meat, or simply smoked meat in Quebec, is a type of kosher-style deli meat product made by salting and curing beef brisket with spices. The brisket is allowed to absorb the flavours over a week, then hot smoked to cook through, and finally steamed to completion before being sliced and served, most famously in sandwiches on rye bread with mustard.

CourseMain course
Place of originMontreal, Quebec, Canada
Main ingredientsBeef brisket, spices, mustard, rye bread

History

The dish is associated with Jewish delicatessens founded by Eastern European immigrants in Montreal in the early 20th century, including establishments such as Schwartz's (founded 1928) and the Main Deli. Its spice cure, heavier on cracked peppercorns and coriander and lighter on sugar than New York pastrami, gives it a distinctive character.

Preparation

Brisket is dry-cured with salt, pepper, coriander, garlic and other spices, then smoked, and finally steamed for hours until tender. It is hand-sliced to order, typically ordered by fat content: lean, medium, medium-fat, or fat.
