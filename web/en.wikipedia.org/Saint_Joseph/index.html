Saint Joseph

Venerated inRoman Catholic Church; Eastern Orthodox Church; Oriental Orthodox Churches; Anglican Communion; Lutheranism
Feast19 March; 1 May (Joseph the Worker)
PatronageThe universal Church; workers; fathers; carpenters; a happy death

Saint Joseph was, according to the canonical Gospels, the husband of Mary, the mother of Jesus, and the legal father of Jesus. He is described as a righteous man and a craftsman (often rendered "carpenter") of Nazareth, descended from the house of David.

In the Gospels

Joseph appears chiefly in the infancy narratives of Matthew and Luke. Informed by an angel in dreams, he took Mary as his wife, fled with the family to Egypt to escape Herod, and later settled in Nazareth. He is last mentioned during Jesus' childhood; tradition holds that he died before the start of Jesus' public ministry.

Veneration

Devotion to Saint Joseph flourished from the late Middle Ages onward. Pope Pius IX declared him patron of the universal Church in 1870, and Pope Pius XII instituted the feast of Saint Joseph the Worker on 1 May. He is patron of numerous countries, dioceses and professions.
