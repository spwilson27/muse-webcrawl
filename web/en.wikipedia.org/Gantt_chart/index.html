A Gantt chart is a bar chart used in project management to illustrate a schedule. Tasks are listed along the vertical axis and time intervals along the horizontal axis; the position and length of each bar show start dates, end dates and duration. Modern versions commonly mark dependencies, milestones, progress and the critical path.

History

Earlier graphical production schedules were developed by Karol Adamiecki in the 1890s. Henry Gantt popularised a related chart in the 1910s for industrial production and wartime planning, and the format became widely associated with his name.

Use

Managers use Gantt charts to plan work breakdown structures, assign resources, coordinate hand-offs and communicate deadlines. Software tools can roll tasks into summary bars and recalculate dates when dependencies change.

Limitations

A Gantt chart can become cluttered on large projects and may imply more certainty than a schedule really has. It shows timing clearly but does not by itself show task size, uncertainty or resource load as effectively as some complementary views.
