Charax (Greek: Χάραξ) was a Roman military settlement and fort on Cape Ai-Todor, on the southern coast of Crimea, not far from modern Yalta. Its name, meaning "palisade" or "fortification," reflects its role guarding the coast of the Bosporan Kingdom, a Roman client state.

History

The fort was established under the emperor Vespasian in the 1st century AD, around AD 70, when Roman forces consolidated control over the southern Crimean coast. Legions including Legio I Italica and Legio XI Claudia were garrisoned there at various times. Charax was the largest Roman military site in Crimea, covering roughly 4.5 hectares behind walls on the cape. The Romans withdrew around AD 244 amid Gothic pressure, after which the site was abandoned.

Archaeology

Excavations, begun in the 19th century and continued in the 20th, uncovered defensive walls, barracks, a cistern, baths, and a sanctuary. Today the lighthouse on Ai-Todor stands within the ancient site.
