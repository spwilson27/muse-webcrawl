Jadeite Cabbage

TypeJadeite sculpture
Date19th century, Qing dynasty
Dimensions18.7 cm × 9.1 cm
LocationNational Palace Museum, Taipei

The Jadeite Cabbage (Chinese: 翠玉白菜; pinyin: Cuìyù Báicài) is a famous Chinese sculpture carved from a single piece of jadeite in the form of a Chinese cabbage head. Dating to the Qing dynasty (19th century), it is one of the most celebrated treasures of the National Palace Museum in Taipei, Taiwan, frequently described as the museum's most famous masterpiece.

Description

The work is small — only about 19 cm long — but brilliantly exploits the natural colours of the stone: the translucent white of the cabbage stalk shades into the deep green of the leaves, and even a crack-like imperfection in the jade has been incorporated into the veins of the leaves. Hidden among the leaves are two insects, a katydid and a locust, rendered in fine detail.

History and symbolism

The cabbage is thought to have formed part of the dowry of Consort Jin (the future Dowager Imperial Noble Consort Wenjing), a consort of the Guangxu Emperor, and was originally displayed in the Forbidden City in Beijing. In Chinese symbolism the white stalk of the cabbage (báicài) stands for purity, while the insects concealed in the leaves are emblems of fertility and abundant offspring — wishes for the young bride. The sculpture was among the imperial treasures evacuated from Beijing during the 1930s–40s and ultimately taken to Taiwan in 1949.

Fame

Together with the Meat-shaped Stone and the Mao Gong Ding, the Jadeite Cabbage is one of the "Three Treasures" of the National Palace Museum. It is so popular that museum merchandise — replicas, key rings and stationery — is sold throughout Taiwan, and the work has occasionally been lent for exhibitions abroad, including in Japan.
