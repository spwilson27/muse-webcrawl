Visual field test

Automated perimetry maps the visual field

Other names: Perimetry, campimetry

Field: Ophthalmology, neurology

A visual field test is an eye examination that can detect dysfunction in central and peripheral vision caused by conditions such as glaucoma, stroke, pituitary disease, brain tumours, or other neurological deficits. The visual field is the entire area that can be seen when the eye is directed forward — about 100 degrees temporally and 60 degrees nasally in each eye. Testing maps the field and reveals blind spots (scotomas) beyond the normal physiological blind spot.

Methods

The simplest method is the confrontation visual field exam, in which the examiner compares the patient's field with their own by presenting fingers in each quadrant. More precise methods include tangent screen campimetry, Goldmann kinetic perimetry — in which a moving light stimulus is mapped — and automated static perimetry, most commonly with the Humphrey Field Analyzer, which presents stationary lights of varying brightness and measures the threshold sensitivity at each point, producing a numerical map.

Patterns of loss

Characteristic defects localize lesions along the visual pathways: a bitemporal hemianopia suggests compression of the optic chiasm, homonymous hemianopia points to retrochiasmal damage, and the arcuate defects and nasal steps of glaucoma reflect damage to retinal nerve fibre bundles.
