Vendée

The Vendée (French pronunciation: [vɑ̃de]) is a department in the Pays de la Loire region in Western France, on the Atlantic coast. Its prefecture is La Roche-sur-Yon, founded by Napoleon in 1804. The department takes its name from the Vendée river and has a population of around 700,000.

History

The Vendée is best known for the War in the Vendée (1793–1796), a counter-revolutionary insurrection during the French Revolution, in which the largely rural, Catholic population rose against the revolutionary government's conscription and religious policies. The brutal suppression of the revolt, including the notorious colonnes infernales, caused enormous loss of life and remains a subject of historical controversy.

Geography and tourism

The department includes marshland (the Marais Poitevin to the south and the Marais Breton to the north), bocage countryside inland, and sandy Atlantic beaches. The islands of Noirmoutier and Île d'Yeu lie off its coast. The Vendée is also known for the Puy du Fou historical theme park and as the starting and finishing port of the Vendée Globe solo round-the-world yacht race, held every four years from Les Sables-d'Olonne.
