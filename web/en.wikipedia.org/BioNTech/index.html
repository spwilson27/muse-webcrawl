BioNTech SE (short for Biopharmaceutical New Technologies) is a German biotechnology company headquartered in Mainz. Founded in 2008 by Uğur Şahin, Özlem Türeci, and Christoph Huber, the company develops immunotherapies based on messenger RNA (mRNA) technology, originally focused on individualized cancer treatments.

BioNTech became internationally prominent in 2020 when, in partnership with Pfizer, it developed Comirnaty (BNT162b2), one of the first vaccines authorized for emergency use against COVID-19. The vaccine was the first mRNA product ever approved for use in humans and generated tens of billions of dollars in revenue, which the company reinvested in its oncology pipeline and global manufacturing network.

History

BioNTech was founded in 2008 as a spin-off of the Johannes Gutenberg University Mainz, based on research by Şahin and Türeci into mRNA-based cancer vaccines and immunomodulators. The company raised substantial private funding before listing on the NASDAQ stock exchange in October 2019. It acquired several companies to build out its cell therapy and antibody capabilities, including Neon Therapeutics in 2020.

COVID-19 vaccine

In January 2020, BioNTech launched "Project Lightspeed" to develop a vaccine against the newly identified SARS-CoV-2 virus. In March 2020 it partnered with Pfizer (and with Fosun Pharma for China). The vaccine candidate BNT162b2 was selected for large-scale trials; on 9 November 2020 the companies announced interim efficacy above 90%, and regulatory authorizations followed in the United Kingdom, the United States, and the European Union in December 2020. Full approval by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration followed in August 2021.

Pipeline

After the pandemic, BioNTech returned its focus to oncology, with individualized neoantigen vaccines (such as autogene cevumeran, developed with Genentech), shared-antigen cancer vaccines, CAR T-cell therapies, and next-generation antibody-drug conjugates. It is also developing mRNA vaccines against infectious diseases including influenza, malaria, tuberculosis, and shingles.
