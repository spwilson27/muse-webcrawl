Jamón serrano (Spanish for "mountain ham") is a Spanish dry-cured ham produced from the hind leg of white pig breeds such as Landrace, Large White or Duroc. It is one of the most recognisable foods of Spanish cuisine and is protected as a Traditional Speciality Guaranteed (TSG) product in the European Union.

Production

Fresh hams are trimmed, salted and hung to drain, then washed and dried in ventilated curing houses (secaderos) where temperature and humidity are gradually adjusted. Curing lasts from about seven months to over a year, during which the ham loses roughly a third of its weight and develops its characteristic flavour. The traditional mountain air of regions such as Teruel, Trevélez and Serón gives the product its name.

Classification

By curing time, jamón serrano is graded as bodega (9–12 months), reserva (12–15 months) and gran reserva (over 15 months). It is distinct from the more expensive jamón ibérico, which comes from Iberian pigs.

Serving

The ham is sliced very thin and served at room temperature, often with bread, as a tapa, or in dishes such as huevos rotos and croquetas. Whole legs are traditionally held in a jamonero stand for carving.
