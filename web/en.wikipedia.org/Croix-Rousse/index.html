Croix-Rousse

Croix-Rousse is a hill and neighbourhood in the city of Lyon, France, forming the city's 4th arrondissement together with the plateau, while its slopes belong to the 1st arrondissement. The name means "russet cross", after a cross of reddish-brown stone erected there in the sixteenth century. Croix-Rousse is famous as the historic centre of Lyon's silk industry and for its two great workers' revolts of the nineteenth century.

History

In Roman times the hill overlooked the colony of Lugdunum. From the eighteenth century the silk weavers of Lyon, the canuts, settled on the plateau of Croix-Rousse because the tall buildings there could accommodate their large Jacquard looms. The district is threaded by traboules, covered passageways through buildings that allowed workers to carry silk goods across the hill sheltered from the rain.

The canut revolts

In 1831 and 1834 the canuts rose in revolt over piece rates and working conditions, briefly taking control of Lyon under the banner "live working or die fighting". The revolts are regarded as among the first recognisably modern workers' uprisings and influenced later socialist thought.

The district today

Croix-Rousse retains a village-like atmosphere, with a daily market, the Maison des Canuts museum, and the Jardin des Chartreux overlooking the Saône. It is commonly paired with Fourvière: Fourvière is "the hill that prays", Croix-Rousse "the hill that works".
