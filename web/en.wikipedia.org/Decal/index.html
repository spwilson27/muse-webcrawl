Decal


Applying a waterslide decal

A decal (short for decalcomania, from French décalquer, "to transfer") is a plastic, cloth, paper, or ceramic substrate that has printed on it a pattern or image that can be moved to another surface upon contact, usually with the aid of heat or water.

Decals are widely used on models, ceramics, glassware, vehicles, and windows. Common types include waterslide decals, used by model builders; dry transfers, applied by rubbing; and vinyl decals, cut from adhesive film and used for signage and decoration.

History

The decalcomania process developed in Britain in the 1750s as a method of transfer printing for pottery and was patented by Simon François Ravenet in 1756. It allowed manufacturers to decorate ceramics with detailed designs that previously required hand-painting. In the 19th century, decalcomania briefly became a popular hobby craze.

Types

Waterslide decals consist of a printed film on water-soluble gum over carrier paper; soaking releases the film, which is slid into place and allowed to dry. Pressure-sensitive decals use adhesive and are common for labels and vehicle graphics. Iron-on transfers apply images to fabric with heat.
