Clingmans Dome is a mountain in the Great Smoky Mountains of Tennessee and North Carolina in the south-eastern United States. At an elevation of 6,643 feet (2,025 m), it is the highest mountain in the Smokies, the highest point in the state of Tennessee, and the highest point along the 2,192-mile (3,528 km) Appalachian Trail. It is also the third highest point in mainland Eastern North America, after Mount Mitchell (6,684 feet) and Mount Craig (6,647 feet).


The observation tower atop Clingmans Dome, reached by a paved trail.

Geography and access

The summit straddles the Tennessee–North Carolina state line and the boundary of Great Smoky Mountains National Park. A seven-mile spur road from Newfound Gap leads to a parking area, from which a steep paved trail of half a mile climbs to a 45-foot (14 m) concrete observation tower built in 1959, offering panoramic views that on clear days can exceed 100 miles. The road is closed in winter. The Appalachian Trail crosses the summit, and several other trails radiate from it.

Ecology

The summit is capped by a Southern Appalachian spruce–fir forest, a boreal relict ecosystem dominated by red spruce and Fraser fir. The forest has been severely damaged by the balsam woolly adelgid, an introduced insect that has killed most mature Fraser firs since the 1960s, and by acid precipitation and air pollution carried from industrial regions. The summit's climate is cool and wet, comparable to southern Canada, with annual precipitation around 140 inches (3,600 mm) and snow possible from October into May.

Name

The Cherokee called the mountain Kuwahi, "mulberry place", and regarded it as a sacred place. It was renamed by surveyor Arnold Guyot for Thomas Lanier Clingman, a North Carolina senator and Confederate general who had promoted exploration of the region and disputed with Elisha Mitchell over which peak was highest in the East.
