Gatefold

A gatefold is a type of fold used in printing and packaging in which two end flaps fold inward to meet in the middle, like a pair of gates. The term is most widely known for the gatefold sleeve, a record cover for LP albums that opens out like a book to twice its width, and for gatefold pages in magazines, which unfold to reveal an oversized image or advertisement.

Record sleeves

Gatefold LP covers became popular in the 1960s and 1970s, offering space for large artwork, lyrics, photographs, and liner notes that a standard single sleeve could not provide. They were especially associated with concept albums and progressive rock; celebrated examples include the Beatles' Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band (1967) and Pink Floyd's The Dark Side of the Moon (1973). Double albums almost always require gatefold packaging to hold two discs. Gatefolds are more expensive to produce than single sleeves and were sometimes also used for single LPs as a prestige or deluxe format.

Magazines

In magazines, a gatefold is a page folded to a larger size that opens outward, used for dramatic photographs, fold-out posters, and premium advertising. Playboy's centerfold is a well-known variant of the form. Gatefolds are also used in brochures, maps, and book dust jackets where a wide image or extra area for text is needed.
