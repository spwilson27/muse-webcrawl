Mothball

Mothballs are small balls of chemical pesticide and deodorant, sometimes used when storing clothing and other materials susceptible to damage from silverfish, mold, or moth larvae (especially clothes moths such as Tineola bisselliella).

Composition and action

Older mothballs consisted mainly of naphthalene, once derived from coal tar; most modern products use 1,4-dichlorobenzene (para-dichlorobenzene) instead. Both chemicals are solids that sublime into vapours which are toxic to moth larvae. To be effective, mothballs must be used in airtight containers so the fumes accumulate to lethal concentrations; scattered in open cupboards they are largely ineffective and merely add chemical vapour to the room air.

Health and environmental concerns

Naphthalene is a possible carcinogen and can cause haemolytic anaemia, particularly in people with glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency; 1,4-dichlorobenzene is also classified as a possible carcinogen. Accidental ingestion by children and pets is a recurring poisoning hazard, and several jurisdictions restrict the sale of mothballs or ban naphthalene products. Mothballs are also sometimes misused as animal repellents outdoors, which regulators discourage because of risks to wildlife and groundwater.

The term is also used figuratively: to "mothball" equipment, ships, or facilities means to remove them from service and place them in storage for possible later use.
