Australian megafauna

Australian megafauna is a term used to describe a number of large animal species in Australia that existed during the Pleistocene epoch and became extinct within the last 60,000 years or so. The assemblage was dominated by giant marsupials and also included enormous flightless birds, reptiles and monotremes.

Notable species

The largest was Diprotodon optatum, a rhinoceros-sized wombat relative and the largest marsupial known. Other giants included the marsupial lion Thylacoleo carnifex, the giant kangaroo Procoptodon goliah, the flightless bird Genyornis newtoni, the giant monitor lizard Varanus priscus (Megalania), and the huge snake Wonambi naracoortensis. Fossil sites such as the Naracoorte Caves and Lake Mungo preserve rich records of these animals.

Extinction debate

The causes of the extinctions remain debated. Proposed drivers include hunting by the first Aboriginal Australians, landscape burning altering vegetation, and increasing aridity during and after the last glacial maximum. Most extinctions appear to have occurred between about 50,000 and 40,000 years ago, shortly after human arrival, and many researchers favour a combination of human impacts and climate stress. Some have speculated that Aboriginal Dreaming traditions and depictions in rock art may preserve memories of megafauna.
