Cattle feeding is the practice of providing cattle with diets that meet their nutritional requirements for maintenance, growth, reproduction, and milk production. Because cattle are ruminants, their feeding centres on the rumen — a large fermentation vat in which microbes digest fibrous plant material that monogastric animals cannot use, converting forages into meat and milk.

Nutritional requirements

Rations are formulated around energy (often expressed as total digestible nutrients or net energy), crude protein, fibre (neutral detergent fibre), minerals (notably calcium, phosphorus, salt, and trace elements), and vitamins. Requirements vary with age, weight, breed, stage of production (growing, lactating, gestating), and environment. Water is the most critical nutrient; a lactating cow may drink over 100 litres per day.

Feeds

  • Forages — pasture, hay, and silages (corn silage, grass silage, alfalfa) form the basis of most diets, especially for breeding herds and dairy cows.
  • Concentrates — grains such as corn, barley, and oats, plus byproducts (distillers' grains, soybean meal, cottonseed) provide energy and protein, especially in finishing rations.
  • Supplements — mineral mixes, buffers, and (where legal and used) ionophores and other feed additives that improve feed efficiency.

Production phases

In beef production, calves are typically raised on pasture with their dams (cow–calf phase), then "backgrounded" on forage-based diets, and finally finished either in feedlots on high-grain diets — the dominant system in North America — or on grass ("grass-finished"). Dairy cattle feeding emphasizes high-energy rations to support milk yield, with total mixed rations (TMR) common on larger farms.

Issues

Cattle feeding is debated in relation to feed conversion efficiency, land and water use, methane emissions from enteric fermentation, manure management, and animal welfare. Ruminant diets also intersect with human food supply, since cattle can convert grass and byproducts inedible to humans into food, though grain finishing competes more directly with human-edible crops.
