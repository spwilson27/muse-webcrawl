Pike and shot

Pike and shot was the infantry tactical system of European warfare from the late 15th to the late 17th century, combining pikemen — who protected the formation from cavalry and fought in close combat — with "shot", soldiers armed with arquebuses and later muskets.

Development

The system grew out of Swiss pike squares and the Spanish tercio, which mixed pikes, swords and firearms in large blocks. Dutch reforms under Maurice of Nassau and Swedish reforms under Gustavus Adolphus made formations shallower and more flexible, increasing firepower with volley fire and regimental guns.

Decline

The spread of flintlock muskets, socket bayonets (which turned every musketeer into his own pikeman) and linear tactics around 1700 made the pike obsolete, ending the pike-and-shot era.
