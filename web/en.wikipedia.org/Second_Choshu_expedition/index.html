The Second Choshu expedition, also known as the Second Choshu Conquest or the Summer War, was a punitive military campaign launched by the Tokugawa shogunate in 1866 against the rebellious Choshu Domain in western Japan. Unlike the First Choshu expedition of 1864, which had ended in Choshu's submission without major fighting, the second expedition ended in a humiliating defeat for the shogunate.

The shogunate attacked Choshu on four fronts in the summer of 1866.

Background

After the first expedition, reformists led by Takasugi Shinsaku seized power within Choshu and rebuilt its military along Western lines, mixing samurai with commoner militia units such as the Kiheitai and arming them with modern rifles and warships purchased through foreign traders. Meanwhile, Choshu secretly concluded the Satsuma–Choshu Alliance with its former rival Satsuma in early 1866, brokered by Sakamoto Ryoma.

The war

The shogunate attacked on four fronts in June 1866: toward Oshima, Iwami, Geishu (Aki), and Kokura. Choshu's modernized forces defeated the shogunal armies on most fronts, capturing Suo-Oshima and advancing into Iwami and northern Kyushu. The shogun Tokugawa Iemochi died at Osaka Castle in August 1866, giving the shogunate a pretext to negotiate a ceasefire, which was mediated by Katsu Kaishu. The fighting effectively ended in September, with Choshu retaining its territory.

Consequences

The defeat fatally undermined the prestige and finances of the Tokugawa regime. Within two years the Boshin War and the Meiji Restoration swept the shogunate away, with Choshu and Satsuma men central to the new government.
