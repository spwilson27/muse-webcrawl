Nicolaas Bloembergen (March 11, 1920 – September 5, 2017) was a Dutch-American physicist and Nobel laureate who pioneered the fields of nuclear magnetic resonance and nonlinear optics. He shared the 1981 Nobel Prize in Physics with Arthur Schawlow and Kai Siegbahn for his contribution to the development of laser spectroscopy.

Bloembergen studied at Utrecht University, escaping occupied Netherlands after the war to attend Harvard University, where he completed his PhD under Edward Purcell. His doctoral work on nuclear magnetic relaxation laid foundations for MRI technology. He spent most of his career at Harvard, later moving to the University of Arizona.

Nonlinear optics

In the early 1960s Bloembergen and his group developed the theoretical framework of nonlinear optics, describing how intense laser light changes the optical properties of materials, enabling frequency mixing, harmonic generation and other effects now fundamental to laser science. He also proposed the three-level pumping scheme used in masers and lasers. Among his many honours were the National Medal of Science and the Lorentz Medal.
