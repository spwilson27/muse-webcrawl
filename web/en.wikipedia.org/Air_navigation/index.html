Air navigation

The basic principles of air navigation are identical to general navigation, which includes the process of planning, recording, and controlling the movement of a craft from one place to another. Successful air navigation involves piloting an aircraft from place to place without getting lost, not breaking the laws applying to aircraft, or endangering the safety of those on board or on the ground.

Techniques

Techniques used include pilotage (navigating by visual landmarks), dead reckoning (calculating position from course, speed, and wind), celestial navigation, and radio navigation using ground beacons such as VOR and NDB. Modern air navigation relies overwhelmingly on satellite systems such as GPS, integrated into flight management systems, and on area navigation (RNAV) procedures defined by waypoints rather than ground stations.

Flight rules

Aircraft navigate under visual flight rules (VFR) or instrument flight rules (IFR) depending on weather and airspace. Flight planning must account for wind, fuel, airspace restrictions, and available air traffic control services.
