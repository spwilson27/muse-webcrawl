Shawl

A shawl (from Persian shāl) is a simple item of clothing, loosely worn over the shoulders, upper body, and arms, and sometimes also over the head. It is typically a rectangular or square piece of cloth and is usually folded into a triangle and placed over the shoulders.

History

Shawls woven in Kashmir from fine pashmina wool became prized luxury goods, decorated with intricate boteh (paisley) motifs. In the late 18th and 19th centuries, Kashmiri shawls were imported to Europe in large numbers, and mills in Paisley, Scotland, produced woven imitations that gave the motif its English name. Shawls were fashionable women's garments throughout the Victorian era.

Types and uses

Shawls are made from wool, silk, cotton, and synthetic fibres, and may be knitted, woven, or embroidered. They serve as warm wraps, fashion accessories, and ceremonial garments: the Jewish tallit is a prayer shawl, and shawls feature in folk costume and dance in many cultures, from the Spanish mantón to the rebozo of Mexico.
