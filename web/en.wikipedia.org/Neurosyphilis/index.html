Neurosyphilis

Neurosyphilis is the infection of the central nervous system by Treponema pallidum, the bacterium that causes syphilis. Invasion of the nervous system can occur at any stage of syphilis, though the classic late manifestations develop years to decades after initial infection.

Forms and symptoms

Early neurosyphilis may be asymptomatic or cause meningitis, cranial nerve palsies, and ocular or ear involvement. Late forms include meningovascular syphilis, which can cause stroke; general paresis, a progressive dementia with psychiatric symptoms; and tabes dorsalis, marked by loss of proprioception, lightning pains, and the Argyll Robertson pupil. Neurosyphilis became much less common after penicillin, though it remains a concern, particularly in people with HIV.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis combines blood serology with cerebrospinal fluid examination. Treatment is high-dose intravenous aqueous penicillin G; follow-up testing monitors for treatment success.
