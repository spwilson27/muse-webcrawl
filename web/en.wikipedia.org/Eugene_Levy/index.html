Eugene Levy

Eugene Levy (born 1946), Canadian actor, comedian and writer known for SCTV, American Pie and Schitt's Creek.

Eugene Levy CM (born December 17, 1946) is a Canadian actor and comedian. Known for a career spanning five decades, he first became famous as a cast member of the sketch comedy series SCTV (1976–1984), and later reached wide audiences as Noah Levenstein, "Jim's Dad", in the American Pie film series, and as Johnny Rose in the sitcom Schitt's Creek (2015–2020), which he co-created with his son Dan Levy.

Early life

Levy was born in Hamilton, Ontario, to a Jewish family; his mother was a homemaker and his father a foreman at an automobile plant. He attended McMaster University, where he became involved in student film and comedy, crossing paths with future collaborators including Martin Short, Ivan Reitman and Dave Thomas.

Career

After early work with the Toronto production of Godspell and low-budget films for Reitman, Levy joined the Second City troupe and then SCTV, where his characters included news anchor Earl Camembert and comic Bobby Bittman, winning two Emmy Awards for writing. He became a frequent collaborator of Christopher Guest in the mockumentary films Waiting for Guffman (1996), Best in Show (2000), A Mighty Wind (2003) and For Your Consideration (2006). In film he also appeared in Splash, National Lampoon's Vacation, Cheaper by the Dozen and Bringing Down the House, and voiced Charlie in Finding Dory (2016).

Schitt's Creek and later work

Schitt's Creek, in which Levy played opposite Catherine O'Hara (his long-time collaborator since SCTV) and his children Dan and Sarah Levy, became a critical and commercial success in its later seasons; at the 72nd Primetime Emmy Awards in 2020 the series swept the comedy awards, and Levy won Outstanding Lead Actor in a Comedy Series. He later hosted the Apple TV+ travel series The Reluctant Traveller. Levy was appointed to the Order of Canada, and received the Earle Grey Award for lifetime achievement in Canadian television.
