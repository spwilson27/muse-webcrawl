Ankara is the capital city of Turkey, located in the central part of Anatolia. It is the country's second-largest city, after Istanbul, and has been the capital since the establishment of the Republic of Turkey in 1923. Historically known as Ancyra and Angora, the city has a history stretching back to the Bronze Age Hatti civilisation.

History

Ankara was an important centre under the Phrygians, the Galatians (who made it their capital), and the Romans, for whom Ancyra was the capital of the province of Galatia; the Monumentum Ancyranum preserves the Res Gestae Divi Augusti. It later passed through Byzantine, Seljuk, and Ottoman hands. In 1402 the Battle of Ankara saw Timur defeat Sultan Bayezid I nearby. Following the Turkish War of Independence, Mustafa Kemal Atatürk made Ankara the seat of the provisional government, and it was declared the capital on 13 October 1923, largely because of its central location and distance from the former Ottoman capital.

Government and landmarks

Ankara hosts the Grand National Assembly of Turkey, the presidential complex at Beştepe, and foreign embassies. Anıtkabir, the monumental mausoleum of Atatürk, is the city's best-known landmark. The Museum of Anatolian Civilizations, housed in a restored Ottoman bazaar near Ankara Castle, holds an extensive collection of Anatolian antiquities.

Climate

Ankara has a continental climate with hot, dry summers and cold, snowy winters, moderated by its elevation of about 938 metres on the Anatolian plateau.
