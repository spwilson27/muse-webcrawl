The Oireachtas (Irish pronunciation roughly "eh-rakh-tas"), sometimes called the Oireachtas Éireann, is the legislature of Ireland. Under the Constitution of Ireland it consists of the President of Ireland and two houses: Dáil Éireann, the directly elected lower house, and Seanad Éireann, the upper house or senate.

Composition

Dáil Éireann is elected by universal suffrage using the single transferable vote in multi-seat constituencies; its members are called Teachtaí Dála (TDs). The government (led by the Taoiseach) is chosen by and responsible to the Dáil. Seanad Éireann has 60 members: 43 elected by panels representing vocational interests, 6 by university constituencies, and 11 nominated by the Taoiseach. The Seanad has weaker powers — it can delay but generally not block legislation, and money bills are effectively the preserve of the Dáil.

Powers and functions

The Oireachtas is the sole law-making authority of the state; bills must pass both houses and be signed by the President, who may in certain circumstances refer a bill to the Supreme Court to test its constitutionality. The Oireachtas also approves the budget, authorises taxation, and scrutinises the executive through questions and committees. Its proceedings in both houses are televised and broadcast by Oireachtas TV.

Seat and history

Both houses meet in Leinster House, Dublin, an eighteenth-century ducal palace acquired in 1924. The modern Oireachtas derives from the parliament of the Irish Free State established in 1922, itself succeeding the revolutionary Dáil of 1919; the present constitutional framework dates from 1937. A 2013 referendum proposal to abolish the Seanad was rejected by voters.
