Emblems of the International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement

The emblems of the International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement are protective symbols defined by the Geneva Conventions of 1949 and their Additional Protocols. In armed conflict they mark persons and objects entitled to protection — medical personnel, units, and transports — and their misuse is prohibited under international humanitarian law.

The emblems

The original emblem, adopted in 1863–64, is the red cross on a white background, formed by reversing the colours of the Swiss flag. The red crescent, used by the Ottoman Empire during the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–78, was formally recognised in 1929 and is used by many national societies in Muslim-majority countries. The red crystal, a red frame in the shape of a square on edge, was introduced by the Third Protocol in 2005 as an additional emblem free of national, religious, or cultural connotations; it also provides a framework under which Israel's Magen David Adom could join the Movement.

Uses

The emblems have two uses: a protective use in wartime, indicating neutral medical services, and an indicative use in peacetime, showing that a person or object is linked to a National Red Cross or Red Crescent Society. A red lion and sun emblem, formerly used by Iran, remains reserved in the Conventions although no longer in use.
