Agnosia

Agnosia
SpecialtyNeurology, neuropsychology
CausesStroke, brain injury, neurodegenerative disease
TypesVisual, auditory, tactile; prosopagnosia

Agnosia is a neurological disorder characterized by the inability to recognize and identify objects, persons, sounds, or other sensory stimuli, despite the sense organs themselves functioning normally. The term was coined by Sigmund Freud in 1891, from the Greek a- (without) and gnosis (knowledge).

Types

Visual agnosia impairs recognition of objects by sight; within it, apperceptive agnosia involves failure to form a coherent percept, while associative agnosia leaves perception intact but severs its connection to meaning. Prosopagnosia is the specific inability to recognize familiar faces. Auditory agnosia affects recognition of sounds, and tactile agnosia (astereognosis) prevents identification of objects by touch.

Causes and treatment

Agnosia typically results from damage to the occipitotemporal or parietal cortex, commonly from stroke, head injury, or degenerative disease. There is no direct cure; treatment focuses on rehabilitation, compensatory strategies, and treating the underlying cause.
