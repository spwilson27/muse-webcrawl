Jules Olitski

Jules Olitski (March 27, 1922 – February 4, 2007) was a Russian-born American painter, printmaker, and sculptor. He was a leading figure of Color Field painting and post-painterly abstraction.

Life and work

Born Jevel Demikovsky in Snovsk, in present-day Ukraine, Olitski emigrated with his family to the United States in 1923 and grew up in Brooklyn. He studied at the Pratt Institute and the National Academy of Design, and in Paris on the G.I. Bill. After early work influenced by the Abstract Expressionists, he developed stain paintings in the early 1960s and championed by the critic Clement Greenberg, became associated with the Washington Color School and Color Field movement.

Spray paintings and later work

In 1965 Olitski began spraying thinned acrylic paint onto canvas, producing luminous fields of gradated colour with no visible drawing — among the first major uses of the spray gun on canvas. He represented the United States at the Venice Biennale in 1966 and in 1969 became the first living American artist given a solo exhibition at the Metropolitan Museum of Art. His later work returned to heavily textured, gestural surfaces. Olitski died in New York in 2007.
