Medical test

A medical test is a medical procedure performed to detect, diagnose, or monitor diseases, disease processes, susceptibility, or to determine a course of treatment. Tests range from physical examinations and blood tests to imaging, biopsies, and genetic testing.

Types and purposes

Tests serve screening (testing asymptomatic populations), diagnosis, prognosis, and monitoring. Categories include laboratory tests on blood, urine, or tissue; imaging such as X-ray, CT, MRI, and ultrasound; electrophysiology such as ECG and EEG; and point-of-care rapid tests.

Performance

Test accuracy is described by sensitivity (true-positive rate) and specificity (true-negative rate). Interpretation depends on pre-test probability and prevalence: even accurate tests yield many false positives when screening low-risk populations, a consequence of Bayes' theorem. Reference ranges define typical values in healthy populations.

Risks

Risks include direct harm (radiation, invasive sampling), anxiety, incidental findings, and overdiagnosis leading to unnecessary treatment. Regulation of diagnostic tests varies by jurisdiction.
