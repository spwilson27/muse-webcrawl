Commission for Historical Clarification

The Commission for Historical Clarification (Spanish: Comisión para el Esclarecimiento Histórico, CEH) was a truth commission established in 1994 to investigate human rights violations committed during Guatemala's thirty-six-year civil war. It was created under the Oslo Accords between the government of Guatemala and the Unidad Revolucionaria Nacional Guatemalteca (URNG), as part of the peace process that ended the war in 1996.

Findings

The Commission documented some 42,275 victims in the cases it registered, estimating the total number killed or disappeared at over 200,000, the majority of them Maya civilians. It attributed 93 per cent of the violations it documented to state forces and allied paramilitary groups, and 3 per cent to the guerrillas. Crucially, it concluded that agents of the state had committed acts of genocide against groups of the Maya people in four regions between 1981 and 1983, during the counterinsurgency campaigns of the Ríos Montt era.

Report

Its twelve-volume report, Guatemala: Memory of Silence (Guatemala: Memoria del Silencio), was presented on 25 February 1999 in Guatemala City. The Commission recommended reparations, exhumations, institutional reform, and preservation of the memory of the victims, though it was not empowered to name individual perpetrators or to serve as a judicial body.

Legacy

The report's findings later supported prosecutions, including the 2013 genocide trial of former head of state Efraín Ríos Montt, and remain a central reference on the conflict.
