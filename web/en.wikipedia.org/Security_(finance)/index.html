Security (finance)

A security is a tradable financial asset. The term commonly refers to any form of financial instrument, but its legal definition varies by jurisdiction. Securities are generally divided into debt securities (such as bonds and debentures), equity securities (such as common stocks and shares), and derivative contracts (such as options and futures).

Types

Debt securities

Debt securities represent money borrowed that must be repaid, with terms defining the amount, interest rate and maturity date. They include government and corporate bonds, certificates of deposit and collateralized securities such as mortgage-backed securities.

Equity securities

An equity security represents ownership interest held by shareholders in an entity, realized in the form of shares of capital stock, including shares of both common and preferred stock.

Derivatives

Derivatives derive their value from an underlying asset, index or rate. Common examples include futures, options, swaps and forward contracts; they may trade on exchanges or over the counter.

Regulation

Public offers of securities are regulated to protect investors, typically requiring registration and disclosure. In the United States, the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) enforces the Securities Act of 1933 and the Securities Exchange Act of 1934. Securities are traded on exchanges or over the counter through brokers, and modern holdings are usually dematerialized, recorded electronically by a central securities depository.
