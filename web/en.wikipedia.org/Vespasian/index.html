Vespasian (Latin: Titus Flavius Caesar Vespasianus Augustus; 17 November 9 – 23 June 79 AD) was Roman emperor from 69 to 79, the last of the four emperors who reigned in the Year of the Four Emperors and the founder of the Flavian dynasty, which ruled the empire for 27 years.

Early career

Born into an equestrian family of no great distinction, Vespasian rose through military service, distinguishing himself in the invasion of Britain (43 AD) and as proconsul of Africa. In 66 AD Nero appointed him to suppress the Jewish revolt in Judaea, where he campaigned successfully before events in Rome intervened.

Emperor

After Nero's suicide in 68, the empire plunged into civil war. Vespasian was proclaimed emperor by his legions in Egypt and the east on 1 July 69; his forces, led by Antonius Primus, defeated Vitellius by the end of the year. His son Titus completed the siege of Jerusalem in 70, destroying the Second Temple.

Reforms and building

Vespasian restored the empire's finances — he is associated with the saying pecunia non olet ("money does not stink") over his tax on public urinals — reformed the Senate and the army, and began major building works in Rome, most famously the Flavian Amphitheatre, the Colosseum. He died of natural causes in 79 and was succeeded by Titus.
