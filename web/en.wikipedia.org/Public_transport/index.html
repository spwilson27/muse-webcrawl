Public transport (also known as public transportation, public transit, mass transit, or simply transit) is a system of transport for passengers by group travel systems available for use by the general public, typically managed on a schedule, operated on established routes, and charged a posted fee for each trip.

Modes

Examples of modes of public transport include city buses, trolleybuses, trams (or light rail), passenger trains, rapid transit (metro/subway/underground), and ferries. Public transport between cities is dominated by airlines, coaches and intercity rail. High-speed rail networks are being developed in many parts of the world.

History

The earliest forms of public transport were scheduled stagecoach services and horse-drawn omnibuses, which appeared in European cities in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries; Blaise Pascal organised a carriage service in Paris in 1662. Steam railways in the nineteenth century, followed by electric trams and underground railways, transformed urban growth, enabling the development of suburbs.

Operation and funding

Most public transport services operate along fixed routes with set embarkation and disembarkation points, to published timetables. Services are funded through a mixture of fares, government subsidy and, in some systems, taxation of employers or road users. Many cities pursue fare-free public transport policies to increase ridership.

Impact

Public transport is widely promoted as a means of reducing traffic congestion, air pollution and greenhouse-gas emissions, since shared vehicles carry many passengers in the road space of a few cars. It is also central to accessibility for people unable to drive.
