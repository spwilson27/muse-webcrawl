The Xbox 360 is a home video game console developed by Microsoft. As the successor to the original Xbox, it is the second console in the Xbox series. It competed with Sony's PlayStation 3 and Nintendo's Wii as part of the seventh generation of video game consoles.

History

The Xbox 360 was officially unveiled on MTV on May 12, 2005, and launched on November 22, 2005, in North America, a full year ahead of the PlayStation 3 and Wii. The early launch gave it a head start in the high-definition gaming era. The console was produced in several models, including the slimmer Xbox 360 S (2010) and the Xbox 360 E (2013). Production ended on April 20, 2016.

Hardware

The console is powered by a custom triple-core IBM PowerPC processor ("Xenon") running at 3.2 GHz and an ATI graphics processor ("Xenos") with 10 MB of embedded DRAM, supported by 512 MB of unified GDDR3 memory. Early models suffered from a high hardware failure rate, popularly known as the "Red Ring of Death", which cost Microsoft over a billion dollars in warranty repairs.

Software and services

The Xbox 360 greatly expanded the Xbox Live online service, introducing achievements, downloadable games through Xbox Live Arcade, and media streaming applications. In 2010 Microsoft released Kinect, a motion-sensing camera peripheral that sold rapidly and extended the console's life in the casual market.

Games

Best-selling and critically acclaimed titles included Halo 3, Gears of War, Grand Theft Auto IV and V, Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 2, The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim, and Minecraft, which began on the platform through Xbox Live Arcade publishing arrangements.

Legacy

With over 84 million units sold, the Xbox 360 was Microsoft's best-selling console and is credited with popularizing online console gaming, downloadable indie games, and achievement systems. Much of its library remains playable on later Xbox consoles through backward compatibility.
