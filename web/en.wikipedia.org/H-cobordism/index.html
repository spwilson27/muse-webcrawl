h-cobordism

In geometric topology, an h-cobordism is a cobordism between two manifolds whose boundary inclusions are homotopy equivalences. More precisely, a cobordism W between manifolds M and N is an h-cobordism if the inclusion maps of both M and N into W are homotopy equivalences.

The h-cobordism theorem

The h-cobordism theorem, proved by Stephen Smale in the early 1960s, states that a simply connected h-cobordism of dimension at least 5 is trivial: the cobordism is diffeomorphic to a product M × [0,1], and the two boundary manifolds are diffeomorphic. The theorem was a breakthrough that earned Smale the Fields Medal and was a key step in the proof of the generalised Poincaré conjecture in dimensions five and above.

The s-cobordism theorem

Without simple connectivity, the obstruction to triviality is measured by Whitehead torsion, leading to the s-cobordism theorem of Barry Mazur, John Stallings and Dennis Barden: an h-cobordism is a product if and only if its Whitehead torsion vanishes.
