A Theology of Liberation

A Theology of Liberation
Author: Gustavo Gutiérrez
Original title: Teología de la liberación: Perspectivas
Published: 1971 (Spanish); 1973 (English)
Subject: Liberation theology

A Theology of Liberation (Spanish: Teología de la liberación: Perspectivas) is a 1971 book by Peruvian Dominican priest and theologian Gustavo Gutiérrez. It is widely considered the founding text of liberation theology, the movement that interprets Christian faith from the perspective of the poor and oppressed, and it gave the movement its name.

Content

Gutiérrez argues that theology must be "critical reflection on praxis" — that is, reflection arising from engagement with the world, especially commitment to the poor, rather than abstract speculation. He articulates a "preferential option for the poor," reads the Exodus as a political as well as spiritual liberation, and understands sin not only personally but structurally, as embedded in unjust social systems. Drawing on dependency theory and (cautiously) Marxist social analysis, he critiques "developmentalism" as inadequate to Latin America's situation and calls instead for liberation from oppressive structures.

Influence and controversy

The book appeared three years after the 1968 Medellín conference of Latin American bishops, whose documents it helped to interpret and extend. Liberation theology spread widely through base ecclesial communities in Latin America and influenced theologies worldwide. It also drew sustained scrutiny from the Vatican's Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith under Cardinal Joseph Ratzinger, whose instructions of 1984 and 1986 criticized some liberation theologians' use of Marxist concepts while affirming authentic concern for the poor. Gutiérrez himself was never condemned. Pope Francis later received him warmly, and the phrase "preferential option for the poor" entered mainstream Catholic social teaching.
