Council of Jerusalem

Council of Jerusalem
Datec. AD 48–50
LocationJerusalem
ParticipantsPaul, Barnabas, James, Peter, and the elders
OutcomeApostolic Decree; Gentile converts exempted from circumcision

The Council of Jerusalem was a gathering of the early Christian church held in Jerusalem around AD 49, described in chapter 15 of the Acts of the Apostles. It is considered a landmark in Christian history because it addressed whether Gentile converts had to observe the Mosaic Law, particularly circumcision.

Background

As Paul and Barnabas preached to Gentiles in Syria and Cilicia, some teachers from Judea insisted that converts "cannot be saved" unless circumcised according to Jewish custom. The church at Antioch sent Paul and Barnabas to Jerusalem to settle the question with the apostles and elders.

The decree

After debate in which Peter and James the Just spoke, the council issued the Apostolic Decree: Gentile believers were not required to be circumcised or keep the whole Law, but were asked to abstain from food offered to idols, from blood, from meat of strangled animals, and from sexual immorality. A letter conveying the decision was sent to the churches.

Significance

The decision freed the growing Gentile mission from the requirement of full conversion to Judaism and is seen as a decisive step in Christianity's emergence as a religion distinct from Judaism.
