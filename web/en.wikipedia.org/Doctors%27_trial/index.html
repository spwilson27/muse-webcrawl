Doctors' trial

The Doctors' trial (officially United States of America v. Karl Brandt, et al.) was the first of the twelve "Subsequent Nuremberg trials" for war crimes and crimes against humanity held by United States authorities in their occupation zone in Nuremberg, Germany, after the end of World War II.

Doctors' trial
Dates9 December 1946 – 20 August 1947
LocationPalace of Justice, Nuremberg
Defendants23 physicians and officials
Outcome7 acquitted, 7 death sentences, others imprisoned

Charges

The twenty-three defendants were charged with involvement in Nazi human experimentation and mass murder under the guise of euthanasia. Experiments described at trial included high-altitude, freezing, malaria, sulfanilamide, and seawater experiments conducted on concentration camp prisoners without consent.

Outcome and legacy

Seven defendants were acquitted, seven were sentenced to death and hanged in June 1948 — including Karl Brandt, Adolf Hitler's personal physician — and the remainder received prison terms. The judgment articulated the Nuremberg Code, a set of ethical principles for human experimentation emphasizing voluntary informed consent, which remains foundational in medical ethics.
