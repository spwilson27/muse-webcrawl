Drummer

A drummer is a percussionist who creates music using drums. Most contemporary western bands that play rock, pop, jazz or R&B include a drummer for the rhythm section role, playing a drum kit that combines bass drum, snare, toms, hi-hat and cymbals, operated with sticks, brushes or hands and with pedals for the bass drum and hi-hat.

History

Drumming is among the oldest musical practices; drums appear in Neolithic cultures worldwide. Military drummers long used snare and bass drums to signal manoeuvres and set marching cadence. The modern drum kit coalesced in the early 20th century with New Orleans jazz, when pedals for the bass drum (patented by Ludwig in 1909) let one player cover parts previously requiring several percussionists.

Roles and technique

The drummer establishes tempo and groove, marks song structure with fills and accents, and interacts dynamically with the rest of the band. Techniques span matched and traditional grip, rudiments, independence of the limbs, and in styles such as metal, double bass-drum pedal work. Notable drummers frequently cited include Buddy Rich, John Bonham, Neil Peart, Elvin Jones and Ringo Starr. Drummers also serve in orchestras, marching bands, and as session and touring musicians; many also sing, write, or lead their own groups.
