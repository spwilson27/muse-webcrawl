Theatre

Theatre or theater is a collaborative form of performing art that uses live performers, usually actors, to present experiences — real or imagined — before a live audience in a specific place, often a stage. Performers communicate through gesture, speech, song, music, and dance.

History

Western theatre traces its origins to the religious festivals of ancient Athens, where tragedy and comedy emerged in the 5th century BC with playwrights such as Aeschylus, Sophocles, Euripides, and Aristophanes. Traditions developed independently across the world, including Sanskrit drama in India, Chinese opera, Japanese Noh and Kabuki, and medieval European mystery plays.

Genres and forms

Major forms include drama, tragedy, comedy, musical theatre, opera, ballet, pantomime, improvisational theatre, and experimental or avant-garde performance. The modern era saw the rise of realism under figures such as Ibsen, Chekhov, and Stanislavski, whose system transformed actor training.

Theatre production

A production unites playwright, director, actors, designers of set, costume, lighting, and sound, and stage management. Venues range from grand proscenium-arch houses such as London's West End and New York's Broadway to black-box studios and found spaces.
