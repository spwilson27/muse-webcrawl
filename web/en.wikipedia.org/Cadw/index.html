Cadw

Cadw (Welsh for "keep" or "protect") is the historic environment service of the Welsh Government. It is responsible for caring for Wales's historic buildings, landscapes, and archaeological sites, and for promoting public access to and appreciation of them.

Responsibilities

Cadw manages more than 130 historic properties in state care, including major castles such as Conwy, Caernarfon, Harlech, and Beaumaris — together inscribed as the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Castles and Town Walls of King Edward in Gwynedd". It also maintains the schedule of ancient monuments and the list of historic buildings for Wales, and advises on planning and conservation matters.

History

The agency was established in 1984 as an executive agency of the Welsh Office, taking over responsibilities previously held by UK government departments. Following Welsh devolution in 1999 it became part of the Welsh Government. Its full Welsh title is Cadw: Welsh Historic Monuments.
