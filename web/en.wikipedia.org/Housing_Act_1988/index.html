The Housing Act 1988 is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that reshaped housing law in England and Wales. Introduced by the Conservative government of Margaret Thatcher, it deregulated new private lettings and restructured social housing provision.

Main provisions

The Act created the assured tenancy and, most importantly, the assured shorthold tenancy (AST), under which landlords could recover possession at the end of a fixed term using the section 21 "no-fault" notice procedure, while rents for new lettings were set by the market rather than regulated fair rents. It also made housing associations the main providers of new social housing, enabled large-scale voluntary transfers of council housing stock to associations, and created Housing Action Trusts to regenerate run-down estates.

Impact and reform

By reducing landlord risk, the Act is credited with reviving the private rented sector, which grew from under a tenth to around a fifth of English households over the following decades. The section 21 process later became controversial, with successive governments proposing its abolition in favour of stronger tenant security.
