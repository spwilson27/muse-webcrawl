White wine

White wine is a wine fermented without skin contact, so that the juice does not extract colour from the grape skins. It may be made from "white" (green or yellow) grapes or, by pressing gently and separating the juice quickly, from red-skinned grapes (blanc de noirs). White wines range from dry to very sweet and are produced in every wine region of the world.

Production

Grapes are pressed and the juice is separated from the skins before fermentation, which usually takes place in stainless steel tanks at cool temperatures to preserve fresh fruit aromas. Malolactic fermentation may be encouraged for rounder styles such as many Chardonnays, or blocked for crisp wines such as Riesling and Sauvignon blanc. Some white wines are aged in oak barrels, which add flavours of vanilla and toast.

Styles and varieties

Major white grape varieties include Chardonnay, Sauvignon blanc, Riesling, Pinot grigio (Pinot gris), Gewürztraminer, Viognier and Albariño. Famous white-wine styles include Champagne and other sparkling wines, dry Sancerre, sweet Sauternes, and off-dry German Rieslings. Historically, white wines such as those of the Rhine were among the most prized in Europe.

Serving

White wine is generally served chilled, lighter wines cooler than fuller-bodied oaked wines, and traditionally accompanies fish, poultry, salads and lighter dishes, though pairing is ultimately a matter of taste.
