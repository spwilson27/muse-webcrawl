Chapter 9, Title 11, United States Code

Chapter 9 of Title 11 of the United States Code (the Bankruptcy Code) provides for the reorganisation of financially distressed municipalities — cities, towns, counties, school districts and other political subdivisions and public agencies. Unlike other chapters, it is available only to municipalities, not to individuals or businesses.

Key features

A municipality may file only if it is insolvent, desires to effect a plan of adjustment, and is specifically authorised to file by its state — a requirement reflecting the Tenth Amendment and federalism concerns; states themselves cannot file. Chapter 9 gives the court far less control over the debtor than Chapter 11: the municipality retains control of its property and revenues, and the court cannot interfere with its political or governmental powers. Creditors cannot file an involuntary petition or force liquidation.

Notable cases

Historically, filings are rare — a few hundred since the chapter's predecessor was enacted during the Great Depression. Major cases include Orange County, California (1994), Vallejo, California (2008), Jefferson County, Alabama (2011), and Detroit, Michigan (2013), the largest municipal bankruptcy in US history. The 2016 PROMESA statute created a separate regime for Puerto Rico, which is ineligible for Chapter 9.
