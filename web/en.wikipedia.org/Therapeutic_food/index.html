Therapeutic foods are foods designed for specific nutritional or therapeutic purposes, most notably ready-to-use therapeutic food (RUTF), a high-energy, micronutrient-enriched paste used in the treatment of severe acute malnutrition in children. The best-known product is Plumpy'Nut, developed in the late 1990s by the French researcher André Briend and produced by the company Nutriset.

Composition and properties

RUTF is typically made from peanuts, sugar, milk powder and vegetable oil, fortified with a premix of vitamins and minerals. Because it contains very little water, it resists bacterial contamination, requires no cooking or refrigeration, and has a shelf life of up to two years. A standard sachet contains about 500 kilocalories, and children are typically given one to several sachets per day depending on their weight.

Use in humanitarian programmes

The advent of RUTF transformed the treatment of malnutrition: instead of requiring hospitalisation and therapeutic milk feeds, most children with uncomplicated severe acute malnutrition can be treated at home through community-based management programmes run by organisations such as UNICEF, Médecins Sans Frontières and the World Food Programme. Local production in countries including Malawi, Niger and Ethiopia has expanded supply and reduced costs.
