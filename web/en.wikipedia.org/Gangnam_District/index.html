Gangnam District (Korean: 강남구, romanised: Gangnam-gu, literally "south of the river") is one of the 25 districts (gu) of Seoul, South Korea. It lies southeast of the Han River and is one of the most affluent areas in the country, known for expensive real estate, prestigious schools, luxury shopping, and a dense concentration of corporate headquarters, plastic-surgery clinics, and entertainment companies.

History

Until the 1960s the area south of the Han River was largely farmland and small villages in Gyeonggi Province. Annexed to Seoul in 1963, it was developed rapidly from the 1970s onward as the government relieved congestion in the old city centre north of the river, building wide boulevards, apartment complexes, and the Gangnam Express Bus Terminal. The district was formally established in 1975. Development accelerated with the 1988 Seoul Olympics, and land prices rose to among the highest in South Korea.

Economy and landmarks

Major commercial areas include the Teheran Valley office corridor, home to many technology and finance firms; the COEX convention centre and mall; Apgujeong and Cheongdam-dong, known for luxury retail and fashion; and Sinsa-dong's Garosu-gil. Bongeunsa, a Buddhist temple founded in 794, stands opposite the modern COEX complex. Gangnam Station is among the busiest subway interchanges in Seoul.

In popular culture

The district achieved global recognition in 2012 through Psy's viral hit "Gangnam Style", which satirised the area's wealthy, image-conscious lifestyle.
