Tribe of Ephraim

According to the Hebrew Bible, the Tribe of Ephraim (Hebrew: אֶפְרַיִם, Efráyim) was one of the tribes of Israel. The Tribe of Manasseh together with Ephraim formed the Tribe of Joseph. It is one of the ten lost tribes. The etymology of the name is disputed.

Territory

The central mountainous region allotted to Ephraim, in the hill country of Samaria, was fertile and strategically central; it contained the important sanctuaries of Bethel and Shiloh, where the Ark of the Covenant was kept for a time. Joshua, the successor of Moses, was an Ephraimite.

Role in the northern kingdom

Jeroboam I, the first king of the northern Kingdom of Israel, was an Ephraimite, and because Ephraim was the dominant northern tribe, the prophets sometimes used "Ephraim" as a name for the whole northern kingdom. The tribe disappeared from history with the Assyrian conquest of Israel in about 722 BCE, becoming one of the Ten Lost Tribes.
