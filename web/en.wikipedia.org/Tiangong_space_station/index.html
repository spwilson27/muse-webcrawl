Tiangong (Chinese: 天宫; pinyin: Tiāngōng; lit. 'Heavenly Palace'), officially the Tiangong space station, is a permanently crewed space station constructed by China and operated by the China Manned Space Agency in low Earth orbit between 340 and 450 km above the surface. It is China's first long-term space station, part of the China Manned Space Program, and has a mass of roughly one fifth of the International Space Station.

Assembly

The core module, Tianhe ("Harmony of the Heavens"), was launched on 29 April 2021 atop a Long March 5B rocket from Wenchang. The first crew, Shenzhou 12, arrived in June 2021. Two laboratory modules followed: Wentian ("Quest for the Heavens") in July 2022 and Mengtian ("Dreaming of the Heavens") in October 2022. Their re-docking into lateral ports completed the station's basic T-shaped configuration in November 2022. Crews of three taikonauts rotate roughly every six months via Shenzhou spacecraft, with resupply by Tianzhou cargo craft.

Facilities and research

The station provides about 110 m³ of habitable volume, experiment racks in the laboratory modules, a large robotic arm, and an airlock for spacewalks and deploying CubeSats. Research spans microgravity fluid physics, materials science, life science, and astronomy; a co-orbiting survey telescope, Xuntian, was planned to fly in formation with the station. In 2023 Shenzhou 16 carried China's first civilian taikonaut, payload specialist Gui Haichao.

International cooperation

China has invited foreign participation; experiments selected with the UN Office for Outer Space Affairs are hosted aboard, and China has announced that foreign astronauts may fly to the station in future.
