Old Cairo (Arabic: مصر القديمة, Misr al-Qadima) is a historic area in Cairo, Egypt, which roughly corresponds to the district south of the modern city centre. It includes the site of the Roman-era Babylon Fortress, the Coptic Christian quarter known as Coptic Cairo, and the area of Fustat, the first Muslim capital of Egypt.

History

The Romans built the Babylon Fortress on the east bank of the Nile near the apex of the delta. After the Muslim conquest of Egypt in 641 CE, the general Amr ibn al-As founded Fustat nearby and built the Mosque of Amr ibn al-As, the first mosque in Egypt and Africa.

Coptic Cairo

The quarter contains some of Egypt's oldest churches, including the Hanging Church (Saint Virgin Mary's Church at Haret Zuweila), the Church of the Holy Virgin (Haret El-Roum), the Greek Church of St. George, and the Ben Ezra Synagogue, famous for the Cairo Geniza documents discovered in its storeroom.

Heritage

Old Cairo forms part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site "Historic Cairo", inscribed in 1979.
