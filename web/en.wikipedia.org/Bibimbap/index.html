Bibimbap

Bibimbap (Korean: 비빔밥, "mixed rice") is a Korean rice dish served as a bowl of warm white rice topped with namul (sautéed and seasoned vegetables), gochujang (chili pepper paste), soy sauce or doenjang, and a fried egg and sliced meat (usually beef) commonly added. The dish is stirred together thoroughly just before eating.

Preparation

Vegetables commonly used in bibimbap include julienned cucumber, zucchini, mushrooms, spinach, soybean sprouts, carrots, and fernbrake, each seasoned and cooked separately. The ingredients are arranged in contrasting colors on top of the rice and mixed together at the table. A version served in a hot stone bowl, dolsot-bibimbap, creates a crispy layer of rice at the bottom and cooks a raw egg against the hot sides.

Varieties and history

The city of Jeonju is famous for its version of the dish, Jeonju-bibimbap, traditionally made with rice cooked in beef broth and topped with yukhoe (seasoned raw beef). Bibimbap is thought to derive in part from the custom of mixing leftover side dishes with rice, and from ceremonial foods eaten at the end of ancestral rites. It is one of the most internationally recognized dishes of Korean cuisine.
