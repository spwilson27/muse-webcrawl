Code of Ur-Nammu

The Code of Ur-Nammu is the oldest known surviving law code, written in Sumerian on clay tablets around 2100–2050 BC during the reign of Ur-Nammu, founder of the Third Dynasty of Ur in ancient Mesopotamia.

Contents

The prologue credits Ur-Nammu with establishing equity in the land and protecting the widow and orphan. The surviving laws are casuistic — framed as "if a man does X, then Y" — and cover offences including murder, robbery, sexual offences, false accusation, and injuries. Notably, many bodily injuries are punished with fines in silver rather than by retaliation, contrasting with the later "eye for an eye" principle of the Code of Hammurabi, which it predates by roughly three centuries.

Discovery

Fragments were excavated at Nippur and Ur and identified in the 1950s; additional fragments have since been recognised, allowing partial reconstruction of about forty laws.
