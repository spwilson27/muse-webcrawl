Stealth technology

Stealth technology, also termed low observable technology (LO technology), is a sub-discipline of military tactics and passive and active electronic countermeasures. It covers a range of methods used to make personnel, aircraft, ships, submarines, missiles, satellites, and ground vehicles less visible (ideally invisible) to radar, infrared, sonar, and other detection methods.

Principles

For aircraft, detectability is dominated by the radar cross-section (RCS). Designers reduce RCS by shaping: surfaces are angled to deflect radar energy away from the emitter, edges are aligned in a few directions, and internal weapons carriage avoids reflective stores. Radar-absorbent material (RAM) coatings convert some incoming energy to heat. Infrared signature is reduced by shielding and mixing exhaust with cool air.

History

Soviet physicist Pyotr Ufimtsev's 1964 theory of diffraction enabled Lockheed's Have Blue demonstrator and the F-117 Nighthawk, revealed in 1988. The B-2 Spirit flying-wing bomber and later F-22, F-35, and Chinese J-20 continued the approach, trading aerodynamic and cost penalties for survivability.

Naval and other applications

Warships such as the Visby-class corvettes and Zumwalt-class destroyers use faceted hulls and composite materials; submarines use anechoic tiles against sonar.

Countermeasures

Low-frequency (VHF) radars, multistatic radar networks, passive detection of emissions, and infrared search-and-track systems can partially offset stealth, though usually with poorer targeting accuracy.
