Price fixing

Price fixing is an agreement between participants on the same side of a market to buy or sell a product, service, or commodity at a fixed price, or to maintain market conditions such that the price is maintained at a given level by controlling supply and demand. The intent of price fixing may be to push the price of a product as high as possible, generally leading to profits for all sellers.

Legality

Price fixing is generally illegal under competition (antitrust) laws in most jurisdictions, including the United States under the Sherman Antitrust Act, where it is treated as a per se violation, and the European Union under Article 101 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. Notable prosecuted cartels have involved lysine, vitamins, LCD panels and auto parts, resulting in large corporate fines and, in some countries, imprisonment.

Forms

Related practices include bid rigging, market allocation and output restriction. Price fixing can be horizontal (between competitors) or, in some legal systems, vertical (between firms at different levels of the supply chain, such as resale price maintenance).
