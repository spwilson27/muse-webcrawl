Robert Józef Kubica (Polish pronunciation: [ˈrɔbɛrt kuˈbit͡sa]; born 7 December 1984) is a Polish racing driver. He became the first Polish driver to compete in Formula One, racing for BMW Sauber and Renault between 2006 and 2010, and won the 2008 Canadian Grand Prix. His F1 career was interrupted by a near-fatal rally crash in 2011 that severely injured his right arm; he later returned to top-level racing in rallying, Formula One again with Williams in 2019, and endurance racing, winning the 24 Hours of Le Mans.

Formula One

Kubica won the 2005 Formula Renault 3.5 Series title and became BMW Sauber's test driver, making his race debut at the 2006 Hungarian Grand Prix. In 2007 he survived a huge crash at the Canadian Grand Prix with only minor injuries; a year later at the same circuit he took his first F1 victory, leading BMW Sauber's only 1–2 finish, and finished fourth in the championship. After BMW's withdrawal he joined Renault for 2010, taking three podiums, and signed to remain for 2011.

Rally accident and return

On 6 February 2011, Kubica crashed a Škoda Fabia during the Ronde di Andora rally in Italy; a guardrail pierced the car, partially severing his right forearm. After multiple operations and a long recovery, he returned to competition in rallying, winning the 2013 WRC2 championship, then moved back to circuit racing. His fairytale F1 return came as reserve driver for Williams in 2018 and a full-time seat in 2019, in which he scored Williams's only point of the season in Germany. He later served as Alfa Romeo's reserve driver, standing in at two races in 2021.

Endurance racing

Kubica switched to endurance racing full-time, winning the European Le Mans Series LMP2 title in 2021 and racing in the FIA World Endurance Championship. In 2025 he won the 24 Hours of Le Mans overall with the AF Corse Ferrari 499P, becoming one of the few drivers to win both a Grand Prix and Le Mans.
