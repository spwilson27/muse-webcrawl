Urticaria

Urticaria
Also called: Hives
Symptoms: Raised, itchy wheals
Duration: Acute (<6 weeks) or chronic

Urticaria, commonly known as hives, is a skin condition characterized by raised, red, itchy wheals (welts) that appear suddenly and usually resolve within 24 hours in any given spot, though new ones may appear elsewhere.

Causes

Hives result from histamine release by mast cells in the skin. Triggers include foods, medications, infections, insect stings, pressure, cold, and sunlight; in chronic urticaria (lasting more than six weeks) an autoimmune mechanism is common and a specific trigger is often never found.

Treatment

Second-generation antihistamines such as cetirizine are first-line therapy. Severe cases accompanied by swelling of the lips or airway (angioedema or anaphylaxis) require emergency treatment with epinephrine.
