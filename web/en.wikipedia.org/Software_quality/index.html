Software quality


Quality models relate product characteristics to stakeholder needs

In the context of software engineering, software quality refers to two related but distinct notions: the degree to which a software system meets its specified requirements and user expectations (functional quality), and the degree to which its internal structure supports qualities such as maintainability, reliability, security, and portability (structural quality). Quality is commonly pursued through software quality assurance (SQA)—the set of processes, reviews, testing, and standards applied throughout development—and measured against quality models.

Quality models

The ISO/IEC 25010 standard defines a product quality model with characteristics including functional suitability, performance efficiency, compatibility, usability, reliability, security, maintainability, and portability. Earlier models include McCall's (1977), Boehm's, and the original ISO/IEC 9126. Such models decompose abstract "quality" into measurable attributes—such as defect density, test coverage, cyclomatic complexity, and mean time between failures—that can be tracked with metrics and static analysis tools.

Assurance practices

Practices contributing to software quality include requirements engineering, code review, unit and integration testing, continuous integration, static code analysis, formal verification for critical components, and process frameworks such as the Capability Maturity Model Integration (CMMI) and ISO 9001-based quality management. Cost-of-quality analysis distinguishes prevention and appraisal costs from the internal and external failure costs of defects that escape into production.

Perspectives

Quality is partly a matter of viewpoint: users emphasise fitness for use and reliability; developers emphasise maintainability and testability; managers emphasise predictability and cost. Joseph Juran's "fitness for use" and Philip Crosby's "conformance to requirements" are classic competing definitions, and modern practice treats quality as multidimensional and negotiated among stakeholders.
