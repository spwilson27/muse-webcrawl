Bolt (firearms)

A bolt is the part of a repeating, breechloading weapon that blocks the rear opening (breech) of the barrel chamber while the propellant burns, and moves back and forward to facilitate loading and unloading of cartridges from the magazine. The firing pin and extractor are generally integral parts of the bolt. The term is generally used in the context of bolt-action rifles, but bolts are also found in semi-automatic and automatic firearms, where the bolt cycles under gas pressure, recoil, or blowback.

Function

The bolt seals the chamber during firing, resisting the high pressure of the propellant gases. In most designs, locking lugs on the bolt engage recesses in the receiver or barrel extension to keep the breech closed until pressure drops to safe levels. The bolt also typically carries the firing pin (or striker) and the extractor, which pulls the spent case from the chamber.

Types

Common bolt designs include the rotating bolt, used in most bolt-action rifles and many modern semi-automatic rifles such as the AR-15; the tilting bolt; the roller-locked and roller-delayed systems; and simple blowback bolts, held closed only by spring pressure and their own mass, used in low-powered pistols and submachine guns.
