Lloyd's of London, generally known simply as Lloyd's, is an insurance and reinsurance market located in London, England. Unlike most of its competitors, it is not an insurance company but a corporate body governed by the Lloyd's Act 1871 and subsequent Acts, in which multiple financial backers join syndicates to underwrite risks.

History

The market traces its origins to Lloyd's Coffee House, established by Edward Lloyd on Tower Street in 1686, which became a meeting place for shipowners, merchants and underwriters of marine insurance. Lloyd's List, one of the world's oldest shipping publications, grew out of the same milieu. The market was incorporated by Act of Parliament in 1871.

Structure

Capital is provided by members, historically including individual "Names" with unlimited liability, now overwhelmingly corporate members. Brokers negotiate risks with underwriters in the underwriting room ("the Room") of the Lloyd's building. The market is known for specialist and unusual risks — marine, aviation, energy, and bespoke covers such as body parts of celebrities or satellite launches. Lloyd's survived a crisis in the late 1980s and early 1990s caused by asbestos and pollution claims, leading to the reconstruction of the market and the creation of Equitas in 1996.
