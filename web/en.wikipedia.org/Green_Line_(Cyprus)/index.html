Green Line (Cyprus)

The Green Line is the dividing line between the internationally recognised Republic of Cyprus in the south of the island of Cyprus and the Turkish Cypriot-administered north. It runs for about 180 kilometres across the island and through the capital, Nicosia, which remains Europe's last divided capital. The line is patrolled by the United Nations Peacekeeping Force in Cyprus (UNFICYP) within a buffer zone that varies in width from a few metres in Nicosia to several kilometres in rural areas.

The buffer zone in central Nicosia

History

The line was first drawn in December 1963 by Major-General Peter Young, commander of a British-led truce force, who marked it on a map with a green chinagraph pencil during intercommunal violence between Greek Cypriots and Turkish Cypriots — hence the name. It was extended across the island after the Turkish military intervention of 1974, which followed a coup d'état backed by the Greek military junta. The buffer zone contains abandoned villages and the derelict Nicosia International Airport.

Crossings

For nearly three decades movement across the line was heavily restricted. Restrictions were eased in 2003, and several crossing points now operate, including the pedestrian crossing on Ledra Street in Nicosia, opened in 2008. A settlement of the Cyprus problem remains under negotiation.
