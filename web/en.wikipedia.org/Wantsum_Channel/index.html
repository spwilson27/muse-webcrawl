Wantsum Channel

The Wantsum Channel was a strait in the Isle of Thanet area in Kent, England. In ancient and medieval times it separated the Isle of Thanet from the mainland of Kent, and formed an important and protected shipping route between the English Channel and the Thames estuary. The channel silted up over the centuries and was reclaimed as land, so that Thanet is no longer a true island.

History

In Roman times the channel was a broad strait guarded by the Saxon Shore forts of Regulbium (Reculver) at its northern end and Rutupiae (Richborough) at its southern end, two of the principal Roman ports of entry to Britain. The channel remained navigable through the Anglo-Saxon and medieval periods and was used by St Augustine's landing party in 597. The ports of Sandwich and Sarre flourished on its shores.

Silting and reclamation

From the 13th century onwards the channel progressively silted up as shingle accumulated at its northern mouth and the River Stour deposited sediment. By the 16th century the last navigable stretch had closed, and the former channel became marshland crossed by the River Wantsum, a small stream that still traces part of the old strait's course. Richborough and Reculver, once coastal ports, now stand inland.
