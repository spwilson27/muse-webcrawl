Community college

A community college is a type of undergraduate higher education institution, generally offering two-year associate degrees as well as certificates, vocational and technical training, and transfer pathways to four-year universities. The term is used most prominently in the United States and Canada; related institutions exist in several other countries.

United States

American community colleges are mostly public, open-enrollment institutions with low tuition, serving local ("community") populations including recent school graduates, adult learners, and workers seeking retraining. Students may earn an Associate of Arts or Associate of Science degree and transfer to a university, or complete career and technical education programs. There are roughly a thousand public community colleges in the United States, enrolling several million students. Some community colleges now offer limited bachelor's degrees in applied fields.

History

The first junior college, Joliet Junior College in Illinois, was founded in 1901. The sector expanded rapidly after the Second World War, aided by the G.I. Bill, the 1947 Truman Commission report, and state master plans such as California's 1960 Master Plan for Higher Education, which assigned community colleges the role of open-access first tier.
