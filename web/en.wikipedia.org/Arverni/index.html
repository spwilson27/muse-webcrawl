Arverni

RegionSouth-central Gaul (modern Auvergne, France)
CapitalGergovia, later Augustonemetum (Clermont-Ferrand)

The Arverni (Gaulish: Aruernoi) were a Gallic people dwelling in the modern Auvergne region of south-central France during the Iron Age and the Roman period. They were one of the most powerful tribes of ancient Gaul, at times claiming an empire or hegemony over neighbouring peoples. Their name survives in the modern region of Auvergne.

History

The Arverni are said by the Roman historian Livy to have been among the tribes that took part in the legendary Gallic invasions of Italy under Bellovesus in the 6th century BC. By the 3rd and 2nd centuries BC their kings, notably Luern and his son Bituitus, exercised wide influence in Gaul. Bituitus was defeated by the Romans in 121 BC, after which Arvernian power declined, although they remained wealthy through control of trade routes and mining.

The Arverni are best known as the tribe of Vercingetorix, who in 52 BC united many Gallic peoples in revolt against Julius Caesar during the Gallic Wars. Vercingetorix won a notable victory defending the Arvernian stronghold of Gergovia, but was besieged and forced to surrender at Alesia later the same year. Under Roman rule the Arverni were organized as a civitas with its capital at Augustonemetum, modern Clermont-Ferrand.

Culture

The Arverni minted their own coinage, including gold staters associated with Vercingetorix, and built fortified hilltop settlements (oppida) such as Gergovia and Corent. Excavations at Corent have revealed a major sanctuary and evidence of large public buildings, suggesting it may have served as an earlier capital.
