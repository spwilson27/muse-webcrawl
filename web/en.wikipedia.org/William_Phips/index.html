William Phips

Sir William Phips (February 2, 1651 – February 18, 1695) was an English-born shipwright, ship's captain, treasure hunter, military officer, and colonial administrator in New England. He famously recovered a sunken Spanish treasure ship off the coast of Hispaniola, which earned him a knighthood and great wealth, and he later served as the first royally appointed governor of the Province of Massachusetts Bay.

BornFebruary 2, 1651, Woolwich, Maine (then Massachusetts Bay Colony)
DiedFebruary 18, 1695, London, England
OccupationShipwright, sea captain, governor
Known forRecovery of Spanish treasure; Governor of Massachusetts Bay

Early life

Phips was born in a frontier settlement at Woolwich, in present-day Maine, one of 26 children. Apprenticed as a ship's carpenter, he learned to read and write as an adult and established himself as a shipbuilder in Boston. He married Mary Hull, the widow of a Boston merchant.

Treasure hunting

Learning of sunken Spanish treasure ships in the Caribbean, Phips obtained backing in England and in 1687 located the wreck of the Nuestra Señora de la Concepción, sunk in 1641 on a reef north of Hispaniola. His divers recovered tons of silver, gold, and jewels worth over £200,000. Phips's share made him rich, and King James II knighted him.

Military and political career

In 1690 Phips led the successful New England expedition against Port Royal in Acadia, but failed in an attack on Quebec later that year during King William's War. In 1692 he became the first governor of Massachusetts Bay under its new royal charter. His tenure coincided with the Salem witch trials; Phips initially allowed the special court that condemned many accused witches, but after criticism — and after his own wife was reportedly named — he dissolved the court and pardoned the remaining accused.

Death and legacy

Recalled to England amid political disputes, Phips died in London in 1695 while awaiting a hearing. His life, recorded by Cotton Mather, became a celebrated example of a self-made man in colonial America.
