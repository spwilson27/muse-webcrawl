Shotgun wedding

A shotgun wedding is a wedding arranged in a hurry, often to avoid embarrassment due to an unplanned pregnancy. The phrase is a derivative of the figurative use of the word "shotgun", which refers to the use or threat of force to get someone to complete an action. Such weddings are usually rushed because the parents and/or the community consider a birth outside of marriage to be shameful, or because they wish to reduce speculation about the nature of the couple's premarital relations.

History

Shotgun weddings have historically been associated with rural areas of the United States, particularly the American South. The phrase evokes the stereotype of a father of the pregnant bride holding a shotgun to ensure the groom goes through with the ceremony. Some historians have traced the expression to the frontier, where such compulsion was sometimes dramatized in folklore, cartoons, and literature.

Legal and social context

The practice grew out of social norms that stigmatized unintended pregnancy and births outside marriage. Shotgun weddings were once common enough to be reflected in statistics: in the mid-20th century, a substantial share of first births among white Americans in rural areas were conceived before marriage, and many couples married between conception and birth. As social acceptance of premarital sex and cohabitation grew in the late 20th century, the incidence of shotgun weddings declined in Western countries, though they remain common in some cultures and regions.

In popular culture

The concept has appeared in films, television, and music. The 2022 romantic comedy film Shotgun Wedding, starring Jennifer Lopez and Josh Duhamel, takes its title from the phrase. The trope is also a recurring plot device in sitcoms and dramas in which a pregnancy announcement precipitates a rushed engagement.
