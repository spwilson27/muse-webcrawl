Tandem wing

Tandem wing — an aircraft configuration with two main wing planes, one behind the other, both providing lift (as distinct from a biplane's stacked wings or a canard's small foreplane).

A tandem wing aircraft has two main wings, one located behind the other. Both wings contribute substantially to lift. This contrasts with a conventional configuration, where a smaller tailplane mainly provides trim and control forces, and with a canard, where the foreplane is much smaller than the main wing. Tandem-wing designs place the centre of gravity between the two wings, which must be carefully arranged to control interference from the forward wing's downwash.

Examples

Early experimenters including Stefan Drzewiecki proposed the layout; notable tandem-wing aircraft include the Mignet Pou-du-Ciel ("Flying Flea") homebuilt of the 1930s, the Westland-Hill Pterodactyl IV (tailless with tandem-lifting surfaces), modern UAVs such as the "joined-wing" concepts, and Burt Rutan's designs that blur the line with large canards. The configuration offers compact span for a given wing area, potentially lower structural weight, and inherent pitch stability, but suffers from aerodynamic interference and limited adoption.
