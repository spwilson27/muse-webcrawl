Panthéon, Paris

The Panthéon in Paris is a neoclassical monument in the 5th arrondissement, on the Montagne Sainte-Geneviève overlooking the Latin Quarter.

History

Vowed by Louis XV in 1744 and built between 1758 and 1790 to designs by Jacques-Germain Soufflot, it was secularised in 1791 to serve as the resting place of France's great figures. Its role alternated between church and temple over the 19th century before being definitively established as a republican mausoleum at Victor Hugo's funeral in 1885.

Interior

The crypt holds the tombs of figures including Marie and Pierre Curie, Jean Moulin and Joséphine Baker, inducted in 2021.
