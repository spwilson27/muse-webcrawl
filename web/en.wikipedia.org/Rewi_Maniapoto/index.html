Rewi Maniapoto

Bornc. 1807, Waikato, New Zealand
Died31 May 1894, Kihikihi
IwiNgāti Maniapoto

Rewi Manga Maniapoto (c. 1807–1894) was a Ngāti Maniapoto chief and a principal Māori military leader during the New Zealand Wars, particularly the Invasion of the Waikato in 1863–64. He was a committed supporter of the Kīngitanga (Māori King movement).

Early life

Rewi was born in the Waikato to the Ngāti Maniapoto chief Te Ngohi. In his youth he was influenced by Wesleyan missionaries, taking the baptismal name Rewi (David). He became a leading figure in his iwi and an early backer of Pōtatau Te Wherowhero, the first Māori King.

The Waikato War

Rewi directed the Māori defence during the invasion of Waikato. He is best remembered for the stand at Ōrākau in April 1864, where some 300 defenders held out for three days against a much larger British and colonial force. When called on to surrender, the famous reply attributed to his side was "Ka whawhai tonu mātou, ake! ake! ake!" — "We will fight on forever!" Rewi escaped the final assault.

Later life

After the war Rewi lived in the King Country, from which Europeans were excluded for many years. In the 1880s he participated in negotiations that opened the region, including to the main trunk railway. He died at Kihikihi in 1894.
