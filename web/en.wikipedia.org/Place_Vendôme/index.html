Place Vendôme

Place Vendôme is a square in the 1st arrondissement of Paris, France, located to the north of the Tuileries Gardens and east of the Église de la Madeleine. It is the starting point of the Rue de la Paix. Its regular architecture, by Jules Hardouin-Mansart, and its pedimented screens gave the square its distinctive octagonal outline. The square is world-famous for its association with luxury: it is home to many celebrated jewellers and watchmakers, and to the Ritz Paris hotel.

History

The square was laid out beginning in 1698 as a monument to the glory of the armies of Louis XIV. The site had been occupied by the hôtel of César de Bourbon, Duke of Vendôme, from whom the square takes its name. Hardouin-Mansart designed the uniform classical façades; behind them, lots were sold to private developers.

In the centre of the square stood an equestrian statue of Louis XIV, destroyed during the French Revolution. During the Revolution the square was renamed Place des Piques. Napoleon later erected the Colonne Vendôme, modelled on Trajan's Column in Rome, to commemorate the victory at Austerlitz; it is topped by a statue of Napoleon. The column was pulled down during the Paris Commune of 1871 and re-erected under the Third Republic.

Luxury quarter

From the later 19th century the square became the centre of Parisian high jewellery. Houses such as Boucheron (the first jeweller to open there, in 1893), Chaumet, Van Cleef & Arpels, Cartier, and Bulgari maintain flagship salons on the square, along with prestigious watchmakers. The Ritz Paris, founded by César Ritz in 1898, is one of the world's most famous luxury hotels. The French Ministry of Justice is also located on the square, at the Hôtel de Bourvallais.
