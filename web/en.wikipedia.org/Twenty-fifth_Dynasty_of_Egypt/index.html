Twenty-fifth Dynasty of Egypt

Periodc. 747 – 656 BC
OriginKingdom of Kush (Nubia)
Notable kingsPiye, Shabaqo, Taharqa, Tantamani
CapitalNapata / Memphis

The Twenty-fifth Dynasty of Egypt (notated Dynasty XXV), also known as the Nubian Dynasty or the Kushite Empire, was a line of pharaohs who originated in the Kingdom of Kush, in present-day northern Sudan, and ruled Ancient Egypt for nearly a century, from about 747 to 656 BC, during the Third Intermediate Period.

Rise to power

The Kushite king Kashta first extended Nubian influence into Upper Egypt; his son Piye completed the conquest of Egypt around 747 BC, defeating a coalition of northern rulers and recording the campaign on his Victory Stela. Piye's successors — Shabaqo, Shebitqo, and the powerful Taharqa — reunified the country, revived Egyptian art and temple building, and constructed pyramids at el-Kurru and Nuri in Nubia.

Conflict with Assyria and fall

Taharqa intervened in the Levant against the expanding Neo-Assyrian Empire and was defeated; Assyrian armies under Esarhaddon and Ashurbanipal invaded Egypt, sacking Memphis and Thebes. The last Kushite king, Tantamani, was driven from Egypt by 656 BC, and the Assyrian-backed Twenty-sixth (Saite) Dynasty took power, though Kushite kings continued to rule Nubia from Napata and later Meroë.
