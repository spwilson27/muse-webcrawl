Degrees of freedom (statistics)

In statistics, the number of degrees of freedom is the number of values in the final calculation of a statistic that are free to vary. Estimates of statistical parameters can be based upon different amounts of information or data; the number of independent pieces of information that go into the estimate of a parameter is called the degrees of freedom. In general, the degrees of freedom of an estimate of a parameter are equal to the sample size minus the number of parameters estimated from the same data.

Concept

The classic illustration is the sample variance. For a sample of n observations, once the sample mean is fixed, only n − 1 of the deviations from the mean can vary freely, because the deviations must sum to zero; the last deviation is determined by the others. Dividing the sum of squared deviations by n − 1 (Bessel's correction) rather than by n therefore gives an unbiased estimator of the population variance. Similarly, in fitting a linear regression with p parameters to n observations, the residual degrees of freedom are n − p.

Applications

Degrees of freedom appear throughout classical statistics. The chi-squared, t, and F distributions are each indexed by degrees-of-freedom parameters, which shape the distribution and hence the critical values used in hypothesis tests. In contingency-table analysis, the degrees of freedom of a table with r rows and c columns are (r − 1)(c − 1). Analysis of variance partitions total degrees of freedom among model terms and error. In more complex settings — such as mixed models, smoothing splines, and generalized additive models — effective degrees of freedom may be non-integers and are estimated from the data.

History

The concept developed through the work of statisticians in the late 19th and early 20th centuries and was given its modern form by Ronald Fisher, who connected degrees of freedom to the geometry of data points in multi-dimensional space: restricting data to lie on fitted subspaces reduces the dimension of the space in which residuals can vary.
