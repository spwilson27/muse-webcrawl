The French Navy (French: Marine nationale), informally La Royale, is the maritime arm of the French Armed Forces. One of the world's oldest navies, it is today a blue-water force built around a nuclear-powered aircraft carrier, ballistic-missile submarines carrying France's sea-based nuclear deterrent, and overseas squadrons protecting the world's second-largest exclusive economic zone.

History

Cardinal Richelieu organized a royal navy in the 1620s, and under Louis XIV and his minister Colbert France briefly rivalled England and the Netherlands at sea, winning the Battle of Beachy Head (1690) but losing the initiative after Barfleur (1692). French naval power was decisive in American independence at the Chesapeake (1781), suffered in the Revolutionary and Napoleonic wars, and was rebuilt in the nineteenth century — launching the first ocean-going ironclad, Gloire (1859). In the two World Wars the fleet fought in the Mediterranean and Atlantic; much of it was scuttled at Toulon in 1942 to avoid German seizure.

Modern force

The contemporary Navy operates four Triomphant-class ballistic missile submarines, nuclear attack submarines of the Suffren class, FREMM and Horizon-class surface combatants, three Mistral-class amphibious assault ships, and the carrier Charles de Gaulle. Its naval aviation flies the Rafale M. The Fusiliers marins and Commandos Marine provide naval infantry and special forces.
