The Last Supper (Leonardo)

ArtistLeonardo da Vinci
Yearc. 1495–1498
MediumTempera on gesso, pitch, and mastic
Dimensions460 cm × 880 cm (180 in × 350 in)
LocationSanta Maria delle Grazie, Milan

The Last Supper (Italian: Il Cenacolo) is a mural painting by the Italian High Renaissance artist Leonardo da Vinci, dated to c. 1495–1498. Housed in the refectory of the Convent of Santa Maria delle Grazie in Milan, it depicts the scene of the Last Supper of Jesus with the Twelve Apostles, at the moment after Jesus announces that one among them will betray him.

Composition

The apostles are grouped in four sets of three, with Jesus alone at the centre, framed by a window and drawn with one-point perspective whose vanishing point is at Christ's head, so that the painted room appears to extend the real refectory. Leonardo rejected traditional wet fresco for an experimental technique — tempera on dry plaster — which allowed him to work slowly and revise, but caused the painting to deteriorate within decades.

Condition and restoration

The work has survived a refectory wall being cut for a door, Napoleonic troops' use of the room, and the bombing of Milan in 1943, when sandbags and scaffolding protected the wall. A major restoration, completed in 1999 after some twenty years of work led by Pinin Brambilla Barcilon, removed centuries of overpainting and grime, though only fragments of Leonardo's original paint survive.

Cultural impact

The painting is one of the most reproduced and parodied images in Western art, and has inspired extensive speculation in popular culture about hidden symbolism in the figures and composition.
