Electronic publishing (also e-publishing or digital publishing) is the publication of books, journals, magazines and other media in digital form, distributed through the internet, dedicated e-readers, tablets and computers rather than, or in addition to, print.

Forms

Electronic publishing includes e-books sold through online stores, scholarly journals published online (often with open access options), digital editions of newspapers and magazines, online reference works, and self-publishing platforms that allow authors to reach readers without a traditional publisher.

History

Early milestones include Project Gutenberg (founded 1971), which digitised public-domain texts, and the spread of CD-ROM reference works in the 1980s and 1990s. The World Wide Web and later the Amazon Kindle (2007) and tablet computers brought e-publishing into the mainstream.

Issues

Digital publishing raises questions of digital rights management, pricing and lending models for libraries, long-term preservation, and the economics of authorship, while offering instant distribution, searchability and lower marginal costs.
