Onitsuka Tiger

Founded: 1949, Kobe, Japan

Founder: Kihachiro Onitsuka

Parent: Asics Corporation

Products: Athletic and fashion footwear, apparel

Onitsuka Tiger is a Japanese footwear and fashion brand, one of the oldest shoe companies in Japan. It was founded in 1949 in Kobe by Kihachiro Onitsuka, who began making sports shoes in his living room with the aim of lifting the spirits of post-war Japanese youth through athletics. The brand's distinctive crossed side stripes, introduced in the 1960s, became one of the most recognisable design elements in sneaker history.

History

Onitsuka's first product was a basketball shoe whose sole was inspired by the suction cups of an octopus, giving players better grip on the court. The company went on to produce shoes for marathon runners, wrestlers, and other athletes. The striped design, sometimes called the "Tiger stripes" or "Mexican lines", debuted around the 1968 Mexico City Olympics era; the Mexico 66 model, developed for pre-Olympic trials, became the brand's signature shoe.

In 1964, American runner Phil Knight and his coach Bill Bowerman founded Blue Ribbon Sports to import Onitsuka Tiger shoes into the United States; that company later became Nike, Inc. In 1977, Onitsuka Co., Ltd. merged with two other firms to form Asics Corporation, and the Onitsuka Tiger name was retired as a performance line, although the shoes continued to be produced under the Asics name.

Revival

In 2002 the Onitsuka Tiger brand was revived as a fashion label, reissuing classic models such as the Mexico 66. The brand gained worldwide visibility when Uma Thurman's character in the 2003 film Kill Bill: Volume 1 wore yellow Mexico 66 sneakers with a matching jumpsuit — itself a homage to Bruce Lee's outfit in Game of Death. Onitsuka Tiger now operates flagship stores in major cities worldwide and collaborates with fashion designers, while remaining a lifestyle brand under Asics.
