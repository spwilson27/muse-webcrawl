Headright

A headright was a legal grant of land given to settlers in the English American colonies, particularly in Virginia, Maryland and the Carolinas. The system was created by the Virginia Company in 1618 to encourage settlement and the importation of labour.

Operation

Under the headright system, a person who paid for the passage of an immigrant to the colony received a grant of land — typically 50 acres — for each person ("head") transported. Colonists who paid their own way also received a headright. The resulting certificates, called headrights, could be bought and sold, allowing wealthy planters to accumulate large estates by importing indentured servants.

The system encouraged the expansion of tobacco plantations and the use of indentured servitude in the Chesapeake colonies, and it was later adopted in other colonies, including Georgia in the 18th century, before being superseded by other methods of land distribution.
