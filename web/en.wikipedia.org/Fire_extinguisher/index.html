Fire extinguisher

A fire extinguisher is a handheld active fire protection device, usually filled with a dry or wet chemical, used to extinguish or control small fires, often in emergencies. It is not intended for use on an out-of-control fire, such as one that has reached the ceiling, endangers the user, or requires the expertise of a fire brigade.

Types

Extinguishers are classified by the agent they contain and the classes of fire they suit: water extinguishers for ordinary combustibles (Class A); foam for flammable liquids; carbon dioxide (CO₂) for electrical and liquid fires, since it leaves no residue; dry chemical powder, the most common multipurpose type, using agents such as monoammonium phosphate or sodium bicarbonate; and wet chemical extinguishers for cooking-oil fires (Class K/F). Using the wrong type can be dangerous — water on an electrical or oil fire can spread the flames or cause electrocution.

Operation

Operation is commonly taught with the acronym PASS: Pull the pin, Aim at the base of the fire, Squeeze the handle, and Sweep from side to side. Modern stored-pressure extinguishers expel their agent through a hose or nozzle; they require periodic inspection and maintenance to remain reliable.

History

Portable extinguishing devices date to the 18th century; the first recognisable modern extinguisher was patented in England in 1819 by Captain George William Manby, and the soda-acid extinguisher followed in the 19th century. Halon extinguishers were once common for electronics but were phased out because halons deplete the ozone layer.
