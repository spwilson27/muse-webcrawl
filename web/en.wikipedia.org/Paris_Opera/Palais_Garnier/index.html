Palais Garnier

Palais Garnier
Location: 9th arrondissement, Paris
Architect: Charles Garnier
Opened: 5 January 1875
Capacity: 1,979

The Palais Garnier is a 1,979-seat opera house in Paris, France, built between 1861 and 1875 for the Paris Opera under Napoleon III. Designed by Charles Garnier in an eclectic Napoleon III style with lavish marble, gold leaf and sculpture, it is one of the most famous opera houses in the world.

Features

The auditorium is horseshoe-shaped with a huge crystal chandelier; its ceiling was repainted by Marc Chagall in 1964. Beneath the building lies a subterranean water reservoir, part of the inspiration for Gaston Leroux's 1910 novel The Phantom of the Opera and the famous musical.

Today

Since the opening of the Opéra Bastille in 1989, the Palais Garnier has been used primarily for ballet performances by the Paris Opera Ballet, while opera productions are generally staged at the Bastille.
