Paris Opera

The Paris Opera (Opéra de Paris) is France's principal opera and ballet company. Founded in 1669 by Louis XIV as the Académie d'Opéra, it is the oldest national opera company in the world and includes the renowned Paris Opera Ballet.

Venues

The company performs in two theatres: the Palais Garnier, a lavish 1,979-seat theatre opened in 1875 during the Second Empire, used mainly for ballet; and the Opéra Bastille, a modern 2,745-seat house inaugurated in 1989 for the bicentenary of the French Revolution, used mainly for opera. The Palais Garnier is also famous as the setting of Gaston Leroux's novel The Phantom of the Opera.

History

Granted a monopoly on sung drama in French, the Opéra premiered works from Lully and Rameau to Meyerbeer, Verdi, and Wagner, and codified classical ballet technique through its dance school, the oldest in the world, founded in 1713. Over the centuries the institution has borne many official names, including Académie Royale de Musique and Théâtre de l'Opéra.
