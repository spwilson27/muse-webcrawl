Paenungulata (from Latin paene, "almost," and ungulata, "hoofed animals") is a clade of mammals that groups together the living orders containing elephants (Proboscidea), sea cows — manatees and dugongs (Sirenia) — and hyraxes (Hyracoidea), along with extinct orders such as the Embrithopoda and Desmostylia.

Characteristics

The grouping was proposed by George Gaylord Simpson in 1945 on anatomical grounds — the animals share features such as hoof-like nails, enamel patterns on the cheek teeth, and certain structures of the ankle and ear region, though they were once called "subungulates" because their feet do not bear weight on true hooves in the manner of even- and odd-toed ungulates. Molecular studies have strongly confirmed the clade and place it within the superorder Afrotheria, reflecting an origin in Africa when that continent was isolated during the early Cenozoic.

Evolution

The oldest known paenungulates date to the Paleocene of Africa. The extinct desmostylians were amphibious, hippo-like animals of the North Pacific, while embrithopods such as Arsinoitherium grew to rhinoceros size with paired horns. The close relationship between elephants and sirenians is sometimes recognized as the subclade Tethytheria, named for the ancient Tethys Sea along whose shores their ancestors lived.
