Prehistoric Europe

Prehistoric Europe is the period of human presence and development in Europe before the emergence of written records. It spans from the first appearance of hominins in Europe, more than a million years ago, through the Palaeolithic, Mesolithic, Neolithic, Bronze Age, and Iron Age, ending at different times in different regions with the spread of literacy through Greek and Roman influence.

Palaeolithic and Mesolithic

Early hominins reached Europe by around 1.2–1.4 million years ago (as evidenced at Atapuerca in Spain). Neanderthals later dominated the continent and interbred with incoming anatomically modern humans, who arrived about 45,000 years ago and produced famous cave art such as the paintings of Chauvet and Altamira. After the last Ice Age, Mesolithic hunter-gatherers adapted to the warming climate and rising seas.

Neolithic to Iron Age

Farming spread from Anatolia into Europe from about 7000 BCE, transforming societies along the Danube and the Mediterranean. Megalithic monuments such as Stonehenge date to the later Neolithic. The Bronze Age (from c. 3200 BCE in the Aegean) brought metallurgy, trade networks, and cultures such as the Minoans, Mycenaeans, and the Urnfield culture; the Iron Age saw the Celts, the Greek city-states, and eventually the rise of Rome.
