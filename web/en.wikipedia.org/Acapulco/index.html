Acapulco

Acapulco de Juárez, commonly called Acapulco, is a city and major seaport in the state of Guerrero on the Pacific coast of Mexico, 380 kilometres (240 mi) south of Mexico City. Acapulco is located on a deep, semicircular bay and has been a port since the early colonial period of Mexico's history. It is a port of call for shipping and cruise lines running between Panama and San Francisco, California.

The city is best known as one of Mexico's oldest and best-known beach resorts, which came into prominence in the 1940s through to the 1960s as a getaway for Hollywood stars and millionaires. The name "Acapulco" comes from Nahuatl and is often glossed as "place of dense reeds" or "place where the reeds were washed away".

History

During the colonial era Acapulco was the Pacific terminus of the Manila galleon trade: from 1565 to 1815, galleons sailed between Acapulco and Manila in the Philippines, carrying silver westward and spices, porcelain and silk eastward, making the port one of the most important in New Spain. The Fort of San Diego, a star-shaped fortress built to protect the port from pirates, survives as a museum.

Tourism

Acapulco's resorts line the bay along the Costera Miguel Alemán avenue. Famous attractions include the La Quebrada cliff divers, who have performed dives from heights of up to 35 metres (115 ft) into a narrow inlet since 1934, and the beaches of Caleta, Caletilla, Condesa and Icacos. Tourism declined from the 2000s onward because of drug-related violence in Guerrero, though the city remains a major destination for domestic Mexican tourists. In October 2023 the city was devastated by Hurricane Otis, a Category 5 storm that made landfall directly over Acapulco, causing widespread destruction and a long recovery.

Geography and climate

Acapulco has a tropical wet and dry climate with hot, humid summers and warm, very dry winters. The bay is sheltered by the Sierra Madre del Sur, and the sea remains warm year-round.
