The Battle of Mohi (11 April 1241), also called the Battle of the Sajó River, was the main engagement between the Kingdom of Hungary and the Mongol Empire during the first Mongol invasion of Hungary. The Hungarian royal army under King Béla IV was encamped near the village of Mohi when the Mongols under Batu Khan and the great strategist Subutai attacked and annihilated it, leaving Hungary open to a year of devastating occupation.

Background

Mongol armies invaded Hungary on several axes in March 1241, routing border forces and destroying a Hungarian detachment at the Verecke Pass. Béla IV concentrated his host—barons, county levies, Cuman horse archers (whose murder by a mob soon deprived him of them), and a contingent of Knights Templar—on the plain west of the Sajó River, chaining his wagons into a fortified camp.

The battle

Before dawn on 11 April the Mongols forced the bridge over the Sajó with the aid of stone-throwing engines, while Subutai crossed downstream and attacked from the rear. The Hungarians, crammed in their wagon camp, were subjected to arrow fire and incendiaries and could not deploy. A deliberate gap left in the encirclement allowed fugitives to escape—into a killing ground along the roads. Béla IV escaped with a small escort, but his brother Coloman died of wounds, Archbishop Ugrin and many nobles and the Templar contingent were killed, and the Hungarian field army ceased to exist.

Aftermath

The Mongols occupied the Hungarian plain, massacring or enslaving large numbers; estimates of population loss range from 15 to 50 percent. They withdrew in the spring of 1242 following the death of Ögedei Khan. Béla IV rebuilt Hungary with stone castles and military reforms, earning the title "second founder of the state." Mohi must not be confused with the later Battle of Mohács (1526), where the Ottomans destroyed another Hungarian royal army.
