Pierre Sprey (1937–2021) was a French-born American defense analyst and record producer, known as a member of the informal "Fighter Mafia" that influenced the design of the F-16 Fighting Falcon and A-10 Thunderbolt II aircraft.

Defense work

Educated at Yale and Cornell, Sprey worked in the Pentagon's Office of Systems Analysis in the late 1960s. With John Boyd and others, he argued that American fighter aircraft had become too heavy, complex, and dependent on unreliable missiles, advocating lightweight, maneuverable day fighters. His analyses contributed to the Lightweight Fighter program that produced the F-16 and to the close-air-support A-10. He remained a vocal critic of later programs, notably the F-35.

Music

In his second career, Sprey founded Mapleshade Records, an audiophile label noted for minimalist recording techniques and releases in jazz, blues, and gospel.
