Acre (Hebrew: עַכּוֹ, Akko; Arabic: عكّا, ʿAkkā) is a port city in the Northern District of Israel, on the Mediterranean coast at the northern end of Haifa Bay. One of the oldest continuously inhabited settlements in the world, it was known in the Hellenistic and Roman periods as Ptolemais, and in the Crusader period as Saint-Jean-d'Acre.

History

Acre is mentioned in Egyptian records from about 1500 BC and in the Hebrew Bible. It became a major Phoenician and later Hellenistic port. Conquered by Muslim forces in 638, it was taken by Crusaders in 1104 and became the principal port of the Kingdom of Jerusalem; after the fall of Jerusalem in 1187 it served as the kingdom's capital until its fall to the Mamluks in 1291, an event marking the end of Crusader rule in the Levant.

In the Ottoman period Acre revived under Zahir al-Umar and Ahmad Pasha al-Jazzar, whose defences withstood Napoleon's siege in 1799 — a turning point of the French campaign in Egypt and Syria. The city was bombarded by a British-Austrian-Ottoman fleet in 1840 and became part of Mandatory Palestine, and then of Israel in 1948.

Old City

The walled Old City, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, preserves the Crusader Knights' Halls of the Hospitallers, a Templar tunnel, Ottoman caravanserais such as Khan al-Umdan, the Al-Jazzar Mosque and Turkish baths. Acre is also a holy city for the Baháʼí Faith: Baháʼu'lláh was imprisoned in its citadel and later lived at nearby Bahjí, where he is buried.
