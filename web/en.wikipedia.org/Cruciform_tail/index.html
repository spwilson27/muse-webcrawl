Cruciform tail

A cruciform tail is an aircraft tail arrangement in which the horizontal stabiliser is mounted partway up the vertical fin, so that the tail surfaces form a cross (cruciform) shape when viewed from the front or rear.

Characteristics

The arrangement places the horizontal stabiliser above the fuselage, reducing interference drag at its junction and keeping it partly clear of wing downwash and, on multi-engined aircraft, of propeller slipstreams at some attitudes. It is a compromise between a conventional low-mounted stabiliser and a T-tail.

Examples

The cruciform tail is used on aircraft such as the Avro Shackleton, the Beechcraft King Air (in T-tail form's family of high tails), and notably on jet airliners with rear-mounted engines only in T-tail form; true cruciform arrangements appear on types including the Armstrong Whitworth Argosy and several turboprops and business aircraft where tail position must clear fuselage-mounted systems.
