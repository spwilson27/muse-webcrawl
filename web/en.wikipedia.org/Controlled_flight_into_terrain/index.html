Controlled flight into terrain

Controlled flight into terrain (CFIT) describes an accident in which a properly functioning aircraft, under the control of the crew, is flown into terrain, water, or obstacles, generally without the pilots being aware of the impending collision. The term was coined by engineers at Boeing in the late 1970s.

Causes

Common contributing factors include pilot error, loss of situational awareness, poor visibility, fatigue, and inadequate terrain warning. CFIT was historically one of the leading causes of fatal airliner accidents.

Prevention

The introduction of ground proximity warning systems (GPWS) and later enhanced GPWS with terrain databases, together with better training and procedures, has sharply reduced CFIT accidents since the 1970s. Despite these improvements, CFIT remains a significant risk for general aviation and operations in mountainous regions.
