Future value

Future value is the value of an asset at a specific date in the future, calculated from its present value by applying an assumed rate of growth or interest. It is a core concept in the time value of money: a sum received today is worth more than the same sum received later, because it can be invested in the interim.

Calculation

With compound interest, future value is FV = PV × (1 + i)ⁿ, where PV is present value, i is the interest rate per period, and n is the number of periods. With simple interest, FV = PV × (1 + i·n). For a series of equal payments (an annuity), the future value sums each payment compounded for its remaining periods.

Example

An investment of $1,000 at 5% annual interest compounded yearly has a future value of $1,000 × 1.05¹⁰ ≈ $1,628.89 after ten years.

Applications

Future value calculations are used in retirement planning, loan and bond valuation, capital budgeting, and comparing investment alternatives. The inverse operation — finding present value from a future amount — is called discounting.
