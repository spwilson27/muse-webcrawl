Bugatti Veyron

The Bugatti Veyron EB 16.4 is a mid-engine sports car designed and developed in by the Volkswagen Group and manufactured by Bugatti in Molsheim, France, from 2005 to 2015. Named after racing driver Pierre Veyron, it was named Car of the Decade by the BBC television programme Top Gear.

Development

Volkswagen acquired the Bugatti marque in 1998, and chairman Ferdinand Piëch set the programme's famous targets: 1,000 horsepower and a top speed above 400 km/h, in a car usable every day. The result, the Veyron 16.4 launched in 2005, used an 8.0-litre quad-turbocharged W16 engine producing 736 kW (987 hp), a seven-speed dual-clutch gearbox and permanent all-wheel drive. It accelerates from 0–100 km/h in 2.5 seconds and reaches 407 km/h (253 mph).

Variants

  • Grand Sport (2009) — removable-roof roadster.
  • Super Sport (2010) — 1,200 PS; set a production-car speed record of 431.072 km/h (268 mph).
  • Grand Sport Vitesse (2012) — roadster version of the Super Sport.

Numerous one-off and special editions, including the Hermès, Pur Sang and L'Or Blanc, were built during a production run of 450 cars.

Reception and successor

Although sold at a considerable loss per car, the Veyron redefined the hypercar and the technical ceiling of production automobiles. It was succeeded in 2016 by the Bugatti Chiron.
