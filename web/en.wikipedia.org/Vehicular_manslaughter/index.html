Vehicular manslaughter

Vehicular manslaughter (also called vehicular homicide in some jurisdictions) is a criminal charge for causing the death of another person through the negligent or unlawful operation of a motor vehicle. The offence exists in the laws of many U.S. states and comparable offences exist in other common-law jurisdictions, such as causing death by dangerous driving in the United Kingdom.

Elements

The prosecution must typically show that the defendant drove with gross negligence, criminal negligence, or while committing an unlawful act (such as driving under the influence), and that this conduct caused the death. Charges are usually graded: simple negligence may be a misdemeanour, while intoxication or extreme recklessness can make the offence a serious felony with years of imprisonment.

Related offences

Where intent to kill is alleged the charge becomes murder; many states have specific DUI-murder doctrines. Separate charges commonly accompany the case, such as driving under the influence, hit-and-run, and reckless driving.
