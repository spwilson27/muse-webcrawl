Carnegie Hall

Carnegie Hall
Location: 881 Seventh Avenue, Manhattan, New York City
Opened: 1891
Architect: William Burnet Tuthill
Capacity: 2,804 (Stern Auditorium)

Carnegie Hall is a concert venue in Midtown Manhattan, New York City, at 881 Seventh Avenue. Built by philanthropist Andrew Carnegie in 1891, it is one of the most prestigious venues in the world for both classical and popular music, renowned for its architecture and acoustics.

History

The hall opened on 5 May 1891 with a concert conducted by Walter Damrosch and Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky, who was making his American conducting debut. The main auditorium was renamed in 1998 for Isaac Stern, the violinist who led the campaign that saved the hall from demolition in 1960.

Venues

The building contains three performance spaces: the Isaac Stern Auditorium / Ronald O. Perelman Stage (2,804 seats), the intimate Judy and Arthur Zankel Hall (599 seats), and the Joan and Sanford I. Weill Recital Hall (268 seats). It also houses the Rose Museum and studio towers long home to artists, teachers, and organisations.

Performances

Carnegie Hall has hosted premieres of works such as Dvořák's New World Symphony and Gershwin's An American in Paris, and landmark concerts from Benny Goodman's 1938 jazz concert to performances by Judy Garland, The Beatles, and Bob Dylan. The famous joke — "How do you get to Carnegie Hall?" "Practice, practice, practice" — is closely associated with the venue.
