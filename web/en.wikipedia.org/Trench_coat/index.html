Trench coat

A trench coat is a coat variety made of waterproof heavy-duty fabric, traditionally cotton gabardine, poplin, or leather, generally in a double-breasted style with a belted waist, shoulder straps (epaulettes), a storm flap, and a buckled cuff straps. It typically falls below the knee and is most often khaki, beige, or black.

History

The trench coat was developed during the First World War as an item of clothing for British Army officers in the trenches, from which it takes its name. Two British firms, Burberry and Aquascutum, both claim to have originated the design; Burberry's founder Thomas Burberry had invented the breathable waterproof fabric gabardine in 1879. Features such as epaulettes for rank insignia, D-rings on the belt, and the storm shield were functional military details.

In popular culture

After the war the coat became a civilian fashion staple and a cinematic shorthand for detectives and spies, famously worn by Humphrey Bogart in Casablanca and by Peter Falk's Columbo. It remains a classic of outerwear design.
