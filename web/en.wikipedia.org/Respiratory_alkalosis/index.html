Respiratory alkalosis

Respiratory alkalosis is a medical condition in which increased respiration (hyperventilation) raises the blood pH beyond the normal range (7.35–7.45), because excess carbon dioxide is blown off, lowering arterial CO2 (hypocapnia).

Primary change↓ PaCO2
Effect↑ blood pH
CompensationRenal bicarbonate excretion

Causes

Causes include anxiety-driven hyperventilation, pain, fever, hypoxaemia (including at high altitude), pneumonia, pulmonary embolism, sepsis, pregnancy, liver failure, and salicylate poisoning (early phase). Excessive mechanical ventilation can also produce it.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms include light-headedness, confusion, paraesthesia (tingling, especially around the mouth and extremities), muscle cramps and, in severe cases, tetany, because alkalosis reduces ionised calcium. Diagnosis is by arterial blood gas analysis showing high pH and low PaCO2.

Treatment

Treatment addresses the underlying cause; acute anxiety-related hyperventilation may be managed with reassurance and controlled breathing. The kidneys compensate over hours to days by excreting bicarbonate.
