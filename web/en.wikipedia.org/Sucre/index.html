Sucre

Sucre (Spanish pronunciation: [ˈsukɾe]) is the constitutional capital of Bolivia, the capital of the Chuquisaca Department, and the seat of the national judiciary, including the Supreme Court. Although the seat of government is La Paz, Sucre retains its constitutional status as capital. The city lies in the south-central part of the country at an elevation of about 2,790 metres (9,150 ft) and has a population of roughly 280,000.

History

The city was founded on 29 September 1538 by the Spanish as Ciudad de la Plata de la Nueva Toledo, near the indigenous settlement of Charcas, and became a major centre of Spanish colonial administration and the seat of the Real Audiencia of Charcas. The first cry for independence in Spanish America was raised here on 25 May 1809. After independence in 1825 the city was renamed for the revolutionary leader Antonio José de Sucre.

Heritage

Sucre's well-preserved historic centre, with its whitewashed colonial buildings and numerous churches, was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1991. The city is home to the University of San Francisco Xavier, founded in 1624, one of the oldest universities in the Americas. Nearby, the Cal Orck'o site preserves thousands of dinosaur footprints in a former cement quarry.
