Sapindales is an order of flowering plants within the rosid clade of the eudicots. The order contains many familiar and economically important plants, including citrus fruits, maples, horse chestnuts, lychee and rambutan, mangoes, cashews, frankincense and myrrh, mahogany, and neem. Under the APG IV system, Sapindales comprises nine families and roughly 460 genera with about 5,600–6,000 species.

Citrus (Rutaceae) and maples (Sapindaceae) are prominent members of the order.

Families

The families recognised in the APG IV classification are Anacardiaceae (the cashew and mango family), Biebersteiniaceae, Burseraceae (frankincense and myrrh), Kirkiaceae, Meliaceae (the mahogany family), Nitrariaceae, Rutaceae (the rue and citrus family), Sapindaceae (the soapberry family, which in APG classifications includes the maples and horse chestnuts formerly placed in Aceraceae and Hippocastanaceae), and Simaroubaceae.

Characteristics

Members of the order are mostly woody plants — trees and shrubs — often with pinnately compound leaves. Many families are characterised by the presence of resin ducts, aromatic oils, or bitter triterpenoid compounds. The flowers are typically small, radially or bilaterally symmetric, with a nectary disc, and the fruit may be a drupe, berry, capsule, or samara, as in the winged fruits of maples.

Economic importance

Sapindales includes some of the world's most important fruit crops: all citrus (family Rutaceae), mango, cashew and pistachio (Anacardiaceae), and lychee and rambutan (Sapindaceae). Mahogany and other Meliaceae provide valuable timber, while Burseraceae yield frankincense and myrrh resins, and neem has long been used in traditional medicine and as a source of natural insecticide.
