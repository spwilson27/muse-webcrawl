Focus puller

A focus puller, or 1st assistant camera (1st AC), is a member of a film crew's camera department responsible for maintaining the camera's focus on the subject during a shot — a task called "pulling focus." The focus puller works with the camera operator and director of photography and is traditionally regarded as one of the most demanding technical roles on a film set.

Duties

Before filming, the focus puller assembles and checks the camera, lenses, and accessories, measures distances to actors and marks, and prepares focus marks on the follow-focus dial or on the floor at the actors' positions. During a take, the puller rotates the follow-focus control to match the lens's focus distance to the subject's movement, often without looking through the viewfinder, relying on rehearsed measurements and monitor cues.

Technique

Pulling focus requires precise knowledge of depth of field, which shrinks with wide apertures, long lenses, and large formats, sometimes leaving only centimeters of acceptable sharpness. Modern productions supplement traditional techniques with laser rangefinders, ultrasonic distance measurement, and wireless focus systems with monitors at the puller's station.

Role in the crew

The 1st AC supervises the 2nd assistant camera (clapper loader), plans equipment for the camera department, and is accountable if a shot is unusably soft; an out-of-focus take can be impossible to repair in post-production.
