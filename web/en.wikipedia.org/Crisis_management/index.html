Crisis management

Crisis management
Field: Management, public relations
Phases: Prevention, preparation, response, recovery
Related: Business continuity

Crisis management is the process by which an organization deals with a disruptive and unexpected event that threatens to harm the organization or its stakeholders. It spans crisis prevention and planning, the response during an event, and post-crisis recovery and learning.

Stages

Effective programs identify potential crises, maintain a crisis plan and communications protocols, train a response team, and rehearse through simulations. During a crisis, speed, accuracy, and a single credible spokesperson are emphasized; afterward, organizations review what happened and revise plans.

Notable cases

Johnson & Johnson's rapid, transparent response to the 1982 Tylenol tampering murders is a classic positive example, while delayed or evasive responses — in oil spills, product recalls, and data breaches — are studied as failures. Crisis communication theory, such as Timothy Coombs' Situational Crisis Communication Theory, matches response strategies to the responsibility attributed to the organization.
