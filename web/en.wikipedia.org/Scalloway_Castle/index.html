Scalloway Castle is a tower house in Scalloway, on the west coast of Mainland, Shetland, Scotland. It was built in about 1600 by Patrick Stewart, 2nd Earl of Orkney, and is one of only two castles built in Shetland, the other being Muness Castle on Unst.

History

Earl Patrick Stewart, known as "Black Patie", was a notoriously tyrannical ruler of Orkney and Shetland. Using forced labour, he began building the castle in 1599 after demolishing earlier structures on the site, including the house of Andrew Leslie. The castle was completed around 1600 and served as the earl's residence and administrative centre in Shetland.

Patrick was arrested in 1609 and executed for treason in Edinburgh in 1615. The castle subsequently fell into decline, though it was garrisoned by troops during the Civil War period. Poet John Barclay is among those said to have connections with the site in the 17th century.

Architecture

The castle is an L-plan tower house of four storeys, built of harled rubble with sandstone dressings. A corbelled turret projects at the south-east corner. The main entrance leads into a vaulted ground floor, with the great hall occupying the first floor. An inscription once placed over the door referenced the builder's lineage.

Today

The roofless ruin is in the care of Historic Environment Scotland and is open to visitors. It stands beside the Scalloway Museum, which displays information about the castle and the Shetland bus operation of the Second World War.
