Little Ships of Dunkirk

The Little Ships of Dunkirk were a flotilla of about 850 private vessels — pleasure craft, fishing boats, lifeboats, yachts and small motor boats — that sailed from Ramsgate in England to Dunkirk in northern France between 26 May and 4 June 1940 to assist in the Dunkirk evacuation of Allied soldiers during the Second World War.

The evacuation

Operation Dynamo rescued some 338,000 British and French troops trapped by the German advance. Destroyers and transports could not get close to the shallow beaches, so small craft ferried men from the shore and the harbour mole to larger ships, and many carried soldiers all the way back across the English Channel. Some were crewed by naval personnel; others were sailed by their civilian owners.

Legacy

The "Dunkirk spirit" became a lasting symbol of civilian courage and improvisation in British memory. The Association of Dunkirk Little Ships, founded in 1965, preserves surviving vessels, which periodically make commemorative crossings to Dunkirk.
