Mexico–United States relations

Mexico–United States relations cover the diplomatic and economic relationship between the United Mexican States and the United States of America. The two countries share a 3,145-kilometre land border, among the most frequently crossed in the world, and are linked by deep trade, migration and security ties.

History

Relations began in the 1820s and were shaped by U.S. annexation of Texas, the Mexican–American War of 1846–1848, in which Mexico lost roughly half its territory under the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo, the French intervention, cooperation in the Second World War, and the bracero guest-worker program. The North American Free Trade Agreement (NAFTA) of 1994 transformed economic integration; it was replaced by the USMCA in 2020, under which Mexico has become the United States' largest goods trading partner.

Issues

Recurring questions include immigration, border security, drug trafficking and the Mérida Initiative, water sharing under the 1944 treaty, and cooperation on energy and supply chains.
