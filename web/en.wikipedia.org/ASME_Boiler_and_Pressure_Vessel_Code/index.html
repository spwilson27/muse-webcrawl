ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code

The ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code (BPVC) is an American Society of Mechanical Engineers (ASME) standard that regulates the design, construction, inspection, and testing of boilers and pressure vessels, and of components of nuclear power plants. It is one of the world's most widely used engineering codes, adopted in whole or in part by most U.S. states, Canadian provinces, and many other jurisdictions, and underpinning the ASME certification ("stamp") system for pressure equipment.

History

The code arose from the catastrophic boiler explosions of the 19th and early 20th centuries, most notoriously the 1905 Grover Shoe Factory disaster in Brockton, Massachusetts, which killed 58 people. At ASME's urging, Massachusetts adopted the first state boiler rules, and ASME appointed a committee in 1911 that produced the first edition of the code in 1914 (published 1915). It has been revised continuously since, with new editions issued every two years.

Sections

The BPVC is divided into sections, including: Section I (power boilers), Section II (materials, in four parts covering ferrous and nonferrous specifications, properties, and welding consumables), Section III (rules for construction of nuclear facility components), Section IV (heating boilers), Section V (nondestructive examination), Section VI and VII (care and operation of boilers), Section VIII (pressure vessels, in Divisions 1, 2, and 3, covering increasing levels of design rigor), Section IX (welding, brazing, and fusing qualifications), Section X (fiber-reinforced plastic vessels), Section XI (inservice inspection of nuclear components), Section XII (transport tanks), and Section XIII (rules for overpressure protection).

Certification and stamps

Manufacturers accredited by ASME may apply certification marks — U and U2 for pressure vessels, S for power boilers, and the N stamps for nuclear components, among others — attesting that items were designed, fabricated, and inspected under the code, under the surveillance of Authorized Inspectors commissioned through the National Board of Boiler and Pressure Vessel Inspectors. Shop and field compliance is commonly verified by third-party Authorized Inspection Agencies.

Influence

Because of its long history and comprehensive material specifications, the BPVC is used, referenced, or harmonized with in more than 100 countries, and it interacts with other regimes such as the European Pressure Equipment Directive (PED), which uses different design rules and conformity assessment.
