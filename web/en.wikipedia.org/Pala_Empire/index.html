Pala Empire

The Pala Empire was an imperial power in post-classical India, originating in the Bengal region and ruling large parts of the northern Indian subcontinent from the 8th to the 12th century CE. The Palas were followers of Mahayana and Tantric Buddhism.

History

The empire was founded in 750 CE when Gopala was elected king of Gauda amid political disorder. His son Dharmapala (r. c. 770–810) expanded the empire and contested the Kannauj triangle with the Gurjara-Pratiharas and Rashtrakutas. Devapala extended Pala influence into Assam and Orissa.

Culture and legacy

The Palas patronised the great monastic universities of Nalanda and Vikramashila, and the Pala school of sculpture and painting flourished. Pala influence spread Buddhism to Tibet and Southeast Asia, notably through the mission of Atisha. The empire declined in the 12th century and was succeeded in Bengal by the Sena dynasty.
