Filter (mathematics)

In mathematics, a filter is a special subset of a partially ordered set which captures the notion of a collection of "large" sets. Filters were introduced by Henri Cartan in 1937 and have applications in topology, order theory, and logic, notably through the related concept of an ultrafilter.

Definition

A filter on a set X is a non-empty collection F of subsets of X such that: the empty set is not in F; F is closed under finite intersections; and if a set is in F, every superset of it is also in F. A proper filter does not contain the empty set.

Applications

Filters generalise the notion of convergence: a sequence or net converges to a point if the filter of its tails is contained in the neighbourhood filter of the point. Ultrafilters are used in model theory to construct ultraproducts and in the proof of the compactness theorem.
