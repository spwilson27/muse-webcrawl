Aladdin is a 1992 American animated musical fantasy film produced by Walt Disney Feature Animation and released by Walt Disney Pictures. Directed by Ron Clements and John Musker, it is based on the Arabic folktale of the same name from One Thousand and One Nights.

Plot

Aladdin, a kind-hearted street urchin in the fictional city of Agrabah, discovers a magic lamp containing a Genie who grants him three wishes. Disguised as the wealthy Prince Ali, Aladdin seeks to win the heart of Princess Jasmine, while the Sultan's vizier Jafar plots to seize the lamp and the throne.

Cast and music

The film features the voices of Scott Weinger as Aladdin, Linda Larkin as Jasmine, and Robin Williams as the Genie, whose improvisational performance was widely praised. The score and songs were written by Alan Menken, Howard Ashman, and Tim Rice; "A Whole New World" won the Academy Award for Best Original Song.

Reception

Aladdin was the highest-grossing film of 1992, earning over $500 million worldwide. It received two Academy Awards and spawned sequels, a television series, a Broadway musical, and a 2019 live-action remake.
