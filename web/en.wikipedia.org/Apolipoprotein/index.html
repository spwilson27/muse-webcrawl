Apolipoprotein

Apolipoprotein
FunctionStructure, enzyme cofactor, receptor ligand for lipoproteins
Major typesA, B, C, D, E, H, L, (a)

Apolipoproteins are proteins that bind lipids to form lipoproteins, which transport fats through blood and lymph. They stabilise lipoprotein structure, act as enzyme cofactors, and serve as ligands for cell-surface receptors, directing particles to their destinations.

Major classes

ApoA-I is the main protein of HDL and activates LCAT. ApoB (B-100 and B-48) is structural for chylomicrons, VLDL, and LDL and binds the LDL receptor. ApoC-II activates lipoprotein lipase, while ApoC-III inhibits it. ApoE mediates remnant clearance and its isoforms (E2, E3, E4) influence Alzheimer's disease and cardiovascular risk. Apo(a) forms lipoprotein(a).

Clinical significance

Mutations cause dyslipidemias including abetalipoproteinemia, Tangier disease, and familial hypercholesterolemia-like syndromes. Measuring ApoB and ApoA-I, and their ratio, predicts coronary artery disease.
