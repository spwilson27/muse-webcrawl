QR code

A QR code (quick-response code) is a type of two-dimensional matrix barcode, invented in 1994 by the Japanese company Denso Wave for labelling automobile parts. It features black squares arranged on a white background, typically used to store a URL or other information for reading by the camera of a smartphone or dedicated scanner.

History

The QR code system was invented by Masahiro Hara and his team at Denso Wave, a Toyota subsidiary, to track vehicles and parts during manufacturing with faster scanning than conventional barcodes allowed. Denso Wave chose not to exercise its patent rights, greatly aiding adoption. The code became an ISO international standard in 2000 and spread worldwide in the 2010s as smartphone cameras and mobile internet became ubiquitous.

Design and capacity

A QR code consists of a grid of modules (from 21×21 in Version 1 to 177×177 in Version 40), with three distinctive square finder patterns at its corners that let scanners detect the code at any orientation, and alignment and timing patterns for correcting distortion. Reed–Solomon error correction allows a code to remain readable even when up to 30 percent of it is damaged or obscured. A Version 40 code at low error correction can store up to 7,089 numeric characters, 4,296 alphanumeric characters, or 2,953 bytes of binary data.

Uses

QR codes link physical objects to digital content: product packaging, advertisements, restaurant menus, museum labels, and tickets. They are widely used for mobile payments — especially in China (WeChat Pay, Alipay), India (UPI), and Japan — for joining Wi-Fi networks, for two-factor authentication setup, for contact tracing and vaccination certificates during the COVID-19 pandemic, and for cryptocurrency addresses. Risks include "quishing" (QR phishing), in which fraudulent codes direct users to malicious sites.
