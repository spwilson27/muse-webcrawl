Corgi Toys

Corgi Toys were die-cast toy vehicles produced by Mettoy Playcraft Ltd. in Swansea, Wales, from 1956. The brand took its name from the Welsh corgi dog, and its slogan was "the ones with windows" — Corgi models were the first in their class to feature clear plastic windows.

Corgi became famous for licensed film and television models, including the James Bond Aston Martin DB5 from Goldfinger (1965) — complete with working ejector seat, and one of the best-selling toys of its era — the Batmobile, the Lotus Esprit from The Spy Who Loved Me, and vehicles from The Man from U.N.C.L.E. and Chitty Chitty Bang Bang.

Later history

Mettoy went into receivership in 1983; the Corgi brand passed through several owners, including Mattel, and was acquired by Hornby in 2008. Modern Corgi production focuses on the adult collectors' market, with limited-edition die-cast models of cars, aircraft (the Aviation Archive range), and buses.
