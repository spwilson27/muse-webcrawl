First Party System

The First Party System was the system of political parties in the United States from 1792 to 1824. It featured two national parties competing for control of the presidency, Congress, and the states: the Federalist Party, created by Alexander Hamilton, and the rival Democratic-Republican Party, formed by Thomas Jefferson and James Madison.

Origins

The parties crystallised in the 1790s around Hamilton's financial program, the national bank, and foreign policy toward revolutionary France and Britain.

Key issues

Federalists favoured a strong national government, commerce, and Britain; Democratic-Republicans favoured states' rights, agriculture, and France. Jefferson's victory in the "Revolution of 1800" transferred power peacefully.

Decline

The Federalists collapsed after the War of 1812 and the Hartford Convention. The ensuing one-party "Era of Good Feelings" ended with the disputed election of 1824 and the rise of the Second Party System.
