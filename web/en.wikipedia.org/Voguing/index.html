Vogue (dance)

Vogue
OriginBallroom culture, Harlem, New York City, 1960s–1980s
CreatorsAfrican-American and Latino LGBTQ+ communities
ElementsPoses, catwalk, duckwalk, hand performance, floor performance, spins and dips

Vogue, or voguing, is a highly stylized, modern house dance originating from Harlem ballroom culture in the 1960s. It was popularized in the 1980s and reached the mainstream when it was featured in Madonna's 1990 song and video "Vogue" and the documentary Paris Is Burning (1990). Voguing is characterized by striking poses modeled on fashion photographs — the name comes from Vogue magazine — joined by angular, linear arm, leg, and body movements.

Styles

Three main styles are usually distinguished. Old way emphasizes poses, lines, and precision. New way (from around 1990) adds extreme flexibility, contortion-like arm and leg work, and "arms control" illusions. Vogue fem is a fluid style influenced by femininity, built from five elements: hand performance, catwalk, duckwalk, floor performance, and spins and dips.

Ballroom culture

Voguing is performed at balls organized by "houses" — chosen families, often led by a "mother" or "father", such as the House of Xtravaganza and the House of LaBeija — where competitors "walk" categories for trophies and prizes. Figures such as Willi Ninja, known as the "godfather of vogue", brought the form to wider audiences. The dance has remained influential in contemporary pop performance and LGBTQ+ culture worldwide.
