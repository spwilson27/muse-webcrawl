Acidosis

Acidosis
Specialty: Nephrology, critical care
Blood pH: Below 7.35 (acidemia)
Types: Metabolic, respiratory

Acidosis is a process causing increased acidity in the blood and other body tissues. If not further qualified, it usually refers to acidity of the blood plasma, which is termed acidemia when arterial pH falls below 7.35.

Metabolic acidosis

Metabolic acidosis results from increased acid production (as in lactic acidosis or ketoacidosis), loss of bicarbonate (as in diarrhea), or failure of the kidneys to excrete acid. It is analyzed using the anion gap to narrow the cause.

Respiratory acidosis

Respiratory acidosis results from inadequate ventilation, causing carbon dioxide to accumulate. Causes include chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, respiratory muscle weakness, and central respiratory depression.

Signs and compensation

Symptoms may include rapid deep breathing (Kussmaul respiration), confusion, and fatigue. The body compensates by increasing ventilation and renal acid excretion; treatment targets the underlying cause.
