Indian wild ass

The Indian wild ass (Equus hemionus khur), also called the khur, Baluchi wild ass or ghudkhur in the local Gujarati language, is a subspecies of the onager native to South Asia. It is currently listed as Near Threatened by the IUCN. The khur was formerly widespread across the arid regions of northwestern India and Pakistan, but its range contracted drastically during the twentieth century, and it is now largely confined to the Indian Wild Ass Sanctuary in the Little Rann of Kutch in Gujarat.

Description

The Indian wild ass is similar in appearance to other onagers but is distinguished by its sandy-brown coat, which is paler on the belly and legs, and a dark brown stripe running along the back. Adults stand about 110–127 cm at the shoulder and weigh between 200 and 240 kg. Males are generally larger and darker than females. Unlike domestic horses, the khur has short, upright ears and a relatively short mane.

Habitat and behaviour

The khur inhabits saline desert flats, grasslands on the edges of the Rann, and thorn scrub. It is well adapted to arid conditions and can survive long periods without water, obtaining moisture from the vegetation it grazes on. Indian wild asses live in small groups: territorial males, family groups of females with foals, and bachelor herds. They are swift runners capable of speeds of 65–70 km/h, which helps them evade predators such as wolves.

Conservation

Populations declined sharply in the mid-twentieth century due to hunting, competition with domestic livestock, and an outbreak of the surra disease transmitted by flies. The Indian Wild Ass Sanctuary, established in 1972, covers roughly 4,954 km² of the Little Rann. Conservation measures have allowed the population to recover from a low of a few hundred to several thousand; recent censuses have estimated the population at around 6,000 individuals. Some animals have dispersed beyond the sanctuary into surrounding districts of Gujarat, occasionally coming into conflict with farmers.
