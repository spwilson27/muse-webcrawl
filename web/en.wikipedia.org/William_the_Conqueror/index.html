William the Conqueror (c. 1028 – 1087), sometimes called William the Bastard, was Duke of Normandy and, after the Norman Conquest of 1066, the first Norman king of England, reigning as William I.

Early life

The illegitimate son of Duke Robert I of Normandy — a descendant of the Viking Rollo — William succeeded as duke aged about seven and survived a turbulent minority of plots and rebellions. By the 1050s–1060s, through battles such as Val-ès-Dunes and Varaville and marriage to Matilda of Flanders, he had made Normandy a formidable power.

Conquest of England

Claiming Edward the Confessor had promised him the throne, William invaded England in September 1066 after Harold Godwinson had defeated the Norwegian invasion at Stamford Bridge. At the Battle of Hastings on 14 October 1066 Harold was killed and the Norman army prevailed. William was crowned in Westminster Abbey on Christmas Day 1066.

Rule

William crushed English revolts, most brutally in the Harrying of the North (1069–1070), replaced the English elite with Norman lords, built castles including the Tower of London, and in 1086 commissioned the Domesday Book, a great survey of England's landholding. He died in 1087 from injuries after a campaign in France; England passed to his son William Rufus, Normandy to Robert Curthose.

Legacy

The Conquest transformed England's language, aristocracy, church and architecture, binding England to continental Europe.
