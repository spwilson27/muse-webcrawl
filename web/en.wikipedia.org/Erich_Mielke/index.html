Erich Mielke

Erich Fritz Emil Mielke
Born: December 28, 1907, Berlin, Germany
Died: May 21, 2000, Berlin, Germany
Office: Minister for State Security (1957–1989)

Erich Fritz Emil Mielke was a German communist official who served as head of the East German Ministry for State Security (the Stasi) from 1957 until shortly after the fall of the Berlin Wall in 1989. Under his leadership the Stasi became one of the most extensive secret police and intelligence organisations in the world.

Early life

Mielke joined the Communist Party of Germany as a teenager. In 1931 he participated in the murder of police captains Paul Anlauf and Franz Lenck at Bülowplatz, fleeing afterwards to the Soviet Union, where he received political and military training. He later served in the Spanish Civil War and spent the Second World War in exile and underground work.

Head of the Stasi

After helping to build East Germany's security apparatus, Mielke became Minister for State Security in 1957. By 1989 the Stasi employed about 91,000 full-time staff and some 174,000 unofficial collaborators, maintaining files on millions of citizens. Mielke was a powerful member of the SED Politburo and a hardliner opposed to reform.

Fall and trial

Mielke resigned in November 1989 and was arrested in December. In 1993 he was convicted of the 1931 murders of Anlauf and Lenck and sentenced to six years in prison; he was released in 1995 on health grounds and died in 2000.
