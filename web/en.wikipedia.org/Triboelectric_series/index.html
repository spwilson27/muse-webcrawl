Triboelectric series

A triboelectric series is a list of materials, ordered by their tendency to gain or lose electrons when they are in contact with another material, a process known as the triboelectric effect. When two materials in the list are rubbed together or brought into contact and separated, the one higher on the list (which is ordered such that materials at the top tend to acquire a positive charge) will acquire a positive electric charge, while the lower will acquire a negative charge. The tendency of the materials can also be quantified.

Typical ordering

A commonly published series runs, from most positive to most negative: human skin and hair, glass, nylon, wool, lead, silk, paper, cotton, steel, wood, amber, hard rubber, nickel and copper, brass and silver, gold and platinum, polyester, styrofoam, polyurethane, polyethylene, polypropylene, PVC, silicon, and PTFE (Teflon).

Mechanism

The triboelectric effect is not fully understood. Candidate mechanisms include electron transfer driven by differences in effective work function, ion transfer, and transfer of microscopic material fragments. The measured polarity and magnitude of charging depend on surface condition, humidity, temperature, and strain, so published series are empirical and may vary slightly between sources.

Applications

The series is used in designing for electrostatic discharge protection, in selecting materials for triboelectric nanogenerators that harvest mechanical energy, and in industrial processes such as electrostatic separation and xerography.
