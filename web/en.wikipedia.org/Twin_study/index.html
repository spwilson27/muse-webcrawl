Twin study
Field: behavioural genetics
Compares: monozygotic vs dizygotic twins
Goal: estimate heritability and environmental effects

Twin studies are studies conducted on identical or fraternal twins to disentangle the influence of genetics and environment on traits, diseases and behaviours. They are a central tool of behavioural genetics.

Method

The classic design compares correlations between monozygotic (identical) twins, who share nearly all their genes, with those between dizygotic (fraternal) twins, who share about half. If identical twins resemble each other more, genetic influence is inferred. Models partition variation into additive genetics, shared environment and non-shared environment (the ACE model).

Assumptions and criticism

The equal environments assumption — that identical twins are not treated more similarly in trait-relevant ways than fraternal twins — is debated, as are the representativeness of twins and the interpretation of heritability as a population statistic rather than a property of an individual.
