Willem de Kooning

Willem de Kooning (24 April 1904 – 19 March 1997) was a Dutch-American abstract expressionist artist, born in Rotterdam, who became one of the leading figures of the New York School. He is best known for his gestural paintings of women and for works such as Woman I (1950–52) and Interchange (1955), among the most valuable paintings ever sold.

Life and career

De Kooning trained as a commercial artist in Rotterdam and stowed away to the United States in 1926. In New York he worked as a house painter and for the WPA Federal Art Project, and moved in the circle of Arshile Gorky, Jackson Pollock and Franz Kline. His first solo exhibition came in 1948; his black-and-white abstractions of that period and the controversial Woman series of the early 1950s — violently brushed, figurative paintings that seemed to run against pure abstraction — made his reputation.

Style and later work

De Kooning's mature style fused drawing and painting: slashing brushwork, smeared and scraped paint, and forms that hover between figure and landscape. From the 1960s, in East Hampton, Long Island, he painted luminous landscapes and, in the 1980s, spare "ribbon" paintings made as his health declined amid Alzheimer's disease. His Interchange sold for about $300 million in 2015, then the highest price known for a painting.
