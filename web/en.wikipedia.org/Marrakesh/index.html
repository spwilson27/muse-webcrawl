Marrakesh

Marrakesh (also spelled Marrakech) is a major city in Morocco, the fourth-largest in the country after Casablanca, Fez and Tangier. It is the capital of the Marrakesh-Safi region. The city lies west of the foothills of the Atlas Mountains and is known as the "Red City" or "Ochre City" because of the red sandstone used in many of its buildings and walls.

History

Marrakesh was founded around 1070 by Abu Bakr ibn Umar, chieftain and second emir of the Almoravid dynasty. The city became the capital of the Almoravid Empire, which stretched from Senegal to Spain. Under Yusuf ibn Tashufin the city was embellished with mosques, palaces and gardens. After the Almohads conquered the city in 1147, Marrakesh remained an imperial capital, and it later served as capital again under the Saadian dynasty in the 16th century, when Sultan Ahmad al-Mansur built the lavish El Badi Palace and the Saadian Tombs.

The historic medina of Marrakesh is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. At its heart lies Jemaa el-Fnaa Square, famous for its food stalls, storytellers, musicians and market vendors. Other landmarks include the Koutoubia Mosque, whose 77-metre minaret dates to the 12th century, the Bahia Palace, and the Majorelle Garden.

Climate

Marrakesh has a hot semi-arid climate with very hot, dry summers and mild winters. Snow-capped peaks of the High Atlas are visible from the city in winter and spring.

Economy

Tourism is a major part of the economy, with millions of visitors each year. The city is also a trading centre for the surrounding agricultural region and is served by Marrakesh Menara Airport and a railway station linking it to Casablanca and the north.
