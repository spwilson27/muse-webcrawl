Alendronic acid

Alendronic acid, sold under the brand name Fosamax among others, is a bisphosphonate medication used to treat osteoporosis and Paget's disease of bone. It is taken by mouth, usually once weekly. Use is recommended together with vitamin D, calcium supplementation, and lifestyle changes.

Alendronic acid
Pronunciation: a-LEN-dro-nate
Trade name: Fosamax
Drug class: Bisphosphonate
Formula: C₄H₁₃NO₇P₂

Medical uses

Alendronic acid is used for the treatment and prevention of osteoporosis in postmenopausal women, treatment of osteoporosis in men, treatment of glucocorticoid-induced osteoporosis, and treatment of Paget's disease of bone.

Mechanism of action

Like other bisphosphonates, alendronic acid binds to hydroxyapatite in bone and inhibits osteoclast-mediated bone resorption, thereby slowing bone loss and increasing bone mineral density, which reduces the risk of fractures.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include abdominal pain, acid reflux, and gastrointestinal irritation. Because it can irritate the esophagus, patients are instructed to take it with a full glass of water and remain upright for at least 30 minutes afterwards. Rare adverse effects include osteonecrosis of the jaw and atypical femur fractures. It was patented in 1978 and approved for medical use in the United States in 1995.
