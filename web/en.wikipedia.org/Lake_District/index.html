Lake District

The Lake District, also known as the Lakes or Lakeland, is a mountainous region and national park in Cumbria, north-west England. Famous for its lakes, forests and fells (mountains), and for its associations with the Lake Poets — William Wordsworth, Samuel Taylor Coleridge and Robert Southey — it is England's most visited national park and was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2017 as a cultural landscape shaped by farming.

The Lake District National Park, established in 1951, covers about 2,362 km² and contains England's highest mountain, Scafell Pike (978 m), and its largest and deepest lakes, Windermere and Wastwater. The terrain was carved by Ice Age glaciers into radial valleys with ribbon lakes. Tourism, hill farming (notably Herdwick sheep) and former mining for slate, graphite and copper shape its economy and landscape.
