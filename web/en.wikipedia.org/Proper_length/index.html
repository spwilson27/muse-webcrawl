Proper length

Proper length or rest length is the length of an object measured in the object's rest frame — the inertial frame in which the object is at rest. It is the maximum length measurable for the object: in any other frame, moving relative to the object along its length, the measured length is shorter, a phenomenon known as Lorentz contraction (or length contraction). Proper length is a Lorentz-invariant characterisation of the object, analogous to proper time, the time measured by a clock moving with the object.

Length contraction

In special relativity, if an object has proper length L0, an observer moving at speed v relative to it along the object's length measures

L = L0 √(1 − v2/c2) = L0/γ,

where c is the speed of light and γ is the Lorentz factor. The contraction applies only along the direction of motion and only becomes appreciable near light speed. Measuring a length in a frame where the object moves requires recording the positions of its endpoints simultaneously in that frame — and because simultaneity is relative, different frames obtain different lengths, even though all agree on the proper length.

Definitions and subtleties

Proper length may be defined formally as the invariant interval-based separation between two events that are the object's endpoints at simultaneous times in its rest frame. For extended or accelerating objects the notion requires care: a rigid body cannot be accelerated without internal stresses (Born rigidity), and the "proper length" of an accelerating object is defined instantaneously in its momentary rest frame. In cosmology, the term also appears in "proper distance" measures that account for the expansion of space.

Examples

The classic illustration is the muon: muons produced in the upper atmosphere reach the ground in numbers explicable either by time dilation of their decay (in Earth's frame) or by contraction of the atmosphere's thickness (in the muon's frame, where the atmosphere's proper length is its thickness at rest). Both descriptions involve the same invariant physics expressed in different frames.
