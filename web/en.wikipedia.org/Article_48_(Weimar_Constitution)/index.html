Article 48 (Weimar Constitution)

Article 48 of the Weimar Constitution (1919) allowed the President of the German Reich to take any measures necessary to restore public security and order, without the prior consent of the Reichstag, including ruling by emergency decree and suspending fundamental civil rights. Its repeated use is widely seen as undermining parliamentary democracy in Germany and facilitating the Nazi rise to power.

Provisions

The article empowered the president, if public security and order were seriously disturbed or endangered, to intervene with armed force and to suspend, in whole or in part, seven articles of the constitution guaranteeing rights such as personal liberty, freedom of expression, and inviolability of the home. The Reichstag could demand revocation of the measures, but decrees took effect immediately.

Use

President Friedrich Ebert used Article 48 more than 130 times, against uprisings of left and right alike. Under President Paul von Hindenburg from 1930, chancellors Brüning, Papen, and Schleicher governed largely by decree as parliamentary majorities collapsed. After Adolf Hitler became chancellor in January 1933, the Reichstag Fire Decree of 28 February 1933, issued under Article 48, suspended civil liberties and was never repealed, forming a legal cornerstone of the Nazi dictatorship.
