Critical phenomena

Critical phenomena
Field: Statistical physics
Concepts: Critical point, critical exponents, universality
Key tool: Renormalization group

Critical phenomena are the collective behavior of physical systems near continuous (second-order) phase transitions. At the critical point, quantities such as the correlation length diverge, fluctuations occur on all length scales, and physical properties follow power laws characterized by critical exponents.

Universality

Remarkably, systems as different as fluids, magnets, and alloys share the same critical exponents if they have the same dimensionality and symmetry — they belong to the same universality class. Kenneth Wilson's renormalization group (1971) explained this by showing how microscopic details become irrelevant at large scales, work that earned the 1982 Nobel Prize in Physics.

Examples

The liquid–gas critical point, the Curie point of a ferromagnet, and the superfluid transition of helium all display critical phenomena, including visible critical opalescence in fluids as density fluctuations scatter light.
