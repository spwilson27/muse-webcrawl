BAE Systems Tempest

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The BAE Systems Tempest is a sixth-generation fighter aircraft concept developed in the United Kingdom, unveiled as a full-scale mock-up at the Farnborough Airshow in July 2018. Initially a British programme led by BAE Systems with Rolls-Royce, Leonardo UK and MBDA ("Team Tempest"), it became the crewed core of the international Global Combat Air Programme (GCAP) announced in December 2022 by the UK, Japan and Italy, with service entry targeted for the mid-2030s.

Development

Tempest emerged from the UK's 2015 Strategic Defence and Security Review and the 2018 Combat Air Strategy, which sought to preserve a sovereign combat-air industry after the Eurofighter Typhoon. Sweden joined collaboration studies in 2019 before the GCAP merger with Japan's F-X programme superseded the separate national effort. A supersonic flying demonstrator was announced in 2023 as the UK's first crewed combat-air demonstrator in four decades.

Design

The design emphasizes low observability, networked sensors, a wearable-cockpit concept with augmented-reality displays, optionally crewed operation, swarming uncrewed loyal-wingman drones, directed-energy weapons, and an adaptive-cycle engine from Rolls-Royce. Systems are intended to be upgradeable through open architectures, addressing the cost growth of previous generations.

Outlook

GCAP aims to deliver the aircraft around 2035 to replace Typhoons in British and Italian service and the Mitsubishi F-2 in Japan, in competition with the Franco-German-Spanish FCAS programme.
