Pixies are an American alternative rock band formed in Boston, Massachusetts, in 1986 by Black Francis (vocals, guitar), Joey Santiago (lead guitar), Kim Deal (bass, vocals), and David Lovera (drums). Though only modestly successful commercially in their first incarnation, the band exerted an outsized influence on the alternative rock boom of the 1990s; Kurt Cobain famously said that in writing "Smells Like Teen Spirit" he was "basically trying to rip off the Pixies".

History

After the eight-song demo known as The Purple Tape, Pixies signed to the British label 4AD and released the mini-album Come On Pilgrim (1987), followed by Surfer Rosa (1988), produced by Steve Albini, whose raw drum sound and the band's trademark loud–quiet–loud dynamics made it a landmark. Doolittle (1989), produced by Gil Norton, brought wider acclaim with songs such as "Debaser", "Here Comes Your Man", "Monkey Gone to Heaven", and "Hey". Bossanova (1990) and Trompe le Monde (1991) followed amid rising tension between Francis and Deal; the band broke up acrimoniously in 1993, announced by Francis in a radio interview the others had not heard.

Reunion and later work

Pixies reunited in 2004 for a hugely successful world tour. Deal departed in 2013; after brief tenures by Kim Shattuck and Paz Lenchantin, bassist Emma Richardson joined in 2024. The reunited band has released Indie Cindy (2014), Head Carrier (2016), Beneath the Eyrie (2019), Doggerel (2022), and The Night the Zombies Came (2024).

Style and influence

Francis's lyrics mingle surrealism, science fiction, Biblical imagery, and Spanish phrases absorbed during a student exchange in Puerto Rico. The band's dynamic contrasts, abrasive guitar textures, and pop hooks shaped Nirvana, Radiohead, Weezer, and much of modern indie rock; "Where Is My Mind?" gained a second life through the film Fight Club (1999).
