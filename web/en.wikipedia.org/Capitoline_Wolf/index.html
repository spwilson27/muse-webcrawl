The Capitoline Wolf (Italian: Lupa Capitolina) is a bronze sculpture depicting a scene from the legend of the founding of Rome: a she-wolf suckling the twin infants Romulus and Remus. It is one of the most famous symbols of Rome and is displayed in the Capitoline Museums.

Description and history

The wolf, 75 cm high and 114 cm long, stands alert with her head turned toward the viewer, her ribs and teats emphasised. The figures of the twins beneath her are in a different, Renaissance style and were added in the late 15th century, traditionally attributed to the Florentine sculptor Antonio del Pollaiuolo.

Dating controversy

For centuries the wolf was believed to be an Etruscan work of the 5th century BC. Restoration work in the 1990s and 2000s, however, revealed casting techniques characteristic of medieval bronze production, and radiocarbon dating of organic residues in the casting core suggested an 11th–12th century AD origin. The matter remains debated, and the museum presents both views.
