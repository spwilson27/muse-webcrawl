Fall of Granada

The fall of Granada on 2 January 1492 marked the end of the Granada War (1482–1492) and of Muslim rule in the Iberian Peninsula. The last Nasrid Emir, Muhammad XII (known as Boabdil), surrendered the city of Granada and the Alhambra palace to the Catholic Monarchs, Ferdinand II of Aragon and Isabella I of Castile, completing the centuries-long Reconquista.

The war

The Granada War began in 1482, exploiting a civil war within the Nasrid dynasty between Emir Abu l-Hasan Ali, his son Boabdil, and Boabdil's uncle al-Zagal. Castilian forces conquered the emirate town by town—Ronda (1485), Málaga (1487), Almería (1489)—before settling into a siege of Granada itself from the purpose-built camp-town of Santa Fe in 1491.

The surrender

Under the Treaty of Granada (25 November 1491), Boabdil surrendered the city in exchange for guarantees of religious freedom for its Muslim population and a small lordship in the Alpujarras. On 2 January 1492 he handed the keys of the Alhambra to Ferdinand and Isabella. Legend holds that, looking back at the city in exile, he wept—"the Moor's last sigh" (el último suspiro del Moro).
