Robert D. Putnam

Robert D. Putnam, political scientist

Robert David Putnam (born January 9, 1941) is an American political scientist, a public policy professor at the Harvard University Kennedy School of Government. He is best known for developing the theory of social capital and for his book Bowling Alone (2000).

Work

Putnam's earlier book Making Democracy Work (1993), a study of regional governments in Italy, argued that civic traditions and networks of trust — social capital — explain why some democratic institutions succeed. In Bowling Alone he documented the collapse of American civic engagement since the 1960s, using the decline of league bowling as an emblem, and blamed television, generational change, suburbanisation and time pressure.

Later books

With Shaylyn Romney Garrett he wrote The Upswing (2020), tracing an inverted-U curve of American economic and social solidarity across the 20th century. His Our Kids (2015) examined the growing opportunity gap for American children from poor families.

Recognition

Putnam has been a member of the National Academy of Sciences, received the Johan Skytte Prize in Political Science (2006), and was awarded the National Humanities Medal in 2012.
