Siad Barre

Mohamed Siad Barre (c. 1919 – 2 January 1995) was a Somali military officer and politician who served as the President of the Somali Democratic Republic from 1969 to 1991. He seized power in a coup d'état following the assassination of President Abdirashid Shermarke and ruled Somalia for over two decades.

Early life and career

Born in Shilavo in the Ogaden region, Barre served in the Italian colonial police and later rose through the ranks of the Somali National Army, becoming its commander in 1965.

Presidency

Barre proclaimed a programme of "scientific socialism", nationalised industry and banks, and promoted a written Somali language using the Latin script. After the disastrous Ogaden War of 1977–78 against Ethiopia, his regime grew increasingly authoritarian and clan-based in its repression, culminating in the bombing of Hargeisa and massacres of the Isaaq clan in the late 1980s.

Downfall and death

Rebel movements drove Barre from Mogadishu in January 1991, precipitating the Somali Civil War. He fled to Kenya and then Nigeria, dying in exile in Lagos in 1995.
