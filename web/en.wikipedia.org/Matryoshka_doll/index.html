Matryoshka doll

Russian nested wooden dolls

A matryoshka doll, also known as a Russian nesting doll, is a set of wooden dolls of decreasing size placed one inside another. The traditional figure is a smiling peasant woman in a sarafan and headscarf, but modern sets depict everything from political leaders to fairy-tale characters. The name derives from Matryona, a once-common Russian female name associated with the archetype of the mother — fitting the dolls' theme of fertility and motherhood.

History

The first matryoshka set was made in 1890 at the Abramtsevo estate workshops near Moscow: turned by the craftsman Vasily Zvyozdochkin and painted by the artist Sergey Malyutin, possibly inspired by Japanese nesting figures of the Seven Lucky Gods. The dolls won a prize at the Paris Exposition Universelle of 1900 and became a signature Russian souvenir, with the town of Semyonov emerging as a major centre of production. Sets may contain from three to several dozen pieces; record sets exceed a hundred dolls, the largest turned on a lathe and the smallest the size of a grain of rice.

Cultural significance

Beyond tourism, the matryoshka has become a general symbol of Russia and a metaphor for nested structure — in computing, politics and storytelling ("matryoshka" is used wherever objects contain smaller versions of themselves).
