Knarr

A knarr is a type of Norse merchant ship used by the Vikings for long sea voyages and bulk cargo transport. Broader and deeper than a longship, it relied primarily on sail rather than oars.

Characteristics

Knarrs were typically about 16 metres long with a beam of around 4.5–5 metres, clinker-built of oak, able to carry up to 20–30 tons. They crossed the North Atlantic to Iceland, Greenland and North America, carrying settlers, livestock and trade goods.

Finds

The best-known surviving example is Skuldelev 1, a knarr built around 1030 and excavated in Roskilde Fjord, Denmark. Replicas have repeated Viking-age Atlantic crossings, demonstrating the type's seaworthiness.
