Georges Perec (7 March 1936 – 3 March 1982) was a French novelist, filmmaker, documentalist and essayist. A member of the Oulipo group of writers who worked with formal constraints, he is regarded as one of the most innovative French authors of the twentieth century. His best-known work, Life: A User's Manual (1978), won the Prix Médicis.

Early life

Perec was born in Paris to Polish Jewish immigrant parents. His father was killed in 1940 while serving in the French army, and his mother was deported and died at Auschwitz in 1943. Perec was evacuated to the French Alps and later raised by an aunt and uncle. These losses — and the erasure of memory and family they entailed — haunt much of his writing, notably the autobiographical W, or the Memory of Childhood (1975).

Career

After military service in a parachute regiment, Perec worked from 1962 as a research archivist in a neurophysiology laboratory, a mundane job that left him time to write. His first novel, Things: A Story of the Sixties (1965), a cool anatomy of a young Parisian couple's consumer desires, won the Prix Renaudot. In 1967 he joined the Oulipo, the group founded by Raymond Queneau and François Le Lionnais devoted to literature under constraint.

Major works

Perec's writing combines formal games with deep feeling. A Void (La Disparition, 1969) is a novel written entirely without the letter "e" — the most common letter in French. Life: A User's Manual (La Vie mode d'emploi, 1978) tells the interlocking stories of the inhabitants of a Paris apartment block, its structure governed by a knight's tour of a chessboard and intricate lists; it is widely considered his masterpiece. Species of Spaces (1974) reflects on the spaces of everyday life, from the page to the city.

Death and legacy

Perec died of lung cancer in 1982, four days short of his forty-sixth birthday. His reputation has grown steadily since his death; his works have been translated worldwide and his explorations of memory, absence and the "infra-ordinary" continue to influence writers and artists.
