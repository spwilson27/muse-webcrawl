Faithless elector

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In the United States Electoral College, a faithless elector is a member of the Electoral College who does not vote for the presidential or vice-presidential candidate for whom they had pledged to vote — that is, they break their pledge and vote for another person, or abstain. Faithless electors are comparatively rare: pledged electors usually have a strong personal loyalty to their party, and on only a handful of occasions has a faithless vote attracted significant attention, most notably in the election of 2016.

Background

The Constitution gives electors the role of formally choosing the president, and Alexander Hamilton argued in The Federalist No. 68 that electors would exercise independent judgement. In modern practice, however, electors are chosen as pledged supporters of a party's candidate. As of recent election cycles, 33 states and the District of Columbia have laws requiring electors to vote as pledged, though in many states the pledge is unenforced or carries only a small penalty.

Legal status

In Ray v. Blair (1952), the Supreme Court upheld pledge requirements but did not settle enforcement. In Chiafalo v. Washington (2020), the Court ruled unanimously that states may enforce an elector's pledge and penalise faithless electors, holding that the Constitution gives states broad power over the appointment of electors. In a companion case, Colorado Department of State v. Baca, the Court reached the same conclusion regarding states that cancel and replace faithless votes.

History

More than 160 faithless votes have been cast over American history, most of them in the 19th century or involving vice-presidential choices, deceased candidates (as in 1872, when Horace Greeley died between election day and the electoral vote), or protest votes. In 2016, seven electoral votes were counted for persons other than the pledged candidates — the most faithless votes in a single modern election — amid an organised campaign to persuade electors to block Donald Trump. No faithless vote has ever changed the outcome of a presidential election.
