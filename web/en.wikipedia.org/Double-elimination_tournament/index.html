Double-elimination tournament

A double-elimination tournament is a type of elimination tournament competition in which a participant ceases to be eligible to win the tournament's championship upon having lost two games or matches. It stands in contrast to a single-elimination tournament, in which only one defeat results in elimination.

Format

One method of arranging a double-elimination tournament is to break the competitors into two sets of brackets: the winners' bracket and the losers' bracket (also called the lower bracket, or redemption bracket). After the first round, the winners proceed into the winners' bracket and the losers proceed into the losers' bracket. The winners' bracket is conducted like a typical single-elimination tournament; a competitor who loses while in the winners' bracket drops into the losers' bracket, while a competitor who loses in the losers' bracket is eliminated altogether.

The overall champion is determined in a final match between the winner of the winners' bracket and the winner of the losers' bracket, sometimes called the grand final. Because the winners' bracket champion may be undefeated while the losers' champion has already lost once, some formats require the losers' champion to win two consecutive final matches (a "bracket reset") for strict fairness; others stage a single final, favouring the undefeated finalist.

Advantages and disadvantages

The format reduces the influence of a single unlucky result, unlucky draw, or bad day, since a strong entrant that loses early can still come back through the losers' bracket. It is widely used in esports, martial arts, softball, pool and fighting games. Its disadvantages are complexity and length: a double-elimination tournament needs roughly twice as many matches as a single-elimination one, and scheduling the losers' bracket can be intricate.
