CategoryCheese-making technique and family of cheeses
OriginItaly (also widespread in the Balkans, Anatolia and Latin America)

Pasta filata (Italian for "spun paste") is a technique in the manufacture of a family of Italian cheeses in which the curd, after acidification, is stretched and kneaded in hot water or whey. The stretching aligns the casein proteins into parallel fibres, giving the finished cheese its characteristic stringy, elastic texture and excellent melting and stretching behaviour when heated — the quality prized on pizza.

Process

Milk is curdled with rennet and the curd is cut and left to acidify (either by starter bacteria or by direct acidification) until it reaches about pH 5.2–5.4, the point at which it becomes plastic in hot water. The curd is then immersed in water or whey at roughly 75–85 °C and worked — traditionally by hand with a wooden paddle — until smooth and glossy, then moulded into balls, braids, logs or pear shapes and cooled in cold water or brine.

Cheeses

The best-known pasta filata cheese is mozzarella, including buffalo mozzarella from Campania and the low-moisture mozzarella used on pizza. Others include provolone (aged and often smoked or sharp), caciocavallo, scamorza, and burrata, whose mozzarella pouch encloses stracciatella and cream. The technique spread widely: string cheese and Oaxaca (quesillo) cheese in the Americas, kaşar in Turkey, and sulguni in Georgia are all made by the same hot-stretching method.
