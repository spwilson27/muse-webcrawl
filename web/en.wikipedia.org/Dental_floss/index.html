Dental floss

Dental floss is a cord of thin filaments used in interdental cleaning to remove food and dental plaque from between teeth or places a toothbrush has difficulty reaching or is unable to reach. Its regular use is recommended as part of oral hygiene to prevent gingivitis and tooth decay in the areas between the teeth.

History

The use of floss-like implements is ancient, but modern dental floss is credited to Levi Spear Parmly, a New Orleans dentist who in 1819 recommended running a waxed silk thread between the teeth. Commercial floss was first manufactured in 1882, and Johnson & Johnson received the first patent for dental floss in 1898. Nylon floss replaced silk during the Second World War, and modern products use materials such as expanded PTFE.

Use

A length of floss is typically wound around the fingers and guided between the teeth with a gentle sawing motion, then curved around each tooth and slid beneath the gumline to disrupt plaque. Variants include waxed and unwaxed floss, dental tape, flavored floss, disposable floss picks, and air- or water-based interdental cleaners.

Effectiveness and guidance

Professional dental associations generally recommend daily interdental cleaning; evidence reviews find that flossing plus brushing reduces gingivitis compared with brushing alone, though the quality of evidence on plaque and caries outcomes has been debated. Flossing technique matters: snapping floss forcefully can injure the gums.
