ALS Ice Bucket Challenge

The ALS Ice Bucket Challenge, sometimes called the ALS Ice Water Challenge, is a viral activity involving the dumping of a bucket of ice water over a person's head, either by another person or self-administered, to promote awareness of the disease amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS, also known as motor neuron disease or Lou Gehrig's disease) and encourage donations to research. It went viral on social media during July and August 2014.

Rules

Within 24 hours of being challenged, participants were to film themselves announcing the challenge, then have a bucket of ice water poured over their heads. They could then nominate up to three other people to take the challenge. Participants could instead choose to make a charitable donation, or do both the challenge and a donation. In the most common version, a participant who declined the water was expected to donate US$100 to the ALS Association.

History and spread

Ice-bucket dares existed earlier as fundraisers for various causes. In mid-2014 the challenge became linked to ALS through golfer Chris Kennedy and Pat Quinn, and it was popularized by Pete Frates, a former Boston College baseball player living with ALS. Videos spread explosively through Facebook and other platforms: celebrities, athletes, politicians, and technology executives took part, including Bill Gates, Mark Zuckerberg, Oprah Winfrey, LeBron James, and former U.S. president George W. Bush. The phrase "Ice Bucket Challenge" became one of the most searched topics of 2014.

Fundraising impact

The ALS Association reported receiving about US$115 million in donations during the summer of 2014 alone, compared with a small fraction of that in the same period the previous year, and the campaign raised well over US$200 million worldwide for ALS organizations. The funds accelerated research; the ALS Association credited Ice Bucket Challenge funding with helping to support the discovery of the gene NEK1 as a contributor to ALS, announced in 2016, along with projects such as Project MinE. The campaign is widely cited as a landmark in viral charitable fundraising, while also drawing criticism about water waste, short-lived engagement, and "slacktivism".

Legacy

The ALS Association attempted annual revivals of the challenge, and in 2015 co-founder Pat Quinn and others promoted an "Every August Until a Cure" campaign. Pat Quinn died of ALS in 2020 and Pete Frates in 2019; both are remembered as the public faces of the phenomenon.
