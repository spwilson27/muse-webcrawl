Permanent Court of Arbitration

The Permanent Court of Arbitration (PCA) is an intergovernmental organisation located in The Hague, Netherlands. Established in 1899 by the first Hague Peace Conference, it provides services for the resolution of disputes arising out of international agreements, including arbitration, conciliation and fact-finding. Despite its name, it is not a court in the traditional sense: it administers arbitrations conducted by tribunals constituted for each case.

History

The PCA was created by the Convention for the Pacific Settlement of International Disputes, adopted at The Hague in 1899 and revised in 1907. It was the first permanent international institution established for the settlement of disputes between states. Since 1913 it has been housed in the Peace Palace, built with funds donated by Andrew Carnegie, which it shares with the International Court of Justice.

Activities

The PCA administers cases involving states, state entities, intergovernmental organisations and private parties. Its docket has grown to include investor–state arbitrations under bilateral investment treaties and cases under the UNCITRAL Arbitration Rules. High-profile cases have included the South China Sea Arbitration (Philippines v. China, award 2016) and inter-state boundary and treaty disputes. Parties select the arbitrators, often from the PCA's panel of members nominated by contracting states.
