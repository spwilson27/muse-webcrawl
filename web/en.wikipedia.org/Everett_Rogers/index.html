Everett Rogers

Everett M. Rogers (1931–2004), author of Diffusion of Innovations (1962).

Everett M. Rogers (March 6, 1931 – October 21, 2004) was an American communication theorist and sociologist, best known for originating the diffusion of innovations theory and for introducing the term early adopter. His book Diffusion of Innovations, first published in 1962 and repeatedly revised, is one of the most cited works in the social sciences.

Life and career

Rogers was born in Carroll, Iowa, and grew up on a farm, an experience that shaped his early research into why farmers adopted (or resisted) new agricultural practices such as hybrid seed corn and weed spray. He earned his bachelor's, master's and doctoral degrees at Iowa State University, completing his PhD in sociology and statistics in 1957. He taught at Ohio State University, Michigan State University, Stanford University and the University of New Mexico, where he was Regents' Professor and helped found the doctoral programme in communication; he also worked extensively on family planning and development communication in Asia, Africa and Latin America, and at the University of Southern California's Annenberg School.

Diffusion of innovations

Rogers synthesised hundreds of studies into a general model of how new ideas and technologies spread through a social system over time. Its best-known elements are the S-shaped adoption curve; the adopter categories — innovators, early adopters, early majority, late majority and laggards; the five attributes of innovations that affect adoption (relative advantage, compatibility, complexity, trialability and observability); and the role of opinion leaders and change agents in a two-step flow of communication. The framework has been applied across public health, marketing, education and technology management.
