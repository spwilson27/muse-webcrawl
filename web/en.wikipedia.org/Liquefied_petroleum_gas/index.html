Liquefied petroleum gas

Type: Fuel gas
Main components: Propane, butane
Also known as: LPG, LP gas, autogas

Liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) is a fuel gas containing a flammable mixture of hydrocarbon gases, specifically propane, n-butane and isobutane. It is stored under moderate pressure as a liquid and vaporises on release. LPG is used as a fuel in heating appliances, cooking equipment, and vehicles (as autogas), and as a propellant and refrigerant.

Production

LPG is produced during the refining of petroleum (crude oil) or extracted from petroleum and natural gas streams as they emerge from the ground. It was first produced in 1910 by Dr. Walter Snelling, and commercial products appeared in 1912.

Uses

LPG serves as a portable fuel for rural heating and cooking where mains gas is unavailable, as a vehicle fuel, in forklifts, buses and taxis, and as a feedstock in the petrochemical industry. In many developing countries it is a primary cooking fuel, marketed in refillable cylinders.

Safety

LPG is heavier than air and can pool in low areas; an odorant such as ethanethiol is added so leaks can be detected by smell.
