Langton's ant

Langton's ant is a two-dimensional universal Turing machine invented by Chris Langton in 1986. An 'ant' moves on a grid of black and white cells, flipping the colour of each cell it visits and turning left or right, producing complex, apparently chaotic behaviour that eventually settles into a repeating 'highway'.

Rules

On a white square the ant turns right, flips the colour and moves forward; on a black square it turns left, flips the colour and moves forward.

Behaviour

From most starting configurations the ant creates chaotic patterns for about 10,000 steps before building a periodic highway of 104 steps. Generalisations with more colours are denoted RL, RLLR and so on.
