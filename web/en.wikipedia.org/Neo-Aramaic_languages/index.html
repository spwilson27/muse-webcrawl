Neo-Aramaic languages

The Neo-Aramaic or Modern Aramaic languages are varieties of Aramaic that evolved from Middle Aramaic by about the 13th century and continue to be spoken as first languages by communities in northern Iraq, northwest Iran, southeast Turkey, northeast Syria, and a worldwide diaspora.

Groups

Four main groups are distinguished: Western Neo-Aramaic, now confined to a few villages near Damascus such as Maaloula; Central Neo-Aramaic (Turoyo and Mlahsô), from the Tur Abdin region of Turkey; Northeastern Neo-Aramaic (NENA), the largest and most diverse group, spoken by Assyrian Christians and by Kurdish Jews; and Neo-Mandaic, spoken by Mandaeans in Iran.

Status

All Neo-Aramaic varieties are endangered. Wars, the 1915 Assyrian genocide (Sayfo), persecution, and mass emigration have scattered speaker communities; many NENA dialects have died within living memory. The languages are written in Syriac scripts, and efforts at documentation and standardization — including the literary variety called Suret or Classical Syriac-based koine — continue in diaspora communities.
