Structural analysis

Structural analysis is a branch of solid mechanics and structural engineering that uses applied mechanics, materials science, and applied mathematics to compute a structure's deformations, internal forces, stresses, support reactions, accelerations, and stability. The results of the analysis are used to verify a structure's fitness for use, often precluding physical tests.

History

Structural analysis developed from Galileo's work on beam strength, through Euler's column formula and the 19th-century methods of graphical statics and slope deflection, to the matrix methods of the mid-20th century that enabled computer analysis of complex frames.

Loads and methods

Analyses consider dead loads, live loads, wind, snow, seismic forces, and thermal effects, combined per building codes. Structures are classified as statically determinate or indeterminate. Classical methods include the method of joints, moment distribution, and Castigliano's theorems; modern practice relies on the finite element method, in which the structure is idealized as an assembly of elements connected at nodes.

Analysis types

Analyses may be linear or nonlinear, static or dynamic, and may address buckling, vibration, and fatigue. See also structural engineering, finite element method, and strength of materials.
