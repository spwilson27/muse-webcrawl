United Nations Headquarters

The headquarters of the United Nations is a complex in the Turtle Bay neighbourhood of Manhattan, New York City, on land overlooking the East River. Built between 1948 and 1952, it houses the principal organs of the UN — the Security Council, the General Assembly, and the Secretariat — and is legally international territory administered by the UN, though subject to a headquarters agreement with the United States. Its 39-storey Secretariat Building, a glass-curtain-wall slab, became an icon of International Style modernism.

History

After the UN's founding in 1945, its first meetings were held in London and at temporary sites around New York, including Lake Success on Long Island. A last-minute donation of $8.5 million by John D. Rockefeller Jr. secured the Turtle Bay site — then a district of slaughterhouses — in December 1946. The design was developed by an international Board of Design Consultants under Wallace Harrison, in which Le Corbusier and Oscar Niemeyer played central roles; the final scheme largely followed Niemeyer's layout with the Secretariat slab from Le Corbusier's proposals. The cornerstone was laid in 1949, the Secretariat opened in 1951, and the complex was completed in 1952.

The complex

The complex comprises the Secretariat Building, the General Assembly Building (containing the General Assembly Hall, seating over 1,800), the Conference Building (home to the Security Council Chamber, a gift from Norway designed by Arnstein Arneberg), and the Dag Hammarskjöld Library. The grounds display artworks donated by member states, including the Non-Violence ("knotted gun") sculpture by Carl Fredrik Reuterswärd and a segment of the Berlin Wall. A row of flagpoles along First Avenue flies the flags of all member states in English alphabetical order.

Renovation

By the 2000s the buildings lacked sprinklers and contained asbestos; the Capital Master Plan (2008–2014), costing roughly $2 billion, renovated the complex, temporarily relocating staff and the General Assembly while preserving the historic interiors, including the Security Council Chamber.
