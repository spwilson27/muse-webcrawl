The Salve Regina (Latin for "Hail, Queen"), also known as the Hail, Holy Queen, is a Marian hymn and one of the four seasonal antiphons of the Blessed Virgin Mary, traditionally sung at the end of Compline (Night Prayer) in the Roman Catholic Liturgy of the Hours, and also at the conclusion of the Rosary.

Text and origins

The hymn begins "Salve, Regina, mater misericordiae" — "Hail, Queen, mother of mercy" — addressing Mary as queen, mother of mercy, and "our life, our sweetness, and our hope", and describes believers as "poor banished children of Eve" mourning "in this valley of tears" who await her advocacy. Its authorship is uncertain; tradition most often attributes it to the eleventh-century monk Hermann of Reichenau, though Adhemar of Le Puy and Bernard of Clairvaux have also been proposed. The closing invocation "O clemens, O pia, O dulcis Virgo Maria" is popularly credited to Bernard.

Use and settings

From the thirteenth century the antiphon became a fixture of confraternities, processions, and sailors' devotions — Columbus's crews reportedly sang it nightly — and in the traditional Roman Rite it is sung from Trinity Sunday until the Advent season. Hundreds of polyphonic settings survive from composers including Josquin, Palestrina, Victoria, and Lassus, and later settings by Pergolesi, Haydn, Schubert, and Poulenc. The hymn gives its name to Salve Regina University in Newport, Rhode Island, and its first two words open the English para-liturgical hymn "Hail, Holy Queen Enthroned Above".
