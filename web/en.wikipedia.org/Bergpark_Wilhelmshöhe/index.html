Bergpark Wilhelmshöhe

Bergpark Wilhelmshöhe is a landscape park in Kassel, Germany. Covering 2.4 square kilometres, it is the largest European hillside park and was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2013.

History

Construction began in 1696 under Landgrave Charles I of Hesse-Kassel, inspired by Italian Baroque gardens. Its centrepiece, the giant Hercules statue atop an octagonal pyramid, was completed in 1717.

Water features

A gravity-fed system of reservoirs and channels, built over 300 years ago and still functioning without pumps, sends water cascading down the Grand Cascade, aqueduct and waterfalls to the lake below Schloss Wilhelmshöhe during the famous Wasserspiele displays from May to October.

Schloss Wilhelmshöhe

The neoclassical palace (1786–1798) houses the Gemäldegalerie Alte Meister, with works by Rembrandt, Rubens and Dürer, and the park also contains the romantic Löwenburg castle.
