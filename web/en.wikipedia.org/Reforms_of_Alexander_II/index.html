Reforms of Alexander II

The reforms of Alexander II were a series of major liberalising reforms carried out in the Russian Empire during the reign of Emperor Alexander II (1855–1881), prompted in part by defeat in the Crimean War, which exposed Russia's backwardness.

The centrepiece was the Emancipation Reform of 1861, which abolished serfdom and freed over 20 million serfs, though redemption payments burdened peasants for decades. Further reforms created elected local councils (zemstvos, 1864), reformed the judiciary with jury trials and independent judges (1864), modernised municipal government (1870), eased censorship and university statutes, and under Dmitry Milyutin introduced universal military conscription (1874) and reorganised the army.

Alexander was assassinated in 1881 on the day he considered a proposal for a consultative assembly; his successors partly rolled back the reforms. See also Alexander II and Emancipation reform of 1861.
