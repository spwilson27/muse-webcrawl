Mount Gilboa

LocationNorthern Israel, above the Jezreel Valley

Mount Gilboa (Hebrew: הר הגלבוע) is a mountain range in northern Israel overlooking the Jezreel Valley to the north and the Jordan Valley to the east. The ridge extends about 18 km (11 mi) from northwest to southeast, with its highest point, Mount Saul, reaching 508 m (1,667 ft).

Biblical associations

In the Hebrew Bible, Mount Gilboa is the site of the battle in which King Saul and his sons, including Jonathan, were defeated and killed by the Philistines (1 Samuel 31). Upon hearing the news, David lamented in the "Song of the Bow" (2 Samuel 1), cursing the mountain: "Ye mountains of Gilboa, let there be no dew, neither let there be rain upon you." The ridge was also associated with Gideon, who camped at the spring of Harod at its foot before his battle with the Midianites.

Nature and modern use

The mountain is noted for the Gilboa iris (Iris haynei), a large purple wildflower protected in the Gilboa Iris Nature Reserve. Much of the ridge is forested with planted pine, and kibbutzim and moshavim, including Ma'ale Gilboa, sit on its slopes. Wind turbines on the ridge generate electricity.
