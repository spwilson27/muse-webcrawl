Thieboudienne

Alternative namesCeebu jën, tieb, benchin
TypeRice dish
Place of originSenegal
Main ingredientsFish, broken rice, tomato, vegetables

Thieboudienne (Wolof: ceebu jën) is a traditional dish from Senegal, widely regarded as the country's national dish. It consists of fish — traditionally grouper or another firm white fish — cooked with broken rice in a rich tomato sauce, accompanied by vegetables such as carrots, cabbage, cassava, eggplant, okra and turnips. Tamarind and fermented fish products are often used to deepen the flavour.

History

The dish is associated with the port city of Saint-Louis in the nineteenth century. Legend attributes its creation to a cook named Penda Mbaye. Variants developed as the dish spread through West Africa, giving rise to related rice dishes across the region, and it is often cited as an ancestor of West African jollof rice. In 2021, thieboudienne was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Preparation

The fish is commonly stuffed with a parsley, garlic and chilli mixture (roof), fried or braised, and served over the tomato-flavoured broken rice, with the vegetables arranged around it. The dish is traditionally eaten communally from a large shared platter.
