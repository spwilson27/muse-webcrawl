Road

A road in a rural landscape

A road is a thoroughfare, route, or way on land between two places that has been surfaced or otherwise improved to allow travel by foot or by some form of conveyance, including a motor vehicle, cart, bicycle, or horse.

Roads consist of one or two roadways (British English: carriageways), each with one or more lanes and any associated sidewalks (British English: pavement) and road verges. Roads that are available for use by the public may be referred to as public roads or highways.

History

The first indications of constructed roads date from about 4000 BC and consist of stone-paved streets at Ur in modern-day Iraq and timber roads preserved in a swamp in Glastonbury, England. The builders of the late 18th-century roads in Britain were John Metcalf, Thomas Telford and John Loudon McAdam. Professional designers started in the early 20th century.

The Roman Empire built an extensive network of Roman roads, some of which survive today. In the modern era, the motorway or freeway, with controlled access and grade-separated junctions, became the highest-capacity form of road.

Design

Road design is part of highway engineering. Structural road design is concerned with ensuring the pavement is strong and durable enough to carry the expected traffic loads over its design life, while geometric design controls horizontal and vertical alignment, sight distances and junction layout.

Types

Roads are commonly classified by function and capacity, from local residential streets through collector roads and arterials to freeways. Other forms include dirt roads, gravel roads, toll roads and scenic routes.

Safety

Road traffic safety is a major public-health concern; around 1.35 million people die each year in road traffic crashes worldwide, according to the World Health Organization.
