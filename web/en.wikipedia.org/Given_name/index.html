Given name

A given name (also known as a forename or first name) is the part of a personal name that identifies a person, potentially with a middle name as well, and differentiates them from other members of a group (typically a family or clan) who have a common surname. The term "given name" refers to a name usually bestowed at or close to the time of birth, usually by the parents of the newborn.

In most Western cultures, the given name precedes the family name (surname), an arrangement called the Western name order; in many East Asian and some European cultures, the family name comes first. A person may have multiple given names, of which those after the first are often called middle names. Given names may be chosen to honour relatives or saints, for their meaning, or according to fashion, and in many jurisdictions are subject to legal restrictions.

History and customs

Ancient naming systems varied widely: Romans used a praenomen, nomen, and cognomen; many early medieval Europeans had a single name, with bynames added for disambiguation that later hardened into hereditary surnames. In Christian Europe, baptismal names drawn from the calendar of saints predominated for centuries; in Chinese culture, generation names and given names traditionally encode lineage and aspiration; in many Islamic cultures, names expressing religious devotion are common.

Legal aspects and change

Most legal systems allow adults to change their given names by a formal process, and some countries maintain approved-name lists or reject names deemed harmful to a child. Research in social psychology has examined how given names may affect perception, hiring, and other outcomes.
