Five-number summary

The five-number summary is a set of descriptive statistics that provides information about a dataset. It consists of five values: the sample minimum, the first (lower) quartile, the median, the third (upper) quartile, and the sample maximum.

Purpose

Together the five numbers describe the spread and centre of a distribution without assuming any particular statistical model. The difference between the maximum and minimum gives the range, and the difference between the third and first quartiles gives the interquartile range, a robust measure of variability.

Box plots

The five-number summary is the basis of the box plot (box-and-whisker plot), popularised by John Tukey in his work on exploratory data analysis: a box spans the quartiles with a line at the median, and whiskers extend to the extremes (or to adjacent values within 1.5 times the interquartile range).
