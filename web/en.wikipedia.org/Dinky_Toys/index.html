Dinky Toys

Dinky Toys was a brand of die-cast zamac miniature vehicles produced by Meccano Ltd of Liverpool, England, from 1934 until 1979. Originally launched as accessories for Hornby model railways under the name "Modelled Miniatures", they became one of the most popular ranges of toy cars, buses, trucks, aircraft, and military vehicles in the world.

History

The range expanded rapidly in the 1930s, with production also beginning at Meccano's French factory. Output ceased during the Second World War and resumed in 1945. In the 1950s competition from the company's own Corgi Toys (launched 1956 by Mettoy) and later from Mattel's Hot Wheels, with their low-friction wheels, eroded Dinky's position; features such as opening parts, "Speedwheels", and action kits were introduced in response. Meccano Ltd went into receivership in 1964 and was taken over by Lines Bros (Tri-ang), and the Liverpool factory at Binns Road closed in 1979, ending British production.

Legacy

Pre-war and early post-war Dinky models are highly collectible, and the brand name has been revived several times for collector ranges, including by Mattel and later by De Agostini partworks.
