Deformation (engineering)

In engineering, deformation refers to the change in size or shape of an object caused by applied forces, temperature changes, or other effects. Deformation is described quantitatively by strain, the ratio of displacement between particles in a material body to a reference length.

Types of deformation

Elastic deformation is reversible: the object returns to its original shape when the load is removed, as described by Hooke's law for small strains. Plastic deformation is permanent, occurring when stress exceeds the material's yield strength and its internal structure rearranges. Other modes include creep (slow deformation under sustained load at high temperature), fatigue under cyclic loading, and fracture, the extreme case in which the material separates.

Analysis

Engineers plot stress–strain curves from tensile tests to characterise stiffness (Young's modulus), yield and ultimate strength, ductility and toughness. Designing against unwanted deformation — excessive deflection of beams, buckling of columns, warping of machined parts — is a core task in structural and mechanical engineering, and modern practice relies heavily on finite element analysis.
