Alfredo Stéfano Di Stéfano Laulhé (4 July 1926 – 7 July 2014) was an Argentine and Spanish professional footballer and coach. Nicknamed "Saeta Rubia" ("Blond Arrow"), he is widely regarded as one of the greatest footballers of all time. Di Stéfano was the maker of one of the greatest teams of all time, the Real Madrid team of the late 1950s and early 1960s, which dominated Spanish and European football.

Along with Francisco Gento and Miguel Muñoz, Di Stéfano was one of only three players to play a part in all five European Cup victories for Real Madrid between 1956 and 1960, scoring goals in each of the five finals. He played international football mostly for Spain after moving to Madrid, but also played for Argentina and Colombia.

Early career

Di Stéfano was born in the Barracas neighbourhood of Buenos Aires to a family of Italian immigrants. He began his career at River Plate in 1945, winning the Argentine Primera División in 1947. During the 1949 Argentine footballers' strike, he moved to Colombia to play for Millonarios of Bogotá, where he won four league titles and formed part of the celebrated "Ballet Azul" team. His performances in a 1952 friendly tournament in Madrid brought him to the attention of Spanish clubs.

Real Madrid

After a complex transfer dispute between Real Madrid and Barcelona, Di Stéfano joined Real Madrid in 1953. He transformed the club, which had not won the Spanish league in two decades, into the dominant force in European football. In eleven seasons with Madrid he won eight La Liga titles and five consecutive European Cups (1956–1960), scoring in every final — including a hat-trick in the famous 7–3 victory over Eintracht Frankfurt in the 1960 final at Hampden Park. He also won the Ballon d'Or in 1957 and 1959. He scored 308 goals in 396 competitive games for the club.

International career

Di Stéfano won the 1947 South American Championship with Argentina. After becoming a Spanish citizen, he played 31 times for Spain, scoring 23 goals, but never played in a World Cup finals tournament; injuries and Spain's failure to qualify kept him from the biggest stage. He retired in 1966 after two seasons with Espanyol.

Managerial career and later life

As a manager, Di Stéfano won league titles with Boca Juniors and River Plate, and led Valencia to the La Liga title in 1971 and the European Cup Winners' Cup in 1980. In 2000 he was named honorary president of Real Madrid, and a stadium at the club's training ground bears his name. He died in Madrid on 7 July 2014, three days after his 88th birthday.
