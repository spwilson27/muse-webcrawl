3

3 (three) is a number, numeral, and digit. It is the natural number following 2 and preceding 4, and is the smallest odd prime number. It has deep cultural and religious significance in many traditions, and appears throughout mathematics, science, and everyday life.

In mathematics

Three is the first odd prime and the second smallest prime overall. It is a triangular number, a Fibonacci number, and the only prime one less than a perfect square. A triangle, the simplest polygon, has three sides; three non-collinear points determine a plane and a circle. The number is the dimension of ordinary physical space, and the "three-body problem" in mechanics is a classic example of a system with chaotic behaviour. In base 10, a number is divisible by 3 if the sum of its digits is divisible by 3.

In science

Lithium is the chemical element with atomic number 3. Human colour vision is trichromatic, based on three types of cone cells. There are three states of matter in everyday experience (solid, liquid, gas), three generations of fundamental fermions in particle physics, and three laws of motion formulated by Isaac Newton.

In culture and religion

Many religions feature divine triads or trinities, including the Christian Holy Trinity and the Hindu Trimurti. Folklore and storytelling make extensive use of threes: three wishes, three brothers, and the "rule of three" in rhetoric and comedy, whereby a pattern of two items is broken or completed by a third.
