Alan David Baddeley (born 23 March 1934) is a British psychologist known for his work on working memory, particularly his multiple-component model developed with Graham Hitch in 1974.

Career

Baddeley studied at University College London and Princeton University. He directed the Medical Research Council's Applied Psychology Unit in Cambridge from 1974 to 1997, and later worked at the University of Bristol and the University of York.

Working memory model

Baddeley's model proposed that short-term memory consists of multiple components: a central executive controlling two subordinate systems — the phonological loop, which handles verbal information, and the visuospatial sketchpad, which handles visual and spatial information. In 2000 he added a fourth component, the episodic buffer.

Honours

Baddeley was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society and appointed CBE. His model remains one of the most influential frameworks in the psychology of memory.
