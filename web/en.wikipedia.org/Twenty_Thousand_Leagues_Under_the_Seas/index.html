Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas

AuthorJules Verne
Original titleVingt mille lieues sous les mers
Published1870
GenreScience fiction, adventure novel

Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas (French: Vingt mille lieues sous les mers) is a classic science fiction adventure novel by French writer Jules Verne, first published in 1870 as part of his Voyages extraordinaires series. The title refers to the distance travelled under the seas during the voyage — twenty thousand leagues (about 80,000 kilometres) — not to depth, since the maximum depth reached is far less.

Plot

In 1866, ships report attacks by an enormous sea creature. Professor Pierre Aronnax, his servant Conseil, and the Canadian harpooner Ned Land join an expedition aboard the USS Abraham Lincoln to hunt it. Thrown overboard in a collision, they discover the "monster" is in fact a submarine, the Nautilus, commanded by the mysterious Captain Nemo. Held as guests who may never leave, they voyage through the world's oceans — under the polar ice, to Atlantis-like ruins, pearl fisheries and a battle with giant squid — before escaping in the maelstrom off Norway.

Influence

The novel anticipated later submarine development and remains one of the most translated and adapted works of science fiction, including the celebrated 1954 Disney film 20,000 Leagues Under the Sea. Captain Nemo reappears in Verne's The Mysterious Island.
