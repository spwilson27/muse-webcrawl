Marc Randolph

Marc Randolph, co-founder of Netflix
Born:April 29, 1958, Chappaqua, New York
Education:Hamilton College (geology)
Known for:Co-founding Netflix; its first CEO

Marc Bernays Randolph (born April 29, 1958) is an American tech entrepreneur, advisor, and speaker. He is the co-founder and first CEO of Netflix, the streaming and former DVD-by-mail company.

Early life and career

Randolph grew up in Chappaqua, New York. He is the great-grandnephew of psychoanalysis pioneer Sigmund Freud and the nephew of public relations pioneer Edward Bernays, a lineage he has credited for his interest in persuasion and marketing. After graduating from Hamilton College with a degree in geology, he worked in direct-mail and magazine publishing, founded the U.S. edition of Macworld and the mail-order firms MacWarehouse and MicroWarehouse, and held marketing roles at Borland International. He was a co-founder of the software testing company Integrity QA, which was acquired by Pure Atria, where he worked with Reed Hastings.

Netflix

In 1997, after Pure Atria was acquired by Rational Software, Randolph and Hastings co-founded Netflix in Scotts Valley, California, as a DVD-rental-by-mail service built around Randolph's e-commerce and direct-marketing experience. Randolph served as the company's first CEO, chose its name, designed its early user interface and branding, and led it through its launch on April 14, 1998. He handed the CEO role to Hastings in 1999, remained as a producer/executive and board member through the company's 2002 IPO, and left in 2004.

Later work

Randolph has since served as a mentor and advisor to startups, including as a longtime mentor at the accelerator MiddCORE and on the boards of companies and environmental groups, including 1% for the Planet. His 2019 memoir That Will Never Work: The Birth of Netflix and the Amazing Life of an Idea recounts the company's founding, and he hosts a podcast of the same name.
