Letter (paper size)

Letter is a paper size standard defined by the American National Standards Institute, measuring 8.5 by 11 inches (215.9 by 279.4 mm). It is the most common paper size for office and academic use in the United States, Canada and several other countries, in contrast to the international A4 standard used in most of the world.

Dimensions: 8.5 × 11 in (216 × 279 mm)
Standard: ANSI/ASME Y14.1
Used in: US, Canada, Mexico, Philippines and others

History

The size's origins are obscure, possibly deriving from the dimensions of hand-moulded paper sheets. It was adopted by the US federal government in the early 20th century and standardised under Ronald Reagan's administration in the 1980s, when ANSI formalised it as part of the Y14.1 series.

Comparison with A4

Letter is wider and shorter than A4 (210 × 297 mm). The mismatch causes occasional printing and filing inconveniences internationally; most modern printers and software support both. Related North American sizes include Legal (8.5 × 14 in) and Tabloid/Ledger (11 × 17 in).
