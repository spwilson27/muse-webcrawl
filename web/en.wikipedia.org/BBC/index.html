The British Broadcasting Corporation (BBC) is a British public service broadcaster headquartered at Broadcasting House in London. Originally established under a royal charter as the British Broadcasting Company in 1922, it became a corporation in 1927. It is the world's oldest national broadcaster and one of the largest by number of employees, with over 20,000 staff.

The BBC's work is funded principally by an annual television licence fee charged to all British households, companies, and organisations using equipment to receive or record live television broadcasts or to use the BBC's streaming service, iPlayer. It operates numerous television channels and radio networks in the United Kingdom, plus the BBC World Service, which broadcasts in more than 40 languages internationally.

History

The British Broadcasting Company Ltd was formed by a group of wireless manufacturers to provide experimental radio broadcasts. John Reith, its first general manager, defined its mission to "inform, educate and entertain". Granted a royal charter in 1927, the BBC launched the world's first regular television service in 1936 and suspended it during the Second World War, when its radio broadcasts became a vital source of news for occupied Europe. Colour television arrived in 1967, and the BBC expanded into digital and online services from the 1990s, launching BBC Online and later iPlayer (2007).

Services

Major services include the flagship television channels BBC One and BBC Two, the news channel BBC News, children's channels CBBC and CBeebies, national radio networks Radio 1 through Radio 5 Live, and dozens of local radio stations. The World Service broadcasts to a global audience estimated in the hundreds of millions.

Governance and impartiality

The corporation operates under a royal charter renewed roughly every ten years and is regulated by Ofcom. Its editorial guidelines require due impartiality, a principle that has drawn both praise and controversy throughout its history; notable programme controversies have concerned coverage of wars, elections, and public figures.
