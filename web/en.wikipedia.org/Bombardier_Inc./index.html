Bombardier Inc.

Industry: Aerospace

Founded: 1942, in Valcourt, Quebec

Founder: Joseph-Armand Bombardier

Headquarters: Montreal, Quebec, Canada

Bombardier Inc. is a Canadian multinational manufacturer of business jets headquartered in Montreal, Quebec. Founded by Joseph-Armand Bombardier in 1942 to sell the snowmobiles he had invented, the company grew into a diversified transportation conglomerate building aircraft, rail equipment, and recreational products before refocusing on business aviation in the early 2020s.

History

Bombardier built his first snowmobile, the B7, in 1937, and incorporated L'Auto-Neige Bombardier Limitée in 1942. The company's tracked vehicles found markets in logging, mining, and military service. After the founder's death in 1964, his son-in-law Laurent Beaudoin expanded the firm through acquisitions, buying a stake in the Austrian firm Rotax in 1970 and entering the rail business in 1974 with a contract for Montreal subway cars.

Bombardier entered aviation in 1986 by purchasing the government-owned Canadair, maker of the Challenger business jet, and later acquired Short Brothers, Learjet, and de Havilland Canada. Its Bombardier Transportation division became the world's largest rail equipment manufacturer. In 2003 the recreational products division was spun off as BRP.

Restructuring

The costly development of the C Series airliner brought the company to the brink of bankruptcy in 2015. Bombardier sold a controlling stake in the C Series program to Airbus in 2017; the aircraft was rebranded the Airbus A220. Between 2019 and 2021 Bombardier sold its remaining commercial aviation lines and its transportation division, the latter to Alstom, emerging as a pure-play business jet manufacturer built around the Global and Challenger families.

Products

The company's flagship is the ultra long-range Global 7500, joined by the Global 5500/6500 and the Challenger 3500. Its former rail division is described at Bombardier Transportation.
