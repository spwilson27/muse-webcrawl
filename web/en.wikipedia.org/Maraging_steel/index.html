Maraging steel

Type: Low-carbon ultra-high-strength steel alloy
Key elements: Iron, ~18% nickel, cobalt, molybdenum, titanium
Strength: Up to ~2,400 MPa tensile
Developed: 1950s–1960s

Maraging steels are a family of steels known for their superior strength and toughness without losing ductility. The name combines "martensitic" and "aging": the alloys are hardened not by carbon, of which they contain very little, but by precipitation of intermetallic compounds during an aging heat treatment of a soft nickel-rich martensite.

Composition and properties

Classic grades contain about 18% nickel with cobalt, molybdenum and titanium additions. In the annealed state the material is soft and readily machined or welded; aging at around 480 °C precipitates nickel–titanium and related compounds, raising tensile strengths to 1,400–2,400 MPa while retaining good fracture toughness and dimensional stability — properties rare in combination.

Applications

Maraging steels are used for rocket motor cases, aircraft landing gear, tooling and die-casting dies, and high-performance shafts. They are famously the only steel permitted for fencing foil and épée blades at top competition levels because they break more safely than carbon steels. Their use in centrifuges for uranium enrichment has also made certain grades subject to export controls.
