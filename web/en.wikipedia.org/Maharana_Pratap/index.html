Maharana Pratap

Maharana Pratap (9 May 1540 – 19 January 1597), also known as Pratap Singh I, was a king of Mewar, a kingdom in north-western India in the present-day state of Rajasthan. He is celebrated as a folk hero for his prolonged resistance to the expansionism of the Mughal emperor Akbar, at a time when most other Rajput rulers had accepted Mughal suzerainty.

Life and reign

Pratap succeeded his father, Udai Singh II, in 1572. Mewar's capital, Chittorgarh, had fallen to Akbar in 1568. Pratap refused to submit personally to the Mughal court, and at the Battle of Haldighati in 1576 his forces fought the Mughal army led by Man Singh I of Amber. Though the battle was inconclusive or a Mughal tactical success, Pratap escaped — aided, in legend, by his horse Chetak — and continued guerrilla warfare from the Aravalli hills.

Recovery and legacy

By the mid-1580s Pratap had recovered much of Mewar except Chittorgarh and Mandalgarh, establishing a capital at Chavand. His son Amar Singh I continued the struggle until a settlement with Jahangir in 1615. Maharana Pratap remains an icon of Rajput valour and Indian resistance; his anniversaries are publicly celebrated, and statues of him on Chetak stand in many Indian cities.
