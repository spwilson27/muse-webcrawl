Burr–Hamilton duel
Date: July 11, 1804
Location: Weehawken, New Jersey
Participants: Aaron Burr, Alexander Hamilton
Outcome: Hamilton fatally wounded

The Burr–Hamilton duel took place in Weehawken, New Jersey, on July 11, 1804, between Aaron Burr, the sitting Vice President of the United States, and Alexander Hamilton, the former Secretary of the Treasury. Hamilton was mortally wounded and died the following day.

Background

Burr and Hamilton had a long-standing political and personal rivalry. Burr blamed Hamilton for his defeat in the 1804 New York gubernatorial election and demanded satisfaction over remarks Hamilton had allegedly made impugning his honour. Negotiations failed, and a duel — illegal in both New York and New Jersey — was arranged.

The duel

The parties rowed across the Hudson River at dawn. Accounts of the exchange differ: Hamilton's shot went wide (some believe deliberately, into a tree), while Burr's shot struck Hamilton in the abdomen. Hamilton died about 31 hours later in New York City.

Aftermath

Burr was charged with murder in two states but never tried; he finished his term as vice president, his political career ruined. The duel became a symbol of the violence of early American honour culture and feature prominently in the musical Hamilton.
