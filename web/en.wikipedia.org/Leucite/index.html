Leucite

Leucite is a rock-forming mineral of the feldspathoid group, composed of potassium and aluminium tectosilicate, KAlSi2O6. It forms in potassium-rich, silica-poor volcanic rocks and is recognised by its whitish, rounded, trapezohedral crystals.

Properties and formation

Leucite crystallises from lavas that are rich in potassium but deficient in silica, where feldspar cannot form. At high temperatures it is cubic (isometric); on cooling it becomes tetragonal, often causing the crystals to show fine twinning. It is unstable at depth and alters readily to pseudoleucite, a mixture of nepheline and feldspar.

Occurrences

Classic localities include the lavas of Mount Vesuvius and the Roman volcanic province in Italy, where leucite-rich rocks (leucitites and phonolites) are widespread. Leucite has minor uses as a source of potash and alumina and in dental porcelain.
