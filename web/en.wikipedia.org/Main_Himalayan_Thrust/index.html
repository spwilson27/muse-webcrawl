Main Himalayan Thrust

The Main Himalayan Thrust (MHT) is a major low-angle thrust fault (décollement) beneath the Himalaya. It forms the plate boundary along which the Indian Plate underthrusts beneath the Eurasian Plate, accommodating much of the ongoing convergence between the two plates.

Geometry

The fault dips gently to the north beneath the mountain range. Near the front of the range it lies at shallow depth, and its surface trace is marked by the Main Frontal Thrust. Down-dip it flattens into a mid-crustal ramp beneath the Higher Himalaya. Aseismic creep on the deeper portions loads the locked shallower segment.

Seismic hazard

Great earthquakes occur when the locked portion of the MHT ruptures. Historical and paleoseismic records show large events along different segments of the arc, including the 1934 Bihar–Nepal and 2015 Gorkha earthquakes in Nepal. Because strain continues to accumulate, the fault is considered a major source of seismic hazard for the densely populated regions of northern South Asia.

Related structures

Splay faults branching from the MHT include the Main Boundary Thrust and the Main Central Thrust, which bound major lithotectonic units of the Himalayan orogen.
