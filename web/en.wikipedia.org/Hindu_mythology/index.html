Hindu mythology

Hindu mythology is the body of myths and sacred narratives found in Hindu texts such as the Vedas, the Itihasas (the Ramayana and the Mahabharata), and the Puranas. These stories explain the origins of the world, the lives and deeds of gods, goddesses, heroes and mythological creatures, and the cyclical rise and fall of the ages.

Sources

The earliest layer of mythology appears in the Rigveda, whose hymns address deities such as Indra, Agni, Varuna, and Surya. The two great Sanskrit epics — Valmiki's Ramayana, telling of Prince Rama's exile and his battle with the demon-king Ravana, and the Mahabharata, centred on the Kurukshetra War between the Pandavas and Kauravas — form the narrative core of later mythology. The Puranas, composed over many centuries, systematised the genealogies of gods and kings and the myths of Vishnu, Shiva, and the goddess Devi.

Principal deities and themes

The Trimurti of Brahma, Vishnu, and Shiva frame the cosmic functions of creation, preservation, and destruction. Vishnu's ten avatars (dashavatara), including Rama and Krishna, descend to restore dharma. Goddesses such as Lakshmi, Saraswati, Parvati, and Durga embody wealth, learning, devotion, and martial power. Recurring themes include the churning of the ocean of milk, the descent of the Ganges, and the struggles between devas and asuras.
