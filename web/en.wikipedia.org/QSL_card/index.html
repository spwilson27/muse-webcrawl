QSL card

A QSL card is a written confirmation of either a two-way radiocommunication between two amateur or citizens band stations or a one-way reception of a signal from an AM radio, FM radio, television, or shortwave broadcasting station. The name comes from the Q code "QSL", meaning "I confirm receipt of your transmission".

Use in amateur radio

Amateur radio operators traditionally exchange QSL cards to confirm contacts ("QSOs"); a card typically records the call signs, date and time (in UTC), frequency or band, mode, and signal report. Cards are prized as proof of contacts for awards such as DXCC, which requires confirmed contact with at least 100 geographic entities. Cards may be sent directly, through national QSL bureaus, or electronically via systems such as ARRL's Logbook of the World and eQSL, though decorated paper cards remain popular collectors' items.

Shortwave listening

Shortwave listeners send reception reports to international broadcasters and receive QSL cards verifying their reports — a traditional collectible documenting stations heard.
