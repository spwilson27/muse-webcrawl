Degania Alef

Degania Alef is a kibbutz in northern Israel, near the southern shore of the Sea of Galilee. Founded in 1910 by pioneers of the Second Aliyah on land purchased by the Jewish National Fund, it is widely regarded as the first kibbutz — the original model for Israel's communal settlements — and is sometimes called "the mother of the kibbutzim".

History

The founding group, influenced by socialist Zionism and thinkers such as A. D. Gordon, established a collective farm at Umm Juni, working the land communally and sharing income equally. In 1920 a disagreement led part of the membership to found neighbouring Degania Bet. During the 1948 Arab–Israeli War, Degania's defenders halted a Syrian armoured advance in May 1948, an action commemorated by a Syrian tank preserved at the kibbutz entrance.

Today

Like many kibbutzim, Degania Alef has undergone privatisation reforms since the early 2000s, introducing differential wages while retaining collective services. Its economy combines agriculture with industry, including the Toolgal diamond-tool factory, and tourism.
