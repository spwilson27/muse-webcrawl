Kingdom of Buganda

Buganda is a traditional kingdom in present-day Uganda, the largest of the country's traditional kingdoms, occupying the Central Region including the capital Kampala. Its people are the Baganda, its language Luganda, and its king is the Kabaka.

History

Buganda grew from a small chiefdom into a powerful, centralised kingdom by the 18th and 19th centuries, expanding around the northern shores of Lake Victoria. Kabakas such as Mutesa I received European explorers and missionaries in the 19th century. Buganda became the core of the Uganda Protectorate under British rule, and its special status shaped Ugandan politics after independence in 1962. Kingdoms were abolished in 1967; Buganda was restored as a cultural institution in 1993 under Kabaka Ronald Muwenda Mutebi II.

Government and culture

The kingdom is centred at Mengo in Kampala. Its parliament is the Lukiiko, and clan organisation remains central to Baganda identity. The Kasubi Tombs, burial site of four kabakas, are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
