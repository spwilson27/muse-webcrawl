Top-level domain

A top-level domain (TLD) is one of the domains at the highest level in the hierarchical Domain Name System of the Internet, appearing as the final label of a fully qualified domain name — for example, .com in example.com.

Types

TLDs are administered under the authority of ICANN and the IANA root zone. They comprise generic TLDs (gTLDs) such as .com, .org, .net and hundreds of newer strings like .app and .museum; country-code TLDs (ccTLDs) such as .uk, .de and .jp, based on ISO 3166 codes; sponsored TLDs such as .gov and .edu; and the special infrastructure domain .arpa. Some ccTLDs are marketed generically, as with Tuvalu's .tv and Colombia's .co.

History

The original generic domains were defined in 1985. A major expansion programme launched in 2012 added more than a thousand new gTLDs, amid debate over trademark protection and speculation in domain names.
