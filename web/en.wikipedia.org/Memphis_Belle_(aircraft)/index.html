The Memphis Belle is a Boeing B-17F Flying Fortress heavy bomber that served with the United States Army Air Forces in the Second World War. Flying from RAF Bassingbourn in England with the 91st Bombardment Group, it became one of the first USAAF bombers to complete 25 combat missions over Europe, a milestone reached in May 1943.

Combat career

Under its best-known commander, Captain Robert K. Morgan, the Belle flew missions against targets in France and Germany, including submarine pens, airfields, and factories. The aircraft's name and famous nose art — a woman in a red bathing suit, after an Esquire pin-up by George Petty — honored Morgan's fiancée, Margaret Polk of Memphis, Tennessee.

After the war

The crew returned to the United States on a war bond tour, and director William Wyler's 1944 documentary The Memphis Belle: A Story of a Flying Fortress made the aircraft famous; a fictionalized feature film followed in 1990. After decades displayed outdoors in Memphis and a long restoration, the bomber was unveiled in 2018 at the National Museum of the United States Air Force at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base, Ohio, on the 75th anniversary of its 25th mission.
