Middle Francia

Middle Francia (Latin: Francia media) was a short-lived Frankish kingdom created by the Treaty of Verdun in 843, which divided the Carolingian Empire among the three surviving sons of Louis the Pious. The middle realm was allotted to the eldest, Emperor Lothair I, and stretched in a long strip from the North Sea through the Low Countries, Lorraine, Burgundy and Provence to northern and central Italy, including the imperial capital of Aachen and Rome.

The kingdom's elongated, ethnically and linguistically diverse territory proved hard to defend and govern. On Lothair I's death in 855, the Treaty of Prüm divided Middle Francia among his three sons: Italy went to Louis II, Lorraine (Lotharingia) to Lothair II, and Provence and Lower Burgundy to Charles of Provence.

Legacy

Middle Francia disappeared within a generation, but Lotharingia remained a contested borderland between the West and East Frankish kingdoms — and later between France and Germany — for nearly a thousand years. The Verdun partition is often taken as a foundational moment in the separate histories of France and Germany.
