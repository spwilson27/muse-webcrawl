Prime meridian

A prime meridian is an arbitrarily chosen meridian (line of longitude) in a geographic coordinate system at which longitude is defined to be 0°. Together, a prime meridian and its antimeridian form a great circle dividing the globe into eastern and western hemispheres.

The Greenwich meridian

The internationally standard prime meridian passes through the Royal Observatory in Greenwich, London, adopted at the International Meridian Conference of 1884 in Washington, D.C. The modern IERS Reference Meridian used by GPS lies about 102 metres east of the historic Greenwich line. The prime meridian is also the basis for Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) and the world's time zones.

History

Before 1884, many countries used their own prime meridians — through Paris, Ferro, Cadiz, or national observatories — causing confusion for navigation and mapping that the single Greenwich standard resolved.
