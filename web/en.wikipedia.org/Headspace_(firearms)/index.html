Headspace (firearms)

Headspace, in firearms terminology, is the distance measured from a datum line on the cartridge case to the part of the chamber that stops the case's forward movement. Correct headspace is essential for safe, reliable function: it determines how the cartridge is positioned when the action is closed and the round is fired.

How cartridges headspace

Different cartridge designs locate on different features. Rimmed cartridges headspace on the rim; belted magnum cases on the belt; rimless bottleneck rifle cases on the shoulder; and straight-walled pistol cases typically on the case mouth. Rimless pistol cartridges in some designs rely on the extractor for positioning.

Excessive and insufficient headspace

Excessive headspace lets the case stretch or move on firing, causing misfires, case-head separations and escaping high-pressure gas — a safety hazard. Insufficient headspace may prevent the action from closing fully. Gunsmiths check headspace with precision "go", "no-go" and "field" gauges, particularly when fitting new barrels or bolts.
