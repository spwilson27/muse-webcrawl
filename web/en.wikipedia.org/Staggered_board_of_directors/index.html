Staggered board of directors
Field: Corporate governance
Also called: Classified board
Common structure: Three classes of directors

A staggered board of directors, or classified board, is a board structure in which directors are divided into classes (commonly three), with only one class elected each year, so that the whole board is replaced over several years rather than at a single annual meeting.

Effects

Staggered boards provide continuity but are widely regarded as a takeover defence: an acquirer cannot gain board control in one election and may need two annual meetings. Critics argue this entrenches management; supporters cite stability and bargaining power.

Trends

Shareholder pressure has led many large public companies, especially in the S&P 500, to declassify their boards since the 2000s.
