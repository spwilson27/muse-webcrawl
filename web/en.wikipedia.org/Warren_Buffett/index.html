Warren Buffett

Born: August 30, 1930, Omaha, Nebraska, U.S.
Occupation: Investor, businessman
Known for: CEO of Berkshire Hathaway; value investing

Warren Edward Buffett (born August 30, 1930) is an American investor and philanthropist, widely regarded as one of the most successful investors in the world. He is the chairman and CEO of Berkshire Hathaway, a multinational conglomerate holding company based in Omaha, Nebraska, which has earned him the nickname the "Oracle of Omaha."

Early life and education

Buffett was born in Omaha, Nebraska, the son of congressman Howard Buffett. He showed an early aptitude for business and investing, buying his first shares at age 11. He studied at the University of Nebraska and then at Columbia Business School, where he was mentored by Benjamin Graham, the father of value investing.

Investment philosophy

Buffett follows Graham's philosophy of value investing, seeking companies with strong fundamentals trading below their intrinsic value. Together with his longtime partner Charlie Munger, he emphasizes buying wonderful businesses at fair prices, durable competitive advantages ("moats"), and long-term holding periods.

Berkshire Hathaway

Buffett took control of Berkshire Hathaway, then a textile manufacturer, in 1965 and transformed it into a holding company for wholly owned businesses and a large portfolio of stocks, including major stakes in companies such as Apple, Bank of America, Coca-Cola, and American Express. Its Class A shares are among the most expensive single shares in history.

Philanthropy

In 2010 Buffett co-founded The Giving Pledge with Bill Gates, committing to give away the majority of his wealth. He has pledged most of his Berkshire stock to the Gates Foundation and family foundations.
