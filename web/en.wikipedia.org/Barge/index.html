Barge

A pusher tug with a tow of hopper barges. [Image placeholder]

A barge is a flat-bottomed vessel built mainly for transporting heavy and bulk goods on rivers, canals and sheltered waters. Most barges are not self-propelled: they are pushed or towed by tugboats or towboats, or, historically, hauled by draught animals along towpaths. Barges range from small canal narrowboats to enormous ocean-going deck barges; on the Mississippi River system, towboats push "tows" of dozens of lashed-together barges forming a single unit that can exceed 300 metres in length.

Types

  • Hopper barges — open or covered holds for bulk cargo such as grain, coal, aggregates and fertiliser; the workhorse of inland waterways.
  • Tank barges — carry liquid cargoes: petroleum products, chemicals and liquefied gases.
  • Deck barges — flat deck for machinery, containers, project cargo, or use as floating platforms.
  • Crane and derrick barges — heavy-lift platforms used in construction and offshore work.
  • House barges and Dutch barges — converted or purpose-built for residential and leisure use, especially on British and European canals.
  • Royal/ceremonial barges — ornate state vessels, a historic meaning of the word.

History

Barge transport is ancient — Egyptian and Mesopotamian civilisations moved stone and grain by barge. In industrial Britain and Europe, canal barges pulled by horses were central to the transport of coal and manufactured goods during the Industrial Revolution, and the profession of bargeman shaped distinctive folk cultures (including the painted "roses and castles" art of English narrowboats). In the United States, barges remain one of the most fuel-efficient ways to move bulk freight: a single 15-barge tow can carry the load of roughly a thousand trucks.

Modern use

Today barges carry large shares of inland bulk traffic on systems such as the Mississippi, Rhine, Danube, Yangtze and Mekong. Specialised ocean-going barges are used for offshore construction, dredging support, and as semi-permanent accommodation or power-generation platforms ("powerships" and accommodation barges).
