Plato (428/427 or 424/423 – 348/347 BC) was an ancient Greek philosopher of the Classical period, widely considered the founder of Western philosophy together with his teacher Socrates and his student Aristotle. Born into a wealthy and politically active Athenian family, Plato founded the Academy in Athens, the first institution of higher learning in the Western world, and his philosophical writings — mostly dialogues in which Socrates is the main speaker — have been used to teach an unmatched range of subjects including philosophy, logic, ethics, rhetoric and mathematics.

Life

Plato's real name was reportedly Aristocles; "Plato" (from Greek platys, "broad") was a nickname. After Socrates was executed by the Athenian democracy in 399 BC, Plato left Athens and travelled in Greece, Egypt and Italy, where he encountered the followers of Pythagoras. He returned to Athens around 387 BC and founded the Academy in a grove sacred to the hero Academus. He twice attempted, unsuccessfully, to put his political philosophy into practice by advising Dionysius II of Syracuse.

Philosophy

Plato's best-known doctrine is the Theory of Forms: the claim that the material world of change is an imperfect image of a higher, eternal reality of perfect "Forms" or "Ideas" — such as the Good, Beauty and Justice — accessible to intellect rather than sense. In the Republic, his most famous work, he examines justice, describes an ideal city (the Kallipolis) ruled by philosopher kings, and presents the allegory of the cave as an image of human ignorance and enlightenment. The soul, he argues, is tripartite — reason, spirit and appetite — and justice is its proper harmony.

Major works

Some thirty-five dialogues and thirteen letters are attributed to Plato; nearly all the dialogues are generally accepted as genuine. Famous early dialogues include the Apology, Crito and Euthyphro (on Socrates' trial and death); middle dialogues include the Symposium, Phaedo, Phaedrus and Republic; late works include the Theaetetus, Sophist, Timaeus and the unfinished Laws, his longest work.

Legacy

Through the Academy, and later through Neoplatonism and the commentary tradition, Plato shaped philosophy, theology and science for more than two millennia. Alfred North Whitehead famously remarked that European philosophy "consists of a series of footnotes to Plato". His works have survived intact — a distinction shared by few ancient authors — largely because they were continuously copied and studied from antiquity onward.
