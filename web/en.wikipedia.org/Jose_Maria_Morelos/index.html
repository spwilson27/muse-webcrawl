Jose Maria Morelos

José María Teclo Morelos Pérez y Pavón (30 September 1765 – 22 December 1815) was a Mexican Roman Catholic priest, statesman and military leader who led the Mexican War of Independence movement after the execution of its founder, Miguel Hidalgo y Costilla, in 1811. Morelos is regarded as a national hero of Mexico.

Early life

Morelos was born in Valladolid (modern-day Morelia, renamed in his honour) in New Spain, of mixed ancestry. He worked as a muleteer and farmer before entering the Colegio de San Nicolás, where Hidalgo was rector, and was ordained a priest in 1797. He served as a parish priest in Carácuaro.

Insurgency

Morelos joined Hidalgo's revolt in October 1810 and proved a brilliant organiser and strategist. After Hidalgo's death he took command in the south, capturing much of the region, besieging Acapulco, and taking Oaxaca in 1812. He endured the 72-day royalist siege of Cuautla in 1812, breaking out successfully.

Congress of Chilpancingo

In 1813 Morelos convened the Congress of Chilpancingo, which declared the independence of North America from Spain. His political programme, the Sentimientos de la Nación ("Feelings of the Nation"), called for the abolition of slavery and of the caste system, equality before the law, and popular sovereignty. In 1814 the Congress promulgated the Constitution of Apatzingán.

Capture and death

Pursued by royalist forces under Agustín de Iturbide, Morelos was captured in November 1815 while escorting the Congress to safety. He was tried by the Inquisition and a military court in Mexico City, defrocked, and shot at San Cristóbal Ecatepec on 22 December 1815. The Mexican state of Morelos and the city of Morelia are named after him.
