Schwarz integral formula


Recovering a holomorphic function from its real part on a circle

In complex analysis, the Schwarz integral formula, named after Hermann Amandus Schwarz, recovers a holomorphic function on a disc from the values of its real part on the boundary circle, up to an imaginary constant. It is closely related to the Poisson integral formula for harmonic functions.

Statement

Let f be holomorphic on the closed disc of radius R centred at the origin. Then for any point z inside the disc,

f(z) = (1/2πi) ∮ [Re(f(Reiθ)) · (Reiθ + z)/(Reiθ − z)] dθ / eiθ + i·Im(f(0)).

In other words, the real part of a holomorphic function on the boundary determines the whole function in the interior except for the imaginary value at the centre, which must be supplied separately.

Derivation and uses

The formula follows from applying the Cauchy integral formula to f and to its conjugate-related terms, and it underlies the fact that the real and imaginary parts of a holomorphic function are harmonic conjugates. A version holds for the upper half-plane. The result is used in conformal mapping theory and in solving Dirichlet problems.
