Patronage

Patronage is the support, encouragement, privilege, or financial aid that an organization or individual bestows on another. In the history of art, arts patronage refers to the support that princes, popes, and other wealthy and influential people provided to artists such as musicians, painters, and sculptors. It can also refer to the right of distributing offices or church benefices, the business given to a store by a regular customer, and the guardianship of saints.

Arts patronage

From ancient Rome—where the poet Maecenas famously supported Virgil and Horace—through the Italian Renaissance, patronage was the principal economic engine of artistic production. The Medici of Florence, the papal court, and royal courts across Europe commissioned works that remain canonical. Patronage shaped subject matter and style, since works were usually created to a patron's commission.

Political patronage

In politics, patronage denotes the appointment of officials and distribution of favours by a governing party to its supporters—the "spoils system". Reform movements in the nineteenth century replaced many patronage appointments with merit-based civil services, though patronage networks (clientelism) remain influential in many democracies and are studied as a form of political exchange.
