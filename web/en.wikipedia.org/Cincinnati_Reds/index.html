Cincinnati Reds

The Cincinnati Reds are an American professional baseball team based in Cincinnati, Ohio. The Reds compete in Major League Baseball (MLB) as a member of the National League Central Division. The franchise traces its roots to the Cincinnati Red Stockings of 1869, generally considered the first openly all-professional baseball team.

History

The current franchise was founded as the Cincinnati Red Stockings of the American Association in 1881 and joined the National League in 1890. The Reds have won five World Series championships: 1919, 1940, 1975, 1976, and 1990. The 1970s teams, nicknamed the "Big Red Machine", featured Johnny Bench, Pete Rose, Joe Morgan, and Tony Pérez under manager Sparky Anderson and are regarded among the greatest teams in baseball history. The 1990 Reds swept the Oakland Athletics in the World Series. The team was briefly called the Redlegs during the 1950s.

Ballpark and traditions

Since 2003 the Reds have played at Great American Ball Park on the Ohio River, after decades at Crosley Field and Riverfront Stadium. In recognition of the city's place in professional baseball history, the Reds traditionally host the first game of the MLB season at home, marked by the Findlay Market Opening Day Parade.

Notable players

Hall of Famers who played for the Reds include Johnny Bench, Joe Morgan, Tony Pérez, Frank Robinson, Barry Larkin, and broadcaster Waite Hoyt. Pete Rose, baseball's all-time hits leader, remains banned from the Hall of Fame for betting on baseball.
