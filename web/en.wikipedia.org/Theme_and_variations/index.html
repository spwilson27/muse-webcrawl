Theme and variations

Theme and variations is a musical form in which a theme — a melody, bass line, harmonic progression or combination of these — is stated and then repeated in altered versions. Each variation transforms the theme's rhythm, harmony, texture, tempo or character while remaining recognisably derived from it.

History

The form flourished in the Renaissance (as diferencias on a ground bass such as the romanesca and passamezzo), the Baroque (passacaglia and chaconne), and the Classical era, where variation sets by Haydn, Mozart and Beethoven are central works. Famous examples include Bach's Goldberg Variations, Beethoven's Diabelli Variations and Elgar's Enigma Variations.

Types

Variations may be continuous (over an unchanging bass or chord sequence) or sectional, and may end with a return of the theme or a concluding fugue.
