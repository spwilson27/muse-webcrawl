New religious movement

New religious movement (NRM)
Term coinedPopularized by sociologist Eileen Barker, 1980s
ScopeReligious or spiritual groups founded roughly since the mid-19th century, especially post-1945
ExamplesISKCON, Unification Church, Scientology, Falun Gong, modern Pagan movements
Fields studying NRMsSociology of religion, religious studies, psychology
Related term"Cult" (often considered pejorative)

A new religious movement (NRM) is a religious or spiritual group that has modern origins and stands peripheral to its society's dominant religious culture. The term, adopted by scholars of religion from the 1980s onward, was intended as a neutral alternative to the negatively loaded word "cult". NRMs may be novel syntheses of older traditions, schisms from established religions, or imports of traditions into new cultural settings, such as Asian religions in the West.

Characteristics

Scholars note recurrent features: a first generation of converts rather than hereditary members; a charismatic founder or leader; novel doctrines or practices; and tension with surrounding society. Many NRMs arose amid rapid social change in the 19th century (e.g., Mormonism, the Baháʼí Faith, Seventh-day Adventism) and again in the countercultural 1960s–70s, when hundreds of groups — from the Hare Krishna movement to the Children of God — attracted young Western adherents.

Controversies and scholarship

Some NRMs became notorious through violence or coercion, as with the Peoples Temple at Jonestown (1978), Aum Shinrikyo's Tokyo sarin attack (1995), and Heaven's Gate (1997). Such cases fed an "anti-cult movement" and contested claims about "brainwashing". Academic specialists, organized since 1988 partly through INFORM (founded by Eileen Barker), generally argue that most NRMs are harmless, that membership is usually short-lived, and that coercive accounts fit only a minority. Governments in various countries, including France, Russia and China, have adopted restrictive policies toward groups they classify as cults or extremist sects.
