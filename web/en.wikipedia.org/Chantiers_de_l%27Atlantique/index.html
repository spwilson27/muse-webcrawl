Chantiers de l'Atlantique is a French shipyard company based in Saint-Nazaire, at the mouth of the Loire. One of the largest shipyards in the world, it is renowned for building great ocean liners — including the Normandie, France, and Queen Mary 2 — and the Oasis-class ships, the largest cruise ships ever constructed.

History

The yard's origins date to 1861, when the Compagnie Générale Transatlantique established workshops at Penhoët to build its liners locally. The Saint-Nazaire yards built battleships, the carrier-era fleet, and supertankers, and in the 1980s pivoted to cruise ships as that market boomed. Ownership passed through Alstom, Aker, and STX; the French state took a controlling stake and the historic name Chantiers de l'Atlantique was restored in 2018.

Facilities and work

The yard's building dock, over 900 metres long with one of Europe's most powerful gantry cranes, can assemble the largest hulls afloat. Recent output includes Royal Caribbean's Oasis-class ships, MSC Cruises' World-class ships, and naval and offshore-energy structures.
