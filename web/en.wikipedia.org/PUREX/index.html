PUREX

PUREX

Name: Plutonium Uranium Reduction Extraction

Type: Liquid–liquid extraction process

Extractant: Tri-n-butyl phosphate (TBP) in kerosene

Used for: Reprocessing of spent nuclear fuel

PUREX (plutonium uranium reduction extraction) is a chemical method used to purify and decontaminate spent nuclear fuel, separating uranium and plutonium from fission products and minor actinides. It is the standard industrial process for nuclear reprocessing, used at plants such as La Hague in France, Sellafield in the United Kingdom, Mayak in Russia and Rokkasho in Japan.

Process

Spent fuel assemblies are sheared and their fuel dissolved in hot concentrated nitric acid. The resulting solution is contacted with an organic solvent — typically 30 percent tributyl phosphate (TBP) in an odourless kerosene diluent — in a series of mixer-settlers or pulsed columns. TBP forms complexes with uranium(VI) and plutonium(IV), drawing them into the organic phase while most fission products remain in the aqueous phase. The loaded solvent is then treated to separate plutonium from uranium: plutonium is chemically reduced to Pu(III), which is poorly extracted and passes back into water, allowing separate product streams.

The separated outputs are a uranyl nitrate solution (recovered uranium, which can be re-enriched or stored) and plutonium nitrate, converted to plutonium dioxide — for weapons in military programmes, or blended with uranium dioxide as mixed oxide (MOX) fuel for reactors. The residual aqueous stream is high-level liquid waste, containing the fission products and minor actinides, which is typically vitrified into glass for disposal.

History

The process was developed in the United States in the late 1940s, with key work at the Knolls Atomic Power Laboratory by Herbert Anderson and Larned Brown, and was first deployed at production scale at the Hanford Site in 1956, superseding the earlier bismuth phosphate and REDOX processes. It was declassified sufficiently for civil use and became the basis of reprocessing industries worldwide.

Proliferation concerns

Because PUREX yields plutonium in a pure, separated form usable in nuclear weapons, it is a central concern of non-proliferation policy. This has motivated alternative schemes — such as COEX, UREX and pyroprocessing — that avoid producing a pure plutonium stream, and political decisions in some countries, notably the United States since the 1970s, not to pursue commercial reprocessing.
