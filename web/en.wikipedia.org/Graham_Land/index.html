Graham Land

LocationAntarctic Peninsula, Antarctica
CoordinatesApproximately 66°00′S 63°30′W
Named forSir James Robert George Graham
ClaimsBritish Antarctic Territory; also claimed by Argentina and Chile

Graham Land is the portion of the Antarctic Peninsula that lies north of a line joining Cape Jeremy and Cape Agassiz. It forms the northernmost part of mainland Antarctica and consequently the part closest to South America, separated from it by the Drake Passage.

History

The coast was first explored by the expedition of John Biscoe in 1832; Biscoe named the region after Sir James Robert George Graham, then First Lord of the Admiralty. The sealing captain Nathaniel Palmer and others had sighted parts of the peninsula earlier in the 1820s. The British Graham Land Expedition (1934–1937), led by John Rymill, conducted extensive surveys and established that Graham Land was part of a continuous peninsula rather than an archipelago.

Geography

Graham Land is mountainous, with a central plateau and peaks of the Antarctic Peninsula's spine, deeply indented coasts, and numerous offshore islands including Joinville Island, the South Shetland Islands (usually treated separately), and Adelaide Island off its southern Graham Land (Palmer Land) boundary. Its coasts are divided into named stretches such as the Davis Coast, Danco Coast, and Loubet Coast. Research stations operated by several nations are located along the coast, where the relatively mild climate supports breeding colonies of penguins and other wildlife.

Territorial claims

Graham Land lies within the British Antarctic Territory, but overlapping claims are maintained by Argentina (Argentine Antarctica) and Chile (Chilean Antarctic Territory). All claims are held in abeyance under the Antarctic Treaty of 1959, which reserves the continent for peaceful and scientific purposes.
