Lag BaOmer

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Lag BaOmer (Hebrew: ל״ג בעומר), also written Lag B'Omer or Lag LaOmer, is a Jewish holiday celebrated on the 33rd day of the Counting of the Omer, on the 18th day of the Hebrew month of Iyar. The word "Lag" represents the Hebrew letters lamed (30) and gimel (3), giving the number 33.

Origins and customs

During the weeks of the Omer, observances of semi-mourning are traditionally kept, commemorating a plague said to have killed 24,000 students of Rabbi Akiva. On Lag BaOmer the mourning restrictions are lifted: weddings, parties, and haircuts are permitted. The day is also associated with Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai, the 2nd-century sage credited in tradition with authoring the Zohar; his death anniversary (hillula) is marked by a mass pilgrimage to his tomb at Meron in the Galilee.

Celebrations

In Israel and in many Diaspora communities the holiday is celebrated with bonfires, parades, picnics, and children's outings, sometimes with bows and arrows recalling traditions about Rabbi Akiva's students or rainbows. Schoolchildren commonly take field trips on the day.
