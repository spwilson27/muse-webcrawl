Nadia Boulanger

Juliette Nadia Boulanger (16 September 1887 – 22 October 1979) was a French music teacher, conductor and composer who taught many of the leading composers and musicians of the 20th century. A student at the Paris Conservatoire, she won second prize in the Prix de Rome in 1908 and became one of the most influential composition teachers in the Western world.

Life and career

Boulanger was born in Paris into a musical family; her father Ernest taught at the Conservatoire and her younger sister Lili Boulanger was a gifted composer who died young in 1918. Nadia entered the Conservatoire at the age of ten and studied composition with Gabriel Fauré. After Lili's death she largely gave up composition and devoted herself to teaching, conducting and promoting her sister's music.

Teaching

From 1921 she taught at the American Conservatory at Fontainebleau, where generations of American composers — including Aaron Copland, Elliott Carter, Philip Glass and Astor Piazzolla — came to study with her. Her Wednesday afternoon classes in Paris became legendary for their rigour in counterpoint, harmony and score-reading. She was also a pioneering woman conductor, leading major orchestras including the Boston Symphony and the New York Philharmonic.
