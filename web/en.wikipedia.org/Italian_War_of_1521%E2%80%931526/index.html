The Italian War of 1521–1526, sometimes known as the Four Years' War, was part of the Italian Wars between the Habsburg emperor Charles V and King Francis I of France. Fighting centred on Lombardy and northern Italy, and it ended with a decisive Habsburg victory.

Course of the war

War broke out in 1521 as Imperial and Papal forces attacked French-held Milan. The French were defeated at the Battle of Bicocca in 1522, a landmark in the growing dominance of arquebus fire and field fortifications. Francis I personally led a new invasion of Lombardy in 1524, but he was defeated and captured at the Battle of Pavia on 24 February 1525, one of the most decisive battles of the century.

Treaty of Madrid

Francis was taken prisoner to Madrid, where in January 1526 he signed the Treaty of Madrid, renouncing his claims to Italy, Flanders, and Artois and agreeing to surrender Burgundy. Once released, he repudiated the treaty, leading directly to the War of the League of Cognac.
