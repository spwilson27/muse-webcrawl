Count Dracula

Count Dracula is the title character of Bram Stoker's 1897 Gothic horror novel Dracula. He is an undead, centuries-old vampire and Transylvanian nobleman who inhabits a castle in the Carpathian Mountains near the Borgo Pass. Stoker's character is widely considered the prototypical and archetypal vampire in subsequent works of fiction.

Character

In the novel, Dracula purchases the English estate of Carfax and travels to England, where he preys upon Lucy Westenra and Mina Murray before being hunted by Jonathan Harker, Abraham Van Helsing, and their companions, who pursue him back to Transylvania and destroy him. Dracula possesses supernatural abilities including immense strength, shapeshifting into animals such as a wolf or bat, control over the weather and animals, hypnosis, and the ability to defy gravity. He is weakened by sunlight, garlic, crucifixes, and sacred items, and can be killed by a wooden stake through the heart and decapitation.

Origins

Stoker took the name from Vlad III, the fifteenth-century Wallachian prince known as Vlad the Impaler or Vlad Drăculea ("son of the dragon"), though scholars debate how much of Vlad's biography actually informed the character. Elements also drew on earlier vampire fiction, notably Sheridan Le Fanu's Carmilla and John Polidori's The Vampyre.

Legacy

Dracula has appeared in hundreds of films, stage productions, and books, portrayed by actors including Bela Lugosi, Christopher Lee, and Gary Oldman. He is one of the most frequently depicted characters in horror fiction and popular culture.
