Morphogen

A morphogen is a signalling molecule that governs the pattern of tissue development in the process of morphogenesis, and the positions of specialised cell types within a tissue. It spreads from a localised source, forming a concentration gradient across a developing field; cells respond to different concentration thresholds by adopting different fates.

FieldDevelopmental biology
ExamplesBicoid, Sonic hedgehog, retinoic acid, BMP, Wnt
Concept byAlan Turing (theory); Lewis Wolpert (positional information)

The French flag model

Lewis Wolpert's "French flag" model illustrates the idea: a gradient of morphogen across a row of cells causes them to become blue, white or red depending on concentration thresholds, producing a patterned field from a smooth gradient.

Examples

In the fruit fly Drosophila, the Bicoid protein gradient patterns the anterior–posterior axis of the early embryo. In vertebrates, Sonic hedgehog patterns the neural tube and limbs, while retinoic acid, bone morphogenetic proteins and Wnt proteins act as morphogens in many developmental contexts.

Mechanisms

Morphogen gradients can form by diffusion and degradation, by regulated transport, or by cell-to-cell relay, and their interpretation depends on receptor levels, feedback loops and the competence of responding cells.
