Blue Lias

The Blue Lias is a geological formation in southern, eastern and western England and parts of Wales, consisting of a sequence of limestone and shale layers laid down in the late Triassic and early Jurassic, between about 210 and 195 million years ago. Its outcrops along the Jurassic Coast of Dorset and at Lyme Regis are famous for their marine fossils.

Lithology

The formation alternates hard, blue-grey limestone beds with softer mudstone and shale, reflecting rhythmic changes in sea conditions. The rock was formerly quarried extensively for building stone and for the manufacture of cement and hydraulic lime.

Fossils

The Blue Lias is renowned for ammonites, belemnites, ichthyosaurs and plesiosaurs. The cliffs around Lyme Regis and Charmouth yielded many of the specimens collected by Mary Anning in the early 19th century, which helped found the science of palaeontology.
