Great Moravia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Great Moravia (Latin: Regnum Marahensium; Greek: Μεγάλη Μοραβία) was the first major state of the West Slavs, centred in the region of modern Moravia in the Czech Republic and western Slovakia. Emerging in the 830s, it reached its height under Svatopluk I in the late 9th century, when it embraced Bohemia, parts of Poland and Hungary, and stood as a significant power between the Frankish world and Byzantium. It collapsed around 907 under Magyar invasion.

History

The state was founded when Mojmír I unified the Moravian principality and, in 833, annexed the neighbouring principality of Nitra. His successor Rastislav, seeking independence from East Francia, invited a mission from Constantinople; in 863 the brothers Cyril and Methodius arrived, devising the Glagolitic script and translating scripture into Old Church Slavonic — the foundation of Slavic literacy. Under Svatopluk I (871–894) Great Moravia expanded greatly and defied Frankish arms, though after his death his sons' quarrels weakened the realm, and Magyar (Hungarian) incursions destroyed it; the last reference is often linked to the Battle of Pressburg in 907.

Culture and legacy

Great Moravia left strongholds such as Mikulčice and Staré Město, fine jewellery, and the first Slavic literary language. In Czech and Slovak national memory it figures as the first shared state of their ancestors; Slovakia in particular honours the Cyrillo-Methodian heritage, and 5 July, the feast of the two saints, is a public holiday in both countries.
