Žiča Monastery

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Location: Near Kraljevo, Serbia
Founded: Early 13th century
Founders: Stefan the First-Crowned and Saint Sava
Denomination: Serbian Orthodox Church

Žiča Monastery (Serbian: Манастир Жича) is a Serbian Orthodox monastery located near Kraljevo in central Serbia. It was founded in the first decade of the 13th century by Stefan the First-Crowned, the first King of Serbia, and his brother Saint Sava, the first archbishop of the Serbian Church. The monastery is famous for its distinctive red façade and was the coronation church of the medieval Serbian kings.

History

The main church, dedicated to the Ascension of Christ, was built in the Raška architectural style. Saint Sava crowned his brother Stefan there in 1217, and Žiča became the first seat of the Serbian Archbishopric in 1219. Serbian medieval rulers were traditionally crowned at Žiča, and the monastery became a lasting symbol of Serbian statehood and church independence. The complex suffered destruction several times — under Tatar and Ottoman attacks and during both World Wars — and was repeatedly restored; it is protected as a cultural monument of exceptional importance.

Architecture and frescoes

The church preserves fragments of frescoes from the 13th and 14th centuries. Its bold red exterior, renewed in modern restorations, recalls the monasteries of Mount Athos.
