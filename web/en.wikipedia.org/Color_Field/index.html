Color Field

Color Field painting is a style of abstract painting that emerged in New York City during the 1940s and 1950s, characterised by large areas of flat, solid colour spread across the canvas.

Artists

Clement Greenberg championed the tendency. Leading practitioners included Mark Rothko, Barnett Newman, Clyfford Still, Helen Frankenthaler, Morris Louis and Kenneth Noland. Frankenthaler's soak-stain technique, thinning paint to stain unprimed canvas, was adopted by Louis and Noland.

Character

Unlike the gestural action painting of their Abstract Expressionist contemporaries, Color Field painters sought a contemplative, unified surface in which colour itself is the subject.
