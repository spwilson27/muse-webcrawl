Plurisubharmonic function

In mathematics, plurisubharmonic functions (sometimes abbreviated as psh, plsh, or plush functions) form an important class of functions used in complex analysis. On a Kähler manifold, plurisubharmonic functions form a subset of the subharmonic functions. However, unlike subharmonic functions (which are defined on a Riemannian manifold) plurisubharmonic functions can be defined in full generality on complex analytic spaces.

Formal definition

A function f : D → [−∞, ∞) with domain D ⊂ ℂn is called plurisubharmonic if it is upper semi-continuous, and for every complex line {a + bz : z ∈ ℂ} ⊂ ℂn, the function z ↦ f(a + bz) is a subharmonic function on the set {z ∈ ℂ : a + bz ∈ D}.

In full generality, the notion can be defined for an arbitrary complex analytic space X: a function is plurisubharmonic if it is upper semi-continuous and its restriction to every holomorphic disc is subharmonic (or identically −∞).

Properties

  • The logarithm of the modulus of a holomorphic function, log|f|, is plurisubharmonic.
  • A smooth function is plurisubharmonic if and only if its complex Hessian — the Hermitian form given by the Levi form of the (1,1)-form i∂∂̄f — is positive semi-definite.
  • The maximum and (suitably regularized) suprema of families of plurisubharmonic functions are plurisubharmonic.
  • Plurisubharmonic functions are locally integrable and satisfy a mean value inequality on every complex line.

History and applications

The notion was introduced independently by Kiyoshi Oka and Pierre Lelong in the 1940s. Plurisubharmonic functions play a central role in pluripotential theory, the study of domains of holomorphy, and the theory of pseudoconvexity. They are used to define currents in complex geometry and appear in the study of Kähler geometry through the ∂∂̄-lemma and the construction of Kähler potentials.
