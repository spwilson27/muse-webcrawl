Komische Oper Berlin

The Komische Oper Berlin is an opera company and theatre on Behrenstraße in Berlin's Mitte district, near Unter den Linden. Founded in 1947 by Walter Felsenstein, it is one of Berlin's three principal opera houses, alongside the Staatsoper Unter den Linden and the Deutsche Oper Berlin. It is celebrated for innovative, director-led productions and for performing works in German translation.

The building

The theatre opened in 1892 as the Theater Unter den Linden and later operated as the Metropol-Theater, a home of operetta. Badly damaged in the Second World War, its lavish neo-baroque auditorium was reconstructed in the 1960s and survives as one of Berlin's finest theatre interiors.

Felsenstein era

Walter Felsenstein founded the company in 1947 in East Berlin and directed it until 1975. His doctrine of Musiktheater — realistic, text-driven staging in which every singer is an actor — influenced opera production worldwide. His successor Harry Kupfer continued the tradition of provocative, concept-led stagings.

Modern era

Under intendant and chief director Barrie Kosky (2012–2022), the house won international acclaim — including Opera Company of the Year at the 2013 International Opera Awards — for rediscovering Weimar-era operetta and composers such as Paul Abraham alongside bold readings of the standard repertoire. Since 2022 the house has been led by Susanne Moser and Philip Bröking, with productions staged at temporary venues during a major renovation of the Behrenstraße building that began in 2023.
