The Hejaz Railway was a narrow-gauge railway built by the Ottoman Empire from Damascus in Syria to Medina in the Hejaz region of present-day Saudi Arabia, with a branch line to Haifa on the Mediterranean. It was constructed between 1900 and 1908 under Sultan Abdul Hamid II.

Purpose and construction

The railway was intended to speed the Hajj pilgrimage from Damascus to Mecca — reducing a journey of some forty days by caravan to about four days by rail — and to strengthen Ottoman military control of the Arabian provinces. It was financed largely by donations from Muslims across the world and built under the German engineer Heinrich August Meissner ("Meissner Pasha") with a largely Muslim workforce. The line reached Medina in 1908; the planned extension to Mecca was never built.

First World War and after

During the First World War the railway was repeatedly attacked by Arab forces advised by British officers, including T. E. Lawrence, as dramatised in the film Lawrence of Arabia; demolition charges wrecked track, bridges and locomotives. After the empire's collapse the line was divided among Syria, Jordan and Saudi Arabia. Sections in Syria and Jordan have operated, at times intermittently, while the Saudi portion was abandoned, leaving ruined stations and locomotives in the desert. Restoration and heritage projects have been proposed in all three countries.
