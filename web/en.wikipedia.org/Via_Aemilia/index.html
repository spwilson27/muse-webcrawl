Via Aemilia

LocationNorthern Italy
Built187 BC
BuilderMarcus Aemilius Lepidus
RoutePlacentia (Piacenza) to Ariminum (Rimini)

The Via Aemilia (Italian: Via Emilia) was a trunk Roman road in the north Italian plain, running through what is now the Italian region of Emilia-Romagna. It was constructed by the consul Marcus Aemilius Lepidus in 187 BC, running in an almost straight line for about 262 kilometres from Placentia (modern Piacenza) on the River Po to Ariminum (Rimini) on the Adriatic coast, passing through the cities of Parma, Regium Lepidi (Reggio Emilia), Mutina (Modena), Bononia (Bologna), Forum Livii (Forlì) and Caesena (Cesena).

History

The road consolidated Roman control of the Po valley following its conquest from the Celts and gave the region of Emilia its name. A branch, the Via Aemilia Altinate, ran from Bononia toward Patavium (Padua). The modern Strada Statale 9 follows much of the ancient route and is still called Via Emilia.
