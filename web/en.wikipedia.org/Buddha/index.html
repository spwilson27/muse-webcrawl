Buddha

The Buddha — Siddhartha Gautama — was a sage on whose teachings Buddhism was founded. Born in Lumbini (in present-day Nepal), traditionally in 563 or 480 BCE, he was a prince of the Shakya clan who renounced palace life to seek liberation from suffering.

After years of ascetic practice, he attained enlightenment (bodhi) while meditating under the Bodhi tree at Bodh Gaya, and taught for 45 years across the Gangetic plain. His core teachings include the Four Noble Truths and the Noble Eightfold Path.

The Buddha died at Kushinagar at age 80, attaining parinirvana. His relics were distributed among kingdoms and enshrined in stupas; Buddhism subsequently spread across Asia and the world. In later tradition 'Buddha' also denotes any fully awakened being.
