Diene

In organic chemistry, a diene (also diolefin) is a covalent compound that contains two carbon–carbon double bonds. Dienes are a subclass of polyenes and are closely related to alkenes; the simplest acyclic diene is 1,3-butadiene.

Classification

Dienes are classified by the relative positions of their double bonds: conjugated dienes have double bonds separated by one single bond (as in 1,3-butadiene), cumulated dienes (allenes) share a carbon atom, and isolated dienes are separated by two or more single bonds. Conjugated dienes are especially important because the conjugated system confers extra stability and distinctive reactivity.

Reactions and uses

Conjugated dienes undergo the Diels–Alder reaction with dienophiles to form cyclohexene rings, a cornerstone of organic synthesis. 1,3-Butadiene and isoprene (2-methyl-1,3-butadiene) are key monomers for synthetic rubber; isoprene units also build up the terpenes found in nature.
