The MLS Cup is the annual championship game of Major League Soccer (MLS) and the culmination of the MLS Cup Playoffs. The winner is crowned league champion and receives the Philip F. Anschutz Trophy; the match's winning team also typically qualifies for the CONCACAF Champions Cup.

History

The first MLS Cup was played in 1996, when D.C. United defeated the Los Angeles Galaxy 3–2 in extra time at a rain-soaked Foxboro Stadium. In its early years the final was held at predetermined neutral venues; since 2012 it has been hosted by the finalist with the better regular-season record. The LA Galaxy are the most successful club in the competition's history.

Format

The playoffs follow the regular season and involve the top clubs from the Eastern and Western Conferences. The final is a single match; if level after regulation, extra time and, if necessary, a penalty shootout determine the champion.
