Men's rights movement

The men's rights movement (MRM) is a branch of the men's movement that emerged in the 1970s, focusing on issues its adherents believe disadvantage or discriminate against men and boys. Areas of concern include family law and child custody, divorce and alimony, male suicide, occupational deaths and injuries, conscription, circumcision, education outcomes for boys, false accusations of rape, and domestic violence and sexual violence against men.

History

The movement grew out of the men's liberation movement of the 1970s, from which it split over the role of feminism: men's rights advocates argue that feminism has gone too far and institutionalizes advantages for women, a view expressed in books such as Warren Farrell's The Myth of Male Power (1993). Organizations such as the National Coalition for Men (founded 1977) pursued litigation over selective service registration and other issues. From the 2000s the movement expanded online through websites, blogs and forums such as A Voice for Men, becoming part of the loose online network sometimes called the "manosphere".

Reception

The movement is controversial. Supporters describe it as a civil-rights campaign for neglected problems; many feminists and scholars criticize men's rights groups for misogyny and for minimizing violence against women, and the Southern Poverty Law Center has characterized some men's rights websites as hateful toward women. Some fathers' rights and shared-parenting activism overlaps with the movement, though the groups are not identical.
