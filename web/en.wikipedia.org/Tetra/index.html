Tetra

A tetra is one of several species of small freshwater fish in the characin family Characidae and related families, native to the Americas and Africa. Tetras are popular aquarium fish because of their small size, bright colours, and peaceful schooling behaviour.

Characteristics

Most tetras are a few centimetres long and possess an adipose fin, a small fleshy fin on the back between the dorsal and tail fins characteristic of characins. Many species are silvery with iridescent markings; the neon tetra (Paracheirodon innesi) shows a brilliant blue-green stripe and red tail, while the cardinal tetra has red extending along the whole body.

In the aquarium

Tetras are generally kept in groups of six or more in soft, slightly acidic water. Popular species include the neon tetra, cardinal tetra, glowlight tetra, ember tetra, and the larger Congo tetra from Africa. Most are omnivorous and readily accept flake and small live foods.
