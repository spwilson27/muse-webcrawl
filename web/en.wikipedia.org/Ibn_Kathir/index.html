Ismail ibn Umar ibn Kathir al-Qurashi al-Busrawi (Arabic: ابن كثير; c. 1300 – 1373), commonly known as Ibn Kathir, was a highly influential Arab historian, exegete and scholar of the Mamluk era in Syria. He is best known for his Qur'anic commentary, the Tafsir al-Qur'an al-'Azim (popularly called Tafsir Ibn Kathir), and his universal history Al-Bidaya wa'l-Nihaya ("The Beginning and the End").

Life

Ibn Kathir was born around 1300 in Busra, in the Hauran region of Syria. Orphaned young, he moved to Damascus in about 1306 and received a classical Islamic education, memorising the Qur'an and studying hadith, jurisprudence and Arabic. His teachers included the historian al-Dhahabi and, most influentially, the controversial scholar Ibn Taymiyyah, whose method of interpreting scripture primarily through transmitted reports (tafsir bi'l-ma'thur) shaped Ibn Kathir's own exegesis.

He was appointed to teaching posts at leading Damascene institutions and became a prominent professor of hadith at the mosque-college of Tankiz and later at the Umayyad Mosque. He also served as a judge and accompanied diplomatic and military campaigns. He died in Damascus in 1373 and was buried beside Ibn Taymiyyah.

Works

The Tafsir Ibn Kathir is one of the most widely read Qur'an commentaries in the Sunni world. It explains verses using other Qur'anic passages, hadith, and the sayings of the Prophet Muhammad's companions and their successors, and it is noted for cautioning against Isra'iliyyat — narratives derived from Jewish and Christian sources. Al-Bidaya wa'l-Nihaya is a fourteen-volume universal history from the creation of the world to the author's own time (1373), drawing on earlier chronicles and including an extensive eschatological section. His other works include an abridgement of Ibn al-Salah's Muqaddimah on hadith science and biographies of scholars.

Reception

Because of his association with Ibn Taymiyyah, Ibn Kathir's works have been especially influential in later traditionalist and Salafi scholarship, and his tafsir is one of the most frequently translated and excerpted commentaries in print today.
