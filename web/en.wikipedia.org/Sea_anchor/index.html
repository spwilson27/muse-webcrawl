Sea anchor

A sea anchor (also called a drift anchor, drift sock, para-anchor or boat brake) is a device used to stabilise a vessel and slow its drift in heavy weather. It is typically a large parachute-like canopy streamed from the bow on a long line, which fills with water and creates drag, holding the bow into the wind and waves.

Use

Sea anchors are deployed when a boat cannot safely make way in a storm. By keeping the bow pointed into oncoming seas, the device reduces the risk of the vessel being rolled or pitchpoled, and slows wind-driven drift toward hazards. Smaller drift socks are also used by fishing boats and kayaks to control drift speed over fishing grounds.

Related devices

A drogue is a similar drag device streamed from the stern, used to slow a vessel running before a storm so it does not surf out of control or be pooped by following seas. Unlike a conventional anchor, neither device touches the seabed; both work purely by water resistance.
