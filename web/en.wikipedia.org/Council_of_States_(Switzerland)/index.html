The Council of States (German: Ständerat; French: Conseil des États; Italian: Consiglio degli Stati; Romansh: Cussegl dals Stadis) is the upper house of the Federal Assembly of Switzerland, representing the cantons. It has 46 members: each of the 20 full cantons elects two members, and each of the six former half-cantons (Obwalden, Nidwalden, Basel-Stadt, Basel-Landschaft, Appenzell Ausserrhoden, and Appenzell Innerrhoden) elects one.

Powers and workings

The Council of States has equal legislative power with the lower house, the National Council: all federal laws must be approved by both chambers, and the two councils together, sitting as the United Federal Assembly, elect the Federal Council (the federal government), federal judges, and, in times of war, a general. Because it represents the cantons rather than population, smaller rural cantons are proportionally over-represented, somewhat akin to the United States Senate.

Elections

Members are elected for four-year terms under rules set by each canton. Most cantons use a majoritarian system with a possible second round; Jura and Neuchâtel use proportional representation. Elections generally coincide with National Council elections at the federal elections held every four years. Historically, members of the Landsgemeinde cantons were chosen by open-air assembly; Appenzell Innerrhoden still selects its member at the Landsgemeinde.
