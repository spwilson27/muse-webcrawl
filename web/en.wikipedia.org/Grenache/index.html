Grenache (Spanish: Garnacha) is one of the most widely planted red wine grape varieties in the world. It ripens late, so it needs hot, dry conditions such as those found in Spain, where it is believed to have originated, and in the south of France, where it became prominent in regions such as the Rhône Valley. It is generally spicy, berry-flavoured and soft on the palate, producing wines with relatively high alcohol content.

Grenache is the dominant variety in most Southern Rhône wines, especially in Châteauneuf-du-Pape, where it typically makes up 80 percent or more of the blend. In Australia it is typically blended with Shiraz and Mourvèdre in "GSM" blends.

History

Grenache most likely originated in the region of Aragon in northern Spain, and from there spread to other Mediterranean lands under Aragonese influence, including Sardinia, where it is known as Cannonau, and Roussillon in southern France. DNA profiling has supported the Spanish origin, though the Sardinian claim remains debated. It was one of the first varieties introduced to Australia in the 18th century and became the backbone of the Australian fortified wine industry.

Viticulture

The Grenache vine is characterised by its strong wooden frame and upright growth, making it well suited to bush vine training. It is resistant to wind and drought, but the grapes have thin skins and pale colour, and the variety is prone to oxidation. Grenache buds early and requires a long growing season to ripen fully, thriving in hot, dry soils such as the galets roulés of Châteauneuf-du-Pape.

Wine styles

Grenache wines are typically red-fruited, with flavours of strawberry, raspberry and white pepper, soft tannins and high alcohol. The grape is used for still red, rosé and fortified wines. Notable rosés include those of Tavel and Lirac. Old-vine Grenache from Priorat and Barossa Valley can produce concentrated, long-lived wines.

Synonyms

Grenache is known as Garnacha in Spain, Garnatxa in Catalonia, Cannonau in Sardinia, Alicante Bouschet crosses aside, and Granaccia in parts of Italy. Mutations include Grenache blanc and Grenache gris, used for white and rosé wines.
