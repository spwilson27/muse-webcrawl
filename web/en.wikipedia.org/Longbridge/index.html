Longbridge

Longbridge is an area of south-west Birmingham, England, famous for the Longbridge car factory. The plant was founded by Herbert Austin in 1905 as the Austin Motor Company and produced Austin, Morris, BMC, British Leyland, Rover and MG cars for a century.

History

During the Second World War the factory and its Cofton Hackett shadow factory built aircraft including the Fairey Battle and components for Hurricanes and Lancasters, and munitions. Postwar products included the Mini and the Austin Metro. Ownership passed through BMC, British Leyland and the Rover Group (including under BMW) to MG Rover, whose collapse in 2005 ended mass production with the loss of thousands of jobs.

Regeneration

MG Motor UK retained a smaller presence, including design and final assembly for a period. Much of the site has been redeveloped with housing, the Longbridge town centre, Bournville College, retail and parkland around the River Rea.
