False imprisonment

False imprisonment or unlawful imprisonment occurs when a person intentionally restricts another person's movement within a bounded area without legal authority, justification, or the restrained person's permission. In common law it is both a tort and a felony.

Elements

A claim generally requires that the defendant intended to confine the plaintiff, that the plaintiff was confined within boundaries fixed by the defendant, and that the plaintiff was conscious of the confinement or harmed by it. Confinement may be effected by physical barriers, physical force, threats, or asserted legal authority. The shopkeeper's privilege allows merchants in many jurisdictions to briefly detain suspected shoplifters.

By jurisdiction

In England and Wales, false imprisonment is a common law offence and a tort; damages may be substantial even for brief detention. In the United States it is criminalised by state law, and unlawful detention by police can also violate constitutional rights. Related legal concepts include kidnapping, hostage-taking, and wrongful arrest.
