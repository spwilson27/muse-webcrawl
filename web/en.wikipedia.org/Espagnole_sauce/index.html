Espagnole sauce

Espagnole sauce
TypeMother sauce
Place of originFrance
Main ingredientsBrown stock, brown roux, mirepoix, tomato

Espagnole sauce (French: sauce espagnole) is a basic brown sauce in classical French cuisine. It is one of the five mother sauces codified by Auguste Escoffier in the early 20th century. Espagnole is made from a brown roux and brown stock (traditionally veal), with mirepoix and tomato, simmered and skimmed over a long cooking time.

Preparation

A brown stock is prepared by roasting bones and vegetables; a roux of flour cooked in fat until nut-brown is whisked into the stock with tomato purée and mirepoix. The sauce simmers for hours and is repeatedly skimmed to clarify. The name means "Spanish sauce", probably referring to the Spanish tomatoes and peppers used, or to the rich brown colour associated with Spanish leather.

Derivatives

Espagnole combined in equal parts with brown stock and reduced by half yields demi-glace, the foundation of many small brown sauces, including sauce bordelaise, sauce Robert, and sauce chasseur.
