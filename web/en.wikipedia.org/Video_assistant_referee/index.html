Video assistant referee

The video assistant referee (VAR) is a match official in association football who assists the referee by reviewing decisions using video footage. A team of officials — the VAR, assistant VAR, and replay operator — monitors the match from a video operation room and advises the referee on four categories of incident: goals and offences leading to them, penalty decisions, direct red-card incidents, and mistaken identity in awarding a card. The referee retains final authority and may accept the advice or review the footage personally at a pitchside monitor ("on-field review").

History

VAR was developed from the Refereeing 2.0 project in the Netherlands in the early 2010s and trialled in several competitions before being written into the Laws of the Game by IFAB in 2018. Its first World Cup use came at the 2018 FIFA World Cup in Russia, where it was widely judged a success. Major leagues including the Bundesliga, Serie A, La Liga, and (from 2019–20) the Premier League adopted it, with semi-automated offside technology added at the 2022 World Cup.

Protocol

Every check is "silent" until the VAR identifies a possible "clear and obvious error" or a "serious missed incident"; the threshold is deliberately high so that VAR does not re-referee the game. Reviews are either factual (for example, offside position, computed with calibrated lines) or subjective, the latter requiring an on-field review.

Reception

VAR has reduced certain clear errors and is supported in principle by many players and officials, but it remains controversial: long delays, marginal offside calls measured in millimetres, inconsistent application, and poor communication to crowds have drawn persistent criticism, and polls of fans often favour reform or abolition even as governing bodies continue to refine the system.
