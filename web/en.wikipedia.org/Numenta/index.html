Numenta

Founded2005
FoundersJeff Hawkins, Donna Dubinsky, Dileep George

Numenta, Inc. is a machine intelligence company founded in 2005 by Jeff Hawkins, Donna Dubinsky, and Dileep George. Based in Redwood City, California, the company is devoted to understanding how the neocortex works and building technology on those principles, an approach it pursues largely through published research rather than conventional commercial products.

Research

Numenta's work centres on Hierarchical Temporal Memory (HTM), a theory and algorithmic framework describing how layers of neurons in the neocortex learn sequences and make predictions, using sparse distributed representations. Its later "Thousand Brains Theory", popularised in Hawkins' 2021 book A Thousand Brains, proposes that each cortical column builds its own model of objects using reference frames, and that objects are recognised by a voting process across many columns. The company has published a series of peer-reviewed and open papers on cortical circuitry, dendritic computation, and sensorimotor inference, and released its research code (such as NuPIC) under open-source licences.

History

Hawkins and Dubinsky had previously co-founded Palm Computing and Handspring. Numenta was launched after the publication of Hawkins' book On Intelligence (2004), which laid out the memory-prediction framework developed with Sandra Blakeslee. Dileep George left in 2010 to found Vicarious. Numenta has licensed its intellectual property for applications such as anomaly detection in streaming data, while maintaining its primary focus on basic neuroscience theory.
