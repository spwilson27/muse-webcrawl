Pheidippides

Pheidippides (also spelled Philippides) was an Athenian herald or long-distance runner of ancient Greece. According to legend, he was sent from Marathon to Athens to announce the Greek victory over the Persians in the Battle of Marathon in 490 BC, ran the entire distance without stopping, burst into the assembly, exclaimed "we have won", and then collapsed and died.

The legend

The earliest source, Herodotus, recounts that Philippides was sent from Athens to Sparta, a distance of over 240 kilometres, to request help before the battle. The famous run from Marathon to Athens appears in later sources, including Plutarch and Lucian, writing centuries after the event. The story inspired the modern marathon race, first held at the 1896 Olympic Games in Athens over a distance approximating the route from Marathon to Athens.

Legacy

The marathon, standardized at 42.195 kilometres in 1921, commemorates Pheidippides' run. An ultramarathon called the Spartathlon, held annually in Greece, retraces his legendary run from Athens to Sparta.
