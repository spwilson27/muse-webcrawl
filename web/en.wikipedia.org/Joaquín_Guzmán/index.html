Joaquín Guzmán

[Image placeholder: photograph of Joaquín Guzmán after capture]

Joaquín Archivaldo Guzmán Loera (born 4 April 1957), commonly known as "El Chapo" (Spanish for "Shorty"), is a Mexican former drug lord and the longtime leader of the Sinaloa Cartel, an international organisation dedicated to the trafficking of illegal drugs. He is considered to have been one of the most powerful drug traffickers in the world.

Guzmán was born in La Tuna, Badiraguato, Sinaloa, Mexico, into a poor farming family. He entered the drug trade as a young man, first growing marijuana and opium poppies, and rose through the Guadalajara Cartel in the 1980s before founding his own organisation. Under his leadership the Sinaloa Cartel became notorious for its sophisticated smuggling methods, including networks of cross-border tunnels, and for extreme violence.

Captures and escapes

Guzmán was first captured in Guatemala in 1993 and sentenced to 20 years in prison in Mexico, but he escaped from the Puente Grande maximum-security prison in 2001, reportedly hidden in a laundry cart. He was recaptured in Mazatlán in February 2014, then escaped again in July 2015 through a tunnel dug into the shower area of his cell at the Altiplano prison. He was captured for a third time in January 2016 in Los Mochis, Sinaloa.

Trial and imprisonment

Mexico extradited Guzmán to the United States in January 2017. His trial in the United States District Court for the Eastern District of New York began in November 2018 and drew worldwide attention. In February 2019 he was found guilty on all counts, including engaging in a continuing criminal enterprise, drug trafficking, and firearms charges. In July 2019 he was sentenced to life imprisonment plus 30 years and ordered to forfeit more than US$12.6 billion. He is incarcerated at ADX Florence, the federal supermax prison in Colorado.
