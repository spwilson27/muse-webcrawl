Kronotsky Nature Reserve is a strict nature reserve (zapovednik) on the eastern coast of the Kamchatka Peninsula in the Russian Far East. It protects a volcanic landscape of geysers, calderas, glaciers and tundra, and one of Russia's largest populations of brown bears.

Geography

The reserve stretches along the Pacific coast beneath the Eastern Volcanic Range. Its landmarks include the symmetrical Kronotsky volcano (3,528 m), Lake Kronotskoye — Kamchatka's largest freshwater lake — and the Valley of Geysers, one of the largest geyser fields in the world, discovered in 1941 by geologist Tatyana Ustinova. A major landslide in June 2007 dammed the Geysernaya River and reshaped parts of the valley.

Flora and fauna

The reserve's forests of stone birch, Siberian dwarf pine and larch shelter brown bears, sable, foxes, reindeer and Steller's sea eagles. Its rivers host major salmon runs that underpin the ecosystem, and its coastal cliffs support large seabird colonies.

Protection and access

As a zapovednik, the reserve is closed to general tourism; access is limited to scientists and small numbers of visitors on approved helicopter tours to the Valley of Geysers. It forms part of the "Volcanoes of Kamchatka" UNESCO World Heritage Site, inscribed in 1996.
