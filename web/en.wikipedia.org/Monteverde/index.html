Monteverde

Monteverde is a community and region in the Cordillera de Tilarán mountain range of Puntarenas Province, Costa Rica, renowned worldwide for its cloud forests and pioneering ecotourism. The area sits at about 1,400–1,700 metres elevation, where moisture-laden Caribbean trade winds condense against the continental divide.

Monteverde
Province: Puntarenas, Costa Rica
Elevation: c. 1,400 m
Known for: cloud forest reserves, ecotourism

Settlement

The modern community was founded in the 1950s by Quaker families from Alabama who, facing prosecution as conscientious objectors, emigrated to Costa Rica after it abolished its army. They established dairy farms and, with local conservationists, set aside forest that became the Monteverde Cloud Forest Reserve in 1972.

Biodiversity

The Monteverde reserves protect extraordinary biological richness: over 400 bird species, including the resplendent quetzal; more than 100 mammal species; thousands of insects; and the world's densest concentration of orchid species, over 500. The region was the last known home of the golden toad, an emblematic victim of amphibian declines first described from Monteverde and last seen in 1989.

Tourism and research

Monteverde helped define Costa Rican ecotourism, with canopy walkways, zip-line tours, night hikes, and lodges drawing well over 200,000 visitors in peak years. Research stations host scientists studying climate change, which is pushing the cloud base uphill and drying the mountaintop forests.
