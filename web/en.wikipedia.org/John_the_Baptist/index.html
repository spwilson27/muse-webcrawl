John the Baptist (c. late 1st century BC – c. AD 28–36) was a Judaean preacher active in the area of the Jordan River in the early 1st century AD. He is also known as John the Forerunner in Eastern Orthodoxy and John the Immerser in some Baptist traditions. He is revered as a prophet in Christianity, Islam (as Yaḥyā ibn Zakarīyā), the Baháʼí Faith, and Mandaeism, in which he is the central figure.

Life according to the Gospels

According to the Gospel of Luke, John's birth to the priest Zechariah and his wife Elizabeth — a relative of Mary, mother of Jesus — was announced by the angel Gabriel; the unborn John is said to have leapt in the womb at Mary's visit. John began his ministry around AD 27–29 in the wilderness of Judaea, wearing clothing of camel's hair and eating locusts and wild honey. He preached repentance in view of God's coming judgment and baptised converts by immersion in the Jordan. Large crowds, including tax collectors and soldiers, came to hear him.

Baptism of Jesus and death

John baptised Jesus in the Jordan, an event attested in all four Gospels and regarded by most scholars as historically certain. The Gospels describe Jesus as identifying John with the returning Elijah, and John as pointing to Jesus as the "Lamb of God". Some of John's disciples later became followers of Jesus. John publicly denounced the marriage of Herod Antipas to Herodias, his brother's former wife; Antipas imprisoned him at the fortress of Machaerus and, according to the Gospels, had him beheaded at Herodias's instigation, after Salome's dance. The Jewish historian Josephus independently records John's execution by Antipas, though he attributes it to the tetrarch's fear of sedition.

Legacy and veneration

John is remembered in Christianity as the forerunner who prepared the way for Jesus. His nativity (24 June) and beheading (29 August) are ancient feasts. Shrines claiming his relics include the Umayyad Mosque in Damascus, and relics attributed to him are venerated in several European churches. In Islam he is the prophet Yahya; in Mandaeism he is Yuhana, the great prophet and baptiser.
