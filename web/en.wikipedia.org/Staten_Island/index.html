Staten Island is the southernmost of the five boroughs of New York City, coextensive with Richmond County and situated in the southwest portion of the city. The borough is separated from New Jersey by the Arthur Kill and the Kill Van Kull, and from the rest of New York by New York Bay. With a population of about 496,000 in the 2020 census, it is the least populated borough but the third largest in land area.

History

The island was inhabited by the Lenape before European colonization. The Dutch established the first permanent European settlement, Oude Dorp, in 1661. It was named Staaten Eylandt after the States General of the Netherlands. The borough joined greater New York City in the 1898 consolidation. For much of the twentieth century it remained semi-rural; rapid suburban development followed the opening of the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge in 1964.

Geography

Staten Island is the southernmost point of New York State. Todt Hill, at 401 feet (122 m) above sea level, is the highest natural point in New York City and the highest on the Eastern Seaboard south of Maine. The island contains extensive parkland, including the Staten Island Greenbelt, one of the largest public park systems in the city.

Transportation

The borough is connected to Brooklyn by the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge and to New Jersey by the Bayonne Bridge, the Outerbridge Crossing, and the Goethals Bridge. The free Staten Island Ferry carries passengers across the harbor to Manhattan and is one of the city's most visited attractions. The Staten Island Railway provides rail service along the island's east and south shores.

Culture

Staten Island is home to Historic Richmond Town, a living history village; the Snug Harbor Cultural Center; and the Staten Island Zoo. The borough is often nicknamed "the forgotten borough" by its residents, reflecting its relative geographic and political distance from the rest of the city.
