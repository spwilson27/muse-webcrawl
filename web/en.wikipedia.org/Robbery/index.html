Robbery

Robbery is the crime of taking or attempting to take anything of value by force, threat of force, or by use of fear. The precise definition varies by jurisdiction, but the common-law definition requires the taking of property of another from their person or presence by force or intimidation, with the intent to permanently deprive them of it. Unlike theft, robbery involves a person being threatened or subjected to violence; unlike burglary, it does not require unlawful entry.

Types

Common categories include armed robbery (committed with a weapon), aggravated robbery (involving serious injury, a deadly weapon or an accomplice), bank robbery, carjacking (robbery of a vehicle from its driver), highway robbery (historically, robbery on public roads), and mugging (street robbery, often involving surprise attack). Piracy is essentially robbery committed at sea.

Penalties and statistics

Because it combines property crime with violence against the person, robbery is treated as a serious felony in most systems; in England and Wales, under the Theft Act 1968, it carries a maximum sentence of life imprisonment. Robbery rates are a standard criminological indicator: in the United States the FBI counts robbery among the four violent crimes in its Uniform Crime Reports, and rates have declined substantially since their early-1990s peak.
