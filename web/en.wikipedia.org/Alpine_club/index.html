Alpine club

First founded: Alpine Club, London, 1857
Functions: Huts, trails, publications, training, advocacy
Large members: German Alpine Club (DAV), Austrian Alpine Club (ÖAV)
International body: UIAA

An Alpine club is an organisation devoted to mountaineering, climbing, and the protection of mountain environments. Alpine clubs build and maintain mountain huts and trails, publish guidebooks and journals, run training courses, and campaign on access and conservation. Many were founded in the nineteenth century during the golden age of alpinism.

History

The world's first such body, the Alpine Club, was founded in London in 1857 by British climbers including John Ball, its first president. National clubs followed: the Austrian Alpine Club (1862), the Swiss Alpine Club (1863), the Italian Alpine Club (1863), the German Alpine Club (1869), and the Club Alpin Français (1874). Collectively the German, Austrian, and Swiss clubs operate hundreds of staffed huts across the Alps.

Activities

Beyond recreation, clubs such as those supporting Alpine climbing contribute to mountain rescue, glacier monitoring, and scientific research, and many national clubs are members of the UIAA, the international mountaineering federation founded in 1932.
