A typeface (or font family) is a design of letters, numbers and other symbols, to be used in printing or for electronic display. Most typefaces include variations in size (e.g., 24 point), weight (e.g., light, bold), slope (e.g., italic), width (e.g., condensed), and so on; each such variation is a "font".

Terminology

In professional typography, the term typeface is not interchangeable with the word font: historically, a font was a specific combination of typeface, size, and style (for example, 10-point Caslon Italic was one font, and Caslon Bold at the same size was another). In modern digital usage the distinction has blurred, and a "font" often refers to the digital file implementing a typeface design.

History

Typeface design developed with movable type printing, from the blackletter hands used by Gutenberg in the 1450s to the old-style serif designs of Nicolas Jenson and Claude Garamond in the Renaissance. The Industrial Revolution brought bold display and slab-serif types for advertising; the 20th century added geometric and humanist sans-serifs such as Futura (1927) and Helvetica (1957). Phototypesetting and then digital type, beginning with systems such as METAFONT and PostScript in the 1980s, transformed distribution and design.

Anatomy

Letterforms are described by features including the x-height, ascenders and descenders, counters, apertures, stems, bowls, serifs, and terminals. Measurements such as cap height, baseline, and mean line structure the design and its legibility.

Classification

Typefaces are commonly grouped as serif, sans-serif, script, display/decorative, and monospace. The Vox-ATypI classification, adopted in 1962, sorts designs into categories such as Humanist, Garalde, Transitional, Didone, Slab serif, Lineal (sans-serif), Glyphic, Script, and Graphic.

Intellectual property

Typeface designs receive varying legal protection: in the United States, typeface designs as such are not copyrightable, though font software is; many other jurisdictions offer design protection. Typefaces are typically licensed for use.
