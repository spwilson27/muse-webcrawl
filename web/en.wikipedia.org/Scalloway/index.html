Scalloway

Scalloway (Old Norse: Skálavágr) is the largest settlement on the west coast of the Shetland Mainland and the islands' historic capital until Lerwick took over in 1708. The village had a population of roughly 900 at the 2011 census. It is built around a sheltered harbour at the head of a voe, about 6 miles (10 km) west of Lerwick, and is dominated by Scalloway Castle, built in 1599.

History

The village's name comes from Old Norse Skálavágr, "the bay of the house with the hall," reflecting Shetland's Norse heritage. Scalloway served as the capital of Shetland for centuries, hosting the islands' lawthing and, from the early 17th century, the residence of Earl Patrick Stewart, whose castle—built with forced labour—remains the village's landmark. After the capital moved to Lerwick in 1708, Scalloway settled into life as a fishing port.

During the Second World War Scalloway was a principal shore base of the Shetland bus, the clandestine operation that ran fishing boats between Shetland and German-occupied Norway carrying agents, arms, and refugees. The village's Scalloway Museum, opened in 1985 beside the castle, tells the story of the operation and of the town's castle and fishing history.

Economy and community

Fishing and fish processing remain important, and the harbour serves workboats for the salmon-farming industry. The village is home to the Shetland campus of the North Atlantic Fisheries College Marine Centre (part of the University of the Highlands and Islands), a primary school, a swimming pool, and a health centre. Ferries and small boats connect the harbour with the nearby islands of Trondra and Burra, which are joined to the Mainland by bridges through Scalloway's parish.
