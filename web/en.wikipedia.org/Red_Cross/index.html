Red Cross

Red Cross
Emblem: Red cross on white ground
Adopted: 1863–1864
Related emblems: Red Crescent, Red Crystal
Protected under: Geneva Conventions
The red cross emblem is a protective symbol under the Geneva Conventions.

The red cross is the emblem of the International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement, and its use is protected under the Geneva Conventions and international humanitarian law. It is commonly used as shorthand for the Movement itself and for its components: the International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC), the International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies, and the nearly 190 National Societies, such as the American Red Cross and the British Red Cross.

History

The emblem derives from the work of the Swiss businessman Henry Dunant, whose account of the suffering of wounded soldiers after the Battle of Solferino (1859) led to the founding of the International Committee in Geneva in 1863 and the first Geneva Convention in 1864. The conference adopted, as a tribute to Switzerland, the Swiss flag with its colours reversed — a red cross on a white ground — as the distinctive sign of medical personnel and facilities, to be respected by all belligerents.

Emblems

Because the cross was misunderstood by some as a Christian symbol, the red crescent was adopted by the Ottoman Empire in the 1870s and later recognised in the Conventions; both emblems have identical legal status. In 2005 a third emblem, the red crystal, was adopted by Additional Protocol III, allowing Israel's Magen David Adom to join the Movement by using the crystal in international operations. The emblems have two distinct uses: a protective use in armed conflict, for military and civilian medical services entitled to immunity from attack, and an indicative use by National Societies to show their affiliation, under strict rules.

Protection and misuse

Unauthorised use of the emblem is prohibited by the Geneva Conventions and by national law in most countries, even in peacetime, because diluting the sign endangers medical personnel in war. Perfidy — feigning protected status to attack an enemy — is a war crime. In everyday life the red cross is widely, if improperly, used as a generic symbol of medicine and first aid; the Movement and governments act to restrict such uses, and in video games, for example, the emblem has increasingly been replaced by other symbols following objections from National Societies.

The Movement

The organisations bearing the emblem provide disaster relief, blood services, first aid training, health and social care, tracing of missing persons, and assistance to victims of armed conflict, guided by the Movement's seven fundamental principles: humanity, impartiality, neutrality, independence, voluntary service, unity and universality.
