Armistice Day

Poppies are worn in many Commonwealth countries to mark Armistice Day.

Armistice Day, later known as Remembrance Day in the Commonwealth of Nations and Veterans Day in the United States, is a memorial day observed in Commonwealth member states since the end of the First World War to honour armed forces members who have died in the line of duty. It is observed on 11 November, recalling the end of hostilities in 1918: the Armistice of 11 November 1918 took effect at "the eleventh hour of the eleventh day of the eleventh month."

History

The armistice between the Allies of World War I and Germany was signed in a railway carriage in the Forest of Compiègne on 11 November 1918. The first Armistice Day was held at Buckingham Palace in London in 1919, hosted by King George V. Remembrance Sunday is held in the United Kingdom on the Sunday nearest to 11 November.

Observance

A two-minute silence is traditionally observed at 11 a.m. local time in many countries. The red remembrance poppy, inspired by John McCrae's poem "In Flanders Fields," is worn as a symbol of remembrance in the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, and New Zealand. In the United States, Armistice Day was renamed Veterans Day in 1954 to honour American veterans of all wars.
