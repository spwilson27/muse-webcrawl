Elohist

The Elohist (abbreviated E) is, according to the documentary hypothesis, one of four sources of the Torah, alongside the Yahwist, the Deuteronomist and the Priestly source. It is named for its characteristic use of the word Elohim ("God") rather than the Tetragrammaton (YHWH) for the deity in the early narratives.

The Elohist source is generally dated to the 9th or 8th century BCE and is thought to originate in the northern Kingdom of Israel. It is distinguished by its emphasis on dreams, angels and the fear of God, and by its portrayal of God as more distant and transcendent than in the Yahwist source.

Material attributed to E includes parts of Genesis, Exodus and Numbers, notably the stories of Abraham and Isaac, Jacob's dream at Bethel, and the revelation at the burning bush, where God first discloses the name YHWH to Moses. In the supplementary hypothesis, E material is often treated as a supplement to the Yahwist rather than an independent document.
