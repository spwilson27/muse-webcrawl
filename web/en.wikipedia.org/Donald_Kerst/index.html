Donald Kerst

Donald William Kerst
Born: 1 November 1911, Galena, Illinois, U.S.
Died: 19 August 1993, Madison, Wisconsin, U.S.
Field: Accelerator physics
Known for: Inventing the betatron

Donald William Kerst (1911–1993) was an American physicist who invented the betatron, the first successful induction accelerator for electrons, at the University of Illinois in 1940. The betatron became an important tool in physics research, medicine (radiation therapy), and industry.

Career

Kerst worked at the University of Illinois and, during the Second World War, at Los Alamos on instrumentation for the Manhattan Project. He later moved to the University of Wisconsin–Madison, where he helped found the Midwestern Universities Research Association (MURA) and became a pioneer of colliding-beam and storage-ring concepts that underpin modern particle accelerators.

Honors

Kerst was elected to the National Academy of Sciences and received the Comstock Prize in Physics. He died in Madison, Wisconsin, in 1993.
