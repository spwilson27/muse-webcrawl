Surinam (English colony)

Surinam was an English colony in South America on the northeastern coast of the continent, in what is now the Republic of Suriname. Founded in 1650 by settlers from Barbados under the sponsorship of Francis Willoughby, 5th Baron Willoughby of Parham, it was for a time one of England's most prosperous sugar colonies before being ceded to the Dutch in 1667.

History

The colony was established on the Suriname River and named Willoughbyland. Its capital was Torarica. Enslaved Africans were brought to labour on sugar plantations, and the colony also attracted Jewish settlers who established the settlement of Jodensavanne. During the Second Anglo-Dutch War, a Dutch force under Abraham Crijnssen captured Surinam in 1667. By the Treaty of Breda later that year, the Dutch kept Surinam while England retained New Netherland (New York) in North America — an exchange long remarked on by historians.

Legacy

Many English settlers departed for Jamaica and other colonies after the transfer, taking enslaved people and plantation expertise with them, and English influence on Suriname faded as Dutch rule was consolidated, apart from brief British reoccupations during the Napoleonic Wars.
