Highland Park, Illinois

Highland Park is a city in Lake County, Illinois, United States, on the shore of Lake Michigan about 25 miles (40 km) north of downtown Chicago. It is one of the affluent suburbs of the North Shore. The population was 30,176 at the 2020 census. Highland Park is known as the home of the Ravinia Festival, the summer home of the Chicago Symphony Orchestra, and for its architecture, including houses by Frank Lloyd Wright and other notable Prairie School and modernist architects.

History

The area was settled after the Black Hawk War and the 1833 Treaty of Chicago removed the Potawatomi. The city was founded in 1869 and named for its high, wooded position overlooking Lake Michigan. It developed as a summer retreat and commuter suburb after the railroad arrived, attracting wealthy Chicagoans who built large estates. Highland Park annexed the village of Ravinia in 1899; the Ravinia Festival began in 1904 as an amusement park attraction and became a celebrated classical music venue, hosting the Chicago Symphony Orchestra each summer since 1936. On 4 July 2022, a mass shooting at the city's Independence Day parade killed seven people and injured dozens more, an event that drew national attention to the community.

Geography and neighborhoods

The city is built on a moraine bluff above Lake Michigan, cut by wooded ravines that drain to the lake. Neighborhoods include the historic downtown, Ravinia, Braeside, and Highland Park Estates. The Chicago Botanic Garden lies just southwest of the city in Glencoe.

Education and culture

Highland Park High School (District 113, shared with Deerfield) serves the city, and elementary education is provided by North Shore School District 112. The Highland Park Public Library dates to 1887. Besides Ravinia, cultural institutions include the North Shore's summer theatre and the Midwest Young Artists Conservatory.

Notable people

Residents have included basketball player Michael Jordan, whose former Highland Park mansion became famous in its own right; astronaut John M. Grunsfeld; and interior designer and television personality Nate Berkus grew up in the area.
