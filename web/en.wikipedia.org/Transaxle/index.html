Transaxle

A transaxle is a single mechanical device which combines the functions of an automobile's transmission, axle and differential into one integrated assembly. It is standard in front-wheel-drive cars, where the engine's power must be delivered to the driven wheels in a compact space.

Configurations

In front-engine, front-wheel-drive layouts the transaxle sits beside or beneath the transverse engine. Rear-engine cars such as the original Volkswagen Beetle and many mid-engine sports cars also use transaxles; some front-engine, rear-wheel-drive cars (for example the Porsche 924 and Chevrolet Corvette from 1997) place the transaxle at the rear to improve weight distribution, linked to the engine by a torque tube.

Advantages

Combining the components saves space and weight, shortens the drivetrain and simplifies assembly, though it can complicate servicing and concentrates heat in one casing.
