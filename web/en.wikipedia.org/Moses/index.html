Moses

Moses is considered the most important prophet in Judaism and one of the most important prophets in Christianity, Islam, the Baháʼí Faith, and other Abrahamic religions. According to both the Bible and the Quran, Moses was the leader of the Israelites and lawgiver to whom the authorship, or "acquisition from heaven", of the Torah (the first five books of the Bible) is attributed.

According to the Book of Exodus, Moses was born in a period when his people, the Israelites, an enslaved minority, were increasing in population and, as a result, the Egyptian Pharaoh worried that they might ally themselves with Egypt's enemies. Moses' Hebrew mother, Jochebed, secretly hid him in the bulrushes along the Nile, where he was discovered and adopted by Pharaoh's daughter. After killing an Egyptian slave-master who was beating a Hebrew, Moses fled to Midian, where he encountered the Angel of the Lord, speaking to him from within a burning bush on Mount Horeb, revealed as the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.

The Exodus and the Law

God sent Moses back to Egypt to demand the release of the Israelites. After the Ten Plagues, Moses led the Exodus of the Israelites out of Egypt and across the Red Sea, after which they based themselves at Mount Sinai, where Moses received the Ten Commandments and the Law. After 40 years of wandering in the desert, Moses died on Mount Nebo within sight of the Promised Land, at the age of 120.

Historicity and legacy

The modern scholarly consensus is that Moses is a legendary figure, though the possibility of a historical Moses-like leader in the 13th century BCE cannot be ruled out, and the Egyptian background of the name is consistent with such origins. Moses remains a towering figure in religious law, ethics, and art — famously depicted in Michelangelo's sculpture in Rome — and as a symbol of liberation.
