Curaçao (liqueur)

Curaçao is a liqueur flavoured with the dried peel of the Laraha citrus fruit, a bitter orange descendant grown on the Dutch Caribbean island of Curaçao. It is produced in a range of colours — clear, orange, green, red, and most famously blue — though the colour does not affect the flavour.

Curaçao
Type: Liqueur
Origin: Curaçao, Dutch Caribbean
Flavouring: Laraha orange peel
Alcohol: typically 15–40% ABV

History

Spanish colonists planted Valencia oranges on Curaçao, where the arid climate produced the small, bitter Laraha. Distillers on the island — notably the Senior family, distilling since 1896 — began macerating its aromatic dried peel in spirit with spices to create the liqueur. Blue Curaçao, dyed with food colouring, became a staple of tiki and tropical cocktails in the 20th century.

Production and use

The peel is macerated in alcohol, distilled, sweetened, and sometimes coloured. Generic "triple sec" is a drier French style of curaçao. Curaçao is used in cocktails such as the Blue Hawaiian, Blue Lagoon, and (in its orange form) variations of the Margarita and Mai Tai.
