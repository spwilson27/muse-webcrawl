Synthwave

Synthwave
Also called: Retrowave, outrun
Origin: Mid-2000s, internet scenes
Stylistic roots: 1980s film scores, Italo disco, synth-pop, video game music
Artists: Kavinsky, Perturbator, Carpenter Brut, Gunship

Synthwave (also called retrowave or outrun) is a microgenre of electronic music built on nostalgia for 1980s popular culture, especially action and horror film soundtracks, arcade games, and television themes. It is characterized by analog synthesizer sounds, gated-reverb drums, arpeggiated basslines, and neon-drenched retro-futurist imagery.

History

The style emerged in the mid-2000s among internet-based producers influenced by composers such as John Carpenter, Vangelis, and Tangerine Dream, and by French house acts. It gained wider notice when the 2011 film Drive featured tracks by Kavinsky and others. Substyles range from the dark, aggressive "darksynth" of Perturbator and Carpenter Brut to vocal-led, pop-oriented acts like The Midnight and Gunship.

Aesthetics and influence

Synthwave artwork and videos lean on chrome logos, wireframe grids, sunsets, sports cars, and VHS artefacts. The genre's popularity fed into broader "retrowave" aesthetics in fashion, games, and design during the 2010s, and mainstream pop hits such as The Weeknd's "Blinding Lights" (2019) borrowed its sound.
