Walden

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Walden (first published as Walden; or, Life in the Woods) is an 1854 book by American transcendentalist writer Henry David Thoreau. The text is a reflection upon the author's simple living in natural surroundings over the span of two years, two months and two days, in a cabin he built near Walden Pond in Concord, Massachusetts, on land owned by his friend and mentor Ralph Waldo Emerson.

Walden compresses the time into a single calendar year and uses passages of the four seasons to symbolise human development. It is part personal declaration of independence, social experiment, voyage of spiritual discovery, satire and — to some degree — a manual for self-reliance. The book opens with one of the most famous lines in American literature: "I went to the woods because I wished to live deliberately, to front only the essential facts of life, and see if I could not learn what it had to teach."

Background

Thoreau moved into the cabin on 4 July 1845, Independence Day, and left the pond on 6 September 1847. He valued the experiment as a practical test of transcendentalist philosophy and a protest against the materialism and slavery of his society. The cabin cost him $28.12½ to build. During his stay he drafted his first book, A Week on the Concord and Merrimack Rivers, and an early version of the essay that became "Resistance to Civil Government" ("Civil Disobedience").

Contents

The book contains eighteen chapters, including "Economy", "Where I Lived, and What I Lived For", "Reading", "Sounds", "Solitude", "Visitors", "The Bean-Field", "Brute Neighbors", "The Pond in Winter" and "Spring", closing with a "Conclusion". Thoreau recounts his expenses in meticulous detail, muses on the wildlife of the pond, describes battles between ants, and reflects on the changing seasons and his own spiritual renewal.

Reception and legacy

Initially only a modest success, Walden grew steadily in reputation after Thoreau's death and is now regarded as a classic of American literature and a foundational text of environmental writing. It has influenced figures from Gandhi to modern environmentalists and the voluntary simplicity movement. The site of the cabin, marked by a cairn, is preserved within Walden Pond State Reservation, and the pond was designated a National Historic Landmark.
