The 1996 Formula One season was the 47th season of Formula One motor racing. It comprised sixteen Grands Prix held between March and October 1996. Damon Hill of Williams won the Drivers' Championship, becoming the first son of a world champion — his father Graham Hill — to win the title himself, while Williams-Renault took the Constructors' Championship with 175 points.

Season summary

The season was dominated by the Williams FW18, widely considered one of the most complete cars of its era. Hill won eight races, but his rookie team-mate Jacques Villeneuve — son of Gilles Villeneuve and the reigning CART champion — won four and kept the title race alive until the final round at Suzuka. There Hill's title was sealed when Villeneuve retired with a loose wheel; Hill won the race and the championship by 19 points.

Defending champion Michael Schumacher, in his first season with a struggling Ferrari, won three races, including a celebrated wet-weather victory in Spain and back-to-back wins in Belgium and Italy. The rain-soaked Monaco Grand Prix produced one of the great upsets in F1 history: Olivier Panis won for Ligier as only three cars finished. The season also marked the debut of the 107% qualifying rule, which ended the participation of the uncompetitive Forti team mid-season.

Aftermath

Despite winning the championship, Hill was dropped by Williams for 1997 and moved to Arrows, nearly winning the 1997 Hungarian Grand Prix. Villeneuve stayed and won the 1997 title. The 1996 season thus stands as the high point of Damon Hill's career and the close of Williams' most dominant mid-1990s period.
