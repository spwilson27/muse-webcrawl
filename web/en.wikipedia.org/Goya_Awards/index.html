The Goya Awards (Spanish: Premios Goya) are Spain's main national film awards, considered the Spanish equivalent of the American Academy Awards. They are presented annually by the Academia de las Artes y las Ciencias Cinematográficas de España (Academy of Cinematographic Arts and Sciences of Spain), founded in 1986. The first ceremony took place on 17 March 1987 in Madrid.

The trophy

Winners receive a bronze bust of the painter Francisco de Goya, after whom the awards are named. The original statuette was designed by José Luis Fernández; the first ceremony used a sculpture by Miguel Ortiz Berrocal.

Categories

Awards are given in around thirty categories, including Best Film, Best Director, Best Actor, Best Actress, Best New Director, Best Original Screenplay, and Best Ibero-American Film. An honorary Goya (Goya de Honor) recognises lifetime achievement in Spanish cinema.

Notable winners

Directors such as Pedro Almodóvar, Alejandro Amenábar and Fernando Trueba have been frequent winners. The Sea Inside (2004) holds the record with 14 Goyas from 15 nominations.
