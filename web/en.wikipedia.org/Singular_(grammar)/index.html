Singular (grammar)

In grammar, the singular is a grammatical number denoting one person, thing, or instance, in contrast with the plural (more than one) and, in some languages, the dual (exactly two), trial, or paucal. Singular forms are typically the base or citation forms of nouns, pronouns and verbs in dictionaries.

Marking across languages

English marks the singular mostly by the absence of plural endings on nouns, while verbs show a distinctive third-person singular present-tense form (she runs vs they run). Many languages inflect singular and plural symmetrically; others treat the singular as unmarked and derive the plural from it. Some nouns exist only in the singular (singulare tantum), such as most mass nouns — information, furniture. Agreements between subject and verb, and between nouns and their determiners or adjectives, are governed by singular or plural concord.

Notional rather than formal agreement produces well-known edge cases: collective nouns (the team is/are) and, historically, the singular they used with a singular indefinite antecedent.
