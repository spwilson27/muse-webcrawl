American Constitution Society

The American Constitution Society (ACS), formally the American Constitution Society for Law and Policy, is a progressive legal organization in the United States, founded in 2001 and headquartered in Washington, D.C. ACS aims to promote "the vitality of the U.S. Constitution and the fundamental values it expresses: individual rights and liberties, genuine equality, access to justice, democracy and the rule of law." It is widely regarded as the principal liberal counterpart to the conservative Federalist Society, maintaining chapters at law schools and lawyer chapters in cities across the country.

History

ACS grew out of the Madison Society, formed by Georgetown law professor Peter Rubin and others concerned that conservative legal networks — above all the Federalist Society — had achieved outsized influence over legal discourse and judicial selection. It was publicly launched in 2001, with figures such as former Attorney General Janet Reno and then-Senator Hillary Clinton associated with its early activities (Clinton delivered a keynote at its first national convention in 2004). The organization deliberately borrowed the Federalist Society's chapter-based model to build an infrastructure of progressive law students, academics, practitioners, and judges.

Activities

ACS hosts an annual national convention, publishes scholarship and issue briefs (including through its blog, ACSBlog), runs a Supreme Court review, and coordinates pro bono and judicial-nominations advocacy. It has been an influential voice in debates over judicial appointments, voting rights, reproductive rights, gun regulation, and executive power, arguing for a reading of the Constitution attentive to its egalitarian amendments and to the judiciary's role in protecting minorities. Unlike the Federalist Society, ACS as an organization takes positions on legislation and nominations.

Leadership

Past presidents include its founding president Peter Rubin and Caroline Fredrickson (2009–2019), a former ACLU legislative director. Russ Feingold, former U.S. Senator from Wisconsin, became president in 2020; he was succeeded in 2025 by Phil Brest (announced following Feingold's departure). The board has included prominent legal academics such as Goodwin Liu, Pamela Karlan, and Geoffrey Stone.
