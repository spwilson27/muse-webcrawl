Brimstone Hill Fortress National Park

Brimstone Hill Fortress National Park
Location: Saint Kitts, Saint Kitts and Nevis
Designation: UNESCO World Heritage Site (1999)
Built: 1690s–1790s

Brimstone Hill Fortress National Park is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and national park on the island of Saint Kitts in the federation of Saint Kitts and Nevis in the eastern Caribbean. It preserves one of the best-preserved historical fortifications in the Americas, a massive hilltop fortress built by the British over the course of the 18th century, largely using the labour of enslaved Africans.

History

Brimstone Hill rises about 800 feet (240 m) above the sea; its name comes from the smell of sulphur from nearby volcanic vents. The first cannon were mounted on the hill by the British in 1690 during the Nine Years' War against the French. Construction of the fortress — designed by British military engineers and built by enslaved Africans — continued for over a century. The principal structures include the Citadel, with Fort George, the Western Place of Arms, the Eastern Place of Arms, magazines, barracks, a hospital and an officers' quarters.

In 1782, during the American Revolutionary War, a French force under the Marquis de Bouillé besieged Brimstone Hill; the British garrison surrendered after about a month, but the fortress was returned to Britain by the Treaty of Paris (1783). The British abandoned the fortress in the mid-19th century as its strategic value declined.

Restoration and status

Restoration began in the 20th century, and the area was declared a national park in 1987. In 1999 UNESCO inscribed it as a World Heritage Site, recognising it as an outstanding example of 17th- and 18th-century British military architecture adapted to a Caribbean setting, and as a monument bound up with the history of colonialism and African slavery in the Americas. The site includes a visitor centre (the Discovery Centre) and a museum, and is managed by the Brimstone Hill Fortress National Park Society.
