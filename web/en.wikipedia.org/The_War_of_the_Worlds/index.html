The War of the Worlds is a science fiction novel by English author H. G. Wells, first serialized in 1897 in Pearson's Magazine in the United Kingdom and Cosmopolitan magazine in the United States, and published in hardcover in 1898. It is one of the earliest stories to depict a conflict between humankind and an extraterrestrial race, and it has remained one of the most influential works of science fiction, popularizing the idea of an invasion of Earth by Martians.

Plot summary

The story is narrated by an unnamed writer in Surrey, England. After a series of explosions on Mars, cylinders crash to Earth, beginning with one on Horsell Common near Woking. From them emerge Martians—large, tentacled beings—who assemble towering tripod fighting machines armed with a heat-ray and poisonous black smoke. The British military is overwhelmed; London is evacuated amid scenes of panic. The narrator survives episodes including an encounter with a curate who descends into madness and the destruction of the Martian fighting machines' advance by artillery and the torpedo ram HMS Thunder Child, which sinks two tripods before being destroyed. In the end, the Martians die not by human action but from terrestrial bacteria to which they have no immunity, leaving the narrator to reunite with his wife and contemplate the experience.

Themes and context

The novel has been read as a commentary on British imperialism—Wells drew a parallel with the extermination of Tasmanian Aboriginal people by European colonists—and on Victorian anxieties about invasion literature, evolution, and humanity's place in the universe. Its depiction of total war, weapons of mass destruction, and civilian flight anticipated aspects of 20th-century conflict.

Adaptations and legacy

The most famous adaptation is Orson Welles's 1938 radio drama for The Mercury Theatre on the Air, whose news-bulletin format allegedly panicked some listeners. Film versions include Byron Haskin's 1953 film and Steven Spielberg's 2005 War of the Worlds. Jeff Wayne's 1978 musical version became a long-running album and stage production. The novel has inspired countless alien-invasion stories and remains in print continuously since its publication.
