Case Anton

Case Anton (German: Fall Anton) was the military occupation of France carried out by Germany and Italy in November 1942, ending the nominal independence of Vichy France's "free zone" (zone libre).

Background

After the 1940 armistice, northern and western France were occupied by Germany while the south remained under the Vichy government of Marshal Philippe Pétain. On 8 November 1942, Allied forces landed in French North Africa in Operation Torch. Adolf Hitler, fearing an Allied landing on France's Mediterranean coast and doubting Vichy's reliability, ordered the occupation of the remaining zone.

The operation

German and Italian forces moved into the free zone beginning on 11 November 1942, meeting little resistance; Italy occupied the southeast, including Nice and Corsica. Vichy forces were demobilised, and Germany assumed direct control over most of France while leaving the Vichy administration in place as a subordinate apparatus.

Scuttling of the French fleet

On 27 November 1942, as German forces attempted to seize the French fleet at Toulon, French sailors scuttled their own ships — including the battleships Strasbourg, Dunkerque, and Provence — denying the fleet to the Axis. The occupation intensified French resistance activity and eroded what remained of Vichy's legitimacy.
