Milan Cathedral

Duomo di Milano
Location: Piazza del Duomo, Milan, Italy
Denomination: Roman Catholic
Construction: begun 1386; completed 1965
Style: Italian Gothic, Gothic Revival
Length: 158.6 m; spire height: 106.5 m

Milan Cathedral (Italian: Duomo di Milano), dedicated to the Nativity of St Mary, is the cathedral church of Milan, Lombardy, Italy, and the seat of the Archbishop of Milan. One of the largest churches in the world, it took nearly six centuries to complete.

History

Construction began in 1386 under Archbishop Antonio da Saluzzo, with the patronage of Gian Galeazzo Visconti, who gave the project its distinctive Candoglia marble — pink-veined white marble from quarries near Lake Maggiore, transported along canals. The cathedral was consecrated in 1418 while still unfinished; work continued under successive architects and rulers. Napoleon ordered the facade completed in 1805–1813 and was crowned King of Italy in the cathedral in 1805. The final details were finished only in the twentieth century, with the last gate inaugurated in 1965.

Architecture

The Duomo is famed for its forest of pinnacles and spires — 135 spires and some 3,400 statues — and for the gilded copper statue of the Madonnina (1774) atop the main spire, long the city's beloved symbol. The vast interior, divided by massive pillars, contains the crypt of Charles Borromeo and a nail said to be from the True Cross, displayed annually in the Rite of the Nivola. Stained glass windows, some of the largest in Christendom, illustrate biblical scenes. The rooftop terraces offer views across Milan to the Alps.
