Producer surplus

Producer surplus is the difference between the amount a producer receives for a good and the minimum amount they would accept. On a supply-and-demand diagram it is the area above the supply curve and below the market price, up to the quantity sold.

Concepts

Together with consumer surplus it measures total economic welfare. For an individual firm, producer surplus equals revenue minus variable cost; aggregated over an industry it relates to economic rent earned by scarce inputs. Changes in price, taxes, and subsidies redistribute surplus among consumers, producers, and government, and may create deadweight loss.
