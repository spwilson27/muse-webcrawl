The River Conwy (Welsh: Afon Conwy) is a river in north Wales, about 55 kilometres (34 mi) long, which flows from Llyn Conwy on the Migneint moors to the Irish Sea at Conwy. It drains much of eastern Snowdonia (Eryri).

Course

From its source the river flows north past Ysbyty Ifan and Betws-y-Coed, where it is joined by the Afon Llugwy and Afon Lledr and passes the Swallow Falls. It continues through the fertile Conwy valley past Llanrwst and Trefriw to the tidal estuary, where the walled town and castle of Conwy stand at its mouth.

Crossings and ecology

The estuary is crossed by Thomas Telford's Conwy Suspension Bridge (1826), Robert Stephenson's tubular railway bridge (1849) and a modern road bridge and immersed tube tunnel. The Conwy is an important salmon and sea trout river, and its estuary supports mussel fisheries and extensive mudflats of conservation value.
