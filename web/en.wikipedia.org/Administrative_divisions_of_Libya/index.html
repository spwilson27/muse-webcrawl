Administrative divisions of Libya

The administrative divisions of Libya are currently based on 22 districts known as shabiyaat (singular shabiyah). Historically the country was divided into three large regions — Tripolitania in the northwest, Fezzan in the southwest, and Cyrenaica in the east — which became governorates (muhafazat) under the monarchy and early republic.

History

After independence in 1951 Libya was a federation of three states; it became a unitary state in 1963 with ten governorates. Under Muammar Gaddafi, local administration was repeatedly reorganized: municipalities (baladiyaat) were introduced in the 1970s and 1980s, and the shabiyah system followed in the 1990s and 2000s, with the number of districts varying between 13 and 32 at different times before settling at 22 in 2007.

Districts

The 22 districts include Tripoli, Benghazi, Misrata, Jabal al Akhdar, Derna, Tobruk, Sabha, and Sirte. Since the civil conflicts that followed 2011, effective administration has often been exercised by municipalities and local councils, many of which held elections under a 2012 local administration law.
