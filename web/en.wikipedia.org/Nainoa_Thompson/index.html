Nainoa Thompson

Charles Nainoa Thompson (born March 11, 1953) is a Native Hawaiian navigator and the president of the Polynesian Voyaging Society. He is best known as the first Hawaiian in centuries to practise the ancient art of Polynesian wayfinding — navigation by the stars, ocean swells, winds and wildlife — on long-distance voyages aboard the double-hulled canoe Hōkūleʻa.

Navigation career

Thompson studied under the Satawalese master navigator Mau Piailug, one of the last traditional navigators of Micronesia. In 1980 Thompson navigated Hōkūleʻa from Hawaiʻi to Tahiti and back without instruments, the first Native Hawaiian to do so in modern times. He developed a modern Hawaiian star compass adapted from Piailug's teachings. He subsequently navigated voyages throughout Polynesia, including the 1985–87 "Voyage of Rediscovery".

Mālama Honua worldwide voyage

As president of the Polynesian Voyaging Society, Thompson led the Mālama Honua Worldwide Voyage (2013–2019), in which Hōkūleʻa and the sister canoe Hikianalia circumnavigated the Earth to promote care for the environment — "mālama honua" meaning "to care for the Earth".
