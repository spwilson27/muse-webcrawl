Sebastian Thrun

Sebastian Thrun (born May 14, 1967) is a German-American entrepreneur, educator, and computer scientist. A pioneer of self-driving cars, he led the Stanford team whose vehicle "Stanley" won the 2005 DARPA Grand Challenge, co-founded Google X and led the development of Google's self-driving car project (later Waymo), and co-founded the online education company Udacity.

Career

Thrun studied computer science in Germany and earned his doctorate from the University of Bonn in 1995. He held faculty positions at Carnegie Mellon University and Stanford, where his work on probabilistic robotics became a standard reference. In 2005 his Stanford Racing Team's autonomous vehicle Stanley completed the DARPA Grand Challenge desert course, winning the million prize.

Google and Udacity

At Google, Thrun co-founded the semi-secret Google X laboratory and led the self-driving car project, as well as contributing to Google Glass. In 2011 he co-taught a free online artificial intelligence course that attracted some 160,000 students; the experience led him to co-found Udacity in 2012, a major provider of online courses and "nanodegrees".

Flying cars

Thrun later served as chief executive of Kitty Hawk Corporation, an electric flying-vehicle company backed by Larry Page, until its wind-down in 2022.
