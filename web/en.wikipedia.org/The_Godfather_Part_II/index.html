The Godfather Part II is a 1974 American epic crime film produced and directed by Francis Ford Coppola, partially based on Mario Puzo's 1969 novel The Godfather. It is both a sequel and a prequel to The Godfather (1972), interweaving the story of Michael Corleone (Al Pacino) as the new head of the Corleone family in the late 1950s with flashbacks to his father Vito Corleone's (Robert De Niro) rise in early 20th-century Sicily and New York.

Plot

As Michael moves the family business to Nevada and survives an assassination attempt, Senate hearings and betrayals force him into ever colder ruthlessness. In parallel, the young Vito builds his criminal empire after fleeing Sicily as a child. The film closes with Michael alone, having ordered the killing of his brother Fredo and alienated his wife Kay.

Reception and legacy

The film won six Academy Awards, including Best Picture — the first sequel ever to win the award — Best Director, and Best Supporting Actor for De Niro. It is routinely ranked among the greatest films ever made and was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry in 1993.
