Theodore von Kármán

Theodore von Kármán (born Kármán Tódor; May 11, 1881 – May 6, 1963) was a Hungarian-American mathematician, aerospace engineer and physicist, a central figure in 20th-century aerodynamics and fluid mechanics. He is known for the von Kármán vortex street, the Kármán line defining the boundary of space at 100 km altitude, and for co-founding NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory.

BornMay 11, 1881, Budapest, Austria-Hungary
DiedMay 6, 1963, Aachen, West Germany
FieldsAerodynamics, fluid mechanics
InstitutionsCaltech, JPL

Career

After work in Göttingen under Ludwig Prandtl and a professorship at Aachen, von Kármán emigrated to the United States in 1930 to direct the Guggenheim Aeronautical Laboratory at Caltech. His students founded the "Suicide Squad" rocket group that became JPL.

Honours

In 1963 he became the first recipient of the National Medal of Science. Craters on the Moon and Mars bear his name.
