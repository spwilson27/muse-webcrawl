First United Front

Years1924–1927
PartiesKuomintang (KMT) and Chinese Communist Party (CCP)
PurposeUnify China; defeat the warlords

The First United Front was an alliance between the Kuomintang (KMT) and the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) formed in 1924 to end warlordism in China and unify the country under the Republic of China.

Formation

Negotiated with the encouragement of the Soviet Union and the Comintern, the alliance followed the Sun–Joffe Manifesto of 1923. Communists joined the KMT as individuals while retaining their separate party organisation. Soviet advisers, including Mikhail Borodin, helped reorganise the KMT, and the Whampoa Military Academy was founded to train a party army.

Northern Expedition and collapse

In 1926 the allies launched the Northern Expedition under Chiang Kai-shek to defeat the warlords. As the campaign succeeded, tensions between the KMT right and the communists and labour movement erupted. In April 1927 Chiang purged communists in Shanghai, and by July the left-KMT government in Wuhan also broke with the CCP, ending the front and beginning open civil war between the two parties.
