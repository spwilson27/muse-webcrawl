Postcolonial literature

Origins: Mid-20th century, following decolonisation

Key figures: Chinua Achebe, Salman Rushdie, Edward Said, Ngũgĩ wa Thiong'o

Themes: Identity, hybridity, empire, resistance, language

Postcolonial literature is the body of literature written by people from countries formerly colonised by European powers, as well as works that engage with the cultural, political and psychological consequences of colonialism and decolonisation. It encompasses writing from Africa, Asia, the Caribbean, the Pacific and the settler colonies, in English, French, Portuguese and many indigenous languages.

The field emerged in the aftermath of the Second World War, as dozens of nations in Africa and Asia won independence. Writers such as Chinua Achebe, whose novel Things Fall Apart (1958) retold the colonisation of Nigeria from an Igbo perspective, sought to reclaim narrative authority from colonial accounts and to represent their societies in their own terms.

History

Although anti-colonial writing existed earlier — in the work of figures such as Rabindranath Tagore and Aimé Césaire — postcolonial literature is usually dated from the independence movements of the 1940s–1960s. The 1980s saw the rise of postcolonial theory, shaped by Edward Said's Orientalism (1978), and by critics such as Homi K. Bhabha and Gayatri Chakravorty Spivak, whose concepts of hybridity, mimicry and the subaltern became central to the discipline.

Themes

Recurring themes include the recovery of suppressed histories, the tension between indigenous languages and the colonial tongue, migration and diaspora, and the question of national and personal identity after empire. Many writers experiment with form — mixing oral storytelling with the novel, or fragmenting chronology — to resist inherited literary conventions.

Notable authors

Major figures include Wole Soyinka, Salman Rushdie, Derek Walcott, V. S. Naipaul, Jamaica Kincaid, Arundhati Roy, Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie and Ngũgĩ wa Thiong'o, who famously renounced English in favour of writing in Gikuyu.
