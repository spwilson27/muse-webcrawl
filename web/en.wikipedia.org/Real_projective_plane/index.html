Real projective plane

In mathematics, the real projective plane, denoted RP² or P², is the two-dimensional real projective space. It is the space of lines through the origin in three-dimensional space R³, or equivalently the ordinary Euclidean plane together with an added "line at infinity" whose points correspond to directions in the plane, so that any two distinct lines meet in exactly one point.

Topology

Topologically, the real projective plane is a compact non-orientable surface. It can be obtained from a disc by identifying antipodal points on the boundary, or from a Möbius strip by attaching a disc along its boundary circle. It cannot be embedded in ordinary three-dimensional Euclidean space without self-intersection; models such as the cross-cap and Boy's surface (an immersion) illustrate its structure. Its Euler characteristic is 1.

Geometry

Real projective geometry studies the properties of figures invariant under projective transformations. In homogeneous coordinates, points of the plane are represented by triples [x : y : z] up to nonzero scalar multiple. The real projective plane is the simplest example of a projective space and a fundamental example in algebraic geometry, where it is the natural setting for the study of plane curves and theorems such as Bézout's theorem.
