Passion play

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Oberammergau Passion Play, performed roughly every ten years since 1634

A Passion play is a dramatic presentation depicting the Passion of Jesus Christ: his trial, suffering and death. Passion plays grew out of medieval liturgical drama and mystery plays, and they are still performed in many communities, usually during Lent or Holy Week, by amateur casts drawn from the locality.

History

From the ceremonies of Holy Week there developed, in the medieval period, elaborate vernacular dramas of the life and death of Christ, performed in churchyards, marketplaces and purpose-built scaffolds. Whole cycles of mystery plays were mounted by town guilds in places such as York, Chester and Wakefield in England, and in towns across France and Germany. After the Reformation and Counter-Reformation many civic performances were suppressed or brought under tighter clerical control, but the tradition survived strongly in Catholic regions, notably in Bavaria and Austria.

Oberammergau

The most famous Passion play is that of Oberammergau in Bavaria. According to tradition, in 1633, during the Thirty Years' War and an outbreak of plague, the villagers vowed that if God spared them they would perform a play of the Passion every ten years. The first performance took place in 1634, and the play has been staged roughly decennially ever since — in recent cycles involving some two thousand performers, singers and technicians, virtually all residents of the village, before audiences of hundreds of thousands. The text and staging have been repeatedly revised, including to remove elements criticised as antisemitic.

Other traditions

Notable Passion plays are performed in many places, among them the Good Friday re-enactments in Iztapalapa, Mexico City, which draw enormous crowds; the plays of Sordevolo in Italy and Tegelen in the Netherlands; the Great Passion Play at Eureka Springs, Arkansas; and street processions with dramatic elements during Semana Santa in Spain and the Philippines. The genre also stands behind modern film and stage treatments of the Passion.
