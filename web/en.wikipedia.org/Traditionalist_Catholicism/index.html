Traditionalist Catholicism

Traditionalist Catholicism is a movement within the Catholic Church characterised by customs, devotions and liturgical practices associated with the period before the Second Vatican Council (1962–1965), especially the celebration of the Tridentine Mass in Latin. Traditionalists typically resist many of the liturgical and doctrinal changes that followed the Council.

History

The movement emerged in reaction to the reforms of Vatican II and the introduction of the Mass of Paul VI in 1969. Archbishop Marcel Lefebvre founded the Society of Saint Pius X (SSPX) in 1970; his unauthorised consecration of four bishops in 1988 led to declarations of excommunication and a long-standing irregular canonical situation. Other groups, such as the Priestly Fraternity of Saint Peter (FSSP), celebrate the older liturgy in full communion with Rome.

Beliefs and practices

Traditionalist Catholics emphasise the Latin Mass, Gregorian chant, traditional catechesis and devotions, and frequently criticise ecumenism, religious liberty documents and collegiality as departures from earlier teaching. Positions range from acceptance of the Council interpreted in continuity with tradition, to sedevacantism — the belief that the papal see is vacant.
