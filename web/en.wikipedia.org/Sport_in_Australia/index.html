Sport in Australia

Sport in Australia is a popular recreational activity, and forms an important part of Australian culture. Australia has participated in international sports events ranging from the Olympic Games to the Commonwealth Games, and has hosted major events including the Olympic Games in Melbourne in 1956 and Sydney in 2000, and the Commonwealth Games several times, most recently on the Gold Coast in 2018.

History

Organised sport in Australia developed from British traditions introduced during colonisation. Cricket was the first sport to take hold, with the first recorded match played in Sydney in 1803. Horse racing, rowing and boxing also became popular in the nineteenth century. Australian rules football was codified in Melbourne in 1859, making it one of the oldest football codes in the world, and rugby league broke away from rugby union in 1908 over disputes about professionalism.

Popular sports

Cricket is historically the most popular summer sport, and the Australian men's cricket team is among the most successful in the world, having won the Cricket World Cup a record number of times. Australian rules football, played mainly in Victoria, South Australia, Western Australia, Tasmania and the Northern Territory, is the most popular winter spectator sport, governed by the Australian Football League. Rugby league and rugby union dominate the winter sporting landscape in New South Wales and Queensland, while association football (soccer) has the highest participation rates, particularly among children.

Attendance and participation

Australian rules football, cricket, horse racing, motorsport and association football regularly draw large crowds. The Melbourne Cricket Ground, with a capacity of over 100,000, is the largest stadium in the Southern Hemisphere and hosts the AFL Grand Final and the Boxing Day Test match each year.
