Mohawk Valley

The Mohawk Valley is a region of the U.S. state of New York that follows the Mohawk River westward from the Hudson River near Albany to the area around Rome and Utica. The valley forms a natural corridor through the Appalachian Mountains, bounded by the Adirondack Mountains to the north and the Catskill Mountains and Allegheny Plateau to the south. It takes its name from the Mohawk people, one of the Five Nations of the Iroquois Confederacy, whose homeland lay in the valley.

Geography

The Mohawk River flows roughly 149 miles (240 km) from its source in Oneida County eastward to join the Hudson at Cohoes. Because the valley cuts through the Appalachian barrier at a low elevation, it has historically served as a vital transportation route between the Atlantic seaboard and the Great Lakes and the interior of North America. Major communities along the valley include Schenectady, Amsterdam, Herkimer, Utica and Rome.

History

Before European settlement, the valley was the eastern doorway of the Iroquois Confederacy. Dutch and later English colonists established trading posts and farms in the 17th and 18th centuries, and the valley was heavily contested during the French and Indian War and the American Revolutionary War, including the Battle of Oriskany in 1777. The completion of the Erie Canal in 1825, which followed the Mohawk Valley for much of its length, transformed the region into a major commercial and industrial corridor, later paralleled by railroads and the New York State Thruway.

Economy

Nineteenth- and early twentieth-century industry in the valley included textiles, carpet manufacturing in Amsterdam, firearms production in Ilion (Remington Arms) and typewriter and early computing manufacturing. Although heavy industry declined after the Second World War, the region remains known for agriculture, small manufacturing, and newer technology development, including nanotechnology research in the Utica and Albany areas.
