Tokugawa Yoshinobu

Tokugawa Yoshinobu (28 October 1837 – 22 November 1913), also known as Hitotsubashi Yoshinobu, was the 15th and last shōgun of the Tokugawa shogunate of Japan.

Rise

Born into the Mito branch of the Tokugawa clan and adopted into the Hitotsubashi house, Yoshinobu was a leading candidate in the shogunal succession dispute of 1858 but lost to the young Tokugawa Iemochi. After Iemochi's death during the second Chōshū expedition, Yoshinobu became shogun in 1866, inheriting a regime weakened by foreign pressure, inflation and the growing Sonnō jōi and imperial restoration movements.

Fall of the shogunate

In November 1867 Yoshinobu formally returned political authority to the Emperor (Taisei Hōkan), hoping to retain a leading role in a new government. Dissatisfied imperial loyalists instead proclaimed the Meiji Restoration, and after defeat at the Battle of Toba–Fushimi in January 1868 Yoshinobu withdrew to Edo, surrendered Edo Castle, and retired into seclusion, avoiding a long civil war in the capital.

Later life

Pardoned and eventually ennobled as a prince in the new peerage, Yoshinobu devoted himself to photography, cycling and other pursuits, living long enough to become a respected elder figure of the Meiji era.
