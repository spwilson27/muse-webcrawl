Precision engineering

Precision engineering concerns components and machines built to tolerances of micrometres or less.

Precision engineering is a subdiscipline of mechanical engineering and manufacturing concerned with designing machines, fixtures and structures that have exceptionally low tolerances, are repeatable, and are stable over time. It underpins industries such as semiconductor lithography, aerospace, optics, medical devices and scientific instrumentation, where errors must be held to micrometres, nanometres or even picometres.

Principles

Key principles include deterministic design — using exact-constraint (kinematic) mounting so that parts locate repeatably without overconstraint — careful management of error budgets, thermal stability, vibration isolation, and metrology capable of measuring to the accuracy being produced. The classic text Precision Machine Design by Alexander Slocum codified many of these rules, and Taniguchi's curves describe the historical improvement of achievable machining accuracy, from ~100 µm in 1900 toward sub-nanometre levels in modern processes such as ion-beam figuring.

Applications

Precision engineering enables EUV lithography machines that position wafers with nanometre accuracy, ultra-precision diamond turning of optical components, coordinate measuring machines, atomic force microscopes, and the flexure-based nanopositioning stages used in microscopy and photonics.
