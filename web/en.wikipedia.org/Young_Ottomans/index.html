The Young Ottomans (Turkish: Yeni Osmanlılar) were a secret society of Ottoman intellectuals, journalists and officials founded in 1865 who sought to transform the absolute monarchy of the Ottoman Empire into a constitutional government modelled on European parliamentary systems, while grounding reform in Islamic principles and Ottoman patriotism.

Ideas and activity

Members such as the poet-playwright Namık Kemal and Ziya Pasha popularised concepts including vatan (fatherland), liberty (hürriyet) and constitutional rule through newspapers published in Istanbul and, after government suppression, in exile in Paris, London and Geneva. They criticised the Tanzimat bureaucracy's authoritarianism and argued that consultation (meşveret) was authentically Islamic. Playwright İbrahim Şinasi's pioneering journalism helped create an Ottoman reading public.

The constitution of 1876

Allied with the statesman Midhat Pasha, the Young Ottomans helped bring about the deposition of Sultan Abdülaziz in 1876 and the proclamation of the first Ottoman constitution, the Kanun-i Esasi, with an elected parliament. Sultan Abdülhamid II suspended parliament in 1878 and exiled Midhat Pasha, ending the First Constitutional Era — but the Young Ottomans' ideas fed directly into the later Young Turk movement and the 1908 revolution.
