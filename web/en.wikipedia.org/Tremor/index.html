Tremor

A tremor is an involuntary, somewhat rhythmic muscle contraction and relaxation involving oscillations or twitching movements of one or more body parts. It is the most common of all involuntary movements and can affect the hands, arms, eyes, face, head, vocal folds, trunk and legs. Most tremors occur in the hands. In some people, a mild tremor is a normal physiological phenomenon; in others it is a symptom of a neurological or metabolic disorder.

Classification

Tremors are commonly classified by when they occur. Resting tremor appears when the affected part is supported and at rest, and classically accompanies Parkinson's disease ("pill-rolling" tremor). Action tremor occurs during voluntary movement and includes postural tremor (holding a position against gravity), kinetic tremor and intention tremor, which worsens as the hand approaches a target and suggests cerebellar disease.

Causes

The most common pathological tremor is essential tremor, a usually bilateral postural and kinetic tremor of the hands and head that often runs in families and improves temporarily with alcohol. Other causes include enhanced physiological tremor (from anxiety, caffeine, hyperthyroidism or certain drugs such as lithium and valproate), dystonic tremor, multiple sclerosis and stroke. Evaluation considers the tremor's distribution, frequency (typically 4–12 Hz) and associated signs.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the cause. Essential tremor may respond to propranolol or primidone, and severe cases to deep brain stimulation of the thalamus or focused ultrasound thalamotomy. Parkinsonian tremor is managed as part of overall Parkinson's treatment, principally with dopaminergic medication.
