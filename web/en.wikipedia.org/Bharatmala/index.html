Bharatmala

CountryIndia
Launched2017
Implementing agencyNational Highways Authority of India (NHAI)
Phase I scope~34,800 km of highways

Bharatmala Pariyojana is a centrally sponsored highway development programme of the Government of India, approved in October 2017. Described as a "garland of roads" around the country (bharat = India, mala = garland), it is the successor umbrella programme to the National Highways Development Project.

Scope

Phase I, originally estimated at about ₹5.35 lakh crore, covers roughly 34,800 km: some 24,800 km of new national highways plus about 10,000 km of residual NHDP works. Its components include economic corridors linking production and consumption centres, inter-corridor and feeder roads, national corridor efficiency improvements, border and international-connectivity roads, coastal and port-connectivity roads, and greenfield expressways.

Rationale

The programme marked a shift from building roads primarily to relieve congestion toward corridor-based planning based on origin–destination freight studies, with the aims of reducing logistics costs, connecting district headquarters and border regions, and integrating with the Sagarmala port-led development programme. Government studies projected that Bharatmala would move a substantially larger share of freight onto a smaller, higher-standard network of corridors.

Progress and criticism

Implementation has been handled by the NHAI, state public works departments, and the NHIDCL. The programme has faced delays and large cost escalations, attributed by auditors and press reports to land-acquisition difficulties, scope revisions, and slow awarding of projects; revised estimates have run far above the original outlay, prompting refinancing and asset-monetisation measures.
