Sale of Goods Act 1979

Citation1979 c. 54
JurisdictionUnited Kingdom
Royal assent6 December 1979
Commencement1 January 1980
StatusAmended; consumer provisions largely replaced by the Consumer Rights Act 2015

The Sale of Goods Act 1979 is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom which regulated English contract law and UK commercial law in respect of goods sold and bought. It consolidated the Sale of Goods Act 1893 and subsequent amendments, codifying the common-law rules governing contracts for the sale of goods.

Implied terms

The Act's best-known provisions imply terms into contracts of sale. Section 12 implies a term that the seller has the right to sell the goods; section 13 requires goods sold by description to correspond with that description; section 14 (as amended in 1994) requires goods sold in the course of a business to be of satisfactory quality and reasonably fit for any purpose made known to the seller; and section 15 requires goods sold by sample to correspond with the sample. Breach of these terms by the seller generally entitled the buyer to reject the goods and treat the contract as repudiated, subject to rules on acceptance.

Passing of property and risk

Part III of the Act lays down rules, in sections 16–20, for determining when ownership (property) in goods passes from seller to buyer where the contract does not specify, and provides that risk normally passes with property. The Act also protects certain third parties, as where a buyer in possession resells goods to an innocent purchaser.

Consumer reform

For consumer contracts made on or after 1 October 2015, most of the Act's implied-term and remedies regime was replaced by the Consumer Rights Act 2015, which introduced a 30-day short-term right to reject and a structured repair/replacement scheme. The 1979 Act continues to govern business-to-business sales. See also Consumer Rights Act 2015 and English contract law.
