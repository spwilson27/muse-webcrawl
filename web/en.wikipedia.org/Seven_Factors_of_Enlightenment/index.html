Seven Factors of Enlightenment

The Seven Factors of Enlightenment (Pali: satta bojjhaṅgā or satta sambojjhaṅgā; Sanskrit: sapta bodhyaṅga) are seven mental qualities in Buddhism that, when developed and balanced, lead toward awakening (bodhi). They are traditionally listed among the thirty-seven aids to enlightenment.

The seven factors

  1. Mindfulness (sati) — maintaining awareness of phenomena in the present moment.
  2. Investigation (dhamma vicaya) — discerning investigation of phenomena and the teachings.
  3. Energy (viriya) — effort and determination in practice.
  4. Joy or rapture (pīti) — an uplifting interest in the object of meditation.
  5. Tranquility (passaddhi) — calm of body and mind.
  6. Concentration (samādhi) — one-pointed unification of mind.
  7. Equanimity (upekkhā) — even-minded observation without craving or aversion.

Balancing the factors

In the early discourses, the factors are developed in sequence, and practitioners are advised to balance the energizing factors (investigation, energy, joy) against the calming factors (tranquility, concentration, equanimity), with mindfulness overseeing both groups.
