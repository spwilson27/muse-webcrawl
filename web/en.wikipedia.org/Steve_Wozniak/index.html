Stephen Gary Wozniak (born August 11, 1950), also known as "Woz", is an American computer engineer, inventor, and technology entrepreneur who co-founded Apple Inc. with Steve Jobs and Ronald Wayne in 1976. He single-handedly designed the Apple I and Apple II computers, the latter being one of the first highly successful mass-produced microcomputers.

Early life

Wozniak grew up in the Santa Clara Valley (later Silicon Valley), the son of an engineer at Lockheed. He showed an early talent for electronics, building his own amateur radio equipment and winning science fair prizes. At the Homebrew Computer Club he met Steve Jobs, with whom he had earlier collaborated on the "blue box" phone-phreaking device.

Apple

In 1976 Wozniak designed the Apple I, a single-board computer sold as a kit. Its successor, the Apple II (1977), integrated a keyboard, color graphics, and sound in a molded plastic case, and — aided by VisiCalc, the first spreadsheet — became a landmark personal computer. Wozniak also designed the Disk II floppy controller, celebrated for its minimal chip count.

Later career

After a plane crash in 1981 and a period away from Apple, Wozniak returned but left full-time work in 1985, later completing his degree at UC Berkeley under the pseudonym "Rocky Raccoon Clark". He founded companies including CL9 and Wheels of Zeus, supported education and philanthropy, co-founded the Electronic Frontier Foundation, and was inducted into the National Inventors Hall of Fame in 2000.
