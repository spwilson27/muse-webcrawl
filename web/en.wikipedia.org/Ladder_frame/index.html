Ladder frame

A ladder frame is the most widely used form of vehicle chassis in body-on-frame construction. It consists of two long, heavy longitudinal steel rails (stringers) connected by a series of shorter cross members, resembling a ladder when viewed from above. The body, engine, transmission and suspension are all bolted to this frame rather than forming part of a unified structure.

Characteristics

Ladder frames are strong in bending, cheap to build, easy to lengthen or modify, and tolerant of rough use and heavy towing, which is why they remain standard for pickup trucks, large SUVs and commercial vehicles. Their disadvantages are greater weight, lower torsional stiffness than a well-designed monocoque, and poorer crash performance because the rigid frame transmits more impact energy to the cabin. Passenger cars have almost entirely shifted to unibody (monocoque) construction, pioneered in mass production from the 1930s and near-universal by the 1980s.
