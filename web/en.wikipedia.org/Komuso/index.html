Komusō

Komusō (Japanese: 虚無僧, "priest of nothingness") were mendicant monks of the Fuke school of Zen Buddhism during Japan's Edo period (1603–1868). They were recognisable by the tengai, a deep woven basket hat covering the whole head, symbolising absence of ego.

Practice

Komusō played the shakuhachi bamboo flute as a form of meditation known as suizen ("blowing Zen"), performing solo pieces called honkyoku while begging for alms. Many were former samurai (rōnin), and the privileges granted to the order — free travel and anonymity under the hat — led the Tokugawa shogunate to regulate it amid fears of espionage. The Fuke sect was abolished in 1871 during the Meiji Restoration, though the shakuhachi tradition survived.
