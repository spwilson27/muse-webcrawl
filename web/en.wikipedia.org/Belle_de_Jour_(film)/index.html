Belle de Jour (film)

Belle de Jour is a 1967 French drama film directed by Luis Buñuel and starring Catherine Deneuve, Jean Sorel and Michel Piccoli. Based on the 1928 novel of the same name by Joseph Kessel, the film is about a young woman who spends her weekday afternoons as a high-class prostitute while her husband is at work. It is one of Buñuel's most famous and commercially successful films.

Plot

Séverine Serizy (Deneuve), a beautiful young housewife, is unable to share physical intimacy with her husband Pierre despite loving him, and is troubled by violent masochistic fantasies. Hearing that a friend works at a high-class brothel, she begins working there secretly during the afternoons under the name "Belle de Jour". Her double life unravels when a young gangster client becomes obsessed with her.

Reception

The film won the Golden Lion at the Venice Film Festival and is frequently listed among the greatest films ever made. Buñuel deliberately blurs Séverine's fantasies and reality, and the ambiguous ending has been debated by critics for decades.
