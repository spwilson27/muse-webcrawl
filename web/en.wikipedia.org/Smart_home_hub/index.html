Smart home hub

A smart home hub is a hardware device or software application that connects the components of a home automation system and mediates communications between them, allowing devices from different manufacturers to be controlled from one interface — typically a smartphone app or voice assistant. Hubs bridge the many wireless protocols used by smart devices, such as Zigbee, Z-Wave, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and Thread, and execute automation rules or "routines," either locally or through cloud services.

Functions

A hub discovers and registers devices, translates between their protocols, and issues commands — turning lights on at sunset, locking doors when the household leaves, adjusting heating based on occupancy, and so on. Early systems such as SmartThings (launched after a 2012 crowdfunding campaign) and Wink centered on a dedicated physical hub with radios for multiple protocols. Later, smart speakers and displays from Amazon (Echo), Google (Nest) and Apple (HomePod) absorbed hub functions, and some routers and set-top boxes include hub radios as well.

Interoperability and Matter

Fragmentation between ecosystems was long a central complaint of home automation: devices bought for one platform might not work with another. The Matter standard, released in 2022 by the Connectivity Standards Alliance with backing from Apple, Google, Amazon and Samsung, is an application-layer protocol running over Wi-Fi, Ethernet and Thread that is intended to let certified devices work across platforms, with bridges connecting older Zigbee and Z-Wave devices. Hubs increasingly act as "Matter controllers" and Thread border routers.

Local versus cloud control

Hub-based systems differ in how much processing happens locally versus in a vendor's cloud. Cloud dependence can add latency and has left devices orphaned when companies shut down services, as with the Insteon and Wink disruptions, spurring interest in local-first platforms such as the open-source Home Assistant. Privacy and security considerations also weigh toward local processing of sensor and camera data.
