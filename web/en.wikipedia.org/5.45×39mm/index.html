The 5.45×39mm cartridge is a rimless bottlenecked intermediate cartridge. It was introduced into service in 1974 by the Soviet Union for the newly developed AK-74 assault rifle. The 5.45×39mm cartridge gradually replaced the 7.62×39mm cartridge in Soviet and Warsaw Pact service as the primary military service rifle cartridge. It remains in widespread use with the Russian military and many other armed forces.

History

Soviet small-arms designers observed the American 5.56×45mm NATO cartridge used in the M16 rifle during the Vietnam War and sought a comparable small-calibre, high-velocity round. Work began in the late 1960s at TsNIITochMash, and the resulting cartridge was adopted together with the AK-74 in 1974. Its small, light bullet allowed soldiers to carry more ammunition and produced controllable recoil in automatic fire.

Design

The cartridge uses a tapered steel case to aid extraction and a long, slender boat-tail bullet. The standard 7N6 projectile contains a mild steel core with a small air pocket in the tip, which shifts the centre of gravity rearward and promotes yawing in soft tissue. Later loadings include the improved-penetration 7N10, armour-piercing 7N22 and 7N24, and tracer variants such as the 7T3.
