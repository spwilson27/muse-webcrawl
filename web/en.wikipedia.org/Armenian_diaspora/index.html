Armenian diaspora

The Armenian diaspora refers to the communities of Armenians living outside Armenia and the self-proclaimed (now dissolved) Republic of Artsakh. It is estimated that more Armenians live abroad — perhaps eight to eleven million people — than in Armenia itself, whose population is around three million.

History

Armenian merchant and artisan communities existed across Persia, the Ottoman Empire, and Europe for centuries; Shah Abbas I forcibly resettled Armenians to New Julfa in Isfahan in 1604–1605. The modern diaspora was decisively shaped by the Armenian genocide of 1915 in the Ottoman Empire, which scattered survivors across the Middle East, France, the United States, and Latin America. After the Second World War and again after the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991, large numbers emigrated, notably to Russia and the United States.

Major communities

The largest communities are in Russia and the United States (especially Los Angeles, often called "Little Armenia" in its Glendale enclave), followed by France, Georgia, Iran, Lebanon, Syria, Argentina, and Canada. The Armenian Apostolic Church, Armenian schools, and cultural organisations anchor diaspora identity.

Relations with Armenia

The diaspora plays a major role in Armenian politics, remittances, and philanthropy, and campaigns in many countries for official recognition of the Armenian genocide.
