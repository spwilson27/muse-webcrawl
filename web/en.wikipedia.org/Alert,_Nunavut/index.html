Alert, Nunavut

Alert, in the Qikiqtaaluk Region of Nunavut, Canada, is the northernmost continuously inhabited place in the world. It is located on Ellesmere Island (Queen Elizabeth Islands) at latitude 82°30'05" north, approximately 830 kilometres (520 mi) south of the geographic North Pole.

The settlement takes its name from HMS Alert, a British ship that wintered near Cape Sheridan in 1875–76 during the British Arctic Expedition.

History

A weather station was established at Alert in April 1950 as part of the Joint Arctic Weather Station system. In 1958, the Royal Canadian Air Force established a signals intelligence station, which became Canadian Forces Station (CFS) Alert, the most northerly Canadian military base. In 2009, the station's operations were remote-controlled from Canadian Forces Base Kingston.

Population and climate

The permanent population has typically numbered only a handful — census counts have recorded as few as five permanent residents — while military and scientific personnel on rotation bring the total present at any time to several dozen. Alert has a polar climate: winters are long and extremely cold, with average January temperatures around −33 °C, and the settlement experiences months of continuous darkness in winter and continuous daylight in summer.

Transport

Alert Airport, with its gravel runway, is the northernmost airport in the world. It is served by Canadian Forces flights, as there is no road access; the nearest community is Grise Fiord, about 800 km away.
