Hypercalcemia

Hypercalcemia, also spelled hypercalcaemia, is a high calcium (Ca2+) level in the blood serum. The normal range is 2.1–2.6 mmol/L, with levels above 2.6 mmol/L defined as hypercalcemia. People with a slightly elevated level may have no symptoms; higher levels can cause abdominal pain, bone pain, confusion, depression, kidney stones, excessive thirst and urination, and in severe cases abnormal heart rhythms or coma.

Causes

The most common causes are primary hyperparathyroidism and cancer, particularly multiple myeloma and cancers that secrete parathyroid hormone-related protein. Other causes include excess vitamin D, granulomatous diseases such as sarcoidosis, thyrotoxicosis, immobilization, thiazide diuretics and certain genetic conditions such as familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is based on blood calcium corrected for albumin, together with parathyroid hormone levels, which separate parathyroid-driven causes from malignancy and other causes. Treatment of significant hypercalcemia begins with intravenous saline hydration; bisphosphonates, calcitonin, glucocorticoids or calcimimetics may be used depending on cause and severity, and the underlying condition should be treated.
