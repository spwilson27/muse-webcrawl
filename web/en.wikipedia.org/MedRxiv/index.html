medRxiv

TypePreprint server
LaunchedJune 2019
OperatorsCold Spring Harbor Laboratory, BMJ, Yale University
FieldMedicine and health sciences

medRxiv (pronounced "med-archive") is an online archive and distribution server for complete but unpublished manuscripts (preprints) in the medical, clinical and related health sciences. It was launched in June 2019 by Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory together with BMJ and Yale University, modelled on the earlier bioRxiv server for biology.

Operation

Submissions are screened — for plagiarism, non-scientific content and material that might pose a health risk — but are not peer-reviewed before posting. Each preprint receives a digital object identifier (DOI) and can be cited. Authors frequently post revised versions and link the preprint to its eventual peer-reviewed journal publication.

Role in the COVID-19 pandemic

During the COVID-19 pandemic, medRxiv and bioRxiv became central to the rapid dissemination of research on SARS-CoV-2, epidemiology and treatments, posting tens of thousands of manuscripts. This speed also drew criticism when preliminary, unreviewed findings — some later corrected or withdrawn — received wide media attention.

Criticism

Commentators have raised concerns that preprints in medicine, unlike in physics or biology, can directly influence clinical practice and public behaviour before peer review, and medRxiv displays prominent cautions that its manuscripts should not guide clinical practice.
