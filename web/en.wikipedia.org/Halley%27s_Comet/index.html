Halley's Comet (official designation 1P/Halley) is the most famous short-period comet, visible from Earth every 75–76 years. It is named for Edmond Halley, who in 1705 recognised that the comets of 1531, 1607, and 1682 were the same object and predicted its return in 1758. Recorded sightings go back to at least 240 BC; the comet appears in the Bayeux Tapestry's depiction of the Norman Conquest of 1066.

1986 apparition

During its most recent return, in 1986, Halley became the first comet visited by spacecraft: an armada including ESA's Giotto and the Soviet Vega probes photographed its dark nucleus, a potato-shaped body about 15 × 8 km of ice, dust, and rock venting jets of gas. The comet will next reach perihelion in 2061.
