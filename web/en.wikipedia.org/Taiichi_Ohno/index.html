Taiichi Ohno

Taiichi Ohno (大野耐一, February 29, 1912 – May 28, 1990) — Japanese industrial engineer and businessman; considered the father of the Toyota Production System and author of the "seven wastes" concept.

Taiichi Ohno (大野耐一, Ōno Taiichi, February 29, 1912 – May 28, 1990) was a Japanese industrial engineer and businessman. He is considered to be the father of the Toyota Production System, which inspired Lean Manufacturing in the U.S. He devised the seven wastes (or muda in Japanese) as part of this system. He wrote several books about the system, including Toyota Production System: Beyond Large-Scale Production.

Biography

Born in Dalian, China, and a graduate of the Nagoya Technical High School, he joined the Toyoda family's Toyoda Spinning and Weaving Co. Ltd in 1932. In 1943 he moved to the Toyota Motor company, becoming a shop manager in 1947 and ultimately an executive vice president in 1975.

Toyota Production System

Working within postwar Japan's constraints of scarce capital and small production volumes, Ohno developed just-in-time flow production, the kanban pull-signalling system, jidoka (autonomation — automation with a human touch), and relentless elimination of the seven wastes: defects, overproduction, waiting, non-utilized talent, transportation, inventory, motion, and extra-processing. He is also credited with the "five whys" root-cause technique and the practice of drawing a chalk circle on the factory floor in which managers had to stand and observe processes directly (gemba).

Influence

His methods, spread internationally through the lean movement of the 1980s and 1990s, reshaped manufacturing and, later, software development and healthcare operations worldwide.
