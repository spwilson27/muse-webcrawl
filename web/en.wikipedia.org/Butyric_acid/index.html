Butyric acid

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia (recreated from memory)

Butyric acid (IUPAC name butanoic acid) is a straight-chain saturated fatty acid with the formula CH3CH2CH2COOH. It occurs in butter — from which it takes its name, from the Latin butyrum — and is responsible for the characteristic unpleasant odor of rancid butter and vomit. Its salts and esters are called butyrates (butanoates).

Occurrence and production

Butyric acid is produced by obligate anaerobic bacteria, including species of Clostridium, through butyric acid fermentation of sugars. In the human colon, gut microbiota produce butyrate, which is the preferred energy source of colonocytes and plays roles in gut health and immune regulation. Industrially, butyric acid is prepared by the oxidation of butyraldehyde or by fermentation.

Uses

Butyrate esters such as ethyl butyrate and methyl butyrate have fruity odors and are used as flavorings and fragrances. Butyric acid itself is used in the manufacture of plastics such as cellulose acetate butyrate, in animal feed additives, and as a fishing-bait additive. Its foul smell also makes it an ingredient in some stink bombs.

Biological significance

Butyrate is a histone deacetylase inhibitor and has been studied for anti-inflammatory and anti-cancer effects in the colon. Low-fiber diets reduce butyrate production in the gut, one proposed link between diet and colorectal disease.
