Digital image processing

Digital image processing is the use of a digital computer to process digital images through an algorithm. As a subcategory or field of digital signal processing, digital image processing has many advantages over analog image processing. It allows a much wider range of algorithms to be applied to the input data and can avoid problems such as the build-up of noise and distortion during processing.

History

Many techniques of digital image processing were developed in the 1960s at Bell Laboratories, the Jet Propulsion Laboratory, MIT and other institutions, driven by applications in satellite imagery, medical imaging and reconnaissance. Early landmark work included processing of Ranger spacecraft images of the Moon. Falling computing costs in the 1970s and 1980s spread the methods to industrial inspection, printing and eventually consumer photography.

Tasks and techniques

Common operations include image enhancement (contrast and brightness adjustment, histogram equalisation, denoising and sharpening), restoration of degraded images, geometric transformations, segmentation of objects, feature extraction, and compression for storage and transmission. Frequency-domain methods based on the Fourier transform underlie filtering and many compression schemes such as JPEG. Since the 2010s, convolutional neural networks have come to dominate image classification, object detection and generative tasks.

Applications

Digital image processing underpins medical imaging (CT, MRI, microscopy), remote sensing and weather forecasting, machine vision in manufacturing, biometric recognition, astronomy, film restoration and the computational photography built into modern smartphones.
