Geometric topology

In mathematics, geometric topology is the study of manifolds and maps between them, particularly embeddings of one manifold into another. It is distinguished from algebraic topology by its emphasis on geometric constructions and the structure of spaces in low dimensions, especially dimensions two, three, and four.

Low-dimensional topology

In two dimensions, geometric topology studies surfaces and their classification. In three dimensions it includes knot theory and the study of 3-manifolds, transformed by William Thurston's geometrization conjecture, which asserted that every closed 3-manifold decomposes into pieces carrying one of eight geometric structures. The conjecture was proved by Grigori Perelman in 2002–2003 using Ricci flow, simultaneously settling the century-old Poincaré conjecture.

High dimensions

In dimensions five and above, geometric topology is governed by surgery theory and the s-cobordism theorem, tools that allowed Stephen Smale and others to prove the generalized Poincaré conjecture in higher dimensions. Dimension four occupies a peculiar middle ground: it admits exotic smooth structures on Euclidean space and requires gauge-theoretic invariants such as Donaldson theory and Seiberg–Witten theory.

Connections

Geometric topology interacts with differential geometry, hyperbolic geometry, group theory, and theoretical physics, where 3-manifolds and knots appear in the study of quantum field theory and topological phases of matter.
