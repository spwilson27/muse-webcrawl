Site reliability engineering

Site reliability engineering (SRE) is a discipline that applies software engineering practices to infrastructure and operations problems. It originated at Google in the early 2000s under Ben Treynor Sloss and has since been adopted by many large technology organisations.

Core concepts

SRE teams manage availability using service-level indicators (SLIs), objectives (SLOs), and agreements (SLAs). The concept of an error budget — the amount of unreliability an SLO permits over a period — balances reliability work against the pace of new feature releases: if the budget is exhausted, launches may be frozen in favour of reliability fixes. SRE practice emphasises automation, eliminating repetitive manual work ("toil"), blameless postmortems, and monitoring and alerting on symptoms that affect users.

Relationship to DevOps

SRE is often described as a concrete implementation of DevOps principles, with its own job roles, on-call rotations, and capacity-planning practices. Google's books Site Reliability Engineering (2016) and The Site Reliability Workbook (2018) codified the approach for the wider industry.
