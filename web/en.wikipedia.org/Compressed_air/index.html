Compressed air

Industrial air compressors supplying a factory air system

Compressed air is air kept under a pressure greater than atmospheric pressure. It is an important medium for the transfer of energy in industrial processes and is used to power pneumatic tools, air brakes, spray painting equipment, and tire inflation, as well as in diving (as breathing gas in scuba cylinders) and in compressed-air energy storage.

Production

Compressed air is produced by air compressors — reciprocating piston, rotary screw, rotary vane, scroll, or centrifugal machines — which draw in atmospheric air and reduce its volume, raising its pressure. Typical industrial systems operate at 7–10 bar (100–150 psi). Compression generates heat, and intercoolers, aftercoolers, dryers, and filters are used to remove heat, moisture, and oil before distribution.

Uses

Pneumatic tools such as impact wrenches, drills, and nail guns are lighter and simpler than electric equivalents. Compressed air drives air brakes on trains and trucks, operates control valves in process plants, powers dental drills, and conveys granular materials. Recreational uses include paintball markers and air guns. Because leaking and compression losses make it an inefficient energy carrier, compressed air is sometimes called the "fourth utility" in manufacturing, with significant attention paid to leak detection and system efficiency.

Safety

Compressed air can cause serious injury: it can penetrate skin, and particles blown by air jets can damage eyes. Pressure vessels require periodic inspection, and hoses must be secured against whipping if they rupture.
