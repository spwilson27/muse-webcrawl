Grantley Herbert Adams

Sir Grantley Herbert Adams (28 April 1898 – 28 November 1971) was a Barbadian politician, lawyer and trade unionist.

Premier of Barbados: 1954–1958
Prime Minister of the West Indies Federation: 1958–1962

Sir Grantley Herbert Adams was one of the most important political figures in the history of Barbados and the wider English-speaking Caribbean. He co-founded the Barbados Labour Party (BLP) and became the first Premier of Barbados, later serving as the only Prime Minister of the short-lived West Indies Federation.

Early life and education

Adams was born on 28 April 1898 in St Michael, Barbados, the son of Fitzherbert Adams, a headmaster, and Rosa Adams (née Archer). He was educated at Harrison College, one of the island's leading secondary schools, where he won a Barbados Scholarship to study at Codrington College. He subsequently studied law at St Catherine's College, Oxford, and was called to the bar at Gray's Inn in London.

While in England he witnessed the workings of democratic institutions and trade union organisation first-hand, experiences that shaped his reformist politics. On returning to Barbados in the 1920s he built a successful legal practice and became a vocal critic of the island's conservative planter-dominated establishment.

Political career

Adams entered politics at a time when political representation in Barbados was dominated by a narrow propertied class. During the 1930s, amid labour unrest across the Caribbean, he helped to found the Barbados Progressive League (later the Barbados Labour Party) and supported the formation of the Barbados Workers' Union. The disturbances of 1937, sparked by the deportation of the trade union organiser Clement Payne, propelled reformist politics to the centre of Barbadian life.

Elected to the House of Assembly, Adams pressed for universal adult suffrage, social welfare legislation, free secondary education and improved housing. When Barbados achieved internal self-government, Adams became its first Premier in 1954, leading the BLP to victory in the 1956 general election under universal suffrage.

West Indies Federation

Adams was a strong regionalist and backed the creation of the West Indies Federation, formed in 1958. He became its first — and only — Prime Minister, based at Chaguaramas in Trinidad. The federation collapsed in 1962 after Jamaica voted in a referendum to withdraw, and Adams returned to Barbadian politics.

Later life and legacy

Adams remained leader of the BLP until his death but never regained the premiership; in 1961 the Democratic Labour Party under Errol Barrow defeated his party, and Barrow later led Barbados to independence in 1966. Adams died on 28 November 1971. He is remembered as a founding father of modern Barbados and is one of the country's National Heroes, proclaimed in 1998. The international airport in Barbados was renamed Grantley Adams International Airport in his honour, and his portrait appears on the Barbadian $100 bill.
