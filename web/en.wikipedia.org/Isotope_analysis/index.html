Isotope analysis

Isotope analysis is the identification and quantification of the isotopic composition of a material — the relative abundances of the stable or radioactive isotopes of its constituent elements — usually measured with an isotope-ratio mass spectrometer (IRMS). Because physical, chemical and biological processes fractionate isotopes in predictable ways, isotope ratios act as natural "fingerprints" recording diet, climate, geography and provenance.

Principles

Results are usually expressed in delta notation (δ), in per mil (‰) relative to an international standard: δ13C against VPDB, δ15N against air, δ18O and δ2H against VSMOW, and δ34S against VCDT. Lighter isotopes react slightly faster and are preferentially taken up or lost in many processes, so plant photosynthesis, evaporation, trophic feeding and metabolism leave characteristic signatures.

Applications

In archaeology, carbon and nitrogen isotopes in bone collagen reconstruct past diets, while strontium and oxygen isotopes in tooth enamel trace migration. In ecology, stable isotopes map food webs and animal movement. Ice-core and sediment isotope records underpin reconstructions of past climate. Isotope analysis is also used in forensic science, food authentication (detecting adulteration or verifying origin), hydrology, and the study of extraterrestrial materials such as meteorites and returned lunar samples.
