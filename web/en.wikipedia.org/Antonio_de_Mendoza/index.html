Antonio de Mendoza

Antonio de Mendoza, first viceroy of New Spain (1535–1550)

Antonio de Mendoza y Pacheco (c. 1495 – 21 July 1552) was a Spanish colonial administrator who served as the first viceroy of New Spain (1535–1550) and later as the second viceroy of Peru (1551–1552). His relatively capable and humane administration set precedents for Spanish colonial government in the Americas.

Viceroy of New Spain

Appointed to bring royal order to Mexico after the turbulent rule of the first Audiencia and Hernán Cortés, Mendoza arrived in Mexico City in 1535. He promoted agriculture, mining, and ranching, introduced the printing press to the Americas (1539), founded schools including the Colegio de Santa Cruz de Tlatelolco for Indigenous elites, and supported Franciscan missionary work. He suppressed the Mixtón War (1540–1542) in Nueva Galicia and dispatched major expeditions: Coronado's journey to the North American Southwest, Ruy López de Villalobos's voyage to the Philippines, and Juan Rodríguez Cabrillo's exploration of the California coast. The Codex Mendoza, an Aztec manuscript commissioned under his patronage, records tribute and history of the Mexica.

Viceroy of Peru

In 1550 Mendoza was transferred to Peru, where the colony was still unsettled after civil wars among the conquistadors. Aged and in poor health, he died in Lima in 1552.

Legacy

Mendoza County in Argentina and the city of Mendoza are named for his family; historians generally remember him as one of the ablest of Spain's colonial viceroys.
