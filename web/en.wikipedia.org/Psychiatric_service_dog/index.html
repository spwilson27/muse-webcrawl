Psychiatric service dog

A psychiatric service dog (PSD) is a type of assistance dog trained to assist a person with a psychiatric disability, such as post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), anxiety disorders, major depression, or schizophrenia. Unlike emotional support animals, psychiatric service dogs are individually trained to perform specific tasks that mitigate their handler's disability.

Tasks

Tasks may include creating physical space for the handler in crowds, leading the handler to an exit during a panic attack, waking the handler from nightmares, interrupting repetitive or self-harming behaviours, reminding the handler to take medication, and performing deep pressure therapy during episodes of acute anxiety.

Legal status

In the United States, psychiatric service dogs are recognised as service animals under the Americans with Disabilities Act, provided they are individually trained to do work or perform tasks related to the handler's disability. Handlers may be asked only whether the dog is required because of a disability and what task it has been trained to perform. Rules for air travel and housing differ: the Air Carrier Access Act regulations were revised in 2021 to cover trained psychiatric service dogs, and housing providers must generally accommodate them under the Fair Housing Act.

Training

Psychiatric service dogs may be trained by professional organisations or by their handlers themselves, which is permitted under US law. Training typically combines standard obedience and public-access manners with disability-specific task work.
