Mechatronics, also called mechatronics engineering, is an interdisciplinary branch of engineering that integrates mechanical engineering, electronics, computer engineering and control engineering to design and manufacture intelligent products and systems. The word is a portmanteau of "mechanics" and "electronics".

History

The term was coined in 1969 by Tetsuro Mori of the Japanese company Yaskawa Electric, initially as a trademark, and spread from the 1980s as microprocessors made it routine to embed computation and sensing into mechanical devices. Definitions have since broadened to emphasize the synergistic integration of the constituent disciplines from the earliest design stages rather than the sequential design of mechanical then electronic parts.

Scope and applications

A mechatronic system senses its environment, processes information and acts through actuators under feedback control. Examples include industrial robots, anti-lock braking and electronic stability systems in cars, autofocus cameras, hard disk drives, CNC machine tools, medical devices and smart home appliances. University curricula in mechatronics typically combine mechanics and machine design, circuits and embedded systems, sensors and actuators, and control theory.
