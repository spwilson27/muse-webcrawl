Strouhal number

In fluid dynamics, the Strouhal number (St, or sometimes Sr) is a dimensionless number describing oscillating flow mechanisms. The parameter is named after Vincenc Strouhal, a Czech physicist who experimented in 1878 with wires experiencing vortex shedding and singing in the wind.

Definition

The Strouhal number is defined as St = fL/U, where f is the frequency of vortex shedding, L is the characteristic length (for example, the diameter of a cylinder), and U is the flow velocity. Equivalently, it is the ratio of inertial forces due to the local acceleration of the flow to inertial forces due to convective acceleration.

Applications

For a cylinder in cross-flow, the Strouhal number is approximately 0.2 over a wide range of Reynolds numbers, which makes it useful in engineering design: it predicts the frequency at which alternating vortices are shed into a Kármán vortex street, which can induce vibration in structures such as chimneys, periscopes, and overhead power lines.

In biology, the Strouhal number characterises the efficiency of swimming and flying animals. Cruising animals from dolphins to birds tend to operate at Strouhal numbers between 0.2 and 0.4 when the frequency and amplitude of tail or wing beats are combined with their forward speed, suggesting an evolutionary optimum for propulsive efficiency.
