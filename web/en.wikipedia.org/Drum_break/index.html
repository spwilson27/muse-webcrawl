Drum break

A drum break, or breakbeat, is a section of a song in which the other instruments drop out and the drummer plays alone, typically for a few bars. Drum breaks in funk and soul records of the late 1960s and 1970s became foundational source material for hip hop, jungle and drum and bass through the practice of sampling.

In hip hop

In the Bronx in the 1970s, DJ Kool Herc developed the technique of using two turntables to loop the percussive "break" of a record back and forth for dancers, who became known as break-boys and break-girls (b-boys and b-girls). DJs such as Grandmaster Flash and Afrika Bambaataa built sets from celebrated breaks, and producers later sampled them directly.

Famous breaks

The most sampled drum break of all is the "Amen break" — a few seconds of drumming by Gregory Coleman in "Amen, Brother" (1969) by The Winstons. Other staples include "Funky Drummer" by James Brown (drums by Clyde Stubblefield), "Apache" by the Incredible Bongo Band, "Think (About It)" by Lyn Collins and "Impeach the President" by The Honey Drippers.

In electronic music

Chopped and re-pitched breakbeats underpin jungle and drum and bass, where the Amen break is sliced into individual hits and rearranged at high tempo. Collections of breaks have been released as sample libraries, and the break remains a standard building block across electronic genres.
