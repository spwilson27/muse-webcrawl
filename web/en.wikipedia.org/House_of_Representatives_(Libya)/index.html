The House of Representatives (HoR; Arabic: مجلس النواب) is the legislature of Libya, resulting from the 2014 Libyan parliamentary election held on 25 June 2014. It has 200 members, elected as individuals rather than from party lists, and replaced the General National Congress.

Relocation and rival governments

Following its inauguration in August 2014 amid fighting in Tripoli during the Second Libyan Civil War, the House of Representatives relocated to Tobruk in eastern Libya. A rival government backed by the rump General National Congress continued in Tripoli, and Libya's Supreme Court ruled the HoR's election unconstitutional — a ruling the HoR rejected. Under the 2015 Libyan Political Agreement, the HoR was recognised as Libya's legitimate parliament alongside the Government of National Accord in Tripoli, though disputes between the bodies persisted for years.

Recent history

The HoR, presided over by Aguila Saleh Issa, aligned with Khalifa Haftar's Libyan National Army in the east. In 2021 it engaged in plans for national elections that were postponed, and in 2022 it appointed a rival prime minister, renewing the east–west political division. Long-overdue elections to replace the body have repeatedly been delayed.
