A World Heritage Site is a landmark or area with legal protection under the World Heritage Convention adopted by UNESCO in 1972. Sites are designated because they are judged to possess "outstanding universal value" — cultural, historical, natural, or scientific significance so exceptional that it transcends national boundaries and matters to present and future generations of all humanity.

Nomination and criteria

States that have ratified the Convention maintain tentative lists of candidate sites and submit nominations to the World Heritage Committee, a body of 21 elected member states. Advisory bodies — ICOMOS for cultural sites and the IUCN for natural sites — evaluate the nominations. A site must meet at least one of ten criteria: six cultural (masterpiece of creative genius, interchange of values, testimony to a tradition, exemplary type of building or landscape, traditional settlement, association with events or ideas of universal significance) and four natural (superlative natural phenomena or beauty, earth's history, ecological processes, and biodiversity).

Once inscribed, a site is monitored; those facing serious threats may be placed on the List of World Heritage in Danger, and in rare cases — the Arabian Oryx Sanctuary in 2007, Dresden's Elbe Valley in 2009, and Liverpool's maritime mercantile city in 2021 — sites have been removed from the list entirely.

History

The Convention grew out of international campaigns of the 1960s, notably the UNESCO-led rescue of the temples of Abu Simbel in Egypt, threatened by flooding from the Aswan High Dam. It was adopted on 16 November 1972 and came into force in 1975. The first twelve sites were inscribed in 1978, including Yellowstone National Park and the Galápagos Islands. Italy and China hold the largest numbers of sites.

Examples

Famous sites include the Great Wall of China, the Taj Mahal, the historic centre of Rome, Machu Picchu, the Great Barrier Reef, Mount Athos, and Berlin's Museum Island. As of the mid-2020s there are more than 1,200 inscribed properties across more than 165 countries.
