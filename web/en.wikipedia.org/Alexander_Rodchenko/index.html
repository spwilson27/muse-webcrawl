Alexander Rodchenko

Born: 5 December 1891, Saint Petersburg, Russian Empire
Died: 3 December 1956, Moscow, USSR
Movement: Constructivism

Alexander Mikhailovich Rodchenko (5 December 1891 – 3 December 1956) was a Russian and Soviet artist, sculptor, photographer, and graphic designer. A founder of Constructivism and a pioneer of Russian design and photomontage, he profoundly influenced twentieth-century graphic design, typography, and photography.

Early life and painting

Rodchenko was born in Saint Petersburg and studied at the Kazan School of Art, where he met his future wife, the artist Varvara Stepanova. Moving to Moscow in 1915, he came under the influence of Vladimir Tatlin and Kazimir Malevich. His abstract paintings culminated in the 1921 exhibition 5×5=25, where he showed three monochrome canvases — pure red, yellow, and blue — declaring painting at its logical end, and turned to production art: design for industry, print, and everyday life.

Design and photomontage

With the poet Vladimir Mayakovsky, Rodchenko produced striking advertising posters for Soviet state enterprises in the 1920s, combining bold diagonals, flat colour, photomontage, and sans-serif typography. He designed book covers for Mayakovsky's About This (1923), film posters, and layouts for journals such as LEF and Novyi LEF. His Workers' Club interior at the 1925 Paris Exposition won wide acclaim.

Photography

From the mid-1920s Rodchenko revolutionised photography with dramatically tilted viewpoints — shooting from above, below, and on the diagonal — in series such as Pioneer Girl (1930) and his photographs of Moscow streets and the Shukhov Tower. Under Stalin, his formalism was attacked; he turned to photographing sport and circus subjects before his death in Moscow in 1956.
