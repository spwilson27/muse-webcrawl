Marvel Comics is an American comic book publisher and the property of The Walt Disney Company since December 31, 2009, and a subsidiary of Disney Publishing Worldwide since March 2023. Marvel was founded in 1939 by Martin Goodman as Timely Comics, and by 1951 had generally become known as Atlas Comics. The Marvel era began in August 1961 with the launch of The Fantastic Four and other superhero titles created by Stan Lee, Jack Kirby, Steve Ditko, and many others. The Marvel brand, which had been used over the years, was solidified as the company's primary brand.

Marvel counts among its characters such well-known superheroes as Spider-Man, Iron Man, Captain America, Thor, Doctor Strange, Hulk, Daredevil, Wolverine, Black Panther, and Captain Marvel, as well as popular superhero teams such as the Avengers, X-Men, Fantastic Four, and Guardians of the Galaxy. Its stable of well-known supervillains includes Doctor Doom, Magneto, Ultron, Thanos, Kang the Conqueror, Green Goblin, Red Skull, Loki, and Venom. Most of Marvel's fictional characters operate in a single reality known as the Marvel Universe, with most locations mirroring real-life places; many major characters are based in New York City.

History

Timely Comics (1939–1950)

Pulp-magazine publisher Martin Goodman founded the company in 1939 as Timely Publications. Its first publication, Marvel Comics #1 (October 1939), introduced the android Human Torch by Carl Burgos and Namor the Sub-Mariner by Bill Everett, and sold out its print run. In 1941, writer-artist Joe Simon and artist Jack Kirby created Captain America, whose debut issue famously depicted the hero punching Adolf Hitler. After World War II, superhero popularity waned, and Timely shifted to humour, horror, western, and romance titles.

Atlas Comics and the Marvel Age (1951–1970)

Renamed Atlas Comics in 1951, the company struggled under distribution restrictions until Goodman secured distribution through American News Company successors and Independent News. In 1961, responding to the success of DC Comics' Justice League, Goodman assigned writer-editor Stan Lee and artist Jack Kirby to create a new superhero team. The Fantastic Four #1 (November 1961) launched the "Marvel Age of Comics", distinguished by flawed, humanised heroes. Lee, Kirby, Steve Ditko, and others followed with the Hulk, Thor, Ant-Man, Iron Man, the X-Men, Daredevil, and Spider-Man — the last becoming Marvel's flagship character.

Expansion, bankruptcy, and the Disney era

Marvel changed hands several times: Goodman sold to the Perfect Film and Chemical Corporation in 1968, and the company (as Marvel Entertainment Group) was sold to New World Entertainment in 1986 and to Ronald Perelman's MacAndrews & Forbes in 1989. Following the collapse of the comic-book speculator bubble, Marvel filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in December 1996, emerging in 1998 after a merger with Toy Biz. Marvel then rebuilt around licensing and its film studio; the success of the Marvel Cinematic Universe, beginning with Iron Man (2008), transformed the company into an entertainment giant. On December 31, 2009, The Walt Disney Company acquired Marvel Entertainment for approximately $4 billion.

Characters and universe

Beginning in the 1960s, Marvel's titles were linked by a shared continuity in which characters crossed between series — a model that became the industry standard. Major crossover events, from Secret Wars (1984) and the Infinity Gauntlet storyline to Civil War (2006) and Secret Wars (2015), periodically reshape the line. Alongside the main Marvel Universe (designated Earth-616), Marvel has published alternate realities including the Ultimate Universe (2000–2015), whose designs influenced the films.

Market position

Marvel and DC Comics together have historically accounted for the large majority of American comic book sales. In recent decades graphic novels and collected editions have become the industry's largest sales channel, and Marvel's characters anchor the highest-grossing film franchise in history through the Marvel Cinematic Universe.
