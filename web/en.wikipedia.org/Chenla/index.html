Chenla (Khmer: ចេនឡា) was the name given in Chinese records to a kingdom — or shifting group of polities — in mainland Southeast Asia from roughly the mid-6th to the early 9th century CE. It succeeded the kingdom of Funan and preceded the Khmer Empire founded by Jayavarman II in 802.

History

Originally a vassal of Funan in the Mekong basin, Chenla under kings such as Bhavavarman I and Mahendravarman expanded from its heartland around the middle Mekong (near present-day Champasak in southern Laos, site of Wat Phu) and overran Funan in the late 6th and early 7th centuries. Under Īśānavarman I (r. c. 616–637), who founded the capital Īśānapura at Sambor Prei Kuk, the kingdom reached its greatest extent. After about 670, Chinese sources distinguish "Land Chenla" in the interior from "Water Chenla" in the delta, and the region fragmented into competing principalities until reunification under the Khmer Empire.

Culture

Chenla-era art and architecture, notably the brick temple towers of Sambor Prei Kuk, developed from Funan prototypes toward the styles of Angkor. Inscriptions in Sanskrit and Old Khmer record Hindu worship (especially of Shiva) alongside Buddhism, land grants, and royal genealogies.
