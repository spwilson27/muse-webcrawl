Mohamed Bouazizi

Tarek el-Tayeb Mohamed Bouazizi

Born: 29 March 1984, Sidi Bouzid, Tunisia

Died: 4 January 2011 (aged 26), Ben Arous, Tunisia

Occupation: Street vendor

Known for: Self-immolation that catalysed the Tunisian revolution and the Arab Spring

Tarek el-Tayeb Mohamed Bouazizi (Arabic: طارق الطيب محمد البوعزيزي; 29 March 1984 – 4 January 2011) was a Tunisian street vendor who set himself on fire on 17 December 2010 in Sidi Bouzid, in protest against police abuse and the confiscation of his goods. His act became the catalyst for the Tunisian revolution and the wider Arab Spring protests across the Middle East and North Africa.

Background

Bouazizi was born in Sidi Bouzid, a town in central Tunisia. After his father's death, he worked from a young age to support his mother and siblings, selling fruit and vegetables from a cart. According to his family and widely reported accounts, on 17 December 2010 municipal officials confiscated his scales and produce, and a policewoman allegedly slapped and insulted him. When his complaint at the governor's office was refused, Bouazizi doused himself with paint thinner outside the building and set himself alight.

Death and aftermath

Bouazizi died of his burns on 4 January 2011 at a hospital in Ben Arous, after a visit from President Zine El Abidine Ben Ali. Protests that had begun in Sidi Bouzid spread across Tunisia, and on 14 January 2011 Ben Ali fled the country, ending 23 years in power. Demonstrations inspired in part by Bouazizi's death then spread across the Arab world, toppling or challenging governments in Egypt, Libya, Yemen, Syria, Bahrain and elsewhere.

Legacy

Bouazizi was posthumously honoured, including with the Sakharov Prize (awarded jointly to Arab Spring figures in 2011). Boulevards and squares in Tunisia and Paris were named after him, and he is widely remembered as a symbol of protest against corruption, humiliation, and economic exclusion — though later reporting complicated details of the encounter that triggered his protest.
