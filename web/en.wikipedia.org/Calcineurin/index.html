Calcineurin is a calcium- and calmodulin-dependent serine/threonine protein phosphatase, also known as protein phosphatase 2B (PP2B). It is activated by rises in intracellular calcium and plays a central role in coupling calcium signals to cellular responses, most famously in the activation of T lymphocytes of the immune system.

Structure and activation

Calcineurin is a heterodimer of a catalytic A subunit, which binds calcium and contains an autoinhibitory domain, and a regulatory B subunit. When intracellular Ca²⁺ rises, calcium binds the B subunit and calmodulin; calmodulin binding displaces the autoinhibitory domain, switching the phosphatase on. It is abundant in the brain, where it was first characterised.

Role in immunity

In T cells, calcineurin dephosphorylates the transcription factor NFAT (nuclear factor of activated T cells), allowing it to enter the nucleus and switch on genes such as interleukin-2 that drive T-cell proliferation. The immunosuppressant drugs ciclosporin and tacrolimus (FK506) inhibit calcineurin — via the immunophilins cyclophilin and FKBP respectively — and thereby suppress immune responses, making them mainstays of organ transplantation medicine.

Other functions

Calcineurin signalling also participates in cardiac hypertrophy, skeletal muscle fibre-type specification, synaptic plasticity and memory, and neuronal development. Its activity is implicated in Down syndrome and Alzheimer's disease research, and calcineurin inhibitors are used topically for inflammatory skin conditions.
