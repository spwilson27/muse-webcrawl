General classification

The primary ranking in a multi-stage bicycle race, decided by the lowest cumulative finishing time.

The general classification (GC) in bicycle racing, and especially in road bicycle stage racing, is the category of the overall classification that tracks total elapsed time for each rider after every stage of a multi-stage race. The rider with the lowest cumulative time is the overall leader and, in most major races, wears a distinctive jersey—most famously the yellow jersey (maillot jaune) of the Tour de France, the pink jersey (maglia rosa) of the Giro d'Italia and the red jersey (maillot rojo) of the Vuelta a España.

Calculation

A rider's general classification time is the sum of their finishing times on each stage. Time bonuses may be awarded for stage wins and high placings, and time penalties imposed for rule infringements; both adjust the cumulative total. Finishing times within a group are generally recorded identically for all riders in the group, so the decisive GC gaps are usually created in individual time trials and summit finishes, while sprint stages rarely change the order.

Racing tactics

Because the general classification is normally decided by a handful of minutes over three weeks of racing, teams organise their entire strategy around a designated GC leader. Teammates (domestiques) shelter the leader from wind, fetch food and water, chase breakaways and set pace on climbs. Riders who specialise in the general classification—such as Eddy Merckx, Jacques Anquetil, Miguel Indurain, Chris Froome and Tadej Pogačar—are typically strong both in time trials and in the mountains.

Other classifications

Stage races also award subsidiary competitions that run alongside the GC, including the points classification (for sprinters; green jersey at the Tour), the mountains classification (polka-dot jersey at the Tour), and classifications for the best young rider and best team.
