Rydberg formula

The Balmer series of hydrogen, whose wavelengths the Rydberg formula predicts.

The Rydberg formula is an empirical mathematical formula for the wavelengths of the spectral lines of an atom, devised by the Swedish physicist Johannes Rydberg and presented in 1888. It accurately predicted the wavelengths of hydrogen's emission spectrum and was later generalized to hydrogen-like atoms.

The formula

For hydrogen, the inverse wavelength of an emitted photon is given by 1/λ = RH (1/n1² − 1/n2²), where RH is the Rydberg constant for hydrogen (approximately 1.097 × 107 m−1) and n1 and n2 are integers with n2 > n1. Setting n1 = 1 gives the ultraviolet Lyman series, n1 = 2 the visible Balmer series, and n1 = 3 the infrared Paschen series.

Significance

Originally a purely empirical fit that extended Johann Balmer's 1885 formula, the Rydberg formula gained a theoretical foundation in 1913 when Niels Bohr derived it from his model of the atom, interpreting the integers as quantum numbers of electron orbits. Its success was a key early triumph of quantum theory, and the Rydberg constant is now among the most precisely measured constants in physics.
