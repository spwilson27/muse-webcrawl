Arms Trade Treaty

Arms Trade Treaty
Adopted: 2 April 2013 (UN General Assembly)
Opened for signature: 3 June 2013
Entered into force: 24 December 2014
Regulates international trade in conventional arms

The Arms Trade Treaty (ATT) is a multilateral treaty that regulates the international trade in conventional arms. It is the first legally binding international agreement to set common standards for transfers of weapons ranging from small arms to battle tanks, combat aircraft, and warships.

Provisions

The treaty prohibits transfers that would violate UN Security Council arms embargoes or that the exporting state knows would be used in genocide, crimes against humanity, or war crimes. It requires states parties to assess the risk that exported arms could contribute to serious violations of international humanitarian or human rights law, and to maintain national control systems and reporting on transfers.

History

The idea of an arms trade treaty was championed by Nobel Peace laureates including Óscar Arias and by NGOs such as Amnesty International and Oxfam through the Control Arms campaign. After negotiations at the United Nations failed to reach consensus in 2012 and early 2013, the treaty text was adopted by the UN General Assembly by an overwhelming majority vote.

Participation

More than 110 states have ratified the treaty. The United States signed in 2013 but in 2019 announced it was withdrawing its signature; Russia and several other major exporters remain outside.
