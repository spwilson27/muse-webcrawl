Ed Roberts (activist)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Roberts, widely regarded as the father of the independent living movement.

Edward Verne Roberts (January 23, 1939 – March 14, 1995) was an American activist and a pioneering leader of the disability rights movement. He is often called the father of the independent living movement, which holds that people with disabilities are the best experts on their own needs and should control the services that affect their lives.

Early life and education

Roberts contracted polio at age fourteen in 1953, which left him paralysed from the neck down and dependent on a respirator and an 800-pound iron lung at night. After fighting for his high school diploma, he attended the College of San Mateo and then, in 1962, the University of California, Berkeley — initially housed in the campus infirmary at Cowell Hospital, where a group of severely disabled students, calling themselves the "Rolling Quads", began organising for accessibility and self-determination.

Career

In 1972 Roberts co-founded the Center for Independent Living in Berkeley, the first organisation of its kind run by and for people with disabilities; the model spread worldwide. In 1975 Governor Jerry Brown appointed him director of the California Department of Vocational Rehabilitation — the same agency that had once declared him too disabled to work. He later co-founded the World Institute on Disability (1983).

Legacy

Roberts received a MacArthur "Genius" Fellowship in 1984. The Ed Roberts Campus in Berkeley, opened in 2010, houses disability organisations in a universally accessible building named in his honour, and California marks January 23 as Ed Roberts Day.
