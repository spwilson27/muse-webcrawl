MyoD is a master regulator of skeletal muscle development.

MyoD, also known as myoblast determination protein 1 and encoded by the MYOD1 gene, is a basic helix-loop-helix (bHLH) transcription factor that plays a central role in regulating skeletal muscle differentiation. It belongs to the family of myogenic regulatory factors, together with Myf5, myogenin and MRF4.

Discovery and function

MyoD was identified in the 1980s by Harold Weintraub and colleagues in a landmark experiment showing that expression of a single cDNA could convert cultured fibroblasts into muscle cells — one of the first demonstrations that a single transcription factor can determine cell fate. MyoD binds E-box sequences in the regulatory regions of muscle-specific genes, recruits chromatin-modifying complexes, and activates the programme of myogenesis, including withdrawal from the cell cycle and expression of contractile proteins. It also feeds back on its own promoter and can reprogramme several other cell types toward the muscle lineage.

Regulation and significance

MyoD activity is tuned by signalling pathways, inhibitors of differentiation (Id proteins), and cell-cycle machinery; it cooperates with MEF2 family factors and the SWI/SNF chromatin remodelling complex. Because of its reprogramming power, MyoD became a paradigm for induced cell-fate change and a conceptual precursor to later work on induced pluripotent stem cells.
