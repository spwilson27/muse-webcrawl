Addiction recovery groups

Addiction recovery groups are voluntary associations of people who share a desire to overcome an addiction. Many such groups follow the twelve-step programme first developed by Alcoholics Anonymous (AA), founded in 1935, while others use alternative approaches.

Twelve-step groups

Twelve-step fellowships include Alcoholics Anonymous, Narcotics Anonymous, Cocaine Anonymous and Gamblers Anonymous. They are self-supporting, non-professional peer groups whose members work through the Twelve Steps and attend regular meetings. Anonymity is a founding principle.

Alternatives

Non-twelve-step alternatives include SMART Recovery, which uses cognitive behavioural techniques, LifeRing Secular Recovery, and faith-based programmes such as Celebrate Recovery. Research suggests participation in mutual-help groups can support long-term recovery alongside professional treatment.
