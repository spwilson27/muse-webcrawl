Violin concerto

A violin concerto is a concerto for solo violin (occasionally two or more violins) and instrumental ensemble, customarily an orchestra. Since the Baroque period such works have formed a central part of the violin repertoire and of concert programming.

Form

From the Classical period onward the standard form is three movements: a fast opening movement in sonata form, a slow lyrical movement, and a fast finale, often a rondo. A cadenza near the end of the first movement displays the soloist's virtuosity.

Notable concertos

The repertoire includes Vivaldi's Four Seasons, Bach's Double Concerto, the concertos of Mozart (five), Beethoven, Mendelssohn, Brahms, Tchaikovsky, Bruch, Sibelius, Elgar, Prokofiev, Shostakovich and Berg — the Beethoven, Brahms, Mendelssohn and Tchaikovsky works sometimes being called the "four great" German/Russian pillars of the genre.
