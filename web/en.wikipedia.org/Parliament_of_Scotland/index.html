Parliament of Scotland

Parliament of Scotland Existed: c. 1235 – 1707
Seat: Parliament Hall, Edinburgh (from 1639)
Structure: Unicameral; the Three Estates
Dissolved by: Acts of Union 1707
Successor body: Parliament of Great Britain

The Parliament of Scotland (Scots: Pairlament o Scotland; Scottish Gaelic: Pàrlamaid na h-Alba) was the unicameral legislature of the Kingdom of Scotland. The parliament evolved during the early Middle Ages from the king's council of bishops and earls, with the first identifiable parliament being held in 1235 at Kirkliston. It sat for nearly five centuries, until it was adjourned on 25 March 1707 and dissolved under the Acts of Union 1707, which merged Scotland and England into the Kingdom of Great Britain.

The Three Estates

Parliament was composed of the Three Estates sitting together in one chamber: the First Estate of prelates (bishops and abbots), the Second Estate of the nobility and — from the 16th century — commissioners for the shires, and the Third Estate of commissioners for the royal burghs. Legislation was prepared by the Lords of the Articles, a steering committee whose control by the Crown in the 17th century drew persistent criticism. After the Reformation of 1560 the bishops' place was contested, and under the Presbyterian settlements of the 1690s the First Estate disappeared, leaving lords, shire commissioners, and burgh commissioners.

History

The medieval parliament asserted control over taxation and justice and occasionally resisted the crown, notably in the 15th and 16th centuries. Under the Stewart monarchs of the 17th century it was managed through the Lords of the Articles; the Covenanter parliaments of 1639–1651 abolished that committee and governed Scotland during the Wars of the Three Kingdoms. Scotland was represented at Westminster under Cromwell's Commonwealth, regained its own parliament at the Restoration, and after the Revolution of 1689 asserted its independence with the Claim of Right. The final session passed the Act of Union with England, ratifying the Treaty of Union negotiated in 1706, amid riots in Edinburgh and lasting controversy over whether Scotland had been coerced or bought.

Revival

The name was revived in 1999 when the modern Scottish Parliament, a devolved legislature established by the Scotland Act 1998, first met — the first Scottish parliament to sit in Edinburgh in 292 years, though a legally distinct institution.
