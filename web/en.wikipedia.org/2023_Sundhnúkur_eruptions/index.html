The 2023–2024 Sundhnúkur eruptions were a series of volcanic fissure eruptions on Iceland's Reykjanes Peninsula, north of the fishing town of Grindavík. The eruptions began on December 18, 2023, after weeks of intense earthquake activity and ground deformation that led to the evacuation of Grindavík's roughly 3,700 residents in November.

Sequence of eruptions

The first eruption opened a fissure about 4 kilometres long near the Sundhnúkur crater row but waned within days. A second eruption on January 14, 2024, opened fissures closer to Grindavík, and lava destroyed several houses on the town's edge — the first Icelandic homes lost to lava since the 1973 Eldfell eruption on Heimaey. Further eruptions followed in February, March, May, and August 2024, making the episode the most sustained period of volcanic activity on the Reykjanes Peninsula in roughly 800 years.

Impact and defences

Authorities constructed large earthen defence barriers to divert lava away from Grindavík, the Svartsengi power station, and the Blue Lagoon geothermal spa, which closed repeatedly during the unrest. Most residents were unable to return permanently, and in 2024 the Icelandic state offered to purchase homes in the town from displaced owners.
