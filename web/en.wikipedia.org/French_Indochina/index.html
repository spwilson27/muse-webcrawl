French Indochina (French: Indochine française) was a grouping of French colonial territories in Southeast Asia from 1887 until 1954. It comprised the colony of Cochinchina in the south and the protectorates of Annam, Tonkin, Cambodia, and Laos, corresponding to modern Vietnam, Cambodia, and Laos.

History

French expansion began with the capture of Saigon in 1859 and the establishment of Cochinchina and a protectorate over Cambodia in the 1860s. By 1885 France had imposed protectorates over Annam and Tonkin after war with the Nguyen dynasty and Qing China, and the Indochinese Union was formally created in 1887; Laos was added in 1893.

Administration and economy

The federation was ruled by a Governor-General, based first in Saigon and after 1902 in Hanoi. The colonial economy exported rice from the Mekong delta, rubber from plantations, coal, and minerals, supported by monopolies on opium, salt, and alcohol and by corvée labour. Colonial rule developed infrastructure and a romanised Vietnamese script, chữ Quốc ngữ, while generating growing nationalist resistance.

End of Indochina

After Japanese occupation during the Second World War, Ho Chi Minh proclaimed the Democratic Republic of Vietnam in 1945. France's attempt to restore control led to the First Indochina War (1946–1954), ending with the French defeat at Dien Bien Phu and the Geneva Accords of 1954, which dissolved French Indochina.
