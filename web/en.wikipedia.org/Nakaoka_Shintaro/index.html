Nakaoka Shintarō

Nakaoka Shintarō (Japanese: 中岡慎太郎, 6 May 1838 – 17 November 1867) was a Japanese samurai of the late Edo period from Tosa Domain, and a close associate of Sakamoto Ryōma. He played a key role in the movement to overthrow the Tokugawa shogunate and restore imperial rule.

Life

Born in Kitanokawa village in Tosa (now Kōchi Prefecture), the son of a village official, Nakaoka studied swordsmanship and became a disciple of Takechi Hanpeita, leader of the Tosa Loyalist Party. After Takechi's forced suicide, Nakaoka fled Tosa and worked to broker cooperation between the rebel domains.

The Satchō Alliance and assassination

Nakaoka helped arrange the Satchō Alliance between Satsuma and Chōshū domains in 1866, and founded the Rikuentai militia as a counterpart to Sakamoto's Kaientai. On 15 November 1867 (by the old calendar), Nakaoka and Sakamoto were attacked at the Ōmiya inn in Kyoto; Sakamoto died that night and Nakaoka succumbed to his wounds two days later, weeks before the Meiji Restoration he had worked to bring about.
