Bach cantata

A Bach cantata is a cantata composed by Johann Sebastian Bach (1685–1750), the German composer of the Baroque period. Bach composed roughly 300 sacred cantatas for the Lutheran liturgy, of which about 200 survive; he also wrote a smaller number of secular cantatas. The works are catalogued with BWV (Bach-Werke-Verzeichnis) numbers.

Bach cantatas
Composer: Johann Sebastian Bach
Sacred cantatas: c. 300 composed, c. 200 surviving
Main period: 1723–1730s, Leipzig
Catalogue: BWV 1–224 (in part)

History

Bach wrote his earliest cantatas in Mühlhausen and Weimar, including the funeral cantata Gottes Zeit ist die allerbeste Zeit, BWV 106. After his appointment as Thomaskantor in Leipzig in 1723, he undertook the composition of annual cycles of cantatas for every Sunday and feast day of the church year. The second cycle (1724–25) was based predominantly on Lutheran chorales, giving it the name "chorale cantata cycle".

Structure

A typical Leipzig cantata opens with a large choral movement, followed by alternating recitatives and arias for solo voices, and closes with a four-part chorale harmonisation. Texts combine biblical words, contemporary poetry (often by Picander), and hymn stanzas. Well-known examples include Wachet auf, ruft uns die Stimme, BWV 140, Herz und Mund und Tat und Leben, BWV 147 (source of "Jesu, Joy of Man's Desiring"), and the dramatic solo cantata Ich habe genug, BWV 82.
