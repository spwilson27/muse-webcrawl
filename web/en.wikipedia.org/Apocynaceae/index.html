Apocynaceae is a family of flowering plants that includes trees, shrubs, herbs, stem succulents, and vines, commonly known as the dogbane family, because some taxa were used as dog poison. Members of the family are native to the European, Asian, African, Australian, and American tropics or subtropics, with some temperate members. The former family Asclepiadaceae (now known as Asclepiadoideae) is considered a subfamily of Apocynaceae and contains 348 genera. A list of Apocynaceae genera may be found here.

Many species are tall trees found in tropical forests, but some grow in tropical dry (xeric) environments. Also perennial herbs from temperate zones occur. Many of these plants have milky latex, and many species are poisonous if ingested, the family being rich in genera containing alkaloids and cardiac glycosides, those containing the latter often finding use as arrow poisons. Some genera of Apocynaceae, such as Adenium, have both milky latex and resin in their latex.

Description

Plants of this family have simple, usually opposite (rarely whorled or alternate) leaves, and flowers that are typically showy, radially symmetrical, and held in cymose inflorescences. The flowers usually have five united petals forming a tube, five sepals, and five stamens attached to a conspicuous style head. The fruit is typically a pair of follicles, drupes, or berries. Seeds of many species bear a tuft of hairs (a coma) aiding wind dispersal — a trait familiar from milkweeds.

Taxonomy

The family is placed in the order Gentianales. Modern classifications, following the Angiosperm Phylogeny Group, divide Apocynaceae into five subfamilies: Rauvolfioideae, Apocynoideae, Periplocoideae, Secamonoideae, and Asclepiadoideae. The last includes the milkweeds (genus Asclepias), stapeliads, and hoyas.

Uses and notable genera

Well-known members include oleander (Nerium oleander), periwinkles (Vinca and Catharanthus), frangipani (Plumeria), milkweeds (Asclepias), and the desert rose (Adenium). Catharanthus roseus is the source of the anticancer alkaloids vincristine and vinblastine, while Rauvolfia serpentina yielded reserpine, an early antihypertensive drug. Strophanthus species produce cardiac glycosides once used in arrow poisons and heart medicines.

Ecology

Milkweeds are the larval host plants of monarch butterflies, whose caterpillars sequester the plants' cardiac glycosides for their own defense. Many Apocynaceae have complex pollination mechanisms, especially the asclepiads, whose pollen is packaged into pollinia transferred as a unit by insect pollinators.
