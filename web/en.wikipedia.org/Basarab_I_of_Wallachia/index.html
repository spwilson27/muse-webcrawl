Basarab I of Wallachia

Basarab I (died 1351/1352), known as Basarab the Founder, was a voivode and the first independent ruler of Wallachia in south-eastern Europe.

Reign

Basarab united Romanian polities south of the Carpathians and asserted independence from the Kingdom of Hungary, defeating King Charles I of Hungary at the Battle of Posada in 1330.

Dynasty

He founded the House of Basarab, which ruled Wallachia for centuries; the region of Bessarabia is named after the dynasty.

Legacy

Basarab established the first Wallachian institutions, a metropolitan see, and a court at Curtea de Argeș.
