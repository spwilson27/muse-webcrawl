Bengali cuisine

Bengali cuisine is the culinary style of the Bengal region of the Indian subcontinent, encompassing present-day Bangladesh and the Indian state of West Bengal. It is known for its varied use of flavours, its emphasis on rice and freshwater fish, and its extensive range of sweets. With its refined multi-course meal structure, it is sometimes compared to French cuisine in its attention to sequence and detail.

Characteristics

Rice is the staple, accompanied by dal (lentils), vegetable preparations such as shukto and begun bhaja, and fish — above all hilsa, the region's most prized fish. Mustard oil and mustard seeds, along with the five-spice mixture panch phoron (cumin, nigella, fenugreek, fennel and mustard seeds), give Bengali cooking its distinctive flavour. Meals traditionally proceed from bitter to sweet courses, ending with mishti (sweets) such as rasgulla, sandesh and mishti doi, for which Bengal is famous.

Influences

Centuries of trade and rule brought Mughal, Persian, Portuguese, British and Chinese influences; Mughlai dishes entered through the courts of the nawabs, while Kolkata developed its own Indo-Chinese cuisine. The widowed Brahmin women of the 19th century and the thakurbari (household) of the Tagore family also shaped the cuisine's vegetarian repertoire.
