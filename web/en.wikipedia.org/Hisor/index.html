Hisor (Tajik: Ҳисор, romanised Hisor; Russian: Гиссар, Gissar) is a town in western Tajikistan, about 25 km west of the capital Dushanbe, at the eastern end of the Hisor Valley. The town's name derives from the Persian/Tajik word for "fort" or "fortress".

History

Hisor was an important stronghold of the Emirate of Bukhara, serving as the seat of the emir's governor (bek) for the eastern territories. The Hisor fortress, repeatedly rebuilt, was a residence of Ibrahim Bek, the Basmachi leader who resisted Soviet rule in the 1920s. The fortress complex — with its monumental gateway flanked by two cylindrical towers, madrasas and a mausoleum — has been restored and is one of Tajikistan's best-known historical monuments.

Geography

The town lies in the fertile Hisor Valley between the Hisor Range to the north and other ranges to the south, watered by the Khanaka River. The surrounding district is agricultural, producing cotton, grain and fruit, and the town serves as a local market and transport centre on the road west from Dushanbe toward Tursunzoda and Uzbekistan.
