Rotunda (architecture)

A rotunda is any building with a circular ground plan, and sometimes covered by a dome, or the large circular hall within such a building. The term may also denote a round room within any building.

History

The archetype is the Pantheon in Rome, a massive domed circular temple completed under Hadrian around 126 AD. Rotundas appeared in Roman baths and tombs, in early Christian and Romanesque churches (often as baptisteries or martyria), and were revived in Renaissance and neoclassical architecture — famous examples include Palladio's Villa Rotonda, the Radcliffe Camera in Oxford, and the domed rotundas of national capitols such as the United States Capitol.

Usage

Because of their monumental character, rotundas are often used for ceremonial spaces: libraries, museums, university buildings, and the central halls of legislatures.
