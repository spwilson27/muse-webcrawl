Science World

Science World's iconic geodesic dome at False Creek, Vancouver.

Science World is a science centre in Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada, located at the eastern end of False Creek. It is housed in a geodesic dome designed by architect Bruno Freschi, originally built as the Expo Centre for Expo 86, the world's fair held in Vancouver in 1986. The organisation, officially the Arts, Science and Technology Centre, operates interactive science exhibits and outreach programmes across British Columbia.

History

After Expo 86 closed, the dome was repurposed as a science centre and opened to the public in 1989. The building is a city landmark; its dome is illuminated at night and has become a symbol of the Vancouver skyline. In 2005 the naming rights were sold to Telus, and the centre was known for a time as the Telus World of Science before reverting to the name Science World.

Exhibits and programmes

The centre features permanent galleries on the human body, physics, natural history, and sustainability, a giant-screen OMNIMAX (IMAX Dome) theatre, and a stage for live science demonstrations. Its outreach programmes include school workshops, the Super Science Club for children in underserved communities, and travelling exhibitions.
