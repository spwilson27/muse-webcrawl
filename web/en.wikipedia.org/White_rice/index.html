White rice

TypeMilled rice
SourceOryza sativa
Food energy~130 kcal per 100 g (cooked)

White rice is milled rice that has had its husk, bran, and germ removed. This alters the flavour, texture, and appearance of the seed and helps prevent spoilage and extend its storage life. After milling, the rice is polished, resulting in a seed with a bright, white, shiny appearance.

Production

Harvested paddy rice is first hulled to produce brown rice, then milled and polished to strip the bran layers and germ. White rice keeps far longer than brown rice because the oils in the bran can turn rancid, but milling removes much of the fibre, B vitamins, and minerals.

Nutrition and health

Historically, diets based heavily on unenriched white rice contributed to beriberi, a disease caused by thiamine (vitamin B1) deficiency; this link helped lead to the discovery of vitamins. Many producing countries therefore require or practise enrichment of white rice with iron and B vitamins. Cooked white rice is a staple food for more than half the world's population.

Culinary use

White rice is cooked by steaming or boiling in water and served plain as an accompaniment, or used in dishes such as fried rice, rice pudding, pilaf, and sushi rice preparations.
