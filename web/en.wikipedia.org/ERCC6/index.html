Also known as: CSB (Cockayne syndrome protein B)
Gene location: Chromosome 10 (human)
Function: Transcription-coupled nucleotide excision repair
Associated disease: Cockayne syndrome type B

ERCC6 (excision repair cross-complementation group 6) is a gene in humans that encodes the DNA excision repair protein ERCC-6, also known as Cockayne syndrome protein B (CSB). The protein is involved in transcription-coupled nucleotide excision repair, a pathway that removes DNA lesions that block transcription.

CSB (ERCC6) helps restart repair when RNA polymerase stalls at DNA damage.

Function

When RNA polymerase II stalls at a DNA lesion during transcription, the CSB protein recognizes the stalled complex and helps recruit nucleotide excision repair factors to remove the damage, allowing transcription to resume. CSB is an ATP-dependent chromatin remodeler of the SWI2/SNF2 family and also plays roles in the cellular response to oxidative DNA damage.

Clinical significance

Mutations in ERCC6 account for the majority of cases of Cockayne syndrome (type B), a rare autosomal recessive disorder characterized by growth failure, photosensitivity, premature aging features and progressive neurological degeneration. Mutations can also cause cerebro-oculo-facio-skeletal (COFS) syndrome and some forms of UV-sensitive syndrome.
