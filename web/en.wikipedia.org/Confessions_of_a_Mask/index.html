Confessions of a Mask

Confessions of a Mask (Japanese: 仮面の告白, Kamen no Kokuhaku) is a novel by Japanese author Yukio Mishima, published in 1949. It is a semi-autobiographical work narrated by Kochan, a young man who, from early childhood, struggles with his homosexual desires and his fascination with death and violence, and constructs a "mask" of normality to present to the world. The book brought Mishima fame in Japan at the age of 24.

Plot

The narrator recounts formative experiences: his frail childhood under a domineering grandmother, an early fixation on a picture of Saint Sebastian, his love for his classmate Omi, and, during the war years, his failed courtship of Sonoko, the sister of a friend, which ends with his recognition that he cannot love her as she deserves.

Themes and reception

The novel explores identity, desire, and the performance of social roles in wartime and post-war Japan. It is often read alongside Mishima's later works, such as Forbidden Colors. An English translation by Meredith Weatherby appeared in 1958 and helped establish Mishima's international reputation.
