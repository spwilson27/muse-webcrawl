Reflectron

Hero image (placeholder):

A reflectron is a type of ion mirror used in time-of-flight mass spectrometry (TOF-MS) to improve mass resolution. It consists of a series of electrodes that create a retarding electric field; ions entering the reflectron are turned around and sent back in the opposite direction.

Principle

Ions of the same mass-to-charge ratio but slightly different kinetic energies spread out during flight. In a reflectron, more energetic ions penetrate deeper into the retarding field and so travel a longer path, while less energetic ions turn around sooner. Properly tuned, ions of the same m/z arrive at the detector simultaneously despite their energy spread, correcting first-order energy dispersion and greatly improving resolution. The device was developed in the Soviet Union by Boris Mamyrin in the 1970s.
