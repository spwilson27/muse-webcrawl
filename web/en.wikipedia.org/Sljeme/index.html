Sljeme

Sljeme is the highest peak of Medvednica, the mountain range rising immediately north of Zagreb, the capital of Croatia. Reaching an elevation of 1,033 metres (3,389 ft), it is a popular recreational destination for the city's residents and hosts the Sljeme transmitter, a 169-metre television and radio mast that is a landmark visible across the Zagreb region.

Geography and recreation

Medvednica is protected as a nature park, and Sljeme is accessible by road, hiking trails and, since its modern reconstruction, a gondola lift from the Gračani district of Zagreb. The mountain offers marked trails, mountain lodges and, in winter, a small ski area with several pistes served by chairlifts and artificial snowmaking. The ski slopes have hosted FIS Alpine Ski World Cup slalom races — the Snow Queen Trophy, named in honor of Croatian champion Janica Kostelić — drawing large crowds each January.

The transmitter

The Sljeme transmitter, completed in 1973, is a guyed steel mast whose height and elevated position make it the principal broadcast site for Zagreb and north-western Croatia. Its construction gave the summit a silhouette recognizable throughout the region, and an observation deck and restaurant near the summit have made the peak a sightseeing destination as well as a communications site.

Name

In Croatian, sljeme means "ridge" or "top of a roof," describing the summit's position on the crest of Medvednica. The peak should not be confused with the Zagreb city district also called Sljeme, an administrative quarter on the mountain's southern foothills.
