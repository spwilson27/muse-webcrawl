Hirta (Scottish Gaelic: Hiort) is the largest island in the St Kilda archipelago, lying far out in the North Atlantic about 64 km west-northwest of North Uist in the Outer Hebrides, Scotland. It was inhabited continuously for at least two thousand years until the entire remaining population of 36 islanders was evacuated to the mainland on 29 August 1930, at their own request, after illness, hardship and a declining population made the traditional way of life untenable.

Geography and history

Hirta rises steeply from the sea, with Conachair (430 m) the highest sea cliff summit in Britain. The islanders lived in Village Bay, subsisting on seabirds — fulmars, gannets and puffins — harvested from the cliffs, together with sheep (including the primitive Soay breed) and small-scale crofting. The deserted village, with its 19th-century blackhouses and thousands of stone storage structures called cleitean, is preserved by the National Trust for Scotland.

Today

St Kilda is a UNESCO World Heritage Site inscribed for both its natural and cultural significance. Hirta now supports a small rotating presence of National Trust wardens, researchers and a Ministry of Defence personnel contingent serving a tracking station on the island; the waters and cliffs host internationally important seabird colonies.
