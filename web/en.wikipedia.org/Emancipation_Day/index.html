Emancipation Day

Emancipation Day is a holiday in some countries of the former British Empire, and in parts of the United States, that commemorates the emancipation of enslaved people. In much of the Caribbean it is observed on 1 August, marking the anniversary of the Slavery Abolition Act 1833 coming into force in 1834, which ended slavery in most British colonies (though full freedom followed a period of "apprenticeship" ending in 1838).

Observances

August 1 is a public holiday in countries including Jamaica, Barbados, Trinidad and Tobago, and the Bahamas, and is marked with cultural festivals, concerts, and commemorations of African heritage. In Canada, Emancipation Day was officially designated in 2021 for federal observance on August 1.

United States

In the United States, emancipation is commemorated on various dates: Juneteenth (June 19) marks the 1865 announcement of emancipation in Texas and became a federal holiday in 2021, while Washington, D.C. observes April 16, the date of the District of Columbia Compensated Emancipation Act of 1862.
