Sofia Kovalevskaya

Sofia Vasilyevna Kovalevskaya (15 January 1850 – 10 February 1891) was a Russian mathematician who made noteworthy contributions to analysis, partial differential equations and mechanics. She was a pioneer for women in mathematics around the world – the first woman to obtain a doctorate (in the modern sense) in mathematics, the first woman appointed to a full professorship in Europe, and one of the first women to work for a scientific journal as an editor.

Educated privately after her family's nursery walls were papered with calculus lecture notes, Kovalevskaya moved to Germany and studied under Karl Weierstrass in Berlin, since women were barred from university lectures. The University of Göttingen awarded her a doctorate in 1874 for work including what is now known as the Cauchy–Kovalevskaya theorem on the existence of solutions to partial differential equations.

Career

Barred from academic posts in Russia, she eventually accepted a professorship at Stockholm University in 1889, becoming the first woman in modern Europe to hold a chair in mathematics. In 1888 she won the Prix Bordin of the French Academy of Sciences for her memoir on the rotation of a rigid body about a fixed point — the case of the "Kovalevskaya top". She also wrote fiction and memoirs, including A Russian Childhood.

Legacy

Kovalevskaya died of pneumonia in Stockholm in 1891, aged 41. Her name is commemorated by the lunar crater Kovalevskaya and by numerous lectures and prizes encouraging women in mathematics.
