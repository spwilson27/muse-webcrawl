Fortuna (Latin: Fortūna, equivalent to the Greek goddess Tyche) is the goddess of fortune and the personification of luck in Roman religion. She might bring good or bad luck: she could be represented as veiled and blind, as in modern depictions of Lady Justice, and originally came to represent life's capriciousness. She was also a goddess of fate, and her Wheel of Fortune became a central symbol in medieval and early modern thought.

Cult and worship

Fortuna had numerous temples and shrines across the Roman world, often under specific epithets reflecting particular aspects of her power, such as Fortuna Redux ("Fortuna who brings back safely"), Fortuna Primigenia at Praeneste, and Fors Fortuna, whose festival on 24 June was celebrated by the Roman plebs with boat races and revelry on the Tiber. Her cult at Praeneste was famous for its oracle, in which lots were drawn.

Iconography

Fortuna is typically shown holding a ship's rudder, with which she steers the course of life, and a cornucopia representing abundance. She frequently stands on or beside a ball or wheel, emphasizing instability. In medieval literature, her wheel turns kings into beggars and beggars into kings without warning, a theme explored by Boethius in The Consolation of Philosophy.
