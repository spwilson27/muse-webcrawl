Kodak

The Eastman Kodak Company, commonly known as Kodak, is an American technology company headquartered in Rochester, New York, founded by George Eastman in 1888. Kodak dominated the photographic film market for most of the 20th century, popularised amateur photography with products such as the Brownie camera and Kodachrome film, and became a cultural emblem of photography itself.

History

George Eastman introduced roll film and the first Kodak camera in 1888 with the slogan "You press the button, we do the rest." The $1 Brownie (1900) brought photography to the mass market. Kodak's colour products — Kodachrome (1935) and Kodacolor negative film (1942) — defined amateur and professional photography for decades, and Kodak film was used on Apollo missions and for most Hollywood films.

Digital disruption and bankruptcy

Ironically, a Kodak engineer, Steven Sasson, built the first digital camera in 1975, but the company was slow to cannibalise its profitable film business. Competition from Fujifilm and the rapid shift to digital photography and camera phones eroded its market. Kodak filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in January 2012 and emerged in September 2013 as a smaller company focused on commercial printing, enterprise imaging, and licensing its brand.

Present day

Today Kodak manufactures film for motion pictures and still photographers (film sales have revived modestly), produces commercial print and packaging systems, and develops advanced materials and chemicals, including pharmaceutical ingredients.
