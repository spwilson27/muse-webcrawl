W. C. Handy

William Christopher Handy (16 November 1873 – 28 March 1958) was an American composer and musician regarded as the "Father of the Blues". A classically trained musician, Handy was one of the first to publish blues music, taking the regional folk style of the Mississippi Delta to a wider audience.

Career

Born in Florence, Alabama, Handy worked as a bandleader and cornetist and travelled throughout the American South and Cuba, absorbing folk music traditions. His compositions include "Memphis Blues" (published 1912), "St. Louis Blues" (1914) — among the most recorded songs of the early 20th century — "Yellow Dog Blues" and "Beale Street Blues". In 1917 he co-founded the Handy Brothers Music Co. publishing firm in New York.

Legacy

Handy's autobiography, Father of the Blues, appeared in 1941. Memphis honours him with a statue in Handy Park on Beale Street, and his birthplace in Florence is preserved as a museum. He died in New York City in 1958.
