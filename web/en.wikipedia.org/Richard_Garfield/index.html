Richard Garfield


Richard Garfield, designer of Magic: The Gathering

Richard Channing Garfield (born June 26, 1963) is an American mathematician, inventor and game designer, best known as the creator of Magic: The Gathering (1993), the first trading card game, which launched an entire category of games and made its publisher, Wizards of the Coast, a major force in the games industry.

Early life and education

Garfield was born in Philadelphia and spent much of his childhood abroad as his father, an architect, worked overseas. A lifelong game enthusiast, he designed his first game at age 13. He earned a bachelor's degree in computer mathematics from the University of Pennsylvania and a PhD in combinatorial mathematics there in 1993, studying under Herbert Wilf; his dissertation concerned the residue classes of recursive sequences.

Magic: The Gathering

While seeking a publisher for his board game RoboRally, Garfield was asked by Peter Adkison of the small company Wizards of the Coast for a portable game that could be played in short sessions at conventions. Garfield combined ideas from earlier designs to create a collectible card game in which each player's deck is unique and cards are traded between players. Magic: The Gathering, released in August 1993, was an immediate sensation, selling out repeatedly and establishing the trading card game genre. Garfield received a patent for the game's mechanics.

Other work

Garfield has designed many other games, including the card games Netrunner, BattleTech, Vampire: The Eternal Struggle and Star Wars Trading Card Game, the board games RoboRally and King of Tokyo (2011), and the digital card game Artifact (2018) with Valve. He has taught game design and continues to work as an independent designer.
