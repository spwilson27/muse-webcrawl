Primitive cell

In geometry, crystallography, mineralogy and solid state physics, a primitive cell is a minimum-volume cell that, when repeated in all directions by lattice translations, completely fills space without gaps or overlaps. Primitive cells contain exactly one lattice point. A crystal structure is described by its lattice and a basis of atoms within the primitive cell.

Wigner–Seitz cell

There are many possible choices of primitive cell for a given lattice. A canonical choice is the Wigner–Seitz cell, defined as the set of points in space closer to a given lattice point than to any other; it is constructed from the perpendicular bisectors of the lines joining the point to its neighbours and displays the full symmetry of the lattice.

Unit cells

Conventional unit cells are often chosen for convenience and may contain more than one lattice point (for example, body-centred and face-centred cells). The primitive cell has the smallest possible volume among all cells that generate the lattice.
