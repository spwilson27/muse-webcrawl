CommissionerWally Oppal
EstablishedSeptember 2010

JurisdictionBritish Columbia, Canada

The Missing Women Commission of Inquiry was a public inquiry established by the government of British Columbia, Canada, to examine the police investigations into women who went missing from Vancouver's Downtown Eastside, many of whom were later found to have been murdered by serial killer Robert Pickton. The inquiry was announced in September 2010 and headed by former judge and attorney general Wally Oppal.

Mandate

The commission was directed to inquire into the conduct of police investigations between 1997 and 2002, including the decision by Crown counsel in 1998 to stay attempted murder charges against Pickton, and to recommend changes to how police in British Columbia investigate missing persons and suspected multiple homicides.

Hearings and criticism

Evidentiary hearings ran from October 2011 to June 2012. The inquiry was criticised by community groups, who argued that affected Indigenous and community organisations were denied adequate legal representation funding, prompting boycotts by several groups and a parallel people's inquiry organised by community advocates. Indigenous women were heavily overrepresented among the missing.

Report

The commission's final report, Forsaken, was released in December 2012. It contained 63 recommendations and found that the missing women investigations were marred by systemic bias, failures of police coordination between the Vancouver Police Department and the RCMP, and poor management. The case remains a focus of the broader national inquiry into missing and murdered Indigenous women and girls in Canada.
