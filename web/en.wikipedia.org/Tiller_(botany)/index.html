Tiller (botany)

A tiller is a stem produced by grass plants, and refers to all shoots that grow after the initial parent shoot grows from a seed. Tillers are segmented, each segment bearing its own two-part leaves. They are important in the vegetative propagation of grasses and in the yield of cereal crops such as wheat, rice and maize relatives.

Tillering

Tillering, the production of side shoots, is a physiological process regulated by plant hormones including auxin, strigolactones and cytokinins, and by environmental factors such as light, nutrient supply and planting density. Each tiller can potentially produce its own inflorescence and grain, so tiller number is a key component of cereal yield.

Agronomic significance

In turf and lawn grasses, tillering produces a dense sward that resists weed invasion. In crop breeding, the balance between tiller number and grain size per ear is a major selection target, and the domestication of cereals involved changes in tillering behaviour compared with their wild ancestors.
