Langstroth hive

The Langstroth hive is a standard beehive design invented by the American apiarist Lorenzo Lorraine Langstroth, who patented it in 1852. It uses movable frames spaced according to the 'bee space' Langstroth identified, allowing beekeepers to inspect colonies and harvest honey without destroying the comb.

Design

A typical hive consists of stacked rectangular boxes (supers) containing frames, a bottom board, and inner and outer covers. Ten-frame and eight-frame versions are common.

Impact

The design underpins modern commercial and hobby beekeeping worldwide and made migratory beekeeping and large-scale honey production practical.
