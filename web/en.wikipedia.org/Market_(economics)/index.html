Market (economics)

In economics, a market is a composition of systems, institutions, procedures, social relations or infrastructures whereby parties engage in exchange. While parties may exchange goods and services by barter, most markets rely on sellers offering goods or services (including labour) in exchange for money from buyers. A market can be described as the process by which the prices of goods and services are established, enabling exchange to take place.

Types of markets

Markets vary in size, range, geographic scale and structure. They include markets for consumer and capital goods, financial markets (stocks, bonds, currencies, derivatives), labour markets, and informal or black markets. Market structure describes the degree of competition: perfect competition, monopolistic competition, oligopoly and monopoly. Markets may be physical places, like a farmers' market or a stock exchange floor, or entirely virtual, like online marketplaces.

Mechanism

Prices in a market emerge from the interaction of supply and demand: when demand exceeds supply at the prevailing price, price tends to rise, and vice versa, moving the market toward equilibrium. Economists study market failures — externalities, public goods, information asymmetries and market power — where unregulated exchange does not produce efficient outcomes, as well as the legal and institutional framework (property rights, contract enforcement) that markets require to function.
