Proper name

A proper name, and its associated subject of philosophy, a proper noun, is a name for an individual person, place, or organization, spelled with an initial capital letter in English. Examples include "London", "Jupiter", and "Sarah". Proper names contrast with common nouns, which denote classes of things.

Philosophy of language

The meaning of proper names has been a central topic in philosophy since John Stuart Mill argued that names denote but do not connote: they refer to their bearer without describing it. Later theories, such as Saul Kripke's causal-historical theory, treat proper names as rigid designators that pick out the same object in every possible world. Descriptive theories associated with Gottlob Frege and Bertrand Russell instead link names to clusters of identifying descriptions.

Capitalization and grammar

In English orthography, proper nouns are capitalized, though usage varies historically and across languages. German capitalizes all nouns, while some languages do not mark proper names distinctly at all.
