Marion Lois Jones (born 12 October 1975), also known as Marion Jones-Thompson, is an American former world champion track-and-field athlete and former professional basketball player. She won three gold and two bronze medals at the 2000 Summer Olympics in Sydney, but was later stripped of all her Olympic medals after admitting to performance-enhancing drug use.

Athletic career

Jones starred in both track and basketball at the University of North Carolina, helping the Tar Heels win the 1994 NCAA women's basketball championship before concentrating on sprinting. She won the 100 metres and 200 metres at the 1997 and 1999 World Championships and became the face of the Sydney 2000 Games, winning gold in the 100 m, 200 m and 4×400 m relay and bronze in the long jump and 4×100 m relay.

Doping scandal

For years Jones denied using banned substances, but she was implicated in the BALCO laboratory scandal. In October 2007 she admitted in court that she had used the designer steroid tetrahydrogestrinone ("the clear") and lied to federal investigators. She was sentenced to six months in prison in 2008, the IOC formally stripped her medals, and her results from 2000 onward were annulled.

Later life

After her release, Jones briefly played professional basketball in the WNBA with the Tulsa Shock (2010–2011). She has since spoken publicly about her fall and redemption.
