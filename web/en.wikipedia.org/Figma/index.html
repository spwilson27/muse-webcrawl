Figma

Figma is a collaborative web application for interface design, with additional offline features enabled by desktop applications for macOS and Windows. Developed by the company of the same name, founded in 2012 by Dylan Field and Evan Wallace, Figma launched publicly in 2016 and became noted for bringing real-time multiplayer collaboration — multiple designers editing the same file simultaneously — to vector design tools. Its companion product FigJam, released in 2021, provides an online whiteboard for brainstorming.

Features

Figma runs primarily in the browser using WebGL, and offers vector editing, prototyping, design systems with shared components and styles, and developer handoff tools such as Dev Mode. Because files live in the cloud, links can be shared for viewing or commenting without installing software.

History

Field began work on the company while at Brown University, and Figma raised early funding through the Thiel Fellowship. In September 2022, Adobe announced an agreement to acquire Figma for about $20 billion, which would have been Adobe's largest acquisition. The deal was abandoned in December 2023 after regulators in the United Kingdom and the European Union raised competition concerns; Adobe paid Figma a $1 billion termination fee. Figma subsequently continued as an independent company and expanded into additional products including presentation and site-building tools.
