The Maasvlakte is a massive man-made westward extension of the Europoort port complex of Rotterdam, built on land reclaimed from the North Sea at the mouth of the Meuse (Maas) river. Together with Europoort it forms part of the Port of Rotterdam, the largest seaport in Europe. The name combines Maas (the Meuse) with vlakte, Dutch for "plain" or "flat area".

Maasvlakte 1

The original Maasvlakte was created in the 1960s when the Maasvlakte dam and reclamation works closed off part of the coastal waters, and sand dredged from the North Sea and the Brielse Meer was used to build a large industrial area. It hosts oil terminals, bulk and chemical facilities, the E.ON/Maasvlakte power stations, and container terminals including ECT's Delta terminal and APM Terminals Rotterdam. The artificial beach on its western edge (the Slufter and Maasvlakte beach) is popular for recreation and kitesurfing.

Maasvlakte 2

By the early 2000s the port was running out of space, and construction of Maasvlakte 2 (Tweede Maasvlakte) began in 2008. The project reclaimed some 2,000 hectares — expanding the port area by about 20 percent — with a new sea wall, deep-water quays accessible to the largest container ships, and nature compensation areas, including new dunes and seabed protection zones in the Voordelta. The first terminals, Rotterdam World Gateway and APM Terminals Maasvlakte II, opened in 2015 and are notable for remote-controlled, automated container handling.

Transport links

The Maasvlakte is connected to the European hinterland by the Betuweroute freight railway to Germany, motorways (A15), inland shipping terminals for Rhine barges, and short-sea and pipeline links. It is a principal gateway for containers, coal, ores, and chemicals entering the Rhine-Meuse-Scheldt delta.
