The Battle of Kosovo was fought on 15 June 1389 between an army led by the Serbian Prince Lazar Hrebeljanović and the invading Ottoman army of Sultan Murad I, on the Kosovo Field about 5 kilometres northwest of modern Pristina. Both Lazar and Murad were killed in the fighting. The battle became a central element of Serbian national tradition and epic poetry.

Background

The fragmentation of the Serbian Empire after Stefan Dušan's death left the Balkan principalities exposed to Ottoman expansion. After Ottoman victories at Maritsa (1371) and the gradual subjugation of Macedonian and Bulgarian lands, Murad marched into Serbia in 1389. Lazar assembled a coalition including his own forces, troops of his son-in-law Vuk Branković, and a Bosnian contingent under Vlatko Vuković.

The battle

Contemporary sources are sparse and later accounts heavily embroidered by legend. The fighting appears to have been fierce and initially favoured the Christian army, whose heavy cavalry broke the Ottoman left. During or shortly after the battle, Murad was assassinated—Serbian tradition credits the knight Miloš Obilić, who supposedly feigned desertion to reach the sultan's tent. Lazar was captured and executed. Murad's son Bayezid assumed command and, by most accounts, consolidated the Ottoman position before withdrawing to secure his succession.

Aftermath and legacy

Serbia became an Ottoman vassal under Lazar's widow Milica and his son Stefan Lazarević, though full annexation came only in 1459. In Serbian memory the battle grew into the "Kosovo Myth": Lazar's legendary choice of a heavenly over an earthly kingdom, the heroism of Obilić, and the alleged treason of Vuk Branković. The site, marked by the Gazimestan monument, remains symbolically powerful; the 600th anniversary commemoration in 1989 drew enormous crowds on the eve of the Yugoslav wars.
