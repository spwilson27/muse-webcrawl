Pyramid Stage

Location: Glastonbury Festival, Worthy Farm, Pilton, Somerset, England
Role: Main stage of the Glastonbury Festival
First used: 1971 (original pyramid)

The Pyramid Stage is the main stage of the Glastonbury Festival of Contemporary Performing Arts, held at Worthy Farm near Pilton, Somerset, England. Its distinctive pyramid shape has become the festival's most recognisable symbol, and it hosts the event's headline acts.

History

A pyramid stage first appeared at the festival in 1971, designed by Bill Harkin in consultation with Andrew Kerr; the pyramid was said to be aligned with the Glastonbury ley line and intended to focus energy. After the original structure burned down in 1994, a temporary substitute was used before a permanent steel-framed pyramid — 30 metres high, built from reclaimed materials including telegraph poles and Ministry of Defence surplus — opened in 2000.

Performances

Headliners on the Pyramid Stage have included David Bowie (2000), Paul McCartney, Beyoncé (the first female solo headliner, 2011), the Rolling Stones (2013), Adele, Radiohead, Stormzy, Kendrick Lamar, and Elton John, whose 2023 set was billed as his final UK performance. The Sunday afternoon "legends" slot has featured performers such as Dolly Parton, Diana Ross, and Lionel Richie.
