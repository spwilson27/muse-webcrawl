Crucifixion of Jesus

The crucifixion is central to Christian theology and commemorated on Good Friday.

The crucifixion of Jesus occurred in 1st-century Judaea, most likely in AD 30 or AD 33. Jesus's crucifixion is described in the four canonical gospels and is attested in other ancient sources, and is established as a historical event. The collective Christian belief is that Jesus was arrested, tried by the Sanhedrin and sentenced by Pontius Pilate to be scourged and finally crucified.

Narrative

According to the Gospels, after the Last Supper Jesus was arrested in Gethsemane, tried before Jewish and Roman authorities, and condemned. He carried his cross to Golgotha ("the place of the skull") outside Jerusalem's walls, where he was crucified between two criminals, with a titulus reading "Jesus of Nazareth, King of the Jews" (INRI). The accounts record darkness, an earthquake and the tearing of the Temple veil, and Jesus's final sayings — the "seven last words". He died after about six hours; his body was taken down by Joseph of Arimathea and laid in a rock tomb.

Historicity

The crucifixion under Pilate is one of the most widely accepted facts about Jesus among historians: it is mentioned by Josephus (with interpolations) and by Tacitus in Annals 15.44, and satisfies the criterion of embarrassment, since a crucified messiah was a stumbling block to early Christian preaching. Scholarly debate centres on the chronology, the details of the trials, and the burial.

Theology and commemoration

In Christian theology the crucifixion is understood as an atoning sacrifice for the sins of humanity, commemorated in the Eucharist and mourned on Good Friday during Holy Week; it is followed, in Christian belief, by the resurrection celebrated at Easter. The cross became the central symbol of Christianity. Depictions of the crucifixion are a dominant theme in Western art; in Islam, the Quran states that Jesus was not crucified but raised to God.
