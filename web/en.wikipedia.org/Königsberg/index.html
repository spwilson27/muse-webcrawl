Königsberg (German for "King's Mountain") was the historic German city on the Pregel River in East Prussia that is now Kaliningrad, Russia. Founded in 1255 by the Teutonic Knights during the Northern Crusades, it became the capital of the Duchy of Prussia (1525) and later of the Prussian province of East Prussia. For nearly seven centuries it was a major Baltic port and a centre of German culture and learning, until its destruction in the Second World War and annexation by the Soviet Union in 1945.

History

The Teutonic Order built a castle in 1255 on the site of an Old Prussian settlement, naming it in honour of King Ottokar II of Bohemia, who had aided the crusade. Around the castle grew three towns — Altstadt, Löbenicht, and Kneiphof — formally united into one city in 1724. Königsberg joined the Hanseatic League and flourished as a trading port. In 1544 Duke Albert founded its university, the Albertina, which became a stronghold first of Lutheran learning and then of the Enlightenment. In 1701 Elector Frederick III crowned himself King in Prussia in the castle chapel.

Kant and the bridges

The philosopher Immanuel Kant (1724–1804) was born in Königsberg and lived there his entire life, teaching at the Albertina; his tomb stands by the cathedral on Kneiphof island. The city is also immortalised in mathematics by the Seven Bridges of Königsberg problem: in 1736 Leonhard Euler proved it impossible to walk a route crossing each of the city's seven bridges exactly once, a result that founded graph theory and anticipated topology.

Destruction and aftermath

In August 1944 British bombing raids devastated the old city. After a brutal siege, the Red Army captured Königsberg in April 1945. At Potsdam the Allies assigned northern East Prussia to the USSR; the remaining German population was expelled westward by 1950 and replaced by Russian settlers. The city was renamed Kaliningrad in 1946, after Soviet leader Mikhail Kalinin. The ruined castle was demolished in the 1960s, though the Gothic cathedral — and Kant's tomb — were restored in the 1990s.
