Simulation

A simulation is an imitative representation of the function of a process or system that could exist in the real world. In its narrowest sense, simulation means using models to study the behaviour of a system over time, often because direct experimentation is impossible, dangerous, expensive or unethical. Simulations are used in many contexts, such as performance optimisation, safety engineering, training, education and entertainment.

Types of simulation

Simulations may be classified as physical (a physical object substituted for the real one, such as a wind-tunnel model or a patient manikin), interactive (a human participates, as in a flight simulator or driving simulator), or purely computational. Computer simulations use mathematical models executed on a computer and range from weather prediction and molecular dynamics to Monte Carlo methods that sample random variables to estimate quantities that cannot be computed directly.

Applications

Training and education

Flight simulators, medical simulators and military exercises allow trainees to practise dangerous or rare scenarios safely. Business games and classroom simulations teach decision-making by letting students experience the consequences of their choices in a controlled environment.

Science and engineering

Engineers simulate structures, circuits and fluid flows before building them; scientists simulate climate, galaxies, epidemics and economies. Verification and validation — checking that the model is implemented correctly and faithfully represents the real system — are central concerns, since a simulation is only as trustworthy as its underlying model and assumptions.

Simulation hypothesis and fiction

In philosophy, the simulation hypothesis proposes that reality itself could be an artificial simulation, an idea popularised in modern form by Nick Bostrom's 2003 simulation argument. The concept has a long history in thought experiments such as Plato's cave and Descartes' evil demon, and appears frequently in science fiction film and literature.
