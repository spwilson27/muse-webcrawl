Warner Bros. Animation Inc. is an American animation studio owned by Warner Bros. Discovery. Founded in 1980, it is the successor to the classic Warner Bros. Cartoons studio that produced the Looney Tunes and Merrie Melodies shorts. The division is best known for its television work of the 1990s, including Batman: The Animated Series, Animaniacs and Tiny Toon Adventures, produced in association with Steven Spielberg's Amblin Entertainment.

History

Warner Bros. had closed its original animation studio in 1969. In 1980 the studio formally re-entered animation under the Warner Bros. Animation banner, initially producing television specials and compilation features. A turning point came in 1989–1990 with Tiny Toon Adventures, developed with Amblin, which revived the studio's reputation for theatrical-quality television animation.

The 1990s became a celebrated period: Batman: The Animated Series (1992) won Emmy Awards and spawned the DC Animated Universe, followed by Superman: The Animated Series, Batman Beyond and Justice League. Animaniacs (1993) and Pinky and the Brain were also major successes. In 1996, following Time Warner's acquisition of Turner Broadcasting, Hanna-Barbera properties were folded into the studio's orbit, though Hanna-Barbera itself was absorbed in 2001 with its library overseen by Warner Bros. Animation.

Later work

In the 2000s and 2010s the studio produced series such as Teen Titans, The Batman, Batman: The Brave and the Bold, Young Justice, Teen Titans Go! and DC Super Hero Girls, along with direct-to-video DC animated films. It also produces Looney Tunes revivals, including The Looney Tunes Show and New Looney Tunes, and programming for streaming services such as Max.
