Anti-aliasing filter

An anti-aliasing filter (AAF) is a filter used before a signal sampler to restrict the bandwidth of a signal to satisfy the Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem over the band of interest. Since the theorem states that unambiguous reconstruction of the signal from its samples is possible when the power of frequencies above the Nyquist frequency is zero, an effective anti-aliasing filter is a low-pass filter with a stopband beginning at the Nyquist frequency.

Analog and digital implementations

In analog-to-digital conversion, a physical low-pass filter (for example a Butterworth, Chebyshev or Bessel filter) attenuates components above half the sampling rate before sampling. In oversampling converters the analog filter requirements are relaxed because most filtering is done digitally. In digital imaging, an optical low-pass filter — a birefringent plate placed over the sensor in many cameras — blurs the image slightly to reduce moiré patterns caused by aliasing of fine detail.

Audio applications

Compact disc systems sample at 44.1 kHz, so anti-aliasing filters must pass audio up to about 20 kHz while strongly attenuating frequencies above 22.05 kHz, a requirement that motivated steep "brick-wall" filter designs and later oversampling architectures.
