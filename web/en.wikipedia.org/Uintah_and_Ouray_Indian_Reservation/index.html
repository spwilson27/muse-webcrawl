Uintah and Ouray Indian Reservation

The Uintah and Ouray Indian Reservation is located in northeastern Utah, United States, west of the Colorado state line and east of the Wasatch Range. It is the homeland of the Ute Indian Tribe, a federally recognized tribe whose members descend principally from the Uintah, Whiteriver, and Uncompahgre bands of Utes. At more than 4.5 million acres (about 6,800 square miles / 17,500 km²) in total area, it is the second-largest Indian reservation in the United States, though much of its land is held in a checkerboard pattern of tribal, allotted, and non-Indian ownership. The tribal headquarters is at Fort Duchesne.

History

The Uintah Valley Reservation was established by executive order of President Abraham Lincoln in 1861 and confirmed by Congress in 1864. The Uncompahgre (Ouray) Reservation was created in 1882 for Utes removed from Colorado after the Meeker incident of 1879, and Colorado Utes — including the Whiteriver and Uncompahgre bands — were relocated to Utah. The two reservations were combined for administrative purposes. Under the allotment policy, the reservation was opened to non-Indian homesteading in 1905, stripping the tribe of much of its land and setting up jurisdictional disputes that lasted into the 21st century, including the Supreme Court and Tenth Circuit litigation over whether the reservation's boundaries were diminished.

The tribe today

The Ute Indian Tribe has about 3,000 enrolled members, roughly half of whom live on the reservation. The tribe operates enterprises including oil and gas development in the Uinta Basin, ranching, and tourism, and exercises governmental authority through its Business Committee. The Northern Ute language, a Southern Numic language of the Uto-Aztecan family, is taught in tribal programs.
