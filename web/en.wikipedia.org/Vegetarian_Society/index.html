Vegetarian Society

Formation: 30 September 1847
Type: Charitable organisation
Purpose: Promotion of vegetarianism
Headquarters: Altrincham, England

The Vegetarian Society of the United Kingdom is a British registered charity that was established on 30 September 1847 to promote vegetarianism. It is widely credited with coining and popularising the word "vegetarian", and is the oldest vegetarian organisation in the world.

History

The society grew out of the earlier English health-reform and temperance movements influenced by the Bible Christian Church of William Cowherd in Salford. It was founded at a meeting at Northwood Villa in Ramsgate, Kent, in 1847. The Manchester-based society published The Vegetarian Messenger, held annual festivals, and campaigned for meat-free diets on ethical, health and economic grounds. Mahatma Gandhi was a member and served on its executive committee while studying law in London.

Activities

The society runs a cookery school, campaigns to improve vegetarian options in public institutions, and operates a widely used product accreditation scheme (the Vegetarian Society Approved trademarks, including a vegan trademark) used by food manufacturers and restaurants.
