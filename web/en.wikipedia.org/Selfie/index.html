Selfie

A selfie is a self-portrait photograph or a short video, typically taken with an electronic camera or smartphone. The camera and screen are held in the hand or supported by a selfie stick. Selfies are often shared on social media, via social networking services such as Facebook, Instagram, Threads, Twitter, and Snapchat.

The word selfie was first traced to an Australian internet forum in 2002, and it became widespread in the 2010s as front-facing cameras became standard on smartphones. In 2013, "selfie" was named the word of the year by Oxford Dictionaries.

History

Self-portrait photography dates to the earliest days of the medium: Robert Cornelius produced a daguerreotype of himself in 1839, often considered the first photographic self-portrait. Compact digital cameras in the 2000s made self-portraits easier, and social networking sites popularized them. The introduction of the front camera on the iPhone 4 in 2010 helped make the selfie a mainstream phenomenon.

Popularity and culture

Selfies have become a major form of self-expression and communication, used by ordinary users, celebrities, and politicians. Notable examples include the group selfie taken by Ellen DeGeneres at the 86th Academy Awards in 2014, which was for a time the most retweeted photo ever. Museums and public places have responded with selfie-friendly installations, while some have banned selfie sticks for safety reasons.

Criticism and risks

Critics associate excessive selfie-taking with narcissism and body-image pressures, though research findings are mixed. Dangerous selfies taken in risky locations have resulted in numerous injuries and deaths, prompting some governments to designate "no selfie zones". Psychologists have also studied "selfitis", a term popularized in media reports describing compulsive selfie-taking.
