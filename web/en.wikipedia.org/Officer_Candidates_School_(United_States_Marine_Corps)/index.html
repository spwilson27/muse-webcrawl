Officer Candidates School (United States Marine Corps)

Officer Candidates School (OCS) is a training regimen at Marine Corps Base Quantico, Virginia, which screens and evaluates potential Marine Corps officers. All Marine officers, with the exception of those who are graduates of the United States Naval Academy, must successfully complete the coursework and evaluation at OCS, or Officer Instruction prior to commencing The Basic School (TBS). The motto of OCS is Ductus Exemplo, Latin for "Lead by Example".

Overview

The candidates, referred to as officer candidates rather than cadets, are screened physically, mentally, and morally by the staff of the school. Unlike the other United States military services, the U.S. Marine Corps requires all of its commissioned officers to go through OCS or an equivalent program, whether they come through the enlisted ranks (through programs such as the Meritorious Commissioning Program), Reserve Officers' Training Corps (ROTC), or programs such as the Platoon Leaders Class (PLC), the Officer Candidate Course (OCC), or the Naval Reserve Officer Training Corps.

Selection and training

During training, candidates are evaluated on academic performance, leadership, and physical fitness. A significant portion of the evaluation is peer-based, and candidates who fail to meet the standards are dropped from the program. The course is deliberately stressful, designed to simulate the pressures of combat leadership and to identify those who can lead Marines under duress.

Programs

  • Platoon Leaders Class (PLC) — for college freshmen, sophomores, and juniors, split into two six-week summer increments or one ten-week session.
  • Officer Candidate Course (OCC) — a ten-week course for college seniors and graduates.
  • Naval Reserve Officers Training Corps (NROTC) — Marine-option midshipmen attend a six-week course ("Bulldog") between their junior and senior years.
  • Enlisted Commissioning Program (ECP) and Meritorious Commissioning Program (MCP) — for qualified enlisted Marines.

History

The Marine Corps officer training tradition dates to 1891, when a School of Application was established at Marine Barracks, Washington, D.C. The school moved to Quantico in the early 20th century. During World War II, OCS produced the bulk of the Marine Corps' junior officers. Famous graduates include many senior Marine leaders; the school's graduation parade and the awarding of the Eagle, Globe, and Anchor remain central traditions.
