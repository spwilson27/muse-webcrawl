Swachh Bharat Mission

The Swachh Bharat Mission (SBM), or Clean India Mission, is a nationwide campaign launched by the Government of India on 2 October 2014 — the birth anniversary of Mahatma Gandhi — by Prime Minister Narendra Modi. Its original aim was to eliminate open defecation and improve solid waste management throughout India by 2 October 2019, the 150th anniversary of Gandhi's birth.

Structure and results

The mission has two components: Swachh Bharat Mission (Gramin), run by the Ministry of Drinking Water and Sanitation (later the Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation under the Ministry of Jal Shakti) for rural areas, and Swachh Bharat Mission (Urban), run by the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs. The government reported the construction of over 100 million household toilets and declared rural India open defecation free (ODF) on 2 October 2019, with all 603,175 villages declared ODF — claims broadly supported by increases in toilet access recorded in national surveys, though the National Family Health Survey and independent studies found continued open defecation by a minority of households.

Phase II

Phase II (2020–2025) shifted focus to sustaining ODF status ("ODF Plus"), solid and liquid waste management in villages, and faecal sludge management, while the urban mission (SBM-U 2.0, from 2021) targeted garbage-free cities and remediation of legacy dumpsites. Sanitation workers' safety and the eradication of manual scavenging remained areas of concern for critics. The mission is among the largest behaviour-change and sanitation programmes in the world, and the annual Swachh Survekshan survey ranks Indian cities on cleanliness.
