Trove is an Australian online library database aggregator and digital collections platform operated by the National Library of Australia. It provides a single point of access to the collections of libraries, museums, archives, universities and other cultural and research organisations across Australia, and is best known for its vast collection of digitised historical newspapers.

Launched in 2009, Trove grew out of earlier National Library services and discovery tools. It allows users to search across books, journals, maps, photographs, diaries, letters, archived websites, music and oral histories, as well as the full text of millions of pages of digitised newspapers dating back to the earliest Australian publications.

Digitised newspapers

The newspaper collection is the most heavily used part of Trove. Titles digitised under the Australian Newspapers Digitisation Program include metropolitan and regional papers, among them The Sydney Morning Herald, The Argus and The Canberra Times. Users can correct the automatically generated (OCR) text of articles, a crowdsourcing effort that has produced hundreds of millions of corrected lines.

History

Trove was released publicly in November 2009, replacing several separate discovery services. In 2020 the platform was relaunched with a redesigned interface. Later that year, funding uncertainty prompted a public campaign of support, and the Australian government subsequently committed ongoing funding for the service, securing its future.
