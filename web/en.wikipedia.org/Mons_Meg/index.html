Mons Meg

Mons Meg is a medieval supergun — a giant bombard — built around 1449 and held at Edinburgh Castle in Scotland. It is one of the largest cannons in the world by calibre, firing stone balls of about 20 inches (510 mm) in diameter weighing roughly 385 lb (175 kg).

Mons Meg
Type: Bombard
Place of origin: Mons, Duchy of Burgundy
Calibre: c. 510 mm
Weight: c. 6.6 tonnes

History

Mons Meg was forged in Mons in the Duchy of Burgundy (in modern Belgium) and was presented by Philip the Good, Duke of Burgundy, to his great-nephew-by-marriage James II of Scotland in 1454, as part of Burgundian support for Scotland against England. She was hauled across Scotland and used in sieges, including the bombardment of Threave Castle in 1455.

By the later fifteenth century the great gun was retired to ceremonial duty, fired only on special occasions. In 1680 her barrel burst while firing a salute for the visiting Duke of York (later James VII and II), ending her active career. Legend attributes the burst to an English gunner overloading her.

Return to Edinburgh

In the 1750s Mons Meg was taken to the Tower of London along with other Scottish military stores. Following a campaign in which Sir Walter Scott played a prominent part, she was returned to Edinburgh Castle in 1829 with great ceremony, escorted by cavalry, infantry, and artillery. She stands today at the castle near St Margaret's Chapel, one of Scotland's most famous historic artefacts.
