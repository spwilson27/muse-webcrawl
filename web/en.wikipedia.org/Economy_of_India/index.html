Economy of India

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Mumbai, home of the Bombay Stock Exchange and the Reserve Bank of India.

The economy of India is a developing mixed economy with a notable public sector in strategic sectors. It is the world's fifth-largest economy by nominal GDP and the third-largest by purchasing power parity (PPP). India has been among the world's fastest-growing major economies since the liberalisation reforms of 1991, driven by services — especially information technology and business process outsourcing — which account for over half of output, alongside large agricultural and industrial sectors.

History

For most of the centuries before colonisation, the Indian subcontinent accounted for a large share of world output. After independence in 1947, India adopted a planned, protectionist model with extensive state ownership. A balance-of-payments crisis in 1991 prompted reforms under Prime Minister P. V. Narasimha Rao and Finance Minister Manmohan Singh: industrial licensing was largely abolished, trade and foreign investment opened, and the rupee made partly convertible.

Sectors

Agriculture employs the largest share of the workforce — roughly 45% — while producing under a fifth of GDP. Industry includes textiles, chemicals, pharmaceuticals, steel and automobiles. The services sector, led by IT hubs such as Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Pune, contributes the majority of growth and exports.

Challenges

India faces challenges including informal employment, infrastructure gaps, poverty, and the need to create jobs for a young and growing workforce, even as it becomes one of the world's leading economies by aggregate size.
