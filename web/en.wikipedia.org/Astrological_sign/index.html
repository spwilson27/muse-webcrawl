Astrological sign

In Western astrology, astrological signs are the twelve 30-degree sectors of the ecliptic, the apparent path of the Sun across the celestial sphere. The signs are named after the constellations that largely corresponded to them in the Hellenistic era: Aries, Taurus, Gemini, Cancer, Leo, Virgo, Libra, Scorpio, Sagittarius, Capricorn, Aquarius and Pisces. A person's "sun sign" is determined by the Sun's position in this zodiac at their birth.

History

The Babylonian astronomers divided the ecliptic into twelve equal signs by the 5th century BCE. The system was adopted and elaborated in Hellenistic astrology, codified by Ptolemy in the Tetrabiblos, and transmitted through Islamic and medieval European astrology into modern practice. Because of the precession of the equinoxes, the tropical zodiac used in the West no longer aligns with the constellations of the same name; Vedic astrology instead uses a sidereal zodiac.

The twelve signs

The signs run from Aries (beginning at the March equinox in the tropical system) through Taurus, Gemini, Cancer, Leo and Virgo to Libra, Scorpio, Sagittarius, Capricorn, Aquarius and Pisces. Each is traditionally assigned ruling planets, symbols, glyphs, date ranges and personality characteristics, forming the basis of newspaper horoscopes.

Elements and qualities

The signs are grouped into four triplicities by element — fire (Aries, Leo, Sagittarius), earth (Taurus, Virgo, Capricorn), air (Gemini, Libra, Aquarius) and water (Cancer, Scorpio, Pisces) — and into three quadruplicities by quality: cardinal, fixed and mutable.

Criticism

Astrology's claims have been rejected by the scientific community: controlled tests, such as the 1985 double-blind study published in Nature, have found no evidence that natal chart interpretations perform better than chance, and sign-based personality descriptions are explained by the Barnum effect.
