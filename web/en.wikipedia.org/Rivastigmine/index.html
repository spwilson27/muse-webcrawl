Rivastigmine, sold under the brand name Exelon among others, is a cholinesterase inhibitor used for the treatment of dementia associated with Alzheimer's disease and with Parkinson's disease. It is available as capsules, oral solution and a transdermal patch, the patch reducing gastrointestinal side effects.

Mechanism

Rivastigmine is a carbamate derivative that reversibly inhibits both acetylcholinesterase and butyrylcholinesterase, increasing acetylcholine levels in the brain. Unlike irreversible inhibitors its binding is described as "pseudo-irreversible" because the enzyme is carbamylated and recovers slowly.

Side effects

Common side effects include nausea, vomiting, loss of appetite, diarrhoea and dizziness. It has modest symptomatic benefits on cognition and daily functioning but does not alter the progression of the underlying disease. It was approved for medical use in the United States in 2000 and is available as a generic medication.
