Backpacking (wilderness)

Backpacking is the outdoor recreation of carrying one's gear in a backpack for multi-day hiking and camping trips in wilderness or backcountry areas. It combines hiking with self-sufficiency: everything for food, shelter and safety must be carried on the back, typically for trips from a single overnight to weeks or months.

Equipment

Core gear includes a framed rucksack, tent or other shelter, sleeping bag and pad, stove and cookware, water treatment, layered clothing, navigation and first aid supplies. Because every gram is carried, weight is a central concern; the ultralight movement pursues base weights under about 4.5 kg (10 lb) through minimalist and multi-use equipment. A common packing framework is the "Ten Essentials", a list of survival items popularised by mountaineering clubs.

Practice

Long-distance "thru-hikes" of trails such as the Appalachian Trail, Pacific Crest Trail and Continental Divide Trail are an extreme form, taking months. Backpackers are encouraged to follow Leave No Trace principles — packing out waste, camping on durable surfaces and minimising impact — and to manage risks including weather, wildlife (in bear country, food is stored in canisters or hung), altitude and water scarcity.
