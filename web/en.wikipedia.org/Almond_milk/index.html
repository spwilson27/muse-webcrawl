Almond milk

Almond milk is a plant-based beverage made from ground almonds and water, with a creamy texture and mild, nutty flavour. It is consumed as a substitute for cow's milk by vegans and by people avoiding dairy or lactose. Commercial almond milk is typically fortified with calcium and vitamins A, D, and E, and may contain sweeteners, flavourings (such as vanilla), and stabilizers such as gellan gum or lecithin; almonds themselves are often only a small percentage of the product by weight.

History

Almond milk has a long history in medieval cuisine. In the Islamic world and Christian Europe it was used during fasting periods such as Lent, when animal products were forbidden, and it appears in medieval cookery books — the 14th-century Le Viandier includes a recipe. Because it kept better than fresh dairy in warm climates and did not require refrigeration, it was a staple in medieval kitchens, used in pottages, sauces, and dishes for invalids.

Production

Traditionally, almonds are soaked, ground finely with water, and strained through cloth. Industrially, almonds or almond paste are blended with water, homogenized, fortified, and pasteurized or UHT-treated for shelf stability. The leftover pulp can be used as an ingredient in baking.

Nutrition

Unsweetened almond milk is low in calories and naturally lactose-free and cholesterol-free. Its protein content is much lower than cow's milk or soy milk, and unfortified versions are poor sources of calcium and vitamin D. Almonds are naturally a source of vitamin E. Sweetened varieties may contain substantial added sugar.

Environmental and labelling issues

Almond cultivation is water-intensive — most almonds are grown in California's Central Valley — though per litre, almond milk's overall water and carbon footprints are generally lower than dairy milk's, while its land and water use exceed those of oat or soy milk in most life-cycle assessments. Labelling has been contested: the European Union reserves the word "milk" for animal products (almond drinks are sold as "almond drink"), while in the United States the FDA has permitted "almond milk" labelling with guidance that consumers understand it is plant-based.
