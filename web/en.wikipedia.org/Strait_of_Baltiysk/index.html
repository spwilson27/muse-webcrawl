Strait of Baltiysk

The Strait of Baltiysk (Russian: Балтийский пролив, Baltiyskiy proliv) is a strait in Kaliningrad Oblast, Russia, that connects the Vistula Lagoon with the Gulf of Gdańsk in the Baltic Sea. It is the only natural outlet of the Vistula Lagoon to the open sea on the Russian side of the lagoon, and it carries the shipping traffic of the ports of Baltiysk and Kaliningrad.

Geography

The strait cuts through the Vistula Spit near the town of Baltiysk, the westernmost town of Russia. The channel is about 2 kilometres (1.2 mi) long and roughly 400 metres (1,300 ft) wide at its narrowest point. Because the lagoon is shallow, the strait is maintained by dredging to a navigable depth of about 10.5 metres (34 ft), allowing seagoing vessels to reach the sea canal leading to Kaliningrad.

History

The present strait was formed in 1510 when a storm surge broke through the spit near the village of Lochstädt, creating a navigable passage that quickly replaced older, silting outlets near the present Polish side of the lagoon. The town that grew at the strait, Pillau (modern Baltiysk), became an important fortress and naval base guarding the entrance. Control of the strait was contested during the wars of the 17th and 18th centuries between Sweden, Poland, and Prussia.

In the 19th century, Prussia fortified both banks of the strait and, in 1900–1901, completed the Königsberg Sea Canal (now the Kaliningrad Sea Canal), which runs along the northern shore of the lagoon from the strait to Königsberg (Kaliningrad).

Strategic importance

Baltiysk is the headquarters of the Russian Baltic Fleet and its only ice-free port in the Baltic region, so the strait has major military significance. In 2022 Poland completed the Vistula Spit canal across the Polish part of the spit, giving Polish lagoon ports such as Elbląg direct access to the Baltic without passing through the Strait of Baltiysk and Russian territorial waters.
