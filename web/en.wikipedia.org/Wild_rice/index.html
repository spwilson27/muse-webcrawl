Wild rice
Genus: Zizania
Native to: North America, Great Lakes region
Anishinaabe name: manoomin

Wild rice, called manoomin in Ojibwe, is a species of grass in the genus Zizania whose grain was historically gathered and eaten in North America and China. It is not directly related to Asian rice.

Cultivation and harvest

Plants grow in shallow lakes and slow streams. Traditional harvest by the Anishinaabe uses two people in a canoe: one poles, the other knocks ripe grain into the boat with sticks. Commercial paddy cultivation began in Minnesota and Canada in the 20th century.

Cultural significance

Manoomin is central to Anishinaabe migration prophecy and identity, and wild-rice waters are defended against pipelines and mining. It is the state grain of Minnesota.
