Syrian civil war

The Syrian civil war is a multi-sided conflict in Syria that began in 2011 during the Arab Spring, when protests against President Bashar al-Assad's government were met with violent repression and escalated into armed rebellion. It drew in regional and world powers and became one of the deadliest conflicts of the 21st century.

Course of the war

Defectors formed the Free Syrian Army in 2011; by 2012 fighting reached Damascus and Aleppo. The conflict took on sectarian dimensions and fragmented: jihadist groups including Jabhat al-Nusra and the Islamic State (ISIS) seized large territories, prompting a US-led coalition intervention from 2014, while Kurdish forces built the autonomous SDF with coalition support. Russia's military intervention from September 2015, alongside Iran and Hezbollah, turned the war in Assad's favour; government forces retook Aleppo (2016), Eastern Ghouta and Daraa (2018), leaving rebels concentrated in Idlib and Turkish-backed zones in the north.

Fall of the Assad government

In late November 2024, opposition forces led by Hayat Tahrir al-Sham launched a rapid offensive from Idlib, taking Aleppo, Hama and Homs in days. Damascus fell on 8 December 2024 and Bashar al-Assad fled to Russia, ending over five decades of Assad family rule; a transitional government was formed under Ahmed al-Sharaa.

Humanitarian impact

The war destroyed much of Syria's infrastructure, produced documented chemical-weapons attacks, sieges and mass displacement, and drove refugee flows that reshaped politics in neighbouring states and Europe.
