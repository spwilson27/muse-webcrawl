Chicago O'Hare International Airport (IATA: ORD) is a major international airport in Chicago, Illinois, about 27 km (17 miles) northwest of the Loop. One of the busiest airports in the world by both aircraft movements and passengers, it is a principal hub for United Airlines and American Airlines and a flagship gateway of the US Midwest.

History

The site was developed during the Second World War as a Douglas aircraft plant and military field named Orchard Field — the origin of the IATA code ORD. Renamed in 1949 for naval aviator Edward "Butch" O'Hare, it took over Chicago's scheduled traffic from Midway in 1955 and, with its 1960s jet-age terminals designed under a master plan led by Gertrude Kerbis and others, became the world's busiest airport for decades.

Facilities

O'Hare has four main passenger terminals (1, 2, 3 and 5) linked by an automated people mover, and a system of eight runways — the most of any major commercial airport — built out under the O'Hare Modernization Program, which reconfigured the airfield into parallel east–west runways. The O'Hare 21 programme is expanding Terminal 5 and building a new global terminal on the site of Terminal 2.

Traffic

The airport serves well over 70 million passengers in peak years with nonstop service to more than 200 destinations, and has frequently ranked as the world's busiest airport by takeoffs and landings.
