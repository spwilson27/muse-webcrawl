Mickey Mouse is an American cartoon character co-created in 1928 by Walt Disney and Ub Iwerks. The most famous character of the Walt Disney Company, Mickey is an anthropomorphic mouse who typically wears red shorts, large yellow shoes and white gloves. He serves as the mascot and corporate symbol of Disney worldwide.

Creation and early cartoons

Mickey was created as a replacement for Oswald the Lucky Rabbit, whose rights Disney had lost to Universal. After two test cartoons, Plane Crazy and The Gallopin' Gaucho, Mickey debuted publicly in Steamboat Willie (released 18 November 1928), one of the first sound cartoons and an immediate sensation. Disney himself provided Mickey's falsetto voice until 1947.

Comics and merchandising

Floyd Gottfredson drew the Mickey Mouse newspaper comic strip from 1930 for forty-five years, and in Italy and Scandinavia Mickey comics became a cultural institution. Mickey's image was among the first licensed on merchandise, making him one of the most recognisable fictional characters in the world. He appeared in the 1940 film Fantasia as the Sorcerer's Apprentice, one of his most enduring roles.

Later appearances

Though Minnie Mouse, Donald Duck, Goofy and Pluto often shared the spotlight, Mickey remained Disney's emblem. He returned in Mickey's Christmas Carol (1983), Runaway Brain (1995) and the acclaimed 2013 Paul Rudish television shorts. Mickey's earliest incarnations, including Steamboat Willie, entered the United States public domain on 1 January 2024.
