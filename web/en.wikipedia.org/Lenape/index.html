Lenape

The Lenape, also called the Lenni Lenape or Delaware Indians, are an Indigenous people of the Northeastern Woodlands, whose homeland — Lenapehoking — covered the lower Hudson and Delaware valleys, including present-day New Jersey, eastern Pennsylvania, Delaware and parts of New York.

History

Lenape society was organised in matrilineal clans speaking related Algonquian languages, Unami and Munsee. They traded with Dutch and English colonists from the 1600s, then were progressively dispossessed; episodes such as the Walking Purchase of 1737 took land under a fraudulent deed. Most Lenape were forced west through the 18th and 19th centuries.

Today

Lenape descendants live principally in Oklahoma (the Delaware Nation and Delaware Tribe of Indians), Wisconsin (Stockbridge-Munsee), and Ontario, with state-recognised communities remaining in New Jersey and Pennsylvania. Language revitalisation efforts are under way for both Unami and Munsee.
