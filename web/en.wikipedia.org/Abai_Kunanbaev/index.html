Abai Kunanbaev

Born1845, Karauyl, Kazakh steppe
Died1904
OccupationPoet, composer, philosopher

Abai Kunanbaev (1845–1904), commonly known simply as Abai, was a Kazakh poet, composer, and philosopher. He is regarded as the founder of modern Kazakh written literature and one of the most important figures in Kazakh culture.

Life

Abai was born in the Chingiz Mountains region of what is now eastern Kazakhstan, the son of a wealthy and influential clan leader. Educated first in a madrasa and then in a Russian school in Semipalatinsk, he was exposed both to Islamic scholarship and to Russian and European literature. In later life he withdrew from administrative duties to devote himself to writing, composing, and translating.

Works

Abai's poems, written in Kazakh, broke with oral tradition by treating written verse as literature in its own right, and called on Kazakhs to pursue education, honesty, and hard work. His philosophical prose work, The Book of Words (Qara Sözder), consists of 45 short parables and reflections on morality, knowledge, and Kazakh society. He also translated works by Pushkin, Lermontov, and Goethe into Kazakh, composing songs – several set to his own translations – that remain popular today.

Legacy

Abai is commemorated across Kazakhstan in monuments, street names, and institutions; his image and sayings are central to the country's national identity.
