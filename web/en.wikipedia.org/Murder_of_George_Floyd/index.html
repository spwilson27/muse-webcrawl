Murder of George Floyd

DateMay 25, 2020
LocationMinneapolis, Minnesota, U.S.
VictimGeorge Floyd (aged 46)
ConvictedDerek Chauvin (murder); three other officers convicted of related offences
OutcomeWorldwide protests; Chauvin sentenced to 22.5 years (state) and 21 years (federal, concurrent)

On May 25, 2020, George Floyd, a 46-year-old Black man, was murdered by Minneapolis police officer Derek Chauvin during an arrest. After Floyd was handcuffed and pinned face-down, Chauvin knelt on his neck for about nine minutes while Floyd repeatedly said he could not breathe. Three other officers — Tou Thao, J. Alexander Kueng, and Thomas Lane — took part in restraining him or preventing bystanders from intervening. Floyd's death, recorded on video by bystanders, sparked the largest protest movement in United States history and demonstrations worldwide against police brutality and racism.

Protests in Minneapolis in the days after Floyd's death spread across the United States and abroad.

Background and arrest

Floyd had moved to Minneapolis from Houston and worked as a truck driver and security guard; he had recently lost work during the COVID-19 pandemic. Police were called after a convenience-store employee reported that Floyd had used a suspected counterfeit $20 bill. Officers arrested Floyd near the intersection of 38th Street and Chicago Avenue. A struggle occurred as officers tried to place him in a police vehicle; Floyd was then restrained on the ground. An ambulance took him to Hennepin County Medical Center, where he was pronounced dead.

Investigations and trials

The Hennepin County medical examiner ruled the death a homicide caused by cardiopulmonary arrest during restraint, while an independent autopsy commissioned by Floyd's family found asphyxia. All four officers were fired the next day. In April 2021, a jury convicted Chauvin of second-degree murder, third-degree murder, and second-degree manslaughter; he was sentenced to 22.5 years in state prison and later pleaded guilty to federal civil-rights charges, receiving a 21-year concurrent sentence. Kueng, Lane, and Thao were convicted in federal court of violating Floyd's civil rights; Lane and Kueng pleaded guilty to a state charge of aiding and abetting manslaughter, and Thao was convicted of that charge in 2023. The city of Minneapolis settled a wrongful-death lawsuit with Floyd's family for $27 million.

Protests and aftermath

Protests began in Minneapolis on May 26 and spread to hundreds of US cities and more than 60 countries, under banners including Black Lives Matter. Some demonstrations in Minneapolis and elsewhere involved arson, looting, and clashes with police; a police precinct building was burned. The movement prompted police reforms in many jurisdictions, a federal consent-decree process for Minneapolis policing, and the renaming of the intersection where Floyd died as George Floyd Square. In Congress, the George Floyd Justice in Policing Act passed the House but stalled in the Senate.
