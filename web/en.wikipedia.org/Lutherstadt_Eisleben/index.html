Lutherstadt Eisleben

Market square and Luther monument, Eisleben

State: Saxony-Anhalt, Germany

Population: ~22,000

Known for: Birthplace and place of death of Martin Luther

Lutherstadt Eisleben is a town in the district of Mansfeld-Südharz in Saxony-Anhalt, Germany. It is best known as the birthplace (1483) and place of death (1546) of the Protestant reformer Martin Luther, whose houses of birth and death, together with sites in nearby Wittenberg, form the UNESCO World Heritage-listed "Luther Memorials in Eisleben and Wittenberg".

History

Eisleben was first mentioned in 997 and grew wealthy on the copper-slate mining of the Mansfeld region, in which Luther's father Hans worked as a smelter master. Martin Luther was born here on 10 November 1483 and baptised the next day, the feast of St Martin, at the church of St Peter and Paul. In February 1546 Luther returned to Eisleben to mediate a dispute between the counts of Mansfeld, preached his final sermons at St Andrew's Church, and died in the town on 18 February. The official prefix "Lutherstadt" (Luther City) recognises this double connection, a link of fate unique in the reformer's biography.

Sights

The Luther Birth House (Lutherstraße), partly preserved from the 15th century, is among Germany's oldest memorial museums, established in 1693. The House of Luther's Death, on Andreaskirchplatz, and the churches of St Peter and Paul and St Andrew survive alongside the historic market square with its 1917 Luther monument. The town's mining heritage is recalled by slag heaps and miners' settlements around the district.
