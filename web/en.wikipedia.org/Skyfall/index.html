Skyfall

Skyfall is a 2012 spy film and the twenty-third in the James Bond series produced by Eon Productions. Directed by Sam Mendes and written by Neal Purvis, Robert Wade and John Logan, it stars Daniel Craig in his third outing as James Bond, alongside Javier Bardem as the villain Raoul Silva, a former MI6 agent seeking revenge against M, played by Judi Dench. The film's release coincided with the fiftieth anniversary of the series, which began with Dr. No in 1962.

Plot

After an operation in Istanbul goes wrong, Bond is presumed dead and MI6 comes under attack when a hard drive containing the identities of undercover agents is stolen. Bond returns to London and is reinstated, pursuing the thief Patrice to Shanghai and Macau, where he encounters Sévérine, who leads him to Silva, a cyber-terrorist and former agent betrayed by M. Silva is captured but escapes in London, and Bond takes M to his ancestral home, Skyfall Lodge in Scotland, where, with gamekeeper Kincade and new quartermaster Q's help, they withstand Silva's assault. M is mortally wounded, Silva is killed, and Ralph Fiennes's Gareth Mallory succeeds M as head of the service.

Production

Development was suspended in 2010 due to MGM's financial troubles and resumed after the studio emerged from bankruptcy. Mendes was the first director of a Bond film to have won an Academy Award for Best Director. Cinematographer Roger Deakins shot the film digitally, earning wide praise for its visual style, and Thomas Newman composed the score. The title song, "Skyfall," performed by Adele, won the Academy Award for Best Original Song — the first Bond theme to do so.

Reception

Skyfall premiered at the Royal Albert Hall in October 2012 and grossed over $1.1 billion worldwide, becoming the highest-grossing Bond film and, at the time, the seventh-highest-grossing film of all time. It received five Academy Award nominations and won two, for Best Original Song and Best Sound Editing. Critics praised Craig's performance, Bardem's villain, Mendes's direction and Deakins's cinematography, and the film is frequently ranked among the best entries in the franchise.
