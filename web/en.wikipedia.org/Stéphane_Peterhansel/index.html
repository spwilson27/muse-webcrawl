Stéphane Peterhansel (born 6 August 1965) is a French rally driver widely regarded as the most successful competitor in the history of the Dakar Rally. Nicknamed "Monsieur Dakar," he has won the event a record fourteen times — six times on motorcycles with Yamaha in the 1990s and eight times in cars — a combined tally unmatched in any category.

Career

Peterhansel began in enduro racing, winning French championships before entering the Paris–Dakar Rally on a Yamaha in 1988. He won the motorcycle class in 1991, 1992, 1993, 1995, 1997, and 1998. Switching to cars in 1999, he took his first car victory with Mitsubishi in 2004 and repeated in 2005 and 2007.

After Mitsubishi withdrew, he joined the X-raid team, winning in a Mini in 2012 and 2013. He then drove for Peugeot's factory return to the Dakar, winning in 2016 and 2017, and won again with Mini in 2021 in Saudi Arabia. In 2022 he joined Audi's hybrid RS Q e-tron program for the rally's new electric-drive era.

Driving style and legacy

Peterhansel is known for mechanical sympathy, consistency, and navigation over raw speed — an approach suited to marathon rally-raid stages. His fourteen overall victories, spanning more than three decades and three continents of Dakar routes (Africa, South America, and Saudi Arabia), make him the benchmark against which other rally-raid drivers are measured.
