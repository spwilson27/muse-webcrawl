The Secret History (Greek: Apokryphe Historia, "Unwritten History", usually given in Latin as Historia Arcana or Anecdota) is a work by the Byzantine historian Procopius of Caesarea, written around 550 AD but not published during his lifetime. Where Procopius's official Wars of Justinian praised the emperor Justinian I, the Secret History violently attacks Justinian, his wife Theodora, the general Belisarius, and Belisarius's wife Antonina, portraying the imperial couple as cruel, corrupt, and even demonic.

Content

The work alleges, among much else, that Justinian was a tyrant of insatiable greed who plundered the empire, that Theodora rose from a disreputable stage career and dominated her husband, and that Belisarius was henpecked by the unfaithful Antonina. Procopius claims Justinian was a demon in human form whose head would vanish at night, and attributes the empire's catastrophes — plague, earthquakes, floods — to his rule. It also preserves some plausible material, such as criticism of Justinian's fiscal policies and his monopolies.

Discovery and authenticity

The text survived in a handful of manuscripts. Niccolò Alamanni published the first edition in 1623 from a Vatican manuscript, and the Suda lexicon had already referred to the work centuries earlier. Scholars once doubted its authenticity because of its scurrilous tone, but modern scholarship accepts it as genuinely by Procopius, written as a private venting of grievances he could not express in his official histories — though its accusations are read critically rather than as fact.

Influence

The phrase "secret history" has since become a genre label for works purporting to reveal hidden, scandalous truths behind official accounts. The book remains a key, if hostile, source for the reign of Justinian.
