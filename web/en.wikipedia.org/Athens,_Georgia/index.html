Athens, Georgia

Athens is a consolidated city-county (Athens–Clarke County) in the U.S. state of Georgia, about 70 miles (110 km) northeast of Atlanta. It is home to the University of Georgia, the state's flagship and oldest public university, chartered in 1785. The population was about 128,000 at the 2020 census.

The Arch, symbol of the University of Georgia, at the edge of downtown Athens.

History

John Milledge purchased land on the Oconee River in 1801 and donated it for the university; the town that grew around it was named Athens after the Greek city that was the centre of Plato's Academy. In 1991 the city and Clarke County governments unified. Athens is known for the Tree That Owns Itself and for its antebellum architecture.

Culture

Athens is celebrated for its influential music scene, which produced R.E.M., the B-52's, Widespread Panic, and the Drive-By Truckers, and is anchored by venues such as the 40 Watt Club and the Georgia Theatre. The annual AthFest and Twilight Criterium bicycle race draw visitors each year. The University of Georgia Bulldogs compete in the SEC, playing football at Sanford Stadium.
