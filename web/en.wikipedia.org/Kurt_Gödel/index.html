Kurt Gödel

Kurt Gödel (1906–1978), logician and mathematician.

Kurt Friedrich Gödel (28 April 1906 – 14 January 1978) was a logician, mathematician, and philosopher born in Brno, Austria-Hungary (now the Czech Republic), who later emigrated to the United States. He is regarded as one of the most significant logicians in history. His incompleteness theorems, published in 1931, are among the most celebrated results in twentieth-century mathematics and philosophy.

Incompleteness theorems

In 1931, Gödel published his two incompleteness theorems. The first states that any consistent formal system rich enough to express basic arithmetic contains statements that are true but unprovable within the system. The second states that such a system cannot prove its own consistency. The results undermined David Hilbert's programme of founding all of mathematics on a complete, consistent set of axioms, and they were achieved using the technique of Gödel numbering, which encodes statements about arithmetic as numbers within arithmetic itself.

Other work

Gödel's doctoral dissertation proved the completeness theorem for first-order logic. In set theory, he showed that the axiom of choice and the generalized continuum hypothesis are consistent with the standard axioms (via the constructible universe), and in general relativity he discovered the Gödel metric, an exact solution of Einstein's field equations permitting closed timelike curves — "rotating universe" solutions that allow a form of time travel. He was a close friend of Albert Einstein at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton, where the two famously walked together daily.

Later life

Gödel fled Nazi Europe in 1940, travelling via the Trans-Siberian Railway and Japan to the United States, and became an American citizen in 1948. In later life he suffered from severe mental illness and paranoia; fearing poisoning, he refused to eat food not prepared by his wife Adele, and when she was hospitalized in 1977 he starved himself, dying of malnutrition in January 1978.
