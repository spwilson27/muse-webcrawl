Padma Vibhushan

Padma Vibhushan
Type: Civilian award
Country: India
Established: 1954
First awarded: 1954
Precedence: Below Bharat Ratna; above Padma Bhushan

The Padma Vibhushan ("Lotus Decoration") is the second-highest civilian award of the Republic of India, after the Bharat Ratna. Instituted on 2 January 1954, the award is given for "exceptional and distinguished service" in any field. The criteria include service in any field including service rendered by government servants, but excluding those working with public sector undertakings, with the exception of doctors and scientists.

As of recent years, the award has been bestowed upon several hundred people, including a handful of foreign recipients. Recommendations are received from state and union territory governments, ministries of the Government of India, and previous awardees, and are considered by the Padma Awards Committee before approval by the Prime Minister and the President of India. Recipients are announced on Republic Day, 26 January.

History

When instituted in 1954, the Padma Vibhushan was classified as the first of three classes (Pahela Varg) of a single decoration, ranking below the Bharat Ratna. It was restructured as a separate award in 1955. The first recipients included Satyendra Nath Bose, Nandalal Bose, Zakir Husain, V. K. Krishna Menon, Jigme Dorji Wangchuck, and Balasaheb Gangadhar Kher. Awardees include figures such as M. S. Subbulakshmi, A. P. J. Abdul Kalam, Lata Mangeshkar, and Sachin Tendulkar-era contemporaries in public life, science, and the arts.
