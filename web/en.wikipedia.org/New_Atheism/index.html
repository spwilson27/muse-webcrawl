New Atheism
Periodc. 2004 – early 2010s
Key figuresRichard Dawkins, Christopher Hitchens, Sam Harris, Daniel Dennett
Key worksThe End of Faith (2004); The God Delusion (2006); God Is Not Great (2007)
PositionReligion should be criticised and countered, not tolerated by default

New Atheism is a term coined in 2006 for the ideas promoted by a group of late-20th- and early-21st-century Anglo-American atheist writers, journalists and scientists who argued that religion should not simply be tolerated but should be actively examined, criticised and countered with rational argument wherever its influence arises.

The "Four Horsemen"

The movement's leading figures were dubbed the "Four Horsemen" after a 2007 discussion among them: evolutionary biologist Richard Dawkins (The God Delusion, 2006), journalist Christopher Hitchens (God Is Not Great, 2007), neuroscientist and philosopher Sam Harris (The End of Faith, 2004; Letter to a Christian Nation, 2006), and philosopher Daniel Dennett (Breaking the Spell, 2006). Their books were international bestsellers, helped by the climate after the September 11 attacks and public debates over faith in politics.

Arguments

New Atheists typically hold that the "God hypothesis" is a scientific claim that fails empirical tests, that religious faith is not a reliable path to knowledge, and that religion causes or excuses substantial social harm — from violence to resistance to science education. They advocated science and secular ethics as replacements for religion's social roles.

Criticism and legacy

Critics, including theologians, philosophers and other atheists, accused the movement of superficial treatment of theology, of scientism, and (particularly in Harris's and Hitchens's cases) of hostility towards Islam that dovetailed with Western foreign policy. The movement's prominence waned in the 2010s amid internal disputes, though it contributed to the growth of organised secularism and to the rising share of religiously unaffiliated "nones" in Western countries.
