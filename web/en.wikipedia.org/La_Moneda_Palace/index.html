La Moneda Palace

Palacio de La Moneda ("Palace of the Mint"), or simply La Moneda, is the seat of the President of the Republic of Chile, in downtown Santiago. Designed by the Italian architect Joaquín Toesca, it was built between 1784 and 1805 originally as the colonial mint for coinage — hence its name.

History

The neoclassical palace became the presidential residence in 1846 and the seat of government thereafter. It is most famous internationally for the 1973 Chilean coup d'état, during which it was bombed by Hawker Hunter jets of the Chilean Air Force; President Salvador Allende died inside during the attack.

Restoration

The building was restored to its original appearance and reopened in 1981. Since 2000 the forecourt and the underground Centro Cultural La Moneda have opened the palace more fully to the public; a changing of the guard ceremony takes place on alternate days.
