Markermeer

The Markermeer (Dutch: [ˌmɑrkərˈmeːr]) is a 700 km² (270 sq mi) lake in the central Netherlands in between North Holland, Flevoland, and its smaller and larger neighbours, the IJmeer and IJsselmeer. A shallow lake at some 3 to 5 m in depth, matching the reclaimed land to its west, north-west and east it is named after the small former island, now peninsula, of Marken on its west shore.

History

The lake was originally part of the Zuiderzee, a saltwater inlet of the North Sea. Following the completion of the Afsluitdijk in 1932, the Zuiderzee became the freshwater IJsselmeer. The Markermeer was later separated from the IJsselmeer by the Houtribdijk, completed in 1976, which runs between Enkhuizen and Lelystad.

Plans once existed to drain the Markermeer and create a new polder, the Markerwaard, but these were abandoned in the early 2000s, partly because of the lake's ecological importance. The lake is now a protected nature area, designated under the Natura 2000 network, providing habitat for large numbers of waterbirds.

Marker Wadden

Between 2016 and 2021, the Marker Wadden project constructed a group of artificial islands in the north-eastern part of the lake, using silt dredged from the Markermeer itself. The islands are intended to improve water quality and create new wetland habitat for birds and fish.
