The Nasdaq-100 (NDX) is a stock market index of roughly 100 of the largest non-financial companies listed on the Nasdaq stock exchange. Launched in 1985, it is heavily weighted toward the technology sector and is one of the most closely followed growth benchmarks in the world.

Composition and methodology

The index is modified capitalisation-weighted: constituent weights are adjusted quarterly to limit the dominance of the largest companies, and the index is reconstituted annually in December. Financial companies are excluded (they are represented in a separate Nasdaq Financial-100). Major long-standing constituents include Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia, Amazon, Alphabet, Meta Platforms, Broadcom and Tesla.

Investing

The index is tracked by the Invesco QQQ Trust, one of the most heavily traded exchange-traded funds in the world, and serves as the underlying for futures and options traded on the CME and Cboe. Because of its concentration in a small number of mega-cap technology stocks, the Nasdaq-100 tends to be more volatile than broader indices such as the S&P 500.
