Dhu al-Hijjah (Arabic: ذُو ٱلْحِجَّة, Ḏū al-Ḥijja) is the twelfth and final month in the Islamic lunar calendar. Its name means "the [month] of the pilgrimage," because it is the month in which the Hajj — the annual pilgrimage to Mecca that is one of the Five Pillars of Islam — takes place. Dhu al-Hijjah is one of the four sacred months (al-ashhur al-ḥurum) of the Islamic calendar, during which warfare was traditionally prohibited.

The first ten days

The first ten days of Dhu al-Hijjah are regarded in Islamic tradition as among the most blessed days of the year. A well-known hadith states that there are no days on which righteous deeds are more beloved to God than these ten days. Many Muslims fast during the first nine days, increase prayer, charity and remembrance of God (dhikr, especially repeating the takbīr), and give the festival sacrifice. Those not performing Hajj are recommended to fast on the Day of Arafah.

Hajj

The rites of Hajj are performed between the 8th and 13th of the month. Pilgrims enter the state of iḥrām, gather on the plain of Arafat on the 9th day (the Day of Arafah, the climax of the pilgrimage), spend the night at Muzdalifah, and on the 10th stone the pillars at Mina, offer a sacrifice, shave or shorten their hair and perform the ṭawāf around the Kaaba. The pilgrimage draws two to three million people to Mecca in most years.

Eid al-Adha

The 10th of Dhu al-Hijjah is Eid al-Adha, the "Festival of the Sacrifice," one of the two canonical festivals of Islam (the other being Eid al-Fitr). It commemorates Abraham's willingness to sacrifice his son in obedience to God's command, before God provided a ram in his place. Muslims mark it with a special congregational prayer, festive meals, gifts, and the ritual slaughter of a sheep, goat, cow or camel, the meat of which is shared with family, neighbours and the poor.

Shia observances

In Twelver Shia Islam, the 18th of Dhu al-Hijjah is celebrated as Eid al-Ghadir, commemorating the event of Ghadir Khumm, at which Shia Muslims believe Muhammad appointed Ali ibn Abi Talib as his successor. The month also begins the final weeks before Muharram, when Shia commemorate the martyrdom of Husayn at Karbala.
