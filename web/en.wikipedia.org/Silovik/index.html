Silovik

A silovik (Russian: силовик, IPA: [sʲɪlɐˈvʲik]; plural siloviki, силовики) is a person who works for or comes from a Russian "force ministry"—the military, police, or security services, collectively known as the silovye ministerstva or "ministries of force." The term came into wide use in the 1990s and especially under President Vladimir Putin, himself a former KGB officer, as former officials of the FSB, GRU, SVR, the armed forces, and other agencies moved into senior positions in government, the presidential administration, and state corporations.

Role in Russian politics

Analysts such as Olga Kryshtanovskaya documented the rising share of siloviki in the Russian elite during the 2000s—by some measures around a quarter of senior officials. Prominent figures identified as siloviki include Igor Sechin, Nikolai Patrushev, Alexander Bortnikov, and Sergei Shoigu. Scholars debate whether the siloviki form a cohesive faction: some argue that they share a statist, security-focused outlook and corporate loyalties to their home agencies, while others caution against treating them as a unified bloc.

Related terms

The broader grouping of security and military elites is sometimes called the silovarchy. Observers contrast the siloviki with technocratic officials, the "civilian" clan around figures from St. Petersburg's city administration, and the oligarchs of the Yeltsin era, whose influence the siloviki in part displaced.
