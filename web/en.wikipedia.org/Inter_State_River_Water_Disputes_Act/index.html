Inter State River Water Disputes Act

The Inter-State River Water Disputes Act, 1956 is an Act of the Parliament of India enacted under Article 262 of the Constitution of India, on the eve of the linguistic reorganisation of Indian states. Article 262 empowers Parliament to provide by law for the adjudication of disputes relating to waters of inter-state rivers and river valleys, and to bar the jurisdiction of the Supreme Court and other courts in such disputes.

Provisions

Under the Act, when a state government requests the Union government to refer an inter-state water dispute to a tribunal, and the Union is of the opinion that the dispute cannot be settled by negotiation, it constitutes a Water Disputes Tribunal for adjudication. The tribunal investigates and forwards its report and decision to the Union government, which publishes it in the official gazette, whereupon the decision becomes final and binding on the parties. Amendments in 2002 (following the Sarkaria Commission's recommendations) set time limits — one year for constituting a tribunal after a request and three years (extendable by two) for giving its decision — and gave tribunal awards the same force as an order or decree of the Supreme Court.

Tribunals

Tribunals constituted under the Act include those for the Krishna (1969 and 2004), Godavari (1969), Narmada (1969), Ravi and Beas (1986), Cauvery (1990), Mahadayi/Mandovi (2010), Vansadhara (2010) and Mahanadi (2018) rivers. The Cauvery dispute between Karnataka and Tamil Nadu, and the Krishna disputes among Maharashtra, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, have been especially protracted and politically sensitive.

Reform proposals

Delays in tribunal proceedings have led to proposals for a single permanent tribunal with multiple benches and a Dispute Resolution Committee for preliminary negotiation; a bill to amend the Act on those lines was passed by the Lok Sabha in 2019 but later lapsed.
