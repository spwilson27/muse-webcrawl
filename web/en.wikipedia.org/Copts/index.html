Copts

Copts (Coptic: niRemenkimi; Arabic: al-Aqbāṭ) are a Christian ethnoreligious group indigenous to Egypt, traditionally the largest Christian community in the Middle East. The word derives, through Arabic, from the Greek Aigyptios (Egyptian), and originally denoted all Egyptians before becoming restricted to the country's Christians after the Arab conquest. Most Copts belong to the Coptic Orthodox Church of Alexandria; smaller numbers belong to the Coptic Catholic Church and various Protestant churches.

History

Christianity reached Egypt in the first century, traditionally through Saint Mark the Evangelist, founder of the Church of Alexandria. Egypt's church produced foundational figures of early Christianity, including the theologians Clement and Origen and the founders of monasticism, Anthony the Great and Pachomius. After the Council of Chalcedon (451), the majority church in Egypt rejected the council's Christological formula and formed a distinct Oriental Orthodox communion, suffering periodic persecution under Byzantine, and later under some Islamic, rulers. Under Arab rule from the seventh century, Egyptians gradually converted to Islam and adopted Arabic, while the Coptic language survived as the liturgical language of the church.

Demographics and diaspora

Copts form roughly a tenth of Egypt's population — estimates vary widely because official figures are disputed — making them a community of many millions concentrated in Cairo, Alexandria and Upper Egypt. A substantial diaspora has grown since the 1960s in North America, Australia and Europe, with its own churches and bishops.

Culture

Coptic culture preserves ancient Egyptian inheritances alongside Christian tradition: the Coptic calendar of the Martyrs governs the church year, fasting occupies much of the liturgical year, and Coptic art, music and iconography continue pharaonic and late-antique motifs. Copts have played prominent roles in Egyptian public life, business and the professions, though the community has faced episodes of discrimination and sectarian violence in the modern era.
