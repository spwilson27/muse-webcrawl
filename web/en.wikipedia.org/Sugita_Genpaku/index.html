Sugita Genpaku

Sugita Genpaku (1733–1817) was a Japanese scholar of Rangaku ("Dutch learning") and chief of the Han of Obama, known for his translation of a Dutch edition of Johann Adam Kulmus's anatomical tables. A physician in Edo, he pioneered the introduction of Western medicine into Japan during the period of national seclusion, when Dutch traders at Dejima were Japan's only European contact.

Kaitai Shinsho

In 1771 Sugita observed the dissection of an executed criminal alongside Maeno Ryōtaku and other scholars, and was struck by how accurately the Dutch anatomical plates matched the body before them, unlike traditional Chinese texts. Over four years of work he and his collaborators produced the Kaitai Shinsho ("New Book of Anatomy", 1774), the first Japanese translation of a Western medical work. Because none of the team knew Dutch well, the translation was famously laborious; Sugita later recounted the effort in his memoir Rangaku Kotohajime (1815).

Legacy

The project coined many Japanese medical terms still in use and is regarded as a founding moment of Western learning in Japan, opening the way for the broader Rangaku movement and later modernization.
