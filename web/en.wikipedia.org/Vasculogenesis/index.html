Vasculogenesis

Vasculogenesis is the process of blood vessel formation in the embryo, occurring by a de novo production of endothelial cells. It is distinguished from angiogenesis, the formation of new vessels by sprouting and splitting from pre-existing ones.

Mechanism

In the early embryo, mesodermal cells differentiate into hemangioblasts, which aggregate into blood islands in the yolk sac. The outer cells of the islands become endothelial cells (angioblasts) while inner cells form primitive blood cells; the islands then connect into a primitive capillary plexus, which is remodelled into the circulatory system. Growth factors such as VEGF and its receptors are essential signals. Defects in vasculogenesis are generally lethal to the embryo.

In adults

Once thought to occur only during embryonic development, vasculogenesis also contributes to vessel growth in adults through circulating endothelial progenitor cells derived from the bone marrow, participating in wound healing, retinal and limb ischaemia repair, and tumour vascularisation — a finding with implications for cancer therapy and regenerative medicine.
