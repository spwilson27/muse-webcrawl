Banking trojan

A banking trojan is a type of malicious software (trojan horse) designed to steal credentials and financial information from users of online banking systems. Banking trojans typically intercept login details and one-time passwords, manipulate web sessions, and initiate fraudulent transactions, often without visible signs to the victim.

Techniques

Common techniques include keystroke logging, form grabbing, screen capture, and man-in-the-browser attacks, in which the malware injects fake fields or overlays into legitimate banking websites to harvest credentials. Some families, such as Zeus and its successor variants, SpyEye, Carberp, Citadel, and TrickBot, were distributed as crimeware kits that let criminals build customized versions and harvest data through command-and-control servers.

Mitigation

Defenses include multi-factor authentication with transaction signing, endpoint detection software, browser hardening, and customer education about phishing, the most common delivery route. Law enforcement operations have dismantled several major botnets, including the Gameover Zeus network in 2014.
