Islamic marital practices

Marriage in Islam is a legal contract (nikah) as well as a religious covenant.

Islamic marital practices are customs and rules governing courtship, engagement, marriage and divorce among Muslims, derived from the Quran, the hadith, and centuries of jurisprudence (fiqh), and adapted to the cultures in which Muslims live.

The marriage contract

Marriage (nikah) is concluded by a contract between the bridegroom and the bride's guardian (wali), in the presence of witnesses, with a dower (mahr) given by the groom to the bride. The bride's consent is required. Engagements are often marked by an exchange of gifts and a formal proposal (khitbah). Wedding celebrations (walima) typically include a feast hosted by the groom's family.

Related practices

Customs vary widely by region: henna nights in South Asia and the Middle East, elaborate multi-day festivities in North Africa, and modest ceremonies elsewhere. Rules discussed by jurists include the waiting period (iddah) after widowhood or divorce, the prohibition of marriage between close kin and milk-kin, and the conditions under which polygyny (up to four wives) is permitted, subject to equitable treatment and, in many modern states, legal restriction.

Divorce

Divorce may proceed by talaq (repudiation by the husband), khul' (release sought by the wife, usually returning the mahr), or judicial dissolution (faskh). Maintenance, custody and the welfare of children are addressed in both classical law and modern family codes.
