Acrylamide

FormulaC₃H₅NO
Molar mass71.08 g/mol
AppearanceWhite crystalline solid
Main usePrecursor to polyacrylamide

Acrylamide (or acrylic amide) is an organic compound with the chemical formula CH₂=CHC(O)NH₂. It is a white, odourless crystalline solid, soluble in water. Industrially it is produced by the hydration of acrylonitrile and is used almost entirely to manufacture polyacrylamide polymers, used in water treatment, paper making, mining and as gels for electrophoresis in laboratories.

In food

In 2002, Swedish researchers announced that acrylamide forms in starchy foods cooked at high temperatures — such as French fries, crisps, bread crusts and roasted coffee — through the Maillard reaction between the amino acid asparagine and reducing sugars. Acrylamide is classified by IARC as a Group 2A (probable) human carcinogen on the basis of animal studies, and is also a known neurotoxin at high doses; regulators in the EU and elsewhere have set benchmark levels and mitigation measures for food producers, although the risk to humans from dietary levels remains under study.

Health effects

Occupational exposure, historically among workers making polyacrylamide or using grouts, can cause peripheral neuropathy and skin irritation. Acrylamide is metabolised to glycidamide, which forms DNA adducts and is responsible for much of its genotoxicity.
