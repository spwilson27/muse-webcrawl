Bharatiya Janata Party

The Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP; Hindi: भारतीय जनता पार्टी, "Indian People's Party") is one of two major political parties in India, along with the Indian National Congress. Founded in 1980, it is the world's largest political party by claimed membership and has been India's ruling party since 2014 under Prime Minister Narendra Modi.

History

The BJP's origins lie in the Bharatiya Jana Sangh, founded in 1951 by Syama Prasad Mukherjee, which merged into the Janata Party in 1977. After the Janata experiment collapsed, former Jana Sangh members led by Atal Bihari Vajpayee and L. K. Advani founded the BJP in 1980. The party rose to national prominence in the early 1990s through the Ram Janmabhoomi movement. Vajpayee served as prime minister in 1996 and again from 1998 to 2004, heading the National Democratic Alliance (NDA) coalition.

Ideology and governments

The BJP's philosophy is rooted in Hindutva (Hindu nationalism) and "integral humanism", and it is affiliated with the Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS) and the wider Sangh Parivar family of organisations. Under Modi, the party won single-party majorities in 2014 and 2019 and led the NDA to a third term in 2024. Its governments have pursued economic reform and welfare schemes alongside Hindu nationalist measures such as the abrogation of Article 370 in Jammu and Kashmir and the Citizenship Amendment Act, which have drawn both strong support and criticism.
