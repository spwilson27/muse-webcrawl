Novocherkassk (Russian: Новочерка́сск) is a city in Rostov Oblast, Russia, near the confluence of the Tuzlov and Aksay rivers. Founded in 1805 by Matvei Platov, it served as the capital of the Don Cossack Host and remains a symbolic centre of Cossack culture, styled the "world capital of the Cossacks".

History

Novocherkassk ("New Cherkassk") was founded to replace the old Cossack capital Cherkassk, which was prone to flooding and fire; the city was laid out in a planned European style with wide streets by architect Franz de Wollant. During the Russian Civil War it was a stronghold of the anti-Bolshevik Don Army under Ataman Alexey Kaledin.

The city is also remembered for the Novocherkassk massacre of 2 June 1962, when Soviet troops fired on striking workers from the locomotive works protesting food price rises and production increases, killing 26 people. The event was kept secret until after the fall of the Soviet Union; a memorial now stands in the city.

Landmarks and economy

The Ascension (Voznesensky) Cathedral, completed in 1905, is one of the largest Orthodox churches in Russia and contains the tombs of Cossack heroes including Platov and Yermak. Triumphal arches of 1817 commemorate the Cossacks' role in the Napoleonic Wars. The city's industries include the Novocherkassk Electric Locomotive Plant (NEVZ), one of the world's largest builders of electric locomotives, and the South Russian State Polytechnic University, founded in 1907.
