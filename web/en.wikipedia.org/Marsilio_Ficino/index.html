Marsilio Ficino

Born19 October 1433, Figline Valdarno, Florence
Died1 October 1499, Careggi, Florence
EraRenaissance philosophy

Marsilio Ficino (19 October 1433 – 1 October 1499) was an Italian scholar, Catholic priest and one of the most influential humanist philosophers of the early Renaissance. He was the first to translate the complete works of Plato into Latin.

Life and work

Ficino was supported by Cosimo de' Medici, who gave him a villa at Careggi and commissioned translations of Plato and the Hermetic corpus. His Latin Plato (published 1484) and Plotinus made Greek philosophy available to Western readers for the first time in a millennium, shaping Renaissance thought across Europe.

Philosophy

His major original work, Theologia Platonica (1482), argued for the immortality of the soul and sought to reconcile Platonism with Christianity. His commentary on Plato's Symposium, De amore, profoundly influenced ideas of love in Renaissance art and literature. His Three Books on Life (1489) addressed the health of scholars, blending medicine, astrology and natural magic.

Influence

The informal "Platonic Academy" around Ficino included Pico della Mirandola and influenced artists such as Botticelli. Ficino's revival of Neoplatonism is a central episode of Renaissance intellectual history.
