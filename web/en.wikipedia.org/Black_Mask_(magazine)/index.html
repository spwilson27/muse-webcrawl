Black Mask (magazine)

Black Mask was an American pulp magazine founded in 1920 by journalist H. L. Mencken and drama critic George Jean Nathan. It is best known as the birthplace of the hardboiled school of detective fiction, publishing early stories by Dashiell Hammett and Raymond Chandler and launching the careers of many of the genre's most influential writers.

History

Mencken and Nathan created Black Mask as a money-making venture to subsidize their literary magazine The Smart Set, selling the pulp for a profit soon after its launch. Initially a general-interest weekly featuring adventure, mystery, romance, and the occult, the magazine found its identity in the 1920s as it published crime stories grounded in the realism of Prohibition-era America.

The hardboiled school

Under editor Joseph Shaw from 1926 to 1936, Black Mask defined hardboiled detective fiction: terse prose, tough unsentimental private-eye heroes, and contemporary urban settings. Hammett introduced the Continental Op and Sam Spade in its pages; Raymond Chandler published his first short stories there in the 1930s; other regulars included Erle Stanley Gardner, Carroll John Daly (creator of Race Williams, an early hardboiled hero), and Raoul Whitfield. The magazine's fiction became a foundation of film noir in the following decade.

Decline

After Shaw's departure in 1936 the magazine's quality and prestige declined, and it passed through several publishers in digest-sized formats before ceasing publication in 1951, as paperbacks and television displaced the pulps. Black Mask has since been revived briefly by later publishers, and its classic stories have been widely reprinted in anthologies.
