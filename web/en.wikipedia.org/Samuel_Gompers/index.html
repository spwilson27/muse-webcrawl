Samuel Gompers (born Samuel Gumpertz; January 27, 1850 – December 13, 1924) was a British-born American cigar maker and labor union leader. He founded the American Federation of Labor (AFL) in 1886 and served as its president almost continuously until his death, making him the dominant figure in the American labor movement of his era.

Early life

Born in London to a Jewish family of Dutch origin, Gompers emigrated to New York City in 1863 and became a cigar maker like his father. He joined the Cigar Makers' International Union and rose through its ranks, becoming president of its New York local and later a national figure.

The AFL and "pure and simple" unionism

Gompers championed what he called "pure and simple" unionism: trade unions of skilled craft workers focused on concrete economic goals — higher wages, shorter hours and better conditions — rather than political ideology or sweeping social transformation. Asked what labor wanted, he famously answered "More." Under his leadership the AFL grew to millions of members and lobbied for measures such as the eight-hour day, while opposing socialist and communist influence within the movement and favouring craft unions over industrial unions.

Later years

During the First World War Gompers supported the American war effort and served on the Council of National Defense. He died in San Antonio, Texas, in December 1924, shortly after attending a labor conference in Mexico City. The AFL headquarters building in Washington, D.C., is named the Samuel Gompers Building in his honour.
