Bouchon

Type: Restaurant (Lyonnaise)
Origin: Lyon, France
Cuisine: Traditional Lyonnaise
Certification: "Authentique Bouchon Lyonnais" label (since 1997)

A bouchon is a type of restaurant found in Lyon, France, that serves traditional Lyonnaise cuisine, such as sausages, coq au vin, duck pâté and roast pork. Bouchons are characterised by their informal, convivial atmosphere, modest prices and hearty portions, in contrast to haute cuisine establishments.

History

The tradition traces to the small inns of the 17th and 18th centuries that served the silk workers (canuts) passing through Lyon. In the early 20th century the celebrated mères lyonnaises — women cooks such as Mère Brazier and Mère Fillioux — made the bouchon famous for generous home-style cooking.

Cuisine

Typical dishes include quenelles de brochet, salade lyonnaise, andouillette, tablier de sapeur, cervelle de canut (a fresh-cheese preparation) and praline tart. Meals are traditionally accompanied by a pot lyonnais of local wine such as Beaujolais or Côtes du Rhône.

Certification

Since 1997 an association has awarded the "Authentique Bouchon Lyonnais" label to establishments judged to uphold the tradition; only a limited number of restaurants in Lyon hold the designation.
