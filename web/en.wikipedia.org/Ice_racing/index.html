Ice racing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ice racing is a form of racing that uses cars, motorcycles, snowmobiles, all-terrain vehicles, or other motorised vehicles, raced on frozen lakes or rivers or on groomed frozen lots. It is most popular in cold northern climates, including Canada, the northern United States, Scandinavia, and Russia.

Motorcycle ice racing

Motorcycle speedway on ice, governed internationally by the FIM, is contested on oval tracks using bikes fitted with tyres carrying hundreds of sharp metal spikes, which bite into the ice and permit extreme lean angles in corners. Riders wear substantial protective equipment; the sport has produced dominant champions from Russia and Sweden.

Automobile ice racing

Car ice racing ranges from amateur events in standard road cars on frozen lakes to highly specialised professional series. The French Trophée Andros series, held in the Alps and Pyrenees, became famous for purpose-built silhouette cars racing on mountain ice circuits. Studded tyres and rubber-to-ice classes are common formats in North American club racing.

Precautions and organisation

Events depend on sufficient ice thickness, verified before racing, and on carefully prepared surfaces. Because conditions vary, seasons are short and championships are often decided over a few winter weekends.
