Zona Rosa (English: "Pink Zone") is an area in Mexico City known for its shopping, nightlife, gay community, and Korean community. It forms part of the Colonia Juárez, located just west of the historic centre and south of the Paseo de la Reforma. The name, in use since the 1950s, derives from the pink-painted buildings and promotional material of its early commercial establishments.

The area originated in the late 19th and early 20th centuries as an elite residential suburb of mansions built for wealthy residents of the Porfirian era. In the 1950s and 1960s it became a bohemian centre for artists and intellectuals, filled with galleries, cafés, bookshops and nightclubs.

Overview

Today the Zona Rosa is one of Mexico City's best-known entertainment districts, with hotels, restaurants, bars and shops clustered around streets such as Génova, Amberes and Niza. Since the 1990s it has also been a focal point of the city's LGBT community, and a growing Koreatown (Pequeño Seúl) has developed along its western streets. The Angel of Independence monument on Paseo de la Reforma stands at its northern edge.

History

After the devastation of the 1985 Mexico City earthquake, parts of the district declined, and its character shifted toward mass-appeal commercial venues. Restoration efforts and renewed tourism in the 2000s brought back some higher-end establishments, though the area retains a reputation for vibrant, inexpensive nightlife.
