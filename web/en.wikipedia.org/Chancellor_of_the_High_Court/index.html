Chancellor of the High Court

The Chancellor of the High Court is the head of the Chancery Division of the High Court of Justice of England and Wales. The office, created in 2005, replaced the role of Vice-Chancellor as the division's head in title, though the functions are historic.

Role and responsibilities

The Chancellor heads the Chancery Division, which handles business and property cases including company law, insolvency, trusts, probate, and intellectual property, largely sitting in the Rolls Building in London. As one of the senior judges of England and Wales, the Chancellor also has wider leadership responsibilities within the judiciary.

History

The Chancery Division descends from the Court of Chancery, presided over by the Lord Chancellor, whose judicial functions were delegated to Vice-Chancellors from the nineteenth century. The Constitutional Reform Act 2005 transferred the Lord Chancellor's judicial role, and the title of Chancellor of the High Court came into use.
