Electromagnetic scattering

Electromagnetic scattering is the deflection of electromagnetic radiation — such as light, radio waves, or X-rays — by particles or inhomogeneities in a medium, so that energy spreads out from the original direction of propagation.

Regimes

When the scatterers are much smaller than the wavelength, Rayleigh scattering applies, with intensity strongly dependent on wavelength (proportional to 1/λ⁴); it explains the blue color of the sky. For particles comparable to the wavelength, Mie theory gives the exact solution for spheres. For large objects, geometric optics — reflection and refraction — is a good approximation.

Applications

Scattering underlies radar, lidar, weather observation, medical imaging, remote sensing of the atmosphere and oceans, and the study of interstellar dust. The scattering cross section quantifies how much power a target removes from the incident wave.
