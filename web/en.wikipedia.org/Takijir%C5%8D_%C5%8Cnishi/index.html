Vice Admiral Takijirō Ōnishi (1891–1945) was an admiral in the Imperial Japanese Navy during World War II, known as the founder of the kamikaze, the special attack units that deliberately crashed aircraft into Allied ships.

Career

Ōnishi was an early advocate of naval aviation and helped plan air operations early in the Pacific War. In October 1944, commanding the First Air Fleet in the Philippines, he organised the first official kamikaze attacks during the Battle of Leyte Gulf, arguing that suicide attacks were the only way to maximise the effect of Japan's dwindling air power.

Death

After Japan's surrender announcement, Ōnishi committed seppuku on 16 August 1945, refusing assistance so that his death was prolonged; in a farewell note he apologised to the spirits of the special attack pilots and urged surviving young men to work for Japan's reconstruction.
