Beaufort Gyre

Beaufort Gyre
TypeOcean gyre (wind-driven circulation)
LocationBeaufort Sea, Arctic Ocean, north of Alaska and Canada
RotationClockwise (anticyclonic)

The Beaufort Gyre is one of the two major ocean currents in the Arctic Ocean, located roughly north of the Alaskan and Canadian coasts in the Beaufort Sea. It is a wind-driven, clockwise circulation generated by the semi-permanent high-pressure system (the Beaufort High) over the western Arctic, and it drives the motion of sea ice across the pole region toward the Transpolar Drift.

Freshwater reservoir

Because the gyre's rotation piles surface water toward its centre through Ekman transport, it accumulates a vast store of relatively fresh water — from river runoff, precipitation and ice melt — held in a lens that can exceed 15 metres in thickness. In recent decades the gyre has stored an exceptionally large volume of freshwater, on the order of thousands of cubic kilometres.

Climate significance

If prevailing winds shift and the gyre weakens or reverses, its accumulated freshwater can be released into the North Atlantic, where it could freshen surface waters near Greenland and Labrador and potentially affect deep-water formation and the Atlantic meridional overturning circulation. Changes in the gyre also influence Arctic sea-ice distribution, thickness and ecosystems.

Research

The gyre has been monitored since 2003 by the Beaufort Gyre Exploration Project, using moorings and ice-based observatories, alongside satellite measurements of sea-surface height and gravity.
