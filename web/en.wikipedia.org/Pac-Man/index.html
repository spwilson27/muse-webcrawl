Pac-Man

DeveloperNamco
PublisherNamco (Japan), Midway (North America)
DesignerToru Iwatani
ReleaseMay 1980 (Japan)
GenreMaze
ModeSingle-player, 2 players alternating

Pac-Man, originally called Puck Man in Japan, is a 1980 maze action video game developed and released by Namco for arcades. The player controls the titular character, who must eat all the dots in an enclosed maze while avoiding four coloured ghosts — Blinky (red), Pinky (pink), Inky (cyan) and Clyde (orange). Eating large flashing dots, called "Power Pellets", temporarily lets Pac-Man eat the ghosts for bonus points.

Designed by Toru Iwatani, who wanted a non-violent game that would appeal to women as well as men, Pac-Man became an enduring icon of popular culture and a phenomenon of the golden age of arcade video games. It is widely considered one of the greatest video games of all time and one of the highest-grossing, spawning numerous sequels — most famously the unauthorised Midway follow-up Ms. Pac-Man (1982) — an animated television series, and the hit novelty song "Pac-Man Fever".

Gameplay

The player navigates Pac-Man through a maze containing 240 dots and four energisers. Each ghost has a distinct artificial-intelligence behaviour: Blinky chases Pac-Man directly, Pinky targets a point ahead of him, Inky's target depends on both Pac-Man and Blinky, and Clyde alternates between chasing and retreating. Bonus fruit appears under the ghost pen at intervals. A famous bug in the level counter produces a corrupted "kill screen" at level 256, making a perfect game — a maximum score of 3,333,360 points, first achieved by Billy Mitchell in 1999 — the effective completion of the game.

Legacy

Pac-Man was the first video game mascot character and remains one of the most recognisable. The character and the ghosts have been merchandised extensively, and the game's sound effects and imagery are staples of popular culture; a Pac-Man "wakawaka" eating sound is instantly familiar worldwide.
