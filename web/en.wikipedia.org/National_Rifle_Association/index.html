National Rifle Association

The National Rifle Association of America (NRA) is a gun rights advocacy group in the United States. Founded in 1871 to promote rifle marksmanship, it has become one of the most influential lobbying organizations in American politics, opposing most forms of gun control.

Founded17 November 1871
FoundersWilliam Conant Church, George Wood Wingate
HeadquartersFairfax, Virginia
MembershipClaimed ~4–5 million (2010s–2020s)

History

Union veterans Church and Wingate founded the NRA after Civil War experience showed poor marksmanship among troops. For much of its early history it focused on sport shooting and safety training and supported some regulation, including parts of the National Firearms Act of 1934. A leadership change at the 1977 annual meeting (the "Revolt at Cincinnati") redirected it toward uncompromising opposition to gun control, a stance maintained under longtime leader Wayne LaPierre (1991–2024).

Activities and controversies

The NRA runs firearm training and competitions, publishes magazines such as American Rifleman, and lobbies through its Institute for Legislative Action and political action committee. In 2020 the New York attorney general sued over alleged financial misconduct; the NRA's 2021 bankruptcy filing was dismissed, and in 2024 a jury found LaPierre liable for millions in misspent funds.
