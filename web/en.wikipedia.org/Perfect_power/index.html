Perfect power

In mathematics, a perfect power is a natural number that is a product of equal natural factors, that is, a number of the form mk where m and k are natural numbers greater than 1. Perfect powers include perfect squares (k = 2), perfect cubes (k = 3), and higher powers.

Examples and series

The smallest perfect powers are 4 = 22, 8 = 23, 9 = 32, 16 = 24 = 42, 25 = 52, 27 = 33, 32 = 25, 36 = 62 and 49 = 72. Numbers that are both squares and cubes are sixth powers, such as 64 and 729.

Detection

Detecting whether a given number is a perfect power can be done by testing roots for each possible exponent up to log2 n. Efficient algorithms are used in computer algebra and cryptography.

Properties

The series of perfect powers has zero density among the natural numbers. Catalan's conjecture, proved by Preda Mihăilescu in 2002, states that the only consecutive perfect powers greater than 1 are 8 and 9.
