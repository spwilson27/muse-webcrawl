Lord-lieutenant

The lord-lieutenant is the monarch's personal representative in a county.

A lord-lieutenant (also rendered lord lieutenant) is the British monarch's personal representative in each lieutenancy area in the United Kingdom. Historically, each of the administrative counties was the area represented by a lord-lieutenant, known as a county; today the areas correspond broadly to ceremonial counties in England, preserved counties in Wales, and council or historic areas in Scotland. In Ireland, lord-lieutenants existed until 1922, and the king's representative for the whole island was the Lord Lieutenant of Ireland.

Duties

The office, which originated in the Tudor period when lieutenants were responsible for raising the county militia, is now largely ceremonial. Modern duties include arranging royal visits to the county, presenting medals and awards on behalf of the sovereign, acting as a link between the monarch and local organisations, and advising on honours nominations. Lords-lieutenant are appointed by the monarch on the advice of the prime minister, are unpaid, and normally retire at 75. They are assisted by vice lord-lieutenants and deputy lieutenants, and wear a military-style uniform on formal occasions.
