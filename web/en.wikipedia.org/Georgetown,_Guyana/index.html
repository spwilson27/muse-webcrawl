Georgetown is the capital and largest city of Guyana, a country on the northern coast of South America. Located on the Atlantic coast at the mouth of the Demerara River, it is the country's chief port, administrative centre and commercial hub, and is known for its colonial wooden architecture and tree-lined avenues.

History

The city was founded by the Dutch in the 18th century as the capital of the colony of Demerara and was called Stabroek during Dutch and later British rule. The British captured the colony in 1796 and renamed the town Georgetown in 1812, in honour of King George III. After the abolition of slavery, freed Africans pooled their earnings to buy plantations that became some of the city's districts. Guyana became independent from Britain in 1966, with Georgetown as its capital.

Geography and climate

Georgetown lies on a low coastal plain at or below sea level at high tide, protected from the Atlantic by a sea wall and a network of canals and kokers (sluices) inherited from Dutch engineers. It has a tropical rainforest climate with high humidity and two rainy seasons, from May to August and from November to January.

Landmarks

Notable sights include St George's Cathedral, one of the tallest wooden churches in the world; the Stabroek Market with its distinctive clock tower; the Walter Roth Museum of Anthropology; and the Georgetown Botanical Gardens and Zoo. Much of the city's historic wooden architecture was lost in fires in 1945 and 2001, after which rebuilding rules favoured concrete in the commercial centre.
