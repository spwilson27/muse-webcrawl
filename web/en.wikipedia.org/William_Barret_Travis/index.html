William Barret Travis (August 1, 1809 – March 6, 1836) was an American lawyer and soldier, best known as the commander of the Texian forces at the Battle of the Alamo during the Texas Revolution.

Early life

Travis was born in South Carolina and raised in Alabama, where he studied law, taught school, and briefly ran a newspaper. After his law practice and marriage failed, he left for Texas in 1831, abandoning his family, and became involved in the early resistance to Mexican rule, including the Anahuac Disturbances of 1832.

The Alamo

In February 1836 Travis took command of the garrison at the Alamo Mission in San Antonio de Béxar. Besieged by General Santa Anna's army, he wrote his famous "Victory or Death" letter appealing for reinforcements. Travis died on March 6, 1836, when Mexican forces stormed the mission. Tradition holds that he drew a line in the sand and asked those willing to stay and fight to cross it.

Legacy

Travis County, Texas, and Travis High School in Austin are named in his honor, and he remains a central figure in Texas historical memory.
