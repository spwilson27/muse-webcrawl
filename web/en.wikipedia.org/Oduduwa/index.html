Oduduwa (Yoruba: Odùduwà) is a central figure in the traditions of the Yoruba people of West Africa. In Yoruba creation mythology he is the progenitor of humankind and of the Yoruba royal houses, the first Ooni (king) of the sacred city of Ile-Ife, and a deified ancestor venerated as an orisha.

Creation myth

According to Ife oral tradition, the supreme god Olodumare charged Oduduwa with creating the earth. In the best-known version, Oduduwa descended from the heavens by a chain carrying a handful of soil, a five-toed hen and a palm nut. He poured the soil onto the primordial waters; the hen scattered it to form dry land, and he planted the palm nut, from which grew a great tree. The place where the land first formed was Ile-Ife, "the place where the land spreads". Some traditions instead credit Obatala with the task, with Oduduwa taking over when Obatala became drunk on palm wine — a rivalry that explains the taboo on palm wine among Obatala's worshippers.

Historical traditions

Other traditions treat Oduduwa as a historical leader who arrived at Ife — in some accounts from the east — unified the region's settlements, and founded a dynasty. His sons and grandsons are said to have migrated outward to found the other Yoruba kingdoms, including Oyo and Benin; the reigning monarchs of Yorubaland still trace their crowns to Oduduwa, and the Ooni of Ife is regarded as his direct successor and the spiritual head of the Yoruba.

Legacy

Oduduwa's name is carried by institutions across southwestern Nigeria, including Oduduwa University, and he remains a potent symbol of Yoruba unity and identity.
