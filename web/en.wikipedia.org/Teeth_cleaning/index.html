Teeth cleaning

Teeth cleaning is the removal of dental plaque, calculus (tartar), and stains from teeth, performed as part of oral hygiene. It includes both professional cleaning carried out by dentists and dental hygienists and the daily home care of brushing and flossing. Regular cleaning helps prevent tooth decay (caries) and gum disease (gingivitis and periodontitis).

Professional cleaning

A professional cleaning, or prophylaxis, typically involves examining the teeth and gums, scaling to remove plaque and hardened calculus above and below the gumline using hand instruments or ultrasonic scalers, and polishing to remove surface stains and smooth the enamel. Deeper cleaning below the gumline, called scaling and root planing, is used to treat periodontal disease. Fluoride treatment may be applied afterwards.

Home care

Dental authorities generally recommend brushing twice a day with fluoride toothpaste, cleaning between the teeth daily with floss or interdental brushes, and limiting sugary foods and drinks. Electric toothbrushes, mouthwashes, and tongue cleaning can supplement these measures. Recall intervals for professional cleaning are commonly six to twelve months, adjusted to individual risk.

Risks

Cleaning is generally safe; temporary sensitivity and gum soreness can occur. Over-vigorous brushing or abrasive products may wear enamel and recede gums, and professional debridement can release bacteria into the bloodstream, a consideration for a small number of patients who may need antibiotic prophylaxis.
