The Twelfth

Observed: 12 July

Where: Northern Ireland and Ulster

Also called: Orangemen's Day

Commemorates: Battle of the Boyne (1690)

The Twelfth (also called Orangemen's Day) is an Ulster Protestant celebration held on 12 July, commemorating the victory of the Protestant King William of Orange over the Catholic King James II at the Battle of the Boyne in 1690, and the wider Glorious Revolution. It is marked by parades of the Orange Order with marching bands, preceded on the night of 11 July by large bonfires.

History

Celebrations of William's victory date to the 1790s, when the Orange Order was founded. The date also absorbed an older commemoration of the Battle of Aughrim. Parades traditionally proceeded along set routes through towns across Northern Ireland.

Controversy

Some parades passing nationalist areas have been contentious, most famously at Drumcree in the 1990s, leading to the creation of the Parades Commission to adjudicate disputed routes. The Eleventh Night bonfires have also drawn criticism over safety and the burning of symbols. The Twelfth is a public holiday in Northern Ireland.
