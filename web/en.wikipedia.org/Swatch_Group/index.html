The Swatch Group

Image placeholder: Swatch Group headquarters, Biel/Bienne.

The Swatch Group Ltd is a Swiss manufacturer of watches and jewellery, and the world's largest watchmaking company. Headquartered in Biel/Bienne, Switzerland, it was formed in 1983 through the merger of ASUAG and SSIH, the two largest Swiss watch groups, under the leadership of Nicolas G. Hayek.

History

The merger, originally named SMH (Société de Microélectronique et d'Horlogerie), was a response to the quartz crisis. The launch of the Swatch watch in 1983 restored profitability and provided the cash flow to reacquire prestige brands. The company was renamed The Swatch Group in 1998. After Nicolas Hayek's death in 2010, his son Nick Hayek Jr. became chief executive, and his daughter Nayla Hayek chair of the board.

Brands

The group owns brands across all price segments, including Breguet, Blancpain, Glashütte Original, Jaquet Droz, Omega, Longines, Rado, Tissot, Hamilton, Certina, Mido, Calvin Klein watches, and the entry-level Swatch and Flik Flak brands. Its subsidiary ETA SA supplies movements to much of the Swiss watch industry.

Operations

The Swatch Group employs tens of thousands of people and operates production facilities mainly in Switzerland. It has also been an official timekeeper for the Olympic Games through its Omega brand.
