Solera

A solera of stacked casks in Jerez, Spain

Solera is a process for ageing liquids such as wine, beer, vinegar, and brandy by fractional blending, such that the finished product is a mixture of ages, with the average age gradually increasing as the process continues over many years. The system is traditional in the production of sherry in Jerez, Spain, and is also used for Madeira, Marsala, some balsamic vinegars, brandies, and certain rums, whiskies, and craft beers.

Process

A solera consists of several rows or "scales" (criaderas) of barrels stacked from floor level upward. The bottom row — the solera proper, from the Spanish suelo, "ground" — holds the oldest liquid and is the only row from which product is drawn for bottling. Only a fraction is removed; the solera is then topped up from the first criadera above it, which is in turn replenished from the next, and so on up to the youngest row, which receives new wine or spirit. Because only part of each barrel is ever removed, some portion of the oldest liquid theoretically remains in the system indefinitely, giving consistency of style from year to year.

Age statements

Because the bottled product is a blend of many vintages, a solera product cannot carry a simple vintage age. Producers may state an average age: in the sherry region, regulated categories include VOS (vinum optimum signatum, average age over 20 years) and VORS (over 30 years). The system is prized for maintaining house style and for the depth and complexity contributed by the oldest fraction.
