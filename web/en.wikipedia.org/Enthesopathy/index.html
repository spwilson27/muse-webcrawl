Enthesopathy

Enthesopathy
SpecialtyRheumatology, orthopedics
SitesEntheses (tendon/ligament insertions)
CausesOveruse, inflammatory disease, metabolic disorders

An enthesopathy refers to a disorder involving the attachment of a tendon or ligament to a bone. This site of attachment is called the enthesis (plural: entheses). An enthesopathy is any pathologic condition involving an enthesis, whether caused by mechanical overuse, inflammation, degeneration, or metabolic disease. Inflammation of an enthesis is specifically termed enthesitis.

Causes

Enthesopathies commonly arise from repetitive strain or sports injuries, as in epicondylitis (tennis elbow) or insertional Achilles tendinopathy. Inflammatory enthesitis is a hallmark of the spondyloarthropathies, including ankylosing spondylitis and psoriatic arthritis, where the sacroiliac joints and spinal insertions are characteristically affected. Metabolic and endocrine conditions — such as diffuse idiopathic skeletal hyperostosis (DISH), diabetes mellitus, and acromegaly — also produce enthesopathic changes, often with exuberant new bone formation.

Common sites

  • Achilles tendon insertion at the calcaneus
  • Plantar fascia origin (a cause of heel spurs)
  • Lateral and medial epicondyles of the humerus
  • Patellar tendon insertions
  • Rotator cuff insertions at the shoulder
  • Pelvic entheses in ankylosing spondylitis

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is clinical, supported by imaging: radiographs may show erosions, sclerosis, or bony spurs at insertion sites, while ultrasound and MRI can reveal thickening, fluid, and inflammatory change. Treatment depends on the cause — rest, physiotherapy, and non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs for mechanical cases; disease-modifying and biologic therapies for inflammatory spondyloarthropathies.
