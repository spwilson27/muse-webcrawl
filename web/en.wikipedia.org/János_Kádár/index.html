János Kádár

János Kádár (born János József Czermanik; 26 May 1912 – 6 July 1989) was a Hungarian Communist leader who ruled Hungary as General Secretary of the Hungarian Socialist Workers' Party from 1956 until 1988 — the longest-serving leader of a Warsaw Pact state — first installed by the Soviet Union after it crushed the Hungarian Revolution of 1956, and later presiding over the relatively liberal system known as "Goulash Communism".

Early life and rise

Born in Fiume to an unmarried servant mother, Kádár grew up in poverty, trained as a typewriter mechanic, and joined the illegal Communist Party in 1931, enduring repeated arrests under the Horthy regime. After 1945 he rose in the party, became interior minister, then was himself arrested and tortured in the Rajk purges (1951–1954) before rehabilitation.

1956 and consolidation

During the revolution of October 1956 Kádár first joined Imre Nagy's government, then defected to the Soviets and returned with the Red Army, forming a "Revolutionary Workers'-Peasants' Government". The reprisals that followed — hundreds executed, including Nagy in 1958 — secured his rule, but from the early 1960s he proclaimed that "whoever is not against us is with us", released most political prisoners, and traded political quiet for rising living standards.

Goulash Communism

The New Economic Mechanism (1968) introduced market elements, making Hungary the most prosperous and tolerant state in the Eastern Bloc, though reforms stalled in the 1970s and foreign debt mounted in the 1980s. Aging and in declining health, Kádár was removed as general secretary in May 1988 and died on 6 July 1989 — the same day Hungary's Supreme Court formally rehabilitated Imre Nagy.
