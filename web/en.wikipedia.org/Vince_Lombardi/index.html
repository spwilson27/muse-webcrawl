Vincent Thomas Lombardi (11 June 1913 – 3 September 1970) was an American football coach and executive, best known as head coach of the Green Bay Packers during the 1960s. He led the Packers to five NFL championships in seven years, including victories in the first two Super Bowls, and is regarded as one of the greatest coaches in the history of the sport. The Super Bowl championship trophy is named the Vince Lombardi Trophy in his honour.

Early life and playing career

Lombardi was born in Brooklyn to Italian immigrant parents. He played guard at Fordham University as part of the famed "Seven Blocks of Granite" offensive line, graduating in 1937. After brief stints teaching and coaching at the high school and college level, he joined the New York Giants as offensive coordinator in 1954 under Jim Lee Howell.

Green Bay Packers

Hired as head coach and general manager of the Packers in 1959, Lombardi transformed a team that had gone 1–10–1 the previous season into champions. His Packers won the NFL championship in 1961, 1962 and 1965, then won Super Bowl I and Super Bowl II. His signature play was the Packers sweep, and his teams were famous for their discipline and execution. His career regular-season winning percentage was .738.

Later career and legacy

Lombardi stepped down as head coach after Super Bowl II, served one year as Packers general manager, then coached the Washington Redskins in 1969. He died of colorectal cancer in 1970 and was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame the following year. His maxims on effort and winning remain widely quoted in American culture.
