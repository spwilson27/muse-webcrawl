Roscoe Pound

Nathan Roscoe Pound (27 October 1870 – 30 June 1964) was an American legal scholar and educator, dean of Harvard Law School from 1916 to 1936, and a leading figure in sociological jurisprudence.

Born: 27 October 1870, Lincoln, Nebraska
Died: 30 June 1964, Cambridge, Massachusetts
Known for: Sociological jurisprudence; "law in action"

Career and thought

Trained first as a botanist — he earned a PhD and co-authored a survey of Nebraska plants — Pound turned to law, practised in Nebraska, and taught at Northwestern and Chicago before Harvard. He argued that law should be studied as a social institution: his distinction between "law in books" and "law in action", and his theory of law as social engineering balancing competing interests, influenced American legal realism and judicial reform.

Influence

Pound's 1906 address "The Causes of Popular Dissatisfaction with the Administration of Justice" spurred procedural reform. He wrote extensively, including the five-volume Jurisprudence (1959). Later he clashed with the legal realists he had helped inspire. See also sociological jurisprudence and Harvard Law School.
