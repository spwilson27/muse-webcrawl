Open tuning

Open tuning
InstrumentsGuitar, banjo, dobro, other stringed instruments
Common formsOpen G, open D, open E, open C
GenresBlues, folk, country, rock, slide guitar

An open tuning is a tuning of a stringed instrument in which the open strings are tuned to the notes of a single chord, so that a full chord rings out when the strings are strummed without fretting. Open tunings are especially associated with the guitar and with slide guitar playing, because a slide (or a single finger barring all strings at one fret) can then produce any major chord simply by moving along the neck.

Common open tunings

Open G (D–G–D–G–B–D, from the lowest string) is widely used in blues and folk, and was a favourite of Keith Richards of the Rolling Stones, who often removed the lowest string. Open D (D–A–D–F♯–A–D) and open E are common for slide playing, used by Duane Allman and Derek Trucks. Open C (C–G–C–G–C–E) is favoured by fingerstyle players such as John Fahey and by Devin Townsend. The Hawaiian slack-key tradition and the five-string banjo's standard open G tuning are older relatives of the concept.

Technique and repertoire

Because chord shapes change entirely in open tunings, players rely on simple barre shapes, partial chords, and the resonance of sympathetic open strings. Robert Johnson and Muddy Waters built blues styles around open tunings; Joni Mitchell catalogued dozens of her own open and altered tunings; and Jimmy Page used open and modal tunings in Led Zeppelin recordings. Related but distinct are altered tunings such as drop D and DADGAD, in which the open strings do not by themselves form a simple major chord.
