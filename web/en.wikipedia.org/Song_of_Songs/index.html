Song of Songs


The lovers of the Song, from a medieval illuminated manuscript

The Song of Songs, also known as the Song of Solomon or Canticles, is a book of the Hebrew Bible and the Christian Old Testament. It is a collection of love poetry celebrating romantic and erotic love between a man and a woman, structured largely as a dialogue between lovers. Its title derives from its opening words, "The Song of Songs, which is Solomon's", the superlative construction meaning "the greatest of songs".

Structure and content

The book contains eight chapters of lyric poetry. The protagonists are a woman (the Shulamite) and a man, who exchange expressions of longing and admiration, interspersed with descriptions of nature — vineyards, gardens, gazelles, and lilies. Unlike most biblical books, it contains no explicit mention of God, the Law, or the covenant, a feature that made its place in the canon controversial in antiquity.

Authorship and date

Tradition attributes the book to King Solomon in the 10th century BC. Modern scholars, on linguistic grounds — including Persian and Greek loanwords — generally date the final composition of the poems to the Persian or early Hellenistic period, roughly the 5th to 3rd centuries BC, though the poems themselves may incorporate older material.

Interpretation

Jewish and Christian interpreters have traditionally read the Song allegorically: in Judaism as an expression of God's love for Israel (Rabbi Akiva famously declared it the "Holy of Holies"), and in Christianity as Christ's love for the Church or for the individual soul. Modern scholarship often reads it more literally as secular love poetry, possibly connected with wedding celebrations or ancient Near Eastern love songs of the type attested in Egyptian and Mesopotamian literature.
