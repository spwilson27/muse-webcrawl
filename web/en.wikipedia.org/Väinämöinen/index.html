Väinämöinen

Väinämöinen is the central hero of Finnish mythology and the main character of the Kalevala, Finland's national epic compiled by Elias Lönnrot from Karelian and Finnish oral poetry. A wise old sage, shaman, and culture hero, Väinämöinen possesses a powerful singing voice with which he works magic — shaping the world, charming animals and enemies, and even singing a rival into a swamp.

Mythology

In the Kalevala's creation story, Väinämöinen is born of Ilmatar, the spirit of the air, after gestating for centuries on the primeval sea; the world is formed from the eggs of a goldeneye duck laid on his knee. He sows the first barley, clears the forests, and fashions the first kantele (the Finnish zither) — first from the jawbone of a giant pike, later from birch wood with strings made from a maiden's hair.

His adventures include the contest with the young joukahainen, whom he sings into a bog; unsuccessful wooing journeys to Pohjola, the dark northern land ruled by the witch Louhi; the forging of the magical mill, the Sampo, by the smith Ilmarinen; the great battle for possession of the Sampo; and the descent into the belly of the dead giant Antero Vipunen to recover lost words of power.

Departure and legacy

At the end of the epic, the virgin Marjatta gives birth to a son who is baptised king of Karelia, displacing the old pagan order. Väinämöinen, slighted, departs by boat, leaving his kantele and songs to Finland and promising to return when his people need him — a motif echoing other departing culture heroes. The figure strongly influenced J. R. R. Tolkien's Gandalf and Tom Bombadil; Tolkien's youthful retelling, The Story of Kullervo, drew on the same epic.
