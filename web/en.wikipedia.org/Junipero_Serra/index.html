Saint Junípero Serra Ferrer (born Miquel Josep Serra; 1713 – 1784) was a Spanish Franciscan friar who founded the first nine of the 21 Spanish missions in California, from San Diego to San Francisco, beginning in 1769. He was canonized by Pope Francis in Washington, D.C., on 23 September 2015 — the first saint canonized on United States soil — a decision celebrated by many Catholics and protested by many Native American activists.

Life

Serra was born on Mallorca, became a Franciscan at 16, earned a doctorate in theology and taught philosophy before volunteering for the American missions. He arrived in New Spain (Mexico) in 1749 and worked for years in the Sierra Gorda missions of Querétaro. When Spain moved to occupy Alta California against Russian and British encroachment, Serra accompanied the 1769 expedition of Gaspar de Portolá and founded Mission San Diego de Alcalá, the first in California. As father-president of the missions he founded San Carlos Borromeo (Carmel, his headquarters and burial place), San Antonio, San Gabriel, San Luis Obispo, San Francisco de Asís, San Juan Capistrano, Santa Clara and San Buenaventura, travelling thousands of miles on foot despite a chronically injured leg.

Legacy and controversy

Serra is honoured as the "Apostle of California" and represented California in the U.S. Capitol's National Statuary Hall from 1931 until replaced by Ronald Reagan in 2007. Critics point to the mission system's coercion: Indigenous people were gathered into missions, subjected to forced labour, corporal punishment and suppression of their cultures, and died in large numbers from European diseases. During the 2020 protests, several statues of Serra in California were toppled or removed, and debates over his legacy continue.
