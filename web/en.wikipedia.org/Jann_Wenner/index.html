Jann Simon Wenner (born January 7, 1946) is an American magazine magnate who co-founded the popular culture magazine Rolling Stone in 1967 with music critic Ralph J. Gleason. He built Wenner Media around titles that at various times included Us Weekly and Men's Journal, and he was a co-founder of the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame.

Rolling Stone

Wenner founded Rolling Stone in San Francisco while a student activist from UC Berkeley, financing it with family loans. The magazine became the defining chronicle of rock music and counterculture, publishing writers such as Hunter S. Thompson and photographer Annie Leibovitz. Wenner himself conducted celebrated interviews with figures including John Lennon, Bob Dylan, Bill Clinton and Barack Obama.

Later years

Wenner sold controlling stakes in Rolling Stone in 2016–2017. His 2022 memoir Like a Rolling Stone and his 2023 interview book The Masters drew controversy; remarks in a 2023 New York Times interview about Black and female artists led to his removal from the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame Foundation board.
