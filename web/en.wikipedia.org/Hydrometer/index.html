A hydrometer or lactometer is an instrument used to measure the specific gravity (also called relative density) or other properties of a liquid. It is usually made of glass and consists of a cylindrical stem and a bulb weighted with mercury or lead shot to make it float upright. The liquid to be tested is poured into a tall container, and the hydrometer is gently lowered into the liquid until it floats freely; the point at which the surface of the liquid touches the stem is read off the scale.

Principle

The instrument works on Archimedes' principle: a floating body displaces its own weight of liquid. The denser the liquid, the higher the hydrometer floats; the less dense, the deeper it sinks. The scale is therefore inverted, with low specific gravity values near the top of the stem.

History

Hydrometers have been used since late antiquity; a form of the instrument is described in a letter of Hypatia of Alexandria in the early 5th century CE. Modern precision hydrometers became standard laboratory tools in the 18th and 19th centuries, with specialised scales developed for particular industries.

Uses

Specialised hydrometers include the alcoholmeter for spirits, the saccharometer used by brewers and winemakers to measure sugar content (in degrees Brix or Plato), the lactometer for milk, and battery hydrometers that check the state of charge of lead–acid cells by measuring the specific gravity of the electrolyte. In soil science, hydrometer analysis estimates the particle-size distribution of fine sediments.
