Heliosphere

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Heliosphere
Formed bySolar wind from the Sun
Key boundariesTermination shock, heliopause, heliosheath, bow shock/wave
Radius (nose)Roughly 120 AU to the heliopause
Explored byVoyager 1 (crossed heliopause 2012) and Voyager 2 (2018)

The heliosphere is the vast, bubble-like region of space surrounding the Sun and the Solar System, created by the solar wind — the stream of charged particles (plasma) flowing outward from the Sun in all directions at supersonic speed. The heliosphere acts as a shield, deflecting much of the interstellar medium and modulating the flux of galactic cosmic rays reaching the planets. Its boundary, the heliopause, marks where the solar wind's pressure balances that of the interstellar medium; beyond it lies interstellar space.

Structure

As the solar wind expands it eventually slows abruptly at the termination shock, where it becomes subsonic, hot and turbulent, entering the heliosheath, a region of compressed plasma that flows around the heliopause. The heliopause is the outer surface of the heliosphere. Because the Sun moves through the local interstellar medium at about 26 km/s, the heliosphere is thought to be compressed on the "nose" side facing the direction of motion and stretched into a tail (the heliotail) behind — though its exact shape (comet-like, spherical, or croissant-like with twin jets) is an active research question. An interstellar bow shock was once expected ahead of the nose; IBEX data suggest the relative speed may instead produce a gentler bow wave.

Exploration

NASA's Voyager 1 crossed the termination shock in 2004 at about 94 AU and the heliopause in August 2012 at about 121.6 AU, becoming the first human-made object in interstellar space; Voyager 2 crossed the heliopause in November 2018 at about 119 AU, in a different direction, giving a second sample of the boundary. The Interstellar Boundary Explorer (IBEX) maps energetic neutral atoms from the boundary remotely — revealing the unexpected IBEX ribbon — and the Interstellar Mapping and Acceleration Probe (IMAP) was launched to extend such observations. The heliosphere's size breathes with the 11-year solar cycle and with pressure changes in the interstellar medium.

Effects on Earth

The heliosphere modulates cosmic-ray intensity at Earth, which varies inversely with solar activity and may interact with terrestrial climate records and radiation exposure. Its shielding role is a factor in assessing habitability of the Solar System and of exoplanetary systems with analogous "astrospheres".
