The University of Gastronomic Sciences (Italian: Università degli Studi di Scienze Gastronomiche, UNISG) is a private university in Pollenzo, a frazione of Bra in Piedmont, Italy. Founded in 2004 by Carlo Petrini, the founder of the international Slow Food movement, it was the first university devoted entirely to the study of food and gastronomy as an academic discipline.

History

The university opened in 2004 in the restored Agenzia di Pollenzo, a neo-Gothic complex built for King Charles Albert of Savoy in the 19th century. It was established by Slow Food in cooperation with the Piedmont and Emilia-Romagna regions, with the aim of training "gastronomes" — professionals combining humanities, food science, and sustainability.

Programmes

UNISG offers an undergraduate degree in Gastronomic Sciences and Cultures, master's programmes in areas such as food culture, communication, and sustainable food production, and a PhD programme. A distinctive feature of its teaching is the study trip: students travel internationally to study food production systems and cultures on site. Teaching is in English and Italian, and the student body is highly international.
