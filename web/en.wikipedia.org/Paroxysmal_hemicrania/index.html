Paroxysmal hemicrania
Specialty: Neurology

Paroxysmal hemicrania is a rare headache disorder belonging to the group known as trigeminal autonomic cephalalgias. It is characterized by frequent, short, severe unilateral attacks of head pain accompanied by autonomic features such as tearing and nasal congestion.

Signs and symptoms

Attacks last typically from 2 to 30 minutes and may occur many times per day, often more than five times daily. Pain is usually centred around the eye and temple on one side, accompanied by ipsilateral conjunctival injection, lacrimation, rhinorrhea, ptosis, or eyelid oedema.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is based on the clinical pattern and, characteristically, an absolute response to the non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug indomethacin, which is considered a diagnostic criterion.

Treatment

Indomethacin is the treatment of choice and usually provides complete relief. For patients who cannot tolerate it, other options include other NSAIDs, calcium channel blockers, and topiramate.

Epidemiology

The condition is rare, affecting women somewhat more often than men, with onset typically in adulthood.
