Ōita Prefecture (大分県, Ōita-ken) is a prefecture of Japan located on the island of Kyushu. Its capital is the city of Ōita. The prefecture occupies the northeastern part of Kyushu, facing the Seto Inland Sea and the Bungo Channel, and is best known throughout Japan for its abundance of hot springs (onsen).

Geography

Ōita is a largely mountainous prefecture; roughly 70 percent of its land area is forested. Its highest peak is Mount Kujū. The coastline is long and varied, including part of the Seto Inland Sea National Park and the dramatic ria coast of the Saiki area. The prefecture comprises 14 cities, including Beppu, Yufu, Hita, Saiki, Usuki, Nakatsu and Taketa.

History

The area of modern Ōita corresponds largely to the former provinces of Bungo and the southern part of Buzen. During the Sengoku period it was ruled by the Christian daimyō Ōtomo Sōrin, whose domain became an early centre of trade and contact with Portuguese missionaries; Francis Xavier visited Bungo in 1551. The prefecture took its present form in the Meiji period, in 1876.

Hot springs

Ōita has the largest number of hot spring sources and the greatest volume of hot spring water of any prefecture in Japan. The city of Beppu alone has over 2,000 springs and is famous for its "Hells of Beppu" (jigoku), colourful geothermal ponds including the blood-red Chinoike Jigoku. The resort town of Yufuin, beneath the twin peaks of Mount Yufu, is another major destination. The prefecture markets itself as Japan's "Onsen Prefecture".

Economy and culture

Manufacturing — including steel, chemicals, petrochemicals and electronics around the Ōita coastal industrial zone — anchors the economy alongside agriculture, forestry and tourism. Local specialities include kabosu citrus fruit, shiitake mushrooms, sekisaba and sekiaji mackerel, and the noodle-like dangojiru soup. Ōita was a host venue for the 2019 Rugby World Cup, and its football club Ōita Trinita plays in the J.League.
