Farah Mendlesohn (born 1968) is a British academic historian and science fiction critic, and a fan and con-runner. She is known for her critical works on science fiction and fantasy, and for co-editing The Cambridge Companion to Science Fiction (2003) with Edward James, which won the Hugo Award for Best Related Book in 2005.

Career

Mendlesohn received her DPhil from the University of York. She was a lecturer in American history at Middlesex University and later worked in health education research. She managed the exhibits programme for the 2014 World Science Fiction Convention (Loncon 3) and has been active in science fiction fandom and convention organisation.

Works

Her book Rhetorics of Fantasy (2008) proposed an influential taxonomy of fantasy into portal-quest, immersive, intrusive, and liminal modes. Diana Wynne Jones: The Children's Literature of Diana Wynne Jones (2005) examined that author's writing, and The Pleasant Profession of Robert A. Heinlein (2019) was a major critical study of Heinlein. She also edited The Inter-galactic Playground (2009), on children's science fiction.
