Central binomial coefficient

In mathematics the n-th central binomial coefficient is the particular binomial coefficient

(2n choose n) = (2n)! / (n!)²

They are called central since they show up exactly in the middle of the even-numbered rows in Pascal's triangle. The first few central binomial coefficients starting with n = 0 are

1, 2, 6, 20, 70, 252, 924, 3432, 12870, 48620, ...

Properties

The central binomial coefficient satisfies the recurrence a(n+1) = a(n)·(4n+2)/(n+1), and its generating function is 1/√(1−4x). By Stirling's approximation, a(n) ~ 4ⁿ / √(πn), which is used in the analysis of random walks and the estimate of the probability that a simple symmetric random walk returns to the origin.

The Catalan numbers are obtained directly: C(n) = a(n)/(n+1). Erdős showed that central binomial coefficients greater than 6 are never perfect powers, and their prime factorisations are constrained by the fact that all prime factors of a(n) are at most 2n, primes between n and 2n dividing a(n) exactly once.

Related concepts

Squared central binomial coefficients sum to the central Delannoy numbers' relatives, and the central trinomial coefficients and Motzkin numbers form analogous sequences. The quantity (2n choose n) counts lattice paths from (0,0) to (n,n) using unit steps.
