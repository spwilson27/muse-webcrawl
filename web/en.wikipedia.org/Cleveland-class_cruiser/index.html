The Cleveland-class was a group of light cruisers built for the United States Navy during World War II. With 27 ships completed, it was the most numerous class of cruisers ever built, and formed the backbone of the Navy's cruiser force in the Pacific War. The ships were a development of the preceding Brooklyn class, modified to meet treaty tonnage limits while carrying a heavy battery of twelve 6-inch guns and a strong anti-aircraft armament.

Design

The Clevelands displaced about 11,900 tons standard and measured 610 feet overall. Their main battery consisted of twelve 6-inch/47 calibre guns in four triple turrets, supplemented by twelve 5-inch dual-purpose guns, numerous 40 mm and 20 mm anti-aircraft weapons, and facilities for two floatplanes launched by catapult. Armour protection included a belt up to 5 inches thick. The design was famously top-heavy and crowded as wartime additions of radar and anti-aircraft guns strained the original hull.

Service history

Ships of the class fought throughout the Pacific, serving as escorts for fast carrier task forces and providing gunfire support for amphibious landings. Several were heavily damaged by kamikaze attacks and torpedoes, but none was lost. Nine incomplete hulls were converted into Independence-class light aircraft carriers during the war.

Later use

Most of the class was decommissioned shortly after the war. Six ships were converted in the late 1950s into guided missile cruisers of the Galveston and Providence classes, serving into the 1970s. USS Little Rock survives as a museum ship in Buffalo, New York.
