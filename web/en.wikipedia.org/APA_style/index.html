APA style
Publisher: American Psychological Association
Manual: Publication Manual of the APA
Current edition: 7th (2019)
Used in: Psychology, social sciences, education

APA style (also known as APA format) is a writing style and format for academic documents such as scholarly journal articles and books. It is promulgated by the American Psychological Association (APA) in its Publication Manual and is widely used in psychology, education, and the social sciences.

History

The style originated in a seven-page set of instructions published in 1929 in the Psychological Bulletin, drafted by a group of psychologists, anthropologists, and business managers. The first full Publication Manual appeared in 1952. The seventh edition, released in October 2019, is the current version.

Features

APA style specifies rules for manuscript structure (title page, abstract, headings), mechanics of style (punctuation, abbreviations, numbers), bias-free language, tables and figures, and — most distinctively — citations. In-text citations use an author–date system: a work by two authors is cited with both names, and works by three or more authors are shortened with "et al." The reference list at the end gives full bibliographic entries in a hanging-indent format, with the year immediately after the first author's name and DOIs presented as URLs.

The seventh edition notably endorsed the singular "they" as a generic pronoun and simplified guidance for student papers.
