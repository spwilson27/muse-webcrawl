Pyrometer

A pyrometer is a type of remote-sensing thermometer used to measure the temperature of distant objects, originally the high temperatures found in furnaces and kilns. Modern pyrometers determine temperature from the thermal radiation emitted by the target surface, applying the principles of black-body radiation, and so can measure without physical contact.

Pyrometer
Measures: Surface temperature, non-contact
Principle: Thermal (black-body) radiation
Types: Optical, infrared, two-colour (ratio)
Uses: Furnaces, steelmaking, kilns

Types

The classic optical pyrometer compares the brightness of the hot object with that of a calibrated filament viewed through the same eyepiece; the operator adjusts the filament current until it disappears against the target. Infrared pyrometers focus emitted radiation onto a detector such as a thermopile or photodiode. Two-colour or ratio pyrometers measure radiation at two wavelengths, reducing errors caused by unknown emissivity, dust, or a partially filled field of view.

Applications

Pyrometers are essential in pyrometallurgy, steel and glass manufacture, cement kilns, and semiconductor processing, and for measuring moving or inaccessible objects such as turbine blades and molten metal streams. Measurements require care because emissivity varies with material, surface condition, and wavelength.
