Cayenne

Cayenne (French pronunciation: [kajɛn]) is the capital city of French Guiana, an overseas region and department of France located in South America. It is the largest Francophone city of the continent and the main port of French Guiana.

History

French settlers founded Cayenne in 1643 on the island at the mouth of the Cayenne River, though early settlements were repeatedly destroyed in conflicts with indigenous peoples and rival colonial powers. The city later became an administrative centre for the French Guianese penal colonies; political prisoners were held at Fort Cépérou overlooking the town.

Geography and climate

Cayenne sits on a peninsula between the Cayenne and Mahury rivers on the Atlantic coast. It has an equatorial climate with a wet season from December to July and high humidity year-round. Nearby offshore lie the Îles du Salut, including the former penal colony of Devil's Island.

Demographics and culture

The city's population reflects Guiana's diversity: Creole, metropolitan French, Haitian, Brazilian, Surinamese, and Hmong communities, among others. The Cayenne Carnival, held between Epiphany and Ash Wednesday, is a major cultural event, known for the Touloulou masked balls. The city is also historically associated with cayenne pepper.
