Emblem

An emblem is an abstract or representational pictorial image that represents a concept, a group, or a person, much as a symbol does. Emblems may be worn or otherwise displayed as identifying badges — for example the emblems of states, military units, universities and sports teams — and their study overlaps heraldry.

Emblem books

In the Renaissance, "emblem" acquired a specific literary meaning: an emblem consisted of a motto (inscriptio), a picture (pictura) and an explanatory epigram (subscriptio) that together embodied a moral or allegorical lesson. Andrea Alciato's Emblemata (1531) founded the genre, and hundreds of emblem books followed across Europe, influencing art, pageantry and literature for two centuries. Shakespeare and other writers drew images from emblem literature familiar to their audiences.

Emblems and heraldry

An emblem differs from a coat of arms in that it need not follow heraldic rules or be hereditary; personal devices such as the Italian impresa expressed individual aspirations. National emblems — for instance the bald eagle of the United States or the floral emblems of many countries — function as visual shorthand for states and institutions.

Modern use

Today the word covers logos and insignia generally, from corporate marks to the emblems of the Olympic Games. In traffic and safety contexts, standardised emblems communicate regulated meanings at a glance.
