Croatia, officially the Republic of Croatia (Croatian: Republika Hrvatska), is a country in Central and Southeast Europe, on the coast of the Adriatic Sea. It borders Slovenia to the northwest, Hungary to the northeast, Serbia to the east, Bosnia and Herzegovina and Montenegro to the southeast, and shares a maritime border with Italy. Its territory covers 56,594 square kilometres and includes more than a thousand islands and islets along its indented coastline. The capital and largest city is Zagreb.

Dubrovnik, a UNESCO World Heritage Site on the Dalmatian coast.

History

The Croats arrived in the area of present-day Croatia in the early seventh century, organising into duchies that by the ninth century had coalesced under rulers such as Trpimir I. Tomislav became the first king in 925. In 1102 Croatia entered a personal union with the Kingdom of Hungary, and for centuries its lands were divided among Habsburg, Venetian and Ottoman rule. After the First World War Croatia joined the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes, later Yugoslavia; during the Second World War the Axis-allied Independent State of Croatia committed atrocities against Serbs, Jews and Roma. Croatia became a constituent republic of socialist Yugoslavia in 1945, declared independence in 1991, and secured its territory in the Croatian War of Independence, which ended in 1995.

Politics and international relations

Croatia is a unitary parliamentary republic. The president is head of state, while executive power rests chiefly with the government led by the prime minister; the Sabor is the unicameral legislature. Croatia joined NATO in 2009 and the European Union on 1 July 2013, and on 1 January 2023 adopted the euro and entered the Schengen Area.

Economy and tourism

The economy is dominated by services, with tourism accounting for a large share of GDP: the Adriatic coast, historic cities such as Dubrovnik, Split, Rovinj and Zadar, and national parks including Plitvice Lakes draw over 15 million visitors in peak years. Other important sectors include shipbuilding, food processing, chemicals, and information technology.

Culture

Croatia has sixteen UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage inscriptions, from lace-making and gingerbread craft to the klapa multipart singing of Dalmatia. The necktie originates with Croatian mercenaries of the seventeenth century — the French word cravate derives from Hrvat, "Croat". Croatian athletes have achieved notable success in football (World Cup finalists in 2018), handball, water polo and alpine skiing.
