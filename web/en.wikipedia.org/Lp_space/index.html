Lp space

Unit "balls" of the p-norms for several values of p.

In mathematics, the Lp spaces are function spaces defined using a natural generalisation of the p-norm for finite-dimensional vector spaces. They are sometimes called Lebesgue spaces, after Henri Lebesgue, and are fundamental objects in functional analysis and measure theory.

Definition

For 1 ≤ p < ∞ and a measure space (S, μ), the space Lp consists of measurable functions f whose absolute value raised to the p-th power has finite integral, equipped with the norm ‖f‖p = (∫|f|p dμ)1/p. Strictly, functions equal almost everywhere are identified. The space L∞ consists of essentially bounded functions with the essential supremum norm. For p ≥ 1 these norms satisfy the triangle inequality (Minkowski's inequality), making Lp a Banach space; L2 is additionally a Hilbert space.

Key properties

The Hölder inequality controls products of functions in dual spaces: if 1/p + 1/q = 1, then fg is integrable whenever f ∈ Lp and g ∈ Lq. Under mild conditions the dual of Lp (for 1 ≤ p < ∞) is Lq, which makes the spaces central to the theory of weak convergence and partial differential equations. For 1 < p < ∞ the spaces are reflexive, and L2 theory underlies Fourier analysis and quantum mechanics, where wave functions are (square-integrable) elements of L2.

Sequence spaces

The same construction with counting measure gives the sequence spaces ℓp of sequences whose p-th powers are summable; ℓ2 is the standard infinite-dimensional Hilbert space. On finite measure spaces, higher p gives smaller spaces: Lq ⊂ Lp for q > p.

Applications

Lp spaces are used in probability (moments of random variables), statistics (least squares and L1 regression), signal processing, ergodic theory and the modern theory of partial differential equations, where weak solutions are typically sought in Sobolev spaces built on Lp.
