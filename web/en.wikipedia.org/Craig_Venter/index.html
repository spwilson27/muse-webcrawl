John Craig Venter (born October 14, 1946) is an American biotechnologist and businessman. He is known for leading one of the first draft sequences of the human genome and for assembling the first cell with a synthetic genome. Venter founded Celera Genomics, The Institute for Genomic Research (TIGR) and the J. Craig Venter Institute (JCVI).

Early life and education

Venter was born in Salt Lake City, Utah, and served as a hospital corpsman in the United States Navy during the Vietnam War; the experience influenced his decision to enter medicine and science. He earned his bachelor's degree in biochemistry and his PhD in physiology and pharmacology from the University of California, San Diego.

Career

At the National Institutes of Health, Venter developed the expressed sequence tag (EST) method for rapidly identifying genes. In 1992 he founded TIGR, which in 1995 sequenced the first complete genome of a free-living organism, Haemophilus influenzae, using whole-genome shotgun sequencing.

In 1998 Venter co-founded Celera Genomics and announced it would sequence the human genome faster and more cheaply than the public Human Genome Project; the two efforts announced draft sequences jointly in 2000–2001. Venter was listed as first author on Celera's 2001 Science paper.

Synthetic biology

In 2010, Venter's team announced the creation of a bacterial cell controlled by a chemically synthesised genome, Mycoplasma mycoides JCVI-syn1.0, widely described as the first “synthetic life”. In 2016 his team created JCVI-syn3.0, a minimal bacterial genome with 473 genes. His Global Ocean Sampling Expedition (2003–2010) applied shotgun sequencing to marine microbial diversity. Venter is also a co-founder of Human Longevity, Inc.
