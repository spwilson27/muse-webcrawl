Public house

A public house, more usually known as a pub, is a drinking establishment licensed to serve alcoholic drinks for consumption on the premises. The pub is a social institution central to British and Irish culture, and variants are found in Australia, New Zealand, Canada, and elsewhere.

History

Pubs descend from Roman taverns and Anglo-Saxon alehouses; the term "public house" emerged in the late 17th century to distinguish premises open to the public from private houses. The Beerhouse Act 1830 liberalized licensing and multiplied beerhouses across England. Many British pubs are "tied houses" owned by breweries or pub companies, while others are free houses.

Features

Traditional features include a bar counter, handpumps for cask ale, a public bar and a more comfortable saloon or lounge, pub games such as darts, and distinctive hanging signs — once essential for identification in a largely illiterate society. The person in charge is the landlord or landlady (publican). Pub numbers in the UK have declined for decades under pressure from changing drinking habits, taxation, and property values.
