Baggage carousel

A baggage carousel, also known as a baggage reclaim belt or baggage claim device, is a device in an airport that delivers checked luggage to passengers in the arrivals hall after a flight. It consists of a rotating conveyor, typically a flat or sloped loop of linked plates or belts, onto which ground handling staff load bags from within the baggage area.

Operation

Passengers consult information screens to find the carousel assigned to their flight and wait beside it as bags emerge, usually through a one-way opening from the secure baggage-handling area. Carousels may be shared between flights at smaller airports or dedicated at larger hubs. Variants include flat-bed belts, inclined carousels on which bags slide down toward passengers, and multi-level devices.

Design considerations

Designers must balance carousel length and frontage against passenger numbers so that travellers can see and reach their luggage. Unclaimed bags are removed by airline staff. The carousel forms part of the larger baggage handling system of sorters, conveyors and scanners that moves luggage from check-in to the aircraft and back.
