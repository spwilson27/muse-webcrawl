Buddhist art

Buddhist art is the visual art associated with Buddhism, originating on the Indian subcontinent in the centuries after the death of the Buddha (c. 5th–4th century BCE) and spreading across Asia along the Silk Road.

Early Buddhist art was aniconic, representing the Buddha by symbols such as the footprint, the empty throne, the Bodhi tree, and the dharma wheel. Anthropomorphic images emerged around the 1st century CE in Gandhara (influenced by Greco-Roman styles) and Mathura.

Regional traditions produced the cave temples of Ajanta, the colossal Buddhas of Bamiyan (destroyed in 2001), Borobudur in Java, Khmer and Thai sculpture, Tibetan thangka painting, and the Zen-influenced arts of Japan. Common iconography includes mudras (hand gestures), the ushnisha, elongated earlobes, and mandalas.
