Meridian (Chinese medicine)

The meridian system (simplified Chinese: 经络; traditional Chinese: 經絡; pinyin: jīngluò; also called channel network) is a concept from traditional Chinese medicine (TCM). Meridians are paths through which the life-energy known as "qi" is said to flow. They are not anatomical structures, and scientists have found no histological or physiological evidence supporting their existence as distinct channels.

The channels

The network consists of twelve principal meridians, each associated with an organ system (Lung, Large Intestine, Stomach, Spleen, Heart, Small Intestine, Bladder, Kidney, Pericardium, Triple Burner, Gallbladder and Liver), plus eight extraordinary vessels, of which the Conception Vessel (Ren Mai) and Governing Vessel (Du Mai) have their own acupuncture points. Acupuncture points are located along the meridians, and stimulation by needles, pressure, or heat is held to affect the flow of qi.

History

Descriptions of channels appear in the Mawangdui Silk Texts (second century BCE) and in the Huangdi Neijing (Yellow Emperor's Inner Canon), the foundational text of Chinese medicine. Over the following centuries the system was elaborated into a complex network, with diverging views between schools and countries (China, Japan, Korea, Vietnam).

Scientific view

The meridian system is regarded as a historical and cultural model rather than an anatomical one. Studies of acupuncture outcomes do not generally find effects specific to traditional point locations, and no structure corresponding to meridians has been identified.
