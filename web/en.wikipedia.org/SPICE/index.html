SPICE

SPICE (Simulation Program with Integrated Circuit Emphasis) is a general-purpose, open-source analog electronic circuit simulator. It is a program used in integrated circuit and board-level design to check the integrity of circuit designs and to predict circuit behavior.

History

SPICE was developed at the Electronics Research Laboratory of the University of California, Berkeley by Laurence Nagel with direction from his research advisor, Donald Pederson. It was first presented in 1973 and released into the public domain. SPICE2 (1975) became the most widely adopted version before SPICE3 arrived in 1989. Commercial derivatives such as HSPICE and PSpice, and open-source successors such as Ngspice, extended its capabilities.

Analysis types

SPICE can perform several analyses, including DC operating point analysis, DC transfer curve analysis, AC small-signal frequency response, transient time-domain simulation, noise analysis, and sensitivity analysis. Circuits are described as netlists of nodes connected by components such as resistors, capacitors, inductors, independent and dependent sources, and transistor models.

Device models

The simulator includes built-in models for semiconductor devices: diodes, bipolar junction transistors, junction and metal-oxide-semiconductor field-effect transistors (with levels of increasing sophistication such as BSIM models), and others. Because of its ubiquity, "SPICE model" has become the standard way for manufacturers to distribute simulation parameters for their components.
