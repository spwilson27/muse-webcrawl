Sumidero Canyon

Sumidero Canyon (Spanish: Cañón del Sumidero) is a deep natural canyon located just north of the city of Tuxtla Gutiérrez in the state of Chiapas, in southern Mexico. The canyon, carved by the Grijalva River, has vertical walls up to about 1,000 metres high.

Geology and formation

The canyon began to form around 35 million years ago through faults and erosion by the Grijalva River. It is contemporaneous in formation with the Grand Canyon in the United States. The river's course through the canyon was altered by the Chicoasén Dam, one of Mexico's largest hydroelectric projects, completed in 1981.

National park

Sumidero Canyon National Park, established in 1980, covers some 21,789 hectares. Boat tours from Chiapa de Corzo traverse the river, passing features such as the Cave of Colours and the "Christmas tree" formation of moss-covered rock. Wildlife includes American crocodiles, spider monkeys, and numerous bird species. The canyon's image appears in the coat of arms of Chiapas.

History

According to legend, during the Spanish conquest local Chiapa people, rather than submit, threw themselves from the canyon cliffs — an episode commemorated in local tradition, though historians debate its details.
