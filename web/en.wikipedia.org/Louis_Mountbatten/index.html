Louis Mountbatten

Louis Francis Albert Victor Nicholas Mountbatten, 1st Earl Mountbatten of Burma (25 June 1900 – 27 August 1979), commonly known as Lord Louis Mountbatten, was a British naval officer, colonial administrator and close relative of the British royal family. A great-grandson of Queen Victoria, he was born Prince Louis of Battenberg and served in both world wars.

Career

Mountbatten rose to become Supreme Allied Commander, South East Asia Command during the Second World War, overseeing the recapture of Burma from Japanese forces. In 1947 he was appointed the last Viceroy of India, charged with overseeing British India's independence. He presided over the partition of India and Pakistan and served briefly as the first Governor-General of independent India.

He later became First Sea Lord and Chief of the Defence Staff, the professional head of the British Armed Forces.

Assassination

Mountbatten was assassinated on 27 August 1979 when the Provisional Irish Republican Army detonated a bomb aboard his fishing boat, Shadow V, off Mullaghmore, County Sligo, Ireland. He was 79.
