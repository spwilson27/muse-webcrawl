Daytona Prototype

A Daytona Prototype was a type of sports prototype racing car developed for the Grand-Am Road Racing's Rolex Sports Car Series, where it served as the top class from its debut in 2003 until 2016. Named after Daytona International Speedway, home of the series' flagship 24 Hours of Daytona, the cars were designed to be cheaper and closer to production technology than the Le Mans Prototypes used in rival series.

Design

Daytona Prototypes used a mid-engined layout with a steel tube-frame chassis covered by bodywork produced by approved constructors such as Riley Technologies, Dallara, Coyote and Lola. Engines were based on production units from manufacturers including Ford, BMW, Porsche, Lexus and Chevrolet, typically limited to around 500 horsepower. Rules deliberately constrained aerodynamics and technology to control costs and keep competition close.

Generations and successors

Three generations of cars raced: the original designs of 2003, a refined second generation from 2008, and the sleeker, more aerodynamic third generation introduced in 2012, exemplified by the Chevrolet Corvette DP. When Grand-Am merged with the American Le Mans Series to form the IMSA SportsCar Championship in 2014, Daytona Prototypes remained the top class until they were replaced in 2017 by the international Daytona Prototype International (DPi) formula.
