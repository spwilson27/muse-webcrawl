Culture of India

The culture of India refers to the way of life of the people of India, one of the world's oldest and most diverse civilisations. It encompasses the country's languages, religions, dance, music, architecture, food and customs, which vary widely across its regions while sharing deep historical roots in the Indian subcontinent.

Religion and festivals

India is the birthplace of Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism and Sikhism, and home to large populations of Muslims, Christians and others. Major festivals include Diwali, Holi, Eid, Dussehra, Durga Puja, Onam, Pongal, Christmas and Guru Nanak Jayanti. Ritual, pilgrimage and the joint family have traditionally played important social roles, and values such as dharma, karma and respect for elders remain influential.

Language and literature

India has hundreds of languages; the constitution recognises 22 scheduled languages, with Hindi and English used widely for official purposes. Its literary heritage extends from the Sanskrit Vedas, the epics Mahabharata and Ramayana, and classical Tamil Sangam poetry to modern writers such as Rabindranath Tagore, the first non-European Nobel laureate in Literature.

Arts, music and cinema

Classical dance forms include Bharatanatyam, Kathak, Kathakali, Odissi and Manipuri; classical music divides into Hindustani (northern) and Carnatic (southern) traditions. Indian cinema, centred on Hindi-language Bollywood alongside large regional industries, is among the world's most prolific. Yoga, originating in ancient India, is practised worldwide.
