Hindustani (हिन्दुस्तानी / ہندوستانی) is an Indo-Aryan language spoken predominantly in northern India and Pakistan. It is the underlying vernacular of which Standard Hindi and Standard Urdu are the two formal registers: colloquially they are largely mutually intelligible, differing mainly in script — Devanagari for Hindi, Perso-Arabic Nastaliq for Urdu — and in formal vocabulary, Hindi drawing on Sanskrit and Urdu on Persian and Arabic.

History

Hindustani developed in and around Delhi from the Khariboli dialect of Western Hindi, enriched by contact with Persian, Arabic and Turkic during the Delhi Sultanate and Mughal periods. It spread across the subcontinent as a lingua franca of soldiers, traders and Sufi preachers, and became the language of a rich literary culture, of the freedom movement — Mahatma Gandhi championed "Hindustani" as a unifying national language — and today of Bollywood film and song.

Usage

Hindi is an official language of India and Urdu the national language of Pakistan, while spoken Hindustani in its bazaar form serves as a lingua franca across much of South Asia and in a large global diaspora. With hundreds of millions of speakers it is among the most spoken languages in the world.
