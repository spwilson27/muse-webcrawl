Philippine Declaration of Independence

The Philippine Declaration of Independence (Filipino: Pagpapahayag ng Kasarinlan ng Pilipinas) was proclaimed on June 12, 1898, in Cavite el Viejo (present-day Kawit, Cavite), Philippines. It asserted the sovereignty and independence of the Philippines from the colonial rule of Spain, following the Spanish defeat at the Battle of Manila Bay during the Spanish–American War.

Proclamation

The declaration was drafted by Ambrosio Rianzares Bautista, who also read it during the ceremony. General Emilio Aguinaldo proclaimed independence from the balcony of his ancestral home. The event featured the first public performance of the national anthem, Lupang Hinirang (composed by Julián Felipe), and the unveiling of the Philippine flag, sewn by Marcela Agoncillo and her companions in Hong Kong.

Recognition and aftermath

The declaration was ratified on August 1, 1898, by municipal presidents, and again on September 29, 1898, by the Malolos Congress. However, neither Spain nor the United States recognized Philippine independence; Spain ceded the islands to the U.S. under the Treaty of Paris (1898), leading to the Philippine–American War. June 12 is now celebrated annually as Independence Day in the Philippines, a status formalized in 1962.
