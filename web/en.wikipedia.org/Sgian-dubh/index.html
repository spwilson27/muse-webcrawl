Sgian-dubh

A sgian-dubh worn in the top of the kilt hose.

The sgian-dubh (pronounced roughly "skee-an doo"; Scottish Gaelic for "black knife") is a small single-edged knife worn as part of traditional Scottish Highland dress. It is carried tucked into the top of the kilt hose (sock), usually on the side of the dominant hand, with only the hilt visible.

Origins

The sgian-dubh is thought to descend from the sgian achlais, a dagger carried concealed under the arm, and from the small utility knives and eating implements carried in the Highlands. One popular account holds that etiquette required a guest's concealed weapon to be placed visibly in the hose top when visiting friends, converting a hidden blade into an openly worn one; the "dubh" (black) in the name may refer to dark handles of bog oak or horn, or to its once-hidden nature.

Form and modern use

Traditional examples have a short blade of about 8–10 centimetres, often with filework along the spine, and a carved handle of wood, horn or antler, sometimes set with a cairngorm stone. Today the sgian-dubh is primarily ceremonial, worn with Highland dress at weddings and formal events, and many modern versions are blunt or entirely dummy replicas. Because it is a knife, its carriage is subject to modern weapons law; in Scotland, wearing one as part of national dress is a recognised defence to carrying a bladed article in public, though restrictions at schools, airports and secured venues still lead many wearers to choose plastic replicas.
