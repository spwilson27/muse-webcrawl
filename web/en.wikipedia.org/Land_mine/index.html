Land mine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A land mine is an explosive device concealed at or just below the surface of the ground, designed to destroy or disable enemy targets — ranging from combatants to vehicles and tanks — as they pass over or near it. Land mines are typically victim-activated, detonating automatically by pressure, tripwire, or proximity rather than by command.

Types

The two broad categories are anti-personnel mines, intended to injure or kill people, and anti-tank (anti-vehicle) mines, which require heavier pressure and damage vehicles. Mines may kill by blast or by fragmentation. Land mines were used extensively in the First and Second World Wars and in conflicts throughout the twentieth century.

Humanitarian impact and clearance

Mines remain lethal long after conflicts end, killing and maiming civilians and rendering land unusable. The 1997 Ottawa Treaty (Mine Ban Treaty) prohibits anti-personnel mines for its states parties, which number more than 160. Demining — by manual prodding, metal detectors, dogs, and machines — is slow and dangerous, and contaminated regions such as parts of Cambodia, Angola, Afghanistan, and Ukraine remain hazardous decades after emplacement.
