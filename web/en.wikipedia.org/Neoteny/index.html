Neoteny

Neoteny is the retention of juvenile traits in the adult form of an organism, resulting from a slowing or delay of somatic development relative to sexual maturity. It is a form of paedomorphosis and a mechanism of heterochrony in evolutionary developmental biology.

Examples

The axolotl, a Mexican salamander, famously retains its larval gills and aquatic lifestyle throughout life, breeding in the larval form. Many other salamanders, and flightless birds such as the kiwi in some respects, show neotenic features.

Humans

Humans have been described as neotenous apes: adult humans retain juvenile features of other primates, including a large head relative to body size, a flattened face, and prolonged childhood dependency and playfulness. Stephen Jay Gould discussed human neoteny extensively in Ontogeny and Phylogeny (1977).

Mechanisms

Neoteny can arise through changes in the timing of developmental gene expression, often under hormonal control, as in the thyroid-dependent metamorphosis of amphibians.
