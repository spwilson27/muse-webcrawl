Sauvignon blanc

Sauvignon blanc is a green-skinned grape variety originating in the Bordeaux region of France, now one of the world's most widely planted white wine grapes. Its name is often linked to the French sauvage ("wild") and blanc ("white"). DNA profiling has shown it to be a parent, with Cabernet Franc, of Cabernet Sauvignon.

Regions and styles

In the Loire Valley, Sauvignon blanc produces the steely, mineral wines of Sancerre and Pouilly-Fumé. In Bordeaux it is blended with Sémillon for dry whites and the sweet wines of Sauternes. New Zealand's Marlborough region made the variety famous from the 1980s with intensely aromatic wines showing passionfruit, grapefruit and cut-grass notes, attributed to volatile thiols and methoxypyrazines. It is also widely grown in Chile, South Africa, Australia and California, where oak-aged versions are sold as Fumé Blanc.

Viticulture

The vine is vigorous and late-budding but relatively early-ripening, suited to cool climates where it retains acidity. It is susceptible to powdery mildew and botrytis, though noble rot is welcomed in sweet-wine production.
