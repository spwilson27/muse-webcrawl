JA Ranch

The JA Ranch is a historic cattle ranch in the Palo Duro Canyon area of the Texas Panhandle. Founded in 1876 by Charles Goodnight and financed by the English aristocrat John George Adair — whose initials gave the ranch its name — it was the first ranch established in the Panhandle.

History

Goodnight, a former Texas Ranger and trail driver famous for the Goodnight–Loving Trail, selected the Palo Duro Canyon for its shelter, water, and grass, driving in a herd in the winter of 1876–77. Under the Goodnight–Adair partnership the ranch grew to more than 1.3 million acres at its peak. After Adair's death, his widow Cornelia Wadsworth Ritchie Adair became a famous absentee owner, and the partnership was dissolved in 1887, with Goodnight taking a smaller share that became his own ranch.

Later years

The JA continued under Adair's heirs and later owners, shrinking over time through sales and division. It played a notable role in the preservation of the American bison: Goodnight maintained a buffalo herd on the ranch, one of the five foundation herds from which modern bison descend. The ranch remains a working cattle operation in Armstrong and surrounding counties.
