Simple fruit

A simple fruit is a fruit that develops from a single ovary of a single flower — from one pistil, whether simple (one carpel) or compound (several fused carpels). Most familiar fruits are simple fruits, in contrast to aggregate fruits, which form from many pistils of one flower, and multiple fruits, which form from a whole inflorescence.

Fleshy simple fruits

Berries, such as grapes and tomatoes, have a fleshy pericarp throughout. Drupes, such as peaches and cherries, have a stony inner layer enclosing the seed. Pomes, such as apples and pears, develop largely from the floral tube surrounding the ovary, while the hesperidium of citrus fruits has a leathery, oil-glanded rind.

Dry simple fruits

Dry fruits may split open at maturity (dehiscent) — like the legume pod of peas and the capsules of poppies — or remain closed (indehiscent), as in nuts, the caryopsis grains of grasses, and winged samaras. The classification is a standard part of plant morphology and agriculture.
