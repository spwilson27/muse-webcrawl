Candle in the Wind 1997

"Candle in the Wind 1997", also known as "Goodbye England's Rose", is a reworked version of Elton John's 1973 song "Candle in the Wind," originally written in memory of Marilyn Monroe. With new lyrics by Bernie Taupin honouring Diana, Princess of Wales, John performed it at her funeral in Westminster Abbey on 6 September 1997. Released as a single days later, it became the best-selling single of all time since charts began, with sales of over 33 million copies worldwide.

Background

Diana, Princess of Wales died in a car crash in Paris on 31 August 1997. John, a close friend, was asked to perform at her funeral; Taupin rewrote the opening line "Goodbye Norma Jean" as "Goodbye England's rose." Producer George Martin recorded the performance-style single with strings and choir at Abbey Road–affiliated studios in the days that followed.

Release and sales

The double A-side single with "Something About the Way You Look Tonight" sold 658,000 copies in Britain on its first day and 1.5 million in its first week, eventually exceeding 4.9 million UK sales — the country's best-selling single ever. It topped charts in more than 20 countries, spent 14 weeks at number one in the United States, and won the Grammy Award for Best Male Pop Vocal Performance. All artist and label royalties were donated to Diana's charities, raising tens of millions of pounds.

Legacy

John has vowed never to perform the 1997 version in concert again except at the request of Diana's sons, and has performed it only rarely since. The song became inseparable from the public mourning of 1997 and remains the benchmark against which charity singles are measured.
