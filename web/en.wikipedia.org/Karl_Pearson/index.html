Karl Pearson (27 March 1857 – 27 April 1936) was an English biostatistician and mathematician. He is widely regarded as a founder of modern mathematical statistics, having established the discipline at University College London, where he founded the world's first university statistics department in 1911.

Career

Pearson studied mathematics at King's College, Cambridge, and later law, history, and philosophy in Germany. In 1884 he became professor of applied mathematics and mechanics at University College London. Influenced by Francis Galton's work on heredity, he developed the statistical methods of biometrics, founding the journal Biometrika in 1901 with Galton and Walter Weldon.

Contributions

Pearson introduced the Pearson correlation coefficient, the chi-squared test, the concept of the p-value, standard deviation (and its notation σ), the histogram, and the method of moments, as well as the Pearson distribution family. His book The Grammar of Science (1892) influenced the young Albert Einstein and the development of logical positivism.

Eugenics

Pearson was a committed eugenicist and Galton's protégé and biographer; he held the Galton Professorship of Eugenics. His advocacy of eugenics, then a mainstream scientific position, is now widely condemned, and institutions have renamed lectures and buildings honouring him.
