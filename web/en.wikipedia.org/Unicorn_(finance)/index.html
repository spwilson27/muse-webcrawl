Unicorn (finance)

In business and venture capital, a unicorn is a privately held startup company valued at over US$1 billion. The term was coined in 2013 by venture capitalist Aileen Lee, who chose the mythical animal to represent the statistical rarity of such successful ventures. Companies valued at over US$10 billion are sometimes called decacorns, and those over US$100 billion hectocorns.

History

When Lee coined the term she identified only 39 US-based software startups founded since 2003 that qualified. The population then expanded dramatically with cheap capital, large late-stage funding rounds and mutual funds and sovereign wealth funds entering venture markets; by the early 2020s trackers such as CB Insights counted more than 1,000 unicorns worldwide, with aggregate value of several trillion dollars. The 2021 venture boom produced hundreds of new unicorns in a single year before a correction in 2022–2023 slowed formation and forced down-rounds and write-downs.

Characteristics and criticism

Unicorns are concentrated in sectors such as software, fintech, e-commerce, artificial intelligence and biotechnology, and geographically in the United States, China, India and Europe. Critics note that private valuations are set in negotiated funding rounds and may exceed what public markets would pay — a phenomenon mocked by the label "unicorpse" for companies whose value later collapsed. Others question the focus on valuation rather than revenue or profit. Nonetheless the term has become standard in business journalism and policy debates about innovation ecosystems.
