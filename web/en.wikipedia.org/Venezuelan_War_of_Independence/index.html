Venezuelan War of Independence

Date1810–1823
LocationVenezuela and New Granada
ResultPatriot victory; independence from Spain
Key figuresSimón Bolívar, José Tomás Boves, Pablo Morillo

The Venezuelan War of Independence (1810–1823) was one of the Spanish American wars of independence, fought to free Venezuela from Spanish colonial rule. It began with the establishment of a junta in Caracas on 19 April 1810 and ended after the patriot victory at the naval Battle of Lake Maracaibo in 1823.

First and Second Republics

Venezuela declared independence on 5 July 1811, the first Spanish American colony to do so, but the First Republic collapsed in 1812 under royalist counterattack and a devastating earthquake. Simón Bolívar's Admirable Campaign of 1813 briefly restored the republic, which fell again in 1814 to the llanero armies of José Tomás Boves.

Bolívar and liberation

From exile, Bolívar issued the Jamaica Letter (1815) and rebuilt his forces with the support of Haiti and British volunteers. In 1819 he crossed the Andes and won the Battle of Boyacá, liberating New Granada. His decisive victory at Carabobo on 24 June 1821 secured Venezuela, and the remaining royalist strongholds fell by 1823.

Aftermath

Venezuela joined the Republic of Gran Colombia until 1830, when it became a fully independent state under José Antonio Páez. The war devastated the population and economy but established Venezuela as one of the first independent nations of South America.
