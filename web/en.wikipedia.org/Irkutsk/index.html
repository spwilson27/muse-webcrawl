Irkutsk is the largest city and administrative centre of Irkutsk Oblast in south-eastern Siberia, Russia. Located about 850 kilometres south-east of Krasnoyarsk and some 520 kilometres north of Ulaanbaatar, the city sits on the Angara River at its confluence with the Irkut River, from which it takes its name. It is one of the largest cities in Siberia and a major stop on the Trans-Siberian Railway.

History

Irkutsk began in 1652 as a winter camp and trading post established by Ivan Pokhabov on the banks of the Angara. A wooden fort (ostrog) was built in 1661, and the settlement received town status in 1686. Because of its position on the routes to Yakutsk, Chita, Mongolia and China, Irkutsk grew into a key centre of the Siberian fur trade and of commerce with China. Political exiles, including many Decembrists after the failed revolt of 1825, contributed to the city's cultural life, and their houses survive as museums.

In July 1879 a great fire destroyed much of the wooden city; it was rebuilt largely in stone. During the Russian Civil War the city changed hands several times, and the White leader Admiral Alexander Kolchak was executed in Irkutsk in February 1920.

Geography and climate

Irkutsk lies in the rolling hills of the Eastern Sayan foothills, at an elevation of about 440 metres. It experiences a subarctic climate with very cold winters — January averages around −18 °C — and warm summers, with July averaging about 19 °C. Lake Baikal, roughly 70 kilometres to the south-east, moderates the local climate somewhat.

Economy and transport

Major industries include aircraft manufacturing (the Irkutsk Aviation Plant, known for Sukhoi and MC-21 aircraft), aluminium, and hydroelectricity from the Irkutsk Dam on the Angara. The city is served by the Trans-Siberian Railway, federal highways, and Irkutsk International Airport. It is also the principal gateway for visitors to Lake Baikal.

Culture

Irkutsk is known for its ornate wooden architecture, its many Orthodox churches — including the Church of the Saviour and the Epiphany Cathedral — and institutions such as the Irkutsk State University (founded 1918) and the Decembrist museums.
