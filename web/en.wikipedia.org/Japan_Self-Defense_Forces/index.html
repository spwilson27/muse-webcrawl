Japan Self-Defense Forces

Japan Self-Defense Forces (JSDF)
Japanese: 自衛隊, Jieitai
Founded: 1 July 1954
Branches: Ground, Maritime, Air Self-Defense Forces
Headquarters: Ministry of Defense, Ichigaya, Tokyo
Commander-in-chief: Prime Minister of Japan
Active personnel: ~247,000

The Japan Self-Defense Forces (JSDF, Japanese: 自衛隊, romanized: Jieitai) are the unified military forces of Japan. Established in 1954, they comprise the Japan Ground Self-Defense Force (JGSDF), the Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force (JMSDF), and the Japan Air Self-Defense Force (JASDF). The JSDF are controlled by the Ministry of Defense, with the Prime Minister of Japan as commander-in-chief.

Legal basis

Article 9 of the postwar Constitution of Japan renounces war as a sovereign right and prohibits the maintenance of "land, sea, and air forces, as well as other war potential." The government has interpreted this to permit forces maintained strictly for self-defense. In 2015, legislation enabled a limited right of collective self-defense, allowing the JSDF to assist allies under attack under certain conditions.

History

After Japan's defeat in World War II, its armed forces were disbanded. During the Korean War, the United States authorized the creation of a National Police Reserve in 1950, which evolved into the National Safety Force and, in 1954, the Self-Defense Forces under the Self-Defense Forces Law. Since then, the JSDF have participated in disaster relief and in international peacekeeping operations, including in Cambodia, Iraq (non-combat roles), and South Sudan.

Missions

  • Defense of Japan against external aggression
  • Disaster relief following earthquakes, typhoons, and other natural disasters
  • International peacekeeping and humanitarian assistance
  • Ballistic missile defense, and maritime security including anti-piracy operations
