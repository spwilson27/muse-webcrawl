Temperature record of the last 2,000 years

The temperature record of the last 2,000 years is reconstructed from climate proxies such as tree rings, ice cores, corals, and lake sediments, supplemented by the instrumental temperature record of the last roughly 170 years. These reconstructions describe how global and regional temperatures varied during the Common Era.

Key features

Reconstructions generally show a long, slow cooling trend over the first 1,800 years, followed by rapid warming in the 19th and 20th centuries. Periods often discussed include the Medieval Climate Anomaly (roughly 950–1250 CE), a period of relative warmth in some regions, and the Little Ice Age (roughly 1300–1850), a period of relative coolness.

Hockey stick graph

The 1998 reconstruction by Mann, Bradley, and Hughes produced a graph resembling a hockey stick, with a long gradual decline (the shaft) and a sharp 20th-century upturn (the blade). Subsequent studies using different methods and data have broadly confirmed the conclusion that recent decades are the warmest in at least the last millennium.
