BCG vaccine

BCG vaccine
Full name: Bacillus Calmette–Guérin vaccine
Target: Tuberculosis
First used: 1921
Type: Live attenuated vaccine

The BCG vaccine, or Bacillus Calmette–Guérin vaccine, is a vaccine primarily used against tuberculosis (TB). It is named after its inventors Albert Calmette and Camille Guérin. In countries where tuberculosis is common, one dose is recommended in healthy babies as close to the time of birth as possible.

Development

Calmette and Guérin, working at the Pasteur Institute in Lille, attenuated a strain of Mycobacterium bovis by repeated subculture over more than a decade; the vaccine was first used in humans in 1921. BCG is the most widely administered vaccine in history, with billions of doses given, although its efficacy against adult pulmonary tuberculosis varies widely by region.

Effectiveness and uses

BCG reliably protects children against severe forms of TB, including tuberculous meningitis and miliary disease, but its protection against pulmonary TB in adults is inconsistent. The vaccine is also used as an immunotherapy for early-stage bladder cancer, instilled directly into the bladder, and it has protective effects against leprosy and Buruli ulcer. A characteristic scar often forms at the injection site.
