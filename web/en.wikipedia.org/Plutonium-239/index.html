Plutonium-239

General
Symbol: 239Pu
Mass number: 239
Protons: 94 · Neutrons: 145
Half-life: 24,110 years
Decay mode: Alpha decay to uranium-235

Plutonium-239 (239Pu or Pu-239) is an isotope of plutonium. It is the primary fissile isotope used for the production of nuclear weapons, although uranium-235 is also used for that purpose. Plutonium-239 is also one of the three main isotopes used as fuel in thermal spectrum nuclear reactors, along with uranium-235 and uranium-233.

History

Plutonium-239 was first produced and isolated in 1940–1941 by Glenn Seaborg and his team at Berkeley by bombarding uranium-238 with deuterons. During the Manhattan Project, large-scale production reactors were built at the Hanford Site in Washington, and plutonium-239 was used in the Trinity test device and in the "Fat Man" bomb dropped on Nagasaki in August 1945.

Production

Plutonium-239 is produced when a uranium-238 nucleus absorbs a neutron and undergoes two successive beta decays: U-238 → U-239 → neptunium-239 → Pu-239. It is made in breeder and production reactors and is present in spent reactor fuel.

Nuclear properties

With a half-life of 24,110 years, plutonium-239 decays by alpha emission to uranium-235. It has a bare-sphere critical mass of about 10 kilograms. Its high fission cross-section for thermal neutrons makes it suitable both for weapons and reactor fuel (often as mixed oxide, MOX).
