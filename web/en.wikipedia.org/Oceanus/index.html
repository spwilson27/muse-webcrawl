Oceanus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Oceanus (Ancient Greek: Ὠκεανός, Ōkeanós) was a Titan son of Uranus and Gaia in Greek mythology, the divine personification of the sea and of the great fresh-water river that the Greeks and Romans believed encircled the world. In Hesiod's Theogony, Oceanus and his sister-wife Tethys were the parents of the river gods and the Oceanids — three thousand sons and three thousand daughters.

Mythology

Oceanus did not join the other Titans in the war against Zeus, and in the Iliad he is honoured as the origin of the gods. The river Oceanus marked the boundary of the known world: beyond it lay the Isles of the Blessed, the land of the dead, and the garden of the Hesperides. On the shield of Achilles, Hephaestus depicted Oceanus running around its rim. In later geography, as knowledge expanded, "Ocean" came to denote the great outer sea — the origin of the modern word.

Iconography and legacy

In art Oceanus is shown as a mature, muscular, bearded man, often with bull's horns, holding an oar or rudder and pouring water from an urn; Roman mosaics frequently place him at the centre of marine scenes. His name survives in the modern oceans, and Oceanus Procellarum on the Moon is named after him. See also Tethys, Oceanids and Titans.
