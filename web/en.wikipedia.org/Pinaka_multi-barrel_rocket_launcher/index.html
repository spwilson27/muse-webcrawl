Pinaka multi-barrel rocket launcher

The Pinaka is a multiple rocket launcher system produced in India and developed by the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) for the Indian Army. The system is named after Pinaka, the bow of the Hindu god Shiva, and serves as the Indian Army's principal area-saturation artillery rocket system.

Pinaka
Type: Multiple launch rocket system
Origin: India
Designer: DRDO
Calibre: 214 mm, 12 barrels per launcher

Development and service

Development began in the 1980s as an indigenous replacement for the Indian Army's Russian BM-21 Grad systems; the Pinaka was first used in action during the Kargil War of 1999. A Pinaka battery comprises six launch vehicles, each with twelve rockets, supported by loader, replenishment and command vehicles. A complete salvo of 72 rockets can saturate a target area in about 44 seconds.

Variants

The Mk-I has a range of roughly 40 km, while the Mk-II and extended-range and guided variants reach about 75 km and beyond; guided Pinaka rockets with improved accuracy have been progressively introduced. The system is mounted on Tatra heavy trucks, giving it shoot-and-scoot mobility.

Users

Besides the Indian Army, Armenia became the first export customer for the Pinaka system in the 2020s.
