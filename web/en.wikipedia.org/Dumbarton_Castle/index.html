Dumbarton Castle

LocationDumbarton Rock, River Clyde, Scotland
OccupiedFrom at least the 5th century AD
OperatorHistoric Environment Scotland

Dumbarton Castle stands on Dumbarton Rock, a volcanic plug of basalt 240 feet high at the confluence of the River Leven and the River Clyde in Scotland. It has the longest recorded history of any stronghold in Scotland, having been a fortified site since at least the early medieval period.

Early history

The rock was the capital of the Brittonic Kingdom of Strathclyde, known as Alt Clut, "the rock of the Clyde". It was besieged and captured by Vikings in 870 after a four-month blockade, an event recorded in the Irish annals. The name Dumbarton derives from Gaelic Dùn Breatann, "fort of the Britons".

Later history

The medieval castle guarded the western approaches to the Scottish heartland and sheltered Mary, Queen of Scots as a child before her departure to France in 1548. Most surviving buildings date from the 18th century, when the rock was refortified with artillery batteries against Jacobite and later French threats. It remained a military garrison into the Second World War.

Today

The castle is in the care of Historic Environment Scotland and open to visitors, who climb 557 steps to the summit for views over the Clyde. The White Tower Crag and the Beak are the twin peaks of the rock.
