Ferran Adrià i Acosta (born 14 May 1962) is a Spanish chef widely regarded as one of the most influential cooks of the modern era. As head chef of elBulli, a restaurant in Roses, Catalonia, he pioneered techniques associated with molecular gastronomy and what he called "deconstructivist" cooking, transforming familiar dishes into unexpected forms, temperatures, and textures.

Early career

Adrià began washing dishes in a hotel in Castelldefels and learned cooking during his military service. In 1984 he joined the kitchen of elBulli, then a modest restaurant founded by Hans Schilling; within a few years Adrià became its head chef and, with Juli Soler, transformed it into an avant-garde laboratory of cuisine.

elBulli

Under Adrià, elBulli earned three Michelin stars and was named the world's best restaurant a record five times by Restaurant magazine's World's 50 Best list. Signature creations included culinary foams (made famous with a siphon), spherification of liquids into caviar-like pearls, hot gelatins, and deconstructed versions of classics such as the Spanish tortilla. The restaurant opened only about six months a year and received millions of reservation requests for roughly 8,000 seats per season. elBulli served its final meal on 30 July 2011 and closed as a restaurant.

After elBulli

Adrià converted elBulli into the elBullifoundation, dedicated to culinary research, documentation, and creativity, including the encyclopedic Bullipedia project. With his brother Albert Adrià he was also involved in Barcelona ventures such as Tickets. His brother's and his legacy reshaped fine dining worldwide, training a generation of chefs including René Redzepi and Massimo Bottura.

Recognition

Adrià has been described by peers and critics as the most inventive chef of his generation; he was named one of Time magazine's 100 most influential people and received honorary doctorates and numerous international awards.
