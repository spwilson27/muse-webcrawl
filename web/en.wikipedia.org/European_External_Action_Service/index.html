European External Action Service

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The European External Action Service (EEAS) is the diplomatic service in charge of executing all international relations of the European Union. It was established by the Treaty of Lisbon and formally launched on 1 December 2010. The EEAS is led by the High Representative of the Union for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy, who is also a Vice-President of the European Commission.

Functions

The EEAS supports the High Representative in conducting the EU's Common Foreign and Security Policy (CFSP) and Common Security and Defence Policy (CSDP). It manages the EU's network of more than 140 delegations and offices around the world, which represent the Union to third countries and international organisations. Its staff is drawn from the European Commission, the Council secretariat, and the diplomatic services of the member states.

History

The service was created to give the EU a more coherent external voice after the Lisbon Treaty abolished the rotating-presidency system in foreign affairs. Its first High Representative was Catherine Ashton of the United Kingdom, followed by Federica Mogherini (2014–2019) and Josep Borrell (2019–2024); Kaja Kallas took office in December 2024. The EEAS played a central role in the negotiations leading to the 2015 Iran nuclear deal (JCPOA) and in coordinating the EU's response to Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine from 2022.

Organisation

The EEAS headquarters is in Brussels, principally in the Triangle building near the Schuman roundabout. It comprises geographic directorates covering the world's regions, thematic departments (including human rights, security and defence, and crisis response), the EU Military Staff, and intelligence and situation-assessment bodies such as EU INTCEN and the Situation Room.
