HELLP syndrome

FieldObstetrics
AcronymHaemolysis, Elevated Liver enzymes, Low Platelets
Frequency~0.5–0.9% of pregnancies; 10–20% of severe pre-eclampsia cases
Named byLouis Weinstein (1982)
TreatmentStabilisation and delivery of the baby

HELLP syndrome is a serious complication of pregnancy characterised by haemolysis, elevated liver enzymes and low platelet count. It is generally considered a variant or progression of severe pre-eclampsia, although it can occur without preceding hypertension or proteinuria. Most cases arise in the third trimester, and about a third are diagnosed after delivery.

Signs and symptoms

Typical features include epigastric or right upper-quadrant pain (from liver swelling), nausea and vomiting, headache and visual disturbance, and generalised malaise. Signs may include hypertension, proteinuria and oedema. Complications include disseminated intravascular coagulation, placental abruption, liver haematoma or rupture, acute kidney injury and pulmonary oedema.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis rests on laboratory findings: haemolysis (schistocytes on blood film, raised LDH and bilirubin, low haptoglobin), liver transaminases raised to at least twice normal, and a platelet count below 100×10⁹/L (Tennessee criteria). The Mississippi classification grades severity by platelet count.

Management

The definitive treatment is delivery, balanced against gestational age. Management includes magnesium sulfate for seizure prophylaxis, antihypertensive therapy, corticosteroids in selected preterm cases, correction of coagulopathy and close monitoring in a high-dependency setting. With prompt treatment, maternal mortality is around 1%, though perinatal mortality is higher, driven mainly by prematurity.
