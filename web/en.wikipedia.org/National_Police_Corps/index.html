The National Police Corps (Dutch: Korps Nationale Politie) is the national police force of the Netherlands. Established on 1 January 2013, it replaced the previous system of 25 regional police forces and the national services agency (KLPD) with a single unified force under the Police Act 2012.

Organisation

The corps is headed by a Commissioner (korpschef) appointed by the Crown. It consists of ten regional units (eenheden) responsible for general policing, plus a single national unit handling specialised and cross-regional tasks such as motorway patrols, railway and aviation policing, serious organised crime investigation, and protection duties. Administrative support is provided by a Police Service Centre. The force employs roughly 65,000 staff, including about 50,000 sworn officers.

History and reform

The 2013 reorganisation — the largest restructuring of Dutch policing — aimed at efficiency and consistency, but evaluations criticised stretched local capacity and ICT problems, prompting subsequent adjustments strengthening neighbourhood policing and regional commanders' discretion.
