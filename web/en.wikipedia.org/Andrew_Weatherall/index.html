Andrew James Weatherall (6 April 1963 – 17 February 2020) was an English musician, DJ, songwriter, producer and remixer. A pivotal figure in the British acid house and electronic music scenes, he was best known for producing Primal Scream's landmark 1991 album Screamadelica, which fused rock and dance music and won the first Mercury Prize in 1992.

Early life and career

Weatherall was born in Windsor, Berkshire. After leaving school he worked a series of jobs, including in a record shop, while DJing at local clubs. He became a freelance music journalist under the pen name Audrey Witherspoon, writing for Record Mirror, and was a founding member of the Boy's Own fanzine, which documented the emerging acid house scene of the late 1980s. As a DJ he became associated with venues such as Shoom and the Future club nights, and with the Balearic beat style championed by Danny Rampling and others.

Production and remixing

Weatherall's remix work in the early 1990s — including reworks of "Loaded" and "Come Together" for Primal Scream, "Only Love Can Break Your Heart" for Saint Etienne, and productions for Happy Mondays and New Order — helped define the sound of the era. His production on Screamadelica (1991) transformed Primal Scream from an indie rock band into pioneers of the indie-dance crossover.

He formed the group The Sabres of Paradise, whose experimental dub-techno albums included Sabresonic (1993), and later Two Lone Swordsmen with Keith Tenniswood, releasing records on Warp Records. He also ran the labels Sabres of Paradise and Rotters Golf Club.

Later work and death

Weatherall remained a revered DJ for three decades, known for marathon sets spanning rockabilly, dub, techno and cosmic disco, and for residencies and guest spots including "A Love From Outer Space" with Sean Johnston. He released the solo album Convenanza in 2016. Weatherall died on 17 February 2020 at Whipps Cross Hospital, London, from a pulmonary embolism, aged 56. Tributes poured in from across the music world, and he is remembered as one of the most influential British DJs of his generation.
