LORAN

Type: Terrestrial radio navigation
Developed: 1940s, United States
Frequency (LORAN-C): 100 kHz

LORAN (long range navigation) is a terrestrial radio navigation system that determines position from the time differences between signals received from pairs of fixed transmitters. It was developed in the United States during World War II from the British GEE system.

LORAN-C

The most widely used version, LORAN-C, operated at 100 kHz and offered accuracy of about 0.25 nautical miles, with skywave ranges of thousands of miles. Chains of stations served the North Atlantic, North Pacific, and coastal waters of many nations, and the system was widely used by mariners and aviators.

Decline and eLORAN

The spread of GPS led the United States and Canada to shut down their LORAN-C transmissions in 2010, and most other countries followed. Interest in an enhanced version, eLORAN, has persisted as a backup to satellite navigation because its high-power low-frequency signals are difficult to jam.
