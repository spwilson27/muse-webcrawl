Cinema of Canada

The cinema of Canada comprises the film industry of Canada and the films made by Canadian filmmakers at home and abroad. Canada has a long filmmaking history but has always worked in the shadow of the much larger United States industry, with which it shares language (in English-speaking Canada) and much of its talent.

History

Film production in Canada began in the early 20th century with newsreels and sponsored documentaries, including the pioneering work of the Canadian Pacific Railway and later the National Film Board of Canada (NFB), founded in 1939. The NFB, and animators such as Norman McLaren, made Canada a world leader in documentary and animation. The Canadian Film Development Corporation (now Telefilm Canada) was established in 1967 to support feature production.

English and French industries

Canada effectively has two feature industries. Quebec's French-language cinema, with directors such as Denys Arcand (The Barbarian Invasions, winner of the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film) and Xavier Dolan, commands a loyal domestic audience. English-Canadian cinema is associated with filmmakers including David Cronenberg, Atom Egoyan, James Cameron and Denis Villeneuve, though many Canadian directors and actors work mainly in Hollywood. Toronto, Vancouver and Montreal are also major centres for American film and television production.
