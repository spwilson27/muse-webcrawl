Silicon dioxide

Silicon dioxide, also known as silica, is an oxide of silicon with the chemical formula SiO2, most commonly found in nature as quartz and in various living organisms. In many parts of the world, silica is the major constituent of sand. It is one of the most complex and abundant families of materials, existing as a compound of several minerals and as a synthetic product.

Structure

In most silicas, the silicon atom shows tetrahedral coordination, with four oxygen atoms surrounding a central silicon atom. In quartz, each oxygen is shared between two tetrahedra, giving an overall formula of SiO2. Silicon dioxide has several distinct crystalline forms (polymorphs), including quartz, tridymite, and cristobalite, as well as amorphous forms such as fused silica and silica gel. It melts at about 1,713 °C and is a good electrical insulator with great chemical stability.

Uses

Silicon dioxide is a primary ingredient in glassmaking: soda–lime glass is made by melting silica with sodium carbonate and calcium oxide. It is also used in the production of elemental silicon for the semiconductor industry, in optical fibers, as a food additive (anti-caking agent), in ceramics and cements, and as an adsorbent in chromatography. Thermal oxidation of silicon wafers grows a protective SiO2 layer essential to integrated circuits.

Health effects

Inhalation of fine crystalline silica dust over long periods can cause silicosis, a disabling lung disease historically affecting miners, sandblasters, and stonecutters. Amorphous silica is considerably less hazardous. Stringent occupational exposure limits apply to respirable crystalline silica in many countries.
