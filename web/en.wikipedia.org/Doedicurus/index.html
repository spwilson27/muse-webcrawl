Doedicurus

KingdomAnimalia
FamilyChlamyphoridae
SubfamilyGlyptodontinae
GenusDoedicurus
PeriodLate Pleistocene

Doedicurus is an extinct genus of glyptodont, a group of large, heavily armoured armadillo relatives, from the Pleistocene of South America. It is one of the largest glyptodonts known, reaching about 3.3–3.9 metres in length and weighing over a tonne. Its body was protected by a domed carapace of fused bony plates (osteoderms), and it is notable for its clubbed tail armed with spikes, an adaptation inferred to have been used for defence and in intraspecific combat.

Description

Doedicurus had a huge, roughly hemispherical shell covering its back, beneath which the head, limbs and tail protruded. The tail consisted of a long, stiff bony sheath terminating in a large club bearing conical spikes. The vertebrae of the tail were fused, allowing the club to be swung with considerable force. Like other glyptodonts, it was herbivorous, grazing on grasses in the pampas grasslands of Argentina and neighbouring regions.

Discovery and history

The first fossils of Doedicurus were described in the nineteenth century; Richard Owen erected the genus name in the 1840s. Doedicurus lived until near the end of the last Ice Age, around 10,000 years ago, and its bones and shells occur in Pleistocene deposits of Argentina, Uruguay and Brazil. Early human inhabitants of South America may have encountered it, though evidence of direct hunting is limited. Charles Darwin collected glyptodont remains during the voyage of HMS Beagle and noted their resemblance to modern armadillos, an observation that contributed to his thinking on evolution.

Classification

Doedicurus belongs to the glyptodonts (Glyptodontinae), now usually placed within the armadillo family Chlamyphoridae following molecular evidence that glyptodonts are closely related to modern dwarf armadillos. It is among the most derived and largest members of the group, alongside Glyptodon and Panochthus.
