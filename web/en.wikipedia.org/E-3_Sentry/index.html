Boeing E-3 Sentry

The Boeing E-3 Sentry is an American airborne early warning and control (AEW&C) aircraft developed by Boeing. E-3s are commonly known under the name of the system, the Airborne Warning and Control System (AWACS). Derived from the Boeing 707 airliner, it provides all-weather surveillance, command, control and communications, and is used by the United States Air Force, NATO, French Air and Space Force, Royal Saudi Air Force and Chilean Air Force.

Design

The E-3 is distinguished by the large, rotating radar dome (rotodome) mounted on two struts above the rear fuselage. The dome is 9.1 m (30 ft) in diameter and houses a Westinghouse (now Northrop Grumman) AN/APY-1 or AN/APY-2 passive electronically scanned array radar, which can detect and track airborne and maritime targets at ranges exceeding 400 km. The aircraft has a flight crew of four and a mission crew of 13 to 19 specialists.

Operational history

The first E-3 was delivered to the USAF in 1977. The fleet played a major role in Operation Desert Storm in 1991, directing coalition aircraft and logging thousands of operational hours. NATO operates a fleet of E-3s registered in Luxembourg and based at Geilenkirchen, Germany — historically the only military aircraft on the Luxembourg register. The type has been progressively modernized, with the USAF's E-3G Block 40/45 upgrade adding new mission computing and displays. The USAF plans to replace much of its fleet with the Boeing E-7 Wedgetail.
