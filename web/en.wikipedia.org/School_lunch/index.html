School lunch


A typical school lunch tray

A school lunch (or school dinner) is a meal provided to students at school during the school day, usually at midday. School meal programmes exist in many countries and are intended to support children's nutrition, health and learning, particularly for students from low-income families.

By country

In the United States, the National School Lunch Program, established by the National School Lunch Act of 1946, provides free or reduced-price meals to eligible children and serves tens of millions of students daily. In the United Kingdom, school meals were formalised in the early 20th century, and nutritional standards and free school meals for younger pupils have been recurring policy topics. Japan's kyūshoku system serves a common hot lunch prepared to dietary standards, often involving students in serving and cleanup. India operates one of the world's largest programmes, the Mid-Day Meal Scheme, covering over one hundred million children.

Nutrition and debate

School meals are a focus of public-health debate: reformers seek to improve nutritional quality, limit processed foods and sugary drinks, and reduce stigma around free meals, while programmes must balance cost, uptake and children's preferences. Many jurisdictions have adopted nutrient standards, farm-to-school sourcing and universal free-meal options.
