Film noir

Film noir (French: [film nwaʁ], lit. 'black film') is a cinematic term used to describe stylish Hollywood crime dramas, particularly those that emphasize cynical attitudes and motivations.

Characteristics

The term was coined by French critic Nino Frank in 1946. Noir films use low-key, high-contrast cinematography (chiaroscuro), voice-over narration, flashbacks, femmes fatales, hard-boiled detectives, and themes of fatalism, corruption and desire.

Classic period films

Canonical titles include The Maltese Falcon (1941), Double Indemnity (1944), The Big Sleep (1946), Out of the Past (1947), The Third Man (1949), Sunset Boulevard (1950) and Touch of Evil (1958).

Neo-noir

Later revivals are called neo-noir, including Chinatown (1974), Blade Runner (1982, "tech noir") and L.A. Confidential (1997).
