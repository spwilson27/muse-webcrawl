Sámi languages

The Sámi languages are a group of Uralic languages spoken by the Sámi people in Northern Europe, in areas of Norway, Sweden, Finland and Russia collectively known as Sápmi. They are traditionally classified into western and eastern groups. Depending on the classification criteria, there are around ten living Sámi languages, of which Northern Sámi is by far the most widely spoken, with roughly 20,000 speakers.

Classification

The western group includes Northern Sámi, Lule Sámi, Pite Sámi, Ume Sámi and Southern Sámi. The eastern group includes Inari Sámi, Skolt Sámi, Akkala Sámi (now extinct), Kildin Sámi and Ter Sámi. Most of the languages are seriously endangered.

Status and revitalisation

Sámi languages have official recognition in parts of Norway, Sweden and Finland, and language acts guarantee services such as education and public administration in Sámi in designated areas. Revitalisation efforts include language nests, broadcasting such as NRK Sápmi, and Sámi parliaments in the three Nordic countries.

Linguistic features

Like other Uralic languages, the Sámi languages are agglutinative, with rich case systems and consonant gradation. They are closely related to the Finnic languages, with which they may share a common ancestor in a hypothesised Finno-Samic branch.
