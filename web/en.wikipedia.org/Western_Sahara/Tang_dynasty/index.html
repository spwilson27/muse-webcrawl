Tang dynasty

Tang dynasty (唐)
Existed: 618–907 AD
Capital: Chang'an (Xi'an); Luoyang secondary
Founding emperor: Gaozu (Li Yuan)
Notable rulers: Taizong, Wu Zetian, Xuanzong
Population: up to about 80 million

The Tang dynasty (618–907) was an imperial dynasty of China that succeeded the Sui dynasty and preceded the Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms period. Founded by the Li family, it is generally regarded as a high point of Chinese civilisation: a cosmopolitan empire whose capital Chang'an was among the largest cities in the world, and whose poetry, painting and ceramics remain canonical.

History

Li Yuan (Emperor Gaozu) seized the throne in 618; his son Taizong consolidated the empire, which expanded into Central Asia, defeating the Eastern Turkic Khaganate in 630 and contending with the Göktürks' western successors, Tibet and the Umayyads along the Silk Road. Wu Zetian briefly interrupted the dynasty with her own Zhou (690–705), becoming China's only female emperor. The catastrophic An Lushan Rebellion (755–763) devastated the north and permanently weakened central authority; after decades of warlordism the dynasty fell in 907.

Culture

Tang culture was open to foreign influences — music, dress, religions including Buddhism, Nestorian Christianity and Islam — and produced the poets Li Bai, Du Fu and Bai Juyi, considered the summit of Chinese verse. Tributary diplomacy and the equal-field and tax systems shaped East Asia for centuries.
