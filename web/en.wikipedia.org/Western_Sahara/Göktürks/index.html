Göktürks

Göktürks ("Celestial Turks")
Ruling clan: Ashina
Khaganates: First (552–603), Eastern, Western, Second (682–744)
Sacred homeland: Ötüken, Mongolia
Key sources: Orkhon inscriptions, Chinese annals

The Göktürks, literally "Celestial Turks" (Old Turkic: Türük), were a nomadic confederation of Turkic peoples in Inner Asia led by the Ashina clan. Originally blacksmith vassals of the Rouran, they revolted in 552 and founded the First Turkic Khaganate, the first empire to bear the Turkic name.

History

After the khaganate split in 603 into eastern and western halves, the eastern branch was subjugated by Tang China in 630, but was restored in 682 by Ilterish Qaghan as the Second Turkic Khaganate, whose rulers Bilge Qaghan and the commander Tonyukuk raised the great runic inscriptions in the Orkhon valley. The second khaganate fell in 744 to the Uyghurs, Basmyls and Karluks. In the west, the Western Turkic Khaganate gave way to successor states including the Khazar Khaganate.

Language and writing

The Göktürks spoke Old Turkic and developed the Orkhon (Turkic runic) script, used in the eighth-century inscriptions of Kül Tigin, Bilge Qaghan and Tonyukuk — the earliest substantial records of any Turkic language.
