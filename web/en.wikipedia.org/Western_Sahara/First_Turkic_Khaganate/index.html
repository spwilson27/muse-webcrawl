First Turkic Khaganate

First Turkic Khaganate (Göktürk Khaganate)
Existed: 552–603 AD
Founders: Bumin Qaghan and Istämi
Capital area: Ötüken (Mongolia)
Extent: Manchuria to the Black Sea steppe
Splits into: Eastern and Western Turkic Khaganates

The First Turkic Khaganate, also called the Göktürk Khaganate, was a nomadic empire founded by the Ashina clan of the Göktürks in 552 AD. Under Bumin Qaghan and his brother Istämi, the Turks destroyed the Rouran Khaganate and rapidly built the first transcontinental steppe empire, stretching from Manchuria to the Aral Sea and, through alliances, to the Crimea.

History

The khaganate grew rich controlling the Silk Road trade between China and the West, and played Sui-dynasty China, Sasanian Persia and Byzantium against one another; a famous episode is the Sogdian-led embassy of Maniakh to Constantinople in 568 seeking alliance against Persia. Civil war and Chinese pressure divided the empire in 603 into the Eastern Turkic Khaganate and the Western Turkic Khaganate.

Legacy

The khaganate is the first polity known to have used the name "Turk" politically, and its successor the Second Turkic Khaganate left the Orkhon inscriptions, the oldest extensive texts in a Turkic language.
