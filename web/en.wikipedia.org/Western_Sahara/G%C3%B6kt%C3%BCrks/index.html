The Göktürks (Old Turkic: 𐱅𐰇𐰼𐰜, "Celestial Turks"; also Kök Turks or Türks) were a Turkic people of Inner Asia who, under the Ashina clan, founded the First Turkic Khaganate in 552 — the first empire to bear the name "Turk". Within a few years the khaganate stretched from Manchuria to the Black Sea steppe, controlling much of the Silk Road and displacing the Rouran as the dominant power of the steppe.

History

According to Chinese sources the Ashina were iron-workers subordinate to the Rouran Khaganate. In 552 Bumin revolted, defeated the Rouran, and took the title of Il Qaghan; his brother Istämi ruled the west as yabgu, campaigning with Sasanian Persia against the Hephthalites and confronting Byzantium over silk trade. After civil wars, the empire split in 603 into Eastern and Western khaganates. The Eastern khaganate was subjugated by Tang China in 630, but was restored in 682 by Ilterish Qaghan; the Second Turkic Khaganate lasted until 744, when it fell to the Uyghurs.

Culture and legacy

The Göktürks left the Orkhon inscriptions (8th century), the earliest surviving texts in a Turkic language, written in the Old Turkic runiform script and recounting the deeds of Bilge Qaghan and Kül Tigin. Their state organisation, titles (qaghan, yabgu, shad, tegin) and the ethnonym "Turk" were inherited by later steppe polities and survive today in the name of Turkey and the Turkic peoples of Eurasia.
