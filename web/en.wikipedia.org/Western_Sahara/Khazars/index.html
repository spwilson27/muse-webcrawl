Khazars

Khazars
State: Khazar Khaganate (c. 650–969)
Capital: Atil (lower Volga)
Territory: Pontic–Caspian steppe, Caucasus, Crimea
Religion: Tengrism, Judaism (ruling elite), Islam, Christianity
Destroyed by: Kievan Rus' under Sviatoslav I (965–969)

The Khazars were a semi-nomadic Turkic people who established a major commercial empire, the Khazar Khaganate, in the Pontic–Caspian steppe between the seventh and tenth centuries AD. Emerging from the Western Turkic Khaganate, they controlled the trade routes between Eastern Europe, the Caucasus and the Islamic world from their capital Atil at the mouth of the Volga.

History

Khazaria fought two major wars with the Arab Caliphate (642–652 and 722–737), halting Arab expansion north of the Caucasus, and maintained close, sometimes allied, relations with Byzantium. Its khagans extracted tribute from East Slavic tribes of the forest zone. In 965–969 Sviatoslav I of Kiev destroyed the Khazar fortresses and Atil, ending the khaganate.

Religion

Uniquely among steppe empires, the Khazar ruling elite adopted Judaism, probably in stages during the eighth and ninth centuries, while the population practised a mixture of Tengrism, Islam, Christianity and Judaism. The extent and later fate of Khazar Judaism, popularised by Arthur Koestler's The Thirteenth Tribe, remains debated.
