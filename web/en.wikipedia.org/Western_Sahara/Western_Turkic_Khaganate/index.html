Western Turkic Khaganate

Western Turkic Khaganate
Existed: c. 603–657 (fragmented thereafter)
Capital: Suyab; Navekat
Language: Old Turkic; Sogdian
Religion: Tengrism; Buddhism; others

The Western Turkic Khaganate (c. 603–657) was a Turkic khaganate formed after the division of the First Turkic Khaganate. It controlled much of Central Asia west of the Altai Mountains, from the steppes north of the Black Sea and Caucasus to the Tarim Basin, and dominated the northern routes of the Silk Road.

History

The Göktürk empire founded by Bumin Qaghan split into eastern and western halves after a civil war around 603. The western khagans ruled from Suyab in the Chu Valley (present-day Kyrgyzstan). Under Tong Yabghu Qaghan (618–628), the khaganate reached its greatest extent, campaigning with the Byzantine Empire against Sasanian Persia and controlling the trade of Sogdiana. The Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang visited Tong Yabghu's court in 630 and described its splendour.

Decline

After Tong Yabghu's death, factional struggles between the two great tribal confederations, the Duolu and Nushibi, weakened the state. Tang China exploited the division, and in 657 a Tang army under Su Dingfang defeated the khagan Ashina Helu at the Battle of Irtysh River, ending the khaganate; its territory was reorganised into Tang protectorates. Successor peoples in the region included the Türgesh and later the Karluk and Oghuz Turks, while the Khazar Khaganate succeeded the Western Turks on the Pontic steppe.
