Western Sahara is a disputed territory on the north-west coast of Africa, bordered by Morocco to the north, Algeria to the north-east, Mauritania to the east and south, and the Atlantic Ocean to the west. One of the most sparsely populated territories in the world, it consists mainly of desert lowlands. It is listed by the United Nations as a non-self-governing territory and is the subject of one of the world's longest-running territorial disputes.

History

Spain colonised the territory from 1884 as Spanish Sahara. In 1975, under the Madrid Accords, Spain withdrew and the territory was partitioned between Morocco and Mauritania, despite an International Court of Justice advisory opinion affirming the Sahrawi people's right to self-determination. The Polisario Front, representing the indigenous Sahrawi people, proclaimed the Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic (SADR) in 1976 and fought a guerrilla war against both occupiers. Mauritania withdrew in 1979, after which Morocco annexed the remainder. A UN-brokered ceasefire in 1991 promised a referendum on independence, which has never been held.

Present status

Morocco administers roughly the western 80 per cent of the territory, including all the coastal cities and the phosphate deposits at Bou Craa, behind a fortified sand wall (the "Berm") some 2,700 km long. The Polisario Front controls the mostly uninhabited eastern strip and administers refugee camps near Tindouf, Algeria, where many Sahrawis have lived since 1975. The SADR is recognised by dozens of states and is a member of the African Union, but no UN member state formally recognises Moroccan sovereignty, though several countries have endorsed Morocco's autonomy plan as a basis for settlement. The ceasefire broke down in November 2020, and low-intensity hostilities have since resumed along the Berm.

Economy and people

The economy rests on phosphate mining, fishing in the rich Atlantic waters, and Moroccan state investment; the indigenous Sahrawi population speaks the Hassaniya dialect of Arabic. Key settlements include Laayoune, Dakhla (a growing centre for tourism and wind sports), Smara and Boujdour.
