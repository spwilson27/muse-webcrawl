Automatic stay

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Bankruptcy court

The automatic stay is a feature of United States bankruptcy law, codified at 11 U.S.C. § 362, that automatically halts actions by creditors, with certain exceptions, to collect debts from a debtor who has declared bankruptcy. Under section 362 of the United States Bankruptcy Code, a petition filed under chapter 7, 11, 12, or 13 operates as a stay of most collection activity against the debtor or the debtor's property.

Scope

The stay generally stops foreclosures, repossessions, lawsuits, garnishments, and collection calls, giving the debtor a "breathing spell" while the bankruptcy case proceeds. It takes effect immediately upon filing, without a court hearing. Certain actions are excepted, including most criminal proceedings, some family-law matters, and certain tax proceedings.

Relief from stay and abuse

Creditors may ask the court for relief from the stay, for example where a secured creditor lacks adequate protection or the debtor has no equity in the property and it is not needed for reorganization. Since the 2005 BAPCPA amendments, the stay is limited or does not arise for repeat filers: it terminates after 30 days for a debtor with one prior case dismissed within a year, and does not go into effect at all for debtors with two or more such dismissals. Willful violation of the stay can result in damages, including punitive damages.
