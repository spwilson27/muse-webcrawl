J. M. Barrie

Sir James Matthew Barrie, 1st Baronet (9 May 1860 – 19 June 1937), better known as J. M. Barrie, was a Scottish novelist and playwright, best remembered as the creator of Peter Pan.

Life and work

Born in Kirriemuir, Angus, Barrie moved to London and found success with novels such as Auld Licht Idylls and The Little Minister. His friendship with the Llewelyn Davies family inspired his most famous creation: Peter Pan first appeared in the novel The Little White Bird (1902) and in the play Peter Pan, or The Boy Who Wouldn't Grow Up (1904). The novel Peter and Wendy followed in 1911.

Legacy

Barrie gave the rights to Peter Pan to Great Ormond Street Hospital in 1929, an arrangement later given unique protection in UK law. His plays, including The Admirable Crichton and Dear Brutus, were acclaimed in his lifetime; Peter Pan became one of the most enduring works of children's literature.
