Science fiction convention


Fans in costume at a convention

A science fiction convention (commonly called a con or SF con) is a gathering of fans of the speculative fiction genre, including science fiction, fantasy and related media. Conventions provide programming such as panel discussions, readings, art shows, dealers' rooms, gaming, films, costume contests (masquerades) and social events.

History

Science fiction fandom organised itself in the 1930s through clubs and fanzines. The first World Science Fiction Convention (Worldcon) was held in New York in 1939 alongside the World's Fair; it has run almost annually since and presents the Hugo Awards. Regional and specialised conventions multiplied in the post-war decades, and large media-oriented conventions now attract tens of thousands of attendees.

Types

Conventions vary in focus: literary conventions emphasise books and authors; media conventions feature film and television guests; and specialised events cover anime, comics, gaming or particular franchises. Events are typically run by volunteers as non-profit ventures, though commercial promoter-run conventions are also common.

Activities and culture

Typical features include guest-of-honour speeches, book launches, filking (fan music), art auctions, charity events and late-night parties. Conventions helped develop fan culture, the Hugo Awards, and long-standing traditions such as the masquerade and hall costumes.
