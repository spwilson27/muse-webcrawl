United States Marine Corps

The United States Marine Corps (USMC) is the maritime land force service branch of the United States Armed Forces, responsible for conducting expeditionary and amphibious operations through combined arms, implementing its own infantry, armor, artillery and aviation. Founded on 10 November 1775 at Tun Tavern in Philadelphia as the Continental Marines, it is a component of the Department of the Navy — with which it shares a close relationship — but is a distinct service led by its own four-star Commandant. Its motto is Semper Fidelis ("Always Faithful").

History

Marines fought in the Revolutionary War, the Quasi-War and against the Barbary pirates — commemorated in the line "to the shores of Tripoli" in the Marines' Hymn. After small roles in the 19th century, the Corps grew dramatically in World War I, where its stand at Belleau Wood (1918) earned the nickname "Devil Dogs." In World War II the Marine Corps made its reputation in the "island-hopping" campaign against Japan — fighting at Guadalcanal, Tarawa, Iwo Jima (where the famous flag raising occurred) and Okinawa. Marines landed at Inchon in the Korean War, fought at Khe Sanh and Huế in Vietnam, and led ground operations in the Gulf War, Iraq and Afghanistan. In 2020–2021, Commandant David Berger's Force Design 2030 began a controversial restructuring toward littoral operations against China, divesting tanks and tube artillery for missiles and drones.

Culture and training

The Marine Corps maintains a distinctive identity: every Marine is trained first as a rifleman, and enlisted recruits endure a 13-week boot camp at Parris Island or San Diego (the longest of any US service), ending in "The Crucible," before being awarded the Eagle, Globe and Anchor. The Corps' birthday on 10 November is celebrated with a ceremonial cake-cutting, and the Commandant reads the 1921 birthday message of John A. Lejeune. Marines also guard US embassies worldwide through the Marine Security Guard programme and provide the presidential helicopter squadron, HMX-1 ("Marine One").
