Maud Karpeles

Maud Karpeles OBE (12 November 1885 – 14 October 1976) was a British collector of folk songs and dance, a prominent figure in the English folk revival. She worked closely with the composer and collector Cecil Sharp, accompanying him on his collecting expeditions in the Appalachian Mountains of the United States from 1916 to 1918, where they documented hundreds of English ballads preserved in oral tradition.

Career

Karpeles co-founded the English Folk Dance Society in 1911 and helped organize international folk dance festivals. She made later field trips to Newfoundland (1929–30), collecting songs such as those later published in Folk Songs from Newfoundland (1971). She edited Sharp's collections after his death, wrote his biography (1933), and was a founder of the International Folk Music Council (later the International Council for Traditional Music) in 1947.

Legacy

Her work preserved dance and song traditions that might otherwise have been lost, and the Vaughan Williams Memorial Library holds her archives.
