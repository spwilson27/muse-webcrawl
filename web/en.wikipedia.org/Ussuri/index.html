Ussuri

The Ussuri or Wusuli (Russian: Уссури; Chinese: 乌苏里江, Wūsūlǐ Jiāng) is a river in eastern Asia, about 897 kilometres (557 miles) long, that flows northward forming part of the border between Russia (Primorsky Krai and Khabarovsk Krai) and China (Heilongjiang province) before joining the Amur River near Khabarovsk.

Geography

The Ussuri rises in the Sikhote-Alin mountain range and drains the fertile Ussuri valley, an important agricultural and timber region of the Russian Far East. Its tributaries include the Sungacha and the Muling. The river is navigable and freezes in winter.

History

The border along the Ussuri was set by the Treaties of Aigun (1858) and Peking (1860). In March 1969 the river was the scene of the Sino-Soviet border conflict, with armed clashes over Zhenbao (Damansky) Island that brought the two states close to war. The region also gives its name to the Ussuri brown bear and the Ussuri krai (Primorsky Krai).
