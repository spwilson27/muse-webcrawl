Sikelgaita (also Sichelgaita or Sigelgaita; c. 1040 – 16 April 1090) was a Lombard princess, the daughter of Prince Guaimar IV of Salerno and the second wife of the Norman duke Robert Guiscard. Unusually for a medieval noblewoman, she repeatedly accompanied her husband on campaign and was remembered by chroniclers as a warrior duchess who fought in armour.

Marriage and role

Sikelgaita married Guiscard in 1058, after his annulment from Alberada of Buonalbergo. The marriage allied the Normans with the old Lombard princely house of Salerno and helped legitimate Guiscard's rule within the County of Apulia and Calabria; when her brother Gisulf II was deposed, Guiscard took Salerno itself (1076). Sikelgaita was an active political partner: she appears in charters, governed in Guiscard's absences, and negotiated with cities and clergy.

Warrior duchess

The Byzantine princess and historian Anna Komnene, no friend of the Normans, describes Sikelgaita in the Alexiad riding into battle in full armour beside her husband at the Battle of Dyrrhachium (1081) against her father Alexios I Komnenos, rallying fleeing Norman troops with a spear and a Homeric harangue — Anna compared her to Athena. She was present during the campaigns in Greece and, according to some accounts, tended Guiscard in his final illness on Cephalonia in 1085; a hostile rumour even alleged she poisoned him, which most historians reject.

Widowhood and death

As widow, Sikelgaita worked to secure the succession of her son Roger Borsa against the claims of Bohemond, Guiscard's son by Alberada, eventually accepting a settlement that gave Bohemond Taranto and Bari. She made pious donations, including to Montecassino, and died on 16 April 1090; she was buried at Montecassino at her own request.
