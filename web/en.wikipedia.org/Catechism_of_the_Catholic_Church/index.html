Catechism of the Catholic Church

The Catechism of the Catholic Church (Latin: Catechismus Catholicae Ecclesiae) is a reference work summarising the beliefs of the Catholic Church. Promulgated by Pope John Paul II in 1992 (French edition) and 1994 (Latin editio typica, with a revised English translation following in 1997), it was the first comprehensive catechism since the Roman Catechism issued after the Council of Trent in 1566.

Structure

The Catechism is organised in four parts following the traditional structure of catechesis: (1) the Profession of Faith, expounding the Apostles' Creed; (2) the Celebration of the Christian Mystery, on the liturgy and seven sacraments; (3) Life in Christ, on morality and the Ten Commandments; and (4) Christian Prayer, culminating in a commentary on the Lord's Prayer. Its 2,865 numbered paragraphs cite Scripture, the Church Fathers, councils and the lives of the saints.

Revisions and use

Notable amendments include the 2018 revision on the death penalty, which declared capital punishment inadmissible. An abridged Compendium appeared in 2005, and a youth version, Youcat, in 2011. The Catechism serves as the reference point for local catechisms worldwide.
