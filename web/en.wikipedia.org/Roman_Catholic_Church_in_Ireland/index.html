The Roman Catholic Church in Ireland (Irish: An Eaglais Chaitliceach in Éirinn) is part of the worldwide Catholic Church in communion with the Pope. It is organised on an all-island basis, with 26 dioceses spanning both the Republic of Ireland and Northern Ireland, under the primacy of the Archbishop of Armagh. Catholicism has historically been the largest religion in Ireland and remains the largest single denomination, although practice and institutional influence have declined markedly since the late 20th century.

History

Christianity reached Ireland in the 5th century, traditionally with St Patrick. Irish monasticism flourished in the early Middle Ages, sending missionaries across Europe. After the Anglo-Norman invasion of 1169, the Irish church was progressively integrated into the Latin Church. During the Reformation, Ireland largely remained Catholic while the state church became Protestant, and the Penal Laws of the 17th and 18th centuries restricted Catholic worship, landholding and education.

Catholic Emancipation in 1829 restored most civil rights. In the 19th and early 20th centuries the Church, led by figures such as Cardinal Paul Cullen, became central to Irish national life, running most schools and hospitals after independence in 1922. The 1937 constitution recognised the "special position" of the Catholic Church until that clause was removed by referendum in 1972.

Modern period

From the 1990s, a series of scandals concerning clerical sexual abuse and institutions such as Magdalene laundries, together with broader social liberalisation, sharply reduced the Church's authority. Referendums legalising divorce (1995), same-sex marriage (2015) and abortion (2018) passed despite Church opposition. Weekly Mass attendance, once among the highest in Europe, has fallen substantially, though a majority in the Republic still identify as Catholic in censuses.

Organisation

The Church in Ireland comprises four ecclesiastical provinces — Armagh, Dublin, Cashel and Tuam — each headed by a metropolitan archbishop. Vocations and seminarians are trained principally at St Patrick's College, Maynooth. The Irish Catholic Bishops' Conference co-ordinates pastoral activity across the island.
