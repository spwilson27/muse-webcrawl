Dodge Monaco

The Dodge Monaco is an automobile that was marketed by the Dodge division of Chrysler Corporation in three distinct generations: as a full-size car for 1965–1978, as a mid-size car for 1977–1978 (concurrently, in some years), and as a mid-size sedan for 1990–1992.

Full-size generations

Introduced in 1965 as a top-of-the-line personal luxury hardtop derived from the Polara line, the Monaco became Dodge's flagship full-size model, sitting on Chrysler's C-body platform. Through the late 1960s and 1970s it was offered as sedans, hardtops and station wagons, with big-block V8 engines available. A related Royal Monaco appeared in the mid-1970s. The 1974–1978 Monaco achieved enduring pop-culture fame as the "Bluesmobile" in the 1980 film The Blues Brothers, and C-body Monacos were widely used as police cars, a role reinforced by television series of the era.

1990–1992 revival

The Monaco name returned in 1990 on a front-wheel-drive mid-size sedan derived from the Renault 25 via the Eagle Premier, built in Canada; it was sold alongside the nearly identical Premier until both were replaced by the Dodge Intrepid in 1993.
