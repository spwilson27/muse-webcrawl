A Princess of Mars
Author: Edgar Rice Burroughs
Serialized: 1912 (as "Under the Moons of Mars")
Book publication: 1917
Series: Barsoom

A Princess of Mars is a science fantasy novel by American writer Edgar Rice Burroughs, the first of his Barsoom series. It was first serialized as "Under the Moons of Mars" in the pulp magazine The All-Story in 1912 and published in book form in 1917. It is a foundational work of the planetary romance genre.

Plot

Confederate veteran John Carter, prospecting in Arizona after the Civil War, is mysteriously transported to Mars — called Barsoom by its inhabitants. Because of the planet's lower gravity, Carter possesses greatly enhanced strength and agility. He is captured by the nomadic, four-armed green Martians (Tharks), earns standing among them, and falls in love with Dejah Thoris, a red Martian princess of the city-state of Helium, whom he rescues repeatedly amid wars between Martian peoples.

Influence

The Barsoom books influenced generations of science fiction writers and filmmakers; their sword-and-planet formula fed into Flash Gordon, Star Wars, and James Cameron's Avatar. Disney's 2012 film John Carter, based largely on this novel, was a notorious box-office failure despite later reassessment. Because Burroughs died in 1950, the novel has entered the public domain in many jurisdictions.
