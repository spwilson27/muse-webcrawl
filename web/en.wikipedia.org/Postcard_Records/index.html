Postcard Records is a Scottish independent record label founded in Glasgow in 1979 by Alan Horne. Billing itself as "The Sound of Young Scotland", it became the defining label of the early-1980s Scottish indie scene, launching bands whose jangling guitar pop — a reaction against punk's aggression and synthesiser pop alike — influenced generations of indie musicians.

History and artists

Postcard's key signings were Orange Juice (fronted by Edwyn Collins), Josef K, Aztec Camera (Roddy Frame), the Fire Engines and the Bluebells. Singles such as Orange Juice's "Falling and Laughing" (1980) are landmark indie releases, celebrated in the sleevenotes and mythology around the C86 era that followed. Financial difficulties ended the label's original run around 1982, and its bands dispersed to majors; Orange Juice's later hit "Rip It Up" came on Polydor.

Legacy

Postcard's aesthetic — thrift-shop suits, Velvet Underground and Byrds influences, fiercely independent production — shaped British indie pop and has been documented in books and compilations on the "Sound of Young Scotland". The label name has been revived for reissues and new releases in subsequent decades.
