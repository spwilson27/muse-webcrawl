National Gallery of Art

The National Gallery of Art is a national art museum in Washington, D.C., on the National Mall. Opened in 1941, it comprises the neoclassical West Building, the modernist East Building (1978, designed by I. M. Pei), and a sculpture garden. Admission is free.

Established1937 (opened 1941)
FounderAndrew W. Mellon
LocationNational Mall, Washington, D.C.
CollectionOver 150,000 works

History

Financier and Treasury Secretary Andrew Mellon donated his collection and funds for the building; Congress accepted the gift in 1937. Major later benefactors include Samuel H. Kress, Joseph Widener, Chester Dale, and the Ailsa Mellon Bruce and Robert Lehman collections.

Collection

The gallery holds European painting from the Middle Ages to the present, including the only Leonardo da Vinci painting in the Americas, the Ginevra de' Benci, works by Raphael, Vermeer, Rembrandt, Monet, and van Gogh, plus a major collection of American art and the Index of American Design.
