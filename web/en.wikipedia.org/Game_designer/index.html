Game designer

A game designer is a person who designs gameplay, conceiving and shaping the rules, systems, content, and experience of a game — whether a video game, board game, card game, or role-playing game. In video game development, design is one of the central disciplines alongside programming, art, audio, and production.

Roles

Design work spans many specialties: systems designers tune mechanics and economies; level designers build environments and encounters; narrative designers and writers shape story, dialogue, and worldbuilding; and lead or creative designers hold the overall vision. Designers document their work in design documents, prototype mechanics, tune balance using playtesting and telemetry, and iterate throughout development. The field overlaps with user-experience design, since difficulty curves, feedback, and tutorials determine how a game teaches and rewards its players.

Notable designers

Influential figures include Shigeru Miyamoto (Super Mario Bros., The Legend of Zelda), Sid Meier (Civilization), Will Wright (SimCity, The Sims), Hideo Kojima (Metal Gear), and Jane McGonigal; board and tabletop design has its own tradition, from Charles Darrow to modern designers such as Reiner Knizia. Many designers began as programmers or artists, and independent development has blurred the roles further.
