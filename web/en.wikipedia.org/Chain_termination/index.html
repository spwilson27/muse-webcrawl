Chain termination

In polymer chemistry, chain termination is any chemical reaction that ceases the propagation of a growing reactive polymer chain, ending a chain-growth polymerization. During termination the active centre at the chain end — a radical, cation or anion — is destroyed, and the chain can grow no further.


Termination by combination of two radicals

Mechanisms

In free-radical polymerization, termination occurs mainly by combination, in which two growing chains join into one, or by disproportionation, in which a hydrogen atom is transferred from one chain to another, producing one saturated and one unsaturated chain end. Chain transfer to solvent, monomer or a chain-transfer agent is sometimes classed separately, since a new active centre is created. True termination contrasts with reversible deactivation in living polymerization techniques such as ATRP and RAFT, where chains can resume growth.

Significance

The balance between propagation and termination rates determines the average molecular weight and molecular-weight distribution of the polymer, key properties controlling strength, melt behaviour and processability.
