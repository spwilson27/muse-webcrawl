Pulse

Pulse
Common sites: radial, carotid, brachial, femoral
Normal resting rate (adults): 60–100 beats per minute
Assessed as part of: vital signs

In medicine, the pulse is the rhythmic expansion and recoil of an artery as blood is ejected from the heart with each beat, and by extension the tactile or electronic detection of that wave. Pulse rate is usually measured in beats per minute and is one of the standard vital signs, alongside temperature, respiratory rate and blood pressure.

Physiology

When the left ventricle contracts, a pressure wave travels through the arterial system faster than the blood itself flows. The pulse can be felt wherever an artery lies close to the skin over a firm structure, most commonly the radial artery at the wrist and the carotid artery in the neck.

Assessment

Clinicians assess rate, rhythm, volume and character. A normal adult resting heart rate is generally 60 to 100 beats per minute. Tachycardia is a rate above 100 and bradycardia below 60, though trained athletes may have lower resting rates. An irregular pulse may indicate atrial fibrillation. Modern measurement is increasingly automated, using oscillometric blood-pressure monitors and pulse oximeters.

History

Pulse palpation has been central to medicine for millennia: ancient Chinese, Indian, Greek and Egyptian physicians all developed elaborate pulse doctrines. Galen wrote extensively on the pulse, and the invention of the sphygmograph in the nineteenth century first allowed it to be recorded graphically.
