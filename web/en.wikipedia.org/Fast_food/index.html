Fast food

Fast food is a type of mass-produced food designed for commercial resale, with a strong priority placed on speed of service. It is a commercial term, limited to food sold in a restaurant or store with frozen, preheated or precooked ingredients and served in packaging for take-out or take-away. Fast food was created as a commercial strategy to accommodate large numbers of busy commuters, travelers and wage workers. In 2018, the fast-food industry was worth an estimated US$570 billion globally.

A hamburger, French fries, and a soft drink — the archetypal fast-food meal.

History

Fast food has ancient antecedents — Roman thermopolia sold ready-to-eat food, and medieval cities had street vendors — but the modern industry arose in the United States. White Castle (founded 1921) pioneered the standardized hamburger stand; the McDonald brothers' "Speedee Service System" of 1948 applied production-line principles to the kitchen, and Ray Kroc's franchising from 1955 made McDonald's a global archetype. Drive-through service, franchising, and heavy advertising, including to children, became hallmarks of the sector.

Menu and business model

Typical offerings include burgers, fried chicken, pizza, tacos, sandwiches, fries, and soft drinks, produced to uniform specifications across franchised outlets. The model depends on high volume, low margins, limited menus, and labor practices that have made the industry a focus of minimum-wage campaigns such as the "Fight for $15".

Criticism

Fast food is criticized for high levels of fat, sugar, salt and calories, for its association with obesity (the subject of the 2004 film Super Size Me), for environmental impacts of packaging and meat production, and for cultural homogenization. Chains have responded with salads, grilled items, calorie labelling, and regional menu adaptations worldwide.
