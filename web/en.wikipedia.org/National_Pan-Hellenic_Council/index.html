The National Pan-Hellenic Council (NPHC) is a collaborative umbrella council composed of nine historically African American fraternities and sororities, collectively known as the Divine Nine. It was founded on 10 May 1930 at Howard University in Washington, D.C., to promote interaction among its member organisations and to advance the interests of Black Greek-letter life on college campuses and in communities.

Member organisations

The nine members are Alpha Phi Alpha (1906), Alpha Kappa Alpha (1908), Kappa Alpha Psi (1911), Omega Psi Phi (1911), Delta Sigma Theta (1913), Phi Beta Sigma (1914), Zeta Phi Beta (1920), Sigma Gamma Rho (1922), and Iota Phi Theta (1963). The founding members in 1930 were Alpha Kappa Alpha, Delta Sigma Theta, Zeta Phi Beta, Kappa Alpha Psi, and Omega Psi Phi; the others joined over the following decades.

Purpose and activities

Unlike campus councils that govern rush and chapter standards, the NPHC emphasises lifelong membership, community service, voter registration, educational and economic development, and cultural traditions such as stepping and stroll competitions. Membership intake for NPHC organisations typically occurs after students have completed some college coursework, and members often remain active in graduate or alumni chapters throughout their lives.
