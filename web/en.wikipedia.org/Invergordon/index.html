Invergordon (Scottish Gaelic: Inbhir Ghòrdain or Baile Udain) is a town and port in Easter Ross, in Ross and Cromarty, Highland, Scotland. It lies on the north shore of the Cromarty Firth, a deep natural harbour that made the town an important naval anchorage.

History

The town was founded in the 18th century by Sir William Gordon of Invergordon Castle and grew around its harbour, serving the agricultural hinterland of Easter Ross. The Cromarty Firth's deep, sheltered waters made Invergordon a key base for the Royal Navy; it was a major anchorage for the Home Fleet in both World Wars.

In September 1931 the town gave its name to the Invergordon Mutiny, when sailors of the Atlantic Fleet protested against pay cuts imposed during the Great Depression — one of the few mutinies in Royal Navy history, which contributed to Britain abandoning the gold standard days later. The naval base closed in the 1950s.

Economy

After the naval years, the economy turned to industry: a grain whisky distillery (Invergordon Distillery, founded 1959), an aluminium smelter (opened 1971, closed 1981), and the fabrication and maintenance of North Sea oil rigs at the nearby Nigg yard and the port itself. In recent decades the deep-water berths have made Invergordon one of Scotland's busiest cruise ship ports, with large liners calling during the summer season, and the town centre is noted for its series of painted murals depicting local history.
