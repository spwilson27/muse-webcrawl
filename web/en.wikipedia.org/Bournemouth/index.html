Bournemouth is a coastal resort town in Dorset, England, on the south coast between Poole and Christchurch. Known for its seven miles of sandy beaches, Victorian architecture, and pine-scented chines, it is one of Britain's most popular seaside destinations.

History

Bournemouth was founded in 1810 by Lewis Tregonwell, who promoted the remote heathland as a health resort. The arrival of the railway in 1870 spurred rapid growth, and the town became a favoured retreat for wealthy Victorians. Writers including J. R. R. Tolkien spent long periods here.

Geography and attractions

The town sits on cliffs above Poole Bay, with views to the Isle of Wight and the Purbeck Hills. Landmarks include Bournemouth Pier, the Russell-Cotes Art Gallery and Museum, and the Lower, Central and Upper Gardens. The nearby towns of Poole and Christchurch form a conurbation of nearly half a million people.

Modern town

Bournemouth is home to Bournemouth University and a significant financial-services and digital sector. Its football club, AFC Bournemouth, plays at Dean Court (the Vitality Stadium) and reached the Premier League in 2015.
