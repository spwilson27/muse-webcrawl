Vimy Ridge is a prominent escarpment in northern France, about 8 kilometres northeast of Arras in the department of Pas-de-Calais. It is best known as the site of the Battle of Vimy Ridge during the First World War, in which the Canadian Corps captured the ridge from the German Army in April 1917. The Canadian National Vimy Memorial, opened in 1936, stands on land granted by France to Canada in perpetuity.

Geography

The ridge runs roughly 7 kilometres from near Givenchy-en-Gohelle to beyond Vimy village, rising gradually from the Douai plain in the east and dropping steeply to the west. Its highest point, known as Hill 145, offered commanding observation over the surrounding battlefield.

First World War

German forces seized the ridge in 1914 and fortified it extensively. French attacks in 1915 failed to hold its crest. During the Battle of Arras, the four divisions of the Canadian Corps, fighting together for the first time, assaulted the ridge on 9 April 1917 behind a creeping barrage and captured most of it within three days, at a cost of 3,598 Canadians killed and about 7,000 wounded. The victory became a defining event in Canadian national identity.

Memorial and preserved battlefield

The Canadian National Vimy Memorial, designed by Walter Seymour Allward, features twin limestone pylons and allegorical figures. The preserved battlefield park includes restored trenches, tunnels and craters, and the names of 11,285 Canadian soldiers who died in France with no known grave are inscribed on the memorial's ramparts.
