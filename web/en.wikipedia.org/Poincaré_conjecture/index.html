Poincaré conjecture

The Poincaré conjecture is a theorem about the characterization of the 3-sphere, the hypersphere that bounds the unit ball in four-dimensional space. Originally conjectured by Henri Poincaré in 1904, the theorem concerns spaces that locally look like ordinary three-dimensional space but are finite in extent. It states that such a space, if it has the additional property that each loop in the space can be continuously tightened to a point, must be a 3-sphere.

The conjecture remained open for nearly a century and was listed among the seven Millennium Prize Problems by the Clay Mathematics Institute, with a prize of one million dollars. It was finally proved by the Russian mathematician Grigori Perelman using Richard Hamilton's program of Ricci flow, in papers posted on the arXiv in 2002 and 2003. Perelman declined both the Fields Medal (2006) and the Millennium Prize (2010).

Statement

The Poincaré conjecture states: Every simply connected, closed 3-manifold is homeomorphic to the 3-sphere. The conjecture is a special case of Thurston's geometrization conjecture, which Perelman's work also established.

History

Poincaré posed the question at the end of a 1904 paper, having himself found a counterexample (the Poincaré homology sphere) to a stronger claim he had made earlier. Through the 20th century the higher-dimensional analogues were settled first: Stephen Smale proved the generalized conjecture in dimensions five and higher (1960), and Michael Freedman resolved the four-dimensional case (1982), each receiving the Fields Medal. Dimension three, Poincaré's original case, resisted until Perelman's proof.

Ricci flow

Hamilton introduced the Ricci flow — an equation that deforms the metric of a manifold in a manner analogous to the diffusion of heat — in 1982, and used it to prove special cases of the conjecture. Perelman completed the program by analyzing singularities of the flow with "Ricci flow with surgery," controlling the geometry as parts of the manifold pinch off. His proof was verified by several teams of mathematicians by 2006.
