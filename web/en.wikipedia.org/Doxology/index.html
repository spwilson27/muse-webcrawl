Doxology

A congregation singing the Doxology

A doxology (from Ancient Greek doxa, “glory”, and -logia, “saying”) is a short hymn of praise to God in various forms of Christian worship, often involving the Trinity. Doxological expressions occur throughout the Bible, in both the Old and New Testaments.

Well-known forms

The Greater Doxology is the Gloria in excelsis Deo, sung in the Roman Catholic Mass and other liturgies. The Gloria Patri (“Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit”) is the Lesser Doxology, widely appended to psalms and prayers. In many Protestant churches, “the Doxology” specifically refers to the final stanza of Thomas Ken's hymns, beginning “Praise God, from whom all blessings flow”, commonly sung to the tune Old Hundredth.

Usage

Doxologies conclude prayers, hymns, and liturgical services in Catholic, Orthodox, Anglican, and Protestant traditions. A doxological ending to the Lord's Prayer (“For thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory”) appears in many manuscripts and liturgies, though it is absent from the earliest Gospel manuscripts.
