Friedrich Reinhold Kreutzwald

Friedrich Reinhold Kreutzwald (26 December 1803 – 25 August 1882) was an Estonian writer who is considered the father of the national literature of Estonia. He is the author of the Estonian national epic Kalevipoeg ("Son of Kalev").

Life

Kreutzwald was born in Jõepere, in what is now Lääne-Viru County, to serf parents; his father was a shoemaker and later a barn keeper. The family gained freedom in 1815. He trained as an elementary school teacher in Tartu, worked in Tallinn, and then studied medicine at the University of Tartu, qualifying as a doctor in 1833. He practised as the municipal physician of Võru from 1833 to 1877, and was a member of the Learned Estonian Society.

The Kalevipoeg

Encouraged by Friedrich Robert Faehlmann, who had begun collecting Estonian folk tales about the hero Kalevipoeg, Kreutzwald took up the task of assembling the material into a unified epic. Drawing on genuine folk songs, legends, and his own connective verse, he produced an epic of 19,023 verses in the traditional Estonian trochaic metre (regilaul). A first version appeared in 1853; the definitive edition was published in instalments between 1857 and 1861 in a bilingual Estonian–German edition. The Kalevipoeg became a cornerstone of the Estonian national awakening.

Other work

Kreutzwald also wrote fairy tales, didactic literature, and translations, and compiled the popular almanac literature that spread literacy among Estonian peasants. His fairy-tale collection Eesti rahva ennemuistsed jutud (1866) remains a classic.
