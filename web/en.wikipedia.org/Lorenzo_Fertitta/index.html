Lorenzo Fertitta

Lorenzo Joseph Fertitta (born January 3, 1969) is an American businessman and philanthropist. He is chairman of Fertitta Capital and a co-founder of Station Casinos. Together with his brother Frank Fertitta III, he was the controlling owner of Zuffa, the parent company of the Ultimate Fighting Championship (UFC), from 2001 until its sale to WME-IMG in 2016.

Early life and casino career

Fertitta was born and raised in Las Vegas, Nevada, in the family that ran Station Casinos, founded by his father Frank Fertitta Jr. He attended Bishop Gorman High School — where he met future UFC president Dana White — and graduated from the University of San Diego. He worked his way up through the family business, becoming president of Station Casinos, which built a leading position in the Las Vegas locals casino market.

UFC and Zuffa

In 2001 the Fertitta brothers purchased the struggling UFC for $2 million through their company Zuffa, installing White as president. Lorenzo Fertitta, who had served as a commissioner on the Nevada State Athletic Commission, was central to securing sanctioning of mixed martial arts in Nevada and elsewhere, and to the UFC's growth through The Ultimate Fighter reality series and pay-per-view. Under Zuffa the UFC bought rival promotions including Pride Fighting Championships and Strikeforce. In 2016 Zuffa was sold to a group led by WME-IMG for about $4 billion, then the largest deal in sports promotion history; Fertitta stepped down as chairman and CEO.

Other ventures and philanthropy

Through Fertitta Capital and other ventures the family has invested across hospitality, sports and media, and holds a stake in the NHL's Vegas Golden Knights. Fertitta is known for charitable giving in Nevada, including support for Bishop Gorman, veterans' causes and UNLV.
