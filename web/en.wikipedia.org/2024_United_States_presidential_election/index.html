2024 United States presidential election

The 2024 United States presidential election was the 60th quadrennial presidential election, held on Tuesday, November 5, 2024. The Republican ticket of former president Donald Trump and Ohio senator JD Vance defeated the Democratic ticket of vice president Kamala Harris and Minnesota governor Tim Walz.

Background

President Joe Biden initially sought re-election but withdrew from the race on July 21, 2024, following a poor debate performance and pressure from within his party, endorsing Harris. Trump survived an assassination attempt at a rally in Butler, Pennsylvania, on July 13, 2024.

Campaign

Key issues included the economy and inflation, immigration, abortion rights following the Dobbs decision, and democracy. Harris's abbreviated campaign of just over 100 days was the shortest general election campaign for a major-party nominee in modern history.

Results

Trump won 312 electoral votes to Harris's 226, carrying all seven major swing states, and won the national popular vote with about 49.8%—the first Republican popular-vote victory since 2004. Republicans also gained control of the Senate while retaining the House of Representatives.

Aftermath

Trump was inaugurated as the 47th president on January 20, 2025, becoming only the second president to serve non-consecutive terms, after Grover Cleveland.
