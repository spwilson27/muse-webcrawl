Eleanor of Toledo

Eleanor of Toledo
Born: 11 January 1522, Alba de Tormes, Spain
Died: 17 December 1562, Pisa
Spouse: Cosimo I de' Medici
Title: Duchess consort of Florence

Eleanor of Toledo (11 January 1522 – 17 December 1562) was a Spanish noblewoman and, by marriage to Cosimo I de' Medici, the first Duchess of Florence. A daughter of the Viceroy of Naples, she became one of the most powerful women of Renaissance Italy, acting as regent of Florence during her husband's absences.

Agnolo Bronzino's portrait of Eleanor with her son Giovanni (c. 1545), in the Uffizi.

Life

Eleanor was the daughter of Don Pedro Álvarez de Toledo, the Spanish viceroy of Naples. Her marriage to Cosimo in 1539 brought the Medici an alliance with Imperial Spain and a large dowry. In Florence she purchased the Pitti Palace, which became the Medici residence, and commissioned the Boboli Gardens behind it. She governed the duchy when Cosimo was ill or away, supported the Jesuits and other religious orders, and was a generous patron of the arts.

Appearance and legacy

Bronzino's famous portrait shows her in a sumptuous pomegranate-patterned brocade dress, celebrating both her fertility—she bore eleven children, including two Grand Dukes of Tuscany—and the Florentine silk industry she promoted. Eleanor died of malaria at Pisa in 1562, the same epidemic that killed two of her sons. For centuries she was wrongly blamed for family tragedies, but modern scholarship recognises her as an effective political partner to Cosimo and a dynamic force in Medici Florence.
