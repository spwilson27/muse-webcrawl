Marxism–Leninism

Founders: Karl Marx, Vladimir Lenin
Codified: 1920s–1930s, Soviet Union
Key concepts: Vanguard party, democratic centralism, dictatorship of the proletariat

Marxism–Leninism is a communist political ideology developed as the official ideology of the Soviet Union and the parties of the Communist International. It combines the theories of Karl Marx with Vladimir Lenin's concepts of the vanguard party, democratic centralism and imperialism as the highest stage of capitalism.

Doctrine

The ideology holds that a disciplined vanguard party should lead the working class to overthrow capitalism and establish a dictatorship of the proletariat as a transition toward socialism and ultimately communism. It was systematised under Joseph Stalin, notably in works such as the Short Course history of the Soviet party.

Spread

Marxism–Leninism was adopted by ruling parties in China, Cuba, Vietnam and other states, though each developed national variants, and it is distinguished from other Marxist currents such as Trotskyism and council communism.
