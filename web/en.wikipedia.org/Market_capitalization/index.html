Market capitalization

Market capitalization, sometimes referred to as market cap, is the total value of a publicly traded company's outstanding common shares held by stockholders. It is calculated by multiplying the number of outstanding shares by the current market price of one share. The investment community uses the figure to determine a company's size, as opposed to using sales or total asset figures.

Categories

Companies are conventionally classified as large-cap (generally above $10 billion), mid-cap ($2–10 billion), small-cap ($300 million–$2 billion), micro-cap and nano-cap. The thresholds vary by era and market. Index providers such as S&P and MSCI use free-float market capitalisation, counting only shares available for public trading, to weight their indices.

Interpretation

Market cap reflects only the value of a company's equity; it differs from enterprise value, which adds debt and subtracts cash to approximate the price of acquiring the whole business. Market capitalisation can change rapidly without any change in the underlying business, simply because share prices move; it also changes when companies issue or buy back shares.
