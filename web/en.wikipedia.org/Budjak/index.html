Budjak (also spelled Budzhak or Bujak) is a historical region in Ukraine, the southern part of the historic region of Bessarabia. It lies along the Black Sea coast between the Danube and Dniester rivers and is now the southwestern portion of Ukraine's Odesa Oblast, bordered by Moldova to the north and Romania across the Danube to the south.

History

The name derives from the Turkish word bucak, meaning "borderland" or "corner". In antiquity the region was inhabited by Thracian and Scythian peoples and later formed part of the Roman and Byzantine spheres. From the late 15th century it came under Ottoman control, and after the Russo-Turkish War it was ceded to the Russian Empire by the Treaty of Bucharest in 1812. In the 20th century the region passed between Romania and the Soviet Union before becoming part of independent Ukraine in 1991.

Population

Budjak is one of the most ethnically diverse regions of Ukraine, with significant Ukrainian, Bulgarian, Russian, Moldovan, Gagauz, and Albanian communities, a legacy of 19th-century colonization of the steppe.

Geography

The region is a flat steppe plain crossed by the lower Danube. The Danube delta lakes are important wetlands, and the ports of Izmail and Reni handle river and sea trade.
