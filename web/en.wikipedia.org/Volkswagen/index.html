Volkswagen

Volkswagen (VW) is a German automobile manufacturer based in Wolfsburg, Lower Saxony. Founded in 1937 by the German Labour Front under Adolf Hitler to produce a cheap "people's car" (Volkswagen in German), it is the flagship brand of the Volkswagen Group, one of the world's largest automakers by sales. Its best-known models include the Beetle, the Golf, and the Transporter ("Bus").

History

The original Volkswagen, designed by Ferdinand Porsche, was developed as part of the Nazi KdF-Wagen programme; civilian production barely began before the Second World War diverted the factory to military vehicles. After the war the plant, in the British occupation zone, was revived under Major Ivan Hirst, and the Beetle became an icon of West Germany's economic miracle and of 1960s counterculture, ultimately selling over 21 million units. The water-cooled, front-wheel-drive Golf of 1974 succeeded it as the brand's core model and defined the hatchback segment.

Diesel scandal and electrification

In 2015 the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency found that Volkswagen had installed "defeat device" software in some 11 million diesel cars worldwide to cheat emissions tests. The "Dieselgate" scandal cost the company tens of billions of dollars in fines, settlements, and recalls, and accelerated a strategic pivot to electric vehicles, including the ID. series built on the group's MEB platform.

Range and markets

Volkswagen sells cars in more than 150 countries, with major production in Germany, China (its largest market), Mexico, Brazil, and elsewhere. Its range spans small cars such as the Polo, SUVs such as the Tiguan, and the revived all-electric ID. Buzz minivan recalling the classic Bus.
