Full communion

Full communion is a term used in Christian ecclesiology to describe the relationship of communion between different Christian churches or denominations that recognise one another's sacraments, ministry, and faith as complete, without merging into a single organisation. It is contrasted with "partial" or "imperfect" communion.

Catholic usage

In Roman Catholic terminology, the Eastern Catholic Churches are in full communion with the Pope of Rome while retaining their own liturgical traditions and canon law; the Orthodox and Protestant churches are held to be in imperfect communion. Full communion involves shared Eucharistic celebration and mutual recognition of ordained ministry.

Protestant agreements

Among Protestant and Anglican churches, full communion agreements — such as the Porvoo Communion between Anglicans and Lutherans in Europe, and agreements in North America between the Episcopal Church and the Evangelical Lutheran Church in America — allow interchangeability of clergy and shared sacramental life while each church retains its autonomy.
