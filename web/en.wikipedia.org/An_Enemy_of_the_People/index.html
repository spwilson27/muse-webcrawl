An Enemy of the People

An Enemy of the People (Norwegian: En folkefiende) is an 1882 play by Norwegian playwright Henrik Ibsen. It follows Dr. Thomas Stockmann, a medical officer who discovers that the water of his town's popular spa baths is contaminated, and is persecuted by the town when he tries to expose the danger.

An Enemy of the People
Written by: Henrik Ibsen
Original language: Norwegian (Dano-Norwegian)
Date premiered: 13 January 1883, Christiania Theatre, Oslo
Genre: Drama

Plot

Dr. Stockmann discovers that the municipal baths — the town's economic lifeblood — are polluted by runoff from local industry. When he insists on publishing his findings, his brother, the mayor, and the townspeople turn against him; at a public meeting Stockmann denounces the majority and is declared an "enemy of the people".

Themes and legacy

The play explores truth versus majority opinion, the corruption of civic institutions, and the isolation of the whistleblower. Ibsen wrote it partly in response to the harsh reception of his earlier play Ghosts. It has been widely adapted, including Arthur Miller's 1950 adaptation and the 1978 film starring Steve McQueen.
