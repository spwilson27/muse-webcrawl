Art competition

An art competition is a contest in which artists submit works to be judged against one another, typically for prizes, exhibition opportunities or commissions. Competitions range from local shows to international events with open calls.

Olympic art competitions

From 1912 to 1948, art competitions were part of the Olympic Games, reflecting founder Pierre de Coubertin's wish to unite sport and art. Medals were awarded in architecture, literature, music, painting and sculpture for works inspired by sport. The competitions were discontinued after 1948, partly over questions of amateur status.

Modern competitions

Well-known modern examples include portrait prizes, photography contests and open exhibitions such as the Royal Academy Summer Exhibition. Entry fees, jury composition and copyright terms for submitted works are common points of debate.
