Lazio

Lazio (Italian: [ˈlattsjo]; Latin: Latium, [ˈɫati.ũ]) is one of the 20 administrative regions of Italy, located in the central part of the country. It has a population of about 5.7 million, making it the second most populated region of Italy after Lombardy, and its capital and largest city is Rome, the capital of Italy and seat of the Roman Empire. The region corresponds roughly to ancient Latium, the territory of the Latins from which Latin language, Roman law and much of Roman civilization spread.

Geography

Lazio stretches from the Apennine Mountains in the east to the Tyrrhenian Sea in the west. Its landscape includes the volcanic Alban Hills and crater lakes (Bolsena, Bracciano, Albano, Nemi), the formerly marshy Pontine Plain (Agro Pontino, drained in the 1930s), and the coastal ports of Civitavecchia, Anzio and Gaeta. The Tiber River crosses the region through Rome to its mouth at Ostia. Offshore lie the Pontine Islands.

History

Ancient Latium was home first to Italic peoples — Latins, Volscians, Samnites — and bordered Etruria to the north. Rome, founded traditionally in 753 BCE, gradually conquered the region and then the Mediterranean world. After the fall of the Western Roman Empire the area passed through Lombard, Byzantine and Frankish hands before forming the core of the Papal States, which ruled Lazio until Italian unification in 1870, when Rome became the national capital. The modern region was established under the Italian constitution of 1948 and given an elected government in 1970.

Economy

The economy is dominated by services concentrated in Rome: national government, tourism, film and media (Cinecittà Studios), universities and research. Agriculture — wine (Frascati, Est! Est!! Est!!!), olive oil, hazelnuts from Viterbo, buffalo mozzarella, and vegetables from the Pontine Plain — and industry in chemicals, pharmaceuticals and aerospace are significant elsewhere. Tourism draws visitors to Rome's monuments, the Vatican, Etruscan sites at Cerveteri and Tarquinia, Hadrian's Villa and the Villa d'Este at Tivoli, and Ostia Antica.

Transport

Rome's two airports, Fiumicino (Leonardo da Vinci) and Ciampino, serve the region; the port of Civitavecchia handles ferries and cruise ships. Major motorways (A1 toward Naples and Florence, A24/A25 to the Adriatic) and high-speed rail lines radiate from Roma Termini station.

Sport

The region hosts two Serie A football clubs, SS Lazio and AS Roma, who share the Stadio Olimpico — a rivalry expressed in the Derby della Capitale, one of world football's most intense.
