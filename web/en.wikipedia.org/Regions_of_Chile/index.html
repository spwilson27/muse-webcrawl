Regions of Chile

Chile is divided into 16 regions (Spanish: regiones), which are the country's first-level administrative divisions. Each region is headed by an intendente appointed historically by the president and, since reforms culminating in 2021, by a directly elected regional governor (gobernador regional) alongside an elected regional council. Regions are subdivided into provinces, and provinces into communes.

History

The regional structure was created during the military government of Augusto Pinochet, with the country divided into 13 regions in 1974, identified by Roman numerals. Two regions were added in 2007 (Arica y Parinacota, split from Tarapacá, and Los Ríos, split from Los Lagos), and Ñuble was split from Biobío in 2018, bringing the total to 16 and completing the official abandonment of the Roman-numeral designations.
