Shaker village

Shaker villages were the intentional communities established by the United Society of Believers in Christ's Second Appearing, commonly known as the Shakers. Founded in the late 18th century in America, the villages were organized around principles of communal living, celibacy, pacifism, and separation from the world.

At the movement's peak in the mid-19th century, there were about 6,000 Shakers living in 19 communities across the United States. Villages such as Hancock Shaker Village in Massachusetts, Canterbury Shaker Village in New Hampshire, and Pleasant Hill in Kentucky are now preserved as museums.

Shaker villages are noted for their distinctive architecture and craftsmanship, including the famous round stone barn at Hancock, and for innovations in agriculture, furniture making, and the production of seeds and medicinal herbs.
