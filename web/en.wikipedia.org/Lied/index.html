Lied

A Lied (German for "song"; plural Lieder) is a term in classical music for a song setting a German poem for solo voice and piano, characteristic of the Romantic era. The Lied flourished in the 19th century as an intimate art form in which voice and piano are equal partners, the piano part painting moods and images from the text rather than merely accompanying.

Development

Precursors include the songs of Haydn, Mozart, and Beethoven (whose An die ferne Geliebte, 1816, is often called the first song cycle). Franz Schubert transformed the genre, composing more than 600 Lieder, including the cycles Die schöne Müllerin (1823) and Winterreise (1827) and ballads such as Erlkönig. Robert Schumann, Johannes Brahms, Hugo Wolf, Gustav Mahler (who expanded the Lied to orchestral scale), and Richard Strauss were among its great later masters.

Characteristics

Lieder set poems by Goethe, Heine, Eichendorff, Mörike, and others. Musical forms range from simple strophic settings to through-composed narratives and modified strophic designs. Song cycles group Lieder into narratives or emotional journeys. The tradition later influenced French mélodie and English art song.
