Bugs Bunny

Bugs Bunny is an animated cartoon character created in 1938 by Leon Schlesinger Productions (later Warner Bros. Cartoons). A gray-and-white anthropomorphic rabbit, he starred in the Looney Tunes and Merrie Melodies series of theatrical shorts and became an American cultural icon, recognised by his catchphrase "Eh… what's up, doc?"

Creation and development

Early rabbit prototypes appeared in shorts such as Porky's Hare Hunt (1938). The definitive Bugs debuted in Tex Avery's A Wild Hare (1940), which established his cool, wisecracking personality and first paired him with hunter Elmer Fudd. Directors Avery, Chuck Jones, Friz Freleng, Bob Clampett and Robert McKimson refined the character through the golden age of American animation. Voice actor Mel Blanc supplied Bugs's Brooklyn-Bronx accent for nearly five decades.

Notable cartoons

  • Knighty Knight Bugs (1958) — won the Academy Award for Best Animated Short.
  • What's Opera, Doc? (1957) — voted the greatest cartoon of all time in a 1994 poll of animation professionals.
  • Rabbit of Seville (1950), Duck Amuck (1953, with Daffy Duck) and the "Rabbit Season/Duck Season" trilogy.

Later appearances

Bugs fronted television compilations for decades, appeared in Who Framed Roger Rabbit (1988) alongside Mickey Mouse, and starred in Space Jam (1996) and its 2021 sequel. In 1985 he became the second cartoon character, after Mickey Mouse, to receive a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame, and he remains the mascot of Warner Bros. Animation.
