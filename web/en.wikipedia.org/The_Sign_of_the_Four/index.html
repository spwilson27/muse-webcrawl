Author: Arthur Conan Doyle

Published: 1890

Series: Sherlock Holmes

Preceded by: A Study in Scarlet

The Sign of the Four (also published as The Sign of Four) is the second novel featuring Sherlock Holmes, written by Arthur Conan Doyle and published in 1890. It was commissioned at a dinner hosted by the publisher Joseph Stoddart, at which Oscar Wilde also agreed to write The Picture of Dorian Gray.

Plot

Mary Morstan consults Holmes about her father, who vanished ten years earlier, and about the pearls she has received annually since. The case leads to the Agra treasure, stolen during the Indian Rebellion of 1857, the one-legged convict Jonathan Small, his accomplice Tonga, and a steam-launch chase down the Thames. In the course of the case Dr Watson meets and falls in love with Mary Morstan, whom he later marries.

Significance

The novel establishes features of the Holmes canon, including Holmes's cocaine use, his maxim that "when you have eliminated the impossible, whatever remains, however improbable, must be the truth", and details of his monographs and methods.
