Caspian Sea

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Caspian Sea is the world's largest inland body of water, often described as the world's largest lake or a full-fledged sea. An endorheic basin, it lies between Europe and Asia, east of the Caucasus Mountains and west of the broad steppe of Central Asia. It is bounded by Kazakhstan to the northeast, Russia to the northwest, Azerbaijan to the southwest, Iran to the south, and Turkmenistan to the southeast.

Geography

The Caspian Sea has a surface area of about 371,000 km² and a volume of about 78,200 km³. It is an endorheic basin — it has no outflows — and lies about 28 metres below sea level. The Volga River, Europe's longest, supplies roughly 80 percent of its inflowing water. The sea is divided into the shallow northern Caspian, the middle Caspian, and the deep southern Caspian.

Ecology

The basin supports distinctive fauna including the Caspian seal and the sturgeons from which most of the world's caviar is harvested. The Garabogazköl lagoon on the eastern shore is among the saltiest bodies of water on Earth.

Economy and legal status

The region is rich in petroleum and natural gas, both offshore and in surrounding onshore fields. After decades of dispute following the dissolution of the Soviet Union, the five littoral states signed the Convention on the Legal Status of the Caspian Sea in Aktau, Kazakhstan, in August 2018, treating the Caspian as neither a sea nor a lake under standard international law but as a body of water with a special legal status.

History

The Caspian is a remnant of the ancient Paratethys Sea. Its level has risen and fallen dramatically over geological and historical time, leaving ancient shorelines and submerged settlements.
