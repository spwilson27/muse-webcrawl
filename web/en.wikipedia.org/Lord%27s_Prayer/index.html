Lord's Prayer
Also calledOur Father; Pater Noster

Taught byJesus

The Lord's Prayer, also known by its incipit Our Father (Latin: Pater Noster), is a central Christian prayer that, according to the New Testament, Jesus taught his disciples. Two versions appear in the Gospels: a longer form in Matthew's Sermon on the Mount and a shorter form in Luke, given in response to a disciple's request to be taught to pray.

Text and structure

The Matthean version addresses God as "Our Father in heaven", asks that his name be hallowed and his kingdom come, and petitions for daily bread, forgiveness of debts "as we also have forgiven our debtors", and deliverance from temptation and evil. The doxology "For thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory, for ever" appears in later manuscripts and in many liturgical traditions.

Use

The prayer is recited in virtually all Christian worship, is taught to catechumens, and is prayed daily by many Christians. Early Christian manuals such as the Didache already prescribed praying it three times a day. It has been set to music innumerable times and translated into thousands of languages.
