Bull riding

Bull riding is a rodeo sport in which a rider attempts to stay mounted on a bucking bull for eight seconds while the animal tries to buck them off. Often called "the most dangerous eight seconds in sports", it is the signature event of professional rodeo and of the Professional Bull Riders (PBR) circuit.

Rules and scoring

The rider grips a braided bull rope wrapped around the bull's chest with one gloved hand; the free hand must not touch the bull or the rope. A qualified ride lasts eight seconds, after which judges award up to 100 points — half for the rider's control and style, half for the bull's bucking power, spin and kick. Touching the ground before the buzzer, or "slapping" the bull with the free hand, results in no score.

Equipment and animals

Riders wear a protective vest, chaps and often a helmet or cowboy hat. Rodeo bulls are selectively bred for bucking ability and are valuable athletes themselves; celebrated bulls such as Bodacious and Bushwacker achieved fame comparable to champion riders. A flank strap encourages kicking but does not harm the animal.

History and organisations

Bull riding evolved from Mexican charreada and 19th-century ranch contests into a standard rodeo event. The PBR, founded in 1992 by twenty riders, turned it into a standalone televised sport with events in the United States, Brazil, Australia, Canada and Mexico. Champions such as Lane Frost — killed by a bull at Cheyenne in 1989 — and Adriano Moraes became icons of the sport.

Risks

Bull riding has the highest injury rate in rodeo, including concussions, fractures and trampling injuries; protective gear and sports-medicine crews (famously the Justin Sportsmedicine team) are standard at professional events.
