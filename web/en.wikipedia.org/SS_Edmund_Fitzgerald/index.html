SS Edmund Fitzgerald was an American Great Lakes freighter that sank in Lake Superior during a storm on 10 November 1975, with the loss of the entire crew of 29 men. When launched on 7 June 1958, she was the largest ship on North America's Great Lakes, and remains the largest to have sunk there. She was carrying a full cargo of taconite iron ore pellets from Superior, Wisconsin, toward a steel mill near Detroit when she foundered in Canadian waters about 17 miles (27 km) from Whitefish Point, Michigan.

The Edmund Fitzgerald was the largest ship on the Great Lakes when launched.

The sinking

The Fitzgerald departed on 9 November 1975 into deteriorating weather. On the afternoon of 10 November she was battered by hurricane-force winds and waves reported at up to 35 feet; her captain, Ernest McSorley, reported flooding, a list, and the loss of both radars, but sent no distress call. Her last message, at 7:10 p.m., was "We are holding our own." Minutes later she vanished from the radar of the following ship, Arthur M. Anderson. The wreck, found four days later in 530 feet of water, had broken in two; the exact cause of the sinking remains debated. The disaster was commemorated in Gordon Lightfoot's 1976 ballad "The Wreck of the Edmund Fitzgerald".

Legacy

The sinking led to changes in Great Lakes shipping regulations, including requirements for survival suits, freeboard, and emergency position-indicating radio beacons. The ship's bell was recovered in 1995 and is displayed at the Great Lakes Shipwreck Museum at Whitefish Point, where a memorial service is held each 10 November.
