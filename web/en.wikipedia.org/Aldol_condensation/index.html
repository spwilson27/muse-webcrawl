Aldol condensation

An aldol condensation is a reaction in organic chemistry in which an enol or enolate ion reacts with a carbonyl compound to form a β-hydroxyaldehyde or β-hydroxyketone (an aldol), often followed by dehydration to give an α,β-unsaturated carbonyl compound. It is one of the most important carbon–carbon bond-forming reactions.

Mechanism

Under basic conditions, a base abstracts an α-hydrogen from an aldehyde or ketone to form an enolate, which attacks the carbonyl carbon of a second molecule. Protonation gives the aldol addition product; heating usually drives dehydration to the conjugated enal or enone. Acid-catalyzed versions proceed through the enol.

Variants and importance

Crossed (mixed) aldol reactions between two different carbonyl compounds, intramolecular aldols in ring synthesis, and the related Claisen condensation are widely used. Industrial applications include the production of solvents and plasticizer alcohols; the reaction class is named after the aldol addition first described by Alexander Borodin and Charles-Adolphe Wurtz in the 1870s.
