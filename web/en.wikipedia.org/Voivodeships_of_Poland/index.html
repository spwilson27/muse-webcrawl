Voivodeships of Poland

A voivodeship (Polish: województwo) is the highest-level administrative division of Poland, corresponding to a province in many other countries. Since 1 January 1999 Poland has been divided into 16 voivodeships, replacing the 49 smaller voivodeships that existed from 1975 to 1998.

Government

Each voivodeship has a dual administration: a voivode (wojewoda), appointed by the central government to represent it in the region, and an elected regional assembly (sejmik) with an executive board headed by a marshal (marszałek). Voivodeships are subdivided into counties (powiaty) and then municipalities (gminy).

The largest voivodeship by area is Masovian, which is also the most populous, with over five million inhabitants; the smallest and least populous is Opole. The term derives from wojewoda, historically a military commander or governor.
