Mahanadi

Length~900 km
SourceChhattisgarh
MouthBay of Bengal, Odisha
Basin size~142,000 km²

The Mahanadi is a major river in east-central India. It rises in the highlands of Chhattisgarh, flows eastward through Odisha, and empties into the Bay of Bengal, draining an area of about 142,000 square kilometres. Its name derives from Sanskrit mahā (great) and nadī (river).

Course

The upper basin is a saucer-shaped plateau rimmed by hills including the Eastern Ghats. Major tributaries include the Seonath, Jonk, Hasdeo, Mand, Ib, Ong and Tel. At Sambalpur the river is impounded by the Hirakud Dam, one of the longest earthen dams in the world. Below Cuttack the Mahanadi splits into a delta shared with the Brahmani and Baitarani rivers before reaching the sea near Paradip.

Significance

The river irrigates extensive rice lands in Chhattisgarh and coastal Odisha, supports navigation and fisheries, and is prone to severe flooding in its delta. Inter-state water sharing between Chhattisgarh and Odisha has been the subject of legal dispute.
