Ray Harryhausen

Raymond Frederick Harryhausen
Born: June 29, 1920, Los Angeles, California, U.S.
Died: May 7, 2013 (aged 92), London, England
Occupation: Animator, special effects artist, producer
Known for: Stop-motion animation; "Dynamation"

Raymond Frederick Harryhausen (June 29, 1920 – May 7, 2013) was an American animator and special effects creator who created a form of stop-motion model animation known as Dynamation. His best-known works include the animation for Mighty Joe Young (1949), with his mentor Willis H. O'Brien, which won the Academy Award for Best Visual Effects; The 7th Voyage of Sinbad (1958), his first color film; and Jason and the Argonauts (1963), featuring a famous sword fight against skeleton warriors.

Early life

Harryhausen was inspired by King Kong (1933), whose stop-motion effects by Willis O'Brien he saw as a teenager. He began experimenting with his own models and homemade animation stands, and his early work on a short film, Evolution of the World, drew the attention of O'Brien, who encouraged him.

Career

During World War II Harryhausen worked in Frank Capra's Special Services Division making training films. After the war he joined O'Brien on Mighty Joe Young. His solo breakthrough came with The Beast from 20,000 Fathoms (1953), followed by It Came from Beneath the Sea (1955) and Earth vs. the Flying Saucers (1956), the latter two beginning his long partnership with producer Charles H. Schneer.

Their collaboration produced the Sinbad trilogy — The 7th Voyage of Sinbad (1958), The Golden Voyage of Sinbad (1973) and Sinbad and the Eye of the Tiger (1977) — along with The 3 Worlds of Gulliver (1960), Mysterious Island (1961), Jason and the Argonauts (1963), First Men in the Moon (1964), One Million Years B.C. (1966), The Valley of Gwangi (1969) and his final film, Clash of the Titans (1981).

Legacy

Harryhausen's work influenced generations of filmmakers, including Steven Spielberg, George Lucas, Peter Jackson and Tim Burton. In 1992 he received an Academy Honorary Award (the Gordon E. Sawyer Award) for technical achievement. He died in London on May 7, 2013, aged 92.
