Medical guideline

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A medical guideline (also called a clinical guideline, standard treatment guideline, or clinical practice guideline) is a document with the aim of guiding decisions and criteria regarding diagnosis, management and treatment in specific areas of healthcare. Such documents have been in use for thousands of years, but modern guidelines are built on an examination of current evidence within the paradigm of evidence-based medicine.

Development

Guidelines are usually produced by professional societies, government agencies or expert panels that systematically review the literature, grade the quality of evidence and the strength of recommendations, and manage conflicts of interest. Methods such as the GRADE approach are widely used.

Purpose and limitations

Guidelines aim to standardise care, reduce unwarranted variation and improve outcomes. They are not intended to replace clinical judgement for individual patients, and they may lag behind new evidence or reflect the values and resources of particular health systems.

Examples

Well-known guidelines cover topics such as hypertension, diabetes, cancer screening and antimicrobial prescribing, and are issued by bodies including the World Health Organization and national institutes.
