Widener Library

Widener Library
Location: Harvard Yard, Cambridge, Massachusetts
Opened: 1915
Architect: Horace Trumbauer
Donor: Eleanor Elkins Widener
Stacks: about 57 miles (92 km) of shelves

The Harry Elkins Widener Memorial Library, commonly called Widener Library, is the central library of the Harvard Library system at Harvard University. It was built with a gift from Eleanor Elkins Widener in memory of her son Harry, a Harvard alumnus and book collector who died in the sinking of the RMS Titanic in 1912. The library opened on June 24, 1915.

Building and collections

Designed by Horace Trumbauer in the Beaux-Arts style, the building fronts Harvard Yard with a broad staircase and Ionic colonnade. Its ten levels of stacks provide some 57 miles of shelving, making it one of the largest academic library buildings in the world. The Harry Elkins Widener Collection — Harry's own rare books, including a Gutenberg Bible and early Shakespeare editions — is preserved in the Memorial Room. By the terms of the gift, the building's exterior may never be altered.

Legends

Campus folklore holds that the donor required all Harvard students to pass a swimming test because Harry might have survived had he known how to swim; in reality a physical education requirement existed but the swimming-test story is largely a myth. The library's stacks are also the setting for persistent student ghost lore.
