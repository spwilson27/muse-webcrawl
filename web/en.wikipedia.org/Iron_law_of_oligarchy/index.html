Iron law of oligarchy

The iron law of oligarchy is a political theory first developed by the German-born Italian sociologist Robert Michels in his 1911 book Political Parties. It asserts that rule by an elite, or oligarchy, is inevitable as an "iron law" within any democratic organisation as part of the "tactical and technical necessities" of organisation.

Michels' argument

Michels based his theory on a study of socialist parties and trade unions — organisations committed to internal democracy. He argued that large organisations require professional leadership and bureaucracy; leaders acquire expertise, control of communications, and material interests distinct from the rank and file, and consequently act to preserve their own positions. His famous formulation: "Who says organisation, says oligarchy."

Criticism and influence

Critics note counter-examples of organisations that sustain internal democracy and point out that Michels' law is a tendency rather than an inevitability. The concept remains influential in political science and organisation theory, and is echoed in later ideas such as the tendency of bureaucracies to self-perpetuate.
