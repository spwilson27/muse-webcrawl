Continental O-300

The Continental O-300 and the C145 are a family of air-cooled, horizontally opposed six-cylinder aircraft engines built by Continental Motors. First produced in 1947, engines in this family are typically rated at 145 hp (108 kW). The geared variant is designated the GO-300. The O-300 is best known for powering the early Cessna 172, one of the most produced aircraft in history.

Design and development

The engine was developed from the earlier C115/C125 six-cylinder line by increasing the cylinder head size and displacement to 301 cubic inches (4.9 L). It used a carburettor and a single magneto-ignition arrangement with dual spark plugs per cylinder. The GO-300 added a reduction gearbox allowing the engine to run at higher rpm while turning the propeller more slowly; it powered the Cessna 175 Skylark, though it gained a reputation for requiring careful operation.

Variants

Variants included the C145-2, the O-300-A through O-300-E with differing mounts and accessories, and the Rolls-Royce-built O-300 engines produced under licence in the United Kingdom for aircraft such as the Beagle Pup.

Specifications (O-300-A)

Power output was about 145 hp at 2,700 rpm, with a bore of 4.0625 in, stroke of 3.875 in and compression ratio of 7.0:1. Dry weight was roughly 268 lb (122 kg).
