Brigadier general

One-star insignia of a U.S. Army brigadier general

Brigadier general is a senior military rank used in many countries; it is typically the lowest rank of general officer and commands a brigade or serves in senior staff appointments. In the United States it is the rank of a one-star general (pay grade O-7) in the Army, Air Force, Marine Corps, and Space Force. Its naval equivalent is rear admiral (lower half).

History

The title derives from the French brigadier des armées du roi, an appointment created in the seventeenth century for officers commanding brigades. The rank spread through European armies and to the American Continental Army, where brigadier generals commanded brigades under major generals during the Revolutionary War.

By country

In the United States, brigadier general is the first general-officer grade; promotion requires presidential nomination and Senate confirmation. In the United Kingdom, the corresponding appointment is brigadier — technically not classed as a general officer in the British Army but a one-star rank in NATO terms. France uses général de brigade. Many Commonwealth and European forces use brigadier general as the OF-6 grade in the NATO rank system.

Duties

Brigadier generals typically command brigades of several thousand soldiers, serve as deputy commanders of divisions, or direct staff directorates and training establishments. The rank is a key gateway in a general officer's career: only a fraction of colonels are selected for promotion.
