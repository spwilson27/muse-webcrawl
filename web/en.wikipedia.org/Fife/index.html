Fife

Fife (Scottish Gaelic: Fìobha) is a council area and historic county in Scotland, lying between the Firth of Tay and the Firth of Forth. It is Scotland's third most populous local authority area, with a population of around 375,000. Its largest towns include Kirkcaldy, Dunfermline and Glenrothes, and it is home to St Andrews, famed for its university — the oldest in Scotland — and as the "home of golf".

Geography

Fife forms a peninsula bounded by the Firth of Tay to the north, the Firth of Forth to the south and the North Sea to the east. The Lomond Hills rise in the centre, and the Ochil Hills run along the west. The region is crossed by the Forth Bridges and the Tay Road Bridge, linking it to Edinburgh and Dundee respectively. The East Neuk, a chain of picturesque fishing villages along the north shore of the Forth — including Crail, Anstruther and Pittenweem — is noted for its distinctive crow-stepped, red-tiled architecture.

History

Fife was one of the major Pictish kingdoms, known as Fib, and its separate identity has earned it the enduring nickname the "Kingdom of Fife". Dunfermline was a royal seat and burial place of Scottish monarchs. Coal mining and linen manufacture drove industrial growth in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries; the new town of Glenrothes was designated in 1948 partly to serve the coal industry. The Rosyth dockyard and the former naval base remain significant employers.

Economy and culture

Golf tourism, higher education, food and drink, and renewable energy are important sectors. Fife is also remembered as the birthplace of economist Adam Smith (Kirkcaldy) and architect Robert Adam (Kirkcaldy).
