Arnold Johannes Wilhelm Sommerfeld (5 December 1868 – 26 April 1951) was a German theoretical physicist who pioneered developments in atomic and quantum physics, and who educated and mentored more recipients of the Nobel Prize in physics than any other supervisor before or since. He introduced the azimuthal quantum number (orbit eccentricity) and the fine-structure constant.

Career

Sommerfeld studied mathematics and physical sciences at the Albertina (University of Königsberg), completing his doctorate in 1891 under Ferdinand von Lindemann. After posts in Göttingen, Clausthal and Aachen, he became professor of theoretical physics at the University of Munich in 1906, where he built one of the world's leading schools of theoretical physics.

Contributions

In 1916 Sommerfeld extended Niels Bohr's atomic model to elliptical orbits and relativistic corrections, yielding the fine-structure formula involving the constant α ≈ 1/137 that he introduced. His multi-volume textbook Atombau und Spektrallinien ("Atomic Structure and Spectral Lines", 1919) became the "bible" of the old quantum theory. With Paul Drude's model as a basis, he developed the quantum (Sommerfeld) theory of electrons in metals in 1927–28. He also contributed to X-ray theory, hydrodynamics (the Orr–Sommerfeld equation), diffraction theory and the theory of the gyroscope.

Honours and legacy

Sommerfeld was nominated for the Nobel Prize 84 times — more than any other physicist — but never received it. His students Heisenberg, Pauli, Debye and Bethe all became laureates. He died in Munich in 1951 from injuries sustained in a traffic accident while walking with his grandchildren.
