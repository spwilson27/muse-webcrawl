Poale Zion

Poale Zion combined Jewish national revival with socialist politics.

Poale Zion (Hebrew: פועלי ציון, "Workers of Zion"; also spelled Poalei Zion) was a movement of Marxist–Zionist Jewish workers founded in various places in Europe and North America at the beginning of the twentieth century, following the Bund's rejection of Zionism in 1901. Poale Zion parties sought to combine the creation of a Jewish homeland in Palestine with socialist principles, and their members were among the principal builders of the Jewish labour movement in Ottoman and Mandatory Palestine — the nucleus from which the Israeli Labour Party and the Israeli labour establishment emerged.

Ideology

The movement's chief theoretician was Ber Borochov (1881–1917), who argued on Marxist grounds that the economic structure of Jewish life in the diaspora was an "inverted pyramid" — with too many Jews in commerce and too few in primary production — and that only territorial concentration in Palestine would allow a normal class structure and a genuine class struggle to develop. This theory provided a Marxist rationale for Zionism that distinguished Poale Zion from both the socialist Bund, which sought cultural autonomy in the diaspora, and non-socialist Zionist parties.

History

Poale Zion groups formed from about 1900 in Russia, Austria-Hungary, the United States, Britain, Argentina, and elsewhere, uniting in a world union in 1907. In Palestine, members of the Second Aliyah — including David Ben-Gurion and Yitzhak Ben-Zvi — founded local organisations that in 1919 merged into Ahdut HaAvoda. After disputes over relations with the Comintern, the movement split into left and right wings in 1920; the right wing joined the World Zionist Organization and the Socialist International. In Palestine, the party's successors united with Hapoel Hatzair in 1930 to form Mapai, the dominant party of the Yishuv and early Israel. Poale Zion organisations abroad continued as affiliates of the World Union of Zionists and of Labour Zionism; the British Poale Zion, founded in 1903, remains affiliated to the UK Labour Party as the Jewish Labour Movement.

Legacy

Through the Histadrut trade-union federation, the kibbutz movement, and Mapai, Poale Zion's blend of nationalism and socialism shaped the institutions of the State of Israel for its first three decades.
