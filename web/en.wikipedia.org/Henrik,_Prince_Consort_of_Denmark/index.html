Henrik, Prince Consort of Denmark (born Henri Marie Jean André de Laborde de Monpezat; 11 June 1934 – 13 February 2018) was the husband of Queen Margrethe II of Denmark. A French diplomat by career, he became prince consort when his wife acceded to the throne in 1972 and served in the role for more than 46 years, until his death — and remains the only male consort of a Danish monarch in the modern era.

Early life and career

Henri de Monpezat was born in Talence, near Bordeaux, and spent his early childhood in French Indochina, where his family had business interests. He was educated in France and Vietnam, studied law, political science and Asian languages, and served in the French army during the Algerian War. He joined the French foreign ministry and was posted as a junior diplomat to the embassy in London, where he met the then Crown Princess Margrethe of Denmark. They married in Copenhagen on 10 June 1967; he converted to Lutheranism, became a Danish citizen and took the Danish form of his name, Henrik.

Role as consort

As consort, Henrik undertook official duties, patronages and state visits, and was known for his interests in wine — he ran the Château de Cayx estate in Cahors, France — poetry, art, cookery and dachshunds. He published several books in Danish and French, including poetry collections and memoirs, and translated Simone de Beauvoir into Danish with the Queen. At the same time he was frank, sometimes controversially, about his dissatisfaction with his rank: as a male consort he held the title of Prince rather than King, and in 2016 he retired from most official duties and relinquished the title Prince Consort (in Danish usage), being styled thereafter simply as Prince Henrik.

Death and burial

In 2017 the court announced that Henrik suffered from dementia, and he further declared that he did not wish to be buried beside the Queen in Roskilde Cathedral, a longstanding grievance over his status. He died at Fredensborg Palace on 13 February 2018, aged 83. After a funeral in the Christiansborg Palace Chapel, his ashes were partly scattered at sea and partly interred in the private garden at Fredensborg, in accordance with his wishes.
