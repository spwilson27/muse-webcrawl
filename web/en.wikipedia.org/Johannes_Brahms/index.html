Johannes Brahms (7 May 1833 – 3 April 1897) was a German composer, virtuoso pianist and conductor of the mid-Romantic period. His music, rooted in the structures and techniques of the Classical masters, is among the most frequently performed of the Romantic repertoire; he is often grouped with Bach and Beethoven as one of the "Three Bs".

Early life

Born in Hamburg into a Lutheran family, Brahms received his first musical training from his father, a double bassist, and studied piano and composition with Eduard Marxsen. He performed in taverns as a youth to supplement the family income. In 1853, touring with violinist Eduard Reményi, he met Joseph Joachim and then Robert and Clara Schumann in Düsseldorf. Robert Schumann's article "Neue Bahnen" ("New Paths") in the Neue Zeitschrift für Musik proclaimed the 20-year-old a genius — praise that burdened Brahms with expectations of becoming Beethoven's successor. His close, lifelong relationship with Clara Schumann endured after Robert's death in 1856.

Mature career

Brahms worked as a conductor in Detmold and Hamburg, then settled in Vienna in 1862, becoming director of the Gesellschaft der Musikfreunde concerts in the 1870s. The success of A German Requiem (1868), written after his mother's death, established his reputation. Long delaying a symphony under Beethoven's shadow, he completed his Symphony No. 1 in 1876, followed by three more symphonies (1877, 1883, 1885), the Violin Concerto (1878, for Joachim), two piano concertos, the Academic Festival Overture and Tragic Overture.

Music and legacy

Brahms wrote extensively for piano, chamber ensembles, orchestra and voice, including more than 200 songs. His technique of "developing variation" (Schoenberg's phrase, from the essay "Brahms the Progressive") influenced later composers. In the so-called War of the Romantics he was cast as the conservative champion against Wagner and Liszt, though he privately admired Wagner's music. His late works — the clarinet quintet and sonatas inspired by Richard Mühlfeld, and the piano pieces Opp. 116–119 — are noted for their autumnal introspection. He died of liver cancer in Vienna in 1897 and was buried in the Zentralfriedhof near Beethoven and Schubert.
