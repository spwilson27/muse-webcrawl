DeFuniak Springs, Florida

DeFuniak Springs is a city in Walton County, Florida, United States, and its county seat. The population was about 5,900 at the 2020 census. The town is best known for Lake DeFuniak, one of only two naturally occurring, almost perfectly circular spring-fed lakes in the world, and for its well-preserved Victorian-era historic district.

History

DeFuniak Springs was founded in the early 1880s as a stop on the Pensacola and Atlantic Railroad and named for Frederick R. De Funiak, a railroad official. The town grew rapidly after 1885, when the Florida Chautauqua Association chose it as the winter home of its educational and cultural assembly, drawing thousands of visitors for lectures, concerts, and classes. The Chautauqua Hall of Brotherhood, built in 1909, still stands on the lake shore.

Geography and landmarks

The city lies in the Florida Panhandle along Interstate 10 and U.S. Route 90. Circle Drive, which rings Lake DeFuniak, is lined with historic homes and buildings, including the Walton County Heritage Museum, housed in the former railroad depot, and the Walton–DeFuniak Library, founded in 1886 and among Florida's oldest libraries still in operation. Nearby are Ponce de Leon Springs State Park and the Britton Hill, the highest natural point in Florida at 345 feet above sea level.
