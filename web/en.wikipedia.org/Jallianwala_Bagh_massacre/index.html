Jallianwala Bagh massacre

Date13 April 1919
LocationJallianwala Bagh, Amritsar, Punjab, British India
PerpetratorBritish Indian Army under Brig.-Gen. Reginald Dyer
Deaths379 (official); estimates up to 1,000+

The Jallianwala Bagh massacre, also known as the Amritsar massacre, took place on 13 April 1919, when troops of the British Indian Army under Brigadier-General Reginald Dyer fired on a large crowd of unarmed Indian civilians gathered in Jallianwala Bagh, a walled garden in Amritsar, Punjab.

Background

The gathering took place during the festival of Baisakhi; many attendees were protesting the arrest of pro-independence leaders under the repressive Rowlatt Act, while others were pilgrims unaware of a ban on meetings. The garden's narrow entrances were blocked, leaving the crowd few escape routes.

The shooting

Dyer ordered his soldiers to fire on the densest parts of the crowd without warning. Firing continued for about ten minutes, until ammunition was nearly exhausted. The official British inquiry reported 379 dead; Indian estimates put the death toll at 1,000 or more, with many more wounded.

Aftermath

The massacre shocked India and Britain alike and became a turning point in the Indian independence movement, hardening opinion against colonial rule. Dyer was removed from his command but defended by many in Britain. The site is now a national memorial in India.
