Stent

An expanded coronary stent — a wire-mesh tube that holds a narrowed artery open.

A stent is a small mesh tube inserted into a narrowed or weakened passageway in the body to keep it open. Stents are most widely used in coronary arteries narrowed by atherosclerosis, but they are also placed in other blood vessels, the bile duct, oesophagus, colon, trachea and urinary tract.

Coronary stents

In percutaneous coronary intervention, a stent mounted on a balloon catheter is guided to the blockage; inflating the balloon expands the stent against the vessel wall, and the balloon is then withdrawn, leaving the stent permanently in place. Self-expanding stents are used in peripheral vessels. Patients typically receive antiplatelet therapy afterwards to prevent clotting on the device.

Types

Bare-metal stents are made of stainless steel, cobalt–chromium or nitinol. Drug-eluting stents are coated with a polymer that slowly releases an antiproliferative drug (such as sirolimus or paclitaxel), greatly reducing restenosis — the re-narrowing of the vessel by scar tissue that affected a substantial share of early procedures. Bioresorbable scaffolds, designed to dissolve after the vessel heals, have also been developed.

History

Charles Stent was a 19th-century English dentist whose name was given to a dental impression compound; the term later transferred to devices that mould or support tissue. Julio Palmaz developed the balloon-expandable vascular stent in the 1980s, and the first coronary implants followed soon after, transforming the treatment of coronary artery disease.
