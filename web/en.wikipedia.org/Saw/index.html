Saw


Image placeholder: Saw

A saw is a tool consisting of a tough blade, wire, or chain with a hard toothed edge, used to cut through material, most often wood, metal, or stone. The cut is made by placing the toothed edge against the material and moving it forcefully back and forth (or continuously), so that each tooth shears away a small chip of material. Saws are among the oldest cutting tools, known since the Neolithic period in flint form and made in copper and bronze in the ancient world.

Types

Hand saws include the crosscut saw and rip saw for woodworking, the hacksaw for metal, the coping saw for intricate curves, the Japanese pull saw, and the backsaw for joinery. Powered saws include the circular saw, reciprocating saw, jigsaw, band saw, chainsaw, table saw, mitre saw and tile saw. Teeth may be set (bent alternately) to clear a kerf wider than the blade, and are characterized by their count in teeth per inch and their geometry — rip teeth act like chisels, while crosscut teeth act like knives.

Materials and construction

Blades are typically made from high-carbon or high-speed steel; abrasive saws use diamond- or carbide-grit edges for masonry and tile. Specialty surgical saws are used in medicine for cutting bone. The sound of certain saws has even been used as a musical instrument — the musical saw is played with a bow.
