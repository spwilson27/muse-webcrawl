The Battle of Gravelotte, also called the Battle of Gravelotte–St Privat, was the largest battle of the Franco-Prussian War. It was fought on 18 August 1870 between the German armies of the North German Confederation under Field Marshal Helmuth von Moltke and the French Army of the Rhine under Marshal François Achille Bazaine. The French, holding a strong defensive position west of Metz, repulsed repeated German attacks at heavy cost, but Bazaine withdrew into the fortress of Metz that night, where his army was besieged and eventually forced to surrender.

Background

After the battles of Mars-la-Tour and the fighting at Borny earlier in August, Bazaine's Army of the Rhine began retreating toward Verdun and the interior of France. German forces intercepted the retreat, and Bazaine halted his army on a strong ridge line running from Gravelotte through St Privat, west of Metz. Moltke brought up the First Army under Steinmetz and the Second Army under Prince Friedrich Karl of Prussia, totalling over 188,000 men — the largest concentration of the war.

The battle

The French position, anchored on farm strongpoints and villages and defended by troops armed with the superior Chassepot rifle and mitrailleuse machine guns, proved formidable. German frontal attacks across open slopes, particularly at St Privat by the Prussian Guard, suffered devastating losses; the Guard lost some 8,000 men in about twenty minutes. Steinmetz's First Army attacked prematurely and was repulsed at Gravelotte in the south. In the evening, however, the Saxon XII Corps turned the French right flank at Roncourt, and a final assault by the Prussian Guard and II Corps carried St Privat, unhinging the whole French position.

Aftermath

Bazaine ordered a withdrawal into Metz during the night of 18–19 August. The siege of Metz that followed trapped France's largest field army; Bazaine surrendered on 27 October with some 173,000 men. The road to Paris lay open, and the war's outcome was effectively decided even before the Battle of Sedan on 1 September. Gravelotte was the bloodiest battle of the Franco-Prussian War, with combined casualties of over 32,000; the Prussian Guard's sacrifice at St Privat is still commemorated by German regimental tradition.
