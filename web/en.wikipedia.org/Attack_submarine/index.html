Attack submarine

Type: Submarine

Role: Anti-submarine and anti-surface warfare, strike, surveillance

US hull classification: SSN (nuclear), SSK (conventional hunter-killer)

An attack submarine or hunter-killer submarine is a submarine specifically designed for the purpose of attacking and sinking other submarines, surface combatants, and merchant vessels. In the United States Navy, nuclear-powered attack submarines carry the hull classification symbol SSN; in the Soviet and Russian navies they were the primary means of opposing Western ballistic missile submarines.

History

During the Second World War, fleet submarines were used chiefly against surface shipping. The post-war advent of the nuclear-powered USS Nautilus (1955) created a submarine able to remain submerged indefinitely and fight other submarines underwater, prompting the dedicated attack-submarine role. Cold War classes such as the American Los Angeles class and the Soviet Victor and Akula classes were designed for quieting, speed, and advanced sonar.

Armament and missions

Attack submarines are typically armed with torpedoes and tube-launched anti-ship and land-attack cruise missiles; many modern designs add vertical launch tubes. Beyond naval combat, they conduct intelligence gathering, support special forces insertion, lay mines, and perform long-range precision strike with missiles such as Tomahawk.

Propulsion

The United States, United Kingdom, France, Russia, China, and India operate nuclear attack submarines, which offer unlimited endurance but at high cost. Many other navies operate quiet diesel-electric or air-independent propulsion boats (often designated SSK), which are particularly effective in littoral waters.
