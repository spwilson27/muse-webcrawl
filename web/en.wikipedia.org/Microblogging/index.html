Microblogging

TypeOnline broadcast medium
EmergenceMid-2000s
ExamplesTwitter/X, Tumblr, Mastodon, Weibo

Microblogging is an online broadcast medium that exists as a specific form of blogging. A micro-blog differs from a traditional blog in that its content is typically smaller in both actual and aggregated file size. Micro-blogs "allow users to exchange small elements of content such as short sentences, individual images, or video links", which may be the major reason for their popularity.

Services

The first micro-blogs were known as tumblelogs, a term coined in 2005. Services such as Twitter, launched in 2006, popularised the format with short posts originally limited to 140 characters, modelled on SMS text messages. Other prominent services include China's Sina Weibo, the multimedia-oriented Tumblr, and federated open-source networks such as Mastodon, which run on the ActivityPub protocol. Features commonly include hashtags, re-posting ("retweeting"), direct messages, trending topics and follower relationships.

Use and impact

Microblogging has been used for breaking news, political organising, emergency communication, marketing and live event commentary. Its speed and reach have made it influential in protest movements and disaster response, while researchers have also studied its role in spreading misinformation and shaping public discourse. In organisations, status-update services have been adapted for internal communication and knowledge sharing.
