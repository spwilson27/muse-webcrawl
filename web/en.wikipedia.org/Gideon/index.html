Gideon

Gideon (Hebrew: גִּדְעוֹן, Gidʿon), also named Jerubbaal, is a military leader, judge, and prophet of ancient Israel whose story is told in chapters 6–8 of the Book of Judges in the Hebrew Bible. He is remembered for defeating a vastly larger Midianite army with only three hundred men.

Biblical narrative

Gideon, son of Joash of the tribe of Manasseh, was called by an angel while threshing wheat in hiding from Midianite raiders who had oppressed Israel for seven years. Doubtful of his calling, he asked for signs, most famously the miracle of the fleece: a wool fleece left overnight was wet with dew while the ground was dry, and the following night the reverse. After tearing down his father's altar to Baal — earning the name Jerubbaal, "let Baal contend" — Gideon assembled an army of 32,000. God reduced it, first by sending home the fearful and then by selecting only the three hundred men who lapped water from their hands at a spring, so that Israel could not claim victory by its own strength.

Victory over Midian

Attacking the Midianite camp at night, Gideon's men surrounded it blowing trumpets, smashing pitchers, and waving torches while shouting "The sword of the LORD and of Gideon!", throwing the camp into panic and self-destruction. The fleeing Midianite kings Zebah and Zalmunna were captured and executed. Afterwards the Israelites asked Gideon to rule over them, but he refused, declaring that the LORD would rule them; he made a golden ephod from the spoils which, the text says, became an object of idolatry. Israel had rest for forty years in his days. Gideon had seventy sons by many wives and another, Abimelech, by a concubine in Shechem; Abimelech's bloody attempt to make himself king follows in Judges 9. Gideon is listed among the heroes of faith in the New Testament (Hebrews 11).

Legacy

Gideon gives his name to Gideons International, the organisation that places Bibles in hotels, and "Gideon's army" remains a byword for a small force achieving victory against great odds.
