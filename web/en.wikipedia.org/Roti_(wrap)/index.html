Roti (wrap)

A roti wrap is a style of wrap sandwich made with a roti — an unleavened flatbread originating in the Indian subcontinent — rolled around a savoury filling. It is closely associated with Caribbean cuisine in Trinidad and Tobago and Guyana, where Indian indentured labourers brought roti traditions in the 19th century, and with modern South Asian street food.

Varieties

In Trinidad and Tobago, the classic roti wrap uses a paratha roti (also called "buss up shut" for its flaky, torn texture) or dhalpuri roti wrapped around curried goat, chicken, shrimp or vegetables. The wrap is folded so the curry is fully enclosed and eaten by hand. In Guyana, roti is more often served alongside curry rather than wrapped, though wraps are common in roti shops abroad.

Kati roll and frankie

Related Indian forms include the kati roll of Kolkata, in which kebab-style fillings are rolled in paratha, and the Mumbai frankie, a roti or naan wrap with spiced fillings, egg coating and chutneys. In South African Indian communities, the roti wrap appears as the "chip and cheese roti" and other Durban variants. Fast-food adaptations, such as the roti wraps sold by Caribbean roti shops in Toronto, London and New York, have spread the format internationally.
