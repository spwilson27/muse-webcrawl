Gustav Kirchhoff

Gustav Robert Kirchhoff (12 March 1824 – 17 October 1887) was a German physicist and mathematician who contributed to the fundamental understanding of electrical circuits, spectroscopy and black-body radiation.

Gustav Kirchhoff

Born: 12 March 1824, Königsberg
Died: 17 October 1887, Berlin
Known for: Kirchhoff's circuit laws, spectroscopy laws

Work

As a student he formulated Kirchhoff's circuit laws (1845) for current and voltage. At Heidelberg with Robert Bunsen he founded spectrum analysis, discovering caesium and rubidium and explaining Fraunhofer lines in the solar spectrum. His three laws of spectroscopy and his law of thermal radiation — a good absorber is a good emitter — laid groundwork for quantum theory.

Career

He held posts at Breslau, Heidelberg and Berlin. Students included many leading physicists of the era. See also chemistry.
