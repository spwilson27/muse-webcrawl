Canadian Arctic

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Canadian Arctic is the portion of the Arctic region administered by Canada, comprising the vast territory north of the mainland's treeline, including the Arctic Archipelago and the northern mainland coasts of the Arctic Ocean. It covers roughly 40 percent of Canada's total land area and includes parts of Yukon, the Northwest Territories, and Nunavut—the latter created in 1999 as Canada's newest and largest territory.

Geography

The Arctic Archipelago contains some of the world's largest islands, including Baffin Island, Victoria Island, and Ellesmere Island. Canada's northernmost point, Cape Columbia on Ellesmere Island, lies about 769 kilometres from the North Pole. The region is threaded by the Northwest Passage, a sea route through the archipelago connecting the Atlantic and Pacific oceans, whose status as Canadian internal waters is disputed by some countries, notably the United States, which regards it as an international strait.

Climate and environment

Much of the Canadian Arctic has a polar climate with long, severe winters and brief, cool summers. Permafrost underlies most of the land, and vegetation consists mainly of tundra—mosses, lichens, sedges, and low shrubs. Wildlife includes polar bears, muskoxen, caribou, Arctic foxes, narwhals, and beluga whales. The region is undergoing rapid warming, with rates of temperature increase several times the global average and progressive loss of summer sea ice.

People

The Canadian Arctic is home to Inuit communities across the Inuit Nunangat homeland, as well as Dene and other Indigenous peoples and a smaller population of non-Indigenous residents in territorial capitals such as Yellowknife, Whitehorse, and Iqaluit. Settlement is concentrated in small, fly-in coastal communities supplied by annual sealift.

Sovereignty

Canada's claim to the Arctic derives from the British transfer of Arctic territories in 1880 and the sector principle asserted in the early 20th century. Sovereignty has been exercised through RCMP patrols, the establishment of settlements, the Canadian Rangers, and periodic military exercises. Hans Island, disputed with Denmark for decades, was partitioned by agreement in 2022.
