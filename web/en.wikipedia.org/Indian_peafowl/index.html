Indian peafowl

Scientific namePavo cristatus
FamilyPhasianidae
Native rangeIndian subcontinent
StatusLeast Concern (IUCN)

The Indian peafowl (Pavo cristatus), also known as the common peafowl or blue peafowl, is a large pheasant native to the Indian subcontinent. The male, the peacock, is famed for its iridescent blue plumage and spectacular train of elongated upper-tail coverts marked with eyespots, fanned in courtship display. It is the national bird of India.

Description

Males measure up to about 2.3 metres including the train and weigh 4–6 kg; females (peahens) are smaller, mottled brown, and lack the train. Both sexes bear a crest of bare-shafted, fan-tipped feathers. The loud, piercing calls of the peacock carry over long distances, especially during the monsoon breeding season.

Habitat and behaviour

Indian peafowl inhabit open forest, scrub and cultivated land near water, feeding on seeds, fruits, insects and small animals. They roost in trees at night and forage in small groups. During courtship the male fans his train, quivering the feathers to produce a rustling display before the hen. Females lay 3–5 eggs in a ground scrape.

Cultural significance

The peafowl is deeply woven into South Asian religion and art: it is the mount of the god Kartikeya, associated with Krishna, who wears its feather, and it appears in Mughal and Rajput painting. Protected under Indian law, the species has been introduced to many countries as an ornamental bird.
