William S. Clark

BornJuly 31, 1826, Ashfield, Massachusetts
DiedMarch 9, 1886 (aged 59), Amherst, Massachusetts
OccupationEducator, chemist, soldier
Known forFounding head of Sapporo Agricultural College, Japan

William Smith Clark (July 31, 1826 – March 9, 1886) was an American professor of chemistry and botany, a Union Army colonel in the Civil War, and the founding vice-president (acting president) of Sapporo Agricultural College — now Hokkaido University — in Japan, where he is a celebrated figure.

Career

Clark taught at Amherst College and served as president of Massachusetts Agricultural College. In 1876 the Japanese government hired him to help establish an agricultural college in Sapporo, Hokkaido. During his eight months there he introduced American farming methods, curricula, and Christian-influenced moral teaching. His reported farewell words, "Boys, be ambitious!", became one of Japan's most famous phrases, and a statue of Clark stands on Hitsujigaoka hill in Sapporo.
