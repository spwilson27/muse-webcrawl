Omaha (Umoⁿhoⁿ íye) is a Siouan language spoken by the Omaha people of northeastern Nebraska and by Omaha descendants in Oklahoma. It belongs to the Dhegiha branch of Siouan, closely related to Ponca — with which it is nearly mutually intelligible — and more distantly to Osage, Kansa, and Quapaw.

Characteristics

Omaha is a polysynthetic-final language with subject–object–verb word order, nasal vowels, and a distinctive set of positional/stative articles that mark whether referents are standing, sitting, lying, or moving. Nineteenth- and early twentieth-century documentation by James Owen Dorsey and by Alice Fletcher and Francis La Flesche preserved extensive texts.

Endangerment and revitalization

The number of fluent first-language speakers has fallen to a small number of elders, and Omaha is classified as severely endangered. Revitalization efforts include language classes in the Omaha Nation Public School at Macy, Nebraska, immersion programs for children, dictionaries and curricula developed with tribal linguists, and master-apprentice teaching with remaining fluent speakers.
