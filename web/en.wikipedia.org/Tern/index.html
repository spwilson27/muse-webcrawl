Tern

A common tern in flight

Terns are seabirds in the subfamily Sterninae of the family Laridae, which also includes gulls and skimmers. They are found worldwide, mostly near the sea, rivers, and wetlands. Terns are slender, graceful fliers with long pointed wings, forked tails, and sharp bills adapted for plunge-diving on fish.

Description and behaviour

Most terns have light grey or white plumage with a black cap in breeding season. They feed by hovering and diving into the water to catch small fish and invertebrates. Many species are strongly migratory: the Arctic tern (Sterna paradisaea) undertakes the longest known migration of any animal, travelling between Arctic breeding grounds and Antarctic waters each year and seeing two summers annually.

Species

Well-known species include the common tern, Arctic tern, little tern, Caspian tern (the largest), roseate tern, and the sooty tern of tropical oceans. Marsh terns of the genus Chlidonias frequent inland wetlands, while the large-billed tern and noddies show further variation within the group. Several species are threatened by habitat loss and predation at nesting colonies.
