Lübeck

Lübeck is a city in northern Germany, in the state of Schleswig-Holstein, on the Trave River near the Baltic Sea. It is the second-largest city in the state after Kiel, with a population of around 215,000. Its medieval old town, centred on the Altstadtinsel (old town island) enclosed by the Trave, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

Founded in 1143 by Count Adolf II of Schauenburg and Holstein, Lübeck became a founding member and de facto capital of the Hanseatic League, dominating trade in the Baltic for centuries. Its brick Gothic architecture — including the Holstentor gate, St. Mary's Church, and the seven church towers that define its skyline — reflects the wealth of its merchant houses.

Economy and culture

Lübeck is famous for its marzipan, traditionally associated with the Niederegger company, and for its port, the largest German port on the Baltic. The city was the birthplace of writer Thomas Mann and statesman Willy Brandt, and borders on the Baltic resort of Travemünde.
