Northwest Airlines

Northwest Airlines (often abbreviated NWA) was a major United States airline founded in 1926. It was headquartered in Eagan, Minnesota, near Minneapolis–Saint Paul International Airport. Northwest was a pioneer of transpacific aviation, marketing itself for decades as "Northwest Orient Airlines", and became one of the largest carriers across the Pacific. In 2008 it merged with Delta Air Lines; the merger was approved on 29 October 2008, and Northwest ceased operations as an independent airline on 31 January 2010, when its operating certificate was consolidated with Delta's.

History

The airline was founded as Northwest Airways in 1926 by Colonel Lewis Brittin, initially to carry airmail between Minneapolis and Chicago. It began passenger service in 1927 and adopted the name Northwest Airlines in 1934. In 1947 it inaugurated service to Tokyo, Shanghai, and Manila via Alaska and the Aleutian Islands, branding its Pacific network as Northwest Orient. It was among the first airlines to fly the Great Circle route over the North Pacific, dramatically shortening travel times to Asia.

Fleet and network

Northwest operated hubs at Minneapolis/St. Paul, Detroit Metropolitan Airport (where it built the McNamara Terminal in 2002), Memphis, Tokyo Narita, and Amsterdam (in partnership with KLM, with which it formed a pioneering joint venture in 1993). It was the launch customer for the Boeing 747-400, introduced the Airbus A320 family into U.S. service, and ordered the Boeing 787 before the merger transferred the order to Delta. At its peak it carried over 50 million passengers a year.

Merger with Delta

After emerging from Chapter 11 bankruptcy in 2007, Northwest agreed in April 2008 to merge with Delta Air Lines, creating the world's largest airline at the time. The combined carrier retained the Delta name, brand, and Atlanta headquarters. Northwest's WorldPerks loyalty program was merged into Delta's SkyMiles in 2009, and the last flights under the NW code operated in January 2010.
