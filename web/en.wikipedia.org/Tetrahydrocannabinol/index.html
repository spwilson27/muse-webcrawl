Tetrahydrocannabinol

Chemical formulaC21H30O2
Molar mass314.47 g/mol
Legal statusVaries by jurisdiction; Schedule I (US federal)
RoutesOral, inhalation, transdermal

Tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) is a cannabinoid found in the cannabis plant. It is the principal psychoactive constituent of cannabis and one of at least 113 total cannabinoids identified in the plant. The term THC usually refers to the Δ9-THC isomer, chemically named (−)-trans-Δ9-tetrahydrocannabinol. THC was first isolated and its structure elucidated in 1964 by Raphael Mechoulam and colleagues in Israel.

Pharmacology

THC acts as a partial agonist at the CB1 and CB2 cannabinoid receptors, part of the endocannabinoid system. Activation of CB1 receptors in the brain produces the characteristic effects of cannabis: euphoria, altered perception of time, increased appetite, impaired short-term memory, and, at higher doses, anxiety or paranoia. THC is metabolized mainly in the liver to 11-hydroxy-THC, which is itself psychoactive, and then to THC-COOH, the metabolite detected in standard drug tests.

Medical use

A purified form of THC, dronabinol, and a synthetic analogue, nabilone, are approved in several countries to treat chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting and to stimulate appetite in AIDS-related wasting. Evidence for other indications, such as chronic pain and spasticity in multiple sclerosis, is mixed, and nabiximols (a cannabis extract containing THC and CBD) is approved for spasticity in some jurisdictions.

Legal status

Cannabis and THC have historically been prohibited in most countries, and THC remains a Schedule I substance under US federal law, though many US states and several countries, including Canada and Uruguay, have legalized cannabis for medical or recreational use. Regulation varies widely worldwide.
