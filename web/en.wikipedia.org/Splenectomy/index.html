Splenectomy

A splenectomy is the surgical procedure that partially or completely removes the spleen. The spleen is an important organ in the immune system, filtering blood and helping fight encapsulated bacteria.

Indications

Common indications include splenic rupture from trauma, hereditary spherocytosis, immune thrombocytopenic purpura (ITP), certain lymphomas and leukemias, hypersplenism, and splenic tumors or cysts. The operation may be performed open or laparoscopically.

Complications

Removal of the spleen increases the lifetime risk of overwhelming post-splenectomy infection (OPSI), especially from encapsulated organisms such as Streptococcus pneumoniae. Patients are advised vaccination against pneumococcus, meningococcus, and Haemophilus influenzae type b, and may need antibiotic prophylaxis. Reactive increases in platelet count are common after surgery.

Partial splenectomy

In some cases only part of the spleen is removed to preserve immune function, particularly in children with hereditary spherocytosis.
