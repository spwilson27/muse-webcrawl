Gondola lift

A gondola lift is a type of aerial lift in which small enclosed cabins (gondolas), each typically carrying between four and fifteen passengers, circulate continuously on a loop of cable supported by towers, detaching from the cable in stations for slow loading and unloading. It is distinct from the larger cabins of an aerial tramway, whose cars shuttle back and forth rather than circulate.

Types

The most common form is the monocable detachable gondola (MDG), in which a single cable both supports and propels the cabins. Bicable and tricable (2S and 3S) systems use separate support and haul cables, allowing longer spans and greater wind stability. Systems with two separated cable loops running in the same direction are used on very long urban and mountain installations.

Uses

Gondola lifts are staples of ski resorts, scenic mountain access, and amusement parks, and since the early 2000s they have been adopted as urban public transport in cities such as Medellín, La Paz (whose Mi Teleférico network is the world's largest), Mexico City, and Algiers, where they can cross steep or congested terrain cheaply and with low emissions.
