The Battle of the Teutoburg Forest, described by Roman historians as the clades Variana (the Varian disaster), took place in September AD 9, when an alliance of Germanic tribes led by Arminius, a prince of the Cherusci, ambushed and destroyed three Roman legions commanded by Publius Quinctilius Varus. It was one of the most catastrophic defeats in Roman history and effectively halted Roman expansion into Germania.

Background

Rome had been pacifying the lands between the Rhine and the Elbe, and Varus, the governor, was administering the new province. Arminius — a Roman citizen and equestrian who had served as an auxiliary commander — secretly organised a coalition of tribes resentful of Roman taxation and law. He lured Varus into marching toward a supposed local revolt, leading the legions into difficult, wooded terrain.

The battle

Over several days, in rain and mud, the strung-out Roman column of the Seventeenth, Eighteenth and Nineteenth Legions was attacked piecemeal by warriors who knew the ground. Perhaps 15,000–20,000 Roman soldiers were killed; Varus took his own life. The legion numbers XVII, XVIII and XIX were never used again.

Aftermath

Emperor Augustus is said by Suetonius to have cried out, "Quinctilius Varus, give me back my legions!" Revenge campaigns under Tiberius and Germanicus recovered two of the three eagle standards but did not re-establish Roman rule east of the Rhine, which remained the empire's frontier.
