Clannad

Origin: Dundalk, County Louth, Ireland
Genres: Irish traditional, folk, Celtic, new age
Years active: 1970–2023 (farewell tour)
Members (classic line-up): Moya Brennan, Ciarán Brennan, Pól Brennan, Noel Duggan, Pádraic Duggan

Clannad blended Irish traditional music with jazz, folk and pop.

Clannad (Irish pronunciation: [ˈklˠan̪ˠəd̪ˠ]) are an Irish band formed in 1970 in Gweedore, County Donegal, by siblings Ciarán, Pól and Máire (Moya) Brennan and their twin uncles Noel and Pádraic Duggan. Their name derives from the Irish Clann as Dobhar, "family from Dore". Clannad brought Irish language song to international audiences and helped pioneer the Celtic and new-age sounds of the 1980s; the singer Enya, sister of the Brennans, was briefly a member before her solo career.

History

The band began by performing traditional Irish songs at their family's tavern and won a talent contest that led to a recording contract. Through the 1970s they built a reputation in Ireland and Europe with albums of Gaelic songs and harmonised folk arrangements. Their breakthrough came in 1982 with "Theme from Harry's Game", written for a television drama about Northern Ireland; the haunting track became a UK top-five hit and remains the only hit single in Britain sung entirely in Irish.

Sound and later work

Clannad's music layers Moya Brennan's ethereal lead vocals and harp over synthesisers, double bass, flute and jazz-inflected harmony. Albums such as Magical Ring (1983), Macalla (1985) — which included the duet with Bono, "In a Lifetime" — and Anam (1990) sold millions worldwide, and their music featured in films including The Last of the Mohicans and the Robin of Sherwood television series (the album Legend). The group won a Grammy Award for Best New Age Album for Landmarks (1998). After the deaths of Pádraic Duggan (2016) and Noel Duggan (2022), the remaining members concluded a farewell world tour in 2023.

Legacy

Clannad are credited with bringing Irish traditional music into mainstream pop and influencing the global popularity of Celtic music. See also Enya and Moya Brennan.
