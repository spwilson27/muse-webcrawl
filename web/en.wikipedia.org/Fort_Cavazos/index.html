Fort Cavazos

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Fort Cavazos is a United States Army post located near Killeen in Central Texas. The post, known as Fort Hood from 1950 until 2023, is one of the largest military installations in the world by area, covering approximately 214,968 acres (870 km²). It is home to III Armored Corps headquarters, the 1st Cavalry Division, and numerous other units, and supports tens of thousands of soldiers and family members.

History

The installation was established in 1942 as Camp Hood, a tank destroyer training centre, on land acquired largely from local farming communities. It was named for Confederate general John Bell Hood. Made a permanent post in 1950 and renamed Fort Hood, it became the Army's principal armoured training installation, with two armoured divisions stationed there during the Cold War. Units from Fort Hood deployed extensively to Vietnam, the Gulf War, Iraq, and Afghanistan. In 2009 the post was the site of a mass shooting that killed 13 people.

Renaming

Following the recommendations of the federal Naming Commission, which reviewed Department of Defense assets named for Confederate figures, the post was redesignated Fort Cavazos on 9 May 2023, in honour of General Richard E. Cavazos, a Texas-born Korean and Vietnam War veteran who became the Army's first Hispanic four-star general.

Units and role

Major units include III Armored Corps headquarters, the 1st Cavalry Division, 3rd Cavalry Regiment, 1st Army Division West, and the Carl R. Darnall Army Medical Center. Its vast training areas, rail and air deployment facilities, and proximity to the Port of Beaumont make it a key power-projection platform for heavy forces.
