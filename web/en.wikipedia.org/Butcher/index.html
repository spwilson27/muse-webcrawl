Butcher

Butcher
Occupation
Activities: Slaughter, dressing and cutting meat; sausage making; retail
Related jobs: Meat cutter, charcutier

A butcher is a person who may slaughter animals, dress their flesh, sell their meat, or participate in any combination of these three tasks. They may prepare standard cuts of meat and poultry for sale in retail or wholesale food establishments.

History

Butchery is an ancient trade; butchers were organised in guilds in medieval Europe, with separate trades for slaughterers, retail butchers and charcutiers. In many towns, slaughter was confined to designated slaughterhouses for reasons of hygiene, a system formalised in 19th-century public-health legislation.

Work

Modern butchers break down carcasses into primal and retail cuts, trim and debone meat, make sausages and cured products, and advise customers on cooking. The trade involves skilled knife work, knowledge of meat science, food safety and increasingly, in independent shops, whole-animal utilisation.

The trade today

Much slaughtering and cutting is now industrialised in large meatpacking plants, but independent butcher shops persist, often emphasising locally sourced, high-welfare or heritage-breed meat and traditional skills such as dry-ageing.
