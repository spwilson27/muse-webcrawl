The 442nd Infantry Regiment was an infantry regiment of the United States Army composed almost entirely of second-generation Japanese American (Nisei) soldiers. It is the most decorated unit in U.S. military history relative to its size and length of service, earning more than 18,000 awards, including 21 Medals of Honor and eight Presidential Unit Citations.

Formation

The regiment was activated on April 1, 1943, drawing volunteers from Hawaii and from Japanese Americans held in internment camps on the mainland. It incorporated the 100th Infantry Battalion, a Nisei unit that had already seen combat in Italy.

World War II service

The 442nd fought in Italy, southern France, and Germany. Its most famous action was the rescue of the "Lost Battalion" — the 1st Battalion, 141st Infantry — near Biffontaine, France, in October 1944, a battle in which the regiment suffered heavy casualties to save 211 Texans. The unit also participated in the Gothic Line offensive in Italy and helped liberate towns in France such as Bruyères.

Legacy

After the war, the regiment's record helped change American public opinion toward Japanese Americans. In 2010, the unit was awarded the Congressional Gold Medal collectively. Its motto, "Go for Broke," is a Hawaiian pidgin gambling phrase meaning to risk everything on one effort.
