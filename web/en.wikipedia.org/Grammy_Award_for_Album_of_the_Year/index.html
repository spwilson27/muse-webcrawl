Grammy Award for Album of the Year

The Grammy Award for Album of the Year is an award presented by the Recording Academy of the United States to honour "artistic achievement, technical proficiency and overall excellence in the recording industry, without regard to album sales or chart position." It is the most prestigious of the Grammy Awards and one of the ceremony's four "General Field" categories, and has been awarded since the first Grammys in 1959.

History

The first Album of the Year award went to Henry Mancini for The Music from Peter Gunn (1959). Originally given only to the performing artist, the award now also recognises producers, engineers, and featured artists on the winning album. Frank Sinatra, Stevie Wonder, Paul Simon, and Taylor Swift are among the most frequent winners; Swift holds the record with four wins as a lead artist, for Fearless (2010), 1989 (2016), Folklore (2021), and Midnights (2024).

Notable winners and records

  • Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band by the Beatles (1968) was the first rock album to win.
  • Lauryn Hill's The Miseducation of Lauryn Hill (1999) was the first hip hop album to win.
  • The soundtrack to O Brother, Where Art Thou? won in 2002, boosting bluegrass and Americana music.
  • Billie Eilish became the youngest solo winner, at 18, for When We All Fall Asleep, Where Do We Go? in 2020.
  • Adele and Bruno Mars won for 25 (2017) and 24K Magic (2018) respectively; Beyoncé won her first Album of the Year in 2025 for Cowboy Carter.

Selection

Members of the Recording Academy and record companies submit entries; a review committee determines eligibility, and Academy voting members choose nominees and winners through ballots audited by an independent accounting firm. Along with Record of the Year, Song of the Year, and Best New Artist, the category makes up the Grammys' "Big Four."
