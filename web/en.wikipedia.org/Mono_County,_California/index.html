Mono County is a county in the east-central portion of the U.S. state of California, on the eastern slope of the Sierra Nevada, between Yosemite National Park and the Nevada state line. As of the 2020 census its population was 13,195. The county seat is Bridgeport, and the only incorporated municipality is the ski resort town of Mammoth Lakes.

Geography

The county takes its name from Mono Lake, a large saline soda lake known for its limestone tufa towers, lying at the western edge of the Great Basin. Much of the county is mountainous federal land, including parts of the Inyo and Humboldt–Toiyabe national forests, the Ansel Adams and John Muir wildernesses, and the eastern entrances to Yosemite. The ghost town of Bodie, once a booming gold-mining town, is preserved as a State Historic Park. U.S. Route 395 is the principal highway.

History

Mono County was formed in 1861 from parts of Calaveras, Fresno, and Mariposa counties. The area was home to the Mono Paiute people (see Mono people), and mining booms at Bodie, Aurora, and Lundy drew settlers in the 19th century. In the 20th century, the Los Angeles Aqueduct's diversion of the Mono Basin's tributary streams lowered Mono Lake dramatically, sparking a landmark environmental campaign concluded by a 1994 state decision protecting the lake.
