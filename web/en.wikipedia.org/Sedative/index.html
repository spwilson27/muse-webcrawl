Sedative

A sedative or tranquilliser is a substance that induces sedation by reducing irritability or excitement. Sedatives are central nervous system depressants and share many effects with hypnotics (sleep-inducing drugs); at higher doses many can cause slurred speech, impaired judgement, unconsciousness and anaesthesia.

Classes

Important classes include benzodiazepines (such as diazepam), barbiturates (now largely replaced because of their danger in overdose), the non-benzodiazepine "Z-drugs" used mainly as hypnotics, sedating antihistamines, certain antipsychotics, herbal preparations such as valerian, and alcohol. Most act by enhancing the effect of the inhibitory neurotransmitter GABA.

Medical use and risks

Sedatives are used for anxiety, insomnia, seizures, muscle spasm, and as premedication before procedures. Risks include dependence, tolerance, withdrawal syndromes, impaired driving, and dangerous respiratory depression, especially when combined with opioids or alcohol.
