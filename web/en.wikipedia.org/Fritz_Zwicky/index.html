Fritz Zwicky (14 February 1898 – 8 February 1974) was a Swiss astronomer who spent most of his career at the California Institute of Technology. He made far-reaching, often initially neglected contributions to astronomy and cosmology, including the first evidence for dark matter and the proposal of neutron stars.

Career

Born in Varna, Bulgaria, to Swiss parents, Zwicky studied at the ETH Zurich and emigrated to the United States in 1925 to work at Caltech, where he remained for the rest of his career. He held over 120 patents and also worked on jet and rocket propulsion during and after the Second World War.

Contributions

In 1933, studying the Coma Cluster of galaxies, Zwicky found that its galaxies moved too fast for the cluster to be held together by its visible mass, and he postulated invisible "dunkle Materie" (dark matter). With Walter Baade he coined the term "supernova", proposed that supernovae could create neutron stars, and discovered over a hundred supernovae himself. He also suggested that galaxies could act as gravitational lenses, catalogued tens of thousands of galaxies, and developed the "morphological box" method of systematic idea generation.
