Gauge (firearms)

Gauge
DefinitionNumber of lead spheres of bore diameter weighing one pound
Common shotgun gauges12, 16, 20, 28, .410 bore
Inverse relationLarger gauge number = smaller bore

Gauge, in firearms, is a unit used to express the internal diameter of a shotgun barrel. The gauge of a bore is the number of solid spheres of lead, each the diameter of the bore, that together weigh one pound. Thus a 12-gauge barrel has a diameter equal to one-twelfth of a pound of lead formed into a sphere, about 18.5 mm (0.729 in).

Common sizes

The 12 gauge is the most common for hunting, sport and defensive shotguns. The 20 gauge is lighter recoiling and popular for upland game; 16 and 28 gauge are less common, while the .410 is designated by bore diameter in inches rather than gauge.

Relation to calibre

Rifles and handguns are usually described by calibre, the approximate bore diameter in inches or millimetres. Gauge survives mainly for smoothbore shotguns and some historical cannon classifications.

Use and loading

Gauge must match the shotshell exactly: using a smaller shell in a larger chamber can let it lodge in the barrel and create a dangerous obstruction. Shell length is specified separately and also must match the chamber.
