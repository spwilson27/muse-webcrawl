Timothy Geithner

Timothy Franz Geithner (born August 18, 1961) is an American economist and former government official who served as the 75th United States Secretary of the Treasury under President Barack Obama from 2009 to 2013. He was a central figure in the federal government's response to the 2007–2008 financial crisis, first as president of the Federal Reserve Bank of New York and then at the Treasury. After leaving office he became president of the private equity firm Warburg Pincus.

Early life and career

Geithner was born in Brooklyn and spent much of his childhood abroad — in Zimbabwe, India, Thailand and Japan — as his father worked for the Ford Foundation and the U.S. Agency for International Development. He studied government and Asian studies at Dartmouth and earned a master's degree in international economics from Johns Hopkins SAIS in 1985, also studying Japanese and Chinese. After three years at Kissinger Associates he joined the Treasury Department in 1988, rising to Under Secretary for International Affairs under Robert Rubin and Lawrence Summers in the Clinton administration, where he worked on the Mexican and Asian financial crises. He then served at the International Monetary Fund before becoming president of the New York Fed in 2003.

Financial crisis

At the New York Fed, Geithner helped manage the responses to the collapse of Bear Stearns, the bankruptcy of Lehman Brothers and the rescue of AIG in 2008 — decisions that were later both credited with preventing a deeper collapse and criticised for favouring financial institutions. As Treasury Secretary he implemented the Troubled Asset Relief Program (TARP) bank rescues, the administration's bank "stress tests" of 2009, the rescue of the auto industry, and the Dodd–Frank financial reform of 2010. His tenure also included fights over the federal debt ceiling and the European sovereign debt crisis.

Later life

Geithner stepped down in January 2013 and joined Warburg Pincus as president in 2014. His memoir of the crisis, Stress Test (2014), defended the rescue policies. He has taught at Yale and chaired the board of overseers of the International Rescue Committee.
