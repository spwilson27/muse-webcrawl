Bela Julesz

Bela Julesz (1928–2003) was a Hungarian-born American visual neuroscientist and psychophysicist. He is best known for inventing the random-dot stereogram and for his work on texture perception.

Career

Born in Budapest, Julesz emigrated to the United States and worked at Bell Laboratories from 1956 to 1989, then at Rutgers University, where he founded the Laboratory of Vision Research.

Contributions

Random-dot stereograms demonstrated that binocular depth perception can occur without monocular cues, reshaping theories of vision. His texton theory addressed pre-attentive texture discrimination. He received a MacArthur Fellowship in 1983.
