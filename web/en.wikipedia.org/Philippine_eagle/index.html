Philippine eagle

KingdomAnimalia
FamilyAccipitridae
GenusPithecophaga
SpeciesP. jefferyi
StatusCritically endangered

The Philippine eagle (Pithecophaga jefferyi), also known as the monkey-eating eagle or great Philippine eagle, is a critically endangered species of eagle in the family Accipitridae, endemic to forests in the Philippines. It has brown and white plumage, a shaggy crest, and stands around a metre tall, ranking among the largest and most powerful forest raptors in the world.

Description

The Philippine eagle has a massive, deep bill, piercing blue-grey eyes, and long crest feathers forming a mane-like crown. Females are larger than males. Its wingspan can exceed two metres, though its wings are relatively short and broad for maneuvering through dense forest canopy.

Behaviour and diet

Despite its old name, the eagle rarely eats monkeys; its diet varies by island and includes colugos (flying lemurs), civets, squirrels, bats, snakes, and monitor lizards. Pairs are monogamous and raise a single eaglet every two years, with one of the longest breeding cycles of any bird: incubation lasts about two months and the young bird may depend on its parents for well over a year.

Conservation

Deforestation of lowland rainforest has devastated its habitat, and hunting and trapping persistDespite legal protection. The species was declared the national bird of the Philippines in 1995, and the Philippine Eagle Foundation in Davao City runs a captive breeding and education program. Killing a Philippine eagle is punishable by up to 12 years' imprisonment under Philippine law.
