Culinary arts

The culinary arts are the arts of preparing, cooking, and presenting food, usually in the form of meals. Practitioners are generally called chefs or cooks, and their expertise ranges from home cooking to the elaborate traditions of haute cuisine. The term encompasses techniques (knife work, heat management, seasoning), food science, nutrition, sanitation, and the aesthetic presentation of dishes.

History

Professional cookery developed in royal and aristocratic households and, from the 18th century, in restaurants. Figures such as Marie-Antoine Carême and Auguste Escoffier codified French classical cuisine and the brigade de cuisine system that organizes professional kitchens. In the 20th and 21st centuries, movements such as nouvelle cuisine and molecular gastronomy reshaped fine dining, while culinary schools worldwide formalized training and certification.

Training

Culinary education combines apprenticeships with institutional programs teaching stocks and sauces, butchery, baking and pastry, regional cuisines, and kitchen management. Chefs typically progress through stations in the kitchen brigade, from apprentice to executive chef.
