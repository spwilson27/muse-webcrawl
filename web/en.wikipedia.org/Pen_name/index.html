Pen name

A pen name is a pseudonym (or, in some cases, a variant form of a real name) adopted and used by an author, in place of their real (or legal) name. Pen names are also called noms de plume (French for "pen names"), a calque of the English term.

Reasons for use

Authors adopt pen names for many reasons: to conceal gender (as the Brontë sisters and Mary Ann Evans, who wrote as George Eliot, initially did); to separate works in different genres; to protect personal privacy or safety; to create a more memorable or marketable identity; or because a publisher expects a distinctive name. Public figures may also use pen names when writing outside their main field.

Collective and shared pen names

Some pen names are shared by multiple writers, such as Ellery Queen (used by cousins Frederic Dannay and Manfred B. Lee, and later by ghostwriters) or Nicolas Bourbaki in mathematics. "House names" owned by publishers may be used by a series of authors writing a long-running series.

Legal aspects

Writing under a pen name is generally legal; authors may register copyrights under pseudonyms, though the duration of copyright protection may differ in some jurisdictions when the author's identity is not disclosed.
