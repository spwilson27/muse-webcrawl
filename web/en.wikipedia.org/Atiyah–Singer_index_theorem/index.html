Atiyah–Singer index theorem

The Atiyah–Singer index theorem, proved by Michael Atiyah and Isadore Singer in 1963, states that for an elliptic differential operator on a compact manifold, the analytical index — the dimension of the kernel minus the dimension of the cokernel — equals the topological index, computed from characteristic classes. It is one of the landmark results of 20th-century mathematics, uniting analysis, geometry and topology.

Statement

For an elliptic operator D between vector bundles over a compact manifold X, index(D) = dim ker D − dim coker D. The theorem equates this to an integral over X of characteristic classes — built from the Todd class of the tangent bundle and the Chern character of the symbol of D. Because the topological expression is deformation-invariant, the analytical index is a homotopy invariant.

Examples

The theorem subsumes classical results: the Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem in algebraic geometry; the Hirzebruch signature theorem; and the Gauss–Bonnet theorem, which appears as the index of the de Rham operator. Proofs

The original proof used cobordism and K-theory, which Atiyah and Singer developed for this purpose. Later approaches include the heat-equation proof (via the McKean–Singer formula and local index theory) and proofs using supersymmetry in quantum mechanics, each illuminating different aspects of the result.

Impact

The index theorem profoundly influenced differential geometry, algebraic geometry, operator algebras (through its generalisation to families and to K-theory of C*-algebras) and theoretical physics, where index computations count zero modes and underlie anomaly cancellation in gauge theories and string theory. Atiyah and Singer received the 2004 Abel Prize in part for this work.
