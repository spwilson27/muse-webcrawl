Weather

Studied byMeteorology
Key variablesTemperature, pressure, humidity, wind, precipitation
TimescaleMinutes to weeks
ContrastClimate (long-term averages)

Weather is the state of the atmosphere, describing for example the degree to which it is hot or cold, wet or dry, calm or stormy, clear or cloudy. On Earth, most weather phenomena occur in the lowest layer of the planet's atmosphere, the troposphere, just below the stratosphere. Weather refers to day-to-day temperature, precipitation, and other atmospheric conditions, whereas climate is the term for the averaging of atmospheric conditions over longer periods of time.

Causes

Weather is driven by the Sun's uneven heating of the Earth's surface, which creates differences in air pressure and temperature. Air flows from high to low pressure as wind, deflected by the Coriolis effect; rising moist air cools and condenses into clouds and precipitation. Weather systems such as cyclones, anticyclones and fronts organise these processes on scales from thunderstorms a few kilometres wide to mid-latitude storms spanning continents. The jet streams steer many systems in the temperate zones.

Forecasting

Weather forecasting is the application of science and technology to predict the conditions of the atmosphere for a given location and time. Modern forecasts rely on observations from surface stations, weather balloons, radar and satellites, fed into numerical weather prediction models run on supercomputers. Forecasts are highly reliable a few days ahead but degrade with time because the atmosphere is chaotic — small errors grow rapidly, a sensitivity popularised as the "butterfly effect."

Effects

Weather shapes agriculture, transport, energy demand and daily life, and extreme weather — hurricanes, tornadoes, heat waves, blizzards and floods — can be destructive and deadly. Weather modification, such as cloud seeding, attempts to alter these processes deliberately.
