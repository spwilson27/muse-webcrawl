Iliamna Lake is a lake in southwest Alaska, at the north end of the Alaska Peninsula. It is the largest lake in Alaska and the eighth largest lake by area in the United States. The lake drains into Bristol Bay via the Kvichak River.

Geography

The lake is about 124 kilometres long and up to 35 kilometres wide, with a surface area of roughly 2,622 square kilometres. Villages around its shores include Iliamna, Newhalen, Kokhanok, and Pedro Bay.

Ecology

Iliamna Lake supports one of the world's most productive sockeye salmon runs, central to the Bristol Bay fishery. It is also home to one of only two colonies of freshwater seals in Alaska, and local legend tells of a large lake monster, sometimes identified with a giant sturgeon.
