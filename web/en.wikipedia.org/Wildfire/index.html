Wildfire

A wildfire spreads through dry vegetation, driven by wind and low humidity.

A wildfire, forest fire, or a bushfire is an unplanned, uncontrolled and unpredictable fire in an area of combustible vegetation. Some forest ecosystems in their natural state depend on wildfire. Wildfires can be a fire in a forest, grassland, bushland, scrubland, peatland, and other wooded areas. Depending on the type of vegetation present, a wildfire may be more specifically identified as a bushfire (in Australia), desert fire, grass fire, hill fire, peat fire, prairie fire, vegetation fire, or veld fire.

Wildfires are a natural phenomenon and have helped shape the evolution of flora and fauna around the world — many plant species, such as lodgepole pine and eucalyptus, have fire-adapted traits including serotinous cones that open only under heat. Wildfire behaviour is governed by the fire triangle of fuel, weather, and topography: hot, dry, windy conditions can drive extreme fires that generate their own weather, including pyrocumulonimbus clouds and fire whirls.

Causes

Common causes of wildfires include lightning, human carelessness (campfires, discarded cigarettes), arson, utility equipment sparks, and agricultural burning that escapes control. Human activity causes the great majority of ignitions in many regions, though lightning fires dominate remote boreal forests. Once started, a fire spreads through radiant and convective heat and by ember spotting, which can ignite new fires kilometres ahead of the main front.

Effects

Wildfires burn hundreds of millions of hectares globally each year, mostly in savannas. Their smoke harms air quality far downwind, and they release large quantities of carbon dioxide, feeding a climate feedback loop: climate change brings hotter, drier conditions and longer fire seasons in regions such as western North America, Australia, and the Mediterranean. Destructive events include the 2019–20 Australian "Black Summer", the 2023 Canadian wildfires that smoked out North American cities, and California fires that destroyed entire towns such as Paradise (2018) and Lahaina in Hawaii (2023).

Management

Fire agencies fight wildfires with ground crews, engines, aircraft dropping water and retardant, and containment lines, though large fires are often managed rather than directly suppressed until weather moderates. Prevention strategies include prescribed burning, mechanical fuel reduction, defensible space around homes, early detection via satellites and camera networks, and land-use planning in the wildland–urban interface. Indigenous burning practices, long suppressed, are increasingly recognised as effective fuel management.
