Gregory Palamas

Gregory Palamas (1296 – 14 November 1359) was a Byzantine Greek theologian and hesychast monk of Mount Athos. He is best known for his defence of hesychasm during the Hesychast controversy of the 14th century, and for the theological distinction between God's essence and His energies. Palamas was canonised a saint of the Eastern Orthodox Church in 1368.

Early life

Palamas was born in Constantinople in 1296 into a distinguished family; his father Constantine was a courtier of Emperor Andronikos II Palaiologos. Gregory was educated at the imperial court but chose the monastic life, settling on Mount Athos around 1316, where he practised the hesychast method of prayer, involving controlled breathing and the repetition of the Jesus Prayer, aimed at experiencing the uncreated light of God.

Hesychast controversy

In the 1330s the Calabrian philosopher Barlaam of Seminara attacked hesychasm, mocking the monks' claims to perceive the divine light and arguing that God is utterly unknowable. Palamas responded with his Triads in Defence of the Holy Hesychasts (c. 1338), distinguishing between God's incomprehensible essence and His energies or operations, which are truly God and can be experienced by human beings. A series of councils at Constantinople in 1341, 1347, and 1351 vindicated Palamas's teaching, which became official Orthodox doctrine; the decision was incorporated into the Synodikon of Orthodoxy.

Later life

Palamas was imprisoned during the Byzantine civil war of 1341–1347 for his political alignment, but with the victory of John VI Kantakouzenos he was released and, in 1347, consecrated Archbishop of Thessalonica. Captured by Turkish pirates in 1354 and held for a year in Ottoman territory, he engaged in dialogues with Muslim scholars. He died at Thessalonica on 14 November 1359.

Legacy

Palamas's theology, often called Palamism, remains central to Eastern Orthodox thought; the Second Sunday of Lent is dedicated to his memory. His feast day is 14 November.
