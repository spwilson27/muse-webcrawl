Tafsir

A manuscript page of tafsir — the scholarly exegesis of the Quran.

Tafsir (Arabic: تفسير, tafsīr, "interpretation") is the discipline and literature of exegesis and commentary on the Quran, the scripture of Islam. A scholar of tafsir is called a mufassir. Works of tafsir explain the meanings of verses, their historical contexts (occasions of revelation, asbāb al-nuzūl), linguistic and legal implications, and resolve apparent ambiguities or contradictions, for example through the doctrine of abrogation (naskh).

Principles and methods

Traditional methodology interprets the Quran by the Quran itself, then by the Sunnah of Muhammad (whose explanations the hadith preserve), then by the reports of the Companions and Successors. Two broad approaches emerged: tafsīr bi'l-maʾthūr, commentary based on transmitted reports, and tafsīr bi'l-raʾy, commentary employing reasoned opinion and the tools of grammar, rhetoric and jurisprudence — the latter long debated among scholars. Fields auxiliary to tafsir include the study of variant readings (qirāʾāt), Quranic orthography, and the occasions of revelation.

Famous works

The earliest comprehensive surviving commentary is the Jāmiʿ al-bayān of al-Tabari (d. 923), a vast compilation of transmitted interpretations. Later landmarks include al-Zamakhshari's rhetorically brilliant al-Kashshāf (from a Mu'tazilite standpoint), Fakhr al-Din al-Razi's philosophical Mafātīḥ al-ghayb, al-Qurtubi's legal commentary, Ibn Kathir's widely-read transmitted commentary (14th century), and the concise Tafsīr al-Jalālayn of al-Mahalli and al-Suyuti. Shia tradition produced works such as al-Tusi's al-Tibyān and Tabataba'i's 20th-century al-Mīzān. In the modern era, reformist and thematic (mawḍūʿī) approaches — for example Rashid Rida's Tafsīr al-Manār and Sayyid Qutb's Fī Ẓilāl al-Qurʾān — have been highly influential.
