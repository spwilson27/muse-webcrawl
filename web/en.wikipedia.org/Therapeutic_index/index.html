Therapeutic index

The therapeutic index (TI; also referred to as therapeutic ratio) is a quantitative measurement of the relative safety of a drug. It is a comparison of the amount of a therapeutic agent that causes toxicity to the amount that causes the therapeutic effect.

Definition

TI = TD50 / ED50

where ED50 is the median effective dose (producing the desired effect in 50% of subjects) and TD50 is the median toxic dose. In preclinical animal studies, the lethal dose LD50 is often used in place of TD50. A higher therapeutic index indicates a safer drug. Generally, a drug or other therapeutic agent with a narrow therapeutic range — a small difference between toxic and therapeutic doses — is said to have a narrow therapeutic index.

Clinical significance

Drugs with a narrow therapeutic index, such as warfarin, lithium, digoxin, phenytoin, and many immunosuppressants, require careful dosing and often therapeutic drug monitoring of blood concentrations. Because the therapeutic index derives from population medians, it does not capture individual variability; the margin of safety, which compares TD1 with ED99, is sometimes preferred as a more conservative measure.
