The zhonghu (Chinese: 中胡, pinyin: zhōnghú, "alto huqin") is a Chinese bowed string instrument. It is the alto member of the erhu family, developed in the 1940s to give the modern Chinese orchestra a lower, viola-like voice between the erhu and the cello.

Construction and sound

The zhonghu closely resembles the erhu but is larger in every dimension: a longer neck, a bigger sound box covered with python skin, and two thicker strings tuned a perfect fifth lower than the erhu (commonly G–D or A–E). The bow passes between the two strings, as with the erhu. Its tone is deeper, darker, and mellower than the erhu's, and it is rarely a solo instrument.

Role in the orchestra

In the Chinese orchestra the zhonghu section supplies the inner harmony parts in the huqin choir, analogous to the viola section of a Western orchestra. It is used in ensembles throughout China, Singapore, Taiwan, Hong Kong, and the Chinese diaspora.
