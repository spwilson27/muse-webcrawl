Roger Borsa (c. 1060/61 – 22 February 1111) was a Norman Duke of Apulia and Calabria from 1085 to 1111. The son of the conqueror Robert Guiscard and the Lombard princess Sikelgaita, his nickname "Borsa" ("the Purse") is said to derive from his childhood habit of counting and recounting his money.

Succession

Guiscard's succession was contested by Bohemond, his son by his first marriage, who had been disinherited in favour of Roger after the annulment of that marriage. On Guiscard's death in 1085 Roger succeeded in Apulia, but Bohemond fought for his share, and by the settlement of 1086 and subsequent agreements he received Bari, Taranto and other lands, forming the core of the later Principality of Taranto. The rivalry between the half-brothers weakened ducal authority in southern Italy.

Reign

Roger's rule was troubled by the independence of the great Norman counts and by revolts, notably that of his half-brother Bohemond and of William of Grandmesnil. Chroniclers generally judged him a weaker ruler than his father; Orderic Vitalis and others describe a duke unable to control his barons. Nonetheless he retained Salerno and the core ducal lands, granted charters to towns and monasteries, and in 1096–98 managed the passage of crusading forces, including Bohemond's, through his ports. In 1105 he campaigned to recover territory from his nephew's rivals in Calabria.

Family and death

He married Adela of Flanders, widow of King Canute IV of Denmark, by whom he had a son, William, who succeeded him as William II of Apulia. Roger died at Salerno on 22 February 1111 and was buried in the cathedral there. With William's death in 1127, the duchy passed to Roger II of Sicily, unifying Norman southern Italy.
