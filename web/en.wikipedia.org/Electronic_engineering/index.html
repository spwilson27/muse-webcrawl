Electronic engineering

Electronic engineering (also called electronics engineering or electrical engineering in some contexts) is an electrical engineering sub-discipline which emerged in the early 20th century and is distinguished by the use of active components such as semiconductor devices to amplify and control electric current flow.

History

The field grew from radio engineering following the invention of the vacuum tube and the triode amplifier. The invention of the transistor in 1947 and the integrated circuit in 1958 transformed it, enabling miniaturised computers and consumer electronics.

Subfields

Electronic engineers work in analogue and digital electronics, embedded systems, signal processing, telecommunications, control systems, power electronics, and radio-frequency engineering.

Methods

Design relies on circuit theory, simulation tools such as SPICE, printed circuit board layout, and increasingly on hardware description languages for digital systems.

Education and practice

Electronic engineering is usually studied as a degree programme covering mathematics, physics, circuits, and computing. Professional bodies include the IEEE and the IET.
