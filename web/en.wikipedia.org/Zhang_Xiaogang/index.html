Zhang Xiaogang (Chinese: 张晓刚; born 1958) is a Chinese contemporary artist best known for his Bloodline: Big Family paintings — stylised, largely monochrome portraits of Chinese families derived from studio photographs of the Cultural Revolution era, often with a thin red line threading the figures together.

Life and career

Born in Kunming, Zhang studied at the Sichuan Academy of Fine Arts in Chongqing, graduating in 1982. His early work was expressionist and introspective; a 1992 trip to Europe exposed him to Surrealism and Western modernism. In 1993 he began the Bloodline series, inspired by old family photographs, including pictures of his own parents. The series debuted internationally at the 1994 São Paulo Biennial and the 1995 Venice Biennale, propelling Zhang to the forefront of Chinese contemporary art.

Style and themes

The Bloodline portraits depict parents and a child posed rigidly against grey backgrounds, their faces blank and mask-like, with subtle coloured patches and a fine red "bloodline" suggesting family ties, genetics, and the bonds — and burdens — of collective life in Mao-era China. Later series, such as Amnesia and Memory, add objects, interiors, and handwritten texts. During the 2000s art-market boom, Zhang's works achieved record prices for Chinese contemporary art; his triptych Forever Lasting Love sold for about HK$79 million at Sotheby's Hong Kong in 2011.
