Thunder Bay is a city in and the seat of Thunder Bay District, Ontario, Canada. It is the most populous municipality in Northwestern Ontario and the second most populous in Northern Ontario. Situated on Lake Superior, its port is an important link in the shipping of goods from western Canada to the Great Lakes and the St. Lawrence Seaway.

History

The city was formed in 1970 by the amalgamation of the twin cities of Fort William and Port Arthur, together with neighbouring townships. The area was long inhabited by Ojibwe peoples, and a French fur-trading post was established near the Kaministiquia River in the early 18th century. The name refers to the bay on the northern shore of Lake Superior, which French maps called Baie du Tonnerre.

Geography and landmarks

The city lies near the Sleeping Giant, a distinctive mesa formation on the Sibley Peninsula visible across the bay, and Mount McKay of the Nor'Wester Mountains. The Terry Fox Monument, near the spot where Fox ended his Marathon of Hope in 1980, is just east of the city.
