Edward L. Deci

Edward L. Deci (born 1942) is an American psychologist and professor at the University of Rochester. With Richard Ryan, he is the co-founder of self-determination theory (SDT), an influential framework for understanding human motivation, personality, and well-being.

Career

Deci earned his PhD in psychology from Carnegie Mellon University in 1970 and joined the University of Rochester, where he directed the human motivation programme and later the Center for Self-Determination Theory. His early experiments in the 1970s examined how external rewards affect intrinsic motivation.

Research

Deci's best-known experimental finding — the undermining effect — showed that paying people money for an intrinsically interesting activity (such as solving puzzles) can reduce their subsequent free-choice engagement with it, a result elaborated in his 1975 book Intrinsic Motivation. This line of work challenged purely behaviourist, reinforcement-based accounts of motivation.

Self-determination theory, developed with Ryan from the 1980s onward, proposes that humans have three basic psychological needs — autonomy, competence, and relatedness — and that social environments supporting these needs foster intrinsic motivation, internalisation of values, and psychological health. SDT has been applied extensively in education, work organisations, health care, parenting, and sport. Deci's 1995 book Why We Do What We Do brought the theory to a general audience.
