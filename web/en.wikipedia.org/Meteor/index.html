Meteor

A meteor is the visible streak of light produced when a meteoroid enters a planetary atmosphere at high speed and heats up by friction and compression of air, glowing incandescently. Popularly called a "shooting star" or "falling star," a typical meteor flares at altitudes of 75–100 km. If any of the object survives to reach the ground, the remnant is a meteorite.

Terminology

A meteoroid is the small body in space (from dust grains to about a metre across); the meteor is the luminous phenomenon; a meteorite is the solid survivor. Exceptionally bright meteors are called fireballs, and a fireball that explodes in the air is a bolide — such as the 2013 Chelyabinsk event, which injured over 1,500 people, mostly from shattered glass.

Meteor showers

When Earth passes through the debris stream of a comet, many meteors per hour radiate from a single point (the radiant). Major annual showers include the Perseids (August, parent comet Swift–Tuttle), Geminids (December, parent asteroid 3200 Phaethon), Leonids (November), and Quadrantids (January).

Observation

Meteors are studied visually, by radio reflection from ionized trails, and by camera networks that recover meteorites with known orbits. Sporadic meteors not associated with showers number in the millions per day worldwide, mostly microscopic.
