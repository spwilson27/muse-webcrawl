Red tide

Red tide is a common name for a harmful algal bloom in which aquatic microorganisms, especially dinoflagellates, multiply so densely that they discolour coastal waters reddish-brown. The term is a misnomer — the phenomenon is unrelated to tides and blooms are not always red.

Florida red tides are caused mainly by Karenia brevis, which produces brevetoxins that kill fish, manatees and shellfish-eaters and irritate human lungs. Other species produce saxitoxin, responsible for paralytic shellfish poisoning. Nutrient runoff, warm water and ocean currents influence bloom formation.
