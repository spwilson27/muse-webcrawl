Malolactic fermentation

Malolactic fermentation (MLF) is a process in winemaking in which tart-tasting malic acid is converted into softer lactic acid and carbon dioxide. It is carried out by lactic acid bacteria, principally Oenococcus oeni, usually after the primary alcoholic fermentation.

Effects

MLF reduces acidity, softens the wine's mouthfeel and improves microbial stability. It can also produce diacetyl, which contributes buttery aromas — desirable in some styles such as oaked Chardonnay and many red wines, but often avoided in crisp aromatic whites.

Practice

Winemakers may encourage MLF by inoculating with cultured bacteria and keeping the wine warm, or prevent it with sulphur dioxide, filtration and cool storage to preserve freshness in wines such as Riesling. The process also occurs in cider making and some other fermented foods.
