Hakone

Hakone (Japanese: 箱根町, Hakone-machi) is a town in Ashigarashimo District, Kanagawa Prefecture, Japan, located in the mountainous far west of the prefecture, on the eastern side of Hakone Pass. It is famous for its hot springs (onsen), natural scenery, views of Mount Fuji across Lake Ashi, and open-air museums, and forms part of the Fuji-Hakone-Izu National Park.

History

Hakone was an important checkpoint on the Tōkaidō highway linking Edo (Tokyo) with Kyoto during the Edo period; the Hakone Checkpoint (Hakone sekisho) controlled traffic, particularly the movement of women and weapons, and has been reconstructed as a museum. The town developed as a resort from the Meiji period, when its hot springs and cool summers attracted both Japanese and foreign visitors.

Attractions

Principal attractions include the Ōwakudani volcanic valley, where steam vents and sulfurous springs are still active; Hakone Shrine on the shore of Lake Ashi; the Hakone Open-Air Museum, Japan's first open-air art museum (opened 1969); and the Pola Museum of Art. The "Hakone Loop" — a circuit using the Hakone Tozan Railway (Japan's oldest mountain railway, with switchbacks), a funicular, the Hakone Ropeway and sightseeing boats styled as pirate ships across Lake Ashi — is a popular excursion from Tokyo.

Hot springs

Hakone is traditionally said to have "seventeen hot springs" (Hakone Seventeen Spas), including Yumoto, Tonosawa, Miyanoshita, Kowakudani and Sengokuhara, each with distinct mineral waters and inns (ryokan).
