John the Apostle (c. 6 – c. 100 AD) was one of the Twelve Apostles of Jesus according to the New Testament. The son of Zebedee and brother of James the Great, he is presented in the Gospels as one of Jesus's closest followers — with Peter and James he witnessed the Transfiguration and the Agony in the Garden — and Christian tradition identifies him with the "Beloved Disciple" of the Fourth Gospel and, by extension, with John the Evangelist, the author of the Gospel and Epistles of John and of the Book of Revelation, though these identifications are debated by scholars.

In the New Testament

John and his brother James, fishermen on the Sea of Galilee, were called by Jesus while mending their nets; Jesus nicknamed them Boanerges, "sons of thunder." John is listed among the apostles in all four Gospels and appears prominently in Acts alongside Peter, healing at the Temple and facing the Sanhedrin. Paul later names him one of the "pillars" of the Jerusalem church.

The Beloved Disciple

The Gospel of John repeatedly mentions "the disciple whom Jesus loved," who reclined next to Jesus at the Last Supper, stood at the foot of the cross (where Jesus entrusted his mother Mary to his care), and ran with Peter to the empty tomb. From the 2nd century the church father Irenaeus identified this figure with the apostle John, resident at Ephesus.

Later tradition

Tradition holds that John was exiled to the island of Patmos under Domitian, where he received the visions of Revelation, and that he died of old age at Ephesus around 100 AD — the only apostle said to have died a natural death rather than by martyrdom. His feast day in the West is 27 December, and his symbol in the tetramorph is the eagle.
