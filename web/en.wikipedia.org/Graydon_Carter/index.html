Graydon Carter (born 14 July 1949) is a Canadian journalist and magazine editor. He is best known as the editor of Vanity Fair from 1992 to 2017, a tenure that made him one of the most influential figures in American magazine publishing, and as a co-founder (with Kurt Andersen and Thomas Phillips) of the satirical monthly Spy in 1986.

Career

Carter was born in Toronto and briefly studied at the University of Ottawa and Carleton University before working for Canadian Forum and Time magazine in New York. In 1986 he co-founded Spy, whose irreverent coverage of New York's media, society and real-estate worlds — including its running mockery of Donald Trump, whom it famously dubbed a "short-fingered vulgarian" — reshaped magazine satire. After a stint at the New York Observer, Carter succeeded Tina Brown at Vanity Fair in 1992.

Vanity Fair and later work

At Vanity Fair, Carter presided over long-form celebrity and political journalism, staged the magazine's famous Oscar party — a fixture of Hollywood's social calendar — and hosted the New Establishment Summit. He produced documentaries including The Kid Stays in the Picture (2002) and Chicago 10 (2007), and was a New York restaurateur (the Waverly Inn). In 2019 he co-founded, with Alessandra Stanley, the digital weekly Air Mail, which he continues to co-edit. His memoir, When the Going Was Good, was published in 2025.
