Aedui

The Aedui or Haedui were a Gallic tribe dwelling in the modern Burgundy region, between the Saône and Loire rivers, during the Iron Age and Roman period. Their principal oppidum was Bibracte on Mont Beuvray, and their chief rivals were the Arverni and the Sequani. The Roman Senate had formally styled them "brothers and kinsmen" of the Roman people, making them Rome's oldest Gallic allies.

Gallic Wars

In 58 BC the Aedui appealed to Julius Caesar for help against the migrating Helvetii and the Germanic king Ariovistus — the request that opened Caesar's Gallic Wars. The Aeduan noble Diviciacus was Caesar's staunch ally, while his brother Dumnorix covertly opposed Rome. Although initially loyal, the Aedui joined Vercingetorix's general revolt in 52 BC, after which they were pardoned and restored to their privileged position.

Roman period

Under Augustus the Aedui moved from Bibracte to a new city, Augustodunum (modern Autun). In AD 21 they raised the revolt of Sacrovir, the last significant Gallic uprising. In AD 48 the emperor Claudius, himself born in Gaul, successfully proposed admitting Aeduan nobles to the Roman Senate — the first Gauls so honoured.
