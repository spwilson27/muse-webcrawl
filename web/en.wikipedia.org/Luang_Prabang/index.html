Luang Prabang is a city in north-central Laos, at the confluence of the Nam Khan and Mekong rivers, about 300 km north of Vientiane. Capital of the first Lao kingdom, Lan Xang, and the royal capital of Laos until 1975, it is famed for its fusion of Lao temples and French colonial architecture and was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1995.

History

Known earlier as Muang Swa and Xieng Dong Xieng Thong, the city received the Phra Bang Buddha image — from which its modern name derives — and became Fa Ngum's capital when he founded Lan Xang in 1353. After Lan Xang split in 1707, the Kingdom of Luang Prabang survived under growing Siamese and later French influence; Laos became a French protectorate in 1893. The city remained the king's residence under the Kingdom of Laos, through the era of Prime Minister Souvanna Phouma and the civil war, until the Pathet Lao abolished the monarchy in 1975. Nearby highlands are home to Hmong communities.

Sights and culture

Wat Xieng Thong (1560) is the city's most celebrated temple, with sweeping low roofs and a Tree of Life mosaic. The Royal Palace, built 1904–1909, is now the National Museum. Mount Phousi overlooks the old town; each dawn monks process in the tak bat alms-giving ceremony. The Kuang Si Falls and Pak Ou Caves are popular excursions. Night markets sell textiles and local food along the peninsula between the two rivers.

Transport

Luang Prabang International Airport links the city to Vientiane, Bangkok in Thailand, Hanoi and other regional hubs, and the China–Laos railway (opened 2021) has a station nearby.
