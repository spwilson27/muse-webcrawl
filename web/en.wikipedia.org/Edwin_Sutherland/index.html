Edwin Hardin Sutherland (13 August 1883 – 11 October 1950) was an American sociologist, widely regarded as one of the most influential criminologists of the 20th century.

Differential association

Sutherland's theory of differential association, developed in successive editions of his textbook Principles of Criminology (1924 onwards), holds that criminal behaviour is learned through interaction with others, principally in intimate personal groups, and that learning includes both techniques of offending and motives, rationalisations and attitudes favourable to law violation.

White-collar crime

In his 1939 presidential address to the American Sociological Society and his monograph White Collar Crime (1949), Sutherland coined the term "white-collar crime" and challenged the assumption that crime is mainly a lower-class phenomenon, arguing that criminology had ignored offences by persons of respectability and high social status in the course of their occupations.
