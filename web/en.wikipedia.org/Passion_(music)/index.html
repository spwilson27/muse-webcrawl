Passion (music)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A Passion performance in a church setting

A Passion is a musical setting of the Passion of Christ — the Gospel accounts of the suffering and death of Jesus — intended principally for performance during Holy Week. The genre was central to liturgical music from the Middle Ages through the Baroque era, and it has continued to attract composers into modern times.

History

In medieval practice the Passion narrative from one of the four Gospels was sung in plainchant during Holy Week, with different reciting tones or singers for the narrator (Evangelist), Christ and the crowd (turba). Polyphonic settings developed in the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries, in both responsorial and through-composed (motet) forms, by composers such as Byrd, Victoria and Lassus. In Lutheran Germany the tradition flowered into the oratorio Passion, combining Gospel narration with chorales, arias and reflective choruses on free poetic texts. Heinrich Schütz wrote austere a cappella Passions, and the genre reached its summit in the St Matthew Passion and St John Passion of Johann Sebastian Bach.

Characteristics

Typical Baroque Passions are scored for soloists (with the Evangelist as narrator and individual singers for Christ and the other characters), choir — which sings both the turba passages and congregational chorales — and orchestra. Arias interspersed between narrative sections allow emotional and theological reflection on the events described. Handel, Telemann and C. P. E. Bach also wrote significant Passions, the Brockes Passion text being set by several of them.

Later works

After a period of neglect, Bach's Passions were revived in the nineteenth century, Mendelssohn's 1829 performance of the St Matthew Passion being a landmark. Twentieth- and twenty-first-century composers have returned to the form, including Krzysztof Penderecki (St Luke Passion), Arvo Pärt (Passio), Osvaldo Golijov (La Pasión según San Marcos) and James MacMillan, often reinterpreting the genre in contemporary musical languages and settings.
