Steven Levy (born 1951) is an American journalist who has written extensively about computing, artificial intelligence, and the internet. He is editor at large of Wired, which he joined at its founding and for which he wrote the "Plaintext" newsletter. He previously covered technology for Newsweek for over a decade and contributed to Rolling Stone, The New York Times Magazine, and The New Yorker.

Hackers

His first book, Hackers: Heroes of the Computer Revolution (1984), chronicled the early computer hackers at MIT, in California, and in the Homebrew Computer Club, and articulated the hacker ethic — including the maxim that "information wants to be free". It is considered a foundational text of computing history.

Other books

Levy wrote Artificial Life (1992), Insanely Great (1994) on the Macintosh, Crypto (2001) on the fight for public cryptography, The Perfect Thing (2006) on the iPod, and In the Plex (2011), an inside account of Google. His 2020 book Facebook: The Inside Story examined the social network's growth and controversies.

Career

Levy was a founding editor of Wired in 1993 and its chief technology writer; since 2016 he has been based at Wired after stints at Newsweek and Medium's Backchannel. His reporting has included famous interviews with Steve Jobs and Bill Gates and the 1996 "Secrets of the Little Blue Box" era coverage of early phone phreaking culture.
