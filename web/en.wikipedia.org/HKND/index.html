HKND (the HK Nicaragua Canal Development Investment Company) is a Hong Kong-based company founded by Chinese businessman Wang Jing to develop the Nicaragua Grand Canal, a proposed shipping waterway across Nicaragua that would rival the Panama Canal. In June 2013, Nicaragua's National Assembly passed legislation granting HKND a 50-year concession (renewable for a further 50 years) to finance, build and operate the canal and associated projects, including ports, an airport, free-trade zones and an oil pipeline.

The project

The planned route, about 278 km from the Caribbean coast near Bluefields across Lake Nicaragua to the Pacific at Brito, was formally launched with a ceremonial groundbreaking in December 2014 on preliminary access roads. The estimated cost was around US$50 billion. The project drew strong opposition from Nicaraguan farmers and environmentalists concerned about displacement and the ecological impact on Lake Nicaragua, the largest freshwater lake in Central America.

Stall and collapse

Wang Jing's personal fortune, largely tied to the telecom company Xinwei, collapsed in the 2015 Chinese stock market crash, and little construction ever took place. HKND closed its Hong Kong offices in 2018. The Nicaraguan government formally repealed the concession law in 2024, ending the project.
