The Orkney Islands (Old Norse: Orkneyjar) are an archipelago of about 70 islands lying off the north coast of mainland Scotland, where the Atlantic Ocean meets the North Sea. Separated from Caithness by the Pentland Firth, they form one of Scotland's 32 council areas, administered from Kirkwall on the largest island, known as the Mainland.

History

Orkney has been inhabited since the Mesolithic, and its Neolithic monuments — Skara Brae, Maeshowe, the Ring of Brodgar and the Stones of Stenness — form the Heart of Neolithic Orkney UNESCO World Heritage Site. The islands were settled by Norse Vikings from the 8th century and ruled as a Norwegian earldom until they were pledged to Scotland in 1468–69 in lieu of a royal dowry.

Geography and economy

The low-lying farmland of Orkney is fertile and green, in contrast to the sea cliffs of Hoy and the west Mainland. Farming (beef cattle, whisky distilling), fishing, tourism and renewable energy are the mainstays of the economy; Orkney is a world centre for marine and tidal energy testing through the European Marine Energy Centre (EMEC).

Culture

Orcadian culture retains a strong Norse character in place names, dialect and folklore. The Ba' game in Kirkwall, the St Magnus Festival and a distinctive archaeological and literary heritage (including the Orkneyinga saga) mark the islands' identity.
