Customer development

The customer development process: customer discovery, customer validation, customer creation and company building.

Customer development is a formal methodology for building startups and new corporate ventures. It is the process by which a founder or product team tests hypotheses about a business model by getting out of the building and talking directly to prospective customers. The methodology was developed by Silicon Valley serial entrepreneur Steve Blank, who first described it in his 2005 book The Four Steps to the Epiphany and later expanded it in The Startup Owner's Manual (2012), co-authored with Bob Dorf.

Customer development is one of the three core components of the lean startup movement, alongside business model design and agile engineering. Its central claim is that most startups fail not because they lack a product, but because they lack customers and a proven model for finding them.

The four steps

Customer discovery

In customer discovery, founders translate their initial vision into a series of testable business model hypotheses, typically organised using the Business Model Canvas of Alexander Osterwalder. They then test the problem hypothesis — whether customers actually experience the problem the product addresses — and the solution hypothesis, by presenting a minimum viable product or prototype. The output is a validated (or revised) problem–solution fit.

Customer validation

Customer validation tests whether the resulting business model is repeatable and scalable. The team attempts to sell the product using the sales roadmap developed in discovery; only when a small set of passionate early customers ("earlyvangelists") buy repeatedly without heroic sales effort does the company proceed. Failure at this stage sends the team back to discovery in a move Blank calls a "pivot".

Customer creation

Customer creation builds end-user demand and drives it into the sales channel. Its form depends on the type of market the startup is entering: an existing market, a resegmented market, a new market, or a clone of a model proven elsewhere.

Company building

Company building transitions the organisation from an informal, learning-oriented startup into a formal company with departments, processes and a mission-oriented culture.

Relationship to lean startup

Eric Ries, a student of Blank's, combined customer development with agile software practices in The Lean Startup (2011). Where customer development focuses on testing the business model with customers, the lean startup adds the build–measure–learn loop for product development. Blank summarised the combined approach in his May 2013 Harvard Business Review article "Why the Lean Start-Up Changes Everything".

Criticism

Critics note that heavy reliance on customer interviews can bias teams toward incremental improvements rather than visionary products, and that the method is harder to apply in deep-tech or regulated markets where customers cannot articulate requirements. Supporters reply that the method tests behaviour (such as payment) rather than opinions alone.
