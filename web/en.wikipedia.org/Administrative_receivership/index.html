Administrative receivership

JurisdictionUnited Kingdom (England and Wales)
Key statuteInsolvency Act 1986; restricted by Enterprise Act 2002
Appointed byHolder of a floating charge over substantially the whole of a company's property
PurposeRealise assets to repay the appointing secured creditor

Administrative receivership is a procedure in the United Kingdom under which a secured creditor holding a floating charge over substantially the whole of a company's undertaking may appoint an administrative receiver — a licensed insolvency practitioner — to take control of the company and realise its assets for the creditor's benefit.

The administrative receiver displaces the company's directors, may continue to trade the business, sell assets or sell the business as a going concern, and owes his or her primary duty to the appointing charge holder rather than to creditors generally. The procedure derives from the debentures traditionally taken by banks over company assets, and its powers were codified in the Insolvency Act 1986.

Restriction by the Enterprise Act 2002

Administrative receivership was widely criticised for favouring the appointing bank at the expense of unsecured creditors and for offering little incentive to rescue the company. The Enterprise Act 2002 therefore prohibited holders of floating charges created after 15 September 2003 from appointing administrative receivers (with exceptions for certain capital-market, project-finance and similar arrangements), and channelled them instead into the administration procedure, in which the administrator must act in the interests of creditors as a whole and consider rescuing the company. Charges created before the cut-off date can still support a receivership.
