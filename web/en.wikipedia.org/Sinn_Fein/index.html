Sinn Féin

Sinn Féin Leader: Mary Lou McDonald
Founded: 28 November 1905
Headquarters: 44 Parnell Square, Dublin
Ideology: Irish republicanism; democratic socialism; left-wing nationalism
Political position: Left-wing
European affiliation: The Left in the European Parliament

Sinn Féin is an Irish republican and democratic socialist political party active in both the Republic of Ireland and Northern Ireland. Its name is Irish for "we ourselves" or "ourselves alone". The original Sinn Féin organisation was founded in 1905 by Arthur Griffith, and the modern party traces its political lineage through several splits in the republican movement over the course of the twentieth century.

Sinn Féin is currently led by Mary Lou McDonald, who succeeded Gerry Adams as party president in 2018. Following the 2020 Irish general election, Sinn Féin won the largest share of first-preference votes of any party in the Republic, and following the 2022 Northern Ireland Assembly election it became the largest party in the Northern Ireland Assembly, entitling it to the post of First Minister, which Michelle O'Neill took up in February 2024.

History

The original Sinn Féin was founded on 28 November 1905 by Arthur Griffith, drawing together nationalist groups opposed to British rule in Ireland. The party won a landslide in the 1918 general election in Ireland, and its MPs refused to take their seats at Westminster, instead establishing the First Dáil in Dublin in January 1919, an event that coincided with the start of the Irish War of Independence. After the Anglo-Irish Treaty of 1921 the movement split, giving rise over time to the parties that became Fine Gael and Fianna Fáil.

The modern Sinn Féin emerged from the split of 1970, when the republican movement divided into Official and Provisional wings. During the Troubles in Northern Ireland, Sinn Féin was widely regarded as the political wing of the Provisional IRA. Under the leadership of Gerry Adams from 1983, the party increasingly pursued an electoral strategy, a process that culminated in the Good Friday Agreement of 1998 and the party's entry into power-sharing government in Northern Ireland.

Policies

Sinn Féin's central objective is the reunification of Ireland, ending the partition established in 1921. The party campaigns for a referendum on Irish unity, as provided for in the Good Friday Agreement. Domestically it advocates increased public spending on housing and healthcare, the building of large numbers of social and affordable homes, and progressive taxation. In Northern Ireland it supports the full implementation of the Good Friday Agreement and power-sharing institutions.

Electoral performance

In the 2020 Irish general election Sinn Féin won 24.5 per cent of first-preference votes and 37 seats in Dáil Éireann, the best result for the modern party. In the 2022 Northern Ireland Assembly election it won 27 seats and 29 per cent of first-preference votes, becoming the largest party. At the 2024 UK general election the party won seven seats in Northern Ireland, though it maintains its long-standing policy of abstentionism at Westminster.
