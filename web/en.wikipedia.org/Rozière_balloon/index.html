Rozière balloon

A Rozière balloon (or simply Rozière) is a type of hybrid balloon that combines the characteristics of both hot air balloons and gas balloons. It has a sealed cell of lifting gas (originally hydrogen, now usually helium) above a cone-shaped hot air envelope. Heating the air in the lower envelope with a burner allows the pilot to control altitude without venting gas or dropping ballast, while the gas cell provides most of the lift and greatly extends endurance.

History

The design is named after Jean-François Pilâtre de Rozier, who built the first example in 1785 for an attempted crossing of the English Channel. His balloon combined a hydrogen balloon with a hot air balloon directly beneath it, heated by an onboard fire. On 15 June 1785 the craft caught fire and crashed near Wimereux, killing de Rozier and his companion Pierre Romain — the first fatalities in aviation history. Historians attribute the disaster to sparks from the fire igniting the hydrogen.

Modern use

The hybrid principle was revived in the twentieth century once inert helium replaced hydrogen and reliable propane burners replaced open fires. Modern Rozières are the standard craft for long-distance and record balloon flights because they can stay aloft for days or weeks, using burner fuel only to compensate for the day–night cooling cycle.

Famous flights include the first non-stop circumnavigation of the Earth by balloon, completed by Bertrand Piccard and Brian Jones aboard Breitling Orbiter 3 in March 1999; Steve Fossett's first solo circumnavigation in the Spirit of Freedom in 2002; and Fedor Konyukhov's record solo round-the-world flight of just over 11 days in 2016. Don Cameron's company in Bristol, England, became a leading manufacturer of modern Rozière envelopes.
