Aldermaston Marches

The Aldermaston Marches were anti-nuclear weapons demonstrations in the United Kingdom in the late 1950s and early 1960s. The first march, at Easter 1958, was organised by the Direct Action Committee Against Nuclear War and supported by the newly founded Campaign for Nuclear Disarmament (CND); about 4,000 people walked from London to the Atomic Weapons Research Establishment at Aldermaston in Berkshire over four days.

The marches

The 1958 march became an annual Easter event. From 1959 the direction was reversed — marching from Aldermaston to Trafalgar Square in London — and numbers grew to tens of thousands, making the marches among the largest protests in modern British history to that point. Participants included Bertrand Russell, Canon John Collins, and Michael Foot. Gerald Holtom's nuclear disarmament symbol, designed for the first march, became the universal peace sign.

Legacy

The marches helped define CND and the culture of British protest in the 1960s, influencing later movements. Annual Aldermaston marches were discontinued in 1964 as CND changed tactics, though commemorative marches and protests at the site — now the Atomic Weapons Establishment — have continued, including a large 50th-anniversary demonstration in 2008.
