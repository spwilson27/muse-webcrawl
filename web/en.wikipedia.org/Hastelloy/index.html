Hastelloy

Material typeNickel-based superalloy
Trademark holderHaynes International
Known forCorrosion resistance
Hastelloy alloys are used for corrosion-resistant chemical process equipment.

Hastelloy is the registered trademark of Haynes International, Inc. for a family of highly corrosion-resistant metal alloys based primarily on nickel, with significant additions of molybdenum, chromium, and other elements such as iron, cobalt, tungsten, and manganese. The name is derived from "Haynes Stellite alloy" (Stellite being another Haynes alloy family).

Properties

Hastelloy alloys are renowned for resistance to a wide range of severe corrosive environments, including strong acids (hydrochloric, sulfuric, phosphoric), chlorides, and oxidizing and reducing media, as well as resistance to pitting, crevice corrosion, and stress corrosion cracking. Many grades retain strength and oxidation resistance at elevated temperatures, making them a type of superalloy.

Common grades

  • Hastelloy C-276 — a nickel–chromium–molybdenum alloy with tungsten, one of the most widely used corrosion-resistant alloys for chemical processing.
  • Hastelloy C-22 — offers improved resistance to oxidizing media and localized corrosion.
  • Hastelloy B-2 / B-3 — nickel–molybdenum alloys optimized for reducing environments such as hydrochloric acid.
  • Hastelloy X — a solid-solution-strengthened alloy used in gas turbine and aerospace high-temperature components.

Applications

The alloys are used in chemical processing reactors, heat exchangers, scrubbers and flue-gas desulfurization systems, pharmaceutical and pulp-and-paper equipment, sour-gas (oil and gas) service, and aerospace and industrial gas turbine components.
