The politics of Iceland take place in the framework of a parliamentary representative democratic republic, whereby the Prime Minister is the head of government, and of a multi-party system. Executive power is exercised by the government; legislative power is vested in both the government and the parliament, the Althing, founded in 930 and among the oldest parliaments in the world.

Executive branch

The President of Iceland is the head of state, elected by popular vote for a four-year term with no term limit. The role is largely ceremonial, though the president holds a suspensive veto and formally appoints the government. Day-to-day executive power rests with the Prime Minister and cabinet, who must command the confidence of the Althing. Coalition governments are the norm.

Legislative branch

The Althing has 63 members elected for four-year terms by proportional representation in six multi-member constituencies. Iceland consistently ranks among the world leaders in women's parliamentary representation and in 1980 elected Vigdís Finnbogadóttir, the world's first democratically elected female head of state.

Political parties

  • Independence Party — centre-right, historically the largest party.
  • Social Democratic Alliance — centre-left.
  • Progressive Party — centrist agrarian.
  • Left-Green Movement — eco-socialist.
  • Pirate Party — direct democracy and civil liberties.

Judiciary and foreign relations

The judiciary is independent; the Supreme Court of Iceland is the highest court. Iceland is a member of NATO (without its own standing army), the EEA, EFTA and the Schengen Area, but not of the European Union.
