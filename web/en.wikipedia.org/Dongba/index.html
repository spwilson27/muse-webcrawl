Dongba


Dongba pictographic script used in Naxi religious manuscripts.

Dongba refers to the religion, priests, and pictographic writing system of the Naxi people of Lijiang in Yunnan province, southwestern China. The term literally means "wise man" or "teacher" in the Naxi language and designates the priest-scholars who preside over rituals, preserve oral lore, and keep the written records of the faith.

Religion

Dongba religion is an animistic, shamanistic tradition that blends elements of Tibetan Bon, Buddhism, and Taoism with indigenous Naxi ancestor worship and nature cults. Its priests conduct ceremonies for major life events such as weddings and funerals, as well as rituals for healing, exorcism, and the propitiation of nature spirits. Priests are chosen through family lines and trained in the recitation of the scriptures, ritual dance, and painting.

Script

The Dongba script is a pictographic system of roughly 1,400 characters, considered one of the few living pictographic writing systems in the world. It is used alongside the syllabic Geba script. Manuscripts are traditionally written on handmade paper bound in loose-leaf form and are read aloud by priests, the pictures serving as prompts for recited passages much longer than the written text. Dongba manuscripts were inscribed in UNESCO's Memory of the World Register in 2003.
