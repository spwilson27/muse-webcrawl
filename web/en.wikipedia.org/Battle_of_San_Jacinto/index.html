Battle of San Jacinto

Battle of San Jacinto
Date21 April 1836
LocationNear present-day La Porte, Texas
ResultDecisive Texian victory
BelligerentsRepublic of Texas; Mexico
CommandersSam Houston; Antonio López de Santa Anna (captured)

The Battle of San Jacinto was fought on 21 April 1836 and was the decisive battle of the Texas Revolution. The Texian Army under General Sam Houston defeated the Mexican army of General Antonio López de Santa Anna in a fight lasting only about eighteen minutes.

Houston caught Santa Anna's force of roughly 1,300 men encamped near the San Jacinto River, its escape route cut by the destruction of Vince's Bridge. Attacking in the afternoon while the Mexican troops rested, the Texians — shouting "Remember the Alamo!" and "Remember Goliad!" — overran the camp. Some 630 Mexican soldiers were killed and 730 captured, including Santa Anna himself, found the next day hiding in the grass; Texian losses were nine killed and thirty wounded.

Aftermath

In captivity Santa Anna signed the Treaties of Velasco, ordering Mexican forces south of the Rio Grande and effectively ending the war. Texas became an independent republic, though Mexico never formally recognised its independence, and annexation by the United States a decade later led to the Mexican–American War. The battlefield is now marked by the San Jacinto Monument, one of the tallest masonry monuments in the world.
