Camilo Torres Restrepo

Camilo Torres Restrepo (3 February 1929 – 15 February 1966) was a Colombian Roman Catholic priest, sociologist, and revolutionary. A pioneer of liberation theology and a founder of the sociology faculty at the National University of Colombia, he concluded that revolutionary struggle was a Christian duty and joined the National Liberation Army (ELN) guerrillas, in whose ranks he was killed in his first combat.

Early life and studies

Torres was born into a liberal, upper-class Bogotá family. He briefly studied law before entering the seminary and was ordained a priest in 1954. In Belgium he studied sociology at the Catholic University of Louvain, where he co-founded a group of Colombian students and came into contact with Christian democratic and socialist thought.

Academic and political work

Returning to Colombia in 1959, Torres helped found the first sociology faculty in Latin America at the National University and researched poverty and land reform. His advocacy for peasants and the urban poor brought him into conflict with both the Church hierarchy and the state. In 1965 he formed the United People's Front (Frente Unido) as a political platform uniting opposition movements.

Guerrilla and death

Pressed by Cardinal Luis Concha Córdoba, Torres was reduced to the lay state and left public ministry. Declaring that "the Catholic who is not a revolutionary is living in mortal sin," he joined the ELN in late 1965. He was killed on 15 February 1966 in an ambush of an army patrol near El Carmen, Santander. His body was buried in an undisclosed location, and he became an enduring symbol of the revolutionary priest.

Legacy

Torres anticipated key themes of liberation theology, later developed by Gustavo Gutiérrez and others: the preferential option for the poor and the compatibility of Christian faith with revolutionary change. His writings, including the "Platform for a Popular Unity Movement" and his "Message to Christians," remain influential in Latin American political thought.
