Golden handshake

A golden handshake is a clause in an executive's employment contract specifying that the employer will provide a significant severance package if the employee loses their job. It typically includes cash compensation, stock options, pension benefits and continued insurance coverage, and is usually far more generous than statutory redundancy terms.

Purpose

Golden handshakes are intended to attract and retain senior executives, to compensate them for the insecurity of high-profile roles, and to ease transitions of leadership. In India and some other countries, the term is also used more broadly for generous voluntary retirement schemes offered to employees to reduce the workforce.

Controversy

Golden handshakes are frequently criticised when departing executives receive large payouts despite poor company performance — sometimes called "payment for failure." Shareholder groups and governance codes in many countries now press for severance terms to be capped, disclosed and linked to performance. In the United Kingdom, payments for loss of office by company directors are subject to shareholder approval rules under the Companies Act.

Related terms

A golden parachute is a similar package triggered specifically by a change of ownership, such as a takeover or merger, designed both to retain executives during the uncertainty and to make hostile takeovers more expensive. A golden hello is a signing bonus used to recruit an executive. A much smaller payment is sometimes ironically termed a "golden goodbye" or, when minimal, a "tin handshake."
