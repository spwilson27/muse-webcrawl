Matthew Stanley Meselson (May 24, 1930 – September 30, 2024) was an American geneticist and molecular biologist at Harvard University. He is best known for the Meselson–Stahl experiment of 1958, carried out with Franklin Stahl, which demonstrated that DNA replicates by a semiconservative mechanism — frequently described as "the most beautiful experiment in biology."

Early life and education

Meselson was born in Denver, Colorado, and grew up in Los Angeles, California. Interested in chemistry and physics from childhood, he entered the University of Chicago at the age of 16 under Robert Hutchins's program for younger students, receiving his bachelor's degree in 1951. He went on to graduate study in physical chemistry at the California Institute of Technology, where his doctoral adviser was Linus Pauling; he received his PhD in 1957.

The Meselson–Stahl experiment

Working with Franklin Stahl at Caltech, Meselson developed a method of density-gradient centrifugation using caesium chloride that could separate DNA molecules according to their isotopic composition. By growing E. coli in a medium containing the heavy isotope nitrogen-15 and then transferring the bacteria to ordinary nitrogen-14, they showed that after one generation the DNA formed a single band of hybrid (half-heavy) density, and after two generations both hybrid and light bands appeared. The result, published in 1958, provided decisive evidence for the semiconservative model of DNA replication proposed by Watson and Crick.

Later research

Meselson joined the Harvard faculty in 1960. His laboratory contributed to the early evidence for messenger RNA, the discovery and characterization of restriction enzymes, and the study of DNA mismatch repair and recombination. With Sidney Brenner and François Jacob he performed experiments supporting the existence of messenger RNA.

Arms control advocacy

From the 1960s onward Meselson was a prominent scientific voice against chemical and biological weapons. He advised the United States government, investigated the Sverdlovsk anthrax leak of 1979 and the "yellow rain" controversy, and helped argue the case for the Biological Weapons Convention and the Chemical Weapons Convention. He co-chaired the Harvard Sussex Program on Chemical and Biological Weapons.

Honours

Meselson was elected to the National Academy of Sciences and received numerous honours, including the Lasker Award for Special Achievement in Medical Science in 2004.
