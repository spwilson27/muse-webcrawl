Level design

Level design or environment design is a discipline of game development involving the creation of video game levels — locales, stages or missions. Rather than writing rules (the province of game design) or producing assets (art and programming), the level designer works with those assets to build spaces that guide the player, stage challenges and teach mechanics through play.

Process

Development typically proceeds from sketches and paper layouts to a "greybox" or "blockout" built from untextured geometry, which is playtested for flow, pacing and difficulty before art passes add lighting, props and detail. Level designers plan player paths, sightlines, cover, enemy and item placement, scripted events and "affordances" that signal where the player can go. Tools range from dedicated editors such as Valve's Hammer and Unreal Editor to the engines' built-in world builders; historically, classic level editors for Doom and Quake helped launch the modding scene, and designers such as John Romero became known by name.

Principles

Common concerns include guiding the player with light, geometry and landmarks ("weenies"), balancing exploration against linearity, gating progress with keys, abilities or knowledge, and shaping a difficulty curve across a game. Modern open-world and procedurally generated games have broadened the discipline into world design and procedural layout.
