Tour Montparnasse, also known as Tour Maine-Montparnasse, is a 210-metre (689 ft) office skyscraper in the Montparnasse area of Paris, France. Constructed from 1969 to 1973, it was the tallest skyscraper in France until 2011 and remains the only skyscraper in Paris proper, as opposed to the La Défense business district outside the city centre.

History

The tower was built on the site of the old Gare Montparnasse railway station as part of a major urban redevelopment of the district. Its dark, monolithic appearance proved highly controversial; critics charged that it scarred the Paris skyline, and the backlash contributed to a ban on buildings over seven storeys in central Paris imposed two years after its completion. A common Parisian joke holds that the tower's observation deck offers the best view in the city because it is the only place from which the tower itself cannot be seen.

Observation deck and renovation

The 56th floor houses an observatory and restaurant offering panoramic views over Paris. In 2017 an international competition was won by Nouvelle AOM for a renovation ("Demain Montparnasse") intended to give the tower a lighter, greener facade with a greenhouse near the top; the works, delayed, were planned around the 2024 Olympic period.

Climbing

In 1995 the French urban climber Alain Robert, nicknamed "the French Spider-Man", scaled the tower's exterior without authorization or safety equipment.
