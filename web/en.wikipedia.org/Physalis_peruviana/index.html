Physalis peruviana

FamilySolanaceae
Common namesCape gooseberry, goldenberry, uchuva
OriginAndean South America

Physalis peruviana is a species of plant in the nightshade family native to the Andes of South America, cultivated for its sweet, tart, round yellow fruit. It is known in English as the Cape gooseberry or goldenberry, and as uchuva in Colombia.

Description

The plant is a herbaceous perennial growing to about 1–1.6 metres. Its marble-sized berry ripens inside a papery lantern-like calyx, which protects the fruit and turns straw-coloured when ripe.

Cultivation and use

The species has been cultivated since Inca times and was grown at the Cape of Good Hope by the early 19th century, giving rise to the name "Cape gooseberry". The fresh fruit is eaten raw, made into jams and desserts, and is valued for its vitamin C content. Unripe fruit and other parts of the plant are toxic.
