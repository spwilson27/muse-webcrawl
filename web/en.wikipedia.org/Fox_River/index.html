Fox River is the name of several rivers in North America. The best known include the Fox River of Wisconsin, which flows through Lake Winnebago and Green Bay, and the Fox River of northern Illinois, a tributary of the Illinois River. This article focuses on the Illinois river; other rivers of the name are treated in their own articles.

Fox River (Illinois)

The Fox River of Illinois rises in southeastern Wisconsin near Waukesha and flows south about 202 miles through the Chain O'Lakes and the Fox River Valley — including the cities of Elgin, St. Charles, Geneva, Batavia, Aurora, and Yorkville — before joining the Illinois River at Ottawa. The river's valley was settled early by Europeans, powered mills and industry, and today the Fox River is a major recreational resource, lined by trails and forest preserves. A series of dams built for navigation and power now serve principally for recreation and flood management.

Fox River (Wisconsin)

The Wisconsin Fox River — divided into the Upper Fox, flowing into Lake Winnebago, and the Lower Fox, which drops sharply from the lake to Green Bay — was a historic fur-trade and transport route linking the Great Lakes with the Mississippi watershed via the Fox–Wisconsin portage. Its lower course became one of the most industrialized waterways in the United States and the subject of a major PCB contamination cleanup.
