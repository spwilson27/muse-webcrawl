Elias Lönnrot

Elias Lönnrot (9 April 1802 – 19 March 1884) was a Finnish physician, philologist and collector of traditional Finnish oral poetry. He is best known for creating the Finnish national epic, the Kalevala (1835, enlarged 1849), from short ballads and poems he gathered during eleven field expeditions in Finland and Karelia between 1828 and 1844.

Early life and education

Lönnrot was born in Sammatti, in the south of Finland, then part of the Kingdom of Sweden. He studied medicine at the Imperial Academy of Turku, but the Great Fire of Turku (1827) destroyed much of the university and his early manuscripts. He completed his medical degree at the University of Helsinki in 1832, with a dissertation on Finnish folk medicine.

Collecting expeditions

Beginning in 1828, Lönnrot travelled on foot and by boat through eastern Finland and Russian Karelia, visiting runo singers such as Arhippa Perttunen and Ontrei Malinen. He recorded thousands of lines of traditional sung poetry in the trochaic tetrameter known as Kalevala metre. In 1834 he was appointed district doctor of Kajaani, a post he held for twenty years while continuing his collecting work.

The Kalevala

Lönnrot stitched the collected songs into a continuous epic narrative. The first edition, the so-called Old Kalevala, appeared in 1835 with 32 runos; the expanded New Kalevala of 1849 contained 50 runos and 22,795 verses. The work became a cornerstone of Finnish national identity and has been translated into dozens of languages. Lönnrot also compiled the Kanteletar (1840), a collection of lyric folk poems, and produced the first Finnish–Swedish dictionary.

Later life

In 1853 Lönnrot became professor of Finnish language and literature at the University of Helsinki. He continued lexicographical and botanical work, publishing Flora Fennica in 1860. He died in Sammatti in 1884.
