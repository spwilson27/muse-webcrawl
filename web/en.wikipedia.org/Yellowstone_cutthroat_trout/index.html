Yellowstone cutthroat trout

Scientific nameOncorhynchus clarkii bouvieri (also placed in Salmo)
RangeYellowstone River drainage, Wyoming and Montana
Distinguishing marksRed slash under the jaw; fine spotting toward the tail

The Yellowstone cutthroat trout is a subspecies of cutthroat trout native to the Yellowstone River watershed in Wyoming and Montana, including Yellowstone National Park. It is an important food source for wildlife such as grizzly bears, eagles, and otters, and a prized native sport fish.

Biology

Like other cutthroat trout, it bears a distinctive red or orange slash mark beneath the jaw. Spawning runs occur in spring, when fish move from Yellowstone Lake into tributary streams. Yellowstone Lake historically held one of the largest inland cutthroat populations in the world.

Threats and conservation

Populations have declined from introduced lake trout predation in Yellowstone Lake, hybridisation with rainbow trout, whirling disease, and drought. Intensive suppression of lake trout since the 1990s has allowed a partial recovery, and the fish is a focus of native-trout conservation across the Greater Yellowstone Ecosystem.
