Gamepad

A gamepad is a type of game controller held in two hands, where the fingers (especially thumbs) are used to provide input. Gamepads generally feature a set of buttons handled with the right thumb and a direction controller handled with the left; the direction controller has traditionally been a four-way digital cross (D-pad), while most modern gamepads add one or two analog sticks.

History

Early gamepads such as the NES controller (1983) popularized the flat rectangular design with a D-pad, invented by Nintendo's Gunpei Yokoi for the Game & Watch series. The PlayStation controller (1994) added twin grips and shoulder buttons; the DualShock (1997) added dual analog sticks and vibration. The Xbox 360 controller (2005) became a de facto standard layout for PC gaming, and modern pads from Sony, Microsoft and Nintendo share a largely common vocabulary of inputs.

Features

  • Directional pad (D-pad)
  • Analog sticks, usually two, also clickable
  • Action buttons (typically four, face-mounted)
  • Shoulder buttons and analog triggers
  • Start, select/back and home/guide buttons
  • Rumble (haptic) feedback; later HD rumble and adaptive triggers
  • Motion sensors (gyroscope and accelerometer) and touchpads on some models
