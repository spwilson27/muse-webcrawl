Battle of Towton

The Battle of Towton was fought on 29 March 1461 (Palm Sunday) near the village of Towton in Yorkshire, during the Wars of the Roses. It is generally considered the largest and bloodiest battle ever fought on English soil.

Course and aftermath

The Yorkist army of Edward IV defeated the Lancastrian army of Henry VI in a blinding snowstorm. Contemporary sources claimed enormous death tolls — figures of 28,000 or more are commonly cited. The Lancastrian cause was shattered; Henry VI fled to Scotland and Edward's kingship was secured. A mass grave discovered near the battlefield in 1996 has provided archaeological evidence of the violence of the fighting.
