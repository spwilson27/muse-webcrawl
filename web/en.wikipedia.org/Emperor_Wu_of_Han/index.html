Emperor Wu of Han

Emperor Wu of Han (June 30, 156 BC – March 29, 87 BC), born Liu Che, was the seventh emperor of the Han dynasty of China, reigning from 141 to 87 BC — one of the longest reigns in Chinese history. His rule vastly expanded China's territory and established Confucianism as the state orthodoxy.

Emperor Wu centralised power, curtailing the regional kings, and created state monopolies on salt, iron and coinage to finance his campaigns. He sent the envoy Zhang Qian westward, opening the Silk Road, and launched major offensives against the Xiongnu nomads, annexing the Hexi Corridor, the Ordos, parts of Korea (establishing commanderies including Lelang) and territory in the south including Nanyue.

His later reign was marred by costly wars, heavy taxation and the witchcraft scandal of 91 BC, which led to the deaths of his empress Wei Zifu and crown prince Liu Ju. He is nonetheless remembered as one of China's greatest emperors, whose template of imperial Confucian government lasted two millennia.
