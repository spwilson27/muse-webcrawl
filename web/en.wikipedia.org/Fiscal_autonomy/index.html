Fiscal autonomy

Fiscal autonomy is the degree of control that a government — particularly a subnational or devolved government — exercises over its own revenue raising and public spending, as opposed to depending on transfers or block grants from a central government.

Scope

Fiscal autonomy can range from limited powers to vary a few taxes at the margin, through assignment of specific revenue streams such as income tax or VAT shares, to full fiscal autonomy ("devolution max" or "devo-max"), under which a regional government raises essentially all the revenue it spends and pays a contribution toward shared services such as defence and foreign affairs.

Debates

Supporters argue fiscal autonomy improves accountability, since governments that must raise the money they spend face stronger incentives for efficiency and responsiveness. Critics point to risks of tax competition, regional inequality, and macroeconomic instability when subnational governments borrow or set taxes independently. The concept is a recurring theme in debates over devolution in the United Kingdom (especially Scotland), Spain's autonomous communities (including the Basque Country's concierto económico), and federal systems generally.
