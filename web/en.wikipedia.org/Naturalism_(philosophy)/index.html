Naturalism (philosophy)

In philosophy, naturalism is the idea that only natural laws and forces — as opposed to supernatural ones — operate in the universe, and that nothing exists beyond the natural universe, or that if it does, it does not affect the natural universe. Naturalism comprises a family of philosophical positions united by the view that philosophy should be continuous with science, in both its methods and its conclusions.

Ontological and methodological naturalism

Ontological (or metaphysical) naturalism holds that reality consists entirely of natural entities and processes; minds, values and meanings must ultimately be explicable in natural terms. Methodological naturalism is the more modest stance, adopted as a working rule in science, that explanations should appeal only to natural causes, whatever one's metaphysical commitments.

History

Antecedents reach back to the pre-Socratic materialists and to ancient Indian and Chinese thought. In the 20th century, naturalism was shaped by American philosophers such as John Dewey, Ernest Nagel and Sidney Hook, and later by W. V. O. Quine, whose "naturalized epistemology" proposed studying knowledge itself as a natural phenomenon using the methods of psychology. Contemporary naturalists include Daniel Dennett and Patricia and Paul Churchland.

Naturalism in specific fields

  • Ethics — ethical naturalism holds that moral properties are natural properties, knowable by empirical means.
  • Mind — naturalistic theories treat consciousness and intentionality as products of physical brain processes.
  • Religion — religious naturalism combines a naturalistic worldview with religious attitudes toward nature, without belief in supernatural beings.
  • Literature and art — see Naturalism (literature), a related but distinct movement.

Criticism

Critics argue that naturalism struggles to account for consciousness, normativity and mathematical truth, and that a thoroughgoing naturalism may be self-undermining if it cannot underwrite the rationality it presupposes. The philosopher Alvin Plantinga's evolutionary argument against naturalism is a prominent example of such criticism.
