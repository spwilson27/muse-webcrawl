United States Senate Select Committee on Intelligence

The United States Senate Select Committee on Intelligence (SSCI) is a select committee of the United States Senate charged with overseeing the United States Intelligence Community, including the CIA, NSA, FBI intelligence activities, and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence.

History

The committee was established in 1976 in the aftermath of the Church Committee's investigation of intelligence abuses, to provide continuous legislative oversight of covert action and intelligence collection. Unlike most Senate committees, its membership is limited to fixed terms, and its chairmanship and ranking membership include, ex officio, leaders of related committees. The committee's proceedings are often classified, though it holds public hearings, notably for confirmation of senior intelligence officials.

Work

The committee produces the annual Intelligence Authorization Act and has conducted major inquiries, including the joint inquiry into the September 11 attacks and the study of the CIA's post-9/11 detention and interrogation program, whose executive summary was released in 2014.
