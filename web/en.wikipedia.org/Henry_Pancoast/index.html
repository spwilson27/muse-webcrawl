Henry Khunrath Pancoast (February 26, 1875 – May 20, 1939) was an American radiologist regarded as one of the founding figures of radiology in the United States. He is best remembered for his description of the superior pulmonary sulcus tumor, now known as the Pancoast tumor.

Career

Pancoast was born in Philadelphia and earned his medical degree from the University of Pennsylvania in 1898. After an early surgical career, he turned to the new field of X-ray diagnosis and in 1902 became the first professor of roentgenology at the University of Pennsylvania, one of the first such chairs in America. He headed the radiology department at the Hospital of the University of Pennsylvania and trained many of the specialty's early practitioners.

Superior sulcus tumor

In papers published in 1924 and 1932, Pancoast described tumors arising at the apex of the lung that produced a characteristic syndrome of shoulder pain radiating down the arm, rib destruction, and Horner's syndrome. The tumor and its associated clinical picture became known by his name, as did Pancoast syndrome.

Other contributions

Pancoast wrote extensively on radiography of the chest, the gastrointestinal tract, and the skull, and studied occupational radiation hazards. He served as president of the American Roentgen Ray Society and was a leader in standardizing radiological training and practice.
