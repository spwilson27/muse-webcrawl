Repointing

Repointing is the process of renewing the pointing — the external part of mortar joints — in masonry construction. Over time, weathering and decay cause voids in the joints between masonry units, allowing water to penetrate and damage the structure.

Process

Damaged mortar is raked out, typically to a depth of about 20–25 mm, and replaced with new mortar matched in composition, colour, and profile. For historic buildings, lime mortar is preferred; hard Portland cement mortars can trap moisture and cause the masonry itself to spall.

Related techniques

Tuckpointing is a decorative variant in which two contrasting colours of mortar are used, with a fine raised fillet, to imitate finely jointed brickwork. Repointing is a major element of masonry conservation and building maintenance.
