Self-Reliance

"Self-Reliance" is an essay written by American philosopher and essayist Ralph Waldo Emerson, first published in his 1841 collection Essays: First Series. It is one of the most influential works of American Transcendentalism.

Themes

The essay urges the reader to trust their own intuition and inner voice rather than conforming to society, institutions and the opinions of others. Its famous maxims include "Trust thyself", "Whoso would be a man must be a nonconformist", and "A foolish consistency is the hobgoblin of little minds". Emerson argues that each individual has access to a universal spirit, that imitation is a kind of spiritual suicide ("Envy is ignorance; imitation is suicide"), and that genuineness and self-trust are the basis of a worthwhile life.

Influence

"Self-Reliance" became a foundational text of American individualism, influencing generations of writers and thinkers, and remains widely read and quoted. The term is also used more generally for the quality of relying on one's own resources and judgement.
