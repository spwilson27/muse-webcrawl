Microhomology-mediated end joining
Also calledMMEJ, alternative end joining (alt-EJ)
FunctionRepair of DNA double-strand breaks
Key proteinDNA polymerase theta (Pol θ)
AccuracyError-prone; causes deletions

Microhomology-mediated end joining (MMEJ), also known as alternative end joining, is a pathway for repairing double-strand breaks in DNA. Unlike the more precise homologous recombination and classical non-homologous end joining pathways, MMEJ uses short homologous sequences — microhomologies of roughly 5–25 base pairs — exposed during break processing to align the broken ends before joining.

Mechanism

After a double-strand break, end resection by proteins such as MRE11 and CtIP reveals single-stranded overhangs. When matching microhomologies on the two sides anneal, the intervening flaps are trimmed, DNA polymerase theta (Pol θ) extends the annealed ends, and a ligase seals the break. Because the sequence between the microhomologies is lost, MMEJ characteristically produces deletions and is therefore mutagenic.

Biological significance

MMEJ acts throughout the cell cycle and serves as a backup when the main repair pathways fail. Its error-prone character contributes to the deletions, translocations and genomic rearrangements seen in cancer genomes. Because some cancers — particularly those deficient in homologous recombination, such as BRCA-mutant tumours — depend heavily on polymerase theta, Pol θ inhibitors are under development as targeted cancer therapies exploiting synthetic lethality.
