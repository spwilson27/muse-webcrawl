Sidney Mintz

Sidney Wilfred Mintz (November 16, 1922 – December 27, 2015) was an American anthropologist best known for his studies of the Caribbean, the anthropology of food, and his landmark work Sweetness and Power: The Place of Sugar in Modern History (1985). He taught for many years at Yale University and later at Johns Hopkins University.

Early life and education

Mintz was born in Dover, New Jersey, to Eastern European Jewish immigrant parents. He studied at Brooklyn College and earned his PhD at Columbia University in 1951, where he was influenced by Charles Wagley and the circle around Julian Steward. His dissertation was based on fieldwork among sugarcane workers in Puerto Rico.

Career

Mintz's early research, published as Worker in the Cane (1960), examined the life of a Puerto Rican sugarcane worker and was an early example of the anthropological life history. He conducted extensive fieldwork in Puerto Rico, Jamaica, and Haiti, studying peasantries, markets, and plantation systems, and he was a co-founder of the anthropology of the Caribbean as a field.

Sweetness and Power

His most famous book, Sweetness and Power (1985), traced the history of sugar from a luxury spice to a mass-produced staple, connecting Caribbean slave plantations with changing consumption patterns in Britain. The book became a foundational text in food studies and in the anthropology of commodities, linking production and consumption within a global historical framework. His later work Tasting Food, Tasting Freedom (1996) continued his exploration of food and culture.

Death and legacy

Mintz died in 2015 at the age of 93. He is remembered as a founder of the anthropology of food and a pioneer of historically oriented ethnography.
