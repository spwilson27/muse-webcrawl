Rail transport modelling

A model railway layout in HO scale, the most popular scale worldwide.

Rail transport modelling is a hobby in which rail transport systems are modelled at a reduced scale. Model railways typically include scale locomotives, rolling stock, track, scenery, signals, and often buildings and landscapes forming a layout. The hobby ranges from simple train sets to highly detailed, prototypical reconstructions of real railways, and is popular worldwide.

Common scales include OO and HO (both using 16.5 mm gauge track, at 1:76 and 1:87 scale respectively), N scale (1:148–1:160), O scale (1:43–1:48), and larger garden scales such as G. Each scale has standardised track gauges and coupler systems maintained by bodies such as the NMRA (North America) and MOROP/NEM (Europe).

History

Model trains began as clockwork and early electric toys in the late 19th century, with makers such as Märklin in Germany and Lionel in the United States. After the Second World War the hobby matured into scale modelling: smaller scales (HO, then N) made realistic layouts possible in domestic spaces, and kit building, scratch building and detailed scenery became central. Since the 1990s Digital Command Control has allowed independent control of many locomotives on a single track, each fitted with a decoder, often with synchronised sound.

Layouts and practice

Layouts may be permanent home installations, portable exhibition models, or club layouts. Modellers specialise in scenery, weathering, prototypical operation to timetables, and finescale standards. Notable public layouts include Miniatur Wunderland in Hamburg, the largest model railway in the world, which draws millions of visitors.
