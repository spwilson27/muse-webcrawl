Assassination of John F. Kennedy

Assassination of John F. Kennedy
Date: 22 November 1963, 12:30 p.m. CST
Location: Dealey Plaza, Dallas, Texas
Target: John F. Kennedy
Perpetrator: Lee Harvey Oswald (per Warren Commission)

John F. Kennedy, the 35th president of the United States, was assassinated on Friday, 22 November 1963, while riding in a presidential motorcade through Dealey Plaza in Dallas, Texas. Kennedy was fatally shot from the nearby Texas School Book Depository by Lee Harvey Oswald, a former US Marine.

Events

Kennedy was travelling with his wife Jacqueline, Texas governor John Connally and Connally's wife Nellie. Shots rang out as the motorcade turned onto Elm Street. Kennedy was struck in the neck and head; Connally was seriously wounded. The president was pronounced dead at Parkland Memorial Hospital at 1:00 p.m. Vice President Lyndon B. Johnson was sworn in aboard Air Force One two hours later.

Aftermath and investigations

Oswald was arrested that afternoon for the murder of Dallas police officer J. D. Tippit, and was himself shot dead two days later by nightclub owner Jack Ruby while being transferred, live on television. The Warren Commission (1964) concluded Oswald acted alone; a 1979 House committee concluded a conspiracy was probable, a finding later disputed. The assassination remains a defining national trauma and the subject of enduring debate.
