Stephen King

Stephen Edwin King (born September 21, 1947) is an American author of horror, supernatural fiction, suspense, crime, science fiction, and fantasy novels. Described as the "King of Horror", his books have sold more than 400 million copies worldwide, and many have been adapted into films, television series, miniseries, and comic books.

King has published 65 novels, including seven under the pen name Richard Bachman, and five non-fiction books. He has also written approximately 200 short stories, most of which have been published in book collections. His first novel, Carrie (1974), launched his career, followed by 'Salem's Lot, The Shining, The Stand, and It.

Early life

King was born in Portland, Maine. When he was two years old, his father left the family, and King and his elder brother were raised by their mother, at times under conditions of poverty. He spent part of his childhood in Indiana and Connecticut before the family returned to Maine. He attended the University of Maine at Orono, graduating in 1970 with a Bachelor of Arts in English, and worked as a teacher while writing short stories for men's magazines.

Career

King's breakthrough came with Carrie in 1974, the paperback rights of which sold for a then-enormous sum. Subsequent novels such as The Shining (1977), The Stand (1978), and It (1986) cemented his reputation. From 1982 to 2013 he published the eight volumes of The Dark Tower series, which he considers his magnum opus. Under the pseudonym Richard Bachman he wrote Rage, The Long Walk, Roadwork, and The Running Man. In 1999 he was struck by a van while walking and nearly died; the memoir On Writing (2000) recounts his recovery.

Style and themes

King's writing is noted for detailed, colloquial prose, small-town Maine settings such as the fictional towns of Castle Rock and Derry, and the recurrence of characters and places across novels, creating an interconnected fictional universe. Recurring themes include childhood trauma, the abuse of power, addiction, and the persistence of evil.
