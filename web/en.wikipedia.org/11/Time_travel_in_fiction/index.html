Time travel in fiction

Time travel is a common theme in fiction, principally since the late 19th century, and has been depicted in a variety of media, including literature, television, film and video games. Stories explore travelling to the past or the future, changing history, and paradoxes such as the grandfather paradox and bootstrap paradox.

History

Early examples include the Sanskrit epic Mahabharata and Charles Dickens's A Christmas Carol (1843), in which spirits show Scrooge past and future scenes. H. G. Wells's novella The Time Machine (1895) popularised the concept of a vehicle that carries its operator through time, and coined the term "time machine."

Themes and mechanisms

Fictional time travel is achieved by machines, supernatural means, sleep (as in Mark Twain's A Connecticut Yankee in King Arthur's Court), or relativistic effects. Common plot structures include altering the past with unintended consequences, meeting one's earlier self, tourism to historical events, and wars fought across timelines. Notable works include Back to the Future, Doctor Who, The Terminator and Stephen King's 11/22/63, in which a teacher travels back to prevent the Kennedy assassination.
