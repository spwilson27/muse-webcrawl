Horror fiction

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Genre: Speculative fiction intended to frighten, scare or disgust
Precursors: Gothic fiction, folklore, ghost stories

Horror is a genre of speculative fiction that is intended to disturb, frighten or scare. Literary historian J. A. Cuddon defined the horror story as "a piece of fiction in prose of variable length ... which shocks, or even frightens the reader, or perhaps induces a feeling of repulsion or loathing". Horror intends to create an eerie and frightening atmosphere, and is often divided into the psychological horror and supernatural horror sub-genres.

History

Horror has ancient roots in folklore and religious traditions concerning death, the afterlife, evil and demons. It emerged as a distinct literary genre with the Gothic novel, beginning with Horace Walpole's The Castle of Otranto (1764), and developed through works such as Mary Shelley's Frankenstein (1818), the tales of Edgar Allan Poe, Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde (1886) and Bram Stoker's Dracula (1897). In the 20th century H. P. Lovecraft pioneered cosmic horror, and later authors such as Shirley Jackson and Stephen King brought the genre enormous popular success.

Characteristics

Horror fiction frequently features ghosts, vampires, werewolves, demons, monsters and haunted places, as well as human threats such as serial killers. Scholars note that horror often works by confronting readers with the unknown and by evoking both fear and revulsion — the "art-horror" response described by philosopher Noël Carroll.
