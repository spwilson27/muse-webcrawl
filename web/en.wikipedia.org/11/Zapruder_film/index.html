The Zapruder film is a silent 8mm color motion picture sequence shot by Abraham Zapruder with a Bell & Howell home-movie camera, depicting the assassination of United States President John F. Kennedy in Dealey Plaza, Dallas, on November 22, 1963. It is the most complete film recording of the event and has been described as possibly the most studied piece of film in history.

History

Zapruder, a clothing manufacturer, stood on a concrete pedestal in Dealey Plaza and filmed the presidential motorcade as it passed. Within hours, copies were made for the FBI and Secret Service; the original was sold to Life magazine, which published frames from it. The film was subpoenaed for the trial of Clay Shaw in 1969 and first shown publicly on television in 1975. The Warren Commission relied on it extensively in reconstructing the shooting.

Significance

Individual frames, notably frame 313 showing the fatal head wound, have been analysed for decades in debates over the number and direction of shots. In 1999, the Zapruder family received $16 million from the United States government as compensation for the transfer of the film to the National Archives.
