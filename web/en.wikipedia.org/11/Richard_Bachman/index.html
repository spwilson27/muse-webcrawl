Richard Bachman

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Pen name of: Stephen King
Active: 1977–present (intermittently)
Notable works: Rage, The Long Walk, Roadwork, The Running Man, Thinner

Richard Bachman is a pen name used by American author Stephen King for several novels published between 1977 and 1984, and occasionally thereafter. King adopted the pseudonym partly because publishers believed an author should release only one book per year, and partly to test whether his success was due to talent or to luck and marketing.

The Bachman books

The first Bachman novels — Rage (1977), The Long Walk (1979), Roadwork (1981) and The Running Man (1982) — were issued in paperback under the invented biography of Richard Bachman. The 1984 novel Thinner, the first Bachman hardcover, attracted closer scrutiny; in 1985 a bookstore clerk, Steve Brown, publicly demonstrated that Bachman was King by comparing copyright records and writing style. King then announced that Bachman had died of "cancer of the pseudonym".

Later use

King revived the name for The Regulators (1996), published as a companion to his own Desperation, and for Blaze (2007), an early trunk novel. The collected edition The Bachman Books gathers the four early novels; Rage was later allowed to go out of print at King's request.
