Time travel

Time travel is the concept of movement between certain points in time, analogous to movement between different points in space by an object or a person, typically with the use of a hypothetical device known as a time machine. Time travel is a widely recognized concept in philosophy and fiction, particularly science fiction. The idea of a time machine was popularized by H. G. Wells's 1895 novel The Time Machine.

Time travel in physics

It is uncertain if time travel to the past is physically possible, and such travel, if at all feasible, may give rise to questions of causality and paradoxes such as the grandfather paradox. Forward time travel, outside the usual sense of the perception of time, is an extensively observed phenomenon and is well understood within the framework of special relativity and general relativity. Time dilation allows a traveller moving at relativistic speeds, or situated deep in a gravitational well, to experience less elapsed time than distant observers.

In fiction

Stories of people travelling in time appear in early literature, including the Hindu epic Mahabharata and the Japanese tale of Urashima Tarō. Modern science fiction has explored time travel extensively, from Wells's novella to films such as Back to the Future and television series such as Doctor Who. Common devices include machines, natural phenomena such as wormholes, and supernatural means.

Paradoxes

The grandfather paradox proposes that a traveller who goes back in time and kills their own grandfather before their parent's conception would thereby prevent their own birth, creating a contradiction. Proposed resolutions include the Novikov self-consistency principle, which holds that events on a closed timelike curve are constrained to be self-consistent.
