The Warren Commission, officially the President's Commission on the Assassination of President Kennedy, was established by President Lyndon B. Johnson through Executive Order 11130 on November 29, 1963, a week after the assassination of John F. Kennedy in Dallas, Texas. It was chaired by Chief Justice Earl Warren, and its 888-page final report was presented to President Johnson on September 24, 1964.

Conclusions

The commission concluded that Lee Harvey Oswald had acted alone in assassinating President Kennedy and wounding Texas Governor John Connally, and that Jack Ruby had acted alone in killing Oswald two days later. Its findings included the "single-bullet theory", which held that one bullet struck both Kennedy and Connally.

Criticism

The commission's conclusions have remained controversial, and public opinion polls have often shown that a majority of Americans believe there was a conspiracy. Later investigations, including the United States House Select Committee on Assassinations (1979), largely concurred that Oswald fired the shots but concluded there was probably a conspiracy, a finding disputed on methodological grounds. Key evidence reviewed by the commission included the Zapruder film of the assassination.
