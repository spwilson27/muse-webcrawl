Grandfather paradox

The grandfather paradox is a paradox of time travel in which inconsistencies can arise by changing the past. The name comes from the classic thought experiment in which a person travels back in time and kills their own grandfather before the conception of their father or mother, which prevents the time traveller's existence.

Discussion

The paradox was described early in science fiction, including in René Barjavel's 1943 novel Le Voyageur Imprudent. Proposed resolutions include the Novikov self-consistency principle, under which events in a closed timelike curve are always self-consistent, and many-worlds interpretations, in which the traveller alters a past in a different branch of reality.

Related paradoxes

Related problems include the bootstrap paradox (objects or information with no origin) and the predestination paradox, in which attempts to prevent an event cause it.
