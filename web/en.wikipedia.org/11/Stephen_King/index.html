Stephen King
Born: 21 September 1947, Portland, Maine
Occupation: Author
Genre: Horror, supernatural fiction, suspense, fantasy
Notable works: Carrie, The Shining, It, The Stand, Misery

Stephen Edwin King (born 21 September 1947) is an American author dubbed the "King of Horror," widely known for his horror novels and for works in suspense, crime, science fiction and fantasy. His books have sold more than 400 million copies worldwide, and many have been adapted into films, television series and comics.

Career

King was raised in Maine, which provides the setting for much of his fiction, including the fictional towns of Derry and Castle Rock. His first published novel, Carrie (1974), was followed by 'Salem's Lot (1975), The Shining (1977), The Stand (1978) and It (1986). He has also published under the pen name Richard Bachman.

Style and legacy

King's writing combines supernatural elements with detailed small-town realism and character-driven storytelling. He has received Bram Stoker Awards, World Fantasy Awards, the National Book Foundation's Medal for Distinguished Contribution to American Letters (2003) and the National Medal of Arts (2014).
