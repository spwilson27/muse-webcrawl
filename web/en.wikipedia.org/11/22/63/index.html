11/22/63

11/22/63 is a novel by American author Stephen King, published on November 8, 2011. The title refers to the date of the assassination of President John F. Kennedy. The novel follows a high-school English teacher from Maine who discovers a portal to 1958 and embarks on a mission to prevent the assassination, living in the past for five years in the lead-up to November 22, 1963.

Plot

Jake Epping, a divorced teacher in Lisbon Falls, Maine, is shown a "rabbit hole" in the pantry of a local diner by its owner, Al Templeton, who has been using it to buy cheap meat in 1958 and to attempt, failing through illness, to stop Lee Harvey Oswald. Al explains the rules: each trip resets everything, time in the past runs parallel to the present, and the past "pushes back" against change. Jake takes up the mission, living under the name George Amberson in Texas, falling in love with a librarian named Sadie Dunhill, and shadowing Oswald to determine whether he acted alone. He ultimately foils the shooting, but returns to a present drastically altered for the worse — and must decide whether to undo his success.

Publication and reception

King first attempted the concept in 1972, before his first novel was published, but set it aside. The finished novel, of some 850 pages, was widely praised for its research and emotional depth, appearing on numerous best-of-the-year lists. It won the Los Angeles Times Book Prize for Mystery/Thriller and was nominated for other awards.

Adaptation

The novel was adapted as the 2016 Hulu limited series 11.22.63, produced by J. J. Abrams and starring James Franco as Jake Epping.
