Electrical conductance

Electrical conductance
SymbolG
SI unitsiemens (S)
RelationG = 1/R

Electrical conductance is a measure of how easily electric current flows through a material or component. It is the reciprocal of electrical resistance and is measured in siemens (S), a unit formerly called the mho.

Definition

For a component obeying Ohm's law, conductance G is defined as G = I/V = 1/R, where I is the current through the component, V is the voltage across it, and R is its resistance. A conductor with a resistance of one ohm has a conductance of one siemens.

Conductivity

The conductance of a uniform conductor depends on its material and geometry: G = σA/l, where σ is the electrical conductivity of the material, A is the cross-sectional area, and l is the length. Metals such as silver and copper have very high conductivities, while insulators have extremely low values.

Admittance

In alternating-current circuits, the generalisation of conductance is admittance, a complex quantity whose real part is conductance and whose imaginary part is susceptance.
