Rostker v. Goldberg

Rostker v. Goldberg, 453 U.S. 57 (1981), is a United States Supreme Court case holding that the practice of requiring only men to register for the draft with the Selective Service System was constitutional. The Court upheld the Military Selective Service Act, rejecting the claim that male-only registration violated the Due Process Clause of the Fifth Amendment.

Background

In 1980, in response to the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan, President Jimmy Carter reinstated registration for the draft, which had been suspended in 1975, and asked Congress to fund registration of both men and women. Congress funded registration for men only. A class-action suit, originally filed during the Vietnam War era by men challenging the draft, was revived; a three-judge district court in Pennsylvania held that male-only registration was unconstitutional gender discrimination.

Decision

By a 6–3 majority, the Supreme Court reversed. Writing for the majority, Justice William Rehnquist emphasised the deference owed to Congress in matters of national defence and military affairs. Crucially, the Court reasoned that men and women were not similarly situated for purposes of the draft because women were excluded by statute and policy from combat roles; since the purpose of registration was to raise an army of combat troops, Congress could constitutionally register only men. The Court concluded that the gender classification was not invidious discrimination and was substantially related to an important governmental objective.

Dissent

Justices Byron White and Thurgood Marshall dissented, arguing that excluding all women — including those fit for the many noncombat roles in a mobilisation — was not justified and that the burden of the classification was not fairly shared.

Later developments

The decision remained a leading precedent on judicial deference in military affairs and on sex-based classifications. After the Department of Defense opened all combat roles to women in 2015, the reasoning of Rostker was called into question. In 2019 a federal district court in National Coalition for Men v. Selective Service System declared male-only registration unconstitutional, but the Fifth Circuit reversed in 2020 on the ground that only the Supreme Court could overturn Rostker. In June 2021 the Supreme Court declined to hear a challenge, with several justices noting that Congress was actively considering whether to extend registration to women.
