South London and Maudsley NHS Foundation Trust, often abbreviated to SLaM, is an NHS foundation trust providing mental health and substance misuse services in south London, England. It serves the London boroughs of Croydon, Lambeth, Lewisham and Southwark, a combined population of over one million people, and also provides a number of national specialist services.

History

The trust was formed in 1999 from the merger of predecessor mental health services associated with historic hospitals including the Bethlem Royal Hospital — the famous "Bedlam", founded in 1247 — and the Maudsley Hospital in Denmark Hill, opened in the early twentieth century. It became an NHS foundation trust in 2006.

Research and teaching

Together with the Institute of Psychiatry, Psychology and Neuroscience at King's College London, the trust forms part of one of the world's leading centres for mental health research. The partnership hosts the Maudsley Biomedical Research Centre, funded by the National Institute for Health and Care Research, and helped pioneer approaches such as the Maudsley Prescribing Guidelines in psychiatry.
