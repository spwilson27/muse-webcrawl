Winter Palace

The Winter Palace (Russian: Зимний дворец) is a palace in Saint Petersburg, Russia, that served as the official residence of the Russian emperors from 1732 to 1917. The present building, the fourth to stand on the site, was constructed between 1754 and 1762 to designs by Francesco Bartolomeo Rastrelli in the Elizabethan Baroque style. Today it forms the centrepiece of the State Hermitage Museum complex.

History

The first Winter Palace was built for Peter the Great in 1711. Empress Elizabeth commissioned Rastrelli's grander replacement, completed after her death under Catherine the Great, who filled it with art collections that became the Hermitage. A fire in 1837 destroyed the interiors, which were rebuilt within a year. The palace has about 1,500 rooms and was the scene of Bloody Sunday in 1905 and the storming of the Provisional Government during the October Revolution of 1917 — an event whose dramatised version in Sergei Eisenstein's film October became famous.

Architecture and museum

The vast green-and-white facade, adorned with columns, statues and gilded ornament, fronts both Palace Square and the Neva River. Since 1917 the palace has housed the Hermitage, one of the largest and oldest museums in the world, with a collection of over three million items.
