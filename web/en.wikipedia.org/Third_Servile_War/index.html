Third Servile War

The Third Servile War (73–71 BC), also called the War of Spartacus, was the last of the great slave rebellions against the Roman Republic. It began with the escape of some seventy gladiators from a school at Capua led by the Thracian Spartacus.

Course of the war

The rebels, joined by tens of thousands of runaway slaves, repeatedly defeated Roman forces and ranged the length of Italy. In 72 BC the Senate gave Marcus Licinius Crassus an extraordinary command with eight legions; he cornered Spartacus in Bruttium and destroyed his army in 71 BC at the Battle of the Silarius River. Pompey's forces mopped up fugitives in the north.

Aftermath

Some 6,000 captured slaves were crucified along the Appian Way from Capua to Rome. The revolt left no structural change to Roman slavery, but Spartacus became an enduring symbol of resistance to oppression.
