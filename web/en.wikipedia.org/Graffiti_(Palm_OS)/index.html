Graffiti (Palm OS)

The Graffiti input area at the bottom of a Palm device screen

Graffiti is a handwriting recognition system used in the Palm OS platform for personal digital assistants (PDAs). Developed originally by Palm Computing, it allowed users to enter text by drawing simplified single-stroke characters with a stylus in a dedicated writing area on the device's touchscreen.

Design

Rather than attempting to recognize natural handwriting, Graffiti required users to learn a set of unistroke gestures, each corresponding to a letter of the alphabet. Most gestures resembled uppercase printed letters drawn in a single motion; for example, the letter "A" was drawn like an inverted "V" without the crossbar. Numbers and punctuation used additional strokes, and a dot gesture switched to punctuation-shift mode. Because each character was a single unambiguous stroke, recognition was fast and highly accurate once the alphabet was learned.

History

Graffiti was created by Jeff Hawkins and was first released in 1994 for devices such as the Zoomer. It became widely known through the success of the PalmPilot in 1996. A patent dispute with Xerox, whose Unistrokes system was similar, led to litigation; Palm later replaced Graffiti with a licensed system called Graffiti 2, based on Jot from Communication Intelligence Corporation, which permitted more natural two-stroke characters. Many long-time users preferred the original system for its speed and predictability.

Legacy

Graffiti influenced later gesture-based text entry systems and remains an iconic element of early mobile computing. Its approach demonstrated that adapting human input slightly to machine constraints could yield more reliable recognition than attempting full natural handwriting recognition on limited hardware.
