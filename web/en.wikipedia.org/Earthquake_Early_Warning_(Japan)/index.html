Earthquake Early Warning (Japan)

Earthquake Early Warning (緊急地震速報, Kinkyū Jishin Sokuhō) is a warning system operated by the Japan Meteorological Agency (JMA) that alerts the public when an earthquake of significant intensity is expected. The system became generally available to the public on 1 October 2007.

How it works

The system uses a nationwide network of seismometers. When the fast-moving but weak primary waves (P-waves) of an earthquake are detected near the epicentre, computers rapidly estimate the earthquake's location, magnitude, and the expected intensity of the slower, more destructive secondary waves (S-waves). If strong shaking is predicted, a warning is issued seconds to tens of seconds before it arrives.

Distribution

Warnings are broadcast on television and radio, sent to mobile phones, and used to trigger automatic responses such as slowing or stopping trains, halting elevators at the nearest floor, and pausing industrial processes. The warning gives people a brief window to take cover, though for areas very close to the epicentre the alert may arrive after shaking begins — a zone sometimes called the blind zone.

History

Japan's system grew out of earlier urgent earthquake detection systems developed for the Shinkansen bullet trains. Following the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami, the system drew wide attention and has been studied and emulated by earthquake-prone countries around the world.
