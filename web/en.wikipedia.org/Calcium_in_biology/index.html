Calcium in Biology

Calcium ions (Ca2+) contribute to the physiology and biochemistry of organisms' cells. They play an important role in signal transduction pathways, where they act as a second messenger, in neurotransmitter release, muscle contraction, fertilisation, and many other processes.

Distribution

In vertebrates, most calcium resides in bones and teeth as hydroxyapatite. Blood plasma maintains ionised calcium within tight limits (about 2.1–2.6 mmol/L total), regulated by parathyroid hormone, calcitonin, and vitamin D acting on gut, kidney, and bone.

Cellular signalling

Cytosolic free Ca2+ rests near 100 nM, thousands of times lower than extracellular levels. Channels in the plasma membrane and endoplasmic reticulum open to create pulses and waves decoded by proteins such as calmodulin, which activates kinases including CaMK. In muscle, Ca2+ binding to troponin triggers contraction; in neurons it triggers synaptic vesicle fusion.

Plants and other organisms

Plants use Ca2+ signals in responses to stress and hormones, and store calcium oxalate crystals; many invertebrates build shells and tests of calcium carbonate.
