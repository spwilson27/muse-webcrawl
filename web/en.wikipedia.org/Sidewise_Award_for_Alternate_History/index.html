Sidewise Award for Alternate History

The Sidewise Award for Alternate History is a literary award established in 1995 to recognize the best alternate history stories and novels of the year. It is named after Murray Leinster's 1934 short story "Sidewise in Time", one of the earliest works to depict parallel timelines interacting.

Categories

The award is presented in two regular categories:

  • Best Long Form — for works longer than 60,000 words, including novels and complete series.
  • Best Short Form — for works of 60,000 words or fewer, including short stories, novelettes, novellas, and poems.

A Special Achievement Award may also be presented for works published before the award's founding or for an individual's body of work in the genre.

Selected winners

Notable Long Form winners include Paul J. McAuley's Pasquale's Angel, Stephen Baxter's Voyage, Ken MacLeod's The Cassini Division, Harry Turtledove's Ruled Britannia, Lois McMaster Bujold's Paladin of Souls, and Robert Charles Wilson's Julian Comstock. The award is traditionally presented at the World Science Fiction Convention or NASFiC.
