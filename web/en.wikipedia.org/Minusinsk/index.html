Minusinsk

Minusinsk (Russian: Минуси́нск) is a historical town in Krasnoyarsk Krai, Russia, which was the administrative centre of Minusinsky District until its abolition, though it is not a part of it as a municipal division. It lies on the southern bank of the Yenisei River, near its confluence with the Minusa River from which the town takes its name, about 260 kilometres south of Krasnoyarsk. Population: about 71,000 (2021 census).

History

The settlement was founded in 1739 as the village of Minyusinskoye and was granted town status in 1822. In the 19th century Minusinsk grew into a trading centre for southern Siberia and a place of political exile; Vladimir Lenin, while exiled in nearby Shushenskoye (1897–1900), visited the town and its museum. The Minusinsk Basin around the town is archaeologically famous: its steppe preserves the remains of the Afanasievo, Okunev, Andronovo, Karasuk, and Tashtyk cultures, studied from the expeditions organised by the local museum.

Culture and climate

The Minusinsk Regional Museum of Local Lore, founded in 1877 by Nikolai Martyanov, is one of the oldest museums in Siberia, with celebrated archaeological and ethnographic collections. The basin's sheltered, sunny microclimate — unusually warm for Siberia — has made the area known for agriculture; Minusinsk tomatoes are a regional speciality honoured by an annual festival.

Transport

Minusinsk lies on the railway between Abakan and Tayshet and on the R257 Yenisei highway linking Krasnoyarsk with Abakan and Kyzyl. The nearest airport is at Abakan, about 25 km away.
