Solid body

A solid body is a three-dimensional geometric object — a bounded region of three-dimensional space enclosed by a two-dimensional surface. In elementary geometry the term is used for the familiar polyhedra and rounded solids studied since antiquity: cubes, prisms, pyramids, cylinders, cones, and spheres. In physics and mechanics, related notions such as the rigid body describe solids that resist deformation.

In geometry

The Platonic solids — tetrahedron, cube, octahedron, dodecahedron, and icosahedron — are the five regular convex polyhedra. More general classes include the Archimedean solids, Catalan solids, prisms, and antiprisms. The volume and surface area of a solid body are its fundamental measures; classical results such as Euler's formula V − E + F = 2 relate the numbers of vertices, edges, and faces of convex polyhedra.

In physics

In classical mechanics, a solid body is often idealized as a rigid body: a collection of points whose mutual distances remain fixed under motion. Real solids deform under stress, and continuum mechanics and solid mechanics study how they strain, yield, and fracture. The mechanical behavior of solid bodies is governed by their material properties, including elasticity, plasticity, and hardness.
