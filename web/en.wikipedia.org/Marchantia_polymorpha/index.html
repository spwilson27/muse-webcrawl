Marchantia polymorpha

Thalli with gemma cups and archegoniophores
Scientific classification
Kingdom:Plantae
Division:Marchantiophyta
Class:Marchantiopsida
Order:Marchantiales
Family:Marchantiaceae
Genus:Marchantia
Species:M. polymorpha

Marchantia polymorpha, sometimes called the common liverwort or umbrella liverwort, is a large thallose liverwort in the class Marchantiopsida. It is found worldwide in moist, shaded habitats and is a common weed of greenhouses, nurseries, and gardens. Since the early twenty-first century it has become a leading model organism for the study of land plant evolution and development.

Description

The plant body is a flattened, ribbon-like thallus, typically 2–20 cm long, that branches dichotomously and grows prostrate on the substrate, anchored by rhizoids. The upper surface shows a pattern of polygonal air chambers, each with a central pore. Asexual reproduction occurs by gemmae — small discs of tissue produced in cup-shaped structures (gemma cups) on the thallus and dispersed by raindrops. Sexual reproduction involves separate male and female plants: umbrella-shaped antheridiophores on male thalli and rayed, palm-like archegoniophores on females.

Model organism

M. polymorpha is prized in research because the dominant generation is haploid (simplifying genetics), it grows fast, is easily transformed, and has a compact genome that was sequenced in 2017. As a member of one of the earliest-diverging lineages of land plants, it retains ancestral features — for example a reduced set of gene families — making it a window into how plants colonized land some 450–500 million years ago. Subspecies studied include subsp. ruderalis (the usual laboratory strain background, e.g. the Takaragaike accessions).

History and uses

Named by Linnaeus, the liverworts were historically used in herbal medicine under the doctrine of signatures — their liver-shaped thalli suggested treatment of liver ailments — which gave the group its common name. Today M. polymorpha is mostly significant as a greenhouse nuisance and as a scientific model.
