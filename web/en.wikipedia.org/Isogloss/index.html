Isogloss

An isogloss, also called a heterogloss, is the geographic boundary of a certain linguistic feature, such as the pronunciation of a vowel, the meaning of a word, or the use of some morphological or syntactic feature. Major divisions between languages or dialects are marked by bundles of isoglosses, such as the Benrath line that distinguishes High German from other West Germanic languages.

Concept and use

The term is modeled on geographical terms like isotherm and was developed in 19th-century dialectology, for instance in Georg Wenker's dialect atlases of German. Isoglosses are plotted on linguistic maps from survey data; where many isoglosses coincide, they delimit a dialect area. Well-known examples include the La Spezia–Rimini Line dividing Northern and Central Italian dialects, and the Jireček Line in the Balkans, and the bundle of isoglosses separating North and South Slavic features.

Caveats

In reality, isoglosses for different features rarely coincide perfectly, and features diffuse at different rates and through different social networks, producing transition zones and "focal" and "relic" areas. For this reason, modern dialectology treats dialect continua and speaker repertoires, rather than sharp boundaries, as the norm; an isogloss is thus a convenient abstraction rather than a physical edge.
