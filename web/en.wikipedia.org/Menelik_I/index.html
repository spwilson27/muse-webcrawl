Menelik I

Menelik I (Ge'ez: ምኒልክ, Mənilək) was the legendary first emperor of the Ethiopian Empire, according to tradition the son of King Solomon of Israel and Makeda, the Queen of Sheba. His story is told in the 14th-century royal chronicle, the Kebra Nagast (Glory of the Kings).

Legend

According to the Kebra Nagast, Menelik was raised in Ethiopia, travelled as a young man to Jerusalem to meet his father, and returned home accompanied by the firstborn sons of Israel's elders — and, secretly, with the Ark of the Covenant, taken from the Temple. He founded the Solomonic dynasty, which ruled Ethiopia, with interruptions, until 1974, claiming descent from him.

Historical assessment

Menelik I is a legendary rather than a historically documented figure; there is no contemporary evidence for his reign, and modern scholarship treats the narrative as a foundation myth legitimising the dynasty restored in 1270. Nonetheless, the tradition remains central to Ethiopian national and religious identity, and the Ark is held by the Ethiopian Orthodox Church to reside at Aksum.
