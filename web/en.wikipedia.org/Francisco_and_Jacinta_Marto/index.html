Saints Francisco de Jesus Marto (1908–1919) and Jacinta de Jesus Marto (1910–1920) were Portuguese shepherd children who, together with their cousin Lúcia Santos, reported apparitions of the Virgin Mary — venerated as Our Lady of Fátima — at the Cova da Iria in Fátima, Portugal, in 1917. They are the youngest Catholic saints who did not die as martyrs.

The apparitions

The siblings, from the hamlet of Aljustrel, tended their families' sheep with their cousin Lúcia. Between May and October 1917 the three children reported six apparitions of the Virgin Mary, who asked them to pray the rosary daily and to make sacrifices for sinners. The events culminated in the "Miracle of the Sun" on 13 October 1917, witnessed by a large crowd.

Deaths and veneration

Both children fell ill during the influenza pandemic of 1918. Francisco died at home in April 1919, aged ten. Jacinta, after a long and painful illness including treatment in Lisbon, died alone in hospital in February 1920, aged nine; she had offered her sufferings, in accordance with the reported message, for the conversion of sinners. Their cause advanced quickly by historical standards: Pope John Paul II beatified them at Fátima on 13 May 2000, and Pope Francis canonised them at Fátima on 13 May 2017, the centenary of the first apparition. Their tombs are in the Basilica of Our Lady of the Rosary at the Sanctuary of Fátima.
