Beechcraft

Founded: 1932, Wichita, Kansas
Founders: Walter and Olive Ann Beech
Parent: Textron Aviation

Beechcraft is an American brand of civil and military aircraft owned by Textron Aviation since 2014. Founded in 1932 as the Beech Aircraft Corporation by Walter Beech and his wife Olive Ann Beech in Wichita, Kansas, the company built some of the most successful general aviation aircraft in history.

History

The company's first design, the classic Model 17 Staggerwing biplane, set standards for speed and luxury in the 1930s. The twin-engine Model 18 flew for over three decades in production. In 1947 Beech introduced the V-tailed Bonanza, which remains in production in modernised form and is the longest continuously produced aircraft in history. The King Air line of turboprops, launched in 1964, became the best-selling business turboprop family in the world.

Ownership

Beech Aircraft was acquired by Raytheon in 1980 and combined with Hawker in the Raytheon Aircraft Company, later sold as Hawker Beechcraft. After Hawker Beechcraft's 2012 bankruptcy, the restructured Beechcraft Corporation emerged in 2013 and was acquired by Textron in 2014, joining Cessna under Textron Aviation.

Current models

Production models include the Baron and Bonanza piston aircraft, the King Air 260 and 360 turboprops, and military trainers and special-mission aircraft derived from the King Air, such as the T-6 Texan II (built under the Beechcraft Defense line).
