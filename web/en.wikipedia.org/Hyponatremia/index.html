Hyponatremia

Hyponatremia is a low concentration of sodium in the blood. It is generally defined as a sodium concentration of less than 135 mmol/L, with severe hyponatremia being below 120 mmol/L. Symptoms can include nausea, headache, confusion and fatigue; very low levels can cause seizures, coma and death. Hyponatremia is the most common electrolyte disorder encountered in clinical medicine.

Causes

Causes are classified by volume status. Hypovolemic hyponatremia follows losses from vomiting, diarrhoea, diuretics or burns. Euvolemic hyponatremia occurs in the syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion (SIADH), hypothyroidism and adrenal insufficiency. Hypervolemic hyponatremia occurs in heart failure, cirrhosis and kidney failure. Drinking excessive water, as in exercise-associated hyponatremia, can also cause it.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the cause, severity and speed of onset. Severe symptomatic cases are treated with hypertonic saline, but correction must not be too rapid: overly fast correction of chronic hyponatremia can cause osmotic demyelination syndrome. Fluid restriction, stopping offending drugs and treating the underlying disease are mainstays in milder cases.
