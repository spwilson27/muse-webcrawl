Three Gorges

LocationYangtze River, Chongqing and Hubei, China
LengthAbout 200 km (120 mi) in total
GorgesQutang, Wu, Xiling
Known forScenery; Three Gorges Dam

The Three Gorges (Chinese: Sānxiá) are three adjacent gorges along the middle reaches of the Yangtze River in China: from west to east, the Qutang Gorge, the Wu Gorge and the Xiling Gorge. They stretch for roughly 200 kilometres between Baidi Cheng in Chongqing and Nanjing Pass in Hubei, and have been celebrated in Chinese poetry and painting for over a thousand years for their dramatic cliffs, peaks and cultural sites.

The gorges

Qutang Gorge, the shortest at 8 km, is the most spectacular, with sheer cliffs squeezing the river between the Kui Men gate. Wu Gorge (45 km) is known for its forested, mist-shrouded peaks, the "Twelve Peaks" including the Goddess Peak. Xiling Gorge (66 km), the longest, was historically notorious for its shoals and rapids and for the ship trackers who hauled vessels upstream.

Three Gorges Dam

The completion of the Three Gorges Dam in 2006 — the world's largest power station by installed capacity — raised the water level in the gorges by up to 175 metres, submerging towns and archaeological sites and displacing about 1.3 million people, while easing navigation and providing flood control and hydroelectric power. The Lesser Three Gorges on the Daning River and the Shennong Stream remain popular excursions.
