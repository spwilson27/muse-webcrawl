James Augustine Aloysius Joyce (2 February 1882 – 13 January 1941) was an Irish novelist, poet and literary critic, widely regarded as one of the most influential writers of the twentieth century. His novel Ulysses (1922) is a landmark of modernist literature, celebrated for its stream-of-consciousness technique and its detailed rendering of a single Dublin day, 16 June 1904 — now commemorated worldwide as Bloomsday.

Early life

Joyce was born in Rathgar, Dublin, into a once-prosperous family whose fortunes declined through his childhood as his father, John Stanislaus Joyce, fell into alcoholism and debt. Educated by Jesuits at Clongowes Wood College and Belvedere College, and then at University College Dublin, Joyce excelled at languages and philosophy. In 1904 he left Ireland with Nora Barnacle, the chambermaid who became his lifelong partner and later his wife, settling first in Trieste, then Rome, and again Trieste, where he taught English and wrote while struggling financially.

Dubliners and A Portrait

His short-story collection Dubliners, portraying the paralysis of middle-class Dublin life, was delayed for years by publishers' fears of libel and obscenity before appearing in 1914. A Portrait of the Artist as a Young Man (1916), a semi-autobiographical novel following Stephen Dedalus, developed Joyce's free indirect style. His play Exiles appeared in 1918, and the poetry collections Chamber Music (1907) and Pomes Penyeach (1927) also date from this period.

Ulysses and Finnegans Wake

Ulysses, structured in parallel with Homer's Odyssey and following Leopold Bloom, Stephen Dedalus and Molly Bloom through one Dublin day, was serialised in part before being published in full in Paris by Sylvia Beach's Shakespeare and Company on Joyce's fortieth birthday, 2 February 1922. Banned for obscenity in the United States and Britain, it was legalised in the US by a landmark 1933 court decision. Joyce spent his last seventeen years on Finnegans Wake (1939), a vast experimental work written in an idiosyncratic dream-language. Plagued by eye disease and operations, and by his daughter Lucia's mental illness, Joyce died in Zurich in January 1941 after surgery on a perforated ulcer; he is buried in Fluntern Cemetery there.
