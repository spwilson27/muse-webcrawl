Consumer electronics

Smartphones, televisions and computers are the core of the modern consumer electronics market.

Consumer electronics are electronic devices intended for everyday personal use, typically for entertainment, communication, and home or office tasks. The category includes televisions, radios, audio equipment, computers, smartphones, tablets, games consoles, cameras, and wearable devices, as well as "white goods" controls and smart home products.

History

The industry began with radio receivers in the 1920s and expanded through the century with television, hi-fi audio, the transistor radio, the videocassette recorder, the personal computer, and the mobile phone. The shift from analogue to digital technology, the spread of the internet, and the arrival of the smartphone after 2007 transformed the field, merging previously separate product categories into connected computing devices. The annual Consumer Electronics Show (CES) in Las Vegas has been the industry's principal trade fair since 1967.

Industry and trends

Consumer electronics is a global industry worth hundreds of billions of dollars annually, dominated by firms in the United States, South Korea, China, and Japan. Design cycles are short and product lives brief, leading to concerns about electronic waste and planned obsolescence, and to regulatory responses such as right-to-repair rules and common-charger requirements. Recent trends include the integration of artificial intelligence, foldable displays, augmented and virtual reality headsets, and the connection of household devices in the Internet of things.
