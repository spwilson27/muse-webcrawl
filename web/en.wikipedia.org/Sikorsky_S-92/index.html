Sikorsky S-92

Sikorsky S-92 / H-92 Superhawk
National origin: United States
Manufacturer: Sikorsky Aircraft
First flight: 23 December 1998
Introduction: 2004
Status: In service
Primary users: Offshore oil transport, search and rescue, government/VIP operators

The Sikorsky S-92 is an American twin-engine medium-lift helicopter built by Sikorsky Aircraft (now part of Lockheed Martin) for the civil and military helicopter markets. A development of the S-70/H-60 family with a larger cabin, it first flew on 23 December 1998 and entered service in 2004.

Design

The S-92 seats up to 19 passengers in its standard offshore configuration and is powered by two General Electric CT7-8A turboshaft engines driving a four-blade articulated main rotor with composite blades. The military variant is marketed as the H-92 Superhawk. The type features a rear loading ramp, an active vibration control system (on H-92 and later models), and certification to modern FAR/JAR Part 29 safety standards.

Operational use

The S-92 became a workhorse of North Sea and worldwide offshore oil-and-gas crew transport, flying for operators such as Bristow and CHC. It is also widely used for search and rescue — including by the Irish Coast Guard and, under the UK Maritime and Coastguard Agency contract, in the United Kingdom — and as a head-of-state and VIP transport in several countries. In 2014 the S-92 won the competition to replace the U.S. Marine Corps presidential helicopter fleet, leading to the VH-92A Patriot, a derivative closely related to the Sikorsky VH-3D Sea King's replacement programme.

Incidents

The type's safety record includes the 2009 crash of Cougar Helicopters Flight 91 off Newfoundland, which killed 17 of the 18 people aboard and led to design and maintenance changes concerning the main gearbox oil system.
