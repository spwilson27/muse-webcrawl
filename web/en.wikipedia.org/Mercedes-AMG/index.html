Mercedes-AMG GmbH, commonly known as AMG, is the high-performance subsidiary of the Mercedes-Benz Group, headquartered in Affalterbach, Germany. AMG specializes in performance versions of Mercedes-Benz vehicles, distinguished by hand-built engines — assembled under the "one man, one engine" philosophy and signed by their builder — more aggressive styling, sport-tuned suspension and brakes, and extensive use of lightweight materials.

History

AMG was founded in 1967 by former Mercedes-Benz engineers Hans Werner Aufrecht and Erhard Melcher — the initials stand for Aufrecht, Melcher, and Aufrecht's birthplace, Großaspach. It began as an independent racing engine shop in Burgstall an der Murr. Its breakthrough came at the 1971 Spa 24 Hours, where the mighty AMG-modified Mercedes-Benz 300 SEL 6.8 ("Red Pig") won its class and finished second overall. Through the 1980s AMG became famous for tuned road cars such as the "Hammer" coupé. Daimler-Benz took a controlling stake in 1999 and full ownership in 2005, integrating AMG as Mercedes-AMG GmbH.

Models

The range spans compact models (A 35, A 45 S, with one of the most powerful production four-cylinder engines ever made), the C 63 and E 63 sedans and estates, the G 63 off-roader, and dedicated sports cars including the AMG GT series and the limited-production AMG ONE hypercar, which adapts a Formula One-derived 1.6-litre hybrid V6. Since the late 2010s, AMG has developed electrified models under the E PERFORMANCE label, combining V8 or four-cylinder engines with electric motors. AMG also supplies engines to outside manufacturers, historically including Pagani.

Motorsport

AMG has a long record in touring car racing, especially the DTM, and provides the Formula One safety and medical cars. Its sister operation, Mercedes-AMG High Performance Powertrains in Brixworth, England, builds the power units used by the Mercedes-AMG Petronas Formula One Team, which won eight consecutive Constructors' Championships from 2014 to 2021.
