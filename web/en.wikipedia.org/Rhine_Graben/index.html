Rhine Graben


The Upper Rhine Graben, a rift valley in western Europe

The Rhine Graben is a major geological feature of western Europe: a long, narrow rift valley (graben) formed by the extension and subsidence of the Earth's crust along the course of the Rhine River. Its best-known and most studied part is the Upper Rhine Graben, which extends roughly 300 kilometres from Basel in Switzerland northward to Frankfurt am Main in Germany, separating the Vosges Mountains of France to the west from the Black Forest of Germany to the east.

Formation

The graben began to form in the Eocene and Oligocene epochs, beginning about 45–35 million years ago, as part of the European Cenozoic Rift System, a network of rifts that extends from the North Sea to the Mediterranean. Horizontal extension pulled the crust apart along two major border faults, and the block between them subsided by several kilometres. The resulting depression was filled with up to 3–4 kilometres of sediment eroded from the uplifted flanking highlands, recording a detailed history of Cenozoic climate and environment.

Geology and resources

The subsiding basin accumulated thick sequences of sandstones, limestones, evaporites and organic-rich shales. These strata host modest oil and gas accumulations, potash deposits (notably around Mulhouse), and extensive groundwater aquifers. Deep fractures in the graben also circulate hot water, making the region one of Europe's most important areas for geothermal energy; experimental and commercial plants operate at sites such as Soultz-sous-Forêts and Landau.

Seismicity

The graben remains tectonically active. Though earthquakes are mostly small, the region has experienced damaging events, most famously the 1356 Basel earthquake, one of the strongest known historical earthquakes in central Europe, which destroyed much of the city of Basel.

Geography

The floor of the Upper Rhine Graben forms the broad, fertile Upper Rhine Plain, a corridor of intensive agriculture (including the Alsace wine region) and dense settlement, with cities such as Strasbourg, Karlsruhe, Freiburg and Mannheim. The graben continues northward into the Lower Rhine Embayment around Cologne, another seismically active rift segment.
