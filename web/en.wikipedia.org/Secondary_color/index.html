Secondary color

A secondary color is a color made by mixing two primary colors in a given color space. The secondary colors depend on the color model used.

Additive (RGB) and subtractive (CMY/RYB) secondary colors.

Additive mixing (RGB)

In the additive RGB model used for light and electronic displays, the primary colors are red, green, and blue. Mixing pairs of them gives the secondary colors: red + green = yellow, green + blue = cyan, and blue + red = magenta. These secondaries of RGB are the primaries of the subtractive CMY model.

Subtractive mixing (CMY and RYB)

In subtractive mixing of pigments and inks, the CMY primaries (cyan, magenta, yellow) produce as secondaries blue (cyan + magenta), red (magenta + yellow), and green (yellow + cyan)—the primaries of RGB. In the traditional RYB model used in art education, the primaries red, yellow, and blue give the secondaries orange (red + yellow), green (yellow + blue), and violet (blue + red). Equal mixtures of a primary and an adjacent secondary produce intermediate, or tertiary colors, such as red-orange or blue-green.
