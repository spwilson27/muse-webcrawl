Electrical discharge

Electrical discharge
TypeElectrical phenomenon
MediumGas, vacuum, liquid
ExamplesSpark, arc, corona, lightning

An electrical discharge is the release and transmission of electricity through a normally insulating medium, such as air, by the ionisation of that medium under an applied electric field. Discharges range from faint corona glows to violent lightning strikes.

Types

Common forms include the spark discharge, a transient bright channel formed when the field exceeds the dielectric strength of the medium; the electric arc, a sustained high-current discharge; corona discharge, a partial discharge around a sharp conductor; and glow discharge, used in neon signs and discharge lamps.

Mechanism

When the electric field in a gas becomes strong enough, free electrons accelerate and collide with gas molecules, ionising them in an avalanche process described by Townsend theory. The resulting plasma conducts current and emits light and heat.

Applications and hazards

Controlled discharges are used in fluorescent lamps, plasma displays, electrostatic precipitators, and spark-ignition engines. Uncontrolled discharges, such as electrostatic discharge, can damage electronic components, ignite flammable atmospheres, or injure people.
