Don Walsh

Don Walsh
Born: 2 November 1931, Berkeley, California, U.S.
Died: 12 November 2023, Oregon, U.S.
Occupation: Oceanographer, U.S. Navy officer
Known for: First descent to Challenger Deep (1960)

Don Walsh (1931–2023) was an American oceanographer and United States Navy officer. On 23 January 1960, Walsh and Swiss engineer Jacques Piccard were the first people to descend to the bottom of the Mariana Trench aboard the bathyscaphe Trieste, reaching Challenger Deep, the deepest known point in the world's oceans, at a depth of nearly 11,000 metres.

Career

Walsh spent more than three decades in ocean-related work, piloting submersibles, serving on polar expeditions, and advising on ocean policy. He made dives to famous wrecks including the Titanic and the Bismarck. In 2019 he supported his son Kelly Walsh's dive with Victor Vescovo to Challenger Deep.

Honors

Walsh received numerous awards, including the Hubbard Medal from the National Geographic Society, and a cape in Antarctica (Walsh Bluff) and other features were named in his honor. He died in Oregon in November 2023, at age 92.
