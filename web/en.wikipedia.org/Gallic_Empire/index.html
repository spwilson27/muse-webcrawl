Gallic Empire

The Gallic Empire (Latin: Imperium Galliarum) is the modern name for a breakaway part of the Roman Empire that functioned independently from 260 to 274 AD during the Crisis of the Third Century. It originated with the usurpation of Postumus in 260 following the capture of the emperor Valerian by the Persians, and it controlled Gaul, Britannia and (for a time) Hispania, with capitals at Cologne and later Trier.

History

Postumus, governor of Germania Inferior, declared himself emperor after defeating the Franks and quarrelling with the emperor Gallienus's son Saloninus, whom he had killed. His regime defended the Rhine frontier effectively and was recognized by the provinces of Gaul, Spain and Britain. After Postumus was murdered by his own troops in 269, a rapid succession of emperors followed — Marius, Victorinus and finally Tetricus I. In 274, after the Battle of Châlons, Tetricus surrendered to the emperor Aurelian, who reintegrated the Gallic provinces into the Roman Empire.

Administration

The Gallic Empire replicated Roman imperial institutions, with its own senate, consuls, Praetorian Guard and coinage. Its emperors never marched on Rome; their aim appears to have been regional security rather than control of the whole empire.
