Jerez de la Frontera


Jerez de la Frontera is a city in Andalusia, Spain, famous for sherry wine, flamenco, and horse breeding.

Jerez de la Frontera (Spanish: [xeˈɾeθ ðe la fɾonˈteɾa]) is a city and municipality in the province of Cádiz, in the autonomous community of Andalusia, Spain. Located midway between the Atlantic Ocean and the Cádiz Mountains, it is one of the most populated cities in Andalusia, with a population of around 210,000. The name "Frontera" refers to its position on the frontier between Christian and Moorish Spain during the Reconquista.

Sherry

Jerez is the home of sherry (in Spanish, jerez), a fortified wine made from white grapes grown in the region's distinctive chalky albariza soil. Together with nearby Sanlúcar de Barrameda and El Puerto de Santa María, it forms the "Sherry Triangle," the only area permitted to produce wine under the Jerez-Xérès-Sherry denominación de origen. The great sherry bodegas — such as González Byass (Tio Pepe), Domecq, and Sandeman — are major landmarks and visitor attractions.

Culture

Jerez is regarded as one of the birthplaces of flamenco and is home to a celebrated annual Festival de Jerez and the Centro Andaluz de Flamenco. The city is also renowned for the Royal Andalusian School of Equestrian Art and for breeding the Carthusian line of Andalusian horses. The Circuito de Jerez race track nearby hosts the Spanish motorcycle Grand Prix.

History

The area has been inhabited since ancient times and was known to Phoenicians and Romans. Under Moorish rule the city was called Sherish, from which the English word "sherry" derives. It was reconquered by Alfonso X of Castile in 1264. In 1893 the city's famous clock tower was built; in the 20th century Jerez became a centre of the wine trade and, from 1987, of motorcycle racing.
