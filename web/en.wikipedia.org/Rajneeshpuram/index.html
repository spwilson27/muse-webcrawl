Rajneeshpuram was an intentional community in Wasco County, Oregon, incorporated as a city between 1981 and 1988. Its population consisted entirely of Rajneeshees, followers of the spiritual teacher Rajneesh (later known as Osho). The community was built on the Big Muddy Ranch near the small town of Antelope, which Rajneesh's followers briefly renamed Rajneesh.

History

The ranch, some 64,000 acres (260 km²) of overgrazed high desert, was purchased in 1981 for $5.75 million. Thousands of followers arrived from the movement's ashram in Pune, India, and rapidly built housing, a meditation hall, a shopping centre, a water and sewage system, a dam and reservoir, and an airstrip. At its peak, Rajneeshpuram housed nearly 7,000 residents and hosted annual festivals drawing up to 15,000 visitors.

Conflicts and collapse

Relations with neighbouring residents and Oregon officials deteriorated over land-use law, building permits and the incorporation of the city. In 1984, followers of Rajneesh — principally his secretary Ma Anand Sheela and her circle — contaminated salad bars in The Dalles with salmonella, sickening 751 people, in an attempt to suppress voter turnout in a county election. The incident remains the largest bioterror attack in United States history.

Rajneesh was arrested in October 1985 and deported after pleading guilty to immigration violations. The commune collapsed within weeks and the property was eventually sold. The site later became a Christian youth camp.
