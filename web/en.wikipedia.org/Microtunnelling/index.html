Microtunnelling

Microtunnelling or microtunneling is a trenchless construction method used to install underground pipelines without excavating a continuous open trench. A remotely operated, steerable microtunnel boring machine (MTBM) is jacked through the ground from a launch shaft toward a reception shaft, and pipe sections are pushed in behind it, so the finished pipeline is installed in a single pass.

The operator controls the machine from the surface, typically using a laser guidance system to maintain line and grade, which makes the method well suited to accurate gravity sewers as well as water mains, gas pipelines, and cable ducts. Excavated material is removed as a slurry pumped back to a separation plant at the surface, or by auger or conveyor in some variants.

Applications

Microtunnelling is chosen where open excavation would be disruptive or impractical: beneath roads, railways, rivers, runways, and built-up urban areas, and in ground conditions ranging from soft clays to mixed-face conditions and rock. Typical diameters range from about 0.5 to 4 metres, and drives of several hundred metres are achievable, with curved drives possible in modern systems.
