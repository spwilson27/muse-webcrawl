15 February 2003 anti-war protests

15 February 2003 protests
Date: 15 February 2003
Location: 600+ cities worldwide
Participants: 6–10 million (estimates up to 30 million across the protest wave)
Cause: Opposition to the impending invasion of Iraq

On 15 February 2003, a coordinated day of protests against the impending invasion of Iraq took place in more than 600 cities worldwide. It is often described as the largest protest event in human history, with between six and ten million participants on that day alone.

Scale

Rome saw around three million marchers, recognised by Guinness World Records as the largest anti-war rally ever. London drew at least 750,000 to one million, Madrid around 660,000, and large demonstrations occurred in New York, Berlin, Paris, Sydney and hundreds of other cities.

Impact

The protests were organised by a global coalition of peace groups following a call at the European Social Forum. Despite their scale, they did not prevent the US-led invasion of Iraq, which began on 20 March 2003. The New York Times remarked that public opinion had become a "second superpower"; the failure of the marches to stop the war prompted lasting debate about the efficacy of mass protest.
