Bal Gangadhar Tilak (born Keshav Gangadhar Tilak; 23 July 1856 – 1 August 1920), endeared as Lokmanya ("accepted by the people as their leader"), was an Indian nationalist, teacher, and independence activist. He was one third of the Lal Bal Pal triumvirate, and the first leader of the Indian independence movement to command a mass following. The British colonial authorities called him "The father of the Indian unrest," while Mahatma Gandhi later called him "The Maker of Modern India."

Early life and education

Tilak was born into a Marathi Chitpavan Brahmin family in Ratnagiri, Maharashtra. He graduated from Deccan College, Pune, and co-founded the Deccan Education Society (1884) and Fergusson College (1885) with Gopal Ganesh Agarkar and Vishnushastri Chiplunkar, believing education was the foundation of national regeneration. He also co-founded two newspapers, the English-language Mahratta and the Marathi-language Kesari, which became the voice of his politics.

Political career

A leading figure of the Indian National Congress's assertive ("extremist") wing, Tilak popularized the cry "Swaraj is my birthright, and I shall have it!" He mobilized public opinion through the Ganapati and Shivaji festivals, which he reshaped into vehicles of nationalist solidarity in the 1890s. He campaigned against the partition of Bengal (1905) through swadeshi and boycott. Convicted of sedition in 1908 — for articles defending Bengali revolutionaries — he was transported to Mandalay prison in Burma for six years, where he wrote the Gita Rahasya, a renowned commentary on the Bhagavad Gita. On release he rejoined Congress, helped reconcile its factions at Lucknow in 1916, and launched the Home Rule League.

Scholarship and death

Tilak was also a scholar of Sanskrit, astronomy, and mathematics; his books The Orion (1893) and The Arctic Home in the Vedas (1903) attempted to date Vedic texts through astronomical references. He died in Bombay on 1 August 1920, shortly before Gandhi's non-cooperation movement began; his funeral drew one of the largest crowds the city had seen.
