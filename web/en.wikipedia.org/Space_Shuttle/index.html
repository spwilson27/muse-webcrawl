Space Shuttle

The Space Shuttle was a partially reusable low Earth orbital spacecraft system operated by the United States NASA from 1981 to 2011. The system comprised an orbiter — a winged spaceplane carrying crew and cargo — launched vertically with the aid of two reusable solid rocket boosters and an expendable external fuel tank. Five orbiters were built: Columbia, Challenger, Discovery, Atlantis and Endeavour.

History

Development began in the late 1960s as NASA sought a cheaper, reusable successor to expendable rockets. The first orbital flight, STS-1, was flown by Columbia on 12 April 1981. Over 30 years the fleet flew 135 missions, deploying satellites such as the Hubble Space Telescope, launching interplanetary probes, conducting Spacelab and Spacehab science missions, and carrying out most of the assembly of the International Space Station.

Accidents

Two orbiters were lost with their crews. Challenger broke apart 73 seconds after launch on 28 January 1986 when hot gas burned through a joint in a solid rocket booster, killing all seven astronauts. Columbia disintegrated during re-entry on 1 February 2003 after insulating foam damaged its wing's thermal protection during launch, also killing seven. Each accident grounded the fleet for nearly three years and led to extensive redesigns and reviews of NASA's safety culture.

Retirement

The final mission, STS-135 by Atlantis, ended on 21 July 2011. The surviving orbiters were retired to museums: Discovery to the Smithsonian in Virginia, Atlantis at Kennedy Space Center, Endeavour in Los Angeles, and the test vehicle Enterprise in New York. The programme's total cost is estimated at around $200 billion over its lifetime.
