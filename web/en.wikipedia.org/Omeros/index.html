FormEpic poem, 64 chapters in seven books

Omeros is an epic poem by the Saint Lucian poet Derek Walcott, published in 1990. Running to some 325 pages, it is Walcott's most celebrated work and a central achievement of postcolonial literature; it was a major factor in his receiving the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1992.

Structure and story

The poem loosely reimagines Homer's Iliad and Odyssey in a Caribbean setting: the fishermen Achille and Hector compete for the love of the housemaid Helen, while the retired British officer Major Plunkett and his wife Maud, the narrator-poet himself, and historical threads — including the Middle Passage, the extermination of the Arawaks, and journeys to Europe and North America — interweave around them. The title is the Greek form of Homer's name, and the poet-narrator converses with Omeros as a guiding spirit.

Themes and reception

Omeros explores how the Caribbean, scarred by slavery and colonialism, might create its own epic without merely imitating Europe's: Walcott famously holds that "the sea is History." Written largely in a flexible terza-rima-like tercet, the poem was hailed by critics as a masterpiece that placed Caribbean experience at the centre of the epic tradition.
