Chain transfer

Chain transfer
FieldPolymer chemistry
EffectLimits molecular weight of polymer
AgentsMercaptans, solvents, monomer, polymer

Chain transfer is a polymerization reaction by which the activity of a growing polymer chain is transferred to another molecule. During chain transfer, the growing chain is terminated but a new radical (or other active species) is generated that can initiate a fresh chain, so the overall rate of polymerization may be little affected while the average molecular weight of the product is reduced.

Types

Transfer can occur to solvent, to monomer, to initiator, to polymer, or to an added chain-transfer agent. Mercaptans (thiols) are especially effective agents and are used deliberately as molecular-weight regulators in the manufacture of synthetic rubber and plastics. Transfer to polymer produces long-chain branching.

Chain-transfer constant

The effectiveness of a transfer agent is expressed by the chain-transfer constant, the ratio of the rate constant for transfer to that for propagation. The Mayo equation relates the number-average degree of polymerization to the concentration of transfer agent present.

Applications

Controlled chain transfer underlies atom-transfer radical polymerization and RAFT polymerization, in which chain-growth processes are managed to give polymers of predetermined molecular weight and narrow dispersity.
