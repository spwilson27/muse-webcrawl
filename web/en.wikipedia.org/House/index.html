House

A house is a single-unit residential building. It may range in complexity from a rudimentary hut to a complex structure of wood, masonry, concrete or other material, outfitted with plumbing, electrical, and heating, ventilation, and air conditioning systems. Houses use a range of different roofing systems to keep precipitation such as rain from getting into the dwelling space. Houses may have doors or locks to secure the dwelling space and protect its inhabitants and contents from burglars or other trespassers.

Most conventional modern houses in Western cultures will contain one or more bedrooms and bathrooms, a kitchen or cooking area, and a living room. A house may have a separate dining room, or the eating area may be integrated into another room. Some houses in North America have a recreation room. In traditional agriculture-oriented societies, domestic animals such as chickens or larger livestock (like cattle) may share part of the house with humans.

History

The history of the house is closely bound up with the development of settled agriculture and the rise of towns. Early dwellings were simple shelters made of available materials; over time, houses came to reflect local climate, materials, technology, and cultural traditions. From the pit houses and longhouses of early farming cultures to the courtyard houses of the ancient world, domestic architecture evolved alongside social organization.

Types

Houses vary enormously in form and include detached houses, semi-detached houses, terraced houses (rowhouses), cottages, bungalows, mansions, and apartment-like dwellings contained within larger buildings. Construction methods range from timber framing and brick masonry to modern prefabricated and modular construction.

Legal status

The legal status of a house differs by jurisdiction; in many systems houses are treated as real property and confer rights related to ownership, tenancy, and habitation. Issues such as zoning, building codes, and property taxation all shape how houses are built and used.
