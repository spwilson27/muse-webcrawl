Khanate of Khiva

The Khanate of Khiva (Uzbek: Xiva Xonligi) was a Central Asian polity that existed in the historical region of Khwarazm, in present-day western Uzbekistan and neighbouring areas, from 1511 to 1920. Its capital was the oasis city of Khiva, famous for its slave market, caravanserais, and monumental Islamic architecture.

History

The khanate was founded by Ilbars I, a Shaybanid Uzbek prince, in 1511 after the region was taken from Safavid Persia. The state was ruled successively by the Arabshahid and Qongrat dynasties. Khiva became notorious for raids by Turkoman horsemen who captured Persian and Russian subjects and sold them in the Khivan slave market, a practice that drew repeated punitive expeditions from Russia.

In 1873, during the Russian conquest of Central Asia, General Konstantin von Kaufmann's forces captured Khiva. By the Treaty of Gandamak (1873) the khanate became a Russian protectorate, ceding territory on the right bank of the Amu Darya while retaining formal internal autonomy under its khans.

End of the khanate

After the Russian Revolution, the last khan, Sayid Abdullah, was overthrown in 1920, and the khanate was replaced by the Khorezm People's Soviet Republic, which was later absorbed into the Soviet republics of Uzbekistan and Turkmenistan in 1924. The walled inner city of Khiva, Itchan Kala, is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
