Sukarno (1901–1970) was an Indonesian statesman and nationalist who was the first president of Indonesia, serving from 1945 to 1967, and the leader of the country's struggle for independence from the Netherlands.

Independence

An engineer and charismatic orator, Sukarno founded the Indonesian National Party in 1927 and was repeatedly exiled by the Dutch. Under Japanese occupation he cooperated to advance independence, formulated the Pancasila state philosophy in 1945, and with Mohammad Hatta proclaimed Indonesian independence on 17 August 1945, leading the republic through the four-year revolutionary war until Dutch recognition in 1949.

Guided Democracy

After a period of parliamentary instability, Sukarno imposed 'Guided Democracy' in 1959, balancing the army, Islamic groups, and the large Communist Party (PKI) under his Nasakom formula. Internationally he co-founded the Non-Aligned Movement at the 1955 Bandung Conference, hosted in the tradition of Indonesian maritime states such as Srivijaya, and pursued Konfrontasi against Malaysia (1963–66) while drawing closer to China and the USSR.

Downfall

The failed 30 September Movement in 1965 and the mass killings that followed destroyed his power base; General Suharto forced the Supersemar transfer of authority in 1966, and Sukarno was stripped of the presidency in 1967, dying under house arrest in 1970.
