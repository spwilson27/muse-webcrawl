Radio repeater

A hilltop repeater site
TypeRadio relay station
FunctionReceives and retransmits radio signals to extend range
Common usersAmateur radio, public safety, commercial land mobile

A radio repeater is a combination of a radio receiver and a radio transmitter that receives a signal and retransmits it, typically on a different frequency, so that two-way radio signals can cover a much larger area than either station could reach directly. Repeaters are usually installed at high sites — hilltops, towers or tall buildings — where their elevated antennas give wide coverage.

Operation

In a typical FM repeater, users transmit on the repeater's input frequency and listen on its output frequency; the fixed difference between the two is called the offset. A duplexer allows the receiver and transmitter to share one antenna simultaneously. Access may be controlled by a sub-audible tone (CTCSS) or digital code squelch to avoid interference.

Uses

Repeaters are fundamental to amateur radio (especially on the 2-metre and 70-centimetre bands), police, fire and ambulance services, taxi and utility fleets, and trunked radio systems. Linked repeater networks, and internet-linked systems such as IRLP and DMR networks, can connect repeaters across continents. Satellites carrying transponders act as repeaters in space.
