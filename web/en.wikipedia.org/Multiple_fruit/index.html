Multiple fruit

A multiple fruit is a fruiting body formed from a cluster of flowers, called an inflorescence. Each flower in the inflorescence produces a fruit, but these mature into a single mass. After flowering, the mass is joined together. Also called collective fruits, examples include the pineapple, fig, mulberry, Osage orange, and breadfruit.

Development

In the pineapple, the individual berries of dozens of flowers fuse with one another and with the axis of the inflorescence as they ripen, forming a single compound structure crowned by a tuft of leaves. In the fig, the "fruit" (a syconium) is an inverted inflorescence whose flowers line the inside of a hollow receptacle. In the mulberry, the fruits of a whole catkin coalesce into a single elongated body resembling a blackberry.

Distinctions

Multiple fruits differ from aggregate fruits (such as the raspberry), which form from a single flower with many pistils, and from simple fruits, which form from a single ovary. In languages other than English, the distinction between multiple and aggregate fruits is sometimes not maintained.
