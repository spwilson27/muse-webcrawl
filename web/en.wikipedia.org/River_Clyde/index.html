River Clyde

The River Clyde (Scottish Gaelic: Abhainn Chluaidh) is a major river in Scotland. It is the second-longest river in Scotland at about 170 kilometres (106 mi) and flows through the city of Glasgow before reaching the Firth of Clyde on the west coast.

Course

The Clyde rises in the Lowther Hills of South Lanarkshire and flows north-west through Lanark, Hamilton, and Glasgow. At Glasgow it becomes tidal and widens into the Firth of Clyde, passing Greenock and Dumbarton before meeting the Irish Sea.

History

During the Industrial Revolution the Clyde became one of the world's great shipbuilding rivers: "Clyde-built" ships were renowned internationally, and at its peak the river's yards employed tens of thousands. Dredging in the 19th century made Glasgow a major port despite its inland position. Shipbuilding declined sharply in the later 20th century, and the waterfront has since been extensively regenerated.
