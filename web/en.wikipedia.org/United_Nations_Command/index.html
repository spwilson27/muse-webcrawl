United Nations Command

The United Nations Command (UNC) is the unified command structure for the multinational military forces supporting South Korea (the Republic of Korea) during and after the Korean War. It was established on 7 July 1950 following the United Nations Security Council's recognition of North Korean aggression against South Korea. The UNC is headquartered in South Korea.

History

The command was created at the outset of the Korean War after North Korea invaded the South on 25 June 1950. The United States was designated the executive agent, and General of the Army Douglas MacArthur was appointed the first commander. Forces from some twenty nations served under the UNC during the war, which ended in an armistice rather than a peace treaty in 1953.

Role today

Because no peace treaty was signed, the UNC remains in place to maintain and enforce the Korean Armistice Agreement, manage the Military Demarcation Line area through the Military Armistice Commission, and provide a framework for multinational contributions in the event of renewed hostilities. Its headquarters moved from Seoul to Camp Humphreys in Pyeongtaek in 2018.

Member nations

A number of sending states continue to maintain liaison elements with the UNC, including Australia, Canada, France, New Zealand, the Philippines, Thailand, Türkiye, the United Kingdom and others.
