The 2006 Canadian federal election was held on 23 January 2006 to elect members to the House of Commons of Canada of the 39th Parliament of Canada. The Conservative Party of Stephen Harper won a minority government, ending 13 years of Liberal Party rule under Paul Martin.

Background

The election was triggered after the minority Liberal government lost a motion of no confidence on 28 November 2005, following the Gomery Commission's findings on the sponsorship scandal. During the mid-winter campaign, an RCMP announcement of a criminal investigation into income trust tax policy at the Finance Department damaged the Liberal campaign, and the Conservatives campaigned on accountability, a GST cut and a child-care allowance.

Results

The Conservatives won 124 seats with 36.3 percent of the popular vote; the Liberals fell to 103 seats; the Bloc Québécois won 51; and the New Democratic Party under Jack Layton won 29. Harper was sworn in as Canada's 22nd prime minister on 6 February 2006, and his minority government lasted until the 2008 election.
