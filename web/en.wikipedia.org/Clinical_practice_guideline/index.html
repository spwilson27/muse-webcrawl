Clinical practice guideline

A clinical practice guideline (CPG) is a document with the aim of guiding decisions and criteria regarding diagnosis, management and treatment in specific areas of healthcare. Such documents summarise the best available evidence and are produced by medical associations, government bodies and professional organisations to help clinicians and patients make appropriate decisions, reduce unwarranted variation in practice and improve the quality of care.

Development

Modern guidelines are built through systematic reviews of the medical literature followed by appraisal of evidence quality and the formulation of graded recommendations. The GRADE approach — Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation — is widely used to rate certainty of evidence and strength of recommendations. Guidelines are produced by bodies such as the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) in the United Kingdom, the World Health Organization, and specialty societies in the United States and elsewhere; the Institute of Medicine (now the National Academy of Medicine) set standards for trustworthy guidelines emphasising transparency and management of conflicts of interest.

Criticism and implementation

Guidelines have been criticised where recommendations outrun the evidence, where industry funding introduces bias, and where rigid application conflicts with individual clinical judgement and patient preference. Guidelines are advisory rather than legally binding standards of care, though courts may consider them as evidence of accepted practice. Successful implementation relies on dissemination, decision-support tools and audit.
