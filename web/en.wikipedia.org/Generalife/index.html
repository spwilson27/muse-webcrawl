Generalife

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Generalife (Spanish: [xeneɾaˈlife]; Arabic: جَنَّة العَرِيف, romanized: Jannat al-‘Arīf, lit. ' Architect's Garden') was the summer palace and country estate of the Nasrid rulers of the Emirate of Granada in Al-Andalus. It is located directly east of, and uphill from, the Alhambra palace and fortress complex in Granada, Spain. In modern times it consists of a complex of palaces, patios and gardens, and is, with the Alhambra, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

The palace and gardens were built during the reign of Muhammad III (1302–1309) and redecorated shortly after by Abu I-Walid Isma'il (1313–1324). The complex was a country estate (almunia) where the sultans could retire from the court, set among orchards and pastures above the city. After the Christian conquest of 1492 it passed to the Catholic Monarchs and was later granted to the Campotegui family; the state acquired it in the 20th century and restored its gardens.

Description

The best-known feature is the Patio de la Acequia (Court of the Water Channel), a long enclosed courtyard crossed by a narrow canal flanked by water jets, flowerbeds, myrtles and pavilions at each end. Other features include the Court of the Cypresses (Patio de los Cipreses), the Water Stairway (Escalera del Agua), whose handrails are channels carrying running water, and upper gardens laid out in the 20th century. The architecture is comparatively plain and intimate, emphasising gardens, water and views toward the Alhambra and the Darro valley.
