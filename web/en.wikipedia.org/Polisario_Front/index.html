The Polisario Front, Frente Polisario, FRELISARIO, or simply POLISARIO (from Spanish Frente Popular de Liberación de Saguía el Hamra y Río de Oro, "Popular Front for the Liberation of Saguia el-Hamra and Río de Oro") is a Sahrawi nationalist liberation movement seeking to end Moroccan control of the Western Sahara and achieve independence for the Sahrawi people.

The movement is the governing party of the Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic (SADR), a partially recognized state that controls about 20–25% of Western Sahara east of the Moroccan Wall, and administers the Sahrawi refugee camps near Tindouf, Algeria. The United Nations considers the Polisario Front the legitimate representative of the Sahrawi people.

History

The Polisario Front was founded on 10 May 1973 by Sahrawi students, including El-Ouali Mustapha Sayed, to fight Spanish colonial rule in Spanish Sahara. After Spain withdrew in 1975 under the Madrid Accords, Morocco and Mauritania partitioned the territory, and Polisario proclaimed the SADR on 27 February 1976 and fought a guerrilla war against both countries. Mauritania withdrew in 1979, but the war with Morocco continued until a UN-brokered ceasefire in 1991, which promised a referendum on independence that has never been held.

The ceasefire collapsed in November 2020 after Moroccan forces moved into the Guerguerat buffer zone; the Polisario declared the ceasefire over and low-intensity hostilities resumed. Diplomatically, Morocco's autonomy plan gained support from the United States (which recognized Moroccan sovereignty over Western Sahara in 2020), Spain, France, and others, while the SADR remains a member of the African Union and is backed principally by Algeria.

Organization

The Front is led by a Secretary-General elected at its general congresses; after El-Ouali's death in 1976, Mohamed Abdelaziz led the movement for 40 years until his death in 2016, when he was succeeded by Brahim Ghali. Its armed wing is the Sahrawi People's Liberation Army (SPLA). The refugee camps near Tindouf, home to well over 100,000 Sahrawis, are administered by the SADR government.
