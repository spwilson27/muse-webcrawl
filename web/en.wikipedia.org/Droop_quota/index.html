Droop quota

The Droop quota is a formula used in elections conducted under proportional representation systems such as the single transferable vote (STV) to determine the number of votes a candidate must receive to be elected. It is named after the English mathematician Henry Richmond Droop, who proposed it in 1868, though a similar quota was described earlier by others.

Formula

Quota = floor( total valid votes / (seats + 1) ) + 1

The Droop quota is the smallest number of votes such that no more candidates can reach it than there are seats to fill: at most seats candidates can each obtain a Droop quota. It is slightly lower than the older Hare quota, which divides the votes simply by the number of seats.

Use

The Droop quota is the most widely used quota in STV elections, including in Ireland, Malta, Australian Senate elections and many others. In counting, candidates reaching the quota are declared elected, and their surplus votes are transferred to next preferences, typically at a fractional value.

Comparison with other quotas

Because it is lower than the Hare quota, the Droop quota tends to leave fewer votes "wasted" at the end of the count and is sometimes said to slightly favour larger parties or groupings relative to the Hare quota. Variants include the Hagenbach-Bischoff quota, which omits the final "+1".
