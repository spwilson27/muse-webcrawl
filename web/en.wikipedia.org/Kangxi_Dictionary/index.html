Kangxi Dictionary

The Kangxi Dictionary (康熙字典, Kāngxī Zìdiǎn) is a Chinese character dictionary compiled by a team of scholars, chiefly Zhang Yushu and Chen Tingjing, on the orders of the Kangxi Emperor of the Qing dynasty. Work began in 1710 and the dictionary was published in 1716. It remained the standard reference of its kind for more than two centuries and is still widely consulted.

Contents and organisation

The dictionary contains 47,035 character entries, arranged under a system of 214 radicals — reduced from the 540 radicals of the ancient Shuowen Jiezi and standardised in the Ming-dynasty Zihui. Within each radical, characters are ordered by the number of additional strokes. Entries give variant forms, pronunciations (in fanqie and homophone notes), meanings, and citations from classical literature. The 214 "Kangxi radicals" remain the basis of indexing in most traditional character dictionaries and of the radical–stroke ordering used in Unicode.

Legacy

Although the dictionary contains errors — the Daoguang Emperor later commissioned a corrected edition, the Zidian Kaozheng (1831) — it defined the standard inventory of Chinese characters and the traditional forms used across East Asia. Its name is still used generically for the radical system itself.
