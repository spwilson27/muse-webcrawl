The Battle of Heraklion was fought during the Battle of Crete in May 1941. The city of Heraklion, on the north coast of Crete, and its nearby airfield were defended by the British 14th Infantry Brigade under Brigadier Brian Chappel, with Australian and Greek battalions. On 20 May 1941, German paratroopers of the 1st Parachute Regiment under Colonel Bruno Bräuer dropped around the city and airfield. Unlike the German success at Maleme in the west, the Heraklion landings were a costly failure: the defenders destroyed or captured most of the attacking force. Nevertheless, the loss of Crete elsewhere compelled the Allies to evacuate the Heraklion garrison by sea on the night of 28/29 May.

Background

After the fall of mainland Greece in April 1941 (see Battle of Greece), some 42,000 Commonwealth and Greek troops were evacuated to Crete. General Bernard Freyberg organised the island's defence into sectors; Heraklion, the island's largest town, was garrisoned by the 14th Brigade: the Black Watch, the York and Lancaster Regiment, the Leicesters, the Australian 2/4th Battalion, and Greek units, supported by a few Matilda tanks and artillery.

The battle

The German afternoon drop of 20 May went badly wrong. Transport aircraft flew into intense anti-aircraft and ground fire; many paratroopers were killed in the air or on landing, and the drop was scattered. The Black Watch and the other battalions, holding the airfield and the town walls, counter-attacked methodically over the following days. The Germans clung to positions east of the airfield and at the village of Gournes but never took either the airfield or the town. A German seaborne convoy approaching Heraklion was also intercepted by the Royal Navy. By 26 May the surviving paratroopers were on the defensive, short of ammunition and supplies.

Evacuation and aftermath

With Maleme airfield lost and the battle in the west decided, Freyberg ordered a general evacuation. On the night of 28/29 May, Royal Navy cruisers and destroyers embarked about 4,500 men of the Heraklion garrison. The squadron was heavily attacked by dive-bombers on the passage to Alexandria; the destroyers Imperial and Hereward were lost, and several hundred soldiers were killed, but most of the garrison escaped — the only one of Crete's garrisons to be evacuated nearly intact. German troops entered Heraklion on 29–30 May. The parachute regiment's losses at Heraklion contributed to Hitler's decision never again to attempt a major airborne assault.
