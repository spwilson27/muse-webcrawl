Fault-tolerant quantum computation

Fault-tolerant quantum computation is quantum computation that can continue correctly even when its components are noisy. Quantum error-correcting codes encode logical qubits into many physical qubits so errors can be detected and corrected without measuring the encoded information.

The threshold theorem states that if the physical error rate is below a certain threshold, arbitrarily long quantum computations are possible with polylogarithmic overhead. Approaches include the surface code, concatenated codes, and magic state distillation for non-Clifford gates. Achieving fault tolerance at scale is a central goal of quantum computing research.
