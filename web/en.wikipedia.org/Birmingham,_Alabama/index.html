Birmingham, Alabama

Birmingham is a city in the north central region of Alabama, United States. It is the county seat of Jefferson County and, with a population of around 200,000 in the city proper and over a million in its metropolitan area, is one of the state's largest metropolitan centres. Founded in 1871 at the crossing of two rail lines near deposits of iron ore, coal and limestone — the three raw materials of steel — it was named after Birmingham, England, a major industrial city.

History

Birmingham grew rapidly as an iron and steel producer, earning the nicknames "The Magic City" and "Pittsburgh of the South". In the 20th century it became a pivotal battleground of the civil rights movement: the 1963 Birmingham campaign, the bombing of the 16th Street Baptist Church that killed four girls, and Martin Luther King Jr.'s "Letter from Birmingham Jail" all occurred here. The city's economy later shifted from heavy industry toward banking, insurance, medicine and education, anchored by the University of Alabama at Birmingham.

Landmarks

The Vulcan statue, the largest cast-iron statue in the world, overlooks the city from Red Mountain. Sloss Furnaces, a preserved iron-making site, is a National Historic Landmark, and the Birmingham Civil Rights Institute interprets the movement's history.
