WordNet is a large lexical database of English. Nouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs are grouped into sets of cognitive synonyms (synsets), each expressing a distinct concept. Synsets are interlinked by means of conceptual-semantic and lexical relations, so that the resulting network can be navigated as a combination of dictionary and thesaurus.

History

WordNet was created at Princeton University under the direction of psychology professor George Armitage Miller, beginning in the mid-1980s. It was inspired by psycholinguistic theories of how humans organize lexical knowledge in memory. Version 3.1, the most recent major release, contains about 155,000 words organized in roughly 117,000 synsets.

Structure

The fundamental building block of WordNet is the synset, a set of synonyms representing one concept, accompanied by a brief definition (gloss) and example sentences. Relations between synsets include hypernymy and hyponymy ("is a kind of"), meronymy ("is part of"), antonymy, and, for verbs, troponymy. Words are also indexed by polysemy counts indicating how many senses they carry.

Applications

WordNet has been one of the most widely used resources in computational linguistics and natural language processing, supporting tasks such as word-sense disambiguation, information retrieval, text classification, and machine translation evaluation. Wordnets for many other languages have been built following its model, and versions have been linked to ontologies such as SUMO and to knowledge bases like Wikidata.
