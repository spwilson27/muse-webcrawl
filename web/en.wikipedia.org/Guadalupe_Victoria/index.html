Guadalupe Victoria

Guadalupe Victoria (Spanish: [ɡwaðaˈlupe βiɣˈtoɾja]; 29 September 1786 – 21 March 1843), born José Miguel Ramón Adaucto Fernández y Félix, was a Mexican general and political leader who fought for independence against the Spanish Empire in the Mexican War of Independence and later served as the first president of Mexico, from 1824 to 1829.

Independence war

Born in Tamazula, Durango, Fernández studied law in Mexico City before joining the insurgency in 1812 under José María Morelos. He adopted the name Guadalupe Victoria — for Our Lady of Guadalupe and for victory (victoria) — and became one of the most successful insurgent commanders in Veracruz, attacking royalist convoys on the road between Veracruz and Mexico City. After the execution of Morelos and the decline of the movement, he refused amnesty and hid for years in the mountains of Veracruz, re-emerging when Agustín de Iturbide proclaimed the Plan of Iguala in 1821.

Presidency

After the fall of Iturbide's empire, Victoria served on the provisional governing triumvirate and in 1824 was elected the first president of the federal republic under the Constitution of 1824. His administration abolished slavery, established diplomatic relations, founded the National Museum, promoted education, and concluded treaties of friendship with Britain. Despite political unrest and the expulsion of Spaniards from Mexico, he completed his full term and transferred power peacefully to Vicente Guerrero in 1829 — the first orderly presidential succession in Mexican history.

Later life and legacy

Victoria retired to his hacienda El Jobo in Veracruz. He died of epilepsy at Perote in 1843. He is the only Mexican president to have served a full term in the republic's first three decades, and his remains rest in the Column of Independence in Mexico City. The city of Ciudad Victoria, capital of Tamaulipas, is named in his honour.
