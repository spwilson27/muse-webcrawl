The String of Pearls

Author: Attributed to James Malcolm Rymer and Thomas Peckett Prest

Published: 1846–47 (serial)

Genre: Penny dreadful

Villain: Sweeney Todd

The String of Pearls: A Domestic Romance is a Victorian penny dreadful serial published in 1846–47, attributed to James Malcolm Rymer and Thomas Peckett Prest. It introduced the character of Sweeney Todd, the murderous barber of Fleet Street, to popular culture.

Plot

The sailor's clerk Johanna Oakley investigates the disappearance of her lover, Lieutenant Thornhill, last seen carrying a string of pearls to her father's friend. The trail leads to the barber Sweeney Todd, who cuts his customers' throats with his razor and drops them through a trapdoor into the cellar, where Mrs Lovett bakes the bodies into meat pies sold in her shop in Bell Yard.

Legacy

The story was expanded in an 1850 edition and repeatedly adapted for the stage. In the twentieth century it became a play by Christopher Bond (1973), the Stephen Sondheim musical Sweeney Todd: The Demon Barber of Fleet Street (1979), and Tim Burton's 2007 film.
