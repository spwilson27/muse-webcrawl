ULPA

ULPA is an acronym for "Ultra-low particulate air" (or "Ultra-low penetration air"). An ULPA filter is a type of high-efficiency air filter that can remove from the air at least 99.999% of dust, pollen, mould, bacteria and other airborne particles with a size of 0.12 micrometres (µm) or larger, according to common definitions such as those of the Institute of Environmental Sciences and Technology (IEST).

ULPA filters represent a higher grade of filtration than HEPA filters, which are rated to remove at least 99.97% of particles of 0.3 µm. Under the European standard EN 1822, filter classes U15 to U17 correspond to ULPA performance, with efficiencies ranging from 99.9995% to 99.999995% at the most penetrating particle size.

Applications

Because of their very high efficiency, ULPA filters are used where extremely clean air is required, including:

  • Semiconductor fabrication cleanrooms, where even sub-micron contamination can ruin microelectronic devices;
  • Pharmaceutical and biotechnology manufacturing;
  • Hospital operating theatres and isolation rooms, and laboratories handling hazardous biological or radioactive material;
  • Specialised vacuum cleaners and air purifiers designed for allergy control or hazardous dust.

Construction and trade-offs

ULPA filters are typically made of densely pleated borosilicate glass-fibre media, sometimes with membrane media, separated by aluminium or thread separators and sealed into a rigid frame. The dense media creates a higher pressure drop than a HEPA filter of the same size, so systems using ULPA filtration need more fan energy and the filters generally have a shorter service life and higher cost. For many applications HEPA filtration is sufficient, and ULPA is reserved for the most demanding environments.
