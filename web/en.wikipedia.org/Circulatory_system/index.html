Circulatory system

The circulatory system is a system of organs that includes the heart, blood vessels, and blood which is circulated throughout the body of a human or other vertebrate. It includes the cardiovascular system, or vascular system, that consists of the heart and blood vessels (from Greek kardia meaning heart, and from Latin vascula meaning vessels). The circulatory system has two divisions: a systemic circulation that carries blood to most of the body, and a pulmonary circulation that carries blood to and from the lungs.

The circulatory system transports oxygen, carbon dioxide, nutrients, hormones, and blood cells to and from the cells of the body to provide nourishment, fight disease, stabilize temperature and pH, and maintain homeostasis.

Structure

The heart is a muscular pump with four chambers—two atria and two ventricles—that drives blood through the vessels. Arteries carry blood away from the heart, veins return it, and capillaries, the smallest vessels, permit exchange of gases and solutes with tissues. In the pulmonary circuit, deoxygenated blood travels from the right ventricle to the lungs and returns oxygenated to the left atrium; in the systemic circuit, oxygenated blood is pumped from the left ventricle to the body and returns deoxygenated to the right atrium. This double circulation is characteristic of birds and mammals.

Blood

Blood consists of plasma and formed elements: red blood cells, which carry oxygen bound to haemoglobin; white blood cells of the immune system; and platelets involved in clotting. An average adult has about five litres of blood. The lymphatic system is an adjunct network that drains interstitial fluid, absorbs fats, and supports immune defence.

History of understanding

Ancient physicians believed blood ebbed and flowed in the vessels. The 12th-century physician Ibn al-Nafis first described pulmonary circulation, and in 1628 William Harvey demonstrated in De Motu Cordis that blood circulates continuously, driven by the heart—a foundation of modern physiology. Marcello Malpighi later observed the capillaries Harvey's theory required.

Disease

Cardiovascular diseases—including coronary artery disease, stroke, heart failure, and hypertension—are the leading cause of death worldwide. Disorders of the circulatory system may also involve the blood itself, such as anemia, haemophilia, and leukaemia.
