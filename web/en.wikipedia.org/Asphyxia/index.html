Asphyxia

Asphyxia or asphyxiation is a condition of deficient supply of oxygen to the body (hypoxia), generally arising from abnormal breathing or obstruction of the airway. If prolonged, it causes unconsciousness, brain damage and death. The term derives from the Greek a- ("without") and sphyxis ("pulse"), a reference to the absence of a heartbeat in its original usage.

Causes

Asphyxia can result from mechanical obstruction of the airway (choking on a foreign body, strangulation, hanging, smothering, or compression of the chest in traumatic asphyxia), from failure of respiratory effort (drowning, drug overdose, paralysis), or from an atmosphere deficient in oxygen or rich in inert gases (nitrogen, helium, or carbon dioxide accumulation in confined spaces). Perinatal asphyxia refers to oxygen deprivation of a newborn during labour and delivery.

Signs and course

As oxygen falls and carbon dioxide rises, the victim experiences air hunger, panic, rapid pulse and cyanosis (bluish discoloration), followed by loss of consciousness within minutes and irreversible brain injury if breathing and circulation are not restored. In forensic medicine, findings such as petechial haemorrhages and congestion are associated with, but not specific to, asphyxial deaths.

First aid

Immediate treatment is to clear and open the airway, remove the person from the hazardous atmosphere, and begin cardiopulmonary resuscitation and rescue breathing as needed, calling emergency services at once.
