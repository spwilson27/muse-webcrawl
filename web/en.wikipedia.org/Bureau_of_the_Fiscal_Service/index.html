Bureau of the Fiscal Service

Agency overview

Formed: October 7, 2012
Preceding agencies: Bureau of the Public Debt; Financial Management Service
Headquarters: Washington, D.C.
Parent agency: U.S. Department of the Treasury

The Bureau of the Fiscal Service is a bureau of the United States Department of the Treasury. It manages the federal government's accounting, central payment systems, debt financing, and collections, acting in effect as the government's bookkeeper and payments processor.

Formation

The bureau was created on October 7, 2012, when Treasury Secretary Timothy Geithner directed the consolidation of the Bureau of the Public Debt and the Financial Management Service into a single agency, intended to save money and improve the efficiency of the government's financial operations.

Functions

The Fiscal Service borrows the money needed to run the federal government by issuing Treasury bills, notes and bonds; collects revenues and delinquent debts owed to the government; disburses hundreds of millions of payments each year, including Social Security benefits, tax refunds and federal salaries; and produces the government's consolidated financial statements, including the monthly statement of the public debt.

Offices

Its major offices include the Office of Debt Management, the Office of Financial Innovation and Transformation, and operations centers in Parkersburg, West Virginia, and other locations. The bureau also operates the TreasuryDirect system through which individuals buy securities directly from the Treasury.
