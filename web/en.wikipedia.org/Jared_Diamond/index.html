Jared Diamond

Jared Mason Diamond

Born: 10 September 1937, Boston, Massachusetts, U.S.

Alma mater: Harvard University; University of Cambridge

Known for: Guns, Germs, and Steel (1997)

Fields: Physiology, biogeography, anthropology, geography

Jared Mason Diamond (born September 10, 1937) is an American scientist and author best known for his popular science books, including The Third Chimpanzee (1991), Guns, Germs, and Steel (1997, winner of the Pulitzer Prize), Collapse (2005), The World Until Yesterday (2012) and Upheaval (2019).

Career

Trained as a physiologist, Diamond earned a PhD from Cambridge on the physiology of the gallbladder, then became a professor of physiology and later of geography at the University of California, Los Angeles. His fieldwork on the birds of New Guinea, begun in 1964, led to influential work in ecology and biogeography.

Books and ideas

In Guns, Germs, and Steel, Diamond argued that differences in the fortunes of human societies stem largely from environmental and geographic factors — such as the east–west orientation of Eurasia and its stock of domesticable species — rather than from any biological superiority. Collapse examined how environmental damage and societal choices contributed to the failure of past societies. His work has been widely read and also criticized, particularly by anthropologists and historians who question its treatment of evidence and its perceived geographic determinism.
