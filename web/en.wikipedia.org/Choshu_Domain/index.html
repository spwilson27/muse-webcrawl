Choshu Domain

The Choshu Domain (長州藩, Chōshū-han), also called the Hagi Domain, was a feudal domain of Tokugawa Japan occupying Nagato and Suo provinces (modern Yamaguchi Prefecture). Ruled by the Mōri clan from its castle town of Hagi, Choshu played a leading role in the overthrow of the Tokugawa shogunate and the Meiji Restoration.

Background

The Mōri, once among the greatest daimyo of western Japan, were reduced by Tokugawa Ieyasu after the Battle of Sekigahara (1600) to the two provinces of Nagato and Suo, a humiliation the clan nursed for generations. In the 19th century the domain undertook fiscal and military reforms, and its domain school, the Meirinkan, and Yoshida Shōin's private academy, the Shōka Sonjuku, educated many future Restoration leaders.

Bakumatsu conflicts

Choshu became a centre of the sonnō jōi ("revere the emperor, expel the barbarians") movement. After Choshu forces fired on foreign shipping in the Shimonoseki Straits in 1863, Western powers retaliated with the Bombardment of Shimonoseki, and the shogunate launched two punitive expeditions. Choshu modernized its forces with mixed samurai-commoner militia (Kiheitai) under Takasugi Shinsaku and forged the secret Satsuma–Choshu Alliance in 1866, which enabled the imperial victory in the Boshin War (1868–1869).

Legacy

Choshu men dominated the early Meiji government and the Imperial Japanese Army; figures such as Itō Hirobumi, Yamagata Aritomo, and Inoue Kaoru hailed from the domain, and "Choshu cliques" remained influential in Japanese politics for decades.
