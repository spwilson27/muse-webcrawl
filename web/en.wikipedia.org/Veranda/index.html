Veranda

A veranda (also spelled verandah) is a roofed, open-air gallery or porch attached to the exterior of a building, usually extending along the front and sides and enclosed only by a railing. Verandas are a defining feature of domestic architecture in warm climates, providing shade, ventilation, and a transitional living space between indoors and outdoors.

Etymology and origins

The word entered English from Hindi and Bengali baraṇḍā and related forms, themselves possibly influenced by Portuguese varanda, meaning a railing or balcony. The veranda as a building element developed in colonial architecture in India and spread through the British Empire during the 18th and 19th centuries, being adapted to local materials and climates wherever it arrived.

Regional forms

In Australia, the veranda became an iconic element of the Queenslander house and of rural homesteads, often wrapping around all four sides of the building and serving as sleeping space in hot weather. In the southern United States, deep shaded porches and verandas characterise Creole cottages, plantation houses, and shotgun houses. In Brazil, the varanda is a standard feature of houses and apartments. In India, verandas remain common in both traditional homes and colonial-era bungalows.

Distinctions

A veranda differs from a balcony in being at ground level and roofed as part of the main structure; it differs from a terrace or patio in being roofed, and from a portico in running along the length of a facade rather than marking only the entrance. Verandas are typically long and narrow, and may be partially enclosed with screens or lattice to keep out insects.
