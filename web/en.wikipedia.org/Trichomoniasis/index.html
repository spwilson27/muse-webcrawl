Trichomoniasis
Cause: Trichomonas vaginalis
Type: sexually transmitted infection

Trichomoniasis (or "trich") is an infectious disease caused by the parasite Trichomonas vaginalis. About 70% of affected people do not have symptoms when infected. When symptoms occur, they typically begin 5 to 28 days after exposure and can include itching in the genital area, a thin discharge with an unpleasant smell, burning with urination, and pain during sex.

Cause and diagnosis

Trichomoniasis is a sexually transmitted infection spread mainly by vaginal sex. Diagnosis is by microscopy, culture, or nucleic acid amplification testing of genital fluid. It is the most common non-viral sexually transmitted infection worldwide.

Treatment and prevention

Treatment is with metronidazole or tinidazole, taken by mouth, and sexual partners should be treated at the same time. Prevention includes abstinence, condom use, and not douching. Infection increases the risk of acquiring HIV and is associated with preterm delivery.
