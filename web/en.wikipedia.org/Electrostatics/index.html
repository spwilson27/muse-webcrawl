Electrostatics

Electrostatics is a branch of physics that studies slow-moving or stationary electric charges. Since classical antiquity, it has been known that some materials, such as amber, attract lightweight particles after rubbing.

Fundamental concepts

The force between two point charges is described by Coulomb's law: proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of their separation. The electric field and electric potential describe the influence a charge distribution exerts on other charges.

Key equations

Gauss's law relates the electric flux through a closed surface to the enclosed charge, and in the absence of changing magnetic fields the electrostatic field is conservative, derivable from a scalar potential via Poisson's equation.

Phenomena and applications

Electrostatic phenomena include charging by friction (the triboelectric effect), electrostatic induction, and discharge. Applications include photocopiers and laser printers, electrostatic precipitators for air pollution control, paint spraying, and the Van de Graaff generator.
