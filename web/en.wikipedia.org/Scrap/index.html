Scrap

Scrap consists of recyclable materials, usually metals, left over from product manufacturing and consumption, such as parts of vehicles, building supplies and surplus materials. Unlike waste, scrap has monetary value, especially in recovered metals, and the scrap recycling industry is a major part of the global materials economy.

Metal recycling

Scrap metal is divided into ferrous scrap (iron and steel) and non-ferrous scrap (aluminium, copper, brass, zinc, lead and precious metals). Steel is one of the most recycled materials in the world; recycling scrap in electric arc furnaces uses far less energy than producing steel from iron ore. Non-ferrous metals can be recycled repeatedly without loss of quality.

Industry

Scrap is collected by dealers, sorted and processed — shredded, baled or sheared — at scrapyards before being sold to mills and foundries. Prices fluctuate with global demand, and international trade in scrap is substantial, with countries such as Turkey historically among the largest importers of ferrous scrap.

Environmental aspects

Recycling scrap conserves natural resources and reduces emissions, though poorly managed yards and ship-breaking operations can cause pollution and health hazards.
