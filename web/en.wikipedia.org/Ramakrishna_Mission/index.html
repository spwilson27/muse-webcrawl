Ramakrishna Mission

Ramakrishna Mission
Founded: 1 May 1897, Kolkata
Founder: Swami Vivekananda
Headquarters: Belur Math, Howrah, India
Fields: Education, healthcare, relief, rural development

The Ramakrishna Mission is a Hindu religious and spiritual organisation founded by Swami Vivekananda on 1 May 1897. It forms the core of the Ramakrishna movement, also known as the Vedanta movement, and works alongside its monastic counterpart, the Ramakrishna Math. The Mission is headquartered at Belur Math in Howrah, West Bengal.

Ideals and principles

The Mission's work is governed by the principles of karma yoga as taught by Vivekananda: service to humanity as worship of the divine ("Shiva jnane jiva seva" — to serve living beings knowing them to be Shiva). Its stated principles include selfless service, religious harmony, and work conducted without distinction of caste, creed or nationality. The organisation's emblem, designed by Vivekananda, symbolises the union of knowledge, devotion, work and yoga.

Activities

The Mission runs schools, colleges, universities (including Ramakrishna Mission Vivekananda Educational and Research Institute), hospitals, dispensaries, orphanages and libraries across India and in countries including Bangladesh, Fiji, Mauritius, Singapore, South Africa, the United Kingdom and the United States. It is renowned for relief and rehabilitation work after floods, famines, earthquakes and cyclones, beginning with the Madras famine relief of 1897. Its publishing house, Advaita Ashrama, issues the journals Prabuddha Bharata and Vedanta Kesari.

Recognition

The Mission received the Gandhi Peace Prize in 1998, and its work in education and healthcare has been widely commended. Swami Ranganathananda received the Padma Bhushan for his service as the Mission's president.
