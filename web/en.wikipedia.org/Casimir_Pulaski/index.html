Casimir Pulaski

Casimir Pulaski at the Siege of Savannah

Kazimierz Michał Władysław Wiktor Pułaski (Polish: [kaˈʑimjɛʂ puˈwaskʲi]; March 4 or 6, 1745 – October 11, 1779), anglicised as Casimir Pulaski, was a Polish nobleman, soldier, and military commander who has been called, together with Michael Kovats, "the father of the American cavalry". Born in Warsaw, he fought against foreign domination of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth in the Bar Confederation (1768–1772), and after its failure emigrated to North America, where he became a general in the Continental Army during the American Revolutionary War. He was mortally wounded leading a charge at the Siege of Savannah.

In America

Recommended by Benjamin Franklin, Pulaski arrived in America in 1777 and distinguished himself at the Battle of Brandywine, where his cavalry reconnaissance and a timely charge helped cover the American retreat. Congress appointed him brigadier general and chief of cavalry. In 1778 he received permission to raise an independent unit, the Pulaski Legion, which he recruited and trained. He served in the defence of Charleston and joined the Franco-American attempt to retake Savannah in the autumn of 1779. On October 9, 1779, he was gravely wounded by grapeshot while leading a charge, and died two days later.

Legacy

Pulaski is one of only eight people to be awarded honorary United States citizenship (granted posthumously in 2009). Numerous towns, counties, streets, and the Pulaski Skyway in New Jersey are named for him. General Pulaski Memorial Day is observed in Illinois, and October 11 is commemorated nationally as a day of Polish-American heritage.
