Abhinavagupta

Born: c. 950 CE, Kashmir

Died: c. 1016 CE, Kashmir

Known for: Kashmir Shaivism, aesthetics, Tantrāloka

Abhinavagupta (c. 950 – c. 1016 CE) was a philosopher, mystic and aesthetician from Kashmir, regarded as one of the greatest thinkers of classical India. A polymath who studied under many teachers, he synthesised the traditions of non-dual Kashmir Shaivism and wrote influential commentaries on aesthetics, dramaturgy and tantra.

Life

Born into a family of scholars and yogis in the Kashmir Valley, Abhinavagupta studied grammar, logic, the various schools of Shaiva tantra and Buddhist philosophy. Tradition holds that he travelled with a large community of disciples and, at the end of his life, entered a cave at Bhairava reciting a hymn, accompanied by 1,200 followers.

Works

His magnum opus, the Tantrāloka ("Light on the Tantras"), is a vast encyclopaedic synthesis of the Trika Shaiva tradition, presenting ritual, yoga and philosophy as a path to recognising one's identity with Shiva. In aesthetics, his commentary on the Nāṭyaśāstra, the Abhinavabhāratī, developed the theory of rasa (aesthetic relish), arguing that the experience of art culminates in a bliss akin to spiritual liberation. His Īśvarapratyabhijñāvimarśinī expounds the Pratyabhijñā ("Recognition") school of philosophy.

Influence

Abhinavagupta's integration of aesthetics and spirituality shaped Sanskrit literary theory for centuries, and modern scholars such as K. C. Pandey popularised his work in the West. He is remembered as both a systematic metaphysician and a revered tantric guru.
