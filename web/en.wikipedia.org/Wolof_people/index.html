Wolof people

The Wolof people (also spelled Wollof, Jollof, or Jolof) are a West African ethnic group found in northwestern Senegal, the Gambia, and southwestern coastal Mauritania. In Senegal they are the largest ethnic group, making up roughly 40 percent of the population, and their language, Wolof, functions as a lingua franca across much of the region.

Total populationOver 7 million
RegionsSenegal, the Gambia, Mauritania
LanguagesWolof, French, Arabic
ReligionPredominantly Islam (Sufi orders), minority Christianity and traditional beliefs

History

The Wolof were the founders of the Jolof Empire, which from around the 14th century united a number of Wolof and Serer states between the Senegal and Gambia rivers. After the empire's breakup in the later 16th century, independent Wolof kingdoms such as Waalo, Cayor, Baol, and Jolof proper persisted, engaging in trade and at times conflict with French colonial forces in the 19th century.

Society

Traditional Wolof society was stratified into freeborn, artisan, and slave-descended castes, with griots (gewel) serving as hereditary praise-singers, genealogists, and oral historians. The extended family and ideals such as teranga (hospitality) remain central to Wolof social life.

Religion

The great majority of Wolof are Muslims, affiliated especially with the Mouride and Tijaniyya Sufi brotherhoods, whose marabouts have exercised major social and economic influence, notably around the holy city of Touba.
