Preferential option for the poor

The preferential option for the poor is a principle of Catholic social teaching that states that Christians are called to care first and foremost for the needs of the poor and vulnerable. It holds that the moral test of a society is how it treats its most vulnerable members, and that public policy should be judged by its impact on those in poverty.

Origins

The phrase emerged from Latin American theology in the late 1960s. Pedro Arrupe, Superior General of the Jesuits, used the term in a 1968 letter to Latin American Jesuits, and the Latin American bishops' conferences at Medellín (1968) and Puebla (1979) spoke of a preferential option for the poor as a guiding principle for the Church's mission. It became a central concept of liberation theology, especially in the writings of Gustavo Gutiérrez.

Development in Church teaching

Pope John Paul II endorsed the principle, describing it in the encyclical Sollicitudo Rei Socialis (1987) as a "special form of primacy in the exercise of Christian charity". The Compendium of the Social Doctrine of the Church (2004) and Pope Francis's teaching, including Evangelii Gaudium (2013), reaffirm that the option for the poor is a theological category rooted in the Bible's concern for widows, orphans, strangers, and the oppressed.
