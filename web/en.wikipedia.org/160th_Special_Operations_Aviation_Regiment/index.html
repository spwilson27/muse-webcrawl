The 160th Special Operations Aviation Regiment (Airborne), abbreviated 160th SOAR, is a special operations force of the United States Army that provides helicopter aviation support for special operations forces. Its aviators are known as the Night Stalkers.

History

The unit was created after the failed 1980 Iran hostage rescue mission (Operation Eagle Claw) exposed the need for dedicated, specially trained aviation support. It was officially activated in October 1981 at Fort Campbell, Kentucky. Its creed promises to arrive "on target, plus or minus 30 seconds."

Operations

Night Stalkers fly modified MH-6 Little Bird, MH-60 Black Hawk and MH-47 Chinook helicopters, often at night and low level. They have supported operations in Grenada, Panama, Somalia (including the 1993 Battle of Mogadishu), Afghanistan and Iraq, and flew the mission that killed Osama bin Laden in 2011.
