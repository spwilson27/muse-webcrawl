Interleukin-2 receptor

The interleukin-2 receptor (IL-2R) is a heterotrimeric protein expressed on the surface of certain immune cells, such as lymphocytes, that binds and responds to the cytokine interleukin-2. It plays a central role in regulating the proliferation and survival of T cells.

Structure

The receptor consists of up to three subunits: the alpha chain (CD25), the beta chain (CD122) and the common gamma chain (CD132). The trimeric form binds IL-2 with high affinity and is characteristic of activated T cells and regulatory T cells.

Clinical significance

Mutations in the gamma chain cause X-linked severe combined immunodeficiency. Antibodies against CD25, such as basiliximab, are used to prevent transplant rejection, and engineered IL-2 therapies targeting the receptor are explored in cancer immunotherapy.
