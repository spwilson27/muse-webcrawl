Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting

The Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting (CHOGM) is a biennial summit of the leaders of the Commonwealth of Nations. It grew out of the Colonial Conferences of the 19th century and the Imperial Conferences of the early 20th, taking its present form in 1971 when the Singapore Declaration set out the Commonwealth's shared principles.

Format

Meetings are hosted by a different member country each time and are traditionally chaired by the host head of government, who becomes Commonwealth Chair-in-Office. A distinctive feature is the leaders' retreat, where heads of government meet without officials for frank discussion. Each summit ends with a communiqué and, often, a special declaration — such as the Harare Declaration of 1991, which committed the Commonwealth to democracy and human rights and underpins the suspension of members that violate them.

Notable meetings

CHOGMs have debated apartheid South Africa (notably Gleneagles in 1977 and Nassau in 1985), Zimbabwe's suspension and withdrawal in 2002–03, climate change in Malta in 2015, and the association's future leadership. The 2024 meeting was held in Samoa, the first hosted by a Pacific island state.
