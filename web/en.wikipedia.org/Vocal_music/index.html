Vocal music

Vocal music is a type of singing performed by one or more singers, either with instrumental accompaniment or unaccompanied (a cappella). It is the oldest form of music, since it requires no instrument besides the human body. Vocal music may set a text — hymns, songs, opera libretti — or use non-lexical sounds, as in scat singing or yodeling.

Forms

Major traditions of vocal music include choral music sung by choirs; sacred forms such as the Mass, motet, cantata and oratorio; secular art song (Lied, mélodie); opera, which combines singing with drama; and popular song. Voice types are classified by range, including soprano, mezzo-soprano, alto, tenor, baritone and bass.

Techniques

Singing involves control of breath, phonation in the larynx and resonance in the vocal tract. Techniques range from bel canto in classical singing to belting in musical theatre, overtone singing (throat singing) in parts of Central Asia, and extended techniques in contemporary music.
