Dubai (Arabic: دبي, romanized: Dubayy) is the most populous city in the United Arab Emirates (UAE) and the capital of the Emirate of Dubai, the most populous of the country's seven emirates. Established in the 18th century as a small fishing village, Dubai grew into a regional trading hub in the early 20th century and grew rapidly during the late 20th and early 21st centuries with a focus on tourism and luxury. It has the second-most five-star hotels in the world and the tallest building in the world, the Burj Khalifa, which is 828 metres (2,717 ft) tall.

Located on the south-eastern coast of the Persian Gulf, Dubai is a major global transport hub for passengers and cargo. Oil revenue first helped accelerate the city's development, but reserves are limited and production levels are low; today, less than 5% of the emirate's revenue comes from oil. Dubai's economy relies on trade, tourism, aviation, real estate, and financial services.

History

Although stone tools have been found at many archaeological sites, little is known about the early history of the area. The town of Dubai was recorded in 1095 in the Book of Geography by the Andalusian geographer Abu Abdullah al-Bakri. In 1580 the Venetian pearl merchant Gasparo Balbi visited the area and mentioned Dubai for its pearling industry. Modern Dubai traces its origins to 1833, when some 800 members of the Bani Yas tribe, led by the Al Maktoum family, settled at the mouth of Dubai Creek. In 1892 Dubai came under the "exclusive agreements" protection of the United Kingdom, and its merchants prospered as a free-trading port, importing goods—especially from Lingeh in Persia—and re-exporting them across the region.

Oil was discovered in 1966 at the offshore Fateh field, and exports began in 1969. In 1971 Dubai joined with Abu Dhabi and five other emirates to form the United Arab Emirates after the British protectorate ended. Under Sheikh Rashid bin Saeed Al Maktoum and his son Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum, the city built ports (Port Rashid and Jebel Ali), an airline (Emirates), and free zones such as Jebel Ali Free Zone, laying the foundations of its modern economy.

Geography

Dubai is situated on the Persian Gulf coast of the UAE, at sea level. The emirate borders Abu Dhabi to the south, Sharjah to the northeast, and Oman to the southeast; the exclave of Hatta lies in the Hajar Mountains. Dubai Creek, a natural saltwater inlet, divides the city into Deira and Bur Dubai. The city has a hot desert climate, with very hot, humid summers and warm winters.

Economy

Dubai's gross domestic product exceeds US$110 billion. Trade, logistics, transport, tourism, and financial services dominate: Jebel Ali Port is the world's largest man-made harbour and the busiest port in the Middle East, and Dubai International Airport has been the world's busiest airport by international passenger traffic. The city hosts the Dubai International Financial Centre (DIFC), regional headquarters of many multinational firms, and shopping and entertainment destinations that draw millions of visitors annually. Real-estate megaprojects such as the Palm Islands, the World Islands, and the Burj Khalifa district reshaped the coastline and skyline; the 2009 debt crisis, triggered by Dubai World's request for a standstill on its debt, led to a bailout financed by Abu Dhabi.

Architecture and landmarks

Dubai is known for ambitious architecture: the Burj Khalifa (2010), the world's tallest structure; the sail-shaped Burj Al Arab hotel; the Palm Jumeirah artificial archipelago; and the Dubai Mall and Dubai Fountain. Historic districts along the Creek, including Al Fahidi (Bastakiya) with its wind-tower houses, the gold and spice souks of Deira, and traditional abra boats preserve the city's older trading character.

Demographics

Dubai's population is roughly 3.6 million, the great majority expatriates—Indians form the largest group, alongside Pakistanis, Bangladeshis, Filipinos, and many others; Emirati citizens are a minority of residents. Arabic is the official language, but English is widely used in business and daily life. Islam is the official religion, and the city is home to large Christian, Hindu, and other communities with places of worship in designated areas.
