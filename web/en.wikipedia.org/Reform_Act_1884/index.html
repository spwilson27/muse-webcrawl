Reform Act 1884

The Representation of the People Act 1884 (48 & 49 Vict. c. 3), commonly called the Reform Act 1884 or the Third Reform Act, was an act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that extended the voting franchise in the counties to match that of the boroughs, creating a near-uniform household suffrage for men.

Provisions and effects

Passed under William Gladstone's Liberal government, the act enfranchised male householders and lodgers paying £10 rent in the counties — notably agricultural labourers — adding about two million voters and raising the electorate to roughly 5.5 million, some 40% of adult men. Women, and men without a settled residence, remained excluded.

Companion measure

The act was paired with the Redistribution of Seats Act 1885, which redrew constituency boundaries towards equal single-member districts. Together they reshaped British electoral politics before the further reforms of 1918.
