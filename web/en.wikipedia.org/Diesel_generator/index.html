Diesel generator

A diesel generator (DG), or diesel genset, is the combination of a diesel engine with an electric generator (usually an alternator) to generate electrical energy. Diesel generators are used in places without connection to a power grid, or as emergency power supply if the grid fails, as well as for peak shaving and export to the grid.

Sizes and operation

Sets range from a few kilovolt-amperes for homes to several megawatts for hospitals, data centers, and industrial plants. A control panel starts the engine automatically when mains power fails, transfers the load, and shuts the set down when power returns. Prime movers are typically four-stroke, turbocharged compression-ignition engines running at 1,500 or 1,800 rpm to produce 50 or 60 Hz power.

Applications

Critical facilities — hospitals, water treatment plants, airports, and telecommunications sites — rely on diesel standby generation because of its rapid starting and high reliability. Diesel gensets also supply remote mines, ships, and off-grid communities, and they provide black-start capability for recovering power grids after widespread outages.
