Astarte

Region: Phoenicia and the Levant

Consort/equivalent: Baal; Greek Aphrodite; Mesopotamian Ishtar

Symbols: Lion, dove, sphinx, star

Centres: Sidon, Tyre, Byblos

Astarte (from Greek Ἀστάρτη, derived from Phoenician ʿAštart) was a goddess of fertility, sexuality, and war worshipped across the ancient Levant and the Phoenician world. She was the principal female deity of the Phoenician city-states and the cognate of the Mesopotamian Ishtar and the Ugaritic Athtart.

Worship

Astarte was especially honoured at Sidon, where she shared a temple with Eshmun, and at Tyre and Byblos. The Hebrew Bible condemns the worship of "Ashtoreth" by the Israelites and neighbouring peoples, associating her cult with Solomon, Jezebel, and the Philistines, in whose temple at Ashkelon the armour of Saul was placed. Phoenician colonists carried her cult across the Mediterranean, to Cyprus, Carthage, Malta, Sicily, and Iberia.

Attributes and syncretism

Astarte was depicted naked or in a feathered dress, sometimes holding lotus or papyrus stems, and was associated with the morning and evening star, the planet Venus. In Egypt she was adopted as a war goddess during the New Kingdom. The Greeks identified her with Aphrodite, and her temple at Eryx in Sicily was famous throughout the ancient world.
