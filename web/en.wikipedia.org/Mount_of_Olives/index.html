Mount of Olives

The Mount of Olives is a mountain ridge about a kilometre east of the Old City of Jerusalem, separated from it by the Kidron Valley. Named for the olive groves that once covered it, the ridge — about 826 metres at its highest — is one of the most sacred landscapes in Judaism, Christianity and Islam, dense with churches, monasteries and one of the world's oldest Jewish cemeteries.

Religious significance

In Jewish tradition the Mount is bound up with prophecy: Zechariah foretells the feet of the LORD standing on it at the end of days, and the belief that the resurrection of the dead will begin there has made burial on its western slope coveted for some 3,000 years; an estimated 150,000 graves cover the hillside. For Christians the Mount is the setting of Jesus's agony in the Garden of Gethsemane at its foot, of his weeping over Jerusalem, and of the Ascension, marked by the Chapel of the Ascension on the summit. Palm Sunday processions traditionally descend the Mount into the city.

Sites

Churches on the ridge include Dominus Flevit, the Church of All Nations (the Basilica of the Agony) beside Gethsemane, the Russian Orthodox Church of Mary Magdalene with its golden onion domes, and the Church of the Pater Noster. The Hebrew University's Mount Scopus campus lies on the ridge's northern extension, and the Jerusalem neighbourhood of At-Tur occupies the summit area.
