Eyrbyggja saga

Eyrbyggja saga is one of the Icelanders' sagas, written in the thirteenth century. It tells the story of families living on the Snæfellsnes peninsula in Iceland from the settlement era (late ninth century) into the early eleventh century, with the chieftain Snorri Goði as a central figure.

Content

Unlike many sagas, Eyrbyggja has no single protagonist; it moves between feuds, lawsuits, hauntings, and the Christianisation of Iceland. Its episodes include the famous ghost story of Fróðár and the blood-brotherhood of the Kjalleklingar.

Authorship and preservation

The saga is anonymous and survives in several medieval vellum fragments and later paper manuscripts. Its style is unusually episodic and dry, interleaving genealogy and legal detail.
