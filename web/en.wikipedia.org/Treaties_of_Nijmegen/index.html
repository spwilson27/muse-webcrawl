Treaties of Nijmegen

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Treaties of Peace of Nijmegen were a series of treaties signed in the Dutch city of Nijmegen between August 1678 and December 1679. They ended the Franco-Dutch War (1672–1678) and several connected conflicts between France, the Dutch Republic, Spain, the Holy Roman Empire, Brandenburg, Denmark-Norway and Sweden.

Terms

The principal treaty between France and the Dutch Republic (10 August 1678) restored Dutch territory occupied by France and revised commercial tariffs. By the Franco-Spanish treaty (17 September 1678), Spain ceded Franche-Comté and several towns in the Spanish Netherlands to France, including Valenciennes, Cambrai and Ypres, while recovering other places. France also made gains on its eastern frontier, receiving Freiburg in the settlement with the Emperor (5 February 1679).

Significance

Nijmegen marked the height of Louis XIV's early expansion and confirmed France as the dominant continental power. The treaties left the Dutch Republic intact but exhausted, and the territorial changes in Flanders shaped the Franco-Spanish border until the Revolution. The "reunions" policy that followed soon renewed conflict, culminating in the Nine Years' War.
