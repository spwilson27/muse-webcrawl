Imperial State Crown

The Imperial State Crown is one of the Crown Jewels of the United Kingdom. It is worn by the monarch when leaving Westminster Abbey at the end of a coronation, and at formal occasions such as the State Opening of Parliament. The present crown was made in 1937 by Garrard & Co. for George VI, based on the 1838 crown made for Queen Victoria.

Description

The crown has an openwork gold frame in the form of four crosses pattée and four fleurs-de-lis, supporting two arches topped by a monde and cross pattée. It is set with 2,868 diamonds, 269 pearls, 17 sapphires, 11 emeralds, and 5 rubies. Famous stones mounted in it include:

  • The Cullinan II diamond (317.4 carats), the second-largest stone cut from the Cullinan diamond, set in the front.
  • The Black Prince's Ruby (in fact a spinel), set in the cross at the front.
  • The Stuart Sapphire, now at the back, and St Edward's Sapphire, said to have belonged to Edward the Confessor, in the upper cross.

Use and custody

Except when in use, the crown is kept with the other Crown Jewels in the Jewel House at the Tower of London. Because of its weight (about 1.06 kg), some monarchs, including Queen Victoria and Elizabeth II, have at times had the crown carried rather than worn.
