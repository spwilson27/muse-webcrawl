Battle of Breitenfeld (1631)

Battle of Breitenfeld
Date: 17 September 1631 (Gregorian)
Location: Breitenfeld, near Leipzig, Saxony
Result: Swedish–Saxon victory
Part of the Thirty Years' War

The Battle of Breitenfeld, fought on 17 September 1631, was the first major victory of the Protestant forces in the Thirty Years' War. The Swedish army of King Gustavus Adolphus, allied with Saxony, destroyed the Imperial and Catholic League army commanded by Johann Tserclaes, Count of Tilly, on a plain north of Leipzig.

Background

Gustavus Adolphus had landed in Germany in 1630 to resist Imperial expansion and defend Protestantism after the sack of Magdeburg shocked Europe. Reluctantly joined by the Elector of Saxony, John George I, whose territory Tilly was invading, the combined army of about 36,000 faced roughly 31,000 Imperial troops.

The battle

The Imperial cavalry of Gottfried zu Pappenheim attacked prematurely on the Imperial left and was repulsed by Swedish musketeers interspersed with light cannon and cavalry reserves. On the other flank the Saxon army broke and fled, exposing the Swedish left; Gustavus's second line refused its flank and held. Swedish artillery and mobile infantry firepower then ground down Tilly's dense infantry tercios, and a final Swedish cavalry charge, capturing the Imperial guns and turning them on their former owners, completed the rout. Tilly lost perhaps 13,000 men and all his artillery; he was himself wounded.

Significance

Breitenfeld demonstrated the effectiveness of the Swedish system — linear formations, combined arms, and mobile regimental artillery — and transformed the war, restoring the Protestant cause and making Gustavus Adolphus the dominant captain in Germany for the following year.
