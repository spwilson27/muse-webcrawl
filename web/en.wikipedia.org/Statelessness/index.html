Statelessness is the condition of a person who is "not considered as a national by any State under the operation of its law", in the definition of the 1954 Convention Relating to the Status of Stateless Persons. A stateless person may also be called a person of no nationality; some stateless people are also refugees, though not all asylum seekers are stateless and not all stateless persons are refugees.

Causes

Statelessness arises from gaps and conflicts in nationality laws — for example where citizenship is passed only by descent (jus sanguinis) in one country and only by birthplace (jus soli) in another — from gender-discriminatory laws preventing mothers from transmitting nationality, from the dissolution or succession of states, from discriminatory denationalisation of minorities, and from failure to register births. Notable affected populations have included the Rohingya of Myanmar, the Bidoon of the Gulf states, and ethnic minorities stripped of citizenship by discriminatory legislation.

Consequences

Without nationality, people may be denied identity documents, education, healthcare, legal employment, freedom of movement, the right to marry, and political participation, and may face detention and exploitation.

International law

The 1954 Convention defines the status and minimum rights of stateless persons; the 1961 Convention on the Reduction of Statelessness requires states to grant nationality to children born on their territory who would otherwise be stateless. The UN refugee agency, UNHCR, has a global mandate to identify, prevent and reduce statelessness and protect stateless people, running the #IBelong campaign launched in 2014.
