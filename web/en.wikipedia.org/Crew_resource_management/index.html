Crew resource management

Crew resource management or cockpit resource management (CRM) is a set of training procedures for use in environments where human error can have devastating effects. It is used primarily for improving aviation safety and focuses on interpersonal communication, leadership, and decision making in aircraft cockpits or flight decks. Its inventor is generally considered to be John Lauber, a research psychologist at NASA, who identified failures of crew coordination as a major cause of air accidents.

History

CRM grew out of the 1977 Tenerife airport disaster, in which two Boeing 747s collided on the runway, killing 583 people; the accident investigation highlighted the failure of the first officer to effectively challenge the captain's decision to take off. United Airlines Flight 173 (1978), which crashed after running out of fuel while the crew fixated on a landing-gear problem, and the NASA workshop of 1979, "Resource Management on the Flightdeck", gave further impetus. United Airlines became the first major airline to introduce a comprehensive CRM programme, in 1981. By the 1990s CRM training had become a global standard in commercial aviation.

Training themes

CRM training addresses communication, situational awareness, problem solving, decision making, and teamwork, together with the interplay between these skills. It encourages junior crew members to speak up assertively about errors or concerns and captains to actively solicit input, flattening the steep authority gradient that had discouraged challenge in traditional cockpit culture. Training methods include classroom instruction, case studies of accidents, and practical exercises in simulators during line-oriented flight training (LOFT).

Adoption in other fields

CRM principles have been adapted by other high-risk industries, including medicine (where it is often called crisis resource management), maritime transport (bridge and engine-room resource management), firefighting, offshore oil and gas, and nuclear power. In healthcare, CRM-style training underpins team training programmes for operating theatres and emergency departments.
