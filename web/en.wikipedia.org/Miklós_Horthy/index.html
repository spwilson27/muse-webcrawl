Miklós Horthy de Nagybánya (18 June 1868 – 9 February 1957) was a Hungarian admiral and statesman who served as Regent of the Kingdom of Hungary from 1920 to 1944. A former commander-in-chief of the Austro-Hungarian Navy, he led the counter-revolutionary forces that overthrew the Hungarian Soviet Republic and presided over an authoritarian, revisionist regime aligned first with Fascist Italy and then with Nazi Germany.

Regency

Horthy entered Budapest in November 1919 at the head of the National Army, during the "White Terror" against communists and Jews. Elected regent in March 1920, he governed a kingdom without a king, seeking reversal of the Treaty of Trianon, which had stripped Hungary of two-thirds of its territory. The Vienna Awards of 1938 and 1940 returned some lands with German and Italian backing.

World War II

Hungary joined the Axis invasion of Yugoslavia and the Soviet Union in 1941. Horthy resisted German demands to deport Hungary's Jews until Germany occupied the country in March 1944; over the following months some 440,000 Hungarian Jews were deported, most to Auschwitz. Horthy halted the deportations in July 1944, but after his attempt to announce an armistice that October, Germany installed the Arrow Cross regime and forced his abdication.

Later life

Horthy was never tried as a war criminal; he testified at Nuremberg and lived in exile in Portugal, where he died in 1957. His 1993 reburial in Hungary and his historical role — balancing collaboration with the Axis against efforts to limit its demands — remain subjects of political controversy.
