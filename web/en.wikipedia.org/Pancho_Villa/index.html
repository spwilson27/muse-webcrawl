Pancho Villa

Francisco "Pancho" Villa (born José Doroteo Arango Arámbula; 5 June 1878 – 20 July 1923) was a Mexican revolutionary general and one of the most prominent figures of the Mexican Revolution. As commander of the División del Norte (Division of the North) in the Constitutional Army, he was a decisive military factor in the defeat of the government of Victoriano Huerta.

Early life

Villa was born in Durango to poor sharecropper parents. By his own account, he became an outlaw at sixteen after killing a hacienda official who had assaulted his sister, and spent years as a bandit and cattle rustler before joining Francisco Madero's uprising against Porfirio Díaz in 1910.

Revolutionary career

Villa rose to command the División del Norte, at its height some 40,000 men, winning famous victories at Ciudad Juárez, Torreón, and Zacatecas (1914). After the split with Venustiano Carranza, his forces were defeated by Álvaro Obregón at Celaya in 1915. In March 1916 Villa raided Columbus, New Mexico, killing American civilians and soldiers; the United States responded with the Punitive Expedition under General John J. Pershing, which pursued Villa through Chihuahua for nearly a year without capturing him.

Retirement and death

In 1920 Villa accepted amnesty from the interim government and retired to the hacienda of Canutillo in Durango. He was assassinated in Parral, Chihuahua, on 20 July 1923, ambushed while riding in his automobile. He remains a folk hero in northern Mexico and a controversial figure internationally.
