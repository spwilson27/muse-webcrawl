Fezzan

Fezzan (Arabic: فزان, Fazzān) is a historical region of southwestern Libya, in the Sahara Desert. Together with Tripolitania in the north and Cyrenaica in the east, it forms one of the three traditional regions of Libya. Its principal town is Sabha; other oases include Murzuk, Ghat, and Ubari.

Geography

The Fezzan is a largely hyper-arid basin of sand seas (ergs), gravel plains, and volcanic mountains such as the Haruj al-Aswad, dotted with oasis settlements sustained by groundwater. The Wadi Irawan and Wadi al Shatii run along its northern margin. Rock art and lake deposits attest to a much wetter climate in prehistoric times, when the region supported the Garamantian civilization.

History

In antiquity the Fezzan was the land of the Garamantes, whose capital was Garama (Germa) and who traded across the Sahara. It later came under Roman influence, then Arab, Ottoman, and — in the early 20th century — Italian colonial rule; French forces occupied parts of it during the Second World War and administered the Fezzan under a separate military government until Libyan independence in 1951. Trans-Saharan trade in gold, slaves, and salt historically passed through its oases.
