George Washington Vanderbilt II (November 14, 1862 – March 6, 1914) was an American art collector and member of the prominent Vanderbilt family, which amassed a huge fortune through steamboats, railroads and various business enterprises. He built Biltmore Estate near Asheville, North Carolina, the largest privately owned house in the United States.

Biltmore House (completed 1895), Vanderbilt's 250-room château in the Blue Ridge Mountains.

Life

The youngest son of William Henry Vanderbilt, George was a scholarly, retiring heir who read eight languages, collected art, books and objects on a vast scale, and travelled widely. Unlike his brothers, he took little part in the family's railroad businesses. In 1898 he married Edith Stuyvesant Dresser; their daughter Cornelia was born at Biltmore in 1900.

Biltmore

Beginning in 1888–89, Vanderbilt bought some 125,000 acres around Asheville and commissioned architect Richard Morris Hunt to build a 250-room French Renaissance château (completed 1895), with grounds landscaped by Frederick Law Olmsted and forestry directed first by Gifford Pinchot and then Carl A. Schenck — the estate's forests became a cradle of American scientific forestry. After Vanderbilt's sudden death in 1914 from complications of an appendectomy, his widow sold much of the land, forming the core of Pisgah National Forest. Biltmore House, opened to the public in 1930, remains owned by descendants and is a major tourist attraction and National Historic Landmark.
