E-learning

E-learning (short for electronic learning) is the use of information and communication technologies—especially computers and the internet—to support learning and teaching. It includes computer-based training, web-based training, online courses, virtual classrooms, digital collaboration and mobile learning. E-learning can be synchronous (live instruction in virtual classrooms) or asynchronous (self-paced study using recorded lectures, readings and discussion forums), and is often combined with face-to-face teaching in blended learning approaches.

History

Computer-assisted instruction dates to systems such as PLATO in the 1960s and mainframe-based teaching machines. The spread of personal computers in the 1980s and 1990s produced CD-ROM courseware, and the World Wide Web enabled learning management systems such as Moodle, Blackboard and Canvas. In 2012, massive open online courses (MOOCs) from providers including Coursera, edX and Udacity brought free university-level courses to millions of learners. The COVID-19 pandemic in 2020 forced schools and universities worldwide into emergency remote teaching, greatly accelerating adoption.

Applications and effectiveness

E-learning is used in primary, secondary and higher education, corporate training and compliance, professional development, and informal self-study through platforms such as Khan Academy and Duolingo. Research suggests well-designed online learning can be as effective as classroom instruction, with blended learning often performing best; outcomes depend on course design, learner motivation, feedback and interaction rather than technology alone. Criticisms include high dropout rates in MOOCs, lack of social interaction, academic integrity concerns, and unequal access to devices and broadband.

Technologies

Core technologies include learning management systems, videoconferencing tools, virtual learning environments, SCORM and xAPI content standards, adaptive learning software and, increasingly, artificial-intelligence tutors and automated assessment.
