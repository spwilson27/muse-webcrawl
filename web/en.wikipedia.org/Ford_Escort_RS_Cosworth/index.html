Ford Escort RS Cosworth

The Ford Escort RS Cosworth is a homologation special of the fifth-generation Ford Escort, produced from 1992 to 1996 to allow Ford to compete in the World Rally Championship. Despite its name and general appearance, it was built on the floorpan of the four-wheel-drive Sierra RS Cosworth rather than the standard Escort, with longitudinally mounted engine and all-wheel drive.

Specification

The car was powered by a 2.0-litre turbocharged Cosworth YBT engine producing about 224 horsepower in road trim, driving all four wheels through a five-speed manual gearbox with a 34/66 front-rear torque split. It is most recognisable by its large double-deck rear spoiler — the "whale tail" — which, along with wheel-arch extensions, distinguished it from ordinary Escorts. Roughly 7,145 examples were built, the first 2,500 with a larger turbocharger to satisfy homologation rules.

Motorsport

In Group A rallying, the Escort RS Cosworth won numerous World Championship rallies in the hands of drivers such as François Delecour, Miki Biasion and Carlos Sainz, though it never secured a manufacturers' title before being replaced by the Escort WRC in 1997. It has since become a sought-after modern classic.
