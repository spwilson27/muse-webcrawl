Economic status

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Households are commonly grouped into income quintiles when economic status is measured.

Economic status refers to the position of an individual, household or group within the economic structure of a society, typically assessed through income, wealth, occupation and access to goods and services. It is a core component of socioeconomic status (SES), alongside education and occupation, and is widely used in sociology, demography, public health and market research.

Measurement

Indicators of economic status include gross and disposable income, net worth and asset ownership, housing tenure, and the ability to meet expenses or withstand financial shocks. Researchers often group populations into quintiles or deciles by income, or classify households by consumption expenditure, which can be a more stable measure in developing economies.

Relationship to health and outcomes

Economic status is strongly associated with life expectancy, educational attainment, housing quality and exposure to environmental hazards. The Whitehall studies of British civil servants, led by Michael Marmot, documented a gradient in mortality across employment grades even among people who were all employed, suggesting that status itself, not only material deprivation, affects health.

Mobility

Economic mobility describes movement between economic statuses across a lifetime or between generations. Intergenerational mobility varies considerably between countries and is a frequent subject of policy debate.
