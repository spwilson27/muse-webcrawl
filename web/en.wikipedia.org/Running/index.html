Running

Running is a gait in which both feet regularly leave the ground.

Running is a method of terrestrial locomotion by which humans and other animals move rapidly on foot. Running is a gait with an aerial phase in which all feet are above the ground (though there are exceptions), in contrast to walking, where one foot is always in contact with the ground. The term running can refer to a variety of speeds ranging from jogging to sprinting.

History

Running is thought to have evolved about 2.6 million years ago in the genus Homo, possibly as an adaptation for persistence hunting and scavenging. Competitive running grew out of religious festivals in various areas, and races were held at the ancient Olympic Games in Greece from 776 BCE. In the modern era, running became a mass participation activity in the 1970s jogging boom.

Biomechanics

Running gait is divided into stance and swing phases. Unlike walking, running includes a flight phase when neither foot touches the ground. Runners strike the ground with forces several times their body weight, and the legs act like springs, storing and returning elastic energy in tendons such as the Achilles tendon. Foot strike patterns — heel, midfoot, and forefoot — vary between individuals and speeds.

Competitive running

Running events in track and field range from sprints (60 m to 400 m) through middle-distance (800 m, 1500 m) and long-distance events (5000 m, 10,000 m) to the marathon (42.195 km). Road running, cross country, trail running, and ultramarathons extend the sport beyond the track. World records are ratified by World Athletics.

Health effects

Regular running improves cardiovascular fitness, helps maintain a healthy weight, and is associated with reduced risk of cardiovascular disease and improved mood, sometimes described as a "runner's high". However, running also carries a risk of overuse injuries, including runner's knee, shin splints, Achilles tendinopathy, and stress fractures.
