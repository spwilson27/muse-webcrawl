Mundra Port

Location: Mundra, Kutch district, Gujarat, India
Operated by: Adani Ports and SEZ
Opened: 2001
Annual cargo: Over 150 million tonnes (largest commercial port in India)

Mundra Port is a deep-water, all-weather port on the northern shore of the Gulf of Kutch near the town of Mundra in Gujarat, western India. Operated by Adani Ports and Special Economic Zone Limited (APSEZ), it is India's largest commercial port by cargo volume and the flagship of the Adani ports business.

Development

The port was developed from the late 1990s under a concession from the Gujarat Maritime Board, with operations beginning in 2001. Its natural deep draft — among the deepest of any Indian port — allows it to berth fully laden Capesize vessels, the largest bulk carriers in commercial service. Growth was rapid: Mundra overtook government-run ports such as Kandla (Deendayal Port) to become India's busiest port overall in the 2010s, and it was the first Indian port to handle 100 million tonnes of cargo in a single year.

Facilities

Mundra handles containers, dry bulk, liquid cargo, crude oil, automobiles and break-bulk cargo across multiple terminals and berths. It includes one of India's largest container terminals, a single-point mooring for very large crude carriers serving nearby refineries, bulk terminals for coal, fertiliser and grain, and extensive rail and road links to the north Indian hinterland. The port anchors the Mundra Special Economic Zone, which hosts power plants — including the Mundra thermal power station — manufacturing and logistics facilities.

Significance

The port's expansion under the Adani Group, controlled by Gautam Adani, made it a central asset in India's port-led development and a focus of debate over private infrastructure, environmental clearances for its coastal development, and the political economy of large conglomerates in India.
