Cut-up technique

The cut-up technique is a literary method in which a written text is physically cut up and rearranged to create a new text. The concept can be traced to the Dadaists of the 1920s — Tristan Tzara described making a poem by drawing cut-out words from a hat — but it was developed and popularized in the 1950s by painter Brion Gysin and writer William S. Burroughs.

Development

Gysin rediscovered the method in 1959 while cutting a mount for drawings, slicing through layers of newspaper beneath; Burroughs adopted it enthusiastically and used cut-ups in The Soft Machine, The Ticket That Exploded, and Nova Express (the Nova Trilogy). The related "fold-in" method folds a page of text over another so lines interleave. Burroughs argued the technique broke the control of language and revealed hidden meanings: "When you cut into the present the future leaks out."

Influence

Cut-up influenced experimental music and songwriting: David Bowie used it for lyrics, as did Thom Yorke for Radiohead's Kid A; it anticipates sampling and remix culture. Kurt Cobain and Genesis P-Orridge also employed the method. Digital text randomizers now automate the process.
