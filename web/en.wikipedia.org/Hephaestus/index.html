Hephaestus (Greek: Ἥφαιστος) is the Greek god of artisans, blacksmiths, metalworking, carpenters, craftsmen, fire, and volcanoes. Unlike the other Olympians he was depicted as lame and ungainly — by some accounts thrown from Olympus by Hera (or by Zeus), falling for a day to the island of Lemnos, which became a centre of his cult.

Mythology

In his forge beneath the earth, assisted by the Cyclopes, Hephaestus crafted the gods' finest artefacts: the armour of Achilles described at length in the Iliad, Hermes' winged helmet and sandals, Apollo's and Artemis's bows, Zeus's aegis and thunderbolts, and the first woman, Pandora, moulded from earth and water at Zeus's command. Married to Aphrodite, he famously trapped her and her lover Ares in an unbreakable golden net and summoned the gods to witness their shame.

Cult

Hephaestus was worshipped wherever metalworking flourished. His temple in Athens, the Hephaisteion, overlooking the Agora, is among the best-preserved Greek temples. The Chalceia and Hephaestia festivals honoured him alongside Athena, patroness of crafts. His Roman equivalent is Vulcan.
