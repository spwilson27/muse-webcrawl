Northern Fleet

Northern Fleet
Country: Russia (formerly Soviet Union)
Branch: Russian Navy
Headquarters: Severomorsk, Murmansk Oblast
Founded: 1933 (as Northern Flotilla)
Role: Arctic and Atlantic naval operations; strategic submarine deterrent

The Northern Fleet (Russian: Северный флот, Severny flot) is the Russian Navy's fleet in the Arctic Ocean and northwestern Russia, headquartered at Severomorsk on the Kola Peninsula. It is the most powerful of Russia's fleets and operates the bulk of its nuclear-powered submarines, including the ballistic missile submarines that form a key leg of Russia's nuclear deterrent.

History

The fleet traces its origins to the Northern Flotilla formed in 1933. During the Second World War it protected the Arctic convoys that brought Allied supplies to Murmansk and Arkhangelsk. In the Cold War it expanded enormously, becoming the principal Soviet fleet, with huge bases along the ice-free Barents Sea coast made accessible by the warm North Atlantic Current.

Composition

Major units have included the aircraft carrier Admiral Kuznetsov, the Kirov-class battlecruiser Pyotr Velikiy, Borei-class ballistic missile submarines, and Yasen-class attack submarines, supported by naval infantry and naval aviation. The fleet's importance has grown with Russia's strategic focus on the Arctic and the Northern Sea Route.

Incidents

The fleet suffered the loss of the submarine Kursk with all hands in the Barents Sea in August 2000, an event that exposed serious shortcomings in its rescue capability and drew international attention.
