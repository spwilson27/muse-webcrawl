Cornish–Fisher expansion

The Cornish–Fisher expansion is an asymptotic expansion used in statistics to approximate the quantiles of a probability distribution in terms of its cumulants. It generalises the corresponding quantile of the normal distribution by correcting for skewness and kurtosis.

Formulation

If a standardised random variable has skewness γ₁ and excess kurtosis γ₂, then its p-quantile can be approximated from the standard normal quantile z by a polynomial in z whose coefficients are built from γ₁ and γ₂. The first correction adjusts for skewness (making one tail longer than the other), and the second for kurtosis (making both tails heavier or lighter).

Applications

The expansion is widely used in finance to compute modified value at risk (VaR), where asset returns are skewed and fat-tailed, so that a normal-distribution VaR would understate risk. It is also used in quality control and in constructing improved confidence intervals. Its accuracy deteriorates for extreme tail probabilities and very non-normal distributions.

The expansion is named after E. A. Cornish and R. A. Fisher, who published it in 1937.
