Lake Nemi (Italian: Lago di Nemi; Latin: Nemorensis Lacus) is a small circular volcanic lake in the Lazio region of Italy, about 30 km (19 mi) south of Rome. It occupies a crater in the Alban Hills and takes its name from the town of Nemi on its rim. In antiquity it was sacred to the goddess Diana, whose great sanctuary stood on its northern shore.

Sanctuary of Diana

The Temple of Diana Nemorensis ("Diana of the Wood") was one of the most famous sanctuaries of ancient Italy. Its priest, the Rex Nemorensis ("King of the Wood"), was a runaway slave who held office until another slave killed him in single combat after plucking a bough from a sacred tree — the "Golden Bough" that opens the Aeneid and inspired James Frazer's The Golden Bough (1890).

Nemi ships

Two enormous ceremonial ships built under the emperor Caligula lay on the lakebed for nearly two thousand years. Rumours of the wrecks circulated from the Renaissance, and in 1929–1932, under Mussolini, the lake was partially drained and both hulls — floating palaces over 70 metres long, with marble, mosaics, and lead-sheathed timbers — were recovered and housed in a museum. The museum and ships were destroyed by fire in 1944 during the Second World War; surviving artefacts are kept in Rome.

The lake today

Nemi is noted for its wild strawberries, celebrated in an annual festival, and the surrounding Castelli Romani hill towns. The lake's level was already regulated in Roman times by an emissary tunnel bored through the crater rim.
