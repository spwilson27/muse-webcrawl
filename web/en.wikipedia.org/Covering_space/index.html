Covering space

In topology, a covering or covering projection is a map between topological spaces that, intuitively, locally looks like a projection of a product. A space that covers another is a covering space.

Definition

A continuous surjective map p: E → X is a covering if every point of X has a neighbourhood evenly covered by p. Classic examples: the real line covers the circle via the exponential map, and the universal cover of a space is simply connected.

Theory

Coverings correspond to subgroups of the fundamental group, via the Galois correspondence of covering spaces, and are central to algebraic topology.
