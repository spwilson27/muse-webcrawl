France Gall

France Gall (1947–2018), winner of the Eurovision Song Contest 1965.

France Gall (born Isabelle Geneviève Marie Anne Gall; 9 October 1947 – 7 January 2018) was a French singer. She rose to fame as a teenage star of the yé-yé movement and won the Eurovision Song Contest in 1965, then reinvented herself in the 1970s and 1980s in collaboration with her husband, the singer-songwriter Michel Berger, becoming one of France's most beloved performers.

Early career and Eurovision

Born in Paris into a musical family — her father Robert Gall wrote songs for Édith Piaf and Charles Aznavour — she recorded her first single at 16. In 1965, representing Luxembourg at the Eurovision Song Contest in Naples, she won with Serge Gainsbourg's "Poupée de cire, poupée de son," one of the first up-tempo pop songs to win the contest and an international hit. Gainsbourg also wrote her controversial 1966 hit "Les sucettes," whose double meaning she later said she had not understood at the time.

Collaboration with Michel Berger

After a period of German-language recordings, Gall's career was transformed by Michel Berger, whom she married in 1976. Berger wrote and produced a string of albums for her, including France Gall (1976), Paris, France (1980, with "Il jouait du piano debout"), Tout pour la musique (1981), Débranche! (1984) and Babacar (1987), whose title track reflected her humanitarian work in Africa. Their joint single "Ella, elle l'a" (1987), a tribute to Ella Fitzgerald, topped charts across Europe. She also starred in Berger and Luc Plamondon's rock opera Starmania.

Later life and legacy

After Berger's sudden death in 1992 and the death of their daughter Pauline from cystic fibrosis in 1997, Gall largely withdrew from public life, returning in 2015 with the musical Résiste, built around the couple's songs. She died of cancer in Neuilly-sur-Seine on 7 January 2018, aged 70, and was widely mourned in France.
