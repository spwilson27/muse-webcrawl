Elaine Chao

Elaine Lan Chao (born March 26, 1953) is an American businesswoman and former government official who served as U.S. Secretary of Transportation (2017–2021) and Secretary of Labor (2001–2009).

Elaine Lan Chao (born March 26, 1953) is an American businesswoman and former government official. She served as the United States secretary of transportation in the administration of President Donald Trump from 2017 to 2021, and as the secretary of labor under President George W. Bush from 2001 to 2009 — the longest-serving labor secretary since the Second World War. She was the first Asian American woman appointed to a U.S. president's cabinet.

Early life and education

Chao was born in Taipei, Taiwan, to a family with roots in Shanghai; her father, James S. C. Chao, founded the shipping company Foremost Group. She immigrated to the United States at age eight. She earned a bachelor's degree in economics from Mount Holyoke College and an MBA from Harvard Business School.

Career

Chao worked in international banking before entering government, serving as deputy maritime administrator, chairwoman of the Federal Maritime Commission, and deputy secretary of transportation under President George H. W. Bush. She directed the Peace Corps from 1991 to 1992 and then served as president and CEO of United Way of America before joining the Heritage Foundation as a distinguished fellow.

Cabinet service

As labor secretary under George W. Bush, Chao oversaw the department's response to the 2008 financial crisis in her final months. Confirmed as transportation secretary in January 2017, she championed infrastructure modernization and deregulation, and she resigned on January 7, 2021, following the attack on the U.S. Capitol the previous day. Chao is married to Mitch McConnell, the longtime U.S. senator from Kentucky and former Senate majority leader.
