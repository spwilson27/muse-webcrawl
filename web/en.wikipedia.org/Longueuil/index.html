Longueuil
City in Montérégie, Quebec, Canada
Founded: 1657
Population: approx. 254,000 (2021)
Boroughs: Le Vieux-Longueuil, Saint-Hubert, Greenfield Park

Longueuil is a city in the province of Quebec, Canada, on the south shore of the Saint Lawrence River opposite Montreal. It is the seat of the Montérégie administrative region and one of the largest suburbs of Montreal, forming part of its metropolitan community.

History

Charles Le Moyne founded the seigneury in 1657, naming it after a village in his native Normandy region near Dieppe. The modern city was created in 2002 by amalgamating several South Shore municipalities and partly de-merged in 2006, leaving boroughs of Le Vieux-Longueuil, Saint-Hubert and Greenfield Park.

Transport and economy

Longueuil is connected to Montreal by the Jacques Cartier Bridge, the Louis-Hippolyte Lafontaine tunnel, and the Yellow line of the Montreal Metro to Longueuil–Université-de-Sherbrooke station. It hosts the headquarters of the Canadian Space Agency at the John H. Chapman Space Centre and industries including Pratt & Whitney Canada.
