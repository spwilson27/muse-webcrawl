Jotunheimen

Jotunheimen (Norwegian: [ˈjôːtʉnˌhæɪmn̩]; "the home of the Jotnar") is a mountainous area of roughly 3,500 square kilometres (1,400 sq mi) in southern Norway and is part of the long range known as the Scandinavian Mountains. The 29 highest mountains in Norway are all located in the Jotunheimen, including the very highest, Galdhøpiggen (2,469 m or 8,100 ft).

Geography

Jotunheimen straddles the border between Innlandet and Vestland counties. The area contains numerous glaciers, including parts of Jostedalsbreen to the west, and over 250 peaks rising above 2,000 metres. Notable summits include Galdhøpiggen, Glittertind, and Store Skagastølstind. Deep glacial valleys, lakes such as Gjende and Bygdin, and the famous Besseggen ridge attract large numbers of walkers.

National park

Much of the area is protected as Jotunheimen National Park, established in 1980 and covering about 1,151 square kilometres. The name Jotunheimen was coined by the poet Aasmund Olavsson Vinje in the 1860s, drawing on Norse mythology's Jötunheimr, the home of the giants. The region is a classic area for hiking, glacier walking, and ski touring, served by the cabins of the Norwegian Trekking Association (DNT).
