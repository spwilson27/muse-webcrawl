Scotland (Scots: Scotland; Scottish Gaelic: Alba) is a country in northwest Europe that is part of the United Kingdom. It covers the northern third of the island of Great Britain and shares a border with England to the south. It is surrounded by the Atlantic Ocean, the North Sea and the Irish Sea, and includes more than 790 islands, among them the Hebrides, Orkney and Shetland.

History

The Kingdom of Scotland emerged in the ninth century from the union of the Gaelic Dál Riata and the Picts, traditionally under Kenneth MacAlpin. Scotland maintained its independence through the Wars of Scottish Independence against England in the late thirteenth and early fourteenth centuries, associated with William Wallace and Robert the Bruce. The crowns of Scotland and England were united in 1603 under James VI and I, and the Acts of Union 1707 merged the two parliaments into the Kingdom of Great Britain. A devolved Scottish Parliament was re-established in 1999 following a referendum.

Geography

Scotland's geography ranges from the rugged Highlands and the Southern Uplands to the fertile Central Belt, where most of the population lives. The country contains the highest peak in the British Isles, Ben Nevis (1,345 m), and some 31,000 freshwater lochs, most famously Loch Ness and Loch Lomond.

Government and law

The Scottish Parliament at Holyrood in Edinburgh legislates on devolved matters including health, education and justice. Scotland has always retained its own legal system, Scots law, as well as its own established church, the Church of Scotland, and distinct education traditions.

Culture

Scotland is known for its contributions to the Enlightenment — through figures such as David Hume and Adam Smith — and to engineering and invention. National symbols include the thistle, the Saltire flag of St Andrew, tartan, whisky, and the music of the bagpipes.
