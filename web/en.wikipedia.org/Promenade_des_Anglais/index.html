The Promenade des Anglais (Niçard: Camin deis Anglés) is a famous promenade along the Mediterranean waterfront of Nice, on the French Riviera. It stretches about 7 km along the Baie des Anges, from the airport in the west to the Quai des États-Unis and the Colline du Château in the east, and is lined with palm trees, grand hotels and the city's pebble beaches. Locals often call it simply "la Prom".

History

In the early 19th century Nice became a fashionable winter resort for the English aristocracy. In 1822 the English clergyman Lewis Way proposed employing destitute workers to build a walkway along the shore; funded largely by English residents, it was called the Chemin des Anglais ("the English path") by the Niçois. The promenade was widened and extended as Nice grew after annexation by France in 1860, and it became the centrepiece of the resort city, crowned by hotels such as the Hôtel Negresco (1913). Its rows of blue chairs, installed from the 20th century, are an icon of the city. In 2021 the promenade formed part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing of Nice as a "winter resort town of the Riviera".

2016 attack

On the evening of 14 July 2016, during Bastille Day celebrations, a cargo truck was deliberately driven into crowds on the promenade, killing 86 people and injuring hundreds before the driver was shot dead by police. A memorial to the victims stands near the site, and the anniversary is marked annually in Nice.

Present day

The promenade hosts the Nice Carnival's flower battles, the finish of the Paris–Nice cycle race, and daily streams of walkers, joggers and rollerbladers. A cycle path and reconfigured traffic lanes were added in the 2010s–2020s to give more space to pedestrians.
