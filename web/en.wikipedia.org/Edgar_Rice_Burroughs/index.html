Edgar Rice Burroughs

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Burroughs created Tarzan and the Martian world of Barsoom.

Edgar Rice Burroughs (September 1, 1875 – March 19, 1950) was an American speculative fiction writer, best known for his prolific output in the adventure, science fiction and fantasy genres. His most enduring creations are the jungle hero Tarzan, introduced in Tarzan of the Apes (1912), and John Carter, the earthman transported to Mars ("Barsoom") in A Princess of Mars (serialised 1912).

Career

After disappointing ventures including cavalry service, ranching and pencil-sharpener sales, Burroughs began writing at 35, producing stories for the pulp magazines. Tarzan made him famous and wealthy: the character appeared in 24 of his novels, films, comics and merchandise. Burroughs bought a ranch near Los Angeles which he named Tarzana; the surrounding community later adopted the name.

Other series

Beyond Tarzan and Barsoom, he wrote the Pellucidar novels set in a hollow Earth, the Venus (Amtor) series, the Caspak trilogy, westerns and historical fiction — some 70 novels in all. His fiction helped define the planetary romance and sword-and-planet subgenres.

Later life

During the Second World War, in his late sixties, Burroughs served as a war correspondent in the Pacific, among the oldest of the conflict. He died of a heart attack in Encino, California, in 1950. His works fell into public controversy over their racial attitudes, but their influence on adventure and science fiction — from Ray Bradbury to James Cameron's Avatar — is widely acknowledged.
