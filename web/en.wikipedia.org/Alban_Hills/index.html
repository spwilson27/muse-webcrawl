The Alban Hills (Italian: Colli Albani) are a volcanic caldera complex in the region of Lazio, Italy, about 20 kilometres (12 mi) southeast of Rome. The hills form the remnant of an ancient volcano, whose eruptions helped shape the geology of the Roman Campagna. The highest peak is Monte Cavo at 949 metres (3,114 ft).

Geology

The Alban Hills volcano was active from roughly 600,000 to 20,000 years ago. Its eruptions produced vast deposits of tuff and peperino stone used in Roman building. Two crater lakes occupy the caldera: Lake Albano and Lake Nemi. The volcano is considered dormant rather than extinct; minor seismic activity and gas emissions still occur, and the Alban Hills fault system occasionally produces small earthquakes.

History

In antiquity the hills were sacred to Jupiter Latiaris, whose temple stood on Monte Cavo, and were home to the Latin city of Alba Longa, legendary mother city of Rome. Roman patricians built villas here to escape the summer heat of the city, a tradition continued by Renaissance popes and cardinals. The papal summer residence at Castel Gandolfo, overlooking Lake Albano, remains in use.

Towns

The hill towns collectively known as the Castelli Romani include Frascati, Marino, Rocca di Papa, Ariccia, Genzano di Roma and Nemi. They are noted for their white wines — especially Frascati DOC — and for porchetta, the slow-roasted pork of Ariccia. The area is protected as the Parco Regionale dei Castelli Romani.
