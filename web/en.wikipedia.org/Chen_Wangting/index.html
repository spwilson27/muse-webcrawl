Chen Wangting (1580–1660) was a Ming dynasty military officer from Chenjiagou village in Wen County, Henan, who is traditionally credited with creating the earliest form of the Chen family martial art from which tai chi developed.

Life

Chen served as a garrison commander in Shandong in the final decades of the Ming dynasty and was renowned for campaigns against bandits. After the Ming fell in 1644, he retired to Chenjiagou, where family genealogies say he taught martial arts combining military weapons techniques and empty-hand methods.

Creation of the art

According to Chen family records, Chen Wangting synthesized existing local boxing with principles from the military manual of General Qi Jiguang, Taoist daoyin breathing and health practices, and the theory of yin and yang and meridian channels. He is said to have created several routines including long fist and cannon fist sets, along with push-hands exercises.

Historical debate

While the Chen tradition unanimously credits Chen Wangting, some modern historians have proposed competing origins for tai chi, including transmission from the Wudang monk Zhang Sanfeng. Most scholarly accounts treat the mid-17th-century Chenjiagou synthesis as the earliest documentable origin of the art, with the name "taijiquan" itself appearing only in the 19th century.
