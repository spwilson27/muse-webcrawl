Harry Caray

Harry Caray (born Harry Christopher Carabina; March 1, 1914 – February 18, 1998) was an American radio and television sportscaster. He covered the St. Louis Cardinals (1945–1969), Oakland Athletics (1970), Chicago White Sox (1971–1981), and mostly famously the Chicago Cubs (1982–1997) of Major League Baseball. Caray was a seven-decade broadcaster whose exuberant style, trademark oversized glasses, and catchphrase "Holy cow!" made him one of the most recognizable voices in American sports.

Career

Harry Caray

Born: March 1, 1914, St. Louis, Missouri

Died: February 18, 1998 (aged 83), Rancho Mirage, California

Occupation: Sportscaster

Teams: St. Louis Cardinals; Oakland Athletics; Chicago White Sox; Chicago Cubs

Honours: Ford C. Frick Award (1989)

Born in St. Louis and orphaned young, Caray talked his way into a broadcasting job in Joliet, Illinois, before joining the St. Louis Cardinals' broadcast team in 1945. Over 25 seasons in St. Louis he developed his fan-first style, loudly celebrating Cardinals success and critiquing the team when he felt it warranted. After a dispute with ownership, he spent a single season broadcasting the Oakland Athletics in 1970, then moved to Chicago, where he spent eleven years with the White Sox, often paired with Jimmy Piersall.

In 1982 Caray joined the Chicago Cubs, whose games were carried nationally on superstation WGN-TV. His popularity soared: he became the face of afternoon baseball at Wrigley Field, leading the crowd in "Take Me Out to the Ball Game" during the seventh-inning stretch — a tradition he had begun with the White Sox under owner Bill Veeck. In 1989 he received the Ford C. Frick Award from the National Baseball Hall of Fame. He suffered a stroke in 1987 but recovered to continue broadcasting until his retirement after the 1997 season.

Legacy

Caray died in February 1998, a few weeks before he was to begin another season. His seventh-inning stretch tradition continues at Wrigley Field with guest conductors, and his grandson Chip Caray became a major-league broadcaster in his own right. His name is preserved in the Harry Caray's restaurant chain in Chicago and in a statue outside Wrigley Field.
