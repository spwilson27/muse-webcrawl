The Robert C. Byrd Green Bank Telescope (GBT) in Green Bank, West Virginia, is the world's largest fully steerable single-dish radio telescope. Its 100-metre off-axis parabolic dish, completed in 2000, replaced an earlier 300-foot telescope that had collapsed in 1988.

Design

The unblocked, off-axis aperture gives the GBT exceptional sensitivity and low interference from the telescope structure itself. Its surface consists of 2,004 panels adjusted by 2,209 actuators to maintain a precise parabolic shape. The telescope operates from about 100 MHz to 100 GHz and sits within the National Radio Quiet Zone, where radio transmissions are restricted to protect observations.

Science

The GBT is used for pulsar timing (including tests of general relativity and searches for nanohertz gravitational waves with NANOGrav), studies of interstellar molecules and star formation, radar imaging of solar system bodies, and searches for technosignatures with Breakthrough Listen. Notable results include the discovery of some of the most massive neutron stars known.
