Exponential function

In mathematics, the exponential function is the function exp(x) = ex, where e is Euler's number (approximately 2.71828). It is distinguished by being equal to its own derivative, and it models processes of exponential growth and decay throughout science, from population dynamics and radioactive decay to compound interest.

Definitions

The exponential function can be defined by the power series exp(x) = 1 + x + x²/2! + x³/3! + ⋯, as the limit of (1 + x/n)n as n tends to infinity, or as the unique solution of the differential equation y′ = y with y(0) = 1. More generally, the exponential function with base b is bx = ex ln b.

Properties

The exponential function is positive for all real inputs, strictly increasing, and satisfies the addition formula ex+y = exey. Its inverse is the natural logarithm. Extended to complex arguments, Euler's formula eix = cos x + i sin x links exponentiation to trigonometry; the famous special case eiπ + 1 = 0 is Euler's identity.

Applications

Exponential functions describe any quantity whose rate of change is proportional to its current value. They appear in the normal distribution of statistics, the solution of linear differential equations, information theory, and the analysis of algorithms, where exponential time complexity marks a practical boundary of tractability.
