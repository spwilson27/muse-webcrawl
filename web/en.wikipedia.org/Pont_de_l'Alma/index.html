The Pont de l'Alma is a road bridge over the River Seine in Paris, France. It connects the Place de l'Alma and the Avenue du Président Wilson on the Right Bank (8th and 16th arrondissements) with the Left Bank (7th arrondissement) near the Eiffel Tower. The bridge commemorates the Battle of the Alma (1854) in the Crimean War.

History

The original bridge, designed under Napoleon III, was inaugurated in 1856. Its piers carried four statues representing troops of the Crimean War: a Zouave, a grenadier, a chasseur and an artilleryman. The present bridge, a steel arch structure, was built between 1970 and 1974 because the original had settled unevenly; only the Zouave was retained and reinstalled.

The Zouave and Paris floods

The Zouave statue became the Parisians' traditional flood gauge: when the Seine rises, the water level is described by how far up the statue it reaches — feet, knees, waist. During the great flood of 1910 the water reached the Zouave's shoulders. The statue was repositioned in the rebuilding at a slightly different height, so modern comparisons are approximate.

Flame of Liberty and Diana memorial

At the bridge's northern end stands the Flame of Liberty, a full-scale replica of the flame of the Statue of Liberty, donated in 1989. The nearby Alma underpass is where Diana, Princess of Wales, was fatally injured in a car crash on 31 August 1997; the flame above the tunnel entrance has since become an unofficial memorial to her, covered with tributes and messages.
