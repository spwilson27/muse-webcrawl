M/M/1 queue

In queueing theory, a discipline within the mathematical theory of probability, an M/M/1 queue is a queue model where arrivals follow a Poisson process, service times have an exponential distribution, and there is a single server. The model name is written in Kendall's notation: the two M's stand for "Markovian" (memoryless) arrival and service processes, and the 1 denotes one server.

Definition

Customers arrive at rate λ according to a Poisson process and are served first-come, first-served by a single server whose service times are exponentially distributed with rate parameter μ. The traffic intensity is ρ = λ/μ, and a stationary distribution exists only when ρ < 1. The process of the number of customers in the system is a birth–death process.

Stationary behaviour

In steady state, the probability that there are n customers in the system is P(n) = (1 − ρ)ρⁿ. The mean number of customers in the system is ρ/(1 − ρ), the mean number in the queue (excluding the one in service) is ρ²/(1 − ρ), and by Little's law the mean time spent in the system is 1/(μ − λ). The response time is exponentially distributed with rate μ − λ.

Applications

The M/M/1 queue is the simplest non-trivial queueing model and a building block for analysing buffers, routers, and service systems. Variants include the M/M/1/K queue with finite capacity K, the M/M/c queue with c servers, and processor-sharing service disciplines.
