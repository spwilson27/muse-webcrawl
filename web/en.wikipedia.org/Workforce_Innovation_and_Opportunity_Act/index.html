Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act

The Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act (WIOA) is a United States federal law, enacted on July 22, 2014, that governs federal workforce development programs. It reauthorized and reformed the Workforce Investment Act of 1998, funding job training, adult education, and vocational rehabilitation services delivered through a nationwide network of American Job Centers (one-stop career centers).

EnactedJuly 22, 2014 (signed by President Barack Obama)
Administered byU.S. Department of Labor; Department of Education
ReplacedWorkforce Investment Act of 1998
Major programsTitle I Adult/Dislocated Worker/Youth; Title II Adult Education; Title III Wagner-Peyser; Title IV Vocational Rehabilitation

Provisions

WIOA's Title I funds training for adults, dislocated workers, and youth, emphasizing industry-recognized credentials and career pathways. Title II supports adult education and literacy, including English-language instruction. Title III amends the Wagner-Peyser Act governing the public employment service. Title IV modernizes vocational rehabilitation under the Rehabilitation Act, strengthening services for people with disabilities and their transition from school to employment.

Administration and reforms

States must submit unified workforce plans, local workforce development boards (led by business representatives) oversee one-stop centers, and common performance measures — employment, earnings, credential attainment — apply across programs. WIOA passed Congress with overwhelming bipartisan support in 2014 and took effect beginning in 2015–2016. Periodic reauthorization proposals have debated funding levels and program flexibility.
