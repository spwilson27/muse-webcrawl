The Osage language (Wažáže ie) is a Siouan language spoken historically by the Osage people (Wažáže) of the Great Plains, in what is now Oklahoma, Kansas, Missouri and Arkansas. It belongs to the Dhegihan branch of Siouan, most closely related to Kaw (Kansa), Quapaw, Omaha and Ponca. With the death of the last native speaker, Lucille Roubidoux, in 2005, Osage became a dormant language, but an active revitalisation movement by the Osage Nation is teaching it to new generations of speakers.

History

Osage was the language of a powerful Plains nation that controlled trade between the Mississippi valley and the Southwest. Through the 19th and 20th centuries, allotment, boarding-school policies that punished children for speaking Indigenous languages, and pressure to assimilate caused steep decline: by the early 2000s only a handful of elderly fluent speakers remained. Linguist Carolyn Quintero documented the language extensively with those speakers, producing a dictionary and grammar that became foundations for revitalisation.

Phonology and grammar

Osage has a rich consonant inventory with aspirated, ejective and preaspirated stops, and a five-vowel system with nasalised counterparts. Like other Siouan languages it is a head-marking, agglutinative language with subject–object–verb word order, complex pronominal prefixes on verbs, and positional auxiliary verbs distinguishing whether subjects are standing, sitting or lying — a feature characteristic of Dhegihan languages.

Writing system

Osage was historically written with adapted Latin alphabets. In 2006, Herman Mongrain Lookout developed a distinctive new Osage script for the Osage Nation language programme, designed to represent the sounds of Osage precisely; it was encoded in Unicode 9.0 in 2016 (block U+104B0–U+104FF) and is used in teaching materials, signage and digital fonts.

Revitalisation

The Osage Nation's language department (established 2003) runs immersion preschools, community and online classes, teacher training and master-apprentice programmes, and has developed apps and curricula. The language received wider attention through its use in Martin Scorsese's 2023 film Killers of the Flower Moon, for which Osage language teachers coached the cast.
