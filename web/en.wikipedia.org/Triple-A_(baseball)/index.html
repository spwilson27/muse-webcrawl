Triple-A (officially Class AAA) has been the highest level of play in Minor League Baseball in the United States since 1946. Currently, two leagues compete at the Triple-A level: the International League and the Pacific Coast League, each with 20 teams in the International League and 10 in the Pacific Coast League.

History

The Triple-A classification was created before the 1946 season; it initially included the International League, the Pacific Coast League, and the American Association, which folded after 1997. A Triple-A National Championship Game has been played since 2006 to determine a single champion between the winners of the two leagues.

Purpose

Triple-A teams are generally affiliated with a major league club and serve as the final developmental step for players moving to the majors. Rosters often mix top prospects with veterans who have major league experience and provide depth for the parent club's roster moves and rehabilitation assignments.

Playoffs

Each league holds its own postseason; the champions meet in a single-game national championship. Through 2021, the Triple-A All-Star Game pitted the best players of the two leagues against each other.
