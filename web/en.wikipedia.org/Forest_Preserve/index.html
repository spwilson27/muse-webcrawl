Forest preserve

A forest preserve is a protected area of forested land set aside by government or private conservation bodies to preserve natural ecosystems, wildlife habitat, and scenic or recreational values. Forest preserves differ from commercial forests in that timber production and development are restricted or prohibited in favour of conservation.

United States

In the United States, the term is used for several well-known protected areas. New York State's Forest Preserve, established in 1885, comprises nearly three million acres in the Adirondack and Catskill Parks, protected by a "forever wild" clause in the state constitution adopted in 1894. In Illinois, counties such as Cook and DuPage operate extensive forest preserve districts surrounding Chicago, administered by elected boards and funded by property taxes, offering trails, picnic areas, and nature centres.

Management

Forest preserves are managed to maintain biodiversity, protect watersheds, and control invasive species, sometimes using prescribed burns and habitat restoration. Public access for hiking, birdwatching, fishing, and education is usually encouraged, while hunting and extractive uses are limited or banned.
