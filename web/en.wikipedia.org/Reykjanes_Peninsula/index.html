Reykjanes Peninsula

The Reykjanes Peninsula (Icelandic: Reykjanesskagi) is a peninsula in south-west Iceland, characterised by active volcanism, lava fields, hot springs and geothermal areas. It is the surface continuation of the Mid-Atlantic Ridge, where the Eurasian and North American tectonic plates diverge. The peninsula is home to several towns, including Keflavík, Njarðvík and Grindavík, and to Keflavík International Airport, Iceland's main international airport.

Geology

Reykjanes lies on the Reykjanes Ridge segment of the Mid-Atlantic Ridge and contains several volcanic systems, including Reykjanes, Svartsengi, Fagradalsfjall, Krýsuvík, Brennisteinsfjöll and Hengill at its eastern end. Much of the peninsula is covered by young lava fields, and geothermal activity supports power stations at Svartsengi and Reykjanesvirkjun, as well as the Blue Lagoon spa, which formed in the runoff from the Svartsengi plant.

Recent eruptions

After roughly 800 years without eruptions on the peninsula, volcanic activity resumed with the Fagradalsfjall eruption beginning on 19 March 2021. Further eruptions followed in 2022 and 2023. In November 2023, intense seismic activity and ground deformation led to the evacuation of Grindavík; eruptions on the Sundhnúkur crater row began on 18 December 2023 and recurred through 2024 and 2025, with lava barriers constructed to protect the town, the Svartsengi power station and the Blue Lagoon.

Settlements and infrastructure

The largest urban area is Reykjanesbær, formed by the merger of Keflavík, Njarðvík and Hafnir. Other settlements include Grindavík, Sandgerði, Garður and Vogar. The former United States Naval Air Station Keflavik, closed in 2006, adjoins Keflavík International Airport. The Bridge Between Continents at Sandvík symbolically spans the rift between the continental plates.
