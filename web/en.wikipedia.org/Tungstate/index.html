Tungstate

In chemistry, a tungstate is a compound containing an oxyanion of tungsten or a mixed oxide containing tungsten. The simplest tungstate ion is WO42−, a tetrahedral oxoanion analogous to chromate and molybdate. Tungstates occur naturally in the minerals scheelite (calcium tungstate, CaWO4) and wolframite, the principal ores of tungsten.

Chemistry

In acidic solution, tungstate ions condense to form polyoxotungstates, a family of cluster anions such as paratungstate and metatungstate. Sodium tungstate is an important reagent and intermediate in tungsten extraction. Calcium tungstate was historically used in fluorescent screens for X-ray imaging because it emits blue light under X-ray excitation, and synthetic tungstates are used in scintillation detectors, phosphors, and catalysts.
