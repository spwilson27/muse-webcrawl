Nicopolis

Nicopolis (Greek: Νικόπολις, "City of Victory") was a Roman city in Epirus, northwestern Greece, founded by Octavian (the future Augustus) in 29 BC to commemorate his victory over Mark Antony and Cleopatra at the nearby Battle of Actium (31 BC). Its extensive ruins lie near modern Preveza.

History

Octavian populated the new city by moving inhabitants from surrounding communities and made it a free city with extensive territory. Nicopolis prospered as a commercial and cultural centre, hosting the Actian Games, and was visited by figures such as the apostle Paul, who mentions intending to winter there, and the philosopher Epictetus, who taught there after his banishment from Rome. The city declined in late antiquity after earthquakes and invasions but remained inhabited into the Byzantine period.

Archaeology

Surviving remains include city walls, an odeon, a Roman theatre, the nymphaeum, aqueduct remains and the monument of Augustus celebrating the Actium victory. A museum in Preveza displays finds from the site.
