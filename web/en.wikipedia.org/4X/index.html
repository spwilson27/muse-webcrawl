4X

4X (abbreviation of Explore, Expand, Exploit, Exterminate) is a subgenre of strategy-based computer and board games in which players control an empire and must "explore, expand, exploit, and exterminate" — exploring a map, expanding their territory, exploiting resources, and exterminating rival players.

Definition

The term was coined by Alan Emrich in a 1993 preview of Master of Orion, where he adapted the "XXXX" rating he had given the game into the four verbs. 4X games are typically distinguished from other strategy games by their combination of empire building, technology research, diplomacy and military conquest over long sessions, usually with victory conditions beyond pure annihilation.

Notable examples

The genre's landmark computer titles include Sid Meier's Civilization series, Master of Orion, Galactic Civilizations, Endless Legend, Stellaris and Age of Wonders. In board gaming, Twilight Imperium and Eclipse are prominent 4X designs. Space-based variants are sometimes called "4X space strategy" or, when emphasizing conquest, linked historically to the earlier term "space opera game".
