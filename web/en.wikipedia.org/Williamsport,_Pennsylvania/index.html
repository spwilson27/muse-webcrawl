Williamsport is a city in and the county seat of Lycoming County, Pennsylvania, United States. It lies on the West Branch Susquehanna River and had a population of about 27,700 at the 2020 census.

History

Incorporated as a borough in 1806 and as a city in 1866, Williamsport became one of the world's great lumber centres during the late 19th century: logs floated down the river were caught in the Susquehanna Boom and milled in the city's sawmills, making it, by some accounts, the city with the most millionaires per capita in America. Fourth Street's "Millionaires' Row" preserves the lumber barons' mansions. The boom collapsed with the forests' exhaustion and a devastating flood in 1889.

Little League

Williamsport is the birthplace of Little League Baseball, founded there by Carl Stotz in 1939. The Little League World Series has been held each August since 1947, at Lamade Stadium in nearby South Williamsport, drawing international teams and large crowds.
