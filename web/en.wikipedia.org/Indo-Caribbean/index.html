Indo-Caribbean

Indo-Caribbean people are people of Indian subcontinent origin — or their descendants — who live in the Caribbean and its diaspora. They are the largest ethnic group in Guyana and Suriname, the second-largest in Trinidad and Tobago, and significant minorities in Jamaica, Grenada, Saint Lucia, Martinique, Guadeloupe, and French Guiana. In North America, large Indo-Caribbean communities exist in Toronto and New York City.

History

Indo-Caribbeans descend principally from indentured labourers brought by British, Dutch, and French colonial authorities between 1838 and 1917 to work on sugar plantations after the abolition of slavery. The first ship, the Hesperus, arrived in British Guiana in 1838 carrying labourers from India, an event commemorated as Indian Arrival Day in several countries. Over the period of indenture, roughly half a million Indians came to the Caribbean. Most came from the Bhojpur and Awadh regions of present-day Uttar Pradesh and Bihar, with a substantial minority from South India arriving through Madras.

Culture

Indo-Caribbean culture blends South Asian traditions with Caribbean creolization. Many Indo-Caribbeans practise Hinduism or Islam, while others are Christian. Distinctive cultural contributions include chutney music and its offshoot chutney soca, foods such as roti, doubles, and curry dishes adapted to Caribbean ingredients, and festivals including Diwali, Eid, and Hosay. The Bhojpuri-derived language once widely spoken has largely given way to English and Caribbean creoles, surviving in song and ritual.

Notable people

Prominent Indo-Caribbeans include Nobel laureate author V. S. Naipaul of Trinidad, cricketer Shivnarine Chanderpaul of Guyana, and political leaders such as Cheddi Jagan and Bharrat Jagdeo of Guyana and Basdeo Panday and Kamla Persad-Bissessar of Trinidad and Tobago.
