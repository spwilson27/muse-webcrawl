Concert band

A concert band, also called a wind ensemble, symphonic band or wind orchestra, is a performing ensemble consisting of members of the woodwind, brass and percussion families of instruments, and occasionally including a double bass or bass guitar. Concert bands perform in concert settings, unlike marching bands, which perform outdoors while moving.

Instrumentation

Standard instrumentation includes flutes and piccolo, oboes, bassoons, clarinets in several sizes, saxophones, horns, trumpets, trombones, euphoniums and tubas, with percussion ranging from timpani and snare drum to mallet instruments and accessory percussion.

Repertoire

The repertoire includes original works for band — by composers such as Gustav Holst (First Suite in E-flat, 1909), Ralph Vaughan Williams, Percy Grainger, Vincent Persichetti and John Philip Sousa, the "March King" — as well as transcriptions of orchestral works, marches, and contemporary compositions. School, university, military and community bands are widespread, especially in the United States, Japan and Europe.
