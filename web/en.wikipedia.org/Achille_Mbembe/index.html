Achille Mbembe

Joseph-Achille Mbembe (born 1957) is a Cameroonian philosopher, political theorist, and public intellectual. He is widely regarded as one of the leading thinkers in postcolonial studies. His work addresses African history and politics, biopolitics, and the legacies of colonialism.

Life and career

Mbembe was born near Otélé in Cameroon in 1957. He studied in Paris, earning a doctorate in history from the Sorbonne, and later worked in the United States and Senegal. He served as executive secretary of the Council for the Development of Social Science Research in Africa (CODESRIA) in Dakar. He has taught at Columbia University, the University of Pennsylvania, Duke University, and the University of the Witwatersrand in Johannesburg, South Africa, where he is a research professor at the Wits Institute for Social and Economic Research (WiSER).

Work

Mbembe's best-known book, On the Postcolony (2000 in French; English translation 2001), offers a critique of African political discourse and Western representations of Africa. His 2003 essay "Necropolitics" extended Michel Foucault's concept of biopolitics, arguing that sovereignty in the modern world is expressed through the power to dictate who may live and who must die. Later works include Critique of Black Reason (2013) and Brutalism (2020).
