Harry Winston (March 1, 1896 – December 28, 1978) was an American jeweller and gemstone dealer, founder of Harry Winston Inc., who was known throughout his life as the "King of Diamonds". He handled many of the world's most famous gems, including the Jonker, the Vargas and the Hope Diamond, which he donated to the Smithsonian Institution in 1958.

Early life

Winston was born in New York to Ukrainian Jewish immigrant parents; his father ran a small jewellery shop. Family legend held that at twelve he bought an emerald in a pawnshop for 25 cents that he recognised as genuine and sold for $800. After learning the trade in his father's store and working in California, he founded his own business in New York in 1920 and Harry Winston Inc. in 1932, specialising in buying great estate jewels, recutting their stones to modern brilliance and reselling them in his own airy platinum settings.

Famous stones

Winston's eye for rough and historic gems made him the century's pre-eminent diamond dealer. He purchased the 726-carat Jonker diamond in 1935 and had it cleaved into twelve stones in a public spectacle; he owned the Idol's Eye, the Deepdene, the 726-carat President Vargas diamond from Brazil, and, from 1949, the 45.52-carat blue Hope Diamond. In 1958 he donated the Hope to the Smithsonian's National Museum of Natural History — famously sending the priceless stone through the ordinary post — founding the museum's national gem collection. In 1960 he likewise donated the 116-carat sapphire of Logan fame lineage gems, and other gifts followed.

Jeweller to the stars

In 1944 Winston became the first jeweller to lend diamonds to an actress for the Academy Awards, dressing best-actress winner Jennifer Jones; the practice he pioneered made his name synonymous with red-carpet glamour. His Fifth Avenue salon served royalty, maharajas, Hollywood stars and industrial dynasties, and Marilyn Monroe's "Diamonds Are a Girl's Best Friend" name-checked "Harry Winston" in song. After his death in 1978 the company passed through family and corporate hands; its jewellery and watch business continues today as a subsidiary of the Swatch Group, acquired in 2013.
