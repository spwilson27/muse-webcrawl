Epic Games

Epic Games, Inc. is an American video game and software developer and publisher based in Cary, North Carolina. The company was founded by Tim Sweeney as Potomac Computer Systems in 1991, originally located in his parents' house in Potomac, Maryland. Following the release of its first commercial video game, ZZT (1991), the company became Epic MegaGames, Inc. in early 1992 and brought on Mark Rein, who has been its vice president since. In 1999 the studio moved its headquarters to Cary and its name was simplified to Epic Games.

History

Epic Games developed the Unreal Engine, a commercially available game engine which also powers its internally developed video games such as Fortnite and the Unreal, Gears of War, and Infinity Blade series. In 2014, Unreal Engine was named the "most successful videogame engine" by Guinness World Records.

In June 2012, China's Tencent acquired a 48.4% outstanding share, equating to 40% of total Epic, in Epic Games, as part of an agreement aimed at moving Epic towards a games as a service model. The success of Fortnite Battle Royale, launched in September 2017, made Epic a major force in the industry and drove the launch of the Epic Games Store in December 2018, a digital storefront competing with Steam.

Fortnite and the Epic Games Store

Fortnite became a cultural phenomenon, reportedly earning billions of dollars in revenue through cosmetic microtransactions. Epic has used this success to fund its store, offering developers an 88/12 revenue split, more favourable than the industry-standard 70/30 split. In 2020, Epic became embroiled in a high-profile legal dispute with Apple and Google over app-store commissions, filing lawsuits after Fortnite was removed from their storefronts.

Subsidiaries and acquisitions

Epic has acquired numerous studios and technology companies, including Psyonix (developer of Rocket League), Harmonix, Bandcamp, and Sketchfab. It also develops Metahuman Creator, a tool for generating realistic digital humans within Unreal Engine.
