Kingdom of Sarawak

The Kingdom of Sarawak (also known as the Raj of Sarawak) was a state in northwest Borneo founded by the English adventurer James Brooke in 1841 and ruled for a century by his family, the "White Rajahs". Granted by the Sultanate of Brunei in return for suppressing rebellion and piracy, it expanded at Brunei's expense into a territory approximating today's Malaysian state of Sarawak, before becoming a British Crown Colony in 1946.

Founding

James Brooke arrived at Kuching in 1839 aboard the schooner Royalist and helped the Brunei governor crush a rebellion of Malays and Dayaks. In 1841 he was made Rajah and Governor of Sarawak, and in 1842 the Sultan confirmed his independent rule. Brooke campaigned with Royal Navy support against Iban ("Sea Dayak") piracy at the Beting Marau and elsewhere, made Sarawak a British protectorate in 1888, and codified a paternal administration ruling through a Supreme Council including Malay and Dayak chiefs.

The Brooke raj

Charles Brooke reigned for nearly fifty years, expanding Sarawak to the Baram and Limbang rivers, suppressing headhunting and slavery, encouraging Chinese and Malay immigration, and developing exports of sago, pepper, rubber and oil (the Miri field, 1910). He kept European settlement and capital at arm's length and ruled through a small cadre of European officers. Charles Vyner Brooke governed until the Japanese invasion of December 1941. After occupation and liberation, the impoverished Vyner ceded Sarawak to Britain in 1946 — a controversial act that sparked an anti-cession movement in which the British governor Duncan Stewart was assassinated in 1949.

Legacy

Sarawak joined the Federation of Malaysia in 1963. Brooke-era buildings — the Astana, Fort Margherita, the Sarawak Museum and the Old Court House — dominate Kuching's waterfront, and the Brooke dynasty remains a defining chapter of Bornean history, variously celebrated as romantic and criticised as colonial.
