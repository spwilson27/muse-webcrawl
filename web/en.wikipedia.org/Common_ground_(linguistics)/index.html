Common ground (linguistics)

In semantics, pragmatics, and philosophy of language, the common ground of a conversation is the set of propositions that the participants mutually assume, and mutually assume to be part of the common ground. Such a proposition can be an assumption about the physical world, the background, or the behaviour of the participants and the rules of conversation itself.

Theory

The concept was developed in the work of Robert Stalnaker, who treated assertion as a proposal to add a proposition to the common ground, and by Herbert H. Clark, who studied how speakers ground contributions through presentation and acceptance moves. Common ground is built up from communal shared knowledge, personal common ground grounded in joint experience, and information made salient in the immediate situation.

The notion underlies analyses of presupposition, definite descriptions, and implicature: speakers design utterances for what they take to be in the common ground, and utterances that require accommodation can add presupposed content to it when unchallenged.
