Hub-and-spoke model

The hub-and-spoke model is a system of connections arranged like a wheel, in which traffic moves along "spokes" connected to a central "hub". It is widely used in transportation, logistics, telecommunications, and organisational design, in contrast to point-to-point networks.

In aviation

After airline deregulation in the United States in 1978, major carriers reorganised their route networks around hub airports — such as Atlanta, Chicago O'Hare, and Dallas/Fort Worth — funnelling passengers from many origin cities through the hub onto connecting flights. The model increases the number of city pairs a carrier can serve and improves load factors, but adds travel time and vulnerability to delays at the hub.

Other applications

Parcel carriers such as FedEx (Memphis) and UPS (Louisville) built global networks on the model; it also appears in hospital systems, franchise management, computer networks, and distribution warehousing.
