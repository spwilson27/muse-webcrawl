Gushi Khan

Gushi Khan (1582–1655), Oirat khan who established the Khoshut Khanate in Tibet.
Born1582
Died1655
ReignKhan of the Khoshut Khanate, 1642–1655

Gushi Khan (1582–1655) was a Khoshut Oirat prince and founder of the Khoshut Khanate, whose military intervention in Tibet secured the supremacy of the Gelug school of Tibetan Buddhism and established the 5th Dalai Lama, Lozang Gyatso, as Tibet's temporal ruler.

Background

Gushi Khan, born Torobaikhu, was a leader of the Khoshut tribe of the Oirats, a western Mongol confederation. A devout adherent of the Gelug school, he undertook a pilgrimage to Lhasa in 1637, where the 5th Dalai Lama and his regent Sonam Choephel encouraged him to act as the school's military patron against its rivals, particularly the Tsangpa king who favoured the Kagyu school.

Conquest of Tibet

After first establishing his power in the Kokonor (Qinghai) region, where he defeated Choghtu Khong Tayiji in 1637, Gushi Khan invaded Tibet proper. Between 1640 and 1642 his forces defeated the king of Beri, the ruler of Shigatse, and finally Karma Phuntsok Namgyal's successor, the Tsangpa king Karma Tenkyong, unifying Tibet under his control.

Rule

In 1642 Gushi Khan enthroned the 5th Dalai Lama as ruler of Tibet, while retaining for himself the title of Khan and military overlordship; day-to-day administration was handled by the regent (desi). He spent much of his time in the Kokonor region and died in 1655. His descendants, the Khoshut khans, remained nominal rulers of Tibet until the Dzungar invasion of 1717.
