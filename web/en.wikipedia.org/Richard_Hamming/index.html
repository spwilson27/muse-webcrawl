Richard Hamming

Richard Wesley Hamming (February 11, 1915 – January 7, 1998) was an American mathematician whose work had many implications for computer engineering and telecommunications. His contributions include the Hamming code, the Hamming window, Hamming numbers, sphere-packing (or Hamming) bounds, the Hamming distance, and the Hamming predictor-corrector.

Life and career

Born in Chicago, Hamming studied at the University of Chicago, the University of Nebraska, and the University of Illinois, where he received his PhD in 1942. During the Second World War he worked on the Manhattan Project at Los Alamos. In 1946 he joined Bell Telephone Laboratories, where he worked alongside Claude Shannon and created error-correcting codes after becoming frustrated with the unreliability of the Bell Model V computer.

Work

The Hamming(7,4) code, published in 1950, was the first of a family of error-correcting codes central to modern information theory. He later worked on numerical methods, digital filters, and computing education, and taught at the Naval Postgraduate School.

Recognition

Hamming received the Turing Award in 1968 and the IEEE Richard W. Hamming Medal, named in his honour, was established in 1986. His 1986 talk "You and Your Research" remains widely read.
