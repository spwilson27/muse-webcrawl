Red tape

Red tape is a term for excessive regulation, rigid conformity and bureaucracy that is considered redundant or burdensome, slowing or preventing action or decision-making. The phrase derives from the red tape (fabric ribbon) historically used in Britain, Spain and other countries to bind official documents.

Red cloth tape bound legal papers in early modern Spain under Charles V, and British government files were tied with red tape into the 20th century — a practice mocked by Charles Dickens and Thomas Carlyle. Modern governments run periodic 'red tape reduction' programmes, though studies note some rules serve accountability, safety and anti-corruption purposes.
