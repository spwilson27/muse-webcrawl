Rietveld Schröder House

The Rietveld Schröder House (Dutch: Rietveld Schröderhuis) in Utrecht, Netherlands, was built in 1924 by Dutch architect Gerrit Rietveld for Mrs Truus Schröder-Schräder and her three children. It is the only building realised completely according to the principles of the De Stijl movement, and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Design

The house appears as a composition of independent planes and lines in white, grey, black and the primary colours red, blue and yellow characteristic of De Stijl. The ground floor is relatively conventional, but the upper floor is a single open space that can be divided by sliding and revolving partition walls, allowing Schröder to reconfigure the living and sleeping areas as needed. Built-in furniture, including folding beds, maximised the flexibility of the small interior.

History

Truus Schröder lived in the house until her death in 1985, and Rietveld himself kept a studio there in his later years. After restoration by Bertus Mulder, the house passed to the Centraal Museum Utrecht and is now open to visitors. In 2000 UNESCO inscribed it on the World Heritage List as an icon of the Modern Movement in architecture.
