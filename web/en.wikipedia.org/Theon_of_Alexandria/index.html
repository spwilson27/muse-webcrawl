Theon of Alexandria

Bornc. 335 AD, Alexandria, Egypt
Diedc. 405 AD
Known forEdition of Euclid's Elements; commentaries on Ptolemy's Almagest and Handy Tables
ChildrenHypatia

Theon of Alexandria (c. 335 – c. 405 AD) was a Greek scholar and mathematician who lived in Alexandria, Egypt. He edited and arranged Euclid's Elements and wrote commentaries on works by Euclid and Ptolemy. His daughter Hypatia also won fame as a mathematician and philosopher.

Life

Little is known of Theon's life. The Byzantine encyclopedia known as the Suda describes him as a teacher associated with the Mouseion. He made astronomical observations of eclipses in 364 AD and dedicated works to a boy named Epiphanius, who may have been his son.

Works

Edited works

Theon's recension of Euclid's Elements was for centuries the only surviving Greek text of the work and remained the standard edition until the 19th century, when an earlier version was identified in a Vatican manuscript. He also produced an edition of Euclid's Optics and Data.

Commentaries

Theon's commentary on Ptolemy's Almagest survives in large part and is a valuable source for ancient astronomy. He wrote longer and shorter ("small") commentaries on Ptolemy's Handy Tables, explaining their use for computing planetary positions, and a commentary on the poet Aratus. Works on astrolabes and other topics are lost.
