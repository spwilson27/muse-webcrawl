Slate

Slate is a fine-grained, foliated, homogeneous, low-grade metamorphic rock derived from an original shale-type sedimentary rock composed of clay or volcanic ash through low-grade regional metamorphism. It is famous for the characteristic "slaty cleavage" along which it can be readily split into thin, flat sheets, a property that has made it valuable for roofing tiles, flooring, blackboards and billiard-table beds.

Formation and properties

Slate forms when shale or mudstone is subjected to the moderate heat and directed pressure of regional metamorphism. Clay minerals recrystallize into tiny flakes of mica aligned perpendicular to the direction of compression, producing cleavage planes that are independent of the original bedding. Colors range from grey and black through purple, green and red, depending on the iron and organic content of the parent rock. Slate is hard and durable but brittle, resists frost and moisture absorption, and can retain its properties for centuries, which is why slate roofs installed in the nineteenth century often remain in service.

Extraction

Slate is quarried in many regions, historically pre-eminently in North Wales — where the Penrhyn and Dinorwic quarries were among the largest in the world and the Welsh slate landscape was later inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site — as well as in Spain, which is now the largest producer, Vermont and Pennsylvania in the United States, and parts of China and India. Extraction involves sawing large blocks from the quarry face and splitting them by hand or machine along the cleavage.

Uses

The split sheets are used as roofing slates, floor and wall tiles, paving, steps and countertops. Thicker, carefully levelled slate forms the beds of billiard and snooker tables, covered with baize. Before cheap paper, children wrote on framed slate boards in schools, and electrical panels were once made of slate because of its insulating properties. Crushed or powdered slate is used as a filler and pigment, and expanded slate aggregate serves horticultural and construction purposes.
