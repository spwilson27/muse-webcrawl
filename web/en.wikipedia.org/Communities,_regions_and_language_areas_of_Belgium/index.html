Communities, regions and language areas of Belgium

Belgium is a federal state organised into communities and regions, superimposed on four language areas. This layered structure, created through successive state reforms between 1970 and 1993, divides competences between the federal government and the federated entities according to whether a matter is territorial (regions) or related to language, culture, and "person-related" affairs such as education and health policy (communities).

Communities

There are three communities: the Flemish Community (Dutch-speaking), the French Community (known as the Wallonia-Brussels Federation), and the small German-speaking Community in the east. Each has its own parliament and government. The Flemish Community and the Flemish Region were merged into a single set of institutions.

Regions

The three regions are the Flemish Region (Flanders), the Walloon Region (Wallonia), and the Brussels-Capital Region. Regions are responsible for territorial matters including economy, employment, agriculture, water policy, housing, public works, energy, transport, the environment, and supervision of provinces and municipalities.

Language areas

The constitution defines four language areas: the Dutch language area, the French language area, the German language area, and the bilingual Brussels-Capital area. The language area determines the official language of administration and public education. Certain municipalities along the language border grant "facilities" to speakers of the neighbouring language.
