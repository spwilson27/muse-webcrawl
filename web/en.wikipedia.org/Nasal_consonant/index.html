Nasal consonant

In phonetics, a nasal, also called a nasal occlusive or nasal stop in contrast with an oral stop or nasal approximant, is an occlusive consonant produced with a lowered velum, allowing air to escape freely through the nose. The vast majority of consonants are oral consonants. Examples of nasals in English are [n] and [m], in words such as nose and mouth.

Definition

Nasals are almost always voiced. In producing a nasal, the oral tract is blocked at some point of articulation while the soft palate is lowered so that air passes through the nasal cavity. Nasal stops are among the most common consonants in the world's languages; only a few languages, such as certain Salishan and Australian languages, lack them entirely.
