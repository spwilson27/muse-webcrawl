The unification of Germany was a process of building the first nation-state for Germans, culminating in the proclamation of Wilhelm I of Prussia as German emperor at the Palace of Versailles on 18 January 1871. German-speaking Central Europe had long been divided among hundreds of states of the Holy Roman Empire, and after 1815 among the 39 states of the German Confederation, dominated by the rivalry of Prussia and Austria.

Anton von Werner's painting of the proclamation of the German Empire in the Hall of Mirrors, 18 January 1871.

Background

The Napoleonic Wars dissolved the Holy Roman Empire (1806) and stirred German nationalism; the liberal attempt at unification "from below" in the Revolutions of 1848 (the Frankfurt Parliament) failed when Frederick William IV of Prussia refused its offered crown. Meanwhile the Prussian-led Zollverein (customs union, 1834) bound most German states economically while excluding Austria, foreshadowing the "Little German" (kleindeutsch) solution.

Bismarck's wars

Chancellor Otto von Bismarck pursued unification "by blood and iron" through three wars: with Austria against Denmark over Schleswig-Holstein (1864); against Austria in the Austro-Prussian War (1866), after which Prussia annexed Hanover and other states and formed the North German Confederation (1867), excluding Austria; and against France in the Franco-Prussian War (1870–71), which brought the south German states (Bavaria, Württemberg, Baden, Hesse) into the fold. On 18 January 1871 the German Empire was proclaimed, with the Prussian king as hereditary emperor — a federal, authoritarian monarchy dominated by Prussia. (The term "German unification" is also used for the reunification of East and West Germany on 3 October 1990.)
