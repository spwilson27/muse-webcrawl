Commonwealth Secretariat

The Commonwealth Secretariat is the main intergovernmental agency and central institution of the Commonwealth of Nations. Established in 1965, it is responsible for facilitating co-operation between member governments and states, organising Commonwealth summits and meetings, and providing technical assistance, policy advice, and election observation across the Commonwealth.

Organisation

The Secretariat is headed by the Commonwealth Secretary-General, who is elected by heads of government for a term of up to eight years; the first Secretary-General was the Canadian diplomat Arnold Smith. Its headquarters are at Marlborough House in London, a former royal palace made available by the British monarch. Staff are drawn from across the member countries, and the organisation's work is guided by the values of the Commonwealth Charter, including democracy, human rights, development, and good governance.

The Secretariat supports the biennial Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting (CHOGM), runs programmes in areas such as climate change, trade, youth, and small states, and deploys observer groups to elections in member countries.
