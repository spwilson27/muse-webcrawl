Market order

A market order is a buy or sell order to trade a security at the best available current price. It is the most basic type of order and, for a liquid stock that is actively traded, typically ensures an execution within seconds, but does not guarantee the execution price. A market order is to be contrasted with a limit order, in which the investor specifies the price at which they are willing to trade.

Characteristics

For most investors, the bid/ask spread on a stock is small enough that a market order will execute close to the displayed quotes. However, for illiquid securities, fast-moving markets, or very large orders, a market order may be filled at a much worse price than expected, because the order consumes successive levels of the order book. Market orders are generally executed at the next available price after the order reaches the exchange.

Advantages and disadvantages

The main advantage of a market order is speed and certainty of execution: if the investor wants to get into or out of a position immediately, a market order is appropriate. The disadvantage is the lack of control over price; in volatile conditions the difference between the expected and executed price (slippage) can be significant.
