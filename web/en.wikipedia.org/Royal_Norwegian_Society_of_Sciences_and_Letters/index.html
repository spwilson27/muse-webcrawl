The Royal Norwegian Society of Sciences and Letters (Norwegian: Det Kongelige Norske Videnskabers Selskab, DKNVS) is Norway's oldest scientific institution and academy. Founded in Trondheim in 1760, it promotes science through meetings, publications, prizes and the election of distinguished researchers as members.

History

The society was founded as the Trondheim Society (Det Trondhiemske Selskab) in 1760 by Bishop Johan Ernst Gunnerus, rector Gerhard Schøning and councillor Peter Frederik Suhm. It received its royal title and statutes from King Frederick V of Denmark in 1767, during the era when Norway was part of Denmark–Norway. Bishop Gunnerus, a pioneering naturalist, built up the society's collections and co-authored the first flora of Norway, Flora Norvegica.

In the nineteenth century the society's museum grew into one of Norway's principal natural history and antiquities collections. The museum later became part of the Norwegian University of Science and Technology as the NTNU University Museum, while the society itself continued as an independent academy.

Activities

Members are organised into two classes — the Humanities Class and the Natural Sciences Class — and meet regularly in Trondheim. The society publishes the series Skrifter (in print since 1761, among the world's longest-running scientific series) and awards prizes, most notably the Gunnerus Medal, its highest honour, and the annual scientific prizes for younger researchers. It also co-funds the Gunnerus Sustainability Award.

The society's library and archives historically held important medieval manuscripts and early Norwegian printed books; major parts of these collections are now deposited with the NTNU University Library's Gunnerus Library, named after the founder.
