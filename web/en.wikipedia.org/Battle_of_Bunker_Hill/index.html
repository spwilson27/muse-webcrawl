Battle of Bunker Hill

The Battle of Bunker Hill was fought on 17 June 1775 during the Siege of Boston in the opening phase of the American Revolutionary War. Although named for Bunker Hill in Charlestown, Massachusetts, most of the fighting took place on nearby Breed's Hill. British forces under General William Howe eventually captured the Charlestown peninsula, but at a cost so heavy that the battle is often described as a Pyrrhic victory.

Background

Learning that the British planned to occupy the heights around Boston, colonial forces under Colonel William Prescott fortified Breed's Hill overnight on 16–17 June, building a redoubt within range of Boston. Howe landed with over 2,000 regulars to dislodge them.

The battle

Twice the British assaulted the redoubt and were thrown back by disciplined colonial musket fire — tradition attributes to the defenders the order not to fire "until you see the whites of their eyes," conserving scarce powder. On the third assault, with the colonists' ammunition exhausted, the British carried the works in hand-to-hand fighting and the militia withdrew across Charlestown Neck.

Casualties and significance

The British suffered over 1,000 casualties, including 226 killed — nearly half the force engaged — while colonial losses were around 450. The costly victory sobered British commanders, encouraged the American cause by proving militia could stand against regulars, and led to more cautious British strategy thereafter.
