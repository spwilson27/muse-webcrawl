The Linafoot (Ligue Nationale de Football) is the top division of association football in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Created in 1958 under the country's football federation (FECOFA) and reorganised in its modern form in 2010, the championship is contested by leading Congolese clubs in the Linafoot Ligue 1, with a second division, Ligue 2, introduced in 2018.

History

Championship football in the Congo dates to the colonial era, with Kinshasa and Léopoldville clubs prominent. After independence, AS Vita Club, DC Motema Pembe and TP Mazembe of Lubumbashi emerged as the dominant teams. TP Mazembe is the competition's most successful club and one of Africa's strongest, winning the CAF Champions League five times and reaching the FIFA Club World Cup final in 2010, an African first.

Format

In recent seasons sixteen Ligue 1 teams have been split into two groups in a first phase, followed by championship and relegation playoff rounds. The champion and runner-up qualify for the CAF Champions League, with another club entering the CAF Confederation Cup. The league long carried the sponsorship name Vodacom Ligue 1. Congolese football has faced recurring challenges with financing, infrastructure and scheduling, yet remains central to the country's sporting life.
