Air assault

Air assault is the movement of ground-based military forces by vertical take-off and landing (VTOL) aircraft — principally helicopters — to seize and hold terrain, often behind enemy lines, and to engage enemy forces directly. Air assault units are trained and equipped for rapid deployment into combat by air, combining the firepower of infantry with the mobility of aviation.

Distinction from air mobility and airborne operations

Air assault differs from airborne operations, in which troops arrive by parachute, and from simple air mobility (transport), because air assault troops are inserted directly into battle, often under fire, and continue fighting as an organized force with helicopter gunship support. The helicopter also provides resupply, medical evacuation, and artillery displacement (sling-loading howitzers) within the operation.

History

The concept matured in the Vietnam War, where the US 1st Cavalry Division (Airmobile) and later the 101st Airborne Division (Airmobile/Air Assault) used massed helicopters for operations such as the Ia Drang campaign (1965) and the relief of Khe Sanh. The 101st remains the US Army's premier air assault division; its Air Assault School at Fort Campbell teaches rappelling, sling-load operations, and pathfinder skills, and graduates wear the Air Assault Badge. Other armies maintain equivalents, such as Britain's 16 Air Assault Brigade.

Capabilities and vulnerabilities

Air assault forces can bypass terrain and defenses, strike deep, and concentrate quickly, but helicopters are vulnerable to air defenses and small-arms fire, limited by weather, and the inserted force is usually light infantry dependent on continued air support.
