Guy (band)

Guy, pioneers of the new jack swing sound.
OriginHarlem, New York City, U.S.
GenresR&B, new jack swing
Years active1987–1991, 1998–2000, reunions thereafter
MembersTeddy Riley, Aaron Hall, Timmy Gatling (later Damion Hall)

Guy is an American R&B group founded in Harlem, New York City, in 1987 by producer Teddy Riley with singers Aaron Hall and Timmy Gatling. The group is credited with launching the new jack swing genre, which fused R&B singing with hip hop beats and production.

Career

Guy's self-titled debut album (1988) produced hits including "Groove Me," "Teddy's Jam," and "I Like," and established Riley's signature sound. After Gatling's departure, he was replaced by Aaron Hall's brother Damion Hall. The second album, The Future (1990), yielded the singles "Let's Chill" and "D-O-G Me Out." Internal disputes and management problems led to the group's breakup in 1991, after which Riley formed Blackstreet and Aaron Hall pursued a solo career.

Reunions and legacy

The members reunited for the album Guy III (2000) and have performed together periodically since. Guy's combination of soulful vocals, rap-inflected rhythms, and glossy production defined mainstream R&B at the turn of the 1990s and influenced artists from Boyz II Men to contemporary R&B acts.
