Creative destruction

Creative destruction is an economic concept describing the process by which innovation continually dismantles established products, firms, and industries while simultaneously creating new ones. The term was popularized by the Austrian economist Joseph Schumpeter in his 1942 book Capitalism, Socialism and Democracy.

Schumpeter's formulation

Schumpeter described capitalism as an evolutionary process of "industrial mutation" that "incessantly revolutionizes the economic structure from within, incessantly destroying the old one, incessantly creating a new one." Entrepreneurs introducing new products, production methods, or forms of organization undermine incumbent firms, releasing resources for more productive uses.

Examples and criticism

Classic examples include the automobile displacing horse-drawn transport, digital photography displacing film, and streaming media displacing physical rental stores. Critics note the human costs of displacement, including job losses and community decline, and question whether the gains are evenly shared.
