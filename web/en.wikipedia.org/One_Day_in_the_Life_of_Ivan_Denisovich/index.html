One Day in the Life of Ivan Denisovich

AuthorAleksandr Solzhenitsyn
Published1962, in Novy Mir
GenreNovella
SettingA Soviet Gulag labour camp, c. 1951

One Day in the Life of Ivan Denisovich (Russian: Один день Ивана Денисовича) is a novella by Aleksandr Solzhenitsyn, published in November 1962 in the Soviet literary magazine Novy Mir. It was the first published account of life in Stalin's Gulag labour camps, and its appearance — personally approved by Nikita Khrushchev during the Thaw — was a sensation in the Soviet Union and abroad.

Plot

The novella follows a single winter day of Ivan Denisovich Shukhov, prisoner Shch-854, serving ten years in a camp in Kazakhstan for the fabricated crime of espionage after his capture by the Germans in the war. From reveille at five in the bitter cold, through body searches, watery gruel, the struggle over bread rations and a day's bricklaying on a power-station wall in temperatures far below zero, to lights-out, the narrative records the small victories by which Shukhov preserves his dignity: hiding a piece of hacksaw blade, earning an extra bowl of kasha, building the wall well. The day ends with Shukhov almost content — "a day without a dark cloud. Almost a happy day" — one of 3,653 in his sentence.

Significance

Drawn from Solzhenitsyn's own years in the camps, the novella's plain style and use of camp slang broke a decades-long silence about the Gulag in Soviet print. It made its author internationally famous, led to a flood of camp memoirs sent to him by readers, and paved the way for The Gulag Archipelago. Solzhenitsyn received the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1970.
