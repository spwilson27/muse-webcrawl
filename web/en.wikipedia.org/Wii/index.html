The Wii is a home video game console developed and marketed by Nintendo. It was released on 19 November 2006 in North America and on 8 December 2006 in Europe, as the seventh-generation console competing with Microsoft's Xbox 360 and Sony's PlayStation 3. Nintendo stated that the console targeted a broader demographic than its competitors, seeking to attract new players and non-gamers.

Design and controller

The Wii introduced the Wii Remote, a handheld pointing device that detects movement in three dimensions using accelerometers and infrared sensing, enabling motion-controlled gameplay. A secondary Nunchuk controller could be attached for additional analogue input. The console was backward compatible with the GameCube, playing its discs and supporting its controllers and memory cards.

Software and services

The bundled game Wii Sports became a cultural phenomenon and one of the best-selling video games of all time. Other landmark titles include Mario Kart Wii, Wii Sports Resort, New Super Mario Bros. Wii, and Wii Fit, which used the Wii Balance Board peripheral. The Wii Shop Channel sold downloadable games, including Virtual Console re-releases of classic titles, until its closure in 2019.

Reception and legacy

The Wii was a major commercial success, selling 101.63 million units worldwide and outselling both of its rivals, despite comparatively modest processing power. Its motion controls influenced the whole industry, prompting Sony and Microsoft to release the PlayStation Move and Kinect. Production ended in 2013, shortly after the launch of its successor, the Wii U, in 2012.
