The Russian ruble (Russian: рубль, romanized: rublʹ; symbol: ₽; code: RUB) is the currency of the Russian Federation. It is subdivided into 100 kopeks. The ruble has been used in Russian territories since the 13th century, making it one of the world's oldest continuously used currency names.

History

The name derives from the Russian verb rubit', "to chop", referring to chopped pieces of silver. Peter the Great decimalised the ruble in 1704, making it the first decimal currency in Europe. The Soviet ruble replaced the imperial currency after the Revolution, and following the dissolution of the USSR the Russian ruble was introduced in 1992. After hyperinflation in the 1990s, the ruble was redenominated in 1998 at 1 new ruble = 1,000 old rubles.

Coins and banknotes

Coins circulate in denominations of 1, 2, 5 and 10 rubles, with kopek coins now rarely used. Banknotes are issued in denominations from 5 to 5,000 rubles, featuring Russian cities and landmarks; a 200-ruble note introduced in 2017 depicts Sevastopol, and a redesigned 100-ruble note appeared in 2022. The ruble sign ₽ was officially adopted in 2013.
