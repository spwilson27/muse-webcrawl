Formula One (also known as F1) is the highest class of international racing for open-wheel single-seater formula racing cars sanctioned by the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA). The FIA Formula One World Championship has been one of the world's premier forms of motorsport since its inaugural running in 1950. The word formula in the name refers to the set of rules with which all participants' cars must conform.

A Formula One season consists of a series of races, known as Grands Prix. Grands Prix take place in multiple countries and continents on either purpose-built circuits or closed public roads. A points system is used at Grands Prix to determine two annual World Championships: one for the drivers and one for the constructors (the teams). Each driver must hold a valid Super Licence, issued by the FIA.

History

Formula One originated from the European Motor Racing Championships of the 1920s and 1930s. The first World Championship race, the 1950 British Grand Prix, was held at Silverstone and won by Giuseppe Farina, who took the inaugural drivers' title for Alfa Romeo. Juan Manuel Fangio dominated the 1950s with five championships. The decades that followed saw the rear-engined revolution, the rise of British teams such as Lotus and McLaren, turbocharged engines in the 1980s, and the dynasties of Michael Schumacher with Ferrari (2000–2004), Sebastian Vettel with Red Bull (2010–2013), and Lewis Hamilton with Mercedes (2014–2020).

Racing and strategy

A typical race weekend comprises practice sessions, a qualifying session that sets the grid, and the race itself, run over a distance of about 305 kilometres. Pit stops for tyre changes, aerodynamic downforce management, energy recovery systems (ERS) on the current 1.6-litre V6 turbo-hybrid power units, and the Drag Reduction System (DRS) all shape strategy. Points are awarded to the top ten finishers on a 25-18-15-12-10-8-6-4-2-1 scale, with sprint races at selected events awarding additional points.

Records

Lewis Hamilton and Michael Schumacher share the record of seven World Drivers' Championships; Hamilton holds the records for most wins, pole positions, and podiums. Ferrari is the most successful constructor, with 16 Constructors' titles, and the only team to have competed in every season since 1950.
