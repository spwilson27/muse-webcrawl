Saranac Lake is a village in the state of New York, United States, straddling the border of Essex and Franklin counties in the Adirondack Mountains. It lies on the shores of Lake Flower, near the Saranac Lakes chain. Tourism, health care, and education are the principal industries. Together with nearby Lake Placid, it forms a centre for winter sports and outdoor recreation, and it hosts an annual Winter Carnival, dating to 1897, featuring an ice palace built from lake ice.

History

The village became famous in the late nineteenth century as a centre for the treatment of tuberculosis. Dr. Edward Livingston Trudeau, who had recovered his health in the Adirondack air, founded the Adirondack Cottage Sanitarium at Saranac Lake in 1884 and the Trudeau Institute laboratory for the study of the disease. Thousands of patients came to "take the cure" in sanatoria and in private "cure cottages" with wide sleeping porches, many of which survive and are listed on the National Register of Historic Places. Writer Robert Louis Stevenson wintered in a cottage here in 1887–88, and composer Béla Bartók spent his final summers in the village.

Geography and climate

The village sits at an elevation of about 1,545 feet and experiences a humid continental climate with long, cold, snowy winters — Saranac Lake is frequently among the coldest places in the contiguous United States on winter mornings. The surrounding Adirondack Park provides access to hiking, canoeing, and the High Peaks region.
