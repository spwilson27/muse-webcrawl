Trieste is a city and seaport in northeastern Italy, at the head of the Gulf of Trieste on the Adriatic Sea, near the border with Slovenia. It is the capital of the Friuli-Venezia Giulia region. For centuries Trieste was the principal maritime outlet of the Habsburg Monarchy and the Austrian Empire.

History

Originally an Illyrian and Roman settlement (Tergeste), Trieste pledged allegiance to the Habsburgs in 1382 to escape Venetian domination. Declared a free port by Charles VI in 1719, it grew into one of Europe's busiest ports and a cosmopolitan city of Italian, Slovene, German, Greek, Serbian and Jewish communities. After the First World War it passed to Italy; after the Second World War it was the centre of the disputed Free Territory of Trieste, divided in 1954 between Italy and Yugoslavia.

Culture and science

Trieste has a celebrated literary heritage, associated with Italo Svevo, Umberto Saba and James Joyce, who wrote much of Dubliners and A Portrait of the Artist while living in the city. Its coffee-house culture is famous; the Illy coffee company is based there. The city is also a research hub, hosting the International Centre for Theoretical Physics, the SISSA graduate school and the ELETTRA synchrotron.

Landmarks

Sights include the vast Piazza Unità d'Italia opening onto the sea, the white Miramare Castle built for Archduke Maximilian, the Roman theatre, and the hilltop castle and cathedral of San Giusto.
