Grigory Mikhailovich Rodchenkov (Russian: Григорий Михайлович Родченков; born 24 October 1958) is a former Russian anti-doping laboratory director who became the principal whistleblower exposing Russia's state-sponsored doping programme. His testimony underpinned the McLaren report and led to Russia's exclusion, in various forms, from Olympic competition.

Career at the Moscow laboratory

Rodchenkov, a chemist trained at Moscow State University, directed Russia's national anti-doping laboratory in Moscow from 2006 to 2015. In that role he has admitted to helping operate a sophisticated system for doping Russian athletes and concealing it: developing a steroid cocktail known as the "Duchess" mixture, swapping urine samples, and falsifying records. At the 2014 Sochi Winter Olympics, clean samples were passed through a hole in the laboratory wall in exchange for tamper-proof bottles of doped athletes' urine that had been opened using a method worked out with security-service assistance.

Whistleblowing

After a World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA) independent commission report in November 2015 exposed widespread Russian doping, Rodchenkov resigned and fled to the United States, fearing for his safety. In May 2016 he detailed the Sochi sample-swapping operation to The New York Times. WADA's subsequent investigation by Richard McLaren corroborated his account using forensic evidence, and his diaries were later published as key documentary support.

Consequences and later life

Rodchenkov's evidence contributed to the International Olympic Committee's disciplinary proceedings, the stripping of Russian medals from Sochi, and bans affecting Russian participation at the 2018 Winter Olympics and beyond; Russia has consistently denied state sponsorship and denounced him as a traitor. He entered the US witness protection programme and has appeared publicly only in disguise, citing credible threats. He authored a memoir, The Rodchenkov Affair (2020), and featured prominently in the documentary Icarus (2017). The United States enacted the Rodchenkov Anti-Doping Act in 2020, criminalising doping conspiracies at international events.
