Administrative divisions of Poland

Since 1999, Poland's administrative divisions have been based on a three-tier structure: voivodeships (provinces), powiats (counties), and gminas (communes/municipalities). The system was introduced as part of reforms restoring local self-government after the communist era, reducing the previous 49 small voivodeships to 16 larger ones.

Voivodeships

Poland is divided into 16 voivodeships, including Mazowieckie (containing the capital, Warsaw), Śląskie (Silesian), Wielkopolskie, Małopolskie, Dolnośląskie, and Pomorskie. Each has a dual administration: an elected regional assembly (sejmik) with a marshal, and a voivode appointed by the central government.

Counties and communes

The second tier comprises 380 powiats, of which 66 are cities with powiat status. The third tier consists of about 2,477 gminas — urban, urban-rural, and rural communes — which handle local services such as primary education, local roads, and utilities. Gminas may be subdivided into sołectwa (in rural areas) or dzielnice and osiedla (in cities).

History

Poland's territorial organisation changed repeatedly over the centuries: the voivodeships of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, the partitions among Prussia, Austria, and Russia, interwar provinces, and the post-1945 and 1975 reorganisations under the People's Republic all left traces in today's map.
