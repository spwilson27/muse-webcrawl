Partial pressure

Partial pressure
DefinitionPressure a gas would exert if it alone occupied the volume
Symbolpi or Pi
Named afterJohn Dalton (for the law of partial pressures)
SI unitPascal (Pa)

In a mixture of gases, each constituent gas has a partial pressure which is the notional pressure of that constituent gas as if it alone occupied the entire volume of the original mixture at the same temperature. The total pressure of an ideal gas mixture is the sum of the partial pressures of the gases in the mixture (Dalton's law).

Symbol

The symbol for pressure is usually p or P with a subscript to identify the gas in mixture. For example in the air we breathe the partial pressure of oxygen is written PO2 or pO2.

Dalton's law of partial pressures

Dalton's law expresses the fact that the total pressure of a mixture of ideal gases is equal to the sum of the partial pressures of the individual gases in the mixture. This equality arises from the fact that in an ideal gas the molecules are so far apart that they do not interact with each other. In reality, most gases in the real world are very close to ideal.

For example, given a mixture of nitrogen (N2), hydrogen (H2) and ammonia (NH3):

Ptotal = PN2 + PH2 + PNH3

Partial volume

The partial volume of a particular gas in a mixture is the volume of one component of a gas mixture. It is useful in gas mixtures, e.g. air, to focus on one particular gas component, e.g. oxygen. It can be approximated both from partial pressure and molar fraction.

Vapor pressure

Vapor pressure is the pressure of a vapor in equilibrium with its non-vapor phases (i.e., liquid or solid). Most often the term is used to describe a liquid's tendency to evaporate. It is a measure of the tendency of molecules and atoms to escape from a liquid or a solid. A liquid's atmospheric pressure boiling point corresponds to the temperature at which its vapor pressure is equal to the surrounding atmospheric pressure and it is often called the normal boiling point.

Equilibrium constants of reactions involving gas mixtures

It is possible to work out the equilibrium constant for a chemical reaction involving a mixture of gases given the partial pressure of each gas and the overall reaction formula. For a reversible reaction involving gas reactants and gas products, such as the Haber process, the equilibrium constant can be expressed in terms of partial pressures.

Henry's law and the solubility of gases

Henry's law states that at constant temperature, the amount of a given gas that dissolves in a given type and volume of liquid is directly proportional to the partial pressure of that gas in equilibrium with that liquid. This has important applications in diving physiology and anesthesia.
