Scientific nameNatrix tessellata
FamilyColubridae
RangeEurope, North Africa and western/central Asia
Conservation statusLeast Concern (IUCN)

The dice snake (Natrix tessellata) is a Eurasian nonvenomous snake of the family Colubridae, closely related to the grass snake. Its name refers to the regular, chequered ("dice-like") pattern of dark blotches on its olive, grey or brown body. It is a strongly aquatic species, rarely found far from water.

The dice snake is named for its chequered dorsal pattern.

Description

Adults typically measure 1–1.3 metres. The keeled scales give a rough texture, and the narrow head bears eyes positioned high, suited to watching for prey while submerged. Females grow larger than males. The belly is often vividly patterned in yellow, orange or red with black markings.

Behaviour and diet

Dice snakes feed almost exclusively on fish, which they catch underwater, though they occasionally take amphibians. They are active by day in spring and autumn. When threatened, they may release a foul-smelling musk and feign death, rolling onto their backs with mouth open. They are harmless to humans and will bite only rarely.

Distribution

The species ranges from central and southern Europe through the Balkans and Turkey to the Caspian region, Central Asia and China, and south to Egypt and the Levant, inhabiting rivers, lakes, marshes and rocky coasts.
