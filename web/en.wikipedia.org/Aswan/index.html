Aswan

Aswan (Arabic: أسوان, Aswān) is a city in southern Egypt, the capital of the Aswan Governorate. It stands on the east bank of the Nile at the first cataract, just south of the Aswan Dam, and has a population of roughly 300,000.

History

Ancient Swenett (later Syene) was Egypt's southern frontier town, a trading post for ivory, gold, and granite, and the site of famous quarries that supplied stone for obelisks and statues; the unfinished obelisk still lies in its quarry. Eratosthenes used Syene's position on the Tropic of Cancer in his celebrated calculation of Earth's circumference.

The dams

The Aswan Low Dam was completed by the British in 1902. The Aswan High Dam, built with Soviet assistance and opened in 1971, created Lake Nasser, controlled the Nile floods, and supplied hydroelectric power, though it required the UNESCO-led rescue of Nubian monuments including Abu Simbel and displaced tens of thousands of Nubians.

Attractions

Landmarks include Elephantine Island, the Nubian Museum, the Monastery of St Simeon, the Tombs of the Nobles, and the temple of Philae, relocated to Agilkia Island. Aswan is also a starting point for Nile cruises to Luxor.
