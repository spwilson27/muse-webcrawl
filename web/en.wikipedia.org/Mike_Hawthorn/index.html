Mike Hawthorn

John Michael Hawthorn (10 April 1929 – 22 January 1959) was a British racing driver who became the United Kingdom's first Formula One World Champion, winning the drivers' title in 1958 with Ferrari. Known for racing in a bow tie, Hawthorn won three Grands Prix and the 1955 24 Hours of Le Mans, and was a leading figure of 1950s motor racing alongside Stirling Moss and Juan Manuel Fangio.

Career

Hawthorn came to prominence in 1952 driving a Cooper-Bristol, defeating established stars and earning a Ferrari seat for 1953. At the 1953 French Grand Prix at Reims he won a celebrated wheel-to-wheel duel with Fangio — the "race of the century" — at just 24 years old. In 1955, driving for Jaguar, he won the 24 Hours of Le Mans, a race marred by the disaster in which Pierre Levegh's car crashed into the crowd, killing more than 80 spectators; Hawthorn's role in the accident was debated, though he was exonerated by the official inquiry.

After spells with BRM and Vanwall, he returned to Ferrari in 1957. In 1958 he won only one race (France) but scored consistently, benefitting from Moss's sporting intervention that overturned a disqualification in Portugal, and took the championship by a single point from Moss. His close friend and teammate Peter Collins was killed at the Nürburgring that year; the loss, along with a chronic kidney condition, led Hawthorn to retire at the end of the season.

Death

On 22 January 1959, only months after retiring and weeks before his marriage, Hawthorn was killed when his Jaguar saloon crashed on the A3 Guildford bypass, reportedly while racing a Mercedes driven by team manager Rob Walker. He was 29.
