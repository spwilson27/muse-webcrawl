Cetus

Cetus is a constellation in the southern sky, named after the sea monster Cetus from Greek mythology, often depicted as a whale. It lies in a region of water-related constellations sometimes called the Water or the Sea.

Stars and deep-sky objects

Its brightest stars include Beta Ceti (Deneb Kaitos) and Alpha Ceti (Menkar). Cetus contains Mira (Omicron Ceti), the prototype of the Mira variable stars, whose brightness varies dramatically over about 332 days. The constellation also hosts the spiral galaxy Messier 77 and the nearby star Tau Ceti, a Sun-like star of interest in the search for exoplanets.

Mythology

In the myth of Perseus and Andromeda, Cetus was the sea monster sent to devour Andromeda, who had been chained to a rock as a sacrifice, before Perseus rescued her.
