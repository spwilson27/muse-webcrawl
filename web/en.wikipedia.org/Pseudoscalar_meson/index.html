Pseudoscalar meson

In particle physics, a pseudoscalar meson is a meson with total spin 0 and odd parity (usually denoted JP = 0−). Pseudoscalar mesons are commonly seen in proton–proton scattering and proton–antiproton annihilation. The pions were first proposed in the 1930s and were the first mesons discovered.

Overview

The lightest pseudoscalar mesons are the pions (π), kaons (K), the eta (η) and eta prime (η′) mesons, whose masses are much smaller than those of the lightest vector mesons. Because their quantum numbers match those of the vacuum except for spin, pseudoscalar mesons are closely tied to the spontaneous breaking of chiral symmetry in quantum chromodynamics; the pions are the pseudo-Nambu–Goldstone bosons of that breaking.

Properties

Pseudoscalar mesons consist of a quark and an antiquark with aligned total angular momentum zero and a wave function whose parity is negative. In the quark model they form the ground-state nonet together with the corresponding vector mesons. Heavy pseudoscalars include the D, Ds, B and Bc mesons containing charm or bottom quarks.

Decay

Pseudoscalar mesons decay through the weak, electromagnetic, or strong interactions depending on their flavour content. The charged pion decays almost exclusively to a muon and a muon neutrino, a helicity-suppressed process that was historically important in establishing the structure of the weak interaction.
