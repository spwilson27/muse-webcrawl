The McDonnell Douglas DC-9 is an American five-abreast, single-aisle aircraft designed by the Douglas Aircraft Company. It was initially produced by Douglas and, following the company's merger, by McDonnell Douglas. Powered by two rear-mounted Pratt & Whitney JT8D low-bypass turbofans under a T-tail, it was designed for frequent, short flights to small airports with limited ground equipment.

Development and design

Douglas launched the DC-9 after studying the short-haul jet market in the early 1960s. The design featured built-in airstairs in the forward door and an auxiliary power unit so the aircraft could operate independently at minor airports. The prototype first flew in February 1965 and entered service with Delta Air Lines that December.

Variants and derivatives

Production covered five main series, from the initial Series 10 through the stretched Series 50, seating roughly 90 to 139 passengers. The design was developed into the MD-80 series, the MD-90 and ultimately the Boeing 717, giving the family a production span from 1965 to 2006. Military versions, designated C-9, served with the United States Air Force and Navy.

Service history

A total of 976 DC-9s were delivered. Although major passenger airlines retired the type — Delta, the launch customer, flew the last scheduled U.S. major-airline DC-9 service in January 2014 — examples continued for years in cargo and charter service, noted for their ruggedness and long structural life.
