Forest school

Forest school is an outdoor education delivery model in which students visit natural spaces such as woodlands to learn personal, social and technical skills. It has been defined as "an inspirational process that offers all learners regular opportunities to achieve and develop confidence and self-esteem through hands-on learning experiences in a woodland or natural environment with trees."

History

The forest school concept originated in Scandinavia, where outdoor living (friluftsliv) is a longstanding cultural tradition. In Denmark, the first "walking kindergartens" emerged in the 1950s, and forest kindergartens (skovbørnehave) became widespread. The idea was introduced to the United Kingdom in 1993 after a group of childcare students from Bridgwater College in Somerset visited Denmark to observe the approach. Forest School programmes subsequently spread across the UK, North America and elsewhere.

Approach

Central features include regular sessions over an extended period in the same woodland site, a high ratio of practitioners to learners, learner-led exploration and play, and the managed introduction of activities such as den building, tool use and campfire cooking. Practitioners are typically trained through accredited forest school qualifications, and risk assessment balances safety with the benefits of managed risk-taking.

Benefits

Research and practitioner reports associate forest school with improvements in confidence, social skills, communication, motivation, physical skills and knowledge of the natural environment. It has been used with children of all ages as well as with adults in contexts including mental-health support.
