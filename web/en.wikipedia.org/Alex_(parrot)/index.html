Alex (parrot)

Alex (May 1976 – 6 September 2007) was a grey parrot and the subject of a thirty-year experiment by animal psychologist Irene Pepperberg, initially at the University of Arizona and later at Harvard University and Brandeis University. When Alex was about one year old, Pepperberg bought him at a pet shop. Shortly before his death, Alex had identified fifty different objects and could count quantities up to six; he was learning the numbers seven and eight, and could report the colour, shape, and material composition of objects presented to him.

Abilities

Alex understood concepts of same and different, bigger and smaller, same and absent, and could combine labels to identify novel objects. He appeared to grasp the concept of zero (or "none") and used labels referentially rather than by rote. Pepperberg trained him using the "model/rival" technique, in which two trainers demonstrated the desired interaction and competed for the parrot's attention.

Death and legacy

Alex died unexpectedly on 6 September 2007 at the age of 31, apparently of natural causes associated with heart disease. His last words to Pepperberg, a nightly routine, were "You be good, I love you. See you tomorrow." The Alex Foundation continues research into avian cognition, notably with the parrot Griffin. Alex's achievements reshaped scientific debate about animal language and cognition, suggesting that "bird brain" capacities could rival those of primates in some tasks.
