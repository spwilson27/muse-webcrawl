Baked beans

Baked beans are a dish traditionally containing white beans (such as haricot or navy beans) that are parboiled and then baked or stewed at a low temperature for a long period in a sauce. Commercially produced baked beans are commonly sold canned in a tomato sauce.

Regional varieties

Boston baked beans, a speciality of New England, are sweetened with molasses and flavoured with salt pork, a tradition linked to colonial-era Sabbath cooking in which beans were baked slowly overnight. In Britain, baked beans in a mild tomato sauce are a staple of the full English breakfast and of beans on toast; British-style recipes are also widespread in Australia and New Zealand. Other varieties include Quebec-style beans cooked with maple syrup, and dishes such as cassoulet-adjacent bean stews and Greek gigantes baked in tomato.

History and production

Bean dishes cooked slowly in earthenware pots derive in part from Native American cooking, in which beans were baked in pits with maple sugar and bear fat. Mass canning in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, popularised by companies such as Heinz, turned baked beans into an inexpensive convenience food. Although called "baked", most commercial canned beans are steamed or cooked in the can rather than oven-baked.
