Biography

Charlton Heston (born John Charles Carter; October 4, 1923 – April 5, 2008) was an American actor and political activist. As a Hollywood leading man through the 1950s and 1960s he became famous for heroic roles in epic films, playing Moses in The Ten Commandments (1956), the title role in Ben-Hur (1959) — for which he won the Academy Award for Best Actor — and Judah Ben-Hur's contemporaries in productions such as El Cid (1961) and The Agony and the Ecstasy (1965).

Career

Heston served in the United States Army Air Forces during the Second World War, then worked in theatre and early television before his film debut in Dark City (1950). Cecil B. DeMille cast him in The Greatest Show on Earth (1952) and then as Moses. Other major roles included Orson Welles's Touch of Evil (1958), astronaut George Taylor in Planet of the Apes (1968), and science-fiction films The Omega Man (1971) and Soylent Green (1973), as well as disaster films such as Earthquake (1974).

Activism

In the 1960s Heston was an outspoken supporter of the civil rights movement and marched with Martin Luther King Jr. at the 1963 March on Washington. His politics later moved rightward; he campaigned for Republican candidates and served five terms as president of the National Rifle Association (1998–2003), famously declaring that gun-control advocates would have to pry his rifle "from my cold, dead hands."

Heston announced in 2002 that he had symptoms consistent with Alzheimer's disease and retired from public life. He died of pneumonia on 5 April 2008 at his home in Beverly Hills, survived by his wife of 64 years, Lydia Clarke, and their two children.
