Ethane

Ethane is an organic chemical compound with chemical formula C2H6. It is the second member of the alkane series after methane, consisting of two carbon atoms joined by a single bond, each bearing three hydrogen atoms. At standard temperature and pressure it is a colourless, odourless gas.

Occurrence and production

Ethane is a major component of natural gas, typically making up 5–10% after methane, and is separated by cryogenic distillation in natural gas processing. It was first synthesised in 1834 by Michael Faraday by electrolysis of potassium acetate, though he mistook it for methane; its identity was clarified by later chemists including Edward Frankland.

Uses

The principal use of ethane is as a feedstock for steam cracking to produce ethylene, one of the world's most important industrial chemicals and the precursor to polyethylene plastics. Ethane is also used as a refrigerant (R-170) and as a fuel, though it is usually more valuable as a petrochemical feedstock.

Properties and chemistry

Ethane melts at −182.8 °C and boils at −88.6 °C. Rotation about the carbon–carbon bond gives staggered and eclipsed conformations, a classic example in conformational analysis, with a rotational barrier of about 12 kJ/mol. Combustion yields carbon dioxide and water. Ethane has been detected in the atmospheres and lakes of Saturn's moon Titan and in comets.

Health and environment

Ethane is not acutely toxic but is flammable and an asphyxiant at high concentrations. It is a greenhouse gas, though less potent and shorter-lived in direct effect than methane. See also Methane, Ethylene and Alkane.
