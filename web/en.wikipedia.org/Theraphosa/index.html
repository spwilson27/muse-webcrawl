Theraphosa is a genus of South American tarantulas (family Theraphosidae) first described by Tamerlan Thorell in 1870. The genus is famous for containing the largest spiders in the world by mass, including the Goliath birdeater, Theraphosa blondi.

Species and size

The genus includes species such as Theraphosa blondi, Theraphosa stirmi (the burgundy Goliath birdeater), and Theraphosa apophysis (the pinkfoot Goliath). T. blondi can reach a leg span of up to about 30 cm (12 in) and a body mass of up to roughly 175 g, making it the heaviest known spider; it is rivalled in leg span only by the giant huntsman spider. Despite the name "birdeater" — originating from an early engraving of one eating a hummingbird — these spiders feed mainly on invertebrates, frogs, rodents, and other small animals.

Behavior and habitat

Theraphosa species are terrestrial, living in burrows in the rainforest floor of northern South America, including Venezuela, Brazil, Guyana, and Suriname. They are nocturnal ambush predators. For defense they possess large fangs, can produce a hissing sound by stridulation, and can flick irritating urticating hairs from the abdomen; their venom is comparatively mild to humans, likened to a wasp sting. Females are long-lived, and the genus is popular, though demanding, in the exotic pet trade.
