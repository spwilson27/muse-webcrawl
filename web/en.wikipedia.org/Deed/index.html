Deed

A deed is a legal document that is signed and delivered, especially one regarding the ownership of property or legal rights. In common law systems, deeds are formal instruments that pass, affirm or confirm an interest, right or property, and are traditionally distinguished from ordinary contracts by their form and by the absence of any need for consideration.

Requirements

Historically a deed had to be written, sealed and delivered; the seal has largely been replaced by requirements that the document be signed, witnessed and state on its face that it is a deed. Delivery in this context means an intention to be bound, not necessarily physical transfer of the document itself.

Deeds for land

The most familiar use is in conveyancing: a transfer deed conveys title to land from a grantor to a grantee. Common varieties include the warranty deed, in which the grantor guarantees title, and the quitclaim deed, which transfers whatever interest the grantor has without guarantees. Many jurisdictions require deeds to be recorded in a public registry to protect the grantee against later claimants.

Other uses

Deeds are also used for deeds of trust, deeds of gift, powers of attorney in deed form, and for changing a person's name by deed poll in the United Kingdom.
