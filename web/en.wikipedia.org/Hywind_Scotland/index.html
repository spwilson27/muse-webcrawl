Hywind Scotland is the world's first commercial wind farm using floating wind turbines, situated 29 kilometres (18 mi) off Peterhead, Aberdeenshire, Scotland. The farm has five 6 MW Siemens direct-drive turbines mounted on spar-buoy floating foundations, giving a total capacity of 30 MW. It is operated by Hywind (Scotland) Limited, a joint venture between Equinor (75%) and Masdar (25%).

History

The project built on the Hywind Demo prototype, a single 2.3 MW floating turbine installed off Norway in 2009 — the world's first full-scale floating wind turbine. In November 2015 it was announced that Statoil (now Equinor) would build the pilot park off the Scottish coast, with the floating foundations assembled at Stord in Norway and towed across the North Sea. The farm began generating power in October 2017 and was officially opened by Scotland's First Minister, Nicola Sturgeon.

Design

Each turbine sits atop a spar-buoy foundation — a long steel cylinder ballasted with water and rock, extending roughly 78 metres below the surface, moored to the seabed with three mooring lines. The turbines stand in water depths of 95–120 metres, beyond the reach of conventional fixed-bottom foundations. Power is exported to shore at Peterhead via a 30 km cable.

Performance

In its first years of operation the farm achieved capacity factors around or above 50%, outperforming typical fixed offshore wind farms, and survived severe North Sea storms including gusts and waves of over 8 metres. Its performance helped spur investment in larger floating projects, including Hywind Tampen in Norway.
