House edge

The house edge is a term used in gambling to describe the mathematical advantage that the casino (the "house") has over the player in any given game. It is expressed as the percentage of each bet that the casino expects to keep in the long run, and is the inverse of the return to player (RTP).

Examples

The house edge varies widely between games and bets. American roulette, with two green zeroes, has a house edge of about 5.26%, while European roulette with a single zero has about 2.70%. Blackjack played with good basic strategy has an edge of roughly 0.5%, while slot machines commonly range from 2% to 15% or more, and keno can exceed 25%. Some specific bets, such as certain craps propositions, carry edges above 10%.

Significance

Because of the law of large numbers, the house edge ensures that a casino profits over many bets even though individual players may win in the short term. The hold, by contrast, is the actual percentage of money wagered that a casino retains, which also depends on how long players gamble and how often they reinvest winnings.
