John Philip Holland

Born29 February 1840, Liscannor, County Clare, Ireland
Died12 August 1914 (aged 74), Newark, New Jersey, U.S.
OccupationEngineer, inventor

John Philip Holland (29 February 1840 – 12 August 1914) was an Irish-American engineer who developed the first submarine to be formally commissioned by the United States Navy, and also built early submarines for the Royal Navy. He is regarded as the father of the modern submarine.

Early life

Born in Liscannor, County Clare, Holland was educated by the Christian Brothers and became a schoolteacher. He emigrated to the United States in 1873, teaching in Paterson, New Jersey, while developing designs for submersible boats — at first secretly funded by Irish republican Fenians, who hoped to use them against the Royal Navy.

Submarine designs

After his small Fenian Ram (1881), Holland persisted through years of rejected proposals. His Holland VI, launched in 1897, combined a petrol engine for surface running, electric motors submerged, hydroplanes for diving and a torpedo tube. The U.S. Navy bought it in 1900 and commissioned it as USS Holland (SS-1); Britain soon ordered five Holland-class boats.

Electric Boat and death

Holland co-founded the Electric Boat Company in 1899 but was pushed out by financiers by 1904 and lost control of his patents. He died in relative obscurity in Newark in 1914, though Electric Boat went on to build submarines for the U.S. Navy for more than a century.
