23S ribosomal RNA

23S ribosomal RNA
TypeRibosomal RNA (rRNA)
LocationLarge subunit (50S) of the prokaryotic ribosome
Length~2,904 nucleotides (in E. coli)
FunctionPeptidyl transferase activity (ribozyme)

The 23S ribosomal RNA is a component of the large (50S) subunit of the prokaryotic ribosome. In Escherichia coli it is a 2,904-nucleotide-long RNA molecule that forms the structural and catalytic core of the subunit, together with the 5S rRNA and dozens of ribosomal proteins.

Catalytic role

The peptidyl transferase activity of the ribosome — the reaction that forms peptide bonds between amino acids during protein synthesis — resides in the 23S rRNA itself. This makes the molecule a ribozyme: an RNA enzyme. Structural studies, recognized by the 2009 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, showed that proteins are absent near the active site, confirming that the ribosome is fundamentally an RNA machine.

Antibiotic target and studies

Many antibiotics, including chloramphenicol, macrolides such as erythromycin, and linezolid, inhibit protein synthesis by binding to the 23S rRNA. Mutations in the 23S rRNA gene are therefore a known mechanism of antibiotic resistance. Because its sequence changes slowly over evolutionary time, 23S rRNA is also used, alongside the 16S rRNA of the small subunit, in phylogenetic studies of bacteria and archaea. In eukaryotes, its counterpart in the 60S subunit is the 28S rRNA.
