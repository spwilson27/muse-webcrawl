Camp Nou (Catalan for "new field"; officially Spotify Camp Nou for sponsorship reasons since 2022) is a football stadium in Barcelona, Spain, and the home of FC Barcelona since its opening in 1957. It is the largest stadium in Europe and among the largest in the world.

History

The stadium replaced the old Camp de Les Corts, made too small by the popularity of the team led by László Kubala. Designed by Francesc Mitjans and Josep Soteras, it opened on 24 September 1957 with a match against a Warsaw XI. It hosted matches at the 1964 European Championship and the 1982 FIFA World Cup, the 1992 Olympic football final, and two European Cup/Champions League finals (1989 and 1999, the latter famous for Manchester United's stoppage-time comeback against Bayern Munich). The stadium museum is one of Catalonia's most visited attractions.

Renovation

Under the Espai Barça project, a comprehensive redevelopment began in earnest in 2023: FC Barcelona moved temporarily to the Estadi Olímpic Lluís Companys while Camp Nou is rebuilt with a roof over all seats and capacity raised to about 105,000, making it the largest football stadium in the world by capacity on completion.
