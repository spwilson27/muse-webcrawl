Plateresque

Plateresque (Spanish: plateresco, "in the manner of a silversmith") is an architectural style that emerged in Spain in the late 15th century and flourished into the 16th century, characterised by facades covered in intricate, finely wrought ornament resembling silverwork.

Characteristics

Plateresque buildings combine Gothic structure with Renaissance and Mudéjar decorative elements: medallions, shields, grotesques, candelieri and profuse floral carving spread across otherwise flat walls, concentrated around doors and windows. The style is often divided into an early "Isabelline" phase and a later, more classically ordered High Plateresque.

Examples

Notable examples include the facade of the University of Salamanca, the Hospital de Santa Cruz in Toledo, and the facade of the Convent of San Marcos in León. The style was carried to Spain's American colonies, where it influenced colonial architecture in Mexico and Peru.
