Mx is an English-language neologistic honorific that does not specify gender. It is used as a gender-neutral alternative to gendered titles such as Mr. and Mrs., typically by people who do not identify within the gender binary, who wish to avoid revealing their gender, or simply as a matter of preference. The title is usually pronounced like "mix".

History

The term was first proposed in the late 1970s. It gained wider use in the United Kingdom in the 2010s, where it came to be accepted by many government bodies, banks and businesses, and it was added to the Oxford English Dictionary in 2015. In the United States, the title has been adopted by some institutions, airlines and universities, and it appears as an option on various official forms and identification documents.

Usage

Supporters regard Mx as an inclusive complement to the existing courtesy titles, comparable to the introduction of "Ms." in the twentieth century. Adoption varies by jurisdiction and organisation; where offered, it is listed among the standard title options on applications, accounts and correspondence.
