Completing the square

In elementary algebra, completing the square is a technique for converting a quadratic polynomial of the form

ax² + bx + c

to the form

a(x − h)² + k

for some values of h and k. In other words, completing the square places a perfect square trinomial inside a quadratic expression.

A geometric interpretation of completing the square

Overview

The technique is used for solving quadratic equations, graphing quadratic functions, evaluating integrals in calculus (such as Gaussian integrals), and finding Laplace transforms. The name comes from the geometric picture: an expression like x² + bx can be pictured as an incomplete square, to which a corner piece (b/2)² is added to "complete" it.

Basic technique

For a monic polynomial x² + bx + c, one rewrites:

x² + bx + c = (x + b/2)² + (c − b²/4)

For the general case ax² + bx + c, the formula is:

ax² + bx + c = a(x + b/(2a))² + (c − b²/(4a))

Derivation of the quadratic formula

Applying the technique to the general quadratic equation ax² + bx + c = 0 and solving for x yields the quadratic formula:

x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / (2a)

Graphing

Writing a quadratic in vertex form a(x − h)² + k immediately reveals the vertex (h, k) of its parabola, making sketching and analysis straightforward.

History

The method goes back to ancient Babylonian and Greek geometry and was systematised by al-Khwarizmi in the 9th century, whose geometric figures of squares with added rectangles gave algebra its name-giving treatise.
