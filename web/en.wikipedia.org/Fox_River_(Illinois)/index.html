Fox River (Illinois)

The Fox River is a 202-mile-long (325 km) tributary of the Illinois River, flowing through southeastern Wisconsin and northeastern Illinois in the United States. It should not be confused with the Fox River in Wisconsin that flows into Green Bay.

Course

The river rises near Colgate in Washington County, Wisconsin, and flows generally southward through Waukesha, Racine, and Kenosha counties before entering Illinois. In Illinois it passes through the Chain O'Lakes region and the Fox Valley communities of McHenry, Algonquin, Elgin, St. Charles, Geneva, Batavia, Aurora, Montgomery, Oswego, and Yorkville. It joins the Illinois River at Ottawa, Illinois.

History and use

The Fox River valley was home to Native American peoples, including the Fox (Meskwaki) tribe for whom the river is named, long before European settlement. During the nineteenth century the river powered gristmills and factories that spurred the growth of the Fox Valley cities. A series of dams built for navigation, water power, and flood control transformed the river into a chain of pooled reaches. Today the river is used extensively for recreation, including boating, fishing, and kayaking, and its banks are lined with trails and parks in the Fox River Valley.
