FoxPro is a text-based, procedurally oriented programming language and database management system, originally published by Fox Software as FoxBASE in 1984 and derived from the dBase language. Microsoft acquired Fox Software in 1992 and developed the product into Visual FoxPro, adding object-oriented programming and a visual development environment, tightly coupled with its own relational database engine.

Features

FoxPro was known for its fast local database engine, the Rushmore query optimization technology, an integrated development environment with form and report designers, and a language that combined xBase commands with embedded SQL. It was widely used for business applications, particularly in small and medium-sized organizations, and developed a loyal developer community.

End of development

Microsoft released the final version, Visual FoxPro 9.0, in 2004, with service packs following; in 2007 it announced there would be no Visual FoxPro 10, and mainstream support ended in 2010 (extended support in 2015). Despite its discontinuation, a community of developers continued maintaining applications and tools, and Microsoft released parts of the product's extensions as open source ("Sedna" add-ons).
