Operation Cobra was an offensive launched by the First United States Army under Lieutenant General Omar Bradley during the Normandy campaign of World War II. Beginning on 25 July 1944, seven weeks after the D-Day landings, it achieved the decisive Allied breakout from the Normandy lodgement into open country, leading to the liberation of Brittany and the encirclement of German forces in the Falaise pocket.

American armour advancing through the bocage during the breakout.

Background

After D-Day (6 June 1944), Allied progress in Normandy was slowed by the dense hedgerow ("bocage") country and strong German resistance. The British-Canadian attacks around Caen (Operations Goodwood and Atlantic) drew German armour east, while the American capture of Saint-Lô cleared the way for a concentrated breakthrough in the west.

The offensive

Cobra opened with a massive aerial bombardment by some 3,000 aircraft along a narrow front west of Saint-Lô; short-falling bombs killed over 100 American soldiers, including Lieutenant General Lesley J. McNair, the highest-ranking American officer killed in Normandy. Despite the disaster, the bombardment shattered the Panzer Lehr Division, and American infantry and armour poured through. By 28 July Coutances was liberated, and on 31 July Avranches fell, opening the gateway to Brittany. General George S. Patton's Third Army was activated and raced through the gap.

Aftermath

The German counterattack at Mortain (Operation Lüttich) failed, and the retreating German armies were largely destroyed in the Falaise pocket in August. Paris was liberated on 25 August 1944. Cobra is regarded as one of the decisive operations of the Western Front.
