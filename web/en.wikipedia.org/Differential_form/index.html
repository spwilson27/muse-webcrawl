Differential form

In mathematics, a differential form is an object in differential geometry and multilinear algebra that provides a coordinate-independent framework for calculus on manifolds. A differential k-form can be integrated over a k-dimensional surface; its algebra (the wedge product) and its derivative (the exterior derivative) unify the gradient, curl and divergence of vector calculus and the theorems of Green, Gauss and Stokes into the single generalised Stokes theorem.

A 1-form assigns a linear functional to each tangent space.

Basic concepts

A 0-form is simply a smooth function. A 1-form, such as f(x,y) dx + g(x,y) dy, eats a vector and returns a number; in coordinates, forms are written as sums of wedge products of the coordinate differentials dxi, which anticommute: dx ∧ dy = −dy ∧ dx. The exterior derivative d raises the degree by one and satisfies d² = 0, giving the de Rham complex whose cohomology measures the "holes" of a manifold.

Applications

Differential forms simplify integration over curves, surfaces and volumes, and the change-of-variables formula falls out naturally from the wedge product. In physics, electromagnetism is elegantly expressed with the field 2-form F and its potential 1-form A, with Maxwell's equations becoming dF = 0 and d⋆F = J. Forms also appear in Hamiltonian mechanics (the symplectic 2-form) and in modern gauge theory.
