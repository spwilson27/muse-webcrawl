Tabs of the United States Army

In the United States Army, tabs are cloth insignia worn on the shoulder sleeve to denote a special skill, qualification or unit designation. Individual skill tabs sit above a soldier's unit patch (shoulder sleeve insignia) on the left shoulder; unit tabs form an integral part of certain units' insignia.

Individual skill tabs

Four permanent individual tabs are authorised in order of precedence: the President's Hundred tab (for marksmanship competition winners), the Special Forces tab, the Ranger tab (for graduates of Ranger School), and the Sapper tab (for graduates of the Sapper Leader Course). A soldier who earns the Special Forces, Ranger and Sapper tabs is sometimes informally said to hold the "triple canopy" or similar nickname. Wearing all three alongside airborne qualification has its own slang among soldiers.

Unit tabs

Unit tabs include the Airborne tab, worn as part of the insignia of airborne units by all assigned personnel regardless of individual parachutist qualification; the Mountain tab of the 10th Mountain Division; and the Arctic tab worn in Alaska. Honor guard and other distinctive unit tabs also exist.

History

The practice began during the Second World War with the airborne tab; the Ranger tab dates from the Korean War era, and the Special Forces tab was authorised in 1983 when the Special Forces branch was formalised.
