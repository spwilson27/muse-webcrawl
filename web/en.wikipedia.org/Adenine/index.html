Adenine

Chemical formula: C5H5N5
Molar mass: 135.13 g/mol
Classification: Purine nucleobase
Base pairs with: Thymine (DNA), uracil (RNA)

Adenine (symbol A) is a purine nucleobase. It is one of the four nucleobases in the nucleic acid DNA, where it pairs with thymine through two hydrogen bonds, and in RNA, where it pairs with uracil. Adenine also forms part of many biologically important compounds.

Biological roles

Adenine is a component of adenosine triphosphate (ATP), the principal energy currency of the cell, and of the coenzymes NAD, FAD, and coenzyme A, as well as the signaling molecule cyclic AMP. Attached to ribose it forms the nucleoside adenosine; attached to deoxyribose, deoxyadenosine.

History and synthesis

Adenine was named by Albrecht Kossel in 1885, from the Greek aden ("gland"), after its isolation from the pancreas. It can be formed prebiotically from hydrogen cyanide (HCN) polymerization, and has been detected in meteorites, which is of interest for theories of the origin of life.
