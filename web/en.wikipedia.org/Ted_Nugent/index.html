Ted Nugent

Theodore Anthony Nugent (born December 13, 1948) is an American guitarist, singer, songwriter, and political activist. He initially gained fame as the lead guitarist and occasional vocalist of the Amboy Dukes, a psychedelic rock band formed in 1963. He embarked on a solo career in 1975, releasing a string of successful albums including Ted Nugent (1975), Free-for-All (1976), and Cat Scratch Fever (1977), the title track of which became his signature song.

Early life

Nugent was born in Redford, Michigan, and raised in the Detroit area. He began performing in his teens and formed the Amboy Dukes in Chicago in 1964. The band scored a hit in 1968 with "Journey to the Center of the Mind."

Solo career

After the Amboy Dukes dissolved, Nugent signed with Epic Records and became one of the top-grossing concert acts of the late 1970s, known for high-energy performances and guitar showmanship. His multi-platinum albums included Double Live Gonzo! (1978) and Weekend Warriors (1978). In the late 1980s he joined the supergroup Damn Yankees, which released two successful albums.

Activism and public image

Nugent is known for his outspoken conservative political views, advocacy for gun rights and hunting, and service on the board of the National Rifle Association. He has hosted outdoor and hunting television programs, including Ted Nugent Spirit of the Wild, and has authored several books. His statements on political and social issues have frequently drawn public controversy.
