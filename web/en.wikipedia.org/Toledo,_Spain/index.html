Toledo is a city and municipality in central Spain, the capital of the province of Toledo and of the autonomous community of Castilla–La Mancha. Set on a granite hill encircled on three sides by the Tagus River, it was the capital of the Visigothic kingdom, a major city of Islamic al-Andalus, and for centuries the seat of the Spanish court and the primate of the Spanish Church. Its historic centre, declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1986, preserves an extraordinary layering of Christian, Muslim and Jewish monuments, earning Toledo the name "City of the Three Cultures".

History

Roman Toletum was taken by the Visigoths, who made it their capital in the 6th century under Liuvigild; the councils of Toledo shaped the Visigothic church. After the Muslim conquest of 711 the city, known as Ṭulayṭula, was a frontier city of al-Andalus, famous for its silk, swords and scholarship, and frequently rebellious against Córdoba. In 1085 Alfonso VI of Castile took Toledo, a turning point in the Reconquista. Under Christian rule the Toledo School of Translators rendered Arabic and Greek learning into Latin, transmitting science and philosophy to medieval Europe. Toledo became an imperial city under Charles V, but in 1561 Philip II moved the court to Madrid and the city declined, retaining its religious primacy as seat of the Primate of Spain.

Main sights

The Alcázar, a fortress-palace on the highest point of the city, dominates the skyline; it was largely rebuilt after its siege during the Spanish Civil War. Toledo Cathedral (1226–1493) is one of the masterpieces of Spanish Gothic, with a celebrated sacristy containing works by El Greco, Goya and Van Dyck. Other monuments include the mosques of Cristo de la Luz and Tornerías, the synagogues of Santa María la Blanca and El Tránsito, the Monastery of San Juan de los Reyes, and the Hospital de Tavera. The painter El Greco spent his maturity in Toledo; his View of Toledo and The Burial of the Count of Orgaz, in the church of Santo Tomé, are among the city's treasures.

Crafts and cuisine

Toledo is renowned for damascene metalwork, sword-making — its blades famed since Roman times — and marzipan, traditionally made by convent nuns. It lies some 70 km south of Madrid and is connected by high-speed rail in about half an hour.
