Jean-François Champollion (1790–1832) was a French philologist who deciphered the Egyptian hieroglyphs, unlocking the written records of ancient Egypt. Building on the work of Thomas Young and others, he used the Rosetta Stone and his knowledge of Coptic to show that hieroglyphic writing combined phonetic and ideographic signs.

Decipherment

A child prodigy in languages, Champollion studied Coptic and compared the scripts on the Rosetta Stone. In 1822 he announced his breakthrough in the Lettre à M. Dacier, presenting phonetic values for hieroglyphic signs; his Précis du système hiéroglyphique followed in 1824. Legend holds that on making the key discovery he rushed to his brother crying "Je tiens mon affaire!" and collapsed.

Later career

Champollion became curator of the Egyptian collections at the Louvre, led a Franco-Tuscan expedition to Egypt in 1828–1829, and was appointed to a chair of Egyptian history and archaeology at the Collège de France in 1831. Exhausted by overwork, he died in 1832 at age 41; his Egyptian grammar and dictionary were published posthumously.
