Life estate
Interest in real property lasting for the duration of a person's life; on that person's death the property reverts or passes to the remainderman.

A life estate is an interest in land that lasts for the lifetime of a given person, the measuring life. The holder, the life tenant, may use and profit from the property during that lifetime but cannot dispose of the underlying fee simple. On the death of the measuring life, the estate terminates and possession passes to the remainderman or reverts to the grantor.

Creation and types

Life estates are most commonly created by deed or will, for example where a testator leaves a house to a spouse for life, remainder to children. A life estate pur autre vie is measured by the life of someone other than the tenant. The doctrine of waste obliges the life tenant to maintain the property and not permanently impair its value.

Uses

Life estates are used in estate planning to provide for a surviving partner while preserving the property for heirs, and in some jurisdictions to protect a home from certain creditors or to qualify for benefits. Drawbacks include difficulty selling or mortgaging the property, since a buyer can acquire only an interest ending at the measuring life's death.
