USAID

United States Agency for International Development
Formed: November 3, 1961
Headquarters: Ronald Reagan Building, Washington, D.C.
Parent department: U.S. Department of State
Motto: "From the American People"

The United States Agency for International Development (USAID) was an independent agency of the United States government primarily responsible for administering civilian foreign aid and development assistance. Established by President John F. Kennedy in 1961 under the Foreign Assistance Act, it was one of the largest official aid agencies in the world.

History

USAID grew out of post-war assistance programs including the Marshall Plan. For decades it funded programs in global health, food security, disaster relief, education, democracy promotion, and economic development in more than 100 countries.

Programs

Major initiatives included PEPFAR support for HIV/AIDS relief, Feed the Future for food security, and humanitarian assistance delivered through the Bureau for Humanitarian Assistance.

2025 restructuring

In 2025, the second Trump administration moved to dismantle the agency, placing most staff on leave and folding remaining functions into the Department of State.
