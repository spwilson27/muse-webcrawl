Ekadashi

Ekadashi is a day of fasting sacred to Vishnu

Ekadashi (Sanskrit: एकादशी, "the eleventh") is the eleventh lunar day (tithi) of each of the two lunar phases in the Hindu calendar — the Shukla Paksha (waxing fortnight) and the Krishna Paksha (waning fortnight). It is a day traditionally devoted to fasting and worship, especially of the god Vishnu.

Observance

Devotees observe a fast on Ekadashi, abstaining wholly or partially from food, and traditionally avoiding grains and beans entirely. The fast is typically broken on the following day, Dwadashi, within a prescribed time window. Observance is particularly emphasised by Vaishnavas, for whom Ekadashi is among the most important vratas (vows). The twenty-four Ekadashis of the year each carry their own name and legend, recounted in the Puranas; especially significant ones include Vaikuntha Ekadashi, Shayani Ekadashi, and Prabodhini Ekadashi.

Significance

According to the legend told in the Padma Purana, the demon Mura was defeated by a goddess who emerged from Vishnu's body while he slept — she was named Ekadashi. Fasting on this day is believed to confer spiritual merit, cleanse sins, and aid devotion and self-discipline.
