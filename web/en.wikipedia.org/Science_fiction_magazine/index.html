Science fiction magazine

A science fiction magazine is a publication that offers primarily science fiction, either in a hard-copy periodical format or on the Internet. Science fiction magazines traditionally featured short stories, novellas, and serial novels by established and new authors, along with editorials, reviews, and reader letter columns. The magazines were the cradle of the genre in the 20th century: most major science fiction writers began their careers selling short fiction to them.

History

The first magazine devoted entirely to science fiction was Amazing Stories, launched by Hugo Gernsback in 1926. It was followed by competitors such as Astounding Stories (later Analog), Wonder Stories, and Weird Tales (which mixed fantasy and horror). Under editor John W. Campbell, Astounding dominated the "Golden Age" of the late 1930s and 1940s, publishing early work by Isaac Asimov, Robert A. Heinlein, and Arthur C. Clarke. In the 1950s, The Magazine of Fantasy and Science Fiction and Galaxy Science Fiction set higher literary standards.

Digest era and decline

After the pulp era, most science fiction magazines adopted digest size. The paperback boom and the growth of television drew readers away, and newsstand distribution declined through the late 20th century. Long-running surviving titles include Analog, Asimov's Science Fiction (founded 1977), and The Magazine of Fantasy and Science Fiction.

Online magazines

In the 21st century, professional online magazines such as Clarkesworld, Lightspeed, Strange Horizons, Apex, and Uncanny Magazine became major venues, several of them winning Hugo Awards; eligibility for the Hugo Award and Nebula Award for short fiction is closely tied to magazine publication, print or electronic.
