ZDF

ZDF, short for Zweites Deutsches Fernsehen (German: "Second German Television"), is a German public-service television broadcaster based in Mainz, Rhineland-Palatinate. It is run as an independent non-profit institution, founded by all federal states of Germany (Bundesländer). ZDF is financed by television licence fees and advertising revenue.

The broadcaster began transmitting on 1 April 1963 from Eschborn near Frankfurt am Main. It is one of the largest public broadcasters in Europe and operates the main channel ZDF as well as, in cooperation with ARD and others, channels such as 3sat, Arte, Phoenix, KiKa and the digital channels ZDFneo and ZDFinfo.

History

ZDF was created after a 1961 ruling of the Federal Constitutional Court struck down plans for a federally controlled second television channel. The Länder instead established ZDF by interstate treaty, and broadcasting began in 1963. The broadcaster later moved to its present site on the Lerchenberg in Mainz. Historically, ZDF programmes began with a distinctive test card and were anchored by long-running formats such as the heute news bulletin, launched in 1963.

Programming

ZDF is known for news programmes such as heute, the current-affairs magazine Frontal, the crime series Der Alte and SOKO, the long-running entertainment show Wetten, dass..?, and satirical programmes including the heute-show and ZDF Magazin Royale. The station's idents feature the "Mainzelmännchen", six cartoon gnome characters created in 1963.
