Major League Baseball All-Star Game

First played1933 (Comiskey Park, Chicago)
FrequencyAnnual (July)
TeamsAmerican League vs National League
NicknameMidsummer Classic

The Major League Baseball All-Star Game, also known as the Midsummer Classic, is an annual professional baseball game sanctioned by Major League Baseball (MLB) between all-star teams representing the American League (AL) and the National League (NL). It is traditionally held in mid-July, during the All-Star break, and marks roughly the halfway point of the MLB season.

History

The first All-Star Game was played on July 6, 1933, at Comiskey Park in Chicago as part of the 1933 World's Fair, and was won by the American League, highlighted by a home run from Babe Ruth. Fans vote for the starting position players, while pitchers and reserves are selected by players and managers. From 2003 to 2016, the winning league earned home-field advantage in the World Series, a rule introduced after the 2002 game ended in a tie; since 2017 the game has returned to exhibition status.

All-Star Week

The game is the centrepiece of All-Star Week festivities, which include the Home Run Derby, the Futures Game and celebrity softball. The Most Valuable Player of the game receives an award named after Ted Williams.
