Ram Janmabhoomi

LocationAyodhya, Uttar Pradesh, India
DeityRama (Ram Lalla)
SignificanceBelieved birthplace of Rama

Ram Janmabhoomi (literally, "birthplace of Rama") is the site in Ayodhya, Uttar Pradesh, India, that Hindus believe to be the birthplace of Rama, the seventh avatar of the god Vishnu. The site stands on and around the location of the Babri Masjid, a mosque built in 1528–29 on the orders of the Mughal emperor Babur, whose demolition in 1992 and the legal dispute over the land became one of the most consequential controversies in modern Indian history.

History

According to Hindu tradition, Ayodhya was the capital of the ancient kingdom of Kosala and the setting of the Ramayana. Historians note that the identification of the mosque site as Rama's birthplace gained prominence in the 19th century. In 1949, idols of Rama appeared inside the mosque, and the site was locked and placed under court receivership.

Ayodhya dispute

In the 1980s the Vishva Hindu Parishad launched a movement to build a Rama temple at the site. On 6 December 1992, a large crowd of Hindu activists demolished the Babri Masjid, triggering nationwide riots. The ensuing title suits were resolved by the Supreme Court of India on 9 November 2019, which awarded the disputed 2.77-acre land to a trust for temple construction and allotted five acres elsewhere in Ayodhya for a mosque.

Ram Mandir

The foundation stone for the new Ram Mandir was laid on 5 August 2020, and the temple was consecrated (pran pratishtha) on 22 January 2024 by Prime Minister Narendra Modi. Built in the Nagara style of temple architecture, the temple is a centrepiece of a large-scale redevelopment of Ayodhya.
