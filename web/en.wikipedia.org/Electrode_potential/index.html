Electrode potential

In electrochemistry, electrode potential is the electromotive force of an electrochemical cell built from a standard reference electrode and the electrode under investigation. By convention, the standard hydrogen electrode is assigned a potential of exactly 0.00 volts.

Standard electrode potential

The standard electrode potential (E°) of a half-reaction is measured under standard conditions: solutes at 1 mol/L, gases at 1 bar, at 25 °C. Tabulated values predict the direction of redox reactions: a species with a more positive reduction potential is a stronger oxidising agent.

Origin

When a metal electrode is immersed in a solution of its ions, a charge separation develops at the interface until the electrochemical potential of the electrons in the metal balances the chemical potential of the ions in solution.

Nernst equation

Under non-standard conditions the potential is given by the Nernst equation, E = E° − (RT/nF) ln Q, relating potential to the reaction quotient.

Applications

Electrode potentials are used to design batteries, predict corrosion, and rank the reactivity of metals in the electrochemical series.
