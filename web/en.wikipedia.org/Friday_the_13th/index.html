Friday the 13th

Friday the 13th is considered an unlucky day in Western superstition. It occurs when the 13th day of a month falls on a Friday, which happens at least once a year and can occur up to three times in a single calendar year. The irrational fear of the day is sometimes called paraskevidekatriaphobia.

Origins

The superstition appears to combine two older strands: the belief that Friday is an unlucky day (associated in Christian tradition with the Crucifixion) and the belief that thirteen is an unlucky number. The specific combination is not clearly documented before the nineteenth century. A frequently repeated claim connects the day to the arrest of the Knights Templar on Friday, 13 October 1307, but historians regard this link, popularized by twentieth-century fiction, as unsupported by medieval evidence.

Social and economic effects

Studies have found that some people avoid travel, business decisions, and major purchases on Friday the 13th, producing measurable short-term economic effects. Conversely, some research has found fewer traffic accidents on the day in certain countries, possibly because people drive more carefully; other studies have found the opposite.

Folklore and culture

The day has inspired a long-running horror film franchise of the same name, beginning with the 1980 film Friday the 13th, as well as novels, songs, and a name for the phobia associated with the number thirteen, triskaidekaphobia.
