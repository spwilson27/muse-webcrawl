Pawhuska, Oklahoma


Downtown Pawhuska

Pawhuska is a city in and the county seat of Osage County, Oklahoma, United States. It was named after the 19th-century Osage chief Paw-Hiu-Skah, whose name means "White Hair". It is the capital of the Osage Nation and sits within the Osage Nation Reservation, near the Tallgrass Prairie Preserve. The population was about 3,500 at the 2020 census.

History

The town was founded in 1872 when the Osage Agency was established on Bird Creek, after the Osage purchased their reservation from the Cherokee. A trading post and post office followed, and the name was rendered Pawhuska. Oil discovered beneath Osage land in the early 1900s turned Pawhuska into a boomtown; during the 1920s the Osage became, per capita, among the wealthiest people in the world, and the town hosted auctions of oil leases beneath its "Million Dollar Elm". This era, and the notorious Osage Indian murders, were chronicled in David Grann's book Killers of the Flower Moon (2017), adapted into a 2023 film.

Culture

Pawhuska is home to the Osage Nation Museum, the oldest tribally owned museum in the United States, and the historic Constantine Theater. In recent years the town has drawn visitors through Ree Drummond's Pioneer Woman Mercantile, opened in 2016. The Ben Johnson Cowboy Museum and annual rodeo events celebrate the region's ranching heritage.

Geography

Pawhuska lies in north-central Oklahoma, roughly 57 miles northwest of Tulsa, on the edge of the tallgrass prairie ecosystem that the nearby Tallgrass Prairie Preserve protects — the largest remaining tract of tallgrass prairie in the world, with free-roaming bison.
