Boric acid, more specifically orthoboric acid, is a weak, monobasic Lewis acid of boron with the chemical formula H₃BO₃, often written B(OH)₃. It is a white crystalline solid that occurs naturally as the mineral sassolite and is produced industrially mainly by reacting borax (sodium tetraborate) with a mineral acid such as hydrochloric acid.

Unlike typical Brønsted acids, boric acid does not donate a proton directly; instead it accepts a hydroxide ion from water, releasing a hydronium ion — making it a Lewis acid. In the solid state, flat B(OH)₃ molecules are linked by hydrogen bonds into layers, which give the crystals their soft, flaky character.

Uses

Boric acid has a wide range of applications. It is used as an antiseptic and eyewash ingredient, as an insecticide (particularly against cockroaches and ants), in the manufacture of borosilicate glass and ceramics, as a flame retardant and wood preservative, in detergents and cosmetics (as a buffer), and in nuclear power plants, where dissolved boric acid is used to absorb neutrons and help control the rate of fission. It is also a precursor to other boron compounds, including borax and boron carbide.

Safety

Boric acid is of low acute toxicity by ingestion in small quantities but is toxic in larger doses, particularly to infants, and chronic exposure can cause kidney damage. It has been classified in the European Union as a Category 1B reproductive toxicant, and its use in food is prohibited in most countries, though it was historically used as a food preservative.
