The Inter-American Court of Human Rights (Spanish: Corte Interamericana de Derechos Humanos) is an international court based in San José, Costa Rica. It was established in 1979 under the American Convention on Human Rights ("Pact of San José", adopted 1969, in force 1978) to interpret and apply the Convention, together with the Inter-American Commission on Human Rights forming the human rights protection system of the Organization of American States (OAS).

Functions

The Court exercises two principal functions. In its contentious jurisdiction, it hears cases alleging violations of the Convention by states parties that have accepted its jurisdiction; only states and the Inter-American Commission may submit cases, though alleged victims participate directly in proceedings. Its judgments are binding and include reparations — compensation, restitution, guarantees of non-repetition and orders to amend laws. In its advisory jurisdiction, OAS member states and organs may request interpretations of the Convention and of other human-rights treaties in the inter-American system.

Landmark jurisprudence

The Court's first contentious judgment, Velásquez Rodríguez v. Honduras (1988), established states' duty to prevent, investigate and punish human-rights violations, including forced disappearances. Later cases developed jurisprudence on amnesty laws (Barrios Altos v. Peru, 2001), indigenous land rights (Awas Tingni v. Nicaragua, 2001), freedom of expression, judicial guarantees, and the prohibition of the death penalty's expansion. Twenty states currently recognize the Court's contentious jurisdiction; some states have withdrawn or denounced the Convention in the past, including Trinidad and Tobago (1998) and Venezuela (2012).

Composition

The Court consists of seven judges, nationals of OAS member states, elected by the states parties to the Convention for six-year terms (renewable once). Judges sit in their individual capacity. Sessions are held at the seat in San José and, periodically, in other countries of the region.
