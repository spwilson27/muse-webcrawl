Edaphology

Edaphology (from Greek ἔδαφος, edaphos, "ground", and -λογία, -logia) is one of two main divisions of soil science, the other being pedology. Edaphology studies the influence of soils on living organisms, particularly plants, and how soils affect the growth of crops and other vegetation used by humans.

FieldSoil science
FocusSoil as a medium for plant growth
Related disciplinePedology
ApplicationsAgriculture, land use, forestry

Edaphologists study soil in relation to the organisms that depend on it.

Scope

While pedology concentrates on soil formation, morphology, and classification in the natural environment, edaphology looks at soil as a habitat and growing medium. Edaphologists examine soil fertility, nutrient cycling, soil moisture, and the physical and chemical properties that determine how plants and soil organisms live. The distinction dates to the late 19th and early 20th centuries, when Russian scientists such as Vasily Dokuchaev laid the foundations of modern soil science.

Edaphic factors

Properties of the soil that influence the distribution and growth of organisms are called edaphic factors. These include soil texture, structure, pH, organic matter content, salinity, and water-holding capacity. Certain plant communities, such as those on serpentine soils or chalk grasslands, are strongly shaped by edaphic conditions.

Applications

Edaphological knowledge underpins soil fertility management, irrigation planning, land evaluation, and the remediation of degraded or contaminated land. It is closely connected with agronomy and ecology.
