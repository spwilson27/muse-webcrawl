Composite number

A composite number is a positive integer that can be formed by multiplying two smaller positive integers other than 1 and itself. Equivalently, it is a positive integer that has at least one divisor other than 1 and itself. Every positive integer is composite, prime, or the unit 1, so the composite numbers are exactly the numbers that are not prime and not a unit. For example, 14 is composite because it is the product of 2 × 7, while 13 is prime.

Types of composite numbers

One way to classify composite numbers is by counting their prime factors. A composite number with exactly two prime factors (counted with multiplicity in some definitions, or a semiprime when exactly two) is called a semiprime if the two factors may be equal. Numbers that are the product of three primes are sphenic numbers. Composites can also be classified by the parity of the number of prime factors: numbers with an odd number of prime factors relate to the Liouville function.

Properties

  • Every composite number can be written as the product of two or more (not necessarily distinct) primes, by the fundamental theorem of arithmetic.
  • All even numbers greater than 2 are composite.
  • The smallest composite number is 4.
  • If a composite number is a perfect square, it has an odd number of divisors.

Highly composite numbers

A highly composite number has more divisors than any smaller positive integer. Examples include 12, 24, 36, 48, 60 and 120, which were favoured in historical measurement systems for their divisibility.
