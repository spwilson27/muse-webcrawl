Faust is an opera in five acts by Charles Gounod to a French libretto by Jules Barbier and Michel Carré, from Carré's play Faust et Marguerite, in turn loosely based on Johann Wolfgang von Goethe's Faust, Part One. It debuted at the Théâtre Lyrique on the Boulevard du Temple in Paris on 19 March 1859, with spoken dialogue; the composer later added recitatives and, for an 1869 production at the Paris Opéra, the Walpurgis Night ballet. For a time Faust was among the most frequently performed operas in the world — it was the inaugural production of the Metropolitan Opera in New York in 1883.

Synopsis

The aged scholar Faust, despairing of a wasted life, summons Méphistophélès and sells his soul in exchange for youth and the love of Marguerite. Restored to youth, Faust woos Marguerite with a casket of jewels; she falls in love with him but is abandoned, pregnant and shunned. Her brother Valentin, returning from war, curses her and is killed by Faust in a duel arranged by Méphistophélès. In prison, Marguerite, driven mad, rejects Faust's attempt to rescue her and dies as angels proclaim her saved, while Méphistophélès drags Faust away.

Music

Celebrated numbers include Marguerite's "Jewel Song" (Ah! je ris de me voir si belle), Faust's cavatina Salut! demeure chaste et pure, Méphistophélès's mocking Ronde du veau d'or ("Song of the Golden Calf"), Valentin's aria Avant de quitter ces lieux, and the Soldiers' Chorus Gloire immortelle de nos aïeux. Gounod's lyrical style shifted attention from Goethe's metaphysics to the love tragedy of Faust and Marguerite — in German-speaking countries the opera is often billed as Margarethe to distinguish it from Goethe's play.
