Guanyin (Chinese: 觀音; pinyin: Guānyīn) is a bodhisattva associated with compassion as venerated by Mahayana Buddhists in East Asia. She is the East Asian form of the Indian bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara. The name Guanyin is short for Guanshiyin, meaning "[The One Who] Perceives the Sounds of the World" — she hears the cries of suffering beings and rushes to their aid.

Origins and spread

Devotion to Avalokiteśvara travelled from India to China along the Silk Road, where the bodhisattva gradually took on female form — a transformation largely complete by the Song dynasty. The Lotus Sutra, which teaches that Avalokiteśvara can assume any form needed to save beings, provided scriptural support for the bodhisattva's many manifestations. Guanyin became one of the most beloved figures in Chinese Buddhism and folk religion, often addressed as a goddess of mercy.

Iconography

Guanyin is commonly depicted in white robes, holding a vase of pure water, a willow branch, or a lotus. In her thousand-armed and thousand-eyed form she symbolises all-seeing, all-reaching compassion. A popular legend tells of Princess Miaoshan, who sacrificed her own eyes and arms to cure her father and was revealed as an incarnation of Guanyin.

Worship

Mount Putuo in Zhejiang province, an island mountain, is regarded as Guanyin's bodhimaṇḍa (sacred seat) and a major pilgrimage site corresponding to Avalokiteśvara's Potalaka. In Japan she is Kannon, in Korea Gwaneum, in Vietnam Quan Âm. She is also venerated by sailors as a protector against the perils of the sea.
