The Jimi Hendrix Experience

The Jimi Hendrix Experience was an English-American rock band formed in London in October 1966 by the American guitarist, singer and songwriter Jimi Hendrix, with English musicians Noel Redding (bass) and Mitch Mitchell (drums). Despite an active career of less than four years, the trio is widely regarded as one of the most influential rock acts of all time, popularising the psychedelic rock and hard rock sounds of the late 1960s.

History

Hendrix was brought to London by manager Chas Chandler of the Animals, who auditioned Redding and Mitchell to complete the power trio. Their first singles — "Hey Joe", "Purple Haze" and "The Wind Cries Mary" — were major UK hits, and their debut album Are You Experienced (1967) is considered a landmark of psychedelic rock. The band's explosive performance at the Monterey Pop Festival in June 1967, climaxing with Hendrix setting his guitar alight, made them stars in the United States.

Two further studio albums followed: Axis: Bold as Love (1967) and the double album Electric Ladyland (1968), which reached number one in the U.S. and included "Voodoo Child (Slight Return)" and Hendrix's hit cover of Bob Dylan's "All Along the Watchtower". Friction over studio control and touring led Redding to leave in 1969; the original trio gave its last performances in mid-1970, and Hendrix died in London on 18 September 1970.

Legacy

The Experience was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1992. Hendrix's virtuosic, feedback-laden guitar innovations with the band — heard on songs like "Foxy Lady", "Fire" and "Little Wing" — reshaped the role of the electric guitar in rock music.
