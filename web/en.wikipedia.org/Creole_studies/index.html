Creole studies

Creole studies (or creolistics) is the academic field concerned with creole languages: languages that develop from pidgins — simplified contact languages — into full native languages spoken by a community. The field studies how creoles form, their grammars, and their social and historical contexts, often in situations shaped by colonization, slavery, and trade.

Key questions

Creolists debate how creoles arise: whether through gradual nativization of a pidgin, through abrupt creation by children (as in Derek Bickerton's language bioprogram hypothesis), or through ordinary processes of language change and second-language acquisition. The field also examines decreolization, where a creole converges toward its lexifier language.

Examples

Well-studied creoles include Haitian Creole (French-based), Jamaican Patois (English-based), Tok Pisin (Papua New Guinea), and Papiamento (Caribbean). Creole studies intersects with linguistics, anthropology, history, and postcolonial studies.
