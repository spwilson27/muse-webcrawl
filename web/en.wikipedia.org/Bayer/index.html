Bayer

Bayer AG is a German multinational pharmaceutical and biotechnology company and one of the largest pharmaceutical companies in the world. Headquartered in Leverkusen, North Rhine-Westphalia, its areas of business include consumer healthcare products, pharmaceutical drugs, agricultural chemicals, seeds and biotechnology products. The company is famous for commercializing aspirin in 1899.

History

Bayer was founded in 1863 as a dyestuffs factory by Friedrich Bayer and Johann Friedrich Weskott. Its scientists synthesized acetylsalicylic acid in pure form in 1897 (Felix Hoffmann), and the company marketed it as Aspirin from 1899. Bayer became part of the IG Farben conglomerate in 1925 and was re-established as an independent company after the Second World War, when IG Farben was broken up by the Allies.

Acquisitions and expansion

Major acquisitions include Schering AG (2006) and, most controversially, Monsanto (2018) for about $63 billion, making Bayer the world's largest supplier of seeds and pesticides. The Monsanto deal exposed Bayer to massive litigation in the United States over the herbicide glyphosate (Roundup), resulting in settlements exceeding $10 billion and a sharp fall in share price.

Products

Well-known Bayer brands include Aspirin, Claritin, Aleve, Alka-Seltzer, the anticoagulant Xarelto (rivaroxaban), and the eye drug Eylea (developed with Regeneron). Its Crop Science division sells seeds and crop-protection products under brands inherited from Monsanto, including Roundup and Dekalb.
