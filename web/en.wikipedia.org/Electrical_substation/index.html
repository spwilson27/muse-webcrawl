Electrical substation

Electrical substation
FunctionVoltage transformation and switching
Key equipmentTransformers, circuit breakers, busbars

An electrical substation is a node in an electric power transmission and distribution system where voltage is transformed using power transformers, and where circuits are switched, protected and controlled. Substations connect generators, transmission lines and distribution feeders.

Functions

Transmission substations step up voltage from generators for efficient long-distance transmission or step it down for sub-transmission. Distribution substations step voltage down to levels suitable for local distribution to homes and businesses. Substations also house circuit breakers, protective relays, surge arresters and metering equipment.

Types

Substations may be outdoor air-insulated installations or compact gas-insulated switchgear (GIS) sites used where space is limited. Special-purpose substations serve railways, converter stations for high-voltage direct current links, and large industrial customers.

Automation

Modern substations are monitored and controlled remotely through SCADA systems, increasingly using the IEC 61850 standard for communication between intelligent electronic devices.
