Peter Kropotkin

Born9 December 1842, Moscow, Russian Empire
Died8 February 1921, Dmitrov, Russian SFSR
OccupationGeographer, philosopher, anarchist activist
Notable worksMutual Aid: A Factor of Evolution (1902); The Conquest of Bread (1892); Fields, Factories and Workshops (1899)

Peter Alexeyevich Kropotkin (9 December 1842 – 8 February 1921) was a Russian anarchist, socialist, revolutionary, historian, philosopher and geographer. Born a prince of the Rurikid lineage, he renounced his aristocratic life to become the most prominent theorist of anarchist communism and an internationally respected scientist.

Early life and Siberian expeditions

Kropotkin was educated in the Corps of Pages at St Petersburg and served as an officer in Siberia, where expeditions he led in the 1860s made significant contributions to knowledge of the geography and glaciation of northern Asia, including the structure of Siberian mountain ranges. His scientific reputation was such that he was offered the secretaryship of the Imperial Russian Geographical Society's physical geography section, which he declined in 1871 to devote himself to revolutionary politics.

Anarchist activism

Converted to anarchism during a visit to the watchmakers of the Swiss Jura Federation in 1872, Kropotkin joined the anti-authoritarian International. Arrested in Russia in 1874, he escaped from a prison hospital in 1876 and lived in exile in Switzerland, France — where he was imprisoned at Clairvaux from 1883 to 1886 — and England. In London he helped found the anarchist newspaper Freedom and wrote extensively for scientific and political journals.

Thought

Kropotkin's best-known argument, in Mutual Aid: A Factor of Evolution (1902), held that cooperation within species is as important an evolutionary factor as competition, against the "gladiatorial" reading of Darwin popularised by T. H. Huxley. In The Conquest of Bread (1892) he outlined anarchist communism: abolition of wage labour and the state, with production organised through free associations and goods distributed according to need. Fields, Factories and Workshops (1899) advocated decentralised, integrated industry and agriculture.

Later life

Kropotkin returned to Russia after the February Revolution of 1917, but opposed the Bolsheviks' authoritarian methods, criticising Lenin in letters while refusing to aid counter-revolution. He died at Dmitrov in 1921; his funeral in Moscow was the last great anarchist demonstration in Soviet Russia, attended by tens of thousands.
