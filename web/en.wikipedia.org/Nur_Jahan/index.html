Nur Jahan

Nur Jahan (1577–1645), born Mehr-un-Nissa, was the twentieth wife of the Mughal emperor Jahangir and the most powerful empress in Mughal history. For much of Jahangir's later reign she was the effective co-sovereign: imperial decrees were issued in her name alongside the emperor's, and coins were struck bearing her name — honours unprecedented for a Mughal queen.

Early life and marriage

She was born in Kandahar to Persian émigré parents; her father, Mirza Ghiyas Beg, rose to be chief minister (Itimad-ud-Daulah). Mehr-un-Nissa first married a Persian officer, Ali Quli Istajlu, and after his death in 1607 entered the imperial household. She married Jahangir in 1611 and received the title Nur Mahal ("Light of the Palace"), later Nur Jahan ("Light of the World").

Power

Nur Jahan administered the empire with her father, brother Asaf Khan, and stepson Prince Khurram (the future Shah Jahan) in an informal ruling junta. She patronised architecture and the arts, designed gardens and textiles, issued farmans, and personally intervened in politics — even leading troops to attempt Jahangir's rescue when he was captured by the rebel Mahabat Khan in 1626. She built the tomb of her father at Agra, the Itimad-ud-Daulah, a jewel-like precursor of the Taj Mahal.

Fall and death

Backing Shahryar against Khurram in the succession struggle, she lost power when Shah Jahan ascended the throne in 1628. She retired to Lahore on a pension, died in 1645, and was buried in a tomb she had built at Shahdara, near Jahangir's mausoleum.
