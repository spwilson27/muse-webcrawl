The Battle of Alcácer Quibir (also known as the Battle of the Three Kings and the Battle of Oued El Makhazeen) was fought in northern Morocco, near the town of Ksar el-Kebir (Alcácer Quibir in Portuguese), on 4 August 1578. It was a catastrophic defeat for Portugal: King Sebastian I, who had personally led an expedition to intervene in a Moroccan succession dispute, was killed along with much of the Portuguese nobility, and the flower of the Portuguese army was destroyed.

Background

Sebastian, the young and zealously Catholic king of Portugal, dreamed of a crusade against Muslim Morocco. When the deposed Saadian sultan Abu Abdallah Mohammed II sought Portuguese help against his uncle, Abd Al-Malik, who had taken the throne with Ottoman support, Sebastian assembled a large expeditionary force of Portuguese troops and foreign mercenaries and landed at Asilah in the summer of 1578, marching inland despite advice to remain near the coast.

Battle

The two armies met beside a tributary of the Loukkos river. Abd Al-Malik, although seriously ill, concealed his condition and deployed his larger army in a crescent formation with strong cavalry. After an initial Portuguese advance, the Moroccan wings enveloped Sebastian's army; the irrigation channels and the river cut off retreat. All three monarchs died: Abd Al-Malik of his illness (or poison) during the battle, Abu Abdallah drowned while fleeing, and Sebastian was killed in the fighting — though his body was said never to have been conclusively identified, giving rise to the legend of Sebastianism, the belief that the king would return to save Portugal.

Consequences

Some 8,000 Portuguese and allied soldiers were killed and about 15,000 captured, with many nobles held for enormous ransoms that severely drained the Portuguese treasury. Sebastian left no heir; his elderly great-uncle, Cardinal Henry, succeeded him and died in 1580, precipitating a succession crisis that ended with Philip II of Spain annexing Portugal and beginning the Iberian Union, which lasted until 1640. In Morocco, Ahmad al-Mansur succeeded Abd Al-Malik and inaugurated a golden age of the Saadian dynasty.
