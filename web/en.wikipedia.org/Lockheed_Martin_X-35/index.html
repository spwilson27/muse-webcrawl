The Lockheed Martin X-35 was a concept demonstrator aircraft developed under the Joint Strike Fighter (JSF) program to compete against Boeing's X-32. The winning design was developed into the F-35 Lightning II, a family of single-seat, single-engine, stealth multirole fighters.

Development

Lockheed Martin built two demonstrator airframes: the X-35A for conventional takeoff and landing (CTOL), which was later converted into the X-35B short takeoff and vertical landing (STOVL) variant by installing a shaft-driven lift fan behind the cockpit; and the larger-winged X-35C for carrier operations. The X-35A first flew on 24 October 2000, and the program of test flights culminated in July 2001 with the X-35B demonstrating a "mission X" — a short takeoff, supersonic flight, and vertical landing in a single sortie.

On 26 October 2001 the U.S. Department of Defense announced that the X-35 had won the JSF competition, citing its lower risk and the successful lift-fan STOVL demonstration. The production aircraft was designated F-35.

Design

The X-35 was a single-engine aircraft powered by a derivative of the Pratt & Whitney F119 turbofan (designated F135 in production). Its STOVL system used a clutch-engaged lift fan driven by the main engine via a driveshaft, supplemented by a swivelling three-bearing rear nozzle and roll-control ducts in the wings. The demonstrators lacked weapons bays, operational avionics and full stealth treatments, being intended purely to validate the flight characteristics and propulsion concepts of the three variants.

Surviving aircraft

The X-35A/B is displayed at the National Air and Space Museum's Steven F. Udvar-Hazy Center in Virginia, and the X-35C is at the Patuxent River Naval Air Museum in Maryland.
