Alexios IV Angelos (c. 1182 – 8 February 1204) was Byzantine emperor from August 1203 to January 1204, reigning jointly with his father Isaac II. His appeal for Western help to restore his deposed father diverted the Fourth Crusade to Constantinople and set in motion the crusader conquest and sack of the city.

Rise to the throne

When his uncle Alexios III overthrew and blinded Isaac II in 1195, the young Alexios was imprisoned; he escaped around 1201 and fled to the court of his brother-in-law Philip of Swabia in Germany. There he met the leaders of the Fourth Crusade, promising them 200,000 silver marks, 10,000 soldiers for the campaign in Egypt, provisions, and the submission of the Byzantine Church to Rome if they restored his father. The crusaders, already indebted to Venice, accepted and diverted to Constantinople in the summer of 1203; Alexios III fled, and Alexios IV was crowned co-emperor alongside the blind Isaac II.

Fall

The new emperor could not raise the promised sums: melting down church treasures and imposing heavy taxes enraged the populace, while anti-Latin riots and fires devastated the city and relations with the crusaders camped outside its walls collapsed. In January 1204 a palace coup led by Alexios Doukas ("Mourtzouphlos") deposed both emperors; Alexios IV was imprisoned and strangled on 8 February 1204. His overthrow gave the crusaders their pretext for the assault and catastrophic sack of Constantinople that April.
