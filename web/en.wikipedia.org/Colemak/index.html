Colemak

Colemak is a keyboard layout for Latin-script alphabets, designed by Shai Coleman in 2006. It was created as an alternative to the QWERTY and Dvorak layouts, aiming to be more efficient and comfortable for touch typing in English while being easier for existing QWERTY typists to learn than Dvorak, since it changes only 17 keys from QWERTY and keeps common shortcut keys such as Z, X, C, and V in their original positions.

Design

Colemak places the most frequently used letters of English on the home row — A R S T D H N E I O — and is designed to reduce finger travel, favour alternating hands, and avoid awkward same-finger repetitions. The Caps Lock key is remapped to an additional Backspace in Coleman's original design, one of the layout's best-known quirks. Variants such as Colemak-DH modify the positions of D and H to accommodate keyboards with different geometry.

Support and adoption

Colemak is included by default in many operating systems, including macOS, Linux distributions, and BSD systems, and is available on Windows through installers or tools such as Microsoft Keyboard Layout Creator and AutoHotkey. It consistently ranks among the most popular alternative layouts in typing-test communities, though its user base remains a small fraction of QWERTY's.
