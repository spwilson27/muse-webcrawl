Cliffhanger

A cliffhanger or cliffhanger ending is a plot device in fiction which features a main character in a precarious or difficult dilemma or confronted with a shocking revelation at the end of an episode or instalment of a serial work. It is intended to keep audiences engaged and ensure they return to learn how the character resolves the dilemma.

Origins

The device is as old as serial storytelling: Scheherazade in One Thousand and One Nights breaks off each tale at dawn to postpone her execution. In the 19th century, serialised novels by writers such as Charles Dickens ended instalments on unresolved crises. The term itself is often credited to Thomas Hardy's A Pair of Blue Eyes (1873), in which a character is left literally dangling from a cliff face.

Use in film and television

Silent-film serials such as The Perils of Pauline (1914) made the cliffhanger a weekly ritual. Television adopted the device for episode and season finales, most famously the "Who shot J.R.?" storyline of Dallas in 1980, and it remains a staple of streaming-era drama, where endings are engineered to encourage binge-watching.

Related devices

A cliffhanger differs from a mere open ending in that it interrupts a scene of acute danger or revelation rather than simply leaving a plot unresolved.
