Exceptional Lie algebra

Exceptional Lie algebras
FieldMathematics (Lie theory)
TypesG₂, F₄, E₆, E₇, E₈

In mathematics, an exceptional Lie algebra is a complex simple Lie algebra whose Dynkin diagram is of exceptional type: one of G₂, F₄, E₆, E₇, or E₈. These five algebras stand outside the four infinite classical families An, Bn, Cn and Dn in the Killing–Cartan classification of simple Lie algebras.

The five exceptional algebras

The exceptional algebras have dimensions 14 (G₂), 52 (F₄), 78 (E₆), 133 (E₇) and 248 (E₈). G₂ is the smallest, arising as the automorphism group of the octonions; E₈ is the largest and most intricate simple Lie algebra, and its associated Lie group plays a prominent role in some approaches to theoretical physics, including heterotic string theory.

Properties

Because they do not belong to infinite families, the exceptional algebras are often constructed individually, for example using the octonions, Jordan algebras, or the Freudenthal magic square. Their root systems display exceptional symmetries not seen in the classical families.
