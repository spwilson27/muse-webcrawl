Lalitpur

Lalitpur Metropolitan City (Nepali: ललितपुर महानगरपालिका), historically known as Patan, is a city in the Kathmandu Valley, Nepal, south of Kathmandu across the Bagmati River. It is known for its rich cultural heritage, particularly its tradition of arts and craftsmanship, and for Patan Durbar Square, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

Patan is one of the oldest known Buddhist cities; tradition dates its founding to the Kirata period, and the emperor Ashoka is said to have built stupas here in the 3rd century BCE. Under the Malla kings, especially in the 16th–18th centuries, Patan flourished as one of the three royal cities of the Kathmandu Valley, acquiring much of its Durbar Square palace complex, temples and courtyards.

Culture and landmarks

Patan Durbar Square contains the former royal palace, the Krishna Mandir (a stone temple built in 1637), Bhimsen Temple and Taleju Temple. The Patan Museum occupies part of the palace. The city is famed for metalwork, wood carving and stone sculpture, and for festivals such as Rato Machhindranath Jatra, one of Nepal's longest chariot festivals.

2015 earthquake

The April 2015 Gorkha earthquake damaged temples and houses in the historic centre; many monuments have since been restored with local and international support.
