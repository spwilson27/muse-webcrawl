The Musée d'Orsay is a museum in Paris, France, housed in the former Gare d'Orsay, a Beaux-Arts railway station built between 1898 and 1900 on the left bank of the Seine. It holds mainly French art dating from 1848 to 1914, including paintings, sculptures, furniture and photography. It houses the largest collection of Impressionist and post-Impressionist masterpieces in the world, by painters including Monet, Manet, Degas, Renoir, Cézanne, Seurat, Gauguin and Van Gogh.

History

The Gare d'Orsay opened in 1900 for the Exposition Universelle. By 1939 its short platforms had become unsuitable for longer trains, and mainline services moved elsewhere. After decades of varied uses, the station was listed as a historic monument in 1978. The idea of converting it into a museum bridging the Louvre and the modern collections of the Centre Pompidou was approved under President Valéry Giscard d'Estaing, and the museum was inaugurated by François Mitterrand on 9 December 1986. The architect Gae Aulenti led the interior redesign, preserving the great hall, its vaulted ceiling and the celebrated station clock.

Collection

The collection spans the period from the Second Republic to the First World War. Highlights include Van Gogh's Starry Night Over the Rhône and a version of his bedroom at Arles, Monet's Poppy Field and Rouen Cathedral series, Degas's ballet dancers, Renoir's Bal du moulin de la Galette, Cézanne's The Card Players, and Gustave Courbet's monumental A Burial at Ornans and The Artist's Studio. The museum also holds major holdings of decorative arts, Art Nouveau objects and early photography.
