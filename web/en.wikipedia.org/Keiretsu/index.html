Keiretsu

A keiretsu (系列, "series" or "subsystem") is a set of Japanese companies with interlocking business relationships and shareholdings that formed after the Second World War from the dissolution of the great zaibatsu conglomerates. Keiretsu are typically centred on a main bank and a trading company, with member firms holding cross-shareholdings and coordinating through presidents' councils.

Types

Horizontal keiretsu (such as Mitsubishi, Mitsui and Sumitomo) link firms across many industries around a core bank, while vertical keiretsu organise suppliers and distributors around a large manufacturer, as in the automotive and electronics industries.

Influence

The system underpinned Japan's postwar industrial growth, providing stable financing and long-term relationships, though critics argued it insulated management and distorted competition; its importance has declined since the 1990s.
