Hyderabadi cuisine

Hyderabadi biryani, the city's most famous dish

Hyderabadi cuisine is the distinctive food tradition of Hyderabad, in the Indian state of Telangana. It developed under the patronage of the Nizams of Hyderabad, who ruled from the 18th century until 1948, and blends indigenous Telugu and Marathwada cooking with influences from Mughlai, Turkish, Arabic, and Persian cuisines. It forms part of the broader Deccani culinary tradition.

History

The cuisine took shape in the kitchens of the Nizam's court, where dishes from across the Islamic world were adapted to local ingredients and tastes. A characteristic feature is the use of souring agents such as tamarind and lemon, liberal use of spices, and slow-cooking techniques. Hyderabadi dishes are traditionally divided between the refined court cuisine and the home cooking of the region, while the city's Irani cafés contributed their own institutions such as Osmania biscuits and Irani chai.

Signature dishes

  • Hyderabadi biryani — fragrant long-grained basmati rice layered with marinated meat (mutton or chicken) and cooked in the sealed dum style; the raw marinated version is called kacchi biryani, the pre-cooked version pakki biryani. It is usually served with mirchi ka salan and raita.
  • Haleem — a slow-cooked stew of wheat, barley, lentils, and meat, pounded to a paste-like consistency; especially popular during Ramadan.
  • Mirchi ka salan — large green chillies simmered in a peanut, sesame, and coconut curry.
  • Baghara baingan — baby aubergines in a similar rich, tangy sauce.
  • Pathar-ka-gosht — meat cooked on a hot stone slab.
  • Double ka meetha and qubani ka meetha — desserts of fried bread in sweetened milk, and stewed apricots, respectively.

Recognition

Hyderabad was designated a UNESCO Creative City of Gastronomy in 2019, in recognition of its rich and living culinary heritage.
