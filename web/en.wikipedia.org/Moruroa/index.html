Moruroa

[Image: Moruroa atoll in French Polynesia, former French nuclear test site]

Moruroa (also spelled Mururoa) is an atoll in the Tuamotu Archipelago of French Polynesia, in the southern Pacific Ocean, about 1,250 km southeast of Tahiti. Together with neighbouring Fangataufa, it served as France's principal nuclear weapons testing site from 1966 to 1996, after Algeria's independence ended testing in the Sahara.

Nuclear testing

France conducted 193 nuclear tests in French Polynesia, of which the great majority were at Moruroa: 41 atmospheric tests between 1966 and 1974, followed by underground tests detonated in boreholes beneath the atoll and its lagoon until 1996. The tests drew sustained international protest, notably campaigns by Greenpeace; in 1985 French intelligence agents bombed the Greenpeace ship Rainbow Warrior in Auckland, New Zealand, killing photographer Fernando Pereira. France briefly resumed testing in 1995 under President Jacques Chirac, provoking worldwide condemnation and riots in Papeete, before signing the Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty in 1996.

Aftermath

The atoll remains a military zone guarded by French forces, with monitoring of radioactivity and the geological stability of the basalt structure, which was fractured and weakened by underground explosions; scientists have debated the risk of radioactive material leaking into the ocean. Veterans and Polynesian workers have sought compensation for radiation-related illness under France's 2010 compensation law (the "Loi Morin"), whose criteria were subsequently liberalized.
