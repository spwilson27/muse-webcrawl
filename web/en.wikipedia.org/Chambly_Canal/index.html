The Chambly Canal is a National Historic Site of Canada in the province of Quebec. Running about 19 kilometres between the towns of Chambly and Saint-Jean-sur-Richelieu, it bypasses the rapids of the Richelieu River and, together with locks in the United States, completes a navigable waterway between the St. Lawrence River and Lake Champlain.

History

Construction began in 1831 and the canal opened in 1843, serving commercial traffic of timber and agricultural goods moving between Canada and the United States. A combined lock (locks 1, 2, and 3) at Chambly lifts vessels past the greatest change in elevation. Today the canal is operated by Parks Canada and is used almost entirely by pleasure craft.

Features

The canal has nine locks, several of which are still operated largely by hand as they were in the nineteenth century, and a towpath along its length popular with cyclists. Fort Lennox and Fort Chambly lie near its route.
