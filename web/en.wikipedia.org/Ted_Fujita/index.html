Tetsuya Theodore Fujita (1920–1998), known as Ted Fujita or "Mr. Tornado", was a Japanese-American meteorologist at the University of Chicago whose research transformed the study of severe storms. He is best known for the Fujita scale of tornado intensity.

Career

Trained as an engineer in Japan, Fujita studied the damage patterns of the atomic bombings before emigrating to the United States in 1953 at the invitation of Horace Byers. Through painstaking aerial and ground surveys of tornado damage, he developed the F-scale (1971), which rated tornadoes F0 to F5 by the damage they caused, and later proposed the concept of multiple-vortex tornadoes.

Microbursts

Investigating the crash of Eastern Air Lines Flight 66 in 1975, Fujita identified the microburst — a small, intense downdraft that produces dangerous wind shear near airports. His work led to the installation of Doppler radar and low-level wind-shear alert systems at airports worldwide. Fujita died in Chicago in 1998.
