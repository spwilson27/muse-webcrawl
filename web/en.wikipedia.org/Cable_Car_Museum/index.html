Cable Car Museum

The Cable Car Museum is a free museum in the Nob Hill neighbourhood of San Francisco, California. Located in the historic Washington/Mason cable car barn and powerhouse, it displays the history and technology of the San Francisco cable car system and contains the system's operating winding machinery.

TypeMuseum / working powerhouse
Location1201 Mason Street, San Francisco, California
Established1974
AdmissionFree

Exhibits

Visitors can view the huge engines and winding wheels that pull the cables for the city's cable car lines through underground channels. Exhibits include historic cable cars, photographs, mechanical displays, and the first cable car built by Andrew Smith Hallidie in 1873 for the Clay Street Hill Railroad.

The powerhouse

The museum occupies a functioning car barn and powerhouse: cables for the system's lines run continuously beneath the streets and are driven from this building. An underground viewing area lets visitors see the cables entering the powerhouse from the street channels.

History

The building dates from the early twentieth century, when it was rebuilt after the 1906 earthquake. The museum was established in 1974 and is operated in connection with the San Francisco Municipal Railway (Muni), which runs the cable car system.
