Hue

In color theory, hue is one of the main properties of a color, defined technically as "the degree to which a stimulus can be described as similar to or different from stimuli that are described as red, orange, yellow, green, blue, or purple." Hue is one of the three coordinates of common color appearance models, together with lightness (or value) and saturation (or chroma).

Definition

Hue is the attribute of a visual sensation by which an area appears to be one of the perceived colors — red, yellow, green, and blue — or a combination of two of them. It corresponds roughly to the dominant wavelength of light, though distinct wavelengths can share a hue and the same hue can be produced by many different spectra (metamerism). Colors of the same hue but different lightness or saturation, such as pink and red, are distinguished by those other attributes.

Hue in color models

In the HSL and HSV color spaces used in computing, hue is represented as an angle around a central axis, conventionally with red at 0°, green at 120°, and blue at 240°. In the Munsell color system, hues are arranged around a circle in five principal families (red, yellow, green, blue, purple) and five intermediates. The CIE color spaces express hue via a hue angle computed from chromatic coordinates.

Unique hues

Psychologists distinguish "unique hues" — red, green, blue, and yellow — which cannot be described as a mixture of other hues, from binary hues such as orange (red-yellow) or turquoise (green-blue). The opponent-process theory of color vision explains this by postulating red–green and blue–yellow opponent channels in the visual system.
