Kingdom of Portugal
Period: 1139 – 1910
Capital: Lisbon (from 1255)
Dynasties: Burgundy, Aviz, Habsburg, Braganza

The Kingdom of Portugal was the monarchy of Portugal from 1139, when Afonso Henriques proclaimed himself king after the Battle of Ourique, until the revolution of 5 October 1910 abolished the monarchy and proclaimed the Portuguese Republic.

Foundations

Evolving from the County of Portugal within the Kingdom of León, the kingdom won recognition from León by the Treaty of Zamora (1143) and from the papacy in 1179. The Reconquista pushed its southern border to the Algarve by 1249, fixing continental boundaries that are among Europe's oldest.

Age of Discoveries

Under the Aviz dynasty, Portugal pioneered European maritime exploration: Ceuta (1415), the rounding of the Cape of Good Hope by Bartolomeu Dias (1488), Vasco da Gama's sea route to India (1498) and the discovery of Brazil (1500) created a global empire spanning Africa, Asia and South America. The kingdom's wealth peaked in the early 16th century before dynastic crisis led to 60 years of union with Spain (1580–1640), ended by the Restoration War under the House of Braganza.

Decline and fall

The 1755 Lisbon earthquake, the transfer of the court to Brazil during the Napoleonic Wars, Brazilian independence (1822), and 19th-century civil wars weakened the crown. After the British Ultimatum of 1890 and the regicide of 1908, republican revolution in 1910 sent Manuel II into exile, ending 771 years of monarchy.
