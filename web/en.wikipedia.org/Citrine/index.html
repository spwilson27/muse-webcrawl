Citrine

Citrine is a variety of quartz whose colour ranges from pale yellow to brownish orange, owing to trace amounts of iron. Its name derives from the French citron (lemon). Natural citrine is rare; most citrine sold as gemstones is produced by heat-treating amethyst or smoky quartz, which turns them yellow-orange.

Properties and use

Like all quartz, citrine has a Mohs hardness of 7 and the chemical formula SiO2. It is used mainly as an inexpensive gemstone in jewellery, sometimes under trade names inherited from the days when it was sold as "topaz quartz" or confused with yellow topaz. Citrine, together with amethyst, is a traditional birthstone for November. Important deposits of natural material occur in Brazil, Russia, and Madagascar. Ametrine, from Bolivia, combines amethyst and citrine colouring in a single crystal.
