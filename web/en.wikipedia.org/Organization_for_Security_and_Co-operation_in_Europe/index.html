The Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe (OSCE) is a regional security-oriented intergovernmental organisation with 57 participating States spanning North America, Europe and Asia — the world's largest regional security organisation. Its mandate covers arms control, conflict prevention and crisis management, human rights, democratisation, election observation and counter-terrorism. Decisions are taken by consensus and are politically, not legally, binding.

History

The OSCE originated in the Conference on Security and Co-operation in Europe, a product of Cold War détente that culminated in the Helsinki Final Act of 1975, signed by 35 states including the United States, Canada and the Soviet Union. The Act's combination of principles — sovereign equality and inviolability of frontiers alongside commitments on human rights and fundamental freedoms — gave dissidents in the Eastern bloc a lever ("Helsinki groups") and is credited with helping to shape the end of the Cold War. After 1989 the CSCE acquired permanent institutions and field missions and was renamed the OSCE in 1995.

Work

The OSCE works across three "dimensions" of security: politico-military (arms control, confidence-building measures such as the Vienna Document), economic and environmental, and the human dimension (elections, human rights, rule of law). Its Office for Democratic Institutions and Human Rights (ODIHR) in Warsaw runs the world's best-known international election observation missions. Field operations in the Balkans, Eastern Europe, the Caucasus and Central Asia support policing, media freedom, minority rights and border management. From 2014 the OSCE deployed a Special Monitoring Mission to Ukraine; following Russia's full-scale invasion in 2022 and the mission's closure, the organisation has faced its gravest crisis, with consensus decision-making strained by conflict among its own members.

Structure

Summit meetings of heads of state set priorities; the Ministerial Council meets annually, and the Permanent Council in Vienna meets weekly. A rotating Chairpersonship (held by a participating State's foreign minister) leads the organisation day to day, supported by the Secretary General and the Vienna Secretariat.
