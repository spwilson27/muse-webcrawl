Vrishabhavathi River

The Vrishabhavathi River is a minor river in Karnataka, India, and a tributary of the Arkavathi River. It originates in the area of the Bull Temple in Basavanagudi, Bengaluru, and flows through the city before joining the Arkavathi near Kanakapura, which in turn flows into the Kaveri river system.

Course and condition

The river flows for roughly 50 kilometres, receiving the storm-water of the Vrishabhavathi valley, one of Bengaluru's four major drainage valleys. Historically used for irrigation and considered sacred — its name derives from vrishabha (bull), associated with Nandi — the river has become heavily polluted by untreated sewage and industrial effluents, frequently carrying froth and black, foul-smelling water.

Efforts

State authorities have proposed rejuvenation projects including sewage treatment plants along its course and diversion of treated water for irrigation. The river passes landmarks including the Kengeri suburb and the Global Village technology park area of Bengaluru.
