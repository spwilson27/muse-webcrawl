The Nehru Trophy Boat Race is a popular vallam kali (boat race) held in the Punnamada Lake near Alappuzha, Kerala, India. The race of chundan vallams (snake boats) is the main tourist attraction of the season in the region, drawing spectators from around India and abroad. It is held on the second Saturday of August every year and marks the start of Kerala's boat-race season, which coincides with the Onam festival period.

History

The race began in 1952, when Jawaharlal Nehru, India's first Prime Minister, visited Alappuzha and was so taken by an impromptu snake boat race held in his honour that he jumped into the winning boat and later donated a silver trophy — a model snake boat mounted on a wooden pedestal, engraved with the words "To the winners of the boat race which is a unique feature of community life in Travancore-Cochin." The trophy, and the race, took his name.

The boats and race

The chundan vallam is among the largest traditional racing boats in the world, about 100–120 feet long, with a raised prow resembling a snake's hood. Each is paddled by up to 100 or more oarsmen rowing in unison to the rhythm of the vanchipattu (boat song) led by singers and drummers aboard. Besides the snake boat category there are races for smaller classes such as churulan, iruttukuthy and odi boats. Since 2019 the race has also formed part of the Champions Boat League, a franchised league format across Kerala's backwater venues.
