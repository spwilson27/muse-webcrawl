Stinky tofu

Stinky tofu (Chinese: 臭豆腐; pinyin: chòu dòufǔ) is a Chinese form of fermented tofu that has a strong, pungent odor. It is a popular snack in East and Southeast Asia, frequently sold at night markets and roadside stands, and is often compared to blue cheese or Limburger in its acquired-taste appeal.

Production

The odor comes from a fermentation brine made of fermented milk, vegetables, and meat (recipes vary and are often secret), in which fresh tofu is soaked for hours to months. The brine's microbial activity produces the characteristic smell. Commercial production sometimes uses chemical shortcuts, which has drawn regulatory scrutiny in China.

Preparation and serving

Stinky tofu is commonly deep-fried and served with pickled cabbage and chili sauce — the classic form in Taiwan, where it is a signature night market food. It is also steamed (notably in Changsha, Hunan, where it is fried black and dressed with chili), grilled, or added to hot pots and stews. Legend attributes its invention to the Qing dynasty scholar Wang Zhihe, whose unsold tofu fermented in storage.
