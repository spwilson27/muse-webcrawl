Stanley Park

The Stanley Park seawall and forest, Vancouver.

Stanley Park is a 405-hectare (1,001-acre) public park in Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada, that borders downtown Vancouver and is almost entirely surrounded by the waters of Burrard Inlet and English Bay. Created in 1888, it was the first park established in Vancouver and was named for Lord Stanley of Preston, then Governor General of Canada. Unlike most large urban parks, Stanley Park was not designed by a landscape architect but grew from the preservation of an existing temperate rainforest.

Features

The park contains an estimated half million trees, some hundreds of years old, and a network of trails including the famous 8.8-kilometre seawall, begun in 1917 and completed decades later. Attractions include the Vancouver Aquarium, the totem poles at Brockton Point—among the most visited tourist sites in British Columbia—Beaver Lake, Lost Lagoon, the Hollow Tree, Siwash Rock and Prospect Point.

History

The peninsula was a traditional territory and home of the Musqueam, Squamish and Tsleil-Waututh peoples for thousands of years. Designated a park in 1888, it has weathered major storms, notably in 2006 when a windstorm felled thousands of trees and prompted a large restoration programme funded partly by private donations.

Recognition

Stanley Park was designated a National Historic Site of Canada in 1988 and is frequently ranked among the world's great urban parks, alongside venues from national stadiums to royal gardens in lists of landmark civic spaces.
