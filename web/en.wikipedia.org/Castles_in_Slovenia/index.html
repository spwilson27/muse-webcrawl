Castles in Slovenia

Slovenia has an exceptionally high density of castles, manors and fortifications for its size — at their height there were roughly a thousand castle buildings on Slovenian territory, one for every few square kilometres. Built by feudal lords of the Holy Roman Empire, the Habsburgs, the Counts of Celje and Venetian and Hungarian rulers, many survive as museums, hotels and cultural venues, while others stand in ruins.

Notable castles

Bled Castle, perched on a cliff above Lake Bled and first mentioned in 1011, is among the oldest and most visited. Ljubljana Castle dominates the capital from its hill and was largely rebuilt in the twentieth century. Predjama Castle, built into the mouth of a vast cave in the Karst, is famous for its association with the robber knight Erazem Lueger. Other major sites include Ptuj Castle, Celje Castle — seat of the powerful Counts of Celje — Snežnik Castle, Otočec Castle on its river island, and the Renaissance Mokrice and Turjak castles.

History

Most Slovenian castles originated between the eleventh and seventeenth centuries as seats of feudal administration and defence against Hungarian, Ottoman and peasant incursions. Many were damaged in peasant revolts, the 1515 revolt among them, and later by earthquakes and the two World Wars. After 1945 many were nationalised; a number were neglected, but since independence in 1991 restoration has accelerated, with castles increasingly used for weddings, concerts and tourism.
