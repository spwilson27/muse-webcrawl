Gourock


Gourock pier and the Firth of Clyde.

Gourock (Scottish Gaelic: Guarag) is a town in the Inverclyde council area of Scotland, forming part of a conurbation with Greenock and Port Glasgow on the south bank of the Firth of Clyde. With a population of around 10,000, it developed as a fishing village, then as a fashionable Victorian seaside resort for Glasgow, and remains a ferry terminal and commuter town.

History and economy

Gourock became a burgh in 1858. In the 19th century it was a centre for herring fishing, yacht building and the ropeworks trade, and steamer excursions down the Clyde made its pier famous. A notable episode was the swimming of the Gourock outdoor pool, opened in 1909 and one of Scotland's few surviving heated saltwater lido pools. During the Second World War the town was an assembly point for Atlantic convoys, and RMS Queen Mary and other troopships anchored in the "Tail of the Bank" off Gourock.

Transport

Gourock railway station, terminus of the Inverclyde Line from Glasgow Central, adjoins the pier from which Caledonian MacBrayne ferries sail to Kilcreggan and Dunoon. The town is almost unique in Scotland for its main shopping street, Kempock Street, lying between the shore and the railway.
