Tresor (German for "vault") is a techno nightclub and record label in Berlin, Germany, founded in 1991. One of the founding institutions of Berlin's post-reunification techno scene, it is famous for its raw industrial setting — originally the vault of a former department store — and for forging the musical link between Berlin and Detroit techno.

History

Tresor opened in March 1991 in the subterranean safe-deposit vault (Tresorraum) of the former Wertheim department store on Leipziger Straße, near Potsdamer Platz in what had just ceased to be East Berlin. The space, lined with rows of broken-open safe-deposit lockers and reached through a dark stairwell, with a second room called the Globus upstairs, became legendary for punishingly loud, hard techno in near darkness. The club emerged from the milieu of the Ufo club and the Interfisch label/art collective, and its founders included Dimitri Hegemann. Tresor's record label, launched in 1991, released music by Detroit artists such as Jeff Mills, Robert Hood, Joey Beltram and Underground Resistance, as well as German acts including Basic Channel, and its compilations helped define the Berlin–Detroit axis of techno.

Closure and reopening

The original club was forced to close in April 2005 when the Wertheim site was demolished for redevelopment; the vault's steel doors and lockers were preserved and exhibited. In May 2007 Tresor reopened in a decommissioned power plant (the Kraftwerk Berlin) on Köpenicker Straße in Mitte, sharing the vast industrial complex with the club's Globus floor; the new bunker-like basement retained the caged, vault-like atmosphere of the original. The club celebrated its 30th anniversary in 2021–2022 with exhibitions, releases and events, and Tresor Records marked the milestone with the Tresor 30 box set. Alongside Berghain, Tresor is routinely cited among the world's most influential techno clubs, noted for long opening hours, a strict no-photography atmosphere in its early years, and a door policy oriented toward the music.
