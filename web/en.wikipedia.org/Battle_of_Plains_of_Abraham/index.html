Battle of the Plains of Abraham

The Battle of the Plains of Abraham, also known as the Battle of Quebec (French: Bataille des Plaines d'Abraham), was fought on 13 September 1759 during the Seven Years' War (called the French and Indian War in North America). British troops under Major General James Wolfe defeated the French under the Marquis de Montcalm on a plateau outside Quebec City. Both commanders were mortally wounded. The battle decided the fate of New France and, ultimately, of Canada.

Background

Wolfe's expedition had ascended the St. Lawrence River in the summer of 1759 and besieged Quebec for three months, but Montcalm's army held strong positions along the Beauport shore and repelled a British landing at Montmorency Falls. Facing the onset of winter, Wolfe gambled on a landing at Anse-au-Foulon, where a small detachment climbed a steep path up the cliffs west of the city during the night of 12–13 September, securing a foothold on the heights.

The battle

By morning some 4,800 British troops were formed on the Plains of Abraham, named for Abraham Martin, a 17th-century pilot and landowner. Montcalm, worried about his supply lines, attacked at once with roughly equal numbers rather than wait for reinforcements under Bougainville. The French advanced in column and fired at long range; the British held their fire until the French were within about 40 yards, then delivered devastating double-shotted volleys and charged with the bayonet, Highlanders pursuing with broadswords. The main action lasted barely fifteen minutes. Wolfe was shot three times and died on the field; Montcalm, wounded during the retreat, died the next day in Quebec.

Aftermath

Quebec surrendered on 18 September. The French nearly retook it after winning the Battle of Sainte-Foy in April 1760, but British ships arrived first when the St. Lawrence ice broke, and Montreal capitulated that autumn. The Treaty of Paris (1763) ceded Canada to Britain, transforming the political geography of North America.
