Spinal anaesthesia (also called a spinal block, subarachnoid block, or intrathecal anaesthesia) is a form of neuraxial regional anaesthesia involving the injection of a local anaesthetic into the subarachnoid space, usually through a fine needle. The anaesthetic mixes with cerebrospinal fluid and blocks conduction in the spinal nerve roots, producing rapid, dense anaesthesia and muscle relaxation below the level of injection.

Technique

The patient sits or lies on their side, curving the back to open the lumbar interspaces. Under sterile conditions, a fine (often pencil-point) needle is advanced, usually at L3/L4 or L4/L5 — below the end of the spinal cord — until free flow of cerebrospinal fluid confirms placement in the subarachnoid space. A small dose of local anaesthetic, most commonly hyperbaric bupivacaine, is injected; baricity and positioning influence the spread ("height") of the block.

Indications

Spinal anaesthesia is widely used for caesarean section, lower limb orthopaedic surgery (such as hip fracture repair and joint replacement), lower abdominal and perineal surgery, and urological procedures. Compared with general anaesthesia it can reduce blood loss, thromboembolism and postoperative nausea, and allows the patient to remain awake.

Risks and side effects

Common effects include hypotension from sympathetic blockade, shivering and urinary retention. Post-dural-puncture headache occurs in a small percentage of cases, less often with fine pencil-point needles. Serious complications — infection, haematoma, direct nerve injury and total spinal anaesthesia — are rare. Contraindications include patient refusal, coagulopathy, infection at the injection site and severe hypovolaemia.

History

The first planned spinal anaesthesia was performed in 1898 by August Bier in Kiel, Germany, using cocaine — on his patients, and famously on himself and his assistant, both of whom developed severe post-spinal headaches. The technique was refined over the 20th century with safer agents and needles and is now a cornerstone of anaesthetic practice.
