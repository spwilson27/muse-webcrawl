Plato scale

The Plato scale is a scale for measuring the density of beer wort in terms of extract content, expressed as the weight percent of dissolved solids (mainly sugars) in the liquid. It is named after the German scientist Fritz Plato, who developed it around 1900 from the earlier work of Karl Balling.

Definition and use

Degrees Plato (°P) give the extract as a percentage by weight: a wort of 12 °P contains 12 grams of extract per 100 grams of wort. Brewers use the original gravity, measured in degrees Plato before fermentation, and the final gravity, to calculate the alcohol content and attenuation of the beer. A rough conversion is that specific gravity points divided by four approximate degrees Plato (e.g. SG 1.048 ≈ 12 °P).

Related scales

The Plato scale is closely related to the Balling and Brix scales; Brix, used in winemaking and the sugar industry, is numerically very similar for most practical purposes.
