Toulouse

Country: France
Region: Occitania
Department: Haute-Garonne (prefecture)
Population: approx. 500,000 (city)

Toulouse is a city in southwestern France, on the banks of the River Garonne, and the prefecture of the Haute-Garonne department and the Occitania region. It is France's fourth-largest city and is known as la Ville rose ("the Pink City") for the terracotta bricks used in many of its buildings. Toulouse is the centre of the European aerospace industry, home to the headquarters of Airbus and a major hub of space research.

History

Toulouse was an important Roman city (Tolosa) and later the capital of the Visigothic kingdom in the fifth century. In the Middle Ages it was ruled by its own counts and was a centre of Occitan culture and of the Cathar heresy, against which the Albigensian Crusade was directed; the University of Toulouse was founded in 1229. The city grew wealthy from the pastel (woad) trade in the Renaissance, whose merchants built ornate brick mansions, and later from its position on the Canal du Midi, completed in 1681.

Aerospace

Toulouse's aviation industry dates to the pioneering airmail flights of Aéropostale after the First World War. Today Airbus assembles airliners in the city, and the French space agency CNES, Thales Alenia Space, and numerous suppliers employ tens of thousands. The Cité de l'espace museum celebrates this heritage.

Culture

The Capitole, housing the city hall and the Théâtre du Capitole opera house, faces the main square. The basilica of Saint-Sernin is one of the great Romanesque churches of Europe and a stop on the pilgrimage route to Santiago de Compostela. Rugby is deeply rooted in the city; Stade Toulousain is among Europe's most decorated clubs.
