Robert Venturi

Born: Robert Charles Venturi Jr., 25 June 1925, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, U.S.
Died: 18 September 2018, Philadelphia
Occupation: Architect
Awards: Pritzker Architecture Prize (1991)

Robert Charles Venturi Jr. (June 25, 1925 – September 18, 2018) was an American architect, founding principal of the firm Venturi, Scott Brown and Associates, and a major figure of postmodern architecture. His maxim "Less is a bore," a rejoinder to Mies van der Rohe's "Less is more," became emblematic of the postmodern movement.

Early life and education

Venturi was born in Philadelphia to Robert Venturi Sr. and Vanna Luizi. He studied at Princeton University, graduating summa cum laude in 1947 and earning an MFA in 1950. A fellowship at the American Academy in Rome in the mid-1950s exposed him to Italian Mannerist and Baroque architecture, which deeply influenced his work.

Complexity and Contradiction

His 1966 book Complexity and Contradiction in Architecture, published by the Museum of Modern Art, is regarded as one of the most important architecture texts of the twentieth century. It championed richness, ambiguity, and historical reference against the austerity of orthodox modernism. His Vanna Venturi House (1964) in Chestnut Hill, Philadelphia, is considered the first postmodern building. With Denise Scott Brown and Steven Izenour he co-authored Learning from Las Vegas (1972), which analysed the commercial strip and popular taste.

Major works

Notable buildings include the Guild House in Philadelphia, the Allen Memorial Art Museum addition at Oberlin College, the Sainsbury Wing of the National Gallery in London (1991), and the Seattle Art Museum. Venturi received the Pritzker Prize in 1991; controversy later surrounded the failure to include his partner and wife Denise Scott Brown as a joint laureate.
