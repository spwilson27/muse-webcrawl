Guinness

A pint of Guinness Draught, showing the dark body and creamy nitrogenated head.

Guinness is an Irish dry stout that originated in the brewery of Arthur Guinness at St. James's Gate, Dublin, Ireland, in 1759. It is one of the most successful alcohol brands worldwide, brewed in almost 50 countries and available in over 120. Guinness is owned by the British multinational Diageo.

History

Arthur Guinness signed a famous 9,000-year lease on the St. James's Gate brewery in 1759 at £45 per year. The company grew into one of the largest brewers in the world, pioneering quality control — the statistician William Sealy Gosset, publishing as "Student", developed the t-test while working on barley experiments for Guinness. The brand's harp trademark, based on the Brian Boru harp, was registered in 1876.

Characteristics

Guinness Draught is distinguished by its burnt flavour from roasted unmalted barley and its thick creamy head, produced by a mixture of nitrogen and carbon dioxide rather than carbonation alone. The "widget" in cans replicates the draught pour. Variants include Guinness Original/Extra Stout, Foreign Extra Stout, and the alcohol-free Guinness 0.0. The annual celebration of Arthur's Day and the Guinness Storehouse visitor attraction in Dublin are central to its marketing.
