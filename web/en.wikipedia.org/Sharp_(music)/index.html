Sharp (music)

In music, sharp, dièse (from French), or diesis can refer to the tuning, pitch, or symbol of a note. A note is sharp (symbol ♯) when it is raised in pitch by one chromatic semitone. For example, the note C that is raised by a semitone is written C♯ and sounds, in twelve-tone equal temperament, the same pitch as D♭.

Notation

The sharp sign ♯ is placed before the notehead it modifies, or in the key signature at the beginning of a staff. A double sharp (𝄪) raises a note by two semitones. Sharps in a key signature define the major and minor keys of Western tonal music: the order of sharps is F♯, C♯, G♯, D♯, A♯, E♯, B♯.

Intonation

More loosely, "sharp" describes a pitch that is slightly too high relative to a reference, and singers and string players may intentionally sharpen leading tones in just intonation or expressive intonation. Historically, in some tuning systems sharps and their enharmonic flats were distinct pitches, and instruments were built with split keys to play both.
