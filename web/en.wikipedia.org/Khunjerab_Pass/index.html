Khunjerab Pass

Khunjerab Pass
Elevation: 4,693 m (15,397 ft)
Location: Pakistan–China border, Karakoram range
Route: Karakoram Highway (N-35 / China National Highway 314)

The Khunjerab Pass (Urdu: درہ خنجراب; Chinese: 红其拉甫口岸) is a 4,693-metre-high mountain pass in the Karakoram Mountains on the border between Pakistan's Gilgit-Baltistan region and China's Xinjiang autonomous region. It is the highest paved international border crossing in the world and the highest point of the Karakoram Highway.

Geography

The pass sits on the watershed between the Indus basin to the south and the Tarim basin to the north. The Pakistani side lies within Khunjerab National Park, home to snow leopards, Marco Polo sheep and Himalayan ibex. The name derives from Wakhi words meaning "valley of blood", a reference to its historically dangerous terrain.

Strategic importance

The Karakoram Highway across the pass, completed in 1982 and opened to third-country travellers in 1986, links Pakistan with western China along a route near an ancient Silk Road branch. The crossing is a centrepiece of the China–Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC). Traffic drives on the left in Pakistan and on the right in China, switching sides at the border. The pass is typically closed to regular traffic during winter months because of heavy snow, though freight schedules have lengthened in recent years.
