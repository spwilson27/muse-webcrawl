The Pacific Northwest (PNW; sometimes Cascadia) is a geographic region in northwestern North America bounded by the Rocky Mountains to the east and the Pacific Ocean to the west. It typically includes the U.S. states of Washington and Oregon, sometimes Idaho, and the Canadian province of British Columbia. Definitions occasionally extend to southeastern Alaska, northern California, and western Montana.

Geography and climate

The region is defined by the Cascade Range, which divides a wet, mild coastal zone from a drier interior. West of the Cascades, an oceanic climate brings cloudy, rainy winters and dry summers, supporting temperate rainforests of Douglas-fir, western red cedar, and Sitka spruce — among the most productive forest ecosystems on Earth. The Columbia River and Puget Sound are dominant geographic features.

History

Indigenous peoples have inhabited the region for thousands of years, developing complex societies supported by salmon fishing. European and American exploration intensified in the late eighteenth century, and the Oregon Country was jointly occupied by Britain and the United States until the Oregon Treaty of 1846 fixed the boundary at the 49th parallel. Timber, fishing, and agriculture drove the economy through the twentieth century; more recently technology industries in Seattle and Portland have reshaped the region.

Culture

The Pacific Northwest is known for its environmental movement, coffee culture, outdoor recreation, and music scenes — including the grunge movement centered on Seattle in the early 1990s. The term "Cascadia" is used both for the bioregion and for a loosely organized independence movement.
