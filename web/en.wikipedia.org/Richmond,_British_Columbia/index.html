Richmond, British Columbia

Richmond is a city in the coastal Lower Mainland region of British Columbia, Canada. It occupies most of Lulu Island and Sea Island at the mouth of the Fraser River, immediately south of Vancouver. As of the 2021 census, Richmond had a population of about 209,937.

Geography

The city is almost entirely surrounded by water and lies on average one metre above sea level, protected by an extensive system of dykes. It includes the fishing village of Steveston at its southwest corner, and Vancouver International Airport on Sea Island.

History

Indigenous peoples, including the Musqueam, used the area for fishing for millennia. European farming settlement began in the 1860s, and Richmond was incorporated as a municipality on November 10, 1879. Steveston grew into a major salmon-canning centre; its Japanese Canadian community was uprooted by the internment of 1942.

Demographics and economy

Richmond has one of the largest immigrant populations in Canada, with a majority of residents of Asian, particularly Chinese, heritage. Its economy centres on aviation, technology, agriculture (including cranberries and blueberries), and the Richmond Olympic Oval built for the 2010 Winter Olympics speed skating events.
