Jacques Piccard

Born28 July 1922, Brussels, Belgium
Died1 November 2008, Cully, Switzerland
OccupationOceanographer, engineer, explorer

Jacques Piccard (28 July 1922 – 1 November 2008) was a Swiss oceanographer and engineer, known for having developed underwater vehicles for studying ocean currents and for his role in the first manned descent to the deepest point in the world's oceans.

Early life

Piccard was born in Brussels, the younger son of the physicist and explorer Auguste Piccard, who pioneered high-altitude balloon flights and designed the bathyscaphe. Jacques studied economics at the University of Geneva before turning to engineering and oceanography.

The bathyscaphe Trieste

Working with his father, Piccard helped build the bathyscaphe Trieste. On 23 January 1960, Piccard and United States Navy Lieutenant Don Walsh descended in the Trieste to the bottom of the Challenger Deep in the Mariana Trench, reaching a depth of nearly 10,911 metres (35,797 ft) — the first humans to reach the deepest known point of the oceans.

Later work

Piccard went on to design mesoscaphes, including the Auguste Piccard and the Ben Franklin, which in 1969 drifted with the Gulf Stream for a month as a NASA-supported mission. He founded the Foundation for the Study and Protection of Seas and Lakes and campaigned for ocean conservation.

Legacy

Piccard's son Bertrand Piccard became known for the first non-stop balloon flight around the world and the Solar Impulse solar-powered aircraft project. Jacques Piccard died at his home on Lake Geneva in 2008, aged 86.
