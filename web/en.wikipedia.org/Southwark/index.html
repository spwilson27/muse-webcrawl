Southwark (pronounced "SUTH-erk") is a district of Central London on the south bank of the River Thames, forming the northwestern part of the London Borough of Southwark. One of London's oldest settlements, it grew up at the southern end of London Bridge — for centuries the only bridge across the Thames in London — and developed as a place of inns, markets, theatres and entertainments outside the City's jurisdiction.

History

The Romans established a settlement at the bridgehead, and the name derives from Old English Suðrweard-weorc, the "fort of the men of the south". In the Middle Ages Southwark was famous for its inns — Chaucer's pilgrims set out from the Tabard Inn in the Canterbury Tales — and for the stews (brothels) of Bankside licensed by the Bishop of Winchester. In Shakespeare's time Bankside held the Globe, the Rose and the Hope theatres, bear-baiting pits and taverns, beyond the City authorities' reach. The area later became heavily industrialised with wharves, tanneries and breweries.

Modern Southwark

Regeneration since the 1990s has transformed Bankside: Tate Modern opened in the former Bankside Power Station in 2000, Shakespeare's Globe was reconstructed nearby, Borough Market became a celebrated food market, and the Shard (completed 2012) rises above London Bridge station. Southwark Cathedral, largely Gothic with medieval origins, stands beside the market.
