Lincoln–Douglas debates

The Lincoln–Douglas debates were a series of seven debates in 1858 between Abraham Lincoln, the Republican candidate, and Senator Stephen A. Douglas, the Democratic incumbent, in the Illinois campaign for the United States Senate. The principal issue was the expansion of slavery into the territories.

The debates

The debates were held from 21 August to 15 October 1858 in Ottawa, Freeport, Jonesboro, Charleston, Galesburg, Quincy, and Alton, before crowds of many thousands and with extensive national newspaper coverage. In his "House Divided" speech accepting the nomination, Lincoln had argued that the nation could not endure permanently half slave and half free. Douglas defended "popular sovereignty" — letting territorial settlers decide — and at Freeport gave the famous Freeport Doctrine: a territory could effectively exclude slavery despite the Supreme Court's Dred Scott decision by withholding friendly local legislation.

Outcome and significance

Republicans won the popular vote for the state legislature, but districting returned Douglas to the Senate. The debates nonetheless made Lincoln a national figure, and the Freeport Doctrine split the Democratic Party, aiding Lincoln's election as president in 1860. The published texts became classics of American political oratory.
