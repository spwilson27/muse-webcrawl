The East African Community (EAC) is an intergovernmental organisation of eight partner states in East Africa working toward regional integration through a customs union, a common market, a monetary union and ultimately a political federation. Its headquarters are in Arusha, Tanzania.

History

Kenya, Uganda and Tanzania (then Tanganyika) shared common services through much of the colonial and early independence era, and formed the first East African Community in 1967. That community collapsed in 1977 amid ideological and economic tensions. The present EAC was revived by a treaty signed in 1999 and in force from 7 July 2000. Rwanda and Burundi joined in 2007, South Sudan in 2016, the Democratic Republic of the Congo in 2022, and Somalia's accession treaty was signed in 2023.

Integration pillars

A Customs Union protocol took effect in 2005, followed by a Common Market protocol in 2010 providing for free movement of goods, persons, services and capital. A Monetary Union protocol signed in 2013 envisages a single currency, and the partner states have adopted a framework for an eventual East African Federation. Organs of the Community include the Summit of Heads of State, the Council of Ministers, the East African Legislative Assembly and the East African Court of Justice.

Economy and challenges

The bloc encompasses major ports (Mombasa, Dar es Salaam), lake and corridor transport networks, and large infrastructure projects such as standard-gauge railways. Persistent challenges include non-tariff barriers, overlapping memberships in other regional blocs, and political disputes among partner states.
