Arthur C. Clarke

Sir Arthur Charles Clarke (1917–2008) was a British science fiction writer, science writer, and futurist. He is best known for co-writing the screenplay of 2001: A Space Odyssey (1968) with Stanley Kubrick and for proposing the geostationary communications satellite.

Life

Born in Minehead, Somerset, Clarke served as a radar specialist in the Royal Air Force during the Second World War. In a 1945 paper he proposed that satellites in geostationary orbit — now sometimes called the Clarke Orbit — could relay communications worldwide. He emigrated to Ceylon (Sri Lanka) in 1956, where he lived for the rest of his life, pursuing interests in diving and spaceflight.

Works

His novels include Childhood's End (1953), Rendezvous with Rama (1973), which won the Hugo and Nebula awards, and The Fountains of Paradise (1979). His short story "The Sentinel" provided the seed for 2001. Clarke's three laws of prediction are widely quoted, the third stating that "any sufficiently advanced technology is indistinguishable from magic". He was knighted in 2000.
