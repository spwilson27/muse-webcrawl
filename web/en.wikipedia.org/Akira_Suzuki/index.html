Akira Suzuki

Born: 12 September 1930, Mukawa, Hokkaido, Japan

Alma mater: Hokkaido University

Known for: Suzuki reaction

Awards: Nobel Prize in Chemistry (2010)

Akira Suzuki (born 12 September 1930) is a Japanese chemist and Nobel laureate, best known for the discovery of the Suzuki reaction, the palladium-catalysed cross coupling of organoboron compounds with organic halides.

Career

Suzuki studied at Hokkaido University, where he earned his PhD in 1959 and became a professor in 1973. From 1963 to 1965 he did postdoctoral work at Purdue University in the United States with Herbert C. Brown, the pioneer of organoborane chemistry. In 1979, Suzuki and his student Norio Miyaura published the palladium-catalysed cross-coupling reaction that now bears Suzuki's name.

Suzuki reaction

The Suzuki (or Suzuki–Miyaura) coupling joins an organoboron compound, such as a boronic acid, with an organic halide or triflate using a palladium catalyst and a base. Because organoboron compounds are stable, widely available, and low in toxicity, the reaction became one of the most widely used methods for forming carbon–carbon bonds, applied in the synthesis of pharmaceuticals, agrochemicals, liquid crystals, and organic electronic materials.

Recognition

In 2010 Suzuki shared the Nobel Prize in Chemistry with Richard F. Heck and Ei-ichi Negishi "for palladium-catalyzed cross couplings in organic synthesis." He has also received the Order of Culture from the government of Japan.
