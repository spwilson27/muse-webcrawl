Monopsony

A monopsony is a market in which a single buyer faces many sellers — the mirror image of a monopoly. The term was popularised by Joan Robinson in her 1933 work on imperfect competition.

Labour markets

The most studied case is the labour market, where a dominant employer — such as a single large hospital, factory, or retailer in a town — can set wages below the competitive level because workers have few alternative employers. Monopsony power has been invoked in debates over minimum wages, non-compete clauses, and employer concentration.

Effects

A monopsonist purchases less of the input and pays a lower price than under competition, transferring surplus from sellers and creating deadweight loss. Antitrust authorities increasingly scrutinise buyer-side power in mergers.
