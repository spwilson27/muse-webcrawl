Deed poll


A deed poll name change document

A deed poll is a legal document binding on a single person or party expressing an active intention. It is most commonly associated with a voluntary change of name. Historically, deeds were classified as deeds poll — cut (or "polled") straight at the top — in contrast to indentures, which bound more than one party and were cut with an indented edge so that counterparts could be matched.

Name changes

In the United Kingdom and other Commonwealth countries, an adult can change their name by executing a deed poll declaring that they have abandoned the old name and adopted the new one. The document can be enrolled with the Royal Courts of Justice for a public record, but unenrolled deed polls are widely accepted by banks, government departments, and passport offices as evidence of the change.

Other uses

Deeds poll have also been used to change other legal particulars, historically including a change of religion, and in land law contexts such as a deed of gift or a power of attorney granted by one party.
