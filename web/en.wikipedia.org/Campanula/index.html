Campanula

Campanula is one of several genera of flowering plants in the family Campanulaceae commonly known as bellflowers. They take both their common and scientific names from the bell-shaped flowers — campanula is Latin for "little bell". The genus includes over 500 species and several subspecies, distributed across the temperate and subtropical regions of the Northern Hemisphere, with the highest diversity in the Mediterranean region east to the Caucasus.

Description

The range includes annual, biennial and perennial plants, varying in habit from dwarf arctic and alpine species under 5 cm high to large temperate grassland species up to 2 m tall. The leaves are alternate and often vary in shape on a single plant. The flowers are produced in panicles or singly, mostly bell-shaped with five fused petals, usually blue to purple.

Cultivation

Numerous species are popular garden subjects, including the Canterbury bells (Campanula medium), the harebell (C. rotundifolia) and the trailing C. poscharskyana. Some species have edible leaves and roots.
