Flanders

Flanders (Dutch: Vlaanderen) is the Dutch-speaking northern portion of Belgium and one of the communities, regions and language areas of the country. Historically, the name referred to the medieval County of Flanders, a prosperous region centred on the cities of Bruges, Ghent and Ypres, whose territory extended into what is now northern France and the southwestern Netherlands.

Modern Flanders

Today, Flanders comprises the Flemish Region — the provinces of West Flanders, East Flanders, Antwerp, Limburg and Flemish Brabant — together with the Dutch-speaking population of Brussels. The Flemish Region and the Flemish Community were merged into a single set of institutions with their government and parliament seated in Brussels, while the largest city is Antwerp, one of the world's principal ports.

History

The County of Flanders, founded in the 9th century, grew rich on the cloth trade with England and became one of the most urbanised regions of medieval Europe. Flemish cities famously resisted French expansion at the Battle of the Golden Spurs in 1302. Flanders later passed to the Dukes of Burgundy, the Habsburgs, Spain and Austria before becoming part of Belgium at its independence in 1830.

World War I

The name Flanders is known worldwide for the battlefields of the First World War — Ypres, Passchendaele and the "Flanders Fields" of John McCrae's poem — where the flat, drained land was fought over for four years.

Culture

Flanders is noted for the Early Netherlandish painters such as Jan van Eyck and Rogier van der Weyden, the Baroque art of Rubens, and a strong tradition of cycling, including the Tour of Flanders race.
