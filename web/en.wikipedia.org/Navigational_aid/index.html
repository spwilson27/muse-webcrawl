Navigational aid

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A navigational aid (also known as a navaid, aid to navigation, or ATON) is any form of device or marker which aids the traveler in navigation, usually nautical or aviation travel. Common types of such aids include buoys, beacons, lighthouses, day marks, fog signals, and racons, as well as electronic systems such as radio beacons, the Global Positioning System (GPS), and differential GPS.

Types

Visual aids include lateral marks, which indicate the edges of a channel; cardinal marks, which use compass directions to indicate safe water relative to a hazard; safe-water marks; isolated-danger marks; and special marks. In most of the world the IALA Maritime Buoyage System harmonizes these marks, though two regions differ in lateral coloring: in the Americas, Japan, Korea and the Philippines, red marks are kept to starboard when returning from sea ("red, right, returning"), while most other waters use the opposite convention.

Administration

Aids are maintained by national authorities — coast guards, lighthouse services, and port authorities — which publish Lists of Lights and correct nautical charts through Notices to Mariners. Electronic navaids now supplement or replace many physical marks, but physical buoys remain essential where electronics fail. See also Lighthouse and Buoy.
