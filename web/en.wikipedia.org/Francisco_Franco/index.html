Francisco Franco

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Francisco Franco Bahamonde (4 December 1892 – 20 November 1975) was a Spanish general who led the Nationalist forces in the Spanish Civil War and subsequently ruled Spain as a dictator from 1939 until his death in 1975. He styled himself Caudillo ("leader") and headed the only permitted political movement, the FET y de las JONS.

Early career

Franco graduated from the Toledo Infantry Academy and built his reputation in colonial Morocco, where he helped found the Spanish Legion and rose rapidly, becoming, in 1926, the youngest general in Europe at the time. He directed the suppression of the Asturian miners' revolt of 1934 and was made Chief of the General Staff in 1935, before a Popular Front government posted him to the Canary Islands in 1936.

The Civil War

In July 1936 Franco joined the military uprising against the Second Spanish Republic, flying from the Canaries to take command of the Army of Africa. With German and Italian assistance, his Nationalist forces defeated the Republicans by 1 April 1939. The war left hundreds of thousands dead, and Franco's regime carried out extensive repression of the defeated during and after the conflict.

Francoist Spain

Franco kept Spain out of the Second World War (while sending the volunteer Blue Division to the Eastern Front) and survived post-war isolation, aided by Cold War rapprochement with the United States and the 1953 Pact of Madrid. The early autarkic economy gave way after 1959 to liberalizing "technocrat" reforms and the Spanish "economic miracle" of the 1960s, fueled by tourism and emigrant remittances. The regime remained authoritarian, banning parties and independent unions, censoring the press, and suppressing regional languages and autonomy movements. In 1969 Franco designated Prince Juan Carlos as his successor as head of state.

Death and legacy

Franco died in Madrid on 20 November 1975. Juan Carlos succeeded him and presided over the Spanish transition to democracy. Franco was buried behind the altar of the basilica at the Valley of the Fallen; in October 2019 his remains were exhumed and reburied in the Mingorrubio cemetery in El Pardo, as part of Spain's contested politics of historical memory, which also includes the 2007 Historical Memory Law.
