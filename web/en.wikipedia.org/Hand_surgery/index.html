Hand surgery

SpecialtyOrthopaedic surgery / plastic surgery
ScopeHand, wrist, and forearm disorders
TechniquesMicrosurgery, tendon and nerve repair, reconstruction

Hand surgery is the field of medicine dealing with problems of the hand, wrist, and forearm. It draws on orthopaedic surgery, plastic surgery, and microsurgery, and its practitioners treat both surgical and non-surgical conditions, from trauma and infections to congenital differences and degenerative diseases.

Scope

Common conditions treated include carpal tunnel syndrome, trigger finger, Dupuytren's contracture, fractures and dislocations, tendon and nerve lacerations, arthritis of the hand and wrist, ganglion cysts, and congenital hand differences. Hand surgeons perform replantation of amputated digits using microsurgical techniques to repair vessels and nerves under magnification.

History and training

The specialty developed rapidly during and after the Second World War, when figures such as Sterling Bunnell organised hand centres for injured soldiers in the United States. Training typically involves a residency in orthopaedic or plastic surgery followed by a fellowship in hand surgery, with certification offered jointly by surgical boards.
