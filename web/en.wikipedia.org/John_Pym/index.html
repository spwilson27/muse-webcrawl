John Pym (1584 – 8 December 1643) was an English politician who helped initiate the political and constitutional crisis that led to the English Civil Wars. A leader of the Long Parliament, he was one of the Five Members whose attempted arrest by King Charles I in January 1642 precipitated open conflict.

Early career

Pym was educated at Broadgates Hall (now Pembroke College), Oxford, and the Middle Temple. Elected to Parliament in 1614, he became a forceful critic of royal policy, attacking Arminianism, illegal taxation and the Duke of Buckingham.

The Long Parliament

When the Long Parliament met in November 1640, Pym led the prosecution of the Earl of Strafford, steered the Grand Remonstrance through the Commons in 1641, and organised Parliament's finances and alliances — including the Solemn League and Covenant with Scotland — once war began in 1642.

Death and assessment

Pym died of cancer in December 1643 and was buried in Westminster Abbey (his remains were exhumed and dumped after the Restoration). Contemporaries nicknamed him "King Pym" for his dominance of the Commons; historians credit him with inventing much of modern parliamentary party organisation and management.
