Vinland

Vinland
Old Norse: Vínland
Described in: Saga of the Greenlanders, Saga of Erik the Red
Archaeological site: L'Anse aux Meadows, Newfoundland, c. 1000 CE (tree-ring dated to 1021)

Vinland (Old Norse Vínland) was the name given by Norse seafarers to an area of coastal North America explored around the year 1000, roughly five centuries before Columbus. According to the Icelandic sagas, Leif Erikson, sailing from Greenland, reached lands he called Helluland, Markland and finally Vinland, where wild grapes (or berries) grew and winters were mild.

Exploration

The sagas recount expeditions by Leif Erikson, his brother Thorvald, and Thorfinn Karlsefni, who attempted a settlement and encountered Indigenous peoples the Norse called Skrælingjar. Trade gave way to conflict, and the ventures were abandoned after a few years.

Archaeology

The site at L'Anse aux Meadows, at the northern tip of Newfoundland — excavated in the 1960s by Helge and Anne Stine Ingstad — is the only confirmed Norse settlement in North America south of Greenland, with turf buildings, iron working debris and butternuts indicating voyages further south. In 2021 tree-ring analysis dated Norse woodworking there to exactly 1021 CE.
