The Union for a Popular Movement (French: Union pour un mouvement populaire, UMP) was a centre-right political party in France. Founded in 2002 as the Union pour la majorité présidentielle to unite supporters of President Jacques Chirac, it merged the Gaullist Rally for the Republic (RPR) with most of Liberal Democracy and part of the centrist UDF, becoming France's main party of the right for over a decade. In 2015, under Nicolas Sarkozy, it was renamed The Republicans.

History

After Chirac's re-election in 2002, the UMP won a large majority in the National Assembly and held power with prime ministers Jean-Pierre Raffarin and Dominique de Villepin. Sarkozy took the party leadership in 2004 and won the presidency in 2007, governing with François Fillon as prime minister. The party lost the presidency and its Assembly majority in 2012 to François Hollande's Socialists.

The 2012 leadership contest between Jean-François Copé and François Fillon ended in a disputed vote and a near-split, resolved by a re-run election. The party was further damaged by the Bygmalion campaign-finance scandal. Sarkozy returned as president of the party in November 2014 and drove the 2015 name change to The Republicans, intended as a relaunch.

Ideology and factions

The UMP combined Gaullist, liberal-conservative, Christian democratic and conservative-liberal currents, organized in internal movements ("sensibilités") ranging from the national-conservative Droite Populaire to pro-European moderates. It generally favoured market reform, firm law-and-order policies, controlled immigration and a strong French role in the European Union.

Electoral results

The UMP and its allies won the legislative elections of 2002 and 2007 and the presidential elections of 2002 (Chirac) and 2007 (Sarkozy); it came first in the European Parliament elections of 2009 and 2014, and dominated most regional and departmental government for much of its existence.
