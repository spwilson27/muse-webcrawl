Seizure

A seizure is a period of abnormal, excessive or synchronous neuronal activity in the brain, producing signs and symptoms that range from dramatic convulsions to subtle lapses of awareness. Seizures may be provoked by acute insults such as fever, head injury, low blood sugar or alcohol withdrawal, or occur unprovoked as part of epilepsy, a chronic neurological disorder defined by recurrent unprovoked seizures.

Classification

The International League Against Epilepsy classifies seizures by onset. Focal seizures begin in one hemisphere and may preserve or impair awareness; they can produce localised motor jerking, sensory phenomena, or experiential symptoms such as déjà vu, and may evolve into bilateral tonic–clonic seizures. Generalised seizures engage both hemispheres from the outset and include tonic–clonic (formerly "grand mal") convulsions, absence seizures with brief staring spells, and myoclonic, tonic and atonic seizures. A seizure lasting longer than five minutes, or repeated seizures without recovery between them, constitutes status epilepticus, a medical emergency.

Causes and diagnosis

Beyond epilepsy, seizures arise from stroke, brain tumours, infection, metabolic derangement, drug effects and withdrawal, and genetic channelopathies. Diagnosis rests on the clinical history and eyewitness accounts, supported by electroencephalography (EEG), which may capture epileptiform discharges, and by MRI to identify structural causes. Blood tests help exclude metabolic triggers.

Management

First aid for a convulsive seizure consists of protecting the person from injury, timing the event, and placing them in the recovery position afterwards; nothing should be put in the mouth. Long-term treatment generally uses anti-seizure medications such as levetiracetam, lamotrigine or valproate, chosen by seizure type and patient factors. For drug-resistant focal epilepsy, resective surgery, neurostimulation and ketogenic diets are options. Status epilepticus is treated urgently with benzodiazepines followed by second-line agents.
