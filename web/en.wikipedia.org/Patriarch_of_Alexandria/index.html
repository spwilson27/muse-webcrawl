Patriarch of Alexandria


Cathedral of Saint Mark in Alexandria

The Patriarch of Alexandria is the title held by the archbishops of Alexandria, Egypt, heads of one of the most ancient sees of Christianity, traditionally founded by Saint Mark the Evangelist in the 1st century AD. Alexandria was one of the great patriarchates of the early church, alongside Rome, Antioch, Jerusalem, and later Constantinople.

History

The Alexandrian church was a center of early Christian scholarship, home to theologians such as Clement, Origen, Athanasius, and Cyril. Following the Council of Chalcedon in 451, the church divided over Christological doctrine: the majority of Egyptian Christians rejected the council and formed what became the Coptic Orthodox Church, while a Chalcedonian (Melkite) line continued in communion with Constantinople.

Current claimants

Today several church leaders bear titles connected with the Alexandrian patriarchate: the Coptic Orthodox Pope of Alexandria (currently Tawadros II), head of the Oriental Orthodox Coptic Church; the Greek Orthodox Patriarch of Alexandria (currently Theodore II); the Coptic Catholic Patriarch of Alexandria; and the Melkite Greek Catholic Patriarch, who includes Alexandria among his titles. Each line traces its succession back to the apostolic see.
