Firewall (computing)

In computing, a firewall is a network security system that monitors and controls incoming and outgoing network traffic based on predetermined security rules. A firewall typically establishes a barrier between a trusted network and an untrusted network, such as the Internet or another private network.

Types

Firewalls are categorised according to where communication is intercepted and the state being traced. Packet-filtering firewalls inspect individual packets against rules based on addresses, ports and protocols. Stateful firewalls track the state of connections and make decisions in that context. Application-layer firewalls (proxies) inspect the content of specific services such as HTTP, while next-generation firewalls add deep packet inspection, intrusion prevention and application awareness. Host-based firewalls run on individual machines; network firewalls are appliances or software guarding a boundary.

History

The term was borrowed from the fire-resistant walls that separate parts of a building. Network firewall technology emerged in the late 1980s as the Internet grew: early packet filters were described by engineers at Digital Equipment Corporation, and researchers at AT&T Bell Labs, including Bill Cheswick and Steve Bellovin, developed early application gateways and popularised the concept in the 1990s.

Limitations

Firewalls cannot protect against attacks that bypass them — for example through permitted services, malicious content carried in allowed traffic, or insider misuse — and so form only one layer of a defence-in-depth strategy.
