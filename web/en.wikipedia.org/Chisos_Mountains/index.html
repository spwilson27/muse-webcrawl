Chisos Mountains

The Chisos Mountains are a mountain range located in the Big Bend area of the Trans-Pecos region of Texas, United States. The range is contained entirely within Big Bend National Park, making it the only mountain range in the United States to be fully within the boundaries of a national park.

The Chisos Mountains, seen from the desert floor of Big Bend National Park

Geography

The Chisos Mountains are volcanic in origin, formed by eruptions and intrusions during the Eocene epoch roughly 35 to 44 million years ago. The highest point in the range is Emory Peak, which rises to 7,825 feet (2,385 m) above sea level and is the highest point in Brewster County and in Big Bend National Park.

At the heart of the range lies the Chisos Basin, a natural bowl ringed by peaks including Casa Grande Peak, Toll Mountain, and Vernon Bailey Peak. The basin collects rainfall and supports a comparatively lush woodland of piñon pine, juniper, and oak — a striking contrast to the surrounding Chihuahuan Desert lowlands.

Ecology

Because of their elevation, the Chisos Mountains function as a "sky island," harboring species more typical of cooler, wetter climates. Notable wildlife includes black bears, mountain lions, white-tailed deer, and the Carmen Mountains white-tailed deer. The mountains are also a noted birding destination, with species such as the Colima warbler found nowhere else in the United States.

Recreation

The range is a popular destination for hiking and camping. Trails include the Window Trail, the Lost Mine Trail, the South Rim Trail, and the strenuous ascent of Emory Peak. The Chisos Mountains Lodge, located in the basin, is the only lodge within Big Bend National Park.
