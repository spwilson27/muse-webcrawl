Order of Saint Benedict

Ordo Sancti Benedicti (OSB)
Founded: c. 529, Monte Cassino, Italy
Founder: Benedict of Nursia
Motto: Ora et labora ("pray and work")
Rule: Rule of Saint Benedict

The Order of Saint Benedict (Latin: Ordo Sancti Benedicti, abbreviated OSB), sometimes called the Black Monks after the colour of their habits, is a Catholic religious order of monks, nuns and oblates who follow the Rule of Saint Benedict, written in the 6th century by Benedict of Nursia. Founded around 529 at Monte Cassino in Italy, it is the oldest religious order in Western Christianity and a foundational influence on Western monasticism.

The Rule

The Rule of Saint Benedict organises the monastic day around prayer (the Liturgy of the Hours, or opus Dei), sacred reading (lectio divina) and manual work, summarised in the motto ora et labora. Monks take vows of stability, conversion of life and obedience. Each abbey is autonomous, governed by an elected abbot; since 1893 houses have been grouped into congregations under an international Benedictine Confederation headed by an Abbot Primate resident at Sant'Anselmo in Rome.

History

Benedictine monasteries spread across Europe in the early Middle Ages and became centres of learning, agriculture and manuscript preservation. The order was repeatedly reformed — notably through Cluny (from 910) and the Cistercian movement (from 1098), which began as a stricter observance of the Rule. Benedictines were suppressed in many countries during the Reformation and the French Revolution, but revived in the 19th century and established missions and schools worldwide.
