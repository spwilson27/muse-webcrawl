Robert II of France

Robert II, "the Pious", King of the Franks

Robert II (27 March 972 – 20 July 1031), called the Pious (French: le Pieux), was King of the Franks from 996 to 1031, the second ruler of the Capetian dynasty. The son of Hugh Capet, he was crowned junior king in 987 to secure the new dynasty's succession.

Reign

Robert's reign was spent trying to extend royal authority beyond the Île-de-France and Orléanais, the small royal domain, against powerful magnates. He conquered the duchy of Burgundy after a long struggle and annexed the counties of Dreux and Melun, while facing revolts by his own sons late in his reign.

Marriages and excommunication

His second marriage, to his cousin Bertha of Burgundy, was annulled under pressure from Pope Gregory V, who excommunicated Robert for refusing to part from her. He then married Constance of Arles, whose southern court was controversial at Paris. Robert's deep personal piety — he composed hymns and performed acts of charity — earned him his epithet and a reputation for sanctity.

Succession

Robert crowned his son Hugh Magnus co-king in 1017 and, after Hugh's death, his younger son Henry in 1027, continuing the Capetian practice of association that secured the dynasty. He died at Melun in 1031 and was buried at Saint-Denis.
