Bois de Boulogne

The Bois de Boulogne is a large public park on the western edge of Paris, France, in the 16th arrondissement. Covering about 846 hectares — more than twice the size of New York's Central Park — it occupies the site of the ancient forest of Rouvray and includes lakes, gardens, the Longchamp and Auteuil racecourses, the Jardin d'Acclimatation and the Fondation Louis Vuitton museum.

History

The forest was a royal hunting ground from the Middle Ages and haunt of brigands; the name comes from a chapel of Notre-Dame de Boulogne built under Philip IV. Under Napoleon III, Baron Haussmann and engineer Jean-Charles Alphand transformed it from 1852 into an English-style landscape park with lakes (the Lac Inférieur and Lac Supérieur), cascades and carriage drives. It was annexed to Paris in 1860.

Features

Attractions include the Pré-Catelan gardens, the Parc de Bagatelle with its rose gardens, the Roland Garros stadium (home of the French Open, just outside its southern edge), two horse-racing tracks, and boating on the Lac Inférieur.
