Snohomish County, Washington


Downtown Everett, the county seat

Snohomish County is a county located in the U.S. state of Washington. With a population of 827,957 as of the 2020 census, it is the third-most populous county in Washington, after nearby King and Pierce counties. The county seat and largest city is Everett. The county was created out of Island County on January 14, 1861, and is named for the Snohomish tribe. It is included in the Seattle–Tacoma–Bellevue metropolitan statistical area.

Geography

Snohomish County covers 2,196 square miles (5,690 km²), stretching from Puget Sound in the west to the crest of the Cascade Range in the east. Its landscape ranges from coastal lowlands and river valleys—the Snohomish, Skykomish, Snoqualmie, and Stillaguamish rivers—to forested mountains including Mount Pilchuck and Glacier Peak, the county's highest point at 10,541 feet (3,213 m). Much of the eastern county lies within the Mount Baker–Snoqualmie National Forest.

Economy

The county's economy is strongly associated with aerospace manufacturing: Boeing's Everett factory, the largest building in the world by volume, assembles wide-body aircraft including the 767, 777, and 767-based tanker, and employs tens of thousands of workers. Other major industries include healthcare, biotechnology, naval operations at Naval Station Everett, and agriculture in the river valleys.

Government and transport

Snohomish County is governed by a county executive and a five-member county council under a home-rule charter adopted in 1980. Public transport is provided by Community Transit, Everett Transit, and Sound Transit, which operates commuter rail and is extending the Link light rail system into the county. Paine Field (Snohomish County Airport) offers commercial passenger service.
