The Shannon–Erne Waterway (Irish: Uiscebhealach na Sionainne is na hÉirne) is a canalised waterway in Ireland, running 63 km (39 miles) from the village of Leitrim in County Leitrim to Upper Lough Erne in County Fermanagh. It links the River Shannon with the River Erne, creating — together with the Shannon navigation and the Erne system — the longest navigable waterway network in Ireland and Britain, popular with hire-cruiser tourism.

History

The waterway was first built between 1846 and 1860 as the Ballinamore and Ballyconnell Canal, intended to carry commercial traffic between the Shannon and the Erne through a chain of lakes, rivers, and canal cuts, and partly built as a famine-relief and drainage work. Completed just as the railways were destroying canal trade, it was a commercial failure: only a handful of boats — tradition says eight — ever passed through it during its original working life, and it fell derelict by the early 20th century.

Restoration

As a cross-border project of the Irish and British governments, supported by EU funding and promoted as a symbol of cooperation during the Northern Ireland peace process, the canal was restored and reopened on 23 May 1994 by the Irish Minister and British officials. The restored waterway follows the old route through Lough Scur, St John's Lough, Garadice Lough, and a series of smaller lakes, with 16 restored locks. Unusually, the locks are operated by boaters themselves using a smart-card system to power the gates and sluices. The restoration was carried out to a standard suitable for modern cruisers, with new moorings, service blocks, and bridges.

Route and use

From Leitrim village the waterway rises through locks past Keshcarrigan and Ballinamore to the summit near Lough Scur, then descends through Ballyconnell to enter the Erne system at Belturbet and Upper Lough Erne. It is managed by Waterways Ireland, the cross-border body for inland navigations. The waterway is used almost entirely by leisure craft — private boats and the hire fleets based at Carrick-on-Shannon and on the Erne — and is noted for its quiet, rural scenery and wildlife.
