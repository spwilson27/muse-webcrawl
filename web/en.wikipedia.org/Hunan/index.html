Hunan

Hunan (Chinese: 湖南; pinyin: Húnán) is a landlocked province of the People's Republic of China, located in the middle reaches of the Yangtze River and south of Lake Dongting, from which it takes its name ("south of the lake"). Its capital and largest city is Changsha. Hunan borders Hubei to the north, Jiangxi to the east, Guangdong and Guangxi to the south, Guizhou to the west, and Chongqing to the northwest.

With a population of about 66 million, Hunan is one of China's most populous provinces. Its area is about 211,800 square kilometres (81,800 sq mi).

History

Hunan has been an important agricultural region since ancient times, supplying rice to much of China. In the twentieth century the province became famous as the home of Mao Zedong, born in Shaoshan in 1893, and of many other early leaders of the Chinese Communist Party. The Xiang Army, raised in Hunan by Zeng Guofan, played a decisive role in suppressing the Taiping Rebellion in the 1860s.

Geography

The province is ringed by mountains — the Wuling Mountains to the northwest, the Xuefeng Mountains to the west, the Nanling to the south, and the Luoxiao Mountains to the east — with the low-lying Dongting Plain in the north. The Xiang River flows north through the province past Changsha into Lake Dongting. Zhangjiajie, in the northwest, is famed for its towering quartzite sandstone pillars and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Cuisine

Hunan cuisine (Xiang cuisine) is one of the Eight Great Traditions of Chinese cooking, renowned for its liberal use of chili peppers, shallots, and garlic — often said to be even spicier than Sichuan cuisine, with a "dry heat" (gan la) rather than the numbing heat of Sichuan pepper. Famous dishes include Chairman Mao's red-braised pork, steamed fish head with chopped chili, and changsha stinky tofu.
