Shruti

Shruti (Sanskrit: श्रुति, IAST: Śruti), in Sanskrit literally meaning "that which is heard", is an ancient Indian religious text. Shruti is the canon of Hinduism, believed to be divine revelation. It is one of the two categories of Hindu scripture, the other being Smriti ("that which is remembered"), or traditional canonical texts.

The Shruti texts are believed by Hindus to be the word of God, as transmitted to ancient sages (rishis) who heard them. Unlike Smriti texts, which are attributed to specific authors, Shruti texts are considered authorless (apauruṣeya, "not of a man").

Contents

The Shruti canon is composed of the four Vedas: the Rigveda, the Yajurveda, the Samaveda, and the Atharvaveda. Each of the Vedas is subdivided into four components: the Samhitas (mantras and benedictions), the Aranyakas (texts on rituals, ceremonies, sacrifices, and symbolic sacrifices), the Brahmanas (commentaries on rituals, ceremonies, and sacrifices), and the Upanishads (texts discussing meditation, philosophy, and spiritual knowledge).

Role in Hinduism

Since Shruti is considered divine revelation, it is held in higher esteem than Smriti and is accepted as the authority for resolving disputes regarding Hindu doctrine. The preservation of Shruti for millennia relied on oral transmission, with elaborate mnemonic techniques used to ensure the texts were passed down accurately.
