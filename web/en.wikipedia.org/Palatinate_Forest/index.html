Palatinate Forest

The Palatinate Forest (German: Pfälzerwald) is a low-mountain region in southwestern Germany, located in the Palatinate in the state of Rhineland-Palatinate. With an area of about 1,771 square kilometres (684 sq mi), it is the largest contiguous forest in Germany. Together with the northern Vosges in neighbouring France, it forms the cross-border Palatinate Forest–North Vosges Biosphere Reserve, designated by UNESCO.

Geography

The forest covers the western part of the Palatinate region between the Rhine valley in the east and the Saarland in the west. Its landscape is characterised by rolling hills of Bunter sandstone, deeply incised valleys, and distinctive red sandstone cliffs and rock formations, particularly in the Dahner Felsenland near Dahn. The highest elevation is the Kalmit (673 m) near Maikammer, followed by the Kesselberg and the Roßberg.

Nature park and biosphere reserve

The Palatinate Forest Nature Park, founded in 1958, is one of Germany's largest nature parks. In 1992 it was recognised as a UNESCO biosphere reserve, and in 1998 it was merged with the French Northern Vosges Regional Nature Park to form the first cross-border biosphere reserve in the European Union. Mixed forests of pine, beech, and oak predominate, and the area is known for its extensive network of hiking trails, managed huts, and ruined castles such as Trifels, where Richard the Lionheart was held in 1193.

Settlement

Settlement is concentrated in the valleys and on the eastern edge along the German Wine Route, where villages such as Deidesheim and Neustadt an der Weinstraße are surrounded by vineyards. The interior of the forest remains sparsely populated, with small towns including Dahn, Pirmasens, and Kaiserslautern on its margins.
