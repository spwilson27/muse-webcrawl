Peter Lynch

Peter Lynch (born January 19, 1944) is an American investor and former mutual fund manager. As manager of the Magellan Fund at Fidelity Investments from 1977 to 1990, he averaged a 29.2% annual return, more than double the S&P 500, making it the best-performing mutual fund in the world during his tenure.

Investment philosophy

Lynch popularised the idea that individual investors can outperform professionals by using their local knowledge — his famous advice to "buy what you know". He classified companies into categories such as slow growers, stalwarts, fast growers, cyclicals, turnarounds, and asset plays, and coined the term "tenbagger" for a stock that returns ten times its purchase price.

Books

He is the author (with John Rothchild) of the bestsellers One Up on Wall Street (1989) and Beating the Street (1993), and of Learn to Earn (1995). After retiring at 46, he devoted himself to philanthropy through the Lynch Foundation.
