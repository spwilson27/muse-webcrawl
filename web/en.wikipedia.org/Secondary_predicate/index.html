Secondary predicate

A secondary predicate is a predicative expression (often an adjective or adjective phrase) that conveys information about a noun phrase in a sentence but is not part of the main (primary) predication. In "John ate the meat raw", the adjective raw is a secondary predicate describing the meat during the eating; in "She arrived exhausted", exhausted describes the subject.

FieldSyntax, semantics
TypesDepictive; resultative
OrientationSubject-oriented or object-oriented

Types

Depictive secondary predicates describe the state of a participant at the time of the event: "He drank the coffee hot" (object-oriented) or "They left angry" (subject-oriented). Resultative secondary predicates describe the state that results from the event: "She hammered the metal flat" or "The pond froze solid". Resultatives are especially productive in English and Germanic languages and are restricted in many Romance languages.

Analysis

Secondary predicates have been central to syntactic debates about constituency, control, and small clauses. Analyses differ over whether they form a constituent with the noun phrase they predicate of, and how their understood subject is determined by the main clause structure.
