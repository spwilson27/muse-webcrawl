Mercedes-Benz is a German luxury automotive brand of the Mercedes-Benz Group AG, headquartered in Stuttgart. The brand traces its origins to Karl Benz's Patent-Motorwagen of 1886 — the first practical automobile — and to Gottlieb Daimler's motor carriages; the two companies merged in 1926 to form Daimler-Benz, combining the names of Benz & Cie. and Daimler's "Mercedes" marque, named after Mercédès Jellinek, daughter of dealer Emil Jellinek. Mercedes-Benz cars are sold in nearly every world market and are among the best-known luxury vehicles globally.

History

After the 1926 merger, the combined company built prestigious cars such as the SSK and the 500K/540K, and its Grand Prix "Silver Arrows" dominated racing in the 1930s. Production was devoted to the German war effort in the Second World War, using forced labour — a record the company has since documented and acknowledged. Post-war recovery centred on the 300 SL "Gullwing" (1954), which became an icon, and on a steadily expanding range of saloons. Safety innovations pioneered or popularized by Mercedes-Benz include the crumple zone (patented by Béla Barényi), anti-lock brakes (1978), the airbag, and electronic stability control.

Model range

The modern range is organized by letter classes — A-Class, C-Class, E-Class, and the flagship S-Class — alongside GL SUVs, coupés, roadsters, and the EQ electric sub-brand introduced in 2019. Performance versions are built by Mercedes-AMG, and ultra-luxury models carry the Mercedes-Maybach badge. The G-Class (G-Wagen), in production since 1979, is a cult model. The company announced plans to move toward an all-electric range in the 2030s where markets allow.

Motorsport

Mercedes-Benz has competed in Formula One in two eras: 1954–55, with Fangio's two titles, and from 2010 with its works team, the Mercedes-AMG Petronas F1 Team, which won eight consecutive constructors' titles in 2014–2021. The brand also has a storied history at Le Mans, marred by the 1955 disaster that killed driver Pierre Levegh and more than 80 spectators and prompted Mercedes' withdrawal from racing for three decades.
