Ioannis Kapodistrias

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Count Ioannis Kapodistrias
Born: 11 February 1776, Corfu
Died: 9 October 1831 (aged 55), Nafplio, Greece
Governor of Greece: 1828–1831
Known for: First head of state of independent Greece; former Russian foreign minister

Ioannis Kapodistrias (11 February 1776 – 9 October 1831) was a Greek statesman who served as foreign minister of the Russian Empire and then as the first head of state of independent Greece. A distinguished European diplomat of the Congress of Vienna era, he was elected Governor (Kyvernitis) of Greece in 1827 and undertook the building of the new state's administration, army, schools and finances before his assassination in 1831.

Early life and Russian service

Kapodistrias was born in Corfu, then under Venetian rule, into a noble family. He studied medicine, philosophy and law at Padua and served the Septinsular Republic, including as its secretary of state. After Corfu passed to France and then Britain, he entered Russian service in 1809, rising through the diplomatic corps during the Napoleonic Wars. At the Congress of Vienna (1814–15) he was a leading Russian negotiator, helped secure Swiss neutrality and constitution, and in 1816 became joint foreign minister of Russia with Karl Nesselrode. His liberal sympathies and support for the Greek cause made him suspect at the conservative Russian court, and he withdrew from service in 1822, settling in Geneva.

Governor of Greece

During the Greek War of Independence he supported the revolution diplomatically and financially. In April 1827 the Third National Assembly at Troezen elected him Governor of Greece for a seven-year term; he arrived at Nafplio in January 1828. Kapodistrias founded a national bank and a new currency (the phoenix), organised a regular army and administration, built schools and orphanages (including one for war orphans on Aegina), promoted agriculture, and established Greece's first university-level institutions and museums. His centralising, authoritarian methods — suspending the constitution, ruling by decree and postponing elections — alienated powerful regional families, shipowners of Hydra and the westernising political factions.

Assassination and legacy

On 9 October 1831, Kapodistrias was shot and stabbed outside the church of Saint Spyridon in Nafplio by Konstantis and Georgios Mavromichalis, members of the Maniot clan whose leader he had imprisoned. Greece descended into disorder until the Great Powers installed Prince Otto of Bavaria as king in 1832. Kapodistrias is honoured in Greece as the founder of the modern Greek state; the University of Athens bears his name as part of the National and Kapodistrian University of Athens, and his portrait appeared on Greek coinage.
