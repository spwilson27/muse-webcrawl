Ise Grand Shrine

The Ise Grand Shrine (Japanese: 伊勢神宮, Ise Jingū) is a Shinto shrine complex in Ise, Mie Prefecture, Japan, and is considered the most sacred shrine in Shinto. It comprises 125 shrines centred on two main sanctuaries: the Inner Shrine (Naikū), dedicated to the sun goddess Amaterasu, and the Outer Shrine (Gekū), dedicated to the food goddess Toyo'uke.

Ritual rebuilding

The shrine buildings are constructed of untreated Japanese cypress in the archaic shinmei-zukuri style, raised on pillars and thatched with reeds. By the rite of Shikinen Sengū, first held in 690 under Empress Jitō, the main sanctuaries are dismantled and rebuilt identically on an adjacent site every twenty years; the most recent rebuilding, the 62nd, was completed in 2013. The old timbers are redistributed to other shrines across Japan.

History and access

Tradition dates the founding of the Naikū to about 4 BCE, when the sacred mirror — one of the three Imperial Regalia — was enshrined here. Pilgrimage to Ise was a mass phenomenon in the Edo period. The innermost buildings stand behind four wooden fences and can be glimpsed only from outside; the chief priestess, the Saiō, is traditionally an imperial princess.
