Glossary of musical terminology

Musical terminology is rich in words borrowed from Italian, French and German, the languages of the courts and conservatoires where Western art music developed. This glossary covers common terms found in scores and musical writing.

Terms

accelerando
Gradually speeding up the tempo.
adagio
A slow tempo, "at ease"; also a movement in that tempo.
allegro
A fast, bright tempo, faster than andante and moderato.
andante
A tempo "at a walking pace", moderately slow.
arpeggio
The notes of a chord played in succession rather than simultaneously.
cadence
A sequence of chords that brings a phrase or section to a close.
cantabile
"Songlike" — to be played in a smooth, singing style.
crescendo
Gradually getting louder (literally "growing").
da capo (D.C.)
"From the head" — return to the beginning of the piece.
decrescendo / diminuendo
Gradually getting quieter.
fermata
A sign indicating a note or rest held at the performer's discretion.
forte (f) / fortissimo (ff)
Loud / very loud. Piano (p) means soft; pianissimo (pp) very soft.
glissando
A continuous slide between two pitches.
legato
Notes played smoothly and connected, without gaps.
mezzo
"Half" — as in mezzo-forte (moderately loud) or mezzo-soprano.
pizzicato
Playing a string instrument by plucking rather than bowing.
presto
A very fast tempo, faster than allegro.
ritardando (rit.)
Gradually slowing down the tempo.
sostenuto
Sustained, with notes held for their full value.
staccato
Notes played short and detached, indicated by dots above or below noteheads.
tempo
The speed of the underlying beat of a piece of music.
tutti
"All" — a passage for the full ensemble after a solo section.
vibrato
A slight, regular fluctuation of pitch used to enrich a sustained tone.
