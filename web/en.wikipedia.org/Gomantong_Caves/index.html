Gomantong Caves

The Gomantong Caves are an intricate system of caves inside Gomantong Hill in Sandakan Division, Sabah, Malaysia. The hill is the largest limestone outcrop in the Lower Kinabatangan area. Situated in a protected forest reserve of about 3,297 hectares, the caves are famous both for their vast colonies of bats and swiftlets and for the centuries-old harvest of edible bird's nests.

Description

The system has two main complexes. Simud Hitam ("Black Cave"), the more accessible, has a ceiling up to about 90 metres (300 ft) high and takes its name from the "black" nests of the black-nest swiftlet found there. Above it, larger but harder to reach, is Simud Putih ("White Cave"), where the more valuable "white" nests of the white-nest swiftlet are harvested. Boardwalks allow visitors to walk through Simud Hitam past deep deposits of guano.

Wildlife

The caves house enormous populations of wrinkled-lipped free-tailed bats and swiftlets. At dusk, millions of bats stream out of the caves to feed while bat hawks hunt them at the entrances. Inside, the guano supports cockroaches, beetles, and other invertebrates, and cave racers and other snakes prey on the birds and bats.

Nest harvesting

Edible bird's nests have been harvested at Gomantong since at least the 13th century for trade to China, where they are used in bird's nest soup. Harvesting rights are traditionally licensed, and the regulated harvest takes place twice a year, with collectors climbing rattan ladders and ropes to the cave ceilings.
