Regent Diamond

The Regent Diamond is a 140.64-carat (28.128 g) cushion-cut diamond, renowned for its exceptional clarity and pale blue tint. It is kept in the Galerie d'Apollon of the Louvre Museum in Paris as part of the French Crown Jewels.

History

The rough stone, originally about 410 carats, was found in 1698 at the Kollur mine in India's Golconda region. Legend holds that a slave smuggled it out concealed in a wound in his leg. It was acquired by Thomas Pitt, governor of Madras, and sold in 1717 to Philippe II, Duke of Orléans, Regent of France — hence its name. Cut in London over two years at great cost, the Regent was set in the crowns of Louis XV and Louis XVI, worn by Marie Antoinette, and later mounted by Napoleon in the pommel of his sword.

Theft and recovery

Stolen in the 1792 Garde-Meuble theft, it was recovered hidden in a Paris attic a year later. Today it is displayed alongside the Sancy and Hortensia diamonds.
