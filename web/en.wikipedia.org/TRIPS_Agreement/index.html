The Agreement on Trade-Related Aspects of Intellectual Property Rights (TRIPS) is an international legal agreement between all the member nations of the World Trade Organization (WTO). It establishes minimum standards for the regulation by national governments of many forms of intellectual property as applied to nationals of other WTO member nations.

History

TRIPS was negotiated at the end of the Uruguay Round of the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade (GATT) between 1989 and 1990 and is administered by the WTO. It was the first multilateral agreement to bring intellectual property rules into the global trading system, incorporating by reference the substantive obligations of the Paris and Berne Conventions administered by WIPO.

Content

The agreement covers copyright and related rights, trademarks, geographical indications, industrial designs, patents, layout designs of integrated circuits, and protection of undisclosed information. It requires member states to make patent protection available for inventions in all fields of technology for a minimum term of 20 years, and it sets out enforcement procedures and dispute-settlement mechanisms.

Public health and the Doha Declaration

The agreement's effect on access to medicines in developing countries has been controversial. In 2001 WTO members adopted the Doha Declaration on TRIPS and Public Health, affirming that the agreement should be interpreted in a manner supportive of the right to protect public health, including through compulsory licensing of patented pharmaceuticals. A 2017 amendment created a permanent mechanism allowing countries with insufficient manufacturing capacity to import generic medicines produced under compulsory licence.
