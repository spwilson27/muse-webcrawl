Arthur Rostron

Born: 14 May 1869, Bolton, Lancashire

Died: 4 November 1940, Southampton

Occupation: Sea captain

Known for: Rescue of Titanic survivors aboard RMS Carpathia

Sir Arthur Henry Rostron, KBE, RD, RNR (14 May 1869 – 4 November 1940) was a British merchant navy officer and captain of the RMS Carpathia, famous for rescuing the survivors of the sinking of RMS Titanic in April 1912.

The Titanic rescue

On the night of 14–15 April 1912, Carpathia was on passage from New York to Fiume when her wireless operator received Titanic's distress calls. Rostron ordered the ship turned around and raced some 58 nautical miles through ice-strewn waters at record speed, preparing his ship meticulously for rescue operations. Carpathia picked up 705 survivors from the lifeboats and carried them to New York.

Honours and later career

Rostron was feted on both sides of the Atlantic: he received a Congressional Gold Medal from the United States, a silver cup from the survivors' committee presented by Margaret "Molly" Brown, and was later knighted. He served in the First World War and rose to become commodore of the Cunard fleet before retiring in 1931. His memoir, Home from the Sea, was published in 1931.
