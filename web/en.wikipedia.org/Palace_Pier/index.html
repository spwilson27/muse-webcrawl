Palace Pier

LocationBrighton, East Sussex, England
Opened20 May 1899
Length525 metres (1,722 ft)
TypePleasure pier

The Palace Pier, now officially known as the Brighton Pier, is a Grade II* listed pleasure pier in Brighton, England, located in the city centre opposite the Old Steine. It opened in 1899 and remains one of Britain's most popular tourist attractions, featuring amusement arcades, funfair rides, and food outlets.

History

Construction began in 1891, replacing the earlier Chain Pier, which was destroyed in a storm in 1896. The pier was designed by R. St George Moore and opened on 20 May 1899. It was originally known as the Palace Pier, a name still used by many locals despite the formal renaming in 2000. During the Second World War a section of decking was removed to hinder a possible enemy landing. A concert hall near the pier head, opened in 1911, was demolished in 1986 to make way for the funfair.

Attractions

The pier houses amusement arcades, roller coasters and other fairground rides, bars and restaurants. It is a prominent symbol of Brighton and appears in numerous films and television programmes.
