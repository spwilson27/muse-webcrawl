Cross-cultural psychology

Cross-cultural psychology is the scientific study of human behaviour and mental processes under diverse cultural conditions. It examines how individual psychological processes are influenced by culture and seeks to establish which findings — on perception, cognition, emotion, personality and development — are universal and which are culturally specific.

Key themes

A central distinction is between individualistic cultures, which emphasise autonomy and personal goals, and collectivistic cultures, which emphasise group harmony and interdependence; researchers such as Geert Hofstede and Harry Triandis mapped how these orientations shape self-concept, motivation and social behaviour. Other major topics include analytic versus holistic styles of thinking, culturally varying expressions of emotion, and the migration and acculturation of individuals between cultures.

Method and criticism

Cross-cultural psychologists have criticised the field's historical reliance on samples from Western, educated, industrialised, rich and democratic ("WEIRD") populations, arguing that conclusions drawn from such samples do not automatically generalise. Researchers distinguish cross-cultural psychology, which compares cultures, from cultural psychology, which treats culture and mind as mutually constitutive, and from indigenous psychologies developed within particular societies.
