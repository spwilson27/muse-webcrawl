Rodeo

TypeCompetitive equestrian and livestock sport
OriginSpanish and Mexican vaquero traditions; organised sport from late 19th century, United States

Governing bodyProfessional Rodeo Cowboys Association (PRCA)

Rodeo (from Spanish rodear, "to surround") is a competitive equestrian sport that arose out of the working practices of cattle herding in Spain and Mexico and was adapted in the United States and other cattle-producing regions. Modern rodeo events test skills derived from ranch work — riding bucking horses and bulls, wrestling steers, and roping calves and cattle — before paying audiences. It is the official state sport of Wyoming, South Dakota, and Texas.

History

Rodeo grew from the informal contests of Mexican vaqueros and American cowboys, who competed at roundups and festivals in bronc riding, roping, and racing. Claims for the "first" commercial rodeo include events in Pecos, Texas (1883) and Prescott, Arizona (1888), while Buffalo Bill's Wild West shows popularised cowboy spectacle worldwide. The sport was professionalised in the twentieth century: the Cowboys' Turtle Association, founded after a 1936 strike in Boston, evolved into the Rodeo Cowboys Association and finally the PRCA (1975). The National Finals Rodeo, held since 1959 and in Las Vegas since 1985, crowns world champions in each event.

Events

Standard PRCA events are divided into roughstock and timed events. In the roughstock events — bareback riding, saddle bronc riding, and bull riding — the contestant must stay aboard a bucking animal for eight seconds, judged on both the rider's control and the animal's bucking power; saddle bronc riding, rodeo's classic event, descends directly from breaking wild horses. Timed events include steer wrestling (bulldogging), in which a mounted rider leaps onto a running steer's horns and throws it; team roping and tie-down roping; and barrel racing, a women's event in which horse and rider race a cloverleaf pattern around three barrels. Breakaway roping has grown rapidly as a women's event. Bull riding also exists as a standalone sport under the Professional Bull Riders (PBR) organisation.

Animals and welfare

Rodeo stock — bucking horses and bulls — are bred for the purpose and are valuable athletes, worked only seconds per year; flank straps encourage bucking by irritation rather than pain. Nevertheless, animal welfare groups criticise rodeo for injuries to animals and contestants and for practices such as calf roping, and some jurisdictions have banned particular events or devices. Rodeo is also one of the most dangerous sports for humans, with bull riders suffering especially high rates of injury.

Culture

Beyond the PRCA mainstream, rodeo culture includes gay rodeos, Black cowboy circuits, collegiate and high school rodeo, and the Mexican charreada — Mexico's national sport, with its own elaborate events and dress. Famous rodeos include the Calgary Stampede in Canada, Cheyenne Frontier Days in Wyoming, and the Pendleton Round-Up in Oregon.
