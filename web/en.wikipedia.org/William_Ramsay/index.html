William Ramsay

Sir William Ramsay (2 October 1852 – 23 July 1916) was a Scottish chemist who discovered the noble gases and received the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1904 'in recognition of his services in the discovery of the inert gaseous elements in air'.

William Ramsay

Born: 2 October 1852, Glasgow
Died: 23 July 1916, High Wycombe
Known for: noble gases — argon, helium, neon, krypton, xenon

Discoveries

With Lord Rayleigh he discovered argon (1894); he isolated helium on Earth (1895), and with Morris Travers discovered neon, krypton and xenon (1898), placing a new group in the periodic table. He later worked on radioactivity with Frederick Soddy, showing helium is produced in radium decay.

Career

Ramsay held chairs at University College Bristol and University College London. He was knighted in 1902.
