Interferon gamma release assay

Interferon gamma release assays (IGRAs) are medical tests used in the diagnosis of some infectious diseases, especially tuberculosis. They are a type of indirect test: they measure a person's immune response — the release of interferon gamma (IFN-γ) by T cells — when blood is exposed to antigens specific to Mycobacterium tuberculosis, such as ESAT-6 and CFP-10.

Tests and advantages

The two widely used commercial tests are QuantiFERON-TB Gold (an ELISA-based whole-blood test) and T-SPOT.TB (an ELISPOT assay). Because their antigens are absent from the BCG vaccine strain and most non-tuberculous mycobacteria, IGRAs do not produce the false positives that affect the tuberculin skin test in BCG-vaccinated people, and they require only a single patient visit.

Limitations

Like the skin test, IGRAs cannot distinguish latent tuberculosis infection from active disease, and results can be indeterminate in immunocompromised patients. Cost and laboratory requirements have also limited their use in low-resource settings.
