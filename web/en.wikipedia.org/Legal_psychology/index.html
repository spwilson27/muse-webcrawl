Legal psychology

Legal psychology is a subfield of psychology involving the study and application of psychological principles to legal issues and the justice system. It is closely related to, but distinct from, forensic psychology: legal psychology is primarily a research discipline concerned with how people behave within legal contexts, while forensic practice focuses on clinical work such as competency evaluations.


Jury decision-making is a major topic in legal psychology

Topics

Major research areas include eyewitness memory and identification procedures, the reliability of police lineups, interrogation and false confessions, jury selection and deliberation, judicial decision-making, criminal profiling (a controversial area), and the psychology of victims and offenders. Hugo Münsterberg's On the Witness Stand (1908) is considered an early landmark in the field.

Practice

Legal psychologists serve as trial consultants, expert witnesses, and advisers on policies such as double-blind lineup administration and recorded interrogations. Their professional organization in the United States is the American Psychology–Law Society, Division 41 of the American Psychological Association.
