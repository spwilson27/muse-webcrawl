The 1996 Monaco Grand Prix was a Formula One motor race held at the Circuit de Monaco on 19 May 1996. It was the sixth race of the 1996 World Championship and is remembered as one of the most chaotic and unpredictable Grands Prix ever held: run in heavy rain, it produced only three classified finishers and a shock victory for Olivier Panis and the Ligier team.

Race summary

Rain before the start turned the race into a process of attrition. Pole-sitter Michael Schumacher crashed his Ferrari on the first lap; Damon Hill led for Williams until his engine failed in the tunnel. Jean Alesi led briefly before suspension failure eliminated his Benetton.

Panis, who had qualified 14th, climbed through the field, passing Eddie Irvine's Ferrari with a bold move at the Loews hairpin. As the two-hour time limit approached, only a handful of cars remained. David Coulthard — racing in a helmet borrowed from Schumacher — closed in but could not pass before the race was halted at 75 laps. Johnny Herbert finished third for Sauber; only three cars were still running at the finish, with a few others classified on distance.

Legacy

It was Panis's only Formula One victory and Ligier's ninth and final win. The three finishers equalled the record low for a World Championship race, and the result stands as one of the great underdog triumphs in the sport's history.
