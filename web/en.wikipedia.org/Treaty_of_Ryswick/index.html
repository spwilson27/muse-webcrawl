Treaty of Ryswick

Signed20 September 1697
LocationRijswijk, Dutch Republic
PartiesFrance; England; Spain; Dutch Republic; Holy Roman Empire

The Treaty of Ryswick (also spelled Rijswijk) was signed on 20 September 1697 and ended the Nine Years' War between France and the Grand Alliance of England, Spain, the Holy Roman Empire, and the Dutch Republic. It takes its name from the town of Rijswijk in the Netherlands, where the negotiations were held.

Terms

France recognised William III as king of England and undertook to give no further assistance to the Jacobite cause. Louis XIV returned most of the territories seized since the Treaty of Nijmegen in 1678, including Luxembourg and places in the Spanish Netherlands, but retained Strasbourg and Alsace. Spain recovered Barcelona and other Catalan towns; the Dutch were allowed to garrison barrier fortresses in the Spanish Netherlands and obtained favourable commercial terms. France also formally ceded the western part of Hispaniola to Spain, the basis of the modern state of Haiti.

Significance

The treaty marked the first check on Louis XIV's expansion and recognised the legitimacy of the English Revolution settlement of 1688–89, although the peace lasted only a few years before the War of the Spanish Succession.
