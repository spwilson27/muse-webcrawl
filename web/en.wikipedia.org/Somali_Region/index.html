Somali Region

The Somali Region is a regional state in eastern Ethiopia. Its territory is the largest of Ethiopia's regional states, covering roughly 327,000 square kilometres. The capital city is Jijiga. The region borders Djibouti to the north, Somalia to the east and south, Kenya to the southwest, and the Ethiopian regions of Oromia and Afar to the west.

The population consists predominantly of ethnic Somalis, most of them Muslim, and many communities traditionally practise pastoralism across the arid lowlands historically known as the Ogaden. The region's economy is heavily based on livestock, which is exported through ports in Somaliland and Djibouti.

History

The area was incorporated into the Ethiopian Empire in the late nineteenth century under Menelik II. In 1977–78 the Ogaden War was fought between Ethiopia and Somalia over the territory; Somalia's attempt to annex it was repelled with Soviet and Cuban assistance to Ethiopia. After the fall of the Derg regime in 1991, the territory became one of the ethnically defined regional states of the federal Ethiopian constitution of 1995. A long-running insurgency by the Ogaden National Liberation Front (ONLF) ended with a peace agreement in 2018.

Geography and demographics

The region is mostly lowland, lying between 500 and 1,500 metres above sea level, with a hot, dry climate. The Shebelle, Genale, and Dawa rivers are its main waterways. Population estimates vary, but the region is home to several million people, with Jijiga, Gode, Kebri Dahar, and Degehabur among its principal towns.
