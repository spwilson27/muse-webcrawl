Marine salvage

Marine salvage is the process of recovering a ship and its cargo after a shipwreck or other maritime casualty. Salvage may encompass towing, re-floating a vessel, or effecting repairs to a ship. Protecting the coastal environment from oil spill or other contaminants from a wreck is also a priority.

Salvors are compensated under the principle of "no cure, no pay", traditionally embodied in Lloyd's Open Form, under which payment is contingent on successful recovery. The law of salvage has ancient roots in maritime codes such as the Rhodian law.

Modern salvage operations include wreck removal, cargo salvage, and environmental salvage following incidents such as groundings and sinkings.
