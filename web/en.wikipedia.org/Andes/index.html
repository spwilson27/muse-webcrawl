The Andes (Quechua: Anti) are the longest continental mountain range in the world, forming a continuous highland along the western edge of South America. The range stretches about 8,900 km (5,530 mi) from the Caribbean coast of Venezuela in the north to Tierra del Fuego in the south, through Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, Bolivia, Argentina, and Chile. Its width ranges from about 200 to 700 km, and its average elevation is around 4,000 m. The highest peak, Aconcagua in Argentina (6,961 m), is the tallest mountain outside Asia.

Geology

The Andes were formed by the subduction of the oceanic Nazca Plate (and in the south, the Antarctic Plate) beneath the South American Plate, a process that began in the Jurassic and continues today, producing frequent earthquakes and volcanic activity — the range contains most of the world's highest volcanoes, including Ojos del Salado. The central Andes include the Altiplano, a vast high plateau, the second largest in the world after Tibet's, on which lies Lake Titicaca, the world's highest navigable lake.

Climate and ecology

Climate varies dramatically with latitude and altitude, from tropical rainforests on Colombia's western slopes to the hyper-arid Atacama Desert in northern Chile and the glaciers of Patagonia. Vertical zonation produces bands such as the tierra caliente, templada, fría, and the high grasslands of the páramo and puna. The Andes are a biodiversity hotspot, home to the Andean condor, spectacled bear, vicuña, and the cinchona tree, source of quinine; the potato and tomato were domesticated here.

History and people

The Andes were the heartland of the Inca Empire, the largest state in pre-Columbian America, and of earlier civilisations including the Norte Chico, Tiwanaku, and Wari. Spanish conquest in the 1530s, led by Francisco Pizarro, brought devastating epidemics and colonial rule centred on silver mines such as Potosí. Today tens of millions of people live in the Andes, including large Quechua- and Aymara-speaking populations, and major cities such as Bogotá, Quito, and La Paz (the world's highest seat of government).
