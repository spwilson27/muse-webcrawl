Yoshida Shōin (1830–1859) was a Japanese samurai, scholar, and teacher from Chōshū Domain who became one of the most influential intellectual figures of the late Tokugawa period. Though executed at the age of 29, his students went on to lead the Meiji Restoration.

Life

Yoshida was trained in military strategy and became an instructor for the Chōshū domain. Alarmed by Japan's vulnerability after Commodore Perry's arrival, he attempted in 1854 to board one of Perry's black ships to travel abroad and study; the attempt failed and he was imprisoned. Later released, he ran a private academy, the Shōka Sonjuku, in Hagi.

Teaching and execution

At his academy Yoshida taught future leaders including Itō Hirobumi, Yamagata Aritomo, Takasugi Shinsaku, and Kido Takayoshi. His advocacy of imperial loyalism and opposition to the shogunate led to his arrest during the Ansei Purge and his execution in Edo in 1859.
