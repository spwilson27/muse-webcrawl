Livery company

The livery companies of the City of London are trade associations, mostly descended from medieval guilds, that regulated crafts and trades, trained apprentices and governed much of the City's commercial life. There are more than 110 companies, ranked in an order of precedence established in 1515 after disputes between leading companies.

History and role

The oldest, such as the Weavers, received royal charters in the twelfth century; the Mercers, Grocers, Drapers, Fishmongers, Goldsmiths and Merchant Taylors head the "Great Twelve". Members progress from apprentice to freeman, then to liveryman — so called from the distinctive uniform, or livery, worn on ceremonial occasions — and may serve as Master of the company. Liverymen retain a civic role: they elect the Lord Mayor and sheriffs of the City of London at Common Hall.

Today most companies combine charitable and educational work — supporting schools, almshouses and their trades — with ceremonial life centred on their halls. New companies, such as the Information Technologists and the Tax Advisers, continue to be formed for modern professions.
