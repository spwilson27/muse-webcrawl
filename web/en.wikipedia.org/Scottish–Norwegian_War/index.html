Scottish–Norwegian War

The Scottish–Norwegian War was a conflict fought from 1262 to 1266 between the Kingdom of Scotland under Alexander III and the Kingdom of Norway under Haakon IV, over sovereignty of the Hebrides and the Isle of Man, island territories that owed allegiance to the Norwegian crown but which Scottish kings had long sought to acquire.

Background

Since the twelfth century the kings of Scots had pressed claims to the Sudreys — the "Southern Isles" of the Hebrides and Man — ruled by Norse-Gaelic lords under Norwegian overlordship. Alexander II had attempted to buy or conquer the islands before his death in 1249. His son Alexander III renewed the pressure in the early 1260s, sending envoys to Norway and permitting raids into Skye and the western seaboard when negotiations failed.

The campaign of 1263

In response, Haakon IV assembled one of the largest fleets ever to leave Norway — perhaps 120 ships or more — and sailed to Scotland in the summer of 1263, reasserting his authority in Orkney, Shetland and the Hebrides and raiding around the Firth of Clyde. Negotiations near Largs were drawn out by the Scots as autumn approached. In early October a storm drove several Norwegian ships ashore near Largs, and the resulting skirmishes, later magnified in Scottish tradition as the Battle of Largs, ended inconclusively. With winter coming, Haakon withdrew to Orkney, where he died at Kirkwall in December 1263.

Treaty of Perth

Haakon's son and successor, Magnus VI, preferred peace. By the Treaty of Perth (1266), Norway ceded the Hebrides and the Isle of Man to Scotland in return for a payment of 4,000 marks and an annual rent of 100 marks, known as the "annual of Norway". Orkney and Shetland remained Norwegian — they would not pass to Scotland until the fifteenth century, as security for the dowry of Margaret of Denmark. The war thus settled the western frontier of the Scottish kingdom.
