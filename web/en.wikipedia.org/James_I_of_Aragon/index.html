James I the Conqueror (Catalan: Jaume el Conqueridor; 2 February 1208 – 27 July 1276) was King of Aragon, Count of Barcelona, and Lord of Montpellier from 1213 to 1276; King of Majorca from 1231 to 1276; and King of Valencia from 1238 to 1276. His long reign of 62 years is not only the longest of any Iberian monarch, but one of the longest in recorded history. He saw the expansion of the Crown of Aragon on all sides and presided over a flowering of Catalan culture and law.

Early life and accession

James was the son of Peter II of Aragon and Marie of Montpellier. As a child he was made a pawn in the politics of the Albigensian Crusade: his father placed him in the custody of Simon de Montfort, to whom he was betrothed to Montfort's daughter. Peter II was killed fighting Montfort at the Battle of Muret in 1213, and the five-year-old James became king under captivity. Released in 1214 through the intervention of Pope Innocent III, he was raised by the Knights Templar at Monzón Castle. His early reign was turbulent, dominated by regency councils and rebellious nobles.

Conquests

James's reputation rests on his conquests from the Moors. In 1229–1231 he conquered Majorca, followed by Menorca (1232, tributary; annexed later) and Ibiza (1235). From 1233 he campaigned against the Kingdom of Valencia, taking Burriana and Peñíscola before entering the city of Valencia itself on 9 October 1238 — still celebrated as the Valencian Community Day. The conquest of Murcia in 1266, undertaken partly on behalf of his son-in-law Alfonso X of Castile, completed his major campaigns. In 1258 the Treaty of Corbeil with Louis IX of France settled the frontier between Aragon and France; James renounced claims north of the Pyrenees in exchange for France abandoning claims on Catalonia.

Domestic achievements

James codified law (the Furs of Valencia and the Usatges tradition), consolidated the Corts, and founded the Estudi General of Lleida (1300 was after; the university was founded 1300 — he founded institutions of learning in Majorca and promoted Ramon Llull, whose education he supported). He sponsored the Llibre dels fets ("Book of Deeds"), his own autobiography and one of the great Catalan chronicles, dictated in his later years. His patronage contributed to the Catalan literary golden age.

Death and succession

James died at Alzira on 27 July 1276 while campaigning against a Mudéjar revolt, and was buried at the Monastery of Poblet. By his will he divided his realms: Peter III received Aragon, Valencia, and Catalonia, while James II received the Kingdom of Majorca — a division that caused later conflict.
