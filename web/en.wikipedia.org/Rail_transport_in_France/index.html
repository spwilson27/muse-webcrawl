Rail transport in France

Rail transport in France is provided in part by the SNCF, the national state-owned railway company, which operates on a network of about 29,000 kilometres (18,000 miles) managed since 2020 by SNCF Réseau. France has the second-largest rail network in Europe and pioneered high-speed rail with the TGV, which set the world rail speed record of 574.8 km/h in 2007.

A TGV Duplex at speed on a dedicated high-speed line (LGV).

History

The first French railway, from Saint-Étienne to Andrézieux, opened in 1827 for coal traffic. Under the Legrand Star plan of 1842 the network radiated from Paris, and the major private companies were nationalized into the SNCF in 1938. Post-war electrification and the opening of the Paris–Lyon high-speed line in 1981 transformed services; the TGV network has since expanded to connect most major French cities and neighboring countries.

Network and services

Long-distance services include TGV inOui and the low-cost Ouigo brand, plus locomotive-hauled Intercités trains and revived night trains. Regional services operate under the TER brand, financed by the regions. Paris is served by the Métro, RER commuter network, and an expanding tramway system; several other cities have their own metros and trams. International services include Eurostar to London, Brussels, and Amsterdam, and Thalys and cross-border TGV routes.

Operations and reform

Under European Union railway directives, infrastructure management was separated from operations (first through RFF in 1997, later SNCF Réseau), and passenger services have progressively opened to competition since 2020. Freight traffic, long in decline relative to road haulage, is handled mainly by Fret SNCF and private operators.
