Lee Ranaldo

Born: February 3, 1956, Glen Cove, New York, U.S.
Occupation: Musician, singer, songwriter, visual artist, writer
Instruments: Guitar, vocals

Lee Mark Ranaldo (born February 3, 1956) is an American musician, singer-songwriter, guitarist, writer, and visual artist, best known as a co-founder of the alternative rock band Sonic Youth. Alongside Thurston Moore, he pioneered the use of alternate guitar tunings, prepared guitars, and extended techniques that became hallmarks of the band's sound and of noise rock more broadly. Rolling Stone ranked him among the greatest guitarists of all time.

Early life

Ranaldo was born in Glen Cove, Long Island, and studied art and music at Binghamton University. In New York City he became involved with the downtown art and music scene, playing in Glenn Branca's guitar orchestras, where he met Thurston Moore; the experience of Branca's massed, detuned guitars deeply influenced his approach.

Sonic Youth

Ranaldo co-founded Sonic Youth in 1981 with Moore and Kim Gordon. Over three decades the band released sixteen studio albums, including Daydream Nation (1988), Goo (1990), and Dirty (1992), moving from the No Wave underground to major-label prominence and wide influence on alternative rock. Ranaldo contributed songs such as "Eric's Trip" and handled vocals on several tracks per album. The band went on indefinite hiatus in 2011.

Solo work and other activities

Ranaldo has released solo albums since From Here to Infinity (1987), including Between the Times and the Tides (2012), Last Night on Earth (2013) with his band The Dust, and Electric Trim (2017). He is also a published poet and essayist, author of books including Jrnls80s and Road Movies, and has exhibited visual art internationally. He has produced records for other artists and collaborated extensively in improvised music.
