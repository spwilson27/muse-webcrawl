Drilling rig

A drilling rig is an integrated system that drills wells, such as oil or water wells, or holes for piling and other construction purposes, into the earth's subsurface. Drilling rigs can be massive structures housing equipment used to drill water wells, oil wells, or natural gas extraction wells, or they can be small enough to be moved manually by one person and are then called augers.

Types

Drilling rigs are classed by their power source (mechanical, electric, hydraulic, pneumatic, steam), by the pipe they use (cable, conventional, coil tubing), by height (single, double, triple, quad), by the method of rotation or drilling method (rotary, cable tool, directional), and by their position (conventional land, swamp, offshore).

Offshore rigs include jack-up rigs, which rest on legs on the seabed; semi-submersible rigs, which float partly submerged; and drillships, which are vessels fitted with a drilling derrick and dynamic positioning systems for deep water.

Components

The principal parts of a rotary drilling rig include the derrick or mast, drawworks (hoist), rotary table or top drive, drill string (drill pipe and collars), the drill bit, hoisting equipment, mud pumps and tanks for circulating drilling fluid, shale shakers for removing cuttings, and the blowout preventer (BOP), a safety valve assembly that seals the well in case of uncontrolled pressure.
