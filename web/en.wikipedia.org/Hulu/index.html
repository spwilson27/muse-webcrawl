Hulu is an American subscription streaming service majority-owned and controlled by the Walt Disney Company. Launched in 2008 as a joint venture of broadcast networks, it offers next-day television episodes, a library of series and films, live TV, and acclaimed originals such as The Handmaid's Tale.

History

Hulu was founded in 2007 by NBC Universal and News Corporation (later joined by ABC/Disney) as a free, advertising-supported service for current-season network television, under founding CEO Jason Kilar. It added a paid tier, Hulu Plus, in 2010, and became subscription-only in 2016. Landmark originals include The Handmaid's Tale (2017), the first streaming series to win the Emmy for Outstanding Drama Series, plus Only Murders in the Building, Shōgun (via FX on Hulu) and The Bear.

Ownership

Disney gained a controlling stake through its 2019 acquisition of 21st Century Fox, and in 2019 took full operational control under an agreement with Comcast, whose NBCUniversal retained a one-third interest. Disney agreed in 2023–2025 to buy out Comcast's stake, valuing Hulu at over $27 billion, completing full ownership. Disney subsequently integrated Hulu content into Disney+ and announced plans to fold the standalone Hulu app into a unified Disney+ experience.
