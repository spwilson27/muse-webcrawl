S-type asteroid

S-type asteroids are asteroids with a spectral type indicating a siliceous (stony) composition, hence the name. They are the second most numerous spectral type after carbonaceous C-type asteroids, accounting for roughly 17% of known asteroids, and they dominate the inner part of the asteroid belt.

Characteristics

S-type asteroids have a moderate albedo (about 0.10–0.22) and spectra showing absorption features of silicate minerals — olivine and pyroxene — along with metallic iron-nickel. Their composition closely resembles that of stony meteorites, particularly ordinary chondrites, and they are thought to be the parent bodies of most such meteorites. Members include 4 Vesta's broader family characteristics aside, large S-types include 15 Eunomia, 3 Juno, and 7 Iris.

Exploration

The near-Earth asteroid 25143 Itokawa, an S-type, was visited by the Japanese Hayabusa spacecraft, which returned samples to Earth in 2010 confirming the link between S-type asteroids and ordinary chondrite meteorites. Space weathering — micrometeorite bombardment and solar wind exposure reddening the surface — explains why asteroid spectra appear redder than the meteorites they shed. The asteroid 433 Eros, orbited and landed on by NEAR Shoemaker, is also an S-type.

Classification

In the Tholen classification, S-types form a broad group; the later SMASS classification subdivides them into subtypes (Sa, Sk, Sl, Sq, Sr) forming a continuum toward other classes.
