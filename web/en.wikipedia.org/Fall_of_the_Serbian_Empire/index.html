Fall of the Serbian Empire

The fall of the Serbian Empire describes the gradual fragmentation and conquest of the medieval Serbian state in the later fourteenth and fifteenth centuries. The empire, created by Stefan Dušan, who was crowned emperor in 1346, reached its greatest extent under his rule; after his death in 1355 it fragmented among regional lords and was progressively absorbed by the expanding Ottoman Empire, a process completed with the fall of Smederevo in 1459.

Fragmentation

Dušan's successor, Stefan Uroš V, lacked the authority to hold the empire together. Regional magnates such as Vukašin Mrnjavčević and his brother Uglješa in Macedonia, the Balšić family in Zeta, and Lazar Hrebeljanović in the Morava valley became effectively independent. The process was accelerated by the Battle of Maritsa in 1371, where Ottoman forces destroyed the armies of Vukašin and Uglješa, opening Macedonia and southern Serbia to Ottoman expansion and making many Serbian lords Ottoman vassals.

Resistance and final fall

Prince Lazar rallied a coalition that confronted the Ottomans at the Battle of Kosovo in 1389, where both Lazar and Sultan Murad I were killed — a battle that became central to Serbian national memory. Lazar's son Stefan Lazarević, ruling as an Ottoman vassal and later a Hungarian ally, presided over a cultural revival as Despot. The Serbian Despotate survived in the north until the Ottomans captured its capital, Smederevo, in 1459, ending medieval Serbian statehood for centuries.
