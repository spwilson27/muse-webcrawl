Calligraphy is a visual art related to writing. It is the design and execution of lettering with a pen, ink brush, or other writing instrument. A contemporary calligraphic practice can be defined as "the art of giving form to signs in an expressive, harmonious, and skillful manner."

Modern Western calligraphy ranges from functional inscriptions and designs to fine-art pieces where legibility may be secondary. Classical calligraphy differs from type design and non-classical hand-lettering, though a calligrapher may practice both.

History

Calligraphy developed independently in several cultures. In China, the art grew from early seal script carved on bronze vessels and oracle bones; by the Han dynasty, clerical and regular scripts had become refined brush arts. In the Islamic world, where depiction of living beings was often restricted, beautiful writing of the Qur'an became a supreme art form, producing scripts such as Kufic, Naskh, and Thuluth.

In medieval Europe, monks in scriptoria copied manuscripts by hand, developing uncial, Carolingian minuscule, and blackletter scripts, often illuminated with gold leaf. The invention of movable-type printing in the 15th century diminished the need for hand copying, though calligraphy survived as an art and in official documents.

Chinese calligraphy

Chinese calligraphy (shūfǎ) is considered one of the highest art forms in China, traditionally ranked alongside painting. The brush, ink stick, paper, and ink stone — the "Four Treasures of the Study" — are its essential tools. Famous masters include Wang Xizhi of the Jin dynasty, whose Preface to the Poems Collected from the Orchid Pavilion (353 CE) is regarded as the finest work of running script.

Western calligraphy

Western calligraphy uses the Latin alphabet, typically written with a broad-edged pen for scripts such as italic, uncial, and foundational hand. Edward Johnston's revival of the craft in early 20th-century England, and his design work for the London Underground, reshaped modern lettering and typography.

Tools and materials

Tools include brushes, quills cut from feathers, reed pens, dip pens with metal nibs, and modern calligraphy markers. Writing surfaces range from Chinese xuan paper and Japanese washi to parchment and laid papers. Inks are traditionally made from soot and glue, ground on an ink stone with water.
