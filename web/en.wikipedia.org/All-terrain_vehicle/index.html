An all-terrain vehicle (ATV), also known as a quad or four-wheeler, is a motorised off-road vehicle that travels on low-pressure tyres, with a seat straddled by the rider and handlebars for steering. As the name suggests, it is designed to travel over a wider variety of terrain than most other vehicles.

History

Although six-wheeled amphibious ATVs existed from the 1960s, the modern ATV era began in 1970 when Honda introduced the US90 (later ATC90), the first three-wheeled recreational all-terrain cycle. Suzuki launched the first four-wheeled model, the QuadRunner LT125, in 1982, and Honda followed with the TRX200. Safety concerns over three-wheelers' instability led U.S. manufacturers to cease their production under a 1988 consent decree, and four-wheelers became standard.

Types and uses

ATVs divide into sport models (light, fast, two-wheel drive) and utility models (heavier, often four-wheel drive, with racks and towing capacity) used in farming, ranching, land management, military service and emergency response. Side-by-side utility vehicles (UTVs), with car-style seating and steering wheels, form a related category. Engine sizes range from under 50 cc youth models to over 1,000 cc.

Safety and regulation

ATVs are associated with significant injury rates, particularly rollovers and child injuries; safety guidance stresses helmets, training, single riders on one-up machines and keeping off paved roads, where ATVs handle poorly. Many jurisdictions regulate ATV use on public roads and set age and licensing rules, and environmental concerns over trail damage have led to land-use restrictions in some areas.
