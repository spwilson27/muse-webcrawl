Crown Jewels of the United Kingdom

The Crown Jewels of the United Kingdom, originally the Crown Jewels of England, are a collection of royal ceremonial objects kept in the Jewel House at the Tower of London, which include the coronation regalia and vestments worn by British kings and queens at their coronations. Symbols of over 800 years of monarchy, the Crown Jewels include St Edward's Crown and the Imperial State Crown, and are set with famous gems including the Cullinan diamonds, the Koh-i-Nûr, and the Black Prince's Ruby.

Coronation regalia in the Tower of London.

History

The medieval regalia of Edward the Confessor was kept at Westminster Abbey; most of it was sold or melted down after the execution of Charles I in 1649. For the coronation of Charles II in 1661, new regalia was made, including St Edward's Crown, the Sovereign's Orb, and sceptres; these form the core of the present collection. An attempted theft by Thomas Blood in 1671 led to improved security; the Jewels have been displayed to the public at the Tower since the 17th–19th centuries in evolving exhibitions.

Principal pieces

The regalia used at a coronation includes the Ampulla and Spoon (for anointing, the Spoon being medieval), the Sword of State and other swords, the Armills, St Edward's Crown (with which the monarch is crowned), the Orb, and the Ring. The Imperial State Crown, remade for Queen Victoria and again in 1937, is worn at the State Opening of Parliament; it contains the Cullinan II diamond, the Stuart Sapphire, and St Edward's Sapphire. The Sovereign's Sceptre with Cross holds Cullinan I, the Great Star of Africa, the largest clear-cut diamond in the world at 530.2 carats.

Custody

The Crown Jewels are part of the Royal Collection and are held in trust by the monarch for the nation; they are guarded by the Tower's Yeomen Warders and (formerly) Jewel House staff, and are removed only for coronations, State Openings, and other state occasions.
