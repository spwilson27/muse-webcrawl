The Crown Office and Procurator Fiscal Service (COPFS) is the public prosecution service for Scotland, responsible for the investigation and prosecution of crime in Scotland, the investigation of sudden or suspicious deaths, and the investigation of allegations of criminal conduct against police officers. It is headed by the Lord Advocate, who, together with the Solicitor General for Scotland, is the senior Law Officer of the Scottish Government.

Structure

The Crown Office, at Chambers Street in Edinburgh, sets prosecution policy and conducts the most serious cases in the High Court of Justiciary through advocates depute ("Crown counsel"). Locally, procurators fiscal — legally qualified civil servants — receive reports from Police Scotland and other agencies, decide whether prosecution is in the public interest, and prosecute summary cases themselves in sheriff and justice of the peace courts. Unlike in England and Wales, the decision to prosecute in Scotland rests almost entirely with the public prosecutor rather than the police.

Functions

Beyond prosecution, COPFS investigates all sudden, suspicious, unexplained and unexpected deaths through the Scottish Fatalities Investigation Unit, supporting fatal accident inquiries. It also handles proceeds of crime confiscation, extradition requests, and provides a Victim Information and Advice service. Prosecutors may offer alternatives to prosecution such as fiscal fines and warnings.
