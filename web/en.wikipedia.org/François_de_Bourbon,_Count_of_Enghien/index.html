François de Bourbon, comte d'Enghien (23 September 1519 – 23 February 1546) was a French prince of the blood and military commander of the House of Bourbon-Vendôme. He is best known for his victory over Imperial-Spanish forces at the Battle of Ceresole in Piedmont in 1544.

Life

François was a younger son of Charles, Duke of Vendôme, and Françoise d'Alençon, and thus a younger brother of Antoine de Bourbon, Duke of Vendôme, later King of Navarre and father of Henry IV of France. Raised at the court of France, he took up arms in the Italian Wars between Francis I and the Emperor Charles V.

Ceresole

In 1544, aged only twenty-four, Enghien was given command of the French army in Italy. Against the advice of some of his experienced captains he sought battle, and on 14 April 1544 he defeated the Imperial army of Alfonso d'Avalos, Marquis del Vasto, at Ceresole Alba — one of the few full-scale pitched battles of the later Italian Wars, notable for its massed pike-and-shot fighting and the slaughter of the German landsknechts. The victory won him fame throughout Europe, though it brought France little lasting strategic gain.

Death

Enghien died in an accident in February 1546 at the castle of La Rochepot: during a mock combat with snowballs and improvised missiles after dinner, a heavy chest or piece of furniture was thrown or fell from a window and struck him on the head. The countship of Enghien later passed within the Bourbon family and its name became famous again through the Condé line.
