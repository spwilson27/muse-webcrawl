Charleston, South Carolina

Charleston
Founded: 1670 (as Charles Town)
State: South Carolina, United States
Population: ~150,000 (city); ~800,000 (metro)
Nickname: The Holy City

Charleston is a port city in the U.S. state of South Carolina, the state's second-largest city and the seat of Charleston County. Founded in 1670 as Charles Town in honour of King Charles II, it lies where the Ashley and Cooper rivers meet to form Charleston Harbor on the Atlantic coast. It is famed for its well-preserved historic architecture, its role in American history, and a strong tourism and culinary scene.

History

Charles Town grew rich on rice, indigo, and the transatlantic slave trade; by 1770 it was among the largest cities in British America. During the American Revolution it was captured by the British in 1780. In April 1861 the first shots of the Civil War were fired at Fort Sumter in its harbour, and the city endured blockade and bombardment. An 1886 earthquake heavily damaged it. In 2015, a white supremacist murdered nine Black worshippers at Emanuel AME Church, an event of national significance. The city's economy historically depended on enslaved labour, a legacy central to modern interpretation of its past.

Geography and culture

The low-lying peninsula city faces recurrent tidal flooding. Its historic district features the pastel houses of Rainbow Row, the Battery promenade, and "Charleston single" houses adapted to the climate. Nicknamed "The Holy City" for its many church steeples and early religious tolerance, Charleston hosts the Spoleto Festival USA arts festival and an acclaimed restaurant scene; the College of Charleston (founded 1770) is among the oldest colleges in the United States.
