Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch theorem

Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch
FieldAlgebraic geometry
Proved byAlexander Grothendieck (1957)
GeneralisesRiemann–Roch theorem; Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch
Key toolsK-theory, Chow rings, Chern character, Todd class

The Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch theorem is a far-reaching generalisation of the classical Riemann–Roch theorem in algebraic geometry, proved by Alexander Grothendieck in 1957. Where the classical theorem computes the dimension of the space of meromorphic functions with prescribed poles on a curve, Grothendieck's version gives a formula relating the Chern character of the pushforward of a coherent sheaf along a proper morphism to the pushforward of its Chern character, corrected by Todd classes.

Statement

For a proper smooth morphism f : X → Y of schemes, the theorem asserts that ch(f!α) · td(Y) = f*(ch(α) · td(X)) in the Chow ring of Y, where ch is the Chern character, td the Todd class, and f! the derived pushforward in K-theory. The statement's functorial form — rather than a mere numerical identity — exemplifies Grothendieck's relative viewpoint.

History

Grothendieck announced the result in a 1957 letter to Jean-Pierre Serre; the proof appeared in the Borel–Serre paper of 1958. The theorem subsumed the Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem for complex manifolds and provided the model for later index theorems, including the Atiyah–Singer index theorem, whose proof was inspired by Grothendieck's formalism.

Applications

Applied to a morphism from a variety to a point, the theorem yields Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch; applied to families of curves it gives the original Riemann–Roch and formulas used in enumerative geometry and the study of moduli spaces.
