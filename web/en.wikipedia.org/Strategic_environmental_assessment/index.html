Strategic environmental assessment

Strategic environmental assessment (SEA) is a systematic decision support process aimed at ensuring that environmental and possibly other sustainability aspects are considered effectively in policy, plan, and programme making. It extends the principles of project-level environmental impact assessment (EIA) to earlier, more strategic tiers of decision-making, where alternatives are still open and cumulative effects can be addressed.

Purpose

SEA is intended to integrate environmental considerations into strategic decisions before individual projects are fixed, to assess cumulative and indirect effects that project EIA cannot capture, to evaluate reasonable alternatives at the level of plans and programmes, and to strengthen public participation and transparency in strategic planning. Typical applications include transport plans, energy and water plans, waste management strategies, and regional spatial plans.

Legal frameworks

In the European Union, Directive 2001/42/EC (the "SEA Directive") requires an environmental assessment of certain public plans and programmes likely to have significant environmental effects. At the international level, the UNECE Protocol on Strategic Environmental Assessment to the Espoo Convention (the Kyiv Protocol, adopted 2003, in force 2010) extends SEA obligations, including to policies and legislation in some cases. Comparable systems exist in many other jurisdictions, including the strategic assessment provisions of Australia's EPBC Act and Canada's Cabinet Directive on environmental assessment of policy, plan and programme proposals.

Process

A typical SEA follows stages parallel to the planning process: screening (whether an SEA is needed), scoping (which issues and alternatives to study), baseline description and assessment of effects, preparation of an environmental report, consultation with authorities and the public, integration of results into the decision, and monitoring of significant effects during implementation.
