Jean Moréas

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Jean Moréas (French pronunciation: [ʒɑ̃ mɔʁeas]; born Ioannis A. Papadiamantopoulos, Greek: Ιωάννης Παπαδιαμαντόπουλος; 15 April 1856 – 31 March 1910) was a Greek poet, essayist, and art critic, who wrote mostly in French and was one of the founders of the Symbolist movement in French literature.

Life

Moréas was born in Athens into a distinguished family. He studied law at the University of Athens and Paris, and settled in Paris in the late 1870s, frequenting the literary cafés and salons of the Latin Quarter. He published his first verse collections, Les Syrtes (1884) and Les Cantilènes (1886), and became a central figure of the Decadent and Symbolist circles.

Symbolist Manifesto

On 18 September 1886, Moréas published the Symbolist Manifesto (Le Symbolisme) in the literary supplement of Le Figaro. The manifesto gave the movement its name — rejecting Paul Verlaine's preferred label "decadent" — and defined symbolist poetry as the clothing of the Idea in perceptible form, hostile to plain statement, description, and didacticism. It named Charles Baudelaire, Stéphane Mallarmé, and Paul Verlaine as the movement's leading precursors and associated it with poets including Gustave Kahn and Jules Laforgue.

Later career

In 1891 Moréas broke with Symbolism to found, with Charles Maurras and others, the École romane, which advocated a return to classical French and Greco-Latin models. His later poetry, notably Les Stances (1899–1920), is written in a severe classical style and includes some of his most admired work. He died in Saint-Mandé, near Paris, in 1910.
