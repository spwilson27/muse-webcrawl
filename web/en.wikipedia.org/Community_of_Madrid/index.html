Community of Madrid

Community of Madrid (Spanish: Comunidad de Madrid)
Country: Spain
Capital: Madrid
Area: 8,028 km²
Population: approx. 6.8 million
Autonomy granted: 1 March 1983

The Community of Madrid (Spanish: Comunidad de Madrid) is one of the seventeen autonomous communities of Spain. It is located in the centre of the country and of the Iberian Peninsula, on the Southern Meseta Central. Its capital and largest municipality is the City of Madrid, which is also the capital of Spain.

Geography

The region is bordered by the provinces of Guadalajara, Cuenca, Toledo (Castile-La Mancha) and Ávila and Segovia (Castile and León). The Sierra de Guadarrama mountain range occupies the northwest of the territory, rising to Peñalara at 2,428 metres. The Tagus basin drains the south of the region. Despite its small area, the Community of Madrid is the third most populous autonomous community in Spain.

History

The territory was part of the historic region of Castile. Madrid was designated the permanent seat of the Spanish court by Philip II in 1561. During Spain's transition to democracy, the province of Madrid was constituted as a single-province autonomous community; its Statute of Autonomy was approved on 25 February 1983 and published on 1 March 1983.

Government and economy

The regional government, the Government of the Community of Madrid, is headed by a president elected by the Assembly of Madrid. The region is Spain's leading economic centre, with the highest GDP per capita in the country, and hosts the headquarters of many major Spanish companies, national government institutions, and Adolfo Suárez Madrid–Barajas Airport, one of Europe's busiest.
