Metropolitan area

A metropolitan area (or metro) is a region consisting of a densely populated urban core and its surrounding territories sharing industry, housing, and infrastructure. Metropolitan areas are usually anchored by one or more large cities and include suburbs, exurbs, and commuter towns that are socially and economically integrated with the core.

Definitions

Definitions vary by country. In the United States, the Office of Management and Budget defines a Metropolitan Statistical Area as having a core urban area of at least 50,000 inhabitants plus adjacent counties with strong commuting ties. The OECD defines functional urban areas based on population density and commuting flows. There is no single global standard, so population figures for the "largest" metropolitan areas depend on the methodology used.

Largest metropolitan areas

By most estimates, Tokyo is the world's largest metropolitan area, with around 37 million inhabitants, followed by Delhi, Shanghai, São Paulo, and Mexico City. Such massive urban agglomerations are sometimes termed megalopolises when several metropolitan areas merge, as in the Northeast megalopolis of the United States or China's Yangtze River Delta.
