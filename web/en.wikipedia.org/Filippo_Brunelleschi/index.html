Filippo Brunelleschi

Filippo di ser Brunellesco di Lippo Lapi (1377 – 15 April 1446), commonly known as Filippo Brunelleschi, was an Italian architect, designer, goldsmith and sculptor. He is considered to be a founding father of Renaissance architecture and the first modern engineer. His most famous work is the dome of Florence Cathedral (the Duomo), the largest brick dome ever constructed, and he is credited with developing the mathematical technique of linear perspective in art.

Early life and the Baptistery competition

Trained as a goldsmith and clockmaker, Brunelleschi competed in 1401 for the bronze doors of the Florence Baptistery, losing (by most accounts) to Lorenzo Ghiberti; his trial panel of the Sacrifice of Isaac survives. He then travelled to Rome with Donatello to study ancient ruins, measuring classical buildings that informed his new architecture.

The Dome

In 1418 the Florentine wool guild announced a competition for the dome of Santa Maria del Fiore, whose enormous octagonal crossing had stood open for decades. Brunelleschi won with a revolutionary double-shell design built without full wooden centring, using herringbone brickwork and ingenious hoisting machines of his own invention. The dome was completed in 1436 (the lantern followed to his design after his death) and remains the largest masonry dome in the world.

Perspective and other works

Around 1413–1420 Brunelleschi demonstrated linear perspective in two painted panels of Florentine scenes, transforming Western art. His buildings — the Ospedale degli Innocenti (the first Renaissance building), the Old Sacristy of San Lorenzo, Santo Spirito, and the Pazzi Chapel — display the rational proportions and classical orders that defined early Renaissance architecture. He also designed fortifications, stage machinery, and river works, and in 1421 received arguably the first modern patent, for a river transport barge.
