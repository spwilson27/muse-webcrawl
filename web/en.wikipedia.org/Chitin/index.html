Chitin

Chitin
Formula: (C8H13O5N)n
Type: Polysaccharide of N-acetylglucosamine
Occurrence: Arthropod exoskeletons, fungal cell walls, mollusc radulae

Chitin is a long-chain polymer of N-acetylglucosamine, a derivative of glucose, and one of the most abundant natural polymers, second only to cellulose. It is the main component of arthropod exoskeletons and fungal cell walls.

Structure and properties

Chitin units form hydrogen-bonded crystalline microfibrils, giving strength and rigidity. In arthropod cuticle it is combined with proteins and, in crustaceans, calcium carbonate.

Uses

Chitin and its deacetylated derivative chitosan are used in wound dressings, drug delivery, water treatment, agriculture and biodegradable materials. Enzymes called chitinases occur widely in organisms that consume or contain chitin.
