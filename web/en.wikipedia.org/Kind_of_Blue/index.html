Kind of Blue is a studio album by the American jazz trumpeter and composer Miles Davis, released on 17 August 1959 by Columbia Records. Recorded with a sextet including saxophonists John Coltrane and Cannonball Adderley and pianist Bill Evans, it is widely regarded as one of the greatest and most influential jazz albums ever made, and it is the best-selling jazz album of all time.

Background and recording

Davis had become frustrated with the dense chord changes of bebop and hard bop. Drawing on George Russell's theories and Evans's impressionistic playing, he built the album around modes — scales — giving soloists a few scales and simple melodic sketches rather than complex chord progressions. The musicians were given the sketches shortly before the sessions at Columbia's 30th Street Studio, and most tracks were completed in a single take, preserving a spontaneous, spacious feel.

Music

The album contains five tracks: "So What" and "All Blues," which became standards; the ballad "Blue in Green," co-composed with Evans; "Freddie Freeloader," on which Wynton Kelly plays piano; and "Flamenco Sketches." Coltrane's searching tenor solos contrast with Adderley's bluesier alto, while Davis's muted, lyrical trumpet states the themes with famous economy.

Reception and legacy

Kind of Blue has been certified multi-platinum in the United States, was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame and the Library of Congress's National Recording Registry, and is regularly placed at or near the top of lists of the greatest albums in any genre. Its modal approach reshaped jazz improvisation and influenced rock, classical minimalism, and popular music broadly.
