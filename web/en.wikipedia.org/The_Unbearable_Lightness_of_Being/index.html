The Unbearable Lightness of Being (Czech: Nesnesitelná lehkost bytí) is a 1984 novel by the Czech-born French author Milan Kundera, about two women, two men, a dog, and their lives in the Prague artistic and intellectual community during the Prague Spring of 1968 and the subsequent Soviet-led invasion of Czechoslovakia. It was first published in Paris in a French translation in 1984 and in Czech by the exile publisher 68 Publishers in Toronto in 1985; it could not legally appear in Czechoslovakia until after the fall of communism.

Plot and structure

The novel follows Tomáš, a Prague surgeon and inveterate womanizer; his wife Tereza, a photographer haunted by his infidelities; his lover Sabina, a free-spirited painter who embraces lightness and betrayal; and Sabina's lover Franz, a Swiss professor. Interweaving their stories with philosophical digressions, Kundera explores Nietzsche's idea of eternal return against the contrary intuition that life, lived only once, is weightless — "light" — and asks whether that lightness is splendid or unbearable. Tereza and Tomas return from emigration in Zurich to occupied Prague; he loses his medical position for refusing to retract an article and ends his days as a window washer before the couple settles in the countryside with their dog Karenin.

Themes

Central themes include lightness versus weight, kitsch — the aesthetic ideal of the Communist regime and of sentimental feeling generally — the soul and the body, compassion, and the impossibility of repeating experience. The novel is divided into seven parts, alternating among the characters' perspectives and the narrator's philosophical voice.

Adaptation

The novel was adapted into a 1988 film directed by Philip Kaufman, starring Daniel Day-Lewis as Tomas, Juliette Binoche as Tereza, and Lena Olin as Sabina.
