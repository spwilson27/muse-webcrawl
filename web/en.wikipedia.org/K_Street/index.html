K Street

[Image: K Street NW in downtown Washington, D.C.]

K Street is a major thoroughfare in the United States capital of Washington, D.C., known as a centre for lobbying firms and advocacy groups. The street's name has become metonymous with Washington's lobbying industry, much as "Wall Street" denotes the financial industry and "Madison Avenue" the advertising industry.

Geography

K Street runs east–west through both Northwest and Northeast Washington, following the lettered-street scheme of the L'Enfant Plan. Its principal downtown stretch passes through the central business district, the Foggy Bottom and Georgetown neighbourhoods, and includes the K Street corridor of office buildings between Connecticut Avenue and Washington Circle.

The lobbying industry

Numerous think tanks, law firms, trade associations and lobbying firms maintain offices on or near K Street, drawn by proximity to the White House, Capitol Hill and federal agencies. The expression "K Street lobbyist" is standard in American political journalism; major firms and groups historically located there include the U.S. Chamber of Commerce and large government-relations practices. Since the 2000s some firms have dispersed to other downtown locations and to Capitol Hill, but the name retains its symbolic force.
