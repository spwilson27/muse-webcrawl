River-class frigate

The River class was a class of 151 frigates launched between 1941 and 1944 for use as anti-submarine convoy escorts in the North Atlantic. The majority served with the Royal Navy and Royal Canadian Navy, with a handful also employed by the Royal Australian Navy and other Allied navies.

Design

The River class drew on the preceding Flower-class corvette but was larger, faster, and longer-ranged, with twin screws and machinery based on a commercial design that could be built in merchant shipyards. Ships were named after rivers in the United Kingdom and Canada. Armament typically included a 4-inch gun, anti-aircraft weapons, depth charges, and later the Hedgehog ahead-throwing mortar.

Service

River-class frigates were credited with sinking numerous German U-boats and proved a successful wartime design. After the war many were sold to foreign navies or converted to other roles; the design influenced post-war frigate development, including the Loch and Bay classes.
