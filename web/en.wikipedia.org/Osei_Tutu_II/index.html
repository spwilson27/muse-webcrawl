Otumfuo Nana Osei Tutu II (born 6 May 1950) is the 16th Asantehene, the traditional ruler of the Asante Kingdom in present-day Ghana. Enstooled on 26 April 1999, he succeeded his uncle Opoku Ware II and occupies the Golden Stool, the sacred symbol of Asante unity and nationhood founded by his namesake Osei Tutu I in the late 17th century.

Early life and education

Born Nana Barima Kwaku Duah in Kumasi, he is the youngest of five children of Nana Afua Kobi Serwaa Ampem II, the Asantehemaa (queen mother). He studied accounting and human resource management in London, worked in Canada and the United Kingdom, and returned to Ghana to run a transport business before his selection as Asantehene.

Reign

Osei Tutu II is credited with revitalising the Asante chieftaincy and using it for development: his education fund has supported thousands of students; he has promoted health campaigns, including against HIV/AIDS; and he has played a widely praised mediating role in Ghanaian public life, notably chairing the Committee of Eminent Chiefs that helped resolve the Dagbon chieftaincy conflict in 2019. Although Ghana is a republic, the Asantehene retains major cultural and moral authority, presiding over the Akwasidae festival in Kumasi.

Honours

He holds honorary doctorates from several universities and is Chancellor of the Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology in Kumasi.
