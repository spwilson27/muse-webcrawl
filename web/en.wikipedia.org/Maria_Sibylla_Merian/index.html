Maria Sibylla Merian

Maria Sibylla Merian
Born: 2 April 1647, Frankfurt am Main
Died: 13 January 1717 (aged 69), Amsterdam
Fields: Natural history, entomology, scientific illustration
Known for: Documenting insect metamorphosis; Metamorphosis Insectorum Surinamensium

Maria Sibylla Merian (2 April 1647 – 13 January 1717) was a German entomologist, naturalist and scientific illustrator. She was one of the first European naturalists to observe insects directly, and her studies of metamorphosis and of the insects of Suriname are landmarks of early modern natural history.

Early work

Born into a family of engravers and publishers — her stepfather was the still-life painter Jacob Marrel — Merian began collecting and rearing caterpillars as a child. Unlike contemporaries who believed insects arose by spontaneous generation, she documented eggs, larvae, pupae and adults, publishing her Raupenbuch (Caterpillar Book) volumes from 1679, pairing each insect with its food plant.

Suriname

After years with the Labadist religious community in Friesland, Merian moved to Amsterdam. In 1699, aged 52 and without male patronage, she sailed with her younger daughter to the Dutch colony of Suriname, where for two years she observed and painted the colony's insects, reptiles and plants, relying in part on the knowledge of enslaved African and Indigenous people. The resulting Metamorphosis Insectorum Surinamensium (1705), with its large hand-coloured plates showing insects on their host plants, was an immediate sensation.

Legacy

Merian spent her last years in Amsterdam preparing further editions, aided by her daughters, both artists. Long treated mainly as a flower painter, she was re-evaluated in the 20th century as a pioneering field entomologist; species and a portrait on the German 500-deutsche-mark note commemorate her.
