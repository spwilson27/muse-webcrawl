The Haçienda was a nightclub in Manchester, England, which became famous during the "Madchester" years of the late 1980s and early 1990s as a center of the rave and acid house scenes. It was opened by Factory Records and the band New Order, together with Tony Wilson, and was given the catalogue number FAC 51 in the label's numbering system. The cedilla in its name was a stylistic flourish chosen for its appearance.

History

The club occupied a former yacht showroom warehouse, its industrial interior designed by Ben Kelly with bold hazard-stripe graphics and railway-station styling. In its early years it operated as a members' club and lost large sums of money, subsidized by New Order's record sales. From the mid-1980s, DJs such as Mike Pickering and Graeme Park made the Haçienda Britain's leading house music venue; its Friday "Nude" night from 1986 helped launch acid house in the UK, and the club became synonymous with the summers of 1988–89, the rise of bands such as the Stone Roses and Happy Mondays, and the fusion of indie rock and dance music dubbed Madchester.

Decline and closure

The club was plagued by gang violence and drug-related problems; Manchester's first ecstasy-related death occurred there in 1989. Financial difficulties and a deteriorating security situation forced closure in 1997. The building was demolished in 2002 and replaced by the Haçienda Apartments, which retain the name on the site.

Legacy

The Haçienda is remembered as the birthplace of British rave culture and a defining institution of Manchester's music history. It was depicted in the 2002 film 24 Hour Party People, and former DJs and members have staged anniversary events under its name.
