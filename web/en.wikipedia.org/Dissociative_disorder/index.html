Dissociative disorder

Dissociative disorders

Specialty: Psychiatry

Symptoms: Disruptions of memory, identity, awareness, perception

Types: Dissociative identity disorder; dissociative amnesia; depersonalization-derealization disorder

Dissociative disorders are a group of mental disorders characterised by disruptions or discontinuities in the normal integration of consciousness, memory, identity, emotion, perception, body representation, motor control, and behaviour. They are frequently associated with experiences of psychological trauma.

Classification

The DSM-5 recognises three major dissociative disorders:

  • Dissociative identity disorder (DID) — the presence of two or more distinct identity states, with recurrent gaps in recall of everyday events and personal information.
  • Dissociative amnesia — inability to recall important autobiographical information, usually traumatic in nature.
  • Depersonalization-derealization disorder — persistent experiences of feeling detached from oneself (depersonalization) or of the world feeling unreal (derealization).

Causes and risk factors

Dissociative disorders are strongly linked to overwhelming stress and trauma, particularly chronic childhood abuse or neglect. Dissociation is understood by many clinicians as a defensive response that compartmentalises traumatic experience. The link between trauma and DID remains debated, as does the influence of suggestion and sociocultural factors.

Treatment

Psychotherapy is the primary treatment, often phased: establishing safety and stabilisation, processing traumatic memories, and integration and rehabilitation. No medications treat dissociation directly, though they may be used for co-occurring depression, anxiety, or PTSD.

History

Dissociative phenomena were central to early psychiatry: Pierre Janet coined the term "dissociation" in the late 19th century, and cases such as that of "Anna O." shaped early psychoanalysis. Interest surged again in the 1980s with the diagnosis of multiple personality disorder, accompanied by controversy over recovered memories.
