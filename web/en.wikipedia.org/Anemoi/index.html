Anemoi

The Anemoi (Greek: Ἄνεμοι, "Winds") are wind gods in Greek mythology, each ascribed a cardinal direction and associated with particular seasons and weather. They were the sons of the dawn goddess Eos and the Titan Astraeus.

The four winds

  • Boreas — the north wind, bringer of cold winter air, depicted as a bearded, winged old man.
  • Zephyrus (Zephyros) — the gentle west wind, associated with spring and the messenger of mild weather.
  • Notus (Notos) — the south wind, bringing the storms and fogs of late summer and autumn.
  • Eurus (Euros) — the east or southeast wind, the unlucky wind of storms at sea.

Worship and representation

The Anemoi appear in the Odyssey, where Aeolus, keeper of the winds, gives Odysseus a bag containing all but the west wind. In Athens the Tower of the Winds (Horologion of Andronikos, 1st century BC) bears friezes of the eight wind deities, including the lesser winds Kaikias, Apeliotes, Skiron, and Lips. Their Roman equivalents are the Venti: Aquilo, Favonius, Auster, and Vulturnus.
