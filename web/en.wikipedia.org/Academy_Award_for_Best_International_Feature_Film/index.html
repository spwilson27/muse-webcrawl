Academy Award for Best International Feature Film

The Academy Award for Best International Feature Film (known as Best Foreign Language Film prior to 2020) is one of the Academy Awards presented annually by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences (AMPAS). It is given to a feature-length motion picture produced outside the United States with a predominantly non-English dialogue track.

When the first Academy Awards ceremony was held in 1929, no foreign-language film was honoured. Early in the history of the awards, between 1947 and 1955, the Academy presented Special Awards to the best foreign-language films released in the United States. These awards, however, were not handed out on a regular basis and were not competitive, since there were no nominees but simply one winning film per year. For the 1956 Academy Awards, a competitive Academy Award of Merit, known as the Best Foreign Language Film Award, was created for non-English-speaking films and has been given annually since then.

Eligibility

Unlike other Academy Awards, the International Feature Film Award does not require films to be released in the United States in order to be eligible for competition. Films competing in the category must have been first released in the country submitting them during the eligibility period defined by the rules of the Academy, and must have been exhibited for at least seven consecutive days in a commercial movie theatre. The dialogue track must be predominantly in a language or languages other than English, and accurate, legible English-language subtitles are required.

Submission process

Each country is invited to submit one film to the Academy for consideration. Only one film is accepted from each country, and the submission must be designated by an organization, jury or committee composed of people from the film industry in that country. The Academy then compiles a shortlist from the submissions before announcing the final five nominees.

History and notable winners

Italy is the most successful country in the category, having won 14 Academy Awards for films submitted, followed by France. Notable winners include Shoeshine and Bicycle Thieves from Italy (honorary awards), Federico Fellini's films, Ingmar Bergman's Swedish films, Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon (Taiwan, 2000), Parasite (South Korea, 2019) — which also won Best Picture — and Drive My Car (Japan, 2021). In 2020 the category was renamed from "Best Foreign Language Film" to "Best International Feature Film" to reflect a more inclusive global outlook.
