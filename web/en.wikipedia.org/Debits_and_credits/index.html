Debits and credits

In double-entry bookkeeping, debits and credits are the opposing entries used to record business transactions. Every transaction affects at least two accounts, with total debits equalling total credits, keeping the accounting equation—assets = liabilities + equity—in balance.

Terminology

The words derive from the Latin debitum ("what is owed") and creditum ("what is entrusted"). By convention, a debit (Dr) is an entry on the left side of a T-account and a credit (Cr) an entry on the right side. Whether a debit increases or decreases an account depends on the account type: debits increase assets and expenses but decrease liabilities, equity, and revenue; credits do the reverse.

Rules

The standard rules are commonly summarized as: debit the receiver, credit the giver (personal accounts); debit what comes in, credit what goes out (real accounts); and debit expenses and losses, credit incomes and gains (nominal accounts).

History

Double-entry bookkeeping with debits and credits was codified by Luca Pacioli in his 1494 Summa de arithmetica, building on practices used by Venetian merchants. The system remains the foundation of modern accounting.
