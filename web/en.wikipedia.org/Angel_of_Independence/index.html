Angel of Independence

Angel of Independence (El Ángel)
Location: Paseo de la Reforma, Mexico City
Inaugurated: 16 September 1910
Height: 45 m (column and statue)
Architect: Antonio Rivas Mercado

The Angel of Independence (Spanish: El Ángel de la Independencia), universally known in Mexico as El Ángel, is a victory column on a roundabout of the Paseo de la Reforma in Mexico City. It was built to commemorate the centenary of the start of Mexico's War of Independence in 1810 and is one of the capital's most recognisable landmarks.

Design

Designed by architect Antonio Rivas Mercado and sculptor Enrique Alciati, the monument was inaugurated by President Porfirio Díaz on 16 September 1910. A steel-and-stone column in the Corinthian order rises from a stepped base adorned with bronze figures of heroes of independence; at its summit stands a gilded bronze Winged Victory, 6.7 metres tall, holding a laurel wreath over the nation and a broken chain of three links symbolising the end of Spanish rule. An eternal flame honouring the independence heroes was added in 1929.

Mausoleum and history

In 1925 the remains of heroes including Miguel Hidalgo, José María Morelos, Vicente Guerrero, and Nicolás Bravo were interred in a mausoleum within the column's base. The Angel famously toppled in the earthquake of 28 July 1957 and was restored within a year; the monument survived the 1985 and 2017 earthquakes. It has accumulated political meaning as the gathering point for celebrations—above all football victories—demonstrations, and the annual independence festivities of 15–16 September.
