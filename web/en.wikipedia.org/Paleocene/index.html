Paleocene

Chronology: 66 to 56 million years ago
Preceded by: Cretaceous
Followed by: Eocene
Part of: Paleogene Period, Cenozoic Era

The Paleocene, or Palaeocene, is a geological epoch that lasted from about 66 to 56 million years ago (mya). It is the first epoch of the Paleogene Period in the modern Cenozoic Era. The name is a combination of the Ancient Greek palaios meaning "old" and the Eocene Epoch, reflecting the age of the fauna that lived during this interval. The Paleocene began immediately after the Cretaceous–Paleogene (K–Pg) extinction event, which killed off the non-avian dinosaurs and about three quarters of all species on Earth.

Climate and geography

The continents continued to drift toward their modern positions; the North Atlantic was opening, India was moving toward Asia, and Australia was separating from Antarctica. The global climate was warm, with a hothouse peak at the Paleocene–Eocene Thermal Maximum (about 56 mya), a rapid warming event marked by a major carbon isotope excursion and ocean acidification. Forests, including tropical forests, extended to high latitudes, and there were no permanent polar ice caps.

Life

With the large dinosaurs gone, mammals diversified rapidly into the emptied ecological niches, though Paleocene mammals were mostly small and belonged to archaic groups such as multituberculates, condylarths, and early primates. Birds also radiated, including giant flightless forms such as Gastornis in Europe and North America, and the first penguins appeared in the Southern Hemisphere. Reptiles such as giant snakes (Titanoboa in Colombia) and large crocodylians thrived in the warm climate, and marine ecosystems recovered from the mass extinction over several million years.
