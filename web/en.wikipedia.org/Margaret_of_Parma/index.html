Margaret of Parma (1522–1586) was Governor of the Habsburg Netherlands from 1559 to 1567 and again from 1578 to 1582. The illegitimate daughter of Emperor Charles V, she was raised in the Netherlands at the court of her aunts Mary of Hungary and Margaret of Austria.

Governorship

Appointed by her half-brother Philip II of Spain, Margaret governed during the mounting religious and political crisis that preceded the Eighty Years' War. Her tenure saw the Compromise of Nobles, the presentation of the nobles' petition in 1566 — the occasion of the famous "beggars" (gueux) remark — and the Iconoclastic Fury that swept the Netherlands that year. Unable to restore order on Philip's terms, she resigned when the Duke of Alba arrived with a Spanish army in 1567.

Later life

Margaret retired to Italy, where she had earlier been married first to Alessandro de' Medici and then to Ottavio Farnese, Duke of Parma. She briefly returned as governor alongside her son Alexander Farnese before finally retiring to Italy.
