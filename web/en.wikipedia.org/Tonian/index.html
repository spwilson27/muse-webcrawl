The Tonian is the first geologic period in the Neoproterozoic Era. It lasted from 1 billion to 720 million years ago. Instead of being based on stratigraphy, these dates are defined by the International Commission on Stratigraphy using radiometric chronometry. The Tonian is preceded by the Stenian Period of the Mesoproterozoic Era and followed by the Cryogenian.

Geology

The supercontinent Rodinia, which had assembled during the preceding Grenville orogeny, began to break apart during the Tonian, around 825–720 million years ago. Rifting of Rodinia produced numerous passive margins and intracontinental basins in which thick sedimentary successions accumulated, and the breakup is recorded in successions such as the Grand Canyon Supergroup in North America and equivalent strata on other cratons. A major large igneous province event, the Franklin–Irvington and related magmatism, accompanied the later stages of breakup.

Climate

The Tonian was generally warm compared with the severe glaciations that followed. Evidence such as the Bitter Springs isotope anomaly, a notable negative carbon isotope excursion around 810 million years ago, has been interpreted as reflecting changes in the global carbon cycle, possibly linked to the early diversification of eukaryotic life and increased carbon burial.

Life

Rifting around 800 million years ago is accompanied by the evidence of the first radiations of acritarchs, the organic-walled microfossils thought to include early eukaryotes and possibly early algae. The Tonian fossil record also includes some of the earliest evidence interpreted as early animals — including putative sponge biomarkers and the enigmatic fossil Otavia antiqua from Namibia, dated to around 760 million years ago and proposed as an early sponge-grade organism. Crown-group eukaryotes diversified substantially during this period, setting the stage for the dramatic environmental and biological changes of the Cryogenian and Ediacaran.
