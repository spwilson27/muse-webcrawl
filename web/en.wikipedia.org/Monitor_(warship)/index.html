Monitor (warship)

USS Monitor (1862), the type-defining vessel of the class

A monitor is a type of warship characterised by a very low freeboard, heavy armour, and a small number of large-calibre guns mounted in rotating turrets. The type takes its name from USS Monitor, designed by John Ericsson and launched by the US Navy in 1862 during the American Civil War. Monitors were designed for operations in shallow coastal and river waters rather than the open sea, trading seaworthiness for a small target profile and heavy armament.

History

Monitor won fame at the Battle of Hampton Roads on 9 March 1862, fighting the Confederate ironclad CSS Virginia to a draw in the first battle between ironclad warships. The US built dozens of monitors during the war, in single- and twin-turret and riverine forms; the type's weakness at sea was shown when Monitor herself foundered in a storm off Cape Hatteras in December 1862. European navies built monitors and related "breastwork monitors" for coast defence, while shallow-draft monitors served on rivers from the Mississippi to the Danube and the rivers of China.

Later service

Monitors were revived in both world wars as shore-bombardment ships: Britain's Roberts-class monitors of the Second World War mounted a twin 15-inch turret and supported landings from Normandy to Walcheren. Brazil's river monitors served for over a century on the Paraguay River. The last monitors in combat were US Navy riverine craft in the Vietnam War; no navy now operates the type, though the name is occasionally revived for modern designs.
