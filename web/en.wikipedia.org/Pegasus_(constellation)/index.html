Pegasus (constellation)


Pegasus, a large northern constellation.

Pegasus is a constellation in the northern sky, named after the winged horse of Greek mythology. It is one of the 48 constellations listed by the 2nd-century astronomer Ptolemy and remains one of the 88 modern constellations. It is the seventh-largest constellation in the sky.

Features

Its most recognisable feature is the Great Square of Pegasus, an asterism formed by the stars Markab, Scheat, and Algenib together with Alpheratz (which formally belongs to Andromeda). The constellation's brightest star is Epsilon Pegasi (Enif). Pegasus contains 51 Pegasi, the first Sun-like star found to have an exoplanet (announced in 1995, work that contributed to the 2019 Nobel Prize in Physics), and the compact galaxy group Stephan's Quintet. Ik Pegasi is the nearest known supernova progenitor candidate to Earth.

Mythology

In Greek myth the constellation represents the winged horse Pegasus, placed in the sky by Zeus; the figure appears upside-down in the northern sky, with only the front half of the horse depicted.
