German Grand Prix

The German Grand Prix (German: Großer Preis von Deutschland) is a motor race that has been held for most years since 1926, and which was a round of the Formula One World Championship from 1951 — with the exception of 1955 — until 2019. It is one of the oldest Grands Prix still associated with the world championship.

The German Grand Prix at the Hockenheimring.

Circuits

The race's spiritual home is the Nürburgring, whose legendary 22.8-kilometre Nordschleife hosted the Grand Prix until Niki Lauda's near-fatal crash in 1976 led to the event moving to the safer Hockenheimring. In later years the race alternated between the modern Nürburgring Grand Prix circuit and Hockenheim. The most successful driver in the race's history is Michael Schumacher, with four Formula One victories; Rudolf Caracciola won six times in the pre-war era.

Recent history

Financial difficulties led to the race dropping off the calendar after 2019, when it was held at Hockenheim and won by Max Verstappen in a rain-affected event. Although Germany remains central to Formula One through teams, engine manufacturers, and drivers, no German Grand Prix has been scheduled in the years since, the longest absence in the event's championship history.
