Exponentiation

Exponentiation is a mathematical operation, written as bn, involving two numbers: the base b and the exponent or power n. When n is a positive integer, exponentiation corresponds to repeated multiplication of the base:

bn = b × b × … × b  (n times)

Exponentiation is written using superscript notation, as in 23 = 8. It extends the sequence of operations that begins with addition and multiplication, and its inverse operations are roots and logarithms.

Integer exponents

For a positive integer exponent, the power is the product of n copies of the base. The exponent zero gives b0 = 1 for any nonzero base, and negative integer exponents give reciprocals: b−n = 1/bn.

Rational and real exponents

Fractional exponents correspond to roots: b1/2 is the square root of b. Real exponents are defined through limits and through the exponential function, bx = ex ln b, which extends exponentiation smoothly to all real powers.

Identities and laws

The principal laws of exponents are bxby = bx+y, (bx)y = bxy, and (ab)x = axbx. These hold for positive real bases and arbitrary real exponents, and with care also for complex numbers.

Complex exponentiation

Euler's formula, eix = cos x + i sin x, links exponentiation to trigonometry and allows powers of complex numbers to be defined. The special case eiπ + 1 = 0 is known as Euler's identity.

Applications

Exponentiation appears throughout science: exponential growth and decay in biology and physics, compound interest in finance, scientific notation for very large and small numbers, and the running time of algorithms in computer science.
