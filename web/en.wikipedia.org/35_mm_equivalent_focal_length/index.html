35 mm equivalent focal length

In photography, the 35 mm equivalent focal length is a measure that indicates the angle of view of a particular combination of a camera lens and film or image sensor size. The term is useful because most photographers who started with interchangeable-lens cameras using 35 mm film are familiar with the field of view produced by various focal lengths on that format.

Calculation

The equivalent focal length is found by multiplying the lens's actual focal length by the format's crop factor — the ratio of the diagonal of the 35 mm frame (about 43.3 mm) to the diagonal of the sensor in question. For example, a 30 mm lens on an APS-C camera with a crop factor of 1.5 has a 35 mm equivalent focal length of 45 mm, meaning it frames a scene about as a 45 mm lens would on a full-frame camera.

Uses and limitations

The convention is used most heavily for compact and bridge cameras with small sensors, whose short physical focal lengths would otherwise be difficult to interpret. It is also used to compare depth of field: for equivalent framing, a smaller sensor generally yields greater depth of field at the same f-number, so equivalence discussions often extend to aperture and ISO as well. The equivalence is about field of view only — the physical focal length, and therefore properties such as the lens's actual aperture diameter, do not change.
