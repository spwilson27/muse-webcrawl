Gyūdon

Gyūdon (牛丼, "beef bowl") is a Japanese dish consisting of a bowl of rice topped with beef and onion simmered in a mildly sweet sauce flavoured with dashi, soy sauce and mirin. It is one of the most popular fast foods in Japan and is often served with beni shōga (pickled red ginger), shichimi (chilli flakes) and sometimes a raw or poached egg.

History

Gyūdon derives from gyūnabe, a beef hot-pot dish of the Meiji era, when beef eating became common in Japan. The dish was popularised in the late 19th century around Tokyo's working-class districts and markets.

Restaurant chains

Large chains such as Yoshinoya (founded 1899), Matsuya and Sukiya have made gyūdon a ubiquitous, inexpensive meal, frequently sold around the clock. A temporary halt to United States beef imports in 2004, during the BSE crisis, forced chains to suspend or substitute the dish, an episode widely reported in Japan.
