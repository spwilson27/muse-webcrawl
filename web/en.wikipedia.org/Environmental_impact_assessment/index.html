Environmental impact assessment

An environmental impact assessment (EIA) is a process for predicting and evaluating the environmental effects of a proposed project or policy before decisions are made. It is used for dams, highways, mines, industrial plants, ports, and large developments in many legal systems.

Process

Typical stages include screening to decide whether assessment is required, scoping to identify key issues, baseline studies, impact prediction, comparison of alternatives, public consultation, mitigation planning, and monitoring after approval. The results are often presented in an environmental impact statement.

Legal foundations

The United States National Environmental Policy Act of 1969 was an early influential model. The European Union, World Bank, and many national governments later adopted EIA requirements, sometimes extending them to strategic environmental assessment of plans and programs.

Effectiveness and criticism

Good assessments can reveal avoidable damage and improve design; weak ones may become paperwork exercises with biased baselines or ignored cumulative effects. Climate change, biodiversity loss, and environmental justice are increasingly central topics. See also National Environmental Policy Act and strategic environmental assessment.
