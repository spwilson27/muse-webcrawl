The California Privacy Rights Act of 2020 (CPRA) is a California state law that significantly expanded the privacy rights of consumers under the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA). It was approved by voters as Proposition 24 in the November 2020 general election and became operative on January 1, 2023.

Background

The CPRA was principally sponsored by real estate developer Alastair Mactaggart, who had also driven the ballot initiative that prompted the CCPA in 2018. Frustrated by legislative amendments weakening the CCPA, its proponents returned to the ballot to entrench stronger protections and make them harder for the legislature to dilute.

Provisions

The Act gives consumers rights to correct inaccurate personal information and to limit the use of "sensitive personal information" such as precise geolocation, race, religion, genetic data and the contents of private communications. It extends the opt-out of the "sale" of data to the "sharing" of data for cross-context behavioural advertising, adds data-minimisation and storage-limitation principles, and expands rights concerning automated decision-making and profiling. Businesses can be liable for breaches involving email addresses combined with passwords.

Enforcement

The CPRA created the California Privacy Protection Agency, the first dedicated privacy regulator in the United States, governed by a five-member board and funded through a dedicated investment fund. The agency shares enforcement with the state Attorney General and may adopt regulations and impose administrative fines.
