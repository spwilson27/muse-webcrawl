Bohai Sea

LocationNortheastern China
TypeMarginal sea / gulf
AreaApprox. 77,000 km²

The Bohai Sea (Chinese: 渤海; pinyin: Bó Hǎi) is a marginal sea of about 77,000 km² on the east coast of mainland China. It is the northwestern and innermost extension of the Yellow Sea, to which it connects through the Bohai Strait, and it is sometimes called the Bohai Gulf or simply Bohai Bay.

Geography

The sea is enclosed by the Liaodong and Shandong peninsulas, with Liaodong Bay to the northeast, Bohai Bay to the west, and Laizhou Bay to the south. Major rivers flowing into it — including the Yellow River, Hai River, Liao River, and Luan River — carry heavy sediment loads, and the sea is notably shallow, averaging about 18 metres deep.

Economy

The Bohai Economic Rim around the sea includes the cities of Tianjin, Dalian, and Tangshan and is one of China's major industrial regions. The sea supports important fisheries, salt production, and offshore oil fields, including the Penglai field, one of China's largest offshore oil discoveries.

Environment

Rapid industrialisation of the surrounding region has made the Bohai Sea one of China's most polluted marine areas, with river-borne industrial runoff and oil spills — notably the 2011 Penglai spill — posing continuing ecological concerns.
