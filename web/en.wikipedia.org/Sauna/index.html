Sauna

A sauna is a small room or building designed for dry or wet heat sessions, used for recreation and therapeutic purposes. The word and the tradition are Finnish: Finland has an estimated two to three million saunas for a population of about 5.5 million, and sauna bathing is inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

A traditional Finnish sauna interior with tiered benches

Types

The classic Finnish sauna is heated to 70–100 °C by a stove (kiuas) topped with stones, over which water is thrown to produce steam, called löyly. Other forms include the smoke sauna (savusauna), steam rooms, infrared saunas and related traditions such as the Russian banya and the Turkish hammam.

Practice

Bathers typically sit unclothed on tiered benches, sweat for several minutes, then cool off — traditionally by plunging into a lake or rolling in snow — and repeat the cycle. In Finland the sauna is a social institution as well as a place of cleanliness: historically births and treatments of the sick took place there, and business and family life still revolve around it.

Health

Regular sauna use is associated in Finnish cohort studies with reduced cardiovascular mortality, though researchers caution that heat exposure is unsuitable for some medical conditions and should be avoided with alcohol.
