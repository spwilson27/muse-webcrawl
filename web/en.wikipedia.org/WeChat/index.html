WeChat (Chinese: 微信; pinyin: Wēixìn) is a Chinese instant messaging, social media and mobile payment application developed by Tencent. First released in 2011, it grew into one of the world's largest standalone mobile apps and is often described as a "super-app" because of the wide range of services integrated within it.

Features

Beyond text, voice and video messaging, WeChat includes Moments (a social feed), official accounts for brands and media, and "mini programs" — lightweight sub-applications for shopping, transport, government services and games that run inside WeChat without separate installation. WeChat Pay is one of China's two dominant mobile payment systems, used for everything from street food to utility bills, often via QR codes.

History

The app was created by a Tencent team in Guangzhou led by Allen Zhang. Voice messaging and social discovery features such as "Shake" drove early growth; by 2018 it passed one billion monthly active users. The international version, WeChat, is separate from the mainland service, Weixin, though they interoperate.

Criticism and restrictions

WeChat has faced scrutiny abroad over data practices, censorship and surveillance of content, including of users outside China. India banned the app in 2020 along with other Chinese apps, and its use by U.S. government personnel has been restricted.
