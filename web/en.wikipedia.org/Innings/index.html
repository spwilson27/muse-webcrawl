Innings

An innings is one of the divisions of a cricket match during which one team takes its turn to bat. In most formats, a match consists of two innings per side (in Test and first-class cricket) or one innings per side (in limited overs cricket such as One Day Internationals and Twenty20). The term also denotes an individual batsman's turn at the crease, as in "he played a fine innings."

Structure

A team innings ends when ten wickets have fallen (the batting side is "all out"), when the captain declares the innings closed, or when the allocated overs or time expire. In two-innings matches, a side batting second and trailing by a large margin may be asked to follow on and bat its second innings immediately. The word is singular despite the final "s"; in baseball and softball the corresponding unit of play, spelled "inning" without the s in the singular, derives from the same term.

Usage

Scores are recorded by runs and wickets — for example, a team "scored 250 for 5 in their first innings." By extension, "a good innings" has become an English idiom for a long and successful life or career.
