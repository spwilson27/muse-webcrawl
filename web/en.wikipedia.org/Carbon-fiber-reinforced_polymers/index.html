Carbon-fiber-reinforced polymers

Carbon-fiber-reinforced polymers (CFRP), also called carbon-fiber-reinforced plastics or simply carbon fiber, are composite materials consisting of carbon fibers embedded in a polymer matrix, usually an epoxy resin. They combine extremely high strength and stiffness with very low weight, and are used where performance justifies their cost.

Properties

CFRP has one of the highest strength-to-weight and stiffness-to-weight ratios of any engineering material. Its properties are anisotropic: strength is greatest along the fiber direction, so parts are built from layered plies ("laminates") with fibers oriented to match expected loads. The material resists corrosion and fatigue well but is brittle in impact and difficult to recycle.

Manufacture

The carbon fibers themselves are usually made from polyacrylonitrile (PAN) precursor, which is oxidised, carbonised at high temperature, and surface-treated. Parts are made by laying up woven fabric or unidirectional tape, often as resin-impregnated "prepreg", in a mould and curing under heat and pressure, frequently in an autoclave. Other methods include resin transfer moulding, filament winding, and pultrusion.

Applications

CFRP is used extensively in aircraft structures (the Boeing 787 and Airbus A350 have composite primary structures), Formula One monocoques and other motorsport parts, high-end bicycle frames, wind turbine blades, sporting goods, and increasingly in premium automobiles and civil-engineering strengthening.
