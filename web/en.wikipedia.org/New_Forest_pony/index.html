The New Forest pony is one of the recognised mountain and moorland pony breeds of the British Isles. Height varies from about 12 to 14.2 hands, and there is no upper height limit within the breed standard beyond the accepted range; all colours are allowed except piebald, skewbald, and blue-cream. The breed is noted for its intelligence, strength, versatility, calm temperament, and sure-footedness.

History

Ponies have grazed the New Forest in Hampshire since before the Norman Conquest; William the Conqueror's creation of the forest as a royal hunting ground in about 1079 protected the grazing rights of local "commoners," whose descendants still depasture ponies under ancient rights of common pasture. Over the centuries, blood from Welsh, Dartmoor, Exmoor, Highland, Fell, and Dales ponies, and in the 19th century Arabian and Thoroughbred crosses, improved the stock. The New Forest Pony Breeding and Cattle Society, founded in 1891, maintains the stud book; the semi-feral herd of several thousand ponies shapes the forest's distinctive grazed landscape and is managed through annual round-ups called drifts.

Uses

New Forest ponies compete successfully in dressage, show jumping, eventing, gymkhana, and competitive driving, and are popular family ponies. Larger individuals can carry adults; the breed is frequently crossed with Thoroughbreds to produce competition horses.
