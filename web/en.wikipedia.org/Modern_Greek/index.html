Modern Greek


Native to: Greece, Cyprus
Speakers: about 13.5 million
Language family: Indo-European > Hellenic > Greek
Writing system: Greek alphabet

Modern Greek (Νέα Ελληνικά, Néa Elliniká) is the collective term for the dialects and varieties of the Greek language spoken in the modern era, including the standardized form, officially known simply as Greek. It is the official language of Greece and Cyprus and one of the official languages of the European Union.

History

Modern Greek emerged from Medieval (Byzantine) Greek around the 15th century. For much of the modern period, Greek speakers faced a situation of diglossia between the vernacular Demotic (Dimotiki) and the archaizing Katharevousa, a "purified" form used in official and literary contexts. The language question was resolved in 1976, when Demotic was made the official language of Greece; in 1982 the polytonic accent system was replaced in official use by the simpler monotonic system.

Phonology and grammar

Modern Greek has five vowel phonemes and a largely phonemic orthography, though several spellings preserve historical distinctions. The grammar is fusional, with nouns declining in three genders and four cases (nominative, genitive, accusative, vocative), and verbs conjugating for person, number, tense, aspect, mood and voice. Unlike Ancient Greek, Modern Greek has lost the infinitive, the dative case, and vowel length distinctions.

Dialects

Regional varieties include Cypriot Greek, Cretan, the Greek spoken in southern Italy (Griko and Grecanico), Pontic Greek, and Cappadocian Greek. Standard Modern Greek is based largely on Demotic as spoken in Athens, with some influence from Katharevousa vocabulary.
