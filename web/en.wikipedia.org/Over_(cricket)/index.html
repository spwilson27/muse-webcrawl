Over (cricket)

In cricket, an over is a set of six legal deliveries bowled by a single bowler. It is the basic unit of scoring and of the progress of an innings: bowling alternates between ends of the pitch after each over, and a bowler may not bowl two consecutive overs. The captain of the fielding side decides which bowler bowls each over, subject to limits on how many overs any one bowler may bowl in limited-overs matches.

Deliveries that are illegal — wides and no-balls — do not count toward the six legal balls and must be bowled again, so an over may contain more than six physical deliveries. Runs scored from an over are sometimes described as the over "going for" that number of runs; an over in which no runs are scored and no extra is conceded is called a maiden over.

History

The length of an over has varied over cricket history. Four-ball overs were used in England in the 19th century; five-ball overs appeared briefly, and eight-ball overs were used in Australia until the 1979–80 season, and in other countries at various times. The six-ball over became universal in the Laws of Cricket from 1980. In The Hundred, a newer format, bowling is organised in sets of five balls, though 20 balls may be bowled consecutively from the same end.

Over rates and limits

In Test and first-class cricket there is no per-bowler limit on overs, but teams must maintain a minimum over rate — typically 15 overs per hour — and may be penalised for slow rates. In limited-overs cricket, innings are defined by a total number of overs (50 in One Day Internationals, 20 in Twenty20), and each bowler is restricted to at most one fifth of the total overs (10 in a 50-over match, 4 in a T20). The final overs of a limited-overs innings, when batting sides take greater risks, are known as the "death overs".

Notation

Scores conventionally record partial overs in the form overs.balls — for example, "32.4 overs" means 32 completed overs and 4 balls of the next, i.e. 196 legal deliveries, not a decimal fraction. A bowler's figures are likewise given as overs–maidens–runs–wickets.
