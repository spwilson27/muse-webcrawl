The Zwinger (German: Dresdner Zwinger) is a palatial Baroque complex in Dresden, Germany, built under Augustus the Strong, Elector of Saxony and King of Poland. Designed by court architect Matthäus Daniel Pöppelmann with lavish sculpture by Balthasar Permoser, it is one of the most important Baroque buildings in Germany. The name derives from the medieval term for the outer courtyard between a castle's walls.

History

Construction began in 1710 on the site of former fortifications, initially as an orangery and festival ground for court tournaments and celebrations; the complex opened for the wedding of the crown prince in 1719, though work continued until about 1728. Its name notwithstanding, the Zwinger was never a fortress: one side remained open until Gottfried Semper closed the courtyard in the mid-19th century with his gallery building (the Semper Gallery), facing the later Semperoper opera house.

The Zwinger was almost completely destroyed by the bombing of Dresden in February 1945. Reconstruction began immediately after the war with Soviet support and was substantially complete by the early 1960s.

The complex and collections

Four ornate pavilions — including the Rampart Pavilion, the Glockenspiel Pavilion with its Meissen porcelain bells, and the Crown Gate (Kronentor), Dresden's emblem — surround gardens and fountains, among them Permoser's Nymph Bath. As part of the Dresden State Art Collections, the Zwinger houses the Old Masters Picture Gallery (with Raphael's Sistine Madonna and works by Vermeer, Rembrandt and Titian), one of the world's great collections of Chinese, Japanese and Meissen porcelain, and the Royal Cabinet of Mathematical and Physical Instruments.
