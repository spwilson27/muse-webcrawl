Acid–base reaction

Acid–base reaction
Field: Chemistry
Key theories: Arrhenius, Brønsted–Lowry, Lewis
Classic result: Salt + water (neutralization)

An acid–base reaction is a chemical reaction that occurs between an acid and a base. It can be used to determine pH via titration, and several theoretical frameworks describe the underlying proton- or electron-pair transfer.

Theories

The Arrhenius definition treats acids as substances producing H⁺ and bases as producing OH⁻ in water. The Brønsted–Lowry theory (1923) generalizes this to proton donors and acceptors in any solvent. The Lewis theory is broader still: acids accept electron pairs and bases donate them.

Neutralization

When an acid and a base react in equivalent amounts, the result is neutralization, classically producing a salt and water. The pH at the equivalence point depends on the strengths of the acid and base involved.

Applications

Acid–base reactions underpin titration analysis, buffer solutions, antacid medicines, and many industrial processes.
