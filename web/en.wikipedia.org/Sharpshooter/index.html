Sharpshooter

A sharpshooter is a person exceptionally skilled in precision shooting with firearms. The term is used both for military marksmen and, in modern usage, more loosely for any accurate shooter. It is distinguished from a sniper, who typically operates independently with specialised training in concealment and reconnaissance, and from a designated marksman, who extends the effective range of an infantry squad.

History

The word derives from the German Scharfschütze and gained currency in English during the American Civil War, when units such as Berdan's Sharpshooters were equipped with accurate rifles like the Sharps rifle — a name from manufacturer Christian Sharps that reinforced the term. Rifled barrels, telescopic sights, and Minié balls made aimed long-range fire practical for the first time.

Usage

In United States Army marksmanship qualification, "sharpshooter" is the second-highest badge, between marksman and expert. Historically the word has also been applied to trick shooters of exhibition fame, such as Annie Oakley.
