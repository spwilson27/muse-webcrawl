Capillary electrophoresis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Capillary electrophoresis (CE) is a family of electrokinetic separation methods performed in submillimeter diameter capillaries and in micro- and nanofluidic channels. It is used to separate ionic species by their charge and frictional forces and is important in analytical chemistry.

Method

A sample is introduced into a narrow fused-silica capillary filled with an electrolyte solution, and a high voltage (often 10–30 kV) is applied. Analytes migrate at different velocities depending on their electrophoretic mobility, superimposed on the electroosmotic flow of the buffer, and are detected as they pass a window in the capillary, commonly by UV absorbance or fluorescence. Only nanolitre volumes of sample are required.

Modes and applications

Variants include capillary zone electrophoresis, capillary gel electrophoresis (used for DNA sizing), micellar electrokinetic chromatography, and capillary isoelectric focusing. CE was central to DNA sequencing during the Human Genome Project and remains widely used in pharmaceutical analysis, forensics, and proteomics.
