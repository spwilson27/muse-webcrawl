Transgender

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Transgender (often abbreviated as trans) is an umbrella term for people whose gender identity differs from the sex they were assigned at birth. Transgender people may identify as men or women whose gender differs from their assigned sex (binary transgender people), or they may identify outside the gender binary, as in non-binary identities. The opposite of transgender is cisgender, which describes persons whose gender identity matches their assigned sex.

Terminology

The term transgender gained widespread use in the 1990s as an umbrella term encompassing transsexual people, cross-dressers, and gender-nonconforming people, though its boundaries remain debated. Earlier terms include Magnus Hirschfeld's transvestite (1910) and John F. Oliven's transgender (1965), popularised in the 1970s by Virginia Prince.

Transition

Many transgender people undertake a transition: a process of aligning their life and, in some cases, their body with their gender identity. Social transition may involve changes in name, pronouns, and presentation. Medical transition can include hormone therapy and surgeries. Legal transition involves changing name and sex markers on documents. Not all transgender people pursue all or any of these steps.

Gender dysphoria

Some transgender people experience gender dysphoria, distress arising from the incongruence between gender identity and assigned sex. Major medical associations, including the American Medical Association and the American Psychiatric Association, regard access to gender-affirming care as medically necessary for many patients. DSM-5 lists a diagnosis of gender dysphoria; ICD-11 moved "gender incongruence" out of the mental disorders chapter.

History and culture

Gender-variant people are documented across cultures and eras, including hijra communities in South Asia, fa'afafine in Samoa, and Two-Spirit identities in some Indigenous North American cultures. The modern transgender rights movement grew from mid-20th-century activism, including the 1966 Compton's Cafeteria riot and the 1969 Stonewall uprising in the United States.

Demographics and law

Surveys in the United States estimate roughly 0.5–1.6% of adults identify as transgender, with higher proportions among younger cohorts. Legal recognition, healthcare access, and anti-discrimination protections vary widely by jurisdiction and remain subjects of active legislation and litigation.
