Suprachiasmatic nucleus

The suprachiasmatic nucleus or nuclei (SCN) is a tiny region of the brain in the hypothalamus, situated directly above the optic chiasm. It is responsible for controlling circadian rhythms: the SCN generates a roughly 24-hour cycle of activity that regulates sleep–wake patterns, hormone release, body temperature and other physiological functions throughout the body.

Structure and inputs

Each SCN contains around 20,000 neurons, each of which is itself a tiny oscillator built from a transcription–translation feedback loop of clock genes such as Period and Cryptochrome. The nucleus receives direct input from specialised photosensitive retinal ganglion cells via the retinohypothalamic tract, which carries information about ambient light (using the photopigment melanopsin) that entrains the clock to the day–night cycle.

Function

The SCN coordinates peripheral clocks in organs throughout the body through neural and hormonal signals, including control of melatonin secretion by the pineal gland. Lesions of the SCN abolish circadian rhythmicity in experimental animals, while transplanted SCN tissue can restore rhythms with the donor's period — classic experiments in hamsters demonstrated that the SCN is the master pacemaker. The molecular mechanism of the circadian clock was recognised by the 2017 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.

Clinical relevance

Disruption of SCN function or entrainment contributes to jet lag, shift-work sleep disorder, and aspects of ageing and neurodegenerative disease, in which deterioration of the SCN is associated with fragmented sleep.
