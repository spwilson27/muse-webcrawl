Gestational age

An obstetric ultrasound, used to measure the fetus and estimate gestational age.

In obstetrics, gestational age is a measure of the age of a pregnancy taken from the beginning of the woman's last menstrual period (LMP), or from a more accurate method such as ultrasound measurement when available. Gestational age is expressed in completed weeks and days, and a full-term pregnancy is considered to last about 40 weeks by this measure, with birth normally occurring between 37 and 42 weeks.

Terminology

Because counting begins at the last menstrual period, gestational age runs about two weeks ahead of the actual age of the embryo or fetus since fertilisation (fertilisation age). A baby born before 37 completed weeks is preterm; one born after 42 weeks is post-term. Within term births, distinctions are made between early term (37–38 weeks), full term (39–40 weeks), and late term (41 weeks).

Methods of assessment

Gestational age may be estimated from the date of the last menstrual period, from clinical examination of the size of the uterus, or — most accurately in early pregnancy — from ultrasound measurement of the crown–rump length of the embryo or the head circumference and femur length of the fetus. After birth, a newborn's maturity can be assessed by physical and neurological examination, as in the Ballard score.

Clinical significance

Accurate dating guides the timing of prenatal screening tests, decisions about inducing labour, and the interpretation of fetal growth. Misclassification of gestational age can lead to errors in diagnosing growth restriction or prematurity.
