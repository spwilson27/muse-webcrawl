Occitania (administrative region)

Occitania (French: Occitanie) is the southernmost administrative region of metropolitan France, excluding Corsica, created by the territorial reform of French regions in 2014. It resulted from the merger of the former regions of Languedoc-Roussillon and Midi-Pyrénées. The region's capital and largest city is Toulouse, with Montpellier as another major city.

Geography

Occitania stretches from the Rhône valley in the east to the Atlantic foothills in the west, bordered by the Pyrenees to the south (with Spain and Andorra) and the Mediterranean Sea to the southeast. It includes historic cities such as Nîmes, Carcassonne, Narbonne, and Albi.

Name

The name evokes the historic linguistic region of Occitania, where the Occitan language (langue d'oc) was traditionally spoken. The regional council's naming of the new region in 2016 was contested by Catalan groups in the Pyrénées-Orientales, whose identity is acknowledged in the region's subtitle "Pyrénées-Méditerranée".
