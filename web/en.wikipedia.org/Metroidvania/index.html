GenreAction-adventure, platformer subgenre
Named forMetroid and Castlevania series
Key titlesSuper Metroid (1994), Castlevania: Symphony of the Night (1997)

Metroidvania is a subgenre of action-adventure video games, and/or a video game design concept, focused on guided non-linearity and utility-gated exploration and progression. The games usually feature a large interconnected world map the player can explore, although parts of the world are accessible only after gaining specific tools, weapons, or abilities. The term is a portmanteau of Nintendo's Metroid series and Konami's Castlevania series.

Characteristics

Metroidvania games generally are two-dimensional side-scrollers with platforming and combat, an emphasis on exploration and backtracking, and persistent character growth. The design was crystallised by Super Metroid (1994) and Castlevania: Symphony of the Night (1997), the latter directed by Koji Igarashi, who sought to extend play time through exploration and role-playing elements.

Influence

After a period in which the major series went dormant, independent games revived the genre in the 2010s, including Cave Story (an earlier precursor, 2004), Guacamelee!, Ori and the Blind Forest, Hollow Knight, Dead Cells, and Axiom Verge. The design philosophy has also influenced 3D games and other genres, with ability-gated maps appearing across modern action-adventure titles.
