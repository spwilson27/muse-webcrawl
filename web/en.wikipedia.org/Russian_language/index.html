Russian (русский язык, russkiy yazyk) is an East Slavic language of the Indo-European family, native to the Russians. It is the most widely spoken Slavic language and the largest native language in Europe, with about 150 million native speakers and around 260 million speakers in total. Russian is an official language in Russia, Belarus, Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan, a lingua franca across much of the former Soviet Union, and one of the six official languages of the United Nations.

History

Russian developed from Old East Slavic, the language of Kievan Rus'. Under Church Slavonic influence, a literary language took shape in the 18th century through the reforms of Peter the Great — who introduced a civil typeface — and the synthesis achieved by Lomonosov and by Pushkin, whose work is regarded as the foundation of modern literary Russian. The spelling reform of 1918 simplified the alphabet, removing letters such as yat and hard sign in final position.

Grammar

Russian is a highly inflected, fusional language. Nouns decline in six cases and belong to three genders; verbs conjugate for person and number and mark the crucial distinction between perfective and imperfective aspect. Word order is relatively free, stress is mobile, and the phonology features a contrast between palatalised ("soft") and plain ("hard") consonants.

Alphabet

The Russian alphabet consists of 33 Cyrillic letters, including the hard sign (ъ) and soft sign (ь), which modify adjacent sounds rather than representing sounds themselves. Transliteration into Latin script follows several competing systems, such as ALA-LC and ISO 9.
