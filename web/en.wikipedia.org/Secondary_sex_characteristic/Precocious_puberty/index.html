Precocious puberty


Pubertal staging used in diagnosis

In medicine, precocious puberty is puberty occurring at an unusually early age. In most cases, the process is normal in every aspect except the unusually early age and simply represents a variation of normal development. In minority of children with precocious puberty, the early development is triggered by a disease such as a tumor or injury of the brain.

Even when there is no disease, unusually early puberty can have adverse effects on social behavior and psychological development (have a more mature knowledge of reproduction and display more sexual behavior), can reduce adult height potential, and may shift some lifelong health risks. Central precocious puberty can be treated by suppressing the pituitary hormones that induce sex steroid production.

Definitions

Puberty is considered precocious when secondary sex characteristics appear before age 8 in girls or age 9 in boys. Girls are about ten times more likely than boys to be evaluated for the condition. Early menarche may follow if the process is not interrupted.

Types and causes

Central (gonadotropin-dependent) precocious puberty results from early activation of the hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis; most cases in girls are idiopathic, while boys more often have an underlying central nervous system lesion such as a hypothalamic hamartoma. Peripheral (gonadotropin-independent) precocious puberty results from autonomous sex hormone production by the gonads or adrenals, or from exogenous hormone exposure — for example, inadvertent exposure to anabolic steroids or estrogen-containing products.

Evaluation

Evaluation includes growth records and Tanner staging, bone-age radiographs, basal and GnRH-stimulated gonadotropin levels, sex steroid levels, and often MRI of the brain to exclude a central lesion. Management falls within pediatric endocrinology.

Treatment

Central precocious puberty is treated with long-acting GnRH analogs, which suppress pituitary gonadotropin secretion and halt pubertal progression and slow bone-age advancement, preserving adult height. Treatment of peripheral forms addresses the underlying source of hormone production.
