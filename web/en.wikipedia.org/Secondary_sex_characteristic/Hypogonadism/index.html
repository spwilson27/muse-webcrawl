Hypogonadism


The hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis

Hypogonadism means diminished functional activity of the gonads — the testes or the ovaries — that may result in diminished production of sex hormones. Low androgen (e.g., testosterone) levels are referred to as hypoandrogenism and low estrogen as hypoestrogenism. These are responsible for the observed signs and symptoms in both males and females.

Hypogonadism, commonly referred to by the symptom "low testosterone" or "low T", can also decrease other hormones secreted by the gonads including progesterone, DHEA, anti-Müllerian hormone, activin, and inhibin. Sperm development (spermatogenesis) and release of the egg from the ovaries (ovulation) may be impaired by hypogonadism, which, depending on the degree of severity, may result in partial or complete infertility.

Classification

Hypogonadism is classified as primary (hypergonadotropic), when the defect lies in the gonads themselves, or secondary/central (hypogonadotropic), when the defect lies in the hypothalamus or pituitary gland. Causes of primary hypogonadism include Klinefelter syndrome, gonadal damage from chemotherapy or radiation, and autoimmune destruction. Central causes include pituitary tumors, Kallmann syndrome, and hyperprolactinemia.

Signs and symptoms

In males, hypogonadism before puberty causes failure of normal sexual development and absent secondary sex characteristics; after puberty it may cause reduced libido, erectile dysfunction, loss of muscle mass, increased body fat, fatigue, depressed mood, and osteoporosis. In females it may cause amenorrhea, infertility, and symptoms of estrogen deficiency.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is based on signs and symptoms combined with laboratory measurement of sex hormone levels (testosterone in men, estradiol in women) together with the pituitary gonadotropins LH and FSH, which distinguish primary from central disease.

Treatment

Treatment is by hormone replacement: testosterone replacement therapy in men (by injection, gel, or patch), and estrogen with or without progestin in women. Anabolic steroids such as testosterone preparations are the mainstay of therapy. When fertility is desired in central hypogonadism, gonadotropin therapy may be used instead.
