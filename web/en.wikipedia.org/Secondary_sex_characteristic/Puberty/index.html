Puberty

Puberty is the process of physical changes through which a child's body matures into an adult body capable of sexual reproduction. It is initiated by hormonal signals from the brain to the gonads: the ovaries in a girl and the testes in a boy. In response, the gonads produce hormones that stimulate libido and the growth and development of the brain, bones, muscle, blood, skin, hair, breasts, and sex organs.

The Tanner stages describe the progression of pubertal development.

Hormonal control

Puberty begins when the hypothalamus increases pulsatile secretion of gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH), prompting the pituitary to release luteinizing hormone (LH) and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH). These stimulate the gonads to secrete sex steroids—estrogens in girls and testosterone in boys—through the endocrine system. The rising steroids produce the secondary sex characteristics and a rapid growth spurt, and eventually trigger epiphyseal fusion that ends growth in height.

Changes and timing

In girls, puberty typically begins between ages 8 and 13 with breast development (thelarche), followed by pubic hair, a growth spurt, and the first menstrual period (menarche), on average around age 12. In boys, onset is usually between 9 and 14, beginning with testicular enlargement, followed by pubic hair, voice deepening, increased muscle mass, and facial hair. Development is staged clinically using the Tanner scale. Puberty that begins unusually early is called precocious puberty; delayed puberty may reflect constitutional delay or underlying disorders.
