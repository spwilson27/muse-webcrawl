Steroidogenesis


The human steroidogenic pathway from cholesterol

Steroidogenesis is the biological process by which cholesterol is converted to other steroids. Different pathways of steroidogenesis exist in different species, but the human pathway produces the corticosteroids and sex steroids essential for life and reproduction. The products of steroidogenesis include mineralocorticoids (aldosterone), glucocorticoids (cortisol), and the sex hormones: androgens, estrogens, and progestogens such as progesterone.

Pathway

The pathway begins with the transport of cholesterol into mitochondria, facilitated by the steroidogenic acute regulatory protein (StAR), where the enzyme CYP11A1 cleaves its side chain to form pregnenolone — the rate-limiting step. Pregnenolone is then converted via progesterone and 17-hydroxyprogesterone into androstenedione and testosterone; subsequent steps produce dihydrotestosterone by 5α-reduction or estradiol by aromatization. In the adrenal cortex, branch pathways yield cortisol and aldosterone.

Sites

Steroidogenesis occurs in the adrenal cortex, the gonads (testes and ovaries), and the placenta, with each tissue expressing a characteristic subset of enzymes that determines its products. Local steroid synthesis also occurs in the brain (neurosteroids) and skin.

Regulation

Adrenal steroidogenesis is regulated by ACTH from the pituitary gland, and gonadal steroidogenesis by LH and FSH. Regulation of these pathways is a core subject of endocrinology.

Disorders

Enzyme defects in the pathway cause congenital adrenal hyperplasia and other disorders of sex development; the most common is 21-hydroxylase deficiency, which impairs cortisol and aldosterone synthesis and shunts precursors into androgen production.
