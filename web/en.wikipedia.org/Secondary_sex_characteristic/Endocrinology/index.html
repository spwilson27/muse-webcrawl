Endocrinology


The principal glands of the human endocrine system

Endocrinology (from endocrine + -ology) is a branch of biology and medicine dealing with the endocrine system, its diseases, and its specific secretions known as hormones. It is also concerned with the integration of developmental events such as proliferation, growth, and differentiation, and the psychological or behavioral activities of metabolism, growth and development, tissue function, sleep, digestion, respiration, excretion, mood, stress, lactation, movement, reproduction, and sensory perception caused by hormones.

Specializations include behavioral endocrinology and comparative endocrinology. The endocrine system consists of several glands, all in different parts of the body, that secrete hormones directly into the blood rather than into a duct system. Major glands include the pituitary gland, thyroid, parathyroids, adrenal glands, pancreas, ovaries, and testes.

The endocrine system

Endocrinology is the study of the endocrine system in the human body. This is a system of glands which secrete hormones. Hormones are chemicals that affect the actions of different organ systems in the body. Examples include thyroid hormone, growth hormone, insulin, and cortisol. The endocrine system involves a number of feedback mechanisms, so that often one hormone (such as pituitary thyroid-stimulating hormone) will control the action or release of another secondary hormone (such as thyroid hormone).

Diseases

Diseases of the endocrine system are common, including diabetes mellitus, thyroid disease, and obesity. Endocrine disease may result from hormone overproduction (hyperfunction), underproduction (hypofunction), or defective receptors and signaling pathways. Common conditions treated by endocrinologists include diabetes mellitus, hyperthyroidism and hypothyroidism, Cushing's syndrome, Addison's disease, disorders of puberty and growth, and hypogonadism.

History

The study of endocrinology began in China, where by 200 BC the Chinese were isolating sex and pituitary hormones from human urine and using them for medicinal purposes. Modern endocrinology is often dated to the nineteenth century; Arnold Berthold's 1849 experiments on castrated roosters demonstrated that the testes produce a blood-borne substance responsible for male characteristics. The term "hormone" was coined in 1905 by Ernest Starling.

Endocrinologists

An endocrinologist is a physician who has undertaken specialist training in internal medicine or pediatrics followed by fellowship training in endocrinology, diabetes, and metabolism. Endocrinologists diagnose and manage hormone imbalances and related conditions, frequently using laboratory testing and imaging.
