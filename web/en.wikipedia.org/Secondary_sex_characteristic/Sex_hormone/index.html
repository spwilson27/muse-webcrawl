Sex hormones, also known as sex steroids, gonadocorticoids and gonadal steroids, are steroid hormones that interact with vertebrate steroid hormone receptors and regulate sexual development and reproductive function. The naturally produced sex hormones include the androgens (such as testosterone), the estrogens (such as estradiol), and the progestogens (such as progesterone). They are produced primarily by the gonads — the testes in males and the ovaries in females — with smaller amounts secreted by the adrenal glands and, during pregnancy, the placenta.

Types

Androgens

Androgens are the primary male sex hormones. Testosterone, the principal androgen, drives the development of male reproductive tissues and secondary sex characteristics such as increased muscle and bone mass and body hair growth. Androgens are also present at lower levels in females, where they serve as precursors for estrogen synthesis.

Estrogens

Estrogens are the primary female sex hormones. Estradiol is the most potent and prevalent estrogen in women of reproductive age. Estrogens promote the development of female secondary sex characteristics and regulate the menstrual cycle together with progesterone.

Progestogens

Progesterone, the main progestogen, prepares the endometrium for implantation and maintains pregnancy; it also plays roles in mammary gland development.

Synthesis and regulation

All sex steroids are synthesized from cholesterol through a series of enzymatic steps in the gonads and adrenal cortex. Their secretion is controlled by the hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis: gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) from the hypothalamus stimulates the pituitary to release luteinizing hormone (LH) and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), which act on the gonads.

Functions

Sex hormones direct the development of the reproductive system before birth, trigger puberty, maintain fertility, and influence libido, bone density, fat distribution, and other sexually dimorphic traits throughout life.

Synthetic hormones and medicine

Synthetic sex steroids are widely used in hormonal contraception, hormone replacement therapy, treatment of hypogonadism, and gender-affirming care. Anabolic steroids are synthetic androgen derivatives sometimes misused to increase muscle mass.
