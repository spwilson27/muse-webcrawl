Secondary sex characteristic: Adolescence

Secondary sex characteristics emerge during puberty in adolescence.

Secondary sex characteristics are features that appear during puberty in humans and at sexual maturity in other animals. In the context of adolescence — the transitional stage of physical and psychological development between childhood and adulthood — these characteristics are the visible outward signs that the body is maturing under the influence of sex hormones.

Onset and timing

Puberty typically begins between ages 8 and 13 in girls and ages 9 and 14 in boys, triggered by the hypothalamus releasing gonadotropin-releasing hormone, which stimulates the pituitary gland to release luteinizing hormone and follicle-stimulating hormone. These in turn activate the gonads to produce oestrogens and androgens, chiefly testosterone. The sequence of changes is commonly described by the five Tanner stages.

In females

The first sign is usually thelarche, the development of breast buds, followed by growth of pubic hair, a rapid height spurt, widening of the hips, increased body fat distribution to the breasts and hips, and finally menarche, the first menstrual period, which on average occurs around age 12–13. Underarm hair and acne may also appear due to adrenal androgens.

In males

Puberty begins with enlargement of the testes, followed by pubic hair growth, lengthening of the penis, a marked growth spurt, deepening of the voice as the larynx grows, development of facial and body hair, and increased muscle mass and shoulder breadth. Spermarche, the first production of mature sperm, generally occurs around age 13–14.

Psychological and social aspects

Because the timing of these changes varies widely, early or late developers may experience self-consciousness, social comparison and differing treatment by peers and adults. Adolescents whose physical maturation is out of step with their peers are at somewhat increased risk of anxiety and low self-esteem, making accurate education about the normal range of development important.
