Estrogen

Estrogens (also spelled oestrogens) are a category of sex hormones responsible for the development and regulation of the female reproductive system and secondary sex characteristics. They are steroid hormones synthesized mainly in the ovaries, and in smaller amounts in the adrenals, adipose tissue, and, in males, the testes.

Estradiol, the main estrogen in reproductive-age women.

Types and synthesis

The three major endogenous estrogens are estrone (E1), estradiol (E2), and estriol (E3); a fourth, estetrol (E4), is produced only in pregnancy. Estradiol is the most potent and predominates before menopause. All estrogens are derived from androgens—testosterone and androstenedione—by the enzyme aromatase, in the final step of steroidogenesis.

Functions

Estrogens drive the development of female secondary sex characteristics at puberty, regulate the menstrual cycle and the endometrium, and are essential to fertility and pregnancy. Beyond reproduction, they affect bone density, cardiovascular function, skin, and the brain in both sexes. Estrogen acts mainly through nuclear estrogen receptors ERα and ERβ, which modulate gene transcription.

Medical uses and risks

Estrogens are used in hormonal contraception, hormone replacement therapy for menopause, and treatment of certain cancers and conditions of estrogen deficiency. Prolonged unopposed estrogen exposure is associated with increased risks of endometrial cancer, breast cancer, and thromboembolism, which shapes how estrogen therapies are prescribed.
