Testosterone

Testosterone is the primary male sex hormone and an anabolic steroid hormone. In humans it plays a key role in the development of male reproductive tissues such as the testes and prostate, and in promoting secondary sex characteristics such as increased muscle and bone mass and the growth of body hair.

Testosterone, the principal androgen.

Production and regulation

Testosterone is secreted primarily by the Leydig cells of the testes in males, and in much smaller amounts by the ovaries in females and by the adrenal glands in both sexes. Its production is governed by the hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis of the endocrine system: luteinizing hormone stimulates its synthesis from cholesterol. In tissues such as the prostate and hair follicles, testosterone is converted by 5α-reductase to the more potent dihydrotestosterone (DHT), and small amounts are aromatized to estradiol, an estrogen.

Functions

Before birth, testosterone and DHT direct the development of male internal and external genitalia. At puberty, rising levels cause enlargement of the sex organs, deepening of the voice, facial and body hair growth, increased muscle mass and strength, and the adolescent growth spurt. In adults it is necessary for sperm production, libido, erythropoiesis, and maintenance of bone density.

Medical use and misuse

As a medication, testosterone is used to treat male hypogonadism and certain types of breast cancer, and in masculinizing hormone therapy. It is also misused as a performance-enhancing anabolic steroid in sport, where supraphysiological doses carry cardiovascular, hepatic, and reproductive risks and are prohibited by anti-doping rules.
