Cortisol


Cortisol, the primary glucocorticoid in humans

Cortisol is a steroid hormone in the glucocorticoid class of hormones and a chemical messenger. When used pharmaceutically, it is known as hydrocortisone. It is produced in many animals, mainly by the zona fasciculata of the adrenal cortex in the adrenal gland. It is produced in other tissues in lower quantities. It is released with a diurnal cycle and its release is increased in response to stress and low blood-glucose concentration.

Cortisol functions to increase blood sugar through gluconeogenesis, suppress the immune system, and aid in the metabolism of calories, notably fat, protein, and carbohydrate metabolism. It also decreases bone formation. Many of these functions are carried out by cortisol binding to glucocorticoid or mineralocorticoid receptors.

Production and regulation

Cortisol production is controlled by the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal (HPA) axis. The hypothalamus releases corticotropin-releasing hormone, which stimulates the anterior pituitary gland to secrete adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH); ACTH in turn stimulates cortisol synthesis and release from the adrenal cortex. Cortisol exerts negative feedback on both the hypothalamus and pituitary. Secretion follows a circadian rhythm, peaking in the early morning and reaching its lowest levels around midnight.

Functions

Cortisol stimulates hepatic gluconeogenesis and glycogen synthesis, promotes protein catabolism in muscle and fat breakdown in adipose tissue, and modulates immune responses by suppressing inflammation. It also helps maintain blood pressure by increasing the sensitivity of the vasculature to catecholamines.

Disorders

Chronic excess of cortisol causes Cushing's syndrome, characterized by central obesity, hypertension, hyperglycemia, and muscle weakness. Deficiency of cortisol occurs in adrenal insufficiency (Addison's disease), in which inadequate production by the adrenal glands can lead to fatigue, hypotension, and life-threatening adrenal crisis.

Biosynthesis

Cortisol is synthesized from cholesterol in the adrenal cortex through the process of steroidogenesis, via intermediates including pregnenolone, progesterone, and 11-deoxycortisol.
