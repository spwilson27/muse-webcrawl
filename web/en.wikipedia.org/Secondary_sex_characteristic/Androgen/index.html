Androgen


Testosterone, the major androgen in males

An androgen (from Greek andr-, the stem of the word meaning "man") is any natural or synthetic steroid hormone that regulates the development and maintenance of male characteristics in vertebrates by binding to androgen receptors. This includes the embryonic development of the primary male sex organs, and the development of male secondary sex characteristics at puberty. Androgens are synthesized in the testes, the ovaries, and the adrenal glands.

Androgens increase in both males and females during puberty. The major androgen in males is testosterone. Dihydrotestosterone (DHT) and androstenedione are of equal importance in male development. In utero, DHT causes the development of the male penis and scrotum.

Types

The principal androgens are testosterone, dihydrotestosterone (DHT), androstenedione, and dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA). Testosterone is produced primarily by the Leydig cells of the testes in males and by the ovaries in females, with smaller amounts secreted by the adrenal glands in both sexes.

Biological function

During mammalian development, androgens are essential for sexual differentiation of the male fetus. At puberty, androgens drive the development of secondary sex characteristics, including increased muscle and bone mass, growth of body hair, changes to the vocal cords, and increased activity of the sebaceous glands. In adult males, androgens maintain libido, spermatogenesis, muscle mass, and bone density.

Androgens in females

Androgens also play a role in female physiology, serving as precursors for estrogen synthesis and contributing to libido, bone health, and muscle mass. Excess androgen production in women, as in polycystic ovary syndrome, can cause hirsutism, acne, and menstrual irregularities.

Insensitivity

Androgen insensitivity syndrome is a condition in which a genetic male (46,XY) is partially or completely unable to respond to androgens because of mutations in the androgen receptor gene. Complete androgen insensitivity results in a female external phenotype despite male gonads.

Medical use

Androgens are used clinically in testosterone replacement therapy for men with hypogonadism, and anabolic steroids derived from androgens are used for a variety of medical and non-medical purposes.
