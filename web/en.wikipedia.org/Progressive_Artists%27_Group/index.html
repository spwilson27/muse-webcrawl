Progressive Artists' Group

Founded1947, Bombay, India
FoundersF. N. Souza, S. H. Raza, M. F. Husain, K. H. Ara, H. A. Gade, S. K. Bakre
MovementModernism
Dissolved1956

The Progressive Artists' Group (PAG) was a group of modern artists based in Bombay, formed in 1947 — the year of Indian independence — by Francis Newton Souza and colleagues including S. H. Raza, M. F. Husain, K. H. Ara, H. A. Gade and sculptor S. K. Bakre. It is regarded as the founding movement of Indian modern art.

Aims

The group rejected the nationalist revivalism of the Bengal School and sought an international, avant-garde visual language that drew on European modernism while responding to Indian subjects and experience. Its manifesto declared the group would paint with "absolute freedom" for content and technique.

History and legacy

Later associates included V. S. Gaitonde, Krishen Khanna, Ram Kumar and Akbar Padamsee. The group effectively dissolved by 1956 as members dispersed to London and Paris, but its members became the dominant figures of post-independence Indian art, and their works now command record prices.
