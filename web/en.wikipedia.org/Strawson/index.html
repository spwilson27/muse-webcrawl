Strawson

Strawson is a surname. Notable people with the name include the British philosophers P. F. Strawson and his son Galen Strawson, who have both written extensively on metaphysics, the philosophy of language, and free will.

People

  • P. F. Strawson (1919–2006), Sir Peter Frederick Strawson, Waynflete Professor of Metaphysical Philosophy at the University of Oxford. His 1950 paper "On Referring" criticised Bertrand Russell's theory of descriptions; his book Individuals (1959) revived descriptive metaphysics; The Bounds of Sense (1966) is a major study of Kant; and "Freedom and Resentment" (1962) reshaped debate on moral responsibility by emphasising the reactive attitudes.
  • Galen Strawson (born 1952), philosopher of mind and metaphysics, son of P. F. Strawson, known for his "Basic Argument" against ultimate moral responsibility and for work on consciousness and panpsychism.

Concepts associated with the name

Strawsonian descriptive metaphysics refers to P. F. Strawson's project of describing the actual structure of human thought about the world, in contrast to revisionary metaphysics. Strawson's argument in metaethics holds that practices of holding one another responsible are grounded in human reactive attitudes — resentment, indignation, gratitude — that are neither in need of, nor susceptible to, external justification by the truth or falsity of determinism.
