Chichu Art Museum

Chichu Art Museum
Location: Naoshima, Kagawa, Japan
Opened: 18 July 2004
Architect: Tadao Ando
Collection: Monet, James Turrell, Walter De Maria

The Chichu Art Museum (Japanese: 地中美術館, "art museum in the earth") is a museum on Naoshima island in Japan's Seto Inland Sea, designed by architect Tadao Ando and opened in 2004. Built almost entirely underground to avoid disturbing the landscape, it houses permanent installations by just three artists: Claude Monet, James Turrell, and Walter De Maria.

Architecture

Ando buried the building beneath a terraced hillside overlooking the sea. Galleries are lit only by natural light admitted through courtyards and slots in the concrete shell, so the appearance of the works changes with the weather, season, and time of day. Visitors move through a sequence of geometric voids — triangular and rectangular open-air courts — between the buried rooms.

Collection

The Monet room displays five paintings from the late Water Lilies series in a space finished with white plaster and marble chip flooring, entered through slit-like doors and viewed in soft top light. Turrell contributes three works using light as medium, including an "open sky" room. De Maria's Time/Timeless/No Time (2004) fills a vast hall with a granite sphere and gilded wooden sculptures aligned to a skylight. The museum is part of the Benesse Art Site Naoshima project, which has turned several Inland Sea islands into destinations for contemporary art, complemented by the Setouchi Triennale festival.
