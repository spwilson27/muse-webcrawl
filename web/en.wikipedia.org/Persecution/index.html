Persecution

Persecution is the systematic mistreatment of an individual or group by another individual or group. The most common forms are religious persecution, racism and political persecution, though there is naturally some overlap between these terms. Persecution is a human rights violation and, when directed against a group, may constitute a crime against humanity.

Forms

Religious persecution is the systematic mistreatment of individuals because of their religious beliefs, from the Roman persecutions of Christians and the Spanish Inquisition to modern discrimination against religious minorities. Political persecution targets people for their opinions or affiliations, as under McCarthyism or in totalitarian states. Ethnic persecution includes pogroms, forced displacement and genocide.

International law

The 1951 Refugee Convention protects people with a well-founded fear of persecution on grounds of race, religion, nationality, membership of a particular social group or political opinion. The Rome Statute defines persecution against an identifiable group as a crime against humanity when committed as part of a widespread or systematic attack.
