Grant Park (Chicago)

LocationDowntown Chicago, Illinois, U.S.
Area319 acres (129 ha)
Operated byChicago Park District

Grant Park is a large urban park (319 acres or 129 hectares) in the Loop community area of Chicago, Illinois. Located within the city's central business district, the park is known as "Chicago's front yard". Its features include Millennium Park, Buckingham Fountain, the Art Institute of Chicago and the Museum Campus.

History

The park's origins lie in the 1830s, when land east of Michigan Avenue was set aside as public ground. After the Great Chicago Fire of 1871, debris was dumped into Lake Michigan, extending the shoreline and creating the land that became the park. In 1901 it was renamed for Ulysses S. Grant, the Civil War general and 18th President of the United States. Litigation led by Montgomery Ward, fought over decades, preserved the park as "forever open, clear and free" of buildings, a principle rooted in the city's original plats.

Features and events

Grant Park hosts many of Chicago's largest festivals, including Taste of Chicago, the Chicago Blues Festival, the Chicago Jazz Festival and Lollapalooza. Buckingham Fountain, one of the largest fountains in the world, was dedicated in 1927. The park contains the Petrillo Music Shell, Daley Bicentennial Plaza, gardens, sports fields and the Chicago Lakefront Trail.

On November 4, 2008, Barack Obama delivered his presidential election victory speech before a crowd of roughly 240,000 people in the park. Millennium Park, opened in 2004 at the park's northwest corner, houses the Jay Pritzker Pavilion, Cloud Gate ("The Bean") and Crown Fountain.
