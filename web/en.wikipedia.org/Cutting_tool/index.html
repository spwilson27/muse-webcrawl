Cutting tool

In the context of machining, a cutting tool or cutter is typically a hardened metal tool used to remove material from a workpiece by means of shear deformation. Cutting tools are the consumable implements mounted in machine tools such as lathes, milling machines, and drills.

Types

Single-point tools, used in turning and boring, cut with one edge; multi-point tools include drills, end mills, reamers, and broaches. Common tool geometries include inserts — replaceable carbide tips clamped or brazed to a tool holder — which dominate modern production machining.

Materials

Tool materials must be harder than the workpiece and retain hardness at high temperature. They include high-speed steel, cemented carbides, ceramics, cubic boron nitride, and diamond. Coatings such as titanium nitride extend tool life by reducing friction and wear.

Wear

Tools degrade through flank wear, crater wear, chipping, and built-up edge, and are replaced or resharpened when wear begins to spoil surface finish or dimensional accuracy. Coolants and cutting fluids reduce heat and prolong tool life.
