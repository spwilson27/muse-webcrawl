Josef Jungmann (July 16, 1773 – November 14, 1847) was a Czech linguist, lexicographer and a central figure of the Czech National Revival. His life's work was to demonstrate that Czech could serve as a full language of scholarship, literature and public life at a time when German dominated Bohemia's institutions.

Life and work

Jungmann taught at the gymnasium in Litoměřice and then at the Old Town Academic Gymnasium in Prague, where his pupils included several future revivalists. He translated works by Milton (Paradise Lost), Goethe, Schiller and Chateaubriand into Czech, deliberately enriching the language's vocabulary, and collected words from dialects, older Czech literature and other Slavic languages, coining terms where needed.

Dictionary and legacy

His monumental five-volume Slovník česko-německý (Czech–German Dictionary, published 1834–1839) contains some 120,000 entries and remains the foundation document of modern standard Czech. Jungmann Square and a statue in Prague commemorate him; his programme — that a nation is defined by its language — shaped Czech cultural life for a century.
