Fifteenth Amendment to the United States Constitution

The Fifteenth Amendment (Amendment XV), adopted on February 3, 1870, prohibits the federal and state governments from denying a citizen the right to vote on account of race, color, or previous condition of servitude.

It was the third and last of the Reconstruction Amendments. Despite its promise, discriminatory practices including poll taxes, literacy tests and grandfather clauses disenfranchised many Black voters in the South until the Voting Rights Act of 1965 gave it effective enforcement.
