Salome (opera)

Salome, Op. 54, is an opera in one act by Richard Strauss, first performed at the Semperoper in Dresden on 9 December 1905. The German libretto, by the composer, is based on Hedwig Lachmann's translation of Oscar Wilde's French play Salomé (1891), itself derived from the biblical account of Salome and the beheading of John the Baptist.

ComposerRichard Strauss
LibrettistRichard Strauss, after Oscar Wilde
LanguageGerman
Premiere9 December 1905, Semperoper, Dresden

Synopsis

At the court of Herod Antipas in Galilee, the princess Salome becomes fascinated by the imprisoned prophet Jochanaan (John the Baptist), who curses her mother Herodias and rejects Salome's advances. Herod, who lusts after his stepdaughter, promises her anything if she will dance for him. She performs the "Dance of the Seven Veils" and demands as her reward the head of Jochanaan on a silver platter. Horrified but bound by his oath, Herod complies. Salome sings a rapturous final monologue to the severed head and kisses its lips, whereupon the disgusted Herod orders his soldiers to kill her.

Music and reception

Strauss's score, written for a very large orchestra, is famous for its harmonic daring, including passages of bitonality and extreme dissonance, and for set pieces such as the Dance of the Seven Veils and Salome's closing scene. The opera's subject matter caused scandal: early productions were censored or banned in Vienna, London and New York, but the work quickly became an international success and remains a cornerstone of the modern repertoire, though it makes exceptional demands on the soprano in the title role.
