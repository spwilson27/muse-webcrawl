Repeat offender

A repeat offender (or recidivist) is a person who reoffends after having previously been convicted of a crime. Criminal justice systems commonly impose harsher penalties on repeat offenders, on the grounds of deterrence, incapacitation, and public protection.

Habitual offender laws

Many jurisdictions have habitual-offender statutes. In the United States, "three-strikes laws" adopted in the 1990s mandate long sentences — up to life imprisonment — for a third felony conviction. England and Wales provide for imprisonment for public protection and extended determinate sentences for dangerous offenders, while several legal systems historically used special measures such as preventive detention.

Recidivism research

Rates of reoffending vary widely by country and offence type. Studies examine the effects of imprisonment, rehabilitation programmes, and social support on recidivism, which remains a central topic in criminology.
