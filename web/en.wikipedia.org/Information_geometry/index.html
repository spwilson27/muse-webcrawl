Information geometry

Information geometry is an interdisciplinary field that applies the techniques of differential geometry to the study of probability theory and statistics. It studies families of probability distributions as differentiable manifolds, in which each point is a distribution and the geometry of the manifold reflects notions of statistical distinguishability.

The Fisher information metric

The central object is the Fisher information metric, which equips a statistical manifold with a Riemannian metric defined from the Fisher information matrix. C. R. Rao showed in 1945 that this metric can be interpreted as a measure of the amount of information a random variable carries about a parameter, and it underlies the Cramér–Rao bound. A theorem of Čencov (Chentsov) states that the Fisher metric is, up to scale, the unique Riemannian metric on a statistical manifold that is invariant under congruent Markov morphisms — that is, under sufficient statistics.

Dual connections

Shun-ichi Amari, a founder of the modern field, introduced a one-parameter family of affine connections (the α-connections) and pairs of dual connections (such as the exponential and mixture connections) that give the manifold a dually flat structure for exponential families. Duality leads to generalised Pythagorean theorems for divergences and to geometric descriptions of estimation, projection and statistical inference.

Divergences

Instead of a metric alone, information geometry frequently uses divergences — asymmetric measures of difference between distributions, such as the Kullback–Leibler divergence — which induce both a metric (at second order) and a pair of dual connections (at third order). Bregman divergences and f-divergences are important special cases.

Applications

Information geometry is applied in statistical inference, machine learning (including natural gradient descent, which descends along the Fisher metric rather than the Euclidean one), neural networks, signal processing, time series analysis, quantum information (where the Bures metric generalises the Fisher metric) and mathematical finance. Information geometry interacts with maximum entropy methods and the geometry of exponential families, both foundational in statistical physics and machine learning models such as Boltzmann machines.
