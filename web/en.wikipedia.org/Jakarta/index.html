Jakarta

Jakarta, officially the Special Capital Region of Jakarta, is the capital and largest city of Indonesia. Located on the northwest coast of Java, it is the country's economic, cultural and political centre, with a population of over 10 million in the city proper and more than 30 million in the metropolitan area.

History

The area was known as Sunda Kelapa, a trading port of the Sunda Kingdom. In 1527 Fatahillah renamed it Jayakarta. The Dutch founded Batavia in 1619, which became capital of the Dutch East Indies. After Indonesian independence in 1945 it was renamed Jakarta. In 2019 Indonesia announced plans to move the capital to Nusantara in East Kalimantan, though Jakarta remains the seat of many functions.

Geography

Jakarta sits on low, flat alluvial plains on the Bay of Jakarta, crossed by 13 rivers including the Ciliwung. Northern parts are subsiding and prone to flooding. See Indonesia and Java.

Economy and culture

Jakarta hosts the headquarters of major Indonesian companies, the ASEAN Secretariat, and landmarks such as the National Monument (Monas), Istiqlal Mosque, and Kota Tua (Old Town).

Transport

The city is served by Soekarno–Hatta International Airport, TransJakarta bus rapid transit, commuter rail, MRT and LRT lines.
