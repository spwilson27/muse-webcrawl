Cable car

A cable car is a type of railway or tramway in which cars are hauled by a continuously moving cable running at constant speed, rather than by an onboard engine. The term covers street cable systems, funiculars and aerial cable cars (gondola lifts), though these differ in design.

Street cable cars

The most famous surviving system is the San Francisco cable car system, opened in 1873 by Andrew Smith Hallidie, in which cars grip an endless steel cable running in a slot beneath the street and hauled by a central powerhouse. Cable traction spread to cities worldwide in the 1880s and 1890s before being displaced by electric trams; other surviving examples include the Wellington Cable Car in New Zealand.

Aerial cable cars

Aerial systems suspend cabins from overhead cables and are used for mountain access and increasingly for urban transport, as in Medellín and La Paz. Funiculars, by contrast, use a cable to link two counterbalanced cars on a steep slope.
