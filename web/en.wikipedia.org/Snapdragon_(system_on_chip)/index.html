Snapdragon (system on chip)

Snapdragon is a suite of system-on-a-chip (SoC) semiconductor products designed and marketed by Qualcomm for mobile devices. The Snapdragon's central processing unit (CPU) uses the ARM architecture; the platform also integrates Adreno graphics processing units, Hexagon digital signal processors, and cellular modems. Snapdragon chips power a large share of Android smartphones as well as tablets, wearables, automotive systems, and Windows laptops.

History

The first Snapdragon product, the QSD8250, was launched in 2007 and reached devices such as the HTC HD2 and the Google Nexus One. Qualcomm introduced its custom Krait CPU cores in 2012 and the 64-bit Kryo line in 2015. In 2021 the flagship line was renamed from the Snapdragon 800 series to the Snapdragon 8 Gen series; the Snapdragon 8 Gen 1 through Gen 3 emphasize on-device AI acceleration. In 2023–2024 Qualcomm extended the brand to PCs with the Snapdragon X Elite, featuring custom Oryon CPU cores.

Series

Snapdragon mobile platforms are tiered by performance: the 8 series for flagship phones, 7 series for upper midrange, 6 series for midrange, and 4 and 2 series for entry-level devices. Related families include Snapdragon Wear for watches, Snapdragon Ride for vehicles, and Snapdragon XR for virtual and augmented reality headsets.
