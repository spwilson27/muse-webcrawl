National Instant Criminal Background Check System

The National Instant Criminal Background Check System (NICS) is a background check system in the United States used by licensed firearms dealers to determine whether a prospective buyer is eligible to purchase firearms. It was mandated by the Brady Handgun Violence Prevention Act of 1993 and launched by the FBI on 30 November 1998.

How it works

Before transferring a firearm, a Federal Firearms Licensee contacts NICS (directly or via a state point of contact), which searches three FBI-maintained databases: the National Crime Information Center, the Interstate Identification Index and the NICS Index of prohibited persons. Most checks are resolved within minutes with a "proceed", "denied" or "delayed" response; if the FBI does not respond within three business days, federal law permits the dealer to proceed with the sale (the so-called "default proceed").

Scope and criticism

The system covers sales by licensed dealers but generally not private transfers between individuals, a gap critics call the "private sale loophole". The Fix NICS Act of 2018 sought to improve the completeness of records submitted by federal agencies and states.
