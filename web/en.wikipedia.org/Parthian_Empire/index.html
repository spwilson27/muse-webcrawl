Parthian Empire

The Parthian Empire (247 BC – AD 224), also known as the Arsacid Empire, was a major Iranian political and cultural power in ancient Iran. At its height it stretched from the northern reaches of the Euphrates in what is now central-eastern Turkey to present-day Afghanistan and western Pakistan, controlling key stretches of the Silk Road.

History

The empire was founded by Arsaces I, leader of the Parni tribe, who conquered the region of Parthia in north-eastern Iran, then under Seleucid rule. Under Mithridates I (r. 171–132 BC) the Parthians expanded into Media, Mesopotamia and Persis. Mithridates II (r. 124–88 BC) extended control to Armenia and established diplomatic contact with Han China and with Rome.

Wars with Rome

Parthia and Rome fought repeatedly for influence over Armenia and Mesopotamia. At the Battle of Carrhae in 53 BC, a Parthian army under Surena destroyed the army of Marcus Licinius Crassus, largely through horse archers and armoured cataphracts. The "Parthian shot" — firing backwards while retreating on horseback — became proverbial. Later emperors including Trajan and Septimius Severus sacked the Parthian capital Ctesiphon, but neither empire could permanently displace the other.

Fall

Weakened by civil wars and Roman invasions, the empire fell in AD 224 when Ardashir I, a vassal king of Persis, defeated Artabanus IV and founded the Sasanian Empire.
