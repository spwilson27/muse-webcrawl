Convention of Royal Burghs

The Convention of Royal Burghs was an organization representing the royal burghs of Scotland, formally constituted in the 16th century and meeting regularly from 1552 until its abolition in 1975. Its origins are traced to the medieval Curia Quattuor Burgorum (Court of Four Burghs) — originally Berwick, Roxburgh, Edinburgh and Stirling — which settled appeals and trade matters between burghs.

Functions

Commissioners from each royal burgh met annually to apportion the burghs' share of national taxation, regulate trade, standardize weights and measures, promote Scottish commercial interests abroad (including the Scottish staple port at Veere in the Netherlands), and lobby the Parliament of Scotland and later the Parliament of the United Kingdom. The Convention also acted in matters of burgh law and municipal administration, and was a recognized consultative body on local government legislation.

Abolition and legacy

With the reorganization of Scottish local government in 1975, which abolished the burghs as administrative units, the Convention was dissolved and much of its role passed to the Convention of Scottish Local Authorities (COSLA), founded in the same year to represent Scotland's councils. The ceremonial and historical title of royal burgh survives in many Scottish towns.
