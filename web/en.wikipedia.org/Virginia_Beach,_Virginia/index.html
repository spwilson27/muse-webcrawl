Virginia Beach, Virginia

Virginia Beach is an independent city located on the southeastern coast of Virginia, United States. With a population of about 459,000, it is the most populous city in the state and the 43rd-most populous city in the nation. Located at the mouth of the Chesapeake Bay on the Atlantic Ocean, the city is best known for its long stretch of public beaches, a three-mile concrete boardwalk, and the resort area along the oceanfront.

Geography

The city covers about 500 square miles (1,290 km²), of which roughly 249 square miles is land, making it the largest city in Virginia by land area. The terrain is flat, with an average elevation of about 12 feet above sea level. The Chesapeake Bay forms the northern border, and the Atlantic Ocean lies to the east. The 17-mile-long Chesapeake Bay Bridge–Tunnel, one of only a small number of bridge-tunnel systems in the world, connects the city to the Eastern Shore of Virginia.

History

The area was first explored by English colonists in April 1607, when the expedition of Captain Christopher Newport landed at Cape Henry before proceeding up the James River to establish Jamestown. The modern city of Virginia Beach was formed on January 1, 1963, when the small resort city of Virginia Beach merged with Princess Anne County. The city has long been a popular vacation destination; its resort strip grew rapidly in the late 19th and early 20th centuries with the arrival of rail service and the construction of large hotels.

Economy and military

Tourism is a major industry, with millions of visitors annually. The city is also home to several military bases, including Naval Air Station Oceana, one of the largest naval air stations in the world, and Joint Expeditionary Base Little Creek–Fort Story. Agriculture is significant in the southern, more rural part of the city, which produces soybeans, corn, and wheat.

Tourism

The Virginia Aquarium & Marine Science Center is one of the most visited aquariums in the United States. First Landing State Park, the most visited state park in Virginia, commemorates the 1607 landing. Mount Trashmore Park, built on a converted landfill, is a well-known example of landfill reuse. The Neptune Festival is held each fall.
