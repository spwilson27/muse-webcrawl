Joanot Martorell

Joanot Martorell (c. 1410 – 1465) was a Valencian knight and writer, author of Tirant lo Blanch (Tirant the White), one of the most celebrated chivalric romances in European literature and a landmark of Catalan letters.


Tirant lo Blanch, printed 1490

Life

Martorell was born into the Valencian nobility. His life was that of a typical 15th-century knight: he travelled to England, took part in tournaments, and was involved in lawsuits and disputes, including a famous affair of honour pursued through chivalric letters. He began Tirant lo Blanch on 2 January 1460 but died leaving it unfinished; his friend Martí Joan de Galba completed the final portion.

Tirant lo Blanch

The novel follows the Breton knight Tirant, who fights in England, Rhodes and North Africa and defends Constantinople against the Ottoman Turks, dying before his marriage to the princess Carmesina. Unlike earlier romances, it mixes realism, humour, military detail and eroticism. Miguel de Cervantes praised it in Don Quixote as "the best book in the world" of its kind, and it influenced the modern novel; Mario Vargas Llosa championed it as a total novel.

Legacy

The work was first printed in Valencia in 1490. Martorell is regarded as the outstanding prose writer of the Catalan Golden Age (Segle d'Or).
