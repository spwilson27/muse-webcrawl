The Arabian Peninsula (Arabic: شبه جزيرة العرب), or Arabia, is a peninsula in West Asia, situated northeast of Africa on the Arabian Plate. It is the largest peninsula in the world, at 3,237,500 km² (1,250,000 sq mi). The peninsula consists of the countries of Saudi Arabia, Yemen, Oman, the United Arab Emirates, Qatar, Bahrain and Kuwait, as well as parts of southern Iraq and Jordan.

Geography

The peninsula is bounded by the Red Sea to the west and southwest, the Gulf of Aqaba and the Gulf of Suez, the Gulf of Aden and the Arabian Sea to the south and southeast, and the Persian Gulf and Gulf of Oman to the northeast. Much of the interior is desert, including the Rub' al Khali (Empty Quarter), the largest continuous sand desert in the world. The climate is predominantly hot and arid, with more moderate temperatures in the highlands of Yemen and Oman. The southwestern corner receives monsoon rains and supports terraced agriculture.

History

Arabia was home to ancient kingdoms such as Saba (Sheba) in the south, which prospered from the incense trade, and the Nabataeans, whose capital was Petra. In the 7th century CE the peninsula became the birthplace of Islam; the cities of Mecca and Medina remain its holiest sites. In the 20th century the discovery of vast petroleum reserves transformed the region's economies and global importance.
