Land registration

Land registration is any of various systems by which matters concerning ownership, possession, or other rights in land are recorded (usually with a government agency or department) to provide evidence of title, facilitate transactions, and prevent unlawful disposal. The information recorded and the protection provided vary by jurisdiction.

Systems

Two broad approaches exist. In a deeds registration system, the documents themselves (deeds, mortgages, liens) are recorded, and title must be traced through the chain of recorded documents. In a title registration system, most famously the Torrens title system developed in South Australia in 1858, the register itself is the authoritative record of title, and the state typically guarantees its accuracy.

Cadastre

Registration usually works together with a cadastre — an official register of the boundaries, extent, and value of land parcels, often maintained for taxation. Modern systems link textual registers with digital parcel maps.

By jurisdiction

In England and Wales, HM Land Registry maintains the register of title for registered land, registration having become compulsory on most transactions progressively since 1862. In the United States, recording is done at county level under state recording acts (race, notice, and race-notice statutes), and title insurance is commonly purchased to protect against defects. Germany's Grundbuch and similar civil-law registers give the register strong evidentiary force.
