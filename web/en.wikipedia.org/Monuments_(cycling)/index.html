Monuments (cycling)

The Monuments are five classic one-day professional cycling races generally considered to be the oldest, hardest, and most prestigious events in men's road cycling, below only the Grand Tours in stature. Each has a long history — the oldest dates to 1892 — a very long distance (generally over 240 km), and a distinctive character that every rider covets. All five are part of the UCI World Tour.

The five Monuments

RaceCountryFirst heldNickname / character
Milan–San RemoItaly1907"La Primavera"; the longest at ~290 km, a sprinters' classic
Tour of Flanders (Ronde van Vlaanderen)Belgium1913Short, steep cobbled climbs (bergs) such as the Oude Kwaremont and Paterberg
Paris–RoubaixFrance1896"The Hell of the North"; brutal cobbled farm tracks (pavé)
Liège–Bastogne–LiègeBelgium1892"La Doyenne" (The Old Lady); hilly Ardennes course
Giro di Lombardia (Il Lombardia)Italy1905"The Race of the Falling Leaves"; autumn climbers' classic

The season unfolds in sequence: Milan–San Remo in March, Flanders and Roubaix in April, Liège–Bastogne–Liège in late April, and Lombardia in October.

The cobbled sectors of Paris–Roubaix are among the most feared in cycling.

Records

Only three riders have won all five Monuments: Belgians Roger De Vlaeminck, Rik Van Looy, and Eddy Merckx. Merckx holds the record for Monument victories with 19. Tadej Pogačar and Mathieu van der Poel are the leading riders of the modern era, with multiple Monument wins in the 2020s.
