Harz

The Harz is a highland area in northern Germany. It has the highest elevations for that region and its rugged terrain extends across parts of Lower Saxony, Saxony-Anhalt, and Thuringia. The name Harz derives from the Middle High German word Hardt or Hart (hill forest). The Brocken is the highest summit in the Harz with a height of 1,141.1 metres (3,744 ft) above sea level. The Wurmberg (971 metres, 3,186 ft) is the highest peak located entirely within Lower Saxony.

Geography

The range stretches about 110 kilometres from northwest to southeast and is up to 35 kilometres wide. It is divided into the Upper Harz (Oberharz) in the northwest, with higher, forested plateaus and deep valleys, and the Lower Harz (Unterharz) in the southeast, a gentler, partly agricultural landscape. The Harz National Park covers much of the western highlands.

History

The Harz was historically one of Europe's most important mining regions, producing silver, lead, copper, and other metals for over a thousand years. The mining town of Goslar and the Rammelsberg mines are UNESCO World Heritage Sites. In folklore the Harz is strongly associated with witches and the Walpurgis Night celebrations on the Brocken, an image popularised by Goethe's Faust. During the Cold War the Inner German border ran through the mountains.

Tourism

Today the Harz is a popular destination for hiking, skiing, and heritage tourism, served by the narrow-gauge Harz mountain railways, including the Brocken Railway.
