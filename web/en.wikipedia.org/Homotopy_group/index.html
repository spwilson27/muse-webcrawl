Homotopy group

In mathematics, homotopy groups are used in algebraic topology to classify topological spaces. The first and simplest homotopy group is the fundamental group, denoted π1, which records information about loops in a space. Intuitively, homotopy groups record information about the basic shape, or holes, of a topological space.

The n-th homotopy group πn(X) of a pointed space X is the set of homotopy classes of maps from the n-sphere to X that preserve basepoints, given a group structure by a "pinching" construction. For n ≥ 2 these groups are always abelian, while π1 may be non-abelian. Homotopy groups are invariants of homotopy type: homotopy equivalent spaces have isomorphic homotopy groups.

Computation

Unlike homology groups, homotopy groups are generally very hard to compute; even the homotopy groups of spheres were a central open problem for decades and remain only partially known. Tools for computation include long exact sequences of fibrations, the Hurewicz theorem relating the first nontrivial homotopy group to homology, spectral sequences such as the Serre and Adams spectral sequences, and Whitehead's theorem, which states that a map between CW complexes inducing isomorphisms on all homotopy groups is a homotopy equivalence.
