Dynamics (music)

In music, dynamics refers to the variation in loudness between notes or phrases. Dynamics are indicated by specific musical notation, often in some detail, but their interpretation depends on the musical context, the instrument, and performance practice. A pianist, for example, cannot easily fade the volume of a single held note, while a violinist or singer can shape it continuously.

Markings

The principal dynamic levels, from quietest to loudest, are abbreviated from Italian:

  • pp — pianissimo, very soft
  • p — piano, soft
  • mp — mezzo piano, moderately soft
  • mf — mezzo forte, moderately loud
  • f — forte, loud
  • ff — fortissimo, very loud

Gradual changes are shown with crescendo (getting louder) and diminuendo or decrescendo (getting softer) hairpins, or with words such as cresc. Sudden changes may use subito forzando (sfz) or a fortepiano (fp) attack.

History

Explicit dynamic marks were rare before the 18th century, since much earlier music relied on conventions of instrumentation and registration. The rise of the piano — originally the "fortepiano", named for its ability to play soft and loud — and the growing orchestra of the Classical and Romantic eras made graded dynamics central to musical expression, culminating in the extreme ranges demanded by composers such as Mahler and Tchaikovsky (whose Sixth Symphony opens at pppppp).
