National Institutes of Health

The National Institutes of Health (NIH) is the primary agency of the United States government responsible for biomedical and public health research. Part of the Department of Health and Human Services, it is the world's largest public funder of biomedical research, with a budget of roughly $47 billion in the mid-2020s.

Founded1887 (as Hygienic Laboratory)
HeadquartersBethesda, Maryland
Structure27 institutes and centers

Organization

NIH comprises 27 institutes and centers, including the National Cancer Institute (the oldest, founded 1937), the National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases, and the National Library of Medicine. About 80 percent of its budget funds extramural research at universities through competitive grants; an intramural program conducts research on the Bethesda campus, including the NIH Clinical Center hospital.

History and achievements

NIH-supported research contributed to vaccines, the understanding of cholesterol and heart disease, cancer therapies, the Human Genome Project (with the Department of Energy), and rapid COVID-19 vaccine development. Francis Collins served as director from 2009 to 2021, one of the longest tenures.
