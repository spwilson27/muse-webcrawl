Shashlik

Shashlik grilling on a mangal.

Shashlik (Russian: шашлык) is a dish of skewered and grilled cubes of meat, popular across Eastern Europe, the Caucasus, Central Asia, and Russia. The name derives from the Crimean Tatar şışlıq, from şış, "spit" or "skewer". Shashlik is similar to shish kebab but is distinguished by marinating the meat—traditionally lamb, but also pork, beef, or chicken—in an acidic marinade such as vinegar, wine, kefir, or onion juice before grilling.

The skewers (shampury) are usually cooked over a long, narrow charcoal brazier called a mangal, without a grill grate. Chunks of fat or vegetables—onions, peppers, tomatoes—may alternate with the meat on the skewer. Shashlik is typically served with flatbread, marinated onions, herbs, and sauces, and eating it outdoors is a strongly social ritual in much of the former Soviet Union.

History

Grilled skewered meat is ancient across the steppe cultures of Eurasia; shashlik in its Crimean Tatar and Caucasian forms spread through the Russian Empire in the 19th century and became a fixture of Soviet food culture, celebrated in literature and film. Variations exist under different names across the region, such as Georgian mtsvadi, Armenian khorovats, and Central Asian shashlik with distinctive spice blends.
