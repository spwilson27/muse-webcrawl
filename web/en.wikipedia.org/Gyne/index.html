Gyne

In entomology, a gyne is a female member of an insect colony specialised for reproduction — in ordinary language, a queen. The term is used particularly for ants, bees, wasps and termites, where reproductive females differ in form, behaviour and longevity from the sterile worker caste.

Use of the term

"Gyne" was proposed as a precise alternative to "queen", which is an anthropomorphic label. An unmated, uninseminated female with developed reproductive potential may be called a gynomorph or, in ants, an alate virgin female destined to found a new colony after a nuptial flight; once mated and established as the reproductive of a colony, the same individual functions as the queen.

Related terms

Colonies with a single reproductive female are monogynous, while those with several are polygynous. In some species, worker-like reproductives called ergatoid gynes occur, lacking wings entirely.
