Simien Mountains National Park

Simien Mountains National Park is a national park in the Amhara Region of northern Ethiopia. Covering the highest parts of the Simien Mountains, including Ras Dashen, the highest peak in Ethiopia, it is famed for dramatic escarpments formed by ancient lava flows and for endemic wildlife such as the gelada, the walia ibex, and the Ethiopian wolf. It was among the first sites inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List, in 1978.

Geography

The park covers a rugged plateau cut by deep valleys and sheer cliffs dropping more than a kilometre. Millions of years of erosion of flood basalt created jagged peaks and precipices; Afro-alpine moorlands lie above 3,600 metres.

Wildlife

The park protects the endangered walia ibex, large troops of gelada baboons that graze the grasslands, and a small population of Ethiopian wolves. It was placed on the List of World Heritage in Danger in 1996 because of declining walia ibex numbers and human encroachment, and removed in 2017 after conservation improvements.
