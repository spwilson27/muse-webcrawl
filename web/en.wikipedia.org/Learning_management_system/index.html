Learning management system

A learning management system (LMS) is a software application for the administration, documentation, tracking, reporting, automation, and delivery of educational courses, training programmes, materials, or learning and development programmes. It emerged from e-learning and is used by schools, universities, corporations and government agencies to organise online and blended learning.

Features

Typical LMS features include course and catalogue management, enrolment and registration, content delivery (often compatible with standards such as SCORM and xAPI), quizzes and assessments, gradebooks, discussion forums, messaging, progress tracking, and reporting dashboards. Many systems support gamification, mobile apps, virtual classrooms via video conferencing integrations, and accessibility standards.

History

Computer-based training systems date back to PLATO in the 1960s. The term "learning management system" became widespread in the late 1990s as web-based platforms such as WebCT and Blackboard were adopted by universities. Open-source systems, notably Moodle (released in 2002), and later cloud-based products such as Canvas and Google Classroom, broadened adoption, and use expanded sharply during the COVID-19 pandemic's shift to remote learning.

LMS versus related systems

An LMS focuses on delivering and managing learning for enrolled learners, whereas a learning content management system (LCMS) emphasises authoring and reusing content, and a student information system manages institutional records. Modern platforms increasingly combine these functions and incorporate learning analytics.
