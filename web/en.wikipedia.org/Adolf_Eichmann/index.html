Adolf Eichmann

Otto Adolf Eichmann (1906-1962) was a German Nazi SS-Obersturmbannfuhrer and a major organiser of the Holocaust. Heading the Reich Security Main Office section for Jewish affairs, he coordinated the deportation of millions of Jews to extermination camps.

Role in the Holocaust

Eichmann participated in the Wannsee Conference of January 1942 and managed the logistics of deportations across occupied Europe, notably the deportation of Hungarian Jews in 1944.

Escape and capture

After the war he escaped to Argentina, where Israeli Mossad agents captured him in 1960 and brought him to Israel. His trial in Jerusalem in 1961 drew worldwide attention.

Execution

Convicted on charges including crimes against humanity, he was hanged on 1 June 1962. Hannah Arendt's account of the trial popularised the phrase "the banality of evil". See also The Holocaust and Wannsee Conference.
