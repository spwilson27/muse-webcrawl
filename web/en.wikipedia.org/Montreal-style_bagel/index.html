Montreal-style bagel

The Montreal-style bagel is a distinctive variety of bagel from Montreal, Quebec. Compared with the New York-style bagel, it is smaller, thinner, sweeter and denser, with a larger hole. It is always hand-rolled, boiled in honey-sweetened water, and baked in a wood-fired oven, typically coated with sesame or poppy seeds.

Sesame bagels, the most popular Montreal variety.

Method

The dough, which contains egg and honey or malt, is rolled by hand into strips, joined into rings, briefly boiled in honey water, and then baked on long wooden planks in a wood-burning oven. The result is a golden, slightly crisp crust and a chewy, subtly sweet interior.

History

Bagel making was brought to Montreal by Jewish immigrants from Eastern Europe in the early 20th century. The city's two most famous bagel bakeries, St-Viateur Bagel (opened 1957) and Fairmount Bagel (opened 1919, and credited as the city's first bagel bakery), both in the Mile End neighbourhood, remain institutions, producing bagels around the clock. In 2019 a sesame bagel baked in space aboard the International Space Station by astronaut David Saint-Jacques drew attention to the style; the Montreal bagel is widely regarded as one of the city's signature foods.
