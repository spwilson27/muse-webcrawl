Digital Video Broadcasting

Digital Video Broadcasting (DVB) is a set of international open standards for digital television maintained by the DVB Project, an industry consortium, and published by the European Telecommunications Standards Institute (ETSI), CENELEC and the EBU. DVB systems transmit compressed digital audio, video and other data in an MPEG transport stream using coded orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (COFDM) or related modulation schemes.

Standards family

The main transmission standards are DVB-S for satellite, DVB-C for cable, and DVB-T for terrestrial broadcasting, each with second-generation successors (DVB-S2, DVB-C2 and DVB-T2) offering higher spectral efficiency. Other members of the family include DVB-H for handheld devices, DVB-IP for delivery over IP networks, and the DVB-SI service information and DVB-CA conditional access specifications.

History

The DVB Project was founded in 1993, growing out of the European Launching Group for digital television. Its first standards were agreed in 1994, and DVB-S and DVB-C services began appearing in the mid-1990s, followed by DVB-T launches in the United Kingdom and Sweden in 1998. Marketed under a distinctive blue logo licensed to compliant products, DVB became the dominant digital television system across Europe, much of Asia, Africa, Australia and parts of South America.

Technical overview

Source coding uses MPEG-2 and, in newer systems, H.264/MPEG-4 AVC, HEVC or AV1 video compression with AAC or AC-3 audio. Multiple programmes are multiplexed into a single transport stream; receivers demultiplex the stream and use electronic programme guides built from DVB service information tables. Forward error correction based on Reed–Solomon and convolutional or LDPC codes protects the signal against interference.
