Chihuahua (dog)


A long-coat Chihuahua

The Chihuahua (Spanish: chihuahueño) is a Mexican breed of toy dog. It is named for the Mexican state of Chihuahua and is among the smallest of all dog breeds. It is usually kept as a companion animal or for showing. The breed is generally recognised in two varieties, the smooth-coat (short-haired) and the long-coat, and with two head shapes, the rounded "apple head" and the longer-skulled "deer head".

History

DNA studies suggest that Native American dogs entered the Americas from Siberia around 10,000 years ago and were then isolated until the arrival of Europeans roughly 500 years ago. The Chihuahua is believed to descend from the Techichi, a small mute dog kept by the Toltec civilisation of Mexico from at least the 9th century AD. The breed was developed in its modern form in the Mexican state of Chihuahua in the mid-19th century, from where American visitors brought examples back to the United States. The American Kennel Club first recognised the breed in 1904.

Characteristics

The Chihuahua is the smallest breed recognised by some kennel clubs, typically weighing between 1.5 and 3 kg (3.3 and 6.6 lb) and standing 15 to 23 cm (6 to 9 in) at the withers. It has a rounded skull, large erect ears and prominent eyes. Virtually any colour or combination of colours is accepted. The breed is known for longevity, often living 14 to 18 years, but is prone to certain health conditions including patellar luxation, hydrocephalus and dental problems, the latter in part because of the small size of the jaw.

Temperament

Chihuahuas are alert and devoted companion dogs that often bond strongly to a single person. They can be wary of strangers and are prone to barking at other dogs considerably larger than themselves. Because of their small size they are considered fragile and are generally not recommended for households with very young children.
