Aspect ratio (image)

The aspect ratio of an image is the proportional relationship between its width and its height, commonly expressed as two numbers separated by a colon, as in 16:9. It describes the shape of the image rather than its absolute size or resolution.

Common aspect ratios

RatioTypical use
1:1Square format; some social media and medium-format photography
4:3Classic television, early computer monitors, 35mm compact cameras
3:235mm film and most DSLR/mirrorless still photography
16:9HDTV, widescreen monitors, most online video
16:10Computer displays
21:9Ultrawide monitors, some cinema formats
2.39:1Anamorphic theatrical widescreen

Film and photography

Early cinema standardised on the Academy ratio of roughly 1.37:1. Widescreen formats emerged in the 1950s with CinemaScope and Panavision to compete with television. Photographers choose aspect ratios that suit their subject, and many cameras allow cropping to ratios such as 5:4, 7:5 or 1:1.

Display adaptation

When image and display ratios differ, content may be shown with letterboxing or pillarboxing, stretched, or cropped — techniques such as pan and scan — each trading completeness against filling the screen.
