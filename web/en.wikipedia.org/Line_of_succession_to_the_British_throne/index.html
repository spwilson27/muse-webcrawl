Line of succession to the British throne

The line of succession to the British throne is the sequence of individuals, descendants of Sophia, Electress of Hanover, entitled to inherit the throne of the United Kingdom. Succession is governed by the Bill of Rights 1689, the Act of Settlement 1701, and the Succession to the Crown Act 2013, together with common law rules.

Rules

Succession follows absolute primogeniture for those born after 28 October 2011: the eldest child inherits regardless of sex. For those born earlier, male-preference primogeniture applies. Only Protestant descendants of Sophia of Hanover qualify, and the monarch must be in communion with the Church of England. The 2013 Act removed the disqualification arising from marriage to a Roman Catholic and limited the requirement of sovereign consent to marriages to the first six people in line.

Current line

Following Charles III, the first positions are held by William, Prince of Wales; his children Prince George, Princess Charlotte, and Prince Louis; then Prince Harry, Duke of Sussex, and his children. The full list of eligible descendants numbers in the thousands, including members of foreign royal families such as the King of Norway, who is descended from Edward VII.
