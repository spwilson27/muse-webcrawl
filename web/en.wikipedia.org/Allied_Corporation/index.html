Allied Corporation

Founded: 1920 (as Allied Chemical and Dye Corporation)
Defunct: 1999 (merged to form Honeywell)
Industry: Chemicals, aerospace, automotive
Successor: AlliedSignal; Honeywell

Allied Corporation was an American industrial conglomerate. It was founded in 1920 as the Allied Chemical and Dye Corporation by a merger of five chemical companies, organised to give the United States a domestic source of dyes and chemicals after the First World War had cut off German supplies. Over the following decades it expanded into plastics, fibres, and agricultural chemicals.

History

In 1958 the company shortened its name to Allied Chemical Corporation. Under chairman Edward Hennessy, who took over in 1979, Allied diversified aggressively beyond chemicals, acquiring the Bendix Corporation in 1983 after a celebrated takeover battle, and renamed itself Allied Corporation in 1985. The merged company, which included aerospace, automotive components (such as Fram filters and Autolite spark plugs), and signal processing businesses, adopted the name AlliedSignal in 1985. AlliedSignal acquired Honeywell in 1999 and took the Honeywell name for the combined company.

Legacy

Allied left a substantial environmental legacy, including responsibility for contaminated sites such as Onondaga Lake in New York, which Honeywell later undertook to remediate under federal Superfund supervision.
