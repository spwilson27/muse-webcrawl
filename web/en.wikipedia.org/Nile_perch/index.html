Nile perch

Scientific name: Lates niloticus
Family: Latidae
Max size: ~2 m, 200 kg

The Nile perch (Lates niloticus) is a large freshwater fish native to the Nile, Congo, Niger, and other African river basins and lakes. A voracious predator growing up to two metres and 200 kg, it is an important food fish and a notorious invasive species.

Lake Victoria

Introduced into Lake Victoria in the 1950s–60s to boost commercial fishing, the Nile perch population exploded in the 1980s. It is blamed, together with eutrophication, for the extinction or near-extinction of more than 200 endemic cichlid species — one of the largest vertebrate extinction events attributed to a single introduced species. The perch fishery generated large export earnings for Kenya, Uganda, and Tanzania but disrupted local fishing communities, a story told in the 2004 documentary Darwin's Nightmare.

Other regions

In Australia the same species is marketed as barramundi in its Asian/Australian form (Lates calcarifer is a close relative and separate species). Nile perch populations in Lake Victoria have themselves declined through overfishing, allowing some cichlids to recover in rocky refuges.
