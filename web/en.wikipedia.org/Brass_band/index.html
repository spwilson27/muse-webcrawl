Brass band

A brass band is a musical ensemble generally consisting entirely of brass instruments and a percussion section. Ensembles following the British tradition include cornets, flugelhorn, tenor horns, baritones, trombones, euphoniums, and tubas. Brass bands are also common in North America, where traditions include the New Orleans brass band and the marching band.

History

Brass bands emerged in the early 19th century, aided by the invention of valved brass instruments and the mass production of inexpensive instruments. In industrial Britain, bands were sponsored by factories, mines, and churches, and band contests became a working-class cultural institution.

British-style brass band

The standard British contesting band has 25 brass players and percussion. Repertoire ranges from original compositions by composers such as Edward Elgar, Ralph Vaughan Williams, and Philip Sparke, to transcriptions of orchestral works and popular arrangements.

New Orleans brass bands

In New Orleans, brass bands developed from African American funeral processions and parade traditions, blending European instrumentation with jazz improvisation. Modern groups such as the Rebirth Brass Band and the Dirty Dozen Brass Band have popularized the style worldwide.
