Howard Zahniser

Howard Clinton Zahniser (25 February 1906 – 5 May 1964) was an American environmental activist and the principal author of the Wilderness Act of 1964, which created the National Wilderness Preservation System in the United States. As executive secretary of The Wilderness Society from 1945 until his death, he led the campaign for federal protection of wilderness areas.

Career

Zahniser grew up in Tionesta, Pennsylvania, on the edge of the Allegheny National Forest. After graduating from Greenville College in Illinois, he worked for the Bureau of Biological Survey (a forerunner of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service) as a writer and editor, including service connected with the naturalist author Rachel Carson's early publications. In 1945 he became executive secretary (later executive director) of The Wilderness Society and editor of its magazine, The Living Wilderness.

The Wilderness Act

Beginning in 1956, Zahniser drafted and tirelessly promoted wilderness legislation through eight years of congressional hearings and dozens of drafts. His statutory definition of wilderness is famous: "A wilderness, in contrast with those areas where man and his own works dominate the landscape, is hereby recognized as an area where the earth and its community of life are untrammeled by man, where man himself is a visitor who does not remain." Zahniser died of heart disease in May 1964, only months before President Lyndon B. Johnson signed the Wilderness Act into law on 3 September 1964. Mount Zahniser in Alaska's Brooks Range and other features commemorate him.
