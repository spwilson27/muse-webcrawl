Limp Bizkit

Limp Bizkit is an American nu metal band from Jacksonville, Florida, formed in 1994. Its lineup has centred on frontman Fred Durst, guitarist Wes Borland, bassist Sam Rivers, drummer John Otto, and turntablist DJ Lethal. The band is known for fusing metal and hip hop, Durst's aggressive vocal delivery, and Borland's elaborate stage costumes and visual art.

History

After building a following through touring and support from Korn, Limp Bizkit released Three Dollar Bill, Y'all! (1997), which included a cover of George Michael's "Faith". Breakthrough success came with Significant Other (1999) and Chocolate Starfish and the Hot Dog Flavored Water (2000), featuring songs such as "Nookie", "Break Stuff", "My Generation", and "Rollin'". The latter album debuted at number one in the United States.

Later years

Borland's departures and returns punctuated the 2000s. The band continued with albums including Results May Vary (2003), Gold Cobra (2011), and Still Sucks (2021), and remains a major live act associated with the nu metal era, having sold tens of millions of records worldwide.
