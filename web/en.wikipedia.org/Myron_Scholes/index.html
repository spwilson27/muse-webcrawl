Myron Scholes

Myron Samuel Scholes (born July 1, 1941) is a Canadian-American financial economist. He is best known as co-author of the Black–Scholes model of option pricing, for which he shared the 1997 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences with Robert C. Merton (Fischer Black had died in 1995 and was ineligible).

Career

Scholes was born in Timmins, Ontario, and studied at McMaster University before earning his MBA and PhD at the University of Chicago, where he worked with Merton Miller and Eugene Fama. He taught at MIT's Sloan School and at Chicago before moving to Stanford University in 1983, where he became the Frank E. Buck Professor of Finance. With Fischer Black, he published the landmark 1973 paper "The Pricing of Options and Corporate Liabilities", deriving a closed-form formula for the fair price of a European call option that revolutionized derivatives markets.

LTCM and later work

Scholes was a founding partner of Long-Term Capital Management (LTCM), the hedge fund whose near-collapse in 1998 — after the Russian financial crisis invalidated its models' assumptions — required a bailout organized by the Federal Reserve Bank of New York. The episode became a famous cautionary tale about model risk and leverage. Scholes later co-founded Platinum Grove Asset Management and has remained active in research and commentary on risk, liquidity, and financial innovation.
