Charles and Ray Eames

Charles Eames (1907–1978) and Ray Eames (1912–1988) were American designers, architects and filmmakers who made major contributions to modern architecture and furniture design. Working as husband and wife from their studio in Venice, California, they pioneered technologies such as moulded plywood, fibreglass and wire mesh in mass-produced furniture.

Furniture

Their best-known works include the moulded plywood LCW chair (1945), the fibreglass shell chairs developed for MoMA's Low-Cost Furniture competition (1948), the Eames Lounge Chair and ottoman in rosewood and leather (1956), and the Aluminium Group chairs. Their furniture was manufactured by Herman Miller and remains in production.

Architecture and film

The Eames House (Case Study House No. 8, 1949) in Pacific Palisades, built from off-the-shelf industrial components, is a landmark of modernist architecture and served as their home and studio. The Eameses also made more than 100 short films, including the celebrated Powers of Ten (1977), exploring scale from the universe to the atom.

Legacy

Their motto — "the best for the most for the least" — captured their aim of bringing good design to mass production, and their work is held in museum collections worldwide.
