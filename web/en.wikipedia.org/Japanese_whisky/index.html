Japanese whisky

Japanese whisky is a style of whisky developed and produced in Japan. Whisky production in Japan began around 1870, but the first commercial production was in 1923 upon the opening of the country's first distillery, Yamazaki. Broadly speaking, the style of Japanese whisky is more similar to that of Scotch whisky than other major styles, and it has won numerous international awards since the early 2000s, bringing it wide global acclaim.

History

The industry's two founding figures are Shinjiro Torii, founder of Kotobukiya (later Suntory), who built the Yamazaki distillery near Kyoto in 1923, and Masataka Taketsuru, a chemist who had studied whisky-making in Scotland and later founded Nikka Whisky, establishing the Yoichi distillery in Hokkaido in 1934. The two companies, Suntory and Nikka, remain the dominant producers.

Style and production

Japanese distillers historically strove for a clean, precise spirit in the Scottish tradition, using a range of still shapes, fermentation styles, and cask types — including sherry butts, bourbon barrels, and native mizunara oak — to give blenders a wide palette within a single company, since Japanese producers traditionally did not trade casks with competitors. Clear wort fermentation and careful cut points contribute to a lighter, more delicate profile in many expressions.

International recognition

Japanese whiskies began winning top prizes abroad in the 2000s; in 2014, a leading whisky guide named Suntory's Yamazaki Sherry Cask 2013 the best whisky in the world, sparking a boom in demand that outstripped aged stocks and pushed prices sharply upward. In 2021, the Japan Spirits & Liqueurs Makers Association adopted formal standards of identity defining "Japanese whisky" as whisky fermented, distilled, matured, and bottled in Japan.

Major producers

  • Suntory – Yamazaki and Hakushu distilleries (single malts Yamazaki, Hakushu; blends such as Hibiki)
  • Nikka – Yoichi and Miyagikyo distilleries
  • Others include Chichibu, Mars Shinshu, Kirin's Fuji Gotemba, and Akkeshi
