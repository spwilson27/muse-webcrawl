Panamalai

Panamalai is a village in the Viluppuram district of Tamil Nadu, India, located about 23 kilometres north-west of Viluppuram and near Gingee. It is renowned for its 7th-century Pallava-era rock-cut and structural temples, making it an important site for the study of early South Indian temple architecture and painting.

Talagirishwara Temple

The village's principal monument is the Talagirishwara Temple, a structural stone Shiva temple built on a low hill by the Pallava king Narasimhavarman II (Rajasimha, r. c. 700–729 CE). One of the earliest structural stone temples in Tamil Nadu, it contains some of the finest surviving Pallava wall paintings, including a celebrated panel of the goddess Uma (or Parvati) — her figure, in faded red, green, and ochre pigments, is considered a masterpiece of early medieval Indian painting. The temple's vimana and lion pillars follow the style developed under Rajasimha at Kanchipuram and Mahabalipuram.

Other features

Panamalai also preserves a rock-cut cave temple and inscriptions associated with the Pallava period, along with later additions. The site is protected by the Archaeological Survey of India. Its name is sometimes written Panangudi or Panamalai Rajasimha temple in scholarly literature.
