Exposure therapy

TypePsychotherapy (behaviour therapy / CBT)
Used forAnxiety disorders, specific phobias, OCD, PTSD
Related techniquesSystematic desensitization, flooding, prolonged exposure, ERP
Exposure is often arranged as a hierarchy from least to most feared situations.

Exposure therapy is a technique in behaviour therapy in which a patient is exposed to an anxiety-producing stimulus or situation without the danger traditionally associated with it, in order to overcome their anxiety. It is grounded in Pavlovian extinction learning: repeated, safe contact with a feared cue weakens the conditioned fear response and builds new, inhibitory associations.

Forms

Exposure may be in vivo (real situations), imaginal (vividly imagining the feared event), interoceptive (deliberately inducing feared bodily sensations, as in panic disorder), or delivered through virtual reality. It may be graded, following a fear hierarchy as in systematic desensitization, or intensive, as in flooding. Exposure and response prevention (ERP) is the standard treatment for obsessive–compulsive disorder, adding prevention of the compulsive ritual; prolonged exposure is a first-line treatment for post-traumatic stress disorder.

Effectiveness

Exposure-based treatments are among the most strongly evidence-supported interventions in clinical psychology, with large effect sizes for specific phobias and substantial support for social anxiety, panic disorder, OCD, and PTSD. Modern accounts emphasize inhibitory learning and expectancy violation rather than simple habituation: maximizing the mismatch between what the patient fears will happen and what actually occurs improves outcomes.
