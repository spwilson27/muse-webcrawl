Eurostat

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Eurostat is a Directorate-General of the European Commission located in Luxembourg. Its main responsibilities are to provide statistical information to the institutions of the European Union (EU) and to promote the harmonisation of statistical methods across the member states of the EU, the EFTA countries, and candidate countries.

History

Eurostat originated in the Statistical Office of the European Coal and Steel Community, founded in 1953. It took the name Eurostat in 1959. It coordinates the European Statistical System (ESS), the partnership between Eurostat and the national statistical institutes (such as INSEE in France and Destatis in Germany) that produce official statistics in each member state according to a European Statistics Code of Practice.

Work

Eurostat publishes harmonised data on the economy and finance (including GDP, government debt, unemployment, and the Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices used to measure euro-area inflation), population and social conditions, industry, trade, agriculture, environment, and regions. Its figures are central to EU policymaking: government deficit and debt data underpin the Excessive Deficit Procedure of the Stability and Growth Pact, and regional GDP data determine eligibility for EU cohesion funding. The major Greek government-debt revisions of 2009–2010 led to Eurostat being granted stronger audit powers over national statistics.

Most Eurostat data are available free of charge through its online database and dissemination tools, and are widely used by journalists, researchers, and market analysts.
