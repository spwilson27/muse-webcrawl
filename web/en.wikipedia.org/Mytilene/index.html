Mytilene is the capital and port of the island of Lesbos in the northeastern Aegean Sea, Greece. Built on the southeast coast of the island around two harbours, it is the administrative centre of the North Aegean region and the seat of a municipality of the same name.

History

Mytilene was founded in antiquity and competed with Methymna for dominance of Lesbos. In 428 BC the city revolted against Athens during the Peloponnesian War, prompting the famous Mytilenean Debate in the Athenian assembly, recorded by Thucydides, over whether its male population should be executed — a decree that was reversed at the last moment. Aristotle lived and taught in Mytilene in the 340s BC, conducting early biological studies in the nearby lagoon of Kalloni. The city later flourished under Rome and was the birthplace of the poets Sappho and Alcaeus (from the island generally) and of the statesman Pittacus, one of the Seven Sages of Greece.

In the Byzantine and Genoese periods the city was fortified; its imposing castle was expanded by the Gattilusi family and later by the Ottomans, who took Lesbos in 1462. Mytilene remained under Ottoman rule until 1912, when it became part of Greece during the First Balkan War. The city retains neoclassical mansions from the nineteenth-century olive-oil trade, the church of Saint Therapon with its distinctive dome, and archaeological and Byzantine museums.
