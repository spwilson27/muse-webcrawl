Breechblock

A breechblock (or "breech block") is the part of the firearm action that blocks the rear of the breech of a weapon (at the back of the barrel) during firing. In firearms terminology, the breechblock is also frequently referred to as a bolt when it is part of a repeating or self-loading action.

The breechblock seals the chamber and supports the base of the cartridge case against the pressure of the propellant gases when the weapon is fired. It typically houses the firing pin and extractor, and it must withstand the full force generated by the cartridge — tens of thousands of pounds per square inch in modern rifle cartridges.

Designs

Breechblocks have taken many forms across the history of breech-loading firearms:

  • Falling block — the block slides vertically down to expose the chamber, as in the Sharps rifle and many single-shot designs, and in various artillery pieces.
  • Rolling block — the block pivots backwards on a pin to open the breech, as in the Remington Rolling Block rifle.
  • Trapdoor — a hinged block that lifts up like a trapdoor, as in the Springfield Model 1873 conversion.
  • Rotating bolt — the block (bolt) turns so that locking lugs engage recesses in the receiver or barrel extension, the dominant design in modern military rifles and machine guns.
  • Tilting block — the rear of the block tips down into a recess to lock, as in the FN FAL.
  • Sliding breechblock — used in artillery, either vertical or horizontal, sliding across a threaded or interrupted-screw breech.

Artillery

In larger guns, the breechblock is often a massive steel block containing an interrupted screw thread (as in the Welin breech) that can be swung into and out of the breech, or a sliding wedge. Sealing against gas leakage (obturation) is provided by a pad, ring, or the cartridge case itself.
