Salah

Salah (Arabic: صَلَاة, ṣalāh, "[the] prayer"), also known by its Persian name namaz, is the formal worship and prayer performed by Muslims. It is one of the Five Pillars of Islam in the Sunni tradition and one of the Ten Practices of the Religion in Shia Islam. The obligatory prayers are performed five times a day at prescribed times, facing the Qibla — the direction of the Kaaba in Mecca.

Significance

Salah is considered the most direct form of worship of Allah, repeated throughout a Muslim's day to maintain mindfulness of God. According to Islamic tradition, the five daily prayers were prescribed during Muhammad's Night Journey (Isra and Mi'raj). The Quran repeatedly commands believers to "establish prayer" (aqīmū ṣ-ṣalāh) and to give zakat.

Performance

Before praying, a Muslim performs wudu, a ritual washing of the hands, face, arms, and feet. The prayer itself consists of units called rak'ah, combining standing (reciting the opening chapter of the Quran, Al-Fatiha, and other verses), bowing (ruku), prostration (sujud) with the forehead to the ground, and sitting. It concludes with the tashahhud and the greeting of peace (taslim), "as-salamu alaykum wa rahmatullah".

Congregational and Friday prayer

Prayers may be performed alone or in congregation; congregational prayer in a mosque is strongly encouraged, led by an imam. On Fridays, the noon prayer is replaced by Jumu'ah, a congregational prayer preceded by a sermon (khutbah), obligatory for adult male Muslims.
