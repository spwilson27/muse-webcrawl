Juan de Fuca Plate

Location: Offshore of the Pacific Northwest, North America
Boundaries: Cascadia subduction zone (east); Juan de Fuca Ridge (west)
Movement: Subducting beneath the North American Plate at roughly 3–4 cm/year
Named for: Strait of Juan de Fuca

The Juan de Fuca Plate is a small tectonic plate off the Pacific Northwest coast of North America, lying between the North American Plate to the east and the Pacific Plate to the west. It is a remnant of the once-vast Farallon Plate, most of which has subducted beneath North America. Its slow subduction built the Cascade Range and poses a major earthquake and tsunami hazard to the region.

Geology

The plate extends from northern California to British Columbia, offshore of Oregon and Washington. New crust forms at the Juan de Fuca Ridge on its western edge, while its eastern edge disappears into the Cascadia subduction zone at a rate of roughly 3–4 centimetres per year. The Explorer Plate to the north and the Gorda Plate to the south are closely related fragments; all three were separated from the Farallon Plate as it broke up beginning about 30 million years ago.

Volcanism and seismicity

As the slab sinks and heats, it releases water that melts the overlying mantle, feeding the Cascade Volcanic Arc — including Mount St. Helens, Mount Rainier, and Mount Hood. The subduction zone last ruptured in a megathrust earthquake of about magnitude 9 on January 26, 1700, an event known from Japanese tsunami records and Indigenous oral traditions. Geological evidence suggests such great earthquakes recur at intervals of a few hundred years, and the zone also produces "slow slip" events and deep earthquakes within the slab.

Importance

Because it is small, young, warm, and well instrumented, the Juan de Fuca Plate is one of the most intensively studied subduction systems in the world, monitored by observatories such as Ocean Networks Canada's NEPTUNE array. Its behaviour informs building codes and hazard planning for millions of people in Seattle, Portland, and Vancouver.
