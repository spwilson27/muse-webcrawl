Muslim conquest of Persia

Date633–654
LocationSasanian Empire (Mesopotamia, Persia, Central Asia)
ResultRashidun victory; fall of the Sasanian Empire
BelligerentsRashidun Caliphate vs. Sasanian Empire

The Muslim conquest of Persia, also called the Arab conquest of Iran, was carried out by the Rashidun Caliphate between 633 and 654 and led to the fall of the Sasanian Empire and the eventual decline of the Zoroastrian religion in Persia. The conquest began under Caliph Abu Bakr and was largely completed under Umar and Uthman.

Background

The Sasanian Empire had been weakened by a long and exhausting war with the Byzantine Empire (602–628), civil strife following the overthrow of Khosrow II, plague, and economic strain. When Muslim Arab armies entered Mesopotamia in 633 under Khalid ibn al-Walid, they encountered a politically fragmented state.

Course of the conquest

After victories in Iraq, the decisive Battle of al-Qadisiyyah (636) opened the way to the Sasanian capital Ctesiphon, which fell in 637. The Battle of Nahavand in 642 — called the "Victory of Victories" — broke organized Persian resistance in the Iranian plateau. Yazdegerd III, the last Sasanian shah, fled eastward and was killed at Merv in 651, ending the dynasty.

Consequences

Persia was incorporated into the caliphate, and over subsequent centuries the population gradually converted to Islam, though Persian language and culture survived and profoundly shaped the Islamic world. Persian administrators, scholars, and artistic traditions became central to the Abbasid Caliphate, and a Persian cultural revival under dynasties such as the Samanids preserved the Persian language in Arabic script.
