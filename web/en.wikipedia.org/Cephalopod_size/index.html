Cephalopod size

Cephalopod size covers an extraordinary range, from tiny squid of about a centimetre to the giant squid and colossal squid, which are among the largest known invertebrates, and in historical accounts to much exaggerated sizes once attributed to the kraken of legend.

Largest species

The colossal squid (Mesonychoteuthis hamiltoni) is estimated to reach 10–14 metres in total length and has the largest known eyes in the animal kingdom. The giant squid (Architeuthis dux) can reach comparable lengths, mostly in its long feeding tentacles. The largest octopus, the giant Pacific octopus, has a much smaller arm span, generally a few metres.

Measurement

Size in cephalopods is usually reported as mantle length, total length, or arm span, and comparisons are complicated because soft bodies stretch and shrink after death. Beaks recovered from sperm whale stomachs have historically been used to estimate the size of deep-sea squid.
