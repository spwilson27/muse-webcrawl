Challenger Deep
LocationMariana Trench, Pacific Ocean
Depth~10,935 m (35,876 ft) below sea level

The Challenger Deep is the deepest known point of the seabed of Earth. It lies in the Mariana Trench, near the southern end of the trench close to Guam, at a depth of about 10.9 kilometres (6.8 miles) below sea level. It is named after the British survey ship HMS Challenger, whose expedition of 1872–1876 made the first recordings of its depth.

Descents

The first crewed descent was made on 23 January 1960 by the bathyscaphe Trieste, with Jacques Piccard and Don Walsh aboard. In 2012 James Cameron made the first solo descent in the Deepsea Challenger, and from 2019 Victor Vescovo's Limiting Factor set records with repeated dives. Despite the pressure of more than 1,000 atmospheres, shrimp-like amphipods, sea cucumbers, and microbial life thrive on the trench floor.

Geology

The trench marks the subduction zone where the Pacific Plate slides beneath the Mariana Plate. The Challenger Deep is the deepest of a series of depressions along the trench axis.
