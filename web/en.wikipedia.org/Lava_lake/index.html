Lava lake

A lava lake is a large volume of molten lava, usually basaltic, contained in a volcanic crater, vent, or depression. Lava lakes are rare, long-lived features that require a balance between heat supplied by rising magma and gas and heat lost at the surface. The term is also applied to lakes of solidified lava, whether still partly molten or fully cooled.

Behaviour

In an active lava lake, the surface continually renews itself: a thin, dark crust forms, cracks, and founders in plates resembling miniature plate tectonics, revealing incandescent lava beneath, while fountains and gas bubbles stir the surface in a slow convective cycle. The level of the lake rises and falls with the pressure of magma and gas in the volcano's conduit system; a falling level can precede crater collapse, as occurred at Kīlauea in 2018.

Persistent lava lakes

Only a handful of volcanoes have maintained persistent lava lakes for years or decades. They include Erta Ale in Ethiopia, Mount Nyiragongo in the Democratic Republic of the Congo — whose low-viscosity nephelinite lake is the largest in the world — Villarrica in Chile, Mount Erebus in Antarctica (a rare phonolite lake), Ambrym in Vanuatu, and Kīlauea in Hawaiʻi, where lakes have occupied Halemaʻumaʻu crater during several eruptive periods, most recently from 2020 onwards. Nyiragongo's lake drained catastrophically through flank fractures in 1977 and 2002, sending fast-moving lava flows into the city of Goma.
