Oregon Territory

The Territory of Oregon was an organized incorporated territory of the United States, created on 14 August 1848 by an act of Congress out of the American portion of the jointly occupied Oregon Country. It originally embraced the lands north of 42°N and west of the Continental Divide to the Pacific — present-day Oregon, Washington, Idaho, and parts of Montana and Wyoming — with its capital first at Oregon City and later at Salem.

Created:14 August 1848
Capital:Oregon City, then Salem
First governor:Joseph Lane
Ceased to exist:14 February 1859 (Oregon statehood)

History

The Oregon Treaty of 1846 with Great Britain had fixed the boundary with British North America at the 49th parallel, ending the joint occupation agreed in 1818. American settlers had already organized a provisional government from 1843, and migration over the Oregon Trail brought thousands of settlers in the 1840s. Territorial government followed news of the Whitman massacre and the ensuing Cayuse War, which pressed Congress to act.

Division and statehood

In 1853 the northern portion was split off as the Washington Territory. Oregon's constitutional convention met in 1857, and on 14 February 1859 Oregon entered the Union as the 33rd state with roughly its present boundaries; the remaining eastern parts of the territory were attached to Washington Territory. Joseph Lane served as the territory's first governor, and its delegate in Congress later included Samuel Thurston.
