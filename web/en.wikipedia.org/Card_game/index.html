Card game

TypeGame
Players1 or more
MaterialsPlaying cards

A card game is any game using playing cards as the primary device with which the game is played, whether the cards are of a traditional design or specifically created for the game. Countless card games exist, including families of related games such as poker. A small number of card games played with traditional decks have formally standardized rules, but most are folk games whose rules vary by region, culture, and person.

Types of card games

Card games are commonly classified by their mechanism: trick-taking games (bridge, whist, spades), matching games (rummy family), shedding games (crazy eights, Uno), catch and collect games, fishing games, comparing games (poker and other gambling games), and patience or solitaire games for one player.

History

Playing cards were invented in China during the Tang dynasty and spread via the Mamluk world to Europe in the late 14th century. The standard 52-card deck derives from French designs of the 15th century.

Rules and etiquette

In many games the dealer shuffles and deals the cards, players hold their hands concealed from opponents, and play proceeds by turns. Misdeals, revokes, and exposed cards are governed by conventions that differ between casual and tournament play.
