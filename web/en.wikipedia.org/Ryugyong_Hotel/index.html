The Ryugyong Hotel (Korean: 류경호텔) is an unfinished pyramid-shaped skyscraper in Pyongyang, North Korea. At 330 metres with 105 floors, it is the tallest building in the country and was intended to be the world's tallest hotel. Nicknamed the "Hotel of Doom," it stood as a bare concrete shell for decades and has never opened.

History

Construction began in 1987, reportedly in response to a South Korean firm's completion of Singapore's Westin Stamford, then the world's tallest hotel. The hotel was planned to open in 1989 for the World Festival of Youth and Students, containing some 3,000 rooms, restaurants — including revolving restaurants in its upper levels — and casinos. Work halted in 1992 amid North Korea's economic crisis and famine, leaving the structure topped out but windowless, a conspicuous symbol often edited out of official photographs.

Later developments

In 2008 the Egyptian Orascom Group resumed work, installing glass panels and telecommunications equipment; the exterior was completed by 2011. Interior work by the Kempinski group was announced and then suspended in 2013. LED panels installed on one face have since been used for light shows and propaganda displays, but as of the mid-2020s the building remains unopened.
