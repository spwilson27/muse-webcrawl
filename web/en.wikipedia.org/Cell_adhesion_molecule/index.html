Cell adhesion molecule

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cell adhesion molecules (CAMs) are proteins located on the cell surface that mediate binding between cells, or between cells and the extracellular matrix. They are essential for building tissues, guiding embryonic development, directing immune-cell trafficking, wound healing, and synaptic plasticity. Most CAMs are transmembrane receptors that link externally to other cells or matrix and internally, through adaptor proteins, to the cytoskeleton and signalling pathways.

Major families

  • Cadherins — calcium-dependent adhesion molecules that form adherens junctions; classical cadherins (E-, N-, P-cadherin) bind homophilically and connect via catenins to actin. Loss of E-cadherin is a key step in epithelial cancers becoming invasive.
  • Integrins — heterodimeric receptors for matrix proteins (fibronectin, collagen, laminin) and for counter-receptors on other cells; they anchor cells at focal adhesions and hemidesmosomes and signal bidirectionally.
  • Immunoglobulin superfamily CAMs (IgSF) — include NCAM, ICAM-1, VCAM-1 and PECAM-1; calcium-independent, important in neural development and inflammation.
  • Selectins — carbohydrate-binding (lectin) CAMs (E-, P- and L-selectin) that mediate the initial rolling of leukocytes on endothelium before firm arrest and extravasation.

Functions and signalling

Beyond mechanical attachment, CAMs transduce signals: integrins activate focal adhesion kinase and pathways controlling survival, proliferation and migration; cadherin engagement modulates β-catenin/Wnt signalling. During development, changing CAM expression lets cells sort into tissues and allows neural crest cells and growth cones to migrate.

Clinical relevance

Defects cause diseases such as leukocyte adhesion deficiency (integrin/selectin pathway defects) and blistering skin diseases (autoantibodies or mutations affecting adhesion). Pathogens including viruses and bacteria exploit CAMs as entry receptors. CAMs are drug targets: natalizumab blocks integrin α4 in multiple sclerosis, and selectin antagonists are used in sickle-cell disease. Soluble forms of CAMs serve as biomarkers of endothelial activation in cardiovascular disease and sepsis.
