Television in the United Kingdom

Television in the United Kingdom began with the BBC Television Service in 1936, the world's first regular high-definition television service. British television is dominated by five main free-to-air channels — BBC One, BBC Two, ITV1, Channel 4 and Channel 5 — supported by a large number of digital channels distributed via terrestrial (Freeview), satellite (Sky and Freesat), cable (Virgin Media) and streaming platforms.

History

The BBC held a monopoly until commercial television arrived with ITV in 1955, funded by advertising; the BBC itself is funded principally by the television licence fee. BBC Two launched in 1964 and introduced regular colour broadcasts in 1967. Channel 4 began in 1982 with a remit for minority and innovative programming, and Channel 5 followed in 1997. Satellite and cable multichannel television expanded rapidly in the 1990s, and the United Kingdom completed the switchover from analogue to digital terrestrial television in 2012.

Regulation and public service broadcasting

Broadcasting is regulated by Ofcom. The public service broadcasters have statutory obligations for news, regional programming and original British content. British television exports — drama, documentary and formats such as Who Wants to Be a Millionaire? and The Great British Bake Off — are sold worldwide.
