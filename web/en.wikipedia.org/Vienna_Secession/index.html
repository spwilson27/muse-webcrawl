Vienna Secession

Founded3 April 1897, Vienna, Austria-Hungary
FoundersGustav Klimt, Koloman Moser, Josef Hoffmann, Joseph Maria Olbrich, Max Kurzweil, and others
TypeArt movement (Art Nouveau / Jugendstil)
Motto"To every age its art, to every art its freedom"

The Vienna Secession (German: Wiener Secession) was an art movement formed in 1897 by a group of Austrian painters, graphic artists, sculptors and architects, including Josef Hoffmann, Koloman Moser, Otto Wagner and Gustav Klimt, who resigned from the conservative Association of Austrian Artists in protest against its support for traditional artistic styles. The Secession was the Austrian branch of the broader Art Nouveau movement (known in German as Jugendstil).

Secession Building

In 1898 Joseph Maria Olbrich designed the group's exhibition hall, the Secession Building, famous for its gilded laurel-leaf dome — nicknamed "the golden cabbage" — and the motto inscribed above its entrance: Der Zeit ihre Kunst, der Kunst ihre Freiheit ("To every age its art, to every art its freedom"). The fourteenth Secession exhibition in 1902, designed by Josef Hoffmann and centred on Max Klinger's Beethoven statue, displayed Klimt's Beethoven Frieze. The journal Ver Sacrum published the group's work until 1903.

Later history

Klimt and his close associates left the Secession in 1905 over disputes about its direction, but the institution continued and still operates as an independent artists' association with its own gallery.
