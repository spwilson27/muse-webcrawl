GIC (Singaporean sovereign wealth fund)

GIC is headquartered in Singapore's financial district

GIC Private Limited is a global investment company that manages Singapore's foreign reserves. Established by the Government of Singapore in 1981 as the Government of Singapore Investment Corporation, it is one of the world's largest sovereign wealth funds, with well over US$100 billion of assets under management; independent estimates have placed its portfolio in the several hundreds of billions of US dollars. The fund's purpose is to preserve and enhance the long-term purchasing power of the reserves under its management.

History

Before 1981, Singapore's reserves were managed by the Monetary Authority of Singapore. The government, advised by former deputy prime minister Goh Keng Swee, decided that the growing reserves should be invested for higher long-term returns rather than held mainly in liquid, low-yielding assets. GIC was accordingly incorporated on 22 May 1981 as a private limited company wholly owned by the government, with Lee Kuan Yew as its first chairman.

Investments

GIC invests across more than 40 countries in equities, fixed income, real estate, private equity, venture capital, and infrastructure. It is a long-term investor with a diversified, global portfolio; it has offices in Singapore and financial centres including New York, London, San Francisco, Beijing, and Tokyo. During the 2007–2008 financial crisis, GIC made large, widely reported investments in the Swiss bank UBS and in Citigroup. GIC reports its annualised rolling 20-year real return in its yearly report; its nominal returns are reported over 5-, 10-, and 20-year horizons.

Governance

GIC operates commercially, and its board is responsible for investment strategy and performance. Alongside Temasek Holdings and the Monetary Authority of Singapore, it forms part of the framework through which Singapore's reserves are managed and protected under the country's constitution.
