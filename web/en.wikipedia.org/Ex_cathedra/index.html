Ex cathedra

Latin"from the chair"
ContextRoman Catholic theology
DefinedFirst Vatican Council, Pastor aeternus (1870)
Related doctrinePapal infallibility

Ex cathedra is a Latin phrase, literally "from the chair", used in Roman Catholic theology to describe the solemn mode in which the pope teaches. A teaching is said to be given ex cathedra when the pope, exercising his office as pastor and teacher of all Christians, by his supreme apostolic authority defines a doctrine concerning faith or morals to be held by the whole Church. Under the doctrine of papal infallibility, such definitions are held to be free from error.

Definition

The First Vatican Council's constitution Pastor aeternus (1870) set four conditions: the pope must speak as supreme pastor and teacher (not as a private theologian), must define a doctrine, the doctrine must concern faith or morals, and it must be intended as binding on the whole Church. The "chair" alludes to the cathedra, the episcopal throne symbolising teaching authority — in this case the Chair of Saint Peter. Theologians note that infallibility also attaches to the solemn definitions of ecumenical councils and, under the doctrine of the ordinary and universal magisterium, to teachings constantly held by the bishops worldwide.

Instances

Only two instances are universally agreed to satisfy the conditions since the 1870 definition: Pope Pius IX's definition of the Immaculate Conception of Mary (Ineffabilis Deus, 1854, retroactively recognised) and Pope Pius XII's definition of the Assumption of Mary (Munificentissimus Deus, 1950). Whether earlier papal acts — such as Boniface VIII's Unam Sanctam (1302) — count as ex cathedra is debated among theologians, since the canonical criteria were formalised only in 1870.

Ordinary usage

In everyday language, the phrase has detached from theology: to speak ex cathedra is to speak with the air of final authority on a subject, a usage often ironic. The related adjective is sometimes applied to any pronouncement delivered as if it were beyond question.
