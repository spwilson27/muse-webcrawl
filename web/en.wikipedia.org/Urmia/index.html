Urmia (Persian: ارومیه; Azerbaijani: Urmiya; Kurdish: Ûrmiye) is a city in northwestern Iran and the capital of West Azerbaijan Province. It lies in a fertile plain west of Lake Urmia, near the borders with Turkey and Iraq, at an elevation of about 1,330 metres. With a population of roughly three-quarters of a million, it is among the ten most populous cities in Iran.

History

The Urmia plain has been inhabited since prehistoric times and was part of the ancient Urartian and later Median, Persian, and successive empires. The city is noted for its long-standing religious and ethnic diversity: Azerbaijanis form the majority, alongside Kurds, Persians, Armenians and Assyrians, and the city contains historic churches, mosques and synagogues. Tradition holds it to be a birthplace of the prophet Zoroaster. During the First World War the region saw heavy fighting and atrocities against its Christian population. The city was officially renamed Rezaieh under Reza Shah Pahlavi (1935–1979), after which the name Urmia was restored.

Geography and economy

Urmia has a continental climate with cold winters and warm, dry summers. The surrounding plain is a major agricultural area producing apples, grapes, tobacco and other crops. Lake Urmia, once one of the world's largest salt lakes, shrank dramatically from the 1990s due to drought and water diversion, though restoration programmes and wetter years have partly raised its level since the late 2010s.

Landmarks

Notable sites include the Jameh Mosque of Urmia, the ancient church of St. Mary (associated with an early Christian tradition), the Seh Gonbad tomb tower, and Urmia University, founded in 1965.
