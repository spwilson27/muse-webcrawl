Peacock Throne

The Peacock Throne was a famous jewelled throne that was the seat of the Mughal emperors of India. Commissioned by Shah Jahan in the early 17th century, it stood in the Diwan-i-Khas (Hall of Private Audience) in the Red Fort at Delhi. It was named for the peacocks figured in its design and was studded with precious stones, reportedly including the Koh-i-Noor diamond.

Peacock Throne
Commissioned by: Shah Jahan
Location: Red Fort, Delhi (original)
Seized: 1739 by Nader Shah

History

Shah Jahan ordered the throne shortly after his accession in 1628; it took some seven years to complete and cost an enormous sum, employing gold, emeralds, rubies, pearls and other gems. Two peacocks, their tails fanned and encrusted with jewels, surmounted the canopy.

Removal and fate

In 1739 the Persian ruler Nader Shah invaded India, sacked Delhi and carried the throne back to Persia. It was later broken up; its surviving stones and fragments passed into various collections, and the name "Peacock Throne" was later applied to subsequent Persian royal thrones.
