Admiral (abbreviated as ADM) is a four-star commissioned officer rank in the United States Navy, the United States Coast Guard, and the United States Public Health Service Commissioned Corps, with the pay grade of O-10. Admiral ranks above vice admiral and below fleet admiral in the Navy; the Coast Guard and the Public Health Service do not have an equivalent rank above admiral.

History

The United States Navy did not have admirals in its early decades: the senior rank was captain, and commodore was a courtesy title. The rank of rear admiral was created in 1862 during the Civil War, when David Farragut became the Navy's first rear admiral, then vice admiral (1864) and its first full admiral (1866). David Dixon Porter succeeded to the grade of admiral in 1870, and George Dewey was made Admiral of the Navy in 1899, a unique rank senior to all others.

Statutory limits and appointment

By law, the number of four-star admirals on active duty is strictly limited, and appointments are tied to specific positions — such as Chief of Naval Operations, Vice Chief of Naval Operations, commanders of the combatant commands and fleet commands — and require nomination by the President and confirmation by the Senate. Four-star officers generally must retire after 40 years of service or at age 64, unless reappointed or waivered. The five-star rank of fleet admiral was created in 1944 and has not been held since the Second World War era.
