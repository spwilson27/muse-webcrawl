Joseph Babinski

Joseph Jules François Félix Babinski (17 November 1857 – 29 October 1932) was a French-Polish professor of neurology, best known for his 1896 description of the Babinski sign (the Babinski reflex), an upgoing plantar reflex in adults that indicates damage to the corticospinal tract.

Life and career

Babinski was born in Paris to Polish immigrant parents. He studied medicine under Jean-Martin Charcot at the Salpêtrière and became chief of clinic there. Blocked from an academic chair partly by academic politics, he spent his career at the Hôpital de la Pitié, where he refined the clinical neurological examination into a rigorous method for distinguishing organic disease from hysteria.

The Babinski sign

In healthy adults, stroking the sole of the foot makes the toes flex downward; in infants, and in adults with lesions of the pyramidal tract, the big toe extends upward and the other toes fan out. Babinski's short 1896 communication on this phenomenon became one of the most famous signs in clinical medicine. He also introduced terms including "pithiatism" for disorders curable by persuasion, and described the Babinski–Nageotte and Anton–Babinski syndromes. His students included Clovis Vincent and Jean Lhermitte.
