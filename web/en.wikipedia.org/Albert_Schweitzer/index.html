Albert Schweitzer (14 January 1875 – 4 September 1965) was an Alsatian polymath: a theologian, organist, musicologist, philosopher, and physician. He received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1952 for his philosophy of "Reverence for Life" and for founding a hospital in Lambaréné, Gabon.

Scholarship and music

Schweitzer earned a doctorate in philosophy with a study of Kant, and in theology wrote The Quest of the Historical Jesus (1906), a landmark in New Testament scholarship. An acclaimed organist, he also wrote an influential biography of J. S. Bach.

Lambaréné

Deciding to devote his life to direct service, Schweitzer studied medicine and in 1913 sailed to French Equatorial Africa, where he established a hospital at Lambaréné. He spent much of the rest of his life there, raising funds through organ concerts and lectures in Europe.

Reverence for Life

His ethical philosophy centered on the phrase "Reverence for Life": the conviction that all living beings share a will to live and deserve respect. In later years he also campaigned against nuclear weapons testing.
