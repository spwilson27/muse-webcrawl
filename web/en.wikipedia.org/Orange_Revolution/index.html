Orange Revolution

Orange Revolution
Date: 22 November 2004 – 23 January 2005
Location: Kyiv, Ukraine
Causes: Electoral fraud in the 2004 presidential election
Result: Election re-run; Viktor Yushchenko declared president

The Orange Revolution (Ukrainian: Помаранчева революція, Pomarancheva revoliutsiia) was a series of protests and political events that took place in Ukraine from late November 2004 to January 2005, in the immediate aftermath of the run-off vote of the 2004 Ukrainian presidential election, which was claimed to be marred by massive corruption, voter intimidation and direct electoral fraud.

Background

The 2004 election was contested between Viktor Yanukovych, the candidate favoured by outgoing president Leonid Kuchma and supported by Russia, and opposition leader Viktor Yushchenko, who favoured closer ties with the European Union. Yushchenko's campaign was dramatically affected by his dioxin poisoning in September 2004. Official results of the run-off on 21 November gave Yanukovych victory, contradicting exit polls that showed Yushchenko ahead.

Protests

Hundreds of thousands of protesters, wearing orange — the colour of Yushchenko's campaign — occupied Independence Square (Maidan Nezalezhnosti) in Kyiv and other Ukrainian cities, demanding a re-vote. The protests were overwhelmingly peaceful, aided by civil disobedience, sit-ins and general strikes organised by the opposition movement and civil society groups such as Pora!.

Outcome

On 3 December 2004 the Supreme Court of Ukraine annulled the run-off result. A re-run on 26 December gave Yushchenko 52% of the vote, and he was inaugurated on 23 January 2005. The Orange Revolution is often grouped with the colour revolutions in other post-Soviet states. Its gains proved unstable: Yanukovych returned as prime minister in 2006 and won the presidency in 2010.
