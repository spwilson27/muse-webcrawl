A4

This article is about the paper size. For other uses, see A4 (disambiguation).

A4 in the ISO 216 A series of paper sizes.

A4 is a paper size defined in the international standard ISO 216, with dimensions of 210 × 297 millimetres (8.27 × 11.69 inches). It is the most commonly used paper size in most countries of the world, including for letters, magazines, forms, and office documents. The principal exceptions are the United States, Canada, and parts of Latin America, where the similar but slightly different Letter size (8.5 × 11 in) predominates.

The A series

The A series is based on a rectangle with an aspect ratio of 1:√2, chosen so that folding a sheet in half along its long side produces two sheets of the next smaller size with the same proportions. The largest size, A0, has an area of exactly one square metre. Each successive size halves the area: A1 is half of A0, A2 half of A1, and so on. A4 is therefore one sixteenth of a square metre in area, and a standard sheet of 80 g/m² A4 paper weighs exactly 5 grams.

History

The advantages of the √2 format were noted as early as 1786 by the German scientist Georg Christoph Lichtenberg. The system was developed in Germany in the early 20th century by Walter Porstmann and adopted as DIN 476 in 1922. It spread through Europe and was formalised internationally as ISO 216 in 1975. The A4 size itself had been anticipated by the French grand registre format of the revolutionary era.

Usage

A4 is the standard size for business letters in most of the world, and it is the default paper size for printers and photocopiers outside North America. United Nations and other international bodies use A4 for official documents. In the United Kingdom, A4 replaced the older foolscap folio size for most office purposes during the late 20th century.

Tolerances and related sizes

ISO 216 specifies manufacturing tolerances of ±1.5 mm for dimensions up to 150 mm, ±2 mm for lengths between 150 and 600 mm, and ±3 mm above that. Related series include the B series, whose sizes are geometric means between successive A sizes, and the C series, used for envelopes designed to hold A-series sheets — a C4 envelope holds a flat A4 sheet, while a DL envelope holds A4 folded in thirds.
