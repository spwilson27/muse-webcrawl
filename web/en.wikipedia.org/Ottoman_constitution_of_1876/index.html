Ottoman constitution of 1876

The Ottoman constitution of 1876 (Turkish: Kanûn-u Esâsî, "Basic Law"; Ottoman Turkish: قانون اساسی) was the first constitution of the Ottoman Empire, promulgated on 23 December 1876. Drafted largely under the leadership of Midhat Pasha and the Young Ottomans, it inaugurated the First Constitutional Era and established a constitutional monarchy with an elected parliament.

Contents

The constitution declared the indivisibility of the empire, recognized the sultan as caliph and sovereign, but guaranteed Ottoman subjects personal liberty, equality before the law, freedom of the press within legal limits, and security of property. Islam was the state religion, Turkish the official language, and the document created a bicameral General Assembly: an appointed Senate (Heyet-i Ayan) and an elected Chamber of Deputies (Heyet-i Mebusan). However, the notorious Article 113 allowed the sultan to exile anyone deemed a threat to the state, a provision used against Midhat Pasha himself.

Suspension and restoration

Elections were held in 1877, and parliament sat twice, but Sultan Abdülhamid II suspended the constitution in February 1878, citing the war with Russia, and ruled autocratically for thirty years. The constitution was restored by the Young Turk Revolution of July 1908, opening the Second Constitutional Era, and was substantially amended in 1909 to strengthen parliament and limit the sultan's powers. It remained nominally in force until superseded in the Turkish Republic era by the constitution of 1921/1924.
