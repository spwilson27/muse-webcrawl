La Llorona

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In Mexican folklore, La Llorona (Spanish for "The Weeping Woman") is a vengeful ghost who wanders waterfront areas mourning her drowned children. The legend has a wide variety across the Spanish-speaking Americas and is one of the most famous ghost stories in Latin American culture.

The legend

In the most common version, a beautiful woman named María marries a wealthy man and bears him children, but he abandons her for a younger woman. In rage or despair, María drowns their children in a river and then drowns herself. Turned away from the afterlife, she is condemned to wander the earth searching for her lost children, weeping "¡Ay, mis hijos!" ("Oh, my children!"). Children are warned that La Llorona will snatch them if they venture out at night or near water — and she is said to kidnap wandering children who resemble her own.

Origins and influence

The story's roots may combine colonial-era Spanish tales with pre-Hispanic Aztec figures such as the goddess Cihuacōātl, who was said to weep for her children. La Llorona has inspired songs, plays, films — including The Curse of La Llorona (2019) in the Conjuring universe — and remains a living part of oral tradition across Mexico and the southwestern United States.
