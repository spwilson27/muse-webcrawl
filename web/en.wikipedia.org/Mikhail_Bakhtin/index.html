Mikhail Mikhailovich Bakhtin (17 November 1895 – 7 March 1975) was a Russian philosopher, literary critic and scholar who worked on literary theory, ethics and the philosophy of language. His writings, rediscovered in the 1960s, have influenced literary studies, cultural theory, linguistics and anthropology.

Life and works

Born in Oryol, Bakhtin studied classics and philology and gathered a circle of intellectuals — the "Bakhtin Circle" — whose members, including Valentin Voloshinov and Pavel Medvedev, published works whose authorship remains disputed. His book on Dostoevsky, Problems of Dostoevsky's Poetics (1929; revised 1963), introduced the concept of the polyphonic novel, in which characters' voices are not subordinated to the author's single viewpoint. Arrested in 1929, he spent years in exile in Kazakhstan and in provincial teaching posts, and his study of Rabelais, published in 1965 as Rabelais and His World, developed his celebrated account of carnival and popular laughter in medieval culture.

Key concepts

Dialogism holds that meaning arises between voices in dialogue. Heteroglossia describes the plurality of social languages within a single national language — the basis, for Bakhtin, of the novel's artistry. The carnivalesque names carnival's temporary inversion of hierarchy, and the chronotope the intrinsic connectedness of time and space in narrative. These ideas, developed in essays collected in The Dialogic Imagination, reshaped the study of the novel.
