Hulagu Khan

Hulagu Khan (c. 1215 – 8 February 1265) was a Mongol ruler who conquered much of West Asia. A grandson of Genghis Khan and brother of Möngke and Kublai Khan, he founded the Ilkhanate of Persia and is infamous for the sack of Baghdad in 1258.

Hulagu, founder of the Ilkhanate, led the Mongol conquest of Persia and Iraq.

Conquest of Persia and Iraq

Charged by his brother, the Great Khan Möngke, with subduing the Muslim lands, Hulagu set out in 1253 with a vast army. He destroyed the Nizari Ismaili stronghold of Alamut in 1256, then marched on Baghdad. After a brief siege in February 1258 the city fell; the Abbasid Caliph al-Musta'sim was executed, and the ensuing massacre and destruction ended Baghdad's era as the intellectual capital of Islam.

Syria and Ain Jalut

Hulagu's forces took Aleppo and Damascus in 1260, but after Möngke's death he withdrew with the main army. His general Kitbuqa was defeated by the Mamluks of Egypt at the Battle of Ain Jalut (1260) — the first major check to Mongol expansion in the region.

The Ilkhanate

Ruling from Tabriz and Maragheh, Hulagu founded the Ilkhanate, corresponded with European rulers about an alliance against the Mamluks, patronised the astronomer Nasir al-Din al-Tusi and his Maragheh observatory, and warred with his cousin Berke of the Golden Horde. He died in 1265 and was succeeded by his son Abaqa.
