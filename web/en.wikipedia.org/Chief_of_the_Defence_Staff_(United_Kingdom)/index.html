Chief of the Defence Staff (United Kingdom)


Chief of the Defence Staff

The Chief of the Defence Staff (CDS) is the professional head of the British Armed Forces and the most senior uniformed military adviser to the Secretary of State for Defence and the Prime Minister of the United Kingdom. The post was created in 1959, during the implementation of the reforms recommended by the 1958 Defence White Paper, as a means of improving coordination between the three armed services.

The CDS sits at the head of the Chiefs of Staff Committee, alongside the Vice-Chief of the Defence Staff and the professional heads of the three single services: the First Sea Lord (Royal Navy), the Chief of the General Staff (British Army) and the Chief of the Air Staff (Royal Air Force). The CDS is a member of the Defence Council and the Defence Board.

History

The position was established on 1 January 1959, when Field Marshal Sir William Dickson, formerly Chairman of the Chiefs of Staff Committee, became the first Chief of the Defence Staff. The post was created in the wake of reforms intended to strengthen central control of defence policy and to bring the service chiefs together under a single senior officer. In 1964 the functions of the service ministries were absorbed into an enlarged Ministry of Defence, and the CDS became the senior military officer within the new unified ministry.

Since the Levene reforms of 2011, the CDS has held a clearer role as the government's principal military adviser, while the Permanent Under Secretary remains the senior civilian official in the Ministry of Defence.

Appointment

Chiefs of the Defence Staff are appointed by the sovereign on the advice of the Prime Minister, to whom the Secretary of State for Defence is responsible. The CDS is usually a four-star officer drawn from one of the three services, and appointments have rotated between the services, though officers of any service may serve when selected. The term of office is normally around three years, though it may be extended.

Notable holders

Prominent holders of the post have included Admiral of the Fleet Earl Mountbatten of Burma (1959–1965), Field Marshal Lord Bramall (1982–1985), Admiral Lord Boyce (2001–2003), General Lord Walker, Air Chief Marshal Lord Stirrup (2006–2010), General Lord Richards (2010–2013), General Lord Houghton (2013–2016), Air Chief Marshal Lord Peach (2016–2018), General Sir Nick Carter (2018–2021) and Admiral Sir Tony Radakin, who took up the appointment in November 2021.
