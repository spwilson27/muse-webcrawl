The toothed whales (suborder Odontoceti) are a group of cetaceans characterised by having teeth, in contrast to the baleen whales (Mysticeti), which filter-feed through baleen plates. The group includes the oceanic dolphins, porpoises, river dolphins, sperm whales, beaked whales, the narwhal and beluga, and the orca (killer whale). Toothed whales are active predators of fish, squid and, in some species, other marine mammals, and all are capable of echolocation.

Anatomy and echolocation

Toothed whales have a single blowhole (baleen whales have two) and a markedly asymmetrical skull housing the melon, a fatty organ in the forehead used to focus clicks for echolocation. Sound is generated in the nasal passages and received through the lower jaw, allowing the animals to image their surroundings in darkness and murky water. Teeth are typically simple conical pegs used for grasping rather than chewing; counts range from a single tusk in the male narwhal to over 200 small teeth in some dolphins. The sperm whale, the largest toothed predator on Earth, reaches about 16–20 metres; the smallest, the vaquita, is about 1.4 metres.

Behaviour and intelligence

Many toothed whales are highly social, living in pods with complex vocal communication; orcas have distinct, culturally transmitted dialects and hunting traditions. Dolphins and orcas rank among the most intelligent animals, showing self-recognition, cooperative hunting and tool use (such as sponge-carrying in Shark Bay bottlenose dolphins). Toothed whales synchronise a large proportion of their brain's sleep between hemispheres, allowing them to keep surfacing to breathe.

Evolution and conservation

Odontocetes diverged from baleen whales in the Oligocene, roughly 30–35 million years ago, and early forms such as the shark-toothed dolphins preyed in ancient seas. Today several species are threatened: the vaquita is critically endangered from gillnet bycatch, the baiji river dolphin was declared functionally extinct in 2006, and noise pollution, ship strikes and plastic ingestion affect many populations.
