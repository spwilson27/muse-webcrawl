Golden Vale


Pastureland in the Golden Vale.

The Golden Vale (Irish: Machaire Méith na Mumhan) is the richest dairying and agricultural land in Ireland, a low-lying plain stretching across parts of counties Tipperary, Limerick and Cork in the province of Munster.

Geography

The vale lies between the Galtee Mountains and Slieve Felim to the north and the Ballyhoura Mountains to the south, centred roughly on Tipperary town and the valley of the River Suir. Its deep, fertile limestone soils, mild climate and ample rainfall produce lush permanent pasture ideal for cattle grazing.

Agriculture

The region is the heartland of Ireland's dairy industry and historically of its butter trade; the co-operative creameries of the Golden Vale gave their name to the food company Golden Vale plc, later absorbed into the Kerry Group. Beef production, horse breeding and tillage are also significant. Towns associated with the vale include Cashel, Cahir, Mitchelstown, Charleville and Tipperary.
