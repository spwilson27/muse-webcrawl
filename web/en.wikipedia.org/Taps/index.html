Taps

"Taps" is a bugle call played at dusk, during flag ceremonies, and at military funerals in the United States. The melody, consisting of 24 notes drawn from the harmonic series, is one of the most recognisable pieces of American military music.

History

The call is generally credited to Union Army Brigadier General Daniel Butterfield, who in July 1862, during the American Civil War at Harrison's Landing, Virginia, adapted an earlier call — the "Scott Tattoo" — with the help of his bugler, Oliver Wilcox Norton. Butterfield sought a more melodious and peaceful call to signal "lights out" at the end of the day. The new call spread quickly to both Union and Confederate forces. It was officially recognised by the United States Army in 1874.

Use at funerals

"Taps" became associated with military funerals during the Civil War; legend holds that it was first played over a grave when a Union captain ordered it in place of the customary three rifle volleys, fearing gunfire might provoke an attack. It is now played at memorial services, wreath-layings, and the daily lowering of the flag, and at events such as Memorial Day commemorations. Boy Scouts and Girl Scouts in the United States also use it to close camp days.

Lyrics

Although "Taps" has no official lyrics, several verses are traditionally associated with it. The best known begins:

Day is done, gone the sun,
From the lake, from the hills, from the sky;
All is well, safely rest, God is nigh.
