Overline

An overline is a typographical feature — a horizontal line drawn above text — analogous to an underline, which is drawn below. Overlines are used in mathematics, logic, linguistics and computing for several distinct purposes. The overline is not to be confused with the macron (a diacritic placed over a single letter, as in ā) or with the vinculum used over repeating decimals and radicals.

x̄   ĀB̄   0.3̄

Uses

Mathematics

  • Repeating decimals: an overline (vinculum) over a group of digits indicates that they repeat indefinitely, as in 0.123̄ = 0.123123123…
  • Complex conjugate: the conjugate of a complex number z is written z̄.
  • Mean: in statistics, x̄ ("x-bar") denotes the arithmetic mean of a sample.
  • Negation and closure: in logic and set theory the overline may denote complement or topological closure; in Boolean algebra Ā denotes NOT A.
  • Line segments: in geometry, AB̄ denotes the segment joining points A and B.
  • Roman numerals: a vinculum over a numeral multiplies its value by 1,000.

Other fields

In meteorology the overline appears in the notation for averages. In particle physics, an overline distinguishes antiparticles (e.g. the antiproton p̄). In linguistics and philology, overlines have been used in transliteration systems, and in older handwriting an overline over m or n doubled the letter.

In computing

Unicode provides the combining overline U+0305 (̅), which follows the character it modifies, as well as the spacing macron U+00AF and the overline character U+203E (‾). In HTML, overlined text can be produced with the CSS property text-decoration: overline. The markup language TeX supports the command \overline{...} for mathematical overlines.
