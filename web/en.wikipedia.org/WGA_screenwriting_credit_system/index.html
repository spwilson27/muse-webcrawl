WGA screenwriting credit system

On-screen writing credits as determined under the WGA system.

The WGA screenwriting credit system is the set of rules administered by the Writers Guild of America (WGA) that determines how screenwriters are credited on films and television programmes produced under its jurisdiction. Because screen credits affect residuals, bonuses, and reputation, the Guild's rules specify both the forms of credit and a binding arbitration process for disputes.

Forms of credit

The principal credits include:

  • "Written by" — awarded when the same writer (or team) is entitled to both "Story by" and "Screenplay by" credit.
  • "Screenplay by" — for the writer(s) of the screenplay when the story credit goes to another writer or source.
  • "Story by" — for the writer(s) who supplied the story on which the screenplay is based.
  • "Screen Story by" and "Television Story by" — variants used in particular production contexts.

The conjunctions between names carry meaning. An ampersand ("&") joins a writing team who worked together, while "and" separates writers who worked independently at different times. Typically no more than three writers or teams may share a screenplay credit.

Contribution thresholds and arbitration

To receive credit, a writer must have contributed a minimum percentage of the final script — generally more than 33 percent for an original screenplay, and a higher threshold for non-original screenplays and for production executives (such as directors and producers) who write, who must meet stricter standards. When more writers claim credit than the rules allow, or when a participating writer disputes the studio's proposed credits, the Guild convenes an automatic arbitration in which anonymous arbiters read all drafts and a statement of credits, and decide the final attribution. Guild rules also permit the use of approved pseudonyms, a provision famously invoked by blacklisted writers during the McCarthy era.
