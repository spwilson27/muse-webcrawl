Aerobic exercise


Aerobics class participants

Aerobic exercise (also known as endurance activities, cardio or cardio-respiratory exercise) is physical exercise of low to high intensity that depends primarily on the aerobic energy-generating process. "Aerobic" is defined as "relating to, involving, or requiring oxygen", and refers to the use of oxygen to meet energy demands during exercise via aerobic metabolism adequately. Aerobic exercise is performed by repeating sequences of light-to-moderate intensity activities for extended periods of time.

Examples of cardiovascular or aerobic exercise are medium- to long-distance running or jogging, swimming, cycling, stair climbing and walking. According to the World Health Organization, physical inactivity has caused approximately 6% of the burden of disease from coronary heart disease worldwide.

History

The concept of aerobic exercise was popularized by Kenneth H. Cooper, an Air Force physician, whose 1968 book Aerobics promoted exercise forms such as running, swimming, cycling and walking to improve cardiovascular health. In the 1980s, fitness instructor Jackie Sorensen and later Judi Sheppard Missett (with Jazzercise) and Jane Fonda popularized aerobics classes accompanied by music.

Benefits

Regular aerobic exercise improves cardiovascular fitness, strengthens the heart muscle, increases lung capacity, and is associated with reduced risk of heart disease, stroke, type 2 diabetes and some cancers. It also improves mood and sleep, and helps with weight management.
