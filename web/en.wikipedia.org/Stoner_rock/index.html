Stoner rock

The Californian desert, home of the Palm Desert scene

Stoner rock, also known as stoner metal or desert rock, is a rock music fusion genre that combines elements of doom metal, psychedelic rock, and acid rock. The genre emerged during the early 1990s and was pioneered foremost by Californian bands such as Kyuss, Sleep, and Fu Manchu.

Characteristics

Stoner rock typically features slow-to-mid tempo, heavily distorted, groove-laden bass-heavy sound, melodic vocals, and "retro" production. Guitarists commonly tune low and play repetitive riffs through large amplifier stacks, drawing on Black Sabbath and Blue Cheer. The term "desert rock" is often used interchangeably, reflecting the Palm Desert scene in California.

History

Rooted in 1970s heavy rock and the doom of Saint Vitus and the Melvins, the genre coalesced around Palm Desert's generator parties. Kyuss's albums Blues for the Red Sun (1992) and Welcome to Sky Valley (1994) and Sleep's Holy Mountain (1992) are landmark releases; Sleep's hour-long Dopesmoker became a cult landmark. Former Kyuss members later formed Queens of the Stone Age, who brought the sound to mainstream audiences.
