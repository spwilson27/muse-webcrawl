Nyckelharpa

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A modern three-row chromatic nyckelharpa

The nyckelharpa (Swedish for "keyed fiddle") is a traditional Swedish string instrument. It is played with a bow while a row of keys, pressed by the fingers of the left hand, touches the melody strings to change their pitch. The modern instrument typically has 16 strings: four bowed melody and accompaniment strings and twelve sympathetic resonance strings that vibrate freely and enrich the sound.

History

Depictions of keyed fiddles appear in Swedish church paintings from the 14th century, notably at Källunge Church on Gotland (c. 1350) and at Härkeberga Church in Uppland. The instrument survived in an unbroken tradition in the province of Uppland, where it became central to Swedish folk music. Early forms had fewer strings and a single row of keys; the chromatic three-row nyckelharpa was developed in the 20th century by makers and players such as Eric Sahlström.

Technique and repertoire

The player cradles the instrument on the lap or against the body, bowing with the right hand while the left hand's fingers press the tangent keys. Its repertoire includes polskas, marches, and wedding tunes from the Uppland tradition.

Revival

Since the folk revival of the 1960s and 1970s, the nyckelharpa has spread far beyond Sweden, with players and makers across Europe, North America, and Japan. In 2013 the network of nyckelharpa traditions in Sweden was inscribed in national heritage inventories.
