Telecommunication

Telecommunication is the transmission of information over distances for the purpose of communication, using technologies such as wire, radio, optical fibre and other electromagnetic systems. The word derives from the Greek tele ("far") and the Latin communicare ("to share").

History

Early telecommunications relied on visual signals — smoke, beacons and semaphore lines such as the Chappe network in revolutionary France. Electrical communication began with the telegraph in the 1830s–40s, followed by the telephone (1876), wireless radio around 1900, television, satellite links, and finally digital networks and the Internet, which now carry most telecommunications traffic.

Basic elements

A telecommunication system consists of a transmitter that encodes information into a signal, a transmission medium or channel, and a receiver that decodes it. Signals may be analogue or digital, and modern networks use switching and multiplexing to carry many communications over shared infrastructure, organised according to standardised protocols.

Networks and regulation

Telecommunications services are provided by operators running fixed and mobile networks, submarine cables and satellite systems. International coordination of radio spectrum and standards is led by the International Telecommunication Union (ITU), a specialised agency of the United Nations.
