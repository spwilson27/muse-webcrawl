Quantum network

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A quantum network is a network that enables the transmission of quantum information between physically separated quantum processors, for example by distributing entanglement between distant nodes. Quantum networks form an important element of quantum computing and quantum communication systems, sometimes described as a future "quantum internet".

Components

Because quantum signals cannot be amplified without destroying them (the no-cloning theorem), long-distance quantum networks require quantum repeaters: nodes that generate entanglement over short links, store it in quantum memories, and connect the links by entanglement swapping. End nodes range from simple measurement devices used for quantum key distribution to full fault-tolerant quantum computers.

Applications

Proposed applications include networked and distributed quantum computing, quantum-enhanced clock networks and telescope arrays, secure communication via QKD, and blind quantum computing, in which a client delegates a computation to a server without revealing the data or algorithm.

Status

Experimental testbeds have linked several quantum processors within laboratories and across metropolitan fibre, and satellite links have distributed entanglement over more than a thousand kilometres. Large-scale deployment remains a research challenge centred on memory coherence times, repeater rates, and interfacing different qubit technologies.
