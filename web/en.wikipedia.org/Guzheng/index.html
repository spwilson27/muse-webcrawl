Guzheng
Chinese古筝 (gǔzhēng)
ClassificationPlucked zither; chordophone
Strings21 (modern standard; historically 12–26)
TuningPentatonic, commonly D major
OriginChina, over 2,500 years of history

The guzheng (古筝), also known simply as the zheng, is a Chinese plucked zither and one of the most ancient and popular instruments of Chinese music. It consists of a long hollow wooden body over which strings are stretched across individual movable bridges; the player plucks the strings with the right hand — traditionally with plectra taped to the fingers — while the left hand presses strings to produce vibrato, pitch bends and ornaments.

History

The zheng was already popular in the state of Qin during the Warring States period (475–221 BCE); some trace its ancestry to the se. Early instruments had 12 or 13 strings of silk. Metal and nylon-wound strings expanded its volume and range, and by the mid-20th century the 21-string guzheng became standard, tuned pentatonically.

Technique and repertoire

Regional schools — Henan, Shandong, Chaozhou, Hakka and Zhejiang — developed distinctive styles and repertoires. Signature techniques include tremolo (yaoyin), glissando cascades and left-hand pitch-bending (rou, an, hua). Modern compositions extend the instrument's range, and guzheng is now taught worldwide and featured in film scores and fusion music.
