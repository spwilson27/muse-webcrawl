The Cossack Hetmanate (Ukrainian: Гетьманщина), officially the Zaporozhian Host, was a Cossack state in central Ukraine between 1648 and 1764 (with administrative remnants to 1782). It was founded by Hetman Bohdan Khmelnytsky during his uprising against the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth and is regarded as a foundational episode in Ukrainian statehood.

Territory of the Hetmanate in the later 17th century, organised into regiments along the Dnieper.

History

The Khmelnytsky Uprising of 1648 drove Polish authority from much of Ukraine; the Treaty of Zboriv (1649) recognised Cossack self-government in three palatinates. Seeking allies against Poland, Khmelnytsky concluded the Treaty of Pereyaslav (1654) with the Tsardom of Russia, a step whose interpretation remains contested. After Khmelnytsky's death in 1657 the state descended into the civil war known as the Ruin; the Truce of Andrusovo (1667) split Ukraine along the Dnieper between Poland and Russia. Hetman Ivan Mazepa's alliance with Sweden's Charles XII ended in defeat at Poltava (1709), after which Russian control tightened: the Collegium of Little Russia supervised the hetmans, and Catherine II forced the last hetman, Kyrylo Rozumovsky, to resign in 1764. The regimental-hundred administrative system was abolished in 1782 and the Zaporozhian Sich destroyed in 1775.

Government and society

The Hetmanate was administered through a military-territorial hierarchy of regiments (polky) and companies (sotni), with officers (starshyna) elected in councils (radas). It maintained its own courts, finances, army of registered Cossacks, and foreign diplomacy, and fostered a flourishing Ukrainian Baroque culture centred on institutions such as the Kyiv-Mohyla Academy.Over time the officer class consolidated into a landowning nobility while ordinary Cossacks and peasants lost status, fuelling later haidamak revolts in Polish-held Right-Bank Ukraine.
