Appleton, Wisconsin

Country: United States
State: Wisconsin
Counties: Outagamie, Calumet, Winnebago
Population: approx. 76,000 (2020)

Appleton is a city in northeastern Wisconsin, United States, lying on the Fox River about 30 miles southwest of Green Bay. It is the principal city of the Fox Cities metropolitan area and the county seat of Outagamie County, with portions extending into Calumet and Winnebago counties.

History

Appleton was founded in 1847 alongside Lawrence University, one of the oldest coeducational liberal arts colleges in the country. Water power from the Fox River drove an early paper industry, and in 1882 the Vulcan Street Plant, one of the world's first hydroelectric central stations, began operation under Thomas Edison's patents. The city was the childhood home of escape artist Harry Houdini, who claimed it as his birthplace, and of Senator Joseph McCarthy.

Economy and culture

Paper manufacturing remains important alongside insurance, health care and logistics. Attractions include the History Museum at the Castle, the Fox Cities Performing Arts Center, and Hearthstone Historic House Museum, the first home in the world lit by a central hydroelectric station.
