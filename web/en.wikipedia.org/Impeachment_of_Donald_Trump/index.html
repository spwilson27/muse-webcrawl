Impeachment of Donald Trump

Donald Trump, the 45th president of the United States, was impeached twice by the U.S. House of Representatives — the only U.S. president to be impeached twice. He was acquitted by the Senate on both occasions.

First impeachment (2019–2020)

The first impeachment followed revelations that Trump had solicited foreign interference from Ukraine to investigate his political rival Joe Biden, withholding military aid and a White House meeting as leverage. A whistleblower complaint in August 2019 prompted a formal inquiry by Speaker Nancy Pelosi in September 2019. On December 18, 2019, the House impeached Trump on two articles: abuse of power (230–197) and obstruction of Congress (229–198). After a Senate trial, he was acquitted on February 5, 2020, with Senator Mitt Romney the only Republican voting to convict on one article.

Second impeachment (2021)

The second impeachment followed the January 6, 2021 attack on the U.S. Capitol by Trump supporters after he had disputed the results of the 2020 presidential election. On January 13, 2021 — one week before his term ended — the House impeached Trump for "incitement of insurrection" by a vote of 232–197, with ten Republicans joining Democrats, making it the most bipartisan impeachment in U.S. history. The Senate trial took place after he left office; on February 13, 2021, Trump was acquitted when 57 senators voted to convict, ten short of the two-thirds majority required, though seven Republicans voted guilty — the largest bipartisan conviction vote in a presidential impeachment trial.

Background

Calls for Trump's impeachment had begun during his first year in office, and the 2019 inquiry into Ukraine, together with the Mueller investigation into Russian interference in the 2016 election, formed the political backdrop. The impeachments deepened partisan divisions and led to continuing debates about presidential accountability and the scope of the impeachment power.
