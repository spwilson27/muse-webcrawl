Stonemasonry

A stonemason at work

Stonemasonry or stonecraft is the creation of buildings, structures, and sculpture using stone as the primary material. Stonemasons have existed since humanity first sought to build durable dwellings and commemorate the dead; stone construction is among the oldest building technologies, exemplified by monuments such as the pyramids of Giza and stone circles of prehistoric Europe.

Trades and techniques

The craft divides broadly into banker masons, who cut and dress stone in a workshop, and fixer masons, who set it on site. Techniques include quarrying, roughing out, dressing to ashlar, carving ornament and lettering, and laying stone in walls with or without mortar (dry stone work).

Materials and tools

Common building stones include limestone, sandstone, granite, marble, and slate. Traditional tools — mallet, chisels, pitching tools, squares, and plumb rules — have been supplemented by saws, pneumatic hammers, and CNC machinery, though hand-finishing remains central to restoration work.

History

Medieval cathedral building made stonemasons among the most skilled and mobile of craftsmen, organised in lodges and guilds. The craft declined with the rise of brick, steel, and concrete, but survives in conservation, memorial work, and bespoke building.
