A mangrove is a shrub or tree that grows in dense thickets or forests along coastal saline or brackish water in the tropics and subtropics. The term also denotes the whole intertidal habitat — the mangrove forest or mangal. Mangroves thrive in low-oxygen, waterlogged muds where fine sediments accumulate in sheltered shorelines, and they are adapted to daily tidal inundation by salt water, conditions lethal to most plants.

Adaptations

Mangroves cope with salinity by excluding salt at their roots (as in Rhizophora), excreting it through glands on their leaves (as in Avicennia), or concentrating it in old leaves that are shed. To breathe in anaerobic mud, many species have aerial roots: the arching stilt (prop) roots of Rhizophora, the pencil-like pneumatophores rising from the mud around Avicennia and Sonneratia, and the knee roots of Bruguiera. These roots are covered in lenticels that admit air at low tide. Many mangroves are viviparous: their seeds germinate while still attached to the parent tree, producing buoyant, torpedo-shaped propagules that float until they strand in mud and take root.

Ecology

Mangrove forests support rich food webs. Fallen leaves feed detritus-based communities of crabs, shrimp, and molluscs; the root systems shelter juvenile fish — including commercially important species — making mangals vital nurseries. They protect coasts from storm surge and erosion, trap sediments, and store exceptionally large amounts of carbon ("blue carbon") in their waterlogged soils. Mangroves occur in some 120 countries; the largest extent is in Indonesia, and the Sundarbans of Bangladesh and India form the world's biggest single mangrove forest, home to Bengal tigers and the Irrawaddy dolphin.

Threats

Globally, mangrove area has declined substantially since 1980 through conversion to shrimp aquaculture, palm oil and rice farming, coastal development, and upstream changes to freshwater flow. Losses have slowed, and protection and restoration projects — sometimes using hydrological restoration rather than simple planting — are under way, but sea-level rise remains a long-term threat where landward migration is blocked by development.
