Old age

Old age is the range of ages for people nearing and surpassing the human life expectancy. People of old age are also referred to as old people, elderly, elders, senior citizens, seniors, or older adults. Old age is not a definite biological stage: the chronological ages denoted as "old age" vary culturally and historically, though the United Nations generally treats 60 years and over as older persons, and many developed countries use 65, associated with retirement and pension eligibility.

Definitions

Academic gerontology distinguishes the "young old" (roughly 65–74), the "old old" (75–84), and the "oldest old" (85+). Functional definitions emphasise frailty and dependence rather than chronological age. There is no general agreement on the age at which a person becomes old.

Physical and cognitive changes

Ageing brings gradual changes: reduced muscle mass and bone density (sarcopenia and osteoporosis), cardiovascular and sensory decline, slower cognitive processing, and increased risk of chronic disease, dementia, and frailty. The burden of these changes varies widely between individuals and is influenced by genetics, lifestyle, and environment.

Social aspects

Old age commonly involves retirement, changing family roles, bereavement, and questions of housing, care, and income security. Societies respond through pension systems, healthcare provision, and elder care, whether familial or institutional. Population ageing — rising median ages driven by low fertility and longer lives — is a defining demographic trend of the 21st century.

Attitudes

Attitudes toward old age range from veneration of elders' wisdom to ageism and stereotyping. Research on successful ageing emphasises health, engagement, and social connection.
