Tian Shan
Highest point: Jengish Chokusu (7,439 m)
Length: ~2,500 km
Countries: China, Kyrgyzstan, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Tajikistan

The Tian Shan (Chinese: 天山, pinyin Tiān Shān, "Mountains of Heaven" or "Heavenly Mountains") is a large mountain range in Central Asia. It stretches about 2,500 kilometres east–west through western China, Kyrgyzstan, Kazakhstan and neighbouring states, north of the Taklamakan Desert.

Geography

The highest peak is Jengish Chokusu (Victory Peak, 7,439 m) on the Kyrgyzstan–China border, followed by Khan Tengri (7,010 m). Large glaciers include the Inylchek Glacier. Important lakes fed by its meltwater include Issyk-Kul in Kyrgyzstan, one of the world's largest alpine lakes.

History and ecology

The range formed from the collision of the Indian and Eurasian plates. Historic routes of the Silk Road passed through its valleys. It is a UNESCO World Heritage site in both Xinjiang and the western Tian-Shan, noted for snow leopards, argali and diverse alpine ecosystems.
