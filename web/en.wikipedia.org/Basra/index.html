Basra (Arabic: ٱلْبَصْرَة, al-Baṣrah) is the capital city of Basra Governorate in southern Iraq and the country's principal seaport, located on the Shatt al-Arab waterway about 110 kilometres from the Persian Gulf. It is Iraq's second- or third-largest city and the historic centre of its oil industry and date trade.

History

Basra was founded in 636–638 AD as a garrison camp (misr) by the caliph Umar during the Muslim conquest of Persia. Under the Umayyads and Abbasids it became a great commercial port and a centre of learning — the grammarians of the Basra school, the Mu'tazilite theologians, and the Brethren of Purity were associated with the city, and it is the legendary home port of Sinbad the Sailor. The city suffered in the Zanj Rebellion (869–883) and was sacked by the Qarmatians in 923. After centuries under Ottoman rule, with interruptions, Basra was occupied by British forces in 1914 and became Iraq's main outlet to the sea. It was heavily fought over in the Iran–Iraq War (1980–88), the 1991 Gulf War, and the 2003 invasion, after which British forces administered southern Iraq. Since 2003 recurrent protests over water salinity, electricity shortages, corruption, and unemployment have made Basra a focal point of Iraqi discontent.

Geography and economy

Basra has a hot desert climate with some of the highest recorded summer temperatures on Earth. Its economy rests on oil — the nearby Rumaila and other giant fields, exported through Gulf terminals — together with shipping, dates, and petrochemicals.
