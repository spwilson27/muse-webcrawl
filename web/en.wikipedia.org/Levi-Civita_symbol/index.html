Levi-Civita symbol

The Levi-Civita symbol εijk, named after the Italian mathematician Tullio Levi-Civita, is a quantity defined from the sign of a permutation of the natural numbers 1, 2, 3 (or a general ordered set of indices), used in tensor analysis.

Named after: Tullio Levi-Civita
Values: +1, 0, −1
Used in: Tensor calculus, vector calculus

Definition

In three dimensions, εijk is +1 if (i, j, k) is an even permutation of (1, 2, 3), −1 if it is an odd permutation, and 0 if any index is repeated. It is totally antisymmetric: swapping any two indices changes its sign.

Applications

The symbol gives compact formulas for the cross product, a × b, and the scalar triple product (the determinant of three vectors). It appears throughout vector calculus identities, electromagnetism, fluid mechanics and general relativity, where its generalised form — the Levi-Civita tensor, scaled by the square root of the metric determinant — is a true tensor.

Relations

A key identity contracts two symbols into Kronecker deltas: εilmεjlm = δijδll − δilδlj (with summation). Generalisations to n dimensions and to arbitrary index sets follow the same permutation-sign rule.
