Lakshmana Sena

Reign: c. 1178–1206 CE
Dynasty: Sena
Predecessor: Ballala Sena
Capital: Nadia / Vikrampura

Lakshmana Sena (Bengali: লক্ষ্মণ সেন; reigned c. 1178–1206) was the fourth and last notable ruler of the Hindu Sena dynasty of Bengal, succeeding his father Ballala Sena.

Reign

Inscriptions credit Lakshmana Sena with campaigns extending Sena power toward Kamarupa, Kalinga, Kashi, and Prayaga early in his reign. His court at Vikrampura and Nadia was a centre of Sanskrit learning; the poet Jayadeva, author of the Gita Govinda, is traditionally associated with it, as are the scholars Halayudha and Umapatidhara.

Fall

Around 1203–1204, the Turkic general Muhammad Bakhtiyar Khalji raided Nadia, traditionally said to have entered the city with a small party of horsemen mistaken for horse traders. Lakshmana Sena fled to eastern Bengal, where he and his descendants continued to rule for some decades. The conquest began the Muslim rule of Bengal.
