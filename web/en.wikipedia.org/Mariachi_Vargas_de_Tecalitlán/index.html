Mariachi Vargas de Tecalitlán
Origin: Tecalitlán, Jalisco, Mexico
Genres: Mariachi, ranchera, son jalisciense
Years active: 1897–present
Founder: Gaspar Vargas

Mariachi Vargas de Tecalitlán is a Mexican folk ensemble founded in 1897 by Gaspar Vargas in the town of Tecalitlán, Jalisco. Long billed as "el mejor mariachi del mundo" ("the best mariachi in the world"), it is one of the most influential groups in the history of mariachi music.

History

Under Gaspar Vargas and, from the 1930s, his son Silvestre Vargas, the group settled in Mexico City, became the house mariachi of radio station XEW, and backed the great ranchera singers of Mexican cinema's golden age, including Jorge Negrete, Pedro Infante and Javier Solís. Under musical director Rubén Fuentes (from 1944), the ensemble modernised the mariachi sound: refined string arrangements, written scores, and the addition of trumpets helped define the style heard today.

Style and influence

The classic Vargas lineup features violins, trumpets, vihuela, guitar, guitarrón and harp, performing sones, rancheras, boleros, polkas and huapangos. Successive "generations" of the group have toured internationally, recorded hundreds of albums, appeared in films, and inspired mariachi programmes in schools across the United States and Mexico. The ensemble has been directed in recent decades by members including José "Pepe" Martínez and continues to perform worldwide.
