Krzysztof Eugeniusz Penderecki (23 November 1933 – 29 March 2020) was a Polish composer and conductor. His best-known works include Threnody to the Victims of Hiroshima, the opera The Devils of Loudun, the Polish Requiem, and a cycle of eight symphonies.

Career

Penderecki studied at the Academy of Music in Kraków and rose to fame in 1959 when three of his works won the top prizes at the Polish Composers' Union young composers' competition. His Threnody (1960), for 52 string instruments, explored sonorism — clusters, glissandi and extended techniques — and became an emblem of the musical avant-garde; it later reached wide audiences through its use in films such as The Exorcist and The Shining. From the mid-1970s his style turned toward a neo-Romantic idiom drawing on Bruckner and Mahler, exemplified by his symphonies and the Polish Requiem (1980–84, expanded later).

Legacy

Penderecki taught in Poland, Germany and the United States (Yale), founded the European Centre for Music in Lusławice, and was also a passionate arborist, planting an extensive arboretum at his estate. He received numerous honours, including multiple Grammy Awards and honorary doctorates.
