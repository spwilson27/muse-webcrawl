Chinese grammar

Chinese grammar
Word order: Subject–verb–object
Typing: Analytic / isolating
Features: Measure words, aspect markers, topic prominence

The grammar of Chinese is largely analytic: words have little inflection, and grammatical relations are shown mainly by word order and particles. This article focuses on Standard Mandarin, whose basic word order is subject–verb–object.

Nouns and measure words

Nouns are not marked for number or case. When counted or specified, they normally require a measure word (classifier), as in sān běn shū, 'three [volume] book(s)'.

Verbs and aspect

Verbs do not conjugate for tense. Aspect is marked by particles such as le (completed), guo (experiential) and zhe (durative), and time is indicated by adverbs and context. Resultative complements and the bǎ and bèi constructions are characteristic features.

Sentences

Chinese is topic-prominent: sentences often begin with a topic followed by a comment. Questions are formed with particles such as ma or by choice-type (A-not-A) forms rather than word-order inversion.
