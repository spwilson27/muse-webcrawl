Shale

Shale is a fine-grained, clastic sedimentary rock formed from mud — a mixture of flakes of clay minerals and tiny fragments (silt-sized particles) of other minerals, especially quartz and calcite. It is characterized by its tendency to split into thin layers (fissility) along bedding planes.

Formation and properties

Shale forms in environments where fine sediment settles out of slow-moving water, such as deep ocean floors, lake bottoms, and floodplains, and is compacted over geological time. It is the most common sedimentary rock. Dark shales rich in organic matter (oil shales and source rocks) can generate petroleum and natural gas; hydraulic fracturing of shale formations underlies the modern "shale gas" and tight oil industries.

Uses

Shale is used in making bricks, tiles, and cement, and historically was retorted for shale oil. Famous fossil-bearing shales, such as the Burgess Shale of Canada, preserve exceptional records of early animal life.
