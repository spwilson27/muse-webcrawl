Otto Neurath

Otto Karl Wilhelm Neurath (10 December 1882 – 22 December 1945) was an Austrian philosopher of science, sociologist and political economist. He was a leading figure of the Vienna Circle of logical positivists, the inventor of the ISOTYPE pictorial language for statistics, and an advocate of the unity of science.

Career

Neurath took part in the Bavarian revolution of 1919 as a planner of socialisation, and afterwards directed the Museum of Society and Economy in Vienna, where with the artist Gerd Arntz he developed ISOTYPE — the International System of Typographic Picture Education — using repeated pictograms to make statistics legible to everyone. His restless encyclopedism drove the International Encyclopedia of Unified Science, for which he coined the famous image of science as a boat rebuilt plank by plank at sea.

Philosophy

Neurath defended physicalism — the view that all scientific statements can be formulated in a single spatiotemporal language — and attacked metaphysics and foundationalism; his "protocol sentence" debate with Rudolf Carnap and Moritz Schlick shaped logical empiricism. Fleeing Austria in 1934 and the Netherlands in 1940, he settled in Oxford, where he continued ISOTYPE work until his death in 1945.
