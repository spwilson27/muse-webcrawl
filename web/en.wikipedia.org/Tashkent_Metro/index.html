Tashkent Metro

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Tashkent Metro (Uzbek: Toshkent metropoliteni) is the rapid transit system serving Tashkent, the capital of Uzbekistan. Opened on 6 November 1977, it was the first metro system in Central Asia and the seventh built in the Soviet Union. The system is renowned for the lavish, individually themed decoration of its stations, each designed by leading Uzbek artists and architects with marble, ceramics, chandeliers and reliefs celebrating themes from cosmonautics to cotton production.

Network

The metro today comprises four lines — Chilonzor, Oʻzbekiston, Yunusobod and the elevated Ring (Halqa) line opened in 2020 — with over 40 stations and roughly 60 kilometres of track. Because the system was designed in part as a civil-defence shelter and earthquake-resistant structure following the devastating 1966 Tashkent earthquake, stations lie relatively deep and were built to withstand seismic shocks.

History

Planning began in 1968, two years after the earthquake, and construction of the first nine stations of the Chilonzor line followed. For decades photography inside the metro was forbidden because of its military-shelter status; the ban was lifted in 2018, after which the stations became a popular subject for travellers. The system carries several hundred thousand passengers daily.
