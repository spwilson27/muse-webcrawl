Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park

LocationHawaii County, Hawaii, United States
EstablishedAugust 1, 1916 (as Hawaii National Park)
FeaturesKīlauea and Mauna Loa volcanoes
DesignationUNESCO World Heritage Site (1987)

Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park is an American national park on the island of Hawaiʻi in the state of Hawaii. It protects two of the world's most active volcanoes, Kīlauea and Mauna Loa, and spans landscapes from sea level to the 13,677-foot (4,169 m) summit of Mauna Loa, including lava fields, rainforest, and the Kaʻū Desert.

History

The area was first protected in 1916 as part of Hawaii National Park; it became a separate park in 1961. It was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1987. Hawaiian cultural traditions regard Kīlauea's summit caldera, Halemaʻumaʻu, as the home of the volcano goddess Pele.

Volcanic activity

Kīlauea erupted nearly continuously from 1983 to 2018 from the Puʻu ʻŌʻō vent, adding new land to the island. In 2018 a major rift eruption and summit collapse dramatically reshaped the caldera, and eruptions have since resumed within Halemaʻumaʻu crater, often featuring lava lakes visible from park overlooks. Mauna Loa erupted in 2022 for the first time in 38 years.

Visiting

Highlights include Crater Rim Drive, the Chain of Craters Road descending to the coast, the Thurston Lava Tube (Nāhuku), and the Jaggar Museum area overlooks. Because conditions change with eruptions, parts of the park may close for safety.
