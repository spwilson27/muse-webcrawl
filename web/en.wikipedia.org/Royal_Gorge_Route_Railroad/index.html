Royal Gorge Route Railroad

Royal Gorge Route Railroad
Location: Cañon City, Colorado, United States
Route: Along the Arkansas River through the Royal Gorge
Service: Heritage excursion railroad (revived 1999)

The Royal Gorge Route Railroad is a heritage railroad located in Cañon City, Colorado. The railroad runs for about 24 miles round trip along the Arkansas River through the Royal Gorge, beneath the Royal Gorge Bridge — some 1,000 feet above the tracks — on a route once contested in the "Royal Gorge War" of the 1870s between the Denver & Rio Grande and Santa Fe railroads.

History of the line

The line through the gorge was completed by the Denver & Rio Grande in 1879 after litigation (and armed standoffs) resolved its rivalry with the Atchison, Topeka and Santa Fe. It formed part of the Rio Grande's main line to Leadville and over Tennessee Pass, carrying mining traffic and through passenger trains; the route was famous for the Hanging Bridge, built in 1879 where the gorge narrows so tightly that the bridge is suspended from girders anchored in the rock walls. Regular passenger service ended in 1967, and freight over Tennessee Pass ceased in 1997.

Excursion service

In 1998–1999 the tracks through the gorge were purchased and excursion trains were reintroduced as the Royal Gorge Route Railroad. Trains operate from the Santa Fe Depot in Cañon City, running west through the gorge to Parkdale and back, with open-air cars, domed vista cars, and dining services including murder-mystery and holiday trains such as the Santa Express. The railroad carries well over 100,000 passengers a year and is one of Colorado's major heritage-rail attractions, alongside the nearby Royal Gorge Bridge and Park.
