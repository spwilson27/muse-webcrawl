Vostok Station (Russian: станция Восток, "Station East") is a Russian research station in inland Princess Elizabeth Land, Antarctica. Founded by the Soviet Union in 1957 for the International Geophysical Year, it lies near the southern Pole of Cold and the south geomagnetic pole, about 1,300 km from the nearest coast. It is one of the most isolated and climatically extreme inhabited places on Earth.

Climate records

On 21 July 1983, Vostok recorded a temperature of −89.2 °C (−128.6 °F), the lowest natural air temperature ever directly measured on Earth. The station's average winter temperature is about −65 °C, and precipitation is only about 22 mm of water equivalent per year, making it a polar desert. Satellite data have detected surface temperatures as low as −98 °C in shallow depressions near the East Antarctic plateau ridge nearby.

Ice cores and Lake Vostok

Drilling at Vostok produced the famous Vostok ice core, which reached a depth of 3,623 m and yielded a climate record spanning 420,000 years and four glacial cycles, showing the close coupling of temperature and greenhouse gases. Beneath the station, about 4 km under the ice, lies Lake Vostok, one of the largest subglacial lakes in the world; the borehole penetrated into lake water in 2012. A new wintering complex was inaugurated in the 2020s to replace the ageing Soviet-era buildings.
