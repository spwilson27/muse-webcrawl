Honshu

Honshu (本州, "main island") is the largest and most populous island of Japan, home to about 104 million people — the great majority of the country's population. It lies south of Hokkaido, north of Shikoku, and northeast of Kyushu, and contains Tokyo, Osaka, Kyoto, Hiroshima, and most of Japan's major cities.

Geography

Honshu covers about 227,960 km² — roughly 60 percent of Japan's land area — and is about 1,300 km long. Mountainous and volcanic, it is dominated by the Japanese Alps and crowned by Mount Fuji (3,776 m), Japan's highest peak. The island sits along convergent plate boundaries and experiences frequent earthquakes, most devastatingly the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami off its northeastern coast.

Regions and economy

The island is divided into the regions of Tōhoku, Kantō, Chūbu, Kansai, and Chūgoku. The Pacific coastal belt from Tokyo through Nagoya to Osaka forms the Taiheiyō Belt megalopolis, one of the largest industrial and urban corridors in the world. Honshu is linked to the other main islands by bridges and tunnels, including the Seikan Tunnel to Hokkaido.
