The 1958 Formula One season was the 12th season of Formula One motor racing. It featured the 9th World Championship of Drivers and the 1st International Cup for F1 Manufacturers, contested concurrently over an eleven-race series that began on 19 January and ended on 19 October. Mike Hawthorn won the Drivers' Championship for Ferrari, becoming the first British World Champion, while Vanwall won the inaugural Constructors' title.

Mike Hawthorn, the 1958 World Drivers' Champion, in the Ferrari Dino 246.

Season summary

The 1958 season marked a turning point in Formula One. For the first time, the championship regulations banned alcohol-based racing fuels and mandated commercial petrol, a change that initially disadvantaged several teams. The British constructors Vanwall, BRM and Cooper made major strides; Cooper's rear-engined cars, driven by Jack Brabham and Roy Salvadori, foreshadowed the mid-engined revolution that would transform the sport within two years.

Stirling Moss won four races for Vanwall, more than any other driver, but a famous act of sportsmanship cost him the title: at the Portuguese Grand Prix, Moss successfully appealed against the disqualification of his rival Mike Hawthorn, restoring points that ultimately allowed Hawthorn — who won only one race, the French Grand Prix — to take the championship by a single point, 42 to 41.

The season was also one of the deadliest of the era. Luigi Musso was killed at the French Grand Prix at Reims, Peter Collins died at the German Grand Prix at the Nürburgring, and Vanwall driver Stuart Lewis-Evans died from burns sustained in the season-ending Moroccan Grand Prix. The toll prompted Vanwall founder Tony Vandervell to withdraw his team despite winning the Constructors' Cup, and Hawthorn himself retired as champion, only to be killed in a road accident in January 1959.

Championship standings

Hawthorn's consistency — five second places to add to his single victory — proved decisive under the scoring system, which counted only a driver's best six results and awarded an extra point for fastest lap. Vanwall took the inaugural International Cup for F1 Manufacturers with 48 points to Ferrari's 40.

Legacy

The 1958 season is remembered for Moss's sportsmanship, the arrival of the Constructors' Championship, the first championship victory by a British driver and team, and the tragic losses that spurred growing concern for driver safety in the following decade.
