The Four Seasons (Vivaldi)


Title page of the 1725 Amsterdam edition.

The Four Seasons (Italian: Le quattro stagioni) is a group of four violin concertos by the Italian composer Antonio Vivaldi, each giving musical expression to a season of the year. They were written around 1718–1720 and published in Amsterdam in 1725 as the first four works of a set of twelve concertos, Il cimento dell'armonia e dell'inventione ("The Contest Between Harmony and Invention"), Op. 8. They are among the most famous works of Baroque music and are examples of early program music.

The concertos

The concertos — in E major ("Spring", RV 269), G minor ("Summer", RV 315), F major ("Autumn", RV 293), and F minor ("Winter", RV 297) — each have three movements in a fast–slow–fast arrangement. Vivaldi published them with accompanying sonnets, possibly written by himself, describing the scenes the music depicts: birdsong, a sleeping goatherd, and dancing in Spring; oppressive heat, a cuckoo, and a violent thunderstorm in Summer; harvest celebrations, drunkenness, and the hunt in Autumn; and shivering, slipping on ice, and the comfort of a fireside in Winter. Markings in the score align passages of music with lines of the poems.

Musical features

The cycle displays Vivaldi's characteristic ritornello form and his vivid pictorialism: trembling string figures suggest cold winds, repeated notes evoke barking dogs and pattering rain, and the solo violin imitates birds and storms. The soloist narrates the poetic action against the ripieno orchestra.

Reception

Long famous in the 18th century — the "Spring" concerto was a favourite at the French court — Vivaldi's music fell into obscurity until its 20th-century revival, after which The Four Seasons became one of the most recorded and widely heard works in the classical repertoire, used in innumerable films, advertisements, and arrangements.
