Maundy Thursday

Also calledHoly Thursday, Great and Holy Thursday
Observed byChristians worldwide
SignificanceCommemorates the Last Supper and the washing of the feet
DateThursday of Holy Week, the day before Good Friday

Maundy Thursday, also known as Holy Thursday, is the Christian holy day falling on the Thursday of Holy Week, the day before Good Friday. It commemorates the Washing of the Feet and the Last Supper of Jesus Christ with the Apostles, at which the sacrament of the Eucharist was instituted.

Name

The name "Maundy" derives from the Latin mandatum ("commandment"), from the words of Jesus in John 13:34 — "A new commandment I give unto you, that ye love one another" — spoken after he had washed his disciples' feet. In most European languages the day is simply called Holy Thursday.

Liturgy and customs

The central rite of the day is the evening Mass of the Lord's Supper, which in Roman Catholic, Anglican, Lutheran, Methodist and other traditions may include a ceremonial washing of feet (the pedilavium) recalling Christ's example of humility. In Catholic practice the altar is stripped after the service in preparation for Good Friday, and the consecrated hosts are moved to an altar of repose. Orthodox Christians observe Great and Holy Thursday with the washing of feet and a lengthy service of the Twelve Gospels in the evening, properly belonging to Great Friday.

Royal Maundy

In the United Kingdom, the monarch traditionally distributes specially minted Maundy money — small silver coins in denominations of one, two, three and four pence — to elderly recipients at the Royal Maundy service, held in a different cathedral each year. The number of recipients corresponds to the sovereign's age. The custom descends from medieval ceremonies in which English kings washed the feet of the poor.

Public holiday

Maundy Thursday is a public holiday in several countries, including Norway, Denmark, Spain and many Latin American nations.
