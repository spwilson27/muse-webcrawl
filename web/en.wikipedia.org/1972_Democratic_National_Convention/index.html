The 1972 Democratic National Convention was the presidential nominating convention of the Democratic Party, held at the Miami Beach Convention Center in Miami Beach, Florida from July 10 to July 13, 1972. It nominated Senator George McGovern of South Dakota for president and Senator Thomas Eagleton of Missouri for vice president.

The McGovern–Fraser reforms

The convention was the first held under the rules of the McGovern–Fraser Commission, created after the chaos of the 1968 convention in Chicago. The reforms shifted power from party bosses to primary voters and imposed guidelines intended to open delegations to women, minorities, and young people. The most dramatic floor fight concerned the California delegation: McGovern's forces successfully defended seating his full slate, winner-take-all under state law, against a challenge that would have split the delegation proportionally — a decisive test of his nomination.

Nomination

McGovern won the presidential ballot on the first vote, defeating rivals including Hubert Humphrey and Edmund Muskie. Proceedings ran so long that McGovern delivered his acceptance speech — often considered one of the finest of his career — at nearly 3 a.m. Eastern time, when most television viewers were asleep.

The Eagleton affair

Weeks later, revelations that Eagleton had previously received electroconvulsive therapy for depression led McGovern first to declare himself "1,000 percent" behind his running mate and then, under intense pressure, to replace him with Sargent Shriver. The episode badly damaged the campaign; McGovern went on to lose the general election to Richard Nixon in a landslide.
