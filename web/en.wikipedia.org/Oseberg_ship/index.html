Oseberg ship

TypeSnekkja (Viking ship)
Laid downc. 834 AD
Length21.58 m
Beam5.1 m
LocationViking Ship House, Bygdøy, Oslo

The Oseberg ship is a well-preserved Viking ship discovered in a large burial mound at the Oseberg farm near Tønsberg in Vestfold, Norway. It is one of the finest surviving examples of Viking Age craftsmanship. Dendrochronology dates the burial chamber to 834 AD. The ship and its contents were excavated in 1904–1905 under Gabriel Gustafson and Haakon Shetelig.

The burial

The ship, a clinker-built oak snekkja with a spiral-carved prow, was buried with two women—one elderly, long identified (controversially) as Åsa, mother of King Halfdan the Black, and a younger companion. Grave goods included an elaborately carved four-wheeled cart, sleighs, beds, tapestries (the Oseberg tapestry fragments), household utensils, and the remains of horses, oxen, and dogs. Though the mound had been looted of precious metals in antiquity, the surviving woodwork is exceptionally rich.

Display

The ship is housed at the Viking Ship House at Bygdøy in Oslo, part of the Museum of Cultural History, with galleries planned to move to the new Museum of the Viking Age. Full-scale replicas, including Sea Stallion's sister projects and the Oseberg replica launched in Tønsberg in 2012, have demonstrated the ship's sailing qualities.
