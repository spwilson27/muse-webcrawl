Limbic system


The limbic system is a set of brain structures involved in emotion, motivation, memory and learning, including the amygdala, hippocampus and hypothalamus.

The limbic system, also known as the paleomammalian cortex, is a set of brain structures located on both sides of the thalamus, immediately beneath the medial temporal lobe, primarily in the cerebrum. It supports functions including emotion, behaviour, long-term memory, motivation and olfaction.

Structures

Structures commonly included are the amygdala, hippocampus, thalamus, hypothalamus, cingulate cortex and basal ganglia. The hippocampus is essential for forming new memories, while the amygdala plays a central role in emotional responses such as fear.

History

The concept developed from Paul Broca's "limbic lobe" (1878) and James Papez's emotion circuit (1937), and was popularised by Paul MacLean in the 1950s as part of his triune brain model. Modern neuroscientists debate whether the limbic system forms a coherent functional unit.
