5 (five) is a number, numeral and digit. It is the natural number following 4 and preceding 6, and is a prime number. Five is the third smallest prime, the first safe prime, and the third Mersenne prime exponent, yielding the fifth Mersenne prime number 31.

In mathematics

Five is the third prime number and the first prime that is the sum of two primes (2 + 3 is actually 5 via the primes 2 and 3 in the sense of a "prime sum"); it is the only prime that belongs to two twin prime pairs. The five Platonic solids are the only convex regular polyhedra. Five is a Fermat prime, so a regular pentagon is constructible with compass and unmarked straightedge. The Abel–Ruffini theorem states that polynomial equations of degree five or higher cannot generally be solved by radicals.

Evolution of the glyph

The modern glyph for 5 developed through Brahmi and Gupta scripts in India, passing through the Nagari and Arabic forms (٥) before reaching Europe. In most modern typefaces the digit has a descender-free form with a flat top bar.

In science and culture

Most humans have five digits on each hand and foot. Boron has atomic number 5. Traditions of "five" include the five pillars of Islam, the five elements of East Asian philosophy, the five Olympic rings interlaced to represent inhabited continents, and the five senses commonly enumerated since Aristotle.
