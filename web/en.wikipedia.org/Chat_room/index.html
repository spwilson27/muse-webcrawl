A chat room is an online space where users communicate with each other in real time through text messages, usually organised by topic. Chat rooms were one of the earliest forms of social interaction on computer networks and remain embedded in forums, games, livestreaming platforms and workplace tools.

History

The first multi-user chat system, Talkomatic, appeared on the PLATO system in 1973. CompuServe's CB Simulator (1980) brought chat to home users, modelled on citizens band radio. Internet Relay Chat (IRC), created by Jarkko Oikarinen in 1988, standardised the channel-based model with nicknames, operators and bots. In the 1990s, AOL chat rooms, ICQ, MSN Messenger and Yahoo! Chat made chat rooms a mass phenomenon, while web-based chat applets spread to fan and support sites.

Types and culture

Chat rooms may be public or private, moderated or unmoderated, anonymous or identity-based. Distinct etiquette emerged: lurking before posting, avoiding ALL CAPS ("shouting"), and respecting channel operators. Safety concerns about anonymity and minors led to moderation tools and, in some services, verified identities. Modern descendants include Discord servers, Slack channels, Twitch chat and group messaging threads.
