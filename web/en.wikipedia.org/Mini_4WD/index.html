Mini 4WD is a line of 1:32 scale, battery-powered plastic model cars produced by the Japanese company Tamiya since 1982. Unlike radio-controlled cars, a Mini 4WD car has no steering: it is guided around a purpose-built track by guide rollers attached to its bumpers, so competition revolves around tuning speed, torque, stability, and cornering parts rather than driving skill.

Design and racing

Each kit assembles without glue and runs on two AA batteries driving all four wheels through a fixed gear train; a range of official motors, gears, tyres, rollers, and mass dampers allows extensive tuning. Races are run on multi-lane plastic circuits with lane changers, banked curves, jumps, and wave sections; cars that leave the track are eliminated. Organized competition culminates in the Japan Cup, held since 1988, and Mini 4WD racing has large followings in Japan, Southeast Asia, Italy, and elsewhere.

In popular culture

The hobby was popularized among children by manga and anime such as Dash! Yonkuro (1989) and the Bakusō Kyōdai Let's & Go!! series of the 1990s, whose fictional machines became real kits. Successive chassis generations (including the MS, MA, AR, and VZ chassis) keep the line in production with periodic boom cycles.
