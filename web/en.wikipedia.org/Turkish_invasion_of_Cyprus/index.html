Turkish invasion of Cyprus

Date20 July – 18 August 1974
LocationCyprus
ResultTurkish occupation of northern Cyprus; de facto partition of the island

The Turkish invasion of Cyprus, code-named the Attila Operation by Turkey, was a Turkish military invasion of the island country of Cyprus launched on 20 July 1974. It followed the Cypriot coup d'état of 15 July 1974, in which the Greek military junta backed a coup that overthrew President Makarios III and installed Nikos Sampson, with the aim of enosis — union with Greece. Turkey, invoking its rights as a guarantor power under the 1960 Treaty of Guarantee, landed forces on the northern coast near Kyrenia.

Course of the invasion

A ceasefire was declared on 22 July, but after the failure of peace talks in Geneva, Turkey launched a second offensive on 14 August 1974, advancing to occupy roughly the northern 36 per cent of the island. The fighting displaced large populations: Greek Cypriots fled or were expelled from the north, while Turkish Cypriots moved from enclaves in the south into the Turkish-controlled area.

Aftermath

The invasion resulted in the de facto partition of Cyprus, which persists to this day. In 1983 the Turkish Cypriot side unilaterally declared the independence of the "Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus", recognised only by Turkey. The United Nations maintains a buffer zone — the Green Line — across the island, and numerous rounds of reunification talks, including the 2004 Annan Plan referendum, have failed to settle the Cyprus problem.
