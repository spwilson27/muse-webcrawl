Saint-Germain-des-Prés

Saint-Germain-des-Prés (French pronunciation: [sɛ̃ ʒɛʁmɛ̃ de pʁe]) is a neighbourhood in the 6th arrondissement of Paris, on the Left Bank of the Seine. It takes its name from the Abbey of Saint-Germain-des-Prés, founded in the sixth century, whose church — among the oldest in Paris — still stands at its heart. After the Second World War the quarter became famous as the centre of existentialist philosophy and Parisian intellectual life, associated with Jean-Paul Sartre, Simone de Beauvoir, jazz clubs, bookshops, and café culture.

History

The abbey was founded around 558 by the Merovingian king Childebert I to house relics brought from Spain, and was dedicated to Saint Germain, bishop of Paris. The surrounding village grew up outside the city walls, in meadows (prés) along the Seine. During the Middle Ages the abbey was one of the richest and most powerful in France, and the quarter hosted a famous fair. In the seventeenth century the area became fashionable with the aristocracy, and many of its elegant hôtels particuliers date from this period.

Existentialist era

In the 1940s and 1950s Saint-Germain-des-Prés became synonymous with the existentialist movement. Sartre and Beauvoir wrote in the Café de Flore and Les Deux Magots on the Boulevard Saint-Germain, while jazz flourished in cellar clubs such as Le Tabou, where Boris Vian, Miles Davis, and Juliette Gréco performed. The song "Il n'y a plus d'après" and the cult of the "Germans" (germanopratins) captured the quarter's bohemian image. Publishers such as Gallimard and Le Seuil, along with numerous bookshops and art galleries, cemented its literary character.

Today

The neighbourhood is now one of the most expensive in Paris, home to luxury boutiques, art and antique dealers, celebrated cafés, and institutions such as the Institut de France across the river and Sciences Po nearby. The church, the Café de Flore, Les Deux Magots, and the Brasserie Lipp remain landmarks, and the quarter retains its reputation as a centre of publishing and intellectual life.
