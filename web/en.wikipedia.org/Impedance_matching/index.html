Impedance matching

In electronics, impedance matching is the practice of designing or adjusting the input impedance of an electrical load, or the output impedance of a signal source, to maximize power transfer or to minimize signal reflection from the load. According to the maximum power transfer theorem, maximum power is delivered when the load impedance is the complex conjugate of the source impedance.

Concepts

Impedance is the opposition that a circuit presents to alternating current, combining resistance and reactance. When a transmission line is terminated in an impedance different from its characteristic impedance, part of the signal is reflected, producing standing waves. The reflection coefficient and standing wave ratio quantify the mismatch.

Matching networks

Networks of inductors and capacitors (such as L-networks, pi networks, and T networks), quarter-wave transformers, and stub tuners are used to transform impedances. Simple resistive matching works but wastes power. In radio-frequency engineering, antenna tuners and matching circuits are essential for efficient transmission and reception.

Applications

Impedance matching matters in RF and microwave systems, audio engineering, telecommunications, and power systems. In audio, modern practice usually uses voltage "bridging" (a low output impedance driving a high input impedance) rather than true power matching, except in legacy telephone and valve-amplifier contexts.
