Christianization of Kievan Rus'

Key eventBaptism of Vladimir, 988
PlaceKiev and the Rus' lands
Religion adoptedEastern Orthodox Christianity

The Christianization of Kievan Rus' was the adoption of Orthodox Christianity by the East Slavic state of Kievan Rus', conventionally dated to 988, when Grand Prince Vladimir of Kiev was baptised and ordered the mass baptism of his subjects in the Dnieper River. It bound Rus' culturally and politically to the Byzantine world and laid the religious foundations of modern Russia, Ukraine, and Belarus.

Background

Christianity had reached the Rus' gradually: a Rus' bishopric was reported as early as the 860s, and Vladimir's grandmother Olga was baptised in Constantinople around 955, though her son Sviatoslav remained pagan. According to the Primary Chronicle, Vladimir sent envoys to examine Islam, Judaism, Latin Christianity, and Byzantine Orthodoxy, choosing the latter for the splendour of its worship in Hagia Sophia.

The baptism of 988

Vladimir's conversion was bound up with his military aid to the Byzantine emperor Basil II and his marriage to the emperor's sister, Anna. Baptised at Chersonesus in Crimea (by some accounts in Kiev), he returned to Kiev, destroyed pagan idols — the statue of Perun was dragged and thrown into the Dnieper — and summoned the population to baptism.

Consequences

The Church brought Rus' literacy in Church Slavonic, Byzantine art and architecture (such as Kiev's Church of the Tithes and St Sophia Cathedral), canon law, and monasticism, centred on the Kiev Monastery of the Caves. Pagan practices nevertheless persisted in folk religion for centuries in a synthesis sometimes called "dual faith".
