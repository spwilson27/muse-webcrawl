Trance

Trance is a state of semi-consciousness in which a person is not self-aware and is either altogether unresponsive to external stimuli (but nevertheless capable of pursuing and realizing an aim) or is selectively responsive in following the directions of the person (if any) who has induced the trance. Trance states may occur involuntarily and unbidden.

The term has also been used for altered states associated with hypnosis, meditation, religious ritual, shamanic practice, and certain psychiatric conditions.

Types and contexts

Religious and shamanic trance

Across cultures, trance has been induced by drumming, chanting, dancing, fasting, or psychoactive substances in shamanic healing, spirit possession rituals, and ecstatic worship — from Siberian shamanism to Pentecostal services and Sufi dhikr.

Hypnotic trance

Hypnosis is commonly described as inducing a trance-like state of focused attention and heightened suggestibility. The nature and depth of hypnotic trance, and whether it constitutes a distinct altered state, remain debated in psychology.

Clinical descriptions

Trance and possession disorders are recognized in psychiatric classification, where involuntary trance states that cause distress or impairment may be diagnosed. Trance-like absorption also occurs in everyday life, such as "highway hypnosis" during monotonous driving.

History of study

Trance phenomena were central to early investigations of mesmerism and hysteria in the 18th and 19th centuries, influencing figures such as Franz Mesmer, James Braid, and Jean-Martin Charcot, and later anthropological research on ritual and consciousness.
