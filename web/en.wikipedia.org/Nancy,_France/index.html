Nancy (French pronunciation: [nɑ̃si]) is a city in northeastern France, the prefecture of the department of Meurthe-et-Moselle in the region of Grand Est. It lies on the left bank of the river Meurthe, about 10 kilometres upstream from its confluence with the Moselle. Nancy was the capital of the Duchy of Lorraine, which was annexed by France under King Louis XV in 1766.

History

The earliest signs of human settlement in the area date to around 800 BC. The town itself was founded in the 11th century by Gerard, Duke of Lorraine, who built a castle and a small walled town called Nanciacum. Nancy grew as the ducal capital, and in 1477 it was the site of the Battle of Nancy, at which Charles the Bold, Duke of Burgundy, was defeated and killed by René II, Duke of Lorraine.

In the 18th century, Stanisław Leszczyński, the exiled King of Poland and father-in-law of Louis XV, became Duke of Lorraine and transformed Nancy, commissioning the magnificent Place Stanislas, now a UNESCO World Heritage Site together with the Place de la Carrière and the Place d'Alliance.

Art Nouveau

Nancy became a major centre of Art Nouveau around 1900. The École de Nancy, led by the glassmaker Émile Gallé, united artists and industrialists including Louis Majorelle and the Daum brothers. The Musée de l'École de Nancy preserves many of these works.

Education and economy

The University of Lorraine, formed by the merger of Nancy's universities, is one of the largest in France, and the city has a large student population. Nancy's economy is based on services, research, health, and higher education, with historic industries in steel and textiles having declined in the later 20th century.
