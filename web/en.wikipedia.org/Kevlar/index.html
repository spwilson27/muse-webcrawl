Kevlar

Woven Kevlar fabric, showing the characteristic golden-yellow colour of the aramid fibre.

Kevlar is a strong, heat-resistant synthetic fibre, related to other aramids such as Nomex and Twaron. Developed by Stephanie Kwolek at DuPont in 1965, the high-strength material was first used commercially in the early 1970s as a replacement for steel in racing tyres. It is now used in a wide range of applications, from bicycle tyres and sails to bulletproof vests, helmets and other body armour.

Production and structure

Kevlar is the DuPont trade name for polyparaphenylene terephthalamide, a para-aramid. Its molecules are exceptionally stiff rods that align and pack into sheets held together by hydrogen bonds when the polymer is spun from a liquid-crystalline solution in concentrated sulfuric acid. The result is a fibre with a tensile strength-to-weight ratio about five times that of steel: it has high modulus and strength, low elongation, and good resistance to heat and abrasion, though it degrades in ultraviolet light and is weakened by prolonged exposure to moisture.

Applications

Kevlar's best-known use is in soft body armour: woven layers of the fabric deform and spread the energy of a projectile, and bullet-resistant vests made from it have saved thousands of police officers' lives. Grades of the fibre are also used in fibre-optic cable strength members, aerospace and boat-hull composites, brake pads, protective gloves and clothing, speaker cones, drumheads and ropes. Kevlar 49 is used for structural composites, Kevlar 29 for ballistic and general-purpose applications, and Kevlar KM2 for military helmets and vests.

History

Kwolek's discovery arose from work on polymers for lightweight tyre reinforcement during an anticipated gasoline shortage; the cloudy, opalescent solution she obtained — usually a sign of failure — proved to spin into remarkably strong fibres. DuPont invested hundreds of millions of dollars developing the product before its commercial launch in 1971. Kwolek received the National Medal of Technology in part for this work.
