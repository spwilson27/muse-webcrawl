The right to food, also called the right to adequate food, is a human right protecting the right of people to feed themselves in dignity. It implies that sufficient food is available, that people have the means to access it, and that it adequately meets dietary needs. The right derives from the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (ICESCR), whose Article 11 recognises "the right of everyone to an adequate standard of living for himself and his family, including adequate food" and "the fundamental right of everyone to be free from hunger".

Legal framework

The right was first proclaimed in Article 25 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948). In 1999 the UN Committee on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights issued General Comment 12, interpreting the right as requiring food to be available, accessible (economically and physically) and adequate in quantity and quality. States parties undertake to take steps, to the maximum of available resources, toward progressively realising the right, and to guarantee freedom from hunger as a minimum core obligation. The FAO's Voluntary Guidelines of 2004 further support national implementation.

State obligations

States bear obligations to respect (not to interfere with existing access to food), protect (against deprivation by third parties), and fulfil (facilitating access and, where people cannot provide for themselves, providing food directly). Around two dozen countries recognise the right to food explicitly or implicitly in their constitutions; in India, the Supreme Court's interpretation of the right to life underpinned the nationwide midday meal scheme and the National Food Security Act 2013.

Challenges

Despite global food production being sufficient in aggregate, hundreds of millions of people face hunger and malnutrition, driven by poverty, conflict, climate shocks and inequality in land and income. The office of the UN Special Rapporteur on the right to food, created in 2000 and formerly held by Olivier De Schutter and Hilal Elver, monitors violations and promotes agroecological and rights-based approaches.
