Le Classique is the name given to the football rivalry between Paris Saint-Germain (PSG) and Olympique de Marseille (OM), France's two most prominent and widely supported clubs. Unlike a local derby, it opposes the capital, Paris, to Marseille, the country's second city and great Mediterranean port, and is freighted with north–south, class and cultural antagonisms; it is the most watched club fixture in French football.

Origins

Although the clubs first met in 1971, shortly after PSG's foundation in 1970, the fierce rivalry is largely a construction of the late 1980s and early 1990s, deliberately stoked by the clubs' owners — Canal+ at PSG and Bernard Tapie at Marseille — and by the television broadcaster to create a French equivalent of Spain's El Clásico, from which the name derives. Marseille's European Cup triumph in 1993, the era's defining French football achievement, came amid the rivalry's hottest period, which included bitter title races and the VA-OM match-fixing scandal that saw Marseille stripped of the 1993 league title.

Modern era

Since Qatar Sports Investments bought PSG in 2011, the balance of power has shifted decisively to Paris: PSG's star-studded teams have dominated Ligue 1 and the Classique itself, while Marseille have remained France's best-supported club outside the capital and the only French winner of the European Cup/Champions League in the twentieth century (1993). Meetings are heavily policed because of a history of fan violence, and away supporters have frequently been banned. Memorable matches include Marseille's 3–0 win in November 2011 during PSG's transitional season, PSG's 5–1 away victory in February 2017, and the ill-tempered September 2020 match at the Parc des Princes, won 1–0 by Marseille and ending with five red cards.
