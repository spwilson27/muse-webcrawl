Emmeline Pankhurst (née Goulden; 15 July 1858 – 14 June 1928) was a British political activist who organised the British suffragette movement and helped women win the right to vote in Great Britain and Ireland in 1918. In 1999, Time named her as one of the 100 Most Important People of the 20th Century.

Early life

Born in Manchester to politically active parents, Pankhurst was introduced at the age of 14 to the women's suffrage movement. She married Richard Pankhurst, a lawyer and supporter of women's suffrage, in 1880; he died in 1898. Her daughters Christabel and Sylvia became leading figures in the movement alongside her.

WSPU and militancy

In 1903 Pankhurst founded the Women's Social and Political Union (WSPU), an all-women suffrage organisation dedicated to "deeds, not words". Its members, known as suffragettes, used militant tactics including heckling, window-breaking, arson and hunger strikes, to which the government responded with forcible feeding in prison. During the First World War, Pankhurst suspended militancy and encouraged women to contribute to the war effort.

Later life and legacy

The Representation of the People Act 1918 gave the vote to women over 30 meeting property qualifications; full equality came with the Equal Franchise Act 1928, passed weeks after Pankhurst's death. A statue of Pankhurst stands in Parliament Square, London, unveiled in 2018.
