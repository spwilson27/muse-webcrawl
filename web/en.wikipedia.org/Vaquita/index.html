Vaquita

The vaquita (Phocoena sinus) is a species of porpoise endemic to the northern end of the Gulf of California in Baja California, Mexico. Averaging 150 cm (4.9 ft) in length, it is the smallest of all living cetaceans. The vaquita is currently on the brink of extinction; the precipitous decline in abundance has been driven by bycatch in gillnets used in the illegal trade of totoaba swim bladders.

Description

The vaquita is a small, robust porpoise with a characteristically dark ring around its eyes and dark patches on its lips. Females are larger than males. It lives in shallow, turbid lagoons along the shoreline of the upper Gulf, the most restricted range of any marine cetacean.

Decline

First described in 1958, the species numbered about 600 in 1997. Mortality in gillnets set for shrimp and finfish — and especially in illegal nets set for the totoaba, whose swim bladder commands enormous prices in China — drove the population down to roughly 30 by 2016 and around 10 or fewer in recent surveys. The species is listed as Critically Endangered by the IUCN.

Conservation

Mexico banned gillnets in the vaquita's range and established a refuge zone, but enforcement against illegal totoaba fishing has proven difficult. A 2017 attempt to capture vaquitas for a captive-breeding sanctuary (VaquitaCPR) was abandoned after a captured female died. Conservation now centres on removing illegal nets, promoting vaquita-safe fishing gear, and monitoring the remaining animals acoustically and visually.
