Lesser Antilles

The Lesser Antilles form the eastern boundary of the Caribbean Sea.

The Lesser Antilles are a group of islands in the Caribbean Sea, forming part of the West Indies. They make up a long, partly volcanic island arc stretching from the Virgin Islands in the north to the islands off the coast of Venezuela in the south, and together with the Greater Antilles and the Lucayan Archipelago they form the Antilles.

Geography

The islands are traditionally divided into the Leeward Islands in the north, the Windward Islands in the south, and the Leeward Antilles along the South American coast. Many of the islands are volcanic in origin, formed by subduction of the North American Plate beneath the Caribbean Plate; the arc includes active volcanoes such as Soufrière Hills on Montserrat and La Soufrière on Saint Vincent.

Countries and territories

The Lesser Antilles include the sovereign states of Antigua and Barbuda, Barbados, Dominica, Grenada, Saint Kitts and Nevis, Saint Lucia, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, and Trinidad and Tobago, as well as overseas territories of the United Kingdom, France, the Netherlands, and the United States.

History

Before European contact the islands were inhabited by Arawak and later Kalinago (Island Carib) peoples. From the 17th century, Britain, France, the Netherlands, and Spain contested the islands, which became centres of sugar production worked by enslaved Africans. Most became independent during the 20th century.
