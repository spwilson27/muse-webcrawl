Mircea the Elder

Mircea the Elder (Romanian: Mircea cel Bătrân; c. 1355 – 31 January 1418) was the voivode (prince) of Wallachia from 1386 until his death in 1418. He was the father of Vlad II Dracul and grandfather of Vlad the Impaler. Mircea's reign is regarded as a high point of medieval Wallachia: he expanded the principality to its greatest extent, reaching the Black Sea, strengthened its economy and administration, and resisted the expansion of the Ottoman Empire for three decades.

Reign

Mircea succeeded his brother Dan I in 1386, inheriting the principalities of Wallachia and, at various times, territories including Dobruja, the Banat of Severin, and the duchies of Amlaș and Făgăraș in Transylvania. He reorganised the court and fiscal system, issued coinage, supported trade through Black Sea ports such as Kilia, and founded monasteries, most famously Cozia (1388), where he is buried.

Wars with the Ottomans

Mircea is celebrated for his long resistance to the Ottomans. He defeated an Ottoman army at the Battle of Rovine in 1394/1395, reportedly drawing the admiration of contemporaries; he joined the crusade of Sigismund of Hungary that ended in disaster at Nicopolis (1396), then continued fighting alone, winning victories at the Danube and in Dobruja. During the Ottoman Interregnum after 1402, Mircea skilfully backed rival claimants to the Ottoman throne to keep the sultanate divided. Only in his last years, with Mehmed I reunified, did Wallachia accept paying tribute while retaining internal autonomy.

Legacy

Romanian historiography treats Mircea as one of the great medieval rulers: the epithet "the Elder" distinguishes him from his grandson Mircea II. His portrait at Cozia Monastery, his charters, and his role as ancestor of the Drăculești branch of the House of Basarab anchor his place in national memory.
