The University of Alabama (informally The Capstone, UA, or Alabama) is a public research university in Tuscaloosa, Alabama. Established in 1831, it is the oldest and largest of the public universities in the state and the flagship campus of the University of Alabama System.

History

The university opened in 1831 and was largely burned by Union troops in April 1865 during the Civil War; it reopened in 1871. In 1963, Governor George Wallace made his famous "Stand in the Schoolhouse Door" in an unsuccessful attempt to block the enrollment of Black students Vivian Malone and James Hood. The university grew substantially in the twenty-first century, expanding enrollment and research.

Academics and athletics

UA offers programs in thirteen academic divisions, including law, medicine (through affiliated campuses), engineering, and business. Its athletic teams, the Crimson Tide, compete in the Southeastern Conference; the football program, based at Bryant–Denny Stadium, is one of the most successful in college football history.
