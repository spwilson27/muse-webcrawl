Khakassia, officially the Republic of Khakassia, is a federal subject of Russia located in south-central Siberia, in the upper basin of the Yenisei River. Its capital is Abakan, and its indigenous people are the Turkic-speaking Khakas.

Geography

Khakassia covers about 61,600 square kilometres, bordered by Kemerovo Oblast, Krasnoyarsk Krai, Tuva, and the Altai Republic. The western part is mountainous — the Kuznetsk Alatau and the Western Sayan ranges — while the east is rolling steppe drained by the Abakan and Yenisei rivers. The continental climate brings cold winters and warm summers.

History

The region was the heartland of the medieval Yenisei Kyrgyz khaganate, which destroyed the Uyghur Khaganate in 840 AD. Russian forts were established in the early 18th century, and the area was incorporated into the Russian Empire. In 1930 the Khakas Autonomous Oblast was created within the Soviet Union; it became a republic within the Russian Federation in 1991–1992.

Economy

Khakassia is rich in minerals — coal, iron ore, molybdenum, gold, and marble — and in hydroelectric power: the Sayano-Shushenskaya Dam on the Yenisei, Russia's largest power plant, lies on its border with Krasnoyarsk Krai and was the site of a catastrophic turbine accident in 2009. Aluminium smelting, mining, timber, and agriculture are the main industries. The republic is also noted archaeologically for its Bronze Age Okunev culture stelae and numerous kurgans and standing stones across the steppe.
