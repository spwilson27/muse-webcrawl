Murder in English law

Murder in English law is a common-law offence in England and Wales. It is committed when a person of sound mind unlawfully kills another person within the King's peace, with intent to kill or to cause grievous bodily harm.

Elements

The prosecution must prove an unlawful killing (actus reus) accompanied by malice aforethought (mens rea), which in modern law means an intention to kill or to cause really serious harm. Partial defences — loss of control and diminished responsibility under the Homicide Act 1957, as amended — can reduce murder to voluntary manslaughter.

Sentence

Murder carries a mandatory sentence of life imprisonment. The judge sets a minimum term (tariff) before the offender may be considered for release on licence, with starting points set by the Criminal Justice Act 2003; a whole life order may be imposed in the most serious cases. The death penalty for murder was abolished in 1965.
