A single reed is a type of reed used to produce sound in various wind instruments. In a single-reed instrument, one thin strip of material — traditionally cane from the plant Arundo donax — is fixed against the opening of a mouthpiece. When air is blown across it, the reed vibrates against the mouthpiece, chopping the airflow into pulses that excite the air column and produce a musical tone.

Instruments

The most prominent single-reed instruments are the clarinet and the saxophone, together with their extended families. Historical and folk single-reed instruments include the chalumeau, the tárogató, the duduk's relatives with single reeds in some traditions, and simple reed pipes found in many cultures. The player's embouchure, air pressure, and the reed's strength (graded by number) shape tone and response.

The reed

Reeds are cut from cane, soaked before playing, and wear out with use; many modern players also use synthetic reeds of plastic or composite for consistency. Clarinet and saxophone overblow differently — the clarinet at the twelfth, owing to its cylindrical bore, the saxophone at the octave, owing to its conical bore — but both depend on the same single-reed mechanism.
