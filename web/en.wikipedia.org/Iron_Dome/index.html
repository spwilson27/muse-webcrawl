Iron Dome

Iron Dome (Hebrew: כִּיפַּת בַּרְזֶל, romanized: Kippat Barzel) is an Israeli mobile all-weather air defense system developed by Rafael Advanced Defense Systems and Israel Aerospace Industries. The system is designed to intercept and destroy short-range rockets and artillery shells fired from distances of 4 to 70 kilometres away whose trajectory would take them to an Israeli population centre.

Operation

Each Iron Dome battery consists of a detection and tracking radar, a battle management and weapon control system, and missile firing units armed with Tamir interceptor missiles. The system calculates whether an incoming projectile will land in a populated area; if so, it launches an interceptor, and if not, it allows the rocket to fall harmlessly — conserving expensive interceptors.

Service history

Declared operational in March 2011 near Beersheba, Iron Dome made its first interception in April of that year. It has since been used extensively in conflicts with Hamas and other groups, including the 2012, 2014 and 2021 Gaza conflicts and the October 2023 war, with the Israeli military reporting interception rates of around 90 per cent against rockets it engaged. The United States has provided substantial funding for the system and procured batteries for the U.S. Army.
