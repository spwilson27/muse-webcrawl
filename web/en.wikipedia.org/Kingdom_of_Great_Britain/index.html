The Kingdom of Great Britain was a sovereign state in Western Europe from 1707 to 1800, created by the political union of the Kingdom of England and the Kingdom of Scotland under the Acts of Union 1707.

Formation

England and Scotland had shared a monarch since the Union of the Crowns in 1603, but remained separate states. Economic pressures, the failure of Scotland's Darien scheme, and English concerns over the succession after Queen Anne drove negotiations that produced a single Parliament at Westminster, a single flag and a customs and monetary union, while Scotland kept its own legal system and established church.

Politics and society

The kingdom developed the system of cabinet government under the first prime minister, Robert Walpole. Jacobite risings in 1715 and 1745 attempted to restore the exiled Stuarts and were defeated, the latter at Culloden in 1746. The Scottish Enlightenment and the early Industrial Revolution transformed society and economy.

Empire and war

Great Britain fought a series of global wars with France, emerging victorious in the Seven Years' War (1756–1763) with gains in Canada and India, but losing the Thirteen Colonies in the American Revolutionary War. The Royal Navy became the world's dominant maritime force.

End

Following the Irish Rebellion of 1798, the Acts of Union 1800 merged Great Britain with the Kingdom of Ireland, creating the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland on 1 January 1801.
