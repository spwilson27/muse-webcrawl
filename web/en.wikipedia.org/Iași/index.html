Iași

Iași is the third largest city in Romania and the seat of Iași County. Located in the historical region of Moldavia, Iași has traditionally been one of the leading centres of Romanian social, cultural, academic and artistic life. The city was the capital of the Principality of Moldavia from 1564 to 1859, then of the United Principalities from 1859 to 1862, and of Romania from 1916 to 1918, during the First World War.

Culture and education

Known as the Cultural Capital of Romania, Iași is home to the oldest Romanian university, Alexandru Ioan Cuza University, founded in 1860, as well as the first Romanian theatre and the oldest botanical garden in the country. The city's landmark is the Palace of Culture, a vast neo-Gothic building housing museums and the central library.

History

The city developed around a princely court on the Bahlui River and became a major commercial crossroads between Poland, Hungary, Wallachia and Constantinople. In December 1941, during the Second World War, the Iași pogrom killed thousands of the city's Jews. Iași grew substantially during the communist era through industrialisation.
