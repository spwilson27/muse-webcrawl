Roddy Frame (born 29 January 1964) is a Scottish singer, songwriter and guitarist, best known as the founder and frontman of Aztec Camera, the group with which he achieved international success in the 1980s with songs such as "Oblivious" and "Somewhere in My Heart". Renowned for his intricate guitar playing and literate lyric writing, Frame has recorded under his own name since the late 1990s.

Early life and Aztec Camera

Frame was born in East Kilbride, near Glasgow, and was playing guitar and writing songs as a young teenager. In 1980 he formed Aztec Camera, who were signed to Glasgow's influential Postcard Records and released the singles "Just Like Gold" and "Mattress of Wire" to critical acclaim. The band's debut album, High Land, Hard Rain (1983), recorded for Rough Trade when Frame was only nineteen, featured the hit single "Oblivious" and established his reputation as one of Britain's most gifted young songwriters, drawing comparisons to Elvis Costello.

Commercial success

After moving to WEA, Aztec Camera released Knife (1984), produced by Mark Knopfler, and then Love (1987), which adopted a polished pop-soul sound and produced the UK top five single "Somewhere in My Heart" (1988). Subsequent albums Stray (1990) — which included a collaboration with Mick Jones of the Clash — Dreamland (1993), made with Japanese composer Ryuichi Sakamoto, and Frestonia (1995) were less commercially successful, though Frame's songwriting continued to be praised. A duet with Andy Williams on "Dream Sweet Dreams" and Frame's guitar work drew notice for their eclecticism.

Solo career

Frame retired the Aztec Camera name and released his first solo album, The North Star, in 1998. It was followed by the live album Live at Ronnie Scott's, Surf (2002), an intimate acoustic record, Western Skies (2006), and Seven Dials (2014), recorded in London and named after the Covent Garden district. His solo records are noted for their spare arrangements, jazz-tinged harmony, and virtuosic fingerstyle guitar, which has also made him a sought-after session player.

Style and legacy

Frame's playing blends flamenco and jazz inflections with pop melody, and his lyrics are marked by romanticism, verbal wit, and self-deprecation. Though never as commercially prominent as some peers, he is frequently cited by critics and fellow musicians as one of the most accomplished British guitarists and songwriters of his generation, and High Land, Hard Rain regularly appears in lists of the greatest debut albums.
