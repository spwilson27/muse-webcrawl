The Oder–Neisse line (German: Oder-Neiße-Grenze; Polish: Granica na Odrze i Nysie Łużyckiej) is the modern international border between Germany and Poland. It runs mainly along the Oder and Lusatian Neisse rivers and was established after the Second World War.

Origins

At the Potsdam Conference in 1945, the Allied powers placed the former German territories east of the Oder and Neisse under Polish administration pending a final peace settlement, and the German population was expelled westward. Poland's western border thereby shifted far west at the expense of Germany, compensating in part for Polish territory annexed by the Soviet Union in the east.

Recognition

East Germany recognized the line in the Treaty of Zgorzelec (1950), and West Germany accepted its inviolability in the Treaty of Warsaw (1970). A reunified Germany confirmed the border definitively in the German–Polish Border Treaty of 1990.
