Glutathione

Glutathione (γ-L-glutamyl-L-cysteinylglycine) is the cell's principal antioxidant.

Glutathione
Molecular formulaC10H17N3O6S
Molar mass307.32 g/mol
CompositionTripeptide of glutamate, cysteine and glycine
FunctionAntioxidant; detoxification; redox balance

Glutathione (GSH) is an antioxidant present in nearly all living organisms, from bacteria to humans. A tripeptide of glutamate, cysteine and glycine — linked by an unusual gamma-peptide bond — it protects cells against reactive oxygen species such as free radicals, peroxides and heavy metals.

Functions

Glutathione cycles between a reduced form (GSH) and an oxidised dimer (GSSG), donating reducing equivalents to neutralise oxidants; the enzyme glutathione reductase, powered by NADPH, restores the reduced form. GSH also serves as a cofactor for glutathione peroxidases, conjugates toxins for excretion in glutathione S-transferase reactions (important in liver detoxification), and maintains other antioxidants such as vitamins C and E in their active states.

Biosynthesis and clinical relevance

Cells synthesise glutathione in two ATP-dependent steps; cysteine availability is often rate-limiting. The precursor N-acetylcysteine is used clinically to restore glutathione in paracetamol (acetaminophen) poisoning. Altered glutathione levels are associated with ageing, neurodegenerative and metabolic diseases, though the benefits of oral supplementation are limited by digestion of the peptide.
