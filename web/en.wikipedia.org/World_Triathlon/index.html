World Triathlon

World Triathlon is the international governing body for the sport of triathlon and related multisports including duathlon, aquathlon, cross triathlon and paratriathlon. Founded in 1989 as the International Triathlon Union (ITU), it adopted the name World Triathlon in October 2020. Its headquarters are in Lausanne, Switzerland, and it counts more than 170 national federations as members.

History

The ITU was founded in Avignon, France, in April 1989, shortly before the first ITU Triathlon World Championships were held in that city the same year. Triathlon made its Olympic debut at the Sydney 2000 Games, with a standard distance of a 1.5 km swim, 40 km cycle and 10 km run. The federation's founding president was Les McDonald of Canada, who served until 2008; Marisol Casado of Spain became president in 2008, and Antonio Fernández Arimany succeeded her in 2024.

Competitions

World Triathlon organises the World Triathlon Championship Series, the premier season-long circuit for elite athletes, and the World Triathlon Cup as a development tier. World championships are also sanctioned for age-group athletes, under-23 and junior categories, mixed relay (added to the Olympic programme at Tokyo 2020) and paratriathlon, which made its Paralympic debut at Rio 2016.

Governance

The organisation is recognised by the International Olympic Committee and is a member of the Association of Summer Olympic International Federations. It sets the competition rules for the sport, oversees anti-doping and technical official accreditation, and works through five continental confederations.
