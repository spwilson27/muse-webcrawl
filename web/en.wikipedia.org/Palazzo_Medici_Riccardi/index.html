Palazzo Medici Riccardi

LocationVia Cavour, Florence, Italy
ArchitectMichelozzo
Built1444–1484
StyleEarly Renaissance

The Palazzo Medici, also called the Palazzo Medici Riccardi, is a Renaissance palace located in Florence, Italy. Commissioned by Cosimo de' Medici and designed by Michelozzo di Bartolomeo, it was built between 1444 and 1484 and became the prototype of the Renaissance urban palace. In 1659 it was sold to the Riccardi family, who enlarged it and gave it its second name.

Architecture

The palace's rusticated stone façade is divided into three storeys of diminishing rustication, crowned by a massive classical cornice. Its enclosed courtyard, with sgraffito decoration, contrasts with the rugged exterior. Inside is the Magi Chapel, frescoed by Benozzo Gozzoli in 1459–1461 with the Journey of the Magi, in which members of the Medici family appear in the procession — among the most celebrated fresco cycles of the early Renaissance. Luca Giordano later painted the ceiling of the Riccardi gallery (1682–1685).

History

The palace was the Medici family home for about a century, housing Cosimo, Lorenzo the Magnificent, and their court, until the family moved to the Palazzo Vecchio in 1540. Today it houses the Metropolitan City of Florence offices and a museum.
