National parks of Japan

National parks (国立公園, kokuritsu kōen) in Japan are places of scenic beauty designated by the Minister of the Environment under the Natural Parks Law. There are 34 national parks, covering about 5.8 percent of the country's land area. Alongside quasi-national parks and prefectural natural parks, they form Japan's system of protected natural areas.

History

Japan's first national parks — Setonaikai, Unzen, and Kirishima — were established in 1934 under the National Parks Law of 1931. Unlike the North American model, Japanese national parks include privately owned land and inhabited areas, and are managed through zoning that balances conservation with tourism and local livelihoods.

Notable parks

Well-known parks include Fuji-Hakone-Izu, containing Mount Fuji; Nikko, with its shrines and mountain scenery; Akan Mashu in Hokkaido; Daisetsuzan, Japan's largest national park; Iriomote-Ishigaki in the Ryukyu Islands; and Yakushima, noted for its ancient cedar forests and UNESCO World Heritage status.
