Dome

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A dome (from Latin domus, "house") is an architectural element similar to the hollow upper half of a sphere. There is significant overlap with the term cupola, which may also refer to a dome or a structure on top of a dome. Domes have a long architectural lineage extending back into prehistory.

History

Corbelled domes were built by Neolithic peoples in the Middle East, and domed tombs such as the Treasury of Atreus at Mycenae date to the Bronze Age. The form reached monumental scale in Roman engineering: the Pantheon in Rome (completed c. 125 AD) has a 43.3-metre unreinforced concrete dome with a central oculus, still the world's largest of its kind. Byzantine architects developed the pendentive, allowing a dome to sit over a square bay, most famously in Hagia Sophia (537 AD). Islamic architecture adopted and spread the dome across mosques and mausolea, culminating in works such as the Taj Mahal's onion dome (1653).

Renaissance and modern domes

Filippo Brunelleschi's double-shelled dome for Florence Cathedral (1436) revived large-scale dome building in Europe, and Michelangelo's dome for St Peter's Basilica followed. Later landmarks include St Paul's Cathedral in London, the United States Capitol, and in the 20th century Buckminster Fuller's geodesic domes and large sports stadia such as the Astrodome.

Structure

A dome works largely in compression, channelling loads to its supports; the outward thrust at the base is resisted by drums, buttresses, chains or tension rings. Common types include hemispherical, segmental, onion, saucer and geodesic domes, built in masonry, concrete, steel, timber or modern composites.
