Cost of living

Cost of living
FieldEconomics
Measured byCost-of-living index; consumer price index
Key componentsHousing, food, transport, healthcare, taxes

The cost of living is the cost of maintaining a certain standard of living for an individual or a household. Changes in the cost of living over time are typically measured by a cost-of-living index, which tracks the prices of a representative basket of goods and services. Cost of living is often used to compare the affordability of different cities, regions, or countries.

Measurement

A cost-of-living index compares the price of a fixed basket of goods and services between two places or two points in time. Most countries publish a consumer price index (CPI), which measures changes in the cost of living for a typical household. Because households consume different baskets, a single index cannot capture everyone's experience exactly; economists therefore also use measures such as the personal consumption expenditures price index.

Cost-of-living adjustments

Many wages, pensions, and social security benefits include cost-of-living adjustments (COLAs) intended to preserve purchasing power as prices rise. Employers may also pay location-based allowances to staff working in expensive cities.
