Baking powder

Baking powder is a dry chemical leavening agent, a mixture of a carbonate or bicarbonate and a weak acid. It is used to increase the volume and lighten the texture of baked goods, releasing carbon dioxide gas when moistened and heated.

Composition

Most baking powders contain sodium bicarbonate as the base, an acid salt such as cream of tartar or sodium acid pyrophosphate, and a starch filler that absorbs moisture and prevents premature reaction. "Double-acting" powders release some gas on mixing and more in the oven.

History

Baking powder was developed in the mid-19th century; Alfred Bird marketed an early version in England in 1843, and Eben Norton Horsford patented a widely used formulation in the United States in 1859.
