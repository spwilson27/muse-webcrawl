The Federalist Party was the first political party in the United States. Under Alexander Hamilton, it formed during the first term of President George Washington in the early 1790s to support a strong national government, a national bank, funding of the national debt, and close commercial ties with Britain. Its great rival was the Democratic-Republican Party of Thomas Jefferson and James Madison.

History

The Federalists controlled the federal government under John Adams (1797–1801), the only Federalist president. Their Alien and Sedition Acts of 1798, aimed at French sympathisers and opposition newspapers, proved deeply unpopular. Defeat in the election of 1800 began a long decline; opposition by New England Federalists to the War of 1812, crystallised in the Hartford Convention of 1814–15, destroyed the party's national reputation. It effectively ceased to function as a national party after 1816, though Federalist ideas persisted.

Legacy

Although short-lived, the Federalists left an enduring institutional legacy: the financial system designed by Hamilton, a strong federal judiciary shaped by Chief Justice John Marshall, and the precedent that an energetic national government could promote commerce and stability. The Federalist Papers, written by Hamilton, Madison and Jay to defend the Constitution, remain a classic of political theory.
