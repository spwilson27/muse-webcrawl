Rikers Island is a 413-acre (167.2 ha) island in the East River in the Bronx borough of New York City that contains the city's main jail complex. It is the largest penal colony in the world and one of the largest jails and mental institutions in the United States, with a daily population of several thousand detainees, most of whom are awaiting trial.

History

The island is named after Abraham Rycken, a Dutch settler who moved to Long Island in 1638. New York City acquired the island in 1884, and the jail complex opened in 1932, replacing the deteriorating facility on Blackwell's Island. Landfill expanded the island from about 87 to 413 acres.

Conditions and closure

Rikers has long been criticised for violence, solitary confinement practices and poor conditions; the death of Kalief Browder, held for three years without trial, drew national attention. In 2019 the New York City Council voted to close the complex and replace it with four smaller borough-based jails, though the planned closure date has been repeatedly debated and delayed.
