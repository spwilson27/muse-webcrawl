The Millau Viaduct (French: Viaduc de Millau) is a cable-stayed road bridge that spans the gorge valley of the river Tarn near Millau in the Aveyron department of southern France. Designed by French structural engineer Michel Virlogeux and British architect Norman Foster, it is the tallest bridge in the world: one of its seven pylons rises 343 metres above the base of the structure, taller than the Eiffel Tower. It forms the final link of the A75 autoroute between Clermont-Ferrand and Béziers, relieving the notorious summer traffic bottlenecks in Millau.

The viaduct's seven slender pylons cross the Tarn gorge.

Design and construction

The bridge is 2,460 metres long and consists of eight steel spans supported by seven concrete pylons (piers), with six spans of 342 metres and two end spans of 204 metres. The steel roadway, weighing about 36,000 tonnes, was fabricated in sections by the Eiffel company, assembled on both banks, and launched across the piers using hydraulic inchworm pushers guided by GPS. The masts above the deck rise 87 metres and carry the cable stays. Construction began in October 2001 under a concession to the Compagnie Eiffage du Viaduc de Millau, which financed the roughly €394 million cost in exchange for tolls for 75 years. The deck was joined in May 2004 and the bridge was inaugurated by President Jacques Chirac on 14 December 2004, opening to traffic two days later — ahead of schedule.

Records and reception

The viaduct holds several distinctions: the tallest bridge structure in the world, France's highest road bridge, and one of the longest cable-stayed decks. It received the 2006 Outstanding Structure Award from the International Association for Bridge and Structural Engineering. Widely praised for its elegance, it became a tourist attraction in its own right, with a visitor centre and viewing areas, and it features in films and the Top Gear television series.

Practical details

The bridge carries four lanes of the A75 with a speed limit reduced to 110 km/h because of crosswinds; windscreens line the deck. Tolls are collected at a plaza north of the bridge. Cyclists and pedestrians are not permitted on the viaduct, though charity running and cycling events have occasionally closed it for mass crossings.
