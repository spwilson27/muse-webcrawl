Treaty of Big Tree

Signed15 September 1797
LocationGeneseo, New York
PartiesUnited States; Seneca Nation

The Treaty of Big Tree was a formal treaty signed in 1797 between the Seneca Nation and the United States, negotiated at Geneseo in western New York under a large tree on the Genesee River. Under the agreement the Seneca ceded most of their lands west of the Genesee River — some 3.5 million acres — in exchange for a payment of 00,000 and the reservation of about 200,000 acres in several reservations.

Background

The treaty was driven by land speculators, notably Robert Morris, who had acquired the pre-emption rights to Seneca lands and sold them on to the Holland Land Company. Negotiations were conducted on the American side by Jeremiah Wadsworth, acting as commissioner for Morris, with Seneca leaders including Red Jacket and Cornplanter. Mary Jemison, the adopted Seneca known as the "White Woman of the Genesee", played a notable role in securing favourable terms for her community.

Terms and consequences

The Seneca retained reservations including Allegany, Cattaraugus, Tonawanda, and Buffalo Creek, along with smaller tracts. The cession opened western New York to American settlement and the work of the Holland Purchase. Several reservations were later lost or reduced through subsequent agreements, most controversially the Treaty of Buffalo Creek in 1838.
