Temporal lobe

The temporal lobe is one of the four major lobes of the cerebral cortex in the brain of mammals. It is located beneath the lateral fissure on both cerebral hemispheres. The temporal lobe is involved in processing sensory input into derived meanings for the appropriate retention of visual memory, language comprehension, and emotion association.

Structure

The temporal lobe contains the primary auditory cortex, which receives sound information from the ears and secondary areas that process speech and language. Its superior temporal gyrus houses Wernicke's area, essential for understanding written and spoken language. On the medial surface lie the hippocampus, which is crucial for the formation of long-term memory, and the amygdala, involved in emotion. The fusiform gyrus on the underside participates in high-level visual processing, including face recognition.

Function

Functions of the temporal lobes include auditory perception, receptive language, visual object recognition, and the encoding of declarative memory. Damage can cause receptive aphasia, memory impairment (as in the famous case of patient H.M., who lost the ability to form new memories after bilateral medial temporal surgery), and disturbances such as déjà vu associated with temporal lobe epilepsy.
