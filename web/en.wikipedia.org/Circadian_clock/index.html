Circadian clock

A circadian clock, or circadian oscillator, is a biochemical mechanism that oscillates with a stable phase and is synchronized to solar time, generating circadian rhythms of approximately 24 hours in most living organisms, from cyanobacteria to humans.

Mechanism

In mammals the molecular clock is based on transcription–translation feedback loops: the proteins CLOCK and BMAL1 activate expression of the Period (PER) and Cryptochrome (CRY) genes, whose protein products accumulate, enter the nucleus, and repress their own transcription, producing a cycle of about 24 hours. The master clock in mammals is located in the suprachiasmatic nucleus of the hypothalamus and is entrained by light via the retina; peripheral clocks exist in most tissues.

Recognition

Jeffrey C. Hall, Michael Rosbash, and Michael W. Young received the 2017 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for their discoveries of the molecular mechanisms controlling circadian rhythm in fruit flies.
