Rainwater harvesting

A typical rooftop catchment, storage and filtration system.

Rainwater harvesting is the collection and storage of rainwater for reuse on site, rather than allowing it to run off. Water is gathered from roofs or ground surfaces into tanks, cisterns, barrels or aquifers and used for irrigation, livestock, flushing toilets, washing, and with treatment, drinking. The practice is thousands of years old: examples include the johads of Rajasthan, the eris (tanks) of Tamil Nadu, Roman cisterns, and the aguadas of the Maya.

Systems and treatment

A modern system typically comprises a catchment surface, gutters, a first-flush diverter (which discards the initial dirty runoff), filtration, storage and a pump or gravity feed. Water quality depends on the roofing material and storage conditions; disinfection is needed for potable use. Harvesting reduces demand on mains supplies and groundwater, mitigates urban stormwater flooding, and provides resilience in drought; several jurisdictions (including parts of Australia, Bermuda and Indian states such as Tamil Nadu) mandate or encourage it in new buildings.

Limitations

Supply is limited by rainfall and storage volume, and poorly maintained systems can harbour mosquitoes or contamination. Groundwater recharge structures — percolation pits, check dams and recharge shafts — are a related form that directs rainwater into aquifers rather than tanks.
