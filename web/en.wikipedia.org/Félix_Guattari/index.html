Félix Guattari

Pierre-Félix Guattari
Born: 30 April 1930, Villeneuve-les-Sablons, France
Died: 29 August 1992 (aged 62), Cour-Cheverny, France
Occupation: Psychoanalyst, philosopher, activist

Pierre-Félix Guattari (30 April 1930 – 29 August 1992) was a French psychoanalyst, political militant, and philosopher. He is best known for his collaborations with Gilles Deleuze, with whom he wrote Anti-Oedipus (1972) and A Thousand Plateaus (1980), the two volumes of Capitalism and Schizophrenia, as well as What Is Philosophy? (1991). Guattari also founded the experimental La Borde clinic and developed the concepts of schizoanalysis, ecosophy, and the "three ecologies".

Life and career

Guattari was born in Villeneuve-les-Sablons, a working-class suburb of Paris. Trained as a pharmacist and psychoanalyst, he studied under Jacques Lacan and was a member of Lacan's École Freudienne de Paris, though he later broke with Lacanian orthodoxy. From 1955 he worked at the La Borde psychiatric clinic in Cour-Cheverny, founded by Jean Oury, where he developed practices of "institutional psychotherapy" and transversality, breaking down hierarchies between doctors and patients.

A lifelong political activist, Guattari was involved in far-left movements, supported anti-colonial struggles, and participated in the events of May 1968. In the 1970s he co-founded the journal Recherches and the Centre d'études, de recherches et de formation institutionnelles (CERFI).

Work with Deleuze

Guattari met Deleuze in 1969. Their first collaboration, Capitalism and Schizophrenia: Anti-Oedipus (1972), attacked psychoanalysis and capitalism together, proposing "schizoanalysis" and the theory of desiring-production. Its sequel, A Thousand Plateaus (1980), introduced influential concepts such as the rhizome, deterritorialization, the body without organs, and assemblages (agencements).

Later work

In The Three Ecologies (1989), Guattari extended ecological thinking to the environment, social relations, and human subjectivity — an approach he called ecosophy. His solo works include Molecular Revolution (1977), The Winter Years (1986), and the posthumous Chaosmosis (1992). Guattari died of a heart attack at La Borde in 1992.
