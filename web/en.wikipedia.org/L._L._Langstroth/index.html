L. L. Langstroth

Lorenzo Lorraine Langstroth (25 December 1810 – 6 October 1895) was an American apiarist, clergyman and teacher, regarded as the "father of American beekeeping". He invented the Langstroth hive, the movable-frame beehive that remains the standard design for much of the world's beekeeping.

Contributions

Langstroth discovered the concept of "bee space" — the gap of roughly 3/8 inch that bees will not fill with comb or propolis — and in 1851 designed a hive with frames spaced accordingly, so frames could be lifted out for inspection and honey harvest without destroying the colony. He patented the hive in 1852 and published The Hive and the Honey-Bee (1853), a foundational manual that went through many editions.

Life

Born in Philadelphia and educated at Yale, Langstroth served as a Congregational pastor before turning full-time to bees. He spent his later years in Oxford, Ohio, and died in Dayton in 1895.
