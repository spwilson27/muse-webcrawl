Saskatchewan is a province in Western Canada, bordered by Alberta to the west, Manitoba to the east, the Northwest Territories to the north and the United States to the south. It is one of Canada's three Prairie Provinces, and the only province whose borders are entirely straight lines (created by surveying, not geography). Its name comes from the Cree name for the Saskatchewan River, kisiskāciwani-sīpiy, "swift flowing river."

Geography

The southern half is prairie and parkland, part of the breadbasket of Canada, growing wheat, canola, lentils and other pulses — Saskatchewan is the world's largest exporter of lentils and a major potash producer. The northern half is boreal forest of the Canadian Shield, dotted with roughly 100,000 lakes. The province has a continental climate with cold winters and warm summers, and is famous for flat horizons and dramatic skies.

History

Indigenous peoples have lived in the region for thousands of years. European fur traders arrived in the 17th–18th centuries; the territory was part of Rupert's Land and then the North-West Territories. Saskatchewan became a province on 1 September 1905, alongside Alberta. Mass immigration in the early 20th century made it a wheat economy; the province later pioneered North American social democracy under Tommy Douglas, whose CCF government introduced universal hospital insurance (1947) and medicare (1962), models for Canada nationwide.

Economy

Agriculture, mining (potash and uranium), oil and natural gas dominate. Regina hosts the legislature and the RCMP training academy (Depot Division); Saskatoon is a hub for mining and university research at the University of Saskatchewan.
