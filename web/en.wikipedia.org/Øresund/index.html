Øresund

The Øresund (Danish: Øresund; Swedish: Öresund) is a strait between Denmark and Sweden. Together with the Great Belt and the Little Belt, it is one of the Danish straits connecting the Baltic Sea to the Kattegat and the North Sea. The Øresund is one of the busiest shipping lanes in the world.

Geography

The strait runs from the Kattegat in the north to the Baltic Sea near the island of Bornholm in the south. Its narrowest point, between Helsingør in Denmark and Helsingborg in Sweden, is only about 4 kilometres wide. Major cities on its shores include Copenhagen and Malmö. Islands in the strait include Zealand, Amager, Saltholm, Peberholm (an artificial island built for the bridge), and Ven.

History

For centuries Denmark controlled both shores of the Øresund and levied the Sound Dues on foreign ships passing through, collected at Helsingør from 1429 until 1857. The Treaty of Roskilde in 1658 transferred the eastern shore (Scania) to Sweden. Control of the strait was strategically vital throughout the age of sail and during both World Wars.

The Øresund Bridge

The Øresund fixed link, opened on 1 July 2000, combines a bridge, an artificial island, and an undersea tunnel, carrying a four-lane motorway and a double-track railway between Copenhagen and Malmö. It created the cross-border Øresund Region, one of Europe's most integrated metropolitan areas, with tens of thousands of commuters crossing daily.
