Leader of the Official Opposition (Canada)

The Leader of the Official Opposition in Canada is the leader of the largest party in the House of Commons that is not in government — His Majesty's Loyal Opposition. The post is a central feature of Canada's Westminster-style parliamentary system.

Role

The opposition leader leads Question Period scrutiny of the government, appoints a shadow cabinet of critics, and is the alternative prime minister if the government falls. The office carries an official residence, Stornoway in Ottawa, a salary above that of other MPs, and membership of the Privy Council. By convention the leader is an MP, though party leaders without a Commons seat have occasionally served briefly while seeking election.

History

The position was formally recognised in 1905 with a salary, and Stornoway was assigned in 1950. Notable holders who later became prime minister include John Diefenbaker, Pierre Trudeau, Brian Mulroney, Jean Chrétien and Stephen Harper.
