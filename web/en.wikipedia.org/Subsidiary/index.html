Subsidiary

A subsidiary is a company owned or controlled by another company, called the parent or holding company. Control usually comes from owning a majority of the subsidiary's voting shares, though it can also arise from contract or board control. Subsidiaries are separate legal entities from their parents.

Field: Corporate law

Reasons for subsidiaries

Businesses organize operations into subsidiaries to limit legal liability, manage risk, obtain tax advantages, comply with local ownership laws, and keep distinct brands or business lines separate. Acquired companies often continue operating as subsidiaries of their acquirer.

Accounting and law

Parents generally consolidate subsidiaries' financial statements into group accounts. Because a subsidiary is legally distinct, creditors of one subsidiary normally cannot claim against the parent, though courts may pierce the corporate veil in cases of abuse.

Wholly owned subsidiaries

A subsidiary whose shares are entirely owned by the parent is called wholly owned; owning the whole company avoids sharing control or profits with minority shareholders.
