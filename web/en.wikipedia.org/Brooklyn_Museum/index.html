Brooklyn Museum

The Brooklyn Museum is an art museum located in the New York City borough of Brooklyn, at 200 Eastern Parkway near Prospect Park. Founded in 1823 as the Brooklyn Apprentices' Library, it is one of the oldest and largest art museums in the United States, with a collection of roughly 1.5 million works.

The museum's permanent collection includes extensive holdings of Egyptian art, African art, and American art from the colonial period to the present, including works by Edward Hopper, Winslow Homer, Georgia O'Keeffe, and Mark Rothko. Its building, designed by McKim, Mead & White, is a Beaux-Arts landmark.

The museum is known for exhibitions addressing contemporary social issues, its feminist art center named for Judy Chicago's The Dinner Party, and the popular First Saturdays free evening programs.
