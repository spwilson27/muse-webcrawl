Natural product

A natural product is a chemical compound produced by a living organism. In the broadest sense it includes any substance from nature; in chemistry and pharmacology it usually means a secondary metabolite — a compound not directly required for growth or reproduction but conferring ecological advantages.

Classes

Major classes include alkaloids (morphine, caffeine, quinine), terpenoids (menthol, artemisinin, paclitaxel), polyketides (erythromycin, lovastatin), nonribosomal peptides (penicillin, vancomycin), phenolics and flavonoids, and glycosides (digoxin).

Importance in medicine

Natural products and their derivatives have historically supplied roughly half of approved small-molecule drugs, especially antibiotics and anticancer agents. Famous examples include penicillin from Penicillium mould, aspirin (derived from willow-bark salicin), artemisinin from sweet wormwood (recognised by Tu Youyou's 2015 Nobel Prize), and the statins from fungi.

Study

The field combines organic synthesis, biosynthesis and enzymology, pharmacognosy, and genomics; modern work mines microbial genomes for silent biosynthetic gene clusters and engineers them in heterologous hosts.
