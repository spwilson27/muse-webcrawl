Risk assessment

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Risk assessment is the combined effort of identifying and analysing potential events that may negatively affect individuals, assets, or the environment, and making judgments on the tolerability of the resulting risk. It is a core step within the broader discipline of risk management.

Process

A typical risk assessment proceeds in stages. First, hazards are identified: sources of potential harm such as chemicals, machinery, pathogens, or natural disasters. Second, the probability of each hazard causing harm and the severity of that harm are estimated, often using a risk matrix that ranks combinations of likelihood and consequence. Third, the resulting risk levels are compared against criteria to decide whether further controls are required.

Assessments may be qualitative, using descriptive scales; semi-quantitative, using ranked indices; or quantitative, using numerical models and statistical data — an approach known as quantitative risk assessment (QRA), widely used in the nuclear, chemical process, and offshore oil industries.

Applications

Risk assessment underpins workplace health and safety law in many jurisdictions, where employers must assess risks to employees and implement proportionate controls following a hierarchy of controls. It is also central to environmental regulation, food safety (where it forms part of the risk analysis framework alongside risk management and risk communication), public health, finance, cybersecurity, and emergency planning.

Limitations

Assessments are only as good as their data and assumptions. Rare catastrophic events, cascading failures, and human behaviour are difficult to model, and assessors may be subject to cognitive biases such as optimism bias. For these reasons, risk assessment is generally treated as a decision-support tool rather than a definitive prediction.
