World Taekwondo (WT), formerly the World Taekwondo Federation (WTF), is the international governing body for the sport of taekwondo and a member of the Association of Summer Olympic International Federations. It was established on 28 May 1973 at its inaugural meeting held at the Kukkiwon in Seoul, South Korea, with participation from 35 countries.

History

The federation was founded as taekwondo spread internationally during the 1960s and early 1970s. It organized the first World Taekwondo Championships in Seoul in 1973. Taekwondo appeared as a demonstration sport at the 1988 Seoul Olympics and became an official medal sport at the Sydney 2000 Olympic Games. In June 2017 the organization shortened its name to World Taekwondo, partly because the acronym "WTF" had acquired an unrelated internet meaning.

Organization

World Taekwondo is composed of continental unions in Africa, Asia, Europe, Oceania, and the Pan-American region, together with more than 200 member national associations. Its headquarters are in Seoul, with a liaison office in Lausanne, Switzerland. The federation oversees the World Taekwondo Championships, the World Grand Prix series, and qualification for the Olympic Games.

Competitions

WT governs kyorugi (sparring) competitions contested in weight classes, as well as poomsae (forms) championships, para-taekwondo events, and the World Taekwondo Demonstration Team. Athletes compete under rules using electronic scoring protectors that register valid scoring techniques automatically.
