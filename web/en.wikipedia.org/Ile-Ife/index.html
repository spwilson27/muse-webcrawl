Ile-Ife

Ile-Ife (Yoruba: Ilé-Ifẹ̀), also spelled Ife, is an ancient Yoruba city in southwestern Nigeria, in present-day Osun State. It is revered in Yoruba mythology as the place where the world was created and the cradle of Yoruba civilization, and it remains a major spiritual center. The city is famous for its naturalistic bronze, brass, copper, and terracotta sculptures dating from the 12th to the 15th centuries.

Mythology

In Yoruba creation myth, the supreme god Olodumare sent Obatala (in some accounts Oduduwa) down from the sky with a chain, a rooster, and a handful of earth, which was scattered over the primordial waters to create the land at Ife. Oduduwa became the first Ooni (king) of Ife, and his descendants founded the other Yoruba kingdoms, making Ile-Ife the ancestral home and spiritual capital of the Yoruba people.

History

Archaeological evidence suggests settlement at Ife by at least the 9th century, with urban florescence between the 12th and 15th centuries. The kingdom traded glass beads and other goods widely across West Africa. The city's kingship, headed by the Ooni, retained immense religious prestige even as political power later shifted to kingdoms such as Oyo and Benin.

Art

Ife is celebrated for sculptures of extraordinary naturalism, including life-size heads cast in brass and copper by the lost-wax method and finely modeled terracotta heads. German ethnographer Leo Frobenius encountered these works in 1910 and famously (and wrongly) attributed them to a lost Greek colony; they are now recognized as masterpieces of indigenous Yoruba artistry. Many works are held by the National Museum in Lagos and the British Museum.

Modern city

Today Ile-Ife is a city in Osun State and home to Obafemi Awolowo University, founded in 1961. The Ooni of Ife remains one of the most revered traditional rulers in Nigeria.
