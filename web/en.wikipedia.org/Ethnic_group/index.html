Ethnic group

An ethnic group (or ethnicity) is a group of people who identify with each other on the basis of perceived shared attributes that distinguish them from other groups. Those attributes can include a common nation of origin, or common sets of ancestry, traditions, language, history, society, religion, or social treatment within their residing area.

Definitions and approaches

Classic definitions, such as Max Weber's, emphasise a subjective belief in common descent. Fredrik Barth's influential Ethnic Groups and Boundaries (1969) shifted attention from a group's cultural inventory to the boundaries maintained between groups. Scholars distinguish primordialist accounts (ethnicity as a deep, given attachment), instrumentalist accounts (ethnicity as a resource mobilised for advantage), and constructivist accounts (ethnicity as socially produced and situational).

Ethnicity and related concepts

Ethnicity differs from race, which has typically been ascribed on the basis of physical characteristics, and from nationality, which denotes legal membership in a state, though the three often overlap. Ethnic groups may share a distinctive culture, language, or religion, but none of these alone defines them; what matters is mutual identification.

Politics and conflict

Ethnic identification can be a basis for community and cultural preservation, but also for discrimination and violent conflict, from the Balkan wars to the Rwandan genocide. Many states collect ethnic statistics and some organise political representation along ethnic lines.
