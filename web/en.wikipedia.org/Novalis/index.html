Novalis was the pen name of Georg Philipp Friedrich Freiherr von Hardenberg (2 May 1772 – 25 March 1801), a German aristocrat, poet, philosopher, and mining engineer, and a central figure of early German Romanticism. His image of the "blue flower" (die blaue Blume) became the emblem of Romantic longing.

Life

Born into Saxon nobility, Hardenberg studied law at Jena, Leipzig, and Wittenberg, where he met Friedrich Schlegel and other Jena Romantics, and later studied mining at the Freiberg Mining Academy under Abraham Gottlob Werner. In 1795 he became engaged to the twelve-year-old Sophie von Kühn; her death from illness in 1797, days after her fifteenth birthday, devastated him and deeply shaped his writing. He later became engaged to Julie von Charpentier. He worked as a salt-mine administrator while writing intensely, and died of tuberculosis aged 28.

Works

His cycle Hymnen an die Nacht (Hymns to the Night, 1800), blending verse and prose, exalts night and death as passage to a transfigured existence. His unfinished novel Heinrich von Ofterdingen (published posthumously, 1802) tells of a medieval poet's quest for the blue flower. His notebooks, published as fragments, developed a programme of "magical idealism" and the romanticising of the world: "The world must be romanticised." His works were published after his death by friends including Friedrich Schlegel and Ludwig Tieck.
