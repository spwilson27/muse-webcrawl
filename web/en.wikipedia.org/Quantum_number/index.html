Quantum number

In quantum mechanics and in chemistry, quantum numbers describe values of conserved quantities in the dynamics of a quantum system. Quantum numbers correspond to eigenvalues of operators that commute with the Hamiltonian and can consequently be measured precisely at the same time as the system's energy, in accordance with the uncertainty principle.


Atomic orbitals and their quantum numbers

Electron quantum numbers

In atomic physics, the state of an electron in an atom can be specified completely by four quantum numbers:

NameSymbolAllowed values
Principal quantum numbern1, 2, 3, ...
Azimuthal quantum numberℓ0, 1, ..., n − 1
Magnetic quantum numbermℓ−ℓ, ..., +ℓ
Spin quantum numberms+1/2 or −1/2 for electrons

The principal quantum number primarily determines the energy of the electron and the size of its orbital. The azimuthal quantum number gives the orbital angular momentum, the magnetic quantum number its orientation in space, and the spin quantum number the electron's intrinsic spin projection. The Pauli exclusion principle states that no two electrons in an atom can share the same set of four quantum numbers.

Other quantum numbers

Many further quantum numbers arise in particle physics, including electric charge, baryon number, lepton number, isospin, strangeness, charm and parity. In nuclear physics, total angular momentum and its projection label the states of nuclei.
