Richelieu River

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Richelieu River (French: Rivière Richelieu) is a river of Quebec, Canada, and a small portion of northern New York State. It flows northward from Lake Champlain for about 124 kilometres (77 miles) to the St. Lawrence River at Sorel-Tracy. Historically it was a key invasion and trade corridor between Canada and the United States, known to early explorers as the Iroquois River and later named for Cardinal Richelieu.

History

Samuel de Champlain was the first European to travel the river, in 1609. The French built a chain of forts along it — including Fort Richelieu (1642), Fort Saint-Louis (later Chambly), and Fort Sainte-Thérèse — to defend New France against Iroquois raids. The valley was contested during the French and Indian War and the American Revolutionary War, and again during the War of 1812. The Chambly Canal, opened in 1843, bypasses the rapids between Chambly and Saint-Jean-sur-Richelieu and is now a National Historic Site.

Geography and flooding

The river drains a basin of about 23,400 square kilometres, including Lake Champlain and Lake George. Towns along its banks include Saint-Jean-sur-Richelieu, Beloeil, and Saint-Denis-sur-Richelieu, a centre of the Lower Canada Rebellion of 1837. In the spring of 2011 the river experienced record flooding that inundated thousands of homes in the Montérégie region and parts of Vermont and New York.
