Week of Modern Art

The Week of Modern Art (Portuguese: Semana de Arte Moderna) was an arts festival in São Paulo, Brazil, held at the Theatro Municipal between 11 and 18 February 1922. Grouped as a reaction against the conservative cultural establishment, the event launched Brazilian Modernism, presenting new experiments in painting, sculpture, poetry, music and architecture to a scandalized public.

Participants

Organized with the support of the patron Paulo Prado and figures such as Mário de Andrade, Oswald de Andrade, Anita Malfatti, Emiliano Di Cavalcanti, Heitor Villa-Lobos, Graça Aranha, Menotti Del Picchia and Victor Brecheret, the week included exhibitions and three evening sessions of lectures, concerts and poetry readings. The painter Anita Malfatti's expressionist works, earlier attacked by the critic Monteiro Lobato, were central to the show, and Villa-Lobos's modernist compositions shocked audiences.

Legacy

Although the immediate reception mixed boos with applause, the Week became the founding moment of modern Brazilian culture. Its participants went on to found the movements Pau-Brasil and Antropofagia (anthropophagy), Oswald de Andrade's 1928 Manifesto Antropófago famously urging Brazilian artists to "devour" European culture and make it their own. The centenary in 2022 prompted nationwide exhibitions and reassessments.
