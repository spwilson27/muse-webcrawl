Water tower

A water tower is an elevated structure supporting a water tank constructed at a height sufficient to pressurise a water distribution system for potable water. The reservoir supplies water during peak demand and maintains pressure if pumps fail.

Design

Elevating water by about 10 metres yields roughly 100 kPa (about 15 psi) of pressure at ground level. Towers are built of steel, reinforced concrete, or brick, and in many towns bear the community's name as a landmark. Some historic towers have been converted to apartments or observation decks.

Operation

Tanks are filled during off-peak hours and drained during the day. Typical capacities range from a few tens of thousands of litres for small towns to several million litres for larger systems.
