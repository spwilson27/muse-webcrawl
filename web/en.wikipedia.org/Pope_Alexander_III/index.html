Pope Alexander III

Pope Alexander III (c. 1100/1105 – 30 August 1181), born Roland (Italian: Rolando) of Siena, was head of the Catholic Church and ruler of the Papal States from 7 September 1159 to his death in 1181. A canon lawyer and theologian, he is remembered for his long struggle with Emperor Frederick Barbarossa, for presiding over the Third Lateran Council of 1179, and for laying the legal foundations of the papal monarchy of the high Middle Ages.

Early career

Roland was a professor of canon law at Bologna and author of the Summa Rolandi, an early commentary on Gratian's Decretum, and of a book of Sentences. He entered the service of the Roman Church, becoming cardinal deacon in 1150 and chancellor of the Church under Pope Adrian IV. As papal legate he famously confronted Frederick Barbarossa at the Diet of Besançon in 1157 over language implying that the Empire was a benefice granted by the pope.

Pontificate

Alexander's election in 1159 was disputed: a minority of cardinals proclaimed Octavian of Monticelli as Victor IV, the first of a series of imperial antipopes, and Alexander spent much of his early pontificate in exile in France. He supported Thomas Becket against Henry II of England and, after Becket's murder in 1170, canonized him in 1173 and compelled Henry to do public penance. His long conflict with Barbarossa ended after the Lombard League's victory at Legnano (1176) and the Treaty of Venice (1177), at which the emperor recognized Alexander and abandoned his antipopes.

Third Lateran Council and legacy

In March 1179 Alexander presided over the Third Lateran Council, which decreed that a two-thirds majority of cardinals was required for a valid papal election — a rule that, as modified, still governs conclaves — and legislated against heresy, simony and clerical abuses, also ordering that cathedral schools provide free education. His decretals were fundamental to the development of canon law, and he is also credited with laying the foundation stone of Notre-Dame de Paris in 1163. He died at Civita Castellana in 1181.
