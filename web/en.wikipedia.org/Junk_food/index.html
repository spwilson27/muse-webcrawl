Junk food

[Image: A selection of typical junk foods: burgers, fries, soft drinks and candy]

Junk food is a term for food that provides high levels of calories from sugar or fat with little dietary fibre, protein, vitamins or minerals. Such foods typically include salty snacks, candy, sweet desserts, fried fast food and sugar-sweetened beverages. The term is generally pejorative and is used in public-health contexts to describe products associated with obesity and diet-related disease when consumed in excess.

History

The term is commonly attributed to Michael F. Jacobson of the Center for Science in the Public Interest, who used it in the early 1970s. The rise of junk food paralleled the post-war expansion of industrial food processing, supermarket distribution and fast-food chains in the United States and later worldwide.

Health effects

Diets high in junk food are associated with increased risk of obesity, type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease and dental caries. Because such foods are energy-dense but micronutrient-poor, heavy consumption can displace more nutritious foods and contribute to "hidden hunger" — deficiencies of vitamins and minerals despite adequate calories.

Regulation and policy

Governments have responded with measures including taxes on sugar-sweetened beverages (such as Mexico's 2014 soda tax and the UK Soft Drinks Industry Levy), restrictions on advertising to children, front-of-pack warning labels (pioneered by Chile in 2016), and limits on the use of industrial trans fats.
