Riders tackling a cobbled helling (climb) during the Flemish spring classics.

The cobbled classics are a series of one-day professional road cycling races held each spring in Belgium and northern France, distinguished by their rough sectors of cobblestones (pavé) and, in Flanders, by short steep cobbled climbs known as hellingen or bergs. Together with the Ardennes classics that follow them, they form the heart of the spring classics season.

Principal races

  • E3 Saxo Bank Classic (Harelbeke) — a key preparatory race over many of the Tour of Flanders climbs.
  • Gent–Wevelgem — includes the gravel Plugstreets and the Kemmelberg; often decided by wind and echelons.
  • Dwars door Vlaanderen — the midweek race between Gent–Wevelgem and the Tour of Flanders.
  • Tour of Flanders (De Ronde, first held 1913) — a Monument of cycling, finishing in Oudenaarde after climbs such as the Oude Kwaremont, Paterberg, and Koppenberg, and formerly the Muur van Geraardsbergen.
  • Paris–Roubaix (first held 1896) — the "Hell of the North" and "Queen of the Classics," its flat pavé sectors including the Trouée d'Arenberg forest and the Carrefour de l'Arbre, finishing in the Roubaix velodrome.

Character of the races

The cobbled classics reward bike-handling, positioning, and raw power: the pavé shakes riders violently, punctures and crashes are frequent, and the narrow Flemish climbs create natural selection points where races explode. Specialist "Flandriens" — hard, durable riders — dominate the roll of honour: Johan Museeuw, Tom Boonen, and Fabian Cancellara each won multiple editions of both Flanders and Roubaix, while Eddy Merckx and Roger De Vlaeminck (record four Roubaix victories, "Monsieur Paris–Roubaix") remain legendary figures of the pavé.

Women's editions of the major cobbled classics have grown rapidly in the 21st century, with the women's Tour of Flanders established in 2004 and Paris–Roubaix Femmes first held in 2021.
