The Frankfurt Stock Exchange (German: Frankfurter Wertpapierbörse, FWB) is a stock exchange located in Frankfurt, Germany. Operated by Deutsche Börse AG, it is the largest of Germany's seven stock exchanges and one of the largest trading venues in Europe, accounting for the great majority of turnover in the German market.

History

The exchange traces its origins to 1585, when merchants at the Frankfurt fair adopted a decree fixing exchange rates for the many currencies then circulating, one of the earliest steps toward an organised exchange. A dedicated exchange building, the Neue Börse with its famous trading hall and the bull and bear statues outside, opened in 1879 in the city centre. Share trading in Frankfurt grew with German industrialisation, and after the Second World War the exchange consolidated its position as the leading bourse of West Germany.

Trading today

Most trading is now electronic, conducted through the Xetra system, launched in 1997 and also licensed to other exchanges. The exchange's best-known index is the DAX, composed of forty major German blue-chip companies, alongside the MDAX and SDAX for mid- and small-cap shares. Floor trading continues in the historic hall, mainly for bonds, funds, and structured products, though the open-outcry Parkett survives largely in specialised segments.
