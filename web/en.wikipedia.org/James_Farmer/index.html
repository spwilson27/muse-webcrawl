James Farmer

Born12 January 1920, Marshall, Texas, U.S.
Died9 July 1999, Fredericksburg, Virginia, U.S.
Known forCo-founding CORE; Freedom Rides

James Leonard Farmer Jr. (12 January 1920 – 9 July 1999) was an American civil rights activist and a leader of the civil rights movement. He co-founded the Congress of Racial Equality (CORE) in 1942 and organized the 1961 Freedom Rides.

Early life

Farmer was a child prodigy who entered Wiley College at 14 and earned a divinity degree from Howard University, where he studied Gandhian nonviolence under the influence of Howard Thurman.

CORE and the Freedom Rides

In Chicago in 1942, Farmer and colleagues founded CORE to pursue racial equality through nonviolent direct action, pioneering sit-ins against segregated restaurants. In 1961, as CORE's national director, he organized the Freedom Rides, in which interracial groups rode interstate buses into the segregated South, enduring beatings and arrests that forced federal enforcement of desegregation in interstate travel.

Later career

Farmer served as Assistant Secretary of Health, Education, and Welfare under President Richard Nixon, taught at Mary Washington College, and in 1998 received the Presidential Medal of Freedom.
