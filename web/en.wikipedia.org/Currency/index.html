Currency

A currency is a standardisation of money in any form in use or circulation as a medium of exchange, such as banknotes and coins. More generally, it is a system of money in common use within a specific environment, especially a nation. Examples include the United States dollar, the euro, the Japanese yen, and the pound sterling.

Currency
Forms: Banknotes, coins, digital
Issuers: Central banks, treasuries
Most traded: US dollar, euro, yen
System: ISO 4217 currency codes

History

Early currencies included commodity money such as cowrie shells, grain, and precious metals. Coinage appeared in Lydia around the 7th century BC, and paper money developed in China by the Song dynasty. Modern currencies are mostly fiat money, issued and regulated by governments and central banks without intrinsic commodity value.

Exchange and trade

Currencies are traded on the foreign exchange market, the world's largest financial market, where values fluctuate under floating exchange rates or are pegged to other currencies or baskets. Exchange rates affect international trade, investment, and monetary policy.

Modern developments

Most money now exists as bank deposits and electronic records. Central bank digital currencies and private cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin are reshaping debates about the future of currency, payments, and monetary sovereignty.
