Vandals

The Vandals migrated from the Baltic region through Gaul and Spain to North Africa.

The Vandals were a Germanic people who first inhabited what is now southern Poland. During the Migration Period they travelled through Gaul and the Iberian Peninsula, ultimately founding a powerful kingdom in North Africa centred on Carthage, which endured from 435 to 534 AD. Their sack of Rome in 455 gave rise to the modern word vandalism for wanton destruction — a reputation modern historians regard as largely undeserved.

Migration

On the last night of 406, the Vandals, together with Alans and Suebi, crossed the frozen Rhine near Mainz into Roman Gaul, overwhelming resistance and plundering their way south. In 409 they entered Spain, where the Hasding Vandals settled in Gallaecia and the Siling Vandals in Baetica — the region of Andalusia may preserve their name. Roman-Visigothic campaigns destroyed the Siling Vandals and pushed the survivors to merge with the Alans under King Gunderic.

The African kingdom

In 429, Gunderic's half-brother and successor Genseric led the entire people — perhaps 80,000 souls — across the Strait of Gibraltar into North Africa. After seizing Hippo Regius (where Augustine died during the siege) and taking Carthage in 439, the Vandals controlled Rome's grain supply and built a formidable fleet that raided across the Mediterranean. A treaty with Constantinople in 442 recognised the Vandal kingdom. In June 455, invited (according to tradition) by the empress Licinia Eudoxia, Genseric's forces sacked Rome systematically for two weeks, carrying off treasures and captives — but, by agreement with Pope Leo I, sparing the city's buildings and inhabitants from fire and slaughter.

Religion and society

The Vandals were Arian Christians and at times persecuted the Nicene Catholic clergy of Africa, exiling bishops and closing churches, though severity varied by reign. The kingdom preserved much of Roman administration, law, and culture; Latin poetry and learning flourished at the Carthage court.

Fall

Weakened by succession disputes and war with the Moors, the kingdom fell swiftly to the Byzantine general Belisarius in 533–534. King Gelimer surrendered after the battles of Ad Decimum and Tricamarum, and Vandal Africa became a Byzantine province; the Vandals then disappear from history as a distinct people.
