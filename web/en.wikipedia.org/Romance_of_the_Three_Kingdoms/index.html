Romance of the Three Kingdoms (traditional Chinese: 三國演義; simplified Chinese: 三国演义; pinyin: Sānguó Yǎnyì) is a 14th-century historical novel attributed to Luo Guanzhong. It is set in the turbulent years towards the end of the Han dynasty and the Three Kingdoms period in Chinese history, starting in 169 AD and ending with the reunification of China under the Jin dynasty in 280 AD. It is one of the Four Great Classical Novels of Chinese literature.

Overview

The novel, written in 120 chapters, dramatizes the lives of feudal lords and their retainers who vied to succeed the collapsed Han dynasty. Its principal narrative threads follow the kingdoms of Shu, founded by Liu Bei; Wei, founded by Cao Cao and his son Cao Pi; and Wu, founded by the Sun family. Although the story is based on historical records such as Chen Shou's Records of the Three Kingdoms, it departs freely from fact: it glorifies the Shu-Han regime and its heroes — Liu Bei, the strategist Zhuge Liang, and the warriors Guan Yu, Zhang Fei, and Zhao Yun — while casting Cao Cao as a scheming villain.

Famous episodes

  • The Oath of the Peach Garden, in which Liu Bei, Guan Yu, and Zhang Fei swear brotherhood.
  • The Battle of Red Cliffs (208), where the allied forces of Liu Bei and Sun Quan defeat Cao Cao's armada.
  • Zhuge Liang's stratagems, including borrowing arrows with straw boats and the Empty Fort Strategy.
  • Guan Yu's heroic exploits, later leading to his deification as a god of war and loyalty.

Influence

The novel has profoundly shaped Chinese and East Asian popular culture. Its characters and episodes permeate opera, storytelling, video games (notably Koei's Dynasty Warriors and Romance of the Three Kingdoms series), television dramas, and idioms in everyday Chinese speech. Its opening line — "The empire, long divided, must unite; long united, must divide" — is among the most famous in Chinese literature. Major English translations include those of C. H. Brewitt-Taylor (1925) and Moss Roberts (1991).
