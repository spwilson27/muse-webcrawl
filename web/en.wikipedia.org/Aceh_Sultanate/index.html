Aceh Sultanate

Aceh Sultanate
Existed: 1496–1903
Capital: Kutaraja (Banda Aceh)
Religion: Islam
Founder: Ali Mughayat Syah

The Sultanate of Aceh, officially the Kingdom of Aceh Darussalam, was a sultanate centered in modern-day Aceh Province, Indonesia. It was a major regional power in the 16th and 17th centuries and a wealthy entrepôt for the pepper and spice trade.

History

Founded around 1496 by Ali Mughayat Syah, Aceh expanded under sultans such as Alauddin al-Kahar and reached its zenith under Iskandar Muda (r. 1607–1636), controlling much of Sumatra and parts of the Malay Peninsula. Later in the 17th century it was ruled by a remarkable succession of four queens (sultanas).

Decline and the Aceh War

Aceh's power declined in the 18th and 19th centuries. In 1873 the Dutch East Indies declared war; the Aceh War lasted until 1904 and became one of the longest and costliest colonial conflicts in Dutch history.

Culture

Aceh was a center of Islamic scholarship, dubbed the "veranda of Mecca", and maintained diplomatic ties with the Ottoman Empire.
