East Jerusalem

East Jerusalem is the part of Jerusalem that was held by Jordan after the 1948 Arab–Israeli War and captured by Israel in the 1967 Six-Day War. It includes Jerusalem's Old City — with sites holy to Judaism, Christianity and Islam, including the Western Wall, the Church of the Holy Sepulchre and the Al-Aqsa Mosque compound (Temple Mount/Haram al-Sharif) — together with surrounding neighbourhoods and villages incorporated into an expanded municipal boundary of about 70 km².

Status

After 1967 Israel extended its law and administration to East Jerusalem, a step widely described as annexation and consolidated by the 1980 Jerusalem Law declaring united Jerusalem Israel's capital. The international community has generally not recognised this annexation: UN Security Council Resolution 478 declared the Jerusalem Law null and void, and most states regard East Jerusalem as occupied territory and as the intended capital of a future Palestinian state, a position maintained by the Palestinian leadership. Palestinian residents of East Jerusalem mostly hold Israeli permanent-residency status rather than citizenship.

Demographics and issues

East Jerusalem's population is a few hundred thousand, a majority Palestinian (Arab) in the eastern neighbourhoods, alongside Israeli settlements built after 1967 (such as French Hill, Pisgat Ze'ev and Gilo, which lie within the expanded municipal borders) that are considered illegal under international law by most states and legal by Israel. Contested issues include housing demolitions, residency revocations, settlement expansion, and control of and access to the holy sites, disputes over which periodically trigger wider unrest.
