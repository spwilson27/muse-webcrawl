Celera Genomics

Founded1998, Rockville, Maryland
FoundersApplera Corporation; J. Craig Venter (president)

Celera Genomics was a biotechnology company founded in 1998 as a division of the PE Corporation (Applera), with J. Craig Venter as president. Its founding mission was to sequence the human genome faster and more cheaply than the publicly funded Human Genome Project, using whole-genome shotgun sequencing on an industrial scale, and to sell subscription access to its genomic databases.


Automated capillary sequencers of the type used at Celera

The genome race

Celera first sequenced the genome of the fruit fly Drosophila melanogaster (published 2000) as a proof of method, then began on the human genome in September 1999. The rivalry with the public consortium ended in a celebrated tie: in June 2000 the two efforts jointly announced the completion of a working draft at the White House, and their papers appeared in February 2001 — Celera's in Science, the consortium's in Nature. Celera's assembly relied in part on the public project's data, a point of lasting controversy. The genome Celera published was a composite of several donors, with Venter himself later revealed as a major contributor.

Later history

After 2002 Celera shifted from information sales toward drug discovery. In 2011 Quest Diagnostics acquired Celera Corporation for roughly $657 million, and the business became part of Quest's clinical diagnostics operations.
