Skandagupta

Skandagupta (Gupta script: Ska-nda-gu-pta) was an emperor of the Gupta Empire of northern India, reigning from about 455 to 467 CE. He is generally regarded as the last of the great Gupta emperors; his reign is notable for repelling invasions by the Hephthalites (Hunas) even as the effort strained the empire's finances.

Reign: c. 455–467 CE
Predecessor: Kumaragupta I
Successor: Purugupta (disputed)

Reign

The Bhitari pillar inscription records Skandagupta's victories over the Pushyamitras and the Hunas, comparing his defence of the empire to the god Vishnu's feats. His Junagadh inscription describes the repair of the embankment of Lake Sudarshana, an irrigation work first built under Chandragupta Maurya. The succession after his death is murky: later inscriptions suggest his half-brother Purugupta took the throne, and the empire's coherence declined in the following decades.

Coinage

Skandagupta issued gold, silver and copper coins, but the debasement of his later gold coinage is often taken as evidence of the fiscal cost of his wars.
