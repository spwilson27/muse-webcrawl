Suharto (1921–2008) was an Indonesian military officer and politician who served as the second president of Indonesia from 1967 to 1998, ruling for 31 years at the head of the authoritarian 'New Order' regime.

Rise to Power

A career army officer in the independence war against the Dutch, Suharto commanded Kostrad (Army Strategic Reserve) during the failed 30 September Movement coup attempt in 1965, blamed on the Communist Party (PKI). The ensuing anti-communist mass killings of 1965–66 killed an estimated 500,000 to over a million people. Using the Supersemar order of March 1966, Suharto progressively sidelined President Sukarno, becoming acting president in 1967 and president in 1968.

New Order

His government stabilised the economy with technocratic policies, achieving rice self-sufficiency, industrial growth, and poverty reduction, backed by Western support in the Cold War. It also centralised power through the Golkar party, censored the press, committed abuses in East Timor (invaded 1975), Aceh, and Papua, and enabled extensive corruption by the Suharto family.

Fall and Legacy

The 1997 Asian financial crisis triggered protests and riots; Suharto resigned on 21 May 1998, succeeded by B. J. Habibie, ushering in the Reformasi era. He died in 2008 without facing trial, his legacy still contested between development and authoritarianism.
