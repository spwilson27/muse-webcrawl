Game jam

[ Image placeholder: Participants collaborating at a game jam event ]

A game jam is a contest or hackathon in which participants attempt to create a video game (or board game) from scratch within a short period of time, usually between 24 and 72 hours. Participants may be programmers, game designers, artists, writers and others in game development-related fields. Often a game jam is centred on a theme, announced at the start, to which participants must relate their game. Traditionally, the games are smaller, experimental prototypes rather than fully-polished products.

History

The term originated by analogy with the concept of a jam session in music. One of the earliest recognised game jams was the 0th Indie Game Jam in March 2002, organized in Oakland, California, where designers Chris Hecker, Sean Barrett and others experimented with game technology. The Ludum Dare competition, founded in 2002, popularised the online, solo-format jam. The Global Game Jam, first held in 2009, became the largest such event, with tens of thousands of participants at physical sites worldwide creating thousands of games each year.

Format

Jams may be held at physical locations, online, or in hybrid form. Common structures include:

  • Competitions, such as Ludum Dare's "Compo" (48 hours, solo, all assets created during the event), with public voting and rankings.
  • Non-competitive jams, focused on creativity and community rather than prizes.
  • Theme constraints and optional "diversifiers" that add extra challenges or accessibility goals.

Impact

Game jams are valued for rapid prototyping, portfolio building, networking and experimentation. Several commercially successful games began as jam prototypes, including Superhot (7DFPS jam), Surgeon Simulator (Global Game Jam 2013) and Baba Is You (Nordic Game Jam 2017).

Notable jams

Global Game Jam — the world's largest, held simultaneously at physical sites in over 100 countries. Ludum Dare — one of the longest-running online jams. Nordic Game Jam — an annual event in Copenhagen. Numerous smaller jams run year-round on platforms such as itch.io.
