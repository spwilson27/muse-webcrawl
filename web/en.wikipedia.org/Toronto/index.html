Toronto is the most populous city in Canada and the capital of the province of Ontario. With a population of about 2.8 million in 2021, it is the fourth-most-populous city in North America, and the centre of the Greater Toronto Area, home to more than six million people. Located on the northwestern shore of Lake Ontario, Toronto is Canada's principal financial and commercial centre and one of the most multicultural cities in the world, with more than half of its residents born outside Canada.

History

Indigenous peoples inhabited the area for over 10,000 years before European contact; the city's name derives from the Mohawk word tkaronto, "where there are trees in the water". The French established Fort Rouillé in 1750, and the British founded the town of York in 1793 as the capital of Upper Canada. York was raided and burned by American forces during the War of 1812. It was incorporated as the City of Toronto in 1834. The modern city took shape in 1998 with the amalgamation of six municipalities.

Economy and culture

Toronto hosts the Toronto Stock Exchange, the headquarters of Canada's five largest banks in the Financial District, and major technology, media and life-sciences sectors. Its landmarks include the CN Tower — for 32 years the world's tallest free-standing structure — the Royal Ontario Museum, the Art Gallery of Ontario and the Rogers Centre. The city is home to the Toronto Maple Leafs, Raptors, Blue Jays and Toronto FC, and hosts the Toronto International Film Festival, one of the largest film festivals in the world.
