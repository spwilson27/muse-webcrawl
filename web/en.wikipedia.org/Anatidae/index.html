FamilyAnatidae

The Anatidae are the biological family of water birds that includes ducks, geese, and swans. The family contains roughly 170 species in about 50 genera and has a cosmopolitan distribution, occurring on all continents except Antarctica. Most species are adapted to life on or near water, particularly wetlands, lakes, rivers, and coasts.

Characteristics

Anatids are generally heavy-bodied birds with long necks relative to other waterfowl, broad flattened bills, and webbed feet adapted for swimming. Many species show strong sexual dimorphism, with males more brightly coloured than females. Ducks, geese, and swans feed on a variety of plant material, invertebrates, and small aquatic animals, using lamellae along the edges of the bill to filter food from water in many duck species.

Relationship with humans

Several species have been domesticated, including the domestic duck (descended from the mallard) and domestic geese (from the greylag and swan goose). Anatids are also important game birds, and many species are kept in wildfowl collections and zoos. Habitat loss and hunting have threatened some species, and conservation programs protect important wetland sites under agreements such as the Ramsar Convention.
