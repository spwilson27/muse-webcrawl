Class I railroad

A Class I railroad is the largest category of freight railroad in North America, as classified by the U.S. Surface Transportation Board (STB) according to annual operating revenue. The threshold is adjusted for inflation; in recent decades it has stood at several hundred million dollars (about $1 billion in the early 2020s). Smaller carriers are designated Class II (regional) or Class III (short line) railroads.

Current Class I railroads

Following a century of consolidation, the Class I carriers in the United States are BNSF Railway, Union Pacific, CSX Transportation, Norfolk Southern, Canadian National (U.S. operations), Canadian Pacific Kansas City, and, by revenue classification, Amtrak and Via Rail are sometimes treated separately as passenger carriers. The 2023 merger of Canadian Pacific and Kansas City Southern created the first single-line network linking Canada, the United States, and Mexico.

History

The classification originated with the Interstate Commerce Commission in 1911, when hundreds of railroads qualified as Class I. Deregulation under the Staggers Act of 1980 accelerated mergers, producing today's handful of transcontinental systems that handle the great majority of U.S. rail freight—coal, grain, containers, automobiles, and chemicals—over roughly 90,000 route-miles.
