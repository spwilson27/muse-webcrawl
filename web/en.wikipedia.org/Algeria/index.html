Algeria, officially the People's Democratic Republic of Algeria, is a country in North Africa on the Mediterranean coast. It is the largest country in Africa and the tenth-largest in the world by area, with a territory dominated by the Sahara desert in the south and the Atlas Mountains and fertile coastal plain in the north. Its capital and largest city is Algiers.

History

Algeria's history stretches from the ancient Berber (Amazigh) kingdoms and Numidia, through Roman, Vandal, Byzantine and Arab rule, to the Regency of Algiers under the Ottoman Empire, a centre of Barbary piracy. France invaded in 1830 and made northern Algeria an integral part of France, while over a million European settlers (pieds-noirs) colonised the land. The Algerian War of Independence (1954–1962), led by the National Liberation Front (FLN), was exceptionally violent and ended with the Évian Accords and independence in 1962. After decades of one-party and military-dominated rule, a cancelled election in 1992 triggered a brutal civil war in the 1990s. In 2019 the Hirak protest movement forced the resignation of long-serving president Abdelaziz Bouteflika.

Geography and economy

Over 90% of Algerians live on the northern coastal strip; the vast Sahara contains the country's principal wealth: oil and natural gas, of which Algeria is a major exporter to Europe. Hydrocarbons dominate government revenue, and state company Sonatrach is Africa's largest firm. Agriculture, phosphates and a growing manufacturing sector contribute to an economy still heavily dependent on energy prices.

Culture

Algerian culture blends Amazigh, Arab, Ottoman and French influences, reflected in its languages (Algerian Arabic, Tamazight and widespread French), its raï and chaabi music, and landmarks from the Roman ruins of Timgad and Tipaza to the Casbah of Algiers, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
