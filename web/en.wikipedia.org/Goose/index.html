Goose

Kingdom: Animalia

Phylum: Chordata

Class: Aves

Order: Anseriformes

Family: Anatidae

Subfamily: Anserinae

A goose (plural geese) is a bird of any of several waterfowl species in the family Anatidae. This group comprises the genera Anser (the grey geese and white geese) and Branta (the black geese). Some other birds, mostly related to the shelducks, have "goose" as part of their names.

Description

Geese are generally larger than ducks and smaller than swans. They have long necks, stout bills adapted for grazing, and strong legs for walking on land. True geese are strong fliers and many species undertake long migrations, flying in characteristic V-shaped formations that reduce wind resistance and allow the flock to cover great distances.

Species and distribution

There are around 17 to 20 living species of true geese, found on all continents except Antarctica. Well-known species include the Canada goose (Branta canadensis), the greylag goose (Anser anser) — the ancestor of most domestic geese — and the snow goose (Anser caerulescens).

Domestication

Geese were domesticated in Egypt at least 3,000 years ago and in China from the swan goose. Domestic geese are kept for meat, eggs, feathers and down, and as guard animals because of their territorial behaviour and loud alarm calls.
