Richard Rogers

Richard George Rogers, Baron Rogers of Riverside (23 July 1933 – 18 December 2021) was a British-Italian architect noted for his modernist and high-tech buildings, including the Pompidou Centre in Paris, the Lloyd's building and the Millennium Dome in London.

Early life and career

Born in Florence, Italy, Rogers moved to England as a child. He studied at the Architectural Association in London and at Yale University. With Norman Foster, Su Rogers and Wendy Cheesman he co-founded Team 4, and in 1971 he and Renzo Piano won the competition for the Pompidou Centre (opened 1977), whose exposed services and structure became a landmark of high-tech architecture.

Major works

His practice, Richard Rogers Partnership (later Rogers Stirk Harbour + Partners), designed the Lloyd's building (1986), the European Court of Human Rights in Strasbourg, Madrid-Barajas Airport Terminal 4, the National Assembly for Wales (Senedd), Heathrow Terminal 5, and Leadenhall Building ("the Cheesegrater") in London.

Recognition

Rogers won the RIBA Gold Medal (1985), was knighted in 1991 and made a life peer in 1996, and received the Pritzker Prize in 2007. He also advised the Mayor of London on architecture and urbanism and wrote on sustainable cities.
