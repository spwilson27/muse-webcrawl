Regatta

A regatta is a series of boat races. The term derives from Venetian Italian regata ("contest"), originally referring to races between gondoliers in Venice. Today it is used for organised racing events in rowing, sailing, and other watercraft, often combined with social festivities on shore.

Rowing regattas

In rowing, regattas typically consist of multiple races over a straight course, commonly 2,000 metres for international competition. Famous rowing regattas include the Henley Royal Regatta on the River Thames (founded 1839), the Head of the Charles Regatta in Boston, and the Oxford–Cambridge Boat Race — strictly a single "side-by-side" match race rather than a multi-race regatta. Sprint regattas use heats and finals, while "head races" are long-distance time trials held mainly in the autumn.

Sailing regattas

Sailing regattas, such as Cowes Week and Kiel Week, bring together fleets of yachts racing around marked courses over several days. Events range from local club races to the America's Cup match series.

Other regattas

The term is also applied to dragon boat festivals, powerboat races, and university competitions. Many regattas have a strong social and charitable dimension, drawing spectators to waterfront celebrations.
