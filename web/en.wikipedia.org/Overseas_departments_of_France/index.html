Overseas departments of France

The overseas departments of France (départements d'outre-mer) are territorial collectivities of the French Republic located outside Europe. They are integral parts of France, represented in the National Assembly and Senate, and form part of the European Union as its outermost regions.

The five departments

Guadeloupe and Martinique lie in the Caribbean; French Guiana is on the north-east coast of South America; Réunion and Mayotte are in the Indian Ocean near Madagascar. Together they are home to roughly two million inhabitants.

Governance

Each department is administered by a prefect and elected councils. Since reforms in the 2010s, Guadeloupe and Réunion remain combined department-region arrangements with separate councils, while Martinique, French Guiana, and Mayotte have single territorial collectivities exercising both departmental and regional powers.

Distinction from other territories

Overseas departments differ from overseas collectivities such as French Polynesia and New Caledonia, which have greater autonomy and are not part of the European Union proper. The departments use the euro and French law applies in full, subject to specific adaptations.
