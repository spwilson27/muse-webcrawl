Mars Crossing

Mars Crossing is a science fiction novel by Geoffrey A. Landis, published in 2000. It tells the story of the third expedition to Mars, which — following the failure and loss of the first two expeditions — must trek across the planet's surface after their return vehicle is damaged, in order to reach a cache of supplies left by an earlier mission. The novel is noted for its rigorous attention to the practicalities of surviving on Mars.

Plot summary

Two previous expeditions to Mars have ended in disaster. The third expedition lands successfully, but an accident — the discovery that their return craft's fuel has been degraded by a chemical reaction — leaves the crew stranded. Their only hope is to travel overland across thousands of kilometres of Martian terrain to a fuel cache and return vehicle left by the first expedition. The journey, made in pressurised rovers and on foot, becomes an ordeal of attrition as equipment fails, dust storms strike, and crew members die one by one.

Author and background

Geoffrey A. Landis is a NASA scientist who has worked on Mars exploration missions and rover technology, and his professional knowledge informs the novel's hard science fiction treatment of Martian geography, radiation, life support, and traverse planning. Landis had previously won the Hugo Award for his short fiction.

Reception

The novel was well received for its plausibility and tension, and it won the Locus Award for Best First Novel in 2001; it was also a finalist for the Nebula Award. Critics compared its survival narrative to historical polar exploration accounts.
