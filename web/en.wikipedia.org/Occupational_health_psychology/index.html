Occupational health psychology

Occupational health psychology (OHP) is an interdisciplinary area of psychology that is concerned with the health and safety of workers. OHP addresses a number of major topic areas including the impact of occupational stressors on physical and mental health, the incidence of workplace violence, work-family conflict, accidents and safety, and interventions designed to improve or protect worker health.

Occupational health psychology studies the psychological factors that affect worker health and safety.

Although OHP emerged from two distinct disciplines within applied psychology, namely industrial and organizational psychology and health psychology, it is also informed by other disciplines including occupational medicine and occupational hygiene.

History

The field developed in Europe and North America during the second half of the twentieth century, drawing on research into job stress and worker well-being. The Journal of Occupational Health Psychology was founded in 1996, and professional societies such as the Society for Occupational Health Psychology and the European Academy of Occupational Health Psychology helped establish the discipline.

Research topics

  • Job stress and burnout
  • Workplace violence and bullying
  • Work–family conflict and spillover
  • Safety climate and accident prevention
  • Unemployment and job insecurity
  • Long working hours and shift work

Interventions

Interventions derived from OHP research operate at the individual level (for example, stress management training), the job level (job redesign to increase control), and the organizational level (policies reducing exposure to hazards and violence). Related protective frameworks are supported by licensing and regulatory regimes governing working conditions.
