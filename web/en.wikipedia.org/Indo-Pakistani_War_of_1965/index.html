Indo-Pakistani War of 1965

DateAugust – 23 September 1965
TheatreKashmir, Punjab, Rajasthan, Sindh
ResultCeasefire; Tashkent Declaration; return to status quo ante

The Indo-Pakistani War of 1965 was a conflict between India and Pakistan over Kashmir, lasting from August to September 1965. It was the second of the two countries' wars over the disputed territory and ended in a United Nations-mandated ceasefire and the Tashkent Declaration of January 1966.

Causes and course

Following skirmishes in the Rann of Kutch, Pakistan launched Operation Gibraltar in August 1965, infiltrating forces into Indian-administered Kashmir to foment insurgency. India responded by crossing the ceasefire line and, on 6 September, the international border toward Lahore, opening a full-scale war. Pakistan's Operation Grand Slam aimed at Akhnoor. Major tank battles at Asal Uttar and Phillora/Chawinda in the Sialkot sector were among the largest since the Second World War.

Air and naval operations

Both air forces flew extensive combat missions — the Pakistan Air Force's F-86 Sabres and F-104s against the Indian Air Force's larger fleet. The Pakistan Navy bombarded Dwarka in Operation Dwarka, while India's navy remained largely defensive.

Outcome

The UN Security Council's Resolution 211 demanded a ceasefire, effective 23 September. Both sides claimed victory; most neutral assessments describe a strategic stalemate with heavy armour losses on both sides. The Soviet-mediated Tashkent Declaration restored pre-war positions, and Pakistan's president Ayub Khan faced domestic disillusionment afterward.
