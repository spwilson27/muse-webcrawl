Zenith

The zenith (UK: /ˈzɛnɪθ/, US: /ˈziːnɪθ/) is an imaginary point directly "above" a particular location, on the celestial sphere. "Above" means in the vertical direction (plumb line) opposite to the direction of gravity at that location. The zenith is the highest point on the celestial sphere, with an altitude of 90 degrees. The opposite point, directly below the observer, is the nadir.

Etymology and history

The word derives from the Arabic samt ar-raʾs, "direction of the head" or "path above the head", transmitted through medieval Latin. Zenith telescopes and the concept of the zenith were important in classical astronomy and geodesy for measuring star positions and the shape of the Earth.

Relevance and usage

Astronomers define the zenith angle as the angle between the direction to the zenith and the direction to an object. The Sun can pass through the zenith only at locations between the tropics, an event reflected in Lahaina Noon in Hawaii. In common usage, "zenith" also means the highest point or climax of something, as in "the zenith of his career".
