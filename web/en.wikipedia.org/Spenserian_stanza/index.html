Spenserian stanza

The Spenserian stanza is a fixed verse form invented by Edmund Spenser for his epic poem The Faerie Queene (1590–96). Each stanza contains nine lines in total: eight lines in iambic pentameter followed by a single line in iambic hexameter, called an alexandrine. The rhyme scheme is ababbcbcc.

Form

The interlocking rhyme scheme (ababbcbcc) links each stanza internally, with the b and c rhymes carrying across the stanza's two halves. The concluding alexandrine — a twelve-syllable line — provides a sense of closure and weight, often summarising or extending the thought of the preceding lines.

Later use

The stanza was revived by the Romantic poets: John Keats used it in The Eve of St. Agnes, Lord Byron in Childe Harold's Pilgrimage, Percy Bysshe Shelley in Adonais and The Revolt of Islam, and Walter Scott in The Vision of Don Roderick. Earlier, eighteenth-century poets such as James Thomson in The Castle of Indolence had kept the form alive.
