The Consolidated B-24 Liberator is an American heavy bomber designed by Consolidated Aircraft of San Diego. With more than 18,000 built, it remains the most-produced American military aircraft in history and was used in every theatre of the Second World War.

Design

The B-24 featured a high-mounted, long-span "Davis wing" that gave it greater range, speed and bomb load than the contemporary B-17 Flying Fortress, though it was considered harder to fly and less able to absorb battle damage. Its tricycle landing gear and spacious fuselage with a rollback bomb-bay door were distinctive. Production was centred at Consolidated's San Diego and Fort Worth plants, Douglas Tulsa, North American Dallas, and Ford's vast Willow Run factory in Michigan.

Service

Liberators served with the US Army Air Forces, US Navy (as the PB4Y), RAF and other Allied air forces. They flew strategic bombing missions in Europe — including the costly low-level raid on Ploiești in August 1943 — and were crucial in closing the mid-Atlantic gap against U-boats in the Battle of the Atlantic, as transports, tankers and patrol bombers.
