Pure Land

A Pure Land (Sanskrit: buddhakṣetra, "buddha-field") is the celestial realm of a buddha or bodhisattva in Mahayana Buddhism. Pure Lands are understood as realms purified by a buddha's merit and vows, in which conditions are ideally suited to the practice of the Dharma and from which enlightenment is readily attained. The best known is Sukhavati, the "Land of Bliss" of the buddha Amitabha, which is the focus of Pure Land Buddhism, one of the most widely practised forms of Buddhism in East Asia.

Concept

According to Mahayana sutras, buddhas establish Pure Lands through their vows and accumulated merit. Amitabha, in the Larger Sutra of Immeasurable Life, is said to have made forty-eight vows as the bodhisattva Dharmakara, resolving that his land would be free of suffering and that beings who sincerely called his name to mind would be born there. Pure Lands are not heavens in the sense of final destinations; rebirth in Sukhavati is valued because there the practitioner can study under Amitabha directly and progress irreversibly toward Buddhahood.

Other Pure Lands

Other notable Pure Lands include Abhirati, the eastern land of Akshobhya Buddha; the Medicine Buddha's land of Vaiduryanirbhasa; and the Potalaka of Avalokiteshvara. The doctrine is also connected with the concept of the buddha-fields pervading the cosmos described in the Vimalakirti Sutra and the Avatamsaka Sutra.

Practice

Pure Land practice centres on mindfulness of the Buddha (buddhanusmrti), most commonly the recitation of Amitabha's name — the Chinese nianfo and Japanese nembutsu ("Namo Amituofo" / "Namu Amida Butsu"). In Japan, teachers such as Hōnen and Shinran founded independent Pure Land schools, Jōdo-shū and Jōdo Shinshū, emphasizing reliance on Amitabha's "other-power" (tariki) rather than self-power practices.
