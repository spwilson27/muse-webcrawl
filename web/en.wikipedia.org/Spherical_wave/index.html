Spherical wave

A spherical wave is a wave whose wavefronts are concentric spheres expanding from a point source. The amplitude of a spherical wave decreases in proportion to 1/r with distance r from the source, so that its intensity falls as 1/r², consistent with conservation of energy.

Description

A scalar spherical wave satisfies the wave equation in spherical coordinates and has solutions of the form (A/r) ei(kr − ωt). Point sources of sound and light radiate approximately spherical waves when small compared with the wavelength of observation distances.

Applications

Spherical waves are fundamental in acoustics, seismology, antenna theory, and scattering theory, where scattered fields are commonly expanded in spherical harmonics.
