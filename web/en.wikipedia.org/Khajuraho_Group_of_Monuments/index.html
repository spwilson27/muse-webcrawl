The Khajuraho Group of Monuments is a group of Hindu temples and Jain temples in Chhatarpur district, Madhya Pradesh, India, about 175 kilometres (109 mi) southeast of Jhansi. They are a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The temples are famous for their nagara-style architectural symbolism and their erotic sculptures.

Most Khajuraho temples were built between 885 AD and 1050 AD by the Chandela dynasty. Historical records note that the Khajuraho temple site once had 85 temples by the 12th century, spread over 20 square kilometres; only about 25 temples have survived, spread over 6 square kilometres. Of the surviving temples, the Kandariya Mahadeva Temple is decorated with a profusion of sculptures with intricate details, symbolism and expressiveness — among the greatest masterpieces of Indian art.

History

Khajuraho was the religious capital of the Chandelas, a Hindu dynasty that ruled much of what is now Madhya Pradesh and eastern Uttar Pradesh between the 9th and 13th centuries. The site's temples were built over roughly a century of building activity. After the decline of the Chandela kingdom, the temples fell into disuse and were overgrown by jungle. In the 1830s, local Hindus guided the British surveyor T. S. Burt to the site, beginning its modern rediscovery. The temples were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1986.

Architecture and sculpture

The temples are built of sandstone, with a characteristic layout: a high terrace platform, an entrance porch, a hall, a vestibule and a sanctum crowned by a shikhara (spire) covered in sculptural bands. The walls carry thousands of sculptures depicting deities, celestial beings, lovers, animals, warriors, dancers and scenes of everyday life. The famous erotic sculptures — a small minority of the total carving, often estimated at around ten percent — have been interpreted in various ways: as part of tantric practice, as a celebration of kama (one of the legitimate aims of life in Hindu thought), or as symbolic depictions tied to the rituals of the temple.

Temple groups

The monuments are conventionally divided into three groups: the Western Group, the largest and best known, containing the Kandariya Mahadeva, Lakshmana, Vishvanatha and Chitragupta temples; the Eastern Group, with Jain temples such as Parshvanatha and Hindu temples such as Vamana and Javari; and the Southern Group, including the Duladeo and Chaturbhuja temples. Several temples remain in active worship.
