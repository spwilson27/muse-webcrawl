The El Ghriba synagogue (Arabic: كنيس الغريبة, "the extraordinary/marvelous synagogue") is a historic synagogue in the village of Erriadh, on the Tunisian island of Djerba. It is the centre of one of the oldest Jewish communities in North Africa and the destination of an annual pilgrimage on Lag BaOmer that draws thousands of visitors.

Traditions and history

Local tradition holds that the synagogue was founded by priests fleeing Jerusalem either after the destruction of Solomon's Temple in 586 BCE or after the destruction of the Second Temple in 70 CE, and that a stone or door from the Temple was built into its fabric. The present building is much newer, having been rebuilt and enlarged in the 19th and 20th centuries; its interior is noted for blue-tiled walls, carved wooden benches and a large collection of Torah scrolls.

Pilgrimage

During the Lag BaOmer festival, processions carry a large ornamented Torah crown (the menara) from the synagogue through the village, and pilgrims — many of them Tunisian Jews now living in Israel and France — place written wishes and eggs in a small grotto beneath the building.

Attacks

The synagogue has been attacked several times: in 1985 a policeman opened fire, killing three people; in April 2002 an al-Qaeda suicide truck bombing outside the synagogue killed 21 people, mostly German tourists; and in May 2023 a national guardsman shot dead five people, including two pilgrims, near the site during the festival.
