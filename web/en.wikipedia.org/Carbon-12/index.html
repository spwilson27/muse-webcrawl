Carbon-12

Carbon-12
Symbol: 12C
Protons: 6
Neutrons: 6
Natural abundance: ~98.9%
Atomic mass: exactly 12 Da (by definition)

Carbon-12 (12C) is the more abundant of the two stable isotopes of carbon, amounting to about 98.93% of the element on Earth. Its nucleus contains six protons and six neutrons.

Importance

Carbon-12 is of particular importance in physics and chemistry because it serves as the standard by which atomic masses of all nuclides are measured: the dalton (unified atomic mass unit) is defined as exactly 1/12 of the mass of a free carbon-12 atom in its ground state. Until 2019, the mole was also defined in terms of the number of atoms in 12 grams of carbon-12.

Origin

Carbon-12 is produced in stars through the triple-alpha process, in which three helium-4 nuclei fuse. Fred Hoyle famously predicted an excited state of carbon-12 — the Hoyle state, at about 7.65 MeV — to explain the observed abundance of carbon in the universe; the state was subsequently found experimentally.

Properties

The carbon-12 nucleus has spin zero in its ground state. Its most important excited states include the Hoyle state (0+ at 7.654 MeV) and a 2+ state at 4.44 MeV, the decay of which produces a characteristic gamma ray observed in astrophysical settings.
