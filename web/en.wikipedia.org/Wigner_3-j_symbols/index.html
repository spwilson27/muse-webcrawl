Wigner 3-j symbol

In quantum mechanics, the Wigner 3-j symbols, also called 3-jm symbols, are an alternative to Clebsch–Gordan coefficients for the purpose of adding angular momenta.

Definition

The 3-j symbols are related to the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients by a phase factor and a factor of the square root of (2j3 + 1). While Clebsch–Gordan coefficients express the coupling of two angular momenta into a total, the 3-j symbols symmetrically express the coupling of three angular momenta to a total of zero.

Selection rules

A 3-j symbol vanishes unless the three m values sum to zero, each j lies within the triangle formed by the other two, and j1 + j2 + j3 satisfies the appropriate integrality conditions.

Symmetry properties

The 3-j symbols have a much larger symmetry group than the Clebsch–Gordan coefficients: they are invariant under any even permutation of their columns and pick up a sign under odd permutations, with further symmetries discovered by Regge.

Applications

They arise throughout atomic, molecular and nuclear spectroscopy and in the theory of spherical harmonics integrals, where the Gaunt integral is expressed in terms of 3-j symbols.
