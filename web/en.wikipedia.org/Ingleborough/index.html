Ingleborough

Elevation724 m (2,375 ft)
LocationYorkshire Dales, North Yorkshire, England
Parent rangePennines
GeologyCarboniferous limestone and Millstone Grit

Ingleborough is a mountain in the Yorkshire Dales National Park in North Yorkshire, England. At 724 metres (2,375 ft) it is the second-highest summit in the Dales and the best known of the Yorkshire Three Peaks, the others being Whernside and Pen-y-ghent. Its distinctive stepped profile — layers of limestone and shale capped by Millstone Grit — makes it recognisable from far across the surrounding countryside.

Geology and landscape

Ingleborough rises from a great limestone platform riddled with caves and potholes, including the famous systems of White Scar Cave, Ingleborough Cave, and Gaping Gill, into which Fell Beck falls 98 metres. The flanks display classic karst features: limestone pavements, shakeholes, dry valleys, and resurgences. Much of the massif is a National Nature Reserve and a Site of Special Scientific Interest for its upland and limestone habitats.

Archaeology and history

The summit plateau is ringed by a drystone rampart enclosing an Iron Age hillfort, traditionally associated with the Brigantes, with the foundations of round hut circles inside. The mountain's name is thought to combine Old English elements referring to a fortified hill. A short-lived 19th-century tower, built for the celebrations of a local landowner, once stood on the summit; its remains and the large summit cairn and shelter are still visible.

Walking

Ingleborough is usually climbed from Ingleton, Chapel-le-Dale, Horton-in-Ribblesdale, or Clapham, and forms the final peak of the traditional Yorkshire Three Peaks Challenge — a 39 km circuit of the three summits attempted within 12 hours. The village of Ingleton at its foot is a centre for caving in the region.
