National Geographic Society

The National Geographic Society is one of the largest nonprofit scientific and educational organisations in the world. Founded in Washington, D.C. in 1888, it is best known for its magazine, National Geographic, and for funding exploration, research and conservation around the globe.

Founding and mission

The Society was founded on 13 January 1888 by 33 explorers, scientists and prominent citizens "for the increase and diffusion of geographic knowledge." Its first president was Gardiner Greene Hubbard; his son-in-law, Alexander Graham Bell, succeeded him and greatly expanded the magazine's reach. The Society's iconic yellow border (the "yellow frame") became its trademark.

Exploration and grants

Through its Committee for Research and Exploration, the Society has funded thousands of projects, including Hiram Bingham's excavations at Machu Picchu, Richard Leakey's palaeoanthropology, Jane Goodall's chimpanzee research and Robert Peary's polar expeditions. Its media arm, National Geographic Partners, is a joint venture with the Walt Disney Company.
