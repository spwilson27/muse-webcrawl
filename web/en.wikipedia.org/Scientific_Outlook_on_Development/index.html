Scientific Outlook on Development

The Scientific Outlook on Development (Chinese: 科学发展观; pinyin: Kēxué Fāzhǎn Guān) is a guiding socioeconomic and political theory of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP). It was developed under the leadership of Hu Jintao, General Secretary of the CCP from 2002 to 2012, and was written into the party constitution at the 17th National Congress in 2007 and into the state constitution in 2018.

Content

The theory calls for comprehensive, coordinated and sustainable development that puts people first. Its core elements are commonly summarised as: development is the top priority; the approach is people-oriented; the basic requirement is overall consideration and balanced coordination; and the fundamental methodology is to take all factors into account. It was presented as a response to the social inequalities, environmental damage and regional imbalances that accompanied China's rapid economic growth.

Implementation

Under Hu's administration, the outlook informed policies on rural development, environmental protection, energy conservation and the building of a "harmonious society". Officials at all levels were evaluated partly on criteria associated with the theory, and study campaigns promoted it throughout the party and government apparatus.

Legacy

The Scientific Outlook on Development is listed among the guiding ideologies of the CCP alongside Marxism–Leninism, Mao Zedong Thought, Deng Xiaoping Theory and the Three Represents. It is regarded as Hu Jintao's principal theoretical contribution.
