Foot drop

Foot drop
Other names: Drop foot, peroneal nerve palsy
Specialty: Neurology, orthopedics
Signs: Inability to dorsiflex the foot; steppage gait
Causes: Common fibular (peroneal) nerve injury, L5 radiculopathy, stroke, multiple sclerosis

Foot drop is a gait abnormality in which the forefoot drops due to weakness, irritation, or damage to the common fibular (peroneal) nerve, which innervates the muscles that dorsiflex the foot. People with foot drop may drag their toes along the ground or compensate with a high-stepping ("steppage") gait.

Causes

Common causes include compression of the peroneal nerve at the fibular head (from crossing the legs, casts, or squatting), L5 radiculopathy, stroke, multiple sclerosis, Charcot–Marie–Tooth disease, and motor neuron disease.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is based on the clinical examination, supplemented by nerve conduction studies, electromyography, and imaging of the nerve course or spine.

Treatment

Treatment addresses the underlying cause; supportive measures include ankle–foot orthoses (AFOs), physiotherapy, and, in selected cases, nerve decompression or tendon transfer surgery.
