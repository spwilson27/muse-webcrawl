MSCI Inc. is an American finance company headquartered in New York City. It is a major provider of equity, fixed income, hedge fund stock market indices, multi-asset portfolio analysis tools, and ESG (environmental, social, and governance) products. Its best-known indices are the MSCI World Index and the MSCI Emerging Markets Index, against which a very large share of internationally invested assets — commonly estimated at well over US$14 trillion — is benchmarked.

History

The company began in 1968 when Capital Group's Capital International published the first international equity indices. Morgan Stanley acquired the licensing rights in 1986, and the indices took the name MSCI (Morgan Stanley Capital International). MSCI became a fully independent public company in 2007–2009 as Morgan Stanley divested its stake. Acquisitions including RiskMetrics (2010) and Barra broadened its analytics and risk businesses.

Indices and classification

MSCI's market classification framework divides countries into developed, emerging, and frontier markets based on economic development, market size and liquidity, and accessibility to foreign investors. Changes in classification — such as promotions of countries from frontier to emerging status — can move billions of dollars of passive investment flows. The flagship MSCI ACWI (All Country World Index) covers large- and mid-cap stocks across developed and emerging markets.

ESG

MSCI ESG Research rates thousands of companies and securities on environmental, social and governance risks; its ratings and data are widely used by asset managers, making MSCI one of the most influential gatekeepers of sustainable-investing information.
