David A. Kolb (born 1939) is an American psychologist and educational theorist whose interests and publications focus on experiential learning, the individual and social change, career development, and executive and professional education. He is best known for his experiential learning model and the Learning Style Inventory (LSI).

Experiential learning model

Kolb's experiential learning theory, set out in his 1984 book Experiential Learning: Experience as the Source of Learning and Development, describes learning as a four-stage cycle:

  1. Concrete experience — having a new experience or reinterpreting an existing one.
  2. Reflective observation — reflecting on the experience.
  3. Abstract conceptualisation — forming new ideas or modifying existing concepts.
  4. Active experimentation — applying the new ideas to the world, generating further experience.

The model draws on the work of John Dewey, Kurt Lewin, and Jean Piaget, and defines learning as "the process whereby knowledge is created through the transformation of experience."

Learning styles

Crossing the cycle's two dimensions — feeling versus thinking, and doing versus watching — Kolb identified four learning styles: diverging, assimilating, converging, and accommodating. His Learning Style Inventory, a self-assessment questionnaire, has been widely used in education and management training, though the broader "learning styles" concept has attracted scientific criticism.

Career

Kolb received his undergraduate degree from Knox College and his PhD in social psychology from Harvard University. He taught at the MIT Sloan School of Management and spent most of his career at the Weatherhead School of Management at Case Western Reserve University in Cleveland, Ohio, where he became professor emeritus of organizational behavior.
