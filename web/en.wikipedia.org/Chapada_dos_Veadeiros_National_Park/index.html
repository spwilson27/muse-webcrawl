Chapada dos Veadeiros National Park (Portuguese: Parque Nacional da Chapada dos Veadeiros) is a national park in the state of Goiás, Brazil, on the high plateau of the Chapada dos Veadeiros in the Brazilian Highlands. It protects a major area of cerrado savanna — one of the world's oldest and most biodiverse tropical ecosystems — along with quartzite rock formations, canyons and waterfalls. It was created in 1961 by President Juscelino Kubitschek as Tocantins National Park, later renamed, and was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2001 as part of the Cerrado Protected Areas site.

Geography and nature

The park sits at elevations between about 600 and 1,691 metres, on ancient Precambrian quartzite plateaus cut by the Preto River and its tributaries. Its landscapes include the Saltos rapids (80 m and 120 m waterfalls), the Sete Quedas ("Seven Falls") crossings, the Carrossel rapids and the Morro da Baleia viewpoint. Vegetation ranges from open grassland and cerrado woodland to gallery forests and high-altitude rupestrian fields. Wildlife includes maned wolves, giant anteaters, giant armadillos, pampas deer, jaguars, rheas, seriemas and numerous endemic plants and birds.

Access and expansion

The main entrance is near the village of São Jorge, by the town of Alto Paraíso de Goiás, a center for ecotourism and alternative communities that call the plateau a place of special energy because of its crystal quartz deposits. The park was controversially reduced from its original extent over the decades, reaching about 65,000 hectares, but a 2017 presidential decree expanded it to about 240,611 hectares, incorporating areas valued for water supply and biodiversity and partly offsetting the earlier reductions.
