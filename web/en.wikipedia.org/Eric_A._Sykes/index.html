Eric A. Sykes

Born: 5 February 1885
Died: 12 May 1966
Occupation: British Army officer, firearms instructor

Eric Anthony Sykes (5 February 1885 – 12 May 1966), born Eric Anthony Schwabe, was a British soldier and firearms expert. He is most famous for his work with William E. Fairbairn in the development of close-quarters battle techniques and the Fairbairn–Sykes fighting knife.

Career

Sykes spent much of his early career in China, where he served with the Shanghai Municipal Police. There he met Fairbairn, and together they developed methods of hand-to-hand combat, shooting, and knife fighting that were taught to police and later to military special forces.

Second World War

During the Second World War Sykes served as an instructor in unconventional warfare, training British Commandos and agents of the Special Operations Executive in silent killing and close combat. His name remains attached to the Fairbairn–Sykes dagger, which became an iconic weapon of the Commandos, and to the Sykes-Fairbairn training syllabus.

Legacy

Sykes's methods influenced modern military combatives worldwide, and his collaboration with Fairbairn is documented in their writings and in the training manuals produced during the war years.
