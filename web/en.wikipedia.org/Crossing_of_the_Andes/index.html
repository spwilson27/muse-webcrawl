The Crossing of the Andes (Spanish: Cruce de los Andes) was one of the most important feats in the Spanish American wars of independence. In January 1817, General José de San Martín led the Army of the Andes from Mendoza, in the United Provinces of the Río de la Plata (modern Argentina), over the Andes mountains into Chile, where royalist forces were defeated and Chilean independence advanced. It is considered one of the greatest military expeditions in history, comparable to Hannibal's crossing of the Alps.

Preparation and march

San Martín prepared the expedition for two years in Mendoza with the support of governor Toribio de Luzuriaga and the logistical genius of officers and engineers who surveyed passes, founded workshops, and manufactured gunpowder, uniforms, and equipment locally. The army numbered roughly 4,000–5,000 soldiers, including troops of Bernardo O'Higgins' Chilean exiles, together with some 1,200 militiamen, thousands of mules, and over a thousand horses.

Beginning on 19 January 1817, the army crossed in several columns through passes including Los Patos and Uspallata, at altitudes exceeding 4,000 metres. Deception feints misled the royalist governor Casimiro Marcó del Pont about the invasion route. Cold, altitude sickness (puna), and the loss of much of the livestock took a heavy toll en route, but the army descended into Chile intact enough to fight.

Aftermath

On 12 February 1817 the patriots defeated the royalists at the Battle of Chacabuco, allowing the liberation of Santiago. The following year the victory at Maipú (5 April 1818) secured Chilean independence and enabled San Martín's naval expedition to liberate Peru in 1820–1821.
