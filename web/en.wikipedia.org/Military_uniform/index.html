Military uniform

Military uniform
Purposes: Identification, cohesion, protection, concealment
Categories: Full dress, service dress, combat/battledress, mess dress

A military uniform is a standardised outfit worn by members of an armed force. Uniforms identify the wearer's nation, service, unit and rank, foster cohesion and discipline, and serve practical purposes from weather protection to camouflage. International law requires combatants to distinguish themselves from civilians, a role traditionally filled by uniform.

History

Standardised uniforms spread in the mid-seventeenth century as standing armies formed — famously the red coats of England's New Model Army (1645). Eighteenth- and nineteenth-century uniforms were colourful to aid recognition amid black-powder smoke and to impress, with regimental facings, lace and shakos. Accurate rifle fire and smokeless powder drove the change to drab khaki and field-grey around 1900; printed camouflage patterns followed from the mid-twentieth century, culminating in today's pixelated and multi-environment patterns.

Types

Modern forces maintain several categories: ceremonial full dress, often preserving historic colours; service or "walking-out" dress for office and formal duties; mess dress for evening functions; and combat dress with load-bearing equipment, body armour and helmet. Insignia of rank, trade badges, medals and unit flashes complete the system.
