Nürburgring 24 Hours

VenueNürburgring, Germany
First held1970
TrackCombined Nordschleife and GP circuit, about 25 km
FieldOften 150+ cars

The Nürburgring 24 Hours (ADAC 24h Rennen) is an annual endurance race for touring cars and GT cars held since 1970 on a combined configuration of the Nordschleife and Grand Prix circuit at the Nürburgring in Germany, forming one of the longest racing laps in the world.

Format

The race is notable for its enormous and diverse entry — professional factory GT3 teams share the track with amateur-driven production cars across many classes — and for the Eifel region's changeable weather, which can bring rain, fog and even hail to parts of the 25 km lap while other parts stay dry. Manthey Racing and Porsche are among the event's most successful entrants.

Significance

Together with the 24 Hours of Le Mans, Daytona and Spa, it is one of the classic 24-hour races, drawing crowds of over 200,000 spectators who camp around the Nordschleife.
