Lisa del Giocondo (née Gherardini; 15 June 1479 – 14 July 1542) was an Italian noblewoman and member of the Gherardini family of Florence and Tuscany. She is universally known as the sitter for the Mona Lisa, the portrait painted by Leonardo da Vinci and commissioned, in the traditional account, by her husband, the cloth and silk merchant Francesco del Giocondo. In Italian the painting is called La Gioconda — a play on her married name and on the word for "the cheerful woman".

Life

Lisa was born in Florence into the Gherardini, an old and once-powerful Tuscan family that had fallen on harder times. In 1495, aged fifteen, she married Francesco del Giocondo, a successful cloth and silk merchant considerably older than her, becoming his third wife. The couple lived in a respectable house on the Via della Stufa in Florence and had five children — Piero, Camilla, Andrea, Giocondo and Marietta — besides raising Francesco's son by a previous marriage. Her life appears to have been that of a comfortable middle-class Florentine matron; the portrait is believed to have been commissioned around 1503, possibly to celebrate the birth of a child and the purchase of the family house.

In her later years Lisa may have suffered illness; a convent register records that Francesco placed her, in 1538, in the care of the nuns of Sant'Orsola, where one of her daughters was a nun. She died in July 1542, aged 63, and was buried in the family tomb in the Basilica della Santissima Annunziata. A death certificate document discovered in Florentine archives gives her as "wife of Francesco del Giocondo, died 15 July 1542 and buried in Santissima Annunziata" (some records give 14 July).

Identification with the Mona Lisa

The identification rests principally on Giorgio Vasari's biography of Leonardo (1550), which states that Leonardo painted the portrait of Lisa, wife of Francesco del Giocondo — "Mona" being a contraction of Madonna ("my lady"). Circumstantial confirmation came from the discovery, in 2005, of a note by the Florentine official Agostino Vespucci, written in the margin of a volume of Cicero in October 1503, recording that Leonardo was then working on a portrait of Lisa del Giocondo. Although alternative sitters have been proposed over the centuries, Lisa's identification is accepted by the Louvre and by the consensus of Leonardo scholarship.
