Chablis wine

RegionChablis, Yonne, Burgundy, France
Grape varietyChardonnay (locally called Beaunois)
AppellationsPetit Chablis, Chablis, Chablis Premier Cru, Chablis Grand Cru
SoilsKimmeridgian limestone and clay

Chablis wine is produced in the vineyards around the town of Chablis in the Yonne department of northern Burgundy, France. It is made entirely from the Chardonnay grape, known locally as Beaunois. Chablis is the northernmost wine district of Burgundy, and its cool climate produces wines with higher acidity and leaner, more mineral character than Chardonnay grown in warmer parts of the region.

Geology and climate

The defining soil of Chablis is Kimmeridgian limestone, rich in fossilized marine shells, which growers credit for the wines' characteristic flinty, mineral notes. The northern latitude brings cool temperatures and a constant risk of spring frost, which historically devastated vintages; growers combat frost with heaters, smudge pots, and water-sprinkling systems that encase buds in protective ice.

Classifications

Chablis wines are classified in four tiers: Petit Chablis, from plateau vineyards; Chablis, the main appellation; Chablis Premier Cru, covering some forty named climats; and Chablis Grand Cru, from a single hillside northeast of the town comprising seven climats, including Les Clos, Vaudésir, and Bougros.

Winemaking

Traditional Chablis is fermented and aged in stainless steel, producing a crisp, unoaked style. Some producers use small amounts of oak, particularly for Premier and Grand Cru wines, a practice that has periodically divided opinion in the region. Malolactic fermentation is common, softening the wines' naturally high acidity.

Name protection

The name Chablis was long used generically abroad, especially in the United States and Australia, for inexpensive white wines unrelated to the French region. Trade agreements have progressively restricted such usage, though some grandfathered labels persist in the U.S. market.
