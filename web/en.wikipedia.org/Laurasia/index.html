Laurasia

Laurasia was the northern of the two supercontinents formed when Pangaea split during the Mesozoic (the southern being Gondwana). It comprised the landmasses that are now North America, Europe and Asia (excluding India and Arabia) — its name combines Laurentia and Eurasia.

Formation and breakup

Laurasia's core assembled in the Palaeozoic from Laurentia, Baltica and Siberia. As part of Pangaea it began rifting apart in the Jurassic with the opening of the North Atlantic; by the end of the Cretaceous its fragments were approaching their modern configuration, though land bridges such as Beringia long connected Asia and North America, explaining shared (Holarctic) flora and fauna.
