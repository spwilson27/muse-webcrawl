National Highways of India

The National Highways are a network of trunk roads in India owned and maintained by the Government of India through the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways and the National Highways Authority of India (NHAI). They form the country's primary road arteries, carrying about 40% of road traffic on roughly 2% of the total road length.

Network

The network totals more than 146,000 km, having more than doubled since 2014 through programmes such as the National Highways Development Project, which produced the Golden Quadrilateral linking Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai and Kolkata, and the North–South and East–West corridors. Major routes include NH 44 (Srinagar–Kanyakumari, the longest), NH 48 (Delhi–Chennai) and NH 27. A comprehensive renumbering in 2010 gave even numbers to north–south and odd numbers to east–west highways, increasing from north to south and east to west respectively.

Development

Highways are built and upgraded through public funding and public–private partnerships, with tolling common on improved sections. The Bharatmala Pariyojana programme, launched in 2017, targets tens of thousands of kilometres of new corridors, border and coastal roads, and expressways.
