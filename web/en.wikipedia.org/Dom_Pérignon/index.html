Dom Pérignon (/ˌdɒm pɛrɪnˈjɒn/) is a brand of vintage Champagne produced by Moët & Chandon. It is named after Dom Pierre Pérignon (1638–1715), a Benedictine monk who was an important quality pioneer for Champagne wine but — contrary to popular myth — did not discover the Champagne method of making sparkling wine. Dom Pérignon is a prestige cuvée, produced only in declared vintage years from Chardonnay and Pinot noir, and aged for a minimum of seven years on lees before release.

Pierre Pérignon

Pérignon served as cellarer of the Abbey of Hautvillers from 1668 until his death. He improved blending of grapes from different vineyards, pioneered gentle pressing, and advocateded cork stoppers and stronger English glass bottles. The famous exclamation attributed to him — "Come quickly, I am tasting the stars!" — is a 19th-century invention.

The wine

The first vintage, 1921, was released in 1936. Notable vintages include 1961 (served at the wedding of Charles and Diana in 1981). The house releases wines in "Plénitudes": the first after about eight years, a second (P2) after 12–15 years, and a third (P3) after decades of ageing. A rosé version is also produced. Chef de cave since 2019 is Vincent Chaperon, succeeding Richard Geoffroy.

Market and culture

Dom Pérignon is among the world's best-selling prestige champagnes and a prominent luxury symbol, frequently referenced in film and popular music. Bottles of rare vintages fetch high prices at auction.
