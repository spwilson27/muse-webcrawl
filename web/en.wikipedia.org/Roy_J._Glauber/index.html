Roy J. Glauber

Roy J. Glauber
Born: September 1, 1925, New York City
Died: December 26, 2018, Newton, Massachusetts
Field: Theoretical physics
Known for: Quantum optics; coherent states
Awards: Nobel Prize in Physics (2005)

Roy Jay Glauber (1925–2018) was an American theoretical physicist, professor at Harvard University, and one of the founders of quantum optics. He shared the 2005 Nobel Prize in Physics for his contribution to the quantum theory of optical coherence.

Career

Glauber was recruited to the Manhattan Project at Los Alamos as an 18-year-old Harvard undergraduate, among its youngest scientists. After the war he completed his doctorate at Harvard in 1949 and later joined its faculty, working on quantum field theory and the theory of high-energy collisions.

Quantum optics

In a landmark 1963 paper Glauber developed a quantum-mechanical theory of photodetection and coherence, introducing coherent states (now called Glauber states) and a hierarchy of correlation functions that allowed laser light to be distinguished from ordinary thermal light. This framework became the foundation of modern quantum optics. He was also known as the longtime "Keeper of the Mop" at the Ig Nobel Prize ceremonies.
