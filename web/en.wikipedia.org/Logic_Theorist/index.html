Logic Theorist

Logic Theorist is a computer program written in 1955–56 by Allen Newell, Herbert A. Simon, and Cliff Shaw at the RAND Corporation and Carnegie Institute of Technology. It is often regarded as the first artificial intelligence program, designed to mimic the problem-solving skills of a human and to prove theorems in symbolic logic.

Description

The program worked on proving theorems from Whitehead and Russell's Principia Mathematica. It succeeded in proving 38 of the first 52 theorems in the book, and for one theorem (2.85) it found a proof more elegant than the original. To do this it used heuristic search, including means-ends analysis techniques that Newell and Simon developed into the General Problem Solver.

Significance

Logic Theorist was demonstrated at the 1956 Dartmouth workshop that gave artificial intelligence its name. Its list-processing techniques influenced the development of the IPL and Lisp programming languages, and it stands as a landmark in the history of AI and cognitive psychology.
