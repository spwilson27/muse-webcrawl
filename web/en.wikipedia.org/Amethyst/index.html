Amethyst is a violet variety of quartz. The name comes from the Koine Greek amethystos, meaning "not intoxicated", a reference to the ancient belief that the gemstone could protect its owner from drunkenness. Amethyst has been used in jewellery and ornamentation since antiquity, appearing in Egyptian tombs, royal regalia and religious objects.

Composition and colour

Amethyst is a macrocrystalline variety of quartz (silicon dioxide, SiO2) whose purple colour is caused primarily by trace amounts of iron (Fe4+) substituted for silicon in the crystal lattice, combined with natural irradiation. The colour ranges from pale lilac to deep reddish purple. On exposure to heat, amethyst generally yellows, and much of the citrine and ametrine sold commercially is produced by heat-treating amethyst.

Occurrence

Historically, the finest amethyst came from Russia; today the largest deposits are mined in Brazil and Uruguay, with additional production in Zambia, South Korea, Canada and the United States. Large geodes lined with amethyst crystals are characteristic of Brazilian and Uruguayan deposits formed in volcanic rock.

History and uses

The ancient Greeks and Romans wore amethyst and fashioned drinking vessels from it in the belief that it prevented intoxication. In the Middle Ages it was considered a symbol of royalty and appeared in the British Crown Jewels. Amethyst is the traditional birthstone for February and is widely used in rings, earrings, pendants and ornamental carvings.
