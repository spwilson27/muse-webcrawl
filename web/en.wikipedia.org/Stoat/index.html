Stoat

Scientific name Mustela erminea
Family Mustelidae
Order Carnivora

A stoat in winter pelage (ermine)

The stoat (Mustela erminea), also known as the Eurasian ermine or simply ermine, is a mustelid native to Eurasia and the northern portions of North America. Because of its wide circumpolar distribution, it is listed as Least Concern by the IUCN. In its white winter coat, the animal and its fur are called ermine, long prized for ceremonial garments.

Description

The stoat is a small, long-bodied predator, typically 17–33 cm in body length, with short legs and a tail tipped in black. In summer its coat is sandy brown above and white below; in northern populations the winter coat turns entirely white except for the black tail tip.

Behaviour and diet

Stoats are carnivores that hunt rodents, rabbits, birds, and other small animals, often killing prey larger than themselves. They are agile climbers and swimmers, and are active both day and night. Like other mustelids, they may perform a twisting "war dance" that appears to mesmerise prey.

Introduced populations

Stoats were introduced to New Zealand in the late 19th century to control rabbits, but they have become a major predator of native birds and are considered one of the country's most damaging invasive species.
