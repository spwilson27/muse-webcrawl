Mutiny on the Bounty is a 1935 American historical adventure film directed by Frank Lloyd and produced by Irving Thalberg for Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer. It dramatizes the 1789 mutiny aboard HMS Bounty, based on the 1932 novel by Charles Nordhoff and James Norman Hall.

Plot

The film follows the Bounty's voyage to Tahiti to collect breadfruit. Captain William Bligh (Charles Laughton) rules the ship with sadistic discipline, while his master's mate Fletcher Christian (Clark Gable) grows increasingly protective of the crew. After enjoying the freedom of Tahiti, the men face renewed brutality on the return voyage, and Christian leads a mutiny, setting Bligh and his loyalists adrift in an open launch. Bligh survives an extraordinary feat of navigation, and Christian and the mutineers flee to remote Pitcairn Island.

Reception and awards

The film was the highest-grossing film of 1935 and won the Academy Award for Best Picture. Uniquely, its three male leads — Laughton, Gable, and Franchot Tone — were all nominated for the Academy Award for Best Actor, an embarrassment of riches that prompted the Academy to create the supporting-actor categories the following year. It remains regarded as the definitive screen version of the story, ahead of remakes in 1962 and 1984 (The Bounty).

Legacy

The production is also remembered for its authentic full-scale ship, shot on location around Catalina Island and in French Polynesia, and for cementing the popular image of Bligh as a tyrant — a portrayal historians regard as substantially unfair to the real officer.
