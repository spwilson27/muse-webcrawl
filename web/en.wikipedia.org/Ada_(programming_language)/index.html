Ada (programming language)

Ada is a structured, statically typed, imperative, and object-oriented high-level programming language, extended from Pascal and other languages. It was designed under contract to the United States Department of Defense between 1977 and 1983 to supersede the hundreds of programming languages then used by the DoD. Ada is named after Ada Lovelace (1815–1852), who is credited as the first computer programmer.

Features

Ada emphasizes safety and maintainability for large, long-lived systems: strong typing with user-defined ranges and subtypes, extensive compile-time and run-time checking, packages for modularity, generics, exceptions, and built-in concurrency through tasks and protected objects using the rendezvous model. The SPARK subset of Ada supports formal proof of program properties and is used where certification standards such as DO-178C apply.

Applications

Ada is widely used in high-integrity and safety-critical systems: avionics (much of the Boeing 777 and Airbus flight software), air traffic management, railway signaling, defense systems, and space missions. The Ariane 5 Flight 501 failure in 1996 is a famous case study: an unhandled Ada exception arising from reuse of Ariane 4 code caused the loss of the launcher.
