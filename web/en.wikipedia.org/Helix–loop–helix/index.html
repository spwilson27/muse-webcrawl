Helix–loop–helix

A helix–loop–helix (HLH) is a protein structural motif that characterises the largest family of dimerising transcription factors in eukaryotes. The motif consists of two alpha helices connected by a loop. One helix is generally smaller and, owing to the loop's flexibility, allows two monomers to fold together and dimerise, packing against a second helix–loop–helix unit.

Basic helix–loop–helix (bHLH)

In the basic helix–loop–helix (bHLH) proteins, a basic region of about 15 amino acids precedes the first helix and binds DNA, typically at a hexanucleotide sequence called the E-box (CANNTG). bHLH transcription factors bind DNA as homodimers or heterodimers and regulate key developmental processes: myogenesis (MyoD family), neurogenesis (such as the achaete-scute and atonal families), haematopoiesis, and circadian rhythm — where the bHLH-PAS proteins CLOCK and BMAL1 form the core oscillator.

Classification and examples

bHLH proteins are grouped into classes according to their tissue distribution, dimerisation partners, and DNA-binding preferences. Well-known members include c-Myc, whose heterodimer with Max binds E-boxes and is deregulated in many cancers; the Id proteins, which lack the basic region and act as natural dominant-negative regulators by sequestering partner bHLH factors; and HIF-1, the hypoxia-inducible factor that responds to low oxygen. More than one hundred bHLH genes are encoded in the human genome.
