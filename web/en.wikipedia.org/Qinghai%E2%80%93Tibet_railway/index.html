Qinghai–Tibet railway


A train on the Qinghai–Tibet railway crossing the permafrost plateau

The Qinghai–Tibet railway (Chinese: 青藏铁路; Tibetan: མཚོ་བོད་ལྕགས་ལམ།) is a high-elevation railway that connects Xining, Qinghai Province, to Lhasa, Tibet Autonomous Region, in the People's Republic of China. It is the world's highest railway, reaching 5,072 metres (16,640 ft) above sea level at the Tanggula Pass.

Construction

The line was built in two sections. The 814 km Xining–Golmud section opened in 1984. Construction of the 1,142 km Golmud–Lhasa section began on 29 June 2001 and was completed in October 2005; passenger services began on 1 July 2006. Some 960 km of the route lies above 4,000 m, and more than half the Golmud–Lhasa section rests on permafrost, stabilised using crushed-rock embankments, ventilation ducts and cooling pipes (thermosyphons). The Fenghuoshan tunnel, at 4,905 m, is the world's highest rail tunnel.

Operation

Trains are fitted with enriched-oxygen systems and UV-filtered windows to protect passengers from altitude sickness and intense sunlight. Diesel locomotives built by GE Transportation and CSR Qishuyan haul services on the non-electrified high section. The line has greatly increased tourism and freight traffic to Tibet, while also prompting debate about its environmental effects on the plateau ecosystem and its role in demographic change in Tibet.

Extensions

The Lhasa–Shigatse railway opened in 2014, and the Sichuan–Tibet railway is under construction, planned to connect Lhasa with Chengdu.
