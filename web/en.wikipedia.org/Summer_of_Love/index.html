Summer of Love

The Summer of Love was a social phenomenon in the summer of 1967, when as many as 100,000 young people — hippies, students, and seekers — converged on the Haight-Ashbury neighbourhood of San Francisco. Drawn by the promise of an alternative society built on peace, communal living, free love, music and psychedelic drugs, and publicised by media coverage and Scott McKenzie's hit song "San Francisco (Be Sure to Wear Flowers in Your Hair)", the gathering became the defining moment of the 1960s counterculture.

Background and events

The Haight had become a centre of the emerging hippie movement, home to bands such as the Grateful Dead and Jefferson Airplane, the Diggers' free stores and free food, and the Psychedelic Shop. Precursors included the Human Be-In in Golden Gate Park in January 1967 and the Monterey International Pop Festival in June. Young arrivals were met by free clinics — the Haight Ashbury Free Medical Clinic was founded that summer — and by the strains of overcrowding, harder drugs, and exploitation.

Legacy

By autumn, many residents pronounced the summer over; a "Death of the Hippie" ceremony was held in October 1967. But the Summer of Love spread hippie style, music and ideals worldwide, influencing fashion, popular music, and social attitudes for decades, and it remains a touchstone of popular memory of the 1960s — commemorated in San Francisco exhibitions and anniversaries, including the 50th in 2017.
