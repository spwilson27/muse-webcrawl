The Battle of Bornhöved was fought on 22 July 1227 (the feast day of St Mary Magdalene) at Bornhöved in Holstein between the forces of King Valdemar II of Denmark and a coalition of north German princes and cities. The Danish defeat ended Denmark's domination of the southwestern Baltic littoral.

Background

Valdemar II had built a Baltic empire encompassing Holstein, Mecklenburg, Hamburg, Lübeck and territories beyond the Elbe. His capture in 1223 by Count Henry of Schwerin and the concessions extracted for his release weakened his position, and after his release he sought to recover his German possessions, in defiance of terms sworn before the Pope.

The battle

The coalition army — including Count Adolf IV of Holstein, Archbishop Gerhard of Bremen, Duke Albert of Saxony, and contingents from Lübeck, Hamburg and the Ditmarschen — met the Danes at Bornhöved. Tradition holds that the German troops fought with the sun behind them and that the defection or collapse of the Ditmarschen contingent in Danish service helped turn the day. Valdemar fled the field; Adolf IV later fulfilled a vow to found a Franciscan monastery in Kiel.

Consequences

The battle confirmed the independence of Lübeck and the north German cities and principalities from Denmark, opening the way for the rise of the Hanseatic League and confining Danish power largely north of the Eider.
