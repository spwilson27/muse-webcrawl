Bulgogi

Bulgogi (불고기, 'fire meat') is a Korean dish of thinly sliced, marinated beef (or sometimes pork) grilled or stir-fried. The marinade typically combines soy sauce, sugar or pear juice, sesame oil, garlic, and black pepper.

The dish evolved from neobiani, a grilled marinated beef served in the royal court cuisine of the Joseon dynasty, itself descended from maekjeok of the Goguryeo era. Modern bulgogi became widespread after Korean independence as beef availability grew.

Bulgogi is among Korea's best-known dishes internationally, served in restaurants and as fast food in bulgogi burgers and rice bowls. Variations include dak-bulgogi (chicken) and dwaeji-bulgogi (spicy pork).
