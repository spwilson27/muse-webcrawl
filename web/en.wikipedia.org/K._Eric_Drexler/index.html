K. Eric Drexler

Kim Eric Drexler (born April 25, 1955) is an American engineer and writer best known as the founding figure of molecular nanotechnology. His 1986 book Engines of Creation popularised the idea of atomically precise manufacturing and introduced the "grey goo" scenario to public debate.

Work

Drexler earned the first PhD in molecular nanotechnology at MIT in 1991, published in book form as Nanosystems: Molecular Machinery, Manufacturing, and Computation (1992), which set out engineering designs for molecular assemblers and mechanosynthesis. With Christine Peterson he co-founded the Foresight Institute in 1986 to promote beneficial development of nanotechnology.

Debates and later work

Drexler's assembler vision was publicly debated with chemist Richard Smalley in the early 2000s (the "fat fingers" and "sticky fingers" dispute). Drexler later distanced himself from the self-replicating "grey goo" image he had inadvertently popularised, and reframed his work around atomically precise manufacturing. His 2013 book Radical Abundance revisited the economic implications of the technology.
