Chaitanya Mahaprabhu

Chaitanya Mahaprabhu (1486–1534) was an Indian saint and religious reformer regarded by his followers as an incarnation of Krishna. He founded Gaudiya Vaishnavism and popularised the congregational chanting (sankirtana) of the Hare Krishna maha-mantra.

Life

Born Vishvambhara Mishra in Nabadwip (Nadia district, present-day West Bengal), Chaitanya was a brilliant scholar of Sanskrit before a spiritual transformation at Gaya led him to ecstatic devotion to Krishna. He took sannyasa in 1510 and settled at Puri, home of the Jagannath temple, spending his later years in deep devotional states. He is credited with the Shikshashtakam, eight devotional verses.

Legacy

His followers, the Six Goswamis of Vrindavan, systematised his theology of achintya-bheda-abheda (inconceivable simultaneous oneness and difference). The tradition spread worldwide in the 20th century through ISKCON, the International Society for Krishna Consciousness, founded by A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada.
