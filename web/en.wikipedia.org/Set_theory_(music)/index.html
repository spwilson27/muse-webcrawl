Set theory (music)

Musical set theory provides concepts for categorizing musical objects and describing their relationships. Howard Hanson first elaborated many of the concepts for analyzing tonal music (1960). Other theorists, such as Allen Forte, further developed the theory for analyzing atonal music (1973), drawing on the twelve-tone theory of Milton Babbitt.

A musical set is a collection of pitch classes, usually represented in normal order and reduced to its prime form. Sets are labelled by their Forte number, such as 3-11 for the major/minor triad set class. Operations on sets include transposition, inversion, complementation, and multiplication.

Set theory is widely used in the analysis of atonal and post-tonal music, including the works of Schoenberg, Webern, and their successors.
