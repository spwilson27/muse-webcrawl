Definite quadratic form

In mathematics, a definite quadratic form is a quadratic form over the real numbers that takes the same sign (always positive or always negative) for every nonzero vector. A form Q is positive definite if Q(x) > 0 for all x ≠ 0, and negative definite if Q(x) < 0 for all x ≠ 0.

Associated matrices

A quadratic form corresponds to a symmetric matrix, and definiteness can be tested by the matrix's eigenvalues (all positive for positive definite) or by Sylvester's criterion on the leading principal minors. Positive definite forms define inner products and hence notions of length and angle, and their unit spheres are ellipsoids.

Examples and applications

The form x² + y² is positive definite, as is the energy of a stable mechanical system expanded about equilibrium. Positive definite matrices are central to optimisation (where they certify minima), statistics (covariance matrices), and numerical linear algebra (the Cholesky decomposition). Indefinite forms, taking both signs, arise in relativity, where the spacetime interval has Lorentzian signature.
