The 1950 FIFA World Cup was the fourth edition of the FIFA World Cup, the quadrennial international football championship for men's national teams. It was held in Brazil from 24 June to 16 July 1950, the first tournament staged after a twelve-year hiatus caused by the Second World War. It remains the only World Cup decided not by a single final match but by a final round-robin group.

Background

Brazil was chosen as host in 1946, and the country built new stadiums for the tournament, most famously the Maracanã in Rio de Janeiro, then the largest stadium in the world. Several teams withdrew before the tournament, including Scotland, Turkey, and India, leaving thirteen participants. Italy, the defending champions, participated despite the Superga air disaster of 1949 that had killed the core of the national side. England competed in a World Cup for the first time.

Tournament

The thirteen teams were divided into four first-round groups, the winners of which — Brazil, Spain, Sweden, and Uruguay — advanced to a final group. The tournament produced one of the greatest upsets in football history when the United States defeated England 1–0 in Belo Horizonte, with the winning goal scored by Joe Gaetjens.

The deciding match

The final group came down to its last match on 16 July 1950, between Brazil and Uruguay at the Maracanã before an officially recorded crowd of 173,850 (often estimated at around 200,000). Brazil needed only a draw to become champions and took the lead through Friaça early in the second half, but Uruguay equalised through Juan Alberto Schiaffino and then, with eleven minutes remaining, Alcides Ghiggia scored the winning goal. Uruguay's 2–1 victory, known in Brazil as the Maracanaço ("the Maracanã blow"), delivered Uruguay's second World Cup title and caused profound shock in the host country.
