James Maury Henson (1936–1990) was an American puppeteer, animator and filmmaker, best known as the creator of the Muppets and the performer of Kermit the Frog and Ernie, among many other characters.

Career

Henson began building puppets in high school and created the short show Sam and Friends while at the University of Maryland. He founded Muppets, Inc. in 1958. His characters became widely known through Sesame Street (from 1969) and The Muppet Show (1976–1981).

Films and later work

Henson directed The Dark Crystal (1982, with Frank Oz) and Labyrinth (1986), and his Creature Shop pioneered animatronic techniques. He developed Fraggle Rock and The StoryTeller. Negotiations to sell his company to Disney were under way when he died suddenly of a bacterial infection in 1990.

Legacy

The Jim Henson Company and the Jim Henson Foundation continue his work, and his characters remain cultural icons.
