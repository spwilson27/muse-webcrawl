Deptford

Deptford is an area on the south bank of the River Thames in south-east London, England, within the London Borough of Lewisham. It lies south of Greenwich and takes its name from a ford across the River Ravensbourne — the "deep ford". Historically part of Kent, Deptford grew from a small fishing and farming settlement into one of England's most important naval dockyard towns.

History

In 1513 Henry VIII founded a royal dockyard at Deptford, and for more than three centuries the yard built and repaired warships for the Royal Navy. The playwright Christopher Marlowe was killed in Deptford in 1593, in a house on Deptford Strand — officially in a brawl over the bill, though the circumstances have generated centuries of speculation. The diarist John Evelyn lived at Sayes Court in Deptford, and Peter the Great of Russia lodged in his house in 1698 while studying shipbuilding at the yard, notoriously damaging the gardens. The dockyard closed in 1869, after which the area became a working-class district centred on the Foreign Cattle Market and later meat processing.

Modern Deptford

Deptford High Street, known for its market, anchors a district that has seen waves of regeneration since the 1990s, including the Laban dance centre, the Albany theatre, and new housing on the former dockyard site at Convoys Wharf. The area has a notable artistic community associated with Goldsmiths College. Deptford station, opened in 1836 on the London and Greenwich Railway, is among the oldest suburban railway stations in the world, and Deptford is also served by the Docklands Light Railway at Deptford Bridge.
