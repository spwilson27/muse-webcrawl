Seed (sports)

A seed is a competitor or team in a sports tournament who is given a preliminary ranking for the purposes of the draw, usually because they are considered among the stronger entrants. Seeding is used to ensure that the strongest competitors do not meet in the early rounds of a knockout tournament.

Purpose

In a seeded single-elimination bracket, the top seed plays the lowest seed, the second seed plays the second-lowest, and so on, so that the highest-ranked remaining competitors can only meet in later rounds or the final. Seeds are usually assigned from official rankings, league standings, or qualifying results.

Usage

Seeding is prominent in tennis Grand Slam tournaments (which seed 32 players in singles), the NCAA basketball tournament (which seeds 68 teams in four regions), the FIFA World Cup group draw, and chess and esports competitions.

Criticism

Critics note that seeding can entrench advantages for established competitors, and upsets — victories by low seeds over high seeds — are a celebrated part of tournament drama.
