The 2002–2004 SARS outbreak was an epidemic involving severe acute respiratory syndrome (SARS) caused by the coronavirus SARS-CoV (SARS-CoV-1). The outbreak was first identified in Foshan, Guangdong, China, in November 2002, and spread to 29 countries, infecting more than 8,000 people before it was contained in mid-2003.

Spread

The disease spread internationally along air-travel routes, notably through a superspreading event at the Metropole Hotel in Hong Kong in February 2003. Major outbreaks occurred in Hong Kong, Toronto, Singapore, Taiwan and Vietnam. The World Health Organization issued a global alert on 12 March 2003.

Containment

Through case isolation, contact tracing and quarantine, transmission was interrupted, and the WHO declared the outbreak contained on 5 July 2003. Small laboratory-associated cases occurred in late 2003 and early 2004. Italian physician Carlo Urbani, who first identified the new disease, died of SARS in March 2003.

Impact

The outbreak caused major economic disruption in East Asia and spurred long-term reforms in global disease surveillance and outbreak reporting.
