Ciudad Victoria is the capital and largest city of the Mexican state of Tamaulipas, located in the northeast of Mexico at the foot of the Sierra Madre Oriental. It is the seat of the municipality of Victoria and home to the state government, the Autonomous University of Tamaulipas, and several state cultural institutions.

History

The city was founded on 6 October 1750 by José de Escandón as the Villa de Santa María de Aguayo, during the colonisation of Nuevo Santander. After Mexican independence it was renamed Ciudad Victoria in honour of Guadalupe Victoria, the first president of Mexico, and became the state capital in 1825. The Federal Highway 101 and the railway connect it with Tampico on the Gulf coast and with the interior.

Geography

Ciudad Victoria sits on the San Marcos River at about 316 m elevation, with a hot semi-arid climate moderated by its position near the Sierra Madre Oriental; the nearby El Cielo biosphere reserve is a noted area of cloud forest. The metropolitan population is roughly 350,000.
