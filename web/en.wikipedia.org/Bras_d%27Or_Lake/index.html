Bras d'Or Lake

LocationCape Breton Island, Nova Scotia, Canada
Area~1,099 km²
TypeBrackish estuarine lake
DesignationUNESCO Biosphere Reserve (2011)

Bras d'Or Lake is a large brackish estuary occupying the centre of Cape Breton Island in Nova Scotia, Canada. Roughly 109 km long and up to 48 km wide, it is connected to the Atlantic Ocean by channels at its northern end and is often described as an inland sea.

Geography and ecology

The lake's two basins are largely separated by Boularderie Island, and its limited tidal exchange and freshwater inflow create a distinctive ecosystem with both freshwater and marine species. Its sheltered, fog-free waters are prized for sailing. The watershed is home to Mi'kmaq communities, for whom the lake is known as Pitupaq and is central to traditional life; the village of Chapel Island (Potlotek) hosts an annual mission gathering.

History

The name, French for "arm of gold", is often attributed to early explorers. In the late 19th century Alexander Graham Bell built his estate, Beinn Bhreagh, overlooking the lake near Baddeck, and conducted hydrofoil experiments on its waters, setting a marine speed record in 1919. UNESCO designated the lake and its watershed a Biosphere Reserve in 2011.
