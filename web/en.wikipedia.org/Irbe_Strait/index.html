Irbe Strait

Coordinates: 57°48′N 22°12′E

Basin countries: Estonia, Latvia

Max. length: 65 km (40 mi)

Min. width: 33 km (21 mi)

The Irbe Strait (Estonian: Kura kurk; Latvian: Irbes jūras šaurums) is the main exit from the Gulf of Riga to the Baltic Sea, located between the Sõrve Peninsula forming the southern tip of the Estonian island of Saaremaa and Courland in Latvia. It is 27 km (17 mi) wide at its narrowest point and 65 km long. The maximum depth is up to 21 m.

Geography

The strait separates the Estonian island of Saaremaa from the Latvian mainland. Its northern shore is formed by the Sõrve Peninsula, lit by the Sõrve Lighthouse, while the southern shore is formed by the Courland Peninsula in Latvia, marked by the Irbe Lighthouse off the Kolka area. Alongside the strait lies a parallel channel created artificially by the Soviets to make the strait more navigable.

History

During the World Wars the strait and the Gulf of Riga were strategically important and heavily mined. The area saw naval operations including the German Operation Albion in 1917. The strait gives its name to the Irben Strait battle and numerous wrecks rest on its bottom. After the Second World War, the strait formed part of the boundary waters of the Soviet Union; today it lies on the maritime border between Estonia and Latvia.
