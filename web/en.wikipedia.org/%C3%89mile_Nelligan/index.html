Émile Nelligan (24 December 1879 – 18 November 1941) was a francophone poet from Quebec, Canada. Regarded as one of Quebec's greatest poets, he wrote strikingly mature symbolist verse as a teenager before mental illness ended his career at nineteen.

Life and work

Influenced by Charles Baudelaire, Paul Verlaine and the French symbolists, Nelligan gave celebrated readings at the École littéraire de Montréal. His best-known poem, "Le Vaisseau d'or" ("The Golden Ship"), is among the most famous in French-Canadian literature. In 1899 he was committed to a psychiatric asylum, where he remained for the rest of his life.

Legacy

A first collection of his poems was published in 1903. The Prix Émile-Nelligan, awarded to young francophone poets in North America, is named in his honour.
