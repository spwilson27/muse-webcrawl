José María Morelos

José María Morelos
Born: 30 September 1765, Valladolid (now Morelia)
Died: 22 December 1815, San Cristóbal Ecatepec
Occupation: Priest, revolutionary leader

José María Teclo Morelos Pérez y Pavón (30 September 1765 – 22 December 1815) was a Mexican Roman Catholic priest, statesman and military leader who led the Mexican War of Independence movement, assuming its leadership after the execution of Miguel Hidalgo y Costilla in 1811.

Early life

Morelos was born in Valladolid (present-day Morelia) in Michoacán. Of mixed Indigenous, African and Spanish ancestry, he worked as a mule driver and later studied at the Colegio de San Nicolás under Hidalgo, becoming a priest in 1797 and serving in Carácuaro.

Independence campaign

Meeting Hidalgo in October 1810, Morelos was commissioned to raise the south. He captured Acapulco, organised disciplined forces, and convened the Congress of Chilpancingo in 1813, which declared independence and produced the document Sentimientos de la Nación. The Constitution of Apatzingán followed in 1814.

Capture and death

Morelos was captured at Temalaca in November 1815 while protecting the Congress, tried by the Inquisition and civil authorities in Mexico City, defrocked, and executed by firing squad on 22 December 1815. The Mexican state of Morelos and the city of Morelia are named in his honour.
