Hierarchy problem

The hierarchy problem is a problem in theoretical physics, concerning why the weak force is 1032 times stronger than gravity. Both seem to point in different directions: gravity is an extremely weak force, while the energy scale of the weak interaction (the electroweak scale, around 246 GeV) is enormously smaller than the Planck scale (around 1019 GeV) at which gravity becomes strong.

Technical formulation

More technically, the hierarchy problem concerns the Higgs boson mass. In quantum field theory, the mass of the Higgs boson receives radiative corrections from virtual particles that are quadratically divergent, meaning the corrections are proportional to the square of the highest energy scale in the theory. If the Standard Model is valid up to the Planck scale, the bare Higgs mass would have to cancel these enormous corrections to extraordinary precision — a "fine-tuning" of roughly one part in 1032 — in order to leave the observed Higgs mass of about 125 GeV. Many physicists regard such fine-tuning as unnatural.

Proposed solutions

Several extensions of the Standard Model have been proposed to resolve the problem:

  • Supersymmetry — the most popular solution, in which every Standard Model particle has a superpartner whose contributions cancel the quadratic divergences.
  • Large extra dimensions — proposals such as the ADD model, in which gravity spreads into extra spatial dimensions, lowering the true fundamental Planck scale.
  • Warped extra dimensions — the Randall–Sundrum model, which generates the hierarchy from the geometry of a curved extra dimension.
  • Technicolor and composite Higgs models, in which the Higgs is not a fundamental scalar.
  • The relaxion and other dynamical mechanisms.
  • Anthropic reasoning within a multiverse or landscape of vacua.

So far, experiments at the Large Hadron Collider have found no direct evidence for supersymmetric particles or extra dimensions, leaving the hierarchy problem an open question in fundamental physics.
