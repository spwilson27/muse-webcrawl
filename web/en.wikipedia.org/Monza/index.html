Monza

Country: Italy

Region: Lombardy

Province: Monza and Brianza

Population: ~124,000

Monza is a city and comune (municipality) on the river Lambro, a tributary of the River Po, in the Lombardy region of Italy, about 15 kilometres (9 miles) north-northeast of Milan. It is the capital of the province of Monza and Brianza. Monza is best known for its Grand Prix motor racing circuit, the Autodromo Nazionale Monza, home of the Italian Grand Prix.

History

Monza was an important Lombard town; its cathedral, founded by Queen Theodelinda in the late 6th century, houses the Iron Crown of Lombardy, said to contain one of the nails of the Crucifixion, with which kings of Italy were crowned for centuries. In 1805 Napoleon was crowned King of Italy with the Iron Crown in Milan Cathedral. The Royal Villa (Villa Reale), built in the 18th century for Archduke Ferdinand of Austria, is one of the city's principal monuments, surrounded by one of Europe's largest walled parks.

In 1900, King Umberto I of Italy was assassinated in Monza by the anarchist Gaetano Bresci, the first assassination of a European monarch by a non-conspirator in the modern era and the first regicide broadcast by telegraph.

Sport

The Monza Circuit, built in 1922, is one of the oldest and fastest tracks in Formula One. The city is also home to the football club AC Monza.
