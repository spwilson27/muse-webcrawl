Nîmes

Nîmes is a city in the Occitanie region of southern France, the prefecture of the Gard department, with a population of around 150,000. It is famed for some of the best-preserved Roman monuments in the world, legacies of the ancient city of Nemausus.

Founded as a Roman colony under Augustus, Nemausus grew rich on trade and was embellished with temples, an amphitheatre and an aqueduct. The Maison Carrée, a Roman temple of the early 1st century AD, is among the most complete surviving Roman temples anywhere and was inscribed by UNESCO as a World Heritage Site in 2023. The Arena of Nîmes, an amphitheatre seating some 24,000 in Roman times, still hosts concerts and the city's famous ferias. The Pont du Gard, which carried water to the city from Uzès, survives nearby.

Later history and culture

Nîmes was the birthplace of Alphonse Daudet and an early centre of French Protestantism. Its textile trade gave the world denim — the name derives from "serge de Nîmes." Today the city is also known for bullfighting festivals, the modern Carré d'Art museum designed by Norman Foster, and its role as a hub of Languedoc wine production.
