Project Gutenberg

Project Gutenberg (PG) is a volunteer effort to digitize and archive cultural works, as well as to "encourage the creation and distribution of eBooks". It was founded in 1971 by American writer Michael S. Hart and is the oldest digital library. Most of the items in its collection are the full texts of books or individual stories in the public domain. All files can be accessed for free and in open formats such as EPUB, Kindle, HTML and plain text.

History

Hart began the project at the University of Illinois, typing the text of the US Declaration of Independence into a mainframe in 1971. Volunteers initially retyped texts and later used scanners and OCR with extensive proofreading (now coordinated via Distributed Proofreaders, founded in 2000). Milestones include the 10,000th eBook (Dante's Divine Comedy) in 2003 and growth to tens of thousands of titles.

Collection and copyright policy

Project Gutenberg releases works confirmed to be in the US public domain, generally those whose copyright has expired. Texts are distributed in durable, non-proprietary formats; Hart emphasised plain text so files remain readable on any device. Affiliated projects exist for other jurisdictions, such as Project Gutenberg Australia and Projekt Gutenberg-DE, reflecting differing copyright terms.

Reception and impact

Project Gutenberg pioneered the free eBook and the digital library, influenced later efforts from the Internet Archive to Google Books, and remains a principal source of public-domain texts for readers and for computational text corpora.
