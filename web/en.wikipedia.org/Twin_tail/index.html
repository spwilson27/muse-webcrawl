Twin tail
Type: aircraft empennage arrangement
Examples: F-15 Eagle, F/A-18 Hornet, Lockheed Constellation (triple tail variant)

A twin tail is an aircraft empennage configuration in which two vertical stabilisers are mounted at the rear of the aircraft instead of a single central fin. The fins may be attached to the fuselage, to the ends of the horizontal stabiliser, or on twin booms.

Advantages

Twin tails can keep the vertical surfaces within the airflow of wing-mounted propellers, improve rudder effectiveness at high angles of attack, allow a shorter overall tail height for hangar or carrier storage, and provide redundancy. They are common on modern fighter aircraft, where twin fins also reduce radar signature when canted.

Variants

Related arrangements include the twin-boom layout, in which the fins sit on two fuselage booms (as on the P-38 Lightning), and triple tails, used on the Lockheed Constellation to fit into existing hangars.
