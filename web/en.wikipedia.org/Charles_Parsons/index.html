Sir Charles Algernon Parsons (13 June 1854 – 11 February 1931) was an Anglo-Irish engineer, best known for his invention of the compound steam turbine, and as the eponym of the engineering firm C. A. Parsons and Company. His invention transformed marine propulsion and electrical power generation.

Early life

Parsons was the youngest son of the astronomer William Parsons, 3rd Earl of Rosse, builder of the Leviathan of Parsonstown telescope. Educated at Trinity College Dublin and St John's College, Cambridge, he served an apprenticeship at the Elswick works of Sir William Armstrong in Newcastle.

Steam turbine and Turbinia

In 1884, working at Clarke, Chapman and Co., Parsons patented a multi-stage reaction steam turbine coupled to a dynamo, running at the unprecedented speed of 18,000 rpm. In 1889 he founded his own firm at Heaton, Newcastle. To demonstrate the turbine's potential at sea he built the experimental vessel Turbinia, which in 1897 gatecrashed the Spithead naval review for Queen Victoria's Diamond Jubilee, easily outrunning every naval vessel at up to 34 knots. The Admiralty soon ordered turbine-powered destroyers, and turbines were adopted for warships, liners such as the Mauretania, and power stations worldwide.

Later work and honours

Parsons also worked on telescopes, searchlights and the auxetophone. He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1898, knighted in 1911, and awarded the Order of Merit in 1927. He died in 1931 while on a cruise in the Caribbean.
