Heronry

DefinitionColonial nesting site of herons
Typical habitatTrees near wetlands, lakes, and reedbeds
Colony sizeFrom a few pairs to several hundred nests

A heronry is a breeding colony of herons, typically a group of stick nests built in the crowns of trees near water. Grey herons and great blue herons commonly nest colonially in this way, returning year after year to traditional sites, some of which have been occupied for centuries.

Characteristics

Heronries are usually located in tall trees in woodland close to feeding grounds such as marshes, rivers, and estuaries. Nests are large platforms of sticks, added to each season, and colonies may grow to dozens or even hundreds of pairs. Other species, such as egrets, night herons, cormorants, and occasionally spoonbills, often nest alongside herons in mixed colonies.

Behaviour

Breeding begins early in the year, with elaborate courtship displays at the nest. Both parents incubate the eggs and feed the chicks by regurgitation. The noise, droppings, and dropped food beneath a large heronry make it conspicuous, and accumulating guano can eventually damage the nesting trees themselves.

Conservation

Because heronries are traditional and easily disturbed, many are protected as nature reserves or through wardening schemes. Counts of apparently occupied nests at heronries have been used for decades — in Britain since 1928 — as a census method for monitoring heron populations, making the heronry census one of the longest-running bird monitoring schemes in the world.
