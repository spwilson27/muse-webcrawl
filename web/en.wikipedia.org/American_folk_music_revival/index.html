American folk music revival

The American folk music revival was a phenomenon that began in the United States in the 1940s and peaked in popularity in the mid-1960s. Its roots went back to the Great Depression and earlier folk traditions, and it brought performers such as Woody Guthrie, Pete Seeger, and Lead Belly to national attention.

History

Early revivalists were associated with leftist politics and the labor movement. The Weavers' 1950 hit "Goodnight, Irene" launched folk into the mainstream, though McCarthy-era blacklisting soon drove Seeger and others from television and radio. The Kingston Trio's 1958 hit "Tom Dooley" sparked a commercial folk boom across college campuses.

1960s and legacy

The Newport Folk Festival became the movement's showcase, introducing Joan Baez and Bob Dylan, whose songwriting fused folk with protest and poetry and led to folk rock. The revival shaped American popular music and preserved traditional repertoires for new generations.
