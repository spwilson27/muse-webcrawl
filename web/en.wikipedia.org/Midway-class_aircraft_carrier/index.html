Midway-class aircraft carrier

The Midway-class was a class of three aircraft carriers of the United States Navy, built shortly after the end of World War II and in service from late 1945 until 1992. The lead ship, USS Midway, was named for the Battle of Midway; her sisters were USS Franklin D. Roosevelt and USS Coral Sea. They were the largest carriers completed at the time, the first American carriers too wide to transit the Panama Canal, and the first designed with an armoured flight deck.

Originally designed to operate very large air groups of propeller aircraft, all three were extensively modernised in the 1950s with angled flight decks, steam catapults, and mirror landing aids to operate jet aircraft. Franklin D. Roosevelt was decommissioned in 1977 and Coral Sea in 1990; Midway served in the Vietnam War and as flagship in Operation Desert Storm before decommissioning in 1992, after 47 years of service. She is now a museum ship in San Diego, California.
