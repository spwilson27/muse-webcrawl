Television director

A television director is in charge of the activities involved in making a television program or section of a program and is part of the production team. This role is distinct from that of a film director in that the television director may not be solely responsible for the finished product, with creative control sometimes shared with a producer, showrunner, or the lead actor.

Responsibilities

Before production begins, a television director works with producers and writers to visualize the script. During filming, the director oversees the technical crew, directs the actors, and supervises the choice and control of cameras, lighting, and other elements in the studio or on location. In a multi-camera setup, the director sits in the control room (gallery) and calls the shots, while a vision mixer cuts between cameras at the director's command.

Live television

Directors of live television, such as news, talk shows, and sports broadcasts, must make split-second decisions in real time. Because there is no opportunity for retakes, live directing is considered one of the most demanding forms of the craft.
