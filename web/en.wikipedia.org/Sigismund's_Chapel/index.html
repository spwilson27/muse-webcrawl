Sigismund's Chapel

Sigismund's Chapel (Polish: Kaplica Zygmuntowska) is a funerary chapel at Wawel Cathedral in Kraków, Poland. Commissioned by King Sigismund I the Old as a mausoleum for himself and his family, it was built between 1517 and 1533 to the designs of the Florentine architect Bartolomeo Berrecci. The chapel is widely considered the finest example of Tuscan Renaissance architecture north of the Alps.

Architecture

The chapel is a square structure surmounted by a dome resting on an octagonal drum, its roof covered with gilded fish-scale tiles that give it a distinctive golden appearance within the Wawel complex. The interior is adorned with stucco decoration, grotesques, and sandstone carvings executed by Berrecci's workshop, including Giovanni Maria Padovano, who completed much of the sculptural programme.

Tombs

Inside stand the tomb monuments of Sigismund I and his son Sigismund II Augustus, the last Jagiellonian king, whose figure was added after his death in 1572 by Santi Gucci. Opposite them is the tomb of Queen Anna Jagiellon, sister of Sigismund II and wife of Stephen Báthory. Silver altarpieces and a rich liturgical treasury originally furnished the chapel.

Significance

The chapel introduced mature Italian Renaissance forms into Poland, setting the pattern for numerous later funerary chapels of the Polish nobility, and remains one of the most visited parts of Wawel Cathedral.
