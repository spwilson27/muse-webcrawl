New towns of Singapore

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The new towns of Singapore are planned communities located across the city-state, developed by the Housing and Development Board (HDB) since the 1960s to house the country's population in high-rise public housing estates. The vast majority of Singaporeans live in these towns, and most residents own their flats under long leasehold arrangements promoted by the government.

Planning model

A Singapore new town is a self-contained settlement, typically housing 100,000 to 250,000 residents, organised around a town centre with a Mass Rapid Transit (MRT) station, bus interchange, shopping mall, and civic facilities. Residential neighbourhoods are subdivided into smaller precincts, each with its own playgrounds and local shops. Schools, community clubs, markets and hawker centres, polyclinics, parks, places of worship, and light industrial areas are planned according to standard ratios, with the aim that daily needs are met within the town.

Examples

The earliest new towns included Toa Payoh, Queenstown, and Ang Mo Kio. Later, larger towns such as Bedok, Tampines, Jurong West, Woodlands, Sengkang, and Punggol were built as housing expanded to the island's periphery. Tengah, announced in 2016, is the first new town planned with a car-lite "forest town" concept, extensive greenery, and automated waste collection. Town designs have evolved over the decades from simple slabs and point blocks to more varied, integrated developments with mixed uses.

Significance

The new town programme is central to Singapore's post-independence social policy: public housing provided affordable homes, encouraged home ownership and national integration, and supported an ethnic integration policy that allocates flats to maintain a mix of the country's ethnic groups in each block and neighbourhood.
