Hafez al-Assad


Illustration: Hafez al-Assad

Hafez al-Assad (6 October 1930 – 10 June 2000) was a Syrian statesman and military officer who served as President of Syria from 1971 until his death in 2000. A member of the Alawite minority, he rose through the Syrian Air Force and the Ba'ath Party.

Rise to power

Assad participated in the 1963 Ba'athist coup and became commander of the air force and minister of defence. In 1970 he seized power in the "Corrective Movement", and was confirmed as president by referendum in 1971.

Rule

His government combined authoritarian control with a degree of stability, pursued pan-Arab ambitions, fought Israel in the 1973 October War, and intervened in the Lebanese Civil War. The regime violently suppressed an Islamist uprising, culminating in the 1982 Hama massacre. On his death he was succeeded by his son Bashar al-Assad.
