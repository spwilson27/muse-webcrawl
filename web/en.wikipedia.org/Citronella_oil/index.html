Citronella oil

Citronella oil
Source: Leaves and stems of Cymbopogon species
Major components: Citronellal, geraniol, citronellol
Uses: Insect repellent, perfumery, soap

Citronella oil is an essential oil obtained from the leaves and stems of certain species of lemongrass in the genus Cymbopogon, principally Cymbopogon nardus (Ceylon citronella) and Cymbopogon winterianus (Java citronella). It is best known as a plant-based insect repellent and is widely used in candles, sprays, soaps, and cosmetics.

Types and composition

The two commercial types are Ceylon citronella oil, from C. nardus, and Java citronella oil, from C. winterianus. Java oil has a higher content of citronellal and geraniol and commands a higher price. Principal constituents include citronellal, geraniol, citronellol, limonene, and camphene. The oil is produced by steam distillation and is mainly manufactured in Sri Lanka, Indonesia, China, and Vietnam.

Uses

Citronella oil is registered in the United States as a biopesticide with a non-toxic mode of action; it repels mosquitoes and other insects by masking the scents that attract them, though its protection lasts only about two hours without reapplication. It is also a fragrance ingredient, and its components serve as starting materials for the synthesis of other aroma chemicals such as hydroxycitronellal and menthol. In perfumery it provides a fresh, lemony, rosy note, and it has traditionally been used to scent soaps and candles — the citronella candle being a familiar outdoor insect deterrent.
