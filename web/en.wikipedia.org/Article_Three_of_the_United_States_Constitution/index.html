Article Three of the United States Constitution

Article Three of the United States Constitution establishes the judicial branch of the federal government. It vests the judicial power in the Supreme Court and in such inferior courts as Congress may establish, guarantees judges tenure during good behaviour with undiminished salaries, and defines the scope of federal jurisdiction.

Section 1: Federal courts

Section 1 creates the Supreme Court and authorises Congress to create lower federal courts. Federal judges hold office during good behaviour — in practice, for life unless impeached — and their compensation cannot be reduced while in office, protections designed to secure judicial independence.

Section 2: Judicial power and jurisdiction

Section 2 extends the judicial power to cases arising under the Constitution, federal laws and treaties, cases affecting ambassadors, admiralty cases, and controversies between states or between citizens of different states (diversity jurisdiction). It also specifies the Supreme Court's original and appellate jurisdiction and guarantees jury trial for federal crimes, to be held in the state where the crime was committed.

Section 3: Treason

Section 3 defines treason against the United States narrowly — levying war against the United States or adhering to its enemies, giving them aid and comfort — and requires the testimony of two witnesses to the same overt act, or a confession in open court, for conviction. Congress may set the punishment but no attainder of treason may work "corruption of blood" or forfeiture beyond the life of the person attainted.
