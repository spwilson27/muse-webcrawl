Lighter (barge)

A lighter is a type of flat-bottomed barge used to transfer goods and passengers to and from moored ships. Lighters were traditionally unpowered and were moved by oars ("lightermen") or later towed by tugs. The name derives from the practice of making a ship lighter — the process of lightering — by partially unloading it so that it can enter a shallow harbour or berth.

Use

When a vessel is too large for a port's depth or facilities, lighters shuttle its cargo ashore in smaller loads. The process is still used in modern petroleum transport: very large crude carriers partially discharge into smaller tankers offshore (ship-to-ship transfer) before entering port. On the River Thames, lightermen were historically an important trade, licensed by the Watermen's Company; Thames lighters carried cargo between ships in the Pool of London and the wharves.

Lighter aboard ship

The concept was adapted in the 1960s as the LASH (Lighter Aboard Ship) system, in which a specialised mother ship carries loaded barges (lighters) on board, floating them on and off at each port so cargo can proceed inland by waterway without transshipment cranes. Related systems include the Seabee and BACAT designs.
