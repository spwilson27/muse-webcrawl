Omaha is the largest city in the U.S. state of Nebraska and the county seat of Douglas County. It stands on the west bank of the Missouri River, across from Council Bluffs, Iowa, roughly 20 miles north of the Platte River's mouth. Founded in 1854 by speculators from Iowa, Omaha grew as a gateway to the West and briefly served as the Nebraska territorial capital.

History

Omaha's position made it the eastern terminus of the first transcontinental railroad: the Union Pacific Railroad broke ground here in 1863 and still has its headquarters in the city. Stockyards and meatpacking made Omaha one of America's great livestock markets through the mid-20th century; the Omaha Stockyards were among the world's largest. The city hosted the Trans-Mississippi Exposition of 1898 and was the birthplace of Malcolm X and of President Gerald Ford.

Economy

Omaha is home to Berkshire Hathaway, the conglomerate led for decades by Warren Buffett, the "Oracle of Omaha", whose annual shareholder meeting draws tens of thousands of visitors. Other major employers include Union Pacific, Mutual of Omaha, Conagra Brands and the Offutt Air Force Base nearby, headquarters of U.S. Strategic Command. The Henry Doorly Zoo and Aquarium is consistently ranked among the world's best zoos.

Culture and sport

The city hosts the NCAA College World Series every June, a fixture since 1950. Its Old Market district preserves 19th-century warehouses as shops and galleries, and Omaha developed a notable indie-rock scene around the Saddle Creek label in the 1990s and 2000s.
