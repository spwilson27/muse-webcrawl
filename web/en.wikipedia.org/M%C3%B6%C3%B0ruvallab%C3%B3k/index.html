Möðruvallabók ("the book of Möðruvellir") is an Icelandic manuscript of the mid-14th century, catalogued as AM 132 fol. It is the largest surviving single collection of Icelandic family sagas (Íslendingasögur) and one of the most important manuscripts of medieval Icelandic literature.

Contents

The manuscript contains eleven sagas, including Njáls saga, Egils saga, Laxdæla saga, Eyrbyggja saga, Grettis saga, Víga-Glúms saga, Bandamanna saga, Droplaugarsona saga, Ölkofra saga, Hallfreðar saga and Finnboga saga. For several of these texts it is the earliest or principal witness.

History

The codex was probably written in northern Iceland and takes its name from the farm Möðruvellir in Eyjafjörður, where it was kept in the 17th century. The collector Árni Magnússon acquired it in the early 18th century and took it to Copenhagen; like the rest of his collection, it was returned to Iceland in the later 20th century and is now preserved by the Árni Magnússon Institute for Icelandic Studies.
