Sicca syndrome

Sicca syndrome is a combination of dry eyes (xerophthalmia) and dry mouth (xerostomia), resulting from the failure of lacrimal and salivary glands. It may be caused by an autoimmune disease or be a secondary effect of other conditions. The term is derived from the Latin siccus, meaning "dry".

Sicca syndrome is most commonly associated with Sjögren's syndrome, a chronic autoimmune disease in which the body's immune system attacks the moisture-producing glands. However, dryness symptoms can also occur without the full autoimmune picture of Sjögren's syndrome, for example as a side effect of medications, radiation therapy to the head and neck, or ageing.

Signs and symptoms

Patients typically report gritty or burning eyes, difficulty swallowing dry foods, dental decay from reduced saliva, and dryness of the skin and mucous membranes. Complications can include corneal damage and oral candidiasis.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis may involve the Schirmer test to measure tear production, salivary flow measurement, and serological tests for autoantibodies. Treatment is mainly symptomatic: artificial tears, saliva substitutes, good dental care, and, where an autoimmune cause is established, immunomodulatory therapy.
