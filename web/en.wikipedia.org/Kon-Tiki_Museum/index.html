The Kon-Tiki Museum (Norwegian: Kon-Tiki Museet) is a museum on the Bygdøy peninsula in Oslo, Norway, housing vessels and artefacts from the expeditions of explorer and writer Thor Heyerdahl. It stands near the Fram Museum and the Norwegian Maritime Museum.

Exhibits

The centrepiece is the original Kon-Tiki, the balsawood raft on which Heyerdahl and five companions sailed 8,000 km from Peru to Polynesia in 1947 to demonstrate that pre-Columbian South Americans could have crossed the Pacific. Also displayed is the papyrus boat Ra II, which crossed the Atlantic from Morocco to Barbados in 1970, alongside objects from Heyerdahl's expeditions to Easter Island, the Galápagos, and Túcume in Peru, and a reproduction of an Easter Island cave with moai statues.

History

The raft was first exhibited in 1949; the museum's permanent building opened in 1957. The museum also maintains Heyerdahl's library and archives and supports research on his theories of transoceanic contact — theories largely rejected by mainstream archaeology and genetics, though the voyages themselves remain celebrated feats of experimental navigation.
