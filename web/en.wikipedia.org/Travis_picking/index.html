Travis picking

Travis picking is a fingerstyle guitar technique, named after the American country guitarist Merle Travis, in which the thumb maintains a steady alternating bass pattern on the low strings while the fingers pluck syncopated melodies and chords on the higher strings. The style is a cornerstone of country, folk, and Americana guitar playing.

Technique

In Travis picking the thumb plays a regular alternating bass note pattern, usually between the root and fifth of the chord, creating a driving rhythm reminiscent of ragtime piano. Meanwhile the index and middle fingers pick out the melody, often with syncopation against the bass. Travis himself typically used only his thumb and index finger, employing a thumb pick to strengthen the bass notes.

History

The technique grew out of the fingerpicking traditions of western Kentucky, where guitarists such as Mose Rager developed the alternating-bass style. Merle Travis learned from this tradition and popularized it nationally through his recordings in the 1940s and 1950s. Chet Atkins adapted the style with more fingers and greater harmonic sophistication, and it later influenced folk revival players and singer-songwriters.

Notable practitioners

Beyond Travis and Atkins, the style has been used by Doc Watson, Tommy Emmanuel, and many folk and country performers. It remains a standard technique taught to fingerstyle guitarists.
