Queenslander (architecture)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Queenslander is a style of detached house in Queensland, Australia, characterised by a timber-framed, timber-clad construction raised on stumps, a large pitched roof (typically of corrugated iron), and wide verandahs on one or more sides. The style developed from the 1840s and was the dominant form of domestic construction in Queensland until the Second World War; it remains the archetypal housing of Brisbane and regional Queensland towns.

Design features

The design responds to Queensland's subtropical climate and terrain. Elevation on stumps protects against flooding and termites and allows air to circulate beneath the floor; verandahs shade the core rooms, which are arranged around a central hallway to promote through-breezes; and the steep roof sheds heavy rain and vents hot air. Lightweight timber construction also suited rapidly growing settlements with limited masonry skills and materials.

Heritage

Many Queenslanders are protected by local heritage overlays, particularly in Brisbane, and "character housing" controls restrict their demolition. Surviving examples are prized and often renovated with the undercroft enclosed, though the classic stilt profile and verandahs remain an emblem of the state.
