Newport is a seaside city on Aquidneck Island in Rhode Island, United States, situated at the mouth of Narragansett Bay about 33 miles (53 km) southeast of Providence. Founded in 1639, it was a major colonial port and a centre of religious tolerance, later a summer resort of America's wealthiest families during the Gilded Age, and today a celebrated yachting centre and home to the United States Naval War College.

History

Newport was founded by settlers led by William Coddington and others who left Portsmouth, Rhode Island. In the colonial era it became one of the five great American ports, prospering from trade—including, notoriously, the slave trade and rum distilling. The city suffered during British occupation in the Revolutionary War, and its commerce never fully recovered its colonial primacy. From the mid-nineteenth century, wealthy industrialists built palatial summer "cottages" such as The Breakers (1895, for Cornelius Vanderbilt II) and Marble House along Bellevue Avenue and the Cliff Walk, making Newport a byword for Gilded Age opulence.

Sailing and sport

Newport hosted the America's Cup yacht races from 1930 to 1983, when Australia II ended the New York Yacht Club's 132-year winning streak. It remains a premier sailing destination and the finish of the biennial Newport Bermuda Race. Newport is also home to the International Tennis Hall of Fame, founded in 1954 at the Newport Casino, and to the Newport Jazz Festival (established 1954) and Newport Folk Festival (1959), two of America's most storied music festivals.

Military presence

Naval Station Newport hosts the Naval War College (founded 1884), the Naval Justice School, and officer training commands, making the Navy one of the region's largest employers. Presidents Dwight D. Eisenhower and John F. Kennedy both used Newport as a summer White House base.
