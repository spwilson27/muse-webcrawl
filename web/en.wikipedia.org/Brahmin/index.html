Brahmin

Brahmin (Sanskrit: brāhmaṇa) is a varna in Hinduism traditionally associated with priesthood, scholarship and teaching. In the classical four-varna scheme, Brahmins rank first, ahead of Kshatriyas (rulers and warriors), Vaishyas (merchants and farmers) and Shudras (labourers). Historically, Brahmins served as temple priests, royal advisers, astrologers, teachers and custodians of Vedic learning.

History

The concept develops in late Vedic texts such as the Brahmanas and Upanishads, and the varna hierarchy was codified in works like the Manusmriti. Over the centuries Brahmins formed numerous regional communities and subcastes across India and Nepal — among them Iyers and Iyengars in Tamil Nadu, Chitpavan and Deshastha in Maharashtra, and Bengali, Maithil and Saraswat communities — with distinct customs, cuisines and dialects. Under British rule many Brahmins entered colonial administration, education and the professions in large numbers.

Modern position

After Indian independence, caste-based reservation policies in education and government employment sought to redress the historical advantages of forward castes, and Brahmins today work in every profession, in India and in the global diaspora. The role of hereditary priesthood persists in temples and domestic rituals, while scholarly debates continue over the historical origins and social effects of varna.

In other usages

The term "Boston Brahmin" refers to the old elite families of New England, and "Brahmin" is also the name of a breed of cattle (Bos indicus) developed in the United States.
