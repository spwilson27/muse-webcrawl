Frank Serpico

Francesco Vincent Serpico (born April 14, 1936) is an American retired New York Police Department detective, best known for whistleblowing on police corruption in the late 1960s and early 1970s and testifying before the Knapp Commission. His story was told in Peter Maas's 1973 biography Serpico and the Sidney Lumet film of the same name starring Al Pacino.

Whistleblowing

Working in plainclothes in Brooklyn and the Bronx, Serpico refused to take part in the systematic payoffs collected from gamblers and drug dealers by fellow officers. Frustrated by internal channels, he — along with officer David Durk — took the allegations to The New York Times, whose 1970 front-page story prompted Mayor John Lindsay to appoint the Knapp Commission to investigate NYPD corruption.

Shooting and aftermath

On February 3, 1971, Serpico was shot in the face during a drug arrest in Brooklyn, in circumstances that raised questions about the conduct of the backup officers with him. He survived and testified before the Knapp Commission later that year. He retired from the NYPD in 1972 and received the department's Medal of Honor decades later, in 2022.
