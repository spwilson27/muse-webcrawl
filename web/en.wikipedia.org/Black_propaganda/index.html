Black propaganda

A forged leaflet typical of black propaganda operations.

Black propaganda is a form of propaganda intended to create the impression that it was created by those it is supposed to discredit. It is material that purports to come from a source on one side of a conflict but is actually produced by the opposing side, and it typically uses the enemy's own voice, idiom, and grievances to spread disinformation, demoralize, or provoke internal conflict.

Classification

Propaganda is often classified by source attribution: white propaganda comes from an openly identified source; grey propaganda has no identified source; and black propaganda falsely claims to originate from a friendly or enemy source other than its true author. Black propaganda relies heavily on deception and is considered most damaging if its true origin is exposed.

Historical examples

During the Second World War, the British Political Warfare Executive under Sefton Delmer ran German-language radio stations such as Gustav Siegfried Eins and Soldatensender Calais that posed as unofficial German stations while subtly undermining the Nazi regime. Nazi Germany, the Soviet Union, and other states ran similar clandestine broadcasting and forgery operations. In the Cold War and since, forged documents and false-flag publications have been attributed to various intelligence services. Black propaganda techniques have also been alleged in election campaigns and continue in modern online disinformation operations using fake personas and impersonated media outlets.
