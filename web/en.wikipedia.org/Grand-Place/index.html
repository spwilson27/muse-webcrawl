The Grand-Place (French) or Grote Markt (Dutch), meaning "Grand Square", is the central square of Brussels, Belgium. It is surrounded by opulent Baroque guildhalls of the city's former guilds, the Gothic Town Hall, and the neo-Gothic King's House (Maison du Roi), which houses the Brussels City Museum. The square is widely regarded as one of the most beautiful in Europe and was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1998.

History

A market developed on the site by the 12th century. In August 1695, during the Nine Years' War, French artillery bombarded Brussels and destroyed nearly the entire square. Only the Town Hall's tower and façade and some stone walls survived. The guilds rebuilt their halls in just a few years, in a lavish Baroque style rich in gilding, producing the remarkably harmonious ensemble visible today.

Events

The square hosts the Ommegang procession, a biennial flower carpet (tapis de fleurs) of colourful begonias covering most of the square, Christmas markets, and concerts. Victor Hugo, who lived briefly in a house on the square, praised it enthusiastically.
