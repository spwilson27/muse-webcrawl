Beurling's theorem

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Beurling's theorem is a fundamental result in harmonic analysis, proved by the Swedish mathematician Arne Beurling, characterising the closed subspaces of the Hardy space H² on the unit disc that are invariant under multiplication by the coordinate function z (the shift operator).

Statement

A non-zero closed subspace of H² is invariant under the shift if and only if it has the form θH² for some inner function θ — that is, a bounded analytic function on the disc whose boundary values have modulus one almost everywhere. The theorem was published in 1949 in Beurling's paper "On two problems concerning linear transformations in Hilbert space" in Acta Mathematica.

Significance

The result underlies the theory of model spaces and the Beurling–Lax–Halmos generalisation to vector-valued Hardy spaces, and it provides the functional-analytic foundation for the spectral theory of the unilateral shift and for much of operator theory and control theory.
