Rocket from the Tombs

OriginCleveland, Ohio, United States
GenresProtopunk, punk rock, garage rock
Years active1974–1975, 2003–present

Rocket from the Tombs (often abbreviated RFTT) is an American rock band originally active in Cleveland, Ohio, from mid-1974 to mid-1975. Although the band released no recordings during its original lifetime and played only a handful of shows, it is regarded as an important protopunk group, and its members went on to form the influential bands Pere Ubu and the Dead Boys.

History

The band was formed by journalist and musician David Thomas (who performed under the name Crocus Behemoth), guitarist Peter Laughner, bassist Craig Bell, guitarist Gene O'Connor (Cheetah Chrome), and drummer Johnny Madansky (Johnny Blitz). Taking its name partly from the underground newspaper scene and the horror-film atmosphere of late-night television, the band combined the raw energy of the Stooges and the Velvet Underground with original material written principally by Thomas and Laughner.

Rocket from the Tombs never released a record in its original incarnation, but songs written for the band became cornerstones of early punk: "Sonic Reducer" and "Ain't It Fun" were later recorded by the Dead Boys, while "Final Solution" and "Life Stinks" became staples of Pere Ubu's repertoire. After the group's split in 1975, Thomas and Laughner founded Pere Ubu, while O'Connor and Madansky moved to New York and formed the Dead Boys with Stiv Bators.

Posthumous releases and reunion

Rehearsal tapes and live recordings circulated among collectors for decades before the release of The Day the Earth Met the Rocket from the Tombs (2002), a compilation of demos and live tracks issued by Smog Veil Records. The band reformed in 2003 with Thomas, Chrome, and Bell joined by Steve Mehlman and former Television guitarist Richard Lloyd, touring and eventually recording new studio material, including Barfly (2011) and Black Record (2015).

Legacy

Critics including Greil Marcus have cited Rocket from the Tombs as one of the great "lost" bands of the 1970s, a bridge between the garage and proto-punk underground of Cleveland and the punk explosion that followed. The Gun Club's cover of "Life Stinks"-era material and Guns N' Roses' recording of "Ain't It Fun" on The Spaghetti Incident? (1993) further spread the band's songbook.
