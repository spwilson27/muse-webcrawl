Average

FieldStatistics
TypesArithmetic mean, median, mode, geometric mean, harmonic mean

In ordinary language, an average is a single number taken as representative of a list of numbers. Different concepts of average are used in different contexts. Often "average" refers to the arithmetic mean, calculated by summing the values and dividing by the number of values.

Types of average

The main types are the arithmetic mean, the median (the middle value in an ordered list), and the mode (the most frequent value). Other measures include the geometric mean, used for growth rates; the harmonic mean, used for rates and ratios; and generalized (power) means that unify these as special cases. In statistics, averages are examples of measures of central tendency, contrasted with measures of dispersion such as the standard deviation.

Properties and pitfalls

Each average summarizes a distribution differently: the mean uses all values but is sensitive to outliers, while the median is robust. Misleading averages can arise when the underlying distribution is skewed or when different subgroups are combined (Simpson's paradox). In maritime law, "average" historically also meant damage or loss at sea apportioned among interested parties, as in general average.
