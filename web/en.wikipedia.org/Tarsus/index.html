Tarsus

Tarsus (Turkish: Tarsus; Greek: Ταρσός) is a city in Mersin Province, in south-central Turkey, on the Tarsus River (the ancient Cydnus), about 20 km inland from the Mediterranean coast. One of the oldest continuously inhabited settlements in the world, with occupation going back to the Neolithic period, Tarsus was an important city of Cilicia and is best known in the West as the birthplace of Paul the Apostle (Saul of Tarsus).

History

Excavations at the mound of Gözlükule reveal settlement from Neolithic times through the Hittite, Assyrian, Persian, and Hellenistic eras. Under Alexander the Great and his successors Tarsus flourished; in Roman times it became the capital of the province of Cilicia and a renowned centre of learning, its philosophical schools rivalling those of Athens and Alexandria. Mark Antony and Cleopatra famously met at Tarsus in 41 BC. The city remained significant under Byzantine, Arab, Armenian, Crusader, and Ottoman rule, and its population today is in the hundreds of thousands within its district.

Sights

Historic sites include Cleopatra's Gate, the Roman road, the monumental Donuktaş temple remains, the Grand Mosque, and the well associated with Saint Paul, a place of Christian pilgrimage. Nearby are the Tarsus Waterfall on the river and the cave of the Seven Sleepers, venerated in both Christian and Islamic tradition.

Economy

Tarsus lies in the fertile Çukurova plain, a major agricultural region for cotton, citrus, and other crops, with associated textile and food-processing industries.
