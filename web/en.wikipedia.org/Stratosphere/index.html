Stratosphere

The stratosphere is the second layer of the atmosphere of Earth, located above the troposphere and below the mesosphere. It extends from the tropopause — about 10–18 km altitude depending on latitude — up to the stratopause at roughly 50 km. The stratosphere contains the ozone layer, which absorbs most of the Sun's ultraviolet radiation.

Temperature structure

Unlike the troposphere, where temperature decreases with height, the stratosphere warms with altitude because ozone absorbs ultraviolet sunlight. The top of the stratosphere reaches about 270 K (−3 °C), while its base can be as cold as 190 K (−83 °C) over the tropics. This temperature inversion makes the stratosphere very stable, with little convection or turbulence — commercial airliners often cruise in its lower reaches.

Ozone layer

Between roughly 15 and 35 km, ozone concentrations peak. Chlorofluorocarbons released by human activity caused depletion of this layer, notably the Antarctic "ozone hole"; the 1987 Montreal Protocol phased out the responsible chemicals, and recovery is projected over the 21st century.

Dynamics

Stratospheric circulation (the Brewer–Dobson circulation) transports air and trace gases globally. Sudden stratospheric warmings can disturb the polar vortex and influence winter weather at the surface. Volcanic eruptions can inject sulfate aerosols into the stratosphere, cooling the planet for years. See also troposphere, ozone layer, and mesosphere.
