Regulation of gene expression

Regulation of gene expression refers to the control of the amount and timing of the appearance of a gene's functional product — usually a protein, but sometimes a functional RNA. It allows cells to respond to their environment and to specialize during development, and it operates at many stages between DNA and protein.

Levels of regulation

Control can occur through chromatin structure and epigenetic modifications (such as DNA methylation and histone modification), transcription initiation by transcription factors acting on promoters and enhancers, RNA processing and export, mRNA stability, translation, and post-translational modification and degradation of proteins. In bacteria, operons such as the lac operon coordinate the expression of related genes.

Importance

Gene regulation underlies cellular differentiation, morphogenesis, and adaptation, and its disruption contributes to diseases including cancer. Techniques such as RNA sequencing and reporter genes allow researchers to measure and manipulate expression.
