Clifford Hugh Douglas

C. H. Douglas
Born: 20 January 1879, Stockport, England
Died: 29 September 1952
Occupation: Engineer
Known for: Social credit theory

Major Clifford Hugh Douglas (20 January 1879 – 29 September 1952) was a British engineer who pioneered the social credit economic theory.

Early life and career

Douglas studied at Cambridge University and worked as an engineer for companies including the British Westinghouse and in railway construction in India and Latin America. During the First World War he served in the Royal Flying Corps and worked on production problems at the Farnborough aircraft factory, where he began studying the relationship between factory costs and incomes.

Social credit

In works such as Economic Democracy (1920) and Social Credit (1924), Douglas argued that modern economies chronically generate prices faster than they distribute purchasing power — a gap expressed in his "A + B theorem." He proposed that governments close the gap through a national dividend paid to all citizens and a compensated (subsidized) retail price, financed by publicly issued credit.

Influence

Social credit movements formed in several Commonwealth countries. In Canada, the Social Credit Party governed Alberta from 1935 and British Columbia for much of the postwar era, though usually without implementing Douglas's full program. His ideas were controversial among economists, and some of his later writings attracted criticism for conspiratorial and antisemitic themes.
