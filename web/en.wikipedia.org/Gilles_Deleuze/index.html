Gilles Deleuze

Gilles Deleuze (18 January 1925 – 4 November 1995) was a French philosopher who wrote on philosophy, literature, film and art. His major works include Difference and Repetition (1968), The Logic of Sense (1969), and — with psychoanalyst Félix Guattari — Capitalism and Schizophrenia, comprising Anti-Oedipus (1972) and A Thousand Plateaus (1980), as well as the two-volume study of cinema, Cinema 1 (1983) and Cinema 2 (1985).

Deleuze's thought centres on concepts such as difference, multiplicity, the rhizome, deterritorialisation, desire as a productive force, and "schizoanalysis" as an alternative to psychoanalysis. He taught at the University of Paris VIII (Vincennes/Saint-Denis) from 1969 until his retirement in 1987, and his seminars drew an devoted following.

Life

Deleuze studied philosophy at the Sorbonne, taught in lycées, and then held posts at the CNRS and several universities. The upheavals of May 1968 brought him to the experimental University of Vincennes, where he remained despite chronic respiratory illness dating from tuberculosis contracted in his youth. He married Denise Paul "Fanny" Grandjouan in 1956 and had two children. In 1995, suffering worsening illness, he took his own life.

Work

Early in his career Deleuze wrote monographs on Hume, Nietzsche, Kant, Bergson and Spinoza that reinterpreted their thought in strikingly original ways. Difference and Repetition proposed an ontology in which difference precedes identity. The collaborations with Guattari attacked Freudian psychoanalysis and analysed capitalism's decoding and recoding of desire; A Thousand Plateaus developed the rhizome model of non-hierarchical networks and influenced fields from literary theory to geography and architecture. His cinema books proposed a philosophy of film based on the movement-image and time-image.
