St Paul's Cathedral

St Paul's Cathedral
Location: Ludgate Hill, London
Architect: Sir Christopher Wren
Built: 1675–1710
Style: English Baroque
Dome height: 111 m (365 ft)

St Paul's Cathedral is an Anglican cathedral in London, the seat of the Bishop of London and the mother church of the Diocese of London. It sits on Ludgate Hill, the highest point of the City of London, and its dome is among the most famous features of the London skyline.

History

A cathedral dedicated to Saint Paul has stood on the site since AD 604; the medieval building was destroyed in the Great Fire of London in 1666. Sir Christopher Wren designed the present cathedral in an English Baroque style, and it was built between 1675 and 1710. Wren is buried in its crypt beneath the epitaph Lector, si monumentum requiris, circumspice — "Reader, if you seek his monument, look around you."

The dome and interior

The dome rises 365 feet and is composed of three shells: an outer wooden, lead-covered dome, a hidden brick cone and a painted inner dome. The Whispering Gallery runs around its interior, and mosaics and monuments fill the interior, including memorials to the Duke of Wellington and Admiral Lord Nelson, who is buried in the crypt.

Major events

St Paul's has hosted the funerals of Nelson, Wellington and Winston Churchill, the wedding of Prince Charles and Lady Diana Spencer in 1981, and jubilee services for Queen Elizabeth II. Its survival during the Blitz, watched over by the St Paul's Watch, made it an enduring symbol of London's resilience.
