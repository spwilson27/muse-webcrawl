Cannoli

Cannoli (Italian: cannolo siciliano, "little tube") are Italian pastries consisting of tube-shaped shells of fried pastry dough, filled with a sweet, creamy filling containing ricotta — a staple of Sicilian cuisine. The singular is cannolo; in English, "cannoli" is used for both singular and plural by many speakers.

History

Cannoli are believed to have originated in the Palermo area of Sicily, where they were traditionally prepared for Carnevale season, possibly as a fertility symbol. Some accounts trace influences to the island's period of Arab rule, which introduced sugar cane and new confectionery techniques. The pastry spread worldwide with Sicilian emigration and became an icon of Italian-American bakeries.

Preparation

The shell is made from flour, sugar, egg yolk, and wine, rolled thin, wrapped around metal tubes, and deep-fried until crisp. The filling is typically sweetened sheep's-milk ricotta, sometimes flavoured with vanilla, candied citrus peel, or chocolate chips; ends may be garnished with chopped pistachios or candied fruit, and the finished pastry is dusted with powdered sugar. Because the shell softens quickly, cannoli are filled as close to serving as possible — a practice famously summarised in the line "leave the gun, take the cannoli" in the film The Godfather (1972).

Variations

Regional and diaspora variations include miniature cannolicchi, chocolate-dipped shells, and fillings based on mascarpone, custard, or chocolate cream, particularly in the United States.
