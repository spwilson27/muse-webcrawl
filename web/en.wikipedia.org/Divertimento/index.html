Divertimento

A divertimento (from the Italian divertire, "to amuse") is a musical composition, most often of the eighteenth century, in a light and entertaining style for a small chamber ensemble. Divertimenti typically consist of a number of short movements — often more than a symphony of the period — and were composed as background music for social occasions such as banquets and garden parties at aristocratic courts.

Characteristics

The divertimento was a flexible genre. Scoring ranges from string trio or quartet to mixed groups of strings and winds, sometimes a small orchestra. Movements commonly include quick opening and closing movements, minuets (often more than one), slow movements, and sets of variations. Related light multi-movement genres of the period include the serenade, cassation and notturno; the boundaries between them were fluid, and composers sometimes used the titles interchangeably.

History

The type flourished in Austria and southern Germany from about 1750 to 1780. Joseph Haydn wrote dozens of divertimenti, many of which stand close to his early string quartets and symphonies. The young Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart composed numerous divertimenti in Salzburg — most famously the three for strings, K. 136–138 — and the monumental Divertimento in E-flat major for string trio, K. 563 (1788), which at nearly fifty minutes far exceeds the genre's usual modest scale. Ludwig van Beethoven's Septet in E-flat major, Op. 20, follows the divertimento plan.

Later uses

After 1800 the term fell out of common use, but twentieth-century composers revived it for light, neoclassical chamber works. Examples include Béla Bartók's Divertimento for String Orchestra (1939), written for Paul Sacher's Basel Chamber Orchestra, and divertimenti by Igor Stravinsky and Benjamin Britten.
