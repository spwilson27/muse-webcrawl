Nikah

In Islamic law, nikah (Arabic: نِكَاح, romanized: nikāḥ) is a contract exclusively between a man and a woman. Both the groom and the bride are to give their consent to the marriage verbally and in writing. It is a formal, binding contract — considered an integral part of a religiously valid Islamic marriage — that outlines the rights and responsibilities of the groom and bride.

Requirements

A valid nikah typically requires an offer and acceptance (ījāb wa qabūl) in the presence of witnesses, the consent of the bride, a guardian (walī) for the bride in most schools of law, and the payment of a dower (mahr) from the groom to the bride. The contract may include additional conditions agreed by the parties, so long as they do not contradict Islamic law.

Ceremony

The nikah ceremony is often officiated by an imam or qualified officiant, may include a sermon (khutbah), and is followed by celebrations such as the walima banquet. Divorce procedures related to the nikah contract include talaq, khulʿ, and judicial annulment (faskh), which vary across schools of jurisprudence and national legal systems.
