Gorno-Badakhshan


The Pamir Mountains dominate Gorno-Badakhshan.

The Gorno-Badakhshan Autonomous Region (Tajik: Вилояти Мухтори Кӯҳистони Бадахшон, Viloyati Muxtori Ko'histoni Badaxshon; abbreviated GBAO) is an autonomous region in eastern Tajikistan, in the Pamir Mountains. Although it covers about 45% of the country's territory, it is home to only around 3% of its population — roughly 230,000 people. Its capital is Khorog.

Geography

The region is one of the most mountainous inhabited areas on Earth, dominated by the Pamir range — the "Roof of the World" — including Ismoil Somoni Peak (7,495 m), the highest point in the former Soviet Union. The Panj River forms its southern boundary with Afghanistan, and the region borders China to the east and Kyrgyzstan to the north. The high-altitude Pamir Highway (M41) crosses the region through the provincial capital to the Kulma Pass into China.

People and history

Most inhabitants are Pamiri peoples speaking Eastern Iranian languages such as Shughni, Rushani and Wakhi, and most adhere to Ismaili Shi'a Islam, following the Aga Khan, whose development network (AKDN) is a major provider of services in the region. Created as an autonomous oblast within the Soviet Union in 1925, Gorno-Badakhshan was a stronghold of opposition forces during the Tajik civil war of 1992–97. Tensions with the central government in Dushanbe have recurred, including clashes in Khorog in 2012 and protests and a security crackdown in 2021–22.
