Aerobatics

Aerobatics is the practice of flying maneuvers involving aircraft attitudes that are not used in conventional passenger-carrying flights. It includes loops, rolls, spins, hammerheads, and other precision figures, performed for training, recreation, entertainment at air shows, and competition.

Aerobatics
Maneuvers: Loops, rolls, spins, Immelmanns
Contexts: Air shows, competition, training
Governing body: FAI (CIVA)

History

Aerobatic maneuvers emerged in the early 20th century as pilots explored the capabilities of new aircraft; many figures were developed during World War I for combat. Between the wars, barnstormers popularized stunt flying, and aerobatics became a formal competitive sport.

Competition

Competitions are judged on the precision of sequences of standardized figures catalogued in the Aresti Catalog. Pilots fly in categories of increasing difficulty, up to Unlimited level at world championships. Military display teams such as the Red Arrows and the Blue Angels perform formation aerobatics.
