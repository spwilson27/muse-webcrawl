Spectral theorem

In mathematics, particularly linear algebra and functional analysis, a spectral theorem is a result about when a linear operator or matrix can be diagonalized (that is, represented as a diagonal matrix in some basis). This is extremely useful because computations involving a diagonalizable matrix can often be reduced to much simpler computations involving the corresponding diagonal matrix.

Finite-dimensional case

For a Hermitian (or self-adjoint) matrix A on a finite-dimensional complex inner product space, there exists an orthonormal basis consisting of eigenvectors of A. Equivalently, A can be written as A = UΛU*, where U is a unitary matrix whose columns are eigenvectors and Λ is a diagonal matrix of real eigenvalues. A similar statement holds for real symmetric matrices with orthogonal matrices.

Normal operators

The spectral theorem extends to normal operators — those satisfying TT* = T*T — on finite-dimensional inner product spaces: a normal operator can be unitarily diagonalized.

Infinite dimensions

In functional analysis, spectral theorems describe self-adjoint (possibly unbounded) operators on Hilbert spaces in terms of projection-valued measures, and underlie much of the mathematical formulation of quantum mechanics, where observables are self-adjoint operators.
