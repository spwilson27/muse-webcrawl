Molecular mechanics


Method: Classical (Newtonian) mechanics
Key concept: Force field
Force fields: AMBER, CHARMM, GROMOS, OPLS

Molecular mechanics uses classical mechanics to model molecular systems. The potential energy of a system of atoms is calculated from a force field: a set of functions and parameters describing bonded interactions (bond stretching, angle bending, torsional rotations) and non-bonded interactions (van der Waals forces and electrostatics between atoms).

Approach

In molecular mechanics, atoms are treated as point masses connected by springs, following the Born–Oppenheimer approximation that electrons need not be modelled explicitly. The total energy is a sum of terms, each modelled with simple functions such as harmonic potentials for bonds and angles and Lennard-Jones and Coulomb potentials for non-bonded pairs. Parameters are fitted to experimental data and to high-level quantum mechanical calculations.

Applications

Because it avoids electronic structure calculations, molecular mechanics can handle very large systems — tens of thousands to millions of atoms — making it the basis of molecular dynamics simulations of proteins, nucleic acids, membranes, and materials. It is used for energy minimization (finding stable conformations), conformational searching, and as a component of hybrid QM/MM methods. Its main limitations are that bonds cannot break or form, and accuracy depends on the quality and transferability of the force field.
