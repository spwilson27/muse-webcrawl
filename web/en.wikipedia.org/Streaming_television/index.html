Streaming television

Streaming television is the digital distribution of television content, such as television series and films, as streaming media via the Internet. It contrasts with terrestrial, cable and satellite delivery, and allows viewers to watch programmes on demand, often on smart TVs, computers, tablets and phones, rather than according to a broadcast schedule.

History

Early streaming services emerged in the mid-2000s. Netflix, originally a DVD rental company, launched streaming in 2007 and began producing original programming in 2013 with House of Cards. Hulu, Amazon Prime Video, and later entrants including Disney+, HBO Max (later Max), Apple TV+ and Paramount+ transformed the industry through the 2010s and early 2020s, driving "cord-cutting" from traditional pay television.

Business models

  • SVOD — subscription video on demand (e.g. Netflix, Disney+).
  • AVOD — advertising-supported tiers and free services such as Pluto TV and Tubi.
  • TVOD — transactional rental or purchase of individual titles (e.g. Apple TV Store, Amazon).
  • vMVPDs — live TV streaming bundles such as YouTube TV and Hulu + Live TV that replicate cable channel line-ups over the Internet.

Technology

Streaming television is delivered using adaptive bitrate protocols such as HLS and MPEG-DASH, content delivery networks, and codecs including H.264, HEVC and AV1. Digital rights management systems protect licensed content, and recommendation algorithms play a major role in content discovery.

Impact

The shift to streaming has reshaped production, windows and viewing habits — including binge-watching, global simultaneous releases and data-driven commissioning — while prompting consolidation and intense competition known as the "streaming wars".
