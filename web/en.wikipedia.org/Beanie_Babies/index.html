Beanie Babies are a line of stuffed toys created by Ty Warner and manufactured by Ty Inc. First released in 1993, the small plush animals were filled with plastic pellets ("beans") rather than conventional stuffing, giving them a flexible, under-stuffed feel. In the late 1990s they became the centre of a major collecting craze, widely cited as the world's first Internet sensation and a prominent example of a speculative bubble.

The craze

Ty's strategy of deliberately limiting production and "retiring" designs created scarcity. Each toy carried a heart-shaped swing tag with a poem and birthday, and collectors treated the tags' condition as crucial to value. At the peak of the fad around 1998–1999, Beanie Babies were resold on eBay at many times their roughly $5 retail price; eBay sales of the toys reportedly accounted for a significant share of the site's early business, and Ty's sales reached around $1.4 billion in 1998. Some buyers purchased them as financial investments, expecting to fund retirements and college tuition.

Collapse and legacy

The bubble burst in 1999–2000 as the market flooded and prices for most designs fell back toward retail value. Ty announced the line's retirement at the end of 1999 before reversing the decision after a public vote. Beanie Babies continue to be produced, and the phenomenon remains a textbook case in discussions of collectibles speculation and fad economics. See also Tulip mania.
