Sihanouk Trail

Sihanouk Trail
LocationCambodia, from Sihanoukville port inland
Periodc. 1965–1970
Named forPrince Norodom Sihanouk

The Sihanouk Trail was a logistical network running through Cambodia used by North Vietnam during the Vietnam War to supply Viet Cong and People's Army forces in South Vietnam. It complemented the Ho Chi Minh Trail through Laos.

Operation

Supplies shipped from communist-bloc countries arrived at the port of Sihanoukville (Kampong Som), then were trucked — often by Cambodian military intermediaries paid for the service — to base areas near the South Vietnamese border, the so-called Fishhook and Parrot's Beak regions.

American Response

The United States secretly bombed Cambodian sanctuaries under Operation Menu (1969–70), and in April–June 1970 US and ARVN forces launched the Cambodian Campaign to destroy the logistics bases. After Lon Nol deposed Sihanouk in March 1970 and closed the port, the route ceased, and North Vietnam expanded the Ho Chi Minh Trail and seized more Cambodian territory.
