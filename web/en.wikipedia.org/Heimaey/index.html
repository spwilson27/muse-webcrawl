Heimaey

Heimaey is the largest island in the Westman Islands (Vestmannaeyjar) archipelago off the south coast of Iceland, and the only inhabited one. Covering about 13.4 square kilometres, it is home to roughly 4,400 people in the town of Vestmannaeyja, one of Iceland's most important fishing ports.

1973 eruption

Heimaey is famous for the eruption of the volcano Eldfell, which began without warning on 23 January 1973. The island's entire population was evacuated overnight by the local fishing fleet; over the following months, lava flows destroyed hundreds of homes and threatened to close the harbour — the town's economic lifeline. In a celebrated operation, seawater was pumped onto the advancing lava to cool and slow it, helping to save the harbour entrance. When the eruption ended that July, the island had grown by about 2.2 square kilometres. One farmhouse buried by ash has been excavated and is preserved as a museum, earning the site the nickname "Pompeii of the North".

Wildlife and economy

The island hosts one of the largest colonies of Atlantic puffins in the world, with millions of birds nesting in the sea cliffs each summer. Fishing and fish processing remain the economic mainstay, supplemented by tourism drawn by the eruption sites, puffin watching, and the island's golf course and annual music festival.
