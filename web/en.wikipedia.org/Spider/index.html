Spider

Kingdom: Animalia
Phylum: Arthropoda
Class: Arachnida
Order: Araneae

Spiders (order Araneae) are air-breathing arthropods that have eight limbs, chelicerae with fangs generally able to inject venom, and spinnerets that extrude silk. They are the largest order of arachnids and rank seventh in total species diversity among all orders of organisms, with over 50,000 described species.

Anatomy

Unlike insects, spiders have only two main body parts: a fused cephalothorax (prosoma) and an abdomen (opisthosoma), joined by a narrow pedicel. They have eight walking legs and no antennae or wings. Most have eight eyes, though arrangement and acuity vary widely; jumping spiders have excellent vision. Spiders digest food externally, liquefying prey before ingestion.

Silk and webs

Spider silk is a protein fibre produced by spinnerets at the rear of the abdomen. Different silk glands produce silks with different properties, used for webs, draglines, egg sacs, and wrapping prey. Web-building species construct orb webs, sheet webs, funnel webs, or cobwebs, while many spiders are active hunters that use no web at all.

Venom and humans

Nearly all spiders are venomous, but only a small number — including Sydney funnel-web spiders, widow spiders, and recluse spiders — are medically significant to humans. Spiders are important predators of insects and are generally beneficial in agriculture and homes. Fear of spiders, arachnophobia, is one of the most common specific phobias.
