The Mountain Railways of India are three mountain railways built in the hills of India during the British Raj that are collectively designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site for their outstanding engineering solutions to establishing rail links across difficult mountain terrain.

The lines

The Darjeeling Himalayan Railway (1881), a 2 ft narrow-gauge line in West Bengal, climbs from New Jalpaiguri to Darjeeling using loops and zig-zags. The Nilgiri Mountain Railway (completed 1908) in Tamil Nadu is India's only rack railway, ascending from Mettupalayam to Ooty. The Kalka–Shimla Railway (1903) in Himachal Pradesh climbs 1,420 metres through 102 tunnels and 864 bridges.

Heritage

All three lines remain in regular operation and retain much of their historic character, drawing tourists from around the world. The Matheran Hill Railway and Kangra Valley Railway are on UNESCO's tentative list for possible extension of the site.
