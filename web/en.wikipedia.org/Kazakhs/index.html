Kazakhs

Kazakhs (also spelled Qazaqs) are a Turkic ethnic group and the largest ethnic group in Kazakhstan, where they form about 70 percent of the population. Significant Kazakh populations also live in China, Mongolia, Russia, Uzbekistan and Kyrgyzstan.

The Kazakhs emerged in the 15th century from the Kazakh Khanate, formed by nomadic Turkic and Mongolic tribes of the Eurasian steppe. Traditionally nomadic herders organized into three zhuz (hordes) — Senior, Middle and Junior — they speak Kazakh, a Kipchak Turkic language, and are predominantly Sunni Muslims.

History

After the Kazakh Khanate was gradually absorbed into the Russian Empire in the 18th and 19th centuries, Kazakhs suffered catastrophic famine in the early Soviet period (1930–1933). Kazakhstan became independent in 1991, and ethnic Kazakhs have since returned from abroad under repatriation programs.

Culture

Traditional culture centers on horsemanship, the dombra lute, eagle hunting, felt yurts (ui), and dishes such as beshbarmak.
