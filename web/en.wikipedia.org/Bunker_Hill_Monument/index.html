The Bunker Hill Monument is a granite obelisk, 221 feet (67 metres) tall, erected to commemorate the Battle of Bunker Hill of June 17, 1775, one of the first major battles of the American Revolutionary War. It stands on Breed's Hill in Charlestown, Boston, where most of the fighting actually took place, and is one of the earliest American war memorials.

Construction

The Bunker Hill Monument Association was formed in 1823 to preserve the battlefield and build a memorial. The cornerstone was laid on the battle's 50th anniversary in 1825 in a ceremony attended by the Marquis de Lafayette and addressed by Daniel Webster. Construction, to a design by Solomon Willard using granite from Quincy — hauled on one of the first railways in the United States — proceeded slowly as funds ran short; money was raised in part by a great ladies' fair in 1840, and the monument was completed and dedicated in 1843, with Webster again speaking.

The site today

Visitors can climb 294 steps to the top for views over Boston. A statue of Colonel William Prescott, associated with the (possibly apocryphal) order "Don't fire until you see the whites of their eyes", stands at the base. The monument is part of Boston National Historical Park and the northern terminus of the city's Freedom Trail.
