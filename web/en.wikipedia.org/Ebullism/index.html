Ebullism

Ebullism
Specialty: Aerospace medicine
Cause: Exposure to near-vacuum
Threshold: Armstrong limit (~19 km altitude)

Ebullism is the formation of water vapour bubbles in the tissues and fluids of the body when the ambient pressure falls below the vapour pressure of water at body temperature (about 6.3 kilopascals, or 47 mmHg). It occurs on exposure to the near-vacuum of high altitude or space above the Armstrong limit.

Effects

Above the Armstrong limit — roughly 18 to 19 kilometres — water in exposed soft tissues vaporises, causing the body to swell, sometimes dramatically, though elastic tissues and the containment of the skin prevent rupture. Saliva boils on the tongue. Unconsciousness follows within seconds from hypoxia rather than from ebullism itself, and experimental exposures in animals and rare accidental human exposures (such as a 1965 vacuum-chamber accident at NASA) suggest that survival is possible if recompression occurs within about 90 seconds.

Prevention

Full-pressure suits and pressurised cabins protect aviators and astronauts. Ebullism is distinct from decompression sickness, which involves bubbles of dissolved gases such as nitrogen rather than water vapour.
