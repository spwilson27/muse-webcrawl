Esports

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Esports (also written e-sports or electronic sports) is a form of competition using video games, organized as multiplayer tournaments between professional players, individually or as teams. Major competitions fill arenas and attract online audiences in the hundreds of millions, with the largest events rivaling traditional sports in viewership.

History

Competitive gaming traces to arcade and early computer contests of the 1970s and 1980s, such as the 1980 Space Invaders Championship. The growth of PC gaming and the internet in the 1990s produced organized leagues, and South Korea — with its PC bang culture and televised StarCraft leagues — became the first esports heartland. From the 2010s, streaming platforms such as Twitch, franchised leagues, and sponsorship from major brands drove rapid global growth; the industry now generates revenues in the billions of dollars annually.

Games and genres

Leading esports titles span several genres:

  • MOBA: League of Legends and Dota 2 — the latter's tournament, The International, has offered prize pools exceeding $40 million.
  • First-person shooters: Counter-Strike 2 and Valorant.
  • Real-time strategy: StarCraft II.
  • Sports simulations and fighting games: EA FC, Rocket League, Street Fighter, and Super Smash Bros.

Industry and recognition

Professional players typically sign with organizations, train full-time, and retire young due to the demands on reaction time. Esports was a medal event at the 2022 Asian Games, and the International Olympic Committee has moved toward Olympic-branded esports events. Debates continue over whether esports constitutes a "sport", over governance (publishers control their games, unlike traditional sports federations), and over issues including match-fixing, doping, and player welfare.
