Comfort food

Comfort food is food that provides a nostalgic or sentimental value to someone, and may be characterised by its high caloric nature, high carbohydrate level, or simple preparation. The nostalgia may be specific to an individual, or it may apply to a specific culture. Comfort foods are often foods that people associate with childhood, home cooking, or positive memories, and they are frequently consumed in response to stress or low mood.

Definition and studies

The term "comfort food" has been traced back at least to 1966, when the Palm Beach Post used it in a story: "Adults, when under severe emotional stress, turn to what could be called 'comfort food'—food associated with the security of childhood, like mother's poached egg or famous chicken soup." Psychological studies suggest that comfort food consumption can be triggered by negative affect, and that preferences often differ by gender and age: younger people and men tend to prefer savoury, meal-related foods such as steak and casseroles, while women and older people more often choose snack foods such as chocolate and ice cream.

Regional examples

American comfort foods include macaroni and cheese, fried chicken, mashed potatoes, meatloaf, and apple pie. In Britain, examples include fish and chips, roast dinners, and bread and butter pudding. Japanese comfort foods include ramen, miso soup, and onigiri; in Italy, pasta dishes and risotto are often cited; in Poland, pierogi; in India, khichdi and various dals. Many cultures have an equivalent of chicken soup as a restorative dish.
