God in Islam

Key chapterAl-Ikhlas (Qur'an 112)

In Islam, God (Arabic: الله, Allāh) is the absolute, indivisible, and incomparable creator and sustainer of the universe. The central doctrine is tawhid — the absolute oneness of God. Islam teaches that God is unique, eternal, omnipotent and omniscient, has no partners, no equals, and neither begets nor is begotten, as expressed in chapter 112 of the Qur'an, Al-Ikhlas: "Say: He is Allah, the One; Allah, the Eternal Refuge. He neither begets nor is born, nor is there to Him any equivalent."

Names and attributes

Tradition speaks of the 99 names of God (al-asma al-husna), including Ar-Rahman (the Most Merciful), Ar-Rahim (the Most Compassionate), Al-Malik (the King) and Al-Khaliq (the Creator). The declaration of faith, the shahada — "There is no god but God, and Muhammad is the Messenger of God" — is the first of the Five Pillars of Islam.

Aniconism

Islamic tradition forbids depicting God in visual form; God is considered beyond representation and likeness. Religious art therefore developed calligraphy — above all the written name of Allah and Qur'anic verses — and geometric and arabesque ornamentation, especially in mosques. Associating partners with God (shirk) is considered the gravest sin in Islam.

Relation to other Abrahamic faiths

Muslims hold that Allah is the same God worshipped in Judaism and Christianity: the God of Abraham, Moses and Jesus, whom Islam regards as prophets. The word Allah is also used by Arabic-speaking Christians and Jews for God.
