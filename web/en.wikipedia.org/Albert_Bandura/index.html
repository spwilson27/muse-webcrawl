Albert Bandura

Albert Bandura (4 December 1925 – 26 July 2021) was a Canadian-American psychologist and professor at Stanford University, widely regarded as one of the most influential psychologists of the 20th century. He originated social learning theory (later social cognitive theory), the concept of self-efficacy, and the famous Bobo doll experiment on observational learning of aggression.

Early life and education

Bandura was born in Mundare, Alberta, Canada, the son of immigrants from Ukraine and Poland. He earned his bachelor's degree at the University of British Columbia in 1949 and his PhD in clinical psychology from the University of Iowa in 1952. He joined Stanford University in 1953 and remained there for his entire career.

Research

In the Bobo doll experiments (1961–63), children who watched an adult model behave aggressively toward an inflatable doll imitated the aggression, demonstrating learning through observation alone. Bandura's Social Foundations of Thought and Action (1986) set out social cognitive theory, emphasising reciprocal determinism between behaviour, personal factors and the environment, and the role of self-efficacy — belief in one's capacity to succeed — in motivation and achievement.

Honours

Bandura served as president of the American Psychological Association in 1974, was elected to the National Academy of Sciences, and received the National Medal of Science in 2016. He died in Stanford, California, in 2021.
