Lunar rover

A lunar rover or Moon rover is a space exploration vehicle designed to move across the surface of the Moon. Rovers may be robotic and remotely controlled, or crewed vehicles carrying astronauts.

Robotic rovers

The Soviet Union landed the first lunar rover, Lunokhod 1, in November 1970 aboard Luna 17; it operated for nearly a year and travelled about 10.5 km. Lunokhod 2 followed in 1973, covering some 39–42 km. China's Yutu rover landed with Chang'e 3 in December 2013, and Yutu-2, carried by Chang'e 4, made the first soft landing on the far side of the Moon in January 2019 and operated for years, the longest-lived lunar rover. India's Pragyan rover operated during the Chandrayaan-3 mission in 2023.

Crewed rover: the Lunar Roving Vehicle

NASA's Apollo Lunar Roving Vehicle (LRV), the "Moon buggy", was a battery-powered four-wheeled vehicle driven by astronauts on Apollo 15, 16 and 17 (1971–72). Built by Boeing, it folded into a quadrant of the Lunar Module, carried two astronauts with their life-support backpacks and samples at speeds up to about 13 km/h (with a record near 18 km/h), and greatly extended the explorable radius of the last three Apollo landings. The three LRVs remain on the Moon.
