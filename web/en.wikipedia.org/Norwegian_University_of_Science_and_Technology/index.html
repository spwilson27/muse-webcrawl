The Norwegian University of Science and Technology (Norwegian: Norges teknisk-naturvitenskapelige universitet, NTNU) is a public research university in Norway, the country's largest, with its main campus in Trondheim and additional campuses in Gjøvik and Ålesund.

History

NTNU was created in 1996 by the merger of the Norwegian Institute of Technology (NTH, founded 1910) with the University of Trondheim and other institutions. It has roots in the Royal Norwegian Society of Sciences and Letters, founded in 1760. In 2016 NTNU merged with the university colleges of Sør-Trøndelag, Gjøvik and Ålesund.

Academics

The university has about 9,000 employees and more than 40,000 students across nine faculties, with strengths in engineering, natural sciences, medicine and technology. It is affiliated with the Kavli Institute for Systems Neuroscience, home to Nobel laureates May-Britt Moser and Edvard Moser, who received the 2014 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for discovering grid cells in the brain.
