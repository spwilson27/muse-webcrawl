Foreign relations of the European Union

The EU conducts foreign policy through the European External Action Service and its delegations worldwide.

The foreign relations of the European Union encompass the EU's external action: its Common Foreign and Security Policy (CFSP), trade policy, development cooperation, enlargement, and relations with third countries and international organizations. Although its member states retain their own foreign policies and armed forces, the EU acts as a single entity in trade — its exclusive competence — and seeks common positions in diplomacy through the High Representative of the Union for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy and the European External Action Service (EEAS), established in 2011 under the Treaty of Lisbon.

Institutions

The European Council, composed of heads of state or government, sets strategic direction; the Foreign Affairs Council of ministers takes CFSP decisions, usually by unanimity. The High Representative chairs the Foreign Affairs Council, serves as a Vice-President of the European Commission, and heads the EEAS, which operates more than 140 EU delegations around the world functioning much like embassies. The EU has legal personality and concludes international agreements, and it holds enhanced observer status at the United Nations.

Policies and relationships

The EU is the world's largest trading bloc and aid donor; its common commercial policy is negotiated by the European Commission, producing agreements such as CETA with Canada and the EU–Japan Economic Partnership Agreement. Enlargement has been called its most successful foreign policy tool: after the accessions of 2004–2013, candidate countries in the Western Balkans, Ukraine, Moldova, and others are at various stages of the process, given new impetus by Russia's invasion of Ukraine in 2022. The European Neighbourhood Policy covers eastern and southern neighbours.

The Common Security and Defence Policy (CSDP) enables EU military and civilian missions — over forty have been launched since 2003, including anti-piracy operations off Somalia (EU NAVFOR Atalanta) and training missions in Africa — while most European defence remains with NATO. Relations with the United States are the EU's closest partnership; relations with China are framed as partnership, competition, and systemic rivalry simultaneously; and the Union has imposed extensive sanctions on Russia since 2014 and especially 2022.

Challenges

Analysts debate the EU's capacity to act as a geopolitical power: CFSP unanimity, divergent member-state interests, dependence on US security guarantees, and energy vulnerabilities have exposed limits, prompting calls for "strategic autonomy" and qualified majority voting in foreign policy.
