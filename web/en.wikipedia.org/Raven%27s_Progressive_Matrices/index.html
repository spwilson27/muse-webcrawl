Raven's Progressive Matrices


Placeholder image: Raven's Progressive Matrices

Raven's Progressive Matrices is a nonverbal test of abstract reasoning, developed by John C. Raven in 1936. It is widely used as a measure of fluid intelligence and of the general intelligence factor (g).

Format

Each item presents a matrix of geometric patterns with one piece missing; the test-taker selects the missing piece from several options. The Standard Progressive Matrices contain 60 items in five sets of increasing difficulty. Variants include the Coloured Progressive Matrices for children and the Advanced Progressive Matrices for high-ability adults.

Use and findings

Because the test minimises language and cultural knowledge, it has been used across cultures and in studies of the Flynn effect — scores on Raven's have shown some of the largest generational gains. It correlates strongly with other IQ tests and with educational outcomes.
