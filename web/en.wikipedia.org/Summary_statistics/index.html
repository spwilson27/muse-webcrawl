Summary statistics

In descriptive statistics, summary statistics are used to summarise a set of observations in order to communicate the largest amount of information as simply as possible. Statisticians commonly try to describe a set of data, or sample, in terms of its size, centre, and spread.

Measures of location

The centre of a dataset is usually described by the mean (arithmetic average), the median (middle value), and the mode (most frequent value). For roughly symmetric distributions the mean is standard; for skewed data the median is often preferred. Other location measures include the geometric and harmonic means, trimmed means, and the midrange.

Measures of spread

Dispersion is commonly summarised by the range, the interquartile range (IQR), the variance and its square root, the standard deviation. Shape is described by skewness and kurtosis. A popular compact summary is the five-number summary — minimum, first quartile, median, third quartile, and maximum — drawn as a box plot. The standard error of the mean and confidence intervals extend summary statistics into estimation.
