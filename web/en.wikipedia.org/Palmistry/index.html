Palmistry

Also known asChiromancy, palm reading
TypeDivination (pseudoscience)
OriginsAncient India, China, and the Mediterranean

Palmistry, also known as palm reading, chiromancy, or chirology, is the practice of evaluating a person's character or future by studying the palm of their hand. Practitioners interpret the shape of the hand, the fingers, and the lines of the palm — principally the heart line, head line, and life line — as well as mounts (fleshy pads) associated with planets. Palmistry is regarded by the scientific community as a pseudoscience, as there is no empirical evidence that palm features predict personality or future events.

History

The practice has roots in Indian astrology and appeared in ancient China and in the Mediterranean world; palmistry treatises circulated in medieval Europe despite periodic church prohibitions, and the art enjoyed a revival in the 19th century through figures such as Casimir d'Arpentigny and the Irish palmist Cheiro.

Technique

Readers usually examine the dominant hand, said to show developed traits, against the non-dominant hand, said to show potential. Hands are classified by shape into elemental types (earth, air, fire, water), and individual lines, marks, and finger proportions are given specific meanings.
