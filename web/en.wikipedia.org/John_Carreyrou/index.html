John Carreyrou

John Carreyrou is a French-American investigative journalist. He worked for The Wall Street Journal for 20 years, based in Brussels, Paris, and New York, and later joined The New York Times as an investigative reporter. He is best known for exposing the fraud at the blood-testing company Theranos.

Theranos investigation

Beginning in October 2015, drawing on whistleblowers including Tyler Shultz and Erika Cheung and on regulatory inspections, Carreyrou published a series of articles questioning the accuracy of Theranos's finger-stick blood tests and the company's claims about its technology. Despite legal threats from Theranos and its founder Elizabeth Holmes, the reporting led to regulatory sanctions, the company's dissolution in 2018, and criminal convictions of Holmes and Ramesh "Sunny" Balwani. Carreyrou expanded the investigation into the bestselling book Bad Blood: Secrets and Lies in a Silicon Valley Startup (2018) and later hosted the podcast Bad Blood: The Final Chapter.

Awards

Carreyrou shared Pulitzer Prizes in 2003 (explanatory reporting on corporate scandals) and 2015 (investigative reporting on Medicare), and received awards including the George Polk Award for the Theranos series.
