Chinese noodles
Chinese: 麵 / 面 (miàn)
Origin: China, by the Han dynasty or earlier
Main types: Wheat, rice, starch noodles

Chinese noodles (miàn) are a central part of Chinese cuisine, made from unleavened dough—most commonly wheat, rice or starch—and cooked in soup, stir-fried, or served cold with sauce. Noodles symbolise longevity and are served at birthdays and celebrations.

History

In 2005 a bowl of millet noodles about 4,000 years old was reported from the Lajia site in Qinghai, among the earliest known noodles. Written references and wheat noodles became common by the Han dynasty, and noodle-making techniques later spread across Asia.

Varieties

Regional styles include hand-pulled Lanzhou lamian, Shanxi knife-cut noodles (daoxiao mian), Sichuan dan dan noodles, Cantonese wonton noodles with thin egg noodles, and rice noodles such as Shahe fen. Starch noodles include mung-bean cellophane noodles.
