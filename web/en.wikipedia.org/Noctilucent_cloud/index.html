Noctilucent cloud

Noctilucent clouds, or night shining clouds, are tenuous cloud-like phenomena in the upper atmosphere of Earth. Formed of ice crystals at altitudes of about 76 to 85 km (the mesosphere), they are the highest clouds in the atmosphere and are visible in deep twilight, when the Sun is below the horizon but still illuminates them.

Observation and study

They are most often seen during summer months at latitudes between roughly 50° and 70° north and south. First reported in 1885, two years after the eruption of Krakatoa, noctilucent clouds have become somewhat brighter and more frequent, a change studied as a possible indicator of changes in the upper atmosphere, including rising methane concentrations. NASA's AIM satellite, launched in 2007, was dedicated to their study as "polar mesospheric clouds".
