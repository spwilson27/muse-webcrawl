Kearns–Sayre syndrome

Kearns–Sayre syndrome
SpecialtyMedical genetics, ophthalmology
SymptomsProgressive external ophthalmoplegia, pigmentary retinopathy, cardiac conduction defects
CauseLarge deletions of mitochondrial DNA

Kearns–Sayre syndrome (KSS) is a rare mitochondrial neuromuscular disorder first described by Thomas Kearns and George Sayre in 1958. It is characterised by onset before the age of 20, progressive external ophthalmoplegia (paralysis of the eye muscles), and pigmentary degeneration of the retina, together with at least one of several additional features such as cardiac conduction block, elevated cerebrospinal fluid protein or cerebellar ataxia.

Cause

In most cases KSS results from a single large deletion of mitochondrial DNA — most commonly a 4.9-kilobase "common deletion" — arising sporadically rather than being inherited.

Management

There is no cure; treatment is supportive and directed at complications, including pacemaker implantation for heart block, hearing aids or cochlear implants, and management of endocrine problems such as diabetes.
