LTE (telecommunication)

LTE
Long Term Evolution
Developed by: 3GPP
Introduced: 2009
Successor: 5G NR

In telecommunications, LTE (Long Term Evolution) is a standard for wireless broadband communication for mobile devices and data terminals, based on the GSM/EDGE and UMTS/HSPA standards. It improves on those standards with a new radio interface and core network improvements. The standard is developed by the 3GPP (Third Generation Partnership Project).

History

LTE was first proposed in 2004 by NTT DoCoMo of Japan and was adopted as an international standard in 2007. The first publicly available LTE service was launched by TeliaSonera in Stockholm and Oslo on 14 December 2009. Verizon Wireless launched the first large-scale LTE network in the United States in December 2010.

Technology

LTE uses orthogonal frequency-division multiple access (OFDMA) for the downlink and single-carrier FDMA for the uplink, together with multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) antenna technology. LTE operates an all-IP core network called the Evolved Packet Core (EPC). Peak download rates for LTE-Advanced reach 1 Gbit/s under ideal conditions.

Voice

Because LTE is a packet-switched-only system, voice calls were initially handled by falling back to circuit-switched networks (CSFB). Voice over LTE (VoLTE) later provided native IP-based voice using the IP Multimedia Subsystem (IMS).

Deployment

LTE became the dominant fourth-generation (4G) mobile technology worldwide, and its successor is 5G NR. See also UMTS and GSM.
