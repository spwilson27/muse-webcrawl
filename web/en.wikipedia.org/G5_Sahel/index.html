The G5 Sahel (French: G5 du Sahel or G5S) was an institutional framework for coordination of regional cooperation in development policies and security matters in west Africa. It was formed on 16 February 2014 in Nouakchott, Mauritania, by Burkina Faso, Chad, Mali, Mauritania and Niger — five states of the Sahel, the semi-arid belt south of the Sahara facing interlocking crises of insurgency, poverty and climate stress.

Joint force

In 2017 the G5 launched a joint military force (Force conjointe du G5 Sahel, FC-G5S) of about 5,000 troops intended to fight jihadist groups linked to al-Qaeda and the Islamic State, secure borders and combat trafficking. Headquartered initially in Sévaré, Mali, the force operated alongside the French Operation Barkhane and the UN mission MINUSMA, but was chronically underfunded and hampered by weak logistics and human-rights concerns.

Collapse

Military coups in Mali (2020, 2021), Burkina Faso (2022) and Niger (2023) fractured the bloc. Mali withdrew from the G5 in May 2022; Burkina Faso and Niger announced their withdrawal in December 2023, and the three juntas formed the rival Alliance of Sahel States. With Chad and Mauritania the only remaining members, the G5 Sahel effectively ceased to function, and the three withdrawing states proceeded to leave ECOWAS as well.
