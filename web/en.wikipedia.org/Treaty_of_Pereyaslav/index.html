Treaty of Pereyaslav

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Treaty of Pereyaslav (also known as the Pereyaslav Agreement or Pereyaslav Council) was concluded on 18 January 1654 in the town of Pereyaslav (now in Ukraine) between the Cossack Hetmanate, led by Hetman Bohdan Khmelnytsky, and the Tsardom of Russia under Tsar Alexis I. It was negotiated during the Khmelnytsky Uprising (1648–1657) against the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, in which the Cossacks sought a powerful ally.

Terms and interpretation

The Cossack leadership swore allegiance to the Tsar, and Russian envoys promised military support and recognition of the Hetmanate's internal autonomy, including its own administration, courts, and an army register of 60,000 Cossacks. The precise legal nature of the agreement — military alliance, protectorate, personal union, or incorporation — has been debated ever since, as the original documentation is fragmentary and the parties' understandings diverged.

Consequences

The agreement drew Russia into war with Poland (1654–1667) and led, by the Truce of Andrusovo (1667), to the division of Ukraine along the Dnieper. Russian and later Soviet historiography celebrated Pereyaslav as the "reunification of Ukraine with Russia", a reading that in 1954 framed the transfer of Crimea to the Ukrainian SSR; Ukrainian national historiography has often stressed the treaty's limited, conditional character and its subsequent erosion.
