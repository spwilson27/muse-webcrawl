Wedding reception

A wedding reception is a party usually held after the completion of a marriage ceremony as hospitality for those who have attended the wedding. The name comes from the custom of the couple receiving society, in the form of family and friends, in the form of a receiving line. A typical reception includes food and drink, speeches and toasts, music and dancing, and rituals such as the cutting of the wedding cake and the throwing of the bride's bouquet.

Customs

Receptions are commonly held in hotel ballrooms, banquet halls, community halls, gardens or the homes of the couple's family. The event usually begins with a cocktail hour while the couple takes photographs, followed by the couple's grand entrance, dinner, toasts by the best man and maid of honour, the first dance, parent dances, the cake cutting, and open dancing. In some traditions the groom removes and tosses the bride's garter to unmarried male guests.

Around the world

In Chinese weddings, a banquet of many courses is hosted by the groom's family, and guests give red envelopes containing money. Japanese receptions (hirōen) feature formal speeches and costume changes by the bride. In Jewish weddings, guests dance the hora, lifting the couple on chairs. Nigerian and Ghanaian celebrations often feature elaborate attire and the spraying of money on the couple while dancing.
