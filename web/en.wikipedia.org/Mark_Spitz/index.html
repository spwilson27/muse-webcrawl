Mark Spitz

Mark Andrew Spitz
Born: February 10, 1950, Modesto, California
Sport: Swimming
Olympic medals: 9 gold, 1 silver, 1 bronze
College: Indiana University

Mark Andrew Spitz (born February 10, 1950) is an American former competitive swimmer and nine-time Olympic champion. At the 1972 Summer Olympics in Munich, he won seven gold medals, each in world-record time—an achievement that stood as a record for the most golds at a single Olympics until Michael Phelps won eight in 2008. Spitz remains one of the most decorated swimmers in history.


Spitz at Munich 1972

Early life and 1968 Olympics

Spitz grew up in Sacramento and Honolulu and held numerous age-group records; by age ten he had set 17 national age-group records. At the 1968 Mexico City Olympics, the 18-year-old won two gold medals in relays, plus a silver and a bronze individually, falling short of his public prediction of six golds. He then attended Indiana University, training under coach Doc Counsilman and winning eight NCAA titles.

Munich 1972

At Munich, Spitz won gold in the 100 m and 200 m freestyle, the 100 m and 200 m butterfly, and three relays (4×100 m freestyle, 4×200 m freestyle, 4×100 m medley), setting a world record in every final. His seven golds surpassed the previous single-Games record. After the Munich massacre, in which Palestinian terrorists killed Israeli athletes, Spitz—who is Jewish—was escorted out of Germany amid security concerns. He retired from competitive swimming at age 22 shortly afterward.

Later career

Spitz became one of the first Olympic athletes to earn substantial endorsement income, capitalizing on his famous mustache and a poster that became a cultural icon. He worked as a broadcaster, motivational speaker, and stockbroker. In 1991, at age 41, he attempted a comeback for the 1992 Barcelona Olympics, funded by filmmaker Bud Greenspan, but failed to meet the qualifying standard. He was inducted into the International Swimming Hall of Fame in 1977 and the United States Olympic Hall of Fame in 1983.
