William Ewart Gladstone (29 December 1809 – 19 May 1898) was a British statesman and Liberal politician who served four times as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom — more than any other person except Robert Walpole, whose record for total days in office he also rivals. In a career lasting over 60 years he was also Chancellor of the Exchequer four times.

Political career

Gladstone entered Parliament in 1832 as a Tory, served under Robert Peel, and broke with the Conservatives over the repeal of the Corn Laws in 1846. He gradually became the dominant figure of the new Liberal Party. His first premiership (1868–1874) disestablished the Church of Ireland, introduced elementary education reform, secret ballots, and army and judiciary reforms. His famous rivalry with Benjamin Disraeli defined Victorian politics.

His second ministry (1880–1885) passed the Third Reform Act; his third (1886) was consumed by his conversion to Irish Home Rule, which split the Liberal Party and failed in the Commons. His fourth and final ministry (1892–1894) saw a second Home Rule bill pass the Commons but be rejected by the Lords. Known as "the People's William" and "the Grand Old Man," Gladstone retired to Hawarden Castle in Wales, where his library, St Deiniol's, remains a residential research library.
