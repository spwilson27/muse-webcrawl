The Battle of Lugdunum was fought on 19 February AD 197 near Lugdunum (modern Lyon) in Gaul. It was the final and largest battle of the civil wars of the Year of the Five Emperors, pitting the emperor Septimius Severus against the usurper Clodius Albinus, governor of Britain.

Ancient sources give enormous figures for the armies involved — Cassius Dio claimed 150,000 men on each side — making it one of the largest battles ever fought between Roman armies. The fighting was hard and long in doubt; Severus himself was thrown from his horse and tore off his imperial cloak to avoid recognition before his reserves turned the tide. Albinus fled into Lugdunum and died there, by suicide or execution, and Severus had his body desecrated and his family destroyed.

Consequences

The victory made Severus sole master of the Roman Empire and founder of the Severan dynasty. Lugdunum was sacked and lost its privileged status, and Severus purged Albinus's supporters in the Senate, executing a number of senators and confiscating their estates.
