Five Eyes

AbbreviationFVEY
MembersAustralia, Canada, New Zealand, United Kingdom, United States

TypeIntelligence alliance

The Five Eyes (FVEY) is an intelligence alliance comprising Australia, Canada, New Zealand, the United Kingdom, and the United States. The five countries are parties to the UKUSA Agreement, a treaty for joint cooperation in signals intelligence.

History

The alliance grew out of wartime cooperation between Britain and the United States, formalised by the UKUSA Agreement of 1946. Canada joined in the late 1940s, and Australia and New Zealand in the 1950s. During the Cold War the partners divided global signals-intelligence coverage and developed the ECHELON surveillance system.

Scope and controversy

The Five Eyes share intelligence extensively and are generally understood not to spy on one another. The alliance drew public scrutiny after the 2013 disclosures by Edward Snowden revealed the scale of its mass-surveillance programmes. Related groupings include the "Nine Eyes" and "Fourteen Eyes" (SIGINT Seniors Europe), which involve looser cooperation with additional countries.
