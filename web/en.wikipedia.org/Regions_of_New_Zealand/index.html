Regions of New Zealand

New Zealand is divided into sixteen regions administered by regional councils, the top tier of local government. Eleven are governed by regional councils and five by unitary authorities that combine regional and district functions.

List and functions

The regions are: Northland, Auckland, Waikato, Bay of Plenty, Gisborne, Taranaki, Manawatū-Whanganui, Hawke's Bay, Wellington, Tasman, Nelson, Marlborough, West Coast, Canterbury, Otago, and Southland. Regional councils are chiefly responsible for environmental management — water, land, air, and coastal resources — along with flood control, biosecurity, and regional transport planning.

History

The modern system dates from the local government reforms of 1989, which replaced numerous catchment and regional bodies with the present regions, whose boundaries largely follow river catchments.
