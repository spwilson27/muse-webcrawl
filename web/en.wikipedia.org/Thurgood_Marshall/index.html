Thurgood Marshall

Thurgood Marshall (July 2, 1908 – January 24, 1993) was an American civil rights lawyer and jurist who served as an associate justice of the Supreme Court of the United States from 1967 to 1991. He was the first African American to sit on the Court. Before joining the Court, Marshall built his reputation as the NAACP's chief counsel, travelling the segregated South to defend Black clients and challenge Jim Crow laws.

Legal career

Born in Baltimore, Maryland, Marshall graduated from Lincoln University and from Howard University School of Law, where he was mentored by Charles Hamilton Houston. Rejected by the University of Maryland's law school because of his race, he later successfully sued that institution in Murray v. Pearson (1936) to admit a Black student. As the NAACP's chief counsel and later the first director-counsel of the NAACP Legal Defense and Educational Fund, Marshall argued a long series of cases dismantling segregation, culminating in Brown v. Board of Education (1954), in which the Supreme Court unanimously held that racial segregation in public schools was unconstitutional.

President John F. Kennedy appointed Marshall to the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit in 1961, and President Lyndon B. Johnson named him Solicitor General in 1965 — the first African American to hold that office — before nominating him to the Supreme Court in 1967.

Supreme Court tenure

On the Court, Marshall was a staunch liberal voice, consistently supporting civil rights, the rights of criminal defendants, and the expansion of individual liberties under the Fourteenth Amendment. He maintained throughout his career that the death penalty was unconstitutional in all circumstances, most fully in his dissent in Gregg v. Georgia (1976). In later years, as the Court grew more conservative, he frequently wrote in dissent, notably in cases narrowing affirmative action and voting rights protections. He retired in 1991, citing his health, and was succeeded by Clarence Thomas. Marshall died in 1993 and is buried in Arlington National Cemetery.
