Internet Research Agency

The Internet Research Agency (IRA; Russian: Агентство интернет-исследований), also known as the "troll factory" or "troll farm", was a Russian organisation based in Saint Petersburg engaged in online propaganda and influence operations. It was financed by businessman Yevgeny Prigozhin, a close associate of Vladimir Putin, through his Concord Management and Consulting company.

Operations

The IRA employed hundreds of staff working in shifts to post comments, articles and social media content supporting Kremlin narratives and attacking critics. Russian journalists who worked inside the organisation described quotas of posts per shift and coordinated personas. Its best-known overseas operation targeted the United States, where IRA-controlled accounts on Facebook, Twitter, Instagram and other platforms reached millions of users in the run-up to the 2016 presidential election, promoting divisive content on race, immigration and other issues.

Legal and political fallout

In February 2018, US Special Counsel Robert Mueller indicted the IRA, two related companies and 13 Russian nationals for conspiracy to defraud the United States. Technology companies removed thousands of IRA-linked accounts, and the organisation became the archetypal example of state-sponsored social media manipulation. Operations attributed to the group continued in other countries, including during later elections and in Africa. Prigozhin publicly admitted to founding and running the agency in 2023, shortly before his death following the Wagner Group mutiny.
