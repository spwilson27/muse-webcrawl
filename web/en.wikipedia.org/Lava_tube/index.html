Lava tube

A lava tube is a natural conduit formed by flowing lava when the surface of a low-viscosity lava flow cools and crusts over, insulating the molten stream beneath. When the eruption ends, the lava drains away leaving a hollow cave.

Formation and features

Lava tubes are characteristic of pāhoehoe flows on shield volcanoes such as those in Hawai'i, Iceland and the Canary Islands. They may have skylights (collapsed roof sections), lava stalactites and multiple levels, and can extend for tens of kilometres — Kazumura Cave in Hawai'i exceeds 65 km. Similar tubes have been identified on the Moon and Mars, where they are considered candidate sheltered habitats for future exploration.
