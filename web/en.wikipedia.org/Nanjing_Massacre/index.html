Nanjing Massacre

The Nanjing Massacre (also known as the Rape of Nanking) was the mass murder and wartime sexual violence committed by Imperial Japanese troops against the residents of Nanjing, then the capital of the Republic of China, after the city fell on 13 December 1937 during the Second Sino-Japanese War. The atrocities continued for roughly six weeks.

Events

Japanese soldiers murdered Chinese civilians and disarmed prisoners of war in large numbers, and committed widespread rape, looting and arson. Estimates of the death toll vary; the postwar International Military Tribunal for the Far East judged that more than 200,000 civilians and prisoners were killed, while Chinese official estimates put the figure at 300,000. A Safety Zone administered by foreign residents, led by the German businessman John Rabe, sheltered hundreds of thousands of civilians.

Aftermath

General Iwane Matsui and other commanders were tried and executed for war crimes. The massacre remains a central and contentious issue in Sino-Japanese relations, as some revisionists in Japan dispute its scale. The Memorial Hall in Nanjing, opened in 1985, commemorates the victims, and 13 December is observed in China as a national memorial day.
