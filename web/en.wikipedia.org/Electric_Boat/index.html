General Dynamics Electric Boat

General Dynamics Electric Boat (GDEB) is an American shipyard and a subsidiary of General Dynamics that has been the primary builder of submarines for the United States Navy for more than a century. Founded in 1899 as the Electric Boat Company, its main facilities are a shipyard in Groton, Connecticut, and a hull-fabrication yard in Quonset Point, Rhode Island.

History

The company was founded by financier Isaac Rice to complete the submarines designed by John Philip Holland. Its first product, Holland VI, was commissioned by the U.S. Navy in 1900 as USS Holland, the Navy's first modern submarine. Electric Boat built most of the Navy's early submarine classes and supplied submarines to several foreign navies.

During both World Wars the Groton yard mass-produced fleet submarines; in the Cold War it built USS Nautilus (SSN-571), the world's first nuclear-powered submarine, launched in 1954, followed by the first ballistic-missile submarines of the George Washington class. Under admiral Hyman Rickover's nuclear navy, Electric Boat became one of only two yards building nuclear submarines for the U.S., alongside Newport News Shipbuilding.

Current work

Electric Boat is the prime contractor for the Virginia-class attack submarines (built jointly with Newport News) and leads design and construction of the Columbia-class ballistic missile submarines, the Navy's top acquisition priority, which will replace the Ohio class.
