Chalchiuhtlicue (Nahuatl: "She of the Jade Skirt") is an Aztec goddess of water, presiding over rivers, lakes, seas, and streams. She was also a patroness of childbirth and played a central role in the Aztec creation myths as the sun of the fourth world age.

Mythology

In the Aztec legend of the Five Suns, Chalchiuhtlicue ruled the fourth sun, 4-Water (Nahui Atl), an age that ended in a great flood: the people were transformed into fish. Her husband and counterpart was Tlaloc, the rain god, and together they were regarded as the parents of the moon god Tecciztecatl.

Worship

Chalchiuhtlicue was honoured in ceremonies involving newborn children, whose ritual bathing invoked her protection. Priests conducted rites on the shores of Lake Texcoco, and offerings were cast into the waters to secure fertility and protection from drowning and water-borne disease.

Iconography

She is depicted wearing a skirt of green stones (chalchihuitl, jade) with streams of water flowing from its hem, in which small figures of men and women are carried along. A large stone statue identified with the goddess stands in the Museo Nacional de Antropología in Mexico City.
