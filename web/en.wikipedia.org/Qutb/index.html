Qutb, Qutub, Kutb, or Kutub (Arabic: قطب, quṭb, meaning "axis," "pivot," or "pole") is a concept in Sufism designating the most perfect human being (al-insan al-kamil) of an age, the spiritual leader and "pole" around whom the religious life of the world revolves. The Qutb stands at the head of an invisible hierarchy of saints (awliya) believed to sustain and govern the spiritual order of the universe.

"Qutb" — the pole or axis of the age in Sufi thought

The saintly hierarchy

Sufi cosmology commonly describes a ranked hierarchy of hidden saints. Below the Qutb — also honoured as the Ghawth ("Succour") — stand two imams as his ministers, then the four awtad ("pegs" or "pillars"), the seven (or forty) abdal ("substitutes," so called because when one dies another is immediately substituted), and larger bodies such as the nujaba and nuqaba. These saints are usually unknown to the world; when the Qutb dies, the office passes to another. The 9th-century mystic al-Hakim al-Tirmidhi developed an early theory of sainthood, and Ibn Arabi (1165–1240) gave the doctrine of the Qutb its most elaborate metaphysical form, describing the Qutb as the heart of the universe and the pole around which the sphere of existence turns.

Use in Sufi orders

Within the Sufi orders (tariqas), the title Qutb is also applied to the order's supreme spiritual guide or to its founding shaykh, even after death — for example, Abdul Qadir Gilani, founder of the Qadiriyya, is venerated as al-Ghawth al-Azam ("the Supreme Succour"). Shrines of celebrated "poles" became centres of pilgrimage across the Muslim world.

Other uses

Quṭb in the sense of "pole" appears in Arabic astronomy and geography — for example the Qutb as the celestial pole. The word is also a common element in personal names and honorifics, such as that of the Delhi sultan Qutb ud-Din Aibak, builder of the Qutb Minar complex, and the name of the 13th-century Persian astronomer Qutb al-Din al-Shirazi. In the 20th century the Egyptian thinker Sayyid Qutb bore the term as a family name.
