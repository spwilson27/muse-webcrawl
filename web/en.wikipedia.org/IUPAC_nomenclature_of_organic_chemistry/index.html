IUPAC nomenclature of organic chemistry

In chemical nomenclature, the IUPAC nomenclature of organic chemistry is a method of naming organic chemical compounds as recommended by the International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry (IUPAC). It is published in the Nomenclature of Organic Chemistry (informally called the Blue Book). Ideally, every possible organic compound should have a name from which an unambiguous structural formula can be created. There is also an IUPAC nomenclature of inorganic chemistry.

Basic principles

In chemistry, a number of prefixes, suffixes and infixes are used to describe the type and position of the functional groups in the compound. The steps for naming an organic compound are:

  1. Identification of the senior parent structure.
  2. Identification of the principal characteristic groups and prefixes.
  3. Identification of the need for locants or "added hydrogen" atoms.
  4. Construction of the name by assembling the parts in alphabetical order.

Parent structures

The parent structure is the main carbon chain or ring system to which substituents are attached. For acyclic compounds, chains are named by the number of carbon atoms: methane (1), ethane (2), propane (3), butane (4), pentane (5), hexane (6), heptane (7), octane (8), nonane (9), and decane (10).

Functional groups and suffixes

The suffix of the name reflects the principal functional group present: -ol for alcohols, -al for aldehydes, -one for ketones, -oic acid for carboxylic acids, -amine for amines, and -ene/-yne for alkenes and alkynes. When several functional groups are present, a seniority order determines which takes the suffix.
