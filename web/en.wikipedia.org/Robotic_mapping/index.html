Robotic mapping

Robotic mapping is the field of research concerned with how an autonomous robot can construct, or be given, a map of its environment while keeping track of its own location within that map. It is closely related to simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM), motion planning, and computational geometry.

Map representations

Several representations are used. Occupancy grids divide space into cells marked free, occupied, or unknown, and are well suited to range sensors such as lidar and sonar. Topological maps represent places as nodes in a graph connected by paths. Feature-based or landmark maps store the positions of distinctive objects, while metric maps record precise geometry. Dense 3D maps and point clouds are increasingly common with depth cameras.

Sensors

Robots build maps from wheel odometry, inertial measurement units, lidar, cameras, radar, and GPS. Sensor noise and drift are central challenges: small errors accumulate over time, and loop closure — recognising a previously visited place — is used to correct them.

Applications

Mapping underpins self-driving cars, warehouse and domestic robots, planetary rovers, underwater survey vehicles, and search-and-rescue robots. Research issues include mapping dynamic environments with moving people, long-term autonomy, semantic mapping that labels objects, and multi-robot mapping in which teams merge their individual maps.
