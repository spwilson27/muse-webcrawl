Fast Fourier transform

A fast Fourier transform (FFT) is an algorithm that computes the discrete Fourier transform (DFT) of a sequence, or its inverse. Whereas directly evaluating the DFT takes O(N²) arithmetic operations, an FFT reduces this to O(N log N), making spectral analysis practical for large data sets. The most common FFT algorithm is the Cooley–Tukey algorithm, published in 1965; its essential idea had been used by Carl Friedrich Gauss as early as 1805.

The Cooley–Tukey algorithm

The radix-2 Cooley–Tukey algorithm recursively divides a DFT of size N into two interleaved DFTs of size N/2—the "even-indexed" and "odd-indexed" parts—combined through so-called butterfly operations involving twiddle factors (roots of unity). Variants include split-radix and prime-factor algorithms for arbitrary sizes.

Applications

  • Signal processing: audio and image compression (MP3, JPEG use related transforms)
  • Solving partial differential equations
  • Fast multiplication of large integers and polynomials (via convolution)
  • Analysis of time series, seismology, and spectroscopy
  • Convolutional operations in radar, sonar and communications

Computing magazine has called the FFT "the most important numerical algorithm of our lifetime".
