Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates

Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates, named after Martin Kruskal and George Szekeres, who independently discovered them, are a coordinate system for the Schwarzschild geometry in general relativity. They reveal the maximal analytic extension of the Schwarzschild solution, removing the coordinate singularity at the event horizon while retaining the true physical singularity at the centre.

Definition

The coordinates are obtained from the Schwarzschild coordinates (t, r) by defining new null coordinates u and v as exponential functions of the tortoise coordinate and time, with different branches inside and outside the horizon. In these coordinates the metric takes a conformally flat form in the t–r plane, and light cones appear at 45 degrees everywhere, making the causal structure easy to read.

The Kruskal diagram

A spacetime diagram in Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates (a Kruskal diagram) displays four regions: the exterior of the black hole (region I), the black hole interior (region II), a parallel exterior region (region III), and a white hole region (region IV). The two exterior regions are connected by a non-traversable wormhole known as the Einstein–Rosen bridge.

History

The extension was published by Kruskal in 1960 and by Szekeres in the same year; related work by David Finkelstein and others clarified the physical meaning of the horizon as a one-way membrane.
