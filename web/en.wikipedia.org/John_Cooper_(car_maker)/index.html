John Cooper (car maker)

John Newton Cooper CBE (17 July 1923 – 24 December 2000) was an English car maker and racing driver, co-founder with his father Charles Cooper of the Cooper Car Company. He revolutionised Formula One by proving that rear-engined cars were superior, and he created the iconic Mini Cooper.


John Cooper

Cooper Car Company

The Coopers began building single-seat racing cars in their Surbiton garage after World War II, starting with the Cooper 500 for Formula 3 using motorcycle engines mounted behind the driver. The layout — adopted for pragmatic reasons, since the engine drove the rear wheels by chain — proved decisively advantageous. Cooper cars won the Formula One World Constructors' Championship in 1959 and 1960 with Jack Brabham, and thereafter every Formula One car followed the mid/rear-engine configuration.

Mini Cooper

Cooper saw the racing potential of Alec Issigonis's BMC Mini and, over Issigonis's initial reluctance, developed the tuned Mini Cooper (1961) and Cooper S. The cars won the Monte Carlo Rally in 1964, 1965 and 1967 and became cultural icons of the Swinging Sixties. The Cooper name was revived on performance versions of the modern BMW Mini from 2001.
