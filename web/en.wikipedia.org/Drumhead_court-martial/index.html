Drumhead court-martial

A drumhead court-martial is a summary court-martial held in the field during military operations, traditionally convened around an upturned drum serving as a table. The term has come to denote any hasty, improvised trial lacking the usual safeguards of due process, often with a predetermined outcome.

Origin

The expression dates to the era of early modern warfare, when commanders on campaign needed to dispense immediate discipline for offences such as desertion, cowardice or plundering. Officers would assemble around a drum — or, in some accounts, a drumhead (the skin of the drum) used as a writing surface — to hear the charge and pass sentence on the spot; punishment, including execution, could follow immediately.

Usage and connotation

Drumhead trials were a feature of many armies, including British forces in the Napoleonic era and both sides in the American Civil War. Because of their speed and severity, the phrase is now used metaphorically — and pejoratively — for any proceeding in which the accused is effectively condemned without a fair hearing, as in "a drumhead trial in the press".
