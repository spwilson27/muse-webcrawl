AEC Routemaster

The AEC Routemaster is a front-engined double-decker bus designed by London Transport and built by the Associated Equipment Company (AEC) and Park Royal Vehicles. Introduced in 1956 and produced until 1968, the Routemaster became one of the most enduring symbols of London, with 2,876 built and regular service continuing until 2005.

Design

The Routemaster used an aluminium body and unitary construction to save weight, a front-mounted engine beside the driver, independent front suspension, power steering, and a fully automatic gearbox — advanced features for its time. Its most distinctive feature was the open rear platform, which allowed passengers to board and alight quickly and let a conductor collect fares on the move.

Service

Nearly 2,900 Routemasters served London for almost half a century, outliving several intended replacements thanks to their durability and ease of refurbishment. The last regular Routemaster services ended on 9 December 2005, when the type was withdrawn from route 159, replaced by accessible low-floor buses.

Legacy

Routemasters survive in preservation, on sightseeing services, and on London "heritage routes" introduced to celebrate the design. The type inspired the New Routemaster ("Borismaster") introduced in 2012, which revived features such as the open platform (later removed) and rear staircase arrangement.
