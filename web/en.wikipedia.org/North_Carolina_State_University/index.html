North Carolina State University

North Carolina State University (NC State, North Carolina State, NC State University, or NCSU) is a public land-grant research university in Raleigh, North Carolina, United States. Founded in 1887, it is the largest university in the Carolinas by enrollment and forms one of the corners of North Carolina's Research Triangle together with Duke University in Durham and the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill.

History

The North Carolina General Assembly founded the North Carolina College of Agriculture and Mechanic Arts on March 7, 1887, as a land-grant college under the Morrill Act. The college opened in 1889 with 72 students. It became North Carolina State College of Agriculture and Engineering in 1917, and after consolidation into the University of North Carolina system in 1931, it was renamed North Carolina State University at Raleigh in 1965; the "at Raleigh" was dropped for most uses, though it remains in the official name.

Campus

The university's main campus in Raleigh is divided into North, Central, and South campuses, known for their distinctive red-brick architecture. The Memorial Belltower, completed in 1937 as a memorial to alumni killed in the First World War, is the university's symbol. The Centennial Campus, a research park and campus south of the main grounds, opened in 1988 and hosts university, corporate, and government partners, along with the James B. Hunt Jr. Library, which opened in 2013.

Academics

NC State comprises 12 colleges, including the College of Engineering, the College of Agriculture and Life Sciences, the Wilson College of Textiles (the only freestanding textiles college in the United States), and the Poole College of Management. The university is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities — Very high research activity" and is a member of the Association of American Universities as of 2024. It is particularly noted for engineering, agriculture, textiles, statistics, and veterinary medicine.

Athletics

The NC State Wolfpack competes in NCAA Division I as a member of the Atlantic Coast Conference. The men's basketball team famously won national championships in 1974 under Norm Sloan and in 1983 under Jim Valvano, whose "Survive and Advance" team inspired the annual Jimmy V Week for cancer research.
