Camp Lejeune


Placeholder image: Entrance sign at Marine Corps Base Camp Lejeune

Marine Corps Base Camp Lejeune (pronounced "luh-ZHOON") is a 246-square-mile (640 km²) United States military training facility in Jacksonville, North Carolina. Its 14 miles (23 km) of beaches make the base a major area for amphibious assault training; its position between deep-water ports allows for fast deployments.

History

Construction began in 1941 on what was first called Marine Barracks New River; the base was renamed in 1942 in honour of Lt. Gen. John A. Lejeune, the 13th Commandant of the Marine Corps. It is home to the II Marine Expeditionary Force and several major commands, and supports a population of active-duty, dependent, retiree and civilian personnel.

Water contamination

From the 1950s through the 1980s, drinking water at the base was contaminated with solvents including trichloroethylene and perchloroethylene from leaking fuel tanks, industrial activity and an off-base dry cleaning firm. The contamination became the subject of extensive litigation and veterans' health legislation, including the Camp Lejeune Families Act of 2012 and provisions of the PACT Act of 2022 allowing civil claims.
