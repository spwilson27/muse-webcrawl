Pell Grant

TypeUS federal student grant
Established1972 (as Basic Educational Opportunity Grant)
Renamed1980, for Senator Claiborne Pell
Administered byUS Department of Education
RepaymentNot required (grant, not a loan)

The Pell Grant is a subsidy provided by the United States federal government to students who need it to pay for college. It is the largest federal grant program for undergraduate students and forms the foundation of federal student financial aid, to which loans and work-study are often added. Unlike a loan, a Pell Grant generally does not have to be repaid.

History

The program was created by the Higher Education Amendments of 1972 as the Basic Educational Opportunity Grant. It was renamed in 1980 in honour of Senator Claiborne Pell of Rhode Island, its chief sponsor. The maximum award is set by Congress each year; for the mid-2020s award years it has been in the region of US$7,000–7,400.

Eligibility

Eligibility is based on financial need, determined from the Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA). Key factors include the student's Student Aid Index (formerly Expected Family Contribution), the cost of attendance, and enrolment status. The program primarily serves low-income undergraduates who have not yet earned a bachelor's degree, though it is available at roughly 5,400 participating institutions. Lifetime eligibility is limited to the equivalent of twelve full-time semesters (600% of scheduled awards). Several million students receive Pell Grants each year, and the program disburses tens of billions of dollars annually.
