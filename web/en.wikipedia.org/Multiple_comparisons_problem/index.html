Multiple comparisons problem

In statistics, the multiple comparisons problem (also multiple testing problem) occurs when a set of statistical inferences is considered simultaneously: the more hypotheses tested, the more likely it becomes that at least one will appear statistically significant purely by chance. If each of 20 independent tests is run at the 5% significance level, the probability of at least one false positive is not 5% but about 64%.

Error rates

Two frameworks are commonly used. The family-wise error rate is the probability of making one or more false discoveries among all the hypotheses; the false discovery rate (FDR) is the expected proportion of false discoveries among rejected hypotheses, a less stringent criterion suited to large-scale screening such as genomics.

Corrections

  • Bonferroni correction — divides the significance threshold by the number of tests; simple but conservative.
  • Holm–Bonferroni method — a uniformly more powerful step-down procedure.
  • Tukey's HSD and Scheffé's method — designed for pairwise comparisons of group means after ANOVA.
  • Benjamini–Hochberg procedure — controls the false discovery rate.

Related issues

The problem underlies phenomena such as p-hacking, data dredging, the look-elsewhere effect in particle physics, and spurious correlations in large datasets — a major motivation for pre-registration of studies and for reporting how many tests were performed.
