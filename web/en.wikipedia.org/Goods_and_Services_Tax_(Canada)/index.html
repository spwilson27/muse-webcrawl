The Goods and Services Tax (GST) is a federal value-added tax levied in Canada on most goods and services sold for domestic consumption. It was introduced on 1 January 1991 by the government of Prime Minister Brian Mulroney, replacing the hidden 13.5% manufacturers' sales tax. The GST is charged at a rate of 5%, reduced from an initial 7% in 2006–2008.

History

The tax was highly controversial when introduced, contributing to the collapse in support for the Progressive Conservative government before the 1993 election. The succeeding Liberal government of Jean Chrétien campaigned on abolishing it but ultimately retained it, citing fiscal constraints.

Harmonized Sales Tax

Several provinces combined their provincial sales taxes with the GST to form a single Harmonized Sales Tax (HST), administered by the Canada Revenue Agency. Ontario and British Columbia harmonized in 2010, though British Columbia later returned to a separate provincial tax after a 2011 referendum.

Exemptions and zero rating

Basic groceries, prescription drugs, medical devices, most health and educational services, and financial services are exempt or zero-rated. Low-income individuals receive a quarterly GST credit to offset the tax's regressive effects.
