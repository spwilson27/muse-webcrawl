Roubaix (French pronunciation: [ʁubɛ]; Dutch: Robaais) is a city in northern France, in the department of Nord in the Hauts-de-France region, close to the Belgian border. It forms part of the Lille metropolitan area together with Lille and Tourcoing. Once one of the world's great textile manufacturing centres, Roubaix has reinvented itself as a centre for retail, culture, and the arts.

History

First mentioned in the 9th century, Roubaix remained a modest market town until the Industrial Revolution transformed it into a booming textile city — the "Manchester of France" — specialising in wool. Its population exploded from a few thousand in 1800 to over 120,000 by the end of the 19th century, and it became a symbol both of industrial wealth and of working-class conditions. Mail-order giant La Redoute was founded there in 1837, and the Motte-Bossut family built landmark factories. The collapse of the French textile industry in the 1970s brought severe economic and social hardship, from which the city has been recovering through regeneration programmes.

Culture and landmarks

Roubaix's most famous museum, La Piscine, opened in 2000 in a converted Art Deco municipal swimming pool, housing fashion and textile collections, including fabric archives and works by regional artists. The city also hosts the National Archives of the World of Labour. The flamboyant Hôtel de Ville (1911) and Saint-Martin church dominate the Grand-Place.

Paris–Roubaix

The city gives its name to the Paris–Roubaix one-day cycling race, one of the five Monuments of professional cycling, held each April since 1896. Famous for its brutal cobbled sectors (pavé) across the mining country of northern France and nicknamed "the Hell of the North", the race traditionally finishes in the Roubaix Velodrome (Vélodrome André-Pétrieux), where the winner receives a cobblestone trophy. Belgian rider Roger De Vlaeminck shares the record of four victories.

Economy

Roubaix is home to the headquarters of the web-hosting and cloud company OVHcloud, founded there in 1999 by Octave Klaba, and remains an important retail and mail-order centre.
