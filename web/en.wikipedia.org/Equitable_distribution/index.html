Equitable distribution

Equitable distribution divides marital property fairly, though not necessarily equally

Equitable distribution is a principle in the family law of the United States under which a court, upon the dissolution of a marriage, divides the couple's marital property in a manner that is fair, or equitable, even if the division is not exactly equal. It is the governing principle in the majority of U.S. states; the remainder follow the community property system.

Marital and separate property

Only marital property — assets and debts acquired during the marriage — is subject to distribution. Separate property, such as assets owned before the marriage, inheritances, and gifts received by one spouse individually, is generally retained by its owner, although commingling can convert separate property into marital property.

Factors considered

Courts weigh statutory factors that commonly include the length of the marriage; each spouse's age, health, income, and earning capacity; contributions to the marriage, including homemaking and child-rearing; contributions to the acquisition or appreciation of marital assets; the economic circumstances of each party at divorce; the needs of the custodial parent to remain in the family home; and, in some jurisdictions, marital misconduct such as dissipation of assets. Unlike alimony, equitable distribution concerns property rather than ongoing support, and the award is generally not modifiable.

History

Equitable distribution developed as legislatures and courts moved away from the older title-based common-law system, under which property was awarded to the spouse who held legal title — a system criticised for failing to recognise the non-financial contributions of spouses, historically usually wives. New York adopted equitable distribution in 1980 in the wake of the O'Brien v. O'Brien era of divorce reform.

Comparison with community property

In community property states (such as California, Texas, and Arizona), marital assets are generally divided equally, 50/50, whereas equitable distribution permits an unequal division — commonly ratios such as 60/40 or 70/30 — when fairness so requires.
