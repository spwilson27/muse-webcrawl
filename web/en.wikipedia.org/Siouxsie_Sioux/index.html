Siouxsie Sioux (born Susan Janet Ballion, 27 May 1957) is an English singer, songwriter and musician, best known as the lead singer of the rock band Siouxsie and the Banshees (1976–1996). A central figure of the British punk and post-punk scenes, she first gained notoriety as part of the Bromley Contingent of Sex Pistols followers; the Banshees' debut single, "Hong Kong Garden" (1978), was a top-ten hit.

Career

With the Banshees she released eleven studio albums, including The Scream, Kaleidoscope, Juju, and Peepshow, with hits such as "Happy House", "Spellbound", "Cities in Dust", "Peek-a-Boo" and "Kiss Them for Me". Alongside, she recorded with the Creatures, her duo with drummer Budgie, exploring percussion- and marimba-driven music. Her style — severe black hair, dramatic eye makeup and theatrical dress — became an archetype of gothic rock fashion.

After the Banshees disbanded she pursued a solo career, releasing the album MantaRay in 2007. Siouxsie has been cited as an influence by artists across alternative music, including members of Joy Division, the Cure, Radiohead, PJ Harvey and LCD Soundsystem.
