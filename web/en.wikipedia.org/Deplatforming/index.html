Deplatforming

Deplatforming is the practice of removing a person, organisation or content from a platform — such as a social media service, video-sharing site, payment processor or web host — typically for violating the platform's rules on speech or conduct. The term became prominent in the late 2010s amid debates over how online platforms moderate extremist and inflammatory figures.

Notable cases

Well-known instances include the removal of conspiracy theorist Alex Jones from major platforms in 2018, the suspension of U.S. President Donald Trump's accounts following the January 6 Capitol attack in 2021, and the banning of forums and services such as 8chan (which lost hosting services in 2019) and Parler (removed from app stores and cloud hosting in 2021).

Debate

Supporters argue deplatforming reduces the reach of harmful actors; studies have found that banned figures generally lose most of their audience. Critics argue it concentrates control over public discourse in private companies and may push communities to less moderated spaces.
