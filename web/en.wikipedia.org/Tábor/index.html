Tábor is a town in the South Bohemian Region of the Czech Republic, situated on the Lužnice River about 80 kilometres south of Prague. It has a population of roughly 34,000 and is notable as the city founded by the radical wing of the Hussite movement, from which the Taborites took their name.

History

Tábor was founded in the spring of 1420 by Hussites who established a fortified, communally organised settlement on a hilltop near the older village of Hradiště, naming it after Mount Tabor in the Bible. Under leaders including Jan Žižka and Prokop the Great, the town became the military stronghold of the Hussite Wars; its armies, famous for their wagon forts (vozová hradba) and early use of field artillery, repelled repeated crusades. After the defeat of the radical Hussites at the Battle of Lipany in 1434, Tábor gradually accommodated itself to royal authority, and in 1437 it was recognised as a royal town.

Sights

The historic centre preserves a labyrinthine old town, Žižka Square with its Renaissance old town hall (now the Hussite Museum), the Church of the Transfiguration, and an extensive network of medieval underground tunnels excavated as cellars and refuges. Nearby are the ruins of Kozí Hrádek castle, where Jan Hus preached, and the Kotnov castle tower overlooking the Lužnice.
