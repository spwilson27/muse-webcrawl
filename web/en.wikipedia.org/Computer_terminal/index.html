Computer terminal

A computer terminal is an electronic or electromechanical hardware device that can be used for entering data into, and transcribing data from, a computer or a computing system. Early terminals were inexpensive devices but very slow compared to punched cards or paper tape for input; as the technology improved and video displays were introduced, terminals pushed these older forms of interaction from the industry.

History

The earliest terminals were teleprinters, such as the Teletype Model 33 ASR, which printed output on paper. In the late 1960s and 1970s, video display terminals (VDTs) such as the DEC VT100 (1978) became dominant. The VT100 supported ANSI escape codes and became a de facto standard that terminal emulators still follow.

Types

Terminals are commonly divided into "dumb" terminals, which depend entirely on the host computer for processing, and intelligent terminals with some local capability. A thin client is a modern descendant of the dumb terminal concept. Today, most users interact with a terminal emulator — a software application such as xterm or a terminal window in a graphical environment — rather than dedicated hardware.
