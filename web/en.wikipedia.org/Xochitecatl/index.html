Xochitecatl

LocationTlaxcala, Mexico
CultureFormative-period Mesoamerican
Periodc. 800 BC – AD 150
Notable structurePyramid of the Flowers

Xochitecatl is a pre-Columbian archaeological site in the Mexican state of Tlaxcala, near the modern town of San Miguel Xochitecatitla. Together with the neighbouring hilltop site of Cacaxtla, it formed part of an important ceremonial landscape in the Puebla–Tlaxcala valley between the ancient cities of Cholula and Teotihuacan.

History

The site was first settled in the Middle Formative period and reached its height between about 700 and 450 BC, when it may have governed a small polity. Occupation revived in the Late Formative and again in the Epiclassic period (c. AD 650–900), when Cacaxtla flourished nearby, before the area was largely abandoned.

Architecture and finds

The principal structures stand atop a volcanic hill: the Spiral Building, a circular stepped platform; the Serpent Building; the Platform of the Volcanoes; and the Pyramid of the Flowers, among the largest pyramids of its era by volume. Excavations have uncovered numerous female figurines and unusual burials of women and infants, leading some archaeologists to propose that Xochitecatl hosted a cult centred on female deities, possibly connected with fertility and the earth goddess later known as Xochiquetzal. The site's name in Nahuatl means roughly "place of the lineage of flowers".
