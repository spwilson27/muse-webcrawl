Hardy space

FieldComplex analysis, functional analysis
Named afterG. H. Hardy
NotationHp

In complex analysis, a Hardy space is a function space of holomorphic functions on the unit disc or upper half-plane, defined by growth conditions on integrals of the function over circles (or lines). Hardy spaces, denoted Hp, were introduced by Frigyes Riesz and named for G. H. Hardy.

Definition

For 0 < p < ∞, the Hardy space Hp on the unit disc consists of holomorphic functions f for which the means of |f(r eiθ)|p over circles of radius r remain bounded as r approaches 1. H∞ consists of bounded holomorphic functions.

Properties

Functions in Hp have boundary values almost everywhere on the unit circle, and Hp can be identified with a closed subspace of Lp on the circle. Factorisation theorems (inner–outer factorisation) and the theory of Blaschke products are central to the subject.

Real Hardy spaces

In the 1970s, Fefferman and Stein developed real-variable Hardy spaces Hp on Euclidean space, characterised by maximal functions, which became fundamental in harmonic analysis, especially for p ≤ 1 where the complex definition fails.
