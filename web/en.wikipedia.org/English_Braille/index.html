English Braille

English Braille, also known as Grade 2 Braille in its contracted form, is the braille alphabet used for English. It consists of the 26 letters of the Latin alphabet plus punctuation and a set of about 250 contractions and short-form words used in contracted (Grade 2) braille to save space and increase reading speed. English Braille is used in the United Kingdom, the United States, and other English-speaking countries.

Grades

Grade 1 braille is a letter-by-letter transcription of print. Grade 2 braille introduces contractions: single cells or sequences standing for common letter groups (such as "and", "the", "ch", "sh", "ing", "ed") and whole words. Grade 2 was the standard for books and magazines for many years. A proposed Grade 3, a highly abbreviated personal shorthand, never gained official status.

Unified English Braille

In 1991 work began on Unified English Braille (UEB), a single code intended to unify literary, mathematical, and technical braille across English-speaking countries, replacing the older English Braille American Edition (EBAE) and separate subject codes such as the Nemeth Code in certain contexts. The United States adopted UEB in 2016, while retaining the Nemeth Code as an option within UEB contexts; the United Kingdom and other countries had adopted it earlier.

Characteristics

Like other braille systems, English Braille is written in cells of six dots arranged in two columns of three, read by touch. Numbers are formed using the letters a–j preceded by a number sign. Capital letters are indicated by a preceding capital sign. Formatting indicators in UEB signal typefaces such as italics and bold.
