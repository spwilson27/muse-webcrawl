Edvard Beneš

Edvard Beneš (28 May 1884 – 3 September 1948) was a Czech statesman and the second and penultimate president of Czechoslovakia, serving from 1935 to 1938 and again from 1945 to 1948. A close collaborator of Tomáš Garrigue Masaryk, he was a leading figure in the Czechoslovak independence movement during the First World War and one of the architects of the League of Nations.

Beneš, foreign minister and twice president of Czechoslovakia.

Independence and diplomacy

During the First World War, Beneš worked in exile with Masaryk and Milan Rastislav Štefánik to win Allied recognition of an independent Czechoslovak state, achieved in 1918. As foreign minister for seventeen years he built the Little Entente alliance and served as a prominent figure at the League of Nations, chairing its council repeatedly.

Munich and exile

Succeeding Masaryk as president in 1935, Beneš confronted Nazi Germany's demands on the Sudetenland. Abandoned by Britain and France at the Munich Agreement of September 1938, he resigned and went into exile, leading the Czechoslovak government-in-exile in London during the Second World War.

Second presidency

Returning in 1945, Beneš issued the presidential decrees — the Beneš decrees — which stripped citizenship and property from ethnic Germans and Hungarians and underpinned the expulsion of some three million Sudeten Germans. Weakened and ill after the Communist coup of February 1948, he refused to sign the new constitution and resigned in June, dying three months later.
