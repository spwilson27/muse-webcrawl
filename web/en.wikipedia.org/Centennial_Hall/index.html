Centennial Hall

The Centennial Hall (Polish: Hala Stulecia; German: Jahrhunderthalle) is a historic building in Wrocław, Poland, constructed between 1911 and 1913 when the city was part of the German Empire. It was designed by architect Max Berg and inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2006.

Construction

The hall was built to commemorate the centenary of the Battle of Leipzig (1813) and to host exhibitions and mass events in Breslau. Its great reinforced-concrete dome, 69 metres in diameter, was the largest structure of its kind in the world at the time of completion, surpassing even the Pantheon in internal span. The pioneering structure, engineered by Günther Trauer and built by Dyckerhoff & Widmann, demonstrated the potential of reinforced concrete as a monumental building material.

Features

The circular hall seats about 7,000 people and is crowned by a steel-and-glass lantern. Adjacent stand the Pergola with its multimedia fountain, the Four Domes Pavilion, and the grounds of the Wrocław Zoo and Szczytnicki Park.

Events

The hall has hosted exhibitions, sporting events, concerts, and major gatherings, including the 1948 World Congress of Intellectuals and events for EuroBasket and visiting world leaders.
