Biblical inerrancy

Biblical inerrancy is the belief that the Bible, in its original manuscripts, is accurate and totally free from error concerning doctrine, revelation, history, and science. Its proponents hold that Scripture is wholly true in all that it affirms. Closely related is biblical infallibility, the belief that the Bible cannot fail in its purpose, though definitions differ among traditions on whether infallibility is weaker or equivalent to inerrancy.

History

Belief in the full truthfulness of scripture has roots in early Christianity and was affirmed in various forms by theologians through the medieval and Reformation periods. In the 19th and early 20th centuries, Princeton theologians such as A. A. Hodge and B. B. Warfield articulated a strong doctrine of inerrancy in response to modern biblical criticism. The doctrine became a defining mark of fundamentalist and many evangelical churches. The 1978 Chicago Statement on Biblical Inerrancy, signed by nearly 300 evangelical scholars, gave the doctrine a detailed modern formulation.

Views

Proponents generally limit inerrancy to the original autographs, which no longer exist, and allow for the use of ordinary language, round numbers, and phenomenological descriptions in scientific matters. Critics, including many mainline Protestants and the Roman Catholic Church (which teaches inerrancy "in those things that pertain to salvation" in a more limited formulation), argue that the Bible's purpose is theological rather than scientific or historical precision in every detail.
