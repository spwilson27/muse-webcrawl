Adam Neumann

Adam Neumann
Born: 25 April 1979, Beersheba, Israel
Occupation: Businessman
Known for: Co-founding WeWork
Spouse: Rebekah Neumann

Adam Neumann (born 25 April 1979) is an Israeli-American businessman. In 2010, he co-founded WeWork with Miguel McKelvey and his wife Rebekah Neumann, growing the coworking company to a peak private valuation of about US$47 billion in early 2019. He served as its chief executive until September 2019, when he stepped down amid the collapse of the company's planned initial public offering and intense scrutiny of his management, self-dealing and governance arrangements.

Early life

Neumann was born in Beersheba, Israel, and grew up partly on a kibbutz after his parents' divorce. He served as an officer in the Israeli Navy, then moved to New York City, where he studied at Baruch College but left before graduating. His early ventures included a baby-clothing company, Krawlers, and a first attempt at shared office space, Green Desk, in Brooklyn.

WeWork

Founded in 2010, WeWork expanded rapidly with investment led by SoftBank's Vision Fund, leasing office space long-term and renting it short-term to freelancers and companies. Neumann promoted an expansive mission — "to elevate the world's consciousness" — and side ventures such as WeLive housing and WeGrow schooling. The 2019 IPO filing revealed large losses, Neumann's loans against his shares, personal ownership of buildings leased to the company, and his sale of the "We" trademark to it. The IPO was withdrawn, the valuation collapsed to under $10 billion, SoftBank took control, and Neumann departed with an exit package widely reported at around $1.7 billion in total potential value. WeWork ultimately filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in November 2023.

Later ventures

In 2022 Neumann launched Flow, a residential real-estate venture backed by a reported $350 million investment from Andreessen Horowitz. He has also invested through his family office in real estate and climate-related startups, and in 2024 publicly expressed interest in buying WeWork out of bankruptcy.
