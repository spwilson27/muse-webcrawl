Campervan


Placeholder image: A campervan at a campsite

A campervan (or camper van), sometimes referred to simply as a camper, or a caravanette when in Europe, is a self-propelled vehicle that provides both transport and sleeping accommodation. The term describes vans that have been fitted out, often with a coachbuilt body for use as accommodation (usually a camper).

Features

Campervans typically include a small kitchen with a hob, sink and refrigerator, sleeping berths formed from seats or a fixed bed, and storage. Larger models may have a shower and toilet, a pop-up or high-top roof for standing room, and hookups for mains electricity and water. They are smaller and more manoeuvrable than coachbuilt motorhomes, and can often be used as an everyday vehicle.

History

The best-known classic campervan is the Volkswagen Type 2 ("Kombi" or "Microbus") conversion, produced in many factory and third-party variants from the 1950s onward by companies such as Westfalia. Modern conversions are offered on vans from manufacturers including Volkswagen, Mercedes-Benz, Ford and Fiat.
