Allen Telescope Array

LocationHat Creek Radio Observatory, California, US
Dishes42 (first phase) of 6.1 m each

The Allen Telescope Array (ATA) is a radio interferometer at the Hat Creek Radio Observatory in northern California, designed primarily for the search for extraterrestrial intelligence (SETI) and for radio astronomy research.

Design

The array uses a large number of small dishes — 42 hydroformed 6.1-metre antennas in its first phase, with a design goal of 350 — an approach pioneered to cut costs and allow wide-field imaging and simultaneous multi-frequency observing. It is named for its chief benefactor, Microsoft co-founder Paul Allen, whose foundation donated over $25 million.

History and science

First observations began in 2007. Funding shortfalls forced the array into hibernation in 2011; it resumed operations later that year with new support, including observing campaigns searching for signals from exoplanet systems catalogued by the Kepler mission, studies of transient radio sources, and monitoring for the interstellar object ʻOumuamua in 2017.
