Virtual tour

A panoramic node in a virtual tour

Medium: Interactive panoramic photography and 3D models

Uses: Real estate, tourism, education, museums

A virtual tour is a simulation of an existing location, usually composed of a sequence of videos, still images, or 360-degree panoramic photographs, often with sound effects, music, narration, and text. The viewer can navigate between linked viewpoints — called nodes or scenes — to explore a place remotely as if walking through it.

Technology

Most virtual tours are built from stitched panoramic images captured with dedicated 360-degree cameras or DSLR cameras with fisheye lenses. Tours are delivered through web browsers using HTML5 viewers, and can also be experienced in virtual reality headsets. Platforms such as Google Street View, launched in 2007, brought virtual tours of streets and interiors to a mass audience, while 3D scanning systems such as Matterport create navigable "dollhouse" models of buildings.

Applications

Virtual tours are widely used in real estate marketing, allowing prospective buyers or renters to inspect properties remotely. Hotels, tourist attractions, museums, universities, and event venues use them for promotion and accessibility. During the COVID-19 pandemic, virtual tours became a primary way for cultural institutions to reach visitors during closures.
