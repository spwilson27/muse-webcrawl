Klaipeda

The harbour of Klaipeda, Lithuania's gateway to the Baltic Sea.

Klaipeda (Lithuanian: Klaipėda; historically German: Memel) is a port city in Lithuania on the Baltic Sea coast, at the mouth of the Danė River and the entrance to the Curonian Lagoon. Lithuania's third-largest city and its only major seaport, it has a population of roughly 160,000.

History

The site was settled by Baltic tribes before the Teutonic Order founded the castle of Memelburg in 1252, naming it after the Nemunas (Memel) River. The town that grew around the castle was known for centuries as Memel and formed Prussia's north-easternmost city. After the First World War the territory was detached from Germany as the Memel Territory under League of Nations administration; Lithuanian forces seized it in the Klaipėda Revolt of 1923, and it became part of Lithuania. Nazi Germany annexed the city in March 1939 following an ultimatum, and after the Second World War it was incorporated into the Lithuanian SSR of the Soviet Union, its German population expelled. Since 1990 it has been part of independent Lithuania.

Economy and sights

The ice-free port of Klaipeda handles most of Lithuania's maritime trade, including oil products through the Būtingė terminal and a major LNG terminal aboard the vessel Independence. The compact old town preserves German-era half-timbered and brick buildings, warehouse theatres, the Theatre Square with its Ännchen von Tharau fountain, and the sailing ship Meridianas. Ferries link the city with the Curonian Spit and with ports in Germany and Sweden. The annual Sea Festival (Jūros šventė), first held in 1934, is Lithuania's largest summer celebration.
