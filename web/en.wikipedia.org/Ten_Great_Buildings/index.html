The Ten Great Buildings (Chinese: 十大建筑) are ten public buildings constructed in Beijing in 1959 to commemorate the tenth anniversary of the founding of the People's Republic of China. Built in little more than ten months as prestige projects of the Great Leap Forward era, they transformed the capital's skyline and remain landmarks of socialist monumental architecture, combining Soviet neoclassicism, modernist planning, and traditional Chinese decorative elements.

The ten buildings

  1. Great Hall of the People — on the west side of Tiananmen Square; seat of the National People's Congress.
  2. National Museum of China (built as the Museum of Chinese History and the Museum of the Chinese Revolution) — east side of Tiananmen Square.
  3. Cultural Palace of Nationalities — theatre and museum on West Chang'an Avenue.
  4. Beijing railway station
  5. Workers' Stadium — in Chaoyang District (rebuilt 2020–2023).
  6. National Agriculture Exhibition Hall
  7. Diaoyutai State Guesthouse — for visiting heads of state.
  8. Minzu Hotel — one of Beijing's first high-rise buildings.
  9. Overseas Chinese Hotel (later demolished and rebuilt)
  10. Military Museum of the Chinese People's Revolution

Construction and legacy

The projects were initiated under Premier Zhou Enlai in late 1958; hundreds of thousands of workers — soldiers, students, and volunteers alongside professionals — laboured around the clock to finish them by the National Day celebrations of 1 October 1959. The Great Hall of the People alone was erected in about ten months. The Ten Great Buildings became enduring symbols of the new China; in 2007 a new official list of "ten great buildings" of the reform era was selected, echoing the original.
