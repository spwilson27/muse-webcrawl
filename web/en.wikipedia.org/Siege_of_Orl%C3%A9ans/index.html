Siege of Orléans

Date: 12 October 1428 – 8 May 1429
Location: Orléans, France
Result: French victory

The siege of Orléans (12 October 1428 – 8 May 1429) was a turning point in the Hundred Years' War between England and France, and the first major victory associated with Joan of Arc.

Siege

English forces under the Earl of Salisbury, and after his death the Earl of Suffolk, invested Orléans, the key to the Loire and to the unoccupied south of France loyal to the Dauphin Charles. The city, defended by Jean d'Orléans and others, was only partially blockaded but close to starvation by the spring of 1429.

Relief

Joan of Arc arrived with a relief convoy on 29 April 1429. In a series of assaults over the following days the French captured the English forts (bastilles) around the city, and on 8 May the English withdrew. The victory revived French morale and opened the Loire campaign leading to Charles VII's coronation at Reims.
