Minnesota is a state in the Upper Midwest of the United States, bordering Canada to the north, Lake Superior and Wisconsin to the east, Iowa to the south, and North and South Dakota to the west. It became the 32nd state on May 11, 1858. Its name derives from a Dakota word often rendered as "sky-tinted water."

Minnesota is famous for its lakes — more than 11,800 of them exceed ten acres — and for the headwaters of the Mississippi River at Lake Itasca. The Twin Cities metropolitan area of Minneapolis and Saint Paul contains more than half the state's population and is a major centre for business, medicine, education and the arts, home to numerous Fortune 500 companies.

Geography and climate

The state's terrain was shaped by glaciation, leaving rolling plains in the south, the forested lake country of the north, and the iron ranges of the northeast. Minnesota experiences a continental climate with cold winters and warm summers, and is known for dramatic seasonal contrasts.

Economy and culture

Historically built on fur trading, timber, flour milling and iron mining, the economy has diversified into healthcare, finance, retail, food processing and technology. Minnesotans consistently rank near the top nationally in voter turnout, educational attainment and volunteerism, and the state has a distinctive political tradition associated with the Democratic–Farmer–Labor Party. Culturally it is noted for a large Scandinavian and German heritage, its lakes-and-cabins traditions, and the often-parodied "Minnesota nice" manner.
