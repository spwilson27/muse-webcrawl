Odrysian kingdom

The Odrysian kingdom (Ancient Greek: Βασίλειον Ὀδρυσῶν) was an ancient Thracian state that existed from the early 5th century BC until its conquest by the Romans in 46 AD. It was the first and largest Thracian political entity, uniting at its height some forty tribes, and stretched between the Danube, the Black Sea, the Aegean and the Strymon river.

History

The kingdom was founded by King Teres I, who took advantage of the Persian retreat from Europe after 479 BC to unite the Odrysian and neighbouring tribes. His son Sitalces expanded the state enormously, making it a major power courted by Athens during the Peloponnesian War; Thucydides describes its revenues and army in detail. Under Cotys I (r. c. 384–360 BC) the kingdom reached another peak of wealth and Hellenisation.

Decline

After Cotys's assassination the kingdom split among three kings, and Philip II of Macedon progressively conquered Thrace in the 340s BC, reducing the Odrysians to tributary status. The kingdom nevertheless endured in the interior. In the 3rd century BC, King Seuthes III founded the Hellenistic-style capital Seuthopolis near modern Kazanlak, whose royal tomb and the richly furnished Thracian tombs of the region are famous today. The last Odrysian (Sapaean) kings ruled as Roman clients until Claudius annexed Thrace as a province in 46 AD.

Culture

Odrysian kings minted silver coinage, maintained a court influenced by Greek and Persian models, and were buried with spectacular gold and silver treasure, including the Panagyurishte and Rogozen hoards.
