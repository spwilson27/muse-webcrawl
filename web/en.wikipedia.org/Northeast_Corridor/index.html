Northeast Corridor

The Northeast Corridor (NEC) is an electrified railroad line in the Northeast megalopolis of the United States, running for 453 miles (729 km) from Boston, Massachusetts, to Washington, D.C., via Providence, New Haven, New York City, Philadelphia, and Baltimore. Owned mainly by Amtrak, it is the busiest passenger rail line in the United States.

An Acela trainset on the Northeast Corridor

History

The route was assembled from 19th-century railroads, principally the Pennsylvania Railroad (New York-Washington) and the New York, New Haven and Hartford Railroad (New York-Boston). The Pennsylvania electrified its portion in the 1930s; the Shore Line to Boston was electrified only in 2000, enabling Amtrak's Acela high-speed service. Penn Central's bankruptcy led to public takeover under the 1976 Railroad Revitalization and Regulatory Reform Act.

Services

Along with Amtrak's Acela and Northeast Regional trains, the corridor carries eight commuter railroads including the MBTA, Metro-North, NJ Transit, SEPTA, and MARC, plus freight services. It serves a region generating roughly a fifth of U.S. GDP. Decades of deferred maintenance have left a large state-of-good-repair backlog, including century-old bridges and tunnels now targeted by major federal renewal programmes.
