Natural user interface

A natural user interface (NUI) is a user interface that is designed to feel natural to the user, relying on intuitive actions such as touch, gesture, movement, and speech rather than artificial input devices like keyboards and mice. The goal is for interaction to build on everyday skills so that the interface itself becomes effectively invisible.

History

Research into gestural and tangible interfaces dates to the 1970s and 1980s, including work at the MIT Media Lab. Touch interfaces reached mass markets with multi-touch devices such as the iPhone (2007) and surface-computing tables. Microsoft's Kinect sensor (2010) popularised full-body skeletal tracking for gaming and coined much of the popular usage of the term, while voice assistants such as Siri and Alexa brought conversational interfaces to phones and smart speakers.

Modalities

  • Touch and multi-touch — direct manipulation with gestures such as pinch-to-zoom and swipe.
  • Gesture recognition — cameras or sensors track hand and body movements.
  • Speech recognition — spoken commands and dictation.
  • Gaze and brain–computer interfaces — experimental modalities using eye tracking or neural signals.

Design principles

NUI design emphasises direct manipulation, immediate feedback, and affordances learned from the physical world. Critics caution that gestures are not universally "natural" — they must still be learned, can be fatiguing ("gorilla arm"), and raise accessibility and discoverability issues.
