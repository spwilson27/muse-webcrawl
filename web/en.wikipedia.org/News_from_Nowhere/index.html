News from Nowhere

News from Nowhere (1890) is a utopian novel by William Morris, first serialized in the socialist journal Commonweal and published in book form in 1891. Its full title is News from Nowhere; or, An Epoch of Rest, Being Some Chapters from a Utopian Romance.

Plot and ideas

The narrator, William Guest, falls asleep after a meeting of the Socialist League and wakes in the year 2102. England has become a decentralized, moneyless, stateless society of free communes: factories and slums have vanished, work is pleasurable craft, and the Houses of Parliament are used to store manure. Traveling up the Thames with his guide Dick and the wise historian Old Hammond, Guest learns how a revolution and civil war ("the Great Change" of 1952) produced this pastoral commonwealth.

Significance

Written partly in response to Edward Bellamy's technocratic utopia Looking Backward, Morris's book became a classic of utopian socialism, prized for its ecological vision and its argument that fulfilling work, not machinery, is the basis of a good society.
