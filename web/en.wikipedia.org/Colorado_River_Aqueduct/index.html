Colorado River Aqueduct

Length242 miles (389 km)
BeginsLake Havasu, near Parker Dam
EndsLake Mathews, near Riverside, California
Construction1933–1941
OperatorMetropolitan Water District of Southern California

The Colorado River Aqueduct is a 242-mile (389 km) water conveyance system in Southern California that carries water from the Colorado River at Lake Havasu across the Mojave Desert to the coastal plain, where it supplies the Los Angeles metropolitan area.

Construction

Built by the Metropolitan Water District of Southern California during the Great Depression, the aqueduct was one of the largest public works projects of its era, employing some 35,000 workers. Construction ran from 1933 to 1941 and involved five pumping plants that lift the water a total of about 1,617 feet over mountain ranges, together with 92 miles of tunnels, open canals, conduits, and siphons.

Operation

Water enters at the Whitsett Intake Pumping Plant on Lake Havasu, is raised in stages across the desert, and finally passes through the tunnels beneath the San Jacinto Mountains to Lake Mathews, its western terminus reservoir. From there it is distributed to member agencies serving some 19 million people. Deliveries began in 1941, and the aqueduct remains one of the principal water supplies for urban Southern California.
