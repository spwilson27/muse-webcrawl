Villa Farnesina

The Villa Farnesina is a Renaissance suburban villa in the Trastevere district of Rome, Italy, on the Via della Lungara. Built between 1506 and 1510 for Agostino Chigi, the Pope's wealthy Sienese banker, it was designed by Baldassarre Peruzzi, Chigi's fellow Sienese, and was one of the first true villas of the Roman Renaissance.

History

The villa, originally known as the Villa Chigi, was famed for its lavish banquets and decoration. In 1579 it was purchased by Cardinal Alessandro Farnese, from whose family it takes its present name. It later passed to the Bourbons of Naples and is now owned by the Italian state and home to the Accademia Nazionale dei Lincei, which uses it for meetings and exhibitions; it is also open to the public as a museum.

Decoration

The villa contains some of the most celebrated frescoes of the High Renaissance. Raphael and his workshop painted the Loggia of Cupid and Psyche (1518–19), illustrating Apuleius' tale, and the Loggia of Galatea, where Raphael's Triumph of Galatea (1514) appears alongside Sebastiano del Piombo's Polyphemus. Peruzzi himself painted the Sala delle Prospettive with illusionistic city views, and Giovanni Antonio Bazzi (il Sodoma) decorated the main bedroom with scenes from the life of Alexander the Great.
