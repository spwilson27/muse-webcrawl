3-manifold

In mathematics, a 3-manifold is a topological space that locally looks like Euclidean 3-dimensional space: every point has a neighbourhood homeomorphic to an open subset of three-dimensional Euclidean space. The study of 3-manifolds is a major field of low-dimensional topology.

Examples

Basic examples include the 3-sphere S3, Euclidean space R3, the 3-torus obtained by gluing opposite faces of a cube, lens spaces, and the complements of knots and links in the 3-sphere.

Geometrization

William Thurston's geometrization conjecture, proved by Grigori Perelman in 2002–2003, states that every closed 3-manifold can be decomposed into pieces each carrying one of eight model geometries. Perelman's proof also resolved the century-old Poincaré conjecture as a special case.

Structures and invariants

Important tools in the subject include Heegaard splittings, Dehn surgery, hyperbolic geometry, and invariants such as the fundamental group and homology. Many 3-manifolds admit hyperbolic structures, for which Mostow rigidity applies.
