Curry–Howard correspondence

Curry–Howard correspondence
FieldMathematical logic, computer science
Named afterHaskell Curry, William Alvin Howard
SloganPropositions as types; proofs as programs

In programming language theory and proof theory, the Curry–Howard correspondence (also known as the Curry–Howard isomorphism or equivalence, or the proofs-as-programs and propositions-as-types interpretation) is the direct relationship between computer programs and mathematical proofs.

Statement

Under the correspondence, a type in a programming language is a proposition in logic, and a program inhabiting that type is a proof of the proposition. For example, intuitionistic implication corresponds to the function type, conjunction to the product type, and disjunction to the sum type.

History

Haskell Curry observed in 1934 a resemblance between combinatory logic and axioms for intuitionistic implication; William Howard made the correspondence explicit in a 1969 manuscript (published in 1980), extending it to natural deduction and the simply typed lambda calculus.

Consequences

The correspondence underlies proof assistants such as Coq and Agda, where writing a program and proving it correct are the same activity, and it connects logic with type theory and category theory.
