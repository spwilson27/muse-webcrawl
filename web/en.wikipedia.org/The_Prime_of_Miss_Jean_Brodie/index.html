The Prime of Miss Jean Brodie is a novel by Muriel Spark, first published in 1961 in The New Yorker and then in book form. It is Spark's best-known work.

Plot

Set in Edinburgh in the 1930s, the novel follows Jean Brodie, an unconventional teacher at the Marcia Blaine School for Girls, and her chosen set of pupils — "the Brodie set" — whom she educates in art, politics, and life according to her own romantic and increasingly authoritarian ideals, expressing admiration for fascist leaders. The narrative moves back and forth in time, revealing the girls' adult lives and the eventual betrayal of Brodie by one of her own pupils, Sandy Stranger.

Adaptations

The novel was adapted into a stage play and a 1969 film starring Maggie Smith, who won the Academy Award for Best Actress for the role.
