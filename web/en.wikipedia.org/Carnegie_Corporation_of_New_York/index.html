Headquarters of the Carnegie Corporation in New York City

The Carnegie Corporation of New York is a philanthropic foundation established on June 9, 1911, by industrialist and philanthropist Andrew Carnegie to support education and promote the advancement and diffusion of knowledge. It is one of the oldest American grantmaking foundations.

History

Carnegie endowed the Corporation with $125 million, making it at the time the largest single philanthropic trust ever established. The charter empowered the trustees to use the funds "to promote the advancement and diffusion of knowledge and understanding" in the United States, and later in certain British Commonwealth countries. It was the last of the major philanthropic institutions Carnegie created.

Work

Over its history the Corporation has supported the development of public libraries, adult education, public broadcasting (including early support for educational television and programming such as Sesame Street), the study of education and the social sciences, and efforts in international peace and security, including nuclear non-proliferation. In the twenty-first century its programs have focused on education, democracy and civic integration, and international peace and security.
