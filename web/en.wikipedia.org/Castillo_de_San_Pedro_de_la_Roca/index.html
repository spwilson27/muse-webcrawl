Castillo de San Pedro de la Roca

LocationSantiago de Cuba, Cuba
Built1638–1700 (main phases)
ArchitectGiovanni Battista Antonelli
TypeCoastal fortress (Morro castle)
StatusUNESCO World Heritage Site (1997)

The Castillo de San Pedro de la Roca, also known as the Castillo del Morro, is a fortress on the coast of the Cuban city of Santiago de Cuba. It stands on a rocky promontory (el morro) about 6 km southwest of the city centre, guarding the narrow entrance to the bay of Santiago de Cuba.

History

The Italian military engineer Giovanni Battista Antonelli designed the fortress in 1637, and construction began in 1638 to protect the port from pirates and rival powers, replacing an earlier smaller ravelin. Building continued in stages through the seventeenth century and was completed around 1700. The multi-level stone fortress, with its terraces, bastions, and batteries cut into the rock, is considered the most complete and best-preserved example of Spanish-American military engineering based on Italian and Renaissance design principles.

In 1662 English freebooters under Christopher Myngs sacked Santiago and partly destroyed the works, prompting reconstruction and strengthening. In 1898, during the Spanish–American War, the castle's batteries overlooked the bay where the Spanish Caribbean squadron was trapped and destroyed in the Battle of Santiago de Cuba.

Today

The fortress was restored in the twentieth century and houses a museum of piracy and naval history. In 1997 UNESCO inscribed San Pedro de la Roca Castle on the World Heritage List. A daily cannon-firing ceremony at sunset recalls its military past, and the site is one of the principal tourist attractions of eastern Cuba.
