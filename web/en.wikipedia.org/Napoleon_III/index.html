Napoleon III (Charles-Louis Napoléon Bonaparte; 20 April 1808 – 9 January 1873) was the first President of France (1848–1852) and the last monarch of France as Emperor of the French from 1852 to 1870. A nephew of Napoleon I, he was the last French monarch and founded the Second French Empire.

Rise to power

Born in Paris and raised largely in exile after 1815, Louis-Napoléon twice attempted to seize power by coup (Strasbourg 1836, Boulogne 1840) and was imprisoned in the fortress of Ham, from which he escaped in 1846. Following the Revolution of 1848 he was elected President of the Second Republic in December 1848 with a landslide of the popular vote. Barred from re-election, he staged the coup d'état of 2 December 1851, dissolved the Assembly, and a year later proclaimed the Second Empire following a plebiscite.

Reign

Napoleon III modernised the French economy, promoted railways and banking, concluded the Cobden–Chevalier free-trade treaty with Britain (1860), and oversaw Baron Haussmann's grand renovation of Paris. Abroad he fought in the Crimean War (1853–1856), supported Italian unification against Austria (1859), expanded the French colonial empire in Senegal, Indochina and elsewhere, and intervened in Mexico (1861–1867), an adventure that ended in failure with the execution of Emperor Maximilian.

Downfall

In 1870 Napoleon III was drawn into the Franco-Prussian War. On 2 September 1870 he was captured with his army at the Battle of Sedan; two days later the Empire was overthrown in Paris and the Third Republic proclaimed. Released by Prussia, he lived in exile at Camden Place, Chislehurst, England, where he died in 1873 following surgery.
