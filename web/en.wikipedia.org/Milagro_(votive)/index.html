Milagro (votive)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Milagros (Spanish for "miracles") are small religious folk charms, traditionally of metal, offered at shrines and churches in parts of the Roman Catholic world, especially in Mexico, other parts of Latin America, and the Iberian Peninsula. A milagro is presented as a votive offering in petition for, or in gratitude for, a miracle, healing, or blessing.

Forms and use

Milagros are most often cast in silver, tin, gold, or other metals in the shape of the thing they represent: a leg or arm for a healed limb, a heart for a cardiac cure or a prayer of love, an eye, a house, a child, or an animal such as a cow or pig for agricultural concerns. Devotees pin them to the garments of statues of saints, place them on altars, or attach them to crosses and retablos. Elaborate milagros may accompany painted ex-voto panels that narrate the event for which thanks is given.

Cultural significance

The practice descends from ancient Mediterranean votive traditions adapted within Catholic devotional culture. Milagros are also collected as folk art and sold in markets near pilgrimage sites; in the American Southwest they are a familiar element of religious and artistic material culture, and the heart-shaped milagro in particular has become a widely used decorative motif.
