New York City Subway

The New York City Subway is a rapid transit system owned by the City of New York and operated by the New York City Transit Authority, an agency of the Metropolitan Transportation Authority (MTA). Opened in 1904, it is one of the world's oldest and most extensive public transport systems.

History

The first underground line, built by the Interborough Rapid Transit Company (IRT), opened between City Hall and 145th Street in 1904. Rival systems run by the Brooklyn Rapid Transit (later BMT) and the city-owned Independent Subway System (IND) expanded the network under the Dual Contracts and beyond; the three were unified under city ownership in 1940.

System

The subway has 472 stations, more than any other system in the world, and most lines run 24 hours a day, every day of the year. Services are identified by letters and numbers (such as the A, 1, 7 and L trains) and run as locals and expresses on a network of largely four-track trunk lines in Manhattan. Annual ridership reached about 1.7 billion before the COVID-19 pandemic and has since been recovering.

Fares and operation

Riders pay with the MetroCard or the OMNY contactless system. The flat fare applies regardless of distance travelled.
