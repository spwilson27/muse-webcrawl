Augmented reality

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Augmented reality on a smartphone

Augmented reality (AR) is an interactive experience that combines the real world and computer-generated 3D content. The content can span multiple sensory modalities, including visual, auditory, haptic, and olfactory. AR can be defined as a system that incorporates three basic features: a combination of real and virtual worlds, real-time interaction, and accurate 3D registration of virtual and real objects.

History

Early milestones include Ivan Sutherland's head-mounted display (1968) and Myron Krueger's Videoplace installations. The term "augmented reality" is attributed to Boeing researcher Thomas Caudell in 1990. Consumer awareness grew with smartphone AR frameworks such as Apple's ARKit and Google's ARCore (2017) and with the game Pokémon Go (2016).

Technology

AR systems use cameras and sensors with simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM), depth tracking, and rendering to anchor virtual objects in the physical environment. Displays range from handheld screens to optical see-through headsets such as HoloLens and video see-through devices such as Apple Vision Pro.

Applications

Applications include navigation, retail visualisation, industrial maintenance and training, medicine, education, and entertainment. AR differs from virtual reality, which replaces the real environment entirely; both sit on Milgram's reality–virtuality continuum.
