Embargo (journalism)

In journalism and public relations, a news embargo or press embargo is a request or requirement given by a source that the information or news provided by that source not be published until a certain date or certain conditions have been met. When imposed and honoured, embargoes allow journalists time to prepare stories in advance and give sources control over the timing of announcements.

Practice

Embargoes are routine for scientific journal articles, government statistics, corporate earnings, product launches, and official speeches, which are distributed to reporters hours or days ahead of release. The understanding is informal: an outlet that breaks an embargo may be cut off from future advance material.

Controversies

Embargoes sometimes break down when one outlet publishes early, forcing others to follow; the rise of online publishing has shortened embargo discipline in some fields, and critics argue embargoes let sources manage news cycles and discourage independent reporting before a story appears.
