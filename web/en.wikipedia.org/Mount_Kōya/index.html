Mount Kōya (Japanese: 高野山, Kōya-san) is a large temple settlement in the mountains of Wakayama Prefecture, Japan, and the centre of Shingon Buddhism, an important school of Japanese Buddhism. It was founded in 816 by the monk Kūkai (posthumously Kōbō Daishi), who established the monastery of Kongōbu-ji there.

History

Kūkai received permission from Emperor Saga to establish a monastic centre in the remote, lotus-shaped plateau surrounded by eight peaks. The settlement grew into a major religious city. Kūkai's mausoleum at Okunoin, Japan's largest cemetery with some 200,000 graves and memorials beneath towering cedars, remains a premier pilgrimage site; followers believe Kūkai rests there in eternal meditation rather than having died.

Sites

Principal structures include the Kongōbu-ji head temple, the Danjo Garan sacred precinct with the Konpon Daitō great stupa, and the Reihōkan museum. Many of the roughly 120 temples offer lodging (shukubō) where visitors eat monastic vegetarian cuisine (shōjin ryōri) and join morning prayers. In 2004 Mount Kōya was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the "Sacred Sites and Pilgrimage Routes in the Kii Mountain Range".

Access

The mountain is reached by the Nankai Electric Railway to Gokurakubashi station and a cable car, followed by bus; cars are not permitted within the town itself.
