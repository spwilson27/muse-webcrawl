Sitka, Alaska

City and Borough of Sitka Location: Baranof Island, Alexander Archipelago, Alaska
Founded: 1799 (as New Archangel)
Population: approx. 8,500
Former capital of Russian America

Sitka is a unified city-borough in the southeast portion of the U.S. state of Alaska, situated on the west side of Baranof Island in the Alexander Archipelago of the Pacific Ocean. It was the capital of Russian America from 1808 to 1867 under the name New Archangel, and it was in Sitka that the formal transfer of Alaska from Russia to the United States took place on 18 October 1867.

History

The area has been occupied for thousands of years by the Tlingit people, whose clan house and village stood at the site, which they called Sheet'ká. Russian fur traders under Alexander Baranov established a post in 1799; after a Tlingit attack destroyed the first settlement in 1802, the Russians returned in 1804 and, following the Battle of Sitka, established New Archangel, which became the capital of Russian America.

Following the Alaska Purchase, Sitka served as the seat of the territory's government until 1906, when the capital moved to Juneau. The transfer ceremony of 1867 is commemorated annually on Alaska Day, 18 October.

Geography and economy

Sitka is accessible only by air and sea, being served by the Alaska Marine Highway ferry system and Sitka Rocky Gutierrez Airport. Its economy rests on commercial fishing and seafood processing, tourism, healthcare, education and government. Landmarks include St Michael's Cathedral, a Russian Orthodox cathedral rebuilt after a 1966 fire, the Sitka National Historical Park with its totem poles, and the dormant volcano Mount Edgecumbe across Sitka Sound.
