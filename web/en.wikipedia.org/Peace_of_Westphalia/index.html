Peace of Westphalia

Peace of Westphalia
Signed24 October 1648
LocationMünster and Osnabrück, Westphalia
ContextEnd of the Thirty Years' War and the Eighty Years' War

The Peace of Westphalia is the collective name for two peace agreements signed on 24 October 1648 in the Westphalian cities of Münster and Osnabrück. Together with the Treaty of Münster of January 1648 between Spain and the Dutch Republic, they ended the Thirty Years' War in the Holy Roman Empire and the Eighty Years' War between Spain and the Dutch, and brought peace to most of Europe.

Terms

The treaties recognised the independence of the Swiss Confederacy and the Dutch Republic, granted France territory in Alsace and confirmed Sweden's gains in northern Germany. Within the Empire, the settlement confirmed the right of the imperial estates to conduct their own foreign policy, extended the terms of the 1555 Peace of Augsburg to Calvinists, and fixed confessional borders largely as they stood in 1624. The Emperor's authority was correspondingly curtailed, and the Empire remained a decentralised confederation until 1806.

Significance

The peace is a landmark in diplomatic history: negotiated over several years by nearly 200 delegations, it was one of the first great multilateral congresses of Europe. In international-relations theory the "Westphalian system" is conventionally named for the settlement, associating it with the principles of state sovereignty, territorial integrity and non-intervention — although historians debate how far the 1648 treaties actually embodied those later ideas.
