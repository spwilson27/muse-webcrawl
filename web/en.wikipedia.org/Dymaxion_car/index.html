Dymaxion car

The Dymaxion car was designed by American inventor Buckminster Fuller and built in 1933 in Bridgeport, Connecticut, with naval architect Starling Burgess. Part of Fuller's broader Dymaxion philosophy of doing "more with less", the streamlined, three-wheeled vehicle was intended as the ground half of a flying automobile concept.

Design

The car had a teardrop body roughly 6 metres (20 ft) long, seated eleven passengers, and was driven by a rear-mounted Ford V8 engine powering the two front wheels; the single rear wheel steered, allowing the car to turn within its own length. Its aerodynamic aluminium body gave it a claimed fuel efficiency of about 30 miles per gallon and a top speed around 90 mph (140 km/h).

History

Three prototypes were built. The first gained publicity at the 1933 Chicago World's Fair, but a fatal crash at the fairgrounds — later attributed to another vehicle — damaged investor confidence. The project stalled and only one original prototype survives, preserved in a museum collection in Reno, Nevada.

Legacy

Although a commercial failure, the Dymaxion car became an icon of streamlined design and of Fuller's systems thinking, anticipating later interest in aerodynamic efficiency and alternative vehicle layouts.
