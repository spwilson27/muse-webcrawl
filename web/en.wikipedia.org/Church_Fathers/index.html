Church Fathers

The Church Fathers, Early Church Fathers, or Fathers of the Church were ancient and influential Christian theologians and writers who established the intellectual and doctrinal foundations of Christianity. The study of their writings is called patristics, and the period in which they flourished is traditionally divided into the Apostolic Fathers, the Ante-Nicene Fathers, and the Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers.

Apostolic Fathers

The Apostolic Fathers were theologians of the first and second centuries said to have known the apostles personally or to have been strongly influenced by them. Their writings include the Didache, the letters of Clement of Rome and Ignatius of Antioch, the Epistle of Barnabas, and the Shepherd of Hermas.

Greek and Latin Fathers

The Greek Fathers — including Athanasius of Alexandria, Basil of Caesarea, Gregory of Nyssa, Gregory of Nazianzus, and John Chrysostom — wrote in Greek and developed the doctrines of the Trinity and Christology affirmed at the councils of Nicaea and Chalcedon. The Latin Fathers — Tertullian, Cyprian, Ambrose, Jerome, and Augustine of Hippo — wrote in Latin; Augustine in particular exercised an enormous influence on Western theology. The Cappadocian Fathers are honored in both East and West.

Doctors of the Church

Certain Fathers have been given the title Doctor of the Church by the Catholic Church in recognition of their learning and sanctity. In Eastern Orthodoxy, the era of the Fathers is often held to culminate with John of Damascus in the eighth century; the Latin tradition often ends with Isidore of Seville or Bede.
