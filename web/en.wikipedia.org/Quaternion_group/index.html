Quaternion group

In group theory, the quaternion group Q8 (sometimes simply Q) is a non-abelian group of order eight, consisting of the eight elements {1, −1, i, −i, j, −j, k, −k} under multiplication defined by the quaternion relations i² = j² = k² = ijk = −1, first described by William Rowan Hamilton in 1843.

Properties

Q8 is the smallest Hamiltonian group: a non-abelian group in which every subgroup is normal. Its elements i, j, k each generate a cyclic subgroup of order four, and −1 is the unique element of order two — unusual among finite groups. By contrast, the dihedral group D4, the other non-abelian group of order eight, has five elements of order two. The quaternion group has a presentation ⟨x, y | x⁴ = 1, x² = y², yx = x⁻¹y⟩ and arises naturally as a subgroup of the multiplicative group of the quaternions and as the binary dihedral group of order 8.

Representations

Q8 has four one-dimensional representations and one faithful irreducible complex representation of dimension two, related to the Pauli matrices and to spin in physics.
