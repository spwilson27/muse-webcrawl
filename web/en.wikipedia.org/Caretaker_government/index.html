Caretaker government

A caretaker government is a temporary administration that governs a country during a transition — typically after a government has fallen, resigned or lost an election — until a new government can be formed. Caretaker governments are expected to carry out the routine business of the state but, by convention or law, to avoid major policy decisions, significant appointments and binding international commitments.

[Image: Caretaker governments administer the state during transitions between elected governments]

Conventions

In Westminster systems such as the United Kingdom, Australia, Canada and New Zealand, "caretaker conventions" apply from the dissolution of parliament (or the loss of a confidence vote) until the election result is clear and a new ministry is sworn in. Incumbent ministers remain in office but refrain from implementing new policies or making appointments that would bind their successors. The Australian caretaker period of 1975, following the dismissal of the Whitlam government, remains a notable case in which a caretaker prime minister, Malcolm Fraser, held office pending an election.

Other forms

Some countries provide constitutionally for caretaker arrangements: Bangladesh long used a non-party caretaker government to conduct elections (a system abolished in 2011), and Nepal and Pakistan have used similar mechanisms. In Belgium, a caretaker government under Yves Leterme governed for 589 days in 2010–2011 while coalition negotiations dragged on. Technocratic governments installed during crises, such as Mario Monti's in Italy, are related but distinct, since they may pursue an active policy programme with parliamentary support.
