Pilton, Somerset

Pilton is a village and civil parish in Somerset, England, situated about 3 miles (5 km) south-west of Shepton Mallet and 6 miles (10 km) east of Glastonbury. The parish includes the hamlets of West Compton, East Compton, Westholme, Beardly Batch, Kalami and Cannards Grave.

History

Pilton was recorded in the Domesday Book of 1086. The parish church of St John the Baptist dates largely from the 11th to 15th centuries and is a Grade I listed building. The village's tithe barn, once belonging to Glastonbury Abbey, is a notable 14th-century structure; it suffered a serious fire in 1963 and was subsequently restored.

Glastonbury Festival

Pilton is best known internationally as the home of the Glastonbury Festival of Contemporary Performing Arts, staged since 1970 at Worthy Farm, owned by Michael Eavis. Although named after the nearby town of Glastonbury, the festival site lies within the parish of Pilton, and the event is sometimes locally called the "Pilton Pop Festival". During festival weeks the small village's population is swelled by one of the world's largest temporary gatherings.

Governance

The parish council has responsibility for local issues, and Pilton falls within the Mendip district for local government purposes, in the ceremonial county of Somerset.
