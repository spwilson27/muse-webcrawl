Sneakers

Sneakers (US) or trainers (UK) are shoes primarily designed for sports or other forms of physical exercise, but are also widely used for everyday casual wear. They are characterized by flexible soles — traditionally rubber — and uppers of canvas, leather or synthetic materials. The name "sneakers" is often attributed to their quiet rubber soles, which let the wearer "sneak" up on someone, a term popularized in the late 19th century.

History

Rubber-soled "plimsolls" appeared in the 19th century; in 1892 the U.S. Rubber Company introduced Keds, among the first mass-marketed canvas sneakers, followed by Converse's All Star basketball shoe in 1917. Adidas and Puma were founded in Germany in the post-war years, and Nike (originally Blue Ribbon Sports) in the United States in 1964. The jogging boom of the 1970s and the rise of hip-hop and basketball culture in the 1980s transformed sneakers into global fashion items.

Culture and market

Sneakers are a major segment of the global footwear industry, worth tens of billions of dollars annually. Limited-edition releases, celebrity collaborations and resale platforms have created a collector culture ("sneakerheads"), with rare pairs resold for many times their retail price. Major brands include Nike, Adidas, New Balance, Puma and Converse.
