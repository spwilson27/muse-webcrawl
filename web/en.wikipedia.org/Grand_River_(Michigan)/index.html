Grand River (Michigan)

Length252 mi (406 km)
SourceGrand Lake, Hillsdale County, Michigan
MouthLake Michigan at Grand Haven
Basin size5,572 sq mi (14,430 km²)

The Grand River is a 252-mile-long (406 km) river in the Lower Peninsula of the U.S. state of Michigan. It is the longest river entirely within Michigan, rising in Hillsdale County in the south of the state and flowing generally westward to empty into Lake Michigan at Grand Haven.

Course

The river begins near Grand Lake in Liberty Township, Hillsdale County, and flows north through Jackson and Eaton Rapids, then turns west through Lansing, the state capital. It continues through Ionia and Lowell before reaching Grand Rapids, the largest city on the river and the second-largest city in Michigan, which was founded at a set of rapids on the river. From Grand Rapids the river flows west past Grand Haven, where it enters Lake Michigan.

History

The river was long used by Native American peoples, and its valley was an important fur-trading route. Its Ottawa name, Owashtanong, means "far-away water". In the 19th century, dams and log drives powered the lumber and furniture industries that built Grand Rapids, and a shipping channel made the lower river navigable. A sea lamprey barrier at the Sixth Street Dam in Grand Rapids limits the upstream migration of the invasive species.

Watershed

The Grand River watershed covers about 5,572 square miles, the largest in Michigan. Major tributaries include the Red Cedar, Looking Glass, Maple, Flat and Thornapple rivers.
