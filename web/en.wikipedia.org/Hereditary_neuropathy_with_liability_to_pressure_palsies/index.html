Hereditary neuropathy with liability to pressure palsies

Hereditary neuropathy with liability to pressure palsies (HNPP) is an autosomal dominant inherited peripheral nerve disorder in which affected individuals develop episodes of numbness, weakness, and muscle wasting following minor compression or trauma to a peripheral nerve. It is caused by a deletion of one copy of the PMP22 gene on chromosome 17 — the reciprocal of the duplication that causes Charcot–Marie–Tooth disease type 1A.

HNPP
Specialty: Neurology
Inheritance: Autosomal dominant
Gene: PMP22 (deletion)

Symptoms

Typical episodes include wrist drop from radial nerve compression, foot drop from peroneal nerve compression at the fibular head, and numbness in the ulnar distribution after leaning on an elbow. Most episodes recover over days to months, though repeated injury can produce cumulative deficits. Nerve conduction studies often show a background demyelinating polyneuropathy with focal slowing at entrapment sites.

Pathology

Nerve biopsy characteristically shows focal sausage-shaped thickenings of the myelin sheath known as tomacula, reflecting the reduced dosage of peripheral myelin protein 22.

Management

There is no specific drug treatment; management centers on avoiding prolonged pressure, tight casts, and awkward postures, plus protective padding and physiotherapy. See also peripheral neuropathy.
