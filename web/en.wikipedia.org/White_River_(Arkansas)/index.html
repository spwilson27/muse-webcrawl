White River (Arkansas)

[Image: White River (Arkansas)]

The White River is a 722-mile (1,162 km) long river that flows through the U.S. states of Arkansas and Missouri, emptying into the Mississippi River. It rises in the Boston Mountains of northwestern Arkansas, in Washington County, flows north into southern Missouri, then turns southeast back into Arkansas, and finally flows south to its mouth near the Arkansas Post area.

Course and dams

The river is dammed at several points, forming Beaver Lake, Table Rock Lake (in Missouri), Lake Taneycomo, and Bull Shoals Lake, as well as Norfork Lake on its tributary the North Fork River. These reservoirs, built largely by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers in the mid-20th century, provide flood control, hydroelectric power and recreation.

Fishing

The cold tailwaters below Bull Shoals and Norfork dams are famous trout fisheries; the White River is renowned for brown trout, including world-record-class fish, as well as rainbow trout. The town of Cotter, Arkansas, styles itself the "Trout Capital of the USA".

History

In the 19th century the river was an important steamboat route as far upstream as Batesville. The river gave its name to the White River National Wildlife Refuge, one of the largest remaining bottomland hardwood forests in the Mississippi valley, near its lower course.
