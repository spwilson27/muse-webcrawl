The Third United States Army is a field army of the United States Army, currently serving as the Army component of U.S. Central Command (CENTCOM) under the designation United States Army Central (ARCENT). Its headquarters is at Shaw Air Force Base, South Carolina, with a forward element in Kuwait.

History

The Third Army was first activated in France in November 1918, under Major General Joseph T. Dickman, to serve as the American occupation force in Germany after the First World War, occupying the Coblenz bridgehead until it was deactivated in 1919. Reactivated in 1932, it trained troops in the United States until 1944, when it moved to England.

Under Lieutenant General George S. Patton, the Third Army became operational in France on 1 August 1944 and conducted a rapid advance across France, liberating much of Brittany, racing to the Moselle, and fighting through the Saar. In December 1944 it famously pivoted north to relieve the besieged garrison at Bastogne during the Battle of the Bulge, then crossed the Rhine in March 1945 and drove into Germany, Austria and Czechoslovakia, liberating concentration camps including Buchenwald en route. In nine months it advanced farther and faster than any army in history to that point and captured over a million German prisoners.

After further service as an occupation and training headquarters, the Third Army was reactivated in 1982 as ARCENT. It served as the Army component for Operations Desert Shield and Desert Storm (1990–91), commanding the ground offensive that liberated Kuwait, and later supported operations in Afghanistan and Iraq, providing sustainment and theatre support throughout the CENTCOM area of responsibility.
