Lovász local lemma

In probability theory, the Lovász local lemma is a tool, introduced by Paul Erdős and László Lovász in 1975, for showing that certain events can simultaneously be avoided even when they are not fully independent. It extends the probabilistic method in combinatorics.

Statement

Roughly, if a collection of "bad" events each occurs with probability at most p, and each event depends on at most d others, then if ep(d+1) ≤ 1 (in the symmetric version), there is a positive probability that none of the bad events occurs. The lemma gives existence proofs for combinatorial objects such as graph colourings and satisfying assignments.

Algorithmic version

The Moser–Tardos algorithm (2010) turned the lemma into an efficient randomised algorithm by resampling violated events, a breakthrough recognised in subsequent awards.
