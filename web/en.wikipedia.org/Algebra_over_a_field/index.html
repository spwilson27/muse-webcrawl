Algebra over a field

AreaAbstract algebra
StructureVector space with bilinear product
ExamplesPolynomials, matrices, quaternions, Clifford algebras

In mathematics, an algebra over a field (often simply called an algebra) is a vector space equipped with a bilinear product. Thus, an algebra is an algebraic structure consisting of a set together with operations of multiplication and addition and scalar multiplication by elements of a field, satisfying the axioms implied by these operations. The multiplication operation in an algebra may or may not be associative, leading to the notions of associative algebras and non-associative algebras.

As a motivating example, the vector space of polynomials with coefficients in a field, together with polynomial multiplication, is an algebra over that field. Similarly, the set of all n × n matrices with entries in a field forms an algebra under matrix multiplication.

Definition and first consequences

Let K be a field, and let A be a vector space over K equipped with an additional binary operation of multiplication of elements of A, denoted by juxtaposition or a dot. A is an algebra over K if the multiplication is bilinear: for all scalars a, b in K and all elements x, y, z in A,

  • (x + y)·z = x·z + y·z
  • x·(y + z) = x·y + x·z
  • (a x)·(b y) = (a b)(x·y)

The dimension of A as a vector space over K is called the dimension of the algebra. An algebra is called unital or unitary if it has an identity element with respect to multiplication.

Kinds of algebras

An algebra is called associative if its multiplication satisfies (x·y)·z = x·(y·z) for all x, y, z, and commutative if x·y = y·x. Important classes include:

  • Division algebras — every nonzero element has a multiplicative inverse; by the Frobenius theorem, the only finite-dimensional associative division algebras over the real numbers are the reals, the complex numbers, and the quaternions.
  • Lie algebras — non-associative algebras satisfying antisymmetry and the Jacobi identity.
  • Clifford algebras — built from a vector space with a quadratic form, fundamental in geometry and physics.
  • Banach algebras — complete normed algebras, central in functional analysis.

Structure theorems

For finite-dimensional associative algebras, a central result is the Wedderburn–Artin theorem, which characterizes semisimple algebras as finite direct products of matrix algebras over division algebras. The Jacobson radical measures how far an algebra is from being semisimple. The classification of finite-dimensional simple algebras over an algebraically closed field reduces to matrix algebras.

Algebras and representation theory

The study of algebras is closely tied to representation theory: a representation of an algebra A is a vector space together with a homomorphism from A into the algebra of linear operators on that space. Modules over an algebra, particularly over group algebras of finite groups, are a fundamental tool in algebra and number theory.
