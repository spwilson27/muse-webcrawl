Aberconwy Abbey

Order: Cistercian

Founded: c. 1186 at Rhedynog Felen; moved to Conwy 1192

Dissolved: 16th century

Location: Conwy, Wales

Aberconwy Abbey was a Cistercian foundation in Conwy, Wales, and one of the most important religious houses of the princes of Gwynedd. Founded by monks from Strata Florida, the community first settled at Rhedynog Felen near Carnarvon before moving to the mouth of the River Conwy around 1192, under the patronage of Llywelyn ab Iorwerth (Llywelyn the Great).

History

The abbey became the traditional burial place of the princes of Gwynedd; Llywelyn the Great himself was buried there in 1240, dressed in the habit of a Cistercian monk. After Edward I conquered Wales in 1283, the monks were compelled to move inland to Maenan Abbey near Llanrwst in 1284 so that Edward could build Conwy Castle and its walled town on the site. The abbey church, however, survived in adapted form as the parish church of St Mary and All Saints, Conwy, which still contains fabric from the monastic building.

Legacy

Although little of the monastery remains above ground beyond the parish church, Aberconwy was renowned in its day for learning and for its association with Welsh princely culture; Edward I compensated the community with a grant toward their new house at Maenan.
