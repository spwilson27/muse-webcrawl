The Hulk is a superhero appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by writer Stan Lee and artist Jack Kirby, he first appeared in The Incredible Hulk #1 in May 1962. The character is the alter ego of physicist Dr. Robert Bruce Banner, who, after exposure to gamma rays during the detonation of an experimental bomb, transforms into a huge, super-strong green humanoid whenever he is angered or stressed.

Publication history

The original series was cancelled after six issues, but the character gained popularity through guest appearances and as a founding member of the Avengers. A long-running second series of The Incredible Hulk began in 1968. Writers have explored many versions of the persona, from the childlike "savage" Hulk to the cunning Grey Hulk ("Joe Fixit") and the merged "Professor" Hulk.

Fictional biography

Banner, a brilliant but withdrawn scientist, was caught in the blast of his own gamma bomb while rescuing teenager Rick Jones. The radiation altered his body so that emotional stress triggers a metamorphosis into the Hulk, whose strength grows without clear limit as his rage increases. Pursued by the military under General Thaddeus "Thunderbolt" Ross, Banner wanders in search of a cure or control, while the Hulk — often wanting only to be left alone — battles villains and heroes alike. Recent interpretations link Banner's transformations to dissociative identity arising from childhood trauma.

In other media

The character became widely known through the 1978–1982 television series starring Bill Bixby and Lou Ferrigno, and through films including Hulk (2003), The Incredible Hulk (2008), and the Marvel Cinematic Universe Avengers films, in which he is played by Mark Ruffalo.
