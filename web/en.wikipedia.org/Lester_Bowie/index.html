Lester Bowie

Lester Bowie (October 11, 1941 – November 8, 1999) was an American jazz trumpet player and composer. A member of the Association for the Advancement of Creative Musicians (AACM) and co-founder of the Art Ensemble of Chicago, Bowie was a leading figure in avant-garde jazz, known for his theatrical white laboratory coat, his half-valve and smearing techniques, and his juxtaposition of free improvisation with the whole history of jazz trumpet playing.

Born: October 11, 1941, Frederick, Maryland
Died: November 8, 1999, Brooklyn, New York
Genres: Avant-garde jazz, free jazz
Instruments: Trumpet, flugelhorn

Career

Bowie grew up in St. Louis and played in rhythm and blues bands, working as musical director for his wife Fontella Bass and touring with soul acts before joining Chicago's AACM in the mid-1960s. In 1969 he co-founded the Art Ensemble of Chicago, with which he recorded extensively for ECM, Atlantic and DIW. As a leader he recorded albums such as Numbers 1 & 2 (1967) and later formed Lester Bowie's Brass Fantasy, a brass-heavy group that reinterpreted pop and R&B songs alongside originals. He died of liver cancer in 1999.
