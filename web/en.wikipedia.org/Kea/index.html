Kea

The kea (Nestor notabilis) is a species of large parrot in the family Strigopidae found in the forested and alpine regions of the South Island of New Zealand. About 48 cm (19 in) long, it is mostly olive-green with a brilliant scarlet underwing visible in flight. The kea is the world's only alpine parrot.

Behaviour and intelligence

Kea are known for their intelligence and curiosity, both vital to their survival in a harsh mountain environment. They have been filmed and observed solving problems, working cooperatively, and using tools. Their playful, inquisitive behaviour — including investigating cars, backpacks and ski equipment — has brought them into frequent contact with people, sometimes damaging property, which has made them beloved by tourists and a nuisance to some locals.

Diet

The kea's diet consists mainly of roots, leaves, berries, nectar and insects. Birds have also been observed scavenging carrion and, controversially, attacking live sheep — a behaviour that led to a government bounty under which an estimated 150,000 kea were killed in the century after the 1860s before the species received full protection in 1986.

Conservation

The kea is now classified as endangered, with only a few thousand individuals remaining. Threats include predation by introduced stoats, possums and cats, lead poisoning from building materials, and conflict with humans. Conservation programmes, including predator control and captive research, are coordinated by the Department of Conservation and the Kea Conservation Trust.
