Terrace (agriculture)

Terraced fields
PurposeCultivation of sloped land, erosion control, water management
Famous examplesBanaue Rice Terraces (Philippines), Inca terraces (Peru), Yuanyang terraces (China)

In agriculture, a terrace is a piece of sloped plane that has been cut into a series of successively receding flat surfaces or platforms, resembling steps, for the purposes of more effective farming. This type of landscaping is called terracing. Terraced fields reduce both erosion and surface runoff, slow and retain irrigation water, and make it possible to cultivate steep slopes that would otherwise be unusable.

History and distribution

Terracing is ancient and worldwide: the rice terraces of the Philippine Cordilleras, the Inca and pre-Inca andenes of the Andes, the terraced hillsides of Yemen, Nepal, Vietnam, and China's Yuanyang County, and the dry-stone terraces of the Mediterranean all exemplify the technique. Graduated terrace steps are commonly used to farm on hilly or mountainous terrain in both the Old and New Worlds.

Types

Terraces include bench terraces with level platforms retained by stone or earth walls, contour terraces following the slope's natural lines, and broad-base terraces used in mechanized farming to manage runoff. Irrigated paddy terraces add bunds and channels to hold and distribute water from step to step.

Modern use

Terracing remains a key soil-conservation practice, promoted in modern watershed management to reduce erosion and sediment loss. Abandoned terraces, if their retaining walls collapse, can cause increased erosion and landslides, so maintenance is essential.
