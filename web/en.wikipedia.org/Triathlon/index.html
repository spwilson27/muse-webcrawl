Triathlon

A triathlon is an endurance multisport race consisting of swimming, cycling and running over various distances. Triathletes compete for the fastest overall completion time, racing each segment sequentially with the time transitioning between the disciplines included in the total event time. The word derives from the Greek treis (three) and athlos (competition).

History

Multi-sport races involving swimming, cycling and running appeared in France in the 1920s under names such as Les Trois Sports. The modern sport began in San Diego, California, in 1974, when members of a local track club organised a race combining a run, a bike leg and a swim. The first Ironman event was held in Hawaii in 1978. The International Triathlon Union (now World Triathlon) was founded in 1989, and triathlon made its Olympic debut at the Sydney Games in 2000.

Distances

Common formats include the sprint (750 m swim, 20 km bike, 5 km run), the Olympic or standard distance (1.5 km swim, 40 km bike, 10 km run), half-Ironman or middle distance (1.9 km swim, 90 km bike, 21.1 km run), and the full Ironman distance (3.8 km swim, 180 km bike, 42.2 km marathon run). Drafting on the bike is permitted in elite short-course racing but generally forbidden in age-group and long-distance events.

Transitions

The periods between the swim and bike (T1) and between bike and run (T2) are sometimes called the "fourth discipline"; athletes practise changing equipment rapidly, and elite races can be decided by seconds gained or lost in transition.
