Yemeni Civil War (2014–present)

The Yemeni Civil War (Arabic: الحرب الأهلية اليمنية) is an ongoing multilateral civil war that began in late 2014, principally between the internationally recognised government of Yemen and the Houthi movement (Ansar Allah), which seized the capital Sanaa. A Saudi-led coalition intervened militarily in March 2015 in support of the government. The war has produced one of the world's worst humanitarian crises.

Background

Yemen, unified only in 1990, was left unstable by the 2011 uprising that forced President Ali Abdullah Saleh to hand power to Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi. The Houthis, a Zaydi Shia movement from the northern Saada governorate that had fought the government in the 2000s, exploited discontent over fuel prices and the draft constitution. In September 2014 they captured Sanaa; in early 2015 Hadi fled to Aden and then to Riyadh.

Saudi-led intervention

On 26 March 2015 Saudi Arabia and allies (including the UAE) launched airstrikes and later ground operations to restore the Hadi government, imposing a blockade that devastated Yemen's import-dependent economy. The war became a regional proxy conflict, with Saudi Arabia accusing Iran of arming the Houthis. Fighting also involved the UAE-backed Southern Transitional Council, which seeks southern secession and clashed with government forces in Aden.

Humanitarian crisis and truce efforts

The UN has described Yemen as the world's worst humanitarian crisis: the UNDP estimated some 377,000 deaths by the end of 2021 (most indirect, from hunger and disease), with millions displaced and dependent on aid, alongside the largest recorded cholera outbreak. A UN-brokered nationwide truce from April 2022 lapsed in October 2022 but largely held de facto; from late 2023 Houthi attacks on Red Sea shipping, launched in response to the Gaza war, drew US and UK airstrikes and further complicated peace efforts.
