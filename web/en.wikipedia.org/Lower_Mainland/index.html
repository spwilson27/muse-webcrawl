The Lower Mainland is a geographic and cultural region of the mainland coast of British Columbia, Canada, centred on the lowlands of the lower Fraser River. It encompasses the Metro Vancouver Regional District and the Fraser Valley Regional District — including the cities of Vancouver, Surrey, Burnaby, Richmond, Abbotsford, Langley, Coquitlam, Delta, Chilliwack and Mission — and is home to roughly three-fifths of the province's population. The name distinguishes the region from the "Upper Mainland" farther up the coast and from Vancouver Island, and is used colloquially by British Columbians even though it has no single official boundary.

Geography

The region occupies a low-lying deltaic and floodplain landscape built up by the Fraser River where it meets the Strait of Georgia, bounded by the Coast Mountains to the north, the Cascade Mountains to the east, and the Canada–United States border to the south. Its mild, rainy oceanic climate — moderated by the Pacific — is the warmest in Canada in winter. Much of the Fraser Valley floor is rich agricultural land protected by the Agricultural Land Reserve, producing dairy, poultry, berries (notably blueberries and cranberries) and vegetables.

History

The Lower Mainland is the traditional territory of Coast Salish peoples, including the Musqueam, Squamish, Tsleil-Waututh, Stó:lō, Tsawwassen and Semiahmoo nations, whose presence dates back thousands of years. European settlement followed the Fraser Canyon gold rush of 1858; the colony of British Columbia was proclaimed at Fort Langley, and New Westminster became its first capital. The completion of the Canadian Pacific Railway in 1885–86, with Vancouver as its Pacific terminus, shifted the region's centre to Burrard Inlet. Twentieth-century suburbanisation spread development eastward through the Fraser Valley.

Economy and society

Greater Vancouver is Canada's third-largest metropolitan economy, anchored by the Port of Vancouver (the country's largest port), film and television production ("Hollywood North"), technology, tourism and trade with Asia. The region is among the most ethnically diverse in Canada, with large South Asian communities in Surrey and Delta and large Chinese communities in Richmond and Vancouver. Governance is fragmented among more than twenty municipalities coordinated in part by the Metro Vancouver regional district and the TransLink transit authority.
