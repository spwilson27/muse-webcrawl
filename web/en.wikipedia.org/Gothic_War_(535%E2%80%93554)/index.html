The Gothic War between the Eastern Roman (Byzantine) Empire and the Ostrogothic Kingdom of Italy took place from 535 to 554. It was part of Emperor Justinian I's attempt to reconquer the western provinces lost a century earlier, and is one of the longest and most destructive wars of late antiquity, described in detail by the historian Procopius.

Course of the war

The war began with a Byzantine invasion in 535. General Belisarius quickly took Sicily, Naples, and Rome (536), and by 540 had captured Ravenna and King Vitiges. However, under the capable King Totila the Goths recovered most of Italy, sacking Rome in 546 and 550. Justinian then sent the aged general Narses with a large army, who defeated and killed Totila at the Battle of Taginae (552) and his successor Teia at Mons Lactarius (552/553). Remaining Gothic resistance north of the Po ended by 554, though Frankish invaders also had to be defeated.

Consequences

Two decades of warfare, plague, and famine devastated Italy: Rome was besieged and emptied several times, and Milan was destroyed. Although Italy was restored to the empire, it was exhausted and vulnerable; the Lombards invaded in 568 and soon conquered much of the peninsula.
