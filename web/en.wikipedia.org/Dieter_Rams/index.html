Dieter Rams (born 20 May 1932) is a German industrial designer widely considered one of the most influential designers of the 20th century. As head of design at Braun from 1961 to 1995, he shaped the company's clean, functional product aesthetic, and his work for the furniture company Vitsoe, including the 606 Universal Shelving System (1960), remains in production.

Career

Trained as an architect, Rams joined Braun in 1955, initially working on interiors before turning to product design. With the Ulm School of Design (HfG Ulm) collaborators, he developed products such as the SK 4 record player (1956, with Hans Gugelot), nicknamed "Snow White's coffin" for its clear acrylic lid; the ET 66 calculator (1987, with Dietrich Lubs); and the T 1000 world receiver. His approach extended the functionalist tradition of the Bauhaus into postwar consumer electronics.

Ten principles of good design

Rams articulated ten principles: good design is innovative; makes a product useful; is aesthetic; makes a product understandable; is unobtrusive; is honest; is long-lasting; is thorough down to the last detail; is environmentally friendly; and is "as little design as possible" — the maxim Weniger, aber besser ("less, but better").

Influence

Rams's work profoundly influenced Apple designer Jony Ive and a generation of minimal product and interface designers. He is the subject of the 2018 documentary Rams by Gary Hustwit. In later years Rams has advocated greater sustainability and restraint in consumption.
