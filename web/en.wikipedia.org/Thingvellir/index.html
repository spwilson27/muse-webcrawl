Þingvellir (anglicised as Thingvellir) is a national park in the municipality of Bláskógabyggð in southwestern Iceland, about 40 km northeast of Reykjavík. It is a site of historical, cultural and geological significance, best known as the location of the Althing, Iceland's parliament, from 930 to 1798.

History

The Althing, one of the world's oldest surviving parliaments, was founded at Þingvellir ("assembly fields") in 930 AD, where chieftains and their followers gathered each summer to settle disputes, recite the law at the Lögberg (Law Rock), and trade. Major events of Icelandic history took place here, including the adoption of Christianity around 1000 AD and the proclamation of the Republic of Iceland on 17 June 1944.

Geology

Þingvellir lies in a rift valley marking the crest of the Mid-Atlantic Ridge, where the North American and Eurasian tectonic plates are drifting apart by a few millimetres to centimetres per year. The area features dramatic fissures such as Almannagjá and the crystal-clear Silfra fissure, a popular diving site, and borders Þingvallavatn, Iceland's largest natural lake.
