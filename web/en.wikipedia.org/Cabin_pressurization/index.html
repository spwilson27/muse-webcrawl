Cabin pressurization

Cabin pressurization is a process in which conditioned air is pumped into the cabin of an aircraft or spacecraft in order to create a safe and comfortable environment for passengers and crew at high altitudes, where outside air pressure is too low for unaided human survival.

How it works

On jet airliners, bleed air from the engines is cooled and supplied to the cabin, while an outflow valve regulates pressure so that the cabin altitude typically remains between 6,000 and 8,000 feet even when the aircraft cruises at 35,000–43,000 feet. The fuselage is built as a pressure vessel to withstand the differential pressure.

History and safety

The first pressurised airliner was the Boeing 307 Stratoliner of 1938. Pressurisation failures and structural fatigue were factors in the de Havilland Comet accidents of the 1950s. Modern aircraft warn crews of excessive cabin altitude; slow decompression, as in the Helios Airways Flight 522 accident in 2005, can cause hypoxia before occupants realise the danger. Sudden failure triggers automatic deployment of oxygen masks.
