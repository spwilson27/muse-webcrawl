Rosacea

Rosacea is a long-term skin condition that typically affects the central face, characterised by redness, flushing, visible small blood vessels (telangiectasia), and sometimes acne-like papules and pustules. It most often appears after age 30 and is more common in people with fair skin.

Signs and subtypes

Four subtypes are traditionally recognised: erythematotelangiectatic (persistent redness and flushing), papulopustular (bumps and pimples), phymatous (skin thickening, classically rhinophyma of the nose), and ocular rosacea affecting the eyes. Modern classification is increasingly based on observable features ("phenotypes") rather than fixed subtypes.

Causes and triggers

The cause is not fully understood; genetics, immune system responses, Demodex mites, and neurovascular dysregulation are implicated. Common triggers include sunlight, heat, spicy foods, alcohol, and hot drinks.

Treatment

There is no cure, but symptoms can be controlled with trigger avoidance, gentle skin care, sunscreen, topical agents such as metronidazole, azelaic acid, and ivermectin, oral antibiotics such as doxycycline, and laser or light therapy for visible vessels.
