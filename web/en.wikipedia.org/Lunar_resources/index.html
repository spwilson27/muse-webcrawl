Lunar resources
Key materials: water ice, oxygen, silicon, iron, titanium, helium-3
Related concept: In-situ resource utilisation (ISRU)

The Moon may have resources that could be exploited for human exploration and settlement, including water ice in permanently shadowed polar craters, oxygen bound in regolith, metals, and solar energy at near-continuously illuminated polar peaks.

Water ice

Data from Lunar Prospector, Chandrayaan-1 and LCROSS indicate significant deposits of water ice in shadowed craters near the poles. Such ice could provide drinking water, breathable oxygen and hydrogen–oxygen rocket propellant.

Regolith and helium-3

Lunar regolith contains oxygen, silicon, iron, calcium, aluminium and titanium. Solar-wind implantation has also deposited helium-3, sometimes proposed as a fuel for future fusion reactors, though its practical use remains speculative.

Legal status

The 1967 Outer Space Treaty prohibits national appropriation of the Moon; the extent to which it permits resource extraction is debated, and the 1979 Moon Treaty has not been ratified by any spacefaring state.
