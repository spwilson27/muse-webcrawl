Sine wave

A sine wave, or sinusoid, is a periodic wave whose waveform (shape) is the trigonometric sine function. In mechanics, as a linear motion over time, this is simple harmonic motion; as rotation, it corresponds to uniform circular motion. Sine waves occur often in physics, including ocean waves, sound waves, and light waves such as monochromatic radiation. In engineering, signal processing, and mathematics, Fourier analysis decomposes general functions into a sum of sine waves of various frequencies, relative phases, and magnitudes.

General form

A sine wave may be written as y(t) = A sin(ωt − φ) + D, where A is the amplitude (the peak deviation from zero), ω is the angular frequency, φ is the phase, and D is a vertical offset. Its period is 2π/ω and its frequency is ω/2π cycles per unit time.

Properties

The sine wave is important in physics because it retains its waveshape when added to another sine wave of the same frequency and arbitrary phase and magnitude. It is the only periodic waveform that has this property, which makes it central to Fourier analysis. Sinusoids are also the steady-state response of linear systems to sinusoidal inputs, underpinning the analysis of alternating-current circuits and acoustics. A pure sine wave in sound is a simple tone with a single frequency.
