Metrology

Metrology is the scientific study of measurement. It establishes a common understanding of units, which is crucial to link human activities. Modern metrology has its roots in the French Revolution's political goal to standardise units, leading to the metric system and the Metre Convention of 1875, which created the International Bureau of Weights and Measures (BIPM).

Fields

Metrology is divided into three basic overlapping activities: defining units of measurement, realising these units in practice, and traceability — linking measurements made in practice to reference standards. Its subfields include scientific (fundamental) metrology, applied or industrial metrology, and legal metrology, which concerns regulatory requirements.

Traceability and calibration

Traceability is the property of a measurement whereby the result can be related to a reference through a documented unbroken chain of calibrations, each contributing to measurement uncertainty. National metrology institutes, such as NIST in the United States and NPL in the United Kingdom, maintain primary standards.

The SI redefinition

In 2019, the International System of Units was redefined so that all seven base units are defined in terms of fundamental physical constants, including the speed of light and the Planck constant, eliminating dependence on physical artefacts such as the International Prototype of the Kilogram.
