Independence Day (United States)

Independence Day, commonly known as the Fourth of July, is a federal holiday in the United States commemorating the adoption of the Declaration of Independence on July 4, 1776, by which the thirteen American colonies declared themselves independent of the Kingdom of Great Britain. It is the national day of the United States.

History

The Continental Congress voted for independence on July 2, 1776 — the date John Adams expected posterity to celebrate — and adopted the Declaration, drafted principally by Thomas Jefferson, two days later. The first anniversary was marked in 1777 with bonfires, bells, and fireworks in Philadelphia. Massachusetts recognized the holiday by statute in 1781, Congress made it a holiday for federal employees in 1870, and it became a paid federal holiday in 1941.

Observance

The day is celebrated with fireworks displays, parades, barbecues and picnics, concerts, baseball games, and patriotic speeches and ceremonies. Landmark displays include the Boston Pops Fireworks Spectacular and the show over the National Mall in Washington, D.C. Many communities hold hot-dog eating contests, most famously Nathan's Hot Dog Eating Contest at Coney Island. In 2026 the United States marked its semiquincentennial, the 250th anniversary of independence.
