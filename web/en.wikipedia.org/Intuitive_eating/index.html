Intuitive eating

Intuitive eating is an approach to eating that focuses on the body's response to cues of hunger and satiety. It is an adaptive form of eating characterized by a strong connection with internal physiological hunger and satiety cues. The concept was popularized by dietitians Evelyn Tribole and Elyse Resch, whose book Intuitive Eating was first published in 1995.

Principles

Tribole and Resch set out ten principles: rejecting the diet mentality; honoring hunger; making peace with food; challenging the "food police"; discovering the satisfaction factor; feeling fullness; coping with emotions with kindness; respecting the body; joyful movement; and honoring health with gentle nutrition. The approach rejects weight loss as a primary goal and opposes labeling foods as "good" or "bad".

Research

Studies associate intuitive eating with lower body mass index, better psychological well-being, and fewer disordered eating symptoms, though much of the research is correlational. The approach has been incorporated into some eating disorder treatments and is related to mindful eating and the Health at Every Size framework.
