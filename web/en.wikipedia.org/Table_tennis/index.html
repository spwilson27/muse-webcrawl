Table tennis, also known as ping-pong, is a racket sport derived from tennis but distinguished by its playing surface being atop a stationary table, rather than the court on which players stand. It is played by two or four players who hit a lightweight ball, also known as the ping-pong ball, back and forth across a table using small rackets.

History

The sport originated in Victorian England, where it was played among the upper class as an after-dinner parlour game. Early versions used books as rackets and a golf ball. The name "ping-pong" was trademarked in 1901, and the International Table Tennis Federation (ITTF) was founded in 1926.

Rules

A game is won by the player who first reaches 11 points, provided there is a two-point lead. Players serve two points each in turn. The ball must bounce once on the opponent's side after crossing the net, and rallies can reach very high speeds, with elite players imparting heavy spin.

Equipment

The table is 2.74 m long, 1.525 m wide and 76 cm high, divided by a 15.25 cm net. The ball is 40 mm in diameter, made of plastic, and rackets are wooden blades covered with rubber.
