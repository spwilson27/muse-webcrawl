Ivan Konev

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ivan Stepanovich Konev (28 December 1897 – 21 May 1973) was a Soviet general and Marshal of the Soviet Union who led Red Army forces on the Eastern Front during World War II, responsible for taking much of Axis-occupied Eastern and Central Europe.

World War II

Konev commanded the Kalinin Front in the Battle of Moscow, then the Steppe Front in the Battle of Kursk and the advance to the Dnieper. In 1944 his 1st Ukrainian Front broke through in the Lvov–Sandomierz Offensive and liberated much of Poland; in 1945 his forces raced Zhukov's to Berlin, captured Prague, and linked up with American forces at Torgau on the Elbe.

Postwar career

After the war Konev served as commander of Soviet ground forces, commanded the Warsaw Pact forces, and directed the suppression of the Hungarian Revolution of 1956. He was twice named Hero of the Soviet Union and is buried in the Kremlin Wall Necropolis.
