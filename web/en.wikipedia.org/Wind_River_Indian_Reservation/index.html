Wind River Indian Reservation

The Wind River Indian Reservation is a Native American reservation in west-central Wyoming, shared by the Eastern Shoshone and the Northern Arapaho tribes. Established by treaty in 1863 and reduced in later agreements, it covers more than 2.2 million acres in Fremont and Hot Springs counties, making it one of the largest reservations in the United States.

History

The 1863 Treaty of Fort Bridger set aside a vast territory for the Eastern Shoshone under Chief Washakie. An 1868 treaty reduced it, and in 1878 the Northern Arapaho — who had no reservation of their own — were placed on Wind River, an arrangement later confirmed by compensation payments. The grave of Sacagawea, the Shoshone guide of the Lewis and Clark Expedition, is traditionally located near Fort Washakie.

Geography and communities

The reservation spans sagebrush basins, the Owl Creek Mountains, and the foothills of the Wind River Range. Principal communities include Fort Washakie (seat of Eastern Shoshone government), Ethete and Arapahoe (Northern Arapaho), and the town of Riverton, which lies within the historic reservation boundaries but is largely non-Native. The Wind River and its tributaries provide irrigation and water rights affirmed in major litigation.

Government and economy

Each tribe governs itself through an elected business council; together they operate joint programs and enterprises including the Wind River Casino. Oil, gas, ranching, and tourism contribute to the economy, while poverty and the legacy of allotment-era land loss remain significant challenges.
