Citizens United v. Federal Election Commission, 558 U.S. 310 (2010), is a landmark decision of the Supreme Court of the United States holding that the First Amendment prohibits the government from restricting independent expenditures for political communications by corporations, labor unions, and other associations.

Background

Citizens United, a conservative nonprofit corporation, sought to air Hillary: The Movie, a documentary critical of Senator Hillary Clinton, during the 2008 Democratic primaries. Provisions of the Bipartisan Campaign Reform Act (McCain–Feingold) restricted such electioneering communications funded from corporate treasuries.

Decision

By 5–4, the Court held that corporate funding of independent political broadcasts cannot be limited, overruling Austin v. Michigan Chamber of Commerce (1990) and part of McConnell v. FEC (2003). Justice Kennedy's majority opinion reasoned that political speech is protected regardless of the speaker's corporate identity. Justice Stevens' dissent warned of corporate domination of the political process.

Impact

Together with SpeechNow.org v. FEC, the decision enabled the rise of "super PACs" — committees that may accept unlimited contributions for independent expenditures — and greatly increased spending by corporations, unions, and wealthy individuals in U.S. elections. The ruling remains highly controversial and a focus of campaign-finance reform debates.
