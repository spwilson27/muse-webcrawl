Cold chain

A cold chain is a temperature-controlled supply chain comprising refrigerated production, storage, and distribution activities, along with associated equipment and logistics, which maintain quality through a desired low-temperature range. An unbroken cold chain is an uninterrupted series of refrigerated storage and distribution activities that maintain a given temperature range. It is used to preserve and extend the shelf life of products such as fresh agricultural produce, seafood, frozen food, photographic film, chemicals, and pharmaceutical drugs, including vaccines.

Uses

Cold chains are central to the global food trade, allowing perishable goods to be transported long distances. In medicine, many vaccines must be kept between 2 °C and 8 °C from manufacture to administration; some, such as certain COVID-19 mRNA vaccines, required ultra-low temperatures around −70 °C, creating major logistical challenges during the pandemic. Temperature monitoring devices, data loggers, and time–temperature indicators are used to verify that the chain has not been broken.

Breaks in the chain

Excursions outside the specified range can degrade products even if temperatures later return to specification, and such breaks may not be visible. Significant food loss worldwide is attributed to inadequate cold chain infrastructure, particularly in developing countries, while in developed countries energy use and refrigerant emissions are environmental concerns.
