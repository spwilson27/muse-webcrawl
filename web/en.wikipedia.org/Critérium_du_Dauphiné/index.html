The Critérium du Dauphiné, before 2010 known as the Critérium du Dauphiné Libéré, is an annual cycling road race in the Dauphiné region of south-eastern France. It has been held since 1947 and, running over eight stages in June, is widely regarded as the principal preparation race for the Tour de France, which starts a few weeks later.

History

The race was created in 1947 by Georges Cazeneuve and organised by the newspaper Le Dauphiné libéré, whose name it bore until 2009, when the Amaury Sport Organisation took over. The mountainous course in the French Alps frequently previews Tour de France climbs such as Alpe d'Huez, the Col du Galibier and Mont Ventoux.

Winners

Notable winners include Jacques Anquetil, Eddy Merckx, Bernard Hinault, Miguel Indurain and Chris Froome; Bradley Wiggins won in 2011 and 2012 before his Tour victories. Primož Roglič won in 2022 and 2024, and Tadej Pogačar won the 2025 edition. Because the race tests climbing and time-trialling three weeks before the Tour, its winner's jersey — yellow with a blue band — is among the most coveted in stage racing.
