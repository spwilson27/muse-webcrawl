Citrus taxonomy

Citrus taxonomy is the botanical classification of plants in the genus Citrus and related genera in the rue family, Rutaceae. It is notoriously complex because citrus plants hybridise readily, reproduce asexually by apomixis (nucellar embryony), and have been cultivated and spread by humans for millennia.

Ancestral species

Genomic studies published in 2018 traced the origin of citrus to the foothills of the eastern Himalayas. Most cultivated citrus derive primarily from a handful of ancestral ("papyraceous") species, notably:

  • Citrus medica — the citron
  • Citrus maxima — the pomelo
  • Citrus reticulata — the mandarin orange
  • Citrus micrantha — the papeda group, ancestor of some limes

Familiar fruits such as the sweet orange (a pomelo × mandarin hybrid), lemon (citron × bitter orange), grapefruit (pomelo × sweet orange) and key lime are all hybrid taxa. Classification systems by Swingle and by Tanaka differ greatly in the number of recognised species, from 16 to over 150.
