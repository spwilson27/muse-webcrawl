Morena (Spanish: Movimiento Regeneración Nacional, "National Regeneration Movement") is a left-wing political party in Mexico. It was founded in 2011 as a civil association by Andrés Manuel López Obrador following his second presidential campaign, and registered as a political party in 2014. Within a decade it became Mexico's dominant political force.

History

López Obrador, twice the presidential candidate of the Party of the Democratic Revolution (PRD), broke with that party after the disputed 2012 election and built Morena as a mass movement organised around opposition to corruption and neoliberal economic policy. In the 2018 general election, Morena's coalition won the presidency in a landslide, together with majorities in both chambers of Congress and numerous governorships.

In government

The government of López Obrador (2018–2024) described its programme as the "Fourth Transformation" of Mexico, emphasising social welfare programmes, austerity in public administration, large infrastructure projects, and an expanded role for state energy companies. In 2024 Morena's candidate Claudia Sheinbaum won the presidential election, becoming Mexico's first woman president, and the party retained commanding congressional majorities.

Ideology

Morena combines left-wing economic populism, Mexican nationalism and moral rhetoric against corruption. Its platform includes universal pensions, scholarships, labour reform and energy sovereignty, while socially it has been comparatively cautious.
