Kimberlé Williams Crenshaw (born 5 May 1959) is an American civil rights advocate and legal scholar, a professor at the UCLA School of Law and Columbia Law School. She is best known for coining the term intersectionality and for foundational work in critical race theory and Black feminist legal theory.

Education and career

Crenshaw was born in Canton, Ohio, and graduated from Cornell University in 1981 before earning her J.D. from Harvard Law School in 1984 and an LL.M. from the University of Wisconsin Law School, where she was a William H. Hastie Fellow. While at Harvard she was a founding organiser of the Critical Race Theory Workshop. She joined the UCLA law faculty in 1986 and later also Columbia. She co-founded the African American Policy Forum, which she directs, and hosts the podcast Intersectionality Matters!

Intersectionality

In her 1989 article "Demarginalizing the Intersection of Race and Sex" and a 1991 follow-up, "Mapping the Margins", Crenshaw argued that anti-discrimination law, feminist theory, and antiracist politics each operate on a "single-axis framework" that erases people — particularly Black women — whose experiences sit at the intersection of multiple forms of subordination, using a traffic-intersection metaphor: discrimination, like traffic, can flow from several directions at once. The concept has since spread across the social sciences, humanities, and public policy worldwide.

Other work

Crenshaw served on the legal team representing Anita Hill during the 1991 Clarence Thomas confirmation hearings. She co-authored reports on Black girls' school discipline and the #SayHerName campaign (2014), which highlights police violence against Black women. Her scholarship has received numerous honours, and her work has also been at the centre of political controversies over critical race theory in the United States.
