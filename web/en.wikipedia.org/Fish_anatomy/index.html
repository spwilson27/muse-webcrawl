Fish anatomy

Fish anatomy is the study of the form or morphology of fish. It can be contrasted with fish physiology, which is the study of how the component parts of fish function together. In practice, fish anatomy and physiology complement each other, the former dealing with the structure of a fish, its organs and organ systems, and how they are put together, the latter with how those components function.

Body

The body of a fish is divided into a head, trunk and tail, although the divisions are not always externally visible. The body is often fusiform, a streamlined body plan often found in fast-moving fish. Fish may also be filiform (eel-shaped), vermiform (worm-shaped), laterally compressed (flat from side to side) or depressed (flat from top to bottom).

Fins

Fins are the most distinctive features of a fish, composed of bony spines protruding from the body with skin covering them and joining them together. Types include the dorsal fin, caudal (tail) fin, anal fin, paired pectoral fins and paired pelvic fins. Fins are used for propulsion, stability and steering.

Skeleton

The skeleton of a fish may be made of cartilage (cartilaginous fishes) or bone (bony fishes). The main features of the fish skeleton are the vertebral column, skull, jaw, ribs and intramuscular bones.

Gills and respiration

Most fish exchange gases by using gills on either side of the pharynx. Gills consist of threadlike structures called filaments, richly supplied with blood vessels, which give them a bright red appearance. Water taken in through the mouth passes over the gills, where oxygen is absorbed and carbon dioxide released.

Swim bladder

Many bony fish possess a swim bladder, a gas-filled internal organ that contributes to buoyancy, allowing the fish to maintain depth without expending energy in swimming.
