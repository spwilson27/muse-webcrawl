Guillaume Amontons


Illustration: Guillaume Amontons

Guillaume Amontons (31 August 1663 – 11 October 1705) was a French scientific instrument inventor and physicist. His name is chiefly associated with the laws of friction that bear his name.

Work

Amontons, deaf from an early age, taught himself science and invented improved barometers, hygrometers and thermometers, including an air thermometer using the pressure of heated air. In 1699 he published his investigations of friction, stating that the force of friction is directly proportional to the load and independent of the apparent area of contact — laws later confirmed by Coulomb.

Gas laws

Amontons also observed that the pressure of a fixed mass of gas rises in proportion to its temperature, extrapolating toward an absolute zero of temperature — work that anticipated the pressure law of gases later credited to Gay-Lussac. See also Heike Kamerlingh Onnes for later low-temperature physics.
