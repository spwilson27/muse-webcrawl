Hanuman (Sanskrit: हनुमान्, IAST: Hanumān), also known as Maruti, Bajrangbali and Anjaneya, is a Hindu god and a divine vanara (monkey) companion and devotee of the god Rama. He is one of the central figures of the epic Ramayana and a symbol of strength, devotion and selfless service (seva), and is counted among the seven immortal Chiranjeevi.

In the Ramayana

Hanuman leads the search for Rama's abducted wife Sita, discovering her in Lanka, where he confronts the demon king Ravana and sets the city ablaze after being captured and his tail set on fire. In the ensuing war he flies to the Himalayas to fetch the healing herb sanjeevani, carrying back the entire mountain when he cannot identify the plant. His feats include leaping across the ocean to Lanka and expanding or shrinking his form at will.

Worship

Hanuman is worshipped across India and the Hindu diaspora, often in temples dedicated to him or alongside Rama and Sita; Tuesdays and Saturdays are considered his days. The devotional hymn Hanuman Chalisa, composed by the 16th-century saint Tulsidas, is among the most recited texts in Hinduism. Hanuman also appears in Jain and Buddhist traditions and, in Southeast Asian versions of the Ramayana, in Thai and Khmer art.
