The Black Sea Fleet is the fleet of the Russian Navy in the Black Sea, the Sea of Azov and the Mediterranean Sea. Founded on 13 May 1783 by Prince Grigory Potemkin for the Russian Empire, it has its historic headquarters at Sevastopol in Crimea. The fleet traces its lineage through the Imperial Russian and Soviet navies; after the dissolution of the USSR in 1991 its ships and facilities were divided between Russia and Ukraine under agreements reached in 1997, with Russia leasing its Sevastopol bases — an arrangement overtaken by Russia's annexation of Crimea in 2014, which Ukraine and most states do not recognise.

History

The fleet fought the Ottoman navy in the Russo-Turkish wars, won the Battle of Sinop in 1853, and was scuttled during the Siege of Sevastopol in the Crimean War. It mutinied in 1905, centred on the battleship Potemkin. In the Second World War it supported the defences of Odessa and Sevastopol and the Kerch and Novorossiysk operations. During the Cold War it was a major Soviet fleet, operating through the Turkish Straits into the Mediterranean under the terms of the Montreux Convention.

Recent operations

The fleet played a role in the 2008 Russo-Georgian War, the annexation of Crimea in 2014, and Russia's intervention in Syria, launching Kalibr cruise-missile strikes from the eastern Mediterranean. During the full-scale invasion of Ukraine beginning in 2022, the fleet suffered significant losses, including its flagship, the cruiser Moskva, sunk in April 2022, and later attacks by Ukrainian missiles and naval drones on ships and the Sevastopol headquarters, prompting the relocation of many vessels to Novorossiysk.
