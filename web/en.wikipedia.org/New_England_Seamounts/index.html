New England Seamounts

The New England Seamounts are a chain of extinct underwater volcanoes, or seamounts, extending about 1,000 km from the coast of Massachusetts southeastward into the Atlantic Ocean. The chain includes Bear, Physalia, Mytilus, Nashville, Retriever and Picket seamounts.

Formation

The seamounts formed as the North American Plate moved over a mantle hotspot — possibly the Great Meteor hotspot — between roughly 124 and 80 million years ago. Bear Seamount, the oldest and westernmost, rises some 2,000 m above the abyssal plain.

Ecology and protection

Their slopes support deep-sea coral communities and diverse fish and invertebrate fauna. In 2016 the Northeast Canyons and Seamounts Marine National Monument was established to protect part of the chain, the first United States marine national monument in the Atlantic.
