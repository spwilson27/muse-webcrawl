Google Books


The Google Books search interface.

Google Books (previously known as Google Book Search and Google Print) is a service from Google Inc. that searches the full text of books and magazines that Google has scanned, converted to text using optical character recognition (OCR), and stored in its digital database. Books are provided either by publishers and authors through the Google Books Partner Program, or by Google's library partners through the Library Project.

History

The service was introduced at the Frankfurt Book Fair in October 2004 as Google Print. The Library Project, which scans collections of major research libraries including Harvard, Stanford, Oxford, Michigan and the New York Public Library, was announced that December. By 2019 Google reported having scanned more than 40 million books in over 400 languages, making it one of the largest digital book corpora in existence.

Legal controversies

The scanning of in-copyright books provoked lawsuits from authors and publishers. The Authors Guild sued Google in 2005; a proposed settlement was rejected by a federal court in 2011. In 2013, Judge Denny Chin ruled that Google's scanning and snippet display constituted fair use under U.S. copyright law, a decision upheld by the Second Circuit in 2015; the Supreme Court declined to review the case in 2016.

Features

Public-domain works are available in full view and for download, while in-copyright books show only bibliographic information and short snippets around search terms. The corpus also underpins the Google Ngram Viewer, which charts the frequency of phrases across the scanned literature since 1500.
