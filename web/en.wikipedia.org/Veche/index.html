Veche

A veche (Russian: вече; Polish: wiec) was a popular assembly in medieval Slavic countries, most famously in the republics of Novgorod and Pskov. In Novgorod, the veche was the supreme organ of the republic from the 12th century until Moscow annexed the city in 1478.

Powers and practice

The veche was summoned by the ringing of the veche bell and gathered — in Novgorod at Yaroslav's Court or before St Nicholas Cathedral. In principle all free male householders could attend. It elected the posadnik (mayor), tysyatsky, and archbishop, invited and expelled princes, decided questions of war and peace, and enacted legislation. Decisions were taken by acclamation rather than counted votes, and contentious assemblies could descend into fighting between factions or districts.

In practice, power often lay with the boyar elite and the Council of Lords, and historians debate how democratic the veche truly was. After annexing Novgorod, Ivan III had the veche bell removed to Moscow, symbolising the end of the republic; Pskov's veche was abolished in 1510.
