Statistical process control

Statistical process control (SPC) is a method of quality control which employs statistical methods to monitor and control a process. This helps to ensure that the process operates efficiently, producing more specification-conforming products with less waste (rework or scrap).

Tools

The best-known SPC tool is the control chart, developed by Walter A. Shewhart at Bell Laboratories in the 1920s. Control charts plot process measurements over time against control limits, distinguishing common-cause variation (inherent to a stable process) from special-cause variation that signals the process is out of control. Other SPC tools include histograms, Pareto charts, and cause-and-effect diagrams.

History

Shewhart's ideas were developed by W. Edwards Deming, who promoted them in post-war Japan, where SPC became central to Japanese manufacturing quality. SPC was later widely adopted in Western industry and forms a core part of Six Sigma and lean manufacturing methodologies. Key concepts include process capability indices such as Cp and Cpk.

Applications

Although developed for manufacturing, SPC techniques are now applied to service processes, healthcare, and software development.
