Sir Edward Richard George Heath (9 July 1916 – 17 July 2005) was a British politician who served as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom from 1970 to 1974 and Leader of the Conservative Party from 1965 to 1975.

premiership

Heath was a committed Europeanist: his government negotiated Britain's entry into the European Community, which took effect on 1 January 1973. Domestically his premiership was marked by industrial conflict, including miners' strikes in 1972 and 1974, the imposition of a three-day week to conserve electricity, high inflation, and the intensification of the Troubles in Northern Ireland, where he introduced direct rule in 1972 and negotiated the Sunningdale Agreement. After losing two general elections in 1974 he was replaced as Conservative leader by Margaret Thatcher in 1975, with whom he had a famously long feud.

Personal life

Heath was an accomplished yachtsman — he captained Britain's winning Admiral's Cup team in 1971 — and a keen amateur musician and conductor. Unusually for a prime minister, he came from a working-class background, the son of a carpenter and a lady's maid.
