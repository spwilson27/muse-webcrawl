Beppu (別府市, Beppu-shi) is a city in Ōita Prefecture on the island of Kyushu, Japan, situated between the sea and the mountains at the western end of Beppu Bay. It is one of Japan's most famous hot spring resorts, producing more hot spring water than any other resort in the country, with more than 2,000 springs across eight major spring districts known as the Beppu Hatto.

Hot springs

Beppu's onsen range from ordinary bathing springs to spectacular viewing springs called jigoku ("hells"), such as the cobalt-blue Umi Jigoku and the red Chi-no-ike Jigoku, which are too hot for bathing. Visitors also enjoy sand baths, in which bathers are buried in naturally heated sand, and steam cooking of food using spring vapour, known as jigoku mushi.

History and economy

The city was founded in 1924. Tourism centred on the hot springs dominates the economy, and nearby are the nine "hells" tour, Mount Tsurumi, and the Oita Fragrance Museum. Beppu is connected by rail to Ōita City and by ferry across the Seto Inland Sea.
