Lowcountry

The Lowcountry (sometimes Low Country) is a geographic and cultural region along the coast of South Carolina, centred on Charleston and the Sea Islands, and sometimes extended into coastal Georgia. It takes its name from its low elevation, at or near sea level, in contrast to the state's upcountry.

Culture

The region is known for its tidal marshes, rice and indigo plantation history, and the Gullah-Geechee culture of African Americans of the Sea Islands, who preserved a distinctive creole language and traditions. Lowcountry cuisine features seafood and rice dishes such as shrimp and grits, she-crab soup and Lowcountry boils. Its historic architecture, including Charleston's antebellum houses, draws many visitors.
