Rani Rashmoni, zamindar and founder of the Dakshineswar Kali Temple.

Rani Rashmoni (28 September 1793 – 19 February 1861) was an Indian businesswoman, zamindar and philanthropist in colonial Bengal. Widowed in 1836, she managed the family's extensive estates with notable ability and became famous for her charitable works and her defiance of British injustices on behalf of ordinary people.

Life and works

Born in Halisahar into a farming family, Rashmoni married Rajachandra Das of Janbazar, Calcutta. After her husband's death she ran the estate, built roads, ghats (including Babughat), bridges and waterworks, supported education, and famously compelled the East India Company to withdraw a tax on fishermen by blockading the Hooghly river with boats — an episode still celebrated in Bengali folklore.

Dakshineswar Temple

Her most enduring legacy is the Dakshineswar Kali Temple on the Hooghly, consecrated in 1855. Its chief priest, Gadadhar Chattopadhyay — later the saint Ramakrishna — made the temple a centre of 19th-century Bengal's religious life. Rani Rashmoni remains a revered figure in West Bengal, commemorated in statues, institutions and a long-running television biography.
