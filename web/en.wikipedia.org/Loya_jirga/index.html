Loya jirga

A loya jirga (Pashto: لويه جرګه, "grand assembly") is a special type of jirga — an assembly of leaders — that is mainly organised for choosing a new head of state in case of a political transition, adopting a new constitution, or settling major national issues in Afghanistan. Loya jirgas have reportedly been organised since the rise to power of the Hotak dynasty in the early 18th century.

History

The institution originates in the Pashtun assembly tradition of the jirga, in which tribal elders meet to reach consensus on important questions. In 1747, a loya jirga held near Kandahar selected Ahmad Shah Durrani as leader of the Afghans, an event regarded as the founding of the modern Afghan state.

Subsequent loya jirgas approved constitutions in 1923 and 1964, and were convened at moments of crisis, including after the Soviet withdrawal and the fall of the Taliban.

Modern usage

An Emergency Loya Jirga was held in Kabul in June 2002 following the Bonn Agreement, selecting Hamid Karzai as head of the transitional administration. A Constitutional Loya Jirga in December 2003 – January 2004 approved the Constitution of Afghanistan of 2004. Consultative and traditional loya jirgas have since been convened to debate matters such as peace talks and prisoner releases.

Composition

Participants traditionally include tribal and religious leaders, members of parliament, provincial representatives and, in modern convenings, elected delegates from the districts, as well as representatives of refugees, nomads, women and civil society. Decisions are typically reached by consensus rather than by vote.
