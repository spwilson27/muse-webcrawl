Akira Yoshino

Akira Yoshino (吉野 彰, born 30 January 1948) is a Japanese chemist, a fellow of the Asahi Kasei Corporation and a professor at Meijo University. He created the first commercially viable lithium-ion battery and shared the 2019 Nobel Prize in Chemistry with John B. Goodenough and M. Stanley Whittingham for the development of lithium-ion batteries.

Early life and education

Yoshino was born in Suita, Osaka, Japan. He studied petrochemistry at Kyoto University, graduating in 1970 and completing a master's degree in 1972, later receiving a doctorate in engineering from Kyoto University in 2005.

Career and research

Yoshino joined Asahi Kasei in 1972. In the early 1980s he investigated rechargeable batteries using polyacetylene as an anode material, then turned to carbonaceous materials. In 1985 he patented a battery combining a carbon anode with John Goodenough's lithium cobalt oxide cathode, with a carbonate ester electrolyte — the architecture that became the modern lithium-ion battery. The first commercial lithium-ion battery based on this design was launched by Sony in 1991.

Honours

Yoshino shared the 2019 Nobel Prize in Chemistry. He has also received the Charles Stark Draper Prize (2014), the Japan Prize (2018), and the European Inventor Award (2019).
