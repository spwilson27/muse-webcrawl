Neringa is a municipality in western Lithuania, on the Curonian Spit, a narrow, 98 km-long sand-dune peninsula separating the Curonian Lagoon from the Baltic Sea. Formed in 1961 by uniting the settlements on the northern (Lithuanian) half of the spit — Nida, Juodkrantė, Preila, and Pervalka — Neringa is one of the least populous and longest municipalities in Lithuania, stretching some 50 km along the spit. Its administrative seat is Nida.

Geography and environment

The municipality encompasses much of the Lithuanian part of the Curonian Spit National Park. Its landscape of high migrating dunes (including the Parnidis and Nagliai dunes), pine forests, and fishing villages was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2000 as a shared Lithuanian–Russian cultural landscape. Shifting sands once buried whole villages, and extensive dune-stabilisation planting since the 19th century has fixed most of the dunes in place.

Tourism

Neringa is one of Lithuania's premier resorts, prized for its beaches, clean air, artists' colony heritage in Nida (which hosted writers such as Thomas Mann, whose summer house is now a museum), amber, smoked fish, and festivals. Because the spit has no bridge at its Lithuanian end, access is by ferry from Klaipėda across the lagoon mouth, followed by the single road running the length of the spit.
