Vincent Gigante

Vincent Louis Gigante (March 29, 1928 – December 19, 2005), also known as "the Chin", was an American mobster and the boss of the Genovese crime family in New York City from 1981 to 2005. For decades he feigned mental illness — wandering the streets of Greenwich Village in a bathrobe and slippers and muttering incoherently — in an elaborate ruse to throw law enforcement off his trail, earning him the nicknames "the Oddfather" and "the Enigma in the Bathrobe".

Early life

Gigante was born in Manhattan to Neapolitan immigrant parents. He dropped out of high school and became a professional boxer, compiling a record of 21 wins in 25 light-heavyweight bouts between 1944 and 1947. He then became an enforcer for the Genovese family. In 1957 he was the gunman in the failed attempt on the life of mob boss Frank Costello, who survived with a minor wound; Gigante was acquitted of attempted murder after Costello refused to identify him.

Boss of the Genovese family

After the retirement of Philip Lombardo, Gigante became boss of the Genovese family in 1981. He ran the organization quietly and cautiously, insisting that his name never be spoken aloud — subordinates were to point to their chins or form a "C" with their hands instead. Under his leadership the family maintained influence in construction, labor unions, the Fulton Fish Market and the Javits Center, among other rackets.

Feigned insanity and prosecution

Beginning in the late 1960s, Gigante underwent repeated psychiatric evaluations and hospitalizations, building a record of claimed mental illness that he used to defeat or delay prosecutions. He was finally convicted of racketeering and murder conspiracy in 1997 and sentenced to 12 years in prison. In 2003 he pleaded guilty to obstruction of justice, admitting in court that his insanity was an elaborate act designed to deceive psychiatrists and law enforcement. He received an additional three years. Gigante died in prison in Springfield, Missouri, on December 19, 2005.
