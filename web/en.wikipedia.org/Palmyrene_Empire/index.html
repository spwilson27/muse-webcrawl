The Palmyrene Empire was a short-lived breakaway state of the Roman Empire centred on the wealthy caravan city of Palmyra in Syria. During the Crisis of the Third Century, Palmyra's ruler Odaenathus defended Rome's eastern frontier against Sasanian Persia, and after his assassination in 267 his widow Zenobia ruled as regent for their son Vaballathus and expanded Palmyrene power over Egypt, Anatolia, and the Levant.

Rise

Palmyra had grown rich taxing and protecting desert caravan trade. After the emperor Valerian's capture by the Persians in 260, Odaenathus defeated the Persian armies and was recognised by Gallienus as Rome's effective viceroy in the East. Zenobia exploited Roman civil wars to annex Egypt (269–270) and much of Asia Minor, severing Rome's grain supply and eastern revenues.

Fall

The emperor Aurelian campaigned east in 272, defeated the Palmyrene armies at Immae and Emesa, and besieged Palmyra. Zenobia was captured attempting to flee to Persia and was carried to Rome to walk in Aurelian's triumph (accounts of her later life vary). A second Palmyrene revolt in 273 led Aurelian to sack the city; it never recovered its former importance.

Legacy

Though it lasted only about three years as an openly independent empire, Palmyra's splendour — its colonnaded streets, temples, and Aramaic-speaking merchant aristocracy — and Zenobia's reputation as a warrior-queen have made the episode one of the most famous of the later Roman world.
