Cultural genocide

Illustration: Cultural genocide

Cultural genocide is a concept describing the systematic destruction of a group's culture, language, religion, and institutions without necessarily physically exterminating its members. The term is associated with the lawyer Raphael Lemkin, who coined "genocide" and considered cultural destruction one of its techniques.

Legal status

Cultural genocide was debated during the drafting of the 1948 Genocide Convention but was ultimately excluded from the treaty's definition, apart from the forcible transfer of children. It remains a significant concept in international law scholarship and Indigenous rights discourse.

Examples discussed

Historians and commissions have applied the concept to residential school systems for Indigenous children in Canada, where the Truth and Reconciliation Commission concluded in 2015 that the policy amounted to cultural genocide, and to the suppression of minority languages and religions in various states.
