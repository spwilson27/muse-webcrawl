Background check

A background check is the process of looking up and compiling records about a person's identity, history and behaviour, typically for employment, tenancy, licensing or security purposes. Checks may cover criminal records, credit history, employment and education verification, driving records, and reference interviews.

Employment screening

Employers commonly run background checks before hiring, especially for positions of trust involving children, finance, health care or security. In the United States the Fair Credit Reporting Act regulates consumer reports used for employment, requiring disclosure and the subject's consent, and several jurisdictions restrict when criminal history may be asked about ("ban the box" laws). In the United Kingdom, the Disclosure and Barring Service issues basic, standard and enhanced certificates.

Other uses

Background checks are also required for many firearm purchases — in the US, licensed dealers must query the FBI's NICS system — and form part of government security-clearance vetting, which may extend to extensive interviews about a person's past associations and finances. Critics argue that checks can perpetuate disadvantage by penalising old or minor offences long after sentences are served.
