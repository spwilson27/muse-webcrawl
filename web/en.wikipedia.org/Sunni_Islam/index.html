Sunni Islam is the largest branch of Islam, followed by roughly 85–90% of the world's Muslims. Its name derives from Ahl as-Sunnah wa'l-Jama'ah — the people of the Sunnah (the practice of the Prophet Muhammad) and the community.

Beliefs

Sunnis accept the first four caliphs (the Rashidun) as rightful successors of Muhammad, in contrast to Shia Islam's view that leadership belonged to Ali and his line. Authority rests on the Quran and hadith collections such as Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim, interpreted through scholarly consensus.

Law and Theology

Four major schools of jurisprudence (madhhabs) — Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, and Hanbali — differ in legal method but mutually recognise one another. Dominant theological traditions are Ash'arism and Maturidism, alongside traditionalist Athari theology. Sufi orders have historically been integral to Sunni devotional life across Africa and Asia.

Distribution

Sunni majorities span the Middle East, North Africa, Turkey, Central and South Asia, and Indonesia — the world's largest Muslim-majority country, shaped by leaders from Sukarno to Suharto.
