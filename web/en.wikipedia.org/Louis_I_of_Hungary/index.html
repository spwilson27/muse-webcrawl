Louis I of Hungary

Louis I ("the Great")
Reign (Hungary): 1342–1382
Reign (Poland): 1370–1382
Born: 5 March 1326, Visegrád
Died: 10 September 1382, Nagyszombat (Trnava)
House: Capetian House of Anjou

Louis I, also Louis the Great (Hungarian: Nagy Lajos; 5 March 1326 – 10 September 1382), was King of Hungary and Croatia from 1342 and King of Poland from 1370 until his death. He was the second king of the Angevin dynasty in Hungary, succeeding his father Charles I.

Reign

Louis led campaigns to avenge the murder of his younger brother Andrew, Duke of Calabria, invading the Kingdom of Naples twice (1347–48 and 1350). He expanded Hungarian power in the Balkans, making Bosnia, Serbia, Wallachia and Moldavia tributary states at various times, and fought wars against the Republic of Venice, from which he won control of the Dalmatian coast by the Treaty of Zadar (1358). His Balkan campaigns were often framed as crusades against Orthodox Christians and Bogomil heretics.

Domestically, Louis confirmed the liberties of the nobility in his Decree of 1351, which reaffirmed the Golden Bull of 1222, established the system of entail (ősiség), and fixed peasants' dues including the "ninth". He founded the University of Pécs in 1367, the first university in Hungary. His reign is remembered as a high point of medieval Hungarian power, with the kingdom's influence stretching "from sea to sea to sea" — the Baltic, Adriatic and Black Sea.

Succession

In 1370 Louis inherited the Polish crown from his uncle Casimir III the Great. Having no sons, he secured the succession of his daughters through concessions to the nobility, notably the Privilege of Koszyce (1374) in Poland. On his death in 1382 he was succeeded in Hungary by his daughter Mary and in Poland eventually by his younger daughter Jadwiga.
