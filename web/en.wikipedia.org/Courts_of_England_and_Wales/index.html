The courts of England and Wales, supported administratively by His Majesty's Courts and Tribunals Service, are the civil and criminal courts responsible for the administration of justice in England and Wales. The legal system is a common law system founded on case law and statutes.

Hierarchy

At the top is the Supreme Court of the United Kingdom (created in 2009, replacing the Appellate Committee of the House of Lords), followed by the Court of Appeal, divided into Civil and Criminal Divisions. Below are the High Court — with King's Bench, Chancery, and Family Divisions — and the Crown Court for serious criminal trials. County Courts handle most civil claims, and magistrates' courts handle summary criminal offences and preliminary hearings. Specialist tribunals deal with employment, immigration, tax, and other fields.

Personnel

Cases are heard by professional judges and, in magistrates' courts, by lay magistrates advised by legal advisers. Trials for indictable offences are decided by juries in the Crown Court. The judiciary is headed by the Lady Chief Justice of England and Wales.
