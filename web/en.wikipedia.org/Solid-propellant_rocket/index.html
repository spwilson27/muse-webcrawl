Solid-propellant rocket

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A solid rocket booster lifts off

A solid-propellant rocket (or solid rocket) is a rocket with a motor that uses solid propellants (fuel/oxidiser). The earliest rockets were solid-fuelled, powered by gunpowder, used for warfare and fireworks by the medieval Chinese; the technology spread to Europe and was used in artillery rockets such as those of Mysorean and Congreve design.

Design

A solid rocket motor consists of a casing containing a block of propellant with a hollow core (the grain), an igniter, and a nozzle. Once ignited, the propellant burns along the internal surfaces of the core; the grain geometry determines the thrust profile. Because there are no moving parts or plumbing, solid motors are simple, storable, and reliable, but they cannot easily be throttled, stopped, or restarted.

Propellants

Modern composite propellants combine a powdered oxidiser such as ammonium perchlorate with aluminium fuel in a rubbery binder (e.g., HTPB). Double-base propellants use nitrocellulose and nitroglycerin.

Applications

Solid motors are used in missiles, strap-on boosters such as the Space Shuttle and Ariane solid boosters, sounding rockets, and amateur rocketry.
