Atlanta Braves

The Atlanta Braves are an American professional baseball team based in the Atlanta metropolitan area. The Braves compete in Major League Baseball (MLB) as a member club of the National League (NL) East Division. The team plays its home games at Truist Park in Cumberland, Georgia.

Truist Park, home of the Braves since 2017.

History

Founded in 1871 in Boston as the Red Stockings, the franchise played as the Beaneaters, Doves, Rustlers, Bees, and Braves before moving to Milwaukee in 1953, where it won the 1957 World Series with Hank Aaron and Warren Spahn. It relocated to Atlanta in 1966. The Braves have won four World Series titles in total (1914, 1957, 1995, and 2021) — the only MLB franchise to win championships in three different home cities.

The 1990s dynasty

Under manager Bobby Cox and pitching coach Leo Mazzone, Atlanta won a record 14 consecutive division titles from 1991 to 2005, powered by the Hall of Fame rotation of Greg Maddux, Tom Glavine, and John Smoltz, plus Chipper Jones. The run produced one championship, in 1995. The team returned to the top in 2021, defeating the Houston Astros in the World Series.
