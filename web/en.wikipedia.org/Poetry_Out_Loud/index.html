Poetry Out Loud

Poetry Out Loud is a national arts education program and poetry recitation competition for high school students in the United States. Created in 2005 by the National Endowment for the Arts (NEA) in partnership with the Poetry Foundation, the program encourages students to learn about great poetry through memorization and recitation, and to develop public speaking skills and self-confidence.

Format

The competition begins in the classroom, advances through school and regional contests to a state final in each participating state, and culminates in the National Finals held in Washington, D.C. Students choose poems from an official anthology and are judged on physical presence, voice and articulation, dramatic appropriateness, evidence of understanding, and accuracy. At advanced rounds contestants recite multiple poems, including one written before the 20th century and one of 25 lines or fewer.

Prizes

The national champion receives a $20,000 prize, with additional awards for runners-up and for the winning students' schools, which receive funds for poetry materials.

Reach

Since its founding, several million students have participated, and the program has involved tens of thousands of schools and teachers across all fifty states and U.S. territories.
