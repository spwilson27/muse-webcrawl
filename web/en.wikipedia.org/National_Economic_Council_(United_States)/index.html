National Economic Council (United States)

The National Economic Council (NEC) is a body within the Executive Office of the President of the United States, created by President Bill Clinton in 1993 to coordinate economic policy across the federal government. Its director — formally the Assistant to the President for Economic Policy — is one of the president's senior advisers.

Function

Modelled on the National Security Council, the NEC serves as the principal forum for considering economic policy issues, coordinating the economic policymaking process between agencies (Treasury, Commerce, Labor, the Council of Economic Advisers, the Office of Management and Budget and others), monitoring implementation of the president's economic agenda, and ensuring economic considerations are reflected in domestic and international policy decisions.

Directors

The first director was Robert Rubin, who later became Treasury Secretary. Subsequent directors have included Lawrence Summers, Gene Sperling, Gary Cohn and Larry Kudlow; the position's influence varies with the president and director, from central policy driver to coordinator among strong cabinet officers.
