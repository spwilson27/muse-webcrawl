Scoria

Scoria is a highly vesicular, dark-coloured volcanic rock formed from frothy basaltic lava ejected during eruptions. As the molten rock is thrown into the air, escaping gases create abundant bubble-like cavities (vesicles) before it cools and solidifies, giving scoria a sponge-like texture and a density that may nevertheless exceed that of water, so that it usually sinks — unlike pumice.

Formation and characteristics

Scoria is typically basaltic or andesitic in composition and black, dark purple, or reddish-brown in colour, the red hues arising from oxidation of iron. Clasts range from a few millimetres (lapilli) to blocks tens of centimetres across. Scoria accumulates around vents to build cinder cones, such as Parícutin in Mexico and Sunset Crater in Arizona.

Uses

Crushed scoria is used as lightweight aggregate in concrete and cinder blocks, as landscaping and drainage material, in road construction, and as a growing medium in horticulture. Its insulating properties also make it useful in high-temperature applications and as roofing gravel.
