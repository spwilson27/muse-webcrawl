Qʼumarkaj

Qʼumarkaj (also spelled Gumarkaj, Cumarcaj, or known by its Nahuatl name Utatlán) was the capital of the Kʼicheʼ (Quiché) Maya kingdom in the highlands of present-day Guatemala. Founded in the early 15th century by the Kʼicheʼ king Qʼuqʼumatz, it grew into one of the most powerful Maya cities of the Late Postclassic period before being conquered and burned by Spanish forces under Pedro de Alvarado in 1524.

Qʼumarkaj
Culture: Kʼicheʼ Maya
Location: Quiché Department, Guatemala
Founded: c. 1400
Destroyed: 1524
Near modern: Santa Cruz del Quiché

Layout

The city stood on a plateau surrounded by ravines, naturally defensible on all sides. Its core contained great temple-pyramids, including the temple of Tohil, patron god of the Kʼicheʼ, the temple of Awilix, and a temple of Qʼuqʼumatz, together with palaces of the royal lineages, a large plaza, and an I-shaped ballcourt. Structures were originally plastered and painted, but after the conquest the stones were robbed to build the nearby Spanish town of Santa Cruz del Quiché, leaving the site heavily ruined.

Destruction

In 1524 Alvarado marched on Qʼumarkaj. After battles in which the Kʼicheʼ lords Oxib Keh and Belehe Qat were killed, Alvarado entered the city, had the surviving rulers burned alive, and put the city to the torch. The nearby modern town of Santa Cruz del Quiché was founded by the survivors.
