Indian Ocean Whale Sanctuary

The Indian Ocean Whale Sanctuary is an area of the Indian Ocean in which the International Whaling Commission (IWC) has banned commercial whaling. It was established in 1979, making it the first of the IWC's whale sanctuaries, and its designation has been renewed at successive ten-yearly reviews.

Extent

The sanctuary covers the Indian Ocean south from Africa, the Arabian Peninsula and Asia, bounded roughly by 20°E to 130°E and extending south to 55°S. It was proposed largely through the efforts of the Seychelles, whose commissioner at the time, Lyall Watson, championed the measure at the IWC.

Purpose

The sanctuary protects breeding and feeding grounds used by many of the great whale species, provides a refuge complementing the global moratorium on commercial whaling adopted in 1982, and offers a region for non-lethal whale research and monitoring of population recovery. A second, much larger sanctuary, the Southern Ocean Whale Sanctuary, was established in 1994.
