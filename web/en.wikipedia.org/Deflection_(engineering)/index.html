Deflection (engineering)

In structural engineering, deflection is the degree to which a part of a structural element (such as a beam) is displaced under a load. It may refer to an angle (angular deflection) or a distance (linear deflection), and it is determined by the loads, the geometry of the structure, and the stiffness of its material.

Beam deflection

Beam deflections are calculated from the differential equation of the elastic curve, relating the bending moment at each point to the second derivative of the deflection. Standard results exist for common cases: for example, a simply supported beam with a central point load deflects maximally by PL³/48EI at midspan, where E is the modulus of elasticity and I the second moment of area. Methods such as superposition, virtual work, and the moment-area theorems are used for more complex cases.

Design limits

Deflection is usually a serviceability rather than a strength concern: excessive sagging cracks finishes, misaligns machinery, and feels unsafe. Building codes therefore limit deflections to fractions of the span, commonly between L/200 and L/360 depending on the application.
