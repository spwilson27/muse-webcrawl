The International Football Association Board (IFAB) is the body that determines the Laws of the Game of association football. Founded in 1886 by the four British football associations, it remains the guardian of the rules used worldwide, working in partnership with FIFA, which joined the Board in 1913.

The IFAB's annual general meeting is where changes to football's Laws are approved.

History

In the early 1880s each of the four home nations played to slightly different rules. To standardise the game and regulate international matches, representatives of the English, Scottish, Welsh, and Irish associations met in Manchester on 2 June 1886 and formed the IFAB. FIFA, founded in 1904, initially adopted the Board's laws without membership; after negotiations, FIFA was admitted in 1913 with two votes, and the present voting structure dates from 1958.

Structure and voting

Each of the four British associations holds one vote, and FIFA holds four votes on behalf of its other member associations. A proposal to change the Laws requires a three-quarters majority — six of the eight votes — so the British associations collectively can block a change, and FIFA can block one as well. The Board meets at an Annual General Meeting, usually in February or March, rotating among the home nations and other venues; approved changes normally take effect on 1 June.

Role and recent changes

The IFAB maintains the seventeen Laws of the Game, issues clarifications, and authorises experiments. Major modern decisions include approving goal-line technology (2012), authorising video assistant referees (VAR) in the Laws (2018), the comprehensive 2016 revision of the Laws' wording, the temporary — later permanent — increase to five substitutes, and ongoing trials concerning concussion substitutions and referee body cameras. The Board's technical and football advisory panels gather input from across the game before proposals reach the AGM.
