Thar Desert

The Thar Desert, also known as the Great Indian Desert, is a large arid region in northwestern India and eastern Pakistan. Covering about 200,000 square kilometres, it is among the most densely populated deserts in the world, home to millions of people in Rajasthan and Sindh.

Area~200,000 km²
CountriesIndia, Pakistan
Annual rainfall100–500 mm

Geography and climate

The desert is bounded by the Aravalli Range to the southeast, the Indus plain to the west, and the Rann of Kutch to the south. Most of its rain arrives with the summer monsoon; dunes, rocky hamada, and salt flats cover much of the surface, and the ephemeral Luni River drains its eastern portion.

Life and economy

Despite the aridity, the Thar supports pastoralism, drought-resistant crops such as pearl millet, and wildlife including the great Indian bustard, blackbuck, and chinkara gazelles. The Indira Gandhi Canal has brought irrigation to large areas of western Rajasthan. The Indian nuclear tests of 1974 and 1998 were conducted at Pokhran within the desert.
