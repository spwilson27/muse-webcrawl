Pittsburgh is a city in the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania, United States, and the county seat of Allegheny County. It stands in the southwest of the state, at the confluence of the Allegheny and Monongahela rivers, which join to form the Ohio River. With a population of about 303,000 in the 2020 census, it is the second-most populous city in Pennsylvania after Philadelphia, and the centre of a metropolitan area of roughly 2.4 million people.

History

The site was contested between France and Britain in the eighteenth century; Fort Duquesne was built by the French in 1754, and nearby fighting opened the French and Indian War. In 1758 a British expedition under General John Forbes captured the point and built Fort Pitt, named in honour of William Pitt the Elder. The city grew as a gateway to the Ohio country and, in the industrial era, became synonymous with steel: by the early twentieth century Pittsburgh produced a large share of the nation's steel, driven by firms such as Carnegie Steel and later U.S. Steel, and was associated with industrialists including Andrew Carnegie and Henry Clay Frick. The Homestead Strike of 1892 was a landmark of American labour history.

Modern economy and culture

The collapse of the American steel industry in the 1970s and 1980s cost the region tens of thousands of jobs and much of its population. Pittsburgh subsequently reinvented itself around education, medicine, technology and finance, anchored by the University of Pittsburgh, Carnegie Mellon University and the UPMC health system, with growing sectors in robotics and artificial intelligence. Known as the "City of Bridges" for its 446 bridges, the city is also home to major sports teams — the Steelers (NFL), Penguins (NHL) and Pirates (MLB) — and landmarks such as the Duquesne Incline, the Andy Warhol Museum and Phipps Conservatory.
