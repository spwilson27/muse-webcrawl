Tigris (ship)

Tigris was a reed boat built and sailed by the Norwegian explorer and writer Thor Heyerdahl and an international crew in 1977–78. Following his earlier voyages on the balsa raft Kon-Tiki (1947) and the papyrus boats Ra and Ra II (1969–70), Heyerdahl built Tigris to test whether the reed ships of ancient Mesopotamia could have connected the early civilisations of Mesopotamia, Egypt and the Indus Valley by sea trade.

Construction and voyage

The ship, about 18 metres (60 feet) long, was built at Qurna in Iraq, where the Tigris and Euphrates meet, from berdi reeds cut in the Mesopotamian marshes, following depictions on ancient seals and the living boatbuilding traditions of the Marsh Arabs and of reed-boat builders from Lake Titicaca. Launched in November 1977, Tigris sailed down the Shatt al-Arab into the Persian Gulf, called at Bahrain (ancient Dilmun) and Oman, crossed the Arabian Sea to Karachi in Pakistan near the Indus Valley sites, and then returned across the ocean towards the Red Sea.

Burning at Djibouti

After five months at sea and some 6,800 kilometres (4,200 miles), wars in the region — around the Horn of Africa and in Yemen — closed the ports of the Red Sea to the expedition. In April 1978, at Djibouti, Heyerdahl burned Tigris in protest against the wars and the arms supplied by industrial nations, accompanying the act with an open letter to the Secretary-General of the United Nations. The voyage had demonstrated that a properly built reed vessel could survive months in the open ocean, lending plausibility to Heyerdahl's thesis of early maritime contact between the river-valley civilisations.
