Denali

DenaliElevation: 6,190 m (20,310 ft)
Location: Alaska, United States
Range: Alaska Range
First ascent: 7 June 1913

Denali is the highest mountain peak in North America, with a summit elevation of 6,190 metres (20,310 feet) above sea level. It stands in the Alaska Range in the interior of the US state of Alaska and is the centrepiece of Denali National Park and Preserve. Measured from base to summit, its vertical rise is among the greatest of any mountain on Earth.

Name

The name Denali — "the high one" or "the great one" — comes from the Koyukon Athabascan language. A gold prospector in 1896 named it Mount McKinley after presidential candidate William McKinley, and that name became official federally in 1917, though Alaska restored Denali in 1975 and the federal government followed in 2015.

Climbing

The first verifiable ascent was made on 7 June 1913 by Hudson Stuck, Harry Karstens, Walter Harper and Robert Tatum; Harper, an Alaska Native, was first to reach the top. Today most climbers use the West Buttress route, usually taking two to three weeks, contending with extreme cold, high winds and altitude in one of the coldest climbing environments on Earth — temperatures below −50 °C have been recorded near the summit.
