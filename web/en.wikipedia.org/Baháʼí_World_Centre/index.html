Baháʼí World Centre

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Baháʼí World Centre is the spiritual and administrative centre of the Baháʼí Faith, located in Haifa and around Acre in northern Israel. It comprises the Shrine of the Báb and its terraced gardens on Mount Carmel, the Shrine of Baháʼu'lláh at Bahjí near Acre, and the administrative buildings of the Baháʼí Arc on Mount Carmel, including the Seat of the Universal House of Justice, the International Teaching Centre, the Centre for the Study of the Sacred Texts, and the International Baháʼí Archives.

History

The centre's location stems from Baháʼu'lláh's exile by the Ottoman Empire: he was imprisoned in the citadel of Acre in 1868 and lived the rest of his life in the Acre–Haifa area, passing away at Bahjí in 1892. His son ʻAbdu'l-Bahá later interred the remains of the Báb — the faith's co-founder and forerunner — in a shrine on Mount Carmel, on a site Baháʼu'lláh himself had indicated in 1891. Under Shoghi Effendi, Guardian of the faith from 1921 to 1957, the shrines were beautified and the administrative order's institutions developed. The Universal House of Justice, first elected in 1963, governs the worldwide Baháʼí community from Haifa.

Shrines and gardens

The Shrine of the Báb, with its golden dome, is Haifa's most recognizable landmark; its nineteen terraces, completed in 2001, stretch from the German Colony at the foot of Mount Carmel to the summit. The Shrine of Baháʼu'lláh at Bahjí is the most holy place in the Baháʼí Faith, the direction of prayer (Qiblih), and the destination of pilgrimage. Other holy places include the Mansion of Bahjí, the house at Mazra'ih, and the Ridván Garden.

Recognition

In 2008, the Baháʼí Holy Places in Haifa and the Western Galilee were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List for their outstanding universal value, their association with the Baháʼí tradition of pilgrimage, and the exceptional landscape architecture of the terraces and gardens.
