Volunteering

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Volunteering is the practice of giving time and labour, without pay, for the benefit of other people, communities, or causes. Volunteers work for charities, religious bodies, public services, and informal mutual-aid networks, in roles ranging from serving meals and coaching sports to professional pro bono advice, disaster relief, and governance as trustees.

Scope

International estimates suggest around one billion people volunteer each year. Rates vary widely by country and are typically measured through surveys by bodies such as the United Nations Volunteers programme, which has designated 5 December as International Volunteer Day. In many countries roughly a quarter to a third of adults report formal volunteering in a given year, with additional informal helping beyond organisations.

Types

Volunteering may be skills-based (professionals donating expertise), community-based, international (such as the Peace Corps or Voluntary Service Overseas), virtual or micro-volunteering done online in short tasks, and event volunteering. Some schemes, such as court-ordered community service or employer-supported volunteering, blur the boundary of free choice; unpaid internships are a debated borderline case.

Benefits and criticism

Research associates volunteering with improved mental health, social connection, and employability for the volunteer, alongside its direct value to recipients — economists have estimated its global economic contribution in the trillions of dollars. Critics raise concerns about "voluntourism" doing more harm than good, the substitution of unpaid labour for paid jobs, and programmes that primarily benefit the volunteer's CV.
