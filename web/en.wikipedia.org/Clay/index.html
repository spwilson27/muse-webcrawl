Clay

Clay is a type of fine-grained natural soil material containing clay minerals (hydrous aluminium phyllosilicates, e.g. kaolinite, Al2Si2O5(OH)4). Most pure clay minerals are white or light-coloured, but natural clays show a variety of colours from impurities, such as a reddish or brownish colour from small amounts of iron oxide. Clays develop plasticity when wet but can be hardened through firing.

Clay is the longest-used building material, and one of the oldest materials used by humans; clay objects date back at least to the Upper Paleolithic. Clay minerals are very abundant in the Earth's surface rocks and form from the chemical weathering of feldspar and other silicate minerals.

Properties

Clay particles are extremely fine—by definition less than 2 micrometres in soil-science usage—and their plate-like structure gives clay its characteristic plasticity: wet clay can be moulded and holds its shape, drying hard and shrinking, and becoming permanent ceramic when fired at high temperature. Clays have a high cation-exchange capacity and swell or shrink with moisture content, properties that matter for agriculture, geotechnical engineering, and landfill liners. Clay soils can be difficult to build on because of shrink-swell movement.

Types

The principal groups of clay minerals are kaolinite, the smectites (including montmorillonite, the main component of bentonite), illite, and chlorite. Different clays serve different uses: kaolin or china clay for porcelain and paper coating; ball clay for ceramics; fire clay for refractory bricks; bentonite for drilling muds, foundry sand, and cat litter; and common clay for bricks and tiles.

Uses

Humans have used clay since prehistory for pottery—the earliest known ceramic objects are fired-clay figurines more than 25,000 years old—and for writing tablets in Mesopotamia, adobe and brick construction, and sculpture. Today clay is essential to ceramics, papermaking, cement, drilling fluids, cosmetics, and medicine (kaolin), and is studied for its role in the origin of life, since clay minerals can catalyse the formation of organic polymers.
