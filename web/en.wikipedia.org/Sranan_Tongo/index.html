Sranan Tongo (also Sranan, from Sranan "Suriname" + tongo "tongue/language") is an English-based creole language spoken in Suriname, where it serves as the lingua franca for much of the population despite Dutch being the official language.

History

Sranan developed in the second half of the 17th century on the plantations of the Dutch colony of Suriname, first under English rule (1651–1667) and then under the Dutch. Enslaved Africans of diverse linguistic backgrounds created the creole from an English-lexifier base with substantial contributions from Dutch, Portuguese and West African languages. Its close relatives include Saramaccan and the other Surinamese creoles, and it is related to creoles spoken in Guyana and elsewhere in the Caribbean.

Use and status

Although long stigmatised as a "kitchen language", Sranan became a symbol of national identity after Suriname's independence in 1975. It is used in everyday conversation, popular music, advertising and increasingly in writing and media, while Dutch remains the language of government and education. An official spelling was adopted in 1986.

Features

Sranan has a largely analytic grammar: verbs are uninflected, tense and aspect are marked by preverbal particles (e.g. ben for past, o for future, e for progressive), and plurality is marked with the particle den. Its vocabulary is predominantly English-derived — wroko (work), moni (money) — heavily supplemented by Dutch and other sources.
