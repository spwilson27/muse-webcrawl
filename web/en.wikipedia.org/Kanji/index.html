Kanji

[Image: Examples of kanji characters with stroke order]

Kanji (漢字, pronounced [kaɲdʑi]) are the logographic Chinese characters adapted from the Chinese script used in the writing of Japanese. They were made a major part of the Japanese writing system during the time of Old Japanese and are still used, along with the subsequently derived syllabic scripts hiragana and katakana. The characters have Japanese pronunciations, most have two, with one based on the Chinese sound (on'yomi) and another native (kun'yomi).

History

Chinese characters arrived in Japan via Korea, probably around the 5th century CE, on seals, coins and official documents. Over time the Japanese developed two readings for most characters and created some characters of their own, called kokuji ("national characters"), such as 働 (dō, "to work").

Standard character sets

After the Second World War the Japanese government standardised character usage: the tōyō kanji of 1946 were replaced by the jōyō kanji ("regular-use characters"), now numbering 2,136 characters taught through secondary school. Japanese children learn the 1,026 kyōiku kanji during the six years of primary school. Characters outside the standard lists are commonly printed with furigana reading aids.

Readings

Most kanji have multiple readings. The on'yomi derives from the Chinese pronunciation at the time of borrowing (in successive waves: go-on, kan-on and tō-on), while the kun'yomi assigns the character to a native Japanese word. Context determines which reading applies, and irregular readings (jukujikun) exist for whole words, such as 今日 (kyō, "today").
