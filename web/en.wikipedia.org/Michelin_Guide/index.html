Michelin Guide

Publisher: Michelin
First edition: 1900 (France)
Known for: Michelin stars; Bib Gourmand

The Michelin Guides are a series of guide books published by the French tyre company Michelin since 1900, awarding Michelin stars for excellence to a select few restaurants. The guide was created by brothers André and Édouard Michelin to encourage automobile travel — and tyre sales — by giving motorists maps, hotels, and dining recommendations.

Star system

Star ratings were introduced in 1926 and expanded to three levels in 1931: one star ("a very good restaurant in its category"), two stars ("excellent cooking, worth a detour"), and three stars ("exceptional cuisine, worth a special journey"). Anonymous full-time inspectors pay for their meals and judge solely on the food — quality of ingredients, mastery of technique, the chef's personality in the cuisine, value, and consistency. The Bib Gourmand designation (named for Bibendum, the Michelin Man) marks good food at moderate prices, and a green star, added in 2020, highlights sustainability.

Reach and influence

Guides now cover dozens of destinations in Europe, Asia, and the Americas, including Tokyo — the city with the most starred restaurants — New York, and Singapore, whose guide famously awarded stars to hawker stalls in 2016. A Michelin star can transform a restaurant's fortunes, and losing one is a career blow, making the guide one of the most powerful institutions in fine dining despite criticism of French bias and inspector secrecy.
