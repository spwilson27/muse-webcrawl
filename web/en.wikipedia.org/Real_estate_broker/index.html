Real estate broker

A real estate broker, real estate agent, or realtor is a person who represents buyers or sellers of real estate or real property. While a broker may work independently, agents usually work under the umbrella of a licensed broker, who is authorised to operate a brokerage and to supervise other licensees. In the United States, brokers and salespersons are licensed by the states, with requirements for education, examination, and continuing education.

Roles

A seller's (listing) broker markets the property, advises on price, and negotiates on the seller's behalf; a buyer's broker helps locate property and negotiates for the buyer. Brokers are typically compensated by commission, traditionally a percentage of the sale price shared between the listing and buyer's brokerages. The term "Realtor" is a trademark of the U.S. National Association of Realtors and applies only to its members, who subscribe to its code of ethics.

Regulation and change

Licensing regimes vary internationally: in the United Kingdom the corresponding professional is the estate agent, historically with lighter statutory licensing. In the United States, a 2024 settlement by the National Association of Realtors over commission practices led to changes in how buyer's-agent compensation is offered through multiple listing services.
