Swan Hunter

Swan Hunter, formerly known as Swan Hunter & Wigham Richardson, was a British shipbuilding company based at Wallsend on the River Tyne in north-east England. For more than a century it was one of the world's best-known shipyards, building many famous vessels, before shipbuilding ended at the yard in the 2000s and the company transitioned to engineering and design services.

History

The company was formed in 1880 when Charles Sheridan Swan's yard merged interests with George Burton Hunter; in 1903 it amalgamated with Wigham Richardson to form Swan Hunter & Wigham Richardson Ltd. The yard built the RMS Mauretania for Cunard, launched in 1906 — the largest and fastest liner of her day, holder of the Blue Riband for over two decades — and the Carpathia, famous for rescuing survivors of the Titanic in 1912. During both world wars the yard produced large numbers of warships and merchant vessels.

Later years

Nationalised in 1977 as part of British Shipbuilders, Swan Hunter was reprivatised in 1987. In the 1980s and 1990s it built Royal Navy ships including Type 23 frigates. The yard's final shipbuilding project, the auxiliary landing ship dock RFA Lyme Bay, was completed at another yard after costs rose; Swan Hunter's Wallsend facility closed to shipbuilding in 2006–07 and its giant cranes were sold to a yard in India. The company continued as a marine engineering and design consultancy under new ownership.
