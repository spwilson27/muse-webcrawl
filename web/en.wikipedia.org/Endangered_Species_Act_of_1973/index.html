Endangered Species Act of 1973

The Endangered Species Act of 1973 (ESA) is a key United States environmental law for protecting imperiled species and their habitats. Administered by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service and the National Marine Fisheries Service, it provides for listing species as endangered or threatened, designating critical habitat, and preparing recovery plans.

Provisions

Section 7 requires federal agencies to ensure their actions do not jeopardize listed species or destroy critical habitat. Section 9 prohibits taking endangered fish and wildlife. Section 10 permits incidental take under approved habitat conservation plans.

Impact and controversy

Credited with helping recover species such as the bald eagle, gray wolf, and American alligator, the ESA has also generated conflicts over land use and development, from the snail darter and Tellico Dam case to spotted owl logging disputes.
