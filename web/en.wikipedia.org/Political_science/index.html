Political science

Political science studies power, government, and political behaviour.

Political science is the social scientific study of politics: the systematic analysis of government, political institutions, political processes, and political behaviour, both in theory and in practice. Political scientists study how power is distributed and exercised, how policies are made, and how citizens and leaders behave.

Subfields

The discipline is conventionally divided into comparative politics (comparing political systems across countries), international relations (politics among states and other global actors), political theory (the normative and historical study of political ideas), public administration and public policy, and — in the United States — American politics. Political methodology, including statistics, surveys, experiments, and formal game-theoretic modelling, cuts across all of them.

History

The study of politics descends from Aristotle, who called it the "master science", and from later thinkers including Machiavelli, Hobbes, and Montesquieu. It emerged as a distinct academic discipline in the late 19th century — the first department of political science was founded at Columbia University in 1880 — and professionalized with the founding of the American Political Science Association in 1903.

The mid-20th-century "behavioural revolution" shifted attention from formal legal institutions to observable political behaviour, using surveys and quantitative methods; later movements emphasized institutions, rational choice, and interpretive and critical approaches.
