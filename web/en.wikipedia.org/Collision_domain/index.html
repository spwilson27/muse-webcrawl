Collision domain

A collision domain is a network segment in computer networking in which data packets can "collide" with one another when sent on a shared medium, in particular in early Ethernet networks. Only one device in a collision domain may transmit at a time if collisions are to be avoided, and all devices in the domain compete for the medium.

How collisions are handled

In classic half-duplex Ethernet, collisions are managed by the CSMA/CD protocol: a device listens before transmitting, and if two devices transmit simultaneously and a collision is detected, both stop, wait a random backoff interval, and retry. As more devices are added to a shared segment, collisions increase and effective throughput falls.

Segmentation

Network devices divide collision domains differently. Repeaters and hubs forward everything and therefore extend a single collision domain across all their ports. Bridges and switches, by contrast, create a separate collision domain on each port, because they forward frames only where needed; a switch port connected to a single device running full-duplex Ethernet effectively eliminates collisions altogether. Routers separate both collision domains and broadcast domains. Modern switched, full-duplex networks have largely made collision domains a historical concept, though the term remains standard in networking education.
