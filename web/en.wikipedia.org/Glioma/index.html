Glioma

A glioma is a type of tumour that starts in the glial cells of the brain or spinal cord. Gliomas comprise about 30 percent of all brain and central nervous system tumours and about 80 percent of malignant brain tumours.

Classification

Gliomas are classified by the type of glial cell involved and by grade. Astrocytomas arise from astrocytes, oligodendrogliomas from oligodendrocytes, and ependymomas from ependymal cells; glioblastoma is the most aggressive grade IV astrocytoma. Modern classification also uses molecular markers, such as mutations in the IDH genes and codeletion of chromosome arms 1p/19q, which carry prognostic significance.

Signs, treatment and prognosis

Symptoms depend on location and may include headaches, seizures, cognitive changes and focal neurological deficits. Treatment typically combines surgical resection with radiotherapy and chemotherapy (commonly temozolomide). Prognosis varies widely: low-grade gliomas may grow slowly for years, whereas glioblastoma remains difficult to cure despite treatment.
