The University of California, Santa Cruz (UC Santa Cruz or UCSC) is a public land-grant research university in Santa Cruz, California, and one of the ten campuses of the University of California system. Founded in 1965 on the former Cowell Ranch overlooking Monterey Bay, it is known for its residential college system, its redwood-forest campus, and research strengths in astronomy and astrophysics, genomics, and environmental studies.

History

UCSC opened in 1965 as an experimental campus emphasising undergraduate education through small residential colleges, narrative evaluations instead of letter grades, and interdisciplinary study. Under founding chancellor Dean McHenry and college provosts such as Page Smith, the first colleges — Cowell, Stevenson, Crown, and Merrill — were established. Letter grades became standard in 2000, though narrative evaluations remain available.

Campus and colleges

The 2,000-acre campus rises through redwood forest on the hills above Santa Cruz. Ten residential colleges, each with its own academic focus and architecture, house much of the undergraduate population. The university also operates the Long Marine Laboratory and, with the UC system, Lick Observatory on Mount Hamilton.

Research

UCSC researchers played a central role in the Human Genome Project (the first assembly of a human genome sequence was produced there in 2000) and maintain the UCSC Genome Browser, a widely used genomics tool. The campus is a member of the Association of American Universities (since 2019) and is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity".
