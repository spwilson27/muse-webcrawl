Chlorine

Chlorine is a chemical element with symbol Cl and atomic number 17. It is a yellow-green gas at room temperature and the second-lightest halogen. Chlorine is a powerful oxidising agent and is widely used as a disinfectant and in the production of plastics such as polyvinyl chloride (PVC), solvents, and many industrial and household chemicals.

Properties and occurrence

Chlorine exists as diatomic molecules (Cl2) and has a pungent, irritating odour; inhalation damages the respiratory system. Because of its reactivity it never occurs free in nature, but chloride salts — above all sodium chloride in seawater and rock salt deposits — are abundant. Carl Wilhelm Scheele first prepared the gas in 1774, and Humphry Davy established it as an element in 1810, naming it from the Greek chlōros, "pale green."

Production and uses

Industrial chlorine is produced mainly by electrolysis of brine in the chloralkali process, yielding chlorine, sodium hydroxide, and hydrogen. Its largest use is in PVC manufacture; it is also used for water treatment — chlorination revolutionised public health by disinfecting drinking water and swimming pools — pulp bleaching, and the manufacture of chlorinated solvents and intermediates.

Safety

Chlorine gas was used as a chemical weapon in the First World War, notably at the Second Battle of Ypres in 1915. Some chlorine compounds, such as chlorofluorocarbons, have damaged the stratospheric ozone layer, and organochlorine pesticides like DDT proved persistent environmental pollutants.
