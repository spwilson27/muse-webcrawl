In ancient Greek religion and mythology, Demeter is the Olympian goddess of the harvest and agriculture, presiding over crops, grains, food, and the fertility of the earth. Although she is mostly known as a grain goddess, she also appeared as a goddess of health, birth, and marriage, and had connections to the Underworld. She is also called Sito, "grain giver".

Demeter was one of the Twelve Olympians, the children of the Titans Cronus and Rhea, and a sister of Zeus, Hera, Hestia, Poseidon, and Hades.

The myth of Persephone

Demeter's most famous myth concerns her daughter Persephone, whom Hades abducted to be his queen in the Underworld. Demeter searched the earth in grief, neglecting her duties, so that crops failed and famine threatened mankind. Zeus eventually brokered a compromise: because Persephone had eaten pomegranate seeds in the underworld, she would spend part of each year with Hades and part with her mother — a myth explaining the cycle of the seasons.

Eleusinian Mysteries

Demeter and Persephone were the central figures of the Eleusinian Mysteries, secret religious rites held at Eleusis that promised initiates a blessed afterlife. Her cult also included the Thesmophoria, a festival celebrated by married women.
