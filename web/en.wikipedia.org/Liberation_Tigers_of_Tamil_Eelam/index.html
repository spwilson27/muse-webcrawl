The Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam (LTTE; Tamil: தமிழீழ விடுதலைப் புலிகள்), commonly known as the Tamil Tigers, was a militant separatist organization that fought to create an independent Tamil state, Tamil Eelam, in the north and east of Sri Lanka.

Origins

Founded in 1976 by Velupillai Prabhakaran, the LTTE grew out of Tamil grievances over discrimination by the Sinhalese-majority governments of Sri Lanka. After anti-Tamil riots in 1983, the conflict escalated into the Sri Lankan Civil War, in which the LTTE became the dominant Tamil armed group, eliminating rival organizations.

Conflict

The Tigers developed conventional military forces, a naval wing (the Sea Tigers), and a nascent air force, and administered a de facto state in northern Sri Lanka. The group pioneered the use of suicide bombings and was designated a terrorist organization by many countries; it assassinated former Indian prime minister Rajiv Gandhi in 1991 and Sri Lankan president Ranasinghe Premadasa in 1993. A Norwegian-brokered ceasefire in 2002 collapsed, and full-scale war resumed in 2006.

Defeat

In May 2009 the Sri Lankan military defeated the LTTE in a final offensive on the Mullivaikkal coast, killing Prabhakaran and the group's leadership. The war's final months caused very heavy civilian casualties and led to international calls for accountability investigations, ending nearly three decades of conflict.
