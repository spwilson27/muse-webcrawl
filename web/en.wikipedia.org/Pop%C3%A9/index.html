Popé or Po'pay (c. 1630 – c. 1692) was a Tewa religious leader from Ohkay Owingeh (San Juan Pueblo) in present-day New Mexico who led the Pueblo Revolt of 1680 against Spanish colonial rule.

The Pueblo Revolt

After being arrested and whipped by Spanish authorities in 1675 for practicing traditional religion, Popé organized a coordinated uprising among the far-flung Pueblo peoples. According to tradition, runners carried knotted cords to each pueblo, counting down the days to the revolt. On 10 August 1680 the Pueblos rose, killing about 400 Spanish colonists and missionaries and driving the survivors south to El Paso. New Mexico remained free of Spanish control for twelve years.

Legacy

Popé's rule over the independent pueblos became contentious, and he died around 1692, shortly before Diego de Vargas's reconquest. In 2005 a statue of Popé by Jemez Pueblo sculptor Cliff Fragua was installed in National Statuary Hall in the U.S. Capitol, representing New Mexico.
