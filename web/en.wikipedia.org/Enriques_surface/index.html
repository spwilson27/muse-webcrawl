Enriques surface

In mathematics, an Enriques surface is an algebraic surface with vanishing irregularity and a canonical divisor that is not trivial but whose double is trivial. Equivalently, it can be defined as a smooth projective surface whose universal cover is a K3 surface, the covering being a free involution. Enriques surfaces are named after the Italian mathematician Federigo Enriques, who studied them in the 1890s.

Properties

Enriques surfaces have Kodaira dimension 0 and are one of the classes in the Enriques–Kodaira classification of compact complex surfaces. Over the complex numbers they have Betti numbers b1 = 0, b2 = 10, and Hodge numbers h1,0 = 0, h2,0 = 0, h1,1 = 10. Their canonical bundle has order exactly two in the Picard group, which distinguishes them from K3 surfaces and from abelian and bielliptic surfaces.

Every Enriques surface contains elliptic fibrations, and their moduli space is 10-dimensional. In characteristic 2 the theory is more subtle: there are "classical", "singular", and "supersingular" Enriques surfaces, distinguished by properties of the canonical class and the Picard scheme, and some of them are actually K3-like quotients with non-reduced structure.

Examples

The classic example is the quotient of a K3 surface by a fixed-point-free involution. Concrete models include certain complete intersections and quotients of products of elliptic curves. Generic Enriques surfaces contain configurations of smooth rational curves related to root systems of the lattice E10.
