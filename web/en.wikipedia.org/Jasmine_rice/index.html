Jasmine rice

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Jasmine rice (Thai: ข้าวหอมมะลิ; RTGS: khao hom mali) is a long-grain variety of fragrant rice (also known as aromatic rice). Its fragrance, reminiscent of pandan (Pandanus amaryllifolius) and popcorn, results from the rice plant's natural production of aromatic compounds, of which 2-acetyl-1-pyrroline is the most salient. Jasmine rice is a relatively new development, bred in Thailand in the mid-20th century.

History

Jasmine rice originated in Thailand. In 1945, a farmer in Phanat Nikhom District, Chonburi Province discovered a fragrant grain of rice in a rice field, and collected the seeds. In 1950 he passed the seeds to a local agricultural officer for study and development. The variety, known as Khao Dawk Mali 105 (KDML 105), was officially certified and released in 1959. The name derives from its pure white colour and jasmine-like fragrance (hom mali means "jasmine-scented").

Characteristics

Jasmine rice is a long-grain rice with a slightly sticky texture when cooked. The grains are translucent and, when fresh, naturally fragrant. The fragrance fades over time after harvesting and milling. Jasmine rice is grown primarily in Thailand, Cambodia, Laos, and Vietnam. Thai hom mali rice grown in the Thung Kula Rong Hai region of northeastern Thailand is particularly prized and has protected geographical indication status.

Cooking

Jasmine rice is commonly steamed or cooked by the absorption method, typically with a lower water-to-rice ratio than many other long-grain varieties because of its moist, soft texture. It is a staple accompaniment to Thai dishes such as green curry, and is also used for fried rice dishes such as khao phat.

Types

  • White jasmine rice — milled and polished, the most common form.
  • Brown jasmine rice — unpolished, retaining the bran layer.
  • Black and red jasmine rice — pigmented varieties with coloured bran.
