Stanwood is a city in Snohomish County, Washington, United States. It is located at the mouth of the Stillaguamish River near Puget Sound, about 50 miles north of Seattle.

History

The area was first settled in the 1870s as a trading post named Centerville; it was renamed Stanwood in 1877 after the maiden name of the wife of postmaster D. O. Pearson. Historically two neighbouring towns, Stanwood and East Stanwood, merged in 1960.

Geography and culture

The city serves as a gateway to Camano Island and is known for Scandinavian heritage, reflected in local festivals and the nearby Pilchuck Glass School founded by Dale Chihuly.
