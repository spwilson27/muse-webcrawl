Descending colon

In human anatomy, the descending colon is the part of the large intestine that travels downward along the left side of the abdomen, from the splenic flexure below the spleen to the point where it joins the sigmoid colon in the pelvis.

Structure

The descending colon is about 25 cm long. Unlike the transverse colon, it is retroperitoneal in most people — fixed against the posterior abdominal wall with peritoneum covering only its front and sides. It is narrower and more deeply placed than the ascending colon.

Function

As material passes through the descending colon, water and electrolytes continue to be absorbed, and the remaining contents are progressively formed into feces, which are stored here before moving into the sigmoid colon and rectum.

Blood supply and clinical notes

Its arterial supply comes chiefly from the left colic branch of the inferior mesenteric artery, and its venous drainage follows the inferior mesenteric vein. The descending colon is a common site examined during colonoscopy; diseases affecting it include diverticulitis and colorectal cancer.
