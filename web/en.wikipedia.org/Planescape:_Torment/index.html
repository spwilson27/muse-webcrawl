Developer: Black Isle Studios
Publisher: Interplay Entertainment
Release: December 12, 1999
Genre: Role-playing video game
Platforms: Microsoft Windows (later macOS, Linux, consoles via Enhanced Edition)

Planescape: Torment is a 1999 role-playing video game developed by Black Isle Studios and published by Interplay Entertainment for Microsoft Windows. It takes place in locations from the multiverse of the Dungeons & Dragons Planescape campaign setting. The game uses the Infinity Engine, previously used in Baldur's Gate, and was designed by a team led by Chris Avellone.

Plot

The player controls the Nameless One, an immortal amnesiac who awakens in a mortuary in the city of Sigil with no memory of his identity or past lives, accompanied by the floating skull Morte. Guided by the recurring question "What can change the nature of a man?", he journeys across the planes to recover his memories and discover why he cannot die — and what he did in his previous incarnations to warrant his condition.

Gameplay and themes

Torment is unusually text-heavy for the genre, with around 800,000 words of dialogue, and emphasizes narrative, character interaction, and moral choice over combat; many encounters can be resolved through conversation, and death is rarely a fail state because of the protagonist's immortality. Themes include identity, memory, guilt, redemption, and the power of belief to reshape reality.

Reception and legacy

Though it sold modestly on release, the game received widespread critical acclaim and is frequently cited among the greatest video games of all time, particularly for its writing. An Enhanced Edition developed by Beamdog was released in 2017 for modern platforms. A thematic successor, Torment: Tides of Numenera, was crowdfunded and released in 2017 by inXile Entertainment.
