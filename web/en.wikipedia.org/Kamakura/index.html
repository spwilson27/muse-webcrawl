Kamakura

Location: Kanagawa Prefecture, Japan

Population: about 170,000

Known for: Great Buddha (Daibutsu), Zen temples, former shogunal capital

Kamakura (鎌倉) is a coastal city in Kanagawa Prefecture, Japan, about 50 kilometres south of Tokyo. Though today a modest city of some 170,000 inhabitants, it was the political centre of Japan from 1185 to 1333 as the seat of the Kamakura shogunate, the country's first military government, founded by Minamoto no Yoritomo.

History

Yoritomo, victor in the Genpei War, established his government in Kamakura, his clan's ancestral power base, rather than in the imperial capital Kyoto; he was appointed shogun in 1192. After his death real power passed to the Hōjō clan, who ruled as regents for figurehead shoguns. Kamakura repelled the Mongol invasions of 1274 and 1281 from its region's armies, but the shogunate fell in 1333 when Nitta Yoshisada stormed the city. The city declined thereafter, though it revived as a cultural and tourist centre in the Meiji period.

Sights

Kamakura is famed for the Daibutsu, the great bronze statue of Amida Buddha at Kōtoku-in (cast in 1252, about 13.35 m tall including its base), the Zen temples Kenchō-ji and Engaku-ji (the "Five Great Zen Temples"), the Shinto shrine Tsurugaoka Hachimangū, and the hydrangea temple Hase-dera. Its beaches, especially Yuigahama, draw Tokyo day-trippers in summer.
