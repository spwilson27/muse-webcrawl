Bob Wills

James Robert Wills (March 6, 1905 – May 13, 1975) was an American Western swing musician, songwriter, and bandleader. Considered by many to be the founder of Western swing, he was known widely as the "King of Western Swing". Wills formed several bands and played radio stations around the South and West until he found popularity as the leader of the Texas Playboys in 1934.

Career

Born into a family of fiddle players in Kosse, Texas, Wills learned to play fiddle and mandolin as a child and performed at ranch dances. In the early 1930s, in Fort Worth, he co-founded the Light Crust Doughboys with Milton Brown, a group sponsored by a flour company and broadcast on radio. In 1934 he moved to Tulsa, Oklahoma, where his Texas Playboys broadcast daily from Cain's Ballroom on station KVOO.

Music

Wills fused country fiddling with jazz, blues, Dixieland, and big-band swing, adding steel guitar, drums, and horn sections at a time when such instrumentation was controversial in country music. His signature songs include "San Antonio Rose" (later "New San Antonio Rose"), "Faded Love", "Take Me Back to Tulsa", and "Ida Red", which influenced early rock and roll. His falsetto hollers and spoken asides ("Ah-ha!") were a trademark.

Legacy

Wills was inducted into the Country Music Hall of Fame in 1968 and, with the Texas Playboys, into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1999. He influenced artists from Willie Nelson to Asleep at the Wheel, who helped revive his music in the 1970s.
