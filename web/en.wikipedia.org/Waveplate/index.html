Waveplate

A waveplate or retarder is an optical device that alters the polarization state of a light wave travelling through it. It is made of a birefringent material — commonly quartz, mica, or polymer film — in which light polarized along two perpendicular axes travels at different speeds.

Principle

The two orthogonal polarization components accumulate a relative phase difference (retardance) proportional to the material's birefringence and thickness. The most common types are the half-wave plate, which introduces a phase shift of half a wavelength (π radians) and rotates the direction of linearly polarized light, and the quarter-wave plate, which introduces a quarter-wavelength shift (π/2) and converts linear polarization to circular polarization and vice versa. Orienting a quarter-wave plate at 45° to a polarizer produces circularly polarized light, a standard laboratory technique.

Types and uses

Waveplates may be zero-order, multiple-order, or achromatic, and are used in lasers, optical isolators, microscopy, photography, liquid-crystal displays, and quantum optics experiments to control polarization.
