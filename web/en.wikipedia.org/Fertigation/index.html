Fertigation

Fertigation through a drip irrigation system

Fertigation is the injection of fertilisers, used for soil amendments, water amendments and other water-soluble products into an irrigation system. The term is a portmanteau of fertilisation and irrigation. Fertigation is common in commercial horticulture and greenhouse production, where it is typically combined with drip or micro-sprinkler irrigation.

Advantages

Because nutrients are dissolved in the irrigation water and delivered directly to the root zone in small, frequent doses, fertigation can increase nutrient uptake efficiency, reduce the total fertiliser required compared with broadcast application, and limit leaching of nutrients into groundwater. Application can be timed precisely to the crop's growth stage.

Systems and drawbacks

Common injection methods include Venturi injectors, dosing pumps and pressure-differential tanks. Disadvantages include the need for careful management: uneven water distribution causes uneven fertiliser distribution, salts can accumulate, and backflow prevention is essential to stop fertiliser contaminating the water supply.
