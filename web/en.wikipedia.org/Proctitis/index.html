Proctitis

Proctitis
SpecialtyGastroenterology
SymptomsRectal pain, tenesmus, discharge, bleeding

Proctitis is an inflammation of the lining of the rectum. It can cause rectal pain, a continual urge to have a bowel movement (tenesmus), discharge, and bleeding. Symptoms may be short-lived (acute) or persistent (chronic).

Causes

Proctitis has many causes. It may occur as part of inflammatory bowel disease, such as ulcerative colitis or Crohn's disease (ulcerative proctitis when confined to the rectum). Infectious proctitis is commonly sexually transmitted, caused by organisms including Neisseria gonorrhoeae, Chlamydia trachomatis, herpes simplex virus, and syphilis. Radiation proctitis may follow radiotherapy for pelvic cancers, and diversion proctitis can occur in bowel segments bypassed by a stoma.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis typically involves sigmoidoscopy or proctoscopy with biopsy, plus testing for infections. Treatment is directed at the underlying cause: antibiotics for infection, anti-inflammatory medicines such as mesalazine for inflammatory proctitis, and topical or surgical measures for radiation injury.
