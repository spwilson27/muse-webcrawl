Kertanegara (full name Sri Maharajadhiraja Kertanegara Wikrama Dharmottunggadewa) was the last and greatest king of the Singhasari kingdom of East Java, reigning from 1268 until his death in 1292. An ambitious expansionist and devout practitioner of Tantric Shiva–Buddha syncretism, he brought much of the Malay world under Singhasari influence.

Reign and expansion

Kertanegara succeeded his father Wisnuwardhana in 1268. He launched the Pamalayu expedition of 1275, which established Singhasari influence over the Melayu kingdom in Sumatra, commemorated by the Amoghapasa statue sent to Dharmasraya. Singhasari forces or alliances extended to Bali (conquered 1284), Sunda, Madura, Borneo (Bakulpur), and the Malay Peninsula, making Kertanegara the first Javanese ruler to project power across the archipelago.

Conflict with the Mongols

Anticipating a threat from Kublai Khan's Yuan dynasty, Kertanegara sought regional alliances. He famously insulted the Khan's envoys — according to tradition, the envoy Meng Qi was branded or had his face tattooed — and refused demands for submission. Kublai prepared a punitive expedition, but before it arrived Kertanegara was overthrown.

Death and aftermath

In 1292 Jayakatwang, the vassal duke of Kediri (Gelang-gelang), rebelled and attacked the capital while Singhasari's army was campaigning abroad. Kertanegara was killed during a Tantric ritual ceremony. His son-in-law, Raden Wijaya, escaped, allied with the arriving Mongol expeditionary force in 1293 to destroy Jayakatwang, then turned on the Mongols and founded the Majapahit empire, vindicating Kertanegara's anti-Yuan stance.
