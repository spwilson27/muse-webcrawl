Canterbury Plains
Location: Canterbury, South Island, New Zealand
Formed by alluvial outwash of braided rivers

The Canterbury Plains (Māori: Kā Pākihi-whakatekateka-o-Waitaha) are an area in New Zealand centred to the south of the city of Christchurch in the Canterbury region. They are the largest expanse of low-lying flat land in New Zealand.

Formation

The plains are a large alluvial fan built from gravel outwash carried by braided rivers — the Waimakariri, Rakaia, Rangitata, and Selwyn — from the eroding Southern Alps during and after glacial periods.

Agriculture

With irrigation, the plains support intensive sheep and dairy farming, arable cropping, and vineyards. Christchurch, Ashburton, and Timaru are the main urban centres.
