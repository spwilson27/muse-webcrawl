Vikings

Vikings
EraViking Age, c. 793–1066
HomelandsScandinavia (Norway, Denmark, Sweden)
LanguageOld Norse

Vikings were seafaring people originally from Scandinavia who, from the late 8th to the late 11th century, raided, traded, explored and settled across wide areas of Europe, Asia and the North Atlantic.

Viking Age

The Viking Age is conventionally dated from the raid on Lindisfarne monastery in 793 to the Battle of Stamford Bridge in 1066. Norse sailors in longships reached Iceland, Greenland and, around the year 1000, Vinland in North America under Leif Erikson. Swedish Vikings — Varangians — travelled the rivers of Eastern Europe, founded settlements linked to the Kievan Rus', traded with Constantinople and served as the Byzantine emperor's Varangian Guard.

Society

Viking society was organised around chieftains and kings, free farmers, and enslaved people (thralls). Assemblies called things settled disputes. Norse religion worshipped gods such as Odin, Thor and Freyja, recorded later in the Icelandic sagas and Eddas; Scandinavia gradually Christianised during the 10th–12th centuries.

Settlement

Vikings settled the Danelaw in England, Normandy in France (from Rollo, ancestor of William the Conqueror), Iceland, the Faroe Islands, and parts of Ireland, Scotland and Russia. Alfred the Great resisted them in Wessex, and kings such as Æthelstan later united England.

Legacy

Vikings left place names, loanwords in English (sky, egg, law), runestones, ship burials such as Oseberg and Gokstad, and a lasting popular image — though the horned helmet is a 19th-century invention.
