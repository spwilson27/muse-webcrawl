Indian subcontinent

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Indian subcontinent is a physiographical region in Southern Asia, situated on the Indian Plate, projecting southwards into the Indian Ocean from the Himalayas. Geopolitically, it generally includes the countries of Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Nepal, Pakistan, and Sri Lanka, and sometimes also the Maldives and Afghanistan. Home to roughly 1.9 billion people, it is the most populous region of its kind in the world.

Geography

The subcontinent is bounded by the Himalaya and Karakoram ranges to the north, the Hindu Kush to the northwest, and the Arakan Mountains to the east; its peninsula tapers to Kanyakumari (Cape Comorin) between the Arabian Sea and the Bay of Bengal. Major river systems — the Indus, Ganges, and Brahmaputra — water vast alluvial plains, while the Deccan Plateau occupies the peninsular interior.

Geology

The region sits on the Indian Plate, which split from Gondwana over 100 million years ago and drifted northeast to collide with the Eurasian Plate, thrusting up the Himalayas — a collision that continues today. The term "subcontinent" reflects this distinct landmass partly separated from the rest of Asia by mountain barriers.

History and culture

The subcontinent hosted the Bronze Age Indus Valley Civilisation, the Vedic culture, and the birth of Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and Sikhism. Successive empires — Mauryan, Gupta, Mughal — unified much of it before British colonial rule; independence in 1947 brought partition into India and Pakistan, with Bangladesh emerging in 1971. The region shares deep cultural continuities, expressed in food, dress, film, and sport, including a passion for cricket.
