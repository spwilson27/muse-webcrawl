Raška

The town of Raška at the confluence of the Raška and Ibar rivers.

Raška (Serbian Cyrillic: Рашка, pronounced [râʃka]) is a town and municipality located in the Raška District of southwestern Serbia. The town stands at the confluence of the Raška and Ibar rivers, near the foothills of the Kopaonik mountain range. According to recent census data, the town itself has several thousand inhabitants, while the municipality has a population of roughly 21,000.

The wider region of Raška holds a central place in Serbian medieval history: it gave its name to Rascia (Raška), the core principality from which the medieval Serbian state of the Nemanjić dynasty developed, and to the historical region around the old capital of Ras, near present-day Novi Pazar.

History

The area was settled in the early Middle Ages by Serbs and became the heartland of the župa of Raška, ruled by the Vukanović and later Nemanjić dynasties. The nearby medieval fortress and episcopal seat of Ras, together with the churches and monasteries of the region — including Studenica and Sopoćani — form the UNESCO World Heritage Site of Stari Ras and Sopoćani. Under Ottoman rule the region declined in importance, and the modern town of Raška developed in the 19th century as a trading post on the Ibar road. The town was formally founded in 1845 by decree of Prince Alexander Karađorđević.

Geography

Raška lies in the Ibar valley at an elevation of about 430 metres, surrounded by the Kopaonik mountains to the east and Golija to the west. The municipality includes a number of villages, mineral springs and part of the Kopaonik ski resort area; the spa town of Jošanička Banja also belongs to the municipality. Mining — historically of coal, lead and zinc around the Trepča complex and at Baljevac — has long been an important part of the local economy.
