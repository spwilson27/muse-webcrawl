Nose

SystemRespiratory, olfactory
NerveOlfactory nerve; trigeminal branches
Identifiers (Latin)nasus

A nose is a structure of the face that protrudes and houses the nostrils, admitting and expelling air for respiration and providing the sense of smell. In humans the nose is centrally located on the face and serves as the primary passageway of the upper respiratory tract, warming, humidifying, and filtering inhaled air. In many animals the nose is the principal olfactory organ and may be developed into snouts, trunks, or beak-like structures.

Anatomy

The external human nose is formed by the nasal bones and upper and lower lateral cartilages, covered by skin. Inside, the nasal cavity is divided by the nasal septum into two passages lined with mucous membrane and fine hairs (vibrissae) that trap particles. Scroll-shaped turbinates (conchae) increase the surface area over which air passes. Olfactory receptors occupy the upper part of the cavity, and the paranasal sinuses drain into the nose.

Function

Beyond smell and breathing, the nose contributes to speech resonance and to taste, since flavour perception depends largely on retronasal smell. Clinical conditions affecting the nose include rhinitis, sinusitis, deviated septum, nosebleeds (epistaxis), and nasal polyps; surgical correction of its shape is called rhinoplasty.
