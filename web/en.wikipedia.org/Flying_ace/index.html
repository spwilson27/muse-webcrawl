Flying ace

A flying ace is a military aviator credited with shooting down five or more enemy aircraft in aerial combat.

A flying ace, fighter ace or air ace is a military aviator credited with shooting down five or more enemy aircraft during aerial combat. The exact number of aerial victories required to officially qualify as an ace is variable, but is usually considered to be five or more.

History

The concept arose in World War I. The French press first used the term l'as ("the ace") for Adolphe Pégoud, who scored five victories in 1915. Air forces developed elaborate confirmation systems for victories, and leading aces such as Manfred von Richthofen (the "Red Baron", 80 victories), René Fonck (75) and Billy Bishop (72) became national heroes whose exploits were used for propaganda.

In World War II the highest scores were achieved on the Eastern Front by German Luftwaffe pilots — Erich Hartmann, with 352 victories, is the highest-scoring ace of all time. Later conflicts, including the Korean and Vietnam wars, produced smaller numbers of aces as air combat changed; the five-victory standard continued to be used by the United States Air Force and Navy.

Accuracy of claims

Historians note that victory claims in all air forces tended to exceed actual losses, owing to the confusion of combat, multiple pilots claiming the same aircraft, and the difficulty of confirming wrecks. Cross-checking against opposing records often reduces official totals substantially.

Other usages

The "ace" concept has been extended to tank commanders ("tank ace"), submarine commanders, and snipers, and to non-combat fields such as "space ace" for astronauts.
