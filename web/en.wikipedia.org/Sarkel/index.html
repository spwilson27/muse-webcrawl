Sarkel

Sarkel (from the Khazar language, meaning "white house") was a large limestone-and-brick fortress built by the Khazars with Byzantine assistance in the 830s–840s AD. It stood on the left bank of the lower Don River, in present-day Rostov Oblast, Russia.

History

The Khazar khagan requested help from the Byzantine emperor Theophilos, who sent engineers under Petronas Kamateros to construct the fortress, which guarded a key portage and trade route between the Don and Volga. In 965 the Rus' prince Sviatoslav of Kiev captured Sarkel; the settlement, renamed Belaya Vezha ("white tower") in Slavic, remained inhabited until the 12th century, when the area fell to the Kipchaks.

Archaeology

The site was excavated by Soviet archaeologists under Mikhail Artamonov in the 1930s and again in 1949–1951, before it was submerged in 1952 by the filling of the Tsimlyansk Reservoir. Artamonov famously identified the site with Khazar culture and developed his controversial theories of Khazar history around it.
