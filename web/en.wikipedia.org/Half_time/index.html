Half time

Half time is the interval between the two halves of a sporting match.

In several team sports, half time (also written halftime or half-time) is the name of an interval between the two halves of play. Typically, after half time, teams swap ends of the field of play in order to reduce any advantage that may be gained from wind or a slope to the playing surface, among other possible factors.

In different sports

In association football, matches consist of two 45-minute halves with a half-time interval of no more than 15 minutes; the law was standardised in the early 20th century, the interval having earlier been taken only when captains agreed. In rugby union and rugby league, halves are 40 minutes with an interval of up to about 10–15 minutes. In American football and Canadian football, games consist of four quarters, and halftime — usually 12 minutes in the NFL regular season — separates the second and third quarters.

In field hockey, the game was historically split into two halves but is now played in quarters at international level. In basketball, by contrast, games are divided into quarters (or, in US college men's basketball, halves) and the mid-game break functions as the equivalent interval.

Entertainment

The half-time break at major events has become a vehicle for entertainment and advertising. The Super Bowl halftime show in American football is one of the most-watched musical performances of the year, featuring headline artists, and is notorious for some of the most expensive television advertising slots sold anywhere. Marching band performances are a traditional halftime feature in US college football. In association football, half-time analysis by television pundits and the sale of refreshments — including the traditional half-time pie in Britain — are longstanding customs, while managers use the interval for tactical adjustments and team talks.

History

The origins of half time lie in early football codes played at English public schools, where changing ends at the midpoint balanced any advantage of ground or weather. The practice was codified as the games were formalised in the 19th century, and when association football and rugby diverged, both retained the half-time switch of ends.
