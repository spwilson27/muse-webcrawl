Howard S. Becker

Howard Saul Becker (18 April 1928 – 16 August 2023) was an American sociologist who taught at Northwestern University. He made major contributions to the sociology of deviance, the sociology of art, and the methodology of qualitative research, and is associated with the Chicago School of sociology and symbolic interactionism.

Work

Becker's best-known book, Outsiders (1963), grew out of his fieldwork among dance musicians and marijuana users. It set out labelling theory: deviance, Becker argued, is not a quality of an act itself but a consequence of the application of rules and sanctions by others — "deviant behaviour is behaviour that people so label". The book became a landmark of the sociology of deviance.

In Art Worlds (1982) he argued that art is collective action: works are produced by networks of people — suppliers, fabricators, critics, dealers, and audiences — cooperating through shared conventions. His later methodological writings, including Writing for Social Scientists (1986) and Tricks of the Trade (1998), addressed how qualitative researchers make and write up their inferences.

Becker was also an accomplished jazz pianist, having played professionally in Chicago as a teenager — experience that informed his early ethnographic work. He served as president of the Society for the Study of Social Problems and received numerous honorary doctorates.
