Barbara Jordan

Barbara Jordan
Born: 21 February 1936, Houston, Texas
Died: 17 January 1996, Austin, Texas
Office: U.S. Representative from Texas (1973–1979)

Barbara Charline Jordan (1936–1996) was an American lawyer, educator and politician who was a leader of the civil rights movement. A Democrat from Texas, she was the first African American elected to the Texas Senate after Reconstruction and the first Southern African-American woman elected to the United States House of Representatives.

Early life and education

Jordan was born in Houston's Fifth Ward and educated at Texas Southern University, where she excelled in debate, before earning her law degree from Boston University in 1959. She returned to Texas, practised law and campaigned for Democratic candidates, helping to organise voter registration.

Political career

After election to the Texas Senate in 1966, Jordan championed fair employment and minimum-wage legislation. In Congress (1973–1979) she gained national fame for her televised opening statement during the 1974 Nixon impeachment hearings, declaring, "My faith in the Constitution is whole, it is complete, it is total." Her keynote address to the 1976 Democratic National Convention — the first by an African American and by a woman — is considered one of the greatest American political speeches.

Later life and legacy

Diagnosed with multiple sclerosis, Jordan retired from Congress and taught at the University of Texas at Austin. She chaired the U.S. Commission on Immigration Reform in the 1990s and received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1994. Her papers and a statue honour her at UT Austin and the Texas State Capitol.
