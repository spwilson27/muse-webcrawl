Bataclan (theatre)

The Bataclan is a theatre and concert venue in the 11th arrondissement of Paris, France, at 50 Boulevard Voltaire. Designed in 1864 by the architect Charles Duval, its facade and roof were classed as historic monuments in 1991. Originally a large café-concert with a distinctive chinoiserie exterior pagoda, it takes its name from Ba-ta-clan, a "chinoiserie musicale" by Jacques Offenbach.

History

The Bataclan began as a café-concert and went through periods as a theatre, cinema (from 1926) and, after a fire and restorations, a rock and pop concert venue noted for legendary performances and live albums. It was threatened with demolition in 1950 but survived, and was restored in 2005–2006, returning its facade to the original colours.

November 2015 attack

On 13 November 2015, during a sold-out concert by the American band Eagles of Death Metal, three Islamist gunmen stormed the theatre during a coordinated series of terrorist attacks across Paris, killing 90 people in the venue before police assaulted the building; the attackers died. The Bataclan became the enduring symbol of the attacks, which killed 130 people in total across Paris. After renovation, the theatre reopened on 12 November 2016 with a concert by Sting.
