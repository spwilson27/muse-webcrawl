Weierstrass elliptic function

In mathematics, the Weierstrass elliptic functions are elliptic functions that take a particularly simple form. They are named for Karl Weierstrass. The class is usually indicated by the symbol ℘, a stylised letter p called Weierstrass p. The function ℘(z; Λ) is a meromorphic function on the complex plane that is periodic with respect to a lattice Λ, with a double pole at each lattice point and no other poles.

Definition

Given a lattice Λ = ℤω₁ + ℤω₂ in the complex plane, the Weierstrass function is defined by the series ℘(z) = 1/z² + Σλ∈Λ∖{0} [1/(z−λ)² − 1/λ²], which converges absolutely and uniformly on compact sets away from the lattice. Its derivative ℘′ is an odd elliptic function with triple poles at lattice points.

Properties

The functions ℘ and ℘′ satisfy the differential equation (℘′)² = 4℘³ − g₂℘ − g₃, where g₂ and g₃ are the Eisenstein series invariants of the lattice. This cubic equation parametrises an elliptic curve, and every elliptic curve over the complex numbers arises in this way. Weierstrass functions also obey elegant addition theorems and are closely related to the sigma and zeta functions bearing Weierstrass's name.
