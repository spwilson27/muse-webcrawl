Heat death of the universe

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The heat death of the universe (also known as the Big Chill or Big Freeze) is a hypothesis on the ultimate fate of the universe, which suggests the universe will evolve to a state of no thermodynamic free energy, and will therefore be unable to sustain processes that increase entropy. In this state the universe reaches maximum entropy: all matter and energy are uniformly distributed, temperatures approach absolute zero, and nothing further can happen. The idea grew out of nineteenth-century thermodynamics, particularly the second law, and was articulated by scientists including William Thomson (Lord Kelvin) and Hermann von Helmholtz.

Origin of the idea

In 1851–1852 Kelvin formulated the tendency of mechanical energy to dissipate as heat, concluding that the Earth and the universe were becoming unfit for habitation over time. Rudolf Clausius, who coined "entropy" in 1865, summarised thermodynamics as: "The energy of the universe is constant; the entropy of the universe tends to a maximum" — the end state being the Wärmetod ("heat death"). The concept entered popular culture widely, prompting responses from writers such as H. G. Wells and Isaac Asimov (whose short story "The Last Question" famously revolves around reversing entropy).

Modern cosmological picture

In current ΛCDM cosmology, the expansion of the universe is accelerating under dark energy. On timescales of trillions of years, star formation ceases as gas is exhausted; stellar remnants cool; galaxies outside the Local Group recede beyond the cosmic event horizon. Around 1014 years from now the stelliferous era ends; degenerate and black-hole eras follow, in which black holes themselves evaporate by Hawking radiation over timescales up to roughly 10100 years. Thereafter the universe would consist of a dilute, near-empty expanse of photons, electrons and other stable particles approaching a uniform, near-zero temperature — effectively heat death. Some theories add speculative escapes, such as quantum fluctuations producing a new low-entropy region ("Boltzmann brain" scenarios) or a false-vacuum decay.

Relation to other end-state scenarios

Heat death contrasts with the Big Crunch (recollapse), the Big Rip (runaway expansion tearing structures apart), and cyclic models. Given current observations of accelerating expansion, heat death (or something close to it) is generally considered the most likely fate of the universe.

Objections and discussion

Nineteenth- and twentieth-century thinkers raised objections: Poincaré recurrence implies fluctuations could, over unimaginable times, restore low-entropy states; Maxwell's demon thought experiments probed the statistical nature of the second law; and it has been debated whether "entropy of the universe" is well defined for a gravitating, expanding system. These debates continue within modern statistical mechanics and cosmology.
