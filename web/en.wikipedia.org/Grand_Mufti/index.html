Grand Mufti

A Grand Mufti is the chief Islamic legal authority of a state or region

A Grand Mufti is the chief mufti of a state or region — the senior and most authoritative interpreter of Islamic law (sharia) in Sunni Islam, empowered to issue fatwas (legal opinions). The institution developed within the Ottoman Empire, where the Şeyhülislam of Istanbul stood at the head of the learned hierarchy and ranked among the most powerful figures of the state.

Role

Grand Muftis are typically appointed by the government or elected by scholars, depending on the country. They head official fatwa bodies, such as Egypt's Dar al-Ifta, advise on religious affairs, and certify matters ranging from the sighting of the Ramadan moon to the conformity of legislation with Islamic principles. Their opinions are influential but, in classical Sunni theory, fatwas are non-binding scholarly opinions rather than court judgments, which are the province of qadis.

Notable offices

The Grand Mufti of Egypt, associated with Al-Azhar, is one of the most prestigious posts in Sunni Islam; Muhammad Abduh held the office from 1899. The Grand Mufti of Saudi Arabia heads the Council of Senior Scholars. In Jerusalem, the title became politically prominent under Haj Amin al-Husseini, appointed Grand Mufti by the British in 1921. Many countries in the Middle East, North Africa, the Balkans, and Southeast Asia maintain the office today.
