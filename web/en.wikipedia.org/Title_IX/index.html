Title IX

Title IX
Full titleTitle IX of the Education Amendments of 1972
Enacted23 June 1972
Codified20 U.S.C. §§ 1681–1688
Key sponsorsRep. Patsy Mink; Sen. Birch Bayh; Rep. Edith Green

Title IX is a landmark federal civil rights law in the United States, enacted as part of the Education Amendments of 1972. Its best-known provision states: "No person in the United States shall, on the basis of sex, be excluded from participation in, be denied the benefits of, or be subjected to discrimination under any education program or activity receiving Federal financial assistance." Title IX applies to schools, colleges and other educational institutions receiving federal funding.

History

The law was championed by Representative Patsy Mink of Hawaii, its principal author and later namesake of the Patsy T. Mink Equal Opportunity in Education Act, together with Representative Edith Green and Senator Birch Bayh. It followed hearings documenting pervasive discrimination against women in admissions, hiring and athletics. Because the statute does not mention sports, its athletics implications were worked out through regulations issued in 1975 and a 1979 policy interpretation establishing the three-prong test for effective accommodation of athletic interests and abilities.

Athletics

Title IX is most famous for transforming women's and girls' sports in the United States. Compliance in athletics is assessed in participation opportunities, athletic scholarships, and other program areas such as facilities and scheduling. Since 1972, girls' participation in high school sports has increased more than tenfold, and women's collegiate participation has risen dramatically, though debates continue over proportionality, roster management, and the treatment of men's non-revenue teams.

Sexual harassment and assault

Beginning especially with guidance issued in 2011, Title IX was applied extensively to campus sexual harassment and sexual violence, obliging schools to respond promptly to complaints and prescribing grievance procedures. Regulations issued in 2020 under the Trump administration narrowed definitions and added live-hearing requirements; a 2024 rule under the Biden administration revised those procedures again, and the rule was subsequently vacated by a federal court in 2025, restoring the 2020 framework. The scope of the law regarding gender identity has likewise shifted across administrations and court rulings.
