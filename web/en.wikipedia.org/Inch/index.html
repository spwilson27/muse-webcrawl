Inch

The inch (symbol: in or ″) is a unit of length in the British imperial and United States customary systems of measurement. Since the international yard and pound agreement of 1959, the inch has been defined as exactly 25.4 millimetres. Twelve inches make one foot, and 36 inches one yard.

History

The inch derives from the Roman uncia (one twelfth). In medieval England an inch was legally defined as the length of three barleycorns laid end to end, a definition formalized under Edward II. Various local inches persisted — including the Scottish inch and the slightly different survey inch used in US land surveying until its retirement at the end of 2022 — until international standardization in the twentieth century.

Usage

The inch remains in everyday use in the United States for most measurements and in Canada and the United Kingdom for some purposes. It is standard worldwide for the diagonal size of display screens, bicycle and vehicle wheel diameters, tyre sizes, and many plumbing and engineering specifications. Inches are commonly divided into halves, quarters, eighths, and sixteenths on rulers, or thousandths ("thou") in machining.
