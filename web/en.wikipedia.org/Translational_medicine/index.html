Translational medicine (also translational research or translational science) is an interdisciplinary field that aims to convert discoveries from laboratory, clinical, and population studies into interventions that improve the health of individuals and the public. It is often summarized as moving knowledge "from bench to bedside" — and back again, as clinical observations generate new laboratory questions.

Phases

Frameworks divide translation into stages: T1 transfers basic-science discoveries into candidate treatments and early (phase I/II) clinical testing; T2 moves proven interventions through phase III trials into clinical guidelines and practice; T3 concerns implementation and dissemination in health systems; and T4 evaluates population-level outcomes and policy.

Institutions

Funding bodies created dedicated programs to bridge the gap between discovery and application, notably the US National Center for Advancing Translational Sciences (NCATS, founded 2011) and its Clinical and Translational Science Awards at academic medical centres, along with translational institutes and journals in Europe and elsewhere.

Challenges

Most candidate drugs fail in development, and the gap between efficacy in trials and effectiveness in routine care — sometimes called the "valley of death" — remains a central concern. Biomarkers, better preclinical models, and data sharing are promoted as ways to improve success rates.
