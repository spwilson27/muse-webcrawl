Mount Scopus (Hebrew: Har HaTsofim, "Mount of the Watchers"; Arabic: Jabal al-Masharif) is a mountain in northeast Jerusalem, 826 metres above sea level, long known for its panoramic view over the city and the Judaean Desert.

History

The viewpoint was used by armies besieging Jerusalem from Roman times onward; the name derives from "scopus", a lookout. The Hebrew University of Jerusalem opened its campus on the mountain in 1925, and Hadassah Hospital followed in 1939. Between 1948 and 1967 the university and hospital formed an Israeli enclave within Jordanian-held territory, supplied by twice-monthly convoys. After 1967 the campus was expanded and again became the university's main seat.

Institutions

Mount Scopus hosts the Hebrew University's humanities and social sciences faculties, the National Library of Israel's original building, Hadassah Medical Center, the Jerusalem British War Cemetery, and the Nicanor Cave.
