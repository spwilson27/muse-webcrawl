Religion of Humanity

The Religion of Humanity (French: Religion de l'Humanité) was a secular religion created by Auguste Comte (1798–1857), the founder of positivism, in the later part of his career. Having concluded that society needed a unifying faith even after rejecting theology, Comte proposed the worship of Humanity itself — personified as the "Great Being" (Grand-Être), the collective of all people past, present and future who contribute to human progress.

Doctrine and practice

Comte elaborated a complete liturgy modelled on Catholicism: a positivist calendar of thirteen months named for great figures of history, sacraments marking the stages of life, priests of Humanity, feast days, and prayers directed not to a god but to Humanity, the Earth, and space. His motto was "Love as principle, Order as basis, Progress as goal," a phrase that survives in shortened form on the flag of Brazil ("Ordem e Progresso").

Influence

The religion attracted organised followings in France, Britain — where the Church of Humanity was led by Richard Congreve — and especially Brazil, where the Positivist Church was founded in 1881 and its temple still operates. Though small, Comte's project influenced later secular and humanist movements, as well as the design of rituals in some socialist traditions.
