Duchy of Bohemia

The Duchy of Bohemia was a medieval principality in Central Europe, founded around 870 by the Přemyslid dynasty and centred on Prague. It was a predecessor of the later Kingdom of Bohemia and of the modern Czech state.

History

Emerging after the fall of Great Moravia, Bohemia was Christianised in the 9th and 10th centuries under rulers such as Bořivoj I and Wenceslaus I, later Saint Wenceslaus. The duchy was formally part of the Holy Roman Empire from the early 10th century, though its dukes retained considerable autonomy. Its territory expanded to include Moravia in the 11th century. In 1198 Ottokar I obtained a hereditary royal title, elevating Bohemia to a kingdom, and in 1212 the Golden Bull of Sicily confirmed its privileged status within the Empire.

Legacy

The duchy laid the foundations of Czech statehood; its symbols, including the lion coat of arms and the cult of Saint Wenceslaus, remain central to Czech identity.
