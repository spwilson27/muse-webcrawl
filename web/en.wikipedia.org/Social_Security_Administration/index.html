The Social Security Administration (SSA) is an independent agency of the U.S. federal government that administers Social Security, the social insurance program covering retirement, disability and survivors' benefits. It also administers the Supplemental Security Income (SSI) program and issues Social Security numbers.

History

The SSA was created by the Social Security Act of 1935 as the Social Security Board; the first lump-sum payment was made in 1937, and regular monthly benefits began in 1940 — the first recipient being Ida May Fuller of Vermont. The agency was part of the Department of Health and Human Services (and its predecessors) for decades before becoming an independent agency again in 1995.

Operations

Headquartered in Woodlawn, Maryland, the SSA operates a network of regional offices and more than 1,200 field offices, and employs around 60,000 people. It maintains earnings records for nearly every American worker, processes millions of claims each year, and distributes over a trillion dollars in benefits annually, making it one of the largest administrative operations in the world.
