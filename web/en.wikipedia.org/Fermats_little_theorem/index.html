Fermat's little theorem

Fermat's little theorem in modular arithmetic

Fermat's little theorem states that if p is a prime number, then for any integer a, the number ap − a is an integer multiple of p. In the notation of modular arithmetic, this is expressed as:

ap ≡ a (mod p).

If a is not divisible by p, the theorem is equivalent to the statement that ap−1 − 1 is an integer multiple of p:

ap−1 ≡ 1 (mod p).

History

Pierre de Fermat first stated the theorem in a letter dated 18 October 1640 to Bernard Frénicle de Bessy, without proof. The first published proof was given by Leonhard Euler in 1736; Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz had produced an essentially identical proof in an unpublished manuscript before 1683.

Applications

The theorem is fundamental in number theory and forms the basis of the Fermat primality test, and it underlies aspects of public-key cryptography, including the RSA algorithm. It is a special case of Euler's theorem and is generalised by Carmichael's theorem. It is distinct from the much harder Fermat's Last Theorem.
