Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons

Adopted7 July 2017
LocationUnited Nations headquarters, New York

DepositarySecretary-General of the United Nations

The Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons (TPNW), or the Nuclear Weapon Ban Treaty, is the first legally binding international agreement to comprehensively prohibit nuclear weapons with the ultimate goal being their total elimination. It was adopted on 7 July 2017, opened for signature on 20 September 2017, and entered into force on 22 January 2021, ninety days after the fiftieth ratification.

Background

The treaty grew out of the "humanitarian initiative" on nuclear weapons and the work of the International Campaign to Abolish Nuclear Weapons (ICAN), which received the 2017 Nobel Peace Prize for its role in bringing the treaty about. Negotiations were mandated by the UN General Assembly in 2016 and were boycotted by all of the nuclear-weapon states and most of their allies.

Provisions

States parties undertake never under any circumstances to develop, test, produce, manufacture, acquire, possess, stockpile, use, or threaten to use nuclear weapons or other nuclear explosive devices. The treaty also prohibits assisting any such activities, and the stationing of nuclear weapons on a party's territory. It requires parties to provide assistance to victims of the use and testing of nuclear weapons and to take measures toward the environmental remediation of contaminated areas. Parties that possess nuclear weapons may join by agreeing to a time-bound plan for their verified elimination.

Support and opposition

The treaty was adopted by a vote of 122 in favour, one against (the Netherlands), and one abstention (Singapore). The nuclear-weapon states, including all five recognised under the Non-Proliferation Treaty, have rejected it, arguing that it undermines the existing nonproliferation regime; NATO members have generally aligned with that position. Supporters argue the treaty stigmatises nuclear weapons and strengthens the global norm against them.
