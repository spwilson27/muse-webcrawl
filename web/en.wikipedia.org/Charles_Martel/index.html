Charles Martel (c. 688 – 22 October 741), surnamed "the Hammer" (Martellus), was a Frankish statesman and military leader who, as Mayor of the Palace and Duke of the Franks, was the de facto ruler of Francia from 718 until his death. He is best known for defeating the Umayyad invasion of Gaul at the Battle of Tours in 732, and as the founder of Carolingian power: his son Pepin the Short deposed the last Merovingian king and took the crown, and his grandson was Charlemagne.

Rise to power

Charles was the illegitimate son of Pepin of Herstal, Mayor of the Palace of Austrasia, by his concubine Alpaida. When Pepin died in 714, Charles's stepmother Plectrude imprisoned him and sought power for her own grandsons. Charles escaped, rallied the Austrasian Franks, and after initial defeats won the civil war with victories at Amblève (716), Vinchy (717), and Soissons (718), making himself mayor of both Austrasia and Neustria while nominal Merovingian kings reigned as figureheads.

Consolidation and campaigns

Martel spent two decades unifying and defending the Frankish realm: he campaigned against the Saxons, Frisians, Bavarians, Alamanni, and Aquitanians, and reorganised the Frankish military, rewarding mounted warriors with benefices — often carved from church lands, a practice that drew clerical condemnation (later tradition pictured him damned for despoiling the Church). His secularisation of ecclesiastical property nevertheless helped fund the forces that defended Francia.

Battle of Tours

In 732 an Umayyad army under Abd al-Rahman al-Ghafiqi, governor of al-Andalus, advanced from Spain through Aquitaine, sacking Bordeaux. Charles met him between Poitiers and Tours in October 732. The Frankish infantry stood in a defensive formation and repelled repeated attacks; Abd al-Rahman was killed, and the Muslim army withdrew south overnight. The victory earned Charles his surname and a reputation in later European historiography as the saviour of Christian Europe, though modern historians debate whether the expedition was a full-scale invasion or a large raid, and note that Muslim forces retained Narbonne and Septimania until the 750s, which Charles and Pepin fought further campaigns to recover (including Charles's failed siege of Narbonne in 737).

Death and succession

Charles died at Quierzy in October 741 and was buried at Saint-Denis among the Merovingian kings — an assertion of his family's status. He divided the mayoralty between his sons Carloman and Pepin the Short. Within a decade Pepin had deposed Childeric III (751) and assumed the kingship, founding the Carolingian dynasty formally crowned with papal support.
