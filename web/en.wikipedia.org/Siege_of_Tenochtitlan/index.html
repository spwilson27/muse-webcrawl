Siege of Tenochtitlan

The siege of Tenochtitlan, the capital of the Aztec Empire, was a decisive battle in the Spanish conquest of the Aztec Empire. It culminated in the fall of the city on 13 August 1521 after a siege lasting roughly three months.

Background

Expelled from Tenochtitlan during La Noche Triste in 1520, Hernán Cortés rebuilt his forces with thousands of indigenous allies, especially the Tlaxcalans, and constructed thirteen brigantines to control Lake Texcoco.

The siege

From late May 1521, Spanish and allied forces cut the causeways, destroyed the Chapultepec aqueduct, and advanced street by street into the city, whose defenders under Emperor Cuauhtémoc were weakened by a smallpox epidemic. Block by block the city was demolished as the attackers filled the canals with rubble.

Aftermath

Cuauhtémoc was captured attempting to flee by canoe. The ruined capital was rebuilt as Mexico City, capital of New Spain, and the Aztec Empire effectively ceased to exist.
