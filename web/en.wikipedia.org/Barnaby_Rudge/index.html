Barnaby Rudge

Barnaby Rudge: A Tale of the Riots of Eighty is a historical novel by the English author Charles Dickens, first published in weekly instalments in 1841 in his periodical Master Humphrey's Clock. It is one of Dickens's two historical novels, the other being A Tale of Two Cities, and is set against the Gordon Riots of 1780.

Plot

The novel centres on the simple-minded Barnaby Rudge, his devoted mother, and his talking raven Grip. Its plot intertwines a murder mystery — the long-unsolved killing of a steward at the Warren, a Chigwell country house — with the anti-Catholic Gordon Riots that swept London in June 1780, during which Barnaby is swept into the mob and nearly executed. Other principal characters include the locksmith Gabriel Varden, his daughter Dolly, the apprentice Simon Tappertit, the landlord John Willet, and the sinister Sir John Chester.

Reception and legacy

Barnaby Rudge was somewhat less popular than Dickens's other novels, though its riot scenes are widely admired. Edgar Allan Poe, reviewing an early instalment, famously deduced aspects of the murder plot before its resolution; the raven Grip is often credited as an inspiration for Poe's poem "The Raven". Grip was based on Dickens's own pet raven, whose stuffed body is now held by the Free Library of Philadelphia.
