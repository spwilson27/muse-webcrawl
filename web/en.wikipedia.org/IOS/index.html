iOS (formerly iPhone OS) is a mobile operating system developed by Apple Inc. exclusively for its hardware. It powers the iPhone and previously the iPod Touch and iPad (the latter now running the derived iPadOS). Unveiled with the first iPhone in January 2007 and released that June, iOS is the world's second-most-installed mobile operating system after Android.

History

The system derives from Apple's Darwin foundation, shared with macOS, and introduced a touch interface based on direct manipulation with multi-touch gestures. Initially it shipped without third-party applications; the App Store opened in July 2008 with the release of iPhone OS 2, transforming the platform and the software business. The system was renamed iOS in 2010, the year the iPad launched. Major redesigns included the flat "skeuomorphism-free" interface of iOS 7 (2013), widgets and the App Library (iOS 14), and the "Liquid Glass" design language announced in 2025, when Apple also aligned version numbers across its platforms (iOS 26).

Features and ecosystem

iOS provides the home screen, notifications, Siri voice assistant, Face ID and Touch ID biometric security, iMessage and FaceTime communication, Apple Pay, and deep integration with services such as iCloud and the Apple silicon ecosystem. Applications are distributed almost exclusively through the App Store, subject to Apple's review guidelines—a model that has drawn antitrust scrutiny, notably under the European Union's Digital Markets Act. Annual major releases are previewed at Apple's Worldwide Developers Conference each June.
