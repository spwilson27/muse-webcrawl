The Battle of Taginae (also known as the Battle of Busta Gallorum) was fought in July 552 between the army of the Byzantine Empire, commanded by the eunuch general Narses, and the Ostrogothic army of King Totila. The decisive Byzantine victory broke the power of the Ostrogothic Kingdom in Italy.

Background

The Gothic War had begun in 535 as Emperor Justinian I's attempt to reconquer Italy. Under Totila, the Goths had recovered much of the peninsula. In 551 Narses was given a large army and marched around the head of the Adriatic into Italy, meeting Totila in Umbria along the Via Flaminia.

The battle

Narses deployed his army in a strong crescent formation, dismounting his Germanic cavalry in the centre and massing archers on the wings. Totila, awaiting reinforcements, delayed with displays of horsemanship, then launched cavalry charges against the Byzantine centre. The charges were shot to pieces by archers on both flanks and broke against the dismounted cavalry. A final Byzantine advance routed the Gothic army; Totila was mortally wounded and died shortly afterward.

Aftermath

With Totila dead and his army destroyed, effective Ostrogothic resistance collapsed. Narses went on to defeat the last Gothic forces at Mons Lactarius later in 552, completing the Byzantine reconquest of Italy — though the peninsula was devastated by two decades of war and was soon invaded by the Lombards.
