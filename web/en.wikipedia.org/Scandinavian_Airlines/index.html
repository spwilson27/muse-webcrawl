Scandinavian Airlines, commonly known as SAS, is the flag carrier of Denmark, Norway and Sweden. Formed in 1946 from the merger of the three countries' national airlines, it was the world's first multinational airline consortium and pioneered transpolar routes in the 1950s with flights from Copenhagen to Los Angeles via Greenland.

A Scandinavian Airlines Airbus at Copenhagen Airport

History

SAS was a founding member of the Star Alliance in 1997. Partially state-owned by its three home countries, the airline faced mounting competition from low-cost carriers and underwent repeated restructuring. In 2022 it filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in the United States; an Air France–KLM-led consortium took a stake, Denmark retained a share, and in 2024 SAS left Star Alliance to join SkyTeam.

Operations

The airline operates hubs at Copenhagen, Oslo Gardermoen and Stockholm Arlanda, serving European, North American and Asian destinations with a fleet centred on Airbus narrow-body and wide-body aircraft. Its frequent-flyer programme is EuroBonus.
