Icewind Dale is a role-playing video game developed by Black Isle Studios and published by Interplay Entertainment in 2000. Set in the Icewind Dale region of the Forgotten Realms campaign setting of Dungeons & Dragons, it uses BioWare's Infinity Engine and a real-time-with-pause combat system, in the manner of Black Isle's Baldur's Gate, but with a greater emphasis on combat and dungeon exploration.

Unlike Baldur's Gate, in which the player creates a single protagonist and gathers companions, Icewind Dale lets the player create an entire party of up to six characters. The story begins in the fishing village of Easthaven, one of the Ten Towns, as an expedition departs for the pass of Kuldahar, threatened by an unknown evil.

Expansions and sequels

An expansion, Icewind Dale: Heart of Winter, followed in 2001, with a free downloadable add-on, Trials of the Luremaster. A sequel, Icewind Dale II, was released in 2002. Beamdog released Icewind Dale: Enhanced Edition in 2014 for modern platforms.

Reception

The game was well received, praised for Jeremy Soule's musical score, its atmospheric art, and its challenging party-based combat, though some reviewers found its story thinner than that of Baldur's Gate.

The fictional region itself was popularised by R. A. Salvatore's The Icewind Dale Trilogy (1988–1990), which introduced the drow ranger Drizzt Do'Urden.
