The naginata (なぎなた, 薙刀) is a Japanese polearm consisting of a curved, single-edged blade mounted on a long wooden shaft. It was used by samurai, foot soldiers, and warrior monks, and became especially associated with women of the samurai class (onna-musha), who were expected to defend their households with it.

History

The naginata developed during the Heian and Kamakura periods and was a prominent battlefield weapon in the wars of the twelfth through fifteenth centuries, valued for its reach and sweeping cuts against infantry and cavalry. As warfare shifted to massed spear (yari) formations and firearms in the sixteenth century, the naginata declined as a battlefield arm but survived as a martial art and a symbol of household defence.

Modern practice

Today the weapon is practised in koryū schools and in the modern sport of atarashii naginata, governed in Japan by the All Japan Naginata Federation. Modern sporting practice uses bamboo-and-oak practice weapons, protective armour similar to kendo equipment, and kata and sparring competitions, and is practised predominantly by women in Japan and by a growing international community.
