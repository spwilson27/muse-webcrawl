Anarcho-primitivism

Anarcho-primitivism is an anarchist critique of civilization that advocates a return to non-"civilized" ways of life through de-industrialization, abolition of the division of labour or specialization, and abandonment of large-scale organization and high technology. Anarcho-primitivists critique the origins and progress of the Industrial Revolution and industrial society. According to anarcho-primitivism, the shift from hunter-gatherer to agricultural subsistence gave rise to social stratification, coercion, alienation, and population growth.

Origins

Anarcho-primitivism draws on earlier currents of thought, including the writings of Henry David Thoreau, Leo Tolstoy, and the Luddites, as well as later critics of technology such as Jacques Ellul and Ivan Illich. Modern anarcho-primitivism is particularly associated with the work of John Zerzan, whose essays such as "Future Primitive" (1994) examined anthropological accounts of hunter-gatherer societies as comparatively egalitarian and free of hierarchy. The journal Fifth Estate published primitivist critiques from the 1970s onward, with Fredy Perlman an influential figure.

Concepts

Primivist thought centres on the claim that civilization — understood as the complex of agriculture, cities, symbolic culture, and technological mediation — is the root of domination. Zerzan argues that symbolic representation itself (language, art, number, time) alienates humans from direct experience. Other primitivists focus more narrowly on domestication, arguing that the domestication of plants and animals was the original template for the domination of human beings.

Criticism

Anarcho-primitivism has been criticized by other anarchists and social theorists, who argue that hunter-gatherer life involved hardship, high infant mortality, and short life expectancy, and that abandoning technology would be catastrophic for a global population of billions, including people dependent on modern medicine. Critics such as Murray Bookchin described primitivist tendencies as "lifestyle anarchism".
