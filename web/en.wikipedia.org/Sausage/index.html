Sausage

A sausage is a type of meat product usually made from ground meat — often pork, beef, or poultry — with salt, spices and other flavourings, typically encased in a skin. The word derives from the Latin salsus, "salted". Sausages may be cooked, cured, smoked or dried, and number among the oldest forms of processed food.

TypeProcessed meat product
Main ingredientsGround meat, fat, salt, spices
VariationsFresh, cooked, cured, fermented, blood sausage

History

Sausage-making was an early food preservation technique: Homer mentions blood sausage in the Odyssey, and the Roman cookery book Apicius gives sausage recipes. Curing and stuffing meat into casings made animal products keep longer and use whole carcasses efficiently. Nearly every meat-eating culture developed local varieties.

Types

Fresh sausages (such as British bangers, Italian salsiccia and American breakfast links) must be cooked before eating. Cooked sausages include frankfurters and mortadella; fermented and dried sausages include salami, chorizo and saucisson; blood sausages such as black pudding and morcilla use blood as a principal ingredient. Well-known regional types include German Bratwurst and Weißwurst, Polish kiełbasa, Spanish chorizo, and Chinese lap cheong. Vegetarian and vegan sausages substitute plant proteins.

Casings and production

Traditional casings are made from cleaned animal intestines; collagen, cellulose and plastic casings are also used. Industrial production involves grinding, seasoning, emulsifying or coarsely filling, linking, and in some cases smoking or curing with nitrites — a process whose health effects, like those of processed meat generally, have been the subject of dietary research and regulation.
