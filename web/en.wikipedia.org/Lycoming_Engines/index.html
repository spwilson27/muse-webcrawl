Lycoming Engines

A Lycoming O-320 flat-four aircraft engine.

Lycoming Engines is an American manufacturer of aircraft engines, based in Williamsport, Pennsylvania. A division of Avco Corporation and subsidiary of Textron, Lycoming produces a line of air-cooled, horizontally opposed piston engines that power a large share of the world's general aviation fleet.

History

The company's roots go back to 1845 as a sewing machine and bicycle manufacturer. Lycoming began building automobile engines in the early 20th century and produced its first aircraft engine, the R-680 radial, in 1929. After the Second World War it concentrated on flat-four and flat-six piston engines for light aircraft.

Products

Lycoming's best-known families are the O-235, O-320, O-360, and O-540 series, fitted to aircraft such as the Cessna 172 and Piper PA-28, as well as the turbocharged and fuel-injected IO- and TIO- variants. The company also manufactures engines for helicopters and the Thunderbolt series for experimental aircraft.
