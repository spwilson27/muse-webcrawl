Iquique (Spanish pronunciation: [iˈkike]) is a port city and commune in northern Chile, capital of both the Iquique Province and the Tarapacá Region. It lies on the Pacific coast at the foot of the coastal cordillera, immediately west of the Atacama Desert, and grew to prominence in the 19th century as the shipping port for the nitrate (saltpeter) fields of the interior.

History

The area was inhabited by the Changos people before Spanish rule and formed part of Peru until the War of the Pacific (1879–1884). Offshore, on 21 May 1879, the Naval Battle of Iquique was fought, in which the Chilean corvette Esmeralda under Arturo Prat was sunk by the Peruvian monitor Huáscar — a foundational episode in Chilean national memory. Chile annexed the province under the Treaty of Ancón (1883).

Iquique prospered on nitrate exports handled through the oficinas of the pampa; it was also the site of the 1907 Santa María School massacre, in which striking nitrate workers and their families were killed by the army. The collapse of the nitrate industry in the 1930s brought decline, later offset by fishing, copper, and commerce.

Modern city

Today Iquique is a free-trade zone (ZOFRI, created 1975), a major import and shopping centre serving Bolivia and the region, and a beach resort noted for Playa Cavancha and paragliding from the enormous Cerro Dragón sand dune. The city's Baquedano Street preserves Georgian-style timber mansions from the nitrate era. Iquique sits in an active seismic zone and was affected by the magnitude 8.2 earthquake of 1 April 2014, which generated a tsunami and mass evacuations.
