Back Bay

Back Bay is an officially recognised neighbourhood of Boston, Massachusetts, famous for its rows of Victorian brownstone homes and regarded as one of the best-preserved examples of 19th-century urban design in the United States.

History

The area was originally exactly what its name says: a tidal bay of the Charles River, receiving the city's sewage. Between the 1850s and 1900 the bay was filled with gravel hauled in by rail from Needham, creating new land that was laid out on a grid of wide avenues.

Architecture

Commonwealth Avenue, with its landscaped mall, anchors the neighbourhood. Streets were built up alphabetically from Arlington to Hereford with elegant brownstone and brick houses in styles from Greek Revival to Beaux-Arts. Landmarks include Trinity Church, the Boston Public Library in Copley Square, and the Prudential Tower and John Hancock Tower at the neighbourhood's western end.

Today

Back Bay is among Boston's most expensive residential and retail districts; Newbury Street is lined with boutiques, galleries and cafés. See also Boston and Copley Square.
