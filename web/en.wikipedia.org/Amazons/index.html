Amazons

The Amazons (Ancient Greek: Ἀμαζόνες, Amazónes, singular Ἀμαζών, Amazṓn) were a tribe of female warriors and hunters in Greek mythology, known for their fierce martial prowess. They were said to live at the outer edges of the known world, variously placed in Asia Minor, Libya, or the steppes north of the Black Sea around the river Thermodon.

In mythology

Amazons appear in many Greek legends. Heracles was sent to obtain the girdle of the Amazon queen Hippolyta as one of his twelve labours. Theseus abducted an Amazon queen, Antiope or Hippolyta, provoking the Attic War, an Amazon invasion of Athens. Achilles killed the Amazon queen Penthesilea, who had come to aid Troy in the Trojan War, and fell in love with her as she died. Famous Amazons include Hippolyta, Penthesilea, Antiope, Melanippe, and the later legendary queen Thalestris, who was said to have visited Alexander the Great.

Historiography

Greek writers such as Herodotus, Strabo and Diodorus Siculus treated the Amazons as a historical people. Modern scholars have connected the legends to the nomadic peoples of the Eurasian steppe: archaeological excavations of Scythian and Sarmatian burials have found that a significant proportion of warrior graves containing weapons belonged to women, lending some basis to the ancient accounts. The Amazon River in South America was named after reports by Francisco de Orellana's expedition of female warriors encountered in 1542.

Legacy

The Amazons remain a popular subject in art and literature, from classical vase painting to the comic-book heroine Wonder Woman, whose home, Themyscira, derives from the legendary Amazon capital.
