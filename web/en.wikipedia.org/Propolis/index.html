Propolis

Bees use propolis to seal gaps and protect the hive.

Propolis, or bee glue, is a resinous mixture produced by honey bees from tree bud resins, sap flows, and other botanical sources, combined with beeswax and bee secretions. Bees use it to seal small gaps in the hive, smooth internal surfaces, and embalm intruders too large to remove, helping protect the colony from infection.

Composition

Propolis typically contains about 50% resins and vegetable balsams, 30% waxes, 10% essential oils, and 5% pollen, with hundreds of identified compounds including flavonoids and phenolic acids. Its composition varies markedly with local flora: poplar-type propolis predominates in temperate zones, while Brazilian green propolis derives mainly from Baccharis.

Uses

Propolis has a long history in folk medicine and is sold in tinctures, lozenges, and cosmetics for purported antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory effects; scientific evidence for most health claims is limited, though it is studied for oral and wound care. It was also historically used as a varnish ingredient, including in violin making, and some people are allergic to it.
