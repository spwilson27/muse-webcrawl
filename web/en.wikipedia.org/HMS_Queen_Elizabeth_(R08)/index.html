HMS Queen Elizabeth (pennant number R08) is the lead ship of the Queen Elizabeth-class aircraft carriers and the largest warship ever built for the Royal Navy. Named by Queen Elizabeth II at Rosyth in 2014 and commissioned in December 2017, she serves as the fleet flagship.

Design

The carrier displaces about 65,000 tonnes and measures 284 metres in length. She is configured for STOVL operations, operating F-35B Lightning II jets from a ski-jump and a range of helicopters, and is powered by an integrated electric propulsion system driven by gas turbines and diesels. Her two islands and two lifts optimise flight operations; unlike her namesake liner and battleship, she was named for Queen Elizabeth I, though commissioned under Elizabeth II.

Service

After sea trials and first-of-class flying trials with F-35Bs off the US east coast, Queen Elizabeth led the UK Carrier Strike Group on its first operational deployment in 2021 (CSG21), a seven-month voyage to the Indo-Pacific with US and allied ships, during which her jets conducted strike operations against Islamic State targets. In 2024 she was withdrawn from NATO exercise Steadfast Defender at short notice because of a propeller shaft issue, with her sister ship HMS Prince of Wales taking her place.
