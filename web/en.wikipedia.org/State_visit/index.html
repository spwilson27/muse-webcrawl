State visit

A state visit features full ceremonial honours.

A state visit is a formal visit by a head of state to a foreign country, at the invitation of the head of state of that country, who acts as the official host for the duration of the trip. State visits are the highest form of diplomatic contact between two nations, and are marked by elaborate ceremonial — including state banquets, 21-gun salutes, and inspection of honour guards — that distinguishes them from official or working visits.

Components

In the United Kingdom, state visits are hosted by the monarch, usually with the guest staying at Buckingham Palace or Windsor Castle, and include a carriage procession and a state banquet. In the United States, state visits feature an arrival ceremony on the South Lawn of the White House and a state dinner. France hosts state visits with ceremonies at the Élysée Palace and Les Invalides. Visits typically last several days and combine pageantry with substantive talks, trade promotion, and cultural events.

Significance

Because heads of state can usually make only a limited number of state visits each year, an invitation signals a close bilateral relationship and is used to celebrate or strengthen alliances and partnerships. State visits are distinguished in protocol from official visits by heads of government and from private or working visits, which involve less ceremony.
