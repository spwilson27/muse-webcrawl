The thousand-armed Avalokiteshvara, whose many hands reach out to all beings in need.

Avalokiteshvara (Sanskrit: अवलोकितेश्वर, "the lord who looks down [in compassion]") is a bodhisattva in Mahayana Buddhism who embodies the compassion of all Buddhas. One of the most widely revered bodhisattvas, Avalokiteshvara is known across Asia by many names: Guanyin in China, Kannon in Japan, Quan Âm in Vietnam, and Chenrezig in Tibet, where the Dalai Lamas are regarded as his emanations.

Scriptural basis

The Lotus Sutra devotes its 25th chapter to Avalokiteshvara, the "Perceiver of the Sounds of the World", describing how the bodhisattva hears the cries of beings in distress and rescues them, taking whatever form is needed. The famous mantra Om mani padme hum is associated with Avalokiteshvara, especially in Tibetan Buddhism. In some traditions the bodhisattva is depicted with a thousand arms and multiple heads to better aid all suffering beings.

Forms across Asia

Originally depicted as male in Indian and early Chinese art, Avalokiteshvara gradually assumed female form as Guanyin in China from around the Song dynasty onward, becoming a beloved goddess-like figure of mercy. In Tibet, Chenrezig is the patron deity of the country, and the Potala Palace in Lhasa is named for Avalokiteshvara's pure land, Mount Potalaka.
