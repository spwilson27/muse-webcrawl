Lowest temperature recorded on Earth

Lowest natural temperature recorded
Direct measurement: −89.2 °C (−128.6 °F), Vostok Station, Antarctica, 21 July 1983
Satellite estimate: about −93.2 °C (−135.8 °F), East Antarctic Plateau, 10 August 2010 (surface temperature, unverified as air temperature)
[Image placeholder: Vostok Station on the polar plateau of Antarctica]

The lowest natural temperature ever directly recorded at the surface of the Earth is −89.2 °C (−128.6 °F), measured at the Soviet Vostok Station in Antarctica on 21 July 1983. The reading was taken at an altitude of about 3,488 metres on the East Antarctic Ice Sheet during the long polar night.

Satellite measurements

Remote sensing has found still colder surface ("skin") temperatures: NASA's Landsat 8 and MODIS instruments recorded roughly −93.2 °C in pockets along the ridge between Dome Argus and Dome Fuji on 10 August 2010. Because these are surface temperatures rather than standard air temperatures measured above the ground, the World Meteorological Organization continues to recognise the Vostok value as the official record.

Other cold poles

The coldest permanently inhabited places are in north-eastern Siberia, notably Oymyakon and Verkhoyansk in the Sakha Republic, where temperatures near −67.7 °C have been recorded. The Northern Hemisphere record is held by Greenland's Klinck research station at −69.6 °C (1991, ratified in 2020).
