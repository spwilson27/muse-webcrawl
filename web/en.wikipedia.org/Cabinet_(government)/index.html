Cabinet (government)

A cabinet is a body of high-ranking state officials, typically consisting of the executive branch's top leaders. Members of a cabinet are usually called cabinet ministers or secretaries. The function of a cabinet varies by system: in some countries it is a collegiate decision-making body with collective responsibility, while in others it may be a purely advisory body or an institution for assisting the decision-making of the head of government.

Origins

The term derives from the small private rooms — cabinets — in which early modern rulers consulted their closest advisers. In Britain, the cabinet developed out of the Privy Council during the 17th and 18th centuries, as the monarch increasingly governed through a small committee of leading parliamentarians. By the 19th century cabinet government with collective ministerial responsibility to Parliament had become the constitutional convention of the United Kingdom.

Parliamentary systems

In parliamentary systems the cabinet is the central organ of executive power. It is headed by a prime minister, its members are typically drawn from the legislature, and it serves only while it retains the confidence of parliament. Decisions are conventionally collective, and ministers present a united front in public under the doctrine of cabinet collective responsibility.

Presidential systems

In presidential systems such as the United States, the cabinet consists of the heads of the executive departments, appointed by the president (with Senate confirmation in the US) and serving at the president's pleasure. The US Cabinet includes the vice president and the heads of 15 executive departments, the most senior being the Secretary of State. Unlike in parliamentary systems, it acts principally as an advisory body; ultimate executive authority rests with the president.

Composition

Cabinets commonly include ministers responsible for finance, foreign affairs, defence, interior/home affairs and justice, together with portfolio ministers for areas such as health, education and transport. Senior cabinet committees handle specialised policy areas, and "inner cabinets" or war cabinets may be formed in crises.
