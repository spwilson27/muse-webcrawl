Ignatius of Loyola

Saint Ignatius of Loyola (Basque: Ignazio Loiolakoa; Spanish: Ignacio de Loyola; Latin: Ignatius de Loyola; born Íñigo López de Oñaz y Loyola; c. 23 October 1491 – 31 July 1556), venerated in the Catholic Church as Saint Ignatius of Loyola, was a Spanish Basque Catholic priest and theologian who, with six companions, founded the religious order known as the Society of Jesus (Jesuits) and became its first Superior General at Paris in 1541.

Early life

Ignatius was born into a local noble family in the castle of Loyola in the municipality of Azpeitia, Gipuzkoa, in the Basque region of Spain. As a young man he pursued a military career and was seriously wounded by a cannonball at the Battle of Pamplona in 1521, which shattered his leg. During his long recovery at Loyola, he read religious books, including a life of Christ and lives of the saints, and underwent a spiritual conversion.

Conversion and studies

After recovering, Ignatius laid his sword before an image of the Virgin Mary at the Abbey of Montserrat and spent months in prayer in a cave at Manresa, where he began composing the Spiritual Exercises, a manual of meditations and prayers that remains central to Jesuit spirituality. He later studied at Barcelona, Alcalá, Salamanca, and Paris, where he gathered companions including Francis Xavier and Peter Faber.

Founding of the Society of Jesus

On 15 August 1534, Ignatius and six companions took vows of poverty and chastity at Montmartre in Paris. The Society of Jesus was formally approved by Pope Paul III in 1540. Ignatius served as the order's first Superior General, directing its worldwide missions from Rome and composing its Constitutions. Under his leadership the Jesuits became a major force in the Counter-Reformation, education, and overseas missions.

Death and legacy

Ignatius died in Rome on 31 July 1556. He was beatified in 1609 and canonized on 12 March 1622 by Pope Gregory XV. His feast day is celebrated on 31 July. His works include the Spiritual Exercises, the Constitutions of the Society of Jesus, and thousands of letters.
