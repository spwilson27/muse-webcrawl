Ken Thompson

Kenneth Lane Thompson (born February 4, 1943) is an American pioneer of computer science. Thompson worked at Bell Labs for most of his career where he designed and implemented the original Unix operating system. He also invented the B programming language, the direct predecessor to the C programming language, and was one of the creators and early developers of the Plan 9 operating system. Since 2006, Thompson has worked at Google, where he co-developed the Go programming language.

Unix and B

In the 1960s Thompson worked on the Multics project at Bell Labs. After Bell withdrew from Multics in 1969, Thompson, with Dennis Ritchie and others, began developing a new operating system on a little-used PDP-7 minicomputer. The result, jokingly named Unics and soon Unix, embodied the philosophy of small, composable tools. In 1973 Thompson rewrote Unix in the new C language developed by Ritchie. Thompson also wrote the first implementation of regular expressions in an editor, developed the QED editor, and created the B language from which C evolved.

Other work

With Rob Pike, Thompson designed the UTF-8 character encoding in 1992, now the dominant encoding of the World Wide Web. He was a principal designer of the Plan 9 and Inferno operating systems. A keen computer-chess programmer, he co-developed the champion chess machine Belle and generated endgame tablebases that bear his influence. His 1983 Turing Award lecture, "Reflections on Trusting Trust", described a self-replicating compiler backdoor and remains a classic of computer security literature.

Google and Go

Thompson joined Google in 2006. Frustrated by slow builds in C++, he, Robert Griesemer and Rob Pike began designing the Go programming language in 2007; Go was released as open source in 2009 and reached version 1.0 in 2012.
