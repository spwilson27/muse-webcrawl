Elevator

This article is about the vertical transport device. In British English it is called a lift.

An elevator (North American English) or lift (British English) is a machine that vertically transports people or freight between levels of a building, vessel or other structure. Elevators are typically powered by electric motors driving traction cables and counterweight systems, or by hydraulic pistons. Together with the escalator, they made the skyscraper — and hence the modern city — practical.

History

Hoists powered by people, animals, water or steam existed from antiquity (Archimedes built one around 236 BC). The key invention was the safety brake: in 1852 Elisha Otis devised a device that locked the car if the hoisting cable broke, dramatically demonstrating it at the 1854 New York Crystal Palace exhibition. The first passenger elevator was installed in a New York store in 1857; electric elevators by Siemens (1880) and others followed, and the Otis Elevator Company became the industry's giant.

Types and operation

Traction elevators are lifted by steel ropes over a sheave, balanced by a counterweight, and serve mid- and high-rise buildings, with gearless machines reaching speeds above 10 m/s; Taipei 101 and the Shanghai Tower use lifts among the fastest in the world. Hydraulic elevators, pushed by a fluid-driven piston, suit low-rise buildings. Modern installations use microprocessor group control and destination dispatch. Safety systems include overspeed governors, buffers, door interlocks and multiple redundant cables.

Use

Building codes in most countries require elevators in multi-storey buildings, and accessibility laws have made step-free access an expectation in public buildings. Freight elevators, vehicle lifts, dumbwaiters and aircraft-carrier lifts are specialised variants.
