Ovoviviparity

Ovoviviparity is a mode of reproduction in which embryos develop inside eggs that are retained within the mother's body until they are ready to hatch; the young are then born live. Unlike truly viviparous animals, the embryos receive nourishment almost entirely from the egg yolk rather than from the mother via a placenta, though she provides protection and gas exchange.

Examples

Ovoviviparity occurs in many sharks and rays, some snakes (such as garter snakes and many vipers), some lizards, and various invertebrates including certain cockroaches and scorpions (in some classifications). In some species, unborn young engage in oophagy (eating unfertilized eggs) or sibling cannibalism in the uterus, as in the sand tiger shark. The term has fallen partly out of favor among biologists, who often describe these animals as "aplacental viviparous" to emphasize that they give live birth.
