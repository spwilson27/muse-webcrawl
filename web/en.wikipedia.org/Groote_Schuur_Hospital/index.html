Groote Schuur Hospital

Groote Schuur Hospital
Location: Observatory, Cape Town, South Africa
Established: 1938
Type: Teaching hospital, tertiary care
Affiliation: University of Cape Town

Groote Schuur Hospital is a large government-funded teaching hospital situated in the Observatory suburb of Cape Town, South Africa, on the slopes of Devil's Peak. It is the chief teaching hospital of the Faculty of Health Sciences of the University of Cape Town.

History

The hospital opened in 1938, taking its name from the nearby Groote Schuur estate, bequeathed by Cecil John Rhodes to the people of South Africa. The name means "Great Barn" in Dutch, after a barn built on the estate in the seventeenth century.

The first heart transplant

Groote Schuur achieved worldwide fame on 3 December 1967, when a surgical team led by Professor Christiaan Barnard performed the first human-to-human heart transplant there. The patient, Louis Washkansky, survived for 18 days. The operation made the hospital a landmark in the history of transplant surgery.

Services

The hospital provides tertiary and quaternary care across most medical and surgical specialities and serves as a major trauma centre for the Western Cape. It works in close association with the Red Cross War Memorial Children's Hospital.
