Deep Purple


Deep Purple performing in 2013

Deep Purple are an English rock band formed in London in 1967 (as Roundabout, renamed in 1968). They are considered pioneers of heavy metal and modern hard rock, alongside Led Zeppelin and Black Sabbath, and were once listed in the Guinness Book of World Records as "the globe's loudest band." They have sold over 100 million albums worldwide.

History

The classic "Mark II" line-up — Ian Gillan (vocals), Ritchie Blackmore (guitar), Roger Glover (bass), Jon Lord (keyboards), and Ian Paice (drums) — produced the band's best-known work, including the albums In Rock (1970), Machine Head (1972), and the live album Made in Japan (1972). Machine Head contains "Smoke on the Water," whose opening guitar riff is among the most famous in rock music; the song describes a fire at the Montreux Casino during a Frank Zappa concert.

The band split in 1976 after several line-up changes — David Coverdale and Glenn Hughes had replaced Gillan and Glover in the Mark III line-up — and re-formed in 1984 with the Mark II band. Guitarist Steve Morse joined in 1994 and remained until 2022, succeeded by Simon McBride. Keyboardist Jon Lord died in 2012. Deep Purple were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2016.
