International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia

The International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia (ICTY) was a body of the United Nations established to prosecute serious crimes committed during the Yugoslav Wars. It was created by Resolution 827 of the United Nations Security Council on 25 May 1993 and sat in The Hague, Netherlands. It was the first war crimes court created by the UN and the first international war crimes tribunal since the Nuremberg and Tokyo tribunals following the Second World War.

Jurisdiction and work

The tribunal had jurisdiction over four clusters of crimes committed on the territory of the former Yugoslavia since 1991: grave breaches of the Geneva Conventions, violations of the laws or customs of war, genocide, and crimes against humanity. In total, it indicted 161 individuals, including heads of state, prime ministers, army chiefs of staff and other senior political and military figures. Proceedings against all of the indicted were concluded.

High-profile cases included those of the Bosnian Serb political leader Radovan Karadžić, convicted of genocide and other crimes in 2016; the military commander Ratko Mladić, convicted of genocide in 2017; and Slobodan Milošević, the former president of Serbia and Yugoslavia, whose trial ended without a verdict when he died in detention in 2006.

Closure and legacy

The ICTY closed on 31 December 2017. Its residual functions — including supervision of sentences, review proceedings and protection of witnesses — passed to the International Residual Mechanism for Criminal Tribunals. The tribunal's jurisprudence was influential in the development of international criminal law, helping to establish that wartime sexual violence, including rape, could be prosecuted as a crime against humanity and a form of torture, and its work laid the groundwork for the permanent International Criminal Court, established in 2002.
