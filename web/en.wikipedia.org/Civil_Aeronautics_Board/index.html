Civil Aeronautics Board

Civil Aeronautics Board (CAB)
Formed: 1940
Abolished: 1985
Headquarters: Washington, D.C.

The Civil Aeronautics Board (CAB) was an agency of the federal government of the United States, formed in 1940 from a split of the Civil Aeronautics Authority, that regulated aviation services (including scheduled passenger airline service) and, until the establishment of the National Transportation Safety Board in 1967, supervised air accident investigations. The agency was abolished in 1985.

Regulation

For four decades the CAB tightly controlled the US airline industry: it decided which routes airlines could fly, set fares, and approved or blocked new entrants. From the agency's formation until deregulation, no new trunk carrier was permitted to enter the scheduled market.

Deregulation and abolition

The Airline Deregulation Act of 1978 phased out the CAB's control over routes and fares, and provided for the board itself to be dissolved on 1 January 1985 — the first federal regulatory agency to be abolished under the deregulation movement. Its remaining functions passed to the Department of Transportation, and its safety-investigation role had already gone to the NTSB.
