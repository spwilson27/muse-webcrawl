Silicon Valley

Silicon Valley is a region in Northern California that serves as a global centre for high technology and innovation. Located in the southern part of the San Francisco Bay Area, it corresponds roughly to the Santa Clara Valley, encompassing cities such as San Jose, Palo Alto, Mountain View, Sunnyvale, Santa Clara, Cupertino, and Menlo Park. The name combines "silicon," for the silicon-based semiconductors pioneered there, with the Santa Clara "Valley."

History

The region's electronics industry grew around Stanford University: Frederick Terman encouraged faculty and graduates to found firms locally, including Hewlett-Packard (1939). After William Shockley established Shockley Semiconductor in Mountain View in 1956, his departing employees — the "traitorous eight" — founded Fairchild Semiconductor (1957), from which Intel, AMD, and much of the semiconductor industry spun off. Venture capital firms on Sand Hill Road financed successive waves of innovation: personal computers (Apple, 1976), the internet boom of the 1990s, and the search, social media, and mobile giants of the 2000s (Google, Facebook/Meta, Netflix, Tesla).

Economy and society

Silicon Valley hosts the headquarters of many of the world's largest technology companies and roughly a third of US venture capital investment. It is known for extremely high incomes and housing costs, a highly educated immigrant workforce, and a distinctive startup culture of risk-taking and equity compensation. The term has become a metonym for the American high-tech sector worldwide.
