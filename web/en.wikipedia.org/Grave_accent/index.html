The grave accent ( ` ) is a diacritical mark used in many written languages, including Catalan, French, Italian, Portuguese, Welsh, Scottish Gaelic, Vietnamese and transliterations of various other languages. It originated in Ancient Greek, where the grave (bareîa) marked a lowering of pitch on a syllable, in contrast to the rising acute accent.

Uses by language

In Italian, the grave accent marks word-final stress and distinguishes homographs, as in è ("is") versus e ("and"), and città ("city"). In French it appears on à, là and où to distinguish homophones, and on è to indicate the open vowel sound /ε/. In Portuguese it marks the contraction of the preposition a with feminine articles (à), a usage called crase. In Welsh it can mark a short vowel in words that would otherwise be pronounced long, and in Scottish Gaelic grave accents historically marked long vowels. In transliteration of Chinese (pinyin), the grave accent denotes the falling fourth tone, as in mà.

In English and computing

In English poetry, the grave accent is occasionally used to indicate that a normally silent -ed ending should be pronounced, as in "learnèd". In computing, the grave accent character (ASCII 96) is commonly called the backtick or backquote; it has important roles in shell scripting, in Markdown (where triple backticks delimit code blocks), and in several programming languages for string interpolation or template literals. In Unicode, the combining grave accent is U+0300, with precomposed characters such as à (U+00E0) widely available.
