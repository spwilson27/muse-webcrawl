Tournament of Roses Parade

The Tournament of Roses Parade, better known as the Rose Parade, is an annual parade held in Pasadena, California, on New Year's Day (or January 2 if January 1 falls on a Sunday). First held in 1890, it features flower-covered floats, marching bands, equestrian units, and the Royal Court headed by the Rose Queen. It is presented by the Pasadena Tournament of Roses Association and precedes the Rose Bowl college football game.

History

The parade was created in 1890 by members of Pasadena's Valley Hunt Club, who wished to showcase California's mild winter climate — "the flowers are blooming and the oranges are ripe" — to visitors from colder states. Early parades featured horse-drawn carriages decorated with flowers, followed by games such as chariot races and ostrich races. The first football game was held in 1902; after a lopsided result it was replaced by other events until football returned permanently in 1916.

Floats

Rose Parade floats are famous for being decorated entirely in natural materials — flowers, seeds, bark, grasses, and other plant matter — applied to a rendered design over many months of volunteer work. Floats are built by professional builders as well as self-built community and university teams, and are judged for awards announced on parade morning. The parade route runs about 5.5 miles (9 km) along Colorado Boulevard, and the event is watched by hundreds of thousands of spectators in person and tens of millions on television.

Traditions

Each parade has a theme and a grand marshal. The Rose Queen and her Royal Court are selected each autumn from Pasadena-area students. The parade has never been held on a Sunday, a tradition known as the "Never on Sunday" rule dating to 1893, originally to avoid disturbing church services and frightening horses outside churches along the route.
