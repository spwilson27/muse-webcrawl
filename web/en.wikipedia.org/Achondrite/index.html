Achondrite

An achondrite is a type of stony meteorite that does not contain chondrules — the round silicate grains characteristic of the more common chondrites. Achondrites have been melted and differentiated on their parent bodies, so their texture and composition resemble terrestrial igneous rocks such as basalt. They make up roughly 8% of observed meteorite falls.

Classification

Achondrites are divided into primitive achondrites (e.g. acapulcoites and lodranites), which retain some chemical signatures of their chondritic precursors despite partial melting, and evolved or planetary achondrites produced by full igneous differentiation. The best-known group is the HED meteorites — howardites, eucrites and diogenites — which come from the asteroid 4 Vesta. Other achondrites originate from the Moon (lunar meteorites), from Mars (the SNC group: shergottites, nakhlites, chassignites), and from other asteroids such as Angra dos Reis (angrites) and aubrites.

Significance

Because they are samples of the crusts and mantles of asteroids and planets, achondrites let scientists study planetary differentiation directly. Comparison of HED spectra with observations of Vesta, confirmed by NASA's Dawn mission, established the Vesta connection. The oldest known volcanic rock from the early Solar System, Erg Chech 002, is an andesitic achondrite dated to about 4.565 billion years.
