Indian Penal Code

The Indian Penal Code (IPC) was the official criminal code of India from 1862 until 2024. Drafted on the recommendations of the first Law Commission of India, chaired by Thomas Babington Macaulay, and enacted in 1860, it entered into force on 1 January 1862. The code was a comprehensive codification of substantive criminal law, organised into 23 chapters and 511 sections covering offences against the state, public order, the human body and property, together with general defences and principles of liability.

History and influence

The IPC was heavily influenced by English law, the French Code pénal and the Louisiana Civil Code of 1825. Its clarity and durability led to its adoption, with modifications, across much of the British Empire; descendants remain in force in countries including Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Malaysia and Singapore.

Notable developments

Several provisions attracted sustained controversy, including section 124A (sedition), section 377 (which criminalised homosexual acts until read down by the Supreme Court in Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India, 2018) and section 497 (adultery, struck down in 2018).

Replacement

On 1 July 2024 the IPC was replaced in India by the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, part of a package of new criminal laws, although offences committed before that date continue to be governed by the IPC.
