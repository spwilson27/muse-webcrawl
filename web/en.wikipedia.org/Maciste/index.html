Maciste

Maciste is a heroic strongman character who appears in a long series of Italian films, one of the oldest recurring characters in cinema. He was created for the silent epic Cabiria (1914), in which he was played by Bartolomeo Pagano, a Genoa dockworker whose physique made him an international star. In Cabiria, written by Gabriele D'Annunzio (who gave the character his name), Maciste is a loyal, immensely strong slave who rescues the heroine.

Silent films

Pagano went on to play Maciste in dozens of silent films through the 1910s and 1920s, in which the character was transplanted to many settings — ancient Rome, the Alps, modern cities, even Hell in Maciste in Hell (1925). The silent Maciste was a folk hero: kind-hearted, humorous, and devoted to defending the weak.

1960s revival

The character was revived during the Italian peplum (sword-and-sandal) boom of the early 1960s, beginning with Maciste nella terra dei ciclopi (1961). Bodybuilders and actors including Gordon Mitchell, Mark Forest, Kirk Morris, and Reg Park played the role across some two dozen films between 1960 and 1965. In English-language releases, the character was often renamed — as Samson, Goliath, or Hercules — though the films remained recognizably Maciste adventures.

Legacy

Maciste became a cultural byword in Italy for a strong, good-natured giant, and the name is still used colloquially to describe a burly man.
