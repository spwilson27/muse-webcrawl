General Council (Andorra)

The General Council (Catalan: Consell General d'Andorra) is the unicameral parliament of the Principality of Andorra, one of the oldest parliaments in Europe. It has 28 members, called counsellors, elected for four-year terms. Half are elected by national constituency and half by the seven parishes, two from each, a system designed to balance population and parish representation in the microstate.

History

The council traces its origins to the Council of the Land created in 1419, when the co-princes of Andorra — the Bishop of Urgell and the Count of Foix — authorised the Andorran valleys to elect representatives. It was reformed in 1866 and again by the Constitution of 1993, which transformed Andorra into a sovereign parliamentary co-principality, made the General Council the supreme legislative body, and introduced universal suffrage-based elections in their modern form. Women first gained the vote in 1970 and first sat in the council in the 1980s.

Functions and seat

The General Council approves laws and the state budget, elects the head of government (the Cap de Govern), oversees the executive, and ratifies international treaties. Its president is the Síndic General. Since 2011 the council has met in a modern parliament building in Andorra la Vella, next to its historic seat, the Casa de la Vall, a manor house built in 1580 that had housed the assembly for centuries and remains in ceremonial use.
