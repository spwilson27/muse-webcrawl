Evil demon

Introduced byRené Descartes
WorkMeditations on First Philosophy (1641)
FieldEpistemology, scepticism
Related conceptsCartesian doubt, brain in a vat, simulated reality

The evil demon, also known as the evil genius (French: malin génie), is a concept in Cartesian philosophy. In the first of his Meditations on First Philosophy (1641), René Descartes proposes the hypothesis of an omnipotent, malevolent being who has devoted himself to deceiving the meditator completely — about the external world, the heavens, the earth, colours, shapes, sounds, and even the truths of mathematics. The device radicalises Descartes's method of doubt: if even such total deception is conceivable, then everything that could possibly be doubted must be set aside in the search for an indubitable foundation of knowledge.

Role in Descartes's argument

The evil demon appears after weaker grounds for doubt — the senses sometimes deceive, and dreams are indistinguishable from waking experience — have already undermined empirical beliefs. The demon goes further, casting doubt even on simple and universal things such as that two and three added together make five. Yet the project backfires on the demon: however much the meditator is deceived, he must exist in order to be deceived. This yields Descartes's famous first certainty, cogito ergo sum — "I think, therefore I am" — which not even an evil demon can falsify.

Interpretations and legacy

Scholars debate whether the demon is meant as a genuine metaphysical possibility or merely a methodological fiction to sustain doubt. Descartes later argued that a benevolent, non-deceiving God guarantees the reliability of clear and distinct perceptions, dissolving the demon hypothesis — though critics since have pressed the charge of circularity (the "Cartesian circle"). The evil demon is an ancestor of later sceptical scenarios, including the brain in a vat and simulation hypotheses, and remains a standard test case in epistemology for any theory claiming to justify beliefs about the external world.
