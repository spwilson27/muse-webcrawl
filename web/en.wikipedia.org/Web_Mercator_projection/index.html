Web Mercator projection

The Web Mercator projection (also called the Google Web Mercator, Spherical Mercator, WGS 84 Web Mercator, or WGS 84/Pseudo-Mercator) is a variant of the Mercator map projection and is the de facto standard for Web mapping applications. It rose to prominence when Google Maps adopted it in 2005. It is used by virtually all major online map providers, including Google Maps, CARTO, Mapbox, Bing Maps, OpenStreetMap, and ArcGIS.

Properties and formulas

Web Mercator applies the formulas of the ellipsoidal Mercator projection to spherical coordinates on the WGS 84 datum, a mathematical mismatch that introduces small positional deviations (up to about 40 km in projected coordinates) but keeps computation simple and tiles uniform. Projected coordinates are x = Rλ and y = R ln[tan(π/4 + φ/2)], where R is the WGS 84 semi-major axis (6,378,137 m). Like all Mercator projections it is conformal locally, preserving angles and shapes of small objects while greatly exaggerating areas near the poles; the map is clipped at about ±85.05° latitude so the world forms a perfect square, suited to slippy-map tile pyramids.

Criticism

Critics note its area distortion (Greenland appearing comparable to Africa), its unsuitability for high-latitude navigation or global statistics, and the datum inconsistency. Nevertheless its speed, tile compatibility, and preservation of local shapes made it the near-universal choice for interactive web maps.
