Machining

Machining is a subtractive manufacturing process in which a cutting tool removes material from a workpiece to shape it to a desired geometry. Principal operations include turning, milling, drilling, boring, and grinding.

Processes

In turning, the workpiece rotates against a stationary tool on a lathe. In milling, a rotating multi-point cutter moves against a fixed workpiece. Drilling creates holes; grinding uses abrasive wheels for fine finishes and tight tolerances.

Equipment and control

Machine tools range from manual lathes and mills to computer numerical control (CNC) machining centers that follow programmed toolpaths from CAD/CAM software, enabling complex parts in metals and plastics with tolerances of thousandths of a millimetre.

Materials and economics

Machinability depends on hardness, ductility, and thermal properties. Cutting fluids cool and lubricate. Machining is central to aerospace, automotive, medical-device, and tool-and-die industries, though additive manufacturing now complements it for some geometries.
