Tony Stewart

Anthony Wayne Stewart (born May 20, 1971), nicknamed "Smoke", is an American former professional racing driver, motorsport executive and team owner. One of the most versatile drivers in American racing history, he is the only driver to win championships in both IndyCar and the NASCAR Cup Series — three Cup titles (2002, 2005, 2011) to add to his 1997 Indy Racing League crown — and in 1995 became the first driver to win USAC's "Triple Crown" of midget, sprint car and Silver Crown championships in a single year.

Career

Stewart raced go-karts and midgets in Indiana before dominating USAC in the mid-1990s. He moved to the Indy Racing League, winning the 1997 championship, and raced the Indianapolis 500 and Daytona 500 on the same day in 1999 and 2001. His NASCAR career began with Joe Gibbs Racing in 1999, when he won three races and Rookie of the Year honours; championships followed in 2002 and 2005. In 2009 he became an owner-driver with Stewart–Haas Racing, and in 2011 he won five of the ten Chase races to take his third Cup title on a tiebreak over Carl Edwards — the first owner-driver champion since Alan Kulwicki. He won 49 Cup races and also triumphed in the Brickyard 400 at his beloved Indianapolis Motor Speedway twice (2005, 2007).

Later years and incidents

Stewart broke his leg in a 2013 sprint car crash, and in August 2014 his sprint car struck and killed fellow driver Kevin Ward Jr., who had walked onto the track at Canandaigua Motorsports Park; a grand jury declined to indict Stewart, and he later settled a wrongful-death suit. He retired from Cup racing after the 2016 season, continued as co-owner of Stewart–Haas Racing until its closure after 2024, owns Eldora Speedway and racing teams, and in the 2020s took up NHRA drag racing, driving a Top Fuel dragster. He was inducted into the NASCAR Hall of Fame in 2020.
