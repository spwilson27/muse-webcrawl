American Civil Liberties Union

The American Civil Liberties Union (ACLU) is an American nonprofit human rights organisation founded in 1920. Its mission, stated by the organisation, is "to defend and preserve the individual rights and liberties guaranteed to every person in this country by the Constitution and laws of the United States." It works primarily through litigation, legislation, and public education.

History

The ACLU grew out of the Civil Liberties Bureau of the American Union Against Militarism during the First World War, when conscientious objectors and anti-war speakers were prosecuted. Under early leaders such as Roger Baldwin and Crystal Eastman, it defended free speech in landmark cases including the Scopes trial and, later, cases on civil rights, reproductive rights, and the rights of criminal defendants.

Activities

The ACLU has affiliates in every state and a legislative office in Washington, D.C. It litigates cases on freedom of speech, voting rights, racial justice, immigrants' rights, privacy, and LGBT rights, sometimes defending unpopular speakers across the political spectrum on First Amendment grounds.
