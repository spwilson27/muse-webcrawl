Heavy metal music

Heavy metal (often shortened to metal) is a genre of rock music that developed in the late 1960s and early 1970s, largely in the United Kingdom and the United States. Rooted in blues rock and psychedelic rock, the bands that created heavy metal developed a thick, massive sound characterised by highly amplified distortion, extended guitar solos, emphatic beats, and overall loudness. Pioneering bands include Led Zeppelin, Black Sabbath, and Deep Purple.

Characteristics

Heavy metal traditionally employs electric guitars (often two, playing rhythm and lead), bass guitar, and drums, sometimes with keyboards. Vocals range from bluesy singing to the high-pitched wails of the New Wave of British Heavy Metal (NWOBHM) bands such as Iron Maiden and Judas Priest, and later to the growls of death metal. Lyrics and imagery frequently draw on fantasy, mythology, war, and social or personal struggle.

Subgenres and history

During the 1980s, glam metal achieved broad commercial success, while underground scenes produced faster and more extreme subgenres: thrash metal (Metallica, Slayer, Megadeth, Anthrax), death metal, and black metal. The 1990s and 2000s added nu metal, metalcore, doom metal revivals, and many fusion styles. Metal remains a global phenomenon with major festivals such as Wacken Open Air in Germany and a devoted international fan culture.
