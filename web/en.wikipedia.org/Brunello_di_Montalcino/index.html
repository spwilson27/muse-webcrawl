Brunello di Montalcino is a red Italian wine produced in the vineyards surrounding the town of Montalcino, in the province of Siena, Tuscany. It is made entirely from Sangiovese grapes — locally known as Brunello, from bruno ("brown"), for the colour of the berries — and is considered among Italy's best-known and most age-worthy wines. In 1980 it was among the first Italian wines to receive Denominazione di Origine Controllata e Garantita (DOCG) status.

History

The wine was developed in the 19th century by Ferruccio Biondi-Santi, whose family estate pioneered the production of a long-lived red wine from a single clone of Sangiovese. For much of the early 20th century Biondi-Santi was nearly the only producer; production expanded greatly from the 1960s onward, and today more than 200 producers make Brunello.

Regulations and style

DOCG rules require 100% Sangiovese grown within the Montalcino commune, maximum yields, and long ageing: the wine must age for at least four years before release (five for riserva), including at least two years in oak barrels and four months in bottle; it cannot be sold until 1 January of the fifth year after harvest. The resulting wines are typically full-bodied, tannic and high in acidity when young, developing aromas of dark cherry, violet, leather and spice with age, and can cellar for decades. The younger, earlier-drinking Rosso di Montalcino DOC is made from the same grapes.
