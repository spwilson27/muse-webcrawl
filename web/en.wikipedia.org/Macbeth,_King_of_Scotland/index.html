Macbeth, King of Scotland

Macbeth (Medieval Gaelic: Mac Bethad mac Findlaích; died 15 August 1057) was King of Scots from 1040 until his death in 1057. He is best known as the title character of William Shakespeare's tragedy Macbeth, though the historical king differs considerably from Shakespeare's portrayal: he appears to have ruled a relatively stable and prosperous kingdom for seventeen years.

Background

Macbeth was the son of Findláech, mormaer (ruler) of Moray in northern Scotland, and possibly a daughter of King Malcolm II. He became mormaer of Moray himself around 1032. He married Gruoch, granddaughter of King Kenneth III, whose first husband Gille Coemgáin, another mormaer of Moray, had been killed in 1032.

Reign

In 1040 Macbeth defeated and killed King Duncan I in battle near Elgin and took the throne. Unlike in Shakespeare's play, there is no contemporary evidence that his rule was considered tyrannical; he made a pilgrimage to Rome in 1050, where he reportedly distributed alms generously. In 1054 an English invasion led by Siward, Earl of Northumbria, defeated Macbeth but failed to displace him. In 1057 he was killed at the Battle of Lumphanan by forces of the future Malcolm III, Duncan's son. He was buried on Iona.

Legacy

Shakespeare's depiction, drawn from Holinshed's Chronicles, transformed Macbeth into a murderous usurper driven by ambition and witchcraft — a portrait shaped partly by the politics of King James VI and I's court. Macbeth was the last Scottish king to be remembered primarily under a Gaelic name.
