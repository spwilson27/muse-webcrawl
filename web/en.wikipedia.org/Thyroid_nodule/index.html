Thyroid nodule

Thyroid nodule
SpecialtyEndocrinology
DiagnosisUltrasound, fine-needle aspiration
PrognosisMostly benign; roughly 5% malignant

Thyroid nodules are lumps that commonly form within an otherwise normal thyroid gland. They are extremely common — ultrasound can detect them in a large fraction of adults — and the great majority are benign. About five percent of nodules are thyroid cancer.

Causes

Nodules may be simple cysts, colloid nodules, inflammatory nodules from thyroiditis, benign tumours (adenomas), or malignancies such as papillary thyroid carcinoma. Some nodules produce excess thyroid hormone ("hot" nodules) and can cause hyperthyroidism.

Evaluation

Assessment typically includes thyroid-stimulating hormone measurement and neck ultrasound, which characterizes size and suspicious features. Fine-needle aspiration biopsy, reported using the Bethesda system, is the key test for deciding between surveillance, surgery, and reassurance.
