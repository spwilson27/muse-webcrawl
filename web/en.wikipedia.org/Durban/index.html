Durban (Zulu: eThekwini, meaning "the port") is the third most populous city in South Africa and the largest city in the province of KwaZulu-Natal. Situated on the Indian Ocean coast, it has the busiest container port in the country and is a major centre of tourism thanks to its subtropical climate and extensive beaches.

History

British traders established a settlement at Port Natal in 1824. In 1835 the town was renamed Durban in honour of Sir Benjamin D'Urban, governor of the Cape Colony. Indentured labourers from India arrived from 1860 to work on sugar plantations, founding what became one of the largest Indian diaspora communities in the world; Mahatma Gandhi lived and practised law in the city from 1893 to 1914 and founded the Phoenix Settlement nearby.

Geography and economy

Durban's economy centres on shipping, refining, sugar, automotive assembly and tourism. Landmarks include the Golden Mile beachfront, uShaka Marine World, the Moses Mabhida Stadium built for the 2010 FIFA World Cup, and the historic Indian Quarter around Dr Yusuf Dadoo Street.

Culture

The city is known for its distinctive cuisine blending Zulu, Indian and colonial influences, most famously the "bunny chow" — a hollowed-out loaf of bread filled with curry — and for the annual Durban July horse race.
