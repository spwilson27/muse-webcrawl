Lapping

Lapping is a machining process in which two surfaces are rubbed together with an abrasive between them, by hand or with a machine, to produce very flat surfaces, close dimensional tolerances, and fine surface finishes. The tool, called a lap, is charged with loose abrasive grains in a liquid or grease carrier.

Process

Lapping removes minute amounts of material, often measured in micrometres, and can achieve flatness to fractions of a wavelength of light in optical work. It is used for gauge blocks, valves, seals, lenses, mirrors, semiconductor wafers and precision mechanical parts.

Types

Hand lapping uses a cast-iron plate; machine lapping uses rotating laps for production work. Fine abrasives include silicon carbide, aluminium oxide, diamond and cerium oxide for glass.
