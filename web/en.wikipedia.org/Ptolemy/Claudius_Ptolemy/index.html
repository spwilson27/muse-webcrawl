Claudius Ptolemy (/ˈtɒləmi/; Greek: Πτολεμαῖος, Ptolemaios; Latin: Claudius Ptolemaeus; c. 100 – c. 170 CE) was a Greco-Roman mathematician, astronomer, geographer, and astrologer who lived in Alexandria, Egypt. He authored several scientific treatises, at least three of which were of lasting importance to Byzantine, Islamic, and Western European science.

The Almagest

His Almagest (originally the Mathematical Syntaxis) presented a complete mathematical model of the motions of the Sun, Moon, and planets based on a geocentric cosmos of deferents and epicycles. It remained the authoritative astronomical text for more than 1,400 years, until the heliocentric model displaced it in the work of Galileo and his predecessors.

Geography and other works

Ptolemy's Geography compiled coordinates for some 8,000 places in the known world and gave instructions for map projections; rediscovered in the Renaissance, it shaped European cartography for centuries. His Tetrabiblos was the standard astrological text of the era, and his Optics studied reflection and refraction. The crater Ptolemaeus on the Moon is named after him.
