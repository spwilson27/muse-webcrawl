Sir Isaac Newton (1643–1727).

Sir Isaac Newton (1643–1727) was an English polymath active as a mathematician, physicist, astronomer, alchemist, theologian, and inventor. He was a key figure in the Scientific Revolution and the Enlightenment that followed, and is widely recognised as one of the greatest mathematicians and physicists of all time.

Principia and gravitation

In the Principia (1687), Newton formulated the laws of motion and universal gravitation, uniting terrestrial and celestial mechanics in a single framework that superseded the astronomy of Ptolemy and the refinements of Kepler. The work dominated the scientific view of the physical universe for more than two centuries.

Mathematics and optics

During the plague years 1665–66 at Woolsthorpe, Newton developed generalised binomial theorem, the beginnings of calculus (his "method of fluxions," later disputed with Leibniz), and theories of colour. His Opticks (1704) showed that white light is composed of a spectrum of colours, and he built the first practical reflecting telescope in 1668. Later he served as president of the Royal Society and Master of the Mint.
