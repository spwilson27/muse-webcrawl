China (Chinese: 中国; pinyin: Zhōngguó), officially the People's Republic of China (PRC), is a country in East Asia. With a population exceeding 1.4 billion, it is the second-most populous country in the world after India. China spans the equivalent of five time zones and borders fourteen countries by land. One of the world's oldest continuous civilisations, China was ruled for millennia by hereditary dynasties before becoming a republic in 1912; the People's Republic was proclaimed by Mao Zedong in 1949. Known to Greco-Roman geographers through the silk trade as Serica or Sinae, China appears in Ptolemy's Geography as the far-eastern land of silk.

History

Neolithic cultures arose in the Yellow River and Yangtze valleys; the Xia, Shang and Zhou dynasties followed. In 221 BC Qin Shi Huang unified China as an empire, a model continued under the Han dynasty, which gave its name to the majority ethnic group and to Chinese characters. Successive dynasties — Tang, Song, Yuan, Ming and Qing — presided over cycles of unity, prosperity and fragmentation, producing inventions including papermaking, printing, gunpowder and the compass. The Qing fell in 1912; after warlordism, Japanese invasion and civil war, the Chinese Communist Party established the PRC. Since the reform and opening up begun in 1978, China has become the world's second-largest economy and largest manufacturer and exporter.

Geography

China's landscape ranges from the deserts of the north-west and the Tibetan Plateau — the "roof of the world" — to subtropical forests in the south and the densely populated eastern plains watered by the Yellow and Yangtze rivers, whose floods and agriculture shaped Chinese history.

Culture

Chinese culture is one of the world's oldest, encompassing Confucian, Buddhist and Taoist thought; calligraphy, painting and poetry; a logographic writing system in use for over three thousand years; and cuisines and festivals (such as the Spring Festival) celebrated across a vast diaspora.
