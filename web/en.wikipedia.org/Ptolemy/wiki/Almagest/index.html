Almagest

The Almagest is a 2nd-century mathematical and astronomical treatise on the apparent motions of the stars and planetary paths by Claudius Ptolemy. Written in Greek as Ἡ μεγάλη πραγματεία ("The Great Treatise"), and later Μαθηματικὴ Σύνταξις ("Mathematical Synthesis"), it is one of the most influential scientific texts of all time. Its geocentric model of the universe was accepted for more than 1,200 years in Western and Islamic astronomy.

Contents

The Almagest is divided into thirteen books. It sets out the mathematical astronomy of the Sun, Moon, and planets: spherical astronomy and chord tables (an early form of trigonometry), solar and lunar theory including eclipses, a catalogue of over 1,000 stars, and detailed models for the five visible planets using combinations of deferents, epicycles, eccentrics, and the equant — devices designed to reproduce the observed irregularities of planetary motion while preserving uniform circular motion in principle.

Transmission and influence

Preserved and developed in the Islamic world — the Arabic name Almagest derives from translations made in 9th-century Baghdad — the work returned to Latin Europe in the 12th century through Gerard of Cremona's translation. It remained the standard astronomical textbook until displaced by the heliocentric astronomy of Copernicus and Kepler.

Star catalogue

Book VII–VIII contains a catalogue of 1,022 stars in 48 constellations, with ecliptic coordinates and magnitudes — the source of the classical constellation system still used today.
