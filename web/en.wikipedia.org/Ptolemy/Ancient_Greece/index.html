Ancient Greece (Greek: Ἑλλάς, Hellás) was a civilisation belonging to a period of Greek history that lasted from the emergence of the Greek-speaking Mycenaean civilisation and the ensuing Dark Ages through the Archaic, Classical and Hellenistic periods to the integration of Greece into the Roman world. Politically fragmented into hundreds of independent city-states (poleis), the Greeks nevertheless shared a language, religion and culture, and founded colonies from Spain to the Black Sea. Ancient Greece is considered the cradle of Western civilisation, having originated democracy, political philosophy, historiography, drama, and foundational forms of mathematics and natural science.

History

The Mycenaean civilisation (c. 1600–1100 BC), known from Homer's epics and Linear B tablets, collapsed around 1200 BC. The Archaic period (c. 800–480 BC) saw the rise of the polis, the adoption of the Phoenician-derived alphabet, the first Olympic Games (traditionally 776 BC) and extensive colonisation. In the Classical period (480–323 BC) Athens and Sparta led the Greek defence against Persia at Marathon, Salamis and Plataea; Athens' democratic experiment and cultural flowering were followed by the ruinous Peloponnesian War. Macedon under Philip II and Alexander the Great then conquered the Persian Empire, ushering in the Hellenistic age, during which Greek culture spread from Egypt to Central Asia — the era in which the Alexandrian astronomer Claudius Ptolemy later worked. Rome annexed the Greek world between 146 BC and 30 BC, but Greek culture continued to dominate the eastern Roman Empire.

Society and culture

Greek religion centred on the Olympian gods, great sanctuaries such as Delphi and Olympia, and Panhellenic festivals. The Greeks produced the philosophy of Socrates, Plato and Aristotle; the tragedies of Aeschylus, Sophocles and Euripides; the histories of Herodotus and Thucydides; and advances in geometry, astronomy and medicine associated with Pythagoras, Euclid, Archimedes and Hippocrates.

Legacy

Greek political thought, art, architecture and science were transmitted through Rome, the Byzantine Empire and the Islamic world, and were revived in Renaissance Europe, shaping the modern Western world. The Greek alphabet is the ancestor of the Latin and Cyrillic scripts.
