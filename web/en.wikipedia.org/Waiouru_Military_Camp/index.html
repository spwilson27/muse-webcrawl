Waiouru Military Camp

Waiouru Military Camp is the largest training base of the New Zealand Army, located beside the township of Waiouru in the Ruapehu District of the central North Island. Set on the high volcanic plateau near Mount Ruapehu and the Rangipo Desert, its extensive training area is used for field exercises, artillery live firing, and cold-weather training.

History and role

The camp was developed at the start of the Second World War, when the government acquired large tracts of tussock country for a major training ground. It grew to include barracks, ranges, and armored and artillery schools, and today hosts many of the Army's trade and leadership schools, including the Army Command School and courses at the Land Operations Training Centre. The Singapore Armed Forces have also conducted artillery live-firing exercises at Waiouru under long-standing arrangements.

QEII Army Memorial Museum

The camp is home to the Queen Elizabeth II Army Memorial Museum, opened in 1978, which displays New Zealand military history from the New Zealand Wars to recent peacekeeping, and holds the country's principal collection of military medals, including Victoria Crosses.
