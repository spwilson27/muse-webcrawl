Mojave National Preserve

Mojave National Preserve is a United States National Preserve located in the Mojave Desert of San Bernardino County, California, between Interstates 15 and 40. Established on 31 October 1994 by the California Desert Protection Act, it encompasses about 1.6 million acres (650,000 hectares), making it the third-largest unit of the National Park System in the contiguous United States. It is administered by the National Park Service.

Geography and ecology

The preserve protects a great diversity of desert habitats where the Mojave, Great Basin and Sonoran deserts meet. Its landscapes include the Kelso Dunes, among the largest sand dune systems in California; the Cima volcanic field with its cinder cones and lava flows; dense Joshua tree forests on Cima Dome; the granite boulders of the New York and Providence Mountains; and the singing sand phenomenon at Kelso Dunes. Elevations range from about 880 feet (270 m) near Baker to 7,929 feet (2,417 m) atop Clark Mountain. Wildlife includes bighorn sheep, desert tortoises, bobcats, and more than 300 species of birds.

Human history

The region was home to Indigenous peoples including the Chemehuevi and Mojave for thousands of years. Later uses included ranching, mining for gold, silver and tungsten, and the railroad town of Kelso, whose restored 1924 Spanish Colonial Revival depot now serves as the preserve's main visitor centre. The designation as a "preserve" rather than a national park permits certain activities, including hunting, under park regulations.

Access

Unlike many national park units, the preserve charges no entrance fee and most of its roads are unpaved. Campgrounds include Hole-in-the-Wall and Mid Hills. The preserve is known for its dark night skies and spring wildflower displays following wet winters.
