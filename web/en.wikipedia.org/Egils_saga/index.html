Egils saga

Egils saga Skallagrímssonar (listen), also spelled Egils saga, is an Icelandic saga generally believed to have been written by Snorri Sturluson in the 13th century. It is one of the most celebrated of the sagas of Icelanders, telling the life story of the Viking warrior-poet Egill Skallagrímsson, along with the history of his family across several generations.

Synopsis

The saga begins in Norway with Egill's grandfather Kveldúlfr ("Evening Wolf") and his sons Þórólfr and Skallagrímr, whose conflicts with King Harald Fairhair drive the family to emigrate to Iceland. The main narrative follows Egill himself: an ugly, violent and immensely gifted skald who composes his first poem at the age of three, travels as a Viking to Scandinavia and England, feuds with King Eiríkr Bloodaxe, and saves his own life at York by composing the praise-poem Höfuðlausn ("Head-Ransom") overnight. In old age, blind and half-deaf, Egill composes Sonatorrek ("Loss of Sons"), a lament for his drowned son Böðvarr that is considered one of the masterpieces of Old Norse poetry.

Authorship and manuscripts

The case for Snorri's authorship rests on the saga's style, its detailed treatment of events in Norway, and genealogical connections between Snorri and Egill's family. The saga survives in several medieval manuscripts, the most complete in Möðruvallabók (AM 132 fol.).

Themes

Scholars have noted the saga's psychological complexity: Egill is both brutal and tender, a killer and a grieving father. The narrative contrasts the pagan Viking age with the Christian era in which it was written, and explores the tension between individual freedom and royal power that drove the settlement of Iceland.
