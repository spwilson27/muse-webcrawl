Celtic music

Celtic music is a broad grouping of music genres that evolved out of the folk music traditions of the Celtic people of Northwestern Europe (the modern Celtic nations). It refers to both orally-transmitted traditional music and recorded music and the styles vary considerably, ranging from traditional music to a wide range of hybrids.

Forms

Irish traditional music centres on dance tunes — reels, jigs, hornpipes and polkas — played on fiddle, flute, tin whistle, uilleann pipes, concertina, accordion and harp, alongside unaccompanied sean-nós singing. Scottish traditions include the Great Highland bagpipe repertoire of ceòl mòr, strathspeys, and Gaelic song; Welsh tradition includes the triple harp and cerdd dant; Breton music features the bombarde and biniou. Music of the Celtic diaspora flourished in Cape Breton, Newfoundland and Appalachia.

Revival and fusion

The folk revival of the 1960s brought groups such as The Dubliners, The Chieftains and Planxty to international audiences, followed by folk-rock acts like Steeleye Span and Horslips, and fusion and world-music artists including Clannad, Enya, Afro Celt Sound System and Loreena McKennitt. Festivals such as the Fleadh Cheoil and the Festival Interceltique de Lorient remain major showcases.

Instruments

Characteristic instruments include the Celtic harp, fiddle, Irish flute, tin whistle, uilleann pipes, Highland bagpipes, bodhrán, bouzouki and accordion. Sessions in pubs remain the principal setting for amateur transmission of the tradition.
