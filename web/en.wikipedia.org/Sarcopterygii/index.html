Sarcopterygii

Sarcopterygii — sometimes considered synonymous with Crossopterygii — is a class of vertebrates traditionally known as the lobe-finned fishes, characterized by fleshy, lobed, paired fins supported by a single bone, homologous to the limbs of land vertebrates. The group includes coelacanths, lungfishes, and, in cladistic terms, all tetrapods (amphibians, reptiles, birds, and mammals), making Sarcopterygii the lineage from which land vertebrates evolved.

Evolution

Sarcopterygians appeared by the late Silurian, over 415 million years ago, and diversified greatly in the Devonian. Their internal nostrils (choanae), enamelled teeth, and limb-like fin skeletons foreshadow tetrapod anatomy. Fossil forms such as Eusthenopteron, Panderichthys, and the transitional Tiktaalik document the fish–tetrapod transition in the Late Devonian.

Living members

Only eight fish species survive: two coelacanths (Latimeria), famously rediscovered alive in 1938, and six lungfishes in Africa, South America, and Australia, which can breathe air through lungs. All other sarcopterygian diversity is now on land, in the form of the more than 30,000 tetrapod species.
