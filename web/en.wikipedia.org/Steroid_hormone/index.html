Steroid hormone

Steroid hormone
PrecursorCholesterol
ClassesCorticosteroids, sex steroids
Produced inAdrenal cortex, gonads, placenta

A steroid hormone is a steroid that acts as a hormone. Steroid hormones are synthesised from cholesterol, are lipid-soluble, cross cell membranes, and typically act by binding intracellular receptors that regulate gene transcription.

Classes

The two main groups are corticosteroids — glucocorticoids such as cortisol (metabolism, immune response) and mineralocorticoids such as aldosterone (salt and water balance), made in the adrenal cortex — and sex steroids: androgens (testosterone), estrogens (estradiol), and progestogens (progesterone), made mainly in the gonads and placenta.

Synthesis and Mechanism

All derive from pregnenolone formed from cholesterol by the enzyme CYP11A1 in mitochondria. Unlike peptide hormones they are not stored but synthesised on demand and carried in blood bound to transport proteins. Their receptors are ligand-activated transcription factors, so effects are slower but longer-lasting than membrane-receptor signalling.

Medical Use

Synthetic steroids are widely used: anti-inflammatory corticosteroids, hormonal contraception, hormone replacement therapy, and treatments for adrenal insufficiency. Anabolic steroid misuse carries cardiovascular and endocrine risks.
