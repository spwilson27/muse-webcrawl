Brazil national football team

The Brazil national football team (Portuguese: Seleção Brasileira), nicknamed Seleção Canarinho ("Canary Squad") for their yellow shirts, represents Brazil in men's international football and is administered by the Brazilian Football Confederation (CBF). Brazil is the most successful national team in the history of the FIFA World Cup, having won the tournament five times — in 1958, 1962, 1970, 1994 and 2002 — and is the only team to have played in every edition of the competition. Its 1970 side, featuring Pelé, is widely regarded as one of the greatest teams of all time.

History

Brazil's first national team was assembled in 1914. After early Copa América successes, the young football nation suffered trauma at home in 1950, when it lost the decisive World Cup match to Uruguay at the Maracanã — the Maracanaço — before 200,000 spectators. Redemption arrived in Sweden in 1958, when a 17-year-old Pelé announced himself as Brazil took its first world title, retained in Chile in 1962 with Garrincha starring. The 1970 team in Mexico — Pelé, Jairzinho, Rivelino, Tostão and Gérson under coach Mário Zagallo — won all its matches with attacking football remembered as the apotheosis of jogo bonito, "the beautiful game".

A 24-year drought followed, ended at the 1994 World Cup in the United States under Carlos Alberto Parreira, with Romário and Bebeto leading a more pragmatic side that beat Italy on penalties. Ronaldo spearheaded a fifth title in 2002 in Korea/Japan. The 21st century has brought Copa América titles (notably at home in 2019) but also the humiliation of the 2014 semi-final on home soil, a 7–1 defeat by Germany known as the Mineiraço, and quarter-final exits in 2018 and 2022.

Kit and identity

Brazil originally played in white; after the 1950 defeat, the colours were changed to the now-iconic yellow shirt with green trim and blue shorts — designed by 19-year-old Aldyr Schlee in a 1953 newspaper competition. The crest carries five stars for the World Cup victories. Brazil's style is culturally bound up with samba, improvisation and ginga, though debates recur between romantic attacking ideals and European-style tactical discipline.

Records

Brazil is the only country to appear at every World Cup and holds records for most wins and the best overall performance in the tournament. Cafu is the most-capped player with 142 appearances and the only player to appear in three consecutive World Cup finals (1994, 1998, 2002). Pelé remains the only player to win three World Cups; Neymar became Brazil's all-time top scorer in 2023, passing Pelé's official FIFA tally of 77 goals.
