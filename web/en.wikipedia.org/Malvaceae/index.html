Malvaceae, or the mallows, is a family of flowering plants estimated to contain 244 genera with 4,225 known species. Well-known members of economic importance include okra, cotton, cacao, roselle and durian. Large genera include Hibiscus, Sterculia, Dombeya, Pavonia and Sida.

Description

Most species are herbaceous plants or shrubs, but some are trees and lianas. The leaves are generally alternate, often palmately lobed or compound and palmately veined. The flowers are usually showy, with five petals, and the numerous stamens are typically fused into a tube around the style. The fruit is most often a capsule or schizocarp.

Taxonomy

In the APG IV system, the family is greatly expanded compared with older classifications: the former families Bombacaceae, Sterculiaceae and Tiliaceae are included within Malvaceae, which is divided into nine subfamilies, including Malvoideae, Bombacoideae, Byttnerioideae, Grewioideae, Helicteroideae, Sterculioideae and Tilioideae.

Economic importance

The family supplies several major crops. Cotton (genus Gossypium) is the world's most important natural fibre. Cacao (Theobroma cacao) is the source of chocolate. Okra (Abelmoschus esculentus), roselle (Hibiscus sabdariffa), durian, kola nut and jute are also produced by members of the family, and many Hibiscus and mallow species are grown as ornamentals.
