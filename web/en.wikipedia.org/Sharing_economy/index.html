Sharing economy

The sharing economy is an economic model in which individuals share access to goods and services, often coordinated through community-based online platforms. It encompasses peer-to-peer sharing of underused assets — spare rooms, cars, tools, skills — as well as business-to-consumer rental models.

History

The term gained prominence in the 2000s with the growth of platforms such as Airbnb (founded 2008, short-term lodging), Uber (2009, ride-hailing), and earlier peer marketplaces like eBay and Craigslist. Related concepts include collaborative consumption, the gig economy, and access-based consumption.

Characteristics

Sharing platforms typically rely on digital marketplaces, reputation and rating systems, dynamic pricing, and idle capacity. Advocates argue the model increases resource efficiency and lowers costs; critics point to regulatory evasion, effects on housing markets and labour protections, and the fact that many "sharing" services are conventional commercial rentals.

Sectors

Major sectors include transportation (ride-hailing, car-sharing, bike-sharing), accommodation, coworking, finance (peer-to-peer lending and crowdfunding), and the sharing of household goods and professional services.
