Rivaroxaban

Rivaroxaban, sold under the brand name Xarelto among others, is an anticoagulant medication (blood thinner) used to treat and prevent blood clots. It was the first available active direct factor Xa inhibitor that is taken by mouth. It is used to treat deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolisms, and to prevent blood clots in atrial fibrillation and following hip or knee surgery.

Mechanism of action

Rivaroxaban inhibits free and clot-bound factor Xa, a serine protease in the coagulation cascade, thereby blocking the conversion of prothrombin to thrombin and preventing clot formation. Unlike warfarin, it does not require routine blood monitoring of the INR, and it has fewer food and drug interactions, though bleeding is its principal adverse effect. The specific reversal agent andexanet alfa can be used for life-threatening bleeding.

History

Rivaroxaban was developed by Bayer and co-developed with Janssen Pharmaceuticals. It was approved for medical use in the European Union in 2008 and in the United States in 2011. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines, and it is available as a generic medication. It belongs to the class of direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) alongside apixaban, edoxaban and dabigatran.
