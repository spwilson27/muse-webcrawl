McCarthyism

McCarthyism is the practice of making accusations of subversion and treason, especially related to communism, without proper regard for evidence. The term originally described the anti-communist pursuits of United States Senator Joseph McCarthy during the Second Red Scare, roughly from the late 1940s to the late 1950s.

Background

Amid Cold War tensions, the Soviet atomic test, the communist victory in China, and the Korean War, fears of communist infiltration of the US government grew. In a February 1950 speech in Wheeling, West Virginia, McCarthy claimed to hold a list of communists in the State Department — the number he cited varied — launching his rise to prominence.

Hearings and downfall

McCarthy chaired Senate investigations, while the House Un-American Activities Committee pursued alleged communists in Hollywood and elsewhere; many careers were destroyed by blacklists. The televised Army–McCarthy hearings of 1954 exposed his methods to the public, and in December 1954 the Senate voted to censure him. He died in 1957. The term has since become a byword for demagogic, unsubstantiated attacks and witch hunts.
