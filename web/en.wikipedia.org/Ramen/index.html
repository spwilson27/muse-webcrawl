Ramen

Ramen (Japanese: ラーメン, romanized: rāmen) is a Japanese noodle dish. It consists of Chinese-style wheat noodles served in a broth; common flavours are soy sauce and miso, with typical toppings including sliced pork (chāshū), nori (dried seaweed), menma (bamboo shoots), and scallions. Ramen has its roots in Chinese noodle dishes and developed a distinct regional culture in Japan, with nearly every region having its own variation.

History

Ramen arrived in Japan from China in the late 19th century, sold in Yokohama Chinatown and later from food stalls across the country. After World War II, cheap American wheat flour and returning soldiers familiar with Chinese food helped ramen spread. In 1958, Momofuku Ando of Nissin Foods invented instant ramen ("Chicken Ramen"), which — followed by Cup Noodles in 1971 — made ramen a global convenience food.

Broth and regional styles

The broth is the defining element: tonkotsu (pork bone, creamy and rich, associated with Fukuoka), shōyu (soy sauce, Tokyo), miso (Sapporo), and shio (salt) are the classic categories. Famous regional styles include Kitakata's flat noodles, Hakata's thin straight noodles with beni shōga pickled ginger, and tsukemen, in which cold noodles are dipped in a concentrated broth.

Noodles and toppings

Ramen noodles are made from wheat flour, salt, water, and kansui, alkaline mineral water that gives them their springy texture and yellow tint. Toppings vary widely — narutomaki fish cake, ajitama marinated eggs, corn and butter in Hokkaidō, and nori — and shops guard their recipes closely.
