Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft

The Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft (DFG, German Research Foundation) is a self-governing organisation for science and research in Germany, serving science and the humanities in universities and publicly funded research institutions. With an annual budget of well over three billion euros, provided mainly by the federal government and the Länder, it is the largest research funding organisation in Germany and one of the largest in Europe.

History

The DFG traces its origins to the Notgemeinschaft der Deutschen Wissenschaft (Emergency Association of German Science), founded in 1920. It took the name Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft in 1929, was re-established after the Second World War in 1949, and merged in 1951 with the German Research Council to form the present organisation, headquartered in Bonn.

Functions

The DFG funds research projects and infrastructure through competitive, peer-reviewed procedures. Its funding instruments include individual grants, research units, priority programmes, collaborative research centres (Sonderforschungsbereiche), research training groups for doctoral candidates, and the Excellence Strategy run jointly with the German Council of Science and Humanities. It also advises parliaments and public authorities on scientific questions and promotes early-career researchers and international cooperation.
