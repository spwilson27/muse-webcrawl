Thali

Thali (Hindi for "plate") is a round platter used to serve food in South Asian cuisine, and by extension the meal itself: a selection of many small dishes served together on one tray, typically in small bowls called katori. A thali offers a balance of sweet, salty, bitter, sour, and spicy flavours in a single sitting.

Composition

A typical thali includes rice, flatbreads such as roti or puri, dal, one or more vegetable curries and sabzi, curd or raita, pickles, papad, chutneys, and a sweet. Regional versions — Gujarati, Rajasthani, Maharashtrian, and South Indian meals served on banana leaves — differ in their characteristic dishes. In restaurants, thalis are often all-you-can-eat, with servers refilling the bowls.

Cultural significance

The thali embodies the Ayurvedic idea of a complete meal combining the six tastes. Elaborate festive thalis are prepared for weddings and religious occasions, while the everyday thali remains the standard lunch format in much of India, Nepal, and the diaspora.
