Lipoma

SpecialtyDermatology, general surgery
TypeBenign soft-tissue tumour
CompositionMature adipose (fat) tissue

A lipoma is a benign tumour composed of mature adipose tissue. Lipomas are the most common soft-tissue tumours in adults, typically appearing as slow-growing, soft, mobile, usually painless lumps just beneath the skin, most often on the trunk, neck, shoulders, and arms. They are generally harmless and are distinct from malignant fatty tumours (liposarcomas), which are rare and usually deep-seated.

Causes and diagnosis

The cause is not fully understood, though a genetic predisposition is common and multiple lipomas can occur in conditions such as familial multiple lipomatosis. Diagnosis is usually clinical — a soft, doughy, movable mass — with ultrasound or MRI used when the diagnosis is uncertain or the lesion is large, deep, or rapidly growing.

Treatment

Most lipomas need no treatment. Removal by surgical excision is performed when a lipoma is painful (for example pressing on a nerve), unsightly, enlarging, or diagnostically uncertain; liposuction and steroid injections are alternatives in selected cases. Recurrence after complete excision is uncommon.
