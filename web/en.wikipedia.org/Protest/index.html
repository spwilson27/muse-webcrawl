Protest

A protest (also called a demonstration, remonstration, or remonstrance) is a public expression of objection, disapproval, or dissent towards an idea or action, typically a political one. Protests can be thought of as acts of cooperation in which numerous people who hold similar goals cooperate by attending and share the potential costs and risks of doing so.

Protests can take many different forms, from individual statements to mass political demonstrations. Protesters may organize a protest as a way of publicly making their opinions heard in an attempt to influence public opinion or government policy, or they may undertake direct action in an attempt to directly enact desired changes themselves.

Forms of protest

Common forms include marches, rallies, sit-ins, strikes, boycotts, hunger strikes, picketing, and civil disobedience. In the digital age, online activism, hashtag campaigns, and hacktivism have become additional forms. Self-immolation is an extreme form of protest used by a small number of people.

History

Mass protests have played a central role in many historical movements, including labour movements, the civil rights movement in the United States, anti-war movements, and the wave of pro-democracy movements associated with the fall of communism in Eastern Europe in 1989 and the Arab Spring beginning in 2010.

Legal status

The right to peaceful protest is protected in many countries by constitutional guarantees of freedom of assembly and freedom of speech, and internationally by instruments such as the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights. Nonetheless, governments may impose restrictions on time, place, and manner, and unauthorised protests may be policed or dispersed.
