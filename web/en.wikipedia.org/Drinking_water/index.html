Drinking water or potable water is water that is safe for ingestion, either when drunk directly or when used in food preparation. In developed countries, tap water supplied by municipal systems is held to regulated drinking-water quality standards covering microbial pathogens, chemical contaminants such as lead and nitrates, and aesthetic qualities such as taste and clarity.

Supply and treatment

Typical treatment involves coagulation and sedimentation, filtration, and disinfection — most often with chlorine or chloramine, increasingly supplemented by ultraviolet light and ozone — followed by distribution through mains under pressure to prevent contamination. Sources include surface water (rivers, reservoirs) and groundwater from aquifers accessed by wells and springs.

Global access

Access to safe drinking water is a key indicator of public health and a United Nations Sustainable Development Goal (SDG 6). The WHO/UNICEF Joint Monitoring Programme estimated that in 2022 about 5.8 billion people used safely managed drinking-water services, while roughly 2.2 billion lacked them, with contaminated water contributing to diarrhoeal disease — a leading cause of death in young children. Historical milestones include John Snow's 1854 investigation of a cholera outbreak in London and the introduction of water chlorination in the early 20th century, which dramatically reduced typhoid and cholera in cities.
