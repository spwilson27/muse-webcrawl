Sixth form college

A sixth form college is an educational institution, where students aged 16 to 19 typically study for advanced school-level qualifications such as A Levels, Business and Technology Education Council (BTEC) and the International Baccalaureate Diploma. The name derives from the traditional English school year numbering, in which the final two years of secondary education were the lower sixth and upper sixth.

Sixth form colleges are distinct from school sixth forms in being separate institutions, and from further education colleges in concentrating on 16–19 academic provision. The first sixth form colleges developed from the 1960s as local authorities reorganised secondary education; the sector expanded after 1992, when colleges were incorporated as independent bodies funded through national funding councils.

Provision and character

Because they recruit from many schools, sixth form colleges are often large, offering a wider range of A Level subjects than a single school sixth form, and they generally treat students more like young adults, without school uniform and with a campus-style timetable. In England there are over one hundred sixth form colleges, represented by the Sixth Form Colleges Association.
