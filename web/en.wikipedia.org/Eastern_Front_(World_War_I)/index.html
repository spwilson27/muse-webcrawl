Eastern Front (World War I)

Eastern Front
Date: 1914–1918
Location: Eastern and Central Europe
Part of: World War I
Result: Central Powers victory in the east; Treaty of Brest-Litovsk

The Eastern Front was a theatre of World War I fought in Eastern and Central Europe, principally between the Russian Empire (later the Russian Provisional Government and then Soviet Russia) and the Central Powers — Germany, Austria-Hungary, the Ottoman Empire and Bulgaria.

Course of the war

The war in the east began with the Russian invasion of East Prussia and Galicia in 1914. The invasions were repelled at the Battle of Tannenberg and the Battle of the Masurian Lakes, though Russia achieved substantial successes against Austria-Hungary in Galicia. In 1915 the Central Powers' Gorlice–Tarnów offensive drove Russia out of Poland. Russia's last great effort, the Brusilov Offensive of 1916, badly damaged Austria-Hungary but failed to knock it out of the war.

Revolution and collapse

The February Revolution of 1917 and the subsequent collapse of the Russian Army led the Bolshevik government to sign the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk in March 1918, removing Russia from the war and ceding vast territories. German forces were then transferred west, but the gains were nullified by Germany's defeat in November 1918.

Character

Unlike the static trench warfare of the Western Front, the Eastern Front covered enormous distances and remained comparatively mobile, with correspondingly large casualties and civilian displacement.
