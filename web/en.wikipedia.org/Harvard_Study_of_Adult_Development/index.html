Harvard Study of Adult Development

The Harvard Study of Adult Development is a longitudinal study, begun in 1938, that has followed the lives of two cohorts of men over more than 80 years in order to identify the psychosocial predictors of healthy aging. It is one of the longest-running studies of adult life ever conducted. Researchers at Massachusetts General Hospital and Harvard Medical School survey participants' physical and mental health, work, relationships, and life histories at regular intervals.

Cohorts

The study merged two independent projects: the Grant Study, begun in 1938, which recruited 268 Harvard College sophomores selected for physical and mental soundness; and the Glueck Study, begun in 1940 by Sheldon and Eleanor Glueck, which followed 456 disadvantaged inner-city Boston youths. The two groups were combined into a single study in the 1970s. In the 2000s the study began enrolling the participants' wives and, later, their adult children (the second-generation study).

Findings

Long-time director George Vaillant summarized the study's findings in books including Adaptation to Life (1977) and Triumphs of Experience (2012), emphasizing the power of warm relationships, mature coping (defense) mechanisms, and recovery from difficult childhoods, and the corrosive effects of alcohol abuse, which he called the study's most destructive factor. Under current director Robert Waldinger, the study's headline conclusion — popularized in a widely viewed 2015 TEDx talk — is that satisfying close relationships, more than wealth, fame, cholesterol levels, or social class, predict happiness and physical health in later life: people who were most satisfied in their relationships at age 50 were the healthiest at age 80, and loneliness was found to be as harmful as smoking or alcoholism. Waldinger and Marc Schulz presented the findings in the 2023 book The Good Life.
