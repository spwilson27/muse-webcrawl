Kannywood

Kannywood is the name given to the Hausa-language film industry based in Kano, northern Nigeria. The nickname combines "Kano" with "Hollywood", on the model of Nollywood, the better-known southern Nigerian industry. Kannywood films are made primarily for Hausa-speaking audiences across northern Nigeria and neighbouring Niger, and the industry produces hundreds of titles, distributed first on VHS and VCD and later by satellite television and streaming.

History

The industry grew out of Hausa television drama and literature in the late 1980s and early 1990s, with early hits such as Turmin Danya (1990). Commercial success followed through the 1990s as video technology made production cheap. Following the adoption of sharia law in Kano State in 2000, the state censorship board imposed strict rules on content: restrictions on singing and dancing, dress codes, and limits on physical contact between unrelated men and women. A major crackdown in 2007–08, after a scandal involving a leaked private video of an actress, suspended much production and jailed some filmmakers.

Style and themes

Kannywood films typically explore love, marriage, polygamy, family conflict and the tension between tradition and modernity, and they borrow song-and-dance conventions from Indian Bollywood cinema, which has long been popular in the north. Stars such as Ali Nuhu, Adam A. Zango, Rahama Sadau and Hadiza Gabon are household names across the Hausa-speaking world.
