Quadrature amplitude modulation

TypeDigital modulation scheme
ModulatesAmplitude of two quadrature carriers
Used inWi-Fi, cable modems, DVB, LTE, 5G

Quadrature amplitude modulation (QAM) is the name of a family of digital modulation methods and a related family of analog modulation methods widely used in modern telecommunications to transmit information. It conveys two analog message signals, or two digital bit streams, by changing (modulating) the amplitudes of two carrier waves that are 90 degrees out of phase with each other.

Digital QAM

In digital QAM, the possible signal states are arranged on a rectangular grid called a constellation diagram. A 16-QAM signal carries 4 bits per symbol, 64-QAM carries 6 bits per symbol, and higher orders such as 256-QAM and 1024-QAM are used in cable television and Wi-Fi standards, at the cost of greater sensitivity to noise.

Applications

QAM is used in digital cable television (DOCSIS), microwave radio links, DSL, Wi-Fi (802.11), LTE and 5G mobile networks, and optical fiber systems using coherent detection.
