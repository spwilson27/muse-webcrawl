Desert locust

Desert locust
Scientific name: Schistocerca gregaria
Family: Acrididae
Order: Orthoptera
Distribution: Africa, Middle East, South-West Asia

The desert locust (Schistocerca gregaria) is a species of short-horned grasshopper in the family Acrididae. It is arguably the most destructive migratory pest in the world, capable of forming enormous swarms that devastate crops across Africa, the Middle East and South-West Asia.

Phase polyphenism

Desert locusts exhibit density-dependent phase polyphenism. In the solitary phase they are cryptically coloured, avoid one another and behave like ordinary grasshoppers. When population density rises — typically after good rains produce abundant vegetation — crowding triggers a transformation to the gregarious phase: the insects become boldly coloured in yellow and black, are attracted to one another, and aggregate into bands of hoppers and swarms of winged adults. The change is mediated by the neurotransmitter serotonin.

Swarms and plagues

A swarm can contain tens of millions of individuals per square kilometre and cover hundreds of square kilometres. Locusts eat roughly their own body weight in vegetation each day, so a swarm can consume the same quantity of food in a day as tens of thousands of people. Swarms are carried by the wind and can travel more than 100 kilometres in a day.

Periodic upsurges develop into regional plagues when breeding succeeds across several seasons. The 2019–2021 upsurge in the Greater Horn of Africa and Arabia, the worst in decades for some countries, affected Ethiopia, Kenya, Somalia, Yemen and neighbouring states, threatening the food security of millions.

Control

The Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO) coordinates monitoring through its Desert Locust Information Service, using satellite rainfall and vegetation data together with field surveys. Control relies mainly on aerial and ground spraying of insecticides against hopper bands and swarms; the biopesticide Metarhizium acridum offers a more environmentally benign alternative.
