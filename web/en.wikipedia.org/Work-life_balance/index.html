Work–life balance

Work–life balance is the equilibrium between personal life and career work. The concept concerns how individuals divide time and energy between paid work and other aspects of life such as family, leisure, and health, and how employers and states support or hinder that balance.

History

The term came into common use in the 1970s and 1980s as dual-earner households became more common and working hours rose in some economies. Campaigns for shorter working weeks and flexible working arrangements grew from these concerns.

Issues and policies

Chronic imbalance is associated with stress, burnout, and ill health. Responses include statutory limits on working time (such as the European Working Time Directive), parental leave, the right to request flexible working, remote work, and four-day-week trials. International comparisons by bodies such as the OECD show wide variation in average hours worked between countries.

Criticism

Some commentators argue the phrase falsely separates work from life and that "integration" or "harmony" better captures reality, while others criticise an individualised framing of a structural problem.
