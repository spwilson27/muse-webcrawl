Visa (document)

Visa
TypeTravel document endorsement
Issued byGovernments / consulates
PurposePermission to enter, stay in, or transit a country

A visa (from the Latin charta visa, meaning "paper that has been seen") is a conditional authorization granted by a polity to a foreigner that allows them to enter, remain within, or leave its territory. Visas typically include limits on the duration of the foreigner's stay, areas within the country they may enter, the dates they may enter, the number of permitted visits, or if the individual has the ability to work in the country in question.

Visas are associated with the request for permission to enter a territory and thus are, in most countries, distinct from actual formal permission for an alien to enter and remain in the country. In each instance, a visa is subject to entry permission by an immigration official at the time of actual entry and can be revoked at any time. Visa evidence most commonly takes the form of a sticker endorsed in the applicant's passport or other travel document, but may also exist electronically.

History

In western Europe in the late 19th century and early 20th century, passports and visas were not generally necessary for moving from one country to another. The modern visa system developed largely after the First World War, as states sought greater control over cross-border movement. Some countries, such as France, retained visa requirements only briefly after the war, while others made them permanent features of their immigration law.

Types of visa

Common types include tourist visas, transit visas, business visas, student visas, work visas, and diplomatic visas. Some countries also issue visas on arrival, while others require applications in advance at an embassy or consulate. Electronic visas (eVisas) and electronic travel authorizations have become increasingly common since the early 21st century.

Visa policies

Each country typically has a range of visa policies and conditions. Visa-free regimes, in which citizens of certain countries may enter without a visa, are often based on reciprocal agreements. Henley & Partners and Arton Capital publish passport indices ranking travel freedom by the number of destinations passport holders can access without a prior visa.
