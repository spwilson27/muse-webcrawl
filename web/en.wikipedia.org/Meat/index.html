Meat

Meat is animal tissue, typically muscle, eaten as food. Most commonly it refers to the flesh of mammals and birds — beef from cattle, pork from pigs, lamb and mutton from sheep, and poultry such as chicken — but definitions sometimes include fish and other seafood.

History

Humans have hunted and later farmed animals for meat since prehistory. Domestication of sheep, goats, pigs and cattle in the Neolithic, followed by selective breeding, industrial farming and refrigeration, transformed meat from a luxury into a mass commodity in the 20th century.

Nutrition, environment and ethics

Meat is rich in protein, iron, zinc and vitamin B12, though high consumption of red and processed meat is associated with health risks. Livestock production has major environmental impacts in land use and greenhouse-gas emissions, and meat eating raises animal-welfare and ethical debates that drive vegetarianism, veganism and cultivated-meat research.
