OriginIndia (Kollur Mine, Golconda)

Meaning of name"Mountain of Light" (Persian)

The Koh-i-Noor ("Mountain of Light") is one of the largest cut diamonds in the world, weighing 105.6 carats. Mined in India, it passed through the hands of Mughal emperors, Persian and Afghan rulers and the Sikh Empire before being ceded to Queen Victoria after the British annexation of the Punjab in 1849. It is now set in the Crown of Queen Elizabeth the Queen Mother and displayed with the Crown Jewels at the Tower of London.

History

The diamond probably came from the Kollur Mine in Golconda. It appears in Mughal treasuries and was set in Shah Jahan's Peacock Throne before Nader Shah of Persia seized it in 1739, reportedly giving it its name. After his assassination it passed to Ahmad Shah Durrani of Afghanistan, then to the Sikh maharaja Ranjit Singh. The Treaty of Lahore (1849) surrendered it to the British East India Company, which presented it to Queen Victoria.

Recutting

Displayed at the Great Exhibition of 1851, the stone disappointed viewers with its dull fire; Prince Albert had it recut in 1852 from 186 carats to its present oval brilliant of 105.6 carats, sacrificing size for brilliance.

Ownership disputes

India, Pakistan, Iran and Afghanistan have all claimed the diamond, and demands for its return recur, raising wider questions about colonial-era acquisitions. A legend holds that the stone brings misfortune to male owners, so it has traditionally been worn only by female members of the British royal family.
