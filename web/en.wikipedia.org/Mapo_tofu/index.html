Mapo tofu

Mapo tofu (Chinese: 麻婆豆腐; pinyin: mápó dòufu) is a popular Chinese dish from Sichuan province. It consists of tofu set in a spicy sauce, typically a thin, oily, and bright red suspension, based on douban (fermented broad bean and chilli paste), and douchi (fermented black beans), along with minced meat, traditionally beef. Variations exist with other ingredients such as water chestnuts, onions, other vegetables, or wood ear fungus. It is one of the most famous dishes of Sichuan cuisine.

Etymology and history

Ma stands for "mazi" (麻子), meaning "pockmarks"; po is short for "popo" (婆婆), an old woman or grandmother. Hence mapo is an old woman whose face is pockmarked, said to be the dish's inventor. Legend holds that it was created in 1874 in Chengdu by the wife of the owner of a small restaurant — Chen Mapo, of the Chen Xingsheng Restaurant — who prepared it for oil-carrying workers using tofu, beef and chilli oil. The name became so famous that the restaurant itself took it. The dish's defining seasoning comes from Pixian doubanjiang, Sichuan pepper, garlic sprouts and chilli oil, producing the signature mala (numbing-spicy) flavour, with the tofu traditionally dusted with ground roasted Sichuan pepper.

Characteristics

Authentic mapo tofu is described by eight traditional Chinese characters: ma (numbing), la (spicy-hot), tang (hot in temperature), xiang (aromatic), su (crisp, from the minced beef), nen (tender tofu), xian (fresh flavour) and huo (lively presentation). The silky tofu is simmered in the sauce, briefly thickened with starch, and served over rice.

Variations

Outside Sichuan the dish is frequently adapted to local tastes: milder versions appear in other Chinese regions; in Japan, the Sichuan-born chef Chen Kenmin and his son Chen Kenichi popularised a Japanese-style mapo tofu (mābō-dōfu) that is a fixture of home cooking; vegetarian versions omit the meat and rely on mushrooms. Instant seasoning packets and ready meals are sold widely.
