Jefferson County is a county in the U.S. state of New York, located in the state's North Country along the eastern shore of Lake Ontario and the St. Lawrence River, bordering Canada. As of the 2020 census its population was about 116,000. The county seat and largest city is Watertown.

History

The county was formed from Oneida County in 1805 and named for President Thomas Jefferson. The lakeshore port of Sackets Harbor was a major American naval base during the War of 1812, and the region developed around agriculture, timber, paper mills and tourism in the Thousand Islands.

Geography and economy

Jefferson County includes part of the Thousand Islands archipelago, resort communities such as Alexandria Bay and Clayton, and the enormous Fort Drum military reservation, home of the U.S. Army's 10th Mountain Division and the county's largest employer. Dairy farming, manufacturing and lake-effect snow tourism also shape its economy; the county is known for some of the heaviest snowfall in the eastern United States.
