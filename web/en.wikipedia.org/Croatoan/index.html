Croatoan

Croatoan was the name of an island and of a Native American group in what is now North Carolina, and it is best known as the single word found carved into a palisade post at the abandoned Roanoke Colony in 1590. The Croatoan — also spelled Croatan — were an Algonquian-speaking people whose main settlement lay on the island now identified with Hatteras Island in the Outer Banks.

The Roanoke connection

When governor John White returned to Roanoke Island in August 1590 after a three-year absence, he found the colony deserted, its houses dismantled, and the word "CROATOAN" carved in capital letters on a post of the fort, with the letters "CRO" carved on a nearby tree. No cross — the agreed distress signal — was found. White interpreted the carving to mean the colonists had moved to Croatoan Island, and a prearranged token of this kind had indeed been agreed before his departure. Bad weather prevented him from searching the island, and the fate of the "Lost Colony" remains one of the most famous mysteries of early American history.

Theories

A leading theory holds that the colonists relocated among the Croatoan and were gradually assimilated, a view supported by later reports of English-speaking or grey-eyed Native Americans in the region and by archaeological finds on Hatteras Island. Other theories propose starvation, disease, conflict, or dispersal to the mainland. The word has since entered American folklore and popular culture as an emblem of unexplained disappearance.
