Eros (concept)

Eros is a concept in ancient Greek philosophy referring to sensual or passionate love, from which the English term erotic is derived. It is one of several Greek words for love, alongside philia, agape, and storge.

In philosophy

In Plato's Symposium, eros is presented as a desire for beauty and the good that can ascend from physical attraction to love of the Forms themselves — the origin of the idea of Platonic love. Socrates, through Diotima, describes eros as a daimon mediating between gods and mortals.

In psychoanalysis

Sigmund Freud used Eros to denote the life instinct, encompassing libido and drives toward survival, pleasure, and reproduction, contrasted with Thanatos, the death drive.
