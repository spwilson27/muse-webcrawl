Generalized Riemann hypothesis

The generalized Riemann hypothesis (GRH) is a conjecture in analytic number theory extending the Riemann hypothesis to Dirichlet L-functions. It states that every nontrivial zero of every Dirichlet L-function lies on the critical line Re(s) = 1/2.

Statement

A Dirichlet character χ is a completely multiplicative periodic arithmetic function. Its Dirichlet L-function L(s, χ) is defined for Re(s) > 1 by the series Σ χ(n) n−s and extends to a meromorphic function on the whole complex plane. The GRH asserts that if L(ρ, χ) = 0 with 0 < Re(ρ) < 1, then Re(ρ) = 1/2. The original Riemann hypothesis is the special case for the trivial character, whose L-function is the Riemann zeta function. A still broader statement, the grand Riemann hypothesis, extends the claim to automorphic L-functions.

Consequences

Many important results in number theory are known conditionally on GRH. These include sharp error terms in Dirichlet's theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions, bounds used in proving that every odd number is the sum of three primes (for sufficiently large cases, later made unconditional by Harald Helfgott), and the polynomial-time analysis of the Miller–Rabin primality test: under GRH it suffices to test bases up to 2(ln n)2. GRH also implies strong bounds on character sums and on the least prime in an arithmetic progression (Linnik's theorem with an improved exponent).

Status

Like the Riemann hypothesis itself, GRH remains unproven, though extensive numerical computation has verified that large numbers of zeros of many Dirichlet L-functions lie on the critical line. It was formulated in general terms by Adolf Piltz in 1884.
