Landscape

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A landscape of farmland beneath mountain ranges]

A landscape is the visible features of an area of land, its landforms, and how they integrate with natural or human-made features. The word combines the physical elements of geophysically defined landforms — such as mountains, hills, rivers, lakes, and the sea — with the living elements of land cover (vegetation) and the human elements of land use, buildings, and structures, together with transitory elements such as lighting and weather conditions.

Etymology

The term entered English from the Dutch landschap in the late sixteenth century, initially as a painter's term meaning a picture depicting inland scenery. The Dutch word combined land with the suffix -schap, denoting a region or tract. As a concept the landscape unites a physical area with the way it is perceived by people, a duality central to the modern geographical and ecological use of the term.

Types and study

Landscapes are studied across many disciplines. Geographers analyse landscapes as the outcome of natural processes and human activity over time; cultural geography, following Carl O. Sauer's concept of the "cultural landscape", treats human modification as formative. Landscape ecology studies the spatial patterning of ecosystems and the flows between them. Landscape architecture concerns the design of outdoor spaces, while landscape painting — from Chinese shanshui to Dutch Golden Age painting and the English landscape garden tradition — made the landscape a major artistic genre.

Cultural landscape

UNESCO's World Heritage programme recognises "cultural landscapes" that represent the combined works of nature and humankind, including designed landscapes such as gardens, organically evolved landscapes such as terraced farmland, and associative landscapes of religious or cultural significance. The European Landscape Convention (2000) defined landscape as "an area, as perceived by people, whose character is the result of the action and interaction of natural and/or human factors", and committed signatories to landscape protection and planning.
