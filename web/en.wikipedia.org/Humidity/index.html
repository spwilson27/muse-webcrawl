Humidity

Summary

Measures: Water vapour in air

Types: Absolute, relative, specific

Instrument: Hygrometer

Humidity is the concentration of water vapour present in the air. Water vapour, the gaseous state of water, is generally invisible to the human eye. Humidity indicates the likelihood of precipitation, dew, or fog and plays a central role in weather, climate, and human comfort.

Types

Absolute humidity is the mass of water vapour per unit volume of air. Relative humidity expresses the current amount of water vapour as a percentage of the maximum amount the air can hold at that temperature; warm air can hold more moisture than cold air. Specific humidity is the ratio of the mass of water vapour to the total mass of the air parcel. The dew point — the temperature at which air becomes saturated and dew forms — is closely related to humidity.

Measurement

Humidity is measured with a hygrometer. Traditional instruments include the psychrometer, which compares dry-bulb and wet-bulb thermometers, while modern weather stations use capacitive or resistive electronic sensors.

Effects

High humidity reduces the effectiveness of sweating, the body's main cooling mechanism, making hot weather feel hotter; indices such as the humidex and heat index quantify this effect. Humidity also affects health, agriculture, the storage of materials, the operation of electronics, and the formation of clouds and precipitation. Tropical climates tend to be humid, while deserts have very low humidity even at high temperatures.
