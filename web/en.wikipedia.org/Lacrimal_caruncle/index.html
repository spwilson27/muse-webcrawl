Lacrimal caruncle

The lacrimal caruncle (Latin: caruncula lacrimalis) is the small, pink, globular nodule at the inner corner (medial canthus) of the eye. It consists of skin, hair follicles, sebaceous and sweat glands, and accessory lacrimal tissue, and contains oil and sweat glands that produce a whitish oily substance which collects at the inner eye, especially during sleep.

The caruncle lies within the lacrimal lake, the small triangular space between the upper and lower eyelids at the medial angle, near the lacrimal puncta through which tears drain. It is a modified piece of skin rather than mucous membrane, distinguishing it from the adjacent plica semilunaris, a vestigial remnant of the nictitating membrane.
