Hugo Rafael Chávez Frías (28 July 1954 – 5 March 2013) was a Venezuelan military officer and politician who served as president of Venezuela from 1999 until his death in 2013. A former army paratrooper who led a failed coup attempt in 1992, he won the 1998 presidential election and launched the "Bolivarian Revolution", a programme of socialist reforms funded by oil revenue.

Rise to power

Born into a poor family in Sabaneta, Barinas, Chávez graduated from the Venezuelan Academy of Military Sciences in 1975. Disillusioned with corruption and the 1989 Caracazo crackdown, he founded the clandestine Revolutionary Bolivarian Movement-200 and led the February 1992 coup attempt against President Carlos Andrés Pérez. Imprisoned, then pardoned in 1994, he built a political movement and won the presidency in December 1998 with 56% of the vote.

Presidency

Chávez convened a constituent assembly that produced the 1999 constitution, renamed the country the Bolivarian Republic of Venezuela, and won re-election in 2000, 2006 and 2012. He survived a brief coup in April 2002 and a recall referendum in 2004. High oil prices funded the Bolivarian Missions — social programmes in health, literacy and housing — while his government nationalised industries, clashed with the United States, and promoted regional blocs such as ALBA. Critics accused him of authoritarianism, economic mismanagement and weakening institutions; supporters credited him with reducing poverty and empowering the poor.

Death

Diagnosed with cancer in 2011, Chávez died in Caracas on 5 March 2013 and was succeeded by Nicolás Maduro.
