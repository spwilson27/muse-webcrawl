The Comarca Lagunera ("Lagoon Region") is a region of north-central Mexico spanning parts of the states of Coahuila and Durango, centred on the metropolitan area of Torreón, Gómez Palacio, and Lerdo. Its name comes from the many shallow lakes once formed where the Nazas and Aguanaval rivers ended in this desert basin.

Geography and economy

The region lies within the Chihuahuan Desert and depends on irrigation from the Nazas River, dammed at Lázaro Cárdenas and Francisco Zarco reservoirs. In the late nineteenth and twentieth centuries it became Mexico's leading cotton-growing district, and land reform under President Lázaro Cárdenas in 1936 collectivised much of its farmland into ejidos.

Today La Laguna is one of the country's most important dairy-producing regions, home to major milk and beef industries, alongside mining, metallurgy, and manufacturing in Torreón. Chronic over-extraction of groundwater, which has caused arsenic contamination in some aquifers, is the region's chief environmental problem.
