Carbon price

A carbon price or price on carbon is a cost applied to greenhouse gas emissions to encourage polluters to reduce the emissions responsible for climate change. Economists widely regard carbon pricing as the most efficient single policy instrument for cutting emissions, because it lets emitters choose the cheapest abatement option.

Instruments

The two main forms are a carbon tax, which fixes the price and lets the quantity of emissions vary, and cap-and-trade (emissions trading), which fixes the total quantity and lets the price vary. The European Union Emissions Trading System, launched in 2005, is the largest carbon market; other systems operate in places including California, China, the United Kingdom, Canada, and the northeastern United States (RGGI).

Design issues

Key debates concern the level of the price relative to the social cost of carbon, competitiveness and "leakage" of emissions to unpriced jurisdictions (addressed by border carbon adjustments), and the use of revenue — whether for tax cuts, dividends ("fee and dividend"), or green spending. Only roughly a quarter of global emissions are covered by an explicit carbon price.
