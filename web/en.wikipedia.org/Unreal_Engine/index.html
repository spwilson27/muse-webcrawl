Unreal Engine (UE) is a game engine developed by Epic Games, first showcased in the 1998 first-person shooter Unreal. Written in C++, it is one of the most widely used engines in the video game industry and has expanded into film and television production, architecture, automotive design, and simulation.

Generations

Successive generations powered landmark titles: Unreal Engine 2 underpinned America's Army; Unreal Engine 3 (2006) powered Gears of War and BioShock and became a de facto standard in the Xbox 360/PlayStation 3 era; Unreal Engine 4 (2014) introduced the Blueprint visual scripting system and a subscription, later royalty-only, model; and Unreal Engine 5, released in April 2022, introduced Nanite virtualized geometry, Lumen global illumination, and MetaHuman realistic characters.

Licensing and adoption

Since 2015 the engine has been free to download with source code access, with Epic collecting a 5% royalty on gross revenue beyond a threshold (raised to $1 million in 2020). Beyond games — including Epic's own Fortnite — Unreal Engine is used for virtual production on LED stages, as in The Mandalorian, and for real-time visualization across industry.
