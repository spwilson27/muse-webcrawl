The Italian Job

The Italian Job
Directed by: Peter Collinson
Written by: Troy Kennedy Martin
Starring: Michael Caine, Noël Coward, Benny Hill
Release date: 2 June 1969
Running time: 99 minutes

The Italian Job is a 1969 British comedy caper film directed by Peter Collinson and starring Michael Caine. The film tells the story of Charlie Croker, a Cockney criminal who assembles a gang to steal a shipment of Chinese gold in Turin, Italy, using three Mini Coopers to escape through the city's gridlocked streets. It is celebrated for its car chases, its Quincy Jones score, and one of the most famous cliffhanger endings in cinema.

Plot

Fresh out of prison, Charlie Croker takes over a plan devised by a murdered associate to steal $4 million in gold bullion — a Chinese payment to Fiat — under cover of a traffic jam engineered by sabotaging Turin's traffic control computers. Backed by the imprisoned crime lord Mr. Bridger (Noël Coward), the gang executes the heist and escapes in red, white, and blue Minis through shopping arcades, sewers, and across rooftops. The film ends literally on a cliffhanger: the gang's coach teeters on the edge of an Alpine road, the gold sliding toward the drop, as Croker says, "Hang on a minute, lads, I've got a great idea."

Production

The chase sequences were filmed in Turin with the cooperation of Fiat; the iconic Mini Cooper S cars performed stunts largely without computer assistance. Michael Caine's line "You're only supposed to blow the bloody doors off!" is among the most quoted in British film.

Legacy

The film became a British cultural touchstone and was ranked among the BFI's top 100 British films. A 2003 American remake, also titled The Italian Job, starred Mark Wahlberg and Charlize Theron and relocated the story to Los Angeles and Venice. The unresolved ending of the original has been the subject of competitions and scientific speculation about how the gang might have escaped.
