Skimming (fraud)

Skimming is a method of identity theft and payment-card fraud in which criminals record the data stored on a card's magnetic stripe using a concealed reading device, often combined with a camera or fake keypad to capture the cardholder's PIN. The stolen data is then used to create counterfeit cards or make fraudulent purchases.

Skimming

Type: Payment card fraud
Targets: ATMs, fuel pumps, point-of-sale terminals
Countermeasures: EMV chips, inspection, contactless payment

Method

A skimmer is typically fitted over the card slot of an ATM or fuel pump and designed to blend with the machine's fascia. Deep-insert and shimmer devices target chip readers. More recently, "e-skimming" malware injected into e-commerce checkout pages steals card details online.

Prevention

The migration to EMV chip cards sharply reduced counterfeit-card fraud, while advice to users includes checking for loose or misaligned card slots, covering the keypad when entering a PIN, and monitoring statements.
