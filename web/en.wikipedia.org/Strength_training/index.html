Strength training

Strength training, also known as weight training or resistance training, is exercise that uses resistance to induce muscular contraction, which builds the strength, anaerobic endurance, and size of skeletal muscles. When properly performed, strength training can provide significant functional benefits and improvement in overall health and well-being, including increased bone, muscle, tendon, and ligament strength and toughness, improved joint function, reduced potential for injury, increased bone density, increased metabolism, and improved cardiac function.

Principles

Training commonly uses the technique of progressively increasing the force output of the muscle through incremental weight increases (progressive overload). Exercises may be performed with free weights (barbells and dumbbells), weight machines, resistance bands, or the body's own weight (as in push-ups, pull-ups and squats). Workouts are organised in sets and repetitions: lower repetitions with heavy loads favour maximal strength, while higher repetitions with moderate loads favour muscular endurance and hypertrophy to varying degrees. Major compound lifts include the squat, deadlift, bench press, overhead press and barbell row.

Health effects and safety

Health guidelines such as those of the World Health Organization recommend muscle-strengthening activities at least twice a week for adults. Strength training is associated with reduced risk of type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease, osteoporosis and all-cause mortality. Injuries most often result from poor technique or excessive load; supervision, gradual progression and proper warm-up reduce risk. Competitive disciplines built on strength training include powerlifting, Olympic weightlifting, bodybuilding and strongman.
