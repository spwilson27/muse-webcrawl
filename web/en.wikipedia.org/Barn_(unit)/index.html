Barn (unit)

A barn (symbol: b) is a metric unit of area equal to 10−28 m2 (100 fm2). Originally used in nuclear physics to express the cross-sectional area of nuclei and nuclear reactions, it is now widely used in particle physics.

Origin

The unit was introduced during Manhattan Project research into nuclear cross sections. Physicists joked that a uranium nucleus was so large, for the purposes of neutron capture, that it was "as big as a barn". One barn is roughly the cross-sectional area of a uranium nucleus. Common multiples include the millibarn (mb) and microbarn (µb); the inverse femtobarn (fb−1) is a standard measure of integrated luminosity at particle colliders such as the Large Hadron Collider.
