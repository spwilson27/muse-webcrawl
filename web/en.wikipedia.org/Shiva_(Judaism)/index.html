Shiva (Judaism)

Shiva (Hebrew: שִׁבְעָה, literally 'seven') is a week-long mourning period in Judaism for first-degree relatives. The ritual is referred to as 'sitting shiva' in English. The shiva period lasts seven days following the burial of a close relative.

During shiva, mourners traditionally remain at home, sit on low stools or boxes, cover mirrors, and refrain from work, bathing for pleasure, and other activities. Friends and family visit to offer condolences, forming a minyan for prayer services at the home.

The seven days are counted from the day of burial, and the observance is shortened if it overlaps with certain Jewish holidays. Shiva is followed by the less intense mourning period of shloshim, lasting thirty days.
