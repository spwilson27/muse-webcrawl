John Edward Lautner (16 July 1911 – 24 October 1994) was an American architect whose inventive, sculptural houses — mostly in the Los Angeles area — became icons of mid-century modern and Googie-influenced residential design.

Early life and training

Lautner grew up in Michigan's Upper Peninsula and studied at the University of Michigan. In 1933 he joined Frank Lloyd Wright's Taliesin Fellowship in Wisconsin and Arizona, remaining a Wright apprentice for six years and supervising construction of several Wright projects. He established his own practice in Los Angeles in 1938; his own Lautner House (1939) in Silver Lake was an early success.

Works

Lautner's designs exploited concrete, dramatic geometry and integration with steep hillside sites. Famous houses include the Chemosphere (1960), an octagonal house perched on a single concrete column in the Hollywood Hills; the Elrod House (1968) in Palm Springs, with its great conical concrete roof, featured in the James Bond film Diamonds Are Forever; the Sheats Goldstein Residence (1963, later remodelled) in Beverly Hills; the Arango House in Acapulco (1973); and the Bob Hope House in Palm Springs (1979), with its vast undulating roof. He also designed Googie coffee shops and commercial buildings, including Henry's restaurant and Googie's (1949, demolished), which gave the style its name. His only high-rise was in Acapulco; much of his later career was spent in Mexico and on large private residences.

Legacy

Largely overlooked by the architectural establishment during much of his career, Lautner's work was reappraised from the 1980s; the Hammer Museum's 2008 exhibition "Between Earth and Heaven" cemented his reputation. The John Lautner Foundation preserves his archive. Many of his houses are sought-after film locations and are protected as historic-cultural monuments.
