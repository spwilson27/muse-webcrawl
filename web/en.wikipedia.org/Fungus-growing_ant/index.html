Fungus-growing ant

Fungus-growing ants (tribe Attini, in the broad sense) comprise some 250 described species of New World ants that cultivate fungi as their primary food source. This mutualism originated roughly 50–60 million years ago in South America and is one of the few examples of agriculture in non-human animals.

Cultivation

The best-known fungus-growers are the leafcutter ants (genera Atta and Acromyrmex), which harvest fresh vegetation not to eat directly but as substrate for their fungal gardens. The fungus, typically Leucoagaricus gongylophorus, produces nutrient-rich structures called gongylidia on which the ants feed. Ants protect their gardens from the parasitic fungus Escovopsis using antibiotic-producing bacteria carried on their bodies.

Castes and colonies

Large leafcutter colonies can contain millions of workers divided into specialised size castes, from tiny garden-tenders to large soldiers. Their nests, with vast underground chambers, are major agents of soil turnover and nutrient cycling in neotropical ecosystems.
