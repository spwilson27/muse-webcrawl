Léopold Sédar Senghor

Born9 October 1906, Joal, Senegal
Died20 December 2001, Verson, France
OfficePresident of Senegal, 1960–1980
Known forNégritude; poetry; statesmanship

Léopold Sédar Senghor (9 October 1906 – 20 December 2001) was a Senegalese poet, politician, and cultural theorist who served as the first President of Senegal, holding office from 1960 until 1980. He was a founder of the Négritude movement and the first African elected to the Académie française.

Early life and education

Senghor was born in the coastal town of Joal, in French Senegal, into a Serer family. Sent to Paris on a scholarship, he studied at the Lycée Louis-le-Grand and the Sorbonne, graduating from the École Normale Supérieure in 1935. There he formed friendships with Aimé Césaire and Léon-Gontran Damas, with whom he developed the concept of Négritude — an affirmation of African cultural values against colonial assimilation.

Politics

After serving in the French army in the Second World War and surviving a period as a prisoner of war, Senghor entered French politics as a deputy for Senegal. Following independence he became Senegal's first president in 1960. He governed as an advocate of African socialism, maintained close ties with France, and made Senegal one of West Africa's most stable states. In 1980 he voluntarily resigned, handing power to his prime minister, Abdou Diouf — an unusually peaceful transfer of power in the region at the time.

Literary career

Senghor's poetry collections include Chants d'ombre (1945) and Hosties noires (1948). His 1948 anthology of Black Francophone poetry carried a celebrated preface by Jean-Paul Sartre, "Orphée noir". He was elected to the Académie française in 1983.
