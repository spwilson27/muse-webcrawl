Ahmedabad

State: Gujarat, India
Founded: 1411, by Ahmed Shah I
Population: approx. 5.6 million (city, 2011 census); approx. 8–9 million (metro)
UNESCO: Historic City of Ahmedabad, World Heritage Site (2017)

Ahmedabad (Gujarati: અમદાવાદ, Amdāvād) is the most populous city in the Indian state of Gujarat and the state's former capital. It stands on the banks of the Sabarmati River, about 25 km from the current capital, Gandhinagar. It is the fifth-most populous city in India and a major economic and industrial hub, historically known as the "Manchester of India" for its textile industry.

History

The city was founded in 1411 by Sultan Ahmed Shah I of the Gujarat Sultanate, who made it his capital. Under Mughal rule it became a prosperous centre of trade and textiles. It passed to the Marathas and then, in 1817, to the British, under whom its textile mills multiplied. Ahmedabad was central to Mahatma Gandhi's campaigns: he founded the Sabarmati Ashram here in 1917 and launched the 1930 Salt March (Dandi March) from the city.

Ahmedabad served as Gujarat's capital from the state's formation in 1960 until the capital shifted to Gandhinagar in 1970. In 2001 the city was struck by a devastating earthquake.

Heritage and culture

The old city, with its dense pols (traditional housing clusters), carved wooden havelis, the Jama Masjid, Sidi Saiyyed Mosque (famous for its stone lattice jali of the "Tree of Life"), and stepwells such as Adalaj, was inscribed as India's first UNESCO World Heritage City in 2017. The Sabarmati Riverfront development has reshaped the river's banks. Ahmedabad hosts the Indian Institute of Management Ahmedabad, designed by Louis Kahn, and major festivals include Uttarayan (the kite festival) and Navratri.

Economy

Textiles, chemicals, pharmaceuticals and automobile manufacturing anchor the economy; the city is also a growing centre for education, information technology and finance, including the nearby GIFT City.
