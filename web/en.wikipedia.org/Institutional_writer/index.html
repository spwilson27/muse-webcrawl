Institutional writer

Institutional writer
FieldProfessional writing, organisational communication
EmployersGovernments, corporations, universities, non-profits
OutputsReports, policies, manuals, speeches, official correspondence

An institutional writer is a professional writer employed by an organisation — such as a government agency, corporation, university, or non-profit body — to produce written material in the institution's name and voice rather than under their own byline. The work is a branch of professional and technical communication.

Role

Institutional writers draft annual reports, policy documents, procedures and manuals, press releases, speeches for executives or officials, grant applications, and web content. Because the texts speak for the organisation, they must conform to house style, legal and regulatory requirements, and the institution's public positions. Authorship is typically anonymous or corporate.

Skills and practice

The role combines writing and editing ability with subject-matter research, interviewing of experts within the organisation, and project coordination. Writers often work with legal, compliance, and design teams. Style guides and templates standardise tone and format across documents and authors.

Distinctions

Institutional writing differs from journalism and literary authorship in that the writer's individual voice is subordinated to the institution's. Related occupations include technical writers, copywriters, speechwriters, and communications officers; the boundaries overlap, and many professionals move between them during a career.
