Saint Stephen

Saint Stephen was a deacon in the early Church at Jerusalem, traditionally venerated as the protomartyr — the first Christian martyr. His story is told in chapters 6 and 7 of the Acts of the Apostles. His feast day is 26 December in the Western Church and 27 December in the Eastern Church.

Appointment as deacon

According to Acts 6, the Hellenistic (Greek-speaking) widows of the community complained of being neglected in the daily distribution of food. The Twelve Apostles appointed seven men of good repute, "full of the Spirit and of wisdom," to serve at tables; Stephen, described as "a man full of faith and of the Holy Spirit," is named first among them. He is also said to have performed "great wonders and signs among the people."

Trial and speech

Members of the Synagogue of the Freedmen disputed with Stephen and, failing to withstand his arguments, brought him before the Sanhedrin on charges of blasphemy against Moses and God. In response Stephen delivered the longest speech in Acts, a survey of Israel's history arguing that God does not dwell in houses made by human hands and accusing his hearers of resisting the Holy Spirit and betraying the Righteous One. He concluded by declaring that he saw "the heavens opened, and the Son of Man standing at the right hand of God."

Martyrdom

Enraged, the crowd dragged Stephen outside the city and stoned him. The witnesses laid their garments at the feet of a young man named Saul — the future Paul the Apostle. As he died, Stephen prayed, "Lord Jesus, receive my spirit," and "Lord, do not hold this sin against them," echoing the words of Jesus on the cross.

Veneration

Stephen is the patron saint of deacons, altar servers, stonemasons and headache sufferers. His relics were said to have been discovered in 415 at Caphar Gamala. In art he is usually depicted as a young deacon in a dalmatic, carrying a palm of martyrdom and stones.
