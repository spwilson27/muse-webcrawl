Pele (deity)

Pele (pronounced [ˈpɛlɛ]) is the Hawaiian goddess of volcanoes, fire, lightning, and wind, and the creator of the Hawaiian Islands. She is one of the most revered figures in Native Hawaiian religion, known as Pele-honua-mea, "Pele of the sacred land".

Mythology

According to tradition, Pele travelled from Kahiki (Tahiti) across the Pacific, creating islands as she went, and finally settled in the Halemaʻumaʻu crater at the summit of Kīlauea on the island of Hawaiʻi, which remains her home. Legends tell of her tempestuous relationships: her rivalry with her sister Nāmaka, goddess of the sea, who pursues her with waves that quench her fires; her love for the mortal chief Lohiʻau; and her family of siblings, including the shark god Kāmohoaliʻi, who guided her voyage. Her eruptions are interpreted as manifestations of her moods and movements.

Cultural significance

Pele remains a living presence in Hawaiian culture. Practitioners leave offerings at Kīlauea, and hula schools dedicated to her perform at the crater's edge. The well-known "Pele's curse" — the belief that removing lava rocks from Hawaiʻi brings bad luck — is a modern folk belief not rooted in traditional religion. Sightings of an elderly woman in white, or a young woman with long dark hair, along Kīlauea's roads are widely reported and attributed to the goddess.
