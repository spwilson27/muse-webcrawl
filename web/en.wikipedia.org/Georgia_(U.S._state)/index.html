Georgia is a state in the Southeastern United States. It borders Florida to the south, Alabama to the west, Tennessee and North Carolina to the north, South Carolina to the northeast, and the Atlantic Ocean to the southeast. Georgia is the eighth most populous state and the 24th largest by area. Its capital and largest city is Atlanta.

History

Founded in 1732 under a charter granted to James Oglethorpe and named for King George II, Georgia was the last of the Thirteen Colonies to be established, serving as a buffer against Spanish Florida and a haven for debtors. It ratified the U.S. Constitution in 1788 as the fourth state. Georgia seceded in 1861 and suffered heavily in the Civil War, including Sherman's March to the Sea in 1864. In the twentieth century Atlanta became a centre of the civil rights movement, the home of Martin Luther King Jr.

Geography

The state's terrain descends from the Blue Ridge Mountains in the northeast, through the Piedmont plateau around Atlanta, to the coastal plain in the south. Georgia has a humid subtropical climate, hot summers, and is noted for peaches, pecans, peanuts, and the Okefenokee Swamp on the Florida border.

Economy

Georgia's economy is anchored by Atlanta, a major transport hub whose airport is among the world's busiest. Large employers include Delta Air Lines, Coca-Cola, Home Depot, and UPS. The Port of Savannah is one of the fastest-growing container ports in the United States, and the state has become a major centre for film and television production, nicknamed "Hollywood of the South."
