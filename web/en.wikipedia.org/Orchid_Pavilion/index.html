Orchid Pavilion

The gathering at the Orchid Pavilion in 353 CE, a landmark of Chinese calligraphy.

The Orchid Pavilion (traditional Chinese: 蘭亭; simplified Chinese: 兰亭; pinyin: Lántíng) is a site near Shaoxing in Zhejiang province, China, famous as the setting of the Orchid Pavilion Gathering of 353 CE during the Eastern Jin dynasty.

The gathering

On the third day of the third month of 353, the calligrapher Wang Xizhi invited forty-one scholar friends to the pavilion for the spring purification festival. Seated along a winding stream, the guests played a drinking game in which cups of wine were floated down the water; whoever the cup stopped before had to compose a poem or drink. Twenty-six guests produced thirty-seven poems, which were collected into an anthology.

The Preface

Wang Xizhi wrote a preface for the collection, the Preface to the Poems Composed at the Orchid Pavilion (蘭亭集序), in a semi-cursive script while slightly drunk. Its 324 characters combine literary meditation on the brevity of life with calligraphy of legendary fluidity and grace. It is widely considered the greatest work of Chinese running-script calligraphy, and Wang Xizhi is revered as the Sage of Calligraphy.

Legacy

The original was said to have been buried with the Tang emperor Taizong, who prized it above all treasures; the work survives through traced copies such as the Shenlong version in the Palace Museum, Beijing. The pavilion and its gathering remain a defining image of literati culture, imitated at later gatherings for more than a millennium.
