List of floods

A flood is an overflow of water that submerges land that is usually dry. Floods may result from overflowing rivers, storm surges, heavy rainfall, dam failures, or rapid snowmelt. They are among the deadliest of all natural disasters: the worst floods in recorded history, on China's Yellow River and Yangtze River, killed millions of people.

Notable floods by region

Europe

The North Sea flood of 1953 killed 2,551 people in the Netherlands, Belgium, and the United Kingdom and prompted construction of the Dutch Delta Works and the Thames Barrier. The Great Flood of 1861–62 inundated much of Central Europe's river valleys. In July 2021, catastrophic flooding in Germany's Ahr valley and in Belgium killed more than 240 people.

North America

The Great Mississippi Flood of 1927 displaced some 700,000 people in the United States. The Johnstown Flood of 1889, caused by the failure of the South Fork Dam in Pennsylvania, killed 2,209 people. Hurricane Katrina in 2005 flooded roughly 80% of New Orleans after levee failures.

Asia

Beyond the Chinese floods listed above, South Asia experiences severe monsoon flooding nearly every year; the 2022 Pakistan floods affected over 33 million people and submerged about a third of the country at their peak.

Flash floods and dam failures

Flash floods, such as the Big Thompson Canyon flood of 1976 in Colorado and the 2018 Kerala floods in India, are distinguished by their speed of onset. Dam failures — including the Vajont Dam disaster of 1963 in Italy, where a landslide-driven overtopping wave killed about 2,000 people — are among the most destructive flood types.
