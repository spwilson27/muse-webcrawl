Plastic recycling

Plastic recycling is the processing of plastic waste into other products. Recycling can reduce dependence on landfill, conserve resources, and protect the environment from plastic pollution and greenhouse gas emissions, although recycling rates remain low — globally only about 9% of plastic waste is recycled, with most of the remainder landfilled, incinerated, or mismanaged. Plastics are sorted by polymer type using resin identification codes (1–7), such as PET and HDPE, which are the most widely recycled.

Processes

Mechanical recycling sorts, washes, shreds, melts, and re-extrudes plastic into pellets for new products; repeated cycles degrade polymer quality ("downcycling"). Chemical recycling — pyrolysis, gasification, and depolymerisation — breaks polymers back into monomers or fuels and can handle contaminated or mixed streams, but is energy-intensive. Primary (closed-loop) recycling returns material to the same product, as in bottle-to-bottle PET recycling.

Challenges

Barriers include low virgin-plastic prices, the diversity of polymer formulations and additives, food contamination, multilayer packaging, and weak end markets. The 2018 Chinese import ban ("National Sword") disrupted global trade in plastic scrap and pushed exporting countries to build domestic capacity.
