MONUSCO

MONUSCO peacekeepers in North Kivu

Full name: United Nations Organization Stabilization Mission in the Democratic Republic of the Congo
Established: 1 July 2010 (successor to MONUC, 1999)
Headquarters: Kinshasa / Goma
Strength: ~12,000–15,000 uniformed personnel

MONUSCO, the United Nations Organization Stabilization Mission in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (French: Mission de l'Organisation des Nations Unies pour la stabilisation en République démocratique du Congo), is a United Nations peacekeeping force in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. It was established by Security Council Resolution 1925 in 2010, taking over from an earlier mission, MONUC, which had operated since 1999 in the aftermath of the Second Congo War.

Mandate

MONUSCO is authorised to use all necessary means to protect civilians, humanitarian personnel, and human rights defenders under imminent threat of physical violence, and to support the Congolese government in stabilising the country and disarming armed groups. In 2013, after the rebel group M23 seized the eastern city of Goma, the Security Council created within MONUSCO a Force Intervention Brigade — the first UN peacekeeping unit with an explicit offensive combat mandate — which, alongside the Congolese army, defeated M23 later that year.

Criticism and drawdown

The mission has faced persistent criticism for failing to prevent massacres and abuses by armed groups in the east, and deadly anti-MONUSCO protests erupted in 2022. At Kinshasa's request, the Security Council set a phased withdrawal in motion, with peacekeepers leaving South Kivu in 2024, though the advance of the resurgent M23 in North Kivu complicated the drawdown.
