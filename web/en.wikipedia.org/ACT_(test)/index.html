The ACT is a standardized test used for college admissions in the United States. It is administered by ACT, Inc., a nonprofit organization based in Iowa City, Iowa, and was first introduced in November 1959 as a competitor to the SAT.

Structure

The test covers four skill areas: English, mathematics, reading, and science reasoning, with an optional writing section. Each section is scored from 1 to 36, and the composite score is the average of the four.

Usage

All major four-year colleges and universities in the United States accept ACT scores. The test is especially prevalent in the Midwest and South, and several states administer it to all public high school students as part of state assessment programs.
