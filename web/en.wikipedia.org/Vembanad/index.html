Vembanad (Vembanad Kayal or Vembanad Kol) is the longest lake in India and the largest in the state of Kerala. It spans the districts of Alappuzha, Kottayam, and Ernakulam, forming the heart of the famous Kerala backwaters.

A houseboat on Vembanad Lake.

Geography

The lake is fed by several rivers including the Pamba, Meenachil, and Periyar, and opens to the Arabian Sea at the port of Kochi. A unique feature is that large areas of the surrounding Kuttanad region lie below sea level, protected by bunds — reclaimed land where rice is cultivated beneath the water level of the lake. The Thanneermukkom Barrage divides the lake's brackish and fresh waters.

Ecology and culture

Vembanad is a designated Ramsar site, rich in fish, prawns, and migratory birds, though threatened by pollution and reclamation. It hosts the renowned Nehru Trophy Boat Race (snake-boat races) held annually at Punnamada, and the resort island of Kumarakom on its eastern shore is a major tourist destination for houseboat cruises.
