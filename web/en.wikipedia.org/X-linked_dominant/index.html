X-linked dominant inheritance

X-linked dominant inheritance is a mode of inheritance in which a single copy of a dominant gene on the X chromosome causes the trait. It is rarer than X-linked recessive inheritance.

X-linked dominant
Examples: Rett syndrome, Fragile X, Vitamin D resistant rickets

Characteristics

Affected fathers pass the trait to all daughters and no sons; affected heterozygous mothers pass it to half of children of either sex. Some conditions are lethal in males, so only females are seen.

Examples

Fragile X syndrome (with anticipation), Rett syndrome (MECP2), incontinentia pigmenti, Aicardi syndrome, and X-linked hypophosphataemic rickets. See sex linkage.
