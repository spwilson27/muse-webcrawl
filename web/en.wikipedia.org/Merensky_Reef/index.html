Merensky Reef

The Merensky Reef is a layer of igneous rock in the Bushveld Igneous Complex of South Africa that contains most of the world's reserves of platinum-group metals (PGMs) — platinum, palladium, rhodium, ruthenium, iridium and osmium — together with significant copper, nickel and gold. The reef, typically less than a metre thick, has been the world's principal source of platinum since its discovery in 1924 by geologist Hans Merensky.

Geology

The reef lies near the top of the Critical Zone of the Rustenburg Layered Suite, a vast ~2.06-billion-year-old layered intrusion. It consists of pegmatoidal pyroxenite with chromitite stringers, marked by pothole structures where the layer is locally disrupted. Its formation is attributed to magma mixing and crystal settling that concentrated sulphide liquid scavenging platinum-group elements. A second, chromitite-hosted layer, the UG2 Reef below it, is now mined on a comparable scale.

Mining

Merensky Reef mines ring the eastern and western limbs of the Bushveld around Rustenburg and in Limpopo province; major producers include Anglo American Platinum, Impala Platinum and Sibanye Stillwater. Conventional narrow-reef stoping is labour-intensive and increasingly deep, with workings extending beyond 2 km. Grades commonly run 3–8 grams per tonne of combined PGMs.
