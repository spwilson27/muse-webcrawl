Empedocles

Empedocles (c. 494 – c. 434 BC) was a Greek pre-Socratic philosopher from Acragas, Sicily. He is best known for originating the cosmogonic theory of the four classical elements — earth, water, air, and fire.

Philosophy

Empedocles held that all things are composed of four eternal roots combined and separated by two cosmic forces, Love and Strife. His cycle of attraction and repulsion explained change without true creation or destruction. He also proposed an early idea resembling natural selection, in which ill-adapted combinations perished.

Works and legend

His poems On Nature and Purifications survive in fragments. Legend claims he leapt into Mount Etna to prove his divinity, a story treated skeptically by ancient sources themselves.
