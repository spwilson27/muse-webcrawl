Insect

Insects
Class: Insecta
Described species: Over 1 million
Body plan: Head, thorax, abdomen; six legs
First appearance: ~479 million years ago

Insects (class Insecta) are hexapod invertebrates and the largest group within the arthropod phylum. They are characterised by a chitinous exoskeleton, a three-part body (head, thorax and abdomen), three pairs of jointed legs, compound eyes, and a pair of antennae. With more than a million described species, insects represent over half of all known living organisms.

Diversity and distribution

Insects are found in nearly all environments, from deserts and rainforests to freshwater, though few inhabit the oceans. Major orders include the beetles (Coleoptera), butterflies and moths (Lepidoptera), flies (Diptera), ants, bees and wasps (Hymenoptera), and true bugs (Hemiptera).

Life cycle

Most insects hatch from eggs and undergo metamorphosis. In complete metamorphosis (holometabolism), the larva differs greatly from the adult and passes through a pupal stage; in incomplete metamorphosis, nymphs gradually resemble adults. Many insects are social or form complex colonies, notably ants, termites, and honey bees.

Relation to humans

Insects pollinate a large share of food crops, decompose waste, and serve as food for other animals, but some are serious pests or vectors of disease, spreading malaria, dengue, and plague. Insecticides, habitat loss, and climate change are driving declines in many insect populations worldwide. See also Arthropod and Entomology.
