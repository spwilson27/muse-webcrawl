Unmanned combat aerial vehicle

An unmanned combat aerial vehicle (UCAV), also known as a combat drone, drone or loyal wingman, is an unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) that carries aircraft ordnance such as missiles, anti-tank guided missiles, or bombs and is used for drone strikes. These drones are usually under real-time human control, with varying levels of autonomy.

Description

Unlike unmanned surveillance and reconnaissance aerial vehicles, UCAVs are used for both drone strikes and battlefield intelligence. Aircraft of this type have no onboard pilot and are operated remotely by a pilot in a ground control station, who can authorise weapons release. UCAVs vary from converted reconnaissance platforms, such as the MQ-1 Predator armed with Hellfire missiles, to purpose-built stealth aircraft designed to penetrate contested airspace.

History

Early experiments with armed unmanned aircraft date to the Cold War, but the modern UCAV emerged in the 1990s when the United States armed the RQ-1 Predator surveillance drone. The MQ-1's successor, the MQ-9 Reaper, became a mainstay of US and allied operations. Dedicated combat designs followed, including the Boeing X-45, Northrop Grumman X-47B carrier-based demonstrator, the BAE Systems Taranis, the Dassault nEUROn, and the Russian Sukhoi S-70 Okhotnik-B. China, Turkey, Iran and other states have developed their own armed drones; the Turkish Bayraktar TB2 gained prominence in conflicts in the 2020s.

Ethics and law

The use of UCAVs for targeted killing has raised legal and ethical debate, including questions about civilian casualties, accountability, and the threshold for the use of lethal force. Fully autonomous weapons — sometimes called lethal autonomous weapons systems — are the subject of international discussion about a possible ban, and most operators state that a human remains responsible for weapons release.

Notable examples

Notable UCAVs and demonstrators include the General Atomics MQ-1 Predator and MQ-9 Reaper, the Baykar Bayraktar TB2 and Akinci, the CASC Rainbow series, the IAI Harop loitering munition, the Boeing MQ-28 Ghost Bat "loyal wingman", and the Kratos XQ-58 Valkyrie.
