Cerro Tololo Inter-American Observatory

LocationCerro Tololo, Coquimbo Region, Chile
Altitude2,207 m
Established1962
Main telescopeVíctor M. Blanco 4 m Telescope

The Cerro Tololo Inter-American Observatory (CTIO) is an astronomical observatory in the Chilean Andes, about 80 km east of La Serena. It is operated by the Association of Universities for Research in Astronomy (AURA) as part of the U.S. National Optical Astronomy Observatory, now integrated into NOIRLab.

Instruments

The observatory's principal instrument is the 4-metre Víctor M. Blanco Telescope, completed in 1974, which hosted the Dark Energy Camera (DECam) used by the Dark Energy Survey. Other telescopes on the summit include the SMARTS consortium telescopes and the SOAR Telescope on nearby Cerro Pachón, which is administered with CTIO.

History

The site was selected in the early 1960s to give northern-hemisphere astronomers access to the southern sky, including the Magellanic Clouds and the galactic centre. It was the first major international observatory built in Chile's Atacama fringe region.
