Doubling cube


Illustration: Doubling cube

The doubling cube is a die-like object used in backgammon to raise the stakes of the game. Marked with the numbers 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 and 64, the cube tracks the current multiplier of the game's point value, and the player who most recently accepted a double owns the cube and alone may offer the next one.

Use

At the start of a game the cube sits in the middle showing 64, representing a stake of one unit. A player who believes they hold an advantage may, before rolling, offer to double the stakes. The opponent may accept ("take"), in which case play continues at the doubled value and the cube passes to them, or refuse ("drop" or "pass"), forfeiting the game at the current stake.

History and strategy

The doubling cube was introduced in American backgammon clubs in the 1920s and transformed the game into one of probability management and risk assessment. Concepts such as the take point and the Crawford rule in match play govern its correct use.
