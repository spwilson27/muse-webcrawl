Klaipėda (German: Memel) is the third-largest city in Lithuania and its only major seaport, located on the Baltic Sea coast at the mouth of the Akmena-Danė River, where it flows into the Curonian Lagoon. It is the capital of Klaipėda County and the centre of western Lithuania's economy, notable for its ice-free port, German-influenced old town, and role as a gateway to the Curonian Spit.

History

The city was founded in 1252 by the Teutonic Knights, who built Memelburg Castle at the site of a Curonian settlement. Known as Memel, it became the northernmost city of Prussia and later the German Empire, a major timber-trading port. After the First World War the Klaipėda Region (Memelland) was detached from Germany and placed under Allied administration; Lithuanian forces seized it in the Klaipėda Revolt of 1923, and it was incorporated into Lithuania. Nazi Germany annexed it in 1939; the Red Army captured the city in January 1945 after fierce fighting that left much of it destroyed. The German population fled or was expelled, and the city was rebuilt as part of the Lithuanian Soviet Socialist Republic. Since 1990 it has been part of independent Lithuania.

Port

The Port of Klaipėda is the northernmost ice-free port on the eastern Baltic coast and Lithuania's principal maritime gateway, handling cargo including containers, oil products, fertilisers, and ro-ro traffic, as well as cruise ships and the ferry routes to Germany, Sweden, and Denmark. An LNG terminal, centred on the floating storage vessel Independence, opened in 2014.

Culture and landmarks

The old town retains half-timbered warehouses and the Theatre Square with its statue of Ännchen von Tharau. The Lithuanian Sea Museum and dolphinarium occupy the old Kopgalis fort at the tip of the Curonian Spit. The city hosts the annual Sea Festival (Jūros šventė), one of Lithuania's largest summer celebrations.
