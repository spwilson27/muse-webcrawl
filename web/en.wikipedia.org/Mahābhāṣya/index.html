Mahābhāṣya

The Mahābhāṣya (Sanskrit: महाभाष्य, "Great Commentary") is a commentary by the ancient Indian grammarian Patañjali (2nd century BCE) on the Aṣṭādhyāyī of Pāṇini and the Vārttika of Kātyāyana. It is one of the three foundational works of the Sanskrit grammatical tradition — the "trimuni vyākaraṇa" — alongside Pāṇini's grammar itself and Kātyāyana's critical notes.

Content

Written in an elegant dialogic style, the Mahābhāṣya examines grammatical rules through discussion of examples, objections and resolutions. Beyond linguistics, it is a rich source for the social, religious and intellectual life of ancient India: it mentions contemporary dynasties, customs, and everyday objects, and contains famous philosophical reflections on language, including discussions of whether words denote universals or individuals and of the eternal nature of the word (sphoṭa theory in embryo). Patañjali also discusses the purpose of grammar study and the definition of correct speech (śiṣṭa usage).

Influence

The Mahābhāṣya shaped all later Sanskrit grammar and influenced Indian philosophy of language for two millennia. It was commented upon by scholars such as Bhartrhari, Kaiyaṭa and Nāgeśa Bhaṭṭa.
