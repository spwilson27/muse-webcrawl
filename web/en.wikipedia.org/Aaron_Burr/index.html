Aaron Burr

Aaron Burr Jr.
Born: 6 February 1756, Newark, New Jersey
Died: 14 September 1836, Staten Island, New York
3rd Vice President of the United States (1801–1805)
U.S. Senator from New York (1791–1797)

Aaron Burr Jr. (February 6, 1756 – September 14, 1836) was an American politician, businessman and lawyer who served as the third vice president of the United States from 1801 to 1805 during Thomas Jefferson's first term. A Founding Father, Burr shot and killed Alexander Hamilton, his political rival, in their famous duel at Weehawken, New Jersey, in 1804.

Early life and career

Born in Newark into a prominent family — his father was president of Princeton and his grandfather was the theologian Jonathan Edwards — Burr studied law and served as an officer in the Continental Army during the Revolutionary War, fighting at Quebec, Monmouth and elsewhere. After the war he became a leading New York lawyer and politician, serving in the state assembly and as state attorney general before defeating Philip Schuyler, Hamilton's father-in-law, for a seat in the U.S. Senate in 1791.

Vice presidency and the duel

In the election of 1800, Burr and Jefferson tied in the Electoral College, throwing the contest into the House of Representatives; Jefferson was chosen president and Burr vice president, an outcome that prompted the Twelfth Amendment. Dropped from the Democratic-Republican ticket in 1804, Burr ran for governor of New York and lost, blaming Hamilton's opposition. After Hamilton reportedly insulted him at a dinner, Burr challenged him to a duel. On 11 July 1804 at Weehawken, Burr fatally wounded Hamilton, who died the next day. Burr was indicted for murder in two states but never tried.

Treason trial and later life

After his term, Burr travelled west and became involved in a scheme — its exact aims remain disputed — that the Jefferson administration prosecuted as treason. Tried in Richmond in 1807 before Chief Justice John Marshall, Burr was acquitted under the Constitution's narrow definition of treason. His reputation destroyed, he spent several years in Europe before returning to New York to practise law. He died in 1836.
