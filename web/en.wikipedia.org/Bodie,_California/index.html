Bodie is a ghost town in the Bodie Hills east of the Sierra Nevada mountain range in Mono County, California, United States. Located about 75 miles (121 km) southeast of Lake Tahoe, the town was founded as a mining camp following the discovery of gold in 1859 and grew into a boomtown in the late 1870s. Today it is preserved as Bodie State Historic Park, maintained in a condition of "arrested decay," with the interiors of its remaining buildings left as they were when the town was abandoned.

History

Gold was discovered in the area in 1859 by Waterman S. Body (also spelled Bodey), after whom the town is named. A mill was established in 1861, but the district remained small until 1876, when the Standard Company discovered a rich body of gold-bearing ore. Within a few years Bodie's population surged to an estimated 7,000–10,000 residents, with some 2,000 buildings, including 65 saloons along its mile-long main street.

Bodie gained a reputation as one of the wildest towns of the American West, with frequent shootings, robberies, and stage holdups. A famous (if possibly apocryphal) diary entry attributed to a young girl moving to the town read: "Goodbye God, I'm going to Bodie."

Decline

The mines produced over $34 million in gold and silver, but yields fell sharply after the early 1880s. Fires in 1892 and 1932 destroyed much of the town. The last mine closed in 1942 under wartime orders, and the post office closed the same year. The town was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1961 and became a California State Historic Park in 1962.

Climate

Bodie sits at an elevation of 8,379 feet (2,554 m) and has a subarctic-influenced highland climate, with long, snowy winters and short, cool summers. It is frequently cited among the coldest inhabited or formerly inhabited places in the contiguous United States.
