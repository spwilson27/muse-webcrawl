Satyr play

The satyr play was a genre of ancient Greek drama, a burlesque form performed after a trilogy of tragedies at the festival of Dionysus in Athens. Each competing tragedian presented three tragedies followed by one satyr play, whose chorus consisted of satyrs — half-human, half-animal followers of Dionysus — led by their father Silenus.

Red-figure vase showing costumed satyr-play performers

Characteristics

Satyr plays took myths as their subjects but treated them with earthy humour, drunkenness and sexual innuendo, offering comic relief after the weight of tragedy while retaining a serious undertone. The costume included a horse's tail and ears and an exaggerated phallus.

Surviving examples

The only complete surviving satyr play is Cyclops by Euripides, based on Odysseus's encounter with Polyphemus. Substantial fragments survive of Sophocles' Ichneutae (The Trackers), recovered from papyri at Oxyrhynchus, in which the satyrs hunt for Apollo's stolen cattle and discover the newly invented lyre.
