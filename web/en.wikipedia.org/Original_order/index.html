Original order

A set in its natural, or "alphabetical", arrangement — the prototype for the abstract notion of an original order.

In mathematics, the original order of a set is the order in which its elements are first given or naturally arranged before any re-ordering is applied. The term is used in combinatorics, algebra, and the theory of ordered sets to distinguish the given ordering of elements from orders induced by permutations, sorts, or algebraic operations.

Usage

When a permutation acts on a set, the image of the set is said to be restored to its original order if the permutation is the identity. In the study of permutations as products of transpositions and in sorting algorithms, "original order" refers to the initial sequence fed to the algorithm. In free groups and free monoids, words are strings whose letters appear in an original order that is changed by the defining relations.

In permutation pattern theory

The concept also appears when comparing a sequence with the relative order of its entries: the order-isomorphism type of a sequence records how its elements rank against one another, abstracting away their original values while preserving their original positions.
