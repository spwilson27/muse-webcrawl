Jay DeFeo (March 31, 1929 – November 11, 1989) was an American visual artist who became celebrated for her monumental painting The Rose. A central figure in the San Francisco art community of the 1950s, she was associated with the Beat generation.

The Rose

DeFeo worked on The Rose from 1958 to 1966 in her Fillmore Street studio. Built up with oil paint mixed with wood and mica to an extraordinary thickness — in places nearly eleven inches — the radial, sculptural canvas eventually weighed more than a ton and measured about 11 by 8 feet. When DeFeo was evicted in 1965, the painting was removed by forklift through a cut-out wall, an event filmed by Bruce Conner (The White Rose, 1967). Unfinished works and financial strain forced DeFeo into a period of relative obscurity and dental problems caused in part by her materials; The Rose itself was hidden behind a wall at the San Francisco Art Institute for decades before conservation and acquisition by the Whitney Museum of American Art, where it is now a centerpiece.

Later career

DeFeo returned to painting and photography in the 1970s and taught at Mills College from 1980 until her death from lung cancer in 1989. A major retrospective at the Whitney in 2013 re-established her reputation as a major figure of postwar American art.
