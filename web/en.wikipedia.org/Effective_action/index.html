Effective action

In quantum field theory, the effective action is a modified expression for the action, which takes quantum corrections into account. It is the quantum analogue of the classical action functional, in the sense that the (quantum-corrected) equations of motion of the theory can be derived from it by extremisation. Introduced by Julian Schwinger and developed extensively by others, the effective action is usually denoted Γ and is defined as the Legendre transform of the logarithm of the partition function in the presence of sources.

Definition

Given a theory with fields φ and a source J coupled to them, one defines the generating functional W[J] = −i ln Z[J], where Z is the path integral. The classical field is the expectation value ϕ = δW/δJ, and the effective action is the Legendre transformation

Γ[ϕ] = W[J] − ∫ d⁴x J(x) ϕ(x).

The quantum equations of motion follow from δΓ/δϕ = −J, so that when the sources are switched off, the vacuum expectation value of the field is an extremum of Γ.

Loop expansion

The effective action can be computed in a loop (equivalently ħ) expansion. At zeroth order it equals the classical action. The one-loop contribution is given by a functional determinant — a (super)trace of the logarithm of the fluctuation operator — and higher-loop corrections are given by one-particle-irreducible (1PI) Feynman diagrams with the full propagators and vertices. For this reason Γ is also called the 1PI effective action (the quantum action principle).

Effective potential

For constant field configurations, the effective action is proportional to the spacetime volume, Γ[ϕ] = −VT · Veff(ϕ), defining the effective potential. The effective potential sums all quantum corrections to the classical potential; its minima determine the vacuum of the theory, and it is the central tool in analysing spontaneous symmetry breaking, as in the Coleman–Weinberg mechanism.

Background field method

The background field method computes the effective action while preserving manifest gauge invariance by splitting the field into a classical background and a quantum fluctuation. Related notions include the Vilkovisky–DeWitt effective action, which is independent of the gauge-fixing and parametrisation choices, and the effective average action of the functional renormalisation group, which interpolates between the bare action and the full effective action as a momentum scale is varied.
