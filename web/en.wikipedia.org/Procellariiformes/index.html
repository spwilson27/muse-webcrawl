Procellariiformes is an order of seabirds that comprises four families: the albatrosses, the petrels and shearwaters, and two families of storm petrels. Formerly called Tubinares and still called tubenoses in English, procellariiforms are often referred to collectively as the petrels, a term that has been applied to all members of the order, or more commonly all the families except the albatrosses. They are almost exclusively pelagic (feeding in the open ocean) and have a cosmopolitan distribution across the world's oceans, with the highest diversity being around New Zealand.

Characteristics

Procellariiforms are distinguished by their tubular external nostrils, which give the group the name "tubenoses" and are associated with an acute sense of smell unusual among birds, used to locate patchily distributed prey at sea and to find their burrow colonies at night. They have salt glands that excrete saline solution to remove excess salt from the bloodstream. The order includes the wandering albatross, which has the largest wingspan of any living bird, up to about 3.5 metres, and tiny storm petrels weighing as little as 20 grams.

All species are long-lived, lay a single egg per breeding attempt, and many undertake immense migrations: the sooty shearwater migrates between New Zealand and the North Pacific, tracing a figure-of-eight of some 64,000 kilometres, among the longest animal migrations known.

Conservation

Many procellariiforms are threatened by introduced predators at their island breeding colonies, by-catch in longline and trawl fisheries, plastic ingestion and light pollution that disorients fledglings. The Agreement on the Conservation of Albatrosses and Petrels (ACAP) coordinates international protection efforts.
