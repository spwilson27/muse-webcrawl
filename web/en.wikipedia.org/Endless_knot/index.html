Endless knot

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The endless knot (Sanskrit: śrīvatsa; Tibetan: dpal be'u) is a traditional symbol in many Asian cultures, representing eternity, longevity, harmony and the interweaving of wisdom and method. It is one of the Eight Auspicious Symbols (Ashtamangala) of Buddhism, Hinduism and Jainism, and appears widely in Tibetan, Mongolian, Chinese and Nepalese art, carpets, jewellery, architecture and ritual objects.

Symbolism

Because the knot has no beginning and no end, it symbolises the endless cycle of existence (samsara), the interconnectedness of all phenomena, and the union of wisdom and method in Buddhist practice. In Tibetan Buddhism it also represents the infinite wisdom of the Buddha and the interdependence of religious doctrine and secular life. On deity statues and thangka paintings the knot is shown as a flowing, symmetrical loop sometimes resembling a stylised heart or ribbon.

History and spread

The motif is ancient: related knotwork appears in Indian art of the early centuries CE, and the śrīvatsa mark is found on images of Vishnu and of the Jain tirthankaras. From India the symbol spread with Buddhism along the Silk Road into Tibet, China and Mongolia. In China the endless knot became associated with longevity and good fortune and was woven into textiles and embroidered badges. Celtic and Norse knotwork, though visually similar, developed as a separate tradition.

Modern use

The endless knot remains a common decorative and religious motif in the Himalayan region and is used as an emblem by Buddhist organisations worldwide. It also appears — in a quite separate development — as a topological diagram in mathematics and as a decorative element in modern graphic design.
