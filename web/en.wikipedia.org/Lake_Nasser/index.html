Lake Nasser (Arabic: بحيرة ناصر‎) is a vast reservoir created by the construction of the Aswan High Dam across the Nile River in southern Egypt. It is one of the largest artificial lakes in the world by surface area and storage capacity, extending about 479 kilometres from Aswan in Egypt southward into Sudan, where the Sudanese portion is known as Lake Nubia.

History

The lake began filling after the completion of the Aswan High Dam in 1970 and reached capacity in the late 1970s. It is named after Gamal Abdel Nasser, president of Egypt during the dam's construction. Filling the reservoir submerged a large part of Lower Nubia, displacing more than 100,000 Nubian people in Egypt and Sudan and threatening numerous ancient monuments. A famous UNESCO campaign relocated threatened temples, most notably Abu Simbel and Philae, to higher ground before the waters rose.

Significance

The reservoir stores several years' worth of Nile flow, buffering Egypt against drought years upstream and against exceptionally high floods. Water from the lake supports perennial irrigation, hydroelectric generation at the dam, and a major fishery — dominated by Nile perch and tilapia — that produces tens of thousands of tonnes of fish annually. Drawbacks include very high evaporation losses (on the order of 10–16 km³ per year from the lake's hot desert surface), siltation, and the loss of the annual silt-laden flood that once fertilized Egyptian farmland.
