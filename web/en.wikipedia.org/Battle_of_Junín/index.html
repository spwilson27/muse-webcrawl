Battle of Junín

Battle of Junín
Date: 6 August 1824
Location: Junín, Peru
Result: Patriot victory
Part of the Peruvian War of Independence

The Battle of Junín was fought on 6 August 1824 on the high plain of Junín in the Peruvian Andes between the patriot army of Simón Bolívar and the Spanish royalist army of José de Canterac. A cavalry battle fought almost entirely with lance and sabre — famously, it is said, without a shot fired — it was the penultimate major engagement of the Spanish American wars of independence in South America.

The battle

Bolívar's United Liberating Army, campaigning in the sierra to destroy the royalist Army of the North, encountered Canterac's cavalry on the pampa beside Lake Junín at over 4,000 metres altitude. The royalist horsemen initially drove back the patriot squadrons, but a squadron under Colonel Manuel Isidoro Suárez, held in reserve near a ravine, charged the royalist flank at a critical moment on Bolívar's orders relayed by Major José Andrés Rázuri (who famously altered the order from retreat to charge). The royalists were routed in about three-quarters of an hour, losing some 250 killed and many prisoners, while patriot losses were much lighter.

Aftermath

Canterac retreated south toward Cuzco, and the moral effect of Junín was great: four months later Antonio José de Sucre destroyed the last main royalist army at the Battle of Ayacucho. Junín is commemorated by an obelisk on the battlefield, a Peruvian national sanctuary, and by the Peruvian cavalry regiment named for Rázuri's charge.
