Récupération

Récupération is a French term used in sociology and political theory, and also the name of an art movement and a style of cycling. In its best-known theoretical sense, developed by the Situationist International, récupération refers to the process by which radical ideas, symbols, and movements are absorbed, neutralised, and commodified by the dominant culture and the capitalist spectacle.

Situationist concept

The Situationists, including Guy Debord, described récupération as the counterpart of détournement: while détournement hijacks elements of the spectacle for subversive ends, récupération is the spectacle's own hijacking of critique. Revolutionary slogans become advertising copy, and rebellion becomes a marketable style. Examples often cited include the sale of Che Guevara imagery as fashion merchandise and the conversion of punk aesthetics into mainstream products.

Art movement

In French art contexts, Récupération also denotes a tendency, related to Arte Povera and assemblage, in which artists create works from discarded, salvaged, or recycled materials — "recovering" waste objects and giving them new aesthetic value.

Cycling

In French cycling terminology, a récupération is an easy recovery ride or rest period undertaken between intense efforts, allowing the body to recuperate.
