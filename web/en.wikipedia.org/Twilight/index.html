Twilight

Twilight
Cause: Sun below horizon, atmosphere scattering sunlight
Phases: civil, nautical, astronomical
Order at dusk: sunset, civil, nautical, astronomical twilight, night

Twilight is the illumination of Earth's lower atmosphere when the Sun is below the horizon and not directly visible, but its light is scattered by the upper atmosphere and reflected to the surface. It occurs both before sunrise (dawn twilight) and after sunset (dusk twilight).

Definitions

Three phases are defined by the Sun's angular depression below the horizon. Civil twilight spans 0° to 6°, during which there is generally enough light for ordinary outdoor activities. Nautical twilight spans 6° to 12°, when sailors can still take star sightings against a visible horizon. Astronomical twilight spans 12° to 18°; beyond 18° the sky is fully dark for astronomical observation.

Duration

Twilight lasts longest near the poles — where it can persist for weeks around the solstices — and is shortest at the equator, where the Sun crosses the horizon nearly vertically.
