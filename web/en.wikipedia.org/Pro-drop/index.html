Pro-drop language

A pro-drop language (from "pronoun-dropping") is a language in which certain classes of pronouns may be omitted when they are pragmatically or syntactically inferable. The phenomenon of a null pronoun is called null-subject when it occurs with subjects; thus the languages in question are also called null-subject languages.

Examples

Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, and Greek are classic pro-drop languages with rich verbal agreement: Spanish hablo ("[I] speak") needs no overt subject. Japanese, Korean, and Chinese permit dropping both subjects and objects, licensed by discourse rather than agreement. English allows limited dropping in informal contexts ("[It] seems..."), and requires overt subjects in nearly all finite clauses.

Theoretical analysis

In principles and parameters theory, pro-drop was modelled by the null-subject parameter. Later research distinguishes consistent null-subject languages, partial ones (such as Finnish and Hebrew), and discourse pro-drop languages, refining the original binary picture.
