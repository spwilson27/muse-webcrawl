Arusha is a city in northern Tanzania, the capital of the Arusha Region, with a population of roughly 416,000 within the city proper and over two million in the wider metropolitan area. Located below Mount Meru on the eastern edge of the eastern branch of the Great Rift Valley, Arusha has a temperate climate despite its proximity to the equator. The city is a major international diplomatic hub and a gateway for safari tourism to northern Tanzania's national parks.

History

The area was first settled by agricultural communities, and the modern town was founded by German colonists in the 1900s after they established a military fort. Arusha grew as an administrative and trading centre during the British mandate period that followed the First World War. After Tanzanian independence in 1961, the city gained prominence as the site of the 1967 Arusha Declaration, President Julius Nyerere's landmark statement of African socialist policy.

In 1994 Arusha became the seat of the International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda (ICTR), established by the United Nations to prosecute those responsible for the Rwandan genocide. The city also hosts the headquarters of the East African Community and the African Court on Human and Peoples' Rights, reinforcing its reputation as a diplomatic centre.

Geography and climate

Arusha lies at an elevation of about 1,400 metres (4,600 ft) at the foot of Mount Meru, an active stratovolcano and Tanzania's second-highest peak. The city experiences two rainy seasons, with temperatures moderated by altitude, generally ranging between 13 and 30 °C (55–86 °F).

Economy

Tourism is a major industry: Arusha is the usual starting point for safaris to the Serengeti, Ngorongoro Crater, Lake Manyara and Tarangire national parks, and for climbs of nearby Mount Kilimanjaro. The surrounding region is also an important agricultural area, producing coffee, flowers for export, wheat and maize. The city is known for trading in tanzanite, a gemstone found only in Tanzania.
