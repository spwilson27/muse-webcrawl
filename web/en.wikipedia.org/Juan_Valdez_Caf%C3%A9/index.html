Juan Valdez Café is a Colombian multinational coffeehouse chain based in Bogotá. It is owned by Procafecol S.A., a company created by the National Federation of Coffee Growers of Colombia (Federación Nacional de Cafeteros, or FNC), and was established to capture more of the value of Colombian coffee for the country's coffee-growing families by selling coffee directly to consumers rather than only as a green commodity.

History

The first Juan Valdez Café opened in 2002 at El Dorado International Airport in Bogotá. The chain takes its name from Juan Valdez, the fictional coffee farmer created in 1958 by the advertising agency Doyle Dane Bernbach to represent Colombian coffee growers; the character, usually shown with his mule Conchita, became one of the most recognized advertising figures in the world. The cafés sell premium Colombian coffees, including single-origin beans from regions such as Huila, Antioquia, Santander and the Sierra Nevada, as well as coffee-derived products and merchandise.

Expansion

From its Colombian base the chain expanded internationally, opening stores in the United States, Spain, Mexico, Chile, Ecuador, Peru, Panama and other countries in the Americas, Europe and the Middle East, often through franchise agreements. By the 2020s it operated several hundred stores. The brand also sells packaged coffee through supermarkets and online channels, positioning itself as a premium competitor in a market dominated internationally by chains such as Starbucks.
