The Pahlavi dynasty was the last Iranian royal dynasty, ruling the Imperial State of Iran from 1925 until the Iranian Revolution of 1979 overthrew the monarchy and established the Islamic Republic. The dynasty was founded by Reza Shah Pahlavi, a former brigadier-general of the Persian Cossack Brigade, who deposed Ahmad Shah Qajar, the last ruler of the Qajar dynasty.

Reza Shah (1925–1941)

Reza Shah undertook an ambitious programme of modernisation: building railways and roads, founding the University of Tehran, secularising the legal and educational systems, and promoting Persian nationalism. During the Second World War, the Anglo-Soviet invasion of Iran forced his abdication in 1941 in favour of his son.

Mohammad Reza Shah (1941–1979)

Mohammad Reza Shah ruled for nearly four decades. His reign saw the nationalisation crisis under Prime Minister Mohammad Mosaddegh and the 1953 coup that restored his authority; the White Revolution of land reform and modernisation; rapid oil-fuelled economic growth; and growing authoritarianism and unrest. He fled Iran in January 1979, and the monarchy was abolished weeks later.
