Negev

The Negev (Hebrew: הַנֶּגֶב, ha-Négev) is a desert and semidesert region of southern Israel. It covers more than half of the country's land area — about 13,000 km² — forming an inverted triangle whose western side borders the Gaza Strip and the Sinai, and whose eastern limit is the Arabah valley.

Geography

The region is rocky and dusty, cut by wadis and by large erosion craters called makhteshim, unique to the Negev and the Sinai. The largest town is Beersheba, the "capital of the Negev," with other towns including Eilat on the Red Sea, Dimona, Arad, Mitzpe Ramon, and the Bedouin city of Rahat.

History and development

The Negev was home to nomadic peoples for millennia; the Nabataeans built incense-route cities such as Avdat, now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. David Ben-Gurion championed settlement of the desert — he retired to Kibbutz Sde Boker — and the region hosts Ben-Gurion University, research institutes in desert agriculture and solar energy, and Israel's Nevatim airbase. Some 25% of the Negev's inhabitants are Bedouin Arabs, many living in unrecognized villages, a long-running source of political dispute.
