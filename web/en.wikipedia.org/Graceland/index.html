Graceland

Graceland is a mansion on a 13.8-acre estate in Memphis, Tennessee, United States, once owned by singer Elvis Presley. It serves as a museum and is among the most visited private homes in America, attracting hundreds of thousands of visitors annually. Presley is buried there, as are his parents, paternal grandmother, grandson, and daughter Lisa Marie Presley.

Location: 3764 Elvis Presley Boulevard, Memphis, Tennessee

Built: 1939

Style: Colonial Revival

Owner: Elvis Presley Enterprises

Designated: National Historic Landmark (2006)

History

The Colonial Revival house was built in 1939 for Ruth Brown Moore, and the estate was named after her great-aunt Grace Toof. Elvis Presley purchased the property in 1957 for about $102,500 at the age of 22, during the filming of Jailhouse Rock. He lived there until his death in 1977, extensively remodelling it — adding the famous music-note gates, a racquetball building, the Jungle Room with its waterfall, and the Meditation Garden where he was buried (after his remains were moved from Forest Hill Cemetery following an attempted theft).

Museum

Priscilla Presley opened Graceland to public tours in 1982, saving the estate from financial difficulty. The tour includes the living room, music room, kitchen, TV room, billiard room, and Jungle Room on the ground floor (the upstairs remains private), plus trophy buildings with gold records, the car museum with the famous pink Cadillac, and Elvis's private jets including the Lisa Marie. In 2017 a large entertainment complex, Elvis Presley's Memphis, opened across the street. Graceland was declared a National Historic Landmark in 2006 — the first rock-and-roll site so designated.
