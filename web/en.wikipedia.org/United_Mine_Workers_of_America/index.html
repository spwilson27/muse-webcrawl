United Mine Workers of America

The United Mine Workers of America (UMWA) is a North American labor union best known for representing coal miners and, in recent decades, workers in other industries and public employment. Founded on January 25, 1890, in Columbus, Ohio, the UMWA was for much of the twentieth century among the most powerful unions in the United States, and its struggles in the Appalachian coalfields became defining episodes in American labor history.

Founding and growth

The UMWA was created by merging the Knights of Labor's miners' assembly with the National Progressive Miners Union. From the start it was unusual among American unions in being an industrial union that organized all workers in and around the mines regardless of skill, and it professed racial inclusiveness unusual for the era. The successful bituminous strike of 1897 and the 1902 anthracite strike — settled after President Theodore Roosevelt threatened to seize the mines and appointed an arbitration commission — established the union nationally.

Coalfield wars and organizing

Attempts to organize southern West Virginia, Kentucky, and other non-union fields produced prolonged violence: the Paint Creek–Cabin Creek strike (1912–13), the Colorado Coalfield War culminating in the Ludlow Massacre (1914), the Matewan Massacre (1920), and the Battle of Blair Mountain (1921), in which some 10,000 armed miners fought deputies and strikebreakers. Federal recognition under the National Industrial Recovery Act (1933) and the Wagner Act let the union organize most of the industry rapidly in the 1930s.

Postwar era

Under John L. Lewis the union won the Krug–Lewis agreement of 1946, creating a welfare and retirement fund financed by royalties per ton of coal, a model of negotiated social welfare. Later decades brought the black lung movement, the 1968 Farmington mine disaster and resulting safety legislation, the 1969 Yablonski murders and the Miners for Democracy reform movement, and the victorious 110-day strike of 1977–78. The union's ranks thinned with mechanization and the decline of coal, from a peak of several hundred thousand members to a fraction of that today; the UMWA has since organized casino dealers, health-care workers, and others while campaigning to preserve miners' pensions and health benefits, including through the American Miners Act of 2019.

Structure

The union is organized into geographic districts and local unions, governed by an international executive board, and holds constitutional conventions. Since 1989 its headquarters have been in Triangle, Virginia, near the Marine Corps base at Quantico; before that it was long headquartered in Washington, D.C.
