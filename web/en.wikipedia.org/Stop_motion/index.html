Stop motion

TypeAnimation technique
VariantsClaymation, puppet animation, object animation, pixilation

Stop motion (also called stop frame animation) is an animation technique in which physical objects are moved in small increments between individually photographed frames, so that they appear to move independently when the series of frames is played back as a continuous sequence.

Techniques

Common forms include puppet animation using jointed figures, clay animation (claymation) using malleable figures, cutout animation, and pixilation, in which human actors are posed frame by frame. Armatures of metal or wire give puppets stable, repeatable poses.

History

Stop motion was used from the earliest days of cinema, notably by J. Stuart Blackton and Willis O'Brien, whose work on The Lost World (1925) and King Kong (1933) defined special-effects animation. Ray Harryhausen's "Dynamation" carried the technique through the mid-twentieth century, and studios such as Aardman Animations and Laika have sustained it in features including Wallace and Gromit and Coraline.
