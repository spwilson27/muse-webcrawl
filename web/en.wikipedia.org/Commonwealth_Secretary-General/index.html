Commonwealth Secretary-General

Commonwealth Secretary-General
Established: 1965
First holder: Arnold Smith (Canada)
Seat: Marlborough House, London
Term: four years, renewable once

The Commonwealth secretary-general is the chief executive of the Commonwealth Secretariat, the intergovernmental agency serving the Commonwealth of Nations. The secretary-general is elected by heads of government at the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting (CHOGM).

Role

The officeholder directs the Secretariat's work in development, democracy and peace, represents the Commonwealth publicly, and exercises "good offices" in disputes between member states. The secretary-general also oversees election observation missions across the Commonwealth.

History

The post was created in 1965 when the Secretariat itself was established, ending the practice of administering Commonwealth affairs through British government departments. Arnold Smith of Canada was the first holder. Successors have included Shridath Ramphal (Guyana), Emeka Anyaoku (Nigeria), Don McKinnon (New Zealand) and Kamalesh Sharma (India).

Election

Candidates are nominated by member governments and elected at CHOGM, conventionally rotating among regions. The term is four years, renewable once.
