Bosniaks

The Bosniaks are a South Slavic ethnic group native to the historical region of Bosnia, in present-day Bosnia and Herzegovina. They are predominantly Muslim, and number around two million in Bosnia and Herzegovina, with significant diaspora communities worldwide.

History

Bosniaks descend largely from the Slavic population of medieval Bosnia, which had its own kingdom and church. Following the Ottoman conquest in the 15th and 16th centuries, much of the population converted to Islam, distinguishing Bosniaks from their Orthodox Serb and Catholic Croat neighbors. During the Yugoslav era, Bosnian Muslims were recognized as a constituent nation (under the name "Muslims" as an ethnic designation); the name "Bosniak" was officially adopted at a congress in Sarajevo in 1993, during the Bosnian War.

Language and culture

Bosniaks speak Bosnian, a standardized variety of the Serbo-Croatian language continuum written in the Latin alphabet. Their culture blends Slavic, Ottoman, and European influences, visible in cuisine (such as ćevapi and burek), music (sevdalinka), and architecture. Under the Dayton Agreement, Bosniaks are one of the three constituent peoples of Bosnia and Herzegovina.
