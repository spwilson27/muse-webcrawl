Tyre (Arabic: صور, Ṣūr; Phoenician: 𐤑𐤓, Ṣūr; Greek: Τύρος, Týros) is a city in Lebanon, one of the oldest continually inhabited cities in the world, though in medieval times for some centuries it had only a tiny population. It was one of the most important Phoenician city-states and a major maritime trading power.

History

Tyre traded throughout the Mediterranean; its purple dye, extracted from murex snails, gave rise to the name "Tyrian purple" and became a symbol of royalty. Tyrian merchants founded colonies including Carthage and Cádiz. The island city famously withstood a 13-year siege by Nebuchadnezzar II and fell to Alexander the Great in 332 BC, after his army built a causeway to the island that permanently joined it to the mainland as a peninsula. Under Rome it prospered, and its Roman hippodrome and triumphal arch survive today.

Modern city

The modern city of Sur lies on the same site in southern Lebanon. The archaeological site of Tyre was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1984. The city was heavily affected by the Lebanese Civil War and subsequent conflicts in the region.
