DTS-HD Master Audio (DTS-HD MA) is a multi-channel, lossless audio codec developed by DTS as an extension of the company's earlier DTS Coherent Acoustics technology. It is one of the optional audio formats specified for the Blu-ray Disc format, alongside Dolby TrueHD and uncompressed LPCM, and was also supported on the short-lived HD DVD format. DTS-HD Master Audio is capable of delivering bit-for-bit identical audio to the original studio master recording at resolutions and channel counts far beyond those of standard-definition media.

History

DTS introduced the DTS-HD family of codecs in 2004 as part of the specification work for the next-generation optical disc formats. The original lossy DTS Coherent Acoustics codec, widely used on DVD-Video and in digital cinema, was limited to a maximum bitrate of 1,509.75 kbit/s and 5.1 channels at 48 kHz/24-bit. To compete for the high-definition formats, DTS designed an extension architecture in which a conventional lossy "core" stream is supplemented by extension data that allows a compliant decoder to reconstruct the original lossless signal.

DTS-HD Master Audio was selected as an optional codec in the Blu-ray Disc specification and a mandatory-supported optional codec on HD DVD players. With the victory of Blu-ray in the format war of 2006–2008, DTS-HD Master Audio became the most widely used lossless surround format on commercial movie releases, appearing on the majority of studio Blu-ray titles. It was later joined in the object-based audio era by DTS:X, which builds on the DTS-HD Master Audio bitstream for immersive audio on Ultra HD Blu-ray.

Technical details

DTS-HD Master Audio supports a variable bitrate with a maximum of 24.5 Mbit/s on Blu-ray Disc (and 18 Mbit/s on HD DVD). It supports the following parameters:

ParameterCapability
ChannelsUp to 7.1 discrete channels in the Blu-ray configuration (more via extension arrangements)
Sample rates48, 96, and 192 kHz
Bit depth16, 20, and 24 bits per sample
Maximum bitrate (Blu-ray)24.5 Mbit/s
Maximum bitrate (HD DVD)18.0 Mbit/s
CompressionLossless (bit-for-bit)

Channel arrangements can include conventional 5.1 and 7.1 layouts as well as alternative speaker configurations defined by the DTS-HD specification. Because the format is lossless, a 7.1-channel, 96 kHz/24-bit soundtrack is reproduced exactly as it left the mixing stage, subject only to any downmixing performed by the playback equipment.

Bitstream structure

A DTS-HD Master Audio stream consists of a core substream and an extension substream. The core is a standard DTS Coherent Acoustics stream (typically encoded at 1,509.75 kbit/s, or 768 kbit/s on some releases) that can be decoded by any legacy DTS decoder, including the decoders built into older AV receivers and the S/PDIF output of a player. The extension substream, known as DTS-HD XLL (eXtension LossLess), carries the residual difference between the lossy core and the original lossless master. A DTS-HD Master Audio decoder combines the two to reconstruct the original signal exactly.

This core-plus-extension design gives the format graceful backward compatibility: a disc carrying only a DTS-HD Master Audio track remains playable, in standard DTS quality, on equipment that predates the high-definition formats.

Applications

DTS-HD Master Audio is used primarily for film soundtracks on Blu-ray Disc and Ultra HD Blu-ray, where it is one of the most common lossless audio options offered by major studios. It is decoded by virtually all Blu-ray players, game consoles with Blu-ray playback, and modern AV receivers, and can also be bitstreamed or decoded by media-player software on personal computers. A related profile, DTS-HD High Resolution Audio, uses the same extension architecture with lossy (rather than lossless) extension data at a maximum bitrate of 6 Mbit/s.
