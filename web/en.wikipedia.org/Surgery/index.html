Surgery

Surgery is a medical specialty that uses manual and instrumental techniques to investigate or treat a pathological condition such as disease or injury, to alter bodily functions, to improve appearance (cosmetic surgery), or to remove/replace damaged areas. The act of performing surgery is called a surgical procedure or operation, and the physician who performs it is a surgeon.

Classification

Surgical procedures are commonly categorised by urgency (elective, urgent, emergency), purpose (diagnostic, therapeutic, reconstructive, cosmetic, transplantation), degree of invasiveness (open versus minimally invasive), and the body system involved. Major subspecialties include general surgery, cardiothoracic surgery, neurosurgery, orthopaedic surgery, ophthalmology, otolaryngology, urology, gynaecology, plastic surgery, vascular surgery, paediatric surgery and trauma surgery.

Principles

Modern surgery rests on aseptic technique, anaesthesia, haemostasis (control of bleeding), careful tissue handling, and postoperative care. The development of anaesthesia in the 1840s and antisepsis under Joseph Lister in the 1860s transformed surgery from a last resort of limited scope into a routine and increasingly safe intervention. Twentieth-century advances — antibiotics, blood transfusion, imaging, and intensive care — broadened its reach further.

Modern developments

Minimally invasive techniques such as laparoscopy and endoscopy reduce trauma and recovery time; robotic-assisted systems allow precise instrument control through small incisions; and image-guided and computer-assisted surgery integrate preoperative imaging with real-time navigation. Ambulatory (day-case) surgery now accounts for a large share of procedures in many health systems.
