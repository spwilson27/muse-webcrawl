Enniskillen

County: Fermanagh

Country: Northern Ireland, United Kingdom

Enniskillen (from Irish Inis Ceithleann, "Ceithleann's island") is the county town of County Fermanagh in Northern Ireland. It is situated on an island in the River Erne between Upper and Lower Lough Erne, near the western edge of Northern Ireland. The town has one of the oldest populations centres in the region and serves as a market and administrative hub for the surrounding lake district.

History

Enniskillen developed around a castle built by the Maguire chieftains in the 15th–16th centuries; Enniskillen Castle, on the riverbank, now houses museums. During the Plantation of Ulster and later conflicts, the town became a garrison settlement, and it gave its name to famous regiments including the Royal Inniskilling Fusiliers — the spelling "Inniskilling" reflecting an older form. In 1689 the town's Williamite defenders played a notable role in the Williamite war in Ireland.

On Remembrance Sunday, 8 November 1987, a Provisional IRA bomb exploded at the town's war memorial during the remembrance ceremony, killing 11 people (a twelfth victim died later) and injuring many more — one of the most notorious atrocities of the Troubles. A permanent memorial now stands at the site.

Geography and tourism

The town is a centre for exploring Lough Erne, with boating, fishing, and nearby attractions such as the Marble Arch Caves, Florence Court, and Castle Coole. In 2013 the nearby Lough Erne Resort hosted the 39th G8 summit.
