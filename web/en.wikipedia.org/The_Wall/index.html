The Wall

The Wall

Artist: Pink Floyd

Released: 30 November 1979

Genre: Progressive rock, rock opera

Producer: Bob Ezrin, David Gilmour, Roger Waters

The Wall is the eleventh studio album by the English rock band Pink Floyd, released in 1979 as a double album. A rock opera conceived largely by bassist and lyricist Roger Waters, it follows the character Pink, a rock star who builds a metaphorical and physical wall of isolation around himself from childhood traumas — the death of his father in war, an overprotective mother, abusive schooling, and failed relationships.

Music and story

Each event becomes "another brick in the wall"; the song "Another Brick in the Wall, Part 2", with its schoolchildren's chorus, became the band's only number-one single in both the UK and US. The narrative culminates in a fascist hallucination and a trial in which the wall is torn down. Other notable tracks include "Comfortably Numb", "Hey You" and "Run Like Hell".

Legacy

The album topped charts worldwide and is one of the best-selling albums of all time. It was adapted into the 1982 film Pink Floyd – The Wall, starring Bob Geldof, and staged as a lavish live tour in 1980–81; Waters revived it as a worldwide solo tour in 2010–2013.
