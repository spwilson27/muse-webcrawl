Joseph Goldberger

Joseph Goldberger
Born: 16 July 1874, Girált, Austria-Hungary
Died: 17 January 1929, Washington, D.C.
Known for: Identifying the dietary cause of pellagra

Joseph Goldberger (16 July 1874 – 17 January 1929) was an American physician, epidemiologist and Public Health Service officer known for his advocacy of scientific and social recognition of the links between poverty, diet and disease, especially pellagra.

Pellagra investigations

Assigned in 1914 to study pellagra, then epidemic in the American South, Goldberger showed through orphanage and prison dietary experiments and "filth party" self-experiments that the disease was caused by a deficient diet (later identified as niacin deficiency), not by infection or heredity.

Opposition and legacy

His conclusions implied criticism of Southern economic conditions and were resisted for years. Pellagra was largely eliminated after niacin was identified in 1937 and flour enrichment followed. Goldberger was nominated several times for the Nobel Prize.
