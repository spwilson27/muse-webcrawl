Room 40

Room 40 was the British Admiralty's signals-intelligence section during the First World War, formally designated NID25. Based in Room 40 of the Old Admiralty Building in Whitehall, London, it decrypted German naval codes and produced intelligence that shaped the naval war, including the handling of the Zimmermann Telegram.

Work

Under the direction of Captain (later Admiral) Reginald "Blinker" Hall, Director of Naval Intelligence, and with codebreakers including Alfred Dillwyn Knox and Nigel de Grey, Room 40 exploited captured German codebooks — notably the Signalbuch der Kaiserlichen Marine recovered from the wreck of SMS Magdeburg in 1914 — to read much German naval traffic. Its decrypts informed the Admiralty before the Battle of Jutland (1916), though operational failures limited their effect.

Zimmermann Telegram and legacy

In January 1917 Room 40 decrypted the Zimmermann Telegram, Germany's proposal of a Mexican alliance against the United States; its careful release helped bring America into the war. After the war Room 40 merged with the War Office's MI1b to form the Government Code and Cypher School at Bletchley Park. See also signals intelligence.
