Negishi coupling

The Negishi coupling is a widely employed transition-metal-catalyzed cross-coupling reaction in organic chemistry. The reaction couples organozinc compounds with organic halides or triflates in the presence of a nickel or palladium catalyst to form new carbon–carbon bonds.

History

The reaction is named after Ei-ichi Negishi, who reported the first palladium- and nickel-catalyzed cross-coupling of organozinc reagents in 1977. Negishi shared the 2010 Nobel Prize in Chemistry with Richard Heck and Akira Suzuki for palladium-catalyzed cross-couplings in organic synthesis.

Mechanism

The catalytic cycle is generally understood to proceed through oxidative addition of the organic halide to the zero-valent metal, transmetalation with the organozinc reagent, and reductive elimination, which releases the coupled product and regenerates the catalyst. Organozinc reagents tolerate a wide range of functional groups, including esters, ketones, and nitriles.

Scope and applications

Negishi coupling is used in the synthesis of pharmaceuticals, agrochemicals, and complex natural products. Its mild conditions and high chemoselectivity make it a standard tool in total synthesis, complementing the Suzuki and Stille couplings.
