Satavahana dynasty

The Satavahana dynasty (also called the Andhras in the Puranas) was an ancient Indian dynasty that ruled much of the Deccan and central India roughly from the 1st century BCE to the early 3rd century CE. After the decline of the Mauryan Empire, the Satavahanas became the dominant power in the Deccan, controlling trade routes between northern and southern India.

History

The dynasty's founder is usually identified as Simuka. Its most celebrated ruler, Gautamiputra Satakarni (r. c. 106–130 CE), defeated the Western Kshatrapa ruler Nahapana and restored Satavahana power; his achievements are recorded in his mother's Nashik inscription. Later kings fought repeated wars with the Western Satraps over the lucrative western ports. The last significant ruler was Yajna Sri Satakarni, after whom the empire fragmented among successor states such as the Western Chalukya predecessors, Ikshvakus, and Abhiras.

Culture and economy

The Satavahanas issued abundant coinage — including famous ship-type coins attesting to maritime trade — patronized Buddhism and Hinduism, and sponsored rock-cut cave temples and stupas at sites such as Karle, Nashik, and Amaravati. Their official language was Prakrit; the anthology of Prakrit love poetry Gatha Saptashati is attributed to King Hala. Trade with the Roman world through ports like Bharuch brought wealth attested in Greco-Roman sources such as the Periplus of the Erythraean Sea.
