Potamoi

The Potamoi (Greek: Ποταμοί, "rivers") are the gods of rivers and streams of the earth in Greek mythology. According to Hesiod's Theogony, they are the sons of the Titans Oceanus and Tethys, and brothers of the Oceanids; their number is given as three thousand, too many to name.

Depictions and character

In art the river gods were depicted as elderly bearded men reclining beside their streams, often with a cornucopia and a pouring urn; as bulls with human heads; or as horned, bull-eared men — types reflecting the power and fertility of rivers. Famous individual potamoi include Achelous, the greatest of rivers, who wrestled Heracles for Deianira; Scamander, who fought Achilles in the Iliad; Inachus of Argos; Alpheus, who pursued the nymph Arethusa; and the Nile god Nilus.

Worship

River gods received local cult throughout the Greek world: youths offered locks of hair to the local river on reaching manhood, sacrifices were made before crossings, and rivers were invoked as witnesses to oaths. Great rivers such as the Danube (Ister), Euphrates, and Nile were counted among the most significant of the potamoi.
