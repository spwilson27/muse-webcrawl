Raetic is an extinct language spoken in the eastern Alps in the area of ancient Raetia, in present-day Austria, Switzerland, northern Italy and southern Germany, during the centuries before the Roman conquest. It is attested in about 280 short inscriptions, mostly on votive objects, dating roughly from the 5th to the 1st century BC.

Classification

Raetic is generally grouped with Etruscan and the Lemnian language in the proposed Tyrsenian language family, based on shared morphological features identified by scholars such as Helmut Rix. The inscriptions are written in alphabets derived from Etruscan, read right-to-left, and the corpus was assembled in modern editions such as Schumacher's Die rätischen Inschriften.

History

Roman authors claimed the Raetians were Etruscans driven into the mountains by Gallic invasions. After Rome annexed Raetia in 15 BC, the language was gradually replaced by Latin, from which the modern Rhaeto-Romance languages (Romansh, Ladin, Friulian) descend — despite the similarity of names, these Romance languages are not descendants of Raetic itself.
