Medical error

A medical error is a preventable adverse effect of care ("iatrogenesis"), whether or not it is evident or harmful to the patient. Errors include inaccurate or incomplete diagnosis, errors in the performance of an operation or procedure, administration of the wrong drug or dose, and failures of equipment or communication.

Scope

The 1999 Institute of Medicine report To Err Is Human estimated that between 44,000 and 98,000 people die each year in United States hospitals as a result of preventable medical errors, spurring a worldwide patient safety movement. Later studies have produced widely varying estimates; a much-cited 2016 analysis ranked medical error as the third leading cause of death in the US, though its methodology has been disputed.

Types and causes

Errors may be active — committed at the point of care — or latent, arising from system design, staffing, fatigue, poor labelling, or fragmented records. Common categories include diagnostic error, medication error (prescribing, dispensing or administering), surgical error including wrong-site surgery, healthcare-associated infection, and falls.

Prevention

Patient-safety interventions include checklists such as the WHO Surgical Safety Checklist, computerised physician order entry and barcode medication administration, standardised handovers, incident reporting systems and "just culture" approaches that encourage disclosure without blame. Many jurisdictions now require or encourage disclosure of errors to patients.
