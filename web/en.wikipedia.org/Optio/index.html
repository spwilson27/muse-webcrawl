Optio

The optio stood at the rear of the century to keep the ranks closed.

An optio (Latin for "chosen one"; plural optiones) was a soldier in the Roman army who held a rank equivalent to a modern second-in-command or executive officer of a century. The position was typically held by men being groomed for promotion to centurion.

Duties

The optio was chosen by the centurion to be his deputy. In battle he stood at the back of the formation, carrying a staff (hastile) tipped with a knob, which he used to keep the soldiers dressed in rank and prevent stragglers. In camp his duties included administrative work, supervising training, distributing orders, and taking charge of the century if the centurion was killed or incapacitated.

Pay and status

Optiones were duplicarii — soldiers on double pay — reflecting their status within the legion's hierarchy of principales, junior officers below the centurionate. Specialist optiones also existed, such as the optio valetudinarii in charge of a hospital, the optio carceris in charge of prisoners, and the optio spei, an optio awaiting a vacant centurion's post.

The most common route for an optio was eventual promotion to centurion, the backbone officer rank of the Roman legions.
