Vladimir Alexandrovich Voevodsky (1966–2017) was a Russian-American mathematician. He was awarded the Fields Medal in 2002 for his work in algebraic geometry and algebraic topology, and later became known for founding univalent foundations of mathematics.

Work

Voevodsky developed motivic cohomology and the theory of motivic homotopy, work that led to his proof of the Milnor conjecture relating Milnor K-theory to Galois cohomology, and later, with Markus Rost, the Bloch–Kato conjecture. From 2006 he turned to the foundations of mathematics, developing homotopy type theory and the univalence axiom, and promoting computer formalization of proofs using proof assistants such as Coq.

He studied at Moscow State University, received his PhD from Harvard University in 1992 under David Kazhdan, and from 2002 was a professor at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton.
