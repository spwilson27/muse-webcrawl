Cartoon

MediumDrawing, print, animation
FormsEditorial cartoon, comic strip, gag cartoon, animated cartoon
Earliest use"Cartoon" from Italian cartone (preparatory drawing)

A cartoon is a type of visual art, typically a drawing or animated sequence, that uses exaggeration, symbolism, and simplification for satirical, humorous, or illustrative effect. The word derives from the Italian cartone, meaning a large sheet of paper, which in Renaissance art referred to a full-scale preparatory drawing for a painting, fresco, or tapestry.

History

In the nineteenth century the term shifted to humorous and satirical drawings in magazines and newspapers, a change cemented by the British magazine Punch, founded in 1841. With the rise of cinema in the early twentieth century, "cartoon" also came to mean animated film, from the early shorts of Émile Cohl and Winsor McCay to the studio cartoons of Walt Disney, Warner Bros., and others.

Types

  • Editorial (political) cartoons — single-panel drawings commenting on current events and public figures.
  • Gag cartoons — humorous single panels, as in The New Yorker.
  • Comic strips — sequential panels telling a story or joke, syndicated in newspapers and online.
  • Animated cartoons — drawn or computer-generated moving images, from theatrical shorts to television series and feature films.
  • Satirical prints and caricature — antecedent forms emphasising distorted likeness.

Techniques and style

Cartooning typically relies on economical line work, caricature, visual metaphor, and captions or speech balloons. Animation historically used hand-drawn cels photographed frame by frame; digital tools now dominate both print cartooning and animation production.
