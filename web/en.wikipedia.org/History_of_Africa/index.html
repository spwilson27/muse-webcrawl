History of Africa

The history of Africa begins with the emergence of hominids and anatomically modern humans on the continent and continues through prehistory, ancient civilisations, states and empires, European colonisation, and the independent nations of the present day. Africa is widely regarded as the birthplace of humankind: the earliest fossils of Homo sapiens, dated to about 300,000 years ago, come from Jebel Irhoud in Morocco.

Ancient and medieval Africa

Agriculture spread independently in several regions, and states arose early along the Nile, where ancient Egypt and Nubia flourished for millennia. Ironworking spread widely after about 1000 BCE. In West Africa the Ghana, Mali, and Songhai empires grew rich on trans-Saharan trade in gold and salt; Timbuktu became a renowned centre of learning. In the east, the Swahili city-states traded across the Indian Ocean, Aksum linked Africa to Arabia and Rome, and Great Zimbabwe dominated the southern plateau. Bantu-speaking peoples migrated across central, eastern, and southern Africa over some two thousand years.

Colonisation and independence

From the 15th century, European trade — above all the Atlantic slave trade, which forcibly removed an estimated 12.5 million Africans — reshaped coastal societies. In the late 19th century the "Scramble for Africa" partitioned nearly the entire continent among European powers. Decolonisation after the Second World War, beginning with Ghana's independence in 1957, created the modern map of more than fifty states, whose subsequent history has been marked by nation-building, Cold War proxy conflicts, democratisation movements, and rapid urbanisation.
