Slack is a cloud-based team communication platform developed by Slack Technologies, now owned by Salesforce. It organises conversations into channels devoted to topics, projects or teams, supplemented by direct messages, voice and video "huddles", file sharing and integrations with hundreds of other applications.

History

Slack began as an internal tool built during the development of Glitch, an online game by Stewart Butterfield's company Tiny Speck. When the game shut down in 2012, the team launched the chat tool publicly in August 2013; the name is an acronym for "Searchable Log of All Conversation and Knowledge". It grew extremely rapidly, reaching a valuation of over $1 billion within about a year, and went public via a direct listing on the New York Stock Exchange in 2019 under the ticker WORK.

Acquisition by Salesforce

In December 2020 Salesforce announced it would acquire Slack for approximately $27.7 billion, its largest acquisition to date; the deal closed in July 2021. Slack has since been integrated into Salesforce's customer-relationship platform and has added features including workflow automation and AI-generated summaries.

Features and reception

Slack's channel-based model, searchable archives and app ecosystem positioned it as an "email killer" for internal communication, competing with Microsoft Teams and Discord. It is credited with popularising emoji reactions and channel-based chat in the workplace, while critics have raised concerns about information overload and the blurring of work–life boundaries.
