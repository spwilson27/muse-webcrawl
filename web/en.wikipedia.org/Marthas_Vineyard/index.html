Martha's Vineyard

Martha's Vineyard is an island in the northeastern United States, located south of Cape Cod in Dukes County, Massachusetts. Together with the Elizabeth Islands and Nomans Land, it forms part of the Outer Lands archipelago. The island is about 20 miles (32 km) long and up to 10 miles wide, with a year-round population of roughly 20,000 that swells to well over 100,000 in summer.

Geography and towns

The island comprises six towns: Edgartown, Oak Bluffs, Tisbury (including Vineyard Haven), West Tisbury, Chilmark, and Aquinnah. Edgartown, the county seat, is known for its whaling-era captains' houses and lighthouse; Oak Bluffs grew around a Methodist camp meeting ground with its famous gingerbread cottages; Aquinnah, long home to the Wampanoag Tribe of Gay Head, is famed for its multicolored clay cliffs.

History

The Wampanoag people inhabited the island — Noepe in their language — for thousands of years before English settlement began in 1642 under Thomas Mayhew. Martha's Vineyard was a prosperous whaling center in the 19th century, and later became a renowned summer resort. It was also the center of a famous deaf community: hereditary deafness was so common in Chilmark and West Tisbury that Martha's Vineyard Sign Language was widely used by hearing and deaf islanders alike into the 20th century.

Tourism and culture

Ferries from Woods Hole are the principal link to the mainland. The island is celebrated as a summer colony for presidents and celebrities, for the filming of Jaws (1975) in its harbors, and for events such as the Martha's Vineyard Agricultural Fair. In 1969, the Chappaquiddick incident, involving Senator Edward Kennedy, drew national attention.
