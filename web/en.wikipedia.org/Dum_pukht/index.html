Dum pukht

A sealed cooking vessel (handi) opened at the table

Dum pukht (Persian: دم‌پخت) is a slow-cooking technique associated with Awadhi cuisine of Lucknow, India, in which food is cooked in a heavy-bottomed vessel (handi) sealed with a dough paste, so that the food steams in its own juices and aromatics. The words mean roughly "to breathe" (dum) and "to cook" (pukht).

Origin and legend

Tradition credits the technique to the court of Nawab Asaf-ud-Daula of Awadh in the late eighteenth century. During a famine, the Nawab ordered large pots of rice and meat to be sealed and slow-cooked to feed workers building the Bara Imambara; when a pot was opened, the aroma so impressed him that dum cooking became a hallmark of his court cuisine. The technique has older roots in Persian and Central Asian cooking.

Technique

Ingredients — layered rice and marinated meat for biryani, or meat with spices and yoghurt for kormas — are placed in the handi, the lid is sealed with wheat dough, and the vessel is set over a very low flame or embers for several hours. The seal keeps the steam inside; traditionally the seal is broken only when the dish is served, releasing the aroma. Saffron, kewra water, rose petals, fried onions and whole spices are characteristic flavourings.

Famous dishes

  • Dum biryani (Lucknowi/Awadhi biryani)
  • Dum ka murgh (chicken)
  • Kakori kebab
  • Shami kebab
  • Dum ki subzi (vegetables)
