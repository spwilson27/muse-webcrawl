Stokely Carmichael

Stokely Carmichael (29 June 1941 – 15 November 1998), later Kwame Ture, was a Trinidadian-American activist who became a leading figure in the civil rights movement and the global Pan-African movement. He popularised the slogan "Black Power".

Stokely Carmichael (1941–1998) was a prominent organizer in the American civil rights movement. Born in Port of Spain, Trinidad, he grew up in New York City, graduated from Howard University, and joined the Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee (SNCC), participating in the Freedom Rides and voter registration campaigns in Mississippi and Alabama.

Black Power

Elected chairman of SNCC in 1966, Carmichael popularised the phrase "Black Power" during the Meredith March Against Fear in Mississippi, arguing that Black Americans needed to build independent political and economic power rather than rely on integration into existing institutions. His stance marked a turning point away from the movement's earlier emphasis on nonviolence and interracial cooperation, and brought him into conflict with leaders such as Martin Luther King Jr.

Later years

Carmichael briefly served as honorary prime minister of the Black Panther Party before breaking with it. In 1969 he moved to Guinea, took the name Kwame Ture — honouring Kwame Nkrumah and Sékou Touré — and devoted the rest of his life to Pan-African organizing. He died of prostate cancer in Conakry in 1998.
