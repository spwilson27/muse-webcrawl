George Inness (May 1, 1825 – August 3, 1894) was a prominent American landscape painter, regarded as one of the most influential American artists of the nineteenth century. His mature works are known for their atmospheric, moody depictions of light and weather.

Career

Born in Newburgh, New York, Inness studied briefly with John Jesse Barker and Régis François Gignoux and travelled to Italy and France, where the Barbizon school deeply influenced his style. Early paintings such as The Lackawanna Valley (1855) followed the detailed Hudson River School manner, but his later work grew progressively looser and more expressive.

Style and philosophy

Inness was influenced by the theology of Emanuel Swedenborg, seeking to express spiritual truths through landscape. His late paintings, such as Home of the Heron (1893), dissolve forms into soft tonal harmonies of dusk and mist. He died in Scotland while travelling in 1894.
