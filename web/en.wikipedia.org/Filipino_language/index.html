Filipino (Wikang Filipino, [wɪˈkɐŋ fɪlɪˈpino]) is the national language of the Philippines and, together with English, one of its two official languages. It is the standardized variety of Tagalog, the Austronesian language native to the Tagalog region around Manila, developed and enriched with elements from other Philippine and foreign languages.

History

The 1937 constitution process under President Manuel L. Quezon selected Tagalog as the basis of the national language; the chosen "Wikang Pambansa" was proclaimed in 1939 and renamed Pilipino in 1959. The 1973 and 1987 constitutions adopted the name Filipino, envisioning it as a language continually enriched by all Philippine languages, though in practice it remains primarily standardized Tagalog as spoken in Metro Manila. Filipino is taught throughout the Philippine school system and is a medium of instruction alongside English.

Relationship to Tagalog

Filipino and Tagalog share essentially the same grammar and core vocabulary; linguists commonly treat Filipino as the national, standardized register of Tagalog, while acknowledging its growing stock of loanwords from Spanish, English, and other Philippine languages, and the code-switching ("Taglish") characteristic of urban speech.

Features

Filipino is written in the Latin alphabet with 28 letters. It has a verb-initial, Austronesian alignment ("focus") system marking topics, a rich system of reduplication, and extensive affixation. Native Tagalog words were historically written in the Baybayin script before Spanish colonization displaced it.
