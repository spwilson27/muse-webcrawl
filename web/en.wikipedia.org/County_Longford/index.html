County Longford (Irish: Contae an Longfoirt) is a county in Ireland, in the province of Leinster. It is one of the smallest counties by area and population. The county town is Longford. The River Shannon forms much of its western boundary, and Lough Ree lies on its southwestern edge.

Geography

Much of the county is low-lying farmland in the basin of the River Shannon, with extensive raised bogs and callows. The highest point is Corn Hill (Cairn Hill), at 278 metres, in the north of the county near the Cavan border. The Royal Canal passes through the county and has been restored for navigation.

History

The territory was historically part of the kingdom of Tethbae and later the lordship of the O'Farrell clan, whose name is still common locally. The county was shired in 1569. In 1798 a French-supported rebellion was defeated near Ballinamuck. The county was the birthplace of writer Oliver Goldsmith's inspiration (the village of Lissoy is associated with "Sweet Auburn" of The Deserted Village), and the novelist Maria Edgeworth lived at Edgeworthstown.

Places of interest

Attractions include the Corlea Trackway, a preserved Iron Age bog road; St Mel's Cathedral in Longford town; the Royal Canal Greenway; and Center Parcs Longford Forest near Ballymahon, opened in 2019.
