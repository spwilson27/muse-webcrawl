International Democracy Union

The International Democracy Union (IDU; formerly the International Democrat Union) is an international alliance of centre-right and conservative political parties. Founded in 1983, it brings together parties committed to democracy, individual liberty and market economies. Member parties include the Republican Party of the United States and many conservative and Christian democratic parties around the world; the European People's Party is among its regional groupings.

History

The organisation was founded in London on 24 June 1983, at a joint meeting of the European Democrat Union, the Caribbean Democrat Union and the Pacific Democrat Union. Its founding declaration was signed by nineteen parties, including the Conservative Party of the United Kingdom, the Christian Democratic Union of Germany, the Liberal Democratic Party of Japan, and the United States Republican Party. Among those involved in its founding were British Prime Minister Margaret Thatcher, US Vice President George H. W. Bush, German Chancellor Helmut Kohl and French statesman Jacques Chirac.

In 2023, the organisation changed its name to the International Democracy Union to emphasise its mission of promoting democracy. Its headquarters are in Munich, Germany.

Organisation

The IDU's chairmen have included Finnish politician Esko Aho and, since 2015 in his predecessor role, former Canadian prime minister Stephen Harper, who has chaired the organisation since 2018. Members include full member parties and observers from more than 60 countries. Regional unions affiliated with the IDU include the European People's Party, the Asia Pacific Democrat Union, the Democrat Union of Africa, and the Union of Latin American Parties.

The organisation states its values as commitments to political democracy and civil liberties, freedom of the press and of association, economic freedom and the rule of law, and it campaigns for democratic development in countries where it is threatened or absent.
