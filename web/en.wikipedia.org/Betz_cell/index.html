Betz cell

Betz cells are giant pyramidal neurons located in layer V of the grey matter of the primary motor cortex. They are among the largest neurons in the human central nervous system, with cell bodies up to about 100 micrometres across, and are named after the Ukrainian anatomist Volodymyr Betz, who described them in 1874.

Function

Betz cells send their axons down through the internal capsule, brainstem and corticospinal tract to synapse directly or indirectly on lower motor neurons in the spinal cord, forming part of the upper motor neuron pathway that initiates voluntary movement. Their axons are thick and heavily myelinated, allowing rapid conduction; they contribute particularly to fine, skilled movements of the distal limbs.

Clinical significance

Betz cells and other upper motor neurons degenerate in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) and are lost in stroke affecting the motor cortex. Their distinctive size and clustering make them a landmark in histological studies of the motor cortex, and recent genetic studies have examined their selective vulnerability in motor neuron diseases.
