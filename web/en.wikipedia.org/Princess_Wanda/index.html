Princess Wanda

Princess Wanda is a legendary Polish princess, the daughter of the legendary King Krakus, founder of Kraków. According to the most popular legend, she saved her people from a German invasion by sacrificing herself, drowning in the Vistula River.

Legend

After Krakus died, Wanda became queen of the Poles. A German prince, Rüdiger, sought her hand and threatened war if she refused. Rather than submit, Wanda threw herself into the Vistula. The mound of Wanda (Kopiec Wandy) in Nowa Huta, Kraków, is traditionally said to be her burial mound.

Cultural legacy

The story was first recorded by the chronicler Wincenty Kadłubek around 1200 and has inspired Polish literature, opera, and poetry, including Cyprian Norwid's drama Wanda. Wanda became a symbol of self-sacrifice for the nation; the saying "Wanda didn't want a German" (Wanda Niemca nie chciała) entered Polish folklore.
