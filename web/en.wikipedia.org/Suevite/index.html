Suevite

Suevite is a rock consisting partly of melted material, typically forming a breccia containing fragments of impact glass and crystal or rock fragments, formed during impact events. It is a type of impactite. The name derives from the Nördlinger Ries crater in Bavaria, Germany — "Swabia" in Latin being Suevia — where the rock was first described.

Formation

During a large meteorite impact, shock pressures melt some of the target rock while other material is fragmented and ejected. Suevite forms from the fall-back of this hot mixture of melt droplets, glass bombs, and shocked debris, which settles as a deposit inside and around the crater and may weld or partially cool into a coherent sheet. In the Ries, suevite up to several hundred metres thick overlies the main breccia (Bunte Breccia), and the town of Nördlingen was partly built from suevite stone — St. George's Church notably contains tiny impact diamonds within its suevite blocks.

Occurrences

Suevite-like breccias have been identified at many craters, including Chicxulub in Mexico and Sudbury in Canada (where the related deposit is called the Onaping Formation), and they inform the interpretation of brecciated lunar samples returned by the Apollo missions.
