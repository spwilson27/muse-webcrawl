Veena

ClassificationString instrument, chordophone
OriginIndia
TypesSaraswati veena, Rudra veena, Vichitra veena, Gottuvadhyam
RelatedSitar, Tanpura, Been

The veena is a family of plucked string instruments from India, among the oldest continuously played instruments in the world. The term refers broadly to several lute-type instruments developed in the Indian subcontinent, of which the Saraswati veena, used in Carnatic music, and the rudra veena, used in Hindustani music, are the best known.

History

References to the veena appear in ancient Sanskrit texts, including the Vedas and the Natya Shastra, where it is described as an accompaniment to vocal music. Early depictions of veena-like instruments survive in temple sculpture from the first millennium CE. The goddess Saraswati, patron of learning and the arts, is traditionally depicted playing the veena, and the instrument retains a sacred aura in Indian culture.

Construction

The Saraswati veena has a large resonating body carved from a single block of jackfruit wood, a long fretted neck with 24 brass frets set in wax, four main playing strings, and three drone strings. The rudra veena (been) consists of a long bamboo or wooden tube mounted on two large gourd resonators, with strings stretched along its length.

Playing technique

Players pluck the strings with wire plectra worn on the fingers of the right hand while the left hand presses and pulls the strings against the frets to produce the slides and ornaments characteristic of Indian classical music. The veena is known for its deep, meditative tone and is a central solo instrument in Carnatic concerts.

Notable players

Celebrated veena artists include Veena Dhanammal, S. Balachander, E. Gayathri, and Jayanthi Kumaresh in the Carnatic tradition, and Zia Mohiuddin Dagar and Asad Ali Khan in the rudra veena tradition.
