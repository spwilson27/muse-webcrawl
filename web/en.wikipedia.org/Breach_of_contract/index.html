Breach of contract

Failure to perform a contract gives rise to legal remedies

Breach of contract is a legal cause of action in common law jurisdictions in which a binding agreement or bargained-for exchange is not honoured by one or more of the parties to the contract, by non-performance, late performance, or interference with the other party's performance.

Types of breach

Breaches are classed by severity. A material breach (or total breach) is a failure so substantial that it defeats the purpose of the contract and excuses the other party from performing, allowing them to sue for all damages. A minor or partial breach permits the injured party to sue for actual damages but does not excuse their own performance. An anticipatory breach (or repudiation) occurs when a party declares in advance that it will not perform its obligations, allowing the other party to treat the contract as ended immediately.

Remedies

Remedies include compensatory (expectation) damages — famously applied in Hawkins v. McGee, the "hairy hand" case — reliance damages, restitution, liquidated damages where stipulated, and equitable remedies such as specific performance, typically reserved for unique goods or land. The injured party generally has a duty to mitigate their losses.
