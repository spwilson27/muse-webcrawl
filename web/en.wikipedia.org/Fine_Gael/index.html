Fine Gael

Fine Gael (Irish: Fine Gael, "Family/Tribe of the Irish") is a centre-right political party in Ireland. Founded in 1933 from a merger of Cumann na nGaedheal, the National Centre Party, and the National Guard (the Blueshirts), it is one of the two parties — with Fianna Fáil — that have historically dominated Irish politics, and it has formed governments led by taoisigh including W. T. Cosgrave, John A. Costello, Liam Cosgrave, Garret FitzGerald, John Bruton, Enda Kenny, and Leo Varadkar.

History

Fine Gael's roots lie in the pro-Treaty side of the Irish Civil War. Cumann na nGaedheal governed the Irish Free State from 1923 to 1932 before losing power to Fianna Fáil. The 1933 merger created Fine Gael, initially under Eoin O'Duffy, soon replaced by Cosgrave. The party declared Ireland a republic while in an inter-party government in 1948. Fine Gael spent long periods in opposition alternating with periods of coalition government, usually with the Labour Party.

Recent history

Under Enda Kenny the party became the largest in the Dáil at the 2011 election and governed in coalition with Labour through the post-financial-crisis austerity period. Leo Varadkar became leader and Taoiseach in 2017, the country's first openly gay head of government and its first of Indian heritage. In 2020 Fine Gael entered a historic coalition with Fianna Fáil and the Green Party, with the role of Taoiseach rotating between the two larger parties; Simon Harris succeeded Varadkar as leader and Taoiseach in 2024.

Ideology

Fine Gael is generally described as centre-right, supportive of free enterprise, fiscal prudence, law and order, and pro-European integration; it is a member of the European People's Party. On social questions the party has liberalised considerably over time, supporting the referendums on marriage equality (2015) and abortion (2018) under Varadkar.
