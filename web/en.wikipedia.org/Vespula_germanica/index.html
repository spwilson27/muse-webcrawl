Vespula germanica

Common nameGerman wasp, German yellowjacket
FamilyVespidae
Native toEurope, North Africa, temperate Asia
Introduced toNorth and South America, Australia, New Zealand, South Africa

Vespula germanica, the German wasp or German yellowjacket, is a species of social wasp native to Europe, North Africa, and temperate Asia. It has been widely introduced elsewhere and is a significant invasive pest in countries including Australia, New Zealand, Argentina, Chile, and South Africa.

Description

Workers are about 12–13 mm long with the classic black-and-yellow wasp pattern: a black anchor-shaped mark on the face, three black spots on each abdominal segment, and black antennae. Queens are larger, up to about 20 mm. The species closely resembles the common wasp, Vespula vulgaris, from which it can be distinguished by facial and abdominal markings.

Behaviour

Colonies are founded in spring by a single queen and may reach several thousand workers by late summer. Nests are built of paper in soil cavities, wall voids, and roof spaces. Workers hunt insects but also scavenge human food and sweet drinks, bringing them into conflict with people; they can sting repeatedly. In mild climates, including parts of New Zealand, colonies may survive the winter and grow to enormous size.

Control

As an invader, V. germanica threatens native insects and birds through competition and predation and is a target of baiting and biocontrol programmes, including the use of parasitoid Sphecophaga wasps and toxic bait stations.
