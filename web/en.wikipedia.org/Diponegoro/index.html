Diponegoro

Prince Diponegoro (born Bendara Raden Mas Mustahar, 11 November 1785 – 8 January 1855), also known as Dipanegara, was a Javanese prince who opposed Dutch colonial rule in Indonesia. He was the eldest son of Sultan Hamengkubuwono III of Yogyakarta. From 1825 to 1830 he led the Java War — the largest and costliest war fought by the Netherlands in its colonial history — against the Dutch East Indies government.

Background

Diponegoro was raised partly at the court and partly at his great-grandmother's estate at Tegalrejo, where he absorbed Islamic learning and Javanese mysticism. Passed over in royal succession disputes and angered by colonial interference, land seizures, and a road built across his family land and ancestral graves, he withdrew to Tegalrejo and, in July 1825, raised revolt when Dutch troops attempted to seize him.

The Java War

Framing his struggle as a holy war (perang sabil) against the infidel Dutch and their Javanese collaborators, Diponegoro drew wide support from peasants, nobles, and religious leaders across central Java. At its height his forces threatened Dutch control of the island. The war devastated central Java: an estimated 200,000 Javanese died, along with some 8,000 Dutch and allied soldiers. The Dutch responded with mobile columns and a system of small forts (bentengstelsel) that gradually confined his movement.

Capture and exile

In March 1830, Diponegoro was arrested at Magelang under a flag of truce during negotiations — an act widely regarded as treacherous. He was exiled first to Manado and then to Makassar, where he wrote his famous autobiography, the Babad Diponegoro, a landmark of Javanese literature. He died in Makassar in 1855.

Diponegoro is a National Hero of Indonesia; his image appears on currency, and Diponegoro University and streets across the country bear his name.
