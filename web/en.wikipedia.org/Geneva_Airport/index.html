Geneva Airport

IATA: GVA   ICAO: LSGG

Location: Meyrin and Grand-Saconnex, near Geneva, Switzerland

Passengers: about 17.8 million (2019)

Runway: one concrete runway, 3,900 m

Geneva Airport (IATA: GVA, ICAO: LSGG), formerly and still unofficially known as Cointrin Airport, is the international airport of Geneva, the second most populous city in Switzerland. It is located about 4 kilometres (2.5 miles) northwest of the city centre, in the municipalities of Meyrin and Grand-Saconnex, and lies entirely within Swiss territory although its northern boundary runs along the Swiss–French border; the airport has a French sector allowing passengers to arrive in France without passing Swiss customs.

Facilities and operations

The airport has a single concrete runway of 3,900 metres (12,795 feet), among the longest in Switzerland, and one main passenger terminal (Terminal 1) plus a smaller Terminal 2 used mainly for charter flights in the winter ski season. It serves as a hub for Swiss International Air Lines and a major base for easyJet Switzerland, and handles around 17–18 million passengers a year, making it Switzerland's second-busiest airport after Zurich. A railway station beneath the terminal provides direct trains to Geneva's main station and across Switzerland.

History

The airfield was established in 1919 at Cointrin and developed into a significant European airport after the Second World War, when a longer runway replaced the original grass strip and Geneva's role as home to the United Nations' European headquarters, the Red Cross, and many international organisations generated steady diplomatic and conference traffic. Expansion is constrained by the surrounding city and the French border, and the airport operates under a night-flight curfew.
