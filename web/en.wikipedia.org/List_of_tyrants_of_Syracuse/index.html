List of tyrants of Syracuse

Syracuse, the great Greek city of Sicily founded from Corinth in 734 BC, was ruled for long stretches of its classical and Hellenistic history by tyrants — rulers who seized or held power outside constitutional norms.

Gelon485–478 BC
Hiero I478–467 BC
Thrasybulus467–466 BC
Dionysius I405–367 BC

Dion357–354 BC
Agathocles317–289 BC
Hiero II270–215 BC
Hieronymus215–214 BC

Gelon's victory over Carthage at Himera in 480 BC made Syracuse the leading power of the Greek west. Dionysius I built an empire across Sicily and southern Italy and fortified the city with the great Euryelus fortress. Timoleon restored democracy in the 340s BC. Agathocles took the war to Africa and later assumed the royal title. Hiero II ruled as king for over fifty years as a loyal ally of Rome; his grandson Hieronymus's defection to Carthage led to the Roman siege in which Archimedes was killed, and Syracuse fell in 212 BC.
