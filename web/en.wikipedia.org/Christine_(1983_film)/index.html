Christine (1983 film)

Christine (titled on screen as John Carpenter's Christine) is a 1983 American supernatural horror film directed by John Carpenter and starring Keith Gordon, John Stockwell, Alexandra Paul, Robert Prosky, and Harry Dean Stanton. Based on Stephen King's 1983 novel of the same name, it follows a sentient, murderous 1958 Plymouth Fury and the nerdy teenager whose personality darkens as he restores her.

Christine, the possessed 1958 Plymouth Fury

Plot

On a Detroit assembly line in 1957, a red-and-white Plymouth Fury — christened "Christine" — injures one worker and kills another. Twenty-one years later in California, bullied teenager Arnie Cunningham buys the now-dilapidated car against his parents' wishes and restores her in Darnell's garage. As the car regenerates and Arnie adopts 1950s dress and a swaggering arrogance, Christine begins killing those who wronged her or threaten to separate her from Arnie, regenerating from every injury. Arnie's friend Dennis and girlfriend Leigh ultimately confront the car, crushing her in a junkyard compactor — but the final shot shows her grille beginning to straighten.

Production

Carpenter accepted the project after the disappointing reception of The Thing (1982), attracted by the chance to adapt King at the height of the author's popularity. More than twenty Plymouth Furys and Belvederes were used in filming; the car's self-repairing regeneration was achieved by filming hydraulic destruction of a car in reverse. The soundtrack combines a Carpenter score with period rock and roll, including George Thorogood's "Bad to the Bone."

Reception

The film was a moderate box-office success, earning about $21 million in the United States, and received generally favorable reviews praising Carpenter's direction and the special effects. It has since gained a cult following and is regarded as one of the stronger Stephen King adaptations of its era.
