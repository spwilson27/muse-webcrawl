Vestal Virgin

The Vestal Virgins (Latin: Virgines Vestales) were the priestesses of Vesta, goddess of the hearth, in the state religion of ancient Rome. Their primary duty was tending the sacred fire of Vesta in the Temple of Vesta in the Roman Forum.

[Image: A row of veiled Vestal Virgins tending the eternal flame of the temple of Vesta.]

Selection and duties

Vestals were chosen by the Pontifex Maximus from girls aged six to ten, and served for thirty years: ten as novices, ten in active service, and ten teaching the novices. They had to remain celibate; a Vestal who broke her vow was, in the Republic's early centuries, entombed alive. Their other duties included preparing the sacred mola salsa (salted flour) used in public sacrifices, and they enjoyed rare privileges, including front seats at games and immunity from patria potestas.

History

Tradition attributed the order's founding to King Numa Pompilius. The college remained central to Roman civic life for over a thousand years until Emperor Theodosius I suppressed the pagan cults, and the sacred fire was extinguished around 394 CE.
