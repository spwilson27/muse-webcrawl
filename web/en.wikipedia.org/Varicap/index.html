Varicap

A varicap diode, also called a varactor diode or tuning diode, is a type of diode designed to exploit the voltage-dependent capacitance of a reverse-biased p–n junction. It is used as a voltage-controlled capacitor in electronic circuits.

Operation

When a p–n junction is reverse-biased, a depletion region forms that acts as the dielectric of a capacitor, with the semiconductor regions as plates. Increasing the reverse voltage widens the depletion region and decreases the capacitance; decreasing it narrows the region and raises the capacitance. The capacitance ratio between minimum and maximum voltage can range from about 2:1 to more than 10:1 depending on the doping profile.

Applications

Varicaps are widely used for electronic tuning in radio and television receivers, replacing mechanically variable capacitors in LC resonators of voltage-controlled oscillators and phase-locked loops. They are also used in frequency multipliers, parametric amplifiers, voltage-controlled filters and FM modulators.

History

The device was developed in the late 1950s, with early work by Sanford Barnes and others at Pacific Semiconductors, and the name "varicap" derives from "variable capacitor".
