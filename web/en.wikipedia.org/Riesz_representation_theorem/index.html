Riesz representation theorem

The Riesz representation theorem, sometimes called the Riesz–Fréchet representation theorem, is a fundamental result in functional analysis. It says that every continuous linear functional on a Hilbert space can be written uniquely as an inner product with a fixed vector of that space.

Statement

Let H be a Hilbert space over ℝ or ℂ, and let φ : H → 𝕜 be a continuous linear functional. Then there exists a unique vector y in H such that

φ(x) = ⟨x, y⟩ for all x ∈ H,

and moreover ‖φ‖ = ‖y‖. Consequently the dual space H* of a Hilbert space is isometrically isomorphic to H itself (conjugate-linearly in the complex case).

History and generalisations

The theorem was proved independently in 1907 by Frigyes Riesz and Maurice Fréchet for L² spaces and later in general form. Several other results share the name: the Riesz–Markov–Kakutani representation theorem describes continuous linear functionals on spaces of continuous functions in terms of measures, and another version identifies the dual of Lp with Lq for conjugate exponents.

Applications

The theorem justifies the bra–ket notation of quantum mechanics, in which bras (elements of the dual space) correspond to kets (state vectors). It is also used to prove the existence of weak solutions to partial differential equations, for example via the Lax–Milgram theorem, a generalisation.
