Artificial pancreas

An artificial pancreas is a system designed to automatically mimic the glucose-regulating function of a healthy pancreas. In its most common form — an automated insulin delivery (AID) system, sometimes called a closed loop — it combines a continuous glucose monitor (CGM), an insulin pump and a control algorithm that adjusts insulin delivery in response to measured glucose levels. Such systems are used primarily by people with type 1 diabetes.

How it works

The CGM measures glucose in interstitial fluid every few minutes and transmits readings to the algorithm, which runs on the pump or a smartphone. The algorithm calculates how much insulin is needed to keep glucose in target range, increasing, reducing or suspending basal insulin delivery automatically. Users still announce meals and administer meal boluses in most current "hybrid closed loop" systems.

Devices and development

The first commercial hybrid closed-loop system, Medtronic's MiniMed 670G, was approved by the US Food and Drug Administration in 2016. Later systems include Tandem's Control-IQ, Insulet's Omnipod 5 and CamAPS FX. Alongside commercial products, a do-it-yourself community developed open-source systems such as OpenAPS and Loop. Research continues into fully closed-loop systems that require no meal announcements and dual-hormone systems delivering both insulin and glucagon.

Other meanings

The term is also used for bioartificial approaches in which insulin-producing islet cells are encapsulated in a protective device or scaffold that shields them from the immune system while allowing glucose sensing and insulin release, and for research into stem-cell-derived beta-cell replacement.
