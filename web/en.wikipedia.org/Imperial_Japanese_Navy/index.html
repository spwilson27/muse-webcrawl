The Imperial Japanese Navy (IJN; Japanese: 大日本帝國海軍, Dai-Nippon Teikoku Kaigun) was the navy of the Empire of Japan from 1868 until its dissolution in 1945 following Japan's defeat in the Second World War. Modelled initially on European navies, it grew within a generation into one of the world's most powerful fleets.

Rise

Built up during the Meiji era with British and French assistance, the IJN defeated the Chinese Beiyang Fleet in the First Sino-Japanese War (1894–95) and then destroyed Russia's Baltic Fleet at Tsushima in 1905 under Admiral Tōgō — the first decisive defeat of a European great power by an Asian state in the modern era. By the 1920s Japan possessed the world's third-largest navy, constrained by the Washington Naval Treaty, and pioneered naval aviation, commissioning one of the first purpose-built aircraft carriers, Hōshō, in 1922.

Second World War

On 7 December 1941 the IJN's carrier strike force attacked Pearl Harbor, opening the Pacific War. After early triumphs across Southeast Asia and the Pacific, the navy suffered a decisive defeat at Midway in June 1942, losing four fleet carriers. Thereafter American industrial superiority, codebreaking, and submarine interdiction ground the IJN down; its super-battleships Musashi and Yamato were sunk by air attack in 1944 and 1945, the latter on a one-way mission to Okinawa. The surviving fleet was surrendered in 1945 and the navy abolished; its traditions passed partly to the postwar Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force.
