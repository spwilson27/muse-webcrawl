Information science

DisciplineInterdisciplinary academic field
FocusAnalysis, collection, classification, storage, retrieval, and dissemination of information
Related fieldsLibrary science, computer science, cognitive science, communication studies

Information science is an academic field concerned with the analysis, collection, classification, manipulation, storage, retrieval, movement, dissemination, and protection of information. Practitioners within and outside the field study the application and usage of knowledge in organisations, along with the interaction between people, organisations, and information systems, with the goal of creating, replacing, improving, or understanding information systems.

History

The field has roots in documentation science and librarianship of the late 19th and early 20th centuries — figures such as Paul Otlet and Henri La Fontaine sought universal systems for organising recorded knowledge. After the Second World War, the rapid growth of scientific literature, Vannevar Bush's vision of the Memex (1945), and the rise of computing gave rise to modern information science and its close sibling, information retrieval. The term gained currency in the 1960s as university programmes and professional societies adopted it.

Scope

Information science is inherently interdisciplinary. Its topics include information behaviour and seeking, knowledge organisation (classification, metadata, ontologies), information architecture, digital libraries and archives, data curation, informetrics, information ethics and policy, and human–computer interaction. It differs from computer science, which focuses on computation and algorithms, by foregrounding the social and human contexts in which information is created and used.

Institutions and education

The field is represented by organisations such as the Association for Information Science and Technology (ASIS&T), founded in 1937 as the American Documentation Institute. Many universities host information schools (iSchools) offering degrees that combine information science with computing, design, and management.
