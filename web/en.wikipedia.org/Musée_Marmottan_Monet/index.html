The Musée Marmottan Monet is an art museum in Paris, France, dedicated to presenting a collection of more than three hundred Impressionist and post-Impressionist works by Claude Monet and other artists including Berthe Morisot, Edgar Degas, Édouard Manet, Alfred Sisley, Camille Pissarro, Paul Gauguin, Paul Signac and Pierre-Auguste Renoir.

History

The building began as a hunting lodge for the Duke of Valmy at the edge of the Bois de Boulogne. It was purchased by Jules Marmottan in 1882 and bequeathed, with his collection, by his son Paul Marmottan to the Académie des Beaux-Arts; the museum opened in 1934. A decisive gift came in 1966, when Michel Monet, the artist's son, bequeathed his collection of his father's paintings, making the museum the world's largest holder of Monet's work.

Collection highlights

The museum holds Impression, Sunrise (1872), the painting whose title gave the Impressionist movement its name, together with many of Monet's Water Lilies and Rouen Cathedral paintings. Its Berthe Morisot collection is the largest in the world. In 1985 nine paintings, including Impression, Sunrise, were stolen by armed robbers; they were recovered in Corsica in 1990.
