The Bay of Bengal is the northeastern part of the Indian Ocean and the largest bay in the world, occupying an area of roughly 2.6 million square kilometres. It is bordered by India and Sri Lanka to the west, Bangladesh to the north, and Myanmar and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands to the east.

Geography

Great rivers — above all the combined Ganges–Brahmaputra–Meghna system — pour enormous quantities of fresh water and sediment into the bay, producing the world's largest delta and the submarine Bengal Fan. Major ports include Chennai, Kolkata, Visakhapatnam, Chittagong and Yangon.

Climate and cyclones

The bay is strongly influenced by the monsoon. Its warm waters and funnel-shaped northern coastline make it one of the world's most cyclone-prone regions: storm surges have caused some of history's deadliest natural disasters, including the 1970 Bhola cyclone, which killed several hundred thousand people in East Pakistan (now Bangladesh), and Cyclone Nargis in Myanmar in 2008.

Ecology and economy

The bay supports major fisheries, offshore gas fields and some of the busiest shipping routes in Asia; the mangrove forests of the Sundarbans, shared by India and Bangladesh, form the largest such forest in the world and habitat for the Bengal tiger.
