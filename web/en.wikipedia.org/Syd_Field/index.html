Syd Field

Born: Sydney Alvin Field, 19 July 1935, Hollywood, California
Died: 17 November 2013, Beverly Hills, California
Occupation: Screenwriting teacher, author
Notable work: Screenplay: The Foundations of Screenwriting

Sydney Alvin Field (July 19, 1935 – November 17, 2013) was an American screenwriting guru and author who wrote several influential books on the subject of screenwriting. He was the first to articulate and popularise the three-act structure paradigm for screenplays.

The paradigm

In his 1979 bestseller Screenplay: The Foundations of Screenwriting, Field described a "paradigm" in which a typical feature screenplay of about 120 pages is divided into three acts — setup (approx. 30 pages), confrontation (approx. 60 pages), and resolution (approx. 30 pages) — separated by two "plot points" that spin the action in a new direction. A midpoint division of the second act was added in later editions.

Influence

Field's books have been translated into many languages and are used in film schools worldwide; his "paradigm" became the default model of mainstream screenwriting instruction, influencing generations of writers and studio readers. Some critics and writers have criticised the model as formulaic, while defenders regard it as a description of classical narrative structure rather than a prescription. Field read thousands of scripts as a story analyst and taught workshops internationally.
