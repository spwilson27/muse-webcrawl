Curve25519

Curve25519 is an elliptic curve used in elliptic-curve cryptography, offering about 128 bits of security. It was designed by Daniel J. Bernstein in 2005 and named after its prime, 2255 − 19. The curve is a Montgomery curve with equation y2 = x3 + 486662x2 + x over the prime field defined by that prime.

X25519

The Diffie–Hellman key agreement function built on the curve, standardized in RFC 7748 as X25519, uses only x-coordinates and the Montgomery ladder, which makes fast, constant-time implementations straightforward and avoids many pitfalls of other curves. X25519 is supported in TLS 1.3, the Signal Protocol, and WireGuard.

Design rationale

Bernstein chose the curve's parameters with public, verifiable criteria ("nothing-up-my-sleeve" numbers): the prime is the closest prime to a power of two for efficient arithmetic, and the coefficient 486662 is the smallest satisfying the stated security conditions. The curve's twist is also secure, and its cofactor structure tolerates implementations that skip some validations. Ed25519, a signature scheme on the related Edwards curve, shares the same field.
