The MGB is a two-door sports car manufactured and marketed by the British Motor Corporation (BMC), later British Leyland, as a four-cylinder, soft-top roadster (1962–1980) and as the three-door MGB GT coupé (1965–1980). It replaced the MGA and became one of the best-selling sports cars of its era, particularly in the North American market.

Design

The MGB was innovative for its unitary (monocoque) construction, which reduced weight and production cost compared with its predecessor's separate chassis. It was powered by the 1.8-litre BMC B-series engine, with crumple zones designed into the structure. The GT version, styled with help from Pininfarina, added a sloping hatchback roof and occasional rear seats. A V8-powered variant, the MGB GT V8, was sold from 1973 to 1976.

History

Changes over the long production run were largely driven by safety and emissions rules in the United States: from 1974 US cars gained large black rubber bumpers and a raised ride height, which drew criticism for dulling the handling and looks. Production at Abingdon ended in October 1980, after more than half a million cars — engines and the Abingdon works had been building MGs since 1929. The car's simple mechanics and strong club support have made it one of the most popular classic cars in Britain.
