Lake Pátzcuaro (Spanish: Lago de Pátzcuaro) is a lake in the municipality of Pátzcuaro, Michoacán, Mexico. It is an endorheic lake — it has no outlet — set high in the mountains of the Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt, and it was the heartland of the Purépecha (Tarascan) civilisation.

Islands and culture

The lake's best-known island is Janitzio, crowned by a 40-metre statue of the independence hero José María Morelos. The lakeside Purépecha communities are famous for Day of the Dead (Noche de Muertos) observances, especially on Janitzio and in Tzintzuntzan, and for traditional butterfly-net fishing from canoes, a celebrated image of the lake.

Ecology

The shallow lake supports endemic species such as the Pátzcuaro whitefish (pescado blanco), now endangered, and the achoque, a relative of the axolotl. Pollution, introduced species, water extraction, and siltation have degraded the lake, prompting conservation and restoration programmes; its water level has declined noticeably in recent decades.
