Tyne Cot Commonwealth War Graves Cemetery and Memorial to the Missing is a burial ground for the dead of the First World War in the Ypres Salient on the Western Front. Located near the village of Passendale in Belgium, it is administered by the Commonwealth War Graves Commission (CWGC). With 11,965 burials, it is the largest CWGC cemetery in the world in terms of burials, and its memorial wall records the names of 33,783 Commonwealth soldiers whose graves are unknown.

History

The name "Tyne Cot" is said to have been given by the Northumberland Fusiliers to a barn at a crossroads here, which they thought resembled Tyneside cottages. The position was captured by Australian troops on 4 October 1917 during the Third Battle of Ypres (Passchendaele) and later used as an advanced dressing station; the cemetery grew around the graves of men who died there.

Design

Designed principally by Sir Herbert Baker, the cemetery retains two German pillboxes within its grounds, one of which supports the Cross of Sacrifice — a siting made, according to tradition, at the suggestion of King George V after his pilgrimage to the war cemeteries in 1922. The curved memorial wall at the rear was added when the Menin Gate in Ypres proved too small to hold all the names of the missing of the Salient.
