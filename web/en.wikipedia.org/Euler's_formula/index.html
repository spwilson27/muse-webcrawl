FieldComplex analysis

Euler's formula, named after Leonhard Euler, is a mathematical formula in complex analysis that establishes the fundamental relationship between the trigonometric functions and the complex exponential function: for any real number θ,

eiθ = cos θ + i sin θ

History

Equivalent results were glimpsed by Roger Cotes in 1714 in logarithmic form. Euler published the formula in its present form in his Introductio in analysin infinitorum (1748), deriving it by comparing the infinite series expansions of the exponential and trigonometric functions.

Consequences

Setting θ = π gives Euler's identity, eiπ + 1 = 0, linking five fundamental constants. The formula expresses any complex number in polar form, z = r eiθ, makes De Moivre's theorem immediate, and underlies Fourier analysis, signal processing (phasors), and the geometry of rotations. Euler's formula for polyhedra (V − E + F = 2) is a different result sharing the name.

Proof sketch

Substituting x = iθ into the Taylor series of ex and separating real and imaginary parts yields exactly the series for cos θ and sin θ, proving the identity for all real (indeed all complex) θ.
