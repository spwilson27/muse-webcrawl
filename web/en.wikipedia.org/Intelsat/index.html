Intelsat S.A. is a multinational satellite services provider with its administrative headquarters in Luxembourg and operational headquarters in the United States. Originally formed as the International Telecommunications Satellite Organization (ITSO), it operated from 1964 until 2001 as an intergovernmental consortium owning and managing a constellation of communications satellites for international broadcast and telephony.

History

Intelsat was established by an agreement signed in August 1964 by 11 founding countries, following US President John F. Kennedy's call for a global satellite communications system and the US Communications Satellite Act of 1962. Its first commercial satellite, Intelsat I ("Early Bird"), was launched on 6 April 1965 and provided the first regular transatlantic commercial satellite service. Intelsat satellites carried famous broadcasts including the Apollo 11 Moon landing in 1969 to a global television audience. By privatization in 2001, the organization had grown to over 140 member countries.

Privatization and later history

On 18 July 2001 Intelsat was privatized, its assets transferred to a private company while ITSO retained a residual oversight role. The company went through leveraged buyouts, listed on the New York Stock Exchange in 2013, and filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in May 2020 to restructure debt while participating in the FCC's C-band clearing programme; it emerged in February 2022. In April 2024, SES announced an agreement to acquire Intelsat for about $3.1 billion, forming one of the largest satellite operators; the deal completed in July 2025.

Operations

Intelsat operates a fleet of geostationary satellites (the "Galaxy" and "Intelsat" series) providing video distribution, network services, in-flight connectivity, maritime communications, and government services worldwide.
