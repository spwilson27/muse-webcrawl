Fujita scale

The Fujita scale (F-Scale), or Fujita–Pearson scale, is a scale for rating tornado intensity based on the damage tornadoes inflict on human-built structures and vegetation. It was introduced in 1971 by Ted Fujita of the University of Chicago in collaboration with Allen Pearson of the National Severe Storms Forecast Center.

The scale

The scale divides tornadoes into six categories, F0 to F5, with estimated wind speeds increasing from roughly 40–72 mph (64–116 km/h) for an F0 to over 200 mph for an F5, where "incredible" damage occurs: strong frame houses are lifted from their foundations and carried, and automobile-sized objects become airborne missiles. Because direct wind measurements inside tornadoes are rare, ratings are assigned after damage surveys, so the scale is effectively a damage scale linked to estimated winds.

Enhanced Fujita scale

In the United States the original scale was replaced on 1 February 2007 by the Enhanced Fujita scale (EF scale), which uses 28 damage indicators with degrees of damage to give more consistent and accurate wind estimates; Canada adopted its own version of the EF scale in 2013. The Fujita scale proper remains in use in some countries and is still applied to historical tornado records.
