Chronic care management

Chronic care management (CCM) is the coordination of care for patients with multiple chronic conditions, such as diabetes, hypertension, heart disease, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, and depression. It emphasises planned, proactive, and continuous care between office visits rather than treatment only when symptoms flare.

United States Medicare programme

The term is closely associated with the United States Medicare programme. Beginning in 2015, the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) began separately paying physicians and other qualified health care professionals for non-face-to-face care coordination services furnished to beneficiaries with two or more chronic conditions expected to last at least 12 months. The core service (CPT code 99490) covers at least 20 minutes per month of clinical staff time directed by a physician or other qualified professional, based on a comprehensive care plan, with requirements including 24/7 access to care, continuity with a designated care team member, and use of certified electronic health record technology. Additional codes, such as 99491 and the complex CCM codes 99487 and 99489, cover personally performed clinician time and more intensive management. In 2022 CMS added principal care management services for patients with a single high-risk chronic condition.

Related models

Chronic care management builds on the Chronic Care Model developed by Edward Wagner and colleagues, which identifies productive interactions between an informed, activated patient and a prepared, proactive practice team as the route to better outcomes. Related Medicare services include transitional care management after hospital discharge and remote physiologic monitoring.
