United States Federal Witness Protection Program

The United States Federal Witness Protection Program (WPP), also known as the Witness Security Program or WITSEC, is a witness protection program administered by the United States Marshals Service that was established under the Organized Crime Control Act of 1970. It protects threatened witnesses before, during, and after trials, usually in cases involving organized crime.

History

The program was developed by Gerald Shur, an attorney in the U.S. Department of Justice's organized crime section. Since its inception it has protected roughly 19,000 witnesses and family members. Protected witnesses receive new identities with documentation, housing, subsistence payments, and help finding employment; in return they must testify truthfully and refrain from crime.

Operations

The Marshals Service reports that no participant who followed program guidelines has been harmed while under active protection. About 95% of protected witnesses are criminals testifying against former associates. States operate their own smaller witness protection programs, sometimes in cooperation with the federal program.
