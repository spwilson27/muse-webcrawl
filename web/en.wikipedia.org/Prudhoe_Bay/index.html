Prudhoe Bay

Prudhoe Bay is a census-designated place on Alaska's North Slope, adjacent to the largest oil field in the United States. The field was discovered in 1968 — the biggest petroleum discovery in North American history — and began production in 1977 after completion of the Trans-Alaska Pipeline System, which carries crude oil 800 miles south to Valdez.

Geography and population

Prudhoe Bay lies on the coast of the Beaufort Sea in the Arctic Ocean. Its permanent population is very small (only a handful of residents in recent censuses), but thousands of rotational workers are housed in camps at Deadhorse, the service community nearby. The area is within the North Slope Borough and experiences polar night in winter and continuous daylight in summer.

The oil field

The Prudhoe Bay Oil Field originally contained an estimated 25 billion barrels of oil, of which roughly 13 billion were considered recoverable. Production peaked in 1988 at about 2 million barrels per day and has since declined to a fraction of that. In 2006 a pipeline leak spilled roughly 200,000 gallons of crude onto the tundra, then the largest oil spill on the North Slope, leading to pipeline replacement and federal scrutiny of operator BP.
