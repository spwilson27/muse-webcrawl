Six Masters of the early Qing period

Six Masters of the early Qing Members: Wang Shimin, Wang Jian, Wang Hui, Wang Yuanqi, Wu Li, Yun Shouping
Period: Early Qing dynasty (17th–early 18th century)

The Six Masters of the early Qing period were the leading Chinese landscape painters of the early Qing dynasty. The group comprises the "Four Wangs" — Wang Shimin, Wang Jian, Wang Hui and Wang Yuanqi — together with Wu Li and Yun Shouping. Their works defined the orthodox school of literati painting favoured at the Qing court.

The Four Wangs

Wang Shimin (1592–1680) and Wang Jian (1598–1677) were senior masters of the first Qing generation, deeply influenced by the Ming critic and painter Dong Qichang, who had formulated the theory of the Northern and Southern schools of painting. Wang Hui (1632–1717), a pupil of both senior Wangs, synthesised their styles and supervised the production of the monumental handscroll series The Kangxi Emperor's Southern Inspection Tour. Wang Yuanqi (1642–1715), grandson of Wang Shimin, served at court and codified orthodox landscape theory in his writings, emphasising brushwork and the "dragon veins" of composition.

Wu Li and Yun Shouping

Wu Li (1632–1718) was a friend of Wang Hui who converted to Catholicism and was ordained a Jesuit priest, combining Chinese landscape conventions with subtle influences from European painting. Yun Shouping (1633–1690), initially a landscape painter, became renowned for his "boneless" flower paintings washed in colour without ink outlines, founding the Choushu school of flower painting.

Legacy

The orthodox manner of the Six Masters dominated official taste for much of the Qing period, though later critics, especially in the twentieth century, sometimes faulted it for derivative imitation of old masters in contrast to the expressive individualism of contemporaries such as Shitao and Bada Shanren.
