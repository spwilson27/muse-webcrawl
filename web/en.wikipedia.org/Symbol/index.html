Symbol

A symbol is a mark, sign, or word that indicates, signifies, or is understood as representing an idea, object, or relationship. Symbols allow people to go beyond what is known or seen by creating linkages between otherwise very different concepts and experiences. All communication (and data processing) is achieved through the use of symbols: they are the basic units studied by semiotics.

Terminology

The word derives from the Greek symbolon, an object cut in half so that the two parts could later be matched to verify the identity of messengers. In the theory of Charles Sanders Peirce, a symbol is one of three kinds of sign, alongside icons (which resemble their object) and indices (which are causally connected with it); a symbol represents its object purely by convention or law, as words do. For Ferdinand de Saussure, the linguistic sign joins a “signifier” (sound pattern) with a “signified” (concept), and the bond between them is arbitrary.

Psychology and religion

Carl Jung distinguished symbols from simple signs: a symbol, for Jung, expresses something not yet fully known and points toward the contents of the collective unconscious, including archetypes. Religious traditions are rich in symbols — the cross in Christianity, the crescent in Islam, the Om in Hinduism, the yin-yang in Daoism — whose meanings condense doctrines and are transmitted across generations.

Symbols in science and mathematics

Science relies on formal symbol systems: chemical symbols denote the elements, mathematical notation compresses complex statements into formulas, and units have standard symbols in the SI system. National and cultural symbols, such as flags and emblems, similarly condense collective identity; their use is often regulated by law.
