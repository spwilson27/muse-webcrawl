Parliament of Canada

The Parliament of Canada is the federal legislature of Canada, seated at Parliament Hill in Ottawa. It consists of three parts: the monarch (represented by the governor general), the Senate, and the House of Commons, following the Westminster system of parliamentary government.

Composition

The House of Commons has 343 elected members (following the 2022 redistribution), each representing a single electoral district. The Senate has 105 members appointed by the governor general on the advice of the prime minister, representing the provinces and territories. Legislation must pass both houses and receive royal assent.

History

Parliament was created by the Constitution Act, 1867 (British North America Act), which united the Province of Canada with Nova Scotia and New Brunswick. The Centre Block, rebuilt after a 1916 fire, houses both chambers.

Procedure

The government is formed by the party or coalition commanding the confidence of the Commons, whose leader becomes prime minister. Proceedings are conducted in both official languages, English and French.
