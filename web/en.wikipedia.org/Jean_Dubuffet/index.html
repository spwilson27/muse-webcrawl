Jean Dubuffet

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Jean Philippe Arthur Dubuffet (31 July 1901 – 12 May 1985) was a French painter and sculptor of the Ecole de Paris (School of Paris). His idealistic approach to aesthetics embraced so-called "low art" and eschewed traditional standards of beauty in favor of what he believed to be a more authentic and humanistic approach to image-making. He is perhaps best known for founding the art movement Art Brut, and for the collection of works—Collection de l'art brut—that this movement spawned. Dubuffet enjoyed a prolific art career, both in France and in America, and was featured in many exhibitions throughout his lifetime.

Early life

Dubuffet was born in Le Havre to a family of wholesale wine merchants. He studied art briefly at the Académie Julian in Paris, and spent years in the family wine trade, painting only sporadically, before turning fully to art in 1942 at the age of 41.

Art Brut

In 1945 Dubuffet began collecting works made outside the official art world — by psychiatric patients, prisoners, children, and other self-taught creators — which he called art brut ("raw art"), contrasting its uncooked authenticity with the "cooked" art of the cultural mainstream (art culturel). In 1949 he published the manifesto L'art brut préféré aux arts culturels and exhibited the collection in Paris. The collection eventually formed the basis of the Collection de l'Art Brut museum in Lausanne, Switzerland, opened in 1976.

Work

Dubuffet's own early paintings used thick impasto mixed with materials such as sand, tar, and straw. He developed series including the Corps de dames (1950), textured landscapes and portraits of rough, graffiti-like figures. From 1962 he produced the Hourloupe cycle, characterised by interlocking forms in red, white, blue, and black, which expanded into monumental sculpture and architecture, including the Jardin d'émail and Group of Four Trees in New York. The Dubuffet Foundation in Paris preserves his work.

Death

Dubuffet died of emphysema in Paris on 12 May 1985, at age 83.
