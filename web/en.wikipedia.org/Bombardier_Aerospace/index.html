Bombardier Aerospace


A Bombardier Global 7500, flagship of the company's business jet range.

Bombardier Aerospace was the aircraft manufacturing division of the Canadian conglomerate Bombardier Inc., headquartered in Montreal, Quebec. For much of its existence it was one of the world's largest aircraft manufacturers, producing regional airliners and turboprops, business jets, and amphibious water bombers. From the late 2010s, Bombardier sold off its commercial aircraft programmes one by one — the CSeries to Airbus (where it became the A220), the Q400 to Viking Air/Longview, and the CRJ series to Mitsubishi Heavy Industries — and in 2021 it also sold its rail division to Alstom, leaving Bombardier as a pure-play manufacturer of business jets (Learjet, Challenger and Global families).

History

Bombardier entered aviation in 1986 by acquiring Canadair, the Montreal-based maker of the Challenger business jet and the CL-215 water bomber, from the Canadian government. It went on to buy the bankrupt Short Brothers of Belfast in 1989, the Learjet Corporation of Wichita in 1990, and de Havilland Canada — maker of the Dash 8 turboprop — from Boeing in 1992. Assembled in less than a decade, these acquisitions made Bombardier the world's third-largest civil aircraft manufacturer by the late 1990s.

The company built its commercial reputation on the CRJ (Canadair Regional Jet), which pioneered the 50-seat regional jet market after its entry into service in 1992, and on the Q400 turboprop. Its most ambitious project, the CSeries, was a clean-sheet 100–130 seat airliner launched in 2008 with a new Pratt & Whitney geared turbofan engine. Development costs and delays strained the company's finances and required government support; after a trade complaint by Boeing led the U.S. Commerce Department to propose duties of nearly 300% in 2017 (later overturned), Bombardier ceded majority control of the programme to Airbus in 2018, and it was rebranded the Airbus A220.

Business jets

The surviving Bombardier business is built on three families: the light-midsize Learjet lineage (production of new Learjets ended in 2022, closing a name that dated to 1963), the super-midsize Challenger 350/3500 and the large-cabin Challenger 650, and the Global family — Global 5500, 6500 and the ultra-long-range Global 7500, which entered service in 2018 with a range among the longest of any business jet, followed by the Global 8000. Bombardier also maintains a defence business adapting Global aircraft for surveillance and other military roles, and a large aftermarket services network.

Products (historical)

Major products over the division's history included the CRJ100/200, CRJ700/900/1000 regional jets; the Dash 8/Q-Series turboprops; the CSeries (CS100/CS300); the Challenger 300/600-series and Global Express business jets; the Learjet 40/45/60/70/75; and the CL-215/CL-415 amphibious firefighting aircraft, beloved for scooping water from lakes to drop on forest fires. Amphibious aircraft production rights, like the Q400, passed to Viking Air in British Columbia.
