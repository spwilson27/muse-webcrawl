The Bombardier CRJ (Canadair Regional Jet) is a family of regional jets introduced in the early 1990s by Bombardier Aerospace. Based on the Canadair Challenger business jet, the CRJ100 and CRJ200 seated about 50 passengers and were followed by the stretched CRJ700, CRJ900, and CRJ1000 series seating up to about 100 passengers.

The CRJ was among the first successful purpose-built regional jets and reshaped regional airline fleets in North America and Europe. Nearly 2,000 aircraft of the family were delivered in total. In 2020, Bombardier sold the CRJ program to Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, which formed MHI RJ Aviation Group to support the fleet; production ended in 2020 with the final CRJ900 delivery.

Variants

Variants include the CRJ100/200 (50 seats), CRJ700 (70 seats), CRJ900 (90 seats), and CRJ1000 (100 seats), as well as the Challenger 800 series business-jet derivatives.
