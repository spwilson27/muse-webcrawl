De Havilland Canada Dash 8

Type: Regional airliner (twin turboprop)

Manufacturer: de Havilland Canada; later Bombardier; De Havilland Aircraft of Canada

First flight: 20 June 1983

Introduced: 1984 (with NorOntair)

Status: In production (Series 400)

The De Havilland Canada DHC-8, commonly known as the Dash 8, is a series of twin-engine, medium-range, turboprop airliners. Introduced by de Havilland Canada (DHC) in 1984, they are now produced by De Havilland Aircraft of Canada Limited. More than 1,200 Dash 8s of all models have been built.

Development

The Dash 8 was developed from the four-engine Dash 7, but with improved cruise performance and lower operational costs. It was designed to fill the gap between smaller commuter aircraft and the larger turboprops, offering short takeoff and landing (STOL) capability inherited from its predecessor, though somewhat reduced. The aircraft entered service in 1984.

Variants

The family comprises the Series 100 (37–39 passengers), the Series 200 (same airframe with more powerful engines), the stretched Series 300 (50–56 seats), and the Series 400 or Q400 (68–90 seats), which introduced a longer fuselage, more powerful Pratt & Whitney Canada PW150A engines, and the Noise and Vibration Suppression (NVS) system that earned the "Q" designation. Bombardier sold the program to Longview Aviation Capital in 2019, which revived the de Havilland Canada name.

Operators and use

Dash 8s are flown by regional airlines worldwide, including Jazz Aviation (Air Canada Express), QantasLink, Widerøe, and Porter Airlines. Military and government variants serve in coast guard, surveillance, and transport roles with operators such as the Royal Canadian Air Force (as the CT-142 "Gonzo") and the Icelandic Coast Guard.
