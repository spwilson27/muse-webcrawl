The Airbus A220 is a family of five-abreast narrow-body airliners developed by Bombardier Aerospace as the Bombardier CSeries. The smaller variant (A220-100, formerly CS100) seats 108 to 130 passengers, while the longer A220-300 (formerly CS300) seats 130 to 160. The aircraft features a composite wing, fly-by-wire controls, and Pratt & Whitney PW1500G geared turbofan engines.

After cost overruns, delays, and a trade dispute with Boeing over subsidies that briefly threatened a nearly 300 percent duty in the United States, Bombardier ceded majority control of the program to Airbus in July 2018, and the aircraft was rebranded as the A220. Final assembly takes place in Mirabel, Quebec, and Mobile, Alabama.

Operators

Major operators include Delta Air Lines, airBaltic, Swiss International Air Lines, JetBlue, and Air France. The A220 is noted for low fuel burn per seat in the 100–130 seat market, a segment largely abandoned by larger manufacturers.
