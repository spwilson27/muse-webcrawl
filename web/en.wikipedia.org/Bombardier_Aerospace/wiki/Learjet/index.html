Learjet

Type: Business jet manufacturer / brand

Founded: 1962 (as Swiss American Aviation Corporation; Lear Jet Corporation)

Founder: Bill Lear

Headquarters: Wichita, Kansas, United States

Parent: Bombardier Aviation (from 1990)

Production ended: 2021 (final delivery 2022)

Learjet was an American manufacturer of business jets for civilian and military use based in Wichita, Kansas. Founded by Bill Lear in the early 1960s, the company produced the first mass-produced business jet, the Learjet 23, which first flew in October 1963 and entered service in 1964. The name "Learjet" became so well known that it is used generically for small private jets in popular culture.

History

Bill Lear adapted the design of an unbuilt Swiss ground-attack aircraft, the FFA P-16, into a fast, luxurious executive transport. Early models such as the 23, 24, and 25 established the type's reputation for speed and climb performance. Later models included the long-range Learjet 35/36 (used by the U.S. Air Force as the C-21), the wingletted 28/29, and the larger 45, 60, 70, 75, and 85 series.

Bombardier era and closure

Bombardier acquired Learjet in 1990, making it part of Bombardier Aerospace alongside Canadair and de Havilland Canada. Under Bombardier, the Learjet 45 and 60XR were popular mid-size jets. In February 2021 Bombardier announced the end of Learjet production to focus on its Challenger and Global families; the final aircraft, a Learjet 75, was delivered in 2022. More than 3,000 Learjets were built.
