Herzog & de Meuron

Herzog & de Meuron Basel Ltd. is a Swiss architecture firm founded in Basel in 1978 by Jacques Herzog and Pierre de Meuron. Internationally acclaimed, its partners received the Pritzker Architecture Prize in 2001. The firm is noted for inventive use of materials and for landmark cultural and sports buildings.

Major works

The firm's conversion of London's Bankside Power Station into Tate Modern (opened 2000, with a major extension in 2016) brought it worldwide recognition. Other celebrated projects include the Beijing National Stadium (the "Bird's Nest," 2008, with Ai Weiwei as artistic consultant), the Allianz Arena in Munich, the de Young Museum in San Francisco, the Elbphilharmonie concert hall in Hamburg (2016), the M+ museum in Hong Kong (2021), and the REHAB Basel clinic. In Basel itself, works such as the Schaulager and the Roche towers have reshaped the skyline.

Approach

Herzog & de Meuron's architecture emphasises surface, material research, and the reinterpretation of familiar forms; each project is developed with extensive model-making and material experimentation. The firm employs several hundred staff across offices in Basel, London, New York, Hong Kong, and elsewhere, and both founders have taught at ETH Zurich and Harvard.
