VKhUTEMAS (Russian: Вхутемас, acronym for Высшие художественно-технические мастерские, "Higher Art and Technical Studios") was a Soviet state art and technical school founded in Moscow in 1920. Often compared to Germany's Bauhaus, it was a major centre of the Russian avant-garde.

History

The school was formed by merging the Moscow School of Painting, Sculpture and Architecture with the Stroganov School of Applied Arts. Its faculties covered painting, sculpture, architecture, textiles, ceramics, metalwork, woodwork, and printing.

Teaching

Students first completed a compulsory Basic Course introducing objective analysis of form, colour, and space, before specializing. Notable teachers included Alexander Rodchenko, El Lissitzky, Lyubov Popova, Varvara Stepanova, Wassily Kandinsky, and the Vesnin brothers. The school promoted Constructivism and Rationalism and strongly influenced Soviet design and architecture.

Dissolution

Under growing political pressure against avant-garde art, VKhUTEMAS was reorganized in 1927 and dissolved in 1930, its faculties absorbed into other institutions.
