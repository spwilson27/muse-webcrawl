Museumplein

Location: Amsterdam-Zuid, Netherlands
Surrounded by: Rijksmuseum, Van Gogh Museum, Stedelijk Museum, Concertgebouw
Known for: Cultural events and demonstrations

The Museumplein (Dutch for "Museum Square") is a large public space in the Amsterdam-Zuid borough of Amsterdam, Netherlands, surrounded by several of the country's most important cultural institutions. It is used for festivals, celebrations, demonstrations, and, in cold winters, ice skating on its pond.

Surroundings

The square is framed by the Rijksmuseum, the Netherlands' national museum of art and history, which stands at its northeastern end; the Van Gogh Museum, holding the world's largest collection of works by Vincent van Gogh; the Stedelijk Museum of modern and contemporary art, with its distinctive white "bathtub" extension; and the Concertgebouw, one of the world's most celebrated concert halls, at its southwestern end. The Moco Museum and the House of Bols attraction also face the square, and the United States consulate stands on its eastern side.

History

The square took shape in the late nineteenth century after the Rijksmuseum opened in 1885 on what had been open ground, part of it laid out on the site of the 1883 International Colonial and Export Exhibition. It was redesigned in 1999 by Swedish/Danish landscape architect Sven-Ingvar Andersson, replacing a road that once cut across it with the present open grass field, pond and underground parking and supermarket. The famous "I amsterdam" letters stood on the square from 2004 until their removal in 2018. Museumplein is a traditional site for mass gatherings, from King's Day festivities to national celebrations when the Dutch football team succeeds at major tournaments, when it fills with orange-clad fans.
