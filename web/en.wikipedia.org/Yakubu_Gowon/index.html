Yakubu Dan-Yumma "Jack" Gowon (born 19 October 1934) is a retired Nigerian army general and statesman who led the Federal military government of Nigeria from 1966 to 1975. He presided over Nigeria during the Nigerian Civil War (1967–1970), in which the attempted secession of Biafra was defeated and the country reunified, and during the oil boom that followed.

Early life and career

Gowon was born in Pankshin in present-day Plateau State, into an Ngas (Angas) Christian family. He was educated at Government College, Zaria, and joined the Nigerian Army, training at Sandhurst in England and later at the Staff College, Camberley, and the Joint Staff College. He served with Nigerian forces in the Congo under the United Nations and rose quickly through the officer corps.

Head of state

After the counter-coup of July 1966 that killed General Johnson Aguiyi-Ironsi, Gowon — then a lieutenant colonel and the most senior northern officer — became head of state at the age of 31. Confronted with ethnic violence and the secession of the Eastern Region as the Republic of Biafra in May 1967, he created twelve states to restructure the federation and prosecuted the ensuing civil war. When Biafra surrendered in January 1970, Gowon proclaimed a policy of "no victor, no vanquished" and pursued reconciliation, reconstruction, and rehabilitation. His government benefited from rising oil revenues, expanded infrastructure, and founded the National Youth Service Corps in 1973. He also played a leading role in founding the Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS) in 1975.

Overthrow and later life

Gowon was overthrown in a bloodless coup in July 1975 while attending an OAU summit in Kampala. He went into exile in Britain, studying political science at the University of Warwick. Later pardoned and rehabilitated, he returned to Nigeria and became an elder statesman, founding the interfaith "Nigeria Prays" initiative and remaining a prominent voice for national unity.
