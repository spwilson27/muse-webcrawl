Hanover

Hanover (German: Hannover) is the capital and largest city of the German state of Lower Saxony, with a population of about 535,000. It lies on the River Leine in north-central Germany and is a major centre of trade fairs, industry, and transport, known for the Hannover Messe and the Herrenhausen Gardens.

History

Hanover grew from a medieval market town at a crossing of the Leine. In the seventeenth century it became the residence of the dukes of Brunswick-Lüneburg; in 1714 its elector, George Louis, ascended the British throne as George I, beginning the personal union between Hanover and Great Britain that lasted until 1837. Hanover was capital of a kingdom, then a Prussian province, and later of the state of Lower Saxony, founded in 1946. Heavy bombing in the Second World War destroyed much of the old town, which was rebuilt in partly modern form.

Economy and sights

Hanover hosts some of the world's largest industrial trade fairs at the Hannover Fairground and is home to companies such as Volkswagen Commercial Vehicles and Continental. Sights include the New Town Hall (Neues Rathaus), the baroque Great Garden at Herrenhausen, the reconstructed Old Town quarter, the Maschsee lake, and the Sprengel Museum of modern art. Leibniz University Hannover is named for the philosopher Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz, who spent much of his career in the city.
