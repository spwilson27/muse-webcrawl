Simple module

In abstract algebra, a simple module over a ring R is a non-zero module M that has no submodules other than 0 and M itself — that is, its only submodules are the trivial ones. Simple modules are also called irreducible modules, and they serve as the basic building blocks of other modules, much as simple groups and prime numbers do in their respective theories.

Examples

  • Over the integers ℤ, the simple modules are exactly the cyclic groups ℤ/pℤ for prime numbers p.
  • Over a field, modules are vector spaces, and the simple ones are the one-dimensional spaces.
  • A simple module over a group ring corresponds to an irreducible representation of the group.

Schur's lemma and structure

Schur's lemma states that every non-zero homomorphism between simple modules over an algebra is an isomorphism, and that the endomorphism ring of a simple module is a division ring. If M is a simple left R-module, then M ≅ R/𝔪 for a maximal left ideal 𝔪. A module that is a direct sum of simple modules is called semisimple; by the Wedderburn–Artin theorem, semisimple rings are exactly finite products of matrix rings over division rings, which is why simple modules and Schur's lemma underpin the representation theory of finite groups and Lie algebras.

Composition series

In a module of finite length, the Jordan–Hölder theorem guarantees that any two composition series — chains of submodules whose successive quotients are simple — have the same length and the same simple quotient factors, up to order. The simple modules thus play the role of "atoms" in the structure theory of modules.
