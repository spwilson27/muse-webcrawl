Branitz Park

Branitz Palace and park near Cottbus

Branitz Park (German: Fürst-Pückler-Park Branitz) is a landscape park in Branitz, near Cottbus in Brandenburg, Germany. It was created from 1846 by Prince Hermann von Pückler-Muskau, the celebrated landscape gardener who had earlier created Muskau Park, after he sold his estate at Muskau and moved to Branitz.

Features

The park is regarded as the prince's last great work and a refined example of the English landscape garden style. Its most striking features are two earth pyramids: a land pyramid and a pyramid rising from the lake (the Tumulus), in which Pückler and his wife Lucie were buried. The park surrounds Branitz Palace (Schloss Branitz), a Baroque palace remodelled under Pückler, which now houses a museum devoted to the prince and the painter Carl Blechen.

Today

The park is maintained by a foundation and is open to the public, covering roughly 112 hectares with zoned landscapes moving from formal pleasure grounds near the palace to a more naturalistic outer park.
