Annexation of Goa

Goa, Daman and Diu were incorporated into India in December 1961
Date18–19 December 1961
OperationOperation Vijay
ResultIndian victory; end of Portuguese India

The annexation of Goa was the process in which the Republic of India annexed the Portuguese Indian territories of Goa, Daman and Diu, starting with the armed action carried out by the Indian Armed Forces in December 1961. In India, this action is referred to as the "Liberation of Goa"; in Portugal, it is sometimes called the "Invasion of Goa".

Background

After Indian independence in 1947, the Indian government sought the return of the Portuguese enclaves, which Portugal — under the Estado Novo regime of António Salazar — refused to negotiate, claiming they were integral parts of metropolitan Portugal. Tension grew through the 1950s, with satyagraha protests, the loss of the Dadra and Nagar Haveli enclaves in 1954, and border incidents.

Operation Vijay

On 18 December 1961, India launched Operation Vijay, a combined air, sea and land offensive involving some 45,000 troops against roughly 3,500 Portuguese defenders. After about 36 hours, the Portuguese Governor-General, Manuel António Vassalo e Silva, surrendered on 19 December, disregarding orders from Lisbon to fight to the last man. Casualties were comparatively light.

Aftermath

Goa, Daman and Diu became a union territory of India; Goa later attained statehood in 1987. Portugal refused to recognise Indian sovereignty until 1974, after the Carnation Revolution. The day of 19 December is celebrated in Goa as Liberation Day.
