Pincian Hill

The Pincian Hill (Italian: Pincio; Latin: Mons Pincius) is a hill in the northeast quadrant of the historical centre of Rome. The hill lies to the north of the Quirinal, overlooking the Campus Martius. It was outside the original boundaries of the ancient city of Rome and was not one of the Seven Hills of Rome, but it lies within the wall built by the Roman emperor Aurelian between 270 and 275.

Ancient Rome

Several important families in ancient Rome had villas and gardens (horti) on the Pincian in the late Roman Republic. The hill was also known as Collis Hortulorum, the "Hill of Gardens". Its name comes from the Pincii, one of the families who occupied it during the 4th century AD. Ancient villas included the Horti Lucullani, the Horti Sallustiani, the Horti Pompeiani, and the Horti Aciliorum. The hill was also home to some of the most important ancient Roman villas, including the Villa Pinciana, which became the Galleria Borghese and sits atop the hill.

Modern era

The Pincio as we see it today was one of the first public parks in Rome, designed by Giuseppe Valadier in the early 19th century. The top of the Pincio has a famous viewpoint facing west, affording a view over the Piazza del Popolo below and the dome of St Peter's Basilica in the distance. The gardens of Villa Borghese gardens, one of Rome's largest public parks, are nearby. Between the Pincio and Villa Borghese is the Villa Medici, seat of the French Academy in Rome.

In the gardens of the Pincio are a number of notable monuments, including an obelisk commissioned by the emperor Hadrian in honour of his lover Antinous, and the famous water clock (Orologio ad acqua) designed by Giovan Battista Embriaco in 1867. Since the later 19th century a series of busts of famous Italians has lined the avenues of the park.
