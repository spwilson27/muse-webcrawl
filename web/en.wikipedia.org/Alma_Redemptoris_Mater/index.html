Alma Redemptoris Mater

Type: Marian antiphon
Language: Latin
Season: Advent to Candlemas (2 February)
Attributed to: Hermannus Contractus (trad.)

Alma Redemptoris Mater ("Loving Mother of the Redeemer") is one of the four seasonal Marian antiphons sung at the end of Compline in the Roman Catholic Liturgy of the Hours. It is assigned to the period from the first Sunday of Advent until the Feast of the Presentation (Candlemas, 2 February). The hymn addresses the Virgin Mary as the gate of heaven and star of the sea, asking her to succour a falling people.

Text and history

The antiphon is traditionally attributed to the eleventh-century monk Hermannus Contractus of Reichenau, though the attribution is uncertain. The text draws on imagery from earlier Marian devotion, including the Ave maris stella. Geoffrey Chaucer's "Prioress's Tale" in The Canterbury Tales is centred on a child's miraculous singing of this antiphon, which the poem calls the Alma Redemptoris.

Musical settings

The chant exists in both a simple and a solemn tone. It has been set polyphonically by many Renaissance composers, including Josquin des Prez, Orlando di Lasso, Tomás Luis de Victoria, and Giovanni Pierluigi da Palestrina, and remains part of the repertoire of Catholic choirs.
