Mediterranean Lingua Franca

The Mediterranean Lingua Franca, or sabir, was a pidgin language used as a lingua franca in the Mediterranean Basin from the 11th to the 19th centuries. It was a basic form of communication used between speakers of different languages, particularly between Arabs and Europeans, for maritime, commercial, and diplomatic contact, and notably in the slave trade and in the Barbary regencies of North Africa.

Structure and vocabulary

The Lingua Franca was based primarily on Italian and Provençal (later also Catalan, Spanish, French and Portuguese) lexicon, with words drawn from Turkish, Persian, Greek and Arabic. Its grammar was drastically simplified: verbs typically appeared in the infinitive, nouns had little inflection, and word order was relatively fixed. It was never anyone's native language; neighbouring varieties are believed to have formed a dialect continuum around the Mediterranean.

Legacy

From this pidgin comes the generic term "lingua franca" for any bridge language. A short dictionary of the language, the Dictionnaire de la langue franque ou petit mauresque, was published anonymously in Marseille in 1830. Sample phrases survive in Molière's comedy Le Bourgeois gentilhomme (1670), where a mock-Turkish ceremony is conducted partly in sabir.
