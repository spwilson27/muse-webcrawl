South Asia is the southern region of Asia, centred on the Indian subcontinent and bounded by the Himalayas to the north, the Indian Ocean to the south, the Hindu Kush to the west, and the hills of Myanmar to the east. It comprises the countries of Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, the Maldives, Nepal, Pakistan, and Sri Lanka, and is home to roughly 1.9 billion people — about one quarter of the world's population.

Geography

The region occupies a distinct tectonic plate, the Indian Plate, whose collision with Eurasia raised the Himalayas, the highest mountain range on Earth. Major river systems — the Indus, the Ganges, and the Brahmaputra — drain from the mountains across fertile alluvial plains. The climate is dominated by the South Asian monsoon, which delivers most of the region's rainfall between June and September.

History

South Asia hosted one of the world's earliest urban civilisations, the Indus Valley civilisation (c. 3300–1300 BCE). Subsequent millennia saw the composition of the Vedas, the rise of Buddhism and Jainism, the Mauryan and Gupta empires, the Delhi Sultanate and the Mughal Empire, and the expansion of Indian Ocean trade. British colonial rule unified much of the region until independence and partition in 1947 created India and Pakistan, followed by Bangladesh in 1971. Hinduism, Islam, Buddhism, Sikhism, and Jainism all originated here, and the region's languages belong mainly to the Indo-Aryan and Dravidian families.

Regional cooperation

The South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation (SAARC), founded in 1985, is the principal intergovernmental organisation, though progress has often been hampered by India–Pakistan tensions.
