GIMP (GNU Image Manipulation Program) is a free and open-source raster graphics editor used for image manipulation, retouching, free-form drawing, transcoding between image file formats, and more specialised tasks. It is extensible through plug-ins and a built-in scripting engine, and is commonly compared with Adobe Photoshop as a no-cost alternative for photo editing and graphic design.

History

GIMP began in 1995 as a project by students Spencer Kimball and Peter Mattis at the University of California, Berkeley. The first public release appeared in February 1996. Originally the acronym stood for "General Image Manipulation Program"; after the project joined the GNU project, it was renamed the GNU Image Manipulation Program. The toolkit created for GIMP, GIMP Toolkit (GTK), became the foundation of the GNOME desktop environment.

The 2.x series, long the stable line of the program, introduced a redesigned interface, the GEGL image-processing library in later releases, and support for higher bit depths. GIMP 3.0, released in March 2025, completed the long-running port to GTK 3 and introduced non-destructive editing with adjustment layers and filters applied as layer effects.

Features

GIMP provides painting tools (brush, pencil, airbrush, clone and healing tools), selection and path tools, layers and layer masks, colour correction curves and levels, and a large library of filters and effects. Its native file format is XCF. Through plug-ins it supports formats including JPEG, PNG, TIFF, GIF, and PSD. Scripting is available through Script-Fu (Scheme) and plug-ins in Python, Perl, and other languages.

Mascot and reception

The GIMP mascot is Wilber, a cartoon animal holding a paintbrush in his mouth, drawn by Tuomas Kuosmanen in 1997. Although praised for its power and zero cost, GIMP has historically been criticised for its interface and for lacking features such as native CMYK support; the 3.0 release addressed many long-standing requests including improved colour management and late binding CMYK workflows.
