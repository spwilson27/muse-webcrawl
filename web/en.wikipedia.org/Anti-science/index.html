Anti-science

Anti-science refers to attitudes, beliefs and movements that reject science and the scientific method, or that oppose the application of scientific findings to public policy. Anti-science positions range from denial of well-established findings — such as the safety and efficacy of vaccines, the role of human activity in climate change, and evolution — to broader philosophical critiques of scientific authority and reductionism.

Forms and causes

Science denialism typically employs tactics catalogued by historians of science: conspiracy theories, cherry-picking data, fake experts, impossible expectations of research, and misrepresentation. Psychological and sociological research links anti-science attitudes to identity-protective cognition, distrust of institutions, misinformation on social media, and conflicts between scientific conclusions and economic or religious commitments. State-sponsored anti-science, such as Lysenkoism in the Soviet Union, which rejected genetics in favour of ideologically acceptable theories, caused lasting damage to Soviet agriculture and biology.

Consequences and responses

Anti-science movements can undermine public health measures, delay environmental action and erode trust in expertise. Responses include science communication, inoculation against misinformation techniques, and policies supporting scientific independence.
