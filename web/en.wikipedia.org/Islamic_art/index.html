Islamic art

Islamic art is a term for the visual arts produced since the 7th century CE by people who lived within territories inhabited or ruled by Muslims. It includes architecture, calligraphy, painting, ceramics, metalwork, textiles, and glass from Spain to Central Asia.

Calligraphy and the word

Because the Qur'an is the word of God, calligraphy became the most esteemed art form in Islamic culture. Scripts such as Kufic, Naskh, and Thuluth were refined for Qur'an manuscripts, architectural inscriptions, and objects, often combined with illumination in gold.

Pattern and arabesque

Islamic decoration is famous for its intricate geometric patterns and the arabesque — interlacing vegetal scrollwork — used across media from tilework and carved stucco to carpets. Aniconism in religious contexts discouraged images of sentient beings in mosques, though figurative art flourished in secular settings, notably Persian, Mughal, and Ottoman miniature painting.

Media

Ceramics achieved high sophistication in lustreware, fritware, and Iznik tiles; metalwork in inlaid brass and bronze; textiles in silk weaving and the celebrated Islamic carpets; and glass in enamelled mosque lamps. The arts of the book — manuscript illumination, miniature painting, and binding — united calligraphy and image.
