World music

Stylistic originsFolk music, traditional music of many cultures
Cultural originsTerm popularized in the 1960s; marketing category from 1987
Typical instrumentsWide variety of regional instruments

World music is a musical category encompassing many different styles of music from around the globe, including traditional, folk, and popular music from non-Western cultures as well as fusion styles combining them with Western genres. The term became common in the music industry in the late 1980s as a marketing category for records that did not fit existing retail bins, though scholars had used it since the 1960s.

Origins of the term

Ethnomusicologist Robert E. Brown is credited with coining "world music" in the early 1960s at Wesleyan University. In 1987, a group of British record-label executives, journalists, and broadcasters formally adopted the term as a retail and chart category, leading record shops to stock "world music" sections and broadcasters to devote airtime to it.

Scope and examples

The category has covered artists as diverse as Nigeria's Fela Kuti (Afrobeat), South Africa's Ladysmith Black Mambazo, Pakistan's Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan (qawwali), Cuba's Buena Vista Social Club, Ireland's traditional and folk performers, and fusion projects such as Paul Simon's Graceland (1986), which introduced South African township music to a mass audience. Annual festivals such as WOMAD, founded by Peter Gabriel in 1982, showcase such music worldwide.

Criticism

The label has been criticized as an overly broad, Eurocentric catch-all that lumps unrelated traditions together and positions Western pop as the unmarked norm. In 2019 the Grammy category "Best World Music Album" was renamed "Best Global Music Album" partly in response to such critiques.
