Battle of Toba–Fushimi

Battle of Toba–Fushimi
Date27–31 January 1868
LocationToba and Fushimi, south of Kyoto, Japan
ResultDecisive Imperial victory
BelligerentsImperial forces of Satsuma, Chōshū and Tosa; Tokugawa shogunate
CommandersSaigō Takamori and Imperial command; Takenaka Shigetaka and shogunal commanders

The Battle of Toba–Fushimi was fought from 27 to 31 January 1868 near Kyoto and was the opening battle of the Boshin War. The forces of the Tokugawa shogunate, marching on Kyoto to reverse the restoration of imperial rule, clashed with the troops of the Satsuma, Chōshū and Tosa domains guarding the roads into the capital at Toba and Fushimi.

Although outnumbered roughly three to one, the Imperial forces were better equipped with modern rifles and artillery, including Armstrong guns and Minié rifles, and their position was transformed when the Imperial Court declared the shogun Tokugawa Yoshinobu an enemy of the court and raised the imperial brocade banner. Demoralised, the shogunal army retreated to Osaka Castle, which Yoshinobu then abandoned, fleeing by warship to Edo.

Significance

Toba–Fushimi decided the fate of the shogunate. The Imperial army advanced east, receiving the bloodless surrender of Edo in May 1868, and although fighting continued in the north until the fall of the Ezo Republic in 1869, the Meiji Restoration was effectively secured by this first battle.
