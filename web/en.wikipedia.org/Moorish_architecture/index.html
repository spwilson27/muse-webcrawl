Moorish architecture is a distinctive style of Islamic architecture that developed in the western Islamic world — the Maghreb (North Africa) and the Iberian Peninsula (al-Andalus) — between the 8th and 16th centuries. The term "Moorish" derives from the Moors, the Muslim inhabitants of these regions. The style synthesised influences from Umayyad Syria, Berber North Africa, and the local Visigothic and Roman traditions of Iberia, producing some of the most celebrated buildings of the medieval world.

Characteristics

Moorish architecture is noted for its horseshoe arches, often with alternating red and white voussoirs (ablaq), intricate carved stucco ornament, geometric and vegetal arabesque patterns, Arabic calligraphy used decoratively, colourful glazed tilework known as zellige, stalactite-like muqarnas vaulting, and serene courtyard gardens organised around pools and fountains. Religious scruples about depicting living beings in sacred contexts encouraged abstract and geometric decoration to an extraordinary level of refinement.

History

Umayyad period

After the Muslim conquest of Hispania in 711, the Umayyad emirate of Córdoba built the Great Mosque of Córdoba (begun 785), whose vast hypostyle hall of double-tiered arches became a prototype for western Islamic architecture.

Taifa, Almoravid and Almohad periods

The fragmentation of the caliphate into taifa kingdoms produced courtly palaces such as the Aljafería in Zaragoza. The Almoravid and Almohad empires, ruling from Marrakesh, spread a more austere monumental style across North Africa and Iberia, exemplified by the Koutoubia Mosque and the Giralda tower in Seville.

Nasrid period

The last Muslim dynasty of Spain, the Nasrids of Granada, created the Alhambra — a palace-city whose delicate ornament, muqarnas domes and water gardens represent the culmination of the Moorish style.

Legacy

After the Christian reconquest, Moorish forms influenced the Mudéjar style used by Christian and Jewish patrons, and later inspired the Moorish Revival architecture of the 19th century in Europe and America.
