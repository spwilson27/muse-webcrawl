Canadian federal election

Canadian federal elections are held to elect members of the House of Commons of Canada. Under the Constitution Act, 1867, a Parliament may last no more than five years; since 2007 federal law has fixed election dates every four years on the third Monday of October, although the governor general may still dissolve Parliament early on the advice of the prime minister.

System

Canada uses first-past-the-post voting in single-member electoral districts ("ridings"). The leader of the party that wins the most seats — or can command the confidence of the House — becomes prime minister. Elections are administered by Elections Canada under the Canada Elections Act.

History

The first federal election followed Confederation in 1867, won by John A. Macdonald's Conservatives. The franchise expanded over time: women voted federally from 1918, and the voting age was lowered to 18 in 1970. Recent general elections include those of 2015, 2019, 2021 and 2025.
