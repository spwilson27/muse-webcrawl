German Instrument of Surrender

Signed8 May 1945
LocationBerlin, Germany
Effective8 May 1945, 23:01 CET
PartiesGerman High Command; Allied Expeditionary Force; Soviet High Command

The German Instrument of Surrender was the legal document that effected the unconditional surrender of Nazi Germany's armed forces to the Allies, ending the Second World War in Europe. It was signed on 8 May 1945 in Berlin by representatives of the German High Command (Oberkommando der Wehrmacht) and representatives of the Supreme Headquarters Allied Expeditionary Force and the Soviet High Command, in the presence of a French representative as witness.

Background

As Allied and Soviet forces closed in on Germany in the spring of 1945, the German government of Admiral Karl Dönitz, who had succeeded Adolf Hitler after his suicide on 30 April 1945, sought to end the war. A first act of military surrender was signed at Reims, France, on 7 May 1945 by General Alfred Jodl, to take effect at 23:01 Central European Time on 8 May. The Soviet leadership, however, insisted that the surrender be ratified in a formal ceremony in Berlin, the captured German capital, arguing that the Reims document was only a preliminary protocol and that a Soviet officer of sufficient rank had not been present.

The Berlin signing

The definitive document was signed shortly before midnight on 8 May 1945 in the Berlin suburb of Karlshorst, in a hall of the Wehrmacht's pioneer school. Field Marshal Wilhelm Keitel signed for the German High Command and the Army, Admiral Hans-Georg von Friedeburg for the Navy, and General Hans-Jürgen Stumpff for the Luftwaffe. Marshal Georgy Zhukov signed on behalf of the Soviet High Command, and Air Chief Marshal Arthur Tedder, deputy to General Dwight D. Eisenhower, signed for the Allied Expeditionary Force. French General Jean de Lattre de Tassigny and American General Carl Spaatz signed as witnesses.

Terms

The brief document required all German armed forces on land, at sea, and in the air to cease operations at 23:01 on 8 May 1945, to remain in their positions, and to surrender their weapons and equipment intact to Allied local commanders. It formed the basis for the subsequent Allied assumption of supreme authority in Germany, formalised in the Berlin Declaration of 5 June 1945.

Commemoration

Because the signing took place late on 8 May Central European Time, it was already 9 May in Moscow, which is why Russia and several other countries commemorate Victory Day on 9 May, while most Western countries mark Victory in Europe Day (V-E Day) on 8 May. The Karlshorst building now houses the Museum Berlin-Karlshorst, where the surrender hall is preserved.
