Effective interest rate

The effective interest rate (EIR), effective annual interest rate, annual equivalent rate (AER) or simply effective rate is the percentage of interest on a loan or financial product if compound interest accumulates over a year during which no payments are made. It is the rate that, when applied to the initial balance, produces the same final amount over one year, taking compounding into account.

Calculation

The effective annual rate is calculated from the nominal interest rate and the number of compounding periods per year:

EIR = (1 + i / n)n − 1

where i is the nominal annual rate and n is the number of compounding periods per year. For example, a nominal rate of 12% compounded monthly gives an effective rate of (1.01)12 − 1 ≈ 12.68%.

Comparison with APR

Unlike the annual percentage rate (APR), which in many jurisdictions is calculated on a nominal basis and may include fees, the effective interest rate reflects compounding alone (in some definitions it may also fold in fees, as in the European Union's "annual percentage rate of charge"). Because compounding makes the effective rate exceed the nominal rate whenever interest is compounded more than once a year, the EIR is a more accurate measure of the true cost of borrowing or return on saving.

Continuous compounding

As the number of compounding periods grows without bound, the effective rate approaches ei − 1, where e is Euler's number. Banks and economists use this limiting case for theoretical models of continuously compounded growth.
