Pariah state

A pariah state (also called an international pariah or global pariah) is a nation considered to be excluded or ostracized by most of the international community, typically because of its domestic repression, aggression, weapons programs, or defiance of international law and norms. The term has no formal legal definition and is applied politically.

Usage and examples

The label was widely applied to apartheid-era South Africa, which faced arms embargoes, sanctions, and expulsion from international bodies; to Rhodesia after its unilateral declaration of independence; and in more recent decades to North Korea over its nuclear weapons program and human rights record. States so described may face comprehensive sanctions, diplomatic isolation, and exclusion from events and organizations.

Characteristics

Pariah status usually combines several features: systematic human rights abuses, pursuit of weapons of mass destruction, support for terrorism, or territorial aggression, together with refusal to comply with United Nations resolutions. Unlike the term "rogue state", which emphasizes perceived threats to others, "pariah" emphasizes social exclusion from the community of nations.
