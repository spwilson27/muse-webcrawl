An inductor, also called a coil, choke, or reactor, is a passive two-terminal electrical component that stores energy in a magnetic field when electric current flows through it. An inductor typically consists of an insulated wire wound into a coil around a core of air, iron, or ferrite. When the current flowing through the coil changes, the time-varying magnetic field induces an electromotive force (voltage) in the conductor that opposes the change in current, in accordance with Faraday's law of induction and Lenz's law.

Theory

The property describing an inductor's effectiveness is its inductance L, measured in henries (H): one henry is one volt-second per ampere. The voltage across an ideal inductor is proportional to the rate of change of current, V = L (dI/dt), and the energy stored is E = ½ L I². Inductance increases with the number of turns, the cross-sectional area of the coil, and the magnetic permeability of the core, and decreases with coil length. In AC circuits an inductor presents an inductive reactance XL = 2πfL that increases with frequency, so inductors pass low frequencies more readily than high ones; in a DC steady state an ideal inductor behaves as a short circuit.

Construction and types

Inductors range from tiny surface-mount components in phones to metre-scale reactors in power grids. Common forms include air-core coils (no saturation, low inductance), iron-core chokes, ferrite-core inductors for high frequencies, toroidal inductors with low external fields, and variable inductors with movable cores. Practical inductors also exhibit resistance, parasitic capacitance between windings, and core losses (hysteresis and eddy currents), summarised in part by the quality factor Q.

Applications

Inductors are used in filters (with capacitors forming LC and resonant circuits), radio tuning, transformers (two coupled inductors), energy storage in switched-mode power supplies and DC–DC converters, electric motors and generators, fluorescent-lamp ballasts, and as chokes that suppress radio-frequency interference on power and signal lines.
