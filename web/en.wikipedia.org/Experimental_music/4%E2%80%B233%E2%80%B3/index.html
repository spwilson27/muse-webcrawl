4′33″

David Tudor's premiere of 4′33″ in Woodstock, New York, framed the ambient sounds of the hall as the music itself.

4′33″ (pronounced "four minutes thirty-three seconds") is a three-movement composition by American experimental composer John Cage, composed in 1952 for any instrument or combination of instruments. The score instructs performers not to play their instruments during the entire duration of the piece, which lasts four minutes and thirty-three seconds. The sounds of the environment heard by the audience during the performance are therefore the content of the work.

Premiere and concept

Pianist David Tudor premiered the piece on 29 August 1952 at the Maverick Concert Hall in Woodstock, New York, marking the start of each movement by closing the piano's keyboard lid and the end by opening it. Cage's ideas were shaped by his experience in an anechoic chamber — where he still heard the sounds of his own nervous system and circulation — and by Robert Rauschenberg's white paintings. The work challenges definitions of music and musicality, and is widely regarded as one of the most famous and controversial compositions of the twentieth century.
