James Stanley Brakhage (January 14, 1933 – March 9, 2003) was an American filmmaker. He is considered to be one of the most important figures in experimental film, with a body of nearly 400 works spanning 1952 to 2003 — lyrical films, autobiographical films, song cycles, and films made without a camera by painting or scratching directly on film stock or collaging material onto it.

Career

Adopted as an infant and raised in Denver, Brakhage made his first film, Interim, in 1952. Early psychodramas such as Desistfilm (1954) and The Wonder Ring (1955, made for Joseph Cornell) gave way to lyrical explorations of perception and family life: Window Water Baby Moving (1959) documented the birth of his first child, and the epic Dog Star Man (1961–64) became his best-known work. Mothlight (1963) was made by pressing moth wings and other debris between tape and printing it, without a camera. The Act of Seeing with One's Own Eyes (1971), filmed in the Pittsburgh morgue, is often cited among the greatest experimental films.

Vision and writings

Brakhage sought an "untutored eye" — a vision unprejudiced by language, naming, and conventional composition — most famously argued in his 1963 essay collection Metaphors on Vision. He taught film history at the University of Colorado Boulder from 1981 and made extensive hand-painted films in his final decades.

Legacy

Brakhage's films are preserved by the Academy Film Archive and Anthology Film Archives. He was married to Jane Brakhage (née Wodening) from 1957 to 1987 and later to Marilyn Brakhage, who continues to curate his legacy.
