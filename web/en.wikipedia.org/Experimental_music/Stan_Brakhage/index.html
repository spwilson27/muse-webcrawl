Stan Brakhage

Brakhage pioneered painting and scratching directly on film.

James Stanley Brakhage (January 14, 1933 – March 9, 2003), better known as Stan Brakhage, was an American experimental filmmaker. One of the most important figures in 20th-century avant-garde cinema, he made nearly 400 films over five decades, most of them silent and exploring the nature of vision itself.

Career

Born in Kansas City, Missouri, Brakhage was adopted and raised in Denver, Colorado. His early works, such as Desistfilm (1954) and Reflections on Black (1955), were psychodramas influenced by Maya Deren and Kenneth Anger. Anticipation of the Night (1958) marked his turn toward a subjective, first-person camera style.

Major works

Window Water Baby Moving (1959) documented the birth of his first child. Dog Star Man (1961–64), a multi-part epic, layered images of a man climbing a mountain with cosmic and bodily imagery. Mothlight (1963) was made without a camera by pressing moth wings and debris between strips of film. Later works, including The Text of Light (1974), filmed light refracted through a glass ashtray, and his late hand-painted films, explored pure visual sensation.

Techniques and influence

Brakhage scratched, painted, and collaged directly onto film stock, advocatedd an "untutored eye" free of conventional perspective and naming, and wrote influential books including Metaphors on Vision (1963). He taught for many years at the University of Colorado Boulder. His work reshaped structural film and lyrical film, influencing generations of experimental filmmakers.
