Experimental music

Experimental music is a general label for any music or music genre that pushes existing boundaries and genre definitions. Experimental compositional practice is defined broadly by sensitivity to radical and exploratory aesthetics, in opposition to institutionalized, conventional, "established" compositional procedures. The composer John Cage was a pioneer of the genre.

Origins

The French composer Pierre Schaeffer pioneered musique concrète in the late 1940s, composing with recorded sounds rather than musical instruments. Around the same time, Karlheinz Stockhausen and others developed electronic music at the WDR studio in Cologne. John Cage's aleatoric (chance) music—notably 4′33″ (1952)—and his use of the prepared piano fundamentally questioned the nature of music itself.

Minimalism and after

In the 1960s and 1970s, minimal music (Terry Riley, Steve Reich, Philip Glass, La Monte Young) reduced composition to repeated patterns and slow processes. The genre has since diversified into noise music, drone, ambient (Brian Eno), electroacoustic improvisation, glitch, and countless hybrid forms.
