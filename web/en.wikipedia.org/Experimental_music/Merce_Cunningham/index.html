Merce Cunningham

BornApril 16, 1919, Centralia, Washington, US
DiedJuly 26, 2009 (aged 90), New York City
OccupationDancer, choreographer
Known forMerce Cunningham Dance Company; chance operations in choreography

Mercier Philip "Merce" Cunningham (April 16, 1919 – July 26, 2009) was an American dancer and choreographer who was at the forefront of American modern dance for more than 50 years. Together with collaborators including composer John Cage, his life partner, he developed radical innovations — the use of chance procedures, the independence of dance and music, and later computer-assisted choreography — that reshaped the art form.

Career

Cunningham danced with the Martha Graham Dance Company from 1939 to 1945, creating roles in works such as Appalachian Spring. He gave his first solo concert with John Cage in 1944 and formed the Merce Cunningham Dance Company in 1953 at Black Mountain College. Landmark works include Suite by Chance (1953), the first dance composed using chance operations; Summerspace (1958) with music by Morton Feldman and decor by Robert Rauschenberg; and Biped (1999), which integrated motion-captured digital figures.

Methods

Following Cage's example, Cunningham used the I Ching to make choreographic decisions about sequence, space, and timing, treating movement as an end in itself rather than as narrative or emotional expression. Dance and music were composed independently and only brought together at performance. From the late 1980s he choreographed with the computer program LifeForms (later DanceForms).

Legacy

Cunningham received the Kennedy Center Honors (1985), a MacArthur Fellowship (1985), and Japan's Praemium Imperiale (2005). Following his death, the company completed a two-year world tour and disbanded in accordance with his Legacy Plan, with his works licensed by the Merce Cunningham Trust.
