Structural film

Structural film was an experimental film movement prominent in the United States in the 1960s and early 1970s, within the broader context of underground and avant-garde cinema. The term was coined by the critic and theorist P. Adams Sitney in his influential 1969 essay "Structural Film," published in Film Culture magazine.

Characteristics

Sitney identified four formal characteristics commonly found in structural films: a fixed camera position (or the appearance of one), the flicker effect, loop printing, and rephotography off a screen. In a structural film, the shape of the film itself — its overall predetermined structure — is the primary content, more important than any individual images or narrative. The films demanded a new kind of spectatorial attention, foregrounding duration, repetition, and the material properties of the medium.

Filmmakers

Key practitioners included Michael Snow, whose Wavelength (1967), a 45-minute slow zoom across a loft, is considered the movement's landmark work; Hollis Frampton (Zorns Lemma, 1970; (nostalgia), 1971); Tony Conrad (The Flicker, 1966); Paul Sharits; Ernie Gehr (Serene Velocity, 1970); George Landow (George Landow/Owen Land); and Joyce Wieland. In Britain, a related tendency called structural/materialist film developed around the London Film-Makers' Co-operative, with filmmakers such as Peter Gidal, who also theorized the movement.

Relation to Stan Brakhage

Stan Brakhage, the most prolific figure of American avant-garde film, represented a contrasting, lyrical and subjective approach — hand-held camera, painted and scratched film, and an emphasis on vision and perception as in Dog Star Man (1961–64) and Mothlight (1963). Sitney contrasted structural film's impersonal, minimal forms with Brakhage's romantic expressivism, though the categories overlapped: Brakhage's work also engaged film materiality, and the two tendencies together defined the poles of postwar American experimental cinema.

Legacy

Structural film reshaped film theory through its alliance with minimalism and conceptual art, and its techniques — loops, flicker, fixed frames — have been absorbed into gallery installation art and digital media practice.
