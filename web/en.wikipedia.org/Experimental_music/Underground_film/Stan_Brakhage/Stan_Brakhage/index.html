Stan Brakhage

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Stan Brakhage (1933–2003) was an American filmmaker and a central figure of the post-war underground film movement. Working largely outside the commercial film industry, in 16 mm and with minimal budgets, he created a vast body of personal, non-narrative films and helped build the alternative institutions — film cooperatives, screening series, and critical journals — on which underground cinema depended.

Career in the underground

Brakhage made his first film, Interim, in 1952, and through the 1950s was part of the New York circle of avant-garde filmmakers around Maya Deren, Jonas Mekas, and Kenneth Anger. Films such as Anticipation of the Night (1958) established his signature subjective style: the camera as an eye and body, recording birth, domestic life, landscape, and death. His frank filming of childbirth in Window Water Baby Moving (1959) was long restricted by censorship and screened mainly through film societies and the Film-Makers' Cooperative, founded in 1962 to distribute underground work outside commercial channels.

From 1953 he lived mostly in Colorado, touring widely as a lecturer. His films were almost all silent, hand-made, and increasingly, in his last decades, painted directly onto the film strip. Brakhage's prolific output and his polemical writings made him the exemplary underground auteur: independent of studios, stars, and stories.
