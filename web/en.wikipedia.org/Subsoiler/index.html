Subsoiler

A multi-shank subsoiler working a field.

A subsoiler or flat lifter is a tractor-mounted farm implement used for deep tillage, loosening and breaking up soil at depths below those worked by normal cultivators, ploughs or disc harrows. Most cultivating tools work the top 5–15 cm of soil; a subsoiler can break up compacted layers 30–60 cm deep, including the "hardpan" that repeated shallow tillage and traffic create just below the plough layer.

Design and use

The implement consists of one or more heavy, curved or straight shanks (tines) mounted on a robust frame, each tipped with a replaceable point or "foot", sometimes with small wings to increase shattering. Because the draft force required is very large, subsoiling demands powerful tractors and is usually done when the soil is relatively dry, so that the compacted layer fractures rather than smearing. The operation improves drainage, root penetration and water infiltration while leaving most crop residue on the surface, making it compatible with conservation tillage systems.

Subsoilers are also used, in smaller single-shank form, to lay cables or pipes and to install drainage, and in forestry and land reclamation to rip compacted ground before planting.
