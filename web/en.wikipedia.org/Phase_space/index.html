Phase space

FieldClassical mechanics, dynamical systems, statistical mechanics
CoordinatesGeneralized positions qi and conjugate momenta pi
Dimension2n for n degrees of freedom
Key resultsLiouville's theorem; Poincaré recurrence theorem

In dynamical systems theory and classical mechanics, a phase space is a space in which all possible states of a system are represented, with each possible state corresponding to one unique point. For a mechanical system, the phase space usually consists of all possible values of position and momentum variables: a system with n degrees of freedom has a 2n-dimensional phase space with coordinates (q1, …, qn, p1, …, pn).

Concept

As a system evolves in time, its representative point traces out a curve in phase space called a trajectory (or orbit). The evolution is governed by Hamilton's equations in the Hamiltonian formulation. For a simple pendulum, the phase space is two-dimensional (angle and angular momentum), and its trajectories are the familiar closed loops for oscillation and open waves for rotation, shown in a phase portrait.

Liouville's theorem

For Hamiltonian systems, Liouville's theorem states that the volume of a region of phase space is preserved under the flow — the "phase fluid" is incompressible, even though the region may become stretched and folded into complicated shapes. This theorem underlies statistical mechanics, where probability distributions over phase space (ensembles) are studied; the density of states and partition functions are computed as phase-space integrals.

Quantum mechanics

In quantum mechanics the classical phase space gives way to Hilbert space, but phase-space formulations of quantum mechanics exist, notably the Wigner quasi-probability distribution, which represents quantum states by functions on a classical-like phase space.

Related ideas

The more general notion of a state space applies to any dynamical system, including ecological, economic, and control systems. Concepts such as attractors, chaos, and Poincaré sections are all analysed in phase space; in chaotic systems nearby trajectories diverge exponentially, measured by Lyapunov exponents.
