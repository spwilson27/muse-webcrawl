Khmelnytsky Uprising

The Khmelnytsky Uprising (1648–1657) was a Cossack rebellion in the Ukrainian territories of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, led by Hetman Bohdan Khmelnytsky of the Zaporozhian Host, allied with the Crimean Tatars. It created a Cossack Hetmanate and permanently weakened the Commonwealth.

Causes and course

Grievances included magnate expansion, restrictions on registered Cossacks, and religious pressure on the Orthodox after the Union of Brest. Khmelnytsky, wronged personally by a Polish official, won victories at Zhovti Vody and Korsun (1648) and Pyliavtsi; the revolt spread and was accompanied by massacres of Poles, Jews and Uniates. The Treaty of Zboriv (1649) recognised a Hetmanate in three voivodeships; defeat at Berestechko (1651) was followed by the Treaty of Bila Tserkva.

Pereyaslav and aftermath

In 1654 Khmelnytsky concluded the Pereyaslav Agreement placing the Hetmanate under the Tsar's protection, triggering the Russo-Polish War (1654–1667) and coinciding with the Swedish Deluge. The Truce of Andrusovo (1667) split Ukraine along the Dnieper between Poland and Russia.
