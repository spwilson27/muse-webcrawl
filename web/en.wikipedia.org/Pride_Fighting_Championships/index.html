Pride Fighting Championships

Pride Fighting Championships (Pride or Pride FC) was a Japanese mixed martial arts promotion company founded in 1997. At its peak in the early 2000s, Pride was the largest and most popular MMA organization in the world, drawing crowds of more than 40,000 to the Tokyo Dome and Saitama Super Arena and featuring many of the era's greatest fighters.

History

The promotion began with an inaugural event in October 1997 headlined by Rickson Gracie. Under Dream Stage Entertainment, Pride developed a distinctive presentation—elaborate entrances, a boxing-style ring rather than a cage, and rules that permitted soccer kicks and knees to a downed opponent while prohibiting elbows. Its Grand Prix tournaments and champions such as Fedor Emelianenko, Wanderlei Silva, Mirko "Cro Cop" Filipović, Antônio Rodrigo Nogueira, and Mauricio "Shogun" Rua made it legendary among fans.

Sale and closure

After losing its Japanese television contract amid a scandal linking its backers to organized crime allegations in 2006, Pride's assets were sold in 2007 to Zuffa, the parent company of the UFC. The merged rosters reshaped the sport, and Pride ceased operations later that year, though its era is still widely regarded as a golden age of heavyweight and light heavyweight MMA.
