Young adult

Young adults in a campus setting

A young adult is a person in the stage of life between adolescence and full adulthood. Definitions vary: Erik Erikson's stages of psychosocial development place young adulthood roughly between the ages of 20 and 40, while many statistical and demographic usages treat it as spanning the late teens to the late twenties or thirties. The United Nations, for statistical purposes, defines "youth" as those aged 15 to 24, and the term young adult is often used for those roughly 18 to 30.

Development

Young adulthood is characterised by the establishment of identity, intimate relationships, and independence from the family of origin. Erikson framed the central conflict of the stage as "intimacy versus isolation". The psychologist Jeffrey Arnett proposed the additional stage of "emerging adulthood" (about 18–29) for the prolonged transition seen in industrialised societies, marked by identity exploration, instability, self-focus, feeling in-between, and a sense of possibilities.

Milestones

Common milestones associated with the stage include completing education, entering the workforce, leaving the parental home, forming long-term partnerships, and parenthood. In many developed countries these milestones now occur later than in the mid-20th century, a trend attributed to longer education, housing costs, and changing labour markets.

In media and publishing

"Young adult" (YA) is also a major category in publishing and film, aimed at readers roughly 12 to 18 years old and often featuring adolescent protagonists; despite the name, the category is distinguished from the developmental stage and has a substantial adult readership.
