Tuareg people


A Tuareg man wearing the indigo tagelmust

The Tuareg people are a large Berber ethnic group inhabiting the Sahara across northern Mali, Niger, Algeria, Libya, and Burkina Faso. Traditionally nomadic pastoralists, they call themselves by names such as Kel Tamasheq ("speakers of Tamasheq"), and are famous for the indigo-dyed veils worn by their men, which earned them the name "Blue People".

Society and culture

Tuareg society was historically organized into confederations with a stratified caste system of nobles, vassals, artisans, and slaves. Unusually for the region, descent and inheritance traditionally followed matrilineal lines, and women enjoy relatively high status and literacy in the ancient Tifinagh script, which the Tuareg preserved. Their caravans once dominated trans-Saharan trade in salt and gold.

Modern history

Colonial borders divided Tuareg lands among new states, and a series of rebellions—in Mali and Niger from the 1960s onward, and the 2012 rebellion that briefly proclaimed the state of Azawad—have sought autonomy. Drought, desertification, and uranium and oil development have pressured the nomadic way of life; Tuareg guitar music, developed in exile, has brought their culture worldwide attention through artists such as Tinariwen.
