Francis Newton Souza

Francis Newton Souza (12 April 1924 – 28 March 2002) was an Indian artist and a founding member of the Bombay Progressive Artists' Group. He was one of the first Indian artists to achieve international recognition after independence, and his expressionist paintings are among the most valuable works of modern Indian art at auction.

Life

Souza was born in Saligao, Goa, to Goan Catholic parents, and raised in Bombay. He studied at the Sir J. J. School of Art, from which he was expelled in 1945 for participating in the Quit India Movement. In 1947 he co-founded the Progressive Artists' Group with artists including M. F. Husain, S. H. Raza, and K. H. Ara, seeking to break with the nationalist Bengal School and create a modern Indian art. Souza moved to London in 1949, where he struggled for several years before achieving success in the mid-1950s with exhibitions at Gallery One and the support of the critic John Berger. In 1967 he settled in New York, where he lived for the rest of his life.

Work

Souza's work is characterized by distorted, aggressive figure painting, heads rendered with multiple eyes and contorted features, and recurring subjects drawn from his Catholic upbringing — crucifixions, saints, and the architecture of Goan churches — alongside landscapes and erotic nudes. His 1955 painting Birth and other works set successive auction records for Indian modern art in the 2000s and 2010s.
