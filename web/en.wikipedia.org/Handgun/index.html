Handgun

A handgun is a short-barreled firearm designed to be held and operated with one or two hands. The two main subtypes are revolvers, which feed from a rotating cylinder, and semi-automatic pistols, which use the energy of firing to cycle the action and chamber the next round from a magazine. Other forms include single-shot pistols, derringers and machine pistols.

History

The earliest handguns appeared in China and Europe in the medieval period as small hand cannons. Wheel-lock, flintlock and caplock pistols followed, typically muzzle-loading and often carried in pairs by cavalry. The invention of the revolving cylinder, mass-produced by Colt from the 1830s, and of self-contained metallic cartridges made practical repeating handguns possible. By the 1890s designers such as Hugo Borchardt, the Mauser brothers and John Browning produced the first successful semi-automatic pistols, a pattern that dominates today.

Types

Revolvers hold cartridges (usually five to eight) in a cylinder that rotates to align each chamber with the barrel; they may be single-action, double-action, or double-action-only. Semi-automatic pistols store ammunition in a detachable box magazine, usually in the grip, with capacities from about six to more than twenty rounds. Modern service pistols such as the Glock, SIG Sauer P320 and Beretta 92 are widely issued to military and police forces.

Uses and regulation

Handguns are used by armed forces as sidearms, by police, for sport shooting, hunting (in some jurisdictions) and personal defence. Because they are easily concealable and are the firearms most commonly involved in crime, handguns are subject to specific regulation in many countries, varying from licensing and registration regimes to near-total prohibition of civilian ownership.
