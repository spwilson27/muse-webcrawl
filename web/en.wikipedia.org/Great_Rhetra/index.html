Great Rhetra

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Great Rhetra (Greek: Μεγάλη Ῥήτρα, "great pronouncement" or "great ordinance") is the name given to the foundational constitutional enactment of ancient Sparta, traditionally attributed to the lawgiver Lycurgus. Preserved in a paraphrase by Plutarch in his Life of Lycurgus, it purports to record an oracle from Delphi authorising Sparta's governing institutions.

Contents

The Rhetra directs the founding of temples to Zeus and Athena, the organisation of the citizens into tribes (phylai) and villages (obai), and the establishment of a council of elders — the Gerousia — of thirty members including the two kings. Regular assemblies (apella) of the citizen body were to meet between Babyca and Cnacion, where proposals were to be put and the people held final decision. A later rider, attributed to the kings Theopompus and Polydorus, provided that if the people adopted a "crooked" decision the elders and kings could dissolve the assembly.

Historical questions

Scholars debate whether the Rhetra is a genuine archaic document — possibly dating to the 7th century BC — or a later construction, and its relationship to a fragment of the poet Tyrtaeus's Eunomia, which describes a similar settlement. Whatever its exact origin, the Rhetra is the earliest evidence for the characteristic Spartan mixed constitution of kings, council, and assembly, and a key text in the study of early Greek constitutional thought.
