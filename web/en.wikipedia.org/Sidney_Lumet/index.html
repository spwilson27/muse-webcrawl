Sidney Lumet

Sidney Arthur Lumet (June 25, 1924 – April 9, 2011) was an American film director, producer, and screenwriter. With over 50 films to his credit, he was nominated for five Academy Awards and was known for socially conscious dramas often set in New York City.

Career

Lumet began as a child actor on Broadway and in Yiddish theatre, then directed live television dramas in the 1950s. His film debut, 12 Angry Men (1957), a courtroom drama set almost entirely in a jury room, won the Golden Bear at Berlin and established his reputation.

Major films

His subsequent works include Serpico (1973), Dog Day Afternoon (1975), Network (1976), The Verdict (1982), and Running on Empty (1988). Late in his career he directed Before the Devil Knows You're Dead (2007). In 2005 he received an Academy Honorary Award.

Style

Lumet was noted for fast, economical shooting, naturalistic performances, and meticulous preparation, rehearsing extensively so that filming proceeded rapidly. His book Making Movies (1995) is regarded as a classic account of the craft.
