Provinces of Sweden

The 25 traditional provinces (landskap) of Sweden.

The provinces of Sweden (Swedish: Sveriges landskap) are historical, geographical and cultural regions that traditionally divide the country. Sweden has 25 provinces, among them Dalarna, Skåne (Scania), Gotland, Lappland, Småland, Uppland, Värmland and Ångermanland. They are grouped into three great lands: Götaland in the south, Svealand in the centre and Norrland in the north.

Status

The provinces have had no administrative function since the county reforms of the seventeenth century replaced them with counties (län) as units of government; Sweden today has 21 counties whose boundaries often, but not always, follow provincial lines. Nevertheless the provinces remain vivid cultural identities: each has its own coat of arms, dialect colouring, traditional dress, and official provincial flower, animal and fish. Swedes commonly identify their home province, and the provinces are used in marketing, tourism and sport.

Historical notes

Several southern provinces — Skåne, Halland and Blekinge — were Danish until the Treaty of Roskilde in 1658, and Bohuslän was Norwegian; their inclusion reflects Sweden's seventeenth-century expansion. The arms of the provinces are borne in the greater coat of arms of Sweden and by members of the royal family, who hold ducal titles of provinces.
