Tecumseh

Bornc. 1768, Ohio Country
DiedOctober 5, 1813 (aged c. 45), Moravian Town, Upper Canada
NationalityShawnee
Known forLeader of Tecumseh's Confederacy; War of 1812

Tecumseh (c. 1768 – October 5, 1813) was a Shawnee chief and warrior who promoted resistance to the expansion of the United States onto Native American lands. He traveled widely, forming a Native American confederacy and promoting intertribal unity. A persuasive orator, he became one of the most celebrated Native American leaders in history.

Early life

Tecumseh was born in the Ohio Country around 1768, during a period of frontier warfare. His father, Puckshinwa, was killed at the Battle of Point Pleasant in 1774, and Tecumseh was raised by his elder brother Cheeseekau. He fought in the Northwest Indian War as a young man.

The confederacy

With his younger brother Tenskwatawa, known as the Prophet, who led a religious revival, Tecumseh established Prophetstown in Indiana in 1808. He argued that Native land was held in common and could not be ceded by individual tribes. While Tecumseh was away recruiting allies in 1811, territorial governor William Henry Harrison attacked and burned Prophetstown after the Battle of Tippecanoe.

War of 1812 and death

In the War of 1812 Tecumseh allied with the British, helping capture Detroit, and was commissioned a brigadier general. He was killed at the Battle of the Thames on October 5, 1813, after which his confederacy collapsed.

Legacy

Tecumseh has been honored in place names, naval vessels, and literature. Civil War general William Tecumseh Sherman was named in part for him.
