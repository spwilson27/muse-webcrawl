The National Trails System is a series of trails in the United States designated "to promote the preservation of, public access to, travel within, and enjoyment and appreciation of the open-air, outdoor areas and historic resources of the Nation." Created by the National Trails System Act of 1968 (Public Law 90-543), the system is administered jointly by federal land agencies in partnership with states, local governments, and non-profit trail organisations.

Categories

National scenic trails are long-distance routes through landscapes of exceptional natural beauty; the first two designated in 1968 were the Appalachian Trail and the Pacific Crest Trail. There are now 11, including the Continental Divide Trail and the Arizona and Florida Trails. National historic trails (19) commemorate routes of travel significant in American history, such as the Oregon, California, Mormon Pioneer, Lewis and Clark, and Trail of Tears trails. National recreation trails (more than 1,300) are shorter regional and local trails recognised by the Secretaries of the Interior and Agriculture.

Administration

Only Congress can designate national scenic and historic trails. The National Park Service administers most historic trails and several scenic trails; the Forest Service and Bureau of Land Management administer others, and partners such as the Appalachian Trail Conservancy and the Pacific Crest Trail Association perform much of the volunteer maintenance.
