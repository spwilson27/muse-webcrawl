The American Indian Wars, also known as the American Frontier Wars, and the Indian Wars, were the numerous armed conflicts between European governments and colonists, and later the United States government and its colonists, and the various indigenous peoples of North America. These wars spanned a period of over 300 years, from the early colonial period in the 17th century until the early 20th century, and ranged across the entire continent.

Overview

Conflicts varied from full-scale wars — such as King Philip's War (1675–76) in New England, among the deadliest per capita in American history — to localised raids and massacres. Native nations frequently allied with one European power against another, as in the French and Indian War (1754–63). After American independence, the United States fought wars in the Old Northwest (including Little Turtle's War and Tecumseh's War), in the Southeast (leading to the Trail of Tears removals of the 1830s), and across the Great Plains, Southwest, and Pacific Northwest after the Civil War.

Western wars, 1866–1890

The best-known phase of the wars followed the American Civil War, as settlement, railroads, and mining pressed westward. Campaigns included Red Cloud's War (1866–68), the Great Sioux War of 1876–77 — including the Lakota and Cheyenne victory at the Battle of the Little Bighorn — the Nez Perce War (1877), and the Apache Wars in the Southwest, ending with Geronimo's surrender in 1886. The massacre of Lakota at Wounded Knee Creek on 29 December 1890 is commonly taken as the end of the Indian Wars era, though sporadic violence continued into the 1920s (for example, the Posey War of 1923 in Utah).

Impact

The wars, combined with disease, displacement, and federal policies of removal and allotment, devastated Native populations and landholdings. Native land shrank from the whole continent to reservations totalling about 56 million acres by the 1930s. The conflicts remain a subject of historical reassessment, including ongoing debates over terminology, commemoration, and the return of remains and artefacts.
