The Karamanids (Turkish: Karamanoğulları) were a dynasty of Turkmen origin that ruled the Karamanid Beylik, one of the Anatolian beyliks, in south-central Anatolia from the mid-13th century until 1487. At its height it was the most powerful and longest-lasting rival of the rising Ottoman beylik, and the two states fought repeatedly over Konya and the central Anatolian plateau.

History

The dynasty descended from Karaman Bey, a Turkmen leader who gained control of the mountainous region around Ermenek and Larende as Seljuk authority collapsed after the Mongol victory at Köse Dağ (1243). His successors expanded toward the Mediterranean coast and seized Konya, the former Seljuk capital, several times.

The Karamanids are remembered in Turkish national tradition for Mehmed Bey's 1277 decree making Turkish the official language of his state, issued after he took Konya. Through the 14th and 15th centuries they allied variously with the Mamluks of Egypt, Venice and Hungary against the Ottomans. After repeated campaigns by Mehmed II and Bayezid II, the last Karamanid territories were annexed by the Ottomans in 1487, and much of the population was deported to other parts of the empire; the Karamanlides, Turkish-speaking Orthodox Christians of the region, preserve the dynasty's name.
