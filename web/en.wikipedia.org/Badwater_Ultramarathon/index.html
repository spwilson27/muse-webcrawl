The Badwater Ultramarathon is a 135-mile (217 km) ultramarathon race starting at Badwater Basin in Death Valley and ending at Whitney Portal, the trailhead to Mount Whitney. It is frequently described as "the world's toughest foot race".

Course

The race begins at Badwater Basin, 282 feet (86 m) below sea level, the lowest point in North America, and finishes at Whitney Portal at 8,360 feet (2,548 m). The course crosses three mountain ranges and accumulates roughly 14,600 feet (4,450 m) of elevation gain. Temperatures on the valley floor can exceed 120 °F (49 °C) during the July race window.

History

The first solo crossing on foot was completed by Al Arnold in 1977 after two failed attempts. Jay Birmingham repeated the feat in 1981, and the first competitive race was held in 1987. The event was later organized by AdventureCORPS under race director Chris Kostman.

Notable competitors

Multiple-time champions include Scott Jurek, who won in 2005 and 2006 and set a course record of 24:36:08 in 2005, and Marshall Ulrich, a four-time winner. In 2019 Yoshihiko Ishikawa set the current men's record and Patrycja Bereznowska set the women's record.
