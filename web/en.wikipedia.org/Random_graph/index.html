Random graph

In mathematics, a random graph is a graph generated by a random process. The theory of random graphs lies at the interface between graph theory and probability theory, and was founded by Paul Erdős and Alfréd Rényi in their 1959–1961 papers.

Erdős–Rényi models

The two classic models are G(n, p), in which each of the possible edges among n vertices is included independently with probability p, and G(n, M), in which a graph is chosen uniformly among all graphs with n vertices and M edges.

Properties

Many graph properties exhibit threshold behaviour: as p passes a critical value, properties such as connectedness or containing a Hamiltonian cycle appear with probability tending to one. Random graphs also model real networks, though real-world networks often show clustering and degree distributions better captured by later models such as Watts–Strogatz and Barabási–Albert.
