Transfer of sovereignty over Macau

The transfer of sovereignty over Macau from Portugal to the People's Republic of China took place on 20 December 1999, ending 442 years of Portuguese administration. Macau became a Special Administrative Region (SAR) of China under the principle of "one country, two systems".

Background

Portuguese traders had settled in Macau from 1557. After the 1974 Carnation Revolution, Portugal sought to return its overseas territories; the 1987 Sino-Portuguese Joint Declaration set the date and terms for the handover, modelled in part on the earlier Hong Kong declaration.

The handover

At midnight on 20 December 1999 the Portuguese flag was lowered and the flag of China raised. Under the Basic Law of the Macao SAR, Macau retains its own legal system, currency (the pataca) and a high degree of autonomy for 50 years, while its casino-based economy continued to expand dramatically in the following decades.
