Amenorrhea
SpecialtyGynaecology, endocrinology
TypesPrimary (no menarche by age 15) and secondary (cessation after established cycles)
CausesPregnancy, hormonal disorders, stress, low body weight, anatomical causes

Amenorrhea (also spelled amenorrhoea) is the absence of a menstrual period in a woman of reproductive age. Physiological states of amenorrhea include pregnancy, breastfeeding, and menopause. Primary amenorrhea is the failure of menstruation to begin by around age 15; secondary amenorrhea is the absence of periods for three months or more in someone who previously menstruated.

Causes

Causes span the hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis: functional hypothalamic amenorrhea from stress, exercise, or low energy availability; polycystic ovary syndrome; thyroid disease; hyperprolactinaemia; primary ovarian insufficiency; and anatomical abnormalities such as Müllerian agenesis or an imperforate hymen in primary cases. Genetic conditions, including Turner syndrome and androgen insensitivity syndrome, are also in the differential.

Diagnosis and treatment

Evaluation begins with a pregnancy test and history, followed by hormone assays (including TSH and prolactin) and, where indicated, imaging. Treatment addresses the underlying cause and may include lifestyle modification, hormonal therapy, and measures to protect bone health, since prolonged hypoestrogenism increases the risk of osteoporosis.
