The Gulf of Mexico is an ocean basin and marginal sea of the Atlantic Ocean, largely surrounded by the North American continent. It is bounded by the United States to the north (Florida, Alabama, Mississippi, Louisiana and Texas), Mexico to the west and south (Tamaulipas, Veracruz, Tabasco, Campeche and Yucatán), and Cuba to the south-east. The Gulf is connected to the Caribbean Sea through the Yucatán Channel and to the Atlantic through the Straits of Florida.

Geology and oceanography

The basin formed by seafloor subsidence in the Jurassic period, roughly 200 million years ago. Warm water entering through the Yucatán Channel forms the Loop Current, which exits through Florida as the Florida Current and feeds the Gulf Stream. The Mississippi River discharges large volumes of sediment-laden freshwater into the northern Gulf.

Economy and environment

The Gulf supports major offshore petroleum production, commercial and recreational fisheries (notably shrimp), and dense shipping traffic through ports such as Houston, New Orleans and Veracruz. Environmental pressures include a seasonal hypoxic "dead zone" off Louisiana, coastal wetland loss, and oil spills — most severely the 2010 Deepwater Horizon spill, the largest marine oil spill in history. The region is also exposed to intense hurricanes, including Katrina (2005) and Harvey (2017).

Naming

The name "Gulf of Mexico" has been used since the 16th century. In January 2025 a United States executive order directed federal agencies to use the name "Gulf of America" in domestic usage, while most international bodies and other bordering states retained the historical name.
