BM-21 Grad

BM-21 Grad
Type: Multiple launch rocket system
Origin: Soviet Union
Entered service: 1963
Calibre: 122 mm, 40 launch tubes

The BM-21 "Grad" (Russian: БМ-21 «Град», "hail") is a Soviet truck-mounted 122 mm multiple launch rocket system. Designed in the early 1960s and entering service in 1963, it became one of the most widely used artillery rocket systems in the world, serving in dozens of countries.

Design

The launcher consists of 40 tubes mounted on a Ural-375D (later Ural-4320) 6×6 truck chassis. It can fire its full salvo of 40 rockets in about 20 seconds, with a maximum range of around 20 km for the original rockets and up to 40 km or more with newer ammunition. The rockets are spin- and fin-stabilised and can carry high-explosive, smoke, incendiary, or cluster warheads.

Service history

The Grad first saw combat in the 1969 Sino-Soviet border conflict and has since been used in conflicts worldwide, including wars in the Middle East, Africa, Afghanistan, and Ukraine. Numerous variants and modernised versions have been developed, including improved fire-control systems and new chassis. The system influenced many foreign designs, such as the Romanian APRA-40 and various Chinese types.
