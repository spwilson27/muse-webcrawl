Threshing machine

Threshing machine
Purpose: Separating grain from stalks and husks
Invented: 1784, by Andrew Meikle
Power: Hand, horse, steam, tractor
Superseded by: Combine harvester

A threshing machine or thresher is a piece of farm equipment that threshes grain — that is, it removes the seeds from the stalks and husks — by beating the plant material. Before such machines, threshing was done by hand with flails, an extremely laborious process.

History

The Scottish engineer Andrew Meikle is credited with inventing a practical threshing machine in 1784, using a rotating drum with beaters. Early machines were powered by horses; later, portable steam engines and then tractors drove large threshing rigs via flat belts, and threshing became a communal harvest event in many farming regions.

Legacy

From the late 19th century, reaper-binders and then combine harvesters, which cut and thresh in a single operation, gradually replaced stationary threshers. Threshing machines are now preserved and demonstrated at agricultural shows and steam rallies.
