Central Pacific Railroad

Founded: 1861
Headquarters: Sacramento, California
Key people: The "Big Four": Leland Stanford, Collis Huntington, Mark Hopkins, Charles Crocker
Successor: Southern Pacific

The Central Pacific Railroad was a rail company chartered by the U.S. Congress in 1862 to build the western portion of the first transcontinental railroad across the United States. Building eastward from Sacramento, California, its crews — many of them Chinese immigrant labourers — crossed the Sierra Nevada and the deserts of Nevada and Utah.

Construction

Ground was broken in Sacramento in January 1863. The line's promoters, the "Big Four" — Leland Stanford, Collis P. Huntington, Mark Hopkins, and Charles Crocker — financed construction with federal loans and land grants authorised by the Pacific Railroad Acts. The work involved blasting tunnels through Sierra granite, building snow sheds, and supplying a workforce that at its peak employed more than 10,000 Chinese workers.

Completion

On 10 May 1869 the Central Pacific met the Union Pacific at Promontory Summit, Utah, where the golden spike was driven, completing the first transcontinental railroad. The Central Pacific was later folded into the Southern Pacific system, and its original line remains part of the modern Union Pacific network.
