George I (24 December 1845 – 18 March 1913) was King of Greece from 1863 until his assassination in 1913. Born a Danish prince, he was elected king by the Greek national assembly at the age of 17 and reigned for fifty years, the longest reign of any modern Greek monarch.

Accession and reign

After the deposition of King Otto, the protecting powers Britain, France and Russia selected Prince William of Denmark, second son of Christian IX, who took the name George. Under his reign Greece gained the Ionian Islands, Thessaly, and parts of Epirus and Macedonia. He presided over the first modern Olympic Games in Athens in 1896.

Assassination

During the First Balkan War, George travelled to the newly liberated city of Thessaloniki. On 18 March 1913 he was shot dead by Alexandros Schinas while walking near the White Tower. He was succeeded by his son Constantine I.
