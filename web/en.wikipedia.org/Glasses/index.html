Glasses

Glasses, also known as eyeglasses or spectacles, are vision aids consisting of a pair of lenses mounted in a frame worn in front of the eyes, correcting refractive errors such as myopia (short-sightedness), hyperopia (long-sightedness), astigmatism and presbyopia. Variants also serve as eye protection or, as sunglasses, to reduce glare.

History

Wearable spectacles were invented in Italy in the late 13th century, initially using convex lenses to aid reading. Concave lenses for myopia followed in the 16th century; Benjamin Franklin is credited with bifocals in the 18th century. Modern designs developed in the 20th century with lightweight plastic lenses and frames, progressive lenses, and photochromic coatings.

Lenses and fitting

Lens power is prescribed in dioptres by an optometrist or ophthalmologist after an eye examination. Lenses may be made of glass or plastics such as CR-39 and polycarbonate, with coatings for scratch resistance, anti-reflection and ultraviolet protection. Correct fitting — including lens centration and frame adjustment — is performed by an optician.
