Hypoxia (medical)

Hypoxia
SpecialtyPulmonology, critical care medicine
SymptomsShortness of breath, rapid breathing, confusion, cyanosis
CausesLow oxygen in the air, lung disease, heart disease, anemia

Hypoxia is a condition in which the body or a region of the body is deprived of adequate oxygen supply at the tissue level. Hypoxia may be classified as either generalized, affecting the whole body, or local, affecting a single region. It differs from hypoxemia, which is specifically an abnormally low level of oxygen in the blood, although the two often occur together.

Classification

Hypoxia is traditionally divided into several types. Hypoxemic hypoxia results from a low partial pressure of oxygen in arterial blood, as occurs at high altitude or in lung disease. Anemic hypoxia arises when the blood's oxygen-carrying capacity is reduced, for example in anemia or carbon monoxide poisoning. Stagnant (circulatory) hypoxia results from inadequate blood flow, as in heart failure or shock. Histotoxic hypoxia occurs when cells cannot use the oxygen delivered to them, classically in cyanide poisoning.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms depend on the severity and speed of onset. Acute hypoxia may cause restlessness, headache, rapid heart rate, shortness of breath, and impaired judgment; severe hypoxia can lead to loss of consciousness, seizures, and death. Cyanosis — a bluish discoloration of the skin and mucous membranes — may appear when deoxygenated hemoglobin levels are high. Chronic hypoxia, as in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, can cause fatigue, polycythemia, and pulmonary hypertension.

Causes

Causes include ascent to high altitude (altitude sickness), respiratory diseases such as pneumonia and asthma, cardiac shunts, severe anemia, carbon monoxide exposure, and airway obstruction. In newborns, hypoxia may result from complications of delivery and can cause hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy.

Treatment

Treatment focuses on restoring adequate oxygenation and addressing the underlying cause. Supplemental oxygen is given by nasal cannula, mask, or mechanical ventilation. In specific settings, hyperbaric oxygen therapy may be used, such as for carbon monoxide poisoning or decompression sickness.
