The Yugoslav Wars were a series of separate but related ethnic conflicts, wars of independence, and insurgencies that took place on the territory of the former Socialist Federal Republic of Yugoslavia from 1991 to 2001, leading to its breakup.

Component wars

The sequence began with the Ten-Day War in Slovenia (1991), followed by the Croatian War of Independence (1991–1995) and the Bosnian War (1992–1995), marked by ethnic cleansing and the Srebrenica massacre of July 1995, in which more than 8,000 Bosniak men and boys were killed. The Dayton Agreement ended the Bosnian war in 1995. The Kosovo War (1998–1999) ended after NATO's bombing campaign against the Federal Republic of Yugoslavia, and a final insurgency in Macedonia was settled by the Ohrid Agreement in 2001.

Aftermath

The wars produced six internationally recognised successor states and millions of refugees. The International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia (ICTY) in The Hague prosecuted leaders including Slobodan Milošević, Radovan Karadžić and Ratko Mladić for war crimes and genocide. The conflicts were the deadliest in Europe since World War I, and are often compared with later European conflicts such as the War in Donbas.
