Abstinence

Abstinence is a self-enforced restraint from indulging in bodily activities that are widely experienced as giving pleasure. Most frequently, the term refers to sexual abstinence, abstinence from alcohol, or fasting — abstaining from food and drink. Abstinence may arise from religious asceticism, health considerations, addiction recovery, or personal choice, and it may be temporary or lifelong.

Religious abstinence

Many religions prescribe abstinence: Roman Catholic and Orthodox practice includes fasting and abstinence from meat on certain days; Islam requires fasting from dawn to sunset during Ramadan; Judaism has fast days such as Yom Kippur; and Hindu, Buddhist, and Jain traditions link abstinence from food, sex, or intoxicants to spiritual discipline. Monastic celibacy is a form of lifelong sexual abstinence.

Alcohol and drugs

In addiction medicine, abstinence means completely refraining from the addictive substance; it is the goal of twelve-step programmes such as Alcoholics Anonymous ("teetotalism" in temperance usage). An opposing approach, harm reduction, aims to reduce harmful use without requiring total abstinence. Sudden abstinence after heavy dependence can produce withdrawal syndromes, some medically dangerous, so detoxification may require supervision.

Sexual abstinence

Sexual abstinence is refraining from some or all sexual activity, practised for religious, personal, or health reasons. Abstinence-only sex education, which teaches abstinence as the sole method of preventing pregnancy and sexually transmitted infections, has been widely studied and is controversial: evaluations generally find it ineffective at delaying sexual activity compared with comprehensive programmes, though abstinence itself, when practised, is fully effective.
