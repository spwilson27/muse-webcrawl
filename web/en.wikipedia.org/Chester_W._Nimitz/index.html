Chester W. Nimitz

Chester William Nimitz
Born: 24 February 1885, Fredericksburg, Texas
Died: 20 February 1966, San Francisco, California
Rank: Fleet Admiral, U.S. Navy
Commands: U.S. Pacific Fleet; Chief of Naval Operations

Chester William Nimitz (1885–1966) was a fleet admiral of the United States Navy who commanded Allied air, land, and sea forces in the Pacific Ocean Areas during the Second World War. Ten days after the attack on Pearl Harbor he took command of the shattered Pacific Fleet and led it through the victories of Coral Sea, Midway, and the island-hopping campaigns to Japan's surrender, which he signed aboard USS Missouri in Tokyo Bay on 2 September 1945.

Early career

Born in Fredericksburg, Texas, Nimitz graduated from the Naval Academy in 1905. He became an early submarine officer and an authority on diesel engines, studied in Germany, and later pioneered underway refuelling and the circular formation for fleets. As chief of the Bureau of Navigation he was managing officer assignments when war came.

Pacific War

Appointed Commander in Chief, Pacific Fleet, in December 1941, Nimitz combined calm judgement with trust in subordinates such as Raymond Spruance and William Halsey. His decisions to risk his carriers at the Coral Sea (May 1942) and ambush the Japanese fleet at Midway (June 1942) reversed the momentum of the war. He was promoted to the new five-star rank of fleet admiral in December 1944 and served as Chief of Naval Operations from 1945 to 1947.

Legacy

The Nimitz-class aircraft carriers and the museum at his boyhood home in Fredericksburg commemorate him. He is widely regarded as one of the greatest naval commanders in American history.
