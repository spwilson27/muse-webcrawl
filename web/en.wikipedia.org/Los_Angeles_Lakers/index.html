Los Angeles Lakers

The Los Angeles Lakers are an American professional basketball team based in Los Angeles, California. The Lakers compete in the National Basketball Association (NBA) as a member of the Western Conference Pacific Division. They play their home games at Crypto.com Arena, which they share with the Los Angeles Clippers and the Los Angeles Sparks.

History

The franchise began in 1947 as the Minneapolis Lakers, named for Minnesota's nickname "Land of 10,000 Lakes". Led by center George Mikan, the Minneapolis Lakers won five championships in the NBA's early years. The team relocated to Los Angeles in 1960, keeping the Lakers name despite Southern California's lack of natural lakes.

Showtime era

In 1979 the team was purchased by Jerry Buss, and under coach Pat Riley the "Showtime" Lakers of the 1980s — featuring Magic Johnson, Kareem Abdul-Jabbar, and James Worthy — won five championships with a fast-breaking style of play. Their rivalry with Larry Bird's Boston Celtics became one of the most celebrated in American sports.

Later championships

The Lakers won three consecutive titles in 2000–2002 under coach Phil Jackson with Shaquille O'Neal and Kobe Bryant, and two more in 2009 and 2010 led by Bryant. A 17th championship came in 2020, led by LeBron James and Anthony Davis in the NBA Bubble, tying the Celtics for the most titles in league history at that time. The Lakers are consistently ranked among the most valuable sports franchises in the world.
