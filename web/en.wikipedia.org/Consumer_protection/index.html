Consumer protection

Consumer law covers product safety, fair trading and honest advertising.

Consumer protection is the practice of safeguarding buyers of goods and services, and the public, against unfair practices in the marketplace. Consumer protection law is intended to prevent businesses from engaging in fraud or specified unfair practices in order to gain an advantage over competitors or to mislead consumers. It covers product safety and liability, privacy rights, unfair business practices, fraud, misrepresentation, and consumer contracts.

History

Modern consumer protection developed in the 20th century. In the United States, President John F. Kennedy's 1962 Consumer Bill of Rights articulated rights to safety, to be informed, to choose and to be heard; Ralph Nader's advocacy, including his 1965 book Unsafe at Any Speed, spurred vehicle safety regulation. Regulatory bodies include the Federal Trade Commission (founded 1914) and the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (created by the Dodd–Frank Act in 2010). In the European Union, consumer rights are protected by directives covering unfair contract terms, distance selling and product liability.

Consumer organisations

Non-governmental groups such as Consumers International, the Consumers' Association (publisher of Which?) in the UK, and the Consumers Union (publisher of Consumer Reports) in the US test products and campaign for stronger protections. World Consumer Rights Day is observed on 15 March.
