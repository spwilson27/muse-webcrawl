Airfoil

An airfoil (American English) or aerofoil (British English) is the cross-sectional shape of a wing, blade or sail. An airfoil-shaped body moving through a fluid produces an aerodynamic force, whose component perpendicular to the flow is lift and whose parallel component is drag.

Design

Airfoils are characterised by their chord line, camber (curvature), thickness and the shape of the leading and trailing edges. The NACA airfoil series provided standardised families used widely in twentieth-century aircraft design.

Principles

Lift arises from pressure differences between the upper and lower surfaces, described by circulation theory and related to the angle of attack. At excessive angles of attack the flow separates and the airfoil stalls. Thin airfoil theory gives useful approximations for lift and pitching moment at small angles.
