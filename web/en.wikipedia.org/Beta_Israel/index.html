Beta Israel

Beta Israel (Hebrew: "House of Israel"), also known as Ethiopian Jews, are a Jewish community that lived for centuries in the area of present-day Ethiopia, principally in the Gondar region. They are sometimes historically called "Falashas", a term many consider derogatory.

History

The origins of Beta Israel are debated, with traditions claiming descent from ancient Israelite tribes and from converts influenced by ancient Judaism. The community preserved a form of Judaism based on the Torah (the Orit), without the Talmud, observing Sabbath, dietary laws and festivals. They faced persecution and forced conversion attempts in the medieval and early modern periods.

Emigration to Israel

Following recognition of their Jewish status by Israel's Chief Rabbinate and the Israeli government, most of the community emigrated to Israel, notably during Operation Moses (1984) and Operation Solomon (1991), which airlifted thousands from Sudan and Addis Ababa. Today the majority of Beta Israel live in Israel.
