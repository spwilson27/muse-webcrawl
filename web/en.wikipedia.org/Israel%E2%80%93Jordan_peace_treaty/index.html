The Israel–Jordan peace treaty, formally the Treaty of Peace Between the State of Israel and the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan and sometimes called the Wadi Araba Treaty, was signed on 26 October 1994. It normalised relations between the two countries, ending the state of war that had existed since 1948, and settled land and water disputes. Jordan became the second Arab state, after Egypt in 1979, to sign a peace treaty with Israel.

Negotiations and signing

The agreement followed the 1993 Oslo Accords between Israel and the PLO, which removed Jordan's reason to wait for a broader settlement. The Washington Declaration of July 1994, signed by Prime Minister Yitzhak Rabin and King Hussein in the presence of US President Bill Clinton, ended the state of belligerency and paved the way. The treaty itself was signed at a ceremony in the Arava valley north of Eilat and Aqaba, attended by Clinton.

Contents

The treaty demarcated the international boundary along the Jordan and Yarmouk rivers and the Dead Sea, with small reciprocal territorial swaps; two areas — Baqoura/Naharayim and Al-Ghamr/Zofar — were leased back to Israeli farmers for 25 years (Jordan declined to renew the leases in 2019). It allocated shared waters from the Jordan and Yarmouk systems and the Arava aquifer, established full diplomatic relations and trade, and included security cooperation and agreements on tourism, transport and the environment.

Aftermath

Relations, though often strained by regional crises — earning the description "cold peace" — have been maintained ever since, with particularly deep, if quiet, security and water cooperation.
