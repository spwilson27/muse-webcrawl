Studies on Hysteria

Studies on Hysteria (German: Studien über Hysterie) is an 1895 book by Sigmund Freud and Josef Breuer which contains their findings on the psychotherapy of hysteria. It is generally considered the founding text of psychoanalysis, introducing the "talking cure", the method of free association in embryonic form, and the theory that hysterical symptoms arise from repressed traumatic memories.

Contents

The book opens with a joint preliminary communication, first published in 1893, arguing that "hysterics suffer mainly from reminiscences": symptoms are determined by traumatic experiences whose associated affect has been suppressed, and they disappear when the memory is brought into consciousness and the affect discharged — a process Breuer called catharsis and his famous patient "Anna O." (Bertha Pappenheim) called the "talking cure" and "chimney sweeping".

Five case histories follow: Breuer's case of Anna O., and Freud's cases of Emmy von N., Lucy R., Katharina, and Elisabeth von R. A final theoretical chapter by Freud discusses the psychotherapy of hysteria, including transference and resistance — concepts that became central to psychoanalytic technique.

Reception and legacy

Initial sales were modest and the critical response mixed, but the book's influence grew rapidly. The Anna O. case has been the subject of extensive scholarly debate, including questions about the completeness of her supposed cure. Studies on Hysteria remains a foundational document in the history of psychotherapy and is reprinted in the first volume of the Standard Edition of Freud's works.
