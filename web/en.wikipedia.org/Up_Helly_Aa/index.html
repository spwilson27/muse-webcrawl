Up Helly Aa (Scots: [ʌp ˌhɛli ˈɑː] roughly "Up Holy [Day] All") is a type of fire festival held annually in the Shetland Islands of Scotland in the middle of winter to mark the end of the Yule season. The largest festival is held in Lerwick on the last Tuesday in January, when up to a thousand costumed participants ("guizers") march through the town carrying flaming torches, culminating in the burning of a replica Viking galley.

History

The festival grew out of the older Shetland custom of "tar barrelling", in which young men dragged burning barrels of tar through the town on Christmas and New Year holidays. After tar barrelling was banned in the 1870s on safety grounds, a torchlight procession was substituted, and the first galley was burned in 1889. The festival acquired its Viking theme in the early 20th century, drawing on Shetland's Norse heritage. It was suspended during both world wars and in 2021–2022 because of the COVID-19 pandemic.

The Lerwick festival

The principal celebration is led by the Guizer Jarl, chief guizer, who heads the Jarl Squad dressed as a figure from Norse saga. The day includes the posting of a satirical proclamation (the "Bill") in the town, a morning procession, civic receptions, and the visit of the Jarl Squad to schools and care homes. In the evening, squads of guizers in themed costumes march with torches to the burning site, where the galley is set alight as the crowd sings "The Norseman's Home". Festivities then continue in local halls throughout the night with music, dancing and squad performances.

Other festivals

Smaller Up Helly Aa festivals are held in other Shetland communities, including Scalloway, Sumburgh, Nesting, Bressay, Cullivoe and Uyeasound, mostly between January and March. In 2023, the South Mainland Up Helly Aa admitted women and girls to its procession for the first time, and the Lerwick festival likewise opened participation to all.
