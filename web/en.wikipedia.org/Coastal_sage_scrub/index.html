Coastal sage scrub

Coastal sage scrub is a low, soft-leaved shrubland plant community of coastal California and northern Baja California. It occurs on dry, rocky slopes and mesas near the coast, in a Mediterranean climate of wet winters and long dry summers.

Vegetation

Characteristic plants include California sagebrush (Artemisia californica), black sage, white sage, California buckwheat, and prickly pear cactus. Many shrubs are drought-deciduous, dropping leaves in summer, and are highly aromatic.

Ecology and threats

The community supports species such as the coastal California gnatcatcher and coastal cactus wren. Coastal sage scrub has been heavily reduced by urban development in the Los Angeles, Orange County, and San Diego regions, and remaining habitat is fragmented; conservation plans such as Natural Community Conservation Planning aim to protect it.
