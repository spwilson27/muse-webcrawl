Boxing Day

Boxing Day is a major shopping and sporting day in many Commonwealth countries

Boxing Day is a holiday celebrated on 26 December, the day after Christmas Day. It originated in Great Britain and is celebrated in a number of countries that previously formed part of the British Empire, including the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, and South Africa. It is also Saint Stephen's Day in the liturgical calendar.

Origins

The name is traditionally explained by the custom of giving "Christmas boxes" — gifts of money or goods — to servants, tradespeople, and the poor on the day after Christmas, when servants were permitted to visit their families. Another proposed origin is the opening of alms boxes in churches, whose contents were distributed to the poor.

Customs

Modern Boxing Day traditions include shopping in post-Christmas sales, sporting events — notably football fixtures in England, cricket Test matches in Australia (the Boxing Day Test at Melbourne), and horse racing — and outdoor pursuits such as fox hunting (now usually trail hunting) in Britain. In some countries, if 26 December falls on a weekend, the holiday is moved to the following Monday or Tuesday.
