Carvacrol

Carvacrol
Chemical formulaC₁₀H₁₄O
AppearanceColourless to pale yellow liquid
SourcesOregano, thyme, marjoram oils

Carvacrol is a monoterpenoid phenol, C₁₀H₁₄O, that is a major constituent of the essential oils of oregano, thyme, marjoram, and other plants of the mint family. It is an isomer of thymol and gives oregano oil much of its characteristic pungent, warm odour.

Properties and uses

Carvacrol has been studied for antibacterial, antifungal, and antioxidant activity, which underlie traditional uses of oregano and thyme oils as food preservatives and folk remedies. It is used as a flavouring in foods, a fragrance ingredient, and a feed additive in livestock production as an alternative to antibiotic growth promoters. In laboratory research it interacts with TRP ion channels and has been investigated for anti-inflammatory and analgesic effects.

Sources

Oregano oils vary widely in carvacrol content, from trace amounts to over 80 percent depending on species and origin. Carvacrol can also be synthesised from cymene or from carvone.
