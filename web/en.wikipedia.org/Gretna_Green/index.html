Gretna Green is a parish and village in the south of Scotland, in Dumfries and Galloway near the border with England and the mouth of the River Esk. It is historically famous for runaway marriages, and remains a popular wedding destination.

Runaway marriages

Under the Marriage Act 1753 (Lord Hardwicke's Act), marriages in England and Wales required parental consent for those under 21, banns or a licence, and a church ceremony. Scots law, by contrast, allowed "irregular" marriages by simple declaration before witnesses, with boys of 14 and girls of 12 able to marry without consent. Gretna Green was the first village in Scotland reached by the main coaching road from London via Carlisle, and from 1754 eloping couples flocked there to be married by whoever would officiate — most famously the blacksmiths at the Old Blacksmith's Shop, whose ceremonies "over the anvil" became legendary. The tollkeeper and other villagers also acted as "priests".

Decline and modern era

An 1856 act requiring one party to have resided in Scotland for 21 days cooled the trade, and in 1940 irregular marriage by declaration was abolished in Scotland. Nevertheless, Gretna Green retains its romantic reputation: thousands of weddings are held there each year in the Old Blacksmith's Shop (a visitor attraction since the 1880s), the Gretna Hall and other venues, and the village's economy is substantially built around weddings and tourism.

Transport

The village lies just off the A74(M) motorway and is served by Gretna Green railway station on the Glasgow South Western Line. The neighbouring town of Gretna, a planned settlement built during the First World War for munitions workers, lies just to the west.
