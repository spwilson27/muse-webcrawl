Stellite is a range of cobalt–chromium alloys designed for wear resistance. The alloys may also contain tungsten or molybdenum and a small amount of carbon. Stellite is a registered trademark of Kennametal Inc.; the alloys were originally developed by the American inventor and metallurgist Elwood Haynes in the early 1900s as an alternative to rust-prone steel cutlery and tool alloys.

Properties

Stellite alloys are hard, tough and exceptionally resistant to abrasion, galling, corrosion and high temperatures, retaining their hardness at red heat. Their wear resistance comes from hard carbide particles dispersed in a cobalt-rich matrix. They are non-magnetic and difficult to machine, so parts are usually cast, ground, or deposited as a hardfacing layer by welding.

Applications

Classic uses include lathe cutting tools, saw teeth for cutting wood, valve seats and faces in engines and chemical plant, steam-turbine erosion shields, and the hardfacing of industrial valves. Stellite 6 is the most widely used grade for general wear applications. Cobalt-based alloys of this family have also been used in dentistry and orthopaedic implants, and Stellite-tipped machine gun barrels were valued in the Second World War for resisting erosion.
