Sixth generation of video game consoles

Sixth generation consoles Consoles: Dreamcast (1998); PlayStation 2 (2000); GameCube (2001); Xbox (2001)
Best seller: PlayStation 2 (over 160 million units)

The sixth generation of video game consoles began with the release of Sega's Dreamcast in Japan in November 1998. The era's home consoles also included Sony's PlayStation 2 (2000), Nintendo's GameCube (2001) and Microsoft's Xbox (2001), which marked Microsoft's entry into the console market. The generation was characterised by the adoption of optical disc media, the growth of online gaming services and increasingly powerful 3D graphics.

The consoles

The Dreamcast pioneered built-in modem support for online play but was discontinued in 2001 as Sega withdrew from the hardware business. The PlayStation 2 became the best-selling home console of all time, selling over 160 million units, aided by its built-in DVD player, backwards compatibility with PlayStation games and a vast software library. The GameCube, Nintendo's first console to use optical discs, adopted proprietary miniDVD-format discs, while the Xbox offered a built-in hard drive and the Xbox Live online service, launched in 2002.

Handhelds

The dominant handheld of the era was Nintendo's Game Boy Advance (2001) and its backlit successors, the Game Boy Advance SP and Game Boy Micro. Competitors included the short-lived Nokia N-Gage, which combined a mobile phone with a games console.

Legacy

The sixth generation ended as the seventh generation began with the Xbox 360 in 2005, though PlayStation 2 production continued until 2013. The era saw landmark titles such as Grand Theft Auto III, Halo: Combat Evolved and Super Smash Bros. Melee, and established online console gaming as a mainstream feature.
