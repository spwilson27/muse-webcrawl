The Nürburgring is a motor racing complex around the village of Nürburg in the Eifel mountains of Germany. It is famous for the Nordschleife ("North Loop"), a 20.8 km circuit built in the 1920s through forested hills, long regarded as one of the most demanding race tracks in the world; Jackie Stewart nicknamed it "The Green Hell".

History

The original circuit opened in 1927. The Nordschleife hosted the German Grand Prix until 1976, when Niki Lauda's near-fatal crash highlighted its dangers for Formula One. A modern, safer Grand Prix circuit opened in 1984 on the site of the old South Loop and has hosted F1 and other championships.

Today

The Nordschleife is used for endurance races including the Nürburgring 24 Hours, industry testing and manufacturer lap records, and public "tourist drives" (Touristenfahrten) on which road cars may lap for a fee.
