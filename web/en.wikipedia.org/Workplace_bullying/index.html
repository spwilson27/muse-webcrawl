Workplace bullying

Workplace bullying is a persistent pattern of mistreatment from others in the workplace that causes either physical or emotional harm. It can include behaviors such as verbal abuse, offensive conduct, work interference, and intimidation. The term was popularized by British journalist Andrea Adams in the early 1990s, and the phenomenon was studied extensively by psychologist Heinz Leymann, who used the term mobbing for group harassment at work.

Workplace bullying can involve exclusion, unreasonable demands, and persistent criticism.

Forms

Common forms include excessive monitoring, withholding information needed to do the job, assigning impossible deadlines, public humiliation, spreading rumors, and social exclusion. Researcher Gary Namie distinguished bullying from incivility by its intensity, frequency, and duration, and by the power imbalance between perpetrator and target.

Effects

Targets may experience anxiety, depression, post-traumatic stress symptoms, and physical illness. Organizations face costs through absenteeism, turnover, and reduced productivity.

Legal status

Some countries, including Sweden, France, and Australia, have laws addressing psychological harassment at work. In the United States there is no general federal anti-bullying statute for workplaces, though hostile work environment law applies where harassment is tied to a protected characteristic.
