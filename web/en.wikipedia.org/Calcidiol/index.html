Calcidiol

Also known as: Calcifediol; 25-hydroxyvitamin D; 25(OH)D

Formula: C27H44O2

Type: Vitamin D metabolite (prohormone)

Calcidiol, also called calcifediol or 25-hydroxyvitamin D, is a prehormone produced in the liver from vitamin D (cholecalciferol or ergocalciferol) by the enzyme vitamin D 25-hydroxylase. It is the main circulating form of vitamin D in the blood, and measuring serum calcidiol concentration is the standard clinical test of a person's vitamin D status.

Metabolism

After vitamin D is synthesized in the skin or ingested, it is transported to the liver and hydroxylated to calcidiol. Calcidiol is carried in blood bound to vitamin D-binding protein and is converted in the kidneys (and some other tissues) by 1α-hydroxylase into calcitriol, the active hormonal form that regulates calcium and phosphate absorption and bone metabolism. Calcidiol itself has a half-life of about two to three weeks in circulation, much longer than calcitriol, which is why it best reflects vitamin D reserves.

Clinical use

Low serum calcidiol defines vitamin D insufficiency or deficiency, associated with rickets in children, osteomalacia and osteoporosis in adults, and secondary hyperparathyroidism. Calcifediol is itself prescribed as a drug in some countries: because it bypasses hepatic 25-hydroxylation and is more potent, milligram for milligram, than ordinary vitamin D supplements, it is used to treat deficiency in patients with liver impairment and certain kidney disorders, and to raise vitamin D levels more rapidly than cholecalciferol.
