Aden Emergency

The Aden Emergency, also called the 14 October Revolution, was an armed insurgency fought by the National Liberation Front (NLF) and the Front for the Liberation of Occupied South Yemen (FLOSY) against the British colonial presence in the Federation of South Arabia and the Aden Protectorate between 1963 and 1967.

Background

Aden, a strategic port at the entrance to the Red Sea, had been a British colony since 1839. In 1963 Britain merged the colony with the surrounding protectorates into the Federation of South Arabia. Arab nationalist sentiment, fuelled by Egypt's Gamal Abdel Nasser and the nearby North Yemen civil war, drove growing resistance.

Course of the conflict

The emergency began on 14 October 1963 with an uprising in the Radfan mountains and a grenade attack on the British High Commissioner at Aden airport in December. Guerrilla warfare spread through Aden's districts, and in June 1967 mutinies by South Arabian police and army units killed British soldiers of the Argyll and Sutherland Highlanders in the Crater district, which British forces later reoccupied. The last British troops withdrew on 30 November 1967.

Outcome

The NLF seized power and established the People's Republic of South Yemen, the first and only Marxist-Leninist state in the Arab world, which in 1990 unified with North Yemen to form the Republic of Yemen. Some 237 British personnel were killed during the emergency.
