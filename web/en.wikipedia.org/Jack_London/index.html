Jack London

John Griffith London (1876 – 1916), born John Griffith Chaney, was an American novelist, journalist and social activist, best known for the adventure novels The Call of the Wild and White Fang.

Born12 January 1876, San Francisco, California
Died22 November 1916, Glen Ellen, California
Notable worksThe Call of the Wild; White Fang; Martin Eden
MovementNaturalism

Life

Raised in Oakland in poverty, London worked as an oyster pirate, sailor, gold prospector in the Klondike and factory labourer before turning to writing. His Klondike stories made him one of the first American authors to achieve international fame and fortune from magazine fiction.

Works and views

London wrote more than twenty novels and hundreds of short stories, including the semi-autobiographical Martin Eden and the dystopian The Iron Heel. A passionate socialist, he also wrote reportage such as The People of the Abyss about London's East End slums. He died aged 40 at his ranch in Glen Ellen, now a state historic park.
