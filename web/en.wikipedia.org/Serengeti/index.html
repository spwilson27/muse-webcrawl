The Serengeti ecosystem is a geographical region in Africa spanning northern Tanzania and southwestern Kenya, covering about 30,000 square kilometres. Its name derives from the Maasai word siringet, "the place where the land runs on forever." The region hosts the largest terrestrial mammal migration in the world and is renowned for its vast savanna plains, acacia woodlands, and extraordinary concentrations of wildlife.

The Great Migration

Each year, roughly 1.5 million blue wildebeest, together with hundreds of thousands of zebras and gazelles, move in a great circular route between the Serengeti and Kenya's Maasai Mara in search of fresh grazing, driven by seasonal rains. The migration, including perilous crossings of the Grumeti and Mara rivers where crocodiles wait, is one of the most famous wildlife spectacles on Earth.

Wildlife

The Serengeti supports large populations of lions — among the largest in Africa — as well as leopards, cheetahs, African elephants, black rhinoceroses, giraffes, hippos, and more than 500 bird species, including ostriches and kori bustards. The Ngorongoro Conservation Area, with its huge volcanic caldera, lies at the ecosystem's southeastern edge.

Conservation

Serengeti National Park was established in 1951 and inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1981. The work of Bernhard Grzimek, author and filmmaker of Serengeti Shall Not Die (1959), was instrumental in early conservation. Ongoing challenges include poaching, habitat pressure, and proposed infrastructure through migration corridors.
