Ice wine

Type: Dessert wine
German name: Eiswein
Harvest temperature: Typically −7 to −8 °C or colder
Main producers: Germany, Austria, Canada

Ice wine (German: Eiswein) is a type of dessert wine produced from grapes that have frozen while still on the vine. Freezing concentrates sugars and acids because the water in the berries turns to ice and is left behind when the grapes are pressed while frozen, yielding a small volume of intensely sweet, high-acid juice.

History and production

Accidental frozen harvests were recorded in Franconia in 1794, and deliberate Eiswein became an established, rare specialty in Germany, classified at Beerenauslese-level ripeness or higher. Grapes — typically Riesling in Germany, and Vidal or Cabernet Franc in Canada — hang into deep winter, netted against birds, and are picked by hand at night at temperatures below about −8 °C in Canada, whose VQA rules are strict. Canada, especially Ontario's Niagara Peninsula, is now the largest producer. Fermentation of the very sweet must is slow, producing wines around 8–13% alcohol with residual sugar often above 150 g/L.

Style

Ice wines show concentrated flavours of peach, apricot, honey, and tropical fruit balanced by bracing acidity, and age well. Because yields are tiny and risks high — rot, animals, a winter that never freezes — prices are correspondingly high.
