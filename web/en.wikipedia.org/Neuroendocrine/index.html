Neuroendocrine

Neuroendocrine systems and cells bridge the nervous and endocrine systems: neuroendocrine cells receive neuronal input and respond by releasing hormones or neuropeptides into the bloodstream. The term also describes the study of how the brain controls endocrine activity and how hormones in turn affect the brain and behaviour.

Major systems

The hypothalamus is the principal neuroendocrine interface. Magnocellular neurons of the paraventricular and supraoptic nuclei release oxytocin and vasopressin from the posterior pituitary, while parvocellular neurons secrete releasing and inhibiting hormones that govern the anterior pituitary, controlling growth, reproduction, thyroid function, and the stress response via the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis.

Diffuse system and tumours

Neuroendocrine cells are also scattered through the gut, lungs, and other organs, where they regulate digestion and local blood flow. Tumours arising from these cells — neuroendocrine tumours, including carcinoid tumours and pancreatic neuroendocrine tumours — may secrete hormones causing distinctive syndromes such as carcinoid syndrome.
